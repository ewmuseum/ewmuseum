Renamed   European Championship 6 March 2001 — PCW scrapped the Rising Star Championship for the European Championship, in its own on-air voice: Tyler Nelson — "the Rising Star Title was caught in a numbers game. With our addition to the Euro TV Satellite Networks, it only seemed natural to create a European title, so the decision was made to scrap the Rising Star strap." The belt came back thirty-three days later, at Spring Fling 2001 of 8 Apr 2001: "the European Belt is retired, and the Rising Star Title has been reinstated in PCW!" TWO READINGS, recorded both (#sources-disagree): in period the fed staged a SCRAP-AND-CREATE (two belts, the Rising Star champion Slade still billed "the current PCW Rising Star Champion" on Turmoil 27 of 3 Apr 2001 while The Chef carried the European belt), while the fed's own later newsdesk trivia line — carried on its site from 2002 on — calls it a RENAME: "That PCW once renamed it's Rising Star title to the European title, and was mostly held by Hall of Famer, the Chef?" The event is filed as the later voice names it and the in-period staging is recorded here; the European Championship carries its one reign either way, and the Rising Star reigns are unchanged. Source: PCW's own results page for Turmoil 24, 6 Mar 2001.
Unified into   Cruiserweight Championship 17 September 2002 — PCW merged the Rising Star Championship into the Cruiserweight Championship on this night and said so in its own voice: "the Cruiserweight and Rising Star titles are merged", "This is the last match ever where the Rising Star title will be seen", and "The following match is scheduled for one fall! And will crown the undisputed Primetime Championship Wrestling Cruiserweight Champion!". Anarchy (Rising Star) beat Brandi (Cruiserweight) and left with one belt. The fed's own title log carries no row for the merge. PCW REVIVED the Rising Star Championship four months later — Shanghaied 2003 of 26 Jan 2003 crowns "the first Rising Star Champion in a very long time" and Fusion 108 of 7 Feb 2003 calls it "the newly revived Rising Star division" — so this records a dated event, not a final state. Source: PCW's own results page for Turmoil 96, 17 Sep 2002.

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

PCW's Rising Star Championship was first won by Dan Hollywood on 26 November 2000. The fed's own record carries 36 reigns on it, across 35 different champions. The longest is Dorn Tracer's, 58 days from 21 February 2003. Six of its reigns ended outside a match — stripped from the holder or vacated. PCW scrapped it for the European Championship on 6 March 2001 and brought it back a month afterwards. PCW merged it into the Cruiserweight Championship on 17 September 2002, and revived it four months later. James 'Lunar' Edwards held it at the end of the surviving record.
