Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Created for a fourteen-man tournament that began at the Curt Hennig Memorial Show and ended with a three-way at Vox Hiberionacum on 29 March 2003, where Doc Silver became the first champion. Psycho took it from him in a four-way at the first pay-per-view and Silver escaped a razor-wire cage to win it back three weeks later. Shawn Hart took it from him two falls to one at the Spirit of NGEN on 16 August 2003, winning the last fall, a Last Man Standing match, on the special referee's ten count. Hart kept it against Luster at New World Madness in October 2003 and against Michael Manson in a Chicago street fight at Ghoulish Games, the fed's pay-per-view of 15 November. Scotty Michaels took it from Hart in a Winter Wonderland match in Central Park at Merrython on 30 December 2003, with Luster's help; Sean Edmunds won it from him in a sixty-minute Ironman match at Great Expectations on 18 February 2004, five falls to four; and Alex Wylde, the fed's President, won it from Edmunds at Road to Glory on 10 March 2004. Michael Manson took it from Wylde in a ladder match at the Superbowl of Wrestling on 18 April 2004, with Joey Melton as special referee, and kept it against Richard Gideon at BOMB Voyage on 9 June 2004. Manson kept it against Felix Red at Cherry Blossom Chaos II on 15 August 2004. Manson kept it against El Arco Iris, two falls to one under Lucha rules, at Guerra Interminable on 3 November 2004.

The fed's broadcast says this title and the North American Title "resume and carry on the lineage of the old NGEN World Heavyweight Title" (Vox Hiberionacum, March 2003), and a commentator calls it "descended from both the NGEN & BAD World Heavyweight Titles" (Rage in the Cage). These are the fed's claims; its first champion was crowned in 2003 and no earlier reign is carried onto this title. The fed's own post of its champions, headed "NGEN/WFW World Heavyweight Title History" (December 2003, kept up in later years), begins with Doc Silver at Vox Hiberionacum and carries no NGEN reign; it dates each change a few days after the first post of the broadcast that told it. The same list dates the Michaels, Edmunds and Wylde changes to 31 December 2003, 25 February 2004 and 21 March 2004, a few days after the first posts of the broadcasts that told them (30 December 2003, 18 February 2004 and 10 March 2004), which this wing's rows use. The same list dates the Manson change to 29 April 2004, the day the post telling the ladder match went up; this wing's rows use the date of the first post of the broadcast, 18 April 2004.
