Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The promotion's own top prize, and the belt that opened it: "The Idol" Bill Dynamite beat "The Dealer" Dylan Dunn two falls to one at eWo's first pay-per-view in September 2004, in a tournament final refereed by Erin Jacobs, and the fed's own history calls that night the true start of its success. Dunn is separately recorded as an eWo World Heavyweight Champion, on the same Hall of Fame page. The belt came back with the promotion: Cody Clark pulled it out of a briefcase at BWF Danger Zone 2007 and handed it to The Legend, who put down the BWF Championship to take it. Legend lost it to Czecher in a four-way at Hell & Back, regained it from Czecher at Body Count with a new finisher, then defended it through Supremacy, License to Thrill and Guilty As Charged before Frank Murdoch became the first man ever to pin him in eWo, at The New Black — the reign eWo closed on, in November 2007, with no further word. C.M. Vyrus was reigning by the 2008 return's own sixth of April; Kyle Cross was carrying it into a barbed-wire main event at the record's own last word.

The fed's own Hall of Fame page lists "eWo Champion" and "eWo World Heavyweight Champion" as separate lines for separate men. Whether those are one lineage under two names or two belts is not settled anywhere in the surviving record, and both readings are left standing.
