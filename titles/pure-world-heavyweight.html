Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

PURE announced a World Heavyweight Championship in 2002 and did not award it. The fed built a championship-history page for the belt and filled it with a single line — a first champion to be determined at a pay-per-view in the Tokyo Dome — and rewrote that line twice without ever naming a holder; the belt was still vacant when PURE re-opened in January 2003. It was decided on 2 March 2003 at Final Destinies, in the Tokyo Dome after all, by a four-man tournament Jesse Ramey won over Eddie Mandrake. Ramey defended it a week later at Budokan Hall against Jason Marah, and the surviving record carries no later change.

Attested by the fed's own title-history page (/promotions/pure/archive/world-heavyweight-championship-history.html) and by its own division rankings (/promotions/pure/archive/columns.html). That page is rendered from newsdata board 78 and was rewritten in place three times: the held copy reads "#1 - To be determined at 'Future Destinies' (8/14/02)"; the deposit's January 2003 layer reads "#1 - To be determined at a later date (???)"; and the deposit's last layer no longer carries this belt at all, having been overwritten with the tag titles' own history. Source: Argyle's PURE deposit (collection 0159).

Jesse Ramey2 March 2003—Won the four-man tournament PURE ran to crown its first World Heavyweight Champion, beating Eddie Mandrake in the final by pinfall.
The belt was announced in 2002 and stood vacant until this night: PURE's own news row of 18 February 2003 has the fed's vice president say "all the titles will be on this line, including the vacant PURE Heavyweight Championship". Defended at Budokan Hall on 9 March 2003 against Jason Marah, and the surviving record carries no later change. The fed billed the belt as both the PURE World Heavyweight Title (its champions page, its own title-history page, Ramey's roster card) and the PURE Heavyweight Championship (the Final Destinies and Budokan scripts) in the same weeks.
