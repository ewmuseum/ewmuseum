NCCW World Tag Team Championship

Placard

Promotion   NCCW (1998)
Active   1998–1999

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Changed hands nine times across the fed's own opening fifteen months, including one team discarding the belts on the same night they won them and a masked team's own mid-reign unmasking, before Coast-to-Coast Connection's second reign closes the fed's own dated record.

European Towers23 May 1998—Won the inaugural World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
Coast-to-Coast Connection17 June 1998—Defeated European Towers for the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
The fed's own titles page notes the team left the league undefeated (4-0-0) and forfeited the belts to Untouchables after losing them at the 4 July pay-per-view.
Untouchables4 July 1998—Defeated Coast-to-Coast Connection for the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
New Austrian Powers29 July 1998—Defeated Untouchables for the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
The fed's own titles page notes the team threw away the titles immediately after winning them and called a tag-team tournament “to remove all doubt of titleholders' legitimacy.”
Don Cameron & Geoffrey Quartz ("The Dominator" Don Cameron, "GQ" Geoffrey Quartz)26 August 1998—Won the tournament final called after New Austrian Powers discarded the titles, per the fed's own titles page.
J. Oliver Bradley & “Suicyco” Gauge Thompson9 September 1998—Defeated Don Cameron & Geoffrey Quartz for the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
New Austrian Powers (2nd)30 September 1998—Regained the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page — the same card on which Assassin won the World Heavyweight Championship.
The Insiders31 October 1998—Defeated New Austrian Powers for the World Tag Team Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
The fed's own titles page: the masked team soon unmasked as Les Canadiens (billed in-fiction as Alain MacDonald and Marc Denis), who then renamed themselves Dominatrix; when they left NCCW in January 1999 a lottery tournament decided new champions at the February pay-per-view.
Coast-to-Coast Connection (2nd)20 February 1999—Won the lottery tournament called after Dominatrix (formerly The Insiders, formerly billed Les Canadiens) left the fed, per the fed's own titles page. The fed's own video-library page confirms a four-way title match at this event among Hired Guns, Coast-to-Coast Connection, J. Oliver Bradley & “Missile” Mike Divine, and “Shooting Star” Ryan Douglas & Purple Pride.
No further Tag Team title change is recorded before the fed's own dated record ends (6 August 1999); the last champion this collection can confirm.
