ACW Spirit of ACW Championship

aka Spirit of ACW, ACW Spirit Championship

Placard

Promotion   ACW (2002–13)
Active   1993–2013

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The belt ACW claims as its oldest and buried twice. Its own history segment dates it to 2 April 1993 and names four champions before the fed split it into the United States and Television titles; its own belt page lists six reigns from June 1993 to its retirement in March 1995, and the two do not agree on the names. It carries a rule no other belt in the promotion had: whoever holds it picks the stipulation every match it is defended under, match by match or across a whole reign. It came back on 26 January 2006, carried to the ring by the man billed as its first champion, and the Television Championship match already booked to open Legends II became the match that crowned it instead. Andy Sharp won it and held it until the fed's belt page vacated it on 10 July 2006. When ACW came back, Steve Knox won it in a ladder match on the Courage of 9 April 2009, and nine more reigns followed in the next two years; Dean Denton pinned Andy Sharp for it at Dia de los Muertos on 27 February 2011, the last card of that run. On the fan board's run Seymour Almasy, billed as Orphan, came to the Nippon Budokan in January 2012 as its champion and kept it against KIRU, and by February 2013 the fed called him “the longest-reigning Spirit of ACW in history”; Jesse Ramey was billed as its brand-new champion that month.
