Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

"Lethal" Lex Taylor held the AAWC Television title in what the fed's own broadcast called a vice-grip, broken once by Big Mack and regained in a tag team rematch. It came off him at Oktoberfest '96 in a triangle match against Xavier Virile and Bloodhunt, under a stipulation that the first man to pin anybody won it. Taylor had the match won — he hit the Lex Plex on Virile and raised his own hand — but Bloodhunt had tagged himself in a half-second earlier, put Taylor down with the Headshot, tagged Virile's arm and rolled him, barely conscious, on top of the champion. Dr. Silver's entire group came to the ring afterwards, and Silver handed the belt to Virile himself in exchange for a bottle of water: the night the AAWC's own commentary worked out that Virile had been Silver's from day one. Nine months later it was around Troy Windham's waist, booked into a four corners defence at World War Games 3 and billed on a rival promotion's own card as the belt he carried into a World title match there.

Attested by the fed's own Oktoberfest '96 (Night 1) card of 25 October 1996 (promotions/nfw/archive/news-1996-10-25-aawc-oktoberfest-96-night-1.html).
