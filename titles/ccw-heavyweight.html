Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Nineteen men are named as having held it, and the fed named them itself: Revolution opens on a montage of nineteen former champions flickering past with the belt, from The Supreme Machine through Ghettodogg, Hardcore Gorilla Mask and El Mierda to Jason McAllister. Almost none of those reigns is recoverable. What the record does hold is the shape of the belt's own crises — a 104-day reign by Phil Burner in 2000 that the fed still called its record, a month in the spring of 2002 when its own rankings listed the title vacant and two men tied at the top, Blayze losing it at Revolution after pulling the referee into a charge, and Legacy taking it off Jason Dread in January 2005 and putting him through a table on his way out of the ring. That is where the record stops.

The alliance's own member profile of the fed calls it the World Heavyweight Championship in its champions block and the CCW Heavyweight Title in its own history on the same page; the fed's own ring announcer reads it out at Revolution as the CCW Heavyweight Championship of the World. Every form is the record's own.
