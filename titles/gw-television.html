Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A weekly television championship the federation defended almost every show, and one night of it survives in this record: 21 August 2005 at Evolution, in Las Vegas, where the masked Emoticon rolled the champion Dawn McFarlane up and the referee — the federation's own word for him is stunned — handed the belt over. He was still carrying it when the record runs out. The rest of the belt's life is in Global's own scripts and not yet chained here.

THE CHAIN OF THIS BELT IS NOT BUILT. Global Wrestling's own show texts carry at least twelve further changes and defences of this championship between 2004 and 2006 — among them a champion stripped for not defending it weekly, the vacancy that filled, and six other holders — every one receipted line by line in the federation's own scripts and none of them yet minted. What this record holds is one recovered reign inside a longer history, not the belt's lineage.

Emoticon21 August 2005—Beat the champion Dawn McFarlane, the federation's own match list recording the finish as a victory-roll counter. The served PTCpedia entry tells the same night from the other side — "catch her off guard with a victory roll and become the Global Television Champion" — and lists the belt among his accomplishments as "Global Wrestling Television Championship (1-time, as Emoticon)".
Defended once on the record, against Elia Smith on Evolution of 29 August 2005, when Tessa Windsor knocked the challenger out behind the referee's back and he pinned her with La Magistral; he was still billed Television Champion at Whiplash. McFarlane had held the belt thirteen days, having taken it from Shadow at Master of the Mat VI on 8 August 2005. No later change of the championship survives in Global's own text, which by January 2006 lists him among the champions who left; the reign is left open at the record's end rather than closed on a guess.
