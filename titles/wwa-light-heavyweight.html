Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Never tracked on the fed's own title-history ledger, and inconsistently billed by the fed's own broadcasts as the "Light Heavyweight" and "Cruiserweight" Championship in different shows of the same era (Eddie Van Dorn — same man, same weight, same hometown billing — defends it as "Cruiserweight" on 4 March 2002 and loses it as "Light Heavyweight" on 15 April 2002, then immediately calls himself "the Cruiserweight champion" in the very next breath as the new champion — the same belt, not two). The surviving record opens on Tuesday Showdown, 22 February 2000, where the reigning champion Mystique lost it to Denrol in a lumberjack-style main event after Denrol swapped places with a disguised Pitt Viper — the show's own commentary crowned the win as making Denrol the WWA's first-ever Grand Slam Champion, not the belt's own first titleholder. "Sky Flyin" Jimmi Zion was champion by the earliest served 2002 shows; Eddie Van Dorn won it from him off-panel before 25 February 2002 and held it through a 4 March 2002 defense, before Trent LeBeaux (wrestling as Tracer Bullet under his own registered ring name) beat him for it in the 15 April 2002 Monday Night Meltdown main event. Grunge took it from LeBeaux on 12 August 2002, the belt's own last confirmed change in the recovered record. Ramsey Kaine stole it from Grunge with a cheated rollup on 23 September 2002, then no-showed his own next defense; the fed restarted the match as a straight bout between the two men who did turn up, and EkoSyde beat Grunge for it on 7 October 2002. EkoSyde -- unmasked on-air as Enigma, who also held the WWA Extreme Championship at the same time -- lost it in a 2-on-1 handicap match on 6 January 2003, where the referee, unable to award a single champion, named Chance and "Sky Flyin" Jimmi Zion joint champions of both belts at once -- the belt's own last change directly staged in the recovered record. A later show (10 February 2003) refers back to Jimmi Zion having won the belt outright from Chance at an untraced "Rage In A Cage 6," ending the joint reign; that PPV itself was never captured, so the win is reconstructed from the later show's own retrospective billing rather than a staged title change. The belt's own end is dated precisely, even though its middle stretch isn't: Commissioner Raine retired it on-air on 8 March 2003's Fiesta, unifying it with the Extreme Championship into the new WWA GenX Championship, with Zion's own unified reign dated a day earlier in the fed's own ledger. One clean pre-2000 change is now recovered: Jak "The Boil" Lancer lost the title to Griffin (wrestling masked as "Lone Wolf") in an I Quit match on wwa-1998-11-08 — Lancer's own preceding win date remains unrecovered.
