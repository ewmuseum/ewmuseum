Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

At Wrestlebowl I (31 January 2005), Avery Prosser — fresh off seizing NFW's own North division with his “Seven Deadly Sins” — produced the real UWA's own vacant World Championship belt on air and declared he would “RESURRECT this title” in the name of the man he called its rightful champion, presenting it to Troy Windham as “your DIVINE RIGHT.” Windham, taking the mic, claimed sole credit for having “MADE the UWA” seven years earlier. It was a political gift, not the result of a contest, and it did not survive the night: in the brawl that followed, Craig Miles pulled the belt off the fallen Windham and handed it to Tom Adler, and NFW's own text leaves the title “held up/vacant” for the rest of the season. It came to a decision at Midnight Madness 5 (18 November 2005): Adler won the vacant title from Windham by disqualification, after Zoltan and August De La Rossi interfered and Windham sprayed him with an unspecified liquid — and Adler surrendered it within minutes, in the very next segment, picking up a microphone and saying only “I Quit” before handing it to Eddie Mayfield without further contest. Mayfield held it through two more defenses in the surviving record without it changing hands again: a no-contest against Eli Flair at UWA Pirate TV (24 December 2005), and a no-decision against Adler at Wrestlestock I (20 April 2006). No later NFW document shows the belt again. The prop is staged throughout as the same belt the real UWA left vacant in 1997, not a new one — commentary calls it a title “resurrected... for this season,” and Windham's own promo reaches back to that history directly. The real UWA's own ledger (uwa-world) records only what its own 1992–1997-era evidence supports; this row holds NFW's later fiction about it.

Staged inside NFW's own continuity as a revival of the real UWA's own vacant, never-resolved World Championship (see that title's own record, uwa-world) — an in-fiction storyline device, not a claim that the real 1992–1997 Philadelphia UWA continued operating. Crowned across two of NFW's own served cards, Wrestlebowl I (31 Jan 2005) and Midnight Madness 5 (18 Nov 2005).
