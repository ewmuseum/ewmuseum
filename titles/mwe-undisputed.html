Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The promotion's own top belt. David Whitman won it in a ten-man battle royal at Crowning Glory II on 10 August 2008 and lost it, reclaimed it and lost it again across the next four months, to Sonny Williams at Turmoil and to Brian Michaels on a December Monday Night Mayhem. At Adrenaline Rush II on 28 December 2008, in a Three Stages of Hell match at Madison Square Garden, D.C. beat Michaels to unify the MWE Championship and the World Championship into a single Undisputed Championship. Whitman took it back in a triple threat two weeks later and held it into the summer of 2009, billed at Tribulation that May the longest-reigning champion in MWE history — two apparent losses along the way, to Ace Cannon in March and to Joseph Sharpman in May, both controversial finishes the fed's own text and its own on-air ruling declined to recognize as title changes. His real, final loss came on 8 June 2009, to Sinc Mercier in a Triple Threat also involving Kevin Hardaway. Mercier held it at least into that August, retaining at Independence Day and, after an overturned double-DQ, at Crowning Glory.

The fed's own championship ledger (board topic 7550985, "Championship Histories") heads this lineage "MWE Undisputed Championship History" while its show scripts bill the same belt as the MWE World Heavyweight Championship, the MWE World Championship and the MWE Championship interchangeably; both billings are recorded rather than one resolved into the other. Between the November 2008 brand draft and the Adrenaline Rush II unification the fed staged a second, Genesis-exclusive world title, won by D.C. on the 12 December 2008 Genesis and absorbed into this one — the ledger records no separate reign history for it.
