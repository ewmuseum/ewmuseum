Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A championship PURE ran through the last three months of its record, and the one belt it moved between its two touring brands. Senji Yulav was its first champion, billed as such from the fed's own January 2003 shows onward. Kohana took it from him at Final Destinies in the Tokyo Dome on 2 March 2003, by submission. A week later at Budokan Hall, Raizo Tenzan beat Kohana for it in ten minutes and thirty-nine seconds, in a match PURE booked to settle which of its two brands the belt would follow; Tenzan took it from the referee's hands rather than be presented with it. The surviving record carries no later change.

Attested by PURE's own show scripts for Final Destinies (2 March 2003) and the Budokan Ikusa (9 March 2003), by its own news rows of 7 and 18 February 2003, and by the roster cards for Senji Yuvlav ("First Ever Pure All Asia Champion") and Raizo Tenzan. Whether this belt is the same championship as the PURE Asian National Title the fed ran a tournament for in May 2002 is not settled by the record: the names differ, the eras do not overlap, and Yuvlav's own card calls him the first ever holder. Filed as a distinct title, and the question is recorded rather than answered.
