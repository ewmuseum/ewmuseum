NCCW World Cruiserweight Championship

Placard

Promotion   NCCW (1998)
Active   1998–1999

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Limited to wrestlers under 230 pounds and rated by the fed's own FAQ a close second to the World Title: changed hands eight times in fifteen months, twice by forced vacancy (an injury, a departure) and the rest inside the ring, with Superstar's own three separate reigns the title's own throughline.

“Bullet” Ricky Revolution23 May 199825 July 1998Won the inaugural World Cruiserweight Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
Unable to defend the belt due to injury; the fed's own titles page dates the vacancy 25 July 1998 and describes a two-week tournament to crown a new champion. NCCW's own Family Tree page and Dec 1998 recruitment ad separately name Ricky Revolution a Classic Championship Wrestling alumnus.
Superstar15 August 1998—Won a tournament final against “Suicyco” Gauge Thompson to fill the vacancy left by Ricky Revolution's own injury, per the fed's own titles page.

Superstar (2nd)16 December 1998—Regained the World Cruiserweight Championship from “Suicyco” Gauge Thompson, per the fed's own titles page.
Aaron Bagwell17 January 1999—Defeated Superstar for the World Cruiserweight Championship, per the fed's own titles page.
Forfeited the title on leaving the fed shortly after winning it, per the fed's own titles page, which records a four-way match for the vacant belt. NCCW's own Dec 1998 recruitment ad (already held) separately names Aaron Bagwell among its roster.
Superstar (3rd)20 February 1999—Won a four-way match for the World Cruiserweight Championship, vacated by Aaron Bagwell's own departure, per the fed's own titles page. The fed's own video-library page separately bills this as a four-way among Suicyco, Superstar, Crisis and “Silver Dragon” Nick Sivart.
“Shooting Star” Ryan Douglas29 May 1999—Defeated Superstar for the World Cruiserweight Championship at the fed's own first-anniversary pay-per-view, per the fed's own titles page. The fed's own video-library page confirms the same match, billed “Superstar vs. 'Shooting Star' Ryan Douglas.”
“Shotgun” Gauge Thompson21 July 1999—Defeated “Shooting Star” Ryan Douglas for the World Cruiserweight Championship, per the fed's own titles page. The fed's own table repeats the “Re-Ignition PPV” label for this row despite a date nearly two months after, and a city different from, the fed's own confirmed 29 May Re-Ignition (Madison Square Garden, New York) — likely the fed's own transcription slip; recorded as the document states, not resolved.
Successfully defended the title against Crisis on a disqualification finish at the fed's own Battle in Seattle pay-per-view, 7 August 1999 — a champion retains on a disqualification, so no title change follows. The last champion this collection can confirm from the fed's own dated record.
