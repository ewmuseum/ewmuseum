PURE Asian National Title

Placard

Promotion   PURE (2002)
Cadence   standing

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A belt PURE ran a tournament for and never crowned inside the surviving record. Its own columnist argued the division belonged to Inphino Blitz on three counts and then argued against himself, calling it a bit of an oxymoron to have a gaijin as Asian National Champion; three of the four other men he ranked in it — Shirata Rinjiro, Masanobu Ozaki and Steven Windsor — were released within three weeks. The fed's own pay-per-view announcement of 21 May 2002 promised the first Asian Champion would be named at Future Destinies, alongside the first World Champion. The held record stops before either.

Attested by the fed's own division rankings (/promotions/pure/archive/columns.html) and by its own news page, which reports a first-round tournament match at Ikusa III (/promotions/pure/archive/news.html). PURE ran a separately-named PURE All-Asia Championship through the first three months of 2003, whose own first holder is billed on his own roster card as "First Ever Pure All Asia Champion"; whether that belt is this one renamed or a second championship is not settled by the record, and the two are held as distinct titles. Source: Argyle's PURE deposit (collection 0159).

The museum holds no reigns for this title yet.
