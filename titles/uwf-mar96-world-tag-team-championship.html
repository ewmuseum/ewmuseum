Team Canada ("The Hammer" Trevor Hextall, "Bonebreaker" Bob Jablonski)4 October 2003—
Won the UWF World Tag Team Championship from the Ghost Dancers, adding it to the team's own North American Tag Team Championship. Not defended in a 25 October 2003 rematch against the Ghost Dancers: a fine-print contract clause limited that match to the team's own UWF North American Tag Team Championship alone, which the team lost; this title itself was never on the line and is unaffected. Retained again on 12 November 2003 over Eugene Robinson and Youth Gone Wild, a cross-promotional pairing with no tag title claim of its own. By 5 June 2004 the title was billed on the Privateers; no surviving document in the held record states the match, date, or method by which the reign changed hands. Recorded as a reign of record from the promotion's own billing and commentary; no surviving document in the held record states the match in which Erik and Chad Grimsson won the titles from the Privateers at Rise to Power. Lost the titles to the Hands of Death (Serge Annis, Caliban) in a bout not read by this collection, recapped by Alex Extreme's own tirade on UWF Meltdown, 20 July 2005.
The Ghost Dancers ("Screaming Drillbit" Jason Keening, "Banshee" Michael Keening)unknown4 October 2003
Recorded as a reign of record from the promotion's own repeated billing of this team as champions; no surviving document in the held record states the match in which the titles were won. Retained by default on 9 August 2003 when a three-way defense was ruled a no-contest, before losing the titles to Team Canada by pinfall on 4 October 2003.
The Privateers (Edward Drake, Jacob Drake)unknown2005-05-19 (circa)
Recorded as a reign of record from the promotion's own billing; no surviving document states the match in which the Privateers won the title from Team Canada. Retained at Gold Rush 2004 in a triple threat against the Ghost Dancers and the Hands of Death (Serge Annis and Caliban), Jacob Drake pinning Michael Keening after the Fraternity Boys' triple Conchairto took Annis out of the match; the match's own stipulation held that the title would change hands only if the Privateers themselves were pinned or submitted. Retained again over Strictly Business (Andrew Tucker, Mike Sebastian) by pinfall on 6 October 2004, Edward Drake pinning Sebastian off a DDT while Jacob Drake illegally held down Tucker's own feet. An impromptu challenge from Alex Martinez and "Mr. Excitement" Alex Extreme on 13 November 2004 was ruled a no contest after "Agony" Michael Augustine ran in and broke a Singapore cane over Extreme's head. Retained again over Fury (Rage and Spice) by pinfall on 1 December 2004.
The Hands of Death ("Epitome of Evil" Serge Annis, Caliban)unknownunknown
Recorded as a reign of record from the promotion's own billing; no surviving document in the held record states the match in which the titles changed hands from the Sons of Cacophony. Challenged by Erik Grimsson to a Descent Into Hell defense at Gold Rush on 23 July 2005, after he kidnapped and chained up Caliban inside the structure itself. The reign ended when Serge Annis turned on and ended the career of his own tag partner Caliban at an unread Gold Rush, per the promotion's own commentary on UWF Saturday Night Rampage, 18 March 2006.
The Sons of Cacophony (Erik Grimsson, Chad Grimsson)unknown—
