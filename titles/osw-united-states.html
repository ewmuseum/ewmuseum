Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Cruiserweight belt under a new name. Kid Wonder had called it the Soy Boy Memorial Title; Tyler Nelson won it from him at Crowning Glory 2 on 16 March 2003 and renamed it the United States Title a week later, and Jane Reagan had him vacate it on 4 May 2003, after he won the World Heavyweight Title. The Chaos In The Cage match of 25 May 2003 ended in a simultaneous pin on D-Kay Calyx by Politically Incorrect, William S. Powell and Reginald V. Lovecraft, and the fed named both men co-holders; its lawyers found no way to void it, and the pair were allowed to defend the belt as a tag team. Azala Zameer won it from them, billed on that card as the OSW American Championship, in a Contract versus Contract match at Ascension on 29 June 2003. Ivan Stanislav's roster card gives him the American Championship from 24 August to 14 September 2003. Who held it after him is not recorded; the fed was still keeping a contenders list for the American Title on 28 October 2003.

Named on the fed's own title history (/promotions/osw/archive/ose-title-history-wrestling-site-july-2003-2003-07-21.html): the Cruiserweight Title, which Kid Wonder, champion from 19 October 2002, renamed the Soy Boy Memorial Title, and which Tyler Nelson renamed the United States Title on 23 March 2003. The pages name it three ways: the United States Title or Championship (the title history; Tyler Nelson's and Politically Incorrect's cards; the 27 and 28 May 2003 news posts), the OSW or OSE American Championship (Ascension; the Powell, Zameer and Stanislav cards) and the American Title (/promotions/osw/archive/osw-expands-contender-lists-2003-10-28.html).

Tyler Nelson16 March 20034 May 2003Won the Soy Boy Memorial Title from Kid Wonder at Crowning Glory 2 and renamed it the United States Title on Sunday Night Slam, 23 March 2003. On the 30 March 2003 Slam (osw-slam-2003-03-30) he is billed "the United States Champion" and keeps it over Scott Taylor, by cheating. Vacated it on order of Jane Reagan on 4 May 2003, a week after winning the World Heavyweight Title from Ivan Stanislav.
Neither Crowning Glory 2 nor the 4 May 2003 Slam is served; the dates are the title history's. The 30 March card's own commentary says he "just won the damn title" and shows his "newly fashioned United States title".
Politically Incorrect25 May 200329 June 2003Declared co-holders after the Chaos In The Cage match: Powell and Lovecraft, who interfered in the match, made a simultaneous pin on D-Kay Calyx, and because the only rule was that the first pin or submission won, both were named co-titleholders. The fed's lawyers found no proof the match contract was violated (U.S. Title Follow-up, 27 May 2003), so the pair were allowed to defend the belt as a tag team. On 28 May 2003 they handed the belt to OSW officials, refusing to carry a title with the American flag on it, said two new belts were being made, and promised to reveal them on Slam at a first defence against Asa Fountain; no served card shows that defence. Lost it to Azala Zameer at Ascension (osw-ascension-2003-06-29), where they are billed "the OSW American Champions".
The 25 May 2003 Slam is not served; the date is the title history's, which the 19 May news post ("Chaos In The Cage to determine the new United States Champion") and the 27 May post ("over the past 36 hours") fit. Lovecraft's card of 24 May 2003 still reads "None in OSW" for titles; the team card of 20 June 2003 reads "OSW United States Champions".
Azala Zameer29 June 2003—Won the Contract versus Contract match for the OSW American Championship at Ascension: he drove Lovecraft into the corner with the Spear of Zameer, then pinned Powell after the Ivory Stampede; his own OSW contract was on the line against Lt. Mandela's. His roster card of 8 September 2003 lists "OSE American Championship" under Title History.
The end of the reign rests on Ivan Stanislav's card alone: no served show or news post carries the change on 24 August 2003 (the 21 August Overtime card does not mention the belt).
Ivan Stanislav24 August 2003—Held the American Championship from 24 August to 14 September 2003 by his own roster card's Title History line.
No served show or news post carries the win or the loss, and no served page says who took the belt from him or whether it was vacated. The fed's news post of 28 October 2003 still lists the American Title among its three top-contender lists, so the lineage did not end here.
