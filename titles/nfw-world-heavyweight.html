Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NFW's own top belt for the whole of its run, and the one prize the Ultratitle never replaced. Bloodhunt was the inaugural champion, beating Ricky Zane at Crash 3 in Baltimore in October 2000 after both men won a thirty-man Random Rumble. Zane took it off him at Banned 4 Life in an angle the ledger records in one word — "Zanegate!" — and the belt then went through the fed's own messiest stretch: Rick Ryconik held it for nothing at all, beaten on the night by Armando Montezuma in a pin Commissioner Michael Manson ruled questionable enough to hold the title up, and Shane Southern won the vacancy in a fourway at Crash 8. Bloodhunt's second reign ended with the title defunct from August 2003 until Manson reinstated it in April 2004 and then won it himself. After that it settled: Doc Silver, Felix Red, Kin Hiroshi — who forfeited it by walking out of a threeway at Crash 43 — Dan Ryan, Nova, and Joe The Plumber, whose 860 days are the longest reign on the ledger, ended when President Eddie Mayfield suspended him for refusing a drug test. Castor Strife won the vacancy in a four-man tournament and lost it to Impulse in a scaffold match billed as The Cutting Room Floor. The ledger's own last word leaves Impulse reigning.
