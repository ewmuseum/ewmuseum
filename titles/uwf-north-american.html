Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Restarted from the GIWF, and blank on the earlier-recovered 2003 title-history page under the note “Title Brought Back From the GIWF Abyss & Restarted by New UWF Owner Al Crud” — a fuller 2004 copy fills it in, and a later rpcommunity.com continuation, captured 2007, carries it a further year past that. Seymour Almasy held it twice inside a single day (won, stripped, won back); James von Doom's own reign is credited 265 days by the source's own count, though its own dates only support 224 — recorded as printed, unreconciled. Merged into the Intercontinental belt in January 2004, pulled back out that August, and unified with it in-show by Tessa Windsor's own win over Chris Storm at Sins of the Past (2006), the fed's own champions overview afterward listing her holding both.

The belt left Seymour Almasy on a Wednesday Night Universal card whose results are not located: the fed's own title history records Killer Dude “Defeated Seymour Almasy - WNU” on 5 Nov 2004, and no copy of that show survives. The fed's later cards say how it went: Killer Dude “went into the gauntlet match on Wednesday Night Universal, where he fell in his chances at the first two titles, only to shock Seymour Almasy and capture the North American Title” (Sunday Afternoon Supremacy, 21 Nov 2004). Almasy's own promo of 22 Nov 2004 lays the loss on Mike Montgomery: “I lost the North American Title to a man who’s been nominate twice for the ever-prestigious UWF Jobber of the Year award. All because of Mike Montgomery and his steel chair.” He beat Killer Dude in their non-title Contest of Champions qualifier on 28 Nov 2004.
