## Platform

web

## Users

**Participants come first.** These are the people who played: the handlers, bookers and fedheads of play-by-mail,
tabletop, the dial-up online services, Usenet and the message-board e-feds. They come to find their own characters,
their feds, their title reigns and the shows they wrote, and to see that history held with care. The museum succeeds with them when they want to be part of it badly enough to deposit their own archives
(drives, databases, saved posts). Several of the largest collections arrived that way.

**Second:** researchers of fantasy wrestling and online-community history, and curious visitors.

**Later: strangers arriving cold.** The site is invite-gated today (one word opens it; `/crawler` is the only ungated
path), and a **public open is planned**. Design has to work for someone who was let in by a friend now, and for
someone who knows nothing about e-wrestling later.

## Product Purpose

THE E-WRESTLING MUSEUM preserves the history of fantasy wrestling: the fiction-making hobby of characters, promotions
and stories, not fantasy-sports games about real wrestling. Promotions arrive one at a time, each documented as deep as
the surviving record allows, "never as deep as the current holder's own attachment to it wants." It is a preservation
project, "not a highlight reel and not a shrine."

Success means the record is complete and honest enough that participants trust it with what they still hold. Quality is
the collection engine: much of this history can't be scraped, and it only comes in when the people who kept it choose to
give it.

## Positioning

- **A database-shaped museum, not a fan site or wiki.** The site is a rendering of a structured registry
  (`data/*.json`: promotions, characters, units, titles, reigns, shows, matches, appearances, roleplays, awards,
  memorials), regenerated by script.
- **Governed by a written constitution** (`CURATION.md`) before any schema: evidence grading, provenance on every fact,
  verbatim serving of source texts, no automatic merges of identities, absolutes-only prose, privacy and takedown law.
- **Flat and fair.** Every index is a flat alphabetical (or, for shows, chronological) list with search and filters,
  never a grouping that favours whichever collection got here first. "A 500th promotion costs the same, and sorts on the
  same terms, as the first eleven." The homepage names no promotion; it is a portal of category doors.
- **Bias named, not hidden.** Each collection arrives through one contributor's vantage. The museum says so, and thin
  holdings are "a record of whose drives were kept," not a verdict on importance.

## Operating Context

- **Two curators, two museums, one schema.** The Janitor runs this museum. Argyle (a former fed owner, and the creator of
  the earlier e-wrestling museum this one follows) donated his backstage database and runs his own live museum in parallel.
  Work lands on both sides at the same time; his OK governs his server, never ours.
- **Collections come from** donated databases and drives, Usenet walks (rec.sport.pro-wrestling and .fantasy), and dead
  sites recovered from the Wayback Machine and Common Crawl.
- **A landing cycle publishes the site.** Waves of data land in windows; validators and a gate (`npm run gate`) must pass
  before anything publishes; hosting is Cloudflare R2 behind a Worker that enforces the invite gate.
- **Pages are generated.** Tens of thousands of HTML pages are produced and stamped by scripts (`npm run chrome`, the
  data builders, `cross-reference.mjs`). Site-wide design changes go into templates, `style.css`, `js/` and the stamping
  scripts, never into hand edits of individual pages.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Plain HTML/CSS, no framework, no build step**, "readable and editable by any future contributor, human or AI."
  Index pages ("instrument pages") fetch JSON indexes at runtime (`characters-index.json`, `matches-index.json`,
  `appearances-index.json`, `reigns-index.json`) and render search and filters client-side.
- **Scale.** As of 2026-10-03: 2,140 promotions (wings), 29,019 characters, 10,060 shows, 15 memorials, 4,630
  frontier rows (promotions named in the record but not yet held), about 55,000 HTML pages under `promotions/`. Every
  list and filter design must hold at these sizes and keep growing.
- **Source texts serve verbatim.** Readability wins over faithful reproduction of a source's formatting: formatting may
  change (paragraph breaks restored, lines joined), content changes stay rare (privacy, and rarely content) (the
  Janitor, D246, 2026-10-03).
- **Privacy is enforced in the data**, not left to design: a ledger of real names and contact details, redaction markers
  in served text, handler credits shown as first name and initial at most, and takedown or de-identification "honored
  without argument." Design must never surface what the data redacts.
- **Memorials are dedication-level.** The museum is dedicated to **Sean Simon Williams (1985–2018)** and **Jaymes
  Faulkner (1972–2022)**; the In Memoriam hall (`in-memoriam.html`, `memorials/`) is open to more names.
- **Dates are the record's dates.** A promotion's years are its own founding and closing; the museum's own landing date
  is labelled separately and written in words (the Janitor, 2026-10-03).
- **Open decisions:** when and how the public open happens; an accessibility standard has not been set.

## Brand Commitments

- **Name:** THE E-WRESTLING MUSEUM, kept as a homage to the earlier archive it follows. The line: "a museum of fantasy
  wrestling, built one collection at a time." The About page opens "Forty years of cheap pops." (from the 1985 play-by-mail era to now).
- **Voice:** careful, honest and labelled ("hold it the way a museum holds anything: carefully, honestly, labeled, and
  without pretending the past was other than it was"). Dry and warm, never gushing. Prose speaks in absolutes: a promotion is
  described on its own terms (what it was, when, what it did), never relative to the museum's other holdings or its size
  (`CURATION.md`, the epitaph rule: "would it still be true and non-awkward with 500 promotions held?").
- **The era's own words stand:** "e-wrestling" and "e-fed" wherever the record uses them.
- **The curator is "the Janitor"**, the persona the museum speaks through.

## Evidence on Hand

- The registry: `data/*.json` and `data/shards/<wing>/`.
- Served collections: `promotions/<wing>/` (archive pages, roleplays, shows), `characters/`, `units/`, `titles/`,
  `shows/`, `rps/`, `memorials/`, `collections/`, `essays/`.
- The law and craft: `CURATION.md`, `docs/LAW-INDEX.md`, `docs/PROCESS.md`, `docs/SCHEMA.md`, `docs/OPERATIONS.md`.
- The About page (`about.html`): mission, provenance and bias statement, takedown policy.
- **Absences not to fabricate:** no testimonials, visitor numbers, press or endorsements exist. The holdings are the
  proof; never invent participants' words.

## Product Principles

1. **Quality is the collection engine.** Every page should make a participant proud to see their history there, because
   that is what brings the next archive in.
2. **The record leads.** Show what the sources show, graded and cited; depth follows the surviving record, never the
   holder's attachment.
3. **Care before exposure.** Privacy, memorials and takedowns outrank completeness of display.
4. **No collection is the centre.** Flat, fair indexes; the 500th promotion gets the same treatment as the first.
5. **Readable over facsimile.** Keep the words; make them easy to read.
