In Memoriam

Commemorated, not just documented.

This commemorates the members of this community who died too soon, as remembered by the people who knew them. This is the only part of the museum that attributes real names of private individuals, when the community acknowledged those names. Any member of the memorial will be removed or anonymized upon request by family or friend without hesitation, and any name brought to us for memorialization will be honored with the same care, whether we knew them or not. The foremost priority of this museum will always be to honor the members of this list with love and respect.

ANDY DORAN · 10 November 1982 – 15 December 2015

An English teacher and football coach in Allentown, Pennsylvania, whose obituary noted that he "enjoyed a wrestling based creative writing circle." He wrote as Marcus Broussard and "Cool Hand" Luke Kinsey, served on EMWC's staff until its close in 2003, and was named co-winner of Best Handler in the newsgroup's 2004 year-end awards. He was so beloved within the community that his death led JTF Squaretable to cancel its 2015 Hall of Fame awards, the first break in nearly a decade. When the awards returned in 2017, they came with a new wing of the Hall of Fame honoring out-of-character accomplishments: the Andy Doran Wing. the Janitor

CHAD DUPREE · 11 March 1975 – 27 August 2004

A mainstay on the FW circuit, he wrote as "Mr. Main Event" Rob Sampson, ran the EUWC and co-owned GXW. In a fellow handler's own words, Chad Dupree was "a phenomenal roleplayer, a dedicated federation owner, and a good friend," one who "would stick with me and give me advice when it seemed as though nobody... was on my side." After his death, EUWC named its annual tournament the Chad Dupree Memorial Cup; by 2006, the Cup was being staged across the wider circuit. Years after he died, handlers were still posting on his birthday to raise a toast in his honor. the Janitor

CHRIS WILLIAMS · d. 4 May 1999

The author of "Coldblooded" Kain Threat and a former PWW executive and Television Champion who died in an automobile accident. After his death, the PWW Chris Williams Television Championship was named in his honor. For at least eight years thereafter, the wider e-fedding community and "an ever increasing circle of friends" gathered together to pay respect to his memory with an independent tournament: the Chris Williams Memorial Cup. the Janitor

DANIEL QUIRK · 19 July 1982 – 28 May 2005

He ran the EWF in the late 1990s until it became part of CWF-Pro, after which he wrote as the Punisher. CWF-Pro inducted him into its Hall of Fame in 2001. He became a professional wrestler in 2002 and worked independent wrestling shows across the Northeast as Spider. He died after an accident at an Ultimate Championship Wrestling event in Massachusetts in 2005, aged 22. On June 11, 2005, Connecticut Championship Wrestling dedicated its WrestleJam 2 supercard to Quirk's memory. the Janitor

ED WILSON · 1976–2001

The author of "The Lone Gunmen" characters and the owner of the Championship Wrestling League, described as "a league that visually changed fantasy wrestling." A husband and a doctor, he was killed in 2001, struck by a car as he left the hospital where he worked. The fWo's Ed Wilson Memorial described him as the glue that held the CWL together, who "was always behind the idea that people would get along better by working together." Years after his death, the fWo continued the CWL in his honor. the Janitor

FRANK A. MARANO JR. · d. 1993

The founder of the Universal Wrestling Alliance, a play-by-mail league of the 1980s, who laid the groundwork for generations of future characters. A friend remembered Marano's UWA as "the cream of the crop," with Marano running eight regional UWAs at once, from Canada to Japan, through Hodgkin's disease and a stroke. After cancer took his life in 1993, the community honored him with the Frank A. Marano Jr. Memorial Cup; first held after his death in 1993, the cup was still honoring his legacy at least as of 2010. the Janitor

JAYMES FAULKNER · 1972–2022

A father, husband, brother, uncle and all round amazing person. the Janitor

JEFFREY WARD · 1982–2026

A devoted husband who dedicated his life to his five boys. He wrote the character Angelo Deville for the better part of a decade under the iconic "Sympathy for the Devil" entrance, and was widely recognized as one of the greatest writers in the history of Primetime Central, winning its third Golden Turnbuckle Tournament and the 2005 Wrestler of the Year award. In his own words, every character he wrote was "Deville playing different roles." For a man who wrote so much at such a high level, it takes very little for anyone who knew him to recognize him in text: >=) the Janitor

JOE SMALLZE · d. April 2002

The author of Soulja Smallz and the interfederational stable The Legends for six years. His cousin, who he introduced to the game, described him as "one of the greatest competitors to ever roleplay," who "wanted to have the most fun he could in the shortest amount of time." He unfortunately died of a drug overdose in 2002. Furiously Addictive Wrestling (FAW) retired its Television Championship in his honor, and its roster paid tribute to him on Distortion. the Janitor

MIKE SANZONE · d. 2026

He wrote the character Diamond Rose in Elevate Pro Wrestling, which dedicated an Adrenaline broadcast to him on May 29, 2026, honoring his "presence, passion, and support." the Janitor

PATRICK LITTLE · d. 31 December 1999

He created ICW, Internet Championship Wrestling, in June 1998, running it as Big Dawg and wrestling as Strider, its World Champion. As ICW went under he wrestled for GCW as Titan and Strider, later serving as its Commissioner. He died on 31 December 1999, a day after an accident. GCW dedicated Survivor Match '00 to him and inducted him into its Hall of Fame in 2001. The man who built GCW wrote that "without him, there would be no us," and signed his posts "RIPPL": Rest In Peace Patrick Little. Written by AI, 2026

PAUL SCANLAN · 1978–2011

Of Brentwood, Essex, he wrote Paul Cain and Damien Kahn through NWC: Atlantic, NWC: South, PRIME, CCW and Lone Star Wrestling, where Paul Cain held the NWC: Atlantic Heavyweight and CCW World Heavyweight titles. Paul Cain also wrestled for Ultimate Championship Wrestling, NeWA and Simcoe County Championship Wrestling. A fellow writer remembered him as one of the few who befriended a newcomer, and as "a champion in life." He died on 9 April 2011. Five days later PRIME's ReVolution 244 paid him tribute and dedicated an Intense Championship to his name, the one PRIME title his writing had never won. Written by AI, 2026

RON OLSON · d. 2004

He wrote The Rock, a World Heavyweight title contender in the Almost Illegal Wrestling League, in the years when a league lived on an Angelfire page and a weekly card. A fellow handler remembered him as an average New Englander who grew up in Tewksbury, Massachusetts, played high-school sports and college baseball, and was killed in a car crash in 2004 at twenty-two. Written by AI, 2026

SEAN SIMON WILLIAMS · 1985–2018

An avid lifelong professional wrestling fan who also loved role-playing games and card games, and stood against injustice wherever he found it. He would have loved this. the Janitor

STEELMOOSE · d. 1996

The Extreme Wrestling Association dedicated its work to him in June 1996, soon after he died.

STEVEN W. PAGANO · d. 19 April 2001

He ran World Class Championship Wrestling, its vice president and then its chief executive, and lived “two years past the date doctors had given him.” His friends' memorial event became a tournament in his name.

TIM FITZGERALD · d. 20 March 2008

The creator of WrestleCrazy Entertainment and Mayhem Wrestling Entertainment, and the reason — in his own community's words — that a whole family of federations existed at all: "without Tim Fitzgerald, there would have been NO WcE, WWA, eWa, SWA, WHQ, or MWE." He appeared on his own shows as Rocky Stevens and wrestled in WHQ and, in 2007, in MWE as John Austin, but the work he was loved for happened behind the scenes: welcoming newcomers other boards had chased off, coaching writers one at a time, and posting shows week after week. He died on 20 March 2008, the day MWE closed its doors. His roster rebuilt the company in his honor and marked the first anniversary with a memorial supershow — "TF" armbands in the ring, his portrait hung beside the champions he made, an annual award in his name for the craft he loved best — and a roleplay titled In Tim We Trust, the board’s own pick for roleplay of the year in 2009. Written by AI, 2026
