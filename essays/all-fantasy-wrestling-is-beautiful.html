All Fantasy Wrestling Is Beautiful

8 October 2026

by the Janitor · 8 October 2026

Postal mail Forum Audio Video game Virtual world In person Wiki Website Text Figures Avatars Tabletop Competitive roleplay Angled Open fed Single author Troupe English

I don't want to get into the habit of filling this site with my own essays, as I doubt anyone will come here to see what Some Guy thinks. But there is a curatorial decision for this site that I think is worth some explanation, because I don't think it will be obvious why it was built in this way.

When I started accumulating this, I tried to think through the boundaries of what the museum should include. I repeatedly found myself deciding every boundary I thought up was indefensible gatekeeping, and so I settled on the most core curatorial decision here: all fantasy wrestling is beautiful, and it all deserves to be memorialized.

In effect, this means that the museum does not gatekeep on rulesets, mediums, platforms, character types, languages, etc. If you created your own wrestling canon from any seat in the business — wrestler, booker, promoter, manager, commentator — it's beautiful and in scope for preservation.

I expect this to mean that quite a few communities who feel no kinship with each other would find it weird to sit alongside one another here. I suspect the English-language players of play-by-mail games like the Imaginary Wrestling Association don't feel much kinship with the competitive/collaborative e-wrestling community of "e-feds" like PRIME Wrestling; or the single-author booking diary community on forums like BeTheBooker; or the figure wrestling community with standouts like Brian Myers' and Matt Cardona's podcast-driven Figure Wrestling Federation; or the video game CAW communities on sites like CAWS.ws and Fandom's CAW Wiki, focused on games like WWE2K or Fire Pro Wrestling; or the virtual-world community like Second Life's Virtual Wrestling Entertainment; or the AI-driven community like Creative Force Wrestling; or the tabletop gaming community with games like World Wide Wrestling RPG; or the Spanish-language community with their unique GIF-battle form through directories like Foroactivo or ...

I could keep going much longer there. I have no idea if I'm right to include them all, but if I'm wrong, go slice up the archives and separate them for yourselves! I certainly won't mind. But that said, I think it's endemic to nerdy subcultures that communities fracture and fight over rather trivial differences, and I'd rather highlight our shared creativity and passion here than our differences.

I'm probably old enough now that I should be turning the corner from "the generation before me was mean and out of touch!" to "the generation after me is ruining everything we created!" but all I feel in building this is inspiration and appreciation for every form of the game and every generation who participated. I'm fascinated by studying the history of how the game grew into what I was familiar with, and I'm inspired watching how the game grew after I left it.

So that's my essay. Right or wrong, you at least know where I stand on why I built it this way.

Within this site, all fantasy wrestling is beautiful, and all of us matter.

Written here, 8 October 2026.
