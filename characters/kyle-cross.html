Biography (UWF (2001–07))

Born August 22nd, 1976, Kyle Cross was an exceptional athlete throughout his childhood, and a junior hockey sensation in his teens, destined for the NHL. In 1992, be began playing for the Calgary Hitmen of the Western Hockey League, a team partially owned by wrestler Bret Hart. Cross played well in Calgary, and widely became known as one of the strongest and fastest wingers in the game. A year later, and Cross was already being talked about for the NHL Draft, and after declaring that he was ready to go up to the NHL, speculation ran wild that he would be the number one pick overall. However, just one month before the draft, Cross was injured in a motorcycle accident in Ottawa. Surgery was required, and the knee was reconstructed. He missed the draft, a fact that devastated him emotionally. Facing months of rehab to his damaged left knee, he felt totally alone. It was during this time that Cross was contacted by Bret Hart, who offered a alternative to straight medical rehabilitation. It turns out that a student at the wrestling school run by two of Brets brothers had just undergone similar surgery, and Bret suggested that Kyle come to Calgary and the two men could work out together. Cross agreed and flew back to Calgary. During the fall and winter of '94-'95, Kyle Cross and John J. Nightside (the injured wrestling student) went from rehabbing their injuries into full-out wrestling training, joining up with The Hart Brothers Wrestling School that spring. Everyone was surprised at how quickly Kyle picked up the sport, and quickly adapted to the Canadian submission style that the Hart Brothers taught. In September 1995, Bruce Hart worked out a deal that would send Kyle, John and other students from the school on a six-month exchange to New Japan Pro Wrestling for six months while several junior NJPW superstars would come to Calgary to train at the school. Upon arriving in Japan, the bookers in NJPW suggested that Cross don a mask. Cross agreed, creating The Dark Spyder persona. He participated in many NJPW matches, and during the fall and winter of 1995 worked with some of the best wrestlers in the world, including Ric Flair, Sting, Shinya Hashimoto, and a man who would play a very important role later in his career Keiji Muto. In the end though, the six month exchange was coming to a close, and it looked like Cross would end up returning to Canada and signing with a small, indy promotion. Then he got a phone call from Atsushi Onita. Atsushi Onita, widely viewed as the father of modern extreme, liked The Dark Spyder, and wanted to sign Cross to an extended contract with his FMW promotion. Cross agreed and was introduced to the brutal world of deathmatch wrestling. During those years, Cross (still known as The Dark Spyder) worked with an eclectic cast of characters, including Onita, Mr. Pogo, Hayabusa, The Gladiator Mike Awesome, Masato Tanaka and others. T.D.S. quickly developed a following in Japan, bouncing back and forth between 'garbage' promotions like FMW & Big Japan and national promotions like AJPW, NJPW & NOAH. He eventually lost the mask and became known as 'The Guardian Angel' Kyle Cross. Again, he enjoyed considerable success. Yet Kyle was always looking for the opportunity to cross over to a North American wrestling audience. It would take nearly a decade of barbed wire and spilled blood before the opportunity presented itself. On January 15th, 2004... The Universal Wrestling Federation held TERROR IN TOKYO - REGAL RUMBLE FIVE. The main event was a 60-Man Rumble event. Promoters wanted to use some local puroresu talent in the Rumble, and contacted Cross, who by this time had become a true hardcore legend in Japan, and one of the most popular gajin wrestlers in the country. Cross realized that although it was originally set up to be a one time appearance, if he could impress UWF officials and fans in the Rumble, they would have no choice but to offer him a contract.
