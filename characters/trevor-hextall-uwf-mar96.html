"The Hammer" Trevor Hextall

aka Trevor Hextall

One half of Team Canada and one of the UWF North American Tag Team Champions, whose interference cost Tom Landis a title match before the pair joined the main event's three-way brawl. Written by AI, 2026

Tenures

UWF (23 Mar 1996)

2003-08-09 – 2005-05-28
Observed by the museum, W-L-D (from 16 parsed matches, UWF (23 Mar 1996)): 8-5-0, 3 no contest
Tenure detail
Billed with Bob Jablonski as Team Canada and the reigning UWF North American Tag Team Champions. Interfered in the Rampage Championship match, then competed in the main event three-way, ruled a no-contest, on 9 August 2003. Pinned Jason Keening with a double heartpunch on 4 October 2003 to win the UWF World Tag Team Championship, adding it to the team's own North American Tag Team title. Teamed with the Pride in a non-title, count-out loss on 22 October 2003. Submitted to the Ghost Dancers' stereo scorpion deathlocks on 25 October 2003, losing the UWF North American Tag Team Championship after a fine-print contract clause kept the team's own World Tag Team Championship off the line. Teamed with Jablonski to retain the UWF World Tag Team Championship over Eugene Robinson and Youth Gone Wild on 12 November 2003. Entered the Tower of Doom on 15 November 2003 alongside the Privateers, keeping Chad Grimsson from escaping the cage for several minutes before the Sons of Cacaphony (Erik and Chad Grimsson) escaped first and took the UWF North American Tag Team Championship. Entered the 20 December 2003 twelve-man elimination match with Bob Jablonski as Team Canada; pinned by Chad Grimsson's Rib Wrecker combination for the elimination. Teamed with Bob Jablonski and Scott Daniels on 10 March 2004 in a six-man tag win over the Ghost Dancers and Erik Grimsson, Daniels stealing the pinfall on Michael Keening after two unidentified men in suits ambushed the Ghost Dancers' side. Teamed with Bob Jablonski and Scott Daniels again against Alex Kidd, Paul Wong, and "Doomsayer" Kyle Lee on 15 May 2004 in a six-man tag that ended without a decision amid a mass brawl. Teamed with Bob Jablonski as Team Canada again on 29 May 2004, ambushing debuting Alex Martinez in the aisle with Tom Landis and Kari Stevens before the bell, then legdropping a would-be saving Alex Extreme to protect Jablonski's pinfall over the exhausted Martinez. Teamed with Bob Jablonski as Team Canada in a Gold Rush loss to Strictly Business (Andrew Tucker and Mike Sebastian) on 5 June 2004, pinned after a top-rope Frog Splash. Teamed with Bob Jablonski and Tom Landis in a Meltdown main event win over Quinn Brown and Strictly Business (Andrew Tucker and Mike Sebastian) on 25 August 2004, Landis and Hextall's own Game Misconduct heart-punch combination flooring Andrew Tucker for the pin. Teamed with Bob Jablonski and manager-turned-competitor Kari Stevens in a losing intergender six-way against Fury (Rage, Spice) and Ami Tran by pinfall on 6 October 2004, Tran finishing Stevens with a version of the One-Winged Angel's own Revelator. Teamed with Bob Jablonski as Team Canada in a no-contest against the Hands of Death (Caliban, Serge Annis) on 13 November 2004, clipping Caliban's knee with a hockey stick before the bout dissolved into a mass brawl. Teamed with Bob Jablonski and Scott Daniels as the Pride, beating Amity (the One-Winged Angel, Rage, Spice) in the Bluff City Streetfight main event on 27 November 2004 after Derek Martin turned on the One-Winged Angel to score the pinfall. Beat "Hentai" Ryu Osawa by pinfall (a heart punch, with manager Kari Stevens tying Osawa in the ropes) on 1 December 2004, then joined Bob Jablonski in a beatdown of the fallen Osawa broken up by a returning "Showtime" Rick Marley. On 11 December 2004, brawled with Rage backstage in a Pride-Fury melee, grabbing a lead pipe before Ryu Osawa intervened and was jumped for it. Was part of the Pride's own ringside entrance and locker-room scenes on 28 May 2005, celebrating the group's Rise to Power sweep and welcoming the returning Brett Greene alongside the rest of the group.
