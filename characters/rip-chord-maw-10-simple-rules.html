Character · MAW: 10 Simple Rules (2008)

Rip Chord

MAW's owner, a former SWF and TCW star who was fired from SWF for turning up drunk, now sober and talking the narrator into running his shows; he sits on commentary beside Marv Earnest, wins the October 2008 battle royal and admits he has a son. In January 2009 his son turns out to be Kid Arachnid and beats him down with Rip's own DDT; Rip is off the shows for five months, comes back at Summer Showdown refusing to get in the ring with his son, and at Iron in August 2009 agrees to a war games match on one condition. At Where It All Begins Again in October 2009 he beats his son Jay in a short brawl, finishing him with a chair; the narrator then tells both of them to stay away from the next show, makes Rip answerable for Jay's behaviour in December, and by April 2010, after Jay agrees to re-sign, Rip is standing beside his son and arranging his opponents. Through the summer of 2010 he keeps choosing Jay's challengers and refuses Aaron Andrews's title shot five times, until the Board of Directors overrules him at Fall Of The King in September and orders the match; at Where It All Begins Again in October he rings the bell to stop Andrews's pinfall in the Iron Man match, and Andrews answers by flattening him with a plancha and hitting him between the eyes with the bell. After the night of Remember November in November 2010 he tells the narrator at 3:42 am that he will talk to Jay about a new contract, proposes Matthew Gauge as Aaron Andrews's opponent at Season's Beatings, and announces the Heavyweight title rematch that the Board of Directors has granted Jay, which Jay makes a street fight at New Year, Old Rivalries in January 2011, the match the narrator calls MAW's best ever; he hands Kirk Jameson the Invitational trophy. Through the winter he presses the narrator to push Casey Valentine and Antonio, and in April, told that Jean Cattley is leaving for the SWF, he wrecks the narrator's office and drives him into a wall before walking out. Through the summer of 2011 he turns on the narrator. He sets up the Gold Rush main event for High Noon, in which the three singles champions and three "randomly drawn" opponents contest the Heavyweight title; after Jay's elbow is hurt in the match with Kirk Jameson at Mother, May I? he sits in the narrator's chair, blames him for it and wants Jameson punished, and thereafter lets Jenna carry the messages between their offices. In June he interviews sixteen cheap workers for the touring shows over the narrator's objection ("I own this company"), signs twelve at $200 a night or less and pays for them to join the Tri-State tour show out of his own pocket. At Summernova on 12th August he lifts the narrator off the floor in the dark and tells him not to threaten Jay; in September, when the narrator fires Jay for attacking Hugh de Aske, the diary leaves his answer for a later page, Rip standing behind Jay's chair days afterwards and saying that his son has turned down WLW, INSPIRE and SAISHO and expects his loyalty to be rewarded. At Fall Of The King on 26th September 2011 he takes off his headset to stand at ringside for his son's match with Randy Bumfhole and turns his back on Jay when Jay loses. After Jay's contract runs out he demands that the narrator reverse the main event finish of Where It All Begins Again on 26th October 2011; when the narrator quits the two fight in the office until the narrator breaks his cane over Rip's head, after which Rip admits that he wants Jay to be proud of him and the narrator talks him out of trying to be Richard Eisen. He agrees to sign no one new for a year, retires the Trios titles at Eleventh Hour on 12th November and creates the All Action title, plays Freecell in his office, stays at the announcers' table through the departures to the SWF, and agrees in late December to drop the touring shows for a month or two. In the diary's pages from February to May 2012 he makes the All Action title match at Leap Into The Unknown a straight wrestling match, brings back the Gold Rush match with every champion in it, sets a submission match, a ladder match and a two-out-of-three-falls match for the All Action title, announces Jim Force as Aaron Andrews's challenger at March Madness, puts the All Action and Heavyweight titles on the line together and signs Steve Gumble a couple of hours before South East Crisis so that he can take Andrews's open challenge. In the diary's pages from June to September 2012 he takes charge of the Smith & Singh against Second Sons match at Beach Blast, announces Kid Toma as Aaron Andrews's challenger, introduces Rudy Velasquez at Summernova on 5th August, forces Andrews to accept Rich Money's challenge and declares at Iron on 12th August that the All Action title can change hands anywhere; with the narrator he plans to break Richard Eisen in two when the day comes.
