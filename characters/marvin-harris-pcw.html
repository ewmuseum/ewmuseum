Biography (RUSH (2002))

The story of Marvin Harris is a long and complicated one beginning on the hot evening of February 6th, 1972 in a lower-middle class suburb of Panama City, Florida. Born the middle of three children to Lara and Jacob Harris, a dockworker and housewife, he had a fairly average childhood. He never managed to make an impact scholastically, unlike his older brother Monty, or become a sports hero like his younger brother Mike. Marvin always kind of shied away from social situations and remained perpetually average as far as talent goes. That all changed when he discovered pro-wrestling. What started out as primitive backyard antics between brothers soon blossomed into a part-time hobby in college and later finally became Marvin's career ambition. He had a rough time selling his family on the idea, but after constant pressure they caved to his wishes and sent him off to a local wrestler's school. It was in this environment he flourished, Marvin's natural ability and skill was evident to his teachers. Finally, after a three-month stint of training he was released to go and make himself in the business. He started out wrestling under the un-inspiring moniker “Marvelous” Marvin Harris, a man who asked for nothing more than fair matches and his shot at success. But, the cutthroat nature of the wrestling profession soon became apparent to Marvin as loss after loss were delivered to him due to dirty tricks by his opponents. As his frustration grew, he was advised by his comrades to seek the help of one of the world's leading pro-wrestling psychiatrists, Professor Sickman B. Fraud. Unfortunately, the infamous Professor Fraud, a cruel and twisted man himself, had no intention of curing Marvin of his losing streak. Instead, he began a long process that corrupted Marvin's mind with bizarre untested psychoactive drugs and grueling hours of intense subconscious suggestion. What finally emerged was no longer the timid and fair 'Marvelous' Marvin Harris, but a depraved and reckless individual that wouldn't rule out any underhanded deed in order to secure the easy win. It was at this point that Marvin began adorning his trademark gasmask, a delusional by-product of Professor Fraud's demented brainwashing. He wrestled for years under the watchful eye of Fraud, who'd reward a victory with further treatment and failure with vicious mental and physical torture. However, Fraud finally pushed Marvin too far one night and in retaliation Marvin unflinchingly broke the professor's fragile neck. Marvin had little time to enjoy his freedom though, as soon after the incident he was beaten into a coma by Fraud's personal henchmen, two former assault convicts that Fraud had made into asylum orderlies. He spent the next six months completely out of action. When he finally awoke, he jumped right back into the fray. He competed at a new level of intensity and skill, but without Fraud's discipline, however brutal, he was also out of control. He rampaged through numerous underground federations (most of them Justin Lollie promotions) and became relatively infamous for occasionally breaking an opponent's arms or legs. He continued along this downward spiral until learning of Fraud's return. He tried fleeing his former torturer by enrolling himself into the Primetime Wrestling Academy; the last place he figured the mentally unbalanced shrink would look for him. Unfortunately, Marvin had underestimated the PTC's renown and after only his second television promo for the academy Professor Fraud found him. Fraud, who had used his legal authority as Marvin's psychiatrist, had him once more committed into an asylum. Once more under Fraud's supervision, Marvin was tortured and prodded into becoming a monster. Throughout his time in the PWA he continually escaped the asylum and wreaked havoc before being caught, often because people who he'd believed were friends and family had sold him out. When he was finally promoted into the XWA he was astonished that Professor Fraud had inexplicably decided to release him from custody. Despite the fact he believed a trick was played on him by his old nemesis, he reveled in his newfound freedom and had numerous strange adventures in trying to secure employment, friends, and most importantly a love life. Marvin then found himself once more promoted, this time into RUSH wrestling, but the freedom he once cherished so much began to turn on him. He became increasingly reckless and lackluster in performance, until an odd meeting with his brother led to his reuniting with both of his siblings along with another psychiatrist, one who seemed the exact opposite of Fraud. Marvin felt like, in this new atmosphere, he could really begin to heal from the deep psychological wounds imposed on him by Professor Fraud, but then Scott Cage came along. It started as a simple spat over damaging Cage's truck in an accident and soon led to an extremely dangerous game of one-upmanship involving fast moving vehicles and the lives of innocent people, gaining drastic momentum with Marvin winning the RUSH Network Title. It finally ended in a bloody, career-threatening 'Hell on Wheels' match at the first Ignition PPV. It was there that Marvin Harris went through a windshield and head (or rather mask) first into a tree trunk, causing a severe concussion that turned into a coma. There, for five months, he remained motionless, no signs of brain activity. Until on the evening of October 13th, 2002 when he miraculously awoke with only one thing on his mind; revenge.
