UWF (23 Mar 1996)

2003-10-25 – 2006-06-07
Observed by the museum, W-L-D (from 15 parsed matches, UWF (23 Mar 1996)): 11-4-0
Tenure detail
Billed from Dortmund, Germany, weighing 268 pounds; climbed the guardrail mid-match to threaten the crowd with a chair after being speared out of the ring, then pinned Pierce Bleed with a top-rope superbomb in a non-title singles match. Beat Tragedy by pinfall (electric chair drop) on 12 November 2003, earning a future UWF North American Heavyweight Championship shot. Won the 17 December 2003 fifteen-man battle royal to open the Meltdown Championship tournament, throwing Paul Wong with a Samoan drop for the final elimination, then lost the semi-finals to Papa Legba, pinned by an inverted DDT after injuring his own arm on a missed elbowdrop. Retained the UWF North American Heavyweight Championship over "Screaming Drillbit" Jason Keening on 24 March 2004 by pinfall (Death Valley Driver) after blinding him with fingerpaint smuggled in by manager Simone Sumner. Billed as the reigning UWF North American Heavyweight Champion on 29 May 2004, though no surviving document in the held record states when or how he won the title from its prior holder, Kyle Lee; abandoned his own title defense against a substituting Chris O'Brien to chase down a returning Quinn Brown, losing by countout, though the title did not change hands. Brawled with Brown into the parking garage afterward and was thrown through the windshield of his own car. Retained the UWF North American Heavyweight Championship over Quinn Brown by pinfall (the Violator Mark II) at Gold Rush on 5 June 2004, after Brown's own manager Victoria Marquess shoved Brown off the top rope and then revealed a secret alliance with Frost, kissing him after the match. Beat November by pinfall (a steel chair shot) in a non-title match on 18 September 2004 after manager Victoria Marquess revealed the bout had been arranged with no disqualifications; valets Roxy Mayhem and Simone Sumner interfered throughout. Escaped a Tower of Doom cage match on 30 October 2004 as part of the winning team alongside Edmond Winston IV, Papa Legba, and Luke Kinsey, though the match ended in chaos after the bell when a returning "Doomsayer" Kyle Lee attacked Winston. Retained the UWF North American Heavyweight Championship over Quinn Brown by pinfall (a high-elevation Death Valley Driver) on 13 November 2004, after Roxy Mayhem pulled fellow Violence Girl Simone Sumner into Brown's path to save himself from a missile dropkick. Retained the UWF North American Heavyweight Championship over Uriah Hawkins by pinfall on 17 November 2004, hanging onto the top rope as Hawkins's own Suicide DDT attempt drove Hawkins's head into the mat instead. Fired manager Victoria Marquess on 27 November 2004 after she confronted him over abandoning her and Simone Sumner; Marquess revealed before leaving that she had already signed a mystery challenger for Frost's own UWF North American Heavyweight Championship at Rise to Power. Pressed Jessica Marshall backstage on 11 December 2004 to reveal the mystery challenger signed by his own former manager Victoria Marquess for his UWF North American Heavyweight Championship defense at Rise to Power, learning only that the contract was airtight; separately, Papa Legba signed to challenge him for the same title. Debuted a new manager, Roxy Mayhem, and the "Graphic Violence World Tour 2005" on 28 May 2005, refusing an open challenge from "The Walking Contradiction" Sabbath as beneath his own standards. Traded barbs with Sayaka Daikin-Price backstage on 11 June 2005 with manager Roxy Mayhem at his side, then beat a returning Alex Martinez by pinfall (a chair-assisted spike piledriver, feet on the ropes) after Roxy Mayhem rang the ring bell early to trick Martinez into thinking he had already won. Dismissed Alex Martinez's own Road to the Gold win to reporter Peter Phillips on 6 July 2005 as an injustice, vowing to "liberate" the Unified Television Championship from Marcus Nuit; lost the title match by disqualification after Sabbath ran in and attacked him. Revealed in a hospital-room scene on 20 July 2005 to have been scalped bald by Sabbath's own recent attack, along with valet Roxy Mayhem, whom he blamed for the humiliation; vowed revenge on Sabbath. Teamed with Michael Augustine to beat Miguel Quesada and Jason Keening on 23 July 2005. Survived the main event eight-man elimination match on 3 August 2005 alongside Tumaffi, pinning Sabbath last with a Death Valley Driver after tripping his own partner's top-rope attempt. Returned on 18 March 2006 after a four-month absence, publicly dumped valet Roxy Mayhem, and declared himself number-one contender to the World Heavyweight Championship by replaying his own July 2005 pinfall over Alex Martinez; denied a title shot and booked for a match the following week by an angry Becky Byers. Lost to Corey Irons by pinfall (the Lariat) on 7 June 2006, unable to hold a dragon sleeper as Irons powered to his feet.
