Character · MAW: 10 Simple Rules (2008)

Jay Chord

Rip Chord's grown son, who comes to MAW for one meeting with the narrator and turns down a contract because he wants to wrestle in Japan. The diary then brings him back: Kid Arachnid is unmasked as Jay at The Rip Chord Invitational in January 2009, and he spends the following months as a heel main eventer at war with his father, with the Gauge Brothers and Darryl Devine at his side. He loses the October 2009 match to his father's chair, cashes in the Rip Chord Invitational title shot to take the Heavyweight title from Steven Parker in January 2010, keeps it against Thom Morgan, Stevie Grayson, Amazing Fire Fly and Kirk Jameson, fights Aaron Andrews to a time-limit draw, and re-signs in April 2010 after Rip rings the bell on Aaron Andrews's skull. He keeps the Heavyweight title through the summer of 2010, beating Erik Strong at Summer Showdown, the Masked Patriot at Beach Blast, Fumihiro Ota and Firebird at Summernova, Brandon Smith at Iron, Steve Flash at Back To School and Trent Shaffer at All The Leaves Are Brown, and adds the USPW Television and National titles and, for a week in September, the TCW International title. He loses the Heavyweight title to Aaron Andrews in the thirty-minute Iron Man match at Where It All Begins Again on 26th October 2010, by two falls to one, loses to Stevie Grayson at Eleventh Hour in November and leaves the show early. He beats Fumihiro Ota at Remember November in November 2010 and Kirk Jameson at Season's Beatings, but is fined a quarter of his month's pay after attacking Oscar Golden in the locker room, and wins the Tag Team match with Primus Allen over Aaron Andrews and Steve Flash at End Of Days that earns him a street fight for the Heavyweight title. He loses it to Andrews at New Year, Old Rivalries on 12th January 2011, beats Amazing Fire Fly at the Invitational, loses to Darryl Devine in February, draws with him that month, beats him in March and beats Steve Flash at March Madness; the narrator notes in March that he has become USPW World champion. At Spring Fever on 23rd April 2011 he calls out the new Kid Arachnid, who is wearing his old mask, and wrestles him to a draw; he has turned down 4C and EX2010, tells the narrator in e-mails that he fears being shoehorned into the feud between The Firm and the Second Sons, refuses Erik Strong's offer of an alliance and teams with him to beat Steve Flash and Arachnid on 12th May. He beats Kirk Jameson at Mother, May I? while hurting his elbow, is counted out of the six-man Gold Rush match at High Noon, draws a fifteen-minute Traditional title match with Hugh de Aske at Hotter Than Hot, beats JD Morgan at Beach Blast and in July is put on commentary, in the narrator's place, at Rip's order. He leads the Second Sons' six-man win at Summernova, plants dog dirt in Stevie Grayson's old cap, loses to Hugh at Back To School and attacks him in the locker room, for which the narrator fires him. At Fall Of The King on 26th September 2011 he stands in the ring and says he has been misguided, offers Randy Bumfhole a Heavyweight title shot for a win and loses to the Bumfhole Buster while his father turns his back on him; after the show he argues with Flash Savage and the narrator, who cannot fire him, takes a quarter of his show fee. He does not appear at All The Leaves Are Brown on 12th October and is gone when his contract expires (Rip Chord tells the narrator "Jay's gone"); tew.com reports in November that he left on acrimonious terms and is an SWF target, and in December the narrator learns that he has agreed to tour with INSPIRE until March. In the diary's pages for August 2012, on a late-night telephone call that Rip Chord puts through to the narrator's home he apologises to the narrator for how things played out the year before, says that he has been seeing a psychiatrist in Los Angeles about losing his mother and about his father's absence, that he pushes himself to live up to his father and can be arrogant and rude, and that TCW has been good to him; the narrator accepts the apology without knowing whether it is genuine.
