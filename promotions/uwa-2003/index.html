Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Universal Wrestling Alliance staged its own founding as fiction from the start. An FAQ posted 10 July 2003 introduces the promotion as “the brainchild of California Multi-Millionaire Scott Addison,” conceived in 1999 to rival the WCW and the WWF and only realized once a phone call from a fictionalized Ted Turner brought funding. Its Official Handbook (12 July 2003) restates the same story under a dedicated “fictional story” heading, casting the promotion as a small operation broadcasting monthly by pay-per-view out of Northern California, with USA, ESPN, and FX staged as courting it for a national television deal.

Addison shared on-screen authority with two other invented executives, Adam Wilson as president and James Kurn as commissioner, the handbook describing Kurn as “kind of like Mick Foley... People love him, but is a dumb ass at some times.” The federation ran as a BCC e-mail role-play built around one weekly show, UWA Presents Wednesday Mayhem, called by a bench of announcers — Joey Styles, Jim Ross, Don Callis, Michael Cole, and Mike Tenay — alongside ring announcer Howard Finkel and three backstage interviewers.

Its application process ran two tracks: join with an original creation, backed by a sample roleplay, or claim an already-established “real wrestler” from a list of open names. Three championships anchored the roster — a Universal title defended mainly at pay-per-view, a TV title defended on Mayhem, and a set of Tag Team titles — the handbook promising every wrestler on the roster a feud in time.

No card, roster, or match result survives; its surviving record holds nothing past these two founding documents from July 2003.
