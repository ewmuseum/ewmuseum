Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Internet Wrestling Council opened as a rebrand: on 21 September 1997 its own president announced he was reformatting a prior, “now-defunct” simmed federation of his into a semi-simmed, semi-booked, self-declared “Anti-SHEF” promotion, debuting 1 October under the new name. He said he had already brought on one match writer besides himself and a co-webmaster, and asked for more help with “anything from graphic design to match writing.”

A recruiting call two days later, ahead of the relaunch, asked for help in almost every role — art, rankings, match-writing, or simply handling a wrestler — and offered to advertise other sites during its own cards for anyone who asked.

By 14 October, only two weeks after relaunch, its own operations were already “shaky”: its president and his writers had been “crippled by school,” and he proposed folding struggling smaller federations into the WIWC outright — the WIWC would keep its own name, and each incoming fed head would join a WIWC Executive Board, writing a match a week and getting a say in its affairs, “so that all of the work that I did doesn't go to waste.”

By 26 October the concept had shifted again. Having since wrestled for several other federations himself and decided he preferred booked wrestling to simmed, its own president declared that a semi-booked, semi-simmed name no longer fit a single federation, and repurposed the World Internet Wrestling Council into an umbrella open to any promotion at all — “booked or simmed, extreme or classic, real or fantasy wrestlers.” By then it counted three members: his own federation, the NWF, and the MLCW.

By its last surviving document, 30 October 1997, the council had grown to five member federations and an official opening was described as imminent, “in a few days,” with more members still being recruited. Its president signed that post as the handler of one of his own wrestlers, Johnny “Prime Time” Johnston. No card, result, or further document survives under the WIWC name.
