Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Continental Wrestling Promotion introduced itself in a founding update posted 10 July 1999, styling itself “the next generation in the business of sports-entertainment” and, in its own words, “a new wave . . . new age . . . a revolution” built on “hard work . . . determination.”

Five prizes anchored its ladder: an Intercontinental Heavyweight Title billed as its top prize (a World Title, its own update reasoned, would overreach a promotion “only International”); a National Title for its minor singles division; a Juniorweight Title for wrestlers under 230 pounds; Intercontinental Tag Team Titles; and an annual 32-man Universal Tournament, the U-Cup, whose final eight would settle the winner in a battle royal. Every title stood vacant, with no past champion recorded.

Two regular shows carried the promotion — Wednesday Warfare, its minor card, and Saturday Night, its flagship five- or six-match show with a handful of dark matches — alongside a Friday roleplay-only Pre-Show, all three run out of the Continental Airlines Arena, with pay-per-views planned every three months.

Its own on-screen president fronted a broadcast team of four commentators — David Forthright, billed as its most knowledgeable voice; the brash Seth Stevens; the behind-the-scenes Michael Russenhoff; and senior analyst Donald Blackstone, given a fictional past as a wrestler and manager in smaller promotions — plus ring announcer Deviant Soul, reporter Diana Freedman, and head of security Juggernaut, the update noting a rivalry between the last two over Freedman's affections. Only nine wrestlers were signed at founding: five singles competitors, Perry Perry, Stronghold, Cyclone, Iotoa, and Ghetto Master, and two tag teams, Ogre and Simpleton, billed as Mindless Action, and Pegasus and Juxtapose, billed as Magical Incarnation.

No completed match, title change, or further document survives beyond this 10 July 1999 update.
