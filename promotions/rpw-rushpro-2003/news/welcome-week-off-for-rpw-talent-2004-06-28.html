Welcome Week Off For RPW Talent

28 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This sunday, an interesting thing was happening at the Halton Arena: There was no one there. 'We've had this week built into our schedule for a long time.' Christian Kannon said. 'It's sort of a reward, since most people have worked nearly every Sunday for 5 months now. It's going to give Maxwell and Karina time to unwind after their Japan debacle, and give us in RPW time to rejuvenate the rosters.' The rosters have already felt a bit of a rejuvenation after a few members were released. Among them were William S. Powell and his valet Nichelle Yvette. 'They were struggling to find a place within the company, and with Powell's impending Senate bid, he decided it would be better to make his time spent working on his campaign.' Also released was Akiyama, and his partners Re:Plica, after their failed bid to make the UNCC track team, James Edwards, and the yet to debut team of K.C. Simms and Shawn Arrows. 'Arrows was an arrogant prick, coming in and making demands. We're a small time company that doesn't have room for horrendously large egos.' When asked if there would be more casualties, thanks to RPW's recent profit loss, O'Neil simply shrugged. 'I can't tell. Wrestling's in a down cycle right now, and we're a wrestling show. People want WWE style, not good wrestling. RPW will always be a niche market, which is why out partnership with tSC makes complete sense. They're all about wrestling, and something we strive to be in this company. Hopefully, we can take the lessons learned from their success and eventually apply it to RPW.'
