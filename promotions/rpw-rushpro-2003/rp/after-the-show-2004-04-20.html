After the Show

20 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 20th, 2004 “There are more important things in this world than wrestling.” It was a pretty simple statement. Most would not even give it a second thought. Yet these words drove into the very soul of Chris Storm. To your average wrestler the business was a means of getting a paid check. People like the Dread Pirate Roberts came in and wrestled then left with their money. Chris Storm was not one of these people. When RPW needed to make pay cuts after the El D shake up that lost the television deal, Chris Storm was the first in line to take a cut. It was not that Chris had a place in his heart for RPW or that he wanted to help the company. This was just a place for him to wrestle. But if RPW closed he would have no place to wrestle. Chris needs to wrestle. It is as important to him as shelter, food, and security. Sometimes it even takes priority over the others. People have caught Chris sleeping in a wrestling ring because he trained until the sky was pitch black. His apartment was just a second home for when he was away from the ring. So why now; why now does he allow her words to affect him? After his encounter with Tessa, Chris marched back to his locker room and grabbed his stuff. Without even answering Arizona’s questions, Chris left the arena and drove off. Where did he go after the show? “More important than wrestling? Ha there is nothing more important than wrestling. That is why I’m the Mid South Champion and she is merely a challenger.” The speedometer in his Coup Deville slowly increased. 50…55…60…65…70, always increasing. Chris sat behind the wheel, black sunglasses covering his eyes. His foot pressed down on the gas peddles; he never bothered to look down at his gauges to see how fast he was going. He just needed to get away. “She has no right to speak to me like that, trying to tell me wrestling isn’t important. If it wasn’t so important than why did she wrestle instead of spend time with her family? No one made her pick this profession.” Chris muttered to himself. With his word he pressed his foot down harder and harder. He was becoming lost in the landscape that flew by. He would drive until he ran out of gas. That should give him the time he needed to think. “Wrestling has given me everything I have today. My apartment, my car, and…uh…” Chris’ mind went blank. He could not think of anything else he had to show for all his years of wrestling. Storm had been in the business for nearly six years. Might have been longer, he could not remember the date he first started. What, in those six years, did he have to show? Some could say he is famous but to the fans outside of wrestling he is a stranger. Some might say he is rich but it is far from the truth. Chris is one of the lowest paid members of RPW, despite wrestling more matches than any other member. Merchandise did not add much more money in his pocket. Being one of the most hated men in RPW, it does hurt your t-shirt sells. He had his apartment and his car. That was the extent of his vast fortune. He did not even own a home computer. Chris Storm had no family, had no friends, and had little money. Could wrestling really fill that giant gap? The car started to slowly decrease in speed. His gaze turned to the gas gauge. The needle dropped below the E. Chris seemed unfazed; he just lifted his foot off the gas and allowed it to rest while the car came to a stop. “I’m a wrestler. It is why I wake up and why I breathe. To say it is not important is the talk of a second rate wannabe. When I am the new World champion all my hard work and dedication will have paid off. Then maybe Tessa will realize that wrestling really is that important.” Finally, the car stopped moving. Chris got out of the car and looked around. There was no gas station in sight, looked like it was time to start walking. “Next week…I’m going to show Tessa what it means to be a real wrestler.”
