Date: May 7, 2004 Scott lay there, his body not functioning and his mind, well, let's just say it wasn't doing a whole lot better. It's kind of like when you come to in the mornings but you don't get up until your roommate throws cold water on your face and you get up and throw his or her bloody, bloody corpse out the window. Yeah, that, except without everything after the work “up.” It would have been fair to say that the whole experience was slightly frustrating to the Manbeast. A man who has power rarely matched, that keeps people off his back with intimidation and a hard glare. For his body to not respond is a scary thing. More than scary, really, since he can't even get his eyelids to open. But his ears aren't closed. The fact that he can hear might disturb his friends if they knew. But, as we've already established, that's really not such an option. Scott hears bits and pieces, and as best as he can, tries to filter them through his foggy head. “It’s gonna be all right. I’ve seen this guy go through people in fights like a buzzsaw, this won’t keep him down, not at all.” That was Gabe. He recognized that voice from growing up together. It was Gabe. It was. Who else would know that sort of stuff? Scott's mind made a long distance leap that he was probably comforting Alyssa. “I mean I can pay for it.” “Since when, when the world is tilted over on its axis, Chris Storm can wrestle a good match, and money does INDEED grow on trees?” What... what was that? In Scott's mind, they had suddenly switched topics, but in the amount of time for him to make that leap in his mind, he had actually used up a couple of seconds for one thought. But he heard those two sentences and tried to fight his way up. He tried to claw up through the quagmire that fogged his skull. “No. No. Gabe, I meant to tell you... I meant to!” That's what Scott wanted to say. But in a body as weak as a kitten and unable to even open his eyes, all the determination in the world wasn't going to get his vocal chords to work. He meant to tell Gabe, he did. It just never came up. Scott took things one at a time, and he never got to explain to Gabe during all the fighting... he should've, he told himself. He should have. Now Scott felt even worse, which he hadn't thought humanly possible. Up to now, he could only remember bits and pieces, snippets of time. Things like Alyssa sponging the crud of the bar floor and gravel off his body. Stuff like needles going under his skin. Now he could hear, just not interact. It was panic inducing. In fact, the whole ordeal was quite rough, but this was too much. Scott tried to move any part of his body, threw all of his will into it. He wanted to tell them he was there. He wanted to explain it all. He wanted to fix it. He managed to accomplish something. His heartrate went up. Give that Scott had almost bottomed out, he was hooked up to a monitor and when his heartbeat quickened, this just sent everything straight to hell. The sound of the door swinging open, the feel of Alyssa's hands on his shoulder, the movement of Gabe's hands on his other arm. The feel of a needle hitting his skin. “No! No!” he cried out. And he actually did. The sounds came forth from him and everyone froze. That's when he realized it. He had broken through. “No,” his dry throat rasped, “don't put me back under. Leave me alone. Let me wake up.” Those pleas were enough. Those pleas created a will inside Gabe and Alyssa that meant that nothing was going in him without passing the formidable wall of two loving people protecting their friend. “C'mon, man,” he heard Gabe say. “C'mon, look at us. Look at us, Scott.” Us. He was talking about him and Alyssa. Both of them. No self-serving there. His eyes pulled up slowly, reluctantly, and squinted against the harsh light. His eyes adjusted and there, looking down at him was a beautiful face framed in wavy raven hair and a concerned mess of a kid with bright blue sideburns. He smiled, a real smile of relief and comfort. His friends looked down at him. “I'm... I'm going to rest,” he said slowly. “I'll be back in a little while.” He meant it. It wasn't some game of making them feel better or shoring up hopes; he really meant it. To rest in a state without the influence of anything but exhaustion and to wake up soon. To wake up back to the world that had killed his father, that had forced him on the road to take care of his mom and sister. To the world where he went everywhere with his best friend. To the world where a woman loved him even though he was seen as an uncontrollable monster. He'd return soon.
