The Worst Truth To Face

17 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/17/04 Blood, sweat, and tears. I gave it all, yet I got nothing in return. That seemed to be the way things always went for me when it came to Chris. It seemed he’d always have the upper hand over me. I give my heart to him, he takes it and walks all over it for his own amusement. I give my blood, sweat and tears in hopes of beating him, yet he somehow finds a way to get the upper hand and get the win. It was hopeless. Chris had managed to beat me once more which only added to his ego, and to his claim that he was the better of the two. As much as I hated to admit it, he was right. He had beaten me fair and square in the middle of the ring, and all I could do is suck it up and deal with it....but I couldn’t. I could not forgive myself for letting it happen. I was supposed to go out there and prove that I was the better Schmoopie. I was supposed to go out there and win the Mid South Title. Yet I didn’t. I failed at what I promised myself I would do. I failed at what I promised Malphas what I would do. I failed at what I promised the world I would do, and it ate me alive. It was almost as if the happy go lucky Tessa that everyone knew me to be didn’t exist anymore. “Cheer up Tess, you gave it your best out there.” Malphas had told me multiple times before I left the Halton arena that evening. But even hearing it a hundred times over wouldn’t wipe away the embarrassment I felt. After all, I had bragged to Malphas especially about how easy it would be to defeat Chris if I had managed to meet him in the finals of the tournament. Yet here I stand defeated by a man who managed to wrestle three matches in one night, the last one being mine, and yet he still managed to beat me. I suppose standing here and pouting about it isn’t going to make anything better though. As much as I try and recall all the mistakes I made in the match, and all the moves I should used to capitalize, it still wasn’t going to change a thing. The match was done and over with. Chris was the Mid South champion, I was someone who would surely have to be mocked for losing to a man who was in no shape to compete in a third match. The only question that seemed fit for me to ask now is if anyone could have done any better? I gave it my all, and my all wasn’t enough, plain and simple.
