Reflections Of A Golden Soul

9 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11 - 09 - 03 The sun beams its golden fiery glow down upon the earth as the clouds part to bask mankind in its glory. A quaint, warm wind flows through the salty air of North Carolina this fine afternoon, carrying the squawking of seagulls and the amalgam of bystander voices. Like large gray blood cells traveling down the veins of the heart of the city, cars and other automobiles absolutely clog the streets, polluting the air with exhaust and noise. It doesn't exactly leave a pleasant smell either. But that won't bother Dorn Tracer today. Seated at a small white table beneath a large red and white umbrella in a white t-shirt and black leather pants, his body is unmoving sans the lifting of his coffee cup to his lips. The world before his eyes reflects in the lens of his shades as he stares out at life, an unfeeling smirk upon his clean-shaven face. ‘People are interesting,' he mumbles to himself. ‘Confusing, complex creatures that run like machines overloaded with directives. They cannot decide which one to do first so they try to do them all at once, overloading their circuits and driving them to the edge.' He takes a sip of his coffee and smirks, ‘Uncaring beasts with no sense of direction nor purpose. They live to die.' The Astronomer chuckles to himself and looks around, observing the multitude of pedestrians as they go about their business, ignoring him. Dorn checks their face, their motions, and their mannerisms. You can truly say a lot about someone just by watching what they do when they think no one is paying attention. Embarrassed by what lies within, they hide it until they feel truly alone, escaped from all eyes. ‘But of course, it's just human nature…' Humans are ultimate beings with ultimately nothing to do. Most believe they live because God put them there. Others believe their goal is to make a ton of money, to start a family, or to live out a lonely life of solitude. Humans have wondered their purpose for ages, trying to decide why they were put on Earth, why they have come so far, and how exactly everything came about. It's their nature to wonder such things. It's also their nature to be horribly wrong. Dorn takes another swig of his coffee and shakes his head, ‘An experiment gone wrong, a race gone unchecked. Zenander's creation has managed to live millions of years already. Very impressive. For so long they've avoided the wrath of the Goddess, taunting her anger with their blasphemous claims of other deities. Surviving her burning fury all those ages ago have left those that remember it cocky and arrogant, thinking it'll never happen again. But that's just human nature…' ‘… to be totally wrong. Soon, I will awaken the Goddess once more as her second-in-command… a DEMI-GOD! Then, these miserable creatures will finally meet their fate as they should have all those years ago.' And with that, Dorn takes a final sip of his coffee, puts the cup back down, rises from his seat, and walks away. [End.]
