Old Endings And New Beginnings

25 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25/01/04 Two weeks ago, I bared witness as a man died in the middle of the ring. And all anyone could do was watch, while life faded from his body. We came to OSW almost exactly one year ago as a team known as Vintage. Up until then, we had only been able to find work on the independent circuit, which was pretty sad considering we’d been wrestling longer than half these other guys had been alive. Our attempts at creating the perfect federation for old school wrestlers had failed – albeit, after nearly ten years of making it work – and we were left to try and make ends meet in one of the worst professions known to man. Don’t get me wrong, though. I’m a wrestler and would never give that up. But, the life of fame and fortune that most think comes so easily is the hardest to reach. Too many times we found ourselves living off no-name macaroni and cheese, or the dented reject cans with missing labels. Don’t even mention having a place to stay, because we couldn’t even afford a car to live in. We hitchhiked and walked to most of our bookings, and some days we both felt like giving up and calling it quits. Though, any true wrestler will tell you that once you’re in it, you’re in it for life. And it’s the truth. We couldn’t give up no matter how much we felt we wanted to. The love for the sport kept us going. The rush of hearing the crowd cheer – no matter how big or small – is something you can’t help but love. I hear people say wrestling isn’t a sport because we already know the winners, and that makes me sick to my stomach. Each night, people like us go out and put their lives on the line so the fans in the audience have something to entertain them--something to cheer for. We risk shortening our careers, or leaving the arena unable to walk, so don’t you ever tell me this isn’t a sport. If you can call golf and bowling a sport, then you damn well better call wrestling a sport. It is an unforgiving sport, though. Miss one night due to an injury, and the fans forget about you. You’re old news, long gone, not needed anymore. That’s why anyone who decides to be a wrestler has to have a heart that never dies, a body that never gives in, and a soul meant for the ring. Because that’s what it takes to get ahead. Not just physical strength, but the kind of strength you hear people speak of when a loved one passes on. If you don’t have that, and you’re not willing to sacrifice yourself for the fans, then you’ll never make it in the business. No matter how many boss’ daughters you shack up with. But there does come a time when all the sacrifice pays off. And it came for us in the form of Old School Wrestling. At first we were skeptic, but after being invited to see a few shows, we knew this could very well be what we were looking for our entire careers. The way we wrestled was perfect for what they wanted; a tandem duo to come in and help back up the old school side of OSW. Of course, we weren’t told that we’d be used to put so many people over in such a short amount of time, but that’s beyond the point. If I’ve learned one thing is this business, it’s not what you do to get to the top, but how you keep yourself there. And so we jobbed. A lot. You see; our team wasn’t exactly marketable to the general audience because of how generic the executives thought we were. Two old guys using a dead style of wrestling against people who could perform flips and three dozen spins before hitting the ground. So we were used as a stepping-stone for anyone who needed it--most of whom never stayed with the company. But we didn’t complain, because at least we were able to wrestle and pay the bills. And in the end, that’s really all we’re trying to do. Eventually, though, we grew frustrated with the lack of direction our team was taking. For months, we had been used to give others the rub, and we finally had enough of that and wanted to be able to run with the ball for once. According to the higher-ups, we “weren’t ready.” But they did hand us a feud with Dougie and Freddy, which helped us out more than most would think. From that, we were able to get over as heels after Freddy turned on Dougie and aligned with us, thus starting our climb to the top. But, management came to a decision that Freddy just wasn’t a good enough draw, and had plans to release him only a month after joining The Forsaken. So we approached them with an idea: let us work a cross-promotional feud with Holy Underground after having Freddy be ejected from the group and join the ranks of Old School Fighting. Surprisingly, they went along with it. Even more surprisingly, it worked. From there, we were given more creative control over our team, and began shaping it into what we knew could get over, as opposed to what they thought could get others over. I believe we received two Tag Title shots in that time, and each time we went out and gave it our all to prove we were worthy of holding the straps. But still, certain people didn’t think we had what it took to be the very top of a division. We were determined to prove them wrong. During that time, we were given trial runs with Matt Register and Jude Kensington, to see how we stacked up against different wrestlers. I’m guessing we impressed someone upstairs, because we went on to defeat Sonny Silver and Jude Kensington at the last OSW pay-per-view, as well as win the Tag Titles on the last OSW show to ever air. Which brings us to the end of OSW, and the end of Vintage. Before End Game, we didn’t know who would win our match. I can’t speak for everyone else’s matches, but the referee was the only one who knew the winner in the ring. And we were told that we would be walking out as champions just minutes before the match was set to end. The rush I felt at that moment can’t be explained, and I think Clyde felt it too, because we really started to push ourselves through the pain. Each of us had something planned in case we won, and each of us were hoping to win. If we were in the ring with two teams who weren’t as professional as P.W.A. and Politically Incorrect, there may have been different champions on that night because there would be nothing anyone could do about it. But we were lucky enough to be put in the ring with two teams that define the word “professional.” And it makes me proud to have been able to work with them, and it makes me proud to know them as the great people they are. That night was meant to be our retirement, because we had thought that nobody cared about old school anymore, and nobody cared about us. We were wrong. The sound in the arena was deafening when we won, and that’s the moment we both knew that we couldn’t pack it in and quit. We made a decision on the spot to keep going, whether it meant heading back to the indies or opening our own federation with the payouts we received from OSW’s closure. Either way, we knew our end had not yet come. But there was an ending that night. Something no one had planned for. During the final match, we had all come out to witness Matt Register and Jeffrey Wayne battle it out in the ring, but what we witnessed was something I wish I had never seen. It seemed like forever, yet it was only a few moments. It seemed like a dream, but it was truly a nightmare. I stood and watched, helpless, as life escaped his body. We all watched with tears in our eyes, as Matt Register lay on the mat, virtually unmoving, before his body gave out and he breathed his final breath. If I were a poet, I’m sure I’d see irony in the fact that a man who lived and breathed OSW also died with it. But I’m only a wrestler, and what I saw was something that will haunt me forever. Register wasn’t the most loved person in the wrestling world, but he was still one of our own, and that alone is enough reason to mourn his death. So it is now that I say goodbye to Matt Register and OSW. May you both rest in peace.
