Oh Kari, Where Art Thou? #03 : Part One - You Were At Peace.

13 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13/4/04 'And now...?' A satisfied press of the play button on a silver-grey, triangular Sony remote control ushered in the whisping sound a CD makes before the music begins. Then, chiming out through the calm and sedate surroundings, the rhythmic hammering piano of DJ Shadow's 'Blood on the Motorway' seemed to set Karina Wolfenden's heart-rate. 'Eternity.' Weary eyes closed, housing tarnished emerald irises behind make-up free eyelids, immersing her in a dark world of faint, ghostly light patterns. 'This is what I needed,' her lips mouthed, but not enough air was expelled to form the words. She was known as an Extreme Sports loving Daredevil, a globe-trotter who's passport read like a textual map of the world. People heard the name Karina Wolfenden and they imagined a fast-living, do-or-die young woman, never stopping for a moment's rest. It seemed her life was lived at supersonic speed, high above the clouds that form the ceiling of most peoples' world. 'Be still now.' But even she needed time-out, a chance to recharge the batteries, and that was exactly what she had planned for the whole week. Dinner in Vienna with her old, fleeting college roommate had been cancelled, wiping her deliberately barren schedule clear. Even her upstairs neighbour, who she enjoyed many music-volume jousts with, had gone away until next Wednesday. Dammit, she wanted a bit of peace, okay? 'I am with you. I am deep within you.' Through a pair of brightly striped socks, Karina's big toe tapped in time to the piano, prodding the arm of the all-engulfing sofa she had buried herself in. Comfort was the key. She had become one with the furniture, her head melting into the thick cushions, while copper-tinted hair fell un-tamed down her face, tickling her bottom lip ever so slightly. 'You are at peace.' If this is what would await Karina in heaven. she'd kick herself for sneaking out of Church as a child to roller-blade on a Sunday. All that had happened in the last month was gradually forgotten, balled up and casually tossed into a trash can somewhere at the back of her mind. Even the constant spectre of Magnum Wolf had been locked away in favour of a few days of utter sensory indulgence. She didn't care for a second that she had been sent home from Adrenaline, it was just another minor detail that would wait until the weekend, when she'd jack back into her life and boot things up once more. Clutching one of the extravagantly sized cushions to her, she hugged it as she felt the electronica of the music flood in through her ears and tingle her spine... Allowing each note to massage her aching muscles. Pure. Bliss. Even Karina's 2 cats - Psylocke & Betsy - echoed their owner's intentions, each having found a spot to curl up. Pity the person who sent the serene harmony crashing down all around her. Pity them, indeed. 'If only we could hibernate,' Karina sighed breathlessly, the sight of her sleek body almost lost amidst the cushions. Once-upon-a-time, Wolfenden would have stayed in sedate bliss for days, interrupted only by the doorbell announcing the arrival of take-out. But such care-free days didn't go hand-in-hand with her most recent of pursuits. Wrestling. A once-a-week, 2 hours only hobby had been the original plan, but it seemed the business could take control of a person... No matter how strongly they tried to reject it. And so, the ambient bliss swirling around the K-Wolf was shredded by the shrill ring of her phone, causing Karina to grab a cushion and plunge it over her face, trying to shield her ears in defiance. 'Shut... Up...' she seethed, voice muffled by the soft, malleable fabric and stuffing. Her plea was ignored, however, as the ringing permeated through the cushion's fibres on it's way to her reluctant ears. 'Dammit,' Kari hissed, slamming her right heel down into the sofa in frustration, sending one of the plentiful cushions flying to the soft, white carpet. Beep. At least the ringing had stopped. Could there have been a more intrusive sound for a tired K-Wolf, who's only desire was to relax and unwind? You bet. 'Karina, it's Jeff Ward,' the answering machine taunted, drawling a growl from the K-Wolf that couldn't be softened nor filtered out by the cushion’s stuffing. 'I'm on my way around, seems you forgot out little 'gathering' to discuss the Cruiserweight tournament publicity.' 'It's easy. It's like breathing... Like a heart-beat.' 'Shut uppppp,' a defiant Kari whined from her comfort-zone, her words aimed at both the answering machine and the music's suddenly rather annoying words. 'This has to be a joke...' 'So buzz me up when I arrive,' Ward continued, his usual, emotionless voice not even giving the vaguest hint that he felt he was intruding. 'And don't pretend you're not in, you're bike is outside... And I've never seen you walk further than from the curtain to ringside.' In frustration, Karina took the cushion that was engulfing her head and hurled it straight up... The soft accessory hanging high in the air before dropping back down onto her weary face. 'Ugh,' came a grunt as she swung her body around into a seated position. 'I'm going to kill him, I really am. I'm going to kill him... It's the only way...' Wildly gesturing as she stood, the bemused and disrupted Karina stormed across the inviting carpet to the kitchen corner, where she went straight for the freezer. 'I need something to get me through this,' her down-trodden words revealed, as a bottle of Absolut vodka was pulled out of it's icy habitat. Unscrewing the top as if it were Jeff Ward's neck, Karina then poured a near-mouthful of the cold, syrupy liquor past her rose lips, then knocked it back, Screwing her face up while shaking her head rapidly, Karina waited for the heat to pass down her throat, before returning the bottle to it's home. 'Now, what would he HATE?' Kari pondered as she moped towards the stereo, eyes scanning the vast expanse of CD's she had stacked and stored. Nobody much liked Jeff Ward in RPW, but at least most tolerated him... Which was a giant leap too much for Karina. She just about agreed he had the right to breathe, and even that was pushing it. 'Ah... Kittie.' As if to repel the soon-to-arrive Ward, like a crucifix to a Vampire - albeit a pin-stripe clad corporate blood-sucker - the crunching cords and gruff vocals filled the airy apartment, drawing two glares of aggravation from the felines she cohabited with. Then, just as the ringing had torn shreds off her idyllic atmosphere, a buzz of the building's inter-com forced it's way through the music. Slouching all the way, still resisting the urge to stand alert and to attention, Karina slid her feet along the carpet until she reached the inter-com's chrome housing. Pushing the button in, she didn't even offer any words of greeting to Ward.. After all, he was hardly invited. 'It's Je-' Ward went to announce, but Karina's thumb harshly jabbed the door release button, as if it were his cold, calculating eye. 'Why, thank you.' 'You're welcome,' she shot back with exaggerated, false sincerity before unceremoniously yanking the chain from the door and leaving it open for the ascending Ward. Less than cordially inviting Ward to let himself in, Karina slid her striped feet back through the thick, white carpet before collapsing back into her sofa, triggering once more it's fluid motion as it engulfed her sleek body. Through tired and aggravated eyes, Karina glared through the doorway, wanting the first thing that Ward noticed to be her severe annoyance. 'Notice that the mat doesn't say welcome?' Karina quipped the second Jeff Ward appeared in the doorway. Puzzled, he looked down at the floor, where a mat bearing the word 'GOODBYE' was there, facing into the apartment. 'Cute,' Ward muttered as he closed the door behind him to a protective hiss from Betsy, before venturing into the vast room. And it was, literally, a room. Living by herself, and intending for it to stay that way, Karina found no need for interior walls and doors. They would only be an obstacle and a hindrance. One room. One story. One girl. One unwelcome guest. 'May I?' Ward then enquired, nodding down to one of the chairs, only to receive an apathetic shrug as an answer. 'And how exactly do you have my home address?' a curious K-Wolf demanded, sure she hadn't passed on her sanctuary's address to anyone staffing RPW. After all, work can't call you in if they have nowhere to reach you. 'We decided after about the 10th speeding ticket of yours was sent to RPW, that we'd find the real address to forward them on to,' Ward was smarmily pleased to answer, more than a hint of one-up-manship in his voice. 'I'd hate for you not to get your mail.' 'Ahhhh,' Karina nodded, conceding defeat to Ward on the matter. 'Wondered why I'd suddenly started getting those sent here.' The relationship, or lack there of, between Wolfenden and Ward was an odd one. On-screen, numerous wrestling promotions had employees who hated their boss, and in a lot of cases, it made good TV. After all, who wouldn't want to land one of their boss every now and then? Well, Wolfenden for one. Despite her words and threats to the contrary, Karina didn't want to hit of kick Ward, she just didn't want to talk to him. Ever. To Kari, wrestling wasn't about desks, dollars and demographics. It was about fun. She still remembers the first time she played in a wrestling ring, a frail construct that felt as if it would collapse with every landing. It had height, it had bounce, and it had springy cables all around it. A veritable playground for a girl like Karina, who was used to absorbing a few knocks in the pursuit of her pleasures. As for the danger? Well, anyone who'd hung on to her for dear life as she let her Ducati push it's limits beneath her would tell you where they'd rather be. It wasn't even as if Kari was hard to get along with. Hell, she knew the names of virtually every member of the backstage crew's wives and kids, she was even a witness at one of their weddings. But if you were someone injecting beaurocracy into her fun, you were going to get a shoulder cold enough to give you frost-bite. 'I guess it's pointless asking why you weren't in attendance at our little publicity meeting?' Ward then asked, already expecting a jibe right back from the K-Wolf, who was already sat defensively, cross legged and shielded by a cushion on the sofa. 'I'm assuming you had something very important crop up in your packed schedule?' he then added, looking around the apartment, making his point that Kari was just lazing it. 'Well what would I have done there?' she appealed right back, having made it clear numerous times that she cared little for the verbal battles around matches. 'Said I was going to beat someone I don't even know? Claimed that the all powerful and mighty RPW would crush the infidels?' Wolfenden's probing continued, her words carrying the broken tone of a young woman who just wanted some sleep. Oddly enough, there was no rapid-fire, company-line response from Ward, who just sat and listed... Surprising Kari as she paused, even giving him room to get his words in. 'Odds are whoever I'm meant to be facing is just a normal woman,' Wolfenden then continued, feeling at unease with the brief silence between the pair, a rare happening indeed. 'Who's probably like me, and just wants to wrestle, without having to worry about all this 'pre-match banter' crap. Let's face it, I'm hardly Mike Tyson, and you're pretty far from Don King.' With her head cocked to the side, and eyes peeking out from just under their lids, Karina shot a look at Ward, hoping for some kind of empathy from him. 'Am I getting through to you at all here?' In her head, Karina had already planned her reply to Ward's come-back to her retaliation to his response. She liked to think ahead. But the words Ward offered up to her didn't fit any versions of the script, and even managed something all of Jeff's potent debating skills had yet to... 'Okay,' an almost too casual reply came as Ward opened up his constrictive body language, dropping both hands on the arms of the chair. 'Let's try things your way.' The day was about to get very strange.
