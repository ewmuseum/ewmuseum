CCTV...A wonderful thing

22 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/22/04 (Oxios/Carys joint) As Carys exited the yellow taxi that she took to the arena, arriving a little later than her Sweet Illusion comrades-in-arms, she stopped in her tracks. The cabbie drove off, his fare collected. Ms. Callicker herself seemingly disturbed by the situation at hand. It was something that was instinct. Whatever it was, she didn't like the feeling. It was like she was being watched. Scanning the parking lot, she couldn't see anyone. Carys thought that she had heard a voice, or some breathing, but the sound had stopped when the cab left; a set-up? The whole thing rubbed her the wrong way. She decided to get inside as quickly as possible. Passing car after car, it felt as though whoever was watching her was getting closer. She kept looking over her shoulder, glancing left and right, nothing. The pace sped up. Her footfall against the concrete surface of the ground echoed off of the walls. She wanted to see at least one person sitting out in their cars, but there was no one. Finally, the door was within ten feet. She didn't make the sudden movement of dashing for it; she had more composure than that. She felt uneasy, but never would she be afraid of any situation like this. Placing her hand on the handle, she pressed it downward and pulled the door open. 'SURPRI-- GAK!' A quick thrust to the throat knocked the smaller woman to her knees, gasping for air. Everyone else in the hallway looked at Carys as if she shot the poor girl between the eyes. Her body language seemed unapologetic, but she was sorry for having hit the first person she saw. 'For the love of-- you don't startle someone like that!' Looking down at the intern Cathy, one of Little Al's classmates, Carys said, 'You alright?' The girl just kept holding her throat and coughing. Coolie McRumpshaker escorted her away. For an infinitely long moment, everyone just stood there in silence. They weren't sure how to go on from there. The throat jab pretty much disrupted the flow. After a couple of the longest and most awkward seconds of any of their lives, the city clerk said, 'Would you mind stepping back outside, and we'll try all over again?' Carys, clearly confused by the whole thing, scratched her head as if that would help anything. 'Uh... fine, whatever.' Backing out of the door, Carys let the metal divider close behind her. After a five second wait, during which she wondered whether or not she should just enter through some other doorway, she turned the handle and stepped back in. 'SURPRISE!' Sadly, it went to the same result as Coolie McRumpshaker, moving forward with open arms, received a swift knee to the groin. Another voice said, 'What'd you do that for?' Carys stepped aside from the disgustingly obese young man, the smell of Arby's on his breath and shirt (along with a good deal of barbecue sauce). 'I don't do hugging.' Given that it's Coolie and nobody likes him, everyone shrugged and goes back to what they were doing. Confetti falls from the ceiling, people throw streamers around the hallway, and all are wearing party hats. There's a beach ball flying through the air, and...is that Triple Dutch Chocolate Cake? Hey, Geoff, save me some of that, okay? Thanks. 'What's all this about?' Carys asked, expecting a punch line or a stab in the back at any moment. The city clerk stepped forward. 'Carys Callicker, the city of Charlotte would like to congratulate and recognize you for all of your recent achievements in RPW.' Carys was a little wary of what was going on. 'What's your angle?' 'No angle,' the city clerk said. 'I'm a fifth tier ancillary character who'll never be seen again once this is over.' 'What?' Carys asked. The clerk shrugged, 'Nothing, don't worry about it.' Two middle-aged women, looking like they stepped out of the 1950's, approach Carys with a white sash. They make sure to be slow enough in their approach so as not to get hit anymore. Carys just takes the sash. 'What'd I do to get this?' was Carys' next question. The clerk said, 'Basically, for being a role model for women. You've shown the women of Charlotte, through RPW, that females can be just as good, if not better than men, even in a male-dominated industry.' Carys gave a slight nod. 'Not that I care much about being a role model, but whatever.' Everyone in the hallway began to sing, 'Oh, She's A Jolly Good Fellow.' By the end of it, Carys was ready to plug her ears. Or rip out everyone's vocal chords. It all depended on whether or not she wanted to share the pain she felt listening to these people butcher any song in a failed attempt to sing on key. Thankfully, it was over soon enough. “… So, do I win anything?” she asked. The clerk nodded his head. “Oh, yes…of course.” The clerk produced an envelope from his left coat pocket. Handing it to Carys, he stepped back to give her the respect of space that everyone needs when opening something. Carys ripped the top open with a nail, and then took out the paper inside, the envelope dropping to the floor. She read through it quickly for a few moments before shouting, “A BILL?!” “For the mess that we had to clean up last week,” the shorter clerk stood up on his toes, and then put his weight back on his heels. “Mess?” “Yes, it’s very expensive and time-consuming work trying to clean up 50 liters of raw sewage from a locker room. We were cleaning and disinfecting the room for three days, during which the smell went through the ventilation system and was making people sick. Three days after your little stunt you pulled on Vangellus Oxios and we still couldn’t use the Halton Arena for any events.” From the crowd of people out in the hallway, the District Attorney moved forward. “Charlotte and the Halton Arena have lost tens of thousands of dollars of revenue because of your actions. And rather than press charges, the city and the owners of the Halton Arena would first like to pursue an out-of-court settlement for full reimbursement of the lost profits from those three nights that the Halton Arena could not be used.” Carys tore the bill in half. “Well, piss off I’m not paying you people $19 thousand!” Raising his eyebrows for a moment, the DA said, “I suppose we will have to take the matter to court. But mind you, the entire incident was broadcast live throughout the region. You will lose should this go to trial. And then there might be the possibility of the other charges that we could bring against you.” “Other charges?” things just kept getting better. He had a dossier with him, he did. The District Attorney opened it and skipped to a yellow paper, one of the few in the stack of pages. “In order for your little prank to work, you had to seal the room with a forklift so that Vangellus Oxios and his associates could not escape the room. That’s false imprisonment. Prolonged, unprotected exposure to large amounts of raw sewage can cause severe illness. The sewage has a liquid base, which can conduct electricity; given that there are open electrical outlets in the room, there was the possibility of electric shock. And, because that sewage has a high methane count, had there been an electrical fire sparked, the entire room could have exploded. “You see, at the very least, we can add manslaughter to the charges, but due to the risk of electrocution, we can go all the way up to attempted murder. The only reason that nothing like that happened is because the people you locked into the room covered the electrical outlets and sealed themselves into the bathroom.” Carys couldn’t believe what was happening. Can’t someone just throw sewage into a room anymore? Everyone’s gotta have attorneys involved in things. Bastards. “The only reason that charges haven’t already been filed,” the DA continued, “is because Mr. Oxios declined to file charges. But if the city has to go to court to receive financial restitution, I can assure you that I will pursue those charges.” The clerk rubbed his fingers together in the gesture for money. “So will that be cash or check?” Carys threw her arms into the air. “I don’t have that kind of money lying around!” “Hm…” the clerk muttered. “That is a problem.” “What? Rampage and El Diablo beat innocent people to crap on live television, go harass them! Since when does ANYONE in wrestling actually get prosecuted, anyway?” Carys exclaimed. “When you cost the city money, we most definitely come after you,” answered the self-righteous DA, halfway posturing for the RPW cameras. “Ugh..” Another voice came through the crowd. “Hold up, I think I can help here.” A divine light shone from above and the throngs of onlookers parted as Vangellus Oxios entered the scene to the singing of “Hallelujah.” As he approached, the light became blinding. “Dammit, Bill, turn that thing off.” Having killed the ambiance of the entrance, the choir around the corner stopped singing, the instruments going off-key in a quick halt of play. Bill shuts down the elevated light. “Sorry.” Rolling her eyes, Carys said, “Oh, great…YOU.” Oxios, wearing some overalls and no shirt (apparently he’s doing a Hillbilly Jim impersonation or something), walked up to Carys. “If you’ll indulge me, I think I’ve got a way to solve everyone’s problems.” “How can you help me?” she asked antagonistically. Vangellus Oxios smirked. “Taking care of that hefty fine you’ve got looming over your head like the Sword of Damocles would be one way. But it will cost you.” Carys huffed. “I don’t do blow jobs, you want Alyssa.” Many gasps came from everyone around. Not because they were in any way surprised about Alyssa, but because Carys said the words ‘blow job’ on television. Big deal. “No, no…” Oxios waved his hands. Like he needs Sanders when he’s got a line of normally prudish women ready to strip for him? “I want a match in exchange for this expunging of your record, so to speak.” “Fine, done.” Carys had no problem wrestling anyone; this was killing two birds with one stone. Vangellus shook his head. “It’s not that simple. I mean, after all, I wasn’t the only one in that room. Adrienne, Emi, Harold, Darcellus… they all want your head in a ringer. But since only two of them are really capable of it, I had to tell Harold and Adrienne to sit back.” “Your point, tard?” Carys responded, obviously agitated by the whole situation. “My point is that you’ve got three people who want you in the ring, myself, Darse, and Emi. And I can’t in good conscience tell the other two that they can’t face you if they want to. So for me to take care of this bill for you, you’re going to need to face all three of us in a match.” Carys frowned. “That’s three on one. I’d rather go to court than deal with that.” “I know that’s why you can bring along two of your friends for a six-person tag match. It’s only fair, even though I’m sure that none of you are familiar with the concept.” “How am I going to get any of them to tag with me against you, pissin’ Chewbacca, and some nutcase with a sword?” Shrugging, Oxios said, “That’s not my concern. You’re in hot water either way you look at it. But at least if you accept the match, there won’t be any possibility of prison in the near future.” Grinning, Vangellus turned his back to Carys and exited the way he came, with the light shining bright and the music playing him out. Damn, he’s got BACKSTAGE entrance music and effects. That is pure overkill. Carys kept the frown on her face. She didn’t like being back into corners. But then again, when cornered, she did some of her best work. Still, she had a $19k fine from Charlotte, NC hanging over her head, and someone had to pay it before the night was over.
