You Have No Idea How Dumb These Can Get

7 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless Poking around the same harbour as the last time with much the same worries as he had the last time, we find The Dread Pirate Roberts settled down on the shore in his usual concealing garb. He was sprawled out on his back with his knees bent and his feet flat on the ground, casually holding a fishing pole aloft from his left hand so that the string disappeared into the water no more than a few feet out to sea. Judging by the heavy-lidded-ness of his eyes he had been fishing here for some time with nothing to show for his efforts. Try as he might, he just couldn't get a single bite. Making fun of Magnum Wolf and Karina Wolfenden didn't get him anywhere, nor did mocking El Diablo and Vangellus Oxios, or poking harmless fun at any number of people on the roster. It didn't seem to be a problem with the product or the delivery, as both were exceptional, but more of a lack of interest from the concerned parties, or maybe a lack of understanding. Or perhaps even a lack of cajonas. Did they really dread continued mockery so much that they hoped to make it disappear by ignoring it? Could they not mock in return? That's all the DPR was after; a little bit of acknowledgement, a friendly bite from time to time to let him know his efforts were not in vain. But that was too much trouble. It seemed the others had better things to do than compete and have fun with their fellow wrestlers . . . They were too busy wrestling with themselves. Was a little bit of mockery really too much to ask? It appeared so. Unless . . . Dost thou mocketh me by ignoring mine mockery? Wouldst thou mocketh me so? The DPR didn't think so, and nor do I. It would be too fiendishly clever for their ilk. Together, united by our ego, we decided that it was fear and put an end to any further speculation. But whether it truly was out of fear, ignorance, or even mockery, if they sought to fend off the onslaught through such amateur means they were in for a rude awakening. The Man in Black was like the mighty sea that bore him to this land; he never relented. So it was that we found him fishing. Upon closer inspection, however, this fishing was not at all typical in its application. The business end of the apparatus held neither hook nor traditional bait, and instead of waiting near the bottom or under the bank or in the weeds, it was out there in the plain open, just beneath the surface, bobbing merrily. In place of the hook was a sparkling sphere of a size with a pingpong ball. How it was attached to the string is a secret only Roberts knew, and he wasn't telling me about it no matter how much I pestered. He wasn't telling me what he intended to catch, either, the miserable son of a bitch . . . But I digress. Waiting on the bank, he didn't even bother to jiggle his strange bait in order to attract his would-be victim. He knew it would come sooner or later. Later rather than sooner, with the sun well past its acme, it happened. A rather large fished charged headlong into the ball, instantly knocking it loose from its string and seemingly robbing the DPR of all he had going for him. All of that planning and waiting for nought. But if that was the case, why was he smiling? He leapt to his feet and clapped his hands together triumphantly, then slowly and mysteriously counted to three before plunging into the knee-deep water and reaching into it with both hands. Moments later he pulled up a small cage from directly underneath where the ball had been, his eyes as proud and knowing as they were vindictive as he carried his prize up onto the bank and set it down. Remarkably, his plan seemed to have worked, for trapped inside the cage was the glittering ball and the fish that had hit it. Hey! Wait a minute! The fish he caught was the same fish that punctured his boat the other day! The DPR shook his head as he explained telepathically to me. It would seem that he had rigged the cage underneath the water with a sensor that would swing the door shut three seconds after something entered into it. The door itself faced upwards instead of to the side, which probably seems weird when you consider how fish swim around, but if you'll only stop to think about the ball everything will make sense. Sparkling as it was and seemingly so easily attainable, the DPR knew that the damnable fish wouldn't be able to resist taking a blind stab at it . . . Just as he knew that as soon as the slimy critter got within fin's reach of it it would undoubtedly drop it. Then, unable to comprehend the fact that it was too clumsy to ever pick it up and run with it, the fish would charge down and try to capture it again and again, essentially banging its head into the wall like the old proverb says, stopping at nothing, not even its own blood. And so it was caught. A simple plan, but an ingenious one just the same. 'Well now,' Roberts said cheerfully as he unlatched the door, 'not so special now, are you, my little devil fish?' Amazingly, in that short time the little bastard actually did manage to cut itself open, coercing a trickle of red blood to mix in with the ugly discolouring of its head. Its mouth snapped open and shut frantically, almost like a fish out of water gasping for breath . . . But we know better than that. We know the perverted little idiot was only trying to lick its own blood. 'Cut that out,' the DPR scolded as he picked it up in one hand and held it a foot in front of his face, peering at it with disgust. 'You might develop a taste for the stuff, and then where would you be?' The fish squirmed in protest, but the DPR held it fast. 'That's right. You'd be forever trying to scramble your way back up to the midcard.' He tsked and shook his head. 'GRAH~!' yelled the fish inexplicably. 'I R SHUD BE CHAMP~! TAKE ME TO YOUR LEADER~!' The Man in Black sighed, somehow undisturbed that such a simple being could speak. 'Alright, fish . . . Listen up.' His voice made it plain that he would brook no nonsense from the small and ostensibly shrinking beast. 'I know that you value yourself higher than everything else in existence, but do you really need to tilde every insipid thing that belches forth from your cavernous mouth? Nothing you say is that important, really, and it's all been heard a million times before . . . with half of that million coming from you.' 'GRAH~! U R WANTING TROUBLE?! TROUBLE LIKE I WAS GIVING YOUR SHIP?! GRAH~! I R EVIL~!' The fish tried to puff itself up and look menacing, squirming to inch closer to the DPR's face, but the result was it wiggling around impotently and scaring nothing. 'U R SHUD B F3AR1N6 ME~!' 'Are you mental?' 'F34R M3~! I R 3V1L~!' 'Yes, yes . . . You've already said that,' the DPR yawned, struggling to keep his eyes open. 'Countless times.' 'DON'T B MAKING ME HURT U~!' 'I'll try not to,' Roberts smirked, rolling his eyes as he unsheathed his sword and prepared to rid the world of this parasite. He tried to think of someone who would mourn, of anyone who might care, but his mind came up blank. At least in that he could relate to the fish for a brief moment before it died. As it warbled on about its ownership or the free world and everything in it, the Man in Black drew the sword back, but just as he was about to run it through he felt an iron grip on his upper arm. 'Are you seriously attacking a defenseless fish?' a voice boomed over his shoulder, echoing somehow to make it sound more commanding than it really was. 'You'd better have a license for that.' The Dread Pirate Roberts turned around to face the newcomer, a sardonic cast to his features. 'License?' He spat the word as if he'd never heard anything more stupid in his life. 'This fish here smashed through my boat the other day, spouts gibberish at anyone and anything that gives it half a look, and worst of all, it fancies itself as some sort of modern day vampire. What more license do I need?' 'Very funny,' the man said dryly. He was actually a cop, resplendent in his neatly pressed uniform and brightly shining gold badge. Ruddy brown hair swept down to his shoulders, coming out from under his hat at all angles and obscuring half of his face, which was remarkable only for its unremarkableness. He was your typical Joe Average, but he had paid his dues and earned authority through them, and now he planned to impress that on the lowly citizen. 'But I meant your fishing license. You do have a fishing license, don't you? I'd hate to have to give you a ticket.' The DPR looked at the fish, who shrugged somehow, then cast a doubtful look at the officer. 'I'm afraid I don't follow you. A 'fishing license'?' 'I'll take that as a no,' the policeman huffed, shaking his head. 'All right, then. Throw the fish back in the water and we'll pretend this never happened.' He gave a warm smile, letting the DPR know that he was doing him a solid. Roberts didn't see it that way. 'You'd willingly unleash this idiotic beast back on the public? We have the chance to destroy it here and now! Let's act while we can, to save others the torment we've both undergone in simply having to stare at this hideous thing.' 'I wish I could allow that,' the cop said with obvious regret, 'but I can't. It wouldn't be right.' 'What? It would be a mercy, both to the miserable fish and the entire world.' 'I R GUD'R DEN BOTH OF U~!' the fish interjected, but both men ignored it, as was its due. 'That's not our place to say. Throw it back in the water.' 'Never,' the DPR stated with conviction, pulling back the sword once more. The cop let his hand fall down to his pistol and dangled it there, shouting the threat yet leaving it unspoken. 'Don't make me do this.' 'Nothing's stopping you from turning and walking away . . .' The DPR inched the blade closer and closer to the fish's exposed throat. 'Surrender!' the officer yelled. 'You mean you wish to surrender to me?' The Man in Black was surprised, but not overly. 'Very well; I accept.' 'Give up the fish!' 'Death first!' The point of the sword was mere inches away from the squirming fish now, and the policeman's hand was wrapped firmly around his gun. From out of nowhere a tiny man in a horned red suit materialised on the cop's left shoulder, a pitchfork in hand. 'Kill him!' it shouted, jumping up and down with excitement. The DPR couldn't see this, of course, and nor should you or me, but apparently this policeman was a few coins short of a buried treasure. 'Kill him before it's too late!' The cop seemed to be considering this when another little man appeared on his right shoulder. This one had fluffy white wings and a flowing gray robe, and on its head it wore a golden circlet. 'You can't do that. It wouldn't be right,' it told him pretentiously with a solemn bow of its head. The man in red sneered. 'He's a pirate, damnit. Kill him. You know you want to.' 'No,' the man in white begged. 'He doesn't deserve death for this, and besides, you like him. Admit it. He's a sexy beast.' 'WHAT?!' roared the man in red, brandishing his pitchfork as if he knew how to use it. 'YOU'RE FRICKIN' GAY! I KNEW IT!' 'Being in touch with my feminine side doesn't make me gay,' the angelish pixie said, thrusting his nose in the air. 'And we don't give a flask of piss what you think.' 'A flask?' the devilish one snorted. 'A 'flask'?! BWAHAHAHAHAHA! That proves you're gay! And we? Don't include him in your faggotry! Stop trying to turn our friend here homosexual! Let him kill this guy and get it over with!' 'Never!' 'Enough!' the cop yelled, but the DPR ignored him and stabbed the fish through the head anyway. Realising he was too late to stop it, the policeman shed a single tear as he pulled out his pad and began writing with a shaky hand. 'That's it, pal,' he whined to the DPR, 'you're gonna pay for that.' The Man in Black readied himself for the battle, but that's not the sort of payment this champion of the law had in mind. 'Ten bucks,' he sniffed, tearing the uppermost sheet from his pad and thrusting it at the DPR. 'Payable within thirty days at any courthouse. I hope you've learned your lesson!' With that, he buried his face in the crook of his arm and ran away, crying. Roberts looked down at the ticket, scribbled in pink crayon on a blank sheet of paper, then watched the crazy man tear off into oblivion with wary confusion. 'Uh, yeah,' the DPR said to the air, dragging out the last word. 'Boy do I feel stupid.' Tossing the dead fish and the meaningless paper on the ground, he gave his head a shake and walked away, scouting for more people to make fun of. 'I'll never mess with him again.' Before I leave you, rest assured that he was laughing. Hard. At our beloved champion of justice.
