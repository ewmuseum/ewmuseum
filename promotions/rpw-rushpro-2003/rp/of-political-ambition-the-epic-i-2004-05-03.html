Of Political Ambition - The Epic I

3 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: In this season. “Good night, Joyce.” David Lieberman snapped the door shut of his massive oak door, wishing his secretary a good night sleep. It would be a night of the usual routine, subjecting his wife to another cold night between the sheets with a hallow hole in their mattress, and her heart. The life of a politician was hard. Dave withdrew a small golden clip from his pocket before collapsing in his velvet chair, behind his massive oak desk. Wrapped within the clip was a collection of green bills, kissed with the delightful mug of Benjamin Franklin. Dave thumbed through a few of the bills, estimating the amount he’d need for the activities he had planned with a random hooker. Finding that his funds will suffice the night’s entertainment, Dave searched the top drawer of his desk. Reaching deep within, fishing in places no hand would look in curiosity, the robust Republican finally found what he needed. Wrapped in a small sandwich baggy, twisted with a band, was his eightball of cocaine that would last him the night. “Let’s get a head start.” He mumbled to himself, taking the keys to his Volvo and dipping the tip in to the bag, once un-wrapping the band. A mound of powder, deep within the grooves of the ignition, was slowly brought to the edge of his nostrils, in hopes that he wouldn’t lose any of his $70 investment. When you’re a politician, you tend to make some good friends high up in the narcotics game. For reasons that have been rooted since the ‘20s, the law makers always got the best drugs for free. As he snorted the coke deep in to his capillaries, pushing on the tip of his cheek to catch the excess in his snot just to suck it down, all he could think about was the ulcer he was developing with election season coming up. It was too much of a burden to notice the man that had been sitting in front of him for five minutes, watching his hedonistic actions with grim intentions. “Saturday night on a Tuesday, Lieberman?” Dave was startled. The eightball fell to his Persian carpet, spilling between the tightly woven fibers, as David’s heart pumped twice as fast. It was hard to tell if it was from the cocaine or from the scare, but regardless, he once again returned to his drawer, frantically searching for protection. “Don’t bother, it’s right here.” Lieberman’s pocket protector, a six shooter with the chamber flipped out of its hammer’s reach, stay in the palm of this mysterious man’s hand. Slowly, one by one, he released each bullet of their potential duty by dropping them to the carpet, followed by flipping the chamber back in to place, and tucking it in to the front of his pants. “Do you wonder what you’re wife is thinking right now? Why she has to sleep alone night after night and keep a pretty face for the papers when she hates you more than she hates God for assigning her this fate?” “Wh...-wha?” Was all David could stammer. “I hope you are enjoying yourself, living the ‘American Dream’. The ripe fruit of a dollar bill being pulled from your teeth with help of a stripper’s g-string.” November is half a year away. David Lieberman has six months to campaign for re-election, six months to push the bills his advisors said would benefit him the best come 2004. It was an arduous year, one that would pay off with endorsements if executed correctly. Lieberman wondered who this man in front of him was. His attire was highly unconventional, and held no importance. The only assumption Lieberman could make about his intruder was that he was a black man, although he couldn’t be sure for his face was hidden. Behind a solid black satin mask, no holes for seeing or breathing, was a political fanatic. “Well me, Davie: What is on your political agenda this year? Are you... tackling the long-avoided immigration problem at the border?” The man scoffed. “Of course not. How about tackling the local Kidd Cartel’s transport in to Arizona? No, we wouldn’t want to do that, that would effectively end the deal you have with the Cartel, and the agreement you’ve made with the Arizona-Mexico Border Patrol.” Lieberman’s grip on the arm-rest of his chair tightened, grinding his fingernails in parallel with the frame of the wood. How the intruder knew of David’s dirty dealings scared him even more. With a single phone call to the papers, the Arizona Senator could be removed from office and jailed. “I’m going to cut this facade of fear I’ve imposed and say what I have to say, then do what I have to do. I know you intend to build yourself a viable contender for the 2008 nomination. A susceptible man to succeed Bush. Wonderful, congratulations, etc., but I can’t help but find myself... disappointed in your means.” Unfolding a small stack of papers withdrawn from his jacket pocket, the man began to read from various highlighted sections. “... ‘the downsize of consumer assistance jobs, for the replacement of facilities in over-seas countries for a lower wage rate’.” The man returned the paper to his jacket. This whole time, David couldn’t return his eyes from the black void in his face. The horror and mystery of it all captivated him, as he awaited whatever fate this man had decided. “You’re hurting us, Senator. The people you wish to serve come four years are the same you are killing, just to get on the good side of Big Business for yet another sponsor come November. You’ve dealt a blow to the workforce that shaped the man you were, not the man you are. And for this, David, we have decided to hit back.” The man stood from his seat, the light from the hallway cast a shadow of forbearing over the lowly politician. Despite seeing the man’s hands wrapped inside leather gloves, David was inticed to stay. He couldn’t move, he didn’t have a choice. His mind was paralyzed. The man approached him from around the desk, leaning over the fallen rock of cocaine. He removed the bond that bound the bag, snapping it around his wrist. “Excuse me,” the man muttered, pushing the man aside while searching the drawer at his waist. He removed a silver blade, a letter opener. “Just know, this isn’t unprovoked. This isn’t mindless. This is just a counter-strike against the system you love, an extremely focused counter-strike.” With a closed fist, the man back-handed Lieberman in his throat. Knocked out of his chair, David desperately crawled, in search of haven and gasping for air in hoarse breaths. It was as if being sentenced when he felt the leather-clad hand jerk him by the collar. By the man’s will, not his own, David was placed atop his oak desk. As if restrained by his tie, David’s struggle to breathe rendered him helpless. His lack of oxygen often drawing his eyelids shut, he could barely feel the hand, unhooking his belt and jerking his testicles outside the whole in his boxers, then through the zipper. Black out, back up. “-the way I see it, you have two chances.” “-fueled the machine just enough to keep us scarcely satisfied, just enough to-” Lieberman could feel a hand clamp over his mouth, the band triple-wrapped around his sack, creating a null space of daft circulation. Then, like a tongue in the ear, he felt the unpleasant blade of the letter-knife against his scrotum. “-seed spread no longer.” Just a clean cut. David’s muffled screams couldn’t be heard, the man’s leather muting the chance of help. He took a single, severed testicle, placing it in a zip-lock baggy removed from his pocket. While David drifted asleep from the pain, he heard the man’s final words and instructions. “There is a black unmarked vehicle outside that will take you to a doctor. He has instructions to cauterize and seal your unfortunate wound. I left you a remnant of your manhood as a token of your co-operation tonight. I trust you will keep quiet about this, for you have no proof of the events that transpired aside from what is missing in your pants. The security tapes that have captured any evidence of me have already been erased, further removing all proof that I was even here. You will not find me, if you go looking, and the embarrassment from going public with these events will be more than enough to ruin your chances at getting elected to another office.” “I don’t want you to differ on your path; in fact, I wish you prosperity. I would like you to peak in your political career. Just know, the sun is rising. And with the rise it shall inspire a change long ignored but not forgotten. The sickle and the hammer shall be replaced by the star and the stripe, and the new America shall prosper to greatness.” “The socialist cancer is spreading, and we will take over the system you love. Be it peaceful, or through mutinous actions...”
