The Day Chris Storm Almost Got Away

21 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Feb. 21, 2004 Would you know how uncomfortable sleeping in a bed is when the bed was flipped over? Chris Storm would not. Or at least he could not. Maybe if Chris Storm had been able to fall asleep he would know what it was like but all throughout the twilight hour he kept one eye open. It was not the discomfort of sleeping in a bed that had been flipped over that kept him awake. He believed if he closed his eyes he might never open them again. When you are a wanted man by the Mafia you tend to think like that. Every day became your last. You changed without even noticing. They break you without even touching you. It is how they have gotten away with it for so many years. They force you to break yourself mentally. Then it is easy pickings for them. Chris Storm would not even notice he was doing it to himself. The sleepless night had given Chris a chance to think about his life. His mind kept wondering, as it often did, to the wrestling ring. He would think about his Global title match against AWS Man and the champion, Seymour Almasy. Chris knew about Seymour but he knew nothing about this AWS Man, which actually was rather sad as Chris had wrestled Adam Jacobs in AWS Man’s own league of WMW. When his mind lost focus Chris found his mind often wondering about this mystery known as AWS Man. Morning came, the gentle touch of light caressed Chris’ cheek. Those beaten eyes painfully opened. His right eye, the one that supported a nice cut above it, had already started to swell up. He reluctantly removed himself from the stiff bed and stretched in the light. Chris had been so tired last night he had not even bothered to change his clothes. “Ring, ring, ring!” The sound made Chris’ heart skip a beat. Was it them calling again; the Mafia? It could not be, he still had a week to pay them back. Chris made his way out of his bedroom and through the wrecked remains of once had been a living room. He did his best to steady his hand. His nerves had gotten the best of him and Chris could do little to stop from shaking, sweat forming across his forehead, and his heart beating faster and faster and faster! “Hel…Hello?” he asked. His voice would not stop shaking. The voice on the other end of the line surprised him. “You dead yet?” “GOD DAMNIT ARIZONA, I’M NOT DEAD YET!” Chris screamed. “And don’t do that again, you scared the crap out of me. I thought it was them calling saying they wanted their money back.” He kept on. Chris in his own mind had dodged a bullet there. There was laughter on the other end of the line. “Chill man, I just called to make sure you were alright. No harm in that right?” Arizona did his best to try and lighten the mood of his friend. “Oh both Seymour and AWS Man cut some air time against you.” The news of wrestling did manage to grab Chris’ attention. “Go on…” “Well not surprising, AWS Man is a moron. He’s addicted to porn and…” Arizona started but it did not take long for Chris to interrupt him. “And what is wrong with that? Least you forget I was once a porn star” Chris added in. “ANYWAYS!” Arizona started off. “The guy is clearly crazy. Suplex his head into the mat and put him out of our misery.” Chris absorbed his manager’s information. It had been a wise decision to bring in his old manager to further assist him in RPW and now PTC. “Good, what else you got for me?” The sound of paper could be heard rustling on Arizona’s side of the phone, he looked through his notes. “Seymour Almasy claimed to have you scouted. He says you’re predictable.” Chris just gave an arrogant laugh. “I am predictable. I go in there and wrestle. I suplex you and I pin you. Why did he even waste his time scouting me? He is such a fag. Hey Arizona, did you hear Almasy is so gay he gives fashion advice to the Queer Eye.” Arizona laughed at the joke, “Good one Chris, I have a joke too! Knock, knock.” “Knock this, dumb fucker” was the only reply Arizona got before he heard the phone slam followed by the ring tone. Chris Storm was in no mood for small talk. News of wrestling, though his life seemed to focus around it most of the time, was not something he wanted to worry about. He had much larger problems then worrying what Seymour Almasy and AWS Man were doing at the moment. One of his main problems was making it to the match with both legs still intact. He stood alone in the ruins of his apartment. He had nothing left, the Mafia made sure of that. With a deep sigh and a heavy heart, Chris walked back to his bedroom and entered the closet. He turned to face out of the closet and reached up above the door frame. He knocked on the wall above it. Plaster fell to the littered floor leaving a small hole in the wall. Chris reached into the hole and revealed a small Colt revolver from within the hiding place. With the gun in hand he exited the bedroom and grabbed his keys off the kitchen counter. Chris tucked the gun into the back of his pants and allowed his shirt to hang loose covering it from view. “I’m going for a ride; I have to get out of the city until the heat dies down on me.” Chris said out loud. There was no one around to hear him so why did he say it? Maybe it was to ensure him that his problem were that easy. Take a little vacation and when you get back all your problems will be solved. If only it was that simple. Chris slammed the door to his apartment. He did not bother to lock it, there was nothing left of value to steal. There was just silence for a few seconds; Chris kept his back to the door. The screams of police sirens shattered the silent serenity. Chris shivered at the thought. Police sirens meant crime, crime meant the Mafia, Mafia meant pain, destruction, and even death. The distraction of the whaling sirens broke Chris’ focus. He walked off to the elevator to descend into the dark cavernous parking garage. On his doormat today’s paper rested. Chris was too worried about his own problems to be bothered to read the world’s problems. It was a shame since if he had bothered to even look at the paper he would have seen the headline about PTC coming to town with the Global title match. So what is the problem with that? The match is booked on FAW, a league Chris Storm has never seen eye to eye with. His bloody match with Apocalypse, a six man tag team match involving three members of their roster, and his recent victory over Adam Jacobs are two of the biggest keys of his career involving them. Both would be there at the show. The last thing Chris needed was two bitter wrestlers looking to see that Chris does not win the title. “Ding!” The large metallic doors slowly opened, the damp smell of the parking garage rolled into the elevator like a passenger wanting to go for a ride. Chris moved on through the smell toward his car, his keys dangling in his fingertips. When his eyes fell on his car he let out a silent sigh of relief. His car, a 1997 Coupe Deville, remained in tact. Chris had a bad feeling, much like his apartment, he would find his car demolished. Chris got the ’97 Deville cheap enough used. It turned out the owner of it died. He did not need it anymore, but on an Indy salary, Chris could sure use it. The engine roared to life, the baby had some serious power in it. Chris continued to rev the engine, letting the sound of the mighty machine wash away his fears. There was something about the sound of the grand machine coming to life under Chris’ command that gave him a sense of power and control. Chris knew the plan; he would drive out of the city to New Jersey where he could just hang low until he got the money to pay back the Mafia. He would sneak to the Global title match and wrestle. After putting the bitch slap on AWS Man (Also Known As Bill) and Seymour Almasy, Chris would run off with the Global title and head back to New Jersey again. What could go wrong with an awesome plan like that? Chris threw the car into drive and took off out of the parking garage. He knew he needed to avoid a scene less the Mafia notice something. So Chris drove humbly threw the packed city streets until he found himself out in the open road. The sounds of the mighty engine were drowned out by ‘Rock Superstar’ by Cypress Hill. The concrete jungle that moved by his window was replaced with beautiful and luscious green scenery. In the freedom of the open road Chris let himself have fun and revved up the engine once more. A flash of lights caused Chris’ heart to drop into his stomach. His body went into panic mode. His palms were sweaty, heart raced, head spun, he felt like his entire body would crash on him. Again the lights flashed at him. Chris cocked his head up and looked into his rearview mirror. Some slack jaw yokel seemed to want to race him. Taking his eyes from his mirror, Chris looked down at his speedometer. The needle moved gently down just past 80 mph. Chris had never noticed he had been driving at twice the speed limit. Whoever this redneck was they seemed bent on beating the city slicker that dared to enter his road space. That whiskey bottle in hand, swung from the beat up Dodge truck. It seemed to be his symbol for a need for speed. His trademark grin posed on his lips. If this trailer park trash wanted a race, Chris would show him just what the city slicker could do. “Bring it Billy Bob Joe.” Chris mused to himself. He was enjoying this. Chris was born and bred a wrestler of pure technical stature. He lived off competition, whether it is in the ring against the likes of Seymour Almasy and AWS Man or on the road in a race. The little race would be nothing more than a chance to stroke his ego and further back his claim of Perfection. Chris looked ahead at the empty road ahead of him. Nothing but smooth, paved, asphalt stretched on for miles. He put on his left blinker letting the Dodge truck know it was clear for them to race side by side. The truck pulled up next to him in the other lane. The speedometer climbed up to 90 mph, 95. Surprisingly the rusty white Dodge truck kept up with Chris’ Coupe Deville. Whoever this country bumpkin was, they kept their truck running in impressive condition though the outside could stand to have a little work. Chris tapped the gas, letting him know the true power that slept inside the engine. The man leaned over his passenger seat and smiled at Chris. The man was clearly drunk, the fumes so strong they reached Chris’ car and filled the air with the scent of strong whiskey and cheap beer. Chris rolled up his window; he had enough of this fooling around. Clearly so did the drunk as before Chris could even floor it the truck sped off into the lead. Chris would not be beat now and slammed his foot into the gas causing the speedometer to soar. …95 mph …100 mph …110 mph How could such a ragged truck keep up with Chris’ beautiful Deville? Chris caught up with the Dodge but they soon approached a turn. His gaze turned from the road to the drunkard just a car length beside him. There was not a sign of sweat on his brow as they approached the turn faster and faster. Chris on the other hand started to unfold. As they approached the turn at a blinding speed of 120 mph Chris’ nerves started to give out on him. The only thing still forcing his foot on the gas was his pride, or maybe his ego. Both would be crushed if he faltered now. The mental debate that raged on in his mind was interrupted by the screaming sirens and flashing red and blue. His eyes darted up to his mirror. A police cruiser had decided to join in the exhibition. Chris’ senses took control of the wheel and applied the brakes on this suicide mission. The Coupe Deville’s tires screeched to a halt, smoke leaving a trail behind the car. If it had not been for his seat belt Chris would have been eating glass. His body jerked back into the seat sending him for an impact. Chris eased the car onto the side of the road, the police cruiser right behind him. Chris watched the Dodge truck continue on at amazing speeds. The drunkard’s too the turn and disappeared out of sight, the race won. Chris cursed himself repeatedly for giving in when he did. Those flashing lights and blaring sirens were cut off. Chris again checked his mirror, noticing the cop had exited the cruiser. He fixed up his hair and lowered the visor. Chris practiced his best fake smile, “What seemed to be the problem officer?” he asked him in a clearly false tone. He turned off the radio. The officer approached his door and tapped on the window with his nightstick. Chris rolled down the window and turned to look at him. “Ah!” a gasp escaped his lips. The officer had some weird mask on covering his face. Was it Halloween or something? Two burning eyes peered through the mask at Chris. That false smile had disappeared from Chris’ Romanesque face. “Sir, please put your freakin’ hands where I can freakin’ see them and get out of the freakin’ car slowly” the officer ordered. His words were muffled on the bar cage that covered his mouth. At first Chris did not want to ever get near this psycho looking cop. He looked at the officer’s badge, ‘Bill’. The officer seemed to pick up on Chris’ hesitation. “The freakin’ mask is because I freakin’ broke an orbital bone and was freakin’ ordered to wear this protective freakin’ mask so I don’t further freakin’ damage it.” Chris held up one hand for the officer to see. The other hand moved toward the other seat, reaching for that handgun he had. “Sir get out of the freakin’ car now.” Bill restated, louder and more firmly now. Now wanting to piss of the masked freak anymore, Chris exited his car. After all, he was the Perfect Storm, he could handle this situation. Chris put on his false smile and picked up his chin as he looked at the officer. “What seemed to be the problem?” Maybe if Chris could see past that mask he would have seen the one known as Bill raised his eyebrow. “The freakin’ problem is you were going double the freakin’ speed limit.” “Oh that…yeah…” Chris bit his bottom lip, he needed an excuse. He could not let himself get caught now it might be fatal. “I’m sure an excellent cop like you saw that drunkard chasing me! It was trying to run me off the road. I did everything I could to avoid the psycho.” Chris’ words seemed unable to penetrate that mask. “I’m taking you into the freakin’ station” come the cold words through that mask. “Can’t we talk this out? Surely there must be some other way” Chris asked, he moved back toward the car slowly. Was he going to try and go for his gun? “My name isn’t Surely, its freakin’ Bill. Now get in the freakin’ car.” To further show he meant it he pointed toward his police cruiser. Chris looked uncomfortable. He had left the window door, the gun just a short reach inside the car. Chris moved backward, pressing his body against the body of the car. “Look let me get my license, its right here in the car.” Chris told him, reaching into the car. Bill placed his hand on Chris’ shoulder stopping any further movement. Chris turned and nailed Bill in the face with a hard right. Only problem was Bill did not move. Chris’ fist had stopped on that mask. Even a hard shot from Chris could not break that protective match. “BUZZZZZZZZZZZZ!” Thousands of volts surged through Chris’ body. The entire nerve system shut down and Chris fell to the dusty ground. Bill blew on his tazer and put it away. He lifted Chris and threw him into the back of the police cruiser. He grabbed the radio. “I freakin’ have him.” Bill looked in the back of the car at the unconscious Chris Storm. “Is he hurt?” the other side of the radio replied. The voice seemed to have an Italian accent to it. “Not freakin’ much.” The officer known as Bill grabbed his nightstick and shoved it through the bars separating the back from the front of the cruiser and smacked Chris in the ribs with it. “Good bring him in; the Hate Machine wants to see him.” The voice died after the order. Bill looked at Chris, “you’re freakin’ in trouble now. I would hate to freakin’ be you.” The cruiser pulled off leaving behind the peaceful road in the country. It headed back toward the concrete jungle. Further and further the car drove off, the closer the car moved toward the city the closer the guillotine moved toward Chris’ neck. Could Chris even make it to the match? The Ironman lived and breathed for wrestling. It had become his life. If he missed this match, it would be sure that it was be because Chris had indeed stopped breathing. His life began with wrestling, it would end with it as well.
