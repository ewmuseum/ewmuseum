Curious People

27 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless 'Fancy meeting you here,' came a lofty female voice, condescending from on high to address a peasant. The lady had copious raven hair that cascaded down to her lower back and outlined a scowling face shaped like an acorn. The present twisting of her face was a sorry attempt at a smile, but as I already mentioned, it came across as a scowl. As I didn't mention, perhaps the scowl was intentional despite the smile. There was no love lost between these two, nosiree. 'Lorena,' replied the man, frostier than Alaska in the dead of winter. He wore his black hair in a half-clown-half-Elvis fop, and had adopted what we not-in-the-business derisively call a porn-stache. No smile seemed to be forthcoming, as he wasn't in a mood to pretend. Instead his mouth twisted into a sneer as his eyes scanned her up and down, then stuck his nose in the air and made to move on past her. They were in a small building with all manner of sailing paraphernalia hanging unobtrusively from the wooden walls and ceiling. Several other patrons were there with them, but they were far less fun to moc-- . . . that is to say, they seemed far less interesting than these two. 'How are things,' this was the lady again, Lorena, trying to preserve the contact. You might have noticed the absence of a question mark, and I assure you it was intentional, for she hadn't finished yet. The man stopped and cringed as she threw all the scorn she could muster into the following word-and-a-half: 'Johnny-poo?' She smiled a sick little smile as Johnny-poo turned around with palpable distaste, but hers died when he countered with one of his own. 'Things have been going remarkably well since we separated, thank you.' He tried to make his voice pleasant, but with his teeth ground together in that false grin, the words sounded as his face looked: constipated. He brought his hands up to the lapels of his leisure suit and clutched them arrogantly. 'Actually, believe it or not, I'm now making a living off what you so hideously tried to mangle.' Thrusting his hips forward, he flashed a genuine smile. 'Da package~!' 'That was a long time ago, John, and you more than had it coming to you after what you did to me,' Lorena spat, fixing her hands on her ample hips. She seemed set to pull out a knife and slice it off again, but just then there was a tinkling sound as the outer door opened. The beseeching cries of seagulls penetrated the din for a moment, along with the pungent scent of sea water. More importantly, however, and far more to the taste of this promo, the Man in Black sauntered through the open door and let it slam shut behind him, his eyes immediately riveted to the canoes and appurtenances of sailing that for him made this place great. There was a definite spring in his step as he approached the two would-be lovers, happily looking at everything but them. 'Bullshit. You had no right to treat me like that, bitch.' John was looming over Lorena now, on the verge of violence. The Man in Black sensed the tension instantly after those subtle words, and, good samaritan that he is, he interjected himself. 'Ahoy there,' he greeted the two of them, both of whom turned to glare at him through narrow slits. 'I couldn't help but notice your squabble, and while I'm new to this country, I recently became enlightened as to how every difference here should be resolved, no matter how volatile.' They both stared at him incredulously, so he held up a hand to forestall their protests. 'Seriously. Apparently, what you've done to one another is of very little consequence, and the two of you could be mating like jackrabbits in no time, if one of you would just break down and say you're sorry.' Still they stared blankly, and the Man in Black nodded sagely, his eyes closed. 'I saw it on non-television just the other night.' Neither of them needed to know what non-television was, and nor should you. 'I will never apologize to him,' Lorena declared haughtily, whipping her hair over her left shoulder so it couldn't interfere with her glowering. 'After what he did?' 'I think you've more than paid me back, Lorena. But . . . But . . .' He obviously had trouble getting the sentiment out, but as we all knew would happen, eventually the words came. 'I'm sorry, Lorena.' Well, I suppose someone couldn't see it coming, because judging by the expression on Lorena's face, which looked as if it had been hit by a tonne of bricks, it hit her like a tonne of bricks. The Man in Black's nodding became more rapid and somehow more profound, and he looked at Lorena for her reply. 'Are . . . Are you serious?' She sputtered. He became embarrassed and told her not to make him repeat it, that it was hard enough the first time. 'Oh John . . .' Suddenly cheesy music revved up from out of nowhere, the drumming rhythm of it ideal for the industry the package was always intended for. The pair merged together like dogs in heat, clawing at each other and yowling how sorry they were. In that simple yet magical apology all of their sins transgressed against each other were forgiven if not forgotten. How could any grudge or series of wrongs stand up against the be-all end-all of life that is those two special words? Theirs couldn't. All that was missing now was the make-up sex, and judging by their lustful reactions, it wasn't far off. The whole thing was truly very romantic. The Man in Black moved on, his work done. Winding his way through the other people he couldn't be bothered to 'help', the Man in Black approached the main counter and leaned his elbow on it. 'Shopkeep,' he shouted, much to the chagrin of the store's sole employee, who was no more than two feet away on the other side of the counter. 'I'd like a ship!' The clerk, a young man with shaggy brown hair, feigned surprise at this startling revelation. Clutching at his heart with his left hand and gasping like a fish out of water, he reached up to the heavens with his right as if imploring God for the reasons for such an untimely death by shock. He dropped to one knee as his eyes rolled back into his skull, still struggling for the breath that was so viciously denied him. Weebling around like a top in its final spin, he collapsed to the floorboards with a hollow thud and lay there for several seconds before rising to his feet with a grin. The Man in Black was amused, but not very. 'A little overdone, don't you think?' He asked dryly. 'I used to be an actor,' the guy explained as he dusted himself off. 'Part of a group called 'Ideas From Corpses'.' 'Catchy.' If the Man in Black's voice was dry before, now it was parched enough to combust. 'I could give another demonstration of my skill if you'd like,' the clerk offered desperately, an eager smile spreading over his thin face as he poised himself to recite something pitifully less than Shakespearean. The Man in Black sighed and crossed his arms over his chest, leaning back as he did so he could stare down his nose at this weirdo. Sort of. 'I would rather you smashed an oar over my head, or maybe a guitar, my dear man.' In contrast to his stance and the context of his words, his voice was rich and melodic with pleasure once more, undoubtedly due to his excitement at what would follow. 'As I alluded to when I came in, before you went off on your pointless charade, I am in dire need of a ship!' 'Fine,' the clerk muttered, getting all sulky. 'What type of ship are you looking to rent?' 'Rent?' The Man in Black guffawed. 'Rent? Do I look like the type of man who would rent a ship?' 'Actually, with that mask and that sword, you sort of look like a pirate.' 'Precisely!' The Man in Black was overjoyed at the recognition. Perhaps this man was not an idiot after all. He clapped a hand down on the clerk's shoulder and flashed a brilliant smile. 'And have you ever heard of a pirate who rents?' 'Nah, they all just steal what they want,' the storekeep responded with an apathetic shrug, seemingly completely clueless to the situation. The Man in Black tapped his foot with impatience. After a moment he pursed his lips and spoke, 'y'know, you're making this frightfully awkward.' 'You're the one who came in here and talked to me instead of just taking something from the docks.' 'I regret to say that I already tried that.' The Man in Black shook his head sadly. 'There was a great deal of shrieking by the owners, which I expected and am actually rather accustomed to, but then there were two men in blue coats with pistols and badges telling me to 'freeze' of all things, which caught me by surprise. Luckily,' the grin returned, baring his impending triumph, 'they were very arrogant and didn't expect me to put up a fight. They just swaggered over with their weapons holstered, telling me I was under arrest or some such nonsense. I don't know what 'arrested' means around here, but where I come from it means 'detained' or 'caught', and as I drew my sword I was anything but. Dispatching them was a rather easy task, especially in light of their bravado, and I was about to make off with the ship when somebody shouted that more were on the way. I sincerely doubted I would fool them with the same trick of acquiescence twice, so here I am.' Still grinning, he dropped into a mock bow. The storekeep narrowed his eyes and tilted his head, then his face brightened with recognition. 'Hey!' The shout was full of excitement. 'You look exactly like the Dread Pirate Roberts! The Princess Bride is the greatest movie ever! Is this for a sequel or something? You are him, right?' 'If I say yes,' The Man in Black began slowly, thoughtfully, 'will you point me towards an unoccupied vessel so I might make good my escape?' 'Of course!' The clerk beamed, and the Man in Black beamed back. Then the clerk's face was split by a cruel grin, and a knowing darkness crept over the Man in Black's. 'On one condition,' came the decree, and the Man in Black exhaled forcefully. 'Do I look like I'm in the mood for bargaining?' 'Yes.' 'Very well, then,' the Man in Black conceded, his rubber arm twisted most mercilessly. 'What is it?' 'Talk shit to me as if I'm the prince.' The Man in Black looked around, clearly bewildered. 'The prince of what?' 'Humperdink!' The clerk shouted, on the verge of losing his patience. 'Don't play dumb with me! Talk to me as if I'm Prince Humperdink!' 'I've had about enough of this,' The Man in Black muttered under his breath. Raising his voice, he reached across the counter and grabbed the clerk by scruff of his neck. 'Now you listen to me, you pube-chinned jellyfish-molesting pig . . . If you don't stop screwing around and provide me with the ship I seek I'm going to carve your ass from your miserable body and make you wear it as a hat!' The clerk, his face mere inches from that of the Man in Black, was grinning from ear to ear. 'And I suppose,' he said in a sniffy tone, 'that next you'll pluck my eyes from their sockets to make me truly hideous.' 'Wrong! Your hideousness will serve as a warning to any other who strives to be as stupid as you! I will lop off your head as it is and raise it to the top of my flagpole, to instill the fear of the truly wretched in those I am about to besiege. No man or woman will feel secure with your idiotic visage grinning down at them, for they will know that there are no bounds to my villainy if I am cruel enough to decapitate the truly retarded and fly the head as a trophy! Even the fish will--' 'Ahahahahahahahahahahahahahahahahahaha!' The clerk was laughing hysterically, slapping at the iron clamps that held him fast in a futile attempt to escape so he might double over. The Man in Black, overcome with disgust, released him and began reaching for his sword. 'Ahahahahahahahahahahahahahaha,' the laughter continued, drowning out the metallic shriek of the cold steel being drawn. The Man in Black had heard of dying from laughter before, but he never imagined it in so obnoxious or ignorant a way. 'Ahahahahahahaha . . . fifth boat . . . ahahahahahaha . . . from the right . . . ahahaha!' The Man in Black blinked, surprised by this strange turn of events, then nodded with great satisfaction as he returned the foil to its scabbard. The clerk was still snorting and chuckling, so the Man in Black didn't bother with a 'thank you' before his departure. Instead he just shook his head and wondered at this new culture he had unwittingly immersed himself in. 'Curious people,' he mused as he left the store. 'I shall be glad to have the sea for company once more.'
