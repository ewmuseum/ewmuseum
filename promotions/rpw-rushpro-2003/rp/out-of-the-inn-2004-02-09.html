Out of the Inn

9 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 9th 2004 So I said goodbye to it all. The bright lights, the artificial aurora and buzzing of neon. My Akiharaba… I’ll miss you. For it was now time to erase myself from history again. Gaz Green’s good looks and charming personality, not to mention his sheer fan base, had secured him a place on the silver screen. I was to follow him, but… not with my face. Not with the greedy, vain, selfish and downright obnoxious crowd of Hollywood’s “finest”. California sunshine? Hmph. No, I simply wandered away. What reason do I have to stay in such a place? I wanted somewhere quiet. And I found it in the basement of an inn. Oshima Island was located a fair distance from Akihabara, but when you live forever, time isn’t really an issue. Fishing boats make regular trips to and from the mainland and despite being six feet and seven inches tall, stowing away was easy enough. The Yamamura Inn was a place well recognized by the locals, regarded as an unpopular spot and believed to be a bad omen. The less people around, the better. Even more so when the Inn’s proprietor, Takashi Yamamura, had recently passed away. The basement of the inn was perfect for me… spacious and just dark enough. My previous experiences with enclosed areas and complete darkness had left a deep scratch on my mind, one that could never be covered. Death surrounded the inn on the tongues of locals and occasionally, I would play upon that myth. Soon, the “Yamamura Bakemono”, or the monster of the Yamamuras, became as synonymous as the inn itself. The guest book would resemble a mortician’s report. Of course, the rates were cheap and the poorest of people would stay the night, only to vanish sometime the next day forever. I ripped open throats with voracious zeal, not spilling a drop. Human blood had once again become part of my diet, not the frozen stuff delivered from bribed hospital workers but warm, rich and coppery tasting. Fluffy would consume the bones completely, making it a forensic impossibility to discover us. The basement had no heating or electricity points, so nobody ever came down to check. Or at least, I thought nobody ever would. One day, I lay propped up by the wall beneath the dirty window, wrapped in my wings. Fluffy took his spot in my lap, his quills twitching to and fro as he dreamed of his next meal. Then I felt something outside… not physically, but almost like a smell, an intangible alert. Awakening, neither of us could believe that the person before them was who they were. He stared at a fully nourished, gray-skinned and recovered Nascosto Nerezza, back to his full strength. My hair had remained a white mask to a face nobody would ever see again. And through that mask, I saw him. Angelus Jones was the mask he wore for a short time, but now it was revealed that his name was Angelus Von Kelsig, more commonly known as Hessian. I knew why he was here. I knew that his presence alone meant that the guests and workers of the inn would be dead, their bodies creatively destroyed and shredded beyond recognition. My inn was no more. The monster that haunted the Yamamura family had to leave, because another monster had filled his place. A ghost without a house to haunt is nothing and with no more to do, I listened to the plan. The fiendish plan assembled by Hessian’s mind was matched only by one other. But neither plan can be explained or told by me… nor by another tongue. Time is the only thing that shall tell of it. As I stepped out of the inn, the stench of blood that I couldn’t consume almost drove me to tears. Such a waste. But I had another feeding ground now. Rush Pro Wrestling. The words Hessian said to be broke the silence that I had suffered for too long. It was time for me to sing, to play… the Midnight Carnival had re-opened.
