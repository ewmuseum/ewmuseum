Vagabond

1 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 050104 All stories have a beginning, and this one begins rather typically. It’s a fine spring morning in a small hotel just south of Chicago. “BRRRRRRIIIIIINNNNNGGGG!!!” The small alarm clock on the bedstand rings cheerily, heralding the start of a fresh new day! SMASH! POW! The thin brass crumples nicely as it impacts against the far wall. Hmm… let’s fast-forward a bit, shall we? ---Later--- All stories have a beginning, and this one begins rather typically. It’s a fine spring afternoon... Blink. The eyes open. A rush of jumbled impressions as a mind starts receiving data without the slightest bit of understanding. Light. Window. Cramped legs. Bacon cheeseburger. Gawd-awful country music. A name? “Shut off that gawd-awful country music!” THE Multiple Man thrashes as he struggles to sit upright, wedged as he is in the back of THE Multiple Mobile, a primer gray, diesel guzzlin’ hellbeast of a Volkswagen Rabbit. A sour face with vaguely Asian features glowers back. “Oh, we’re awake are we? There was some debate as to whether it might be easier to just strap you to the roof than shove you in the back. And the music stays.” That’s Keira; she’s small but mean with the hand-to-hand skills to back it up. “Hell no, Keira! It’s two to one now! You’re outvoted!” Okay, how to describe Steve? Hmmm… you know that thing they did with Betty Crocker, how they combined the features of hundreds of women to get that perfectly average look? It’s kinda like that. Keira snarls, “I will personally eat ANY finger other than mine that comes in contact with the radio for the remainder of this trip.” “Does that mean you’re done with the burger? Pass it back here.” Tmm finally squirms vertical, shoving a number of stuffed cardboard boxes to the side. “And what are we doing driving? I’ve got a… whatever, a thing at the CDC to do.” Steve shakes his head. “The burger’s gone except for some flavorful burps, boss. And the CDC tanked.” “What? How’d that happen?” “You overslept.” Tmm whacks his forehead with an open palm. “Dammit! Not again! You think those places would be a little more stable!” “Like I said, you overslept.” Keira snorts, “Turns out, you were the only real person on the roster, the rest of them were just people on paper. Jack says it was all some kind of elaborate shell game. A quote-unquote Development Community to develop local Chicago talent, funded by the parent company. Send back status reports of all these fictional applicants and pocket the cash. It’s a nice idea, but when we snuck in and added you retroactively to the roster, it really cocked things up for them, and poof!” She wriggles her fingers magically. “All of a sudden it was gone like it never existed. Left a lot of building employees confused and pissed off. I hear the parent company folded up like origami soon after.” Tmm arches an eyebrow. “Mmm… messy. What about my paychecks?” “What checks? Nobody actually existed but the office staff! They didn’t even HAVE an accounting department.” “That sucks. Didn’t we get anything?” “A water cooler, some chairs… one of them being of the Swedish ergonomic torture variety, and a computer.” “Not a total loss then.” “Who the hell’s gonna buy an ergonomic chair? NASA? It doesn’t even have a place to sit!” A shrug from Steve, “Thing is, the computer isn’t even complete. We only figured out things minutes before the cops came. As is, we barely got the hard drive. Smuggled it out of the head office in a hollowed out paperback. I just acted confused like everyone else and they let me waltz right out…” “Acted?” mutters Keira. “Pfft! At least I got something potentially worthwhile, not a piece of crappy mutant furniture like some people I know!” “I thought it was modern art!” “Children! Children!” shouts Tmm. “You all did good. But we need to figure out our next move…” Both Keira and Steve respond in unison, “Done!” “Nonsense! We didn’t get to sit around the table and eat donuts yet! That’s a big part of the decision process, y’know!” “Not when there’s freeloading involved.” “God bless America! Lay that hammer down!” VRRROOOOOOOOOOOMMM!!! The little import rockets down the interstate as the camera fades out.
