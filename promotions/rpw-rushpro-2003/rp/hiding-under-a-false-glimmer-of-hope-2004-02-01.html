Hiding Under A False Glimmer Of Hope

1 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/1/04 For the first time in a week the temperature had finally managed to pull itself over 30 degrees and begun to melt layer of white that had covered the ground just the other night. Things finally seemed to be brightening up not only on the outside, but inside my mind as well. Even though Jackson was gone and Chris was still his arrogant self things were beginning to look better with the arrival of Malphas in RPW. He had given me the shoulder that I needed to lean on. Not to mention it seemed that the Vangellus guy I met last week was someone I could get along with well. I just wished that I could bring the same cheer that I was feeling to the kids that may not have been of my blood, but were my own none the less. “You know it’s perfect snowmen weather out there you guys.” I suggested to both Evan and Christa who were moping around the house as if there was not a thing on this earth that could keep them occupied for more than five seconds. “Why don’t you two go out there and build a snowman before the snow melts?” I suggested, hoping that maybe there moping would bring them outside where at least they would find something new to try…and then get bored of five minutes later. Evan seemed to gobble the idea up and dashed towards the stairs to grab everything he would need to keep warm outside. However Christa seemed to careless. It seemed that was the way she felt about anything I’ve said as of late. The second I opened my mouth she’d direct a pair of cold blue eyes in my directions that hinted that she didn’t want to hear about it. She wasn’t the same cheery nine year old that I once knew. I only wish I knew why she hid that smile of hers. Within the span of a few minutes Evan had collected all he would need to keep warm outside and jetted it down to the kitchen. “Will you help me with the snowman Tessa?” Evan asked with those angelic blue eyes of his, which he knew I couldn’t refuse. “I’ll be out there in a few minutes.” I replied as I made way towards the kitchen table which was still a mess from the pancake breakfast I had made for the kids and me this morning. I was quite surprised to look across the table to notice that Christa was helping out as well. Now I knew that there was something going on inside of her mind, and surely enough there was. After hearing the click of the backdoor which confirmed Evan had marched his way out onto the back porch, Christa decided it was time to speak her mind. “I know where you were when you were gone for Christmas.” She confirmed in a low tone, almost as if she regretted bringing it up. “If it is somewhere grandma told you, then she lied.” I replied as I finished stacking the syrup covered plates and made my way towards the sink. “No, I know she is a liar.” She replied as she tracked her bare feet across the cold tile floor, ending her journey at the sink as well. “You were with Chris weren’t you?” She questioned with an accusing frown as if to question me why her and Evan weren’t allowed to come? She was right though, I was Chris. The only thing is I didn’t want to be, I was sort of forced to even though I won’t deny that the entire time I was there I was hoping things would turn around for the better. “I was, but it was only to visit.” I replied, though the visit to his New York apartment was completely against my will. If I had my way Christmas would have been spent at home far away from Chris. But I let Christa imagine that the reason I was there was for all the fairytale reasons. I hated to let her think he would come back one day and the fairytale life we once lived would continue, but it was the only way to protect her from the cold hard truth that if he did come back it would be for all the wrong reasons. “Why couldn’t me and Evan come too then?” She looked up at me, her blue eyes filled with hurt that questioned if she were even a part of the family I had started after my brother had passed away two Septembers ago. “Because….Chris and I needed some time alone.” I felt the weight of such a lie crushing me, but it was the only way I could keep her from the truth. Maybe one day I would tell her the truth, if I had to. But I didn’t want to. To see her realize that the man she trusted and loved like a father was nothing but a complete and total asshole who never cared about her would absolutely crush her heart, and absolutely demolish mine. “So does that mean he’s going to come back and visit us sometime?” She questioned with a glimmer of hope in her eyes. I knew she missed him, as did Evan. After losing her father in an accident that never should have happened I couldn’t blame her. He was the closest thing to a father she had. How someone could be so heartless as to break the heart of such sweet kids I could never understand, but I had a feeling that Christa had come to realize that he was never coming back. The world had turned its back on the poor girl yet again. “Maybe one day dear, just maybe.” I replied in a soft tone, trying my best from breaking down right then and there. With each lie I felt the weight of guilt increase. It was almost as though guilt had trapped me, so I decided that now would be the best time to make my exit and help Evan build that snowman he had asked me to before Christa trapped me under the same glimmer of hope that she had possessed ever since Chris had left. As much as I hated to admit it, I knew how Christa felt which made me feel even worse for the poor girl. I still possessed that same glimmer of false hope as her that maybe one day Chris would realize that he still cares and come back. It’s just too bad it would never happen, and I would just have to realize it. Chris had wandered away from my heart and he was never coming back.
