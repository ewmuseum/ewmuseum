The Greatest Motivation

13 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003 December 13 There’s no greater motivation in the world than being told you can’t do something. Isn’t that right Mr. Mason? A small wax candle sits lonely upon a table of modest proportions. A small bowl substitutes as a temporary home for the small delicate candle. It’s flame…flickers small and large, trying desperately to stay alive. It feels so lonely. It feels so new. It’s never been here before. Twenty-two years ago, something magical happened in the world. Andrea Cruz, a caring mother and gentle housewife, gave birth to a small premature baby. That baby was 8 months old upon birth, the size of a grown man’s hand. She had to live inside an incubator so she can develop enough to fend by herself. Her parents loved her like no other; as well they should. The candle flame flickers to miniscule proportions almost as if showing us how diminutive the child was. It seems so weak. It seems so helpless. It seems so useless. The wax slowly drips down the stem of the candle, looking more like a tear. Then the day finally came when Mother, Father, and Child would be one in the home. Sure the other siblings were jealous of this new baby. “My mom doesn’t like me anymore” was the common coined phrase. There were also tidbits of “Why does she get all the attention?”. But the poor baby, she didn’t know any better. Suddenly a light gentle breeze erupts in the area. It’s too enigmatic to know for sure what its intentions are. The wind is soothing, refreshing. It brushes past your skin as softly as silk. It feels so cool…so relaxing. Maybe it just wants to make friends. The years passed, and the baby was no longer a baby. She became a girl. She went to school. She worked. She did things her own way, no matter what society said. Her family loved her. There was just one problem. They smothered her. They limited her. They punished her for being different. That same wind that seemed so friendly, now seems viler. It quickly moves in over the candle, threatening to extinguish its purity of life: the flame. The flame cowers near the stem of the candle, afraid of the wind ending it. The wind breezes by, and for a second the flame almost succumbs to its end. But it still remains. They wanted her to be what they wanted. They were disgusted with their own failures. Her parents were disgusted with how they let their dreams go to waste. He wanted to be an engineer. She wanted to be an architect. But they never got the chance. So a baby was born to them. A baby that would fulfill their dreams for them. It was such a marvelous plan. Of course she would obey them. Who cares if she had dreams of her own? Life a diving falcon, the wind sweeps down on the candle. And as if it had a mind of its own, the flame laid down on the wax, avoiding the deadly breeze. Having missed its target, the wind dies down. It failed. And the flame…continues to burn brightly, having evaded death for just a few more moments. But she did have dreams of her own. She loved music. She loved to hear the words of many great artists in Columbia and around the world. To her, music was the language of the soul. It was so soothing to hear, and every lyric had meaning. She wanted to sing. She wanted to write songs. That was her dream. And to have her dream, she neglected everything, and everyone, in her life. That was a mistake. No sooner had the flame recovered from its ordeal that a sort of moisture was in the air. It was the dampness felt in a rainforest. Or the humidity felt out at sea, when you’re on a ship for eight long months. Having a life of its own, the flame flickers lowly, unsure of the safety it finds itself in. Her dreams were stupid her parents said. She should be an Engineer, or an Architect. She should be studying the arts and not wasting her time with song writing. Any idiot can write a song, they said. She should be what they want her to be. Because being a Civil Engineer is much more respectable than writing songs that no one will care about. The flame was right. It was not safe. As soon as it stood tall, a drop of water came crushing down on the candle. It was only a small drop, but its intentions were deadly. Luckily for the flame, the drop missed its target. And the flame…flickered again. It was too close of a close call. Well someone did care. That little girl cared. That same little girl who was abused throughout her life, and had her dreams shattered. That same little girl who was told she couldn’t follow her dreams because they were stupid. The same little girl who was told she couldn’t do it, simply because. She cared. I cared. I am that little girl. The wind returned. It failed once before and it was not going to fail again. The flame cowered low again, trying to avoid the wind’s touch of death. But the wind was persistent…and it had a partner. The rain trickled down. Its many drops falling down like a squadron of bombers carpet-bombing enemy territory. The drops splashed down hard near the candle. But the flame kept itself alive. What was wrong with my dream? What was wrong with me? Why couldn’t my parents have supported me? Instead all they did was ridicule and abuse me. Well you know what? They’re dead, and I don’t care anymore. My only regret is that I wasn’t the one the put them out of their misery. Tell me what to do. Don’t you ever tell me what I can’t do. Seeing the rain have some success, the wind begins to pick up a little. The drops fall down without any accuracy, but the wind soon guides the drops towards the flame. The flame leans on the edge, trying to avoid death. The water drops move in closer and closer. Finally, with much assistance from the wind, the water finds its target. The flame flickers on one last time, before it is finally extinguished. Dead. Don’t you ever tell me I can’t do anything. I’m not that little girl anymore. I’m a woman now. A woman that knows what she can do. So go ahead, tell me I can’t fight. Tell me I can’t survive in a business dominated by muscle. Tell me there’s someone better than me, when you know damn well she isn’t. Go ahead Tell me I can’t fight. Tell me I can’t compete. Don’t you ever tell me what I can’t do. Satisfied, the wind and the rain begin to fade away. Their job is done. The small defenseless flame was now dead. For a moment there is silence. The candle now feels so naked. But wait…it’s not over yet. A small spark flickers on the stem. It blinks a little, trying to revive itself. Reflash. The flame reflashes on again, and this time it burns brighter than before. Like a phoenix it has renewed life. Now it burns brightly like the son, having defeated its own death. There is no greater motivation than being told you can’t do something.
