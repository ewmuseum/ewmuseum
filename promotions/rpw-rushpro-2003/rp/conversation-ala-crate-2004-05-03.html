Conversation ala crate.

3 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Begin file.] “I don't care if she's your kid or not! If she gives me directions to hell just one more time, out the window!” “I do not see how you expect her to ever react favorably to you if all you do is yell. And there is no available window.” “It’s metaphorical!” Like last time, this segment of THE Multiple Man's life comes direct to you via the Interstate system of highways... though in a slightly different relative location. You already know about Tmm and Steve and Keira tooling along in THE Multiple Mobile (and if you don't, then you've let your subscription lapse. For shame!), but there are other members of this motley crew known as the Entourage. Currently, though, they're spread out amongst a number of vehicles, so it might be a fair bit before we can get to them all. Quick rundown: You've got Jack Noir in his Buick Skylark, Valerie Dietrich in her Honda Accolade, Raul Espin riding THE Multiple Motorcycle and H.V. Knight driving THE Multiple... umm... Big Rig. But this segment is about none of them. Rather, it is about a pair of robots, Chico and Gunmetal. “Dammit! I KNOW where that part of my anatomy is! I want to know where WE are!” As well as an ornery calculator Gunmetal calls daughter. (Really, just don't ask. It's kinda complicated.) “We are in the back of the trailer, I would have thought that was obvious. Anyway, you are not being fair. TI-290 has not had appropriate time to get used to her new GPS upgrade.” Multi-colored LEDs flare angrily in the darkness. “And you consider it fair that we're stuffed back here with these primitive toys?' Chico holds up a cylindrical object for inspection. 'What use could they possibly have for so many blenders at once?! It's disrespectful!” Gunmetal buzzes thoughtfully. “It is practical. Neither of us has a driver's license.” “You forget I was a cabbie for a while!” “Cabbies operate under different rules than most drivers. Tell me, how many humans can the holder of a standard license run down before said license is revoked?” Chico ponders this puzzler. “More than eight or nine a day, I would think.” Gunmetal holds up a single finger. “ONE?! A DAY?! Preposterous!” “Just one, total.” “You can't be serious!” “But I am. Things are different for non-cabbies” “I'll say! I don’t see how anyone can get anywhere under all that oppression!” “Quite. And you must agree that we are unusual enough that we would be pulled over for certain.” “Maybe YOU would…” “You are quibbling. The fact remains that we are highly distinctive and being noticed is not something that would benefit us. In the short term, it would be better for us to continue to associate with this TEH Multepole Nam, and remain in the shadows. Never mind the added benefits of free parts and maintenance.” “You’re just sucking up because of the great dental plan.” “And YOU fail to place the proper weight on the advantages such an association brings us.” “Like being sealed in a crate? Whoo! The fun don’t ever stop around here!” “It is not a matter of fun. Have you forgotten Cobol?” “…” “I see that you have not.” “Meh.” “Then perhaps I should point out that every moment we remain out of his detection range is a moment that requires his search area to expand. You know that the car compactor will not hold him forever and he WILL come looking for us. You know that.” Chico fiddles with the GPS. “I asked what state we’re in and all I get is ‘your mother’! That’s it! You’re going to Whipped and Chopped City!” He gropes for a nearby blender. “That’s not a blender, that’s an answering machine.” Chico shifts his grip in a menacing fashion. “You don’t think I can do the job with it?” Click! BEEEP! The machine noisily starts rewinding. “What did you do?” “I think I pressed something.” Chico shakes the box experimentally. Click! BEEEP! A cheerful female voice emanates from the answering machine. “Hiya! This is Dr. Valerie Dietrich. I’m not here right now, but if you give the usuals, I’ll get back to you… BEEEEP!” The voice changes to something deep, gravelly, and most assuredly male. “Uh, yeah, Val. This is Alexi. Umm… things didn’t quite turn out like I expected. I don’t think it’s still a good idea for you and your associates to come up this weekend. The experiment wasn’t as… ARRRRGHHGHGH!!!” The message quickly devolves into horrid crunching and tearing and spattering noises. Chico’s head tilts to one side in thought. “I haven’t heard a sound like that since Cobol overloaded his chipper shredder attachment. Think it means anything?” “Negative. If the call were at all important, they would simply call again to make sure the message was received. As no call has arrived, it is safe to assume it an inconsequential matter.” “You’re probably right. Now, YOU start explaining to your wretched little pile of circuits, that all roads do NOT lead to Trenton!” “Pass her over.” [File ends.]
