A Favor For A Favor

4 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today I sat there, looking over O’Neil. I hadn’t been there for longer than 5 minutes when he fell asleep. I don’t really know what he was thinking. He talked to me like we’d been friends for years. I guess that happens when you’re flat on your back in some hospital bed for weeks. I doubt he even remembered my face. I sat there, studying the tubes coming out of his face. I followed the lines of the electrodes coming off his skin to the machines. Thankfully, they’re not keeping alive right now, just stable. With out them, he’d most surely die. Maybe that’s what O’Neil wanted. Maybe it’s what he needed. Perhaps he needed to be welcomed to the Lord that he coveted so much. I thought about yanking off the wires last time I was here. No man deserves to become a machine. Part of me wanted to free him. Part of me wanted him to recover. Part of me didn’t give a shit about any of that, and I’d gladly pull the .44 in my pocket out, and take care of him, and slip out the back door. I didn’t want to bring it along, but figured it may help me get out of a precarious situation. I stood over the clueless bastard, ready to yank his wires. I wanted him dead, not only to ease my suffering, but to ease his suffering as well. “Jason…” I said in his ear. “This is possibly the last voice you’re ever going to hear. Right now, I’m going to turn your machines off. I’m going to yank these electrodes off your chest. I’m going to do, what every man and woman in this building is afraid to do. “Understand, I’m doing this with your best interest at heart. If your God intended you to live like this, you would have been borne to a machine, not in a human body. You don’t deserve this. No one does. Regardless of if it were an accident or not, that’s moot. The point is, you deserve to breathe as I do. You deserve to live your life, without a machine breathing for you, or another machine feeding you. “I’m going to save you, Jason. I’m going to spare you the pain of being here, and just let you go to the Lord. It’s what you wanted, isn’t it?” O’Neil lay motionless, still. The bastard had no clue that I was about to do what I was about to do. I reached out behind him, and grabbed the cord on the wall. I put a hand to the side of his face, and pulled his eye open. I looked into the black pool of nothingness staring back, and smiled. “Go now, in peace Jason. I’m freeing you.” I kissed him on the forehead, as was the custom that I’d established back in my first “hit”, if you will, and prepared to pull the cord. As I embraced the warm feeling rising from my gut, assuring me I was doing right, I heard a door down the hall open, and the bliss turned to bile; there was trouble coming. Then I heard the door to this room open and froze, still clutching the cord. I was finished. It had to be the police. I was about to be arrested. As I turned, there he stood, sword drawn, ready to run me through. I greeted him with a warm smile and prepared to give sound to the welcome, but he cut me off. Turns out he wasn’t here for pleasantries. He knew I was here. He knew my plan to free O'Neil. He wanted him to live as a machine. He wanted O’Neil to live in pain. He didn’t care for O’Neil’s feelings. I did. And I was going to make absolutely sure that his wishes were kept.
