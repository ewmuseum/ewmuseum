Why do I do this?

4 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Feb 4th, 2004 I was so pissed off. I swear, I have every damn right to be. I had just lost on Adrenaline to ANOTHER chick and then I get a phone call. It was from mom, back home near Pikeville, and apparently my dear little sister had pitched a fit about getting a car. I really can't believe this shit. I really can't. I mean, since Dad died... I am SO pissed off right now. The drive took me about five hours and with gas climbing towards a buck seventy a gallon and that didn't help any. I actually rolled in at about eight o'clock at night on a crappy night. It was cold, rainy and slick. I'd love to say I had a nice coat to keep the rain off, but to tell you the truth, I've got a good jacket and a poncho. I've had the same coat for about five years now and the Jeep Wrangler I'm driving used to be Dad's and he'd had it since... like '93 or something. And my little sister is whining about not having a car. Damn, Mom's got a truck and that's all we really afford. Yeah, I make good money wrestling. You know why? Because I never take a break. Every house show I can make, every show, every appearance. I cut corners. I sleep in the Jeep, I eat protein bars instead of a full meal, everything I can do to get by and save some more cash. Everything I don't spend on food or gas, I send home. My little sister is spoiled all to hell and back. She always has been. Mom gets by just fine with what I send and gives Stacy some money so that she can get what she wants. And she does. Does she ever. But this, this is over the line. I throw open the door and pull up the hood of my poncho. My big boots clomp across the driveway as I walk towards the door. I rap it as lightly as I can with my knuckles, but my arms are so damn big, it's like I was trying to knock it down. The door swings open and there's Mom, smiling, happy to see me. She should be. I'm on the road anywhere from forty five to fifty weeks per year. Don't complain to ME about YOUR travel schedule. “Scott,” she says with a huge grin, “welcome home.” “Thanks, Mom,” I reply, stepping in and shaking the water off my poncho. I look over and see her giving me the same look she always does. She wishes I'd buy some stuff for myself. No chance. When I get a bonus, they can get carpet or something. That's more important than me going to Outback or something. “Where's Stacy?” I ask with a sigh. “Oh, she'll be home soon,” Mom replies. “She was so upset she had to come home early. She wanted to run around with Steven.” THAT got my attention. “Steven?” I ask. “Don't tell me it's the same Steven that graduated with me.” Everyone around here with that name goes by Steve except for one prick that graduated in my class. He's got this thing for dating younger girls. He's 21 and my sister is 16. Oh yeah, I'm NOT a happy Manbeast. “We'll save that,” she assures me. “You can take care of that before you leave.” Well, that's done. I walk into the kitchen and grab a glass out of the cabinet. The door swings open and I pour a good measure of milk into the glass. Oh hell, I need something a bit stiffer to drink. That's really the thing, the only thing I spend money on myself. I save a couple of bucks and buy a bottle of cheap whiskey or something. I could go for a White Russian right now, but milk will do fine. Oh son of a BITCH. I hear the screech of tires outside. That would be that stupid firebird that prick drives. I'm not waiting. I set down the glass carefully and march out the front door into the rain. Screw the poncho, this ain't gonna take long. Yup, there it is. That stupid car, with that stupid prick, and my stupid sister. I'd let her put up with that stupidity if I didn't care so much. Maybe I'm stupid for that, too. By this time, I'm already at the car door. I pull the handle out, just about three seconds before I pull Steven out. His stupid leather jacket and greasy hair reminds me of a wanna-be New York mafia rat. I'm getting more and more pissed off as this night goes on. Then I look inside his jacket. Oh HELL no. Blue pills? I recognize them. Rohypnol? Date rape? “You listen to me, you wanna-be prick,” I say in a growl that would make El Diablo think twice, “you keep away from my sister. I swear, if I hear about a roofie in her drink EVER, I'm driving back from anywhere to kick your ass. You got me?” I don't wait to answer, because Stacy is pounding at my shoulder right now. Easily enough, I just pitch him back in the car and slam the door, halfway hoping his leg is still out. Then I turn and toss my sister over my shoulder and march back to the house. She's still pounding at me, but I'm the guy that gets hit with tazers and laughs. What's she going to do? My march back into the house has me tossing her down on the couch. She's indignant, ready to be a full-blown brat, but I'm not in the mood. “If you get up off that couch...” I say, letting it trail in the air. “You're too used to being a tough guy on TV, Scott,” she spits back at me. “You can't intimidate me like that.” “You wanna bet I can't?!” I roar at her. If she doesn't watch it, she's going to push me over the edge. THAT would be a disaster. “Now you listen to me you little ungrateful brat. First off, you stay away from Steven.” I see her mouth open. “Shut up, I saw date rape in his coat. I might go kick his ass for that.” I start pacing in front of her. “You know why I'm here? It's because of you. I could be at a house show tonight, getting a couple hundred to send back here. But you? You don't understand. You're pitching a fit about not getting a car because you say that you're being treated unfairly. Let me tell you about unfair.” I'm not stopping anytime soon. I've been waiting to this for a long, long time. “Unfair is Dad dying and leaving me to provide for the family. Unfair is me working twelve-hour shifts at the lumberyard. Unfair is me hopping in a wrestling ring every damned night. Unfair is me sleeping in the Jeep every night. Unfair is me having nothing new. You buy clothes from Structure and Abercrombie and all those overpriced bullshit stores. Do you know how long it's been since I bought a new t-shirt? More than a damn year! You understand me?” Mom is behind me, I know that, and she's probably scared. But Stacy is looking at me, shaking. Her face is a mix of anger and shame. It's about time. “You're upset that you don't get a car. Guess what? I'm upset because I've got a rage complex and never take time off. I come home for Thanksgiving and Christmas. I don't come home from my birthday. If I'm lucky, on my birthday I have enough money to buy myself a Blizzard if there's a Dairy Queen in whatever Godforsaken place I'm in. Now, you tell me about UNFAIR!” I'm done, my shoulders heaving, my hands shaking. This is what I came home to do. Stacy won't start this again. She may not even behave this way for years. I may as well sleep at home tonight, but tomorrow, I'm heading back down the Carolinas. I'm off to make some more money to send back here. Yeah, it's time for bed.
