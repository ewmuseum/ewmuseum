A Mental Game of Chess

2 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/2/04 The cameras had stopped rolling. The fans had already left. In a few minutes the ring crew would appear and start taking everything down to put in the trucks. There in the dimmed lighted arena the ring stood out alone. No fans watched it, no wrestlers battled in it. From the back a figure walked down toward the ring. He had his bag with him. No music played, no pyros went off. It was just him in and the ring alone. He rolled into the ring, leaving his bag on the metal steps. The person sat in the middle of the ring Indian style. There was no movement for a few seconds. It was like the person was just living off the mat. Finally he stood up. His blonde hair dipped down to his forehead. The man was none other than RPW’s self-proclaimed Last Great Technical Wrestler, Chris Storm. Chris’ body seemed drained after the night’s action. His match against Matt Jackson were he battled tooth and nail until he finally took the victory, his interactions on the Think Tank with Scott Rampage, his backstage fight with Malphas and Chryste, the match with Malphas, the night seemed to never end for him. But now Chris came back to the ring. He wasn’t scheduled to be there, he wanted to be there. His life centered on being in ring and being the very best in it. If Chris was not the best technical wrestler, than he was nothing. So Chris made a tradition of going to the ring after each show was over. He entered the ring and stood there alone for a few minutes, letting himself get a feel for the ring. Then he would start his training. The training was not to make sure he could beat any opponent. He already believed he was good enough to beat anyone, anywhere. Karina Wolfenden? He would break her legs and keep her bound to the mat. Gideon Poe? He would snap the neck again and put him back in the hospital. So then why train? He trained because he loved it. He loved being in the ring and the feeling he got when he hit the perfect suplex. The feeling he got when he landed on that mat, hit those ropes, slammed into the corner. He loved it all. It was all apart of who he was. Chris stretched a little then bounced off the ropes. He had already wrestled twice in the ring and developed a feel for the ring. So Chris would just bounce off the ropes to get a feel for where he needed to hit the ropes for the less impact. (Yes he is that obsessed with wrestling that he must know even how to hit the ropes just right.) After a few minutes of hitting the ropes he got a good idea of just were he could hit it for best spring and for lest impact. Chris moved over to the turnbuckle. Over and over he slammed his body into the unforgiving turnbuckles. He needed to know if there was some way he could angle his body so that he would suffer less impact and be able to spring out to count his opponent quicker. He needed every aspect of his in ring talent to be absolutely perfect. When Chris felt he had worked on the corner as much as he could he walked back to the center of the ring. He took a few deep breaths and closed his eyes. He did not need to see with his eyes what he was about to do. He would see every motion in his head. If his training in the ring paid off then he would not fall out of the ring, into the corner wrong, or hit the ropes badly. In his head he was wrestling against the very best. He could see each move, every detail of it playing out in his head. It was a delicate game of chess. Chris knew he could not make a single mistake and needed to play out three moves ahead of his opponent. The entire time he thought about it his body stayed still. He let the entire thing play out in his mind without moving a single muscle. Then he seemed busted to life. Chris shot forward looking for a Double Leg Takedown. It appeared he grabbed his opponent as he arched back and turned bring the opponent down to the mat. Chris kept his body pressed close to the mat and slowly moved up like he was keeping his weight on his opponent and moving more to have a solid cover over them. Chris acted like he hooked a head and moved his legs toward the head. He arched back and came to his feet, the front face lock still on. He switched his grip to a single Underhook with the other arm hooked over the head forcing it down. With another high arch back Chris practiced a picture perfect Underhook Suplex. He sat up quickly after hitting the mat and changed his grip into a Dragon Sleeper. Chris again moved to a standing base and fell backward getting off a text book Reverse Suplex. The Suplexing Storm, Chris Storm seemed to be actually naming off different suplexs as he wrestled the match in his head. His practice seemed to be cut short tonight as the ring crew came down to the ring. Chris at first did not even seem to notice them making their way to the ring until one of them called out to him. “Hey I’m sorry Chris but time’s up. We have to get this ring in the trucks and get it on the road. We start hitting new cities soon.” Chris stopped what he was doing and opened his eyes. The crew surrounded the ring. They grabbed their wrenches to start taking the ring apart. “Five more minutes guys?” Chris asked. His voice was actually sincere, something that is rarely heard. Chris just truly wanted that extra five minutes. It would be enough time for him to practice one more combo. “I’m sorry Chris, we can’t really wait forever.” He replied. The crew was already starting to take down the ropes. Chris saw them out of the corner of his eye and ran toward them. Chris scared the crew away from their work. “I said five more minutes!” he yelled. He seemed pissed now. This was more of the Chris Storm that everyone had seen. Finally Chris seemed to give up. He knew he needed to get on the road as well. If he was lucky he could get to a motel in time to soak in a hot tub. Not that it would matter. He knew he would have to do it all over again Sunday. The Best of Five series was just starting up and the tournament was moving onto the next round. This was the life of an independent circuit wrestler. Traveling city to city trying to get comfortable and let your body rest before you had to drag yourself into the ring again to compete in front of a small crowd. After the show you just wanted to pack it up and get to a motel to try and rest again. It was the life Chris Storm had grown accustom to. But each week it made him bitter. Each paycheck he got that barely paid for his lifestyle increased the bitterness. Coldness brewed inside him. After his performance he had this week, maybe it is helping him? He is the Last Great Technical Wrestler and he is out to prove it.
