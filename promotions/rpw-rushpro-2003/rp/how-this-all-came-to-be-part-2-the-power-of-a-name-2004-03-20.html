How This All Came To Be: Part 2 'The Power of a Name'

20 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/20/04 Thinking back to Spring Fling back in April I still have yet to decide whether the collision of Chris Storm’s head into a brick wall was a good thing for me or not. Obviously back then it felt like the end of the world, but looking back upon it now every time my eyes meet with Chris’ and he tells me he’s going to bend me in half, I almost wish it would happen again and again, just because someone as cold hearted as him deserves it. But as awkward as it may seem for me to be saying this with the comments Chris had directed towards me this past week, I still insist from the bottom of my heart that he didn’t want to crush me the way he did, but he didn’t know what else to do, and I could tell that just from the look in his eyes the first time I came to visit him after the innocent, the look and the words that followed would be something I’d never forget. “You’re finally here.” The voice of Chris bounced off the blank white walls and echoed out into the hallway which I stood, peaking into his room. For the first time that I could remember he didn’t bother to look over towards me as he spoke. It was obvious that he was disappointed that I couldn’t get there sooner. “Yes, sorry that it took so long.” I apologized as I slowly crept into the room, placing a vase filled with roses on the small plastic end table they had placed beside his bed. Even though guys really weren’t into gifts of flowers, I hoped that maybe the fact that it came from the heart would lighten him up, but unfortunately it seemed to do little good considering there wasn’t the least bit of emotion shown towards this thoughtful contribution whose goal was to ensure his happiness. “That’s fine…I understand that you probably needed to take some time to talk to Jacko about your problems, since I wouldn’t understand with a busted head and all.” He replied in the typical manner he did when referring to Jackson Rust, a manner that contained anger and jealousy. “That’s not why!” I shouted in defense of by myself and Jacko. Neither of us deserved to be put under the burning spotlight of these accusations, especially when our friendship had nothing to do with this whole ordeal. “Then why?” He raised his voice in question, as he finally looked my way, his cold blue eyes staring a hole right through me. “I don’t know why.” I hesitantly replied, knowing that this wouldn’t be an acceptable answer. “They wouldn’t let me in to visit you. They told me I wasn’t on the list of visitors you wanted to see. So you tell me why.” I glared over at him, wondering if this was a plan to play games with my mind. “Obviously you didn’t give them your real name.” He said in a matter of fact kind of way. “Real name?” I questioned with a clearly puzzled expression falling across my face. “I did give them my real name, Tessa Windsor.” “Tessa Windsor?” He glanced at me as if he had never heard the name before, “She doesn’t exist anymore; it was Tessa Green last time I checked.” As much as I wanted to deny it, it was the truth. So I closed my eyes and gulped down the ache that occurred every time that name fell on my ears. “Maybe, but does it really matter? It’s only a last name.” I hoped to change the subject with my plea hidden inside of the question that beckoned to forget the whole Windsor-Green ordeal. “Yeah…only a last name…” Chris repeated to himself quietly, as if he were trying to ensure himself of just that. “But I wanted to say I’m sorry for all that happened.” I quickly changed the subject, hoping to get off the topic of that five letter surname. “Don’t bother apologizing.” He muttered, turning his head away from me. “Does it really matter if we have the belts in the first place? The only purpose they served was keeping our last remaining bond together.” I lowered my head, wishing that I could block out all that I just heard. I hated to think that way, but the reality of those words seemed to be coming true slowly but surely. “I’m not sorry for that…I’m sorry for letting you down.” I replied, as the words continued to stab at me, creating a wound deep inside my heart causing tears of pain to trickle down my cheek. I couldn’t take it anymore, I hated to leave but I had. So without saying another word I turned on my heels and began to journey back to the parking lot without bothering to say goodbye. Perhaps Chris was right, maybe nothing between us mattered anymore.
