Otherworld : From The Past, The Future Hears An Echo.

26 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/5/04 She was stood in the middle of a swirling world. The ground moved slowly beneath her unsteady feet, while the people across the street flashed out of view in the blink of an eye. She was drunk, to the point that all her concentration was given to just staying on her feet. What little funds she had to contribute to her rent had been blown in the last hour, buying a small, farewell round of drinks for total strangers at a grimy bar. They all just looked at her as if she were a waste of life, but were happy to take the drinks her tatty notes bought... While she knew one or 2 were pondering whether she were drunk enough to force themselves onto her. 'To life,' she boomed, taking another swig from the brown paper bag. 'Life rules! Yeah!' Her words barely formed, and parents crossed their children across the street to avoid her. Having tired of her shoes squelching with each step, the young woman had kicked them off, and discarded her socks too, choosing to walk barefoot along the wet streets. 'That's it,' she yelled to those shielding their kids' eyes from her sight, 'keep them away from the crazy drunk gir-Whoa...' Tripping over her own feet, the intoxicated young woman lunged forward, just managing to put her hands out to secure herself against a lamp-post. Leant forward, and with the sudden jolt upsetting her stomach, she gagged... Then vomited into the gutter. Choking coughs followed, before a string of mucus-rich spit was flung from her mouth, finally dropping off her bottom lip and down the drain. 'Wish I had a mother to do that...' Not having pulled her hair back, the front of her mank mane carried flecks of vomit, not that she noticed... Or would care if she did. Everyone, whether they'll admit it or not, has a vision of the way they'll die. Some choose old age, surrounded by their family. Some would want to go down fighting, protecting what is theirs... Few would choose drunk, homeless, inebriated, and without a friend in the world to miss them. There would be no lovingly engraved tombstone in a family plot for her. No tears cried over her, and no sense of loss... That was the way she wanted. 'Disgraceful,' one old lady muttered as she past her, revolted by the sight of vomit being washed into the drains by the pounding rain. 'Yes...' she instinctively shot back, her words slurred and throaty. 'I'm a disgrace to everything... I'm a waste of oxygen... You think I ASKED to be born... Huh?' It was useless though, as the old lady closed her ears to her before the words even came out. She had a point though. So much is talked of a foetus' right to live... As if everyone is grateful to have been born. It's fine for those who have loving parents and a safe home, they can say they’re happy to be alive, grateful even. But what about those for whom everyday is just another 24 hours of misery? They didn't ask to be born... And all they can do is count down the days until they die. For some, the countdown is over. 'Right on time,' she muttered with a hint of a smile as the silver bus washed the sidewalk with waves of water from the street. Moving to the curb, she just looked as if she was waiting to cross the street... But her leading foot hung forward, poised and eager. Not needing to slow down for at least another 100 yards, the driver kept up his speed, guiding the bulky vehicle to it's stop. 'Goodbye,' she mumbled, her head dropping forward and eyes scanning left to see the bus approach. 'Good. Fucking. Bye.' Without looking up, not wanting the driver to have to look into her eyes before mowing her down, the young woman stepped off the kerb, and waited for what little life she had left to be thumped out of her. The bus didn't stop. It didn't need to. There was no bus.
