The Bond

12 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-12-03 Steven Caldera had never made any secret that the wrestling business had been kind to him. Caldera had met with extraordinary success very early in his career, and as a result had made tremendous profits early in his twenties. When his company, Caldera Enterprises, went public, it seemed as if Caldera would be able to retire before the age of thirty. Of course, that’s not Caldera’s style, but that didn’t concern Kimbusa. Kimbusa rolls his eyes as his limo pulls over the crest of a hill. “Here it is,” comes the voice of the driver up front, indicating the tremendous house standing a few hundred yards away. Epiphany’s face lights up as she spots the house. At five foot three, the petite Japanese woman is dwarfed next to Kimbusa as she clings to his arm, but her energy fills every room she appears in. Kimbusa had fallen in love with this girl, and she truly seemed to be the physical manifestation of what little good was left in Kimbusa’s heart. Kimbusa had always preferred to keep a low profile. At five hundred pounds he doesn’t exactly blend in, but he definitely prefers to survive on a minimum of luxury. Caldera, however, wasn’t quite the same. He had selected a beautiful location in the low mountains of Virginia, not far outside Charlottesville. Kimbusa’s disgust for the extravagance of the towering house before him gave way to awe as he took in the majesty of the southern Appalachian mountains. The leaves had long since fallen from their trees, but a wonderful layer of snow had fallen on the mountaintop, creating a beautiful white landscape in front of the house. The limo slowly pulls to a stop outside and Kimbusa timidly steps out, a bit intimidated by his new dwelling. The driver exits the limo and assists Epiphany out, but dares not take Kimbusa’s bags. Kimbusa insists upon carrying his own luggage, and the driver, who looks like he might be approaching one hundred sixty pounds, dares not dispute the sumo. Epiphany scuffles around the limo to return to Kimbusa’s arm. “It’s wonderful,” she whispers, taking in the majesty of the snowy terrain. Kimbusa nods, still a bit skeptical about the palace Caldera had built for him. As the two begin heading for the door, the sounds of diesel engines begin rumbling from below. Kimbusa turns to see another limo leading two large trucks up the hill. Kimbusa heads to meet the limo as it pulls to a stop next to his, and the door opens. Out steps Steven Caldera, neatly groomed and sporting a long black trench coat. Caldera waves what appears to be two moving vans towards the house, before heading to greet Kimbusa. “What do you think?” Caldera inquires excitedly, motioning towards the house. “Well, it’s a bit much, and I don’t approve of-“ Epiphany cuts the rather blunt reply off. “It’s wonderful, Mr. Caldera,” she says, trying to hold back her glee. Having been raised in a small Japanese village and fed rice for her first twenty years, coming to America meant exploring new locales such as this. She was immensely appreciative of Steven Caldera, having long since forgiven him for the treachery he had forced her to endure. Indeed, Epiphany is everything that is good about Kimbusa. But the ease with which she forgave Caldera angered Kimbusa. Somewhere inside, Kimbusa felt as if Epiphany’s good nature was also her weakness. But so help him, he loves the young girl. Caldera turns and immediately takes charge of the moving process, directing the stream of ten workmen carrying in Kimbusa’s new belongings. In truth, he had never seen any of it before, and it was all either from Caldera’s personal collection, or purchased during Caldera’s recent trip to Italy. “Careful with that!” Caldera shouts at a few workers unloading a wooden chest. Kimbusa sighs and turns back to the house, still not sure about how comfortable such a monstrosity could be. Epiphany giggles as she finally notices Kimbusa’s trepidation. “You don’t like it?” she snickers. Kimbusa opens his mouth, but is unable to find the correct words. Fortunately for Kimbusa, Epiphany knew exactly what he wanted to say without needing to say it. “Mr. Caldera is simply trying to make up for lost time, Kimbusa. We wasted so much time fighting each other. He feels bad, though he won’t admit to it. Consider this an apology,” she says in such a sympathetic way that Kimbusa simply can’t argue. …The house really isn’t that big… Epiphany, wearing a pink jacket and pants that look like they were imported from the 1960’s, lightly trots up towards the front door of the house. Caldera begins barking more orders at the workers as they nearly drop a fifty foot painting framed in what is probably solid gold. Kimbusa slowly begins moving up towards the house, pausing only to grab his bags from the limo. Kimbusa follows as Caldera heads into the house at Epiphany’s heels. The interior of the house proves to be rather modest, much to Kimbusa’s liking. Apparently Caldera hadn’t been totally inconsiderate when constructing the house. A charming wooden staircase stands across from the door, and the floors are wooden as well. The result is a house that appears to be rather low-key, but Kimbusa suspects that Caldera will yet sneak in a few modern trinkets. Caldera is standing in the open living room a few feet ahead. The room is empty for the moment, but the movers are rapidly filling the house with furniture and appliances. Kimbusa moves next to Epiphany, whose smile is enough to warm the cold air flowing freely through the halls. “Have a seat, Kimbusa,” Caldera says nonchalantly as he searches his pockets for a cigar. Kimbusa, confused, turns to search the empty room for somewhere to sit. To his surprise, he finds that the movers have somehow places an elegant sofa directly behind him. Unnerved, Kimbusa and Epiphany sit. Caldera finds himself a cigar and lights it, sitting down at the precise moment the movers set a leather recliner behind him. Caldera smiles at Kimbusa, who again finds himself rolling his eyes at the vulgar display of power. At that moment, a pretty young woman enters the room. She is modestly clad in a long white coat, but her presence is instantly sensed by the threesome in the living room. “Madame,” the woman says in a thick French accent, addressing Epiphany directly, “if you will accompany me upstairs, we will begin decorating.” Epiphany, her smile growing ever wider, stands and begins following the French woman up the staircase. As they ascend, Epiphany looks quizzically back to Kimbusa, who ushers her on approvingly. Suddenly alone in the now furnished room, Caldera and Kimbusa lock eyes. “What are your goals, Kimbusa? What do you want out of RPW?” Caldera asks between puffs from his cigar. “I can no longer distinguish one American wrestling promotion from another,” Kimbusa says with distaste. “I honor our agreement and accompany you in your endeavors.” Caldera smiles as a plume of smoke emerges from his lips. “But what about you, Kimbusa? No more vengeance?” Kimbusa shakes his head emphatically, speaking in a deep voice to emphasize his honesty. “The man who disgraced me is dead. Angelo DeVille has paid for his treachery.” Kimbusa suddenly turns his head upward, making certain the two are alone before continuing. “True I wish I could have done the deed myself, but the fact remains. I have had my fill of vengeance. I wish only to live in peace.” Caldera nods. “Well, I’ll tell you what I want, Kimbusa,” he says with a surprising tone of sincerity. “There was a time in my career, my friend, when I was heralded as one of the greatest pure wrestlers of all time. Every critic agreed that I was the future of the wrestling business, that my style would revolutionize the way people compete. But instead I allowed myself to be corrupted by the money they started throwing at me, and I’ve missed my best years. I was behind a desk when I should have been in a ring. I was signing checks instead of earning them. But my injuries are healing, Kimbusa, and I just want to see if I can reclaim some of those accolades. I want to find out if I’m still the wrestler I once was. Because honestly, my worst fear is that I’ll be nothing more than a pencil pusher for the rest of my career.” Kimbusa sits back in a state of shock. Caldera had never opened up to him like that before, he had never shown the slightest weakness in his iron armor. Caldera’s eyes have trailed off to the floor, reflecting back on a career gone awry. “They don’t even know who I am, Kimbusa. They hardly even remember my name,” he says, his voice becoming a bit weak. Kimbusa, still surprised, stands up and lifts his chin into the air. “Then let us remind them. Let us make our final march into battle, and show the world who we are. We shall go down in a blaze of glory, my friend, and they will forever remember our names!” Caldera looks up and quickly stands, his energy returning. He extends his hand to Kimbusa, who accepts it in a firm bond between two titans.
