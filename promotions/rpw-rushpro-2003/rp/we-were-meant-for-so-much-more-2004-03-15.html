We Were Meant For So Much More

15 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/15/04 The early hours of dawn peaked through the window as Matt Jackson laid half way between the current reality and the next. It was becoming habit for him to see the setting sun and than the raising one only a short time later. He could hear the birds singing the chorus of morning, telling the world that the stars were disappearing from view. A warmth was offered from his billowy comforter, giving his stiff frame a moment of relaxation. A lot had happened in the past couple of hours for the black star. Overdrive had come and gone with a bang, as if fireworks were being lit off on the Fourth of July. People had chanted his name, held on to their seats hoping beyond all hope that he would have walked away with his hand high in the air. He hated to admit it to himself, but it felt good to once again hear the chants. To once again be the hero that the world so badly needed, the one person that stock could be placed in with a safe investment. In Primetime Championship Wrestling, it was different. His name had still been chanted and he had come through countless times for the fans, but his soul was tainted. Illusions of grander always crept into his sanctuary and poisoned his being. This time though, he was pure and when their faces lit up it meant something to the black star that he couldn’t hope to convey with the modern text. Perhaps in a language of another time long forgotten he would be able to share his feelings. He hadn’t felt this way since his time in the Academy. The renewed sense of vigor about his character allowed the locks to be released from the chains that held his soul so diligently. New thoughts entered his mind, ones of hanging his vagabond shoes up once and for all. He didn’t want to get his hopes to high, but he believed that he had finally found a place to call home. The comforter heaved and collapsed with each breath of air that filled his lungs and a semi-smile crossed his lips. Everything in his mind was swirling into a fog, but it would finally be nice for him to obtain sleep. Muscles tensed and relaxed and his eyelids grew heavy. He belonged and it was a feeling that he couldn’t believe.
