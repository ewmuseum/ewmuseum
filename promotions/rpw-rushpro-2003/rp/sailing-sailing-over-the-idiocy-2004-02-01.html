Sailing, Sailing, Over the Idiocy

1 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless It felt good to be back where he belonged. The sights were the same, the sounds were the same, the smells were the same, and even though the sea itself was of a different time and place, the currents were the same. Shorebound waves sucked up to his ship, sloshing rhythmically against the sparkling fibreglass hull, reaching frantically and futilely to be pulled up into his presence and somehow made more. Seagulls cried and circled overheard, following him but pretending not to, watching from the corners of their eyes for any sign that he might throw them a crumb to spare them their mundane existence and shatter their false air of indifference. None of them dared shit on him or his ship, for it would be like cursing God from their deathbed, and they knew it as well as they knew that he was their saviour. They weren't in dire straits by any stretch of the imagination, but with the Man in Black around, they knew that things would get markedly better. From all around the mouth of the sea came a hushed hum, as businessmen and tourists, loiterers and dockmen, bums and professionals, even others who professed to be pirates, all encircled him with a quiet awe, keeping their distance as they went about their normal revelry. Crewless, the Man in Black had to be satisfied with the slow progress his vessel was making through the calm shallows. He yearned for the violence of the deep sea, craved the battle for life and death, but he knew he had to take it slow. He was alone and in a strange land, pretending to be what he was not, and if he didn't move with utmost caution he might find himself beached upon a rock before he could even be warned of the danger, and all of his plans would be ruined. He wasn't going to risk that. Especially not to cater to haste. Instead he hauled ass from bow to stern, from starboard to port, adjusting the rigging like a man possessed as he tried to manoeuvre his ill-begotten ship away from prying eyes and questioning shouts. The boat itself was pint-sized compared to the massive galleons the Man in Black had commanded and commandeered in his past. It was quaint and somehow comforting, a throwback to the days before his notoriety preceded his devilish flag. It had only one mast, two sails, and borne on its back like a hateful scar was an ugly little motor 'in case of emergency'. The Man in Black was irked that seafarers of this country would demean themselves in such a fashion, relying on the machinery of their laziness to get them where they needed to go, shamelessly disguising it as progress. Still, angle or not, he was more than content. He would pretend the automatic propulsion didn't exist and work hard -- work honestly -- just the same. Unostentatious sweat stained his neck and chest as he put his able muscles to the task at hand, wallowing in the effort instead of dreading it and treating it as a chore as so many others did. As I said, it felt good to the Man in Black. This was what he liked doing. This was what life was about. But enough veiled raving and ranting. The Man in Black glanced over the starboard side and broke into a grin as he spotted the ugliest looking fish he had ever seen. Its body was shorter than it should have been, and he could tell that it swam stretched to its full-length at all times in a hapless attempt to make up for the fact. Red colouring -- or more aptly: discolouring -- on the crown of its head gave it a goofy Ronald McDonald-ish appearance, and to make matters worse, there was a stain of black on its chin that made it look as if it had been drooling crap all over itself. Recognising it in some symbolic way, the Man in Black guessed that it probably had been. It probably liked it, too. Inexplicably, he found himself wondering where the fish's limo and champagne were, and why it wasn't proclaiming itself champion of whatever for the simple reason that it existed. Generic stereotypes or not, the fish was obviously a tool. Not even a cool tool like a jackhammer or a sextant, either . . . more like a pedal-powered wheelchair. Wanting to broadcast this abomination to as many people as possible, the Man in Black reached for the video camera the previous owners of the boat had conveniently left for him. Laughing to himself, knowing no one would believe the absolute boobery of the fish unless he had solid evidence of it, he bent over the side with the lens pressed to his eye. Apparently the fish had sensed some evil afoot, however, and it glared up into the camera with the scowling and retarded outrage of the truly deranged. Wanting to spare itself the humiliation that would follow its likeness being transmitted the world over, the ugly little bastard coiled itself up for a second, then sprang forth and punctured a hole in the Man in Black's boat. Being an experienced sailor, the Man in Black had a hand on the boat and so wasn't knocked overboard by the collision, but the camera dropped into the water, lost. Some strange red liquid started leaking out of the hull as water rushed in to replace it, and the damnable fish actually licked some of it before cackling 'I R EVIL~! I R SHUD B CHAMP~!' and scurrying off to the void of creativity and talent it called home. Cursing it, the Man in Black surveyed the damage and guessed that he didn't have much time before the ship sank. The hole wasn't big, but it was enough. He looked back and found that the shore was only about a mile away, close but too far to turn around and sail to. He could use the motor and . . . I won't even pursue that, as he would never use the motor. There were many other ships around him, luckily, and as he considered calling to one of them for assistance, a man in a sparkling green suit materialised on his deck and flashed a capped smile that put even his suit to shame. Put it to shame for ridiculousness, that is. 'Hi there,' the man said in his infomercial voice, striding forward to shake the Man in Black's hand. 'I'm Crusty Emerald! You might remember me from such ads as 'I can't believe he's not better!', 'Everyone looks like some stupid stereotype!', 'How come he can't coin any of his own lines?' and 'What kind of an inbred name is Vangellus?'' The Man in Black arched an eyebrow and slowly mouthed the work 'okay' as he wondered how much resistance this idiot could put up against being thrown overboard. He doubted it would be much, and deep down inside he hoped Emerald couldn't swim. 'Errrr, scratch that last one,' Emerald muttered with an embarrassed laugh, combing his fingers through his toupee. 'Better yet, scratch all of them -- they were random thoughts I had while doing ads, not the ads themselves. Heh.' 'I've had the very same thoughts, I assure you.' The Man in Black's voice was bland as he shook the man's hand, struggling to resist the temptation of launching him into the sea. 'How can you live with yourself while in the employ of such contrived bottom-feeding scum?' 'It's hard, believe me,' Emerald said in a throaty voice wholly different and more realistic than his paid one, secretively bending a hand around his mouth to thwart any lip-readers who might report him, 'but it pays the bills.' Straightening his suit and clearing his throat, he adopted his stage voice once more and began gesturing grandly with his hands. 'Speaking of my glorious employer, he has instructed me to come over here and offer you the help you need.' Emerald turned and waved to a gigantic ship on the horizon, backed by the sun to establish a false brilliance. Someone there likely waved back. The Man in Black was skeptical. 'He has the tools necessary to patch the hole? He'll get my ship back online?' 'Of course!' Emerald chuckled nervously and fussed with his collar, pulling it away from his neck to let air get in. His other hand wiped his brow, and his feet began shifting nervously. 'He would be, ah, delighted to help a, uh, fellow sailor such as yourself.' The Man in Black's voice was questioning and knowing at the same time. 'Then why are you fidgeting?' 'B-because I know something you don't know,' Emerald stammered. 'What's that?' 'His help does not come free of charge.' Emerald offered a weak smile. 'In fact, it's rather expensive.' 'How expensive?' 'He told me to tell you that he'll let you look at the patching equipment for $1000, or that you can rent it until you get to shore for $5000.' Emerald winced, obviously expecting the punch in the face that his employer so richly deserved. Instead of punching him, the Man in Black simply shook his head disgustedly. What sort of an idiot would expect anyone to pay such an outlandish price, and for a temporary solution at that, he didn't want to know. He didn't have many other options, though. 'Signal him over.' Emerald blinked, evidently caught off guard by this strange turn of events. Shrugging his shoulders, he broke into a strange dance, and in moments the great ship of his employer was carving a path across the water. The whole process should have taken less than two minutes, but with his grand sense of style, the owner of the vessel did not one, not two, but three figure-eights in agonizing slow motion before finally coming and looming over the Man in Black's boat. During this idiotic lapse of time, the Man in Black drew his sword. 'I think there's something I aught to tell you,' he said to Emerald, bending the blade this way and that, testing its flexibility. 'What's that?' 'My help does not come free either.' The tip of the blade flicked up under Emerald's chin, pushing his head up by a few degrees. He swallowed hard and began pleading, but the Man in Black cut him off with a shake of his head. 'I understand you could use my help,' came a condescending and prepubescent voice from the luxury liner, whose deck was about fifteen feet above that of the Man in Black's, with that gap widening by the minute. The Man in Black looked up for the source of the voice, but couldn't see it. 'And your zombie here could use mine,' he shouted good-naturedly, pressing the blade a little harder to make Emerald squeal. The display had the desired effect as a head with what looked like strands of dried dog-shit extending from it at odd angles appeared to squint over the side of the liner. A gap-toothed goon who looked like Mike Tyson appeared beside him, looking just as confused. 'Oy,' the sky-high man said. 'Oy indeed,' the Man in Black agreed, letting the sword fall away from Emerald's neck now that the point was made. 'I propose a trade. You send me down the patching kit, and in exchange I will let your monkey return to your ship with his head still attached to his body, so he can continue spouting off about your greatness. I assure you no one else will take up the slack if he should die.' The man nodded slowly, knowing the truth when he heard it even if he couldn't speak it. 'A good deal, hmm?' The man beside Tyson scrunched up his face and agonized over how to turn this to his advantage for a minute, then, apparently stumbling across the solution, laughed loudly. 'Oh, but I don't think we can do it that way,' he called as he rubbed at his chin. 'What's to stop you from simply killing him after I give you the patch? I think you'd both better come up here . . . I could throw down a rope. But I don't suppose you would accept, since I'm only waiting around to screw you.' 'That does put a damper on our relationship.' The Man in Black sighed and drew back his blade. 'I suppose I'll just have to kill your aide and swim to shore.' 'No wait!' Sky-High shouted, and the Man in Black paused. 'I have an idea.' Clearing his throat, he projected his voice as if swearing an oath to god. 'I swear on the fact that I suck more lemons than Sanders does dick that I will not screw you until you set both feet on my deck.' The Man in Black considered this a moment, and then, since he knew how much this guy liked sucking, he conceded. 'Throw down the rope.' The rope was thrown down, and the two men made their way up, the Man in Black pulling himself up ahead of Emerald, who had only gotten halfway by the time the Man in Black arrived on deck. For all of the ship's grandeur and size, the entire deck was empty. It was just one long boring sheet of blah. All of his supporters (he and the Tyson guy) were gathered there, rubbing their hands (and each other's backs) fiendishly. 'He fell for it, Arse!' Sky-High exclaimed to the Tyson look-alike, then turned to the Man in Black. 'Now that you're off my ship . . .' Sky-High trailed off and paused deliberately, his beady little eyes locked excitedly on the Man in Black for his reaction. The reaction was a furrowing of his brow beneath the mask, or, to Sky-High, no reaction at all. Sky-High waited for the Man in Black to ask what he was talking about, so the Man in Black waited for him to elaborate without a word, just for spite. Sky-High's lower lip began trembling, then he stomped his left foot. 'Aren't you going to ask me why I said 'off' instead of 'on'?' The Man in Black shrugged. 'I want to see how you do without a script wrought with cheap dialogue that so obviously panders to your overness. Judging by the increasing moisture in your eyes, you don't take to it very well.' Sky-High stomped his foot again. 'Play along, damn you! It's the only way I can look witty!' 'Sorry. You'll just have to find another way.' Sky-High cried on his silent partner's shoulder for a long while, crying out 'it's not fair!' and pounding useless fists against Arse's ample chest. Arse glared at the Man in Black over Sky-High's shoulder, letting him know that as soon as his buddy stopped blubbering there would be trouble. 'What am I gonna do, Arse? He won't go along with my artificial conversation!' Arse thinks long and hard for all of three seconds, then whispers something into Sky-High's ear. 'That's brilliant.' Straightening up and clearing his throat, Sky-High tried to pretend that he wasn't just blubbering like an infant deprived of his pacifier. Craning his neck as if to hear better, he asked, 'What's that? You want to know why I said 'off' instead of 'on'?' The Man in Black rolled his eyes and shook his head, genuinely embarrassed to be on the same ship with this fool. 'Ahahahahahahaha,' Sky-High laughed profoundly, 'hahahahaha! That was the whole plan, you fool! That ship that you were on? That's mine now, too! I just bought it from the guy at the harbour! Ahahahahahahaha!' Faced with such unbridled stupidity, the Man in Black was speechless. Or, more likely, he just wanted to hear this ass continue braying. 'You fell for the oldest trick in the book!' There was some more laughter by Sky-High and his buddy, Arse, then Sky-High turned away from the Man in Black and looked at his hand. His mouth moved slowly and silently as if he was reading something from it. He nodded to himself, as if it had been memorised, then turned back. 'But don't worry. I do have some good news.' He paused to let the Man in Black ask him what the good news is, leaning forward in his excitement to quote unfunny lines, but again the Man in Black remains silent to spite him. Choking back the tears, Sky-High persevered anyway. 'I just saved a bunch of money on my car insurance by switching to Geico.' Sky-High, Arse, and everyone else they paid to think that was funny burst into hysterics, slapping at their thighs and pointing at the Man in Black as if he should feel burned somehow. Instead, he was understandably dumbfounded. Even though they were out in the middle of the sea where no crickets had any business being, the undeniable sound of their chirping roared over the laughter, vainly attempting to let them know that the joke wasn't just cliched, but also about as funny as Pete not wearing a dress and not singing I'm a Little Tea Pot. Wanting to end this monstrosity before it could get any worse, the Man in Black suddenly shouted, 'Look! A naked stripper begging to get raped!' and pointed over the side of the ship. Arse, dumber than dumb has a right to be, missed the contradiction of terms and rushed over to the side, bending over it to scour the seas. Sky-High, gayer than two dicks rubbing against an Adam's apple, spotted Arse's vulnerable namesake and rushed over to take advantage. The Man in Black, disgusted yet pleased with himself, pushed them both overboard. Mercifully, neither of them were smart enough to realize what had happened, and they drowned in each other's loving embrace. The Man in Black sauntered over to the rope and looked over the side at Emerald, who was about a foot away from making it on deck. 'Don't kill me,' he begged. 'Offer me money,' The Man in Black said, revelling in the fact that Emerald had been too busy studying how to be an ass-kissing lackey to watch any artistic cinematic gems like The Princess Bride. 'Yes! Yes!' Emerald sputtered desperately. 'Offer me power.' I'll be the first to admit that biting Inigo's style isn't cool, but the Superbowl's on and I've already missed half of it to write this thing before Adrenaline goes up and we have our television contract back, so deal with it. 'Of course!' The poor dolt didn't know he was playing into the Man in Black's capable hands, just as his employers didn't. 'Offer me everything I could ever ask for.' 'ANYTHING!' The television-whore was practically in tears, but he thought he saw a light at the end of the tunnel, and he'd do or say anything to reach it. The Man in Black hardened his tone as he hefted his sword. 'I want the last hour of my life back,' he spat, then cut the rope.
