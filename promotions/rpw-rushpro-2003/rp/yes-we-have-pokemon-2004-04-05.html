Yes, We Have Pokémon!

5 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 5th, 2004 “James, over there! THEY HAVE POKÉMON!!!” Clyde shrieked the last part, causing James to slam on the brakes in the middle of the street. Farwell looked like he was about to have a heart attack, but Walkins appeared more than ecstatic. Pointing at a shop on the right hand side, Clyde stared at the window, reading a faded sign placed behind the glass. “Yes, we have Pokémon!” Clyde grinned. “Yes, they have Pokémon!” He grinned again. Farwell’s left eye began to twitch as he tightened his grip on the steering wheel. Meanwhile, the cars behind them on the two-lane road began honking their horns, impatient drivers hurling obscenities at The Forsaken. “I don’t care if they have the cure for cancer.” James muttered under his breath, growing angrier by the second. “You almost got us killed.” “But Jaaaaaames!” Clyde whined, “I’ve been looking all over, and no one else has Pokémon anymore…” “That’s because they realized it sucks.” And then Clyde’s bottom lip began to quiver, looking almost as if he was readying to cry. James couldn’t handle that, not today. All it would take is a little squeeze, and he’d be gone forever, James thought to himself. But soon he gave in, mostly because he wanted Clyde to shut up. Turning the wheel towards the right, James began to pull in behind a parked car; a black 1984 Firebird, by the looks of it; when Clyde reached over and slammed his foot against the gas pedal. “We gotta get there before someone else catches ‘em all!” Clyde screamed, as James hit the brakes as fast as he could, just in the nick of time to avoid certain death. But not quick enough to stop from rear-ending the car, mind you. “What…the…hell is wrong with you?!” James looked like he was about to blow up, but Clyde was already taking off his seatbelt. From inside the store, a large figure soon appeared. The man appeared to stand between six-foot-three and six-foot-five, probably tipping the scales at the four hundred pound mark, as well. His skin best resembled a chocolate bar left out in the sun, as it looked greasier than any human should ever be, and his appearance was complimented by torn and faded blue jeans, a black shirt with the word “Pokémon” across the chest in yellow, and a black baseball cap with the red-and-white Pokéball on the front. In his arms were approximately forty packages of Pokémon cards. He took one look at the car, dropped his cards on the ground, and screamed like a little girl being run over by a lawnmower. “The car!” He cried out, “You killed it!” But Clyde didn’t really pay any attention to it, as he had already burst from the vehicle, scooping up the man’s fallen Pokémon cards. Walking up to what was apparently his car, the man began caressing it with his hands, looking completely distraught. But then his sadness turned to rage, and he pulled open the trunk, producing an ax handle in his meaty hand. Turning towards Clyde, who was in the process of stealing the cards, he lunged forward, catching Clyde in the back with the chunk of wood. “Owies!” Clyde yelped, before falling forward to the ground. With James still watching from inside the car, the man proceeded to kick Walkins in the midsection, before another man appeared from inside the store. “Dammit, Al!” He said, not yet realizing the beating that was taking place. “That’s the last time I give you a ride to the store just so you can buy those damned cards. You do realize they’re only worth the paper they were printed on, don’t you? I mean…wait a minute, why are you kicking that small child?” This new man, looking to be about the same height as Al, was built in much better condition. Probably weighing in at between two hundred-and-eighty and three hundred pounds, his massive frame was barely concealed by the black tank top underneath his open trench coat. The skin that was visible was a pale white, contrasting the first man’s tone, but the rest of him was blotted out by the glare of the sun behind his back. Yanking the ax handle away, he pushed Al out of the way, before helping Clyde up. Carrying him towards the car James still sat in, the man put two and two together, seeing the open door and the waiting man in the driver’s seat. “I’m sorry about your son. My friend here doesn’t exactly have all his brain cells.” The man started, as he helped Clyde into his seat. “He’s not my son.” James replied, having to say the same thing about twelve times a month. “Your grandson then.” The man continued, without missing a beat. “…He’s not my grandson, either.” The man stopped for a second, then raised his eyebrow. “Love-slave, perhaps?” Farwell started to become more pissed off than when the story began. “He’s almost as old as I am, you know.” Hearing this, the man seemed to suddenly clue in to something. Looking at each of their faces as if he recognized them, he was about to say something when Al pulled him away from the car. “They killed your car!” He screamed, pointing at the man to look. And so he did, seeing the damage done to the back-end instantly. Farwell looked at the two of them, turned the wheel to face left, then hit the gas. Peeling away with Clyde unconscious in the passenger’s seat, James didn’t bother to even look back before he took off down the nearest road, hoping to escape whatever rage may be accumulating inside the mystery man. “Aren’t you gonna go after them?” Al questioned, panicking. “No.” The man replied. “I know who they are. There’s no need to chase someone when you know where they’re gonna be next.” Half-smiling, he looked down into the open trunk, the shining gold of a title belt peeking out from within a black bag. “I think that we’ll be seeing them again very soon.”
