Oh Kari, Where Art Thou? #01 : This. Is. SO. Lame.

27 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27/3/04 How do we kick this one off? Well... It was Tuesday lunchtime, and Karina Wolfenden was trekking up towards the Dale F. Halton Arena. It was Tuesday lunchtime, and Karina Wolfenden was still in Charlotte, North Carolina. It was Tuesday lunchtime, and Karina Wolfenden was being summoned by RPW's figurehead. It was Tuesday lunchtime, and Karina Wolfenden was not happy. For her, wrestling started at 8 on a Sunday evening, and finished 120 minutes later... Although she mildly tolerated over-runs. She gave her Sundays to RPW. She took great delight in flying around their ring to the adulations of the small, but fiercely loyal crowds. She even stuck around most of Monday for any promotional appearances they wanted of her... ...But Tuesday? That was almost mid-week. So with an untainted sun burning into the icy blue sky, Karina continued her reluctant approach towards the meeting, feeling the mild spring air on her pale, freckled face, arms, and midriff. 'They always said never to work with children or animals,' the K-Wolf sighed as she adjusted her rucksack, her thoughts on RPW's teenage figurehead who had called her to the Fed's makeshift home. 'And I get to work for the former, and share a locker room with the latter.' Her lamenting voice was that of a young woman who had better things to do, and on every day bar Sunday, that was just about everything under the sun. Looking back over her shoulder, Karina's emerald eyes checked on the scarlet Ducati 748 Superbike she arrived on, before scanning suspiciously anyone walking near it. It wasn't the attitude many would expect of a wrestler, and if they weren't such a crowd pleaser, it would have been an outlook that dented their popularity. Week in and week out, Karina Wolfenden was surrounded by people who'd devoted their lives to wrestling, who'd become immersed in every aspect of it's locker room culture. She didn't want to be like them. Men creeping into their 40's were driving up and down the east coast every week, performing at any local hall just to get their paycheque. Those people didn't enjoy it. It was their job, just the same as an accountant trekked into work each day to try and balance a stranger's books, just the same as a construction worker clocks in to heave concrete and steel around for 7 hours. Karina had never had a job, and she didn't intend to make wrestling her first. 'What gym do you belong to?' casual acquaintances often asked as part of a throwaway conversation after spying her tight and toned physique. 'None,' was her perpetual reply. Her life was one long workout, funded in rather hypocritical fashion by her trust fund... Something which she wisely kept quiet in the vanguard of anti-capitalist protests. When it wasn't leaping and flipping through the stale air of RPW's home, it was opening the throttle on her 'baby', making the air above fresh snow her home, or treating gravity itself with contempt as she humbled urban geography on a free-run. 'Saved by the bell,' she then quipped through pale rose lips as her pager went off. Ripping open the velcro pouch on her rucksack's shoulder strap, Kari slid the pager out into her red leather gloved hand, and read the display. 'Oh well, I guess something came up.' With the slightest of excuses in her arsenal, Karina spun on the heels of her black and grey vans while gravel crunched under foot. Breezing back towards her scarlet beacon of a bike, Karina breathed a sigh of relief... But was then lassoed by the annoyingly high voice of RPW's custodian; Little Al. Like a kid safely guarding his favourite Playstation game, Al presided over RPW, probably the only 16 year old to play out a real-life Extreme Warfare Revenge. 'Leaving already,' he called after Karina, the words whipping into her ears and jolting the K-Wolf's head back. Stopping still for the briefest of seconds, Karina's body slumped forward, her hear dropping and sending her crimson fringe cascading down over her plain face. 'Dammit,' she hissed, realising her getaway had been thwarted. 'Was just going to... Er... Remind myself what 'behind me' looked like,' she added unconvincingly, turning with an insincere smile for her teenage superior. Karina didn't fear for her job like so many on wrestling’s Indy circuit, as all it would cause was the inconvenience of looking for somewhere new to play. But she followed management's line all the same, she just made her reluctance and opposition to certain things vocal. Many wouldn't take Little Al seriously, after all, what 40 year old grizzled veteran would let himself be ordered around by a 16 year old kid, living off his family's money? Ah, family money. Despite their differences, Karina and Al did have a few things in common. 'Well you're here now,' Al replied to her, raising his voice to carry it along the brisk midday breeze. 'So let's talk shop.' Beside Al stood RPW's creative director, Jeffrey Ward. The voice in Al's ear, and the man many said really ran RPW. He was a smart, calculating mind, always making the logical choice... Which was why Wolfenden always preferred going straight to Al, at least she'd seen him smile. That, and he'd never worn pinstripe, another plus. 'This way, Miss Wolfenden,' Ward gestured, standing aside and gesturing her through the main doors. It was polite to open a door for a lady, but cringe-worthy when done in the highly patronising manner Ward managed. 'And there I was about to phase through the wall and all,' she sarcastically bit back, breezing her hand against the red brick and waving her fingers gloved fingers around. 'Oooooooh, Shadowcat style.' Ward looked unimpressed as the K-Wolf flashed past him with a wink, followed by Al who gave him a 'don't let her get to you' look. It was the first time Karina Wolfenden had seen the interior of the Dale F. Halton Arena on any day but a Sunday... And she quickly realised why. It was a dead, plaster shell. No energy, no passion, no muffled cheering trying with all it's might to burst through the very bricks that made up the building. It was lifeless, cold, and grey. See; Jeff Ward. 'Wow, ghost-town,' the K-Wolf observed, hearing nothing but their footsteps. 'So, why am I here again?' Karina spun around mid-stride to face the trailing duo of Al and Jeff. Walking backwards while holding her arms out to her sides, Kari raised her eyebrows to exclaimate her enquiry. 'And please don't say this is another promotional appearance, if I have to wear one of those RPW baseball caps again I'm going t-' 'Watc-' Al went to interrupt, but his concern was far from warranted. Despite facing away from her path, Karina gracefully hopped to her left, avoiding a crate that had been left over from Adrenaline. As if there was a subconscious radar implanted deep in her psyche, Wolfenden always managed to stay a step ahead, and be at one with everything all around her. If violently swung fists or angry driving knees couldn't connect, what hope did an inanimate crate have? 'Huh?' a curious Kari directed at Al, wondering why he butted in. 'Let's just get to Al's office first,' Jeff Ward interjected himself into the conversation with, bringing a stern and authoritative demeanour back into the fold. 'Then we'll run you through the plan.' Sighing at his starched-collar stuffiness, Karina rolled her burning emerald eyes and spun back around. 'Oooooooo kayyyyyy,' she playfully obeyed, drawing each letter out as a sign of how much Ward's personality clashed with hers. 'Maybe I should have come in a skirt suit.' As they reached Al's office, deep in the bowels of the building, Jeff Ward strode ahead and opened the door for Karina. Stopping in her stride, she looked to the handle, then to Ward. 'Gee, thanks,' came her less-than-appreciative words, making it as clear as the day outside that Ward's presence wasn't wanted, and barely tolerated. 'I'm not sure lil' ol' me couldda worked out that handle thing by myself.' Before Ward - who was getting more than a little bent out of shape by Wolfenden's lack of respect for him - could reply, Kari breezed past and into the office of Little Al... Which was a well constructed cross between a teenager's bedroom and a futuristic techno-den. So, not really how you'd expect a federation's figurehead to decorate his office, unless Vinnie Mac suddenly got a thing for Hentai instead of just ogling his daughter. 'So,' Kari casually began, dropping back into one of the more comfortable chairs that littered the room, just about fighting her temptation to go for the beanbag. She missed the 80's in some respects. 'Why am I being called to the headmaster's office again?' Despite the fact that Karina was looking directly at Ward, showing her contempt for the 'suit' who was trying to pull strings behind the scenes, Little Al answered. 'Well, as you know,' Al began to explain, making the assumption that Wolfenden paid attention to events other than her matches. 'RPW is part of the PTC...' 'The Parent's Tel-' a confused Kari slowly went to confirm, not thinking it sounded right. 'Primetime Central,' the agitated voice of Jeff Ward intervened, rapidly becoming frustrated with the K-Wolf's overly-relaxed, approaching apathetic, manner. 'Ahhh,' Kari used to offer some recognition. 'Must've missed that memo.' Ward's steely gaze met Kari's teasing green eyes, giving her a stern look in an attempt to focus her on the business at hand... Only to have a rapidly flashed-out, pierced tongue reply to him. As the K-Wolf further riled him with a cheeky smile, the creative director turned back to Little Al, ushering on his explanation. 'Where was I? Oh, yeah, PTC,' Al continued, his style of management far more to the K-Wolf's tastes. Al actually enjoyed what he did, not just the pay and sense of power he got from it. 'Well, they’ve set up a tournament for all affiliated Cruiserweights, and, well, as 2-time Eclipse Champion, you're in it!' Al's eagerness wasn't exactly reciprocated by Karina, who looked more bemused than enthralled. 'And they asked for me, why?' she enquired back with narrowed eyes, thoroughly uncomfortable with being dragged from her home under the radar of the wrestling world. 'They didn't,' the dull, blunt words of Ward answered. 'Okay... What form did I just sign without looking at?' Kari cringed, realising that in all likelihood, due to her lack of time for form-filling and beaurocracy, she entered herself into the event. 'All of them,' Ward again bit back, eager to get a few shots back at the K-Wolf. 'Does he have to be here?' Wolfenden pleaded to Al, refusing to look at the grey pinstripe figure to her right out of disgust. 'Last time I looked, we weren't accountants... Or encyclopaedia salesmen' While his 2 conflicting employees continued their verbal sparring, Little Al just waited for the pause in hostilities to get a word in edgeways. 'We entered you,' he finally got to squeeze into the conversation, to the immediate dissatisfaction of Wolfenden. 'Tessa is in, so is Crawford, Jackson wanted in, Carys was up for it... And, well, I added you to the list too. The more the merrier.' 'Oh, yeah,' Kari's frustrated words grumbled back, far from merry. 'Happy happy, joy joy...' Far from pleased by her apathy to the tournament, Jeff Ward couldn't resist a chance to belittle Karina... Their personality clash evident in every aspect of their lives. 'Is that one of your new age philosopher’s mantras or something?' he scoffed while looking down his nose at the personification of everything he didn't want his children to become. 'Yeah,' came Karina's condescending reply, already laced by sarcasm, 'it's by the Generation X gurus that were Ren and Stimpy.' Finally getting annoyed by the 2 squabbling employees across the desk from him, Al's barely-broken voice took on a harsher tone. 'Enough,' he blurted out, making it clear that he was the authority in the room, despite the strong personalities, or lack thereof, of the duo in front of him. 'Karina, I want RPW to win this thing. It's my place, it's the best place, and I want everyone to see that.' In contrast to her loathing of Ward's approach, Kari could understand Al's reasoning. He was proud of the company he'd been handed, and he wanted to show it off to the world... And if anyone in RPW was all about the show, it would be the K-Wolf. 'Now, I know you're not exactly going to string together mesmerising wrestling sequences,' Al conceded, realising the only word in RUSH Pro Wrestling that summed up Karina was the first... The capitalisation very fitting. 'But when it comes to flying, flipping, or just plain kicking someone in the head... You're as good as anyone.' Just as Wolfenden seemed to be drifting over to Al's point of view, Ward had to ruin it, not wanting the rebellious and overly strong-willed employee to be too comfortable. 'Of course, we'll need you to up your profile,' he took great delight in announcing, knowing that Kari was probably the most private member of RPW away from shows... A very stark contrast to the sense of serene showmanship she displayed between - or above - the ropes. 'A few interviews, a bit of trash talking, get your name out there.' 'Or...' Kari suggested, turning to Ward with wide open and disputing eyes. 'I could not.' 'You don't have to walk around dressed head-to-toe in RPW stuff,' the softer voice of Al reassured her. 'We just want to use this thing to get our name out there. And, like it or not, you're popular. You sell... You're money.' 'So just try and run whoever you face down a little,' Ward continued on. 'I have a feeling you can handle throwing a few insults out there.' 'Oh, I have plenty buzzing around in here right now,' the scathing Kari bit back, tapping her temple with her right ring finger. 'Wanna hear a few?' While Ward just deflected her words with a conceited smile, Al grabbed Karina's attention back. 'It's all in the name of RPW,' Al reminded her, realising that if anything, Karina understood how precious the promotion was to him. 'So I expect you to do this with dignity despite your reluctance.' 'Fine,' Karina sighed, 'but wrestling 2 days a week? This is starting to sound like work...' Making it clear that given the choice, she'd screw up every bit of info on the tournament and ram it down Ward's throat, Karina sprung up and made her exit... Her normally chirpy body language further emphasising that she was in a sulk. 'What is that?' the curious K-Wolf then enquired as she opened the office door to be met with the sight of 2 workers carrying past a hard-board backdrop. 'Oh, that?' Al began to explain as he too saw the brick-painted, barb-wire wrapped RPW wall section. 'It's a promo wall, we had it made especially for this PTC thing. You and the others can stand in front of it when you want to make an attack on your opponents, let 'em know that they’re getting verbally bitch-slapped by the ol' RUSH Pro!' 'Great idea, is it not?' a snivellingly smirking Ward beamed as a further dejected Karina walked away in amazement... Her words being heard by all as they drifted in her wake. 'This. Is. SO. Lame.'
