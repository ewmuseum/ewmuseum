Before Today #09 : So Much, So Young

19 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/01/04 It was to be the day that Karina Wolfenden solved the riddle that is life. At the age of 10, her already inquisitive mind shifted into over-drive, and almost everything she ever said was a question. While most children her age were passing the baking summer in swimming pools or on beaches, Kari decided the weeks of freedom from uninspiring education should be put to good use. As she awoke, her east-facing window let in the morning sun, burning through the thin white blind to heat her bedroom. She turned, facing away from it's blinding wake-up call. If she'd have asked any of her classmates what the sun was, they'd have simply pointed up to the blue sky, and told her not to stare directly at it. If Karina were to be asked, 'the sun is a spherical body, over 100 times as wide as the earth, made up of mostly hydrogen which turns to helium,' would have been her automated reply. Life was about knowledge to her, and the young Wolfenden girl wanted to be the Encyclopaedia that understood it. After getting dressed, she was forced to go through the normal morning ritual of breakfast with her family before her father went to work, then helping her mother with the washing up. It's unrelenting routine already bored her, finding that family bonding and conversation was far duller when un-spontaneous. But, it was over, and the time until lunch was hers to fill as she liked. Sports had yet to interest her, the woman who gives her snowboards names and calls her Ducati Superbike her 'baby' not even having an interest in skateboards or bikes at that age. Karina wanted to see the bigger picture... She wanted to discover the meaning of life, and why we're here... All before lunch. Thinking she could work out anything if she thought about it long enough, Karina started at the beginning... The big bang. She'd seen on early morning television an informative program about the birth of the universe, choosing that above the numerous cartoons it fought for viewers with, cartoons that would later inspire her now impressive comic collection. From what she'd taken in, Karina knew the universe began with a huge explosion... But what existed before that? Surely nothing blowing up could make everything in existence? Understanding 'why' was a lot different to understanding 'how'. With a glass of apple juice, she walked out onto the decking that overlooked the vast expanse of the Wolfenden home's back garden. Her father, Lawrence, had recently been promoted to Chief Executive, not that Karina even understood what he did, and the lavish grounds of their new home only helped fuel her inquiring mind. Looking out at nature itself, Karina couldn't stop thinking, 'how could nothing blowing up make a butterfly?' Maybe she'd started a bit far up the ladder, what about earth itself? Even at the age of 10, children new about the history of the earth... The book of Genesis, the Dinosaurs, Cavemen, Ice and Bronze Ages But Karina couldn't care less about words and pictures in books, she wanted to know more than anyone else, she didn't just want to learn, she wanted to understand. Focussed concentration was then broken as the car-flap swung behind her, the family cat - Tabatha - making her way out into the world to. As the feline became lost in the plants, it changed young Karina's path of thought. She'd seen in a Museum the skeleton of a Sabretooth Tiger, she'd begged her parents for a Safari holiday so she could see the Royal majesty of Lions with her own eyes... They were cats too, just like Tabatha. 'How can Tabatha be related to them both?' she'd always wondered, not making the connection between them without guidance. Did they have the same great, great, great - and so on - grandfather? At one point, did a Sabretooth Tiger have a brother who was a Lion? Karina's questions may have not been a master class in evolutionary genetics, but she was thinking... Which was more than a lot of children her age could boast. With her emerald green eyes still looking at the flowerbed where Tabatha merged back into the outside world, the young Karina then looked to the plants for an answer to life. She'd seen pictures of dinosaurs eating the leaves of trees, she'd been forced to read the Bible where God supposedly made all the world's plants, and there they were... In her very own garden. 'Why didn't they die out like the dinosaurs?' Karina had thought, 'and how could somebody just create them, when we were told that pollen made new plants...' If anything, her constant ponderings highlighted the barrage of contradicting information children her age were subjected to. If she'd have just believed the Bible, all the mysteries about plants and animals would have been solved... But then she'd still have been left wondering how the person who created them was created. Then again, the scientific view wasn't exactly clear. All morning she thought about the nature right there in front of her... The birds, the trees, the distant, barking dog that kept shattering her concentration. By the time her mother called Karina in for lunch, she was very thankful for the break. 'What have you been doing?' her mother asked as they waited for Karina's brother, Max, to join them. 'Not much, just thinking,' the innocently smiling Kari answered, washing her hands while her mother took the food to the table. With a questioning tone, her mother looked up and asked, 'really? All this time? What about?' Karina knew her parents, and she knew they already thought she was a little 'odd'. Not too many people her age would waste the summer holidays trying to figure out things most adults couldn't even begin to comprehend. 'Just... Stuff,' she dismissively answered, deciding it was the best reply. Her mother gave her one last look, a mix of slight concern and much bemusement, but nothing else was said about it that day. With lunch over, Karina couldn't wait to get back to the task at hand. So what if she'd failed to understand the Universe before lunch, she'd just have to think extra hard and make sure the world was all figured out in time for dinner. Rather than using the garden for inspiration, Kari scurried up the 2 flights of stairs to her room, where the door was promptly closed behind her. The sun had drifted overhead, making her room a little more bearable, and once she'd opened the windows, little Kari was ready to start again. 'I know what mistake I made,' Kari thought to herself, still trying to justify why her overactive mind couldn't figure the world out. All morning, she'd looked to plants, animals, even space... But she knew little about them all, for the 10 year old Wolfenden was a human. If she could figure out what that meant, she'd be well on her way. Kari knew that humans hadn't always looked like her. She'd seen the 'evolution of man' poster-strip in her school's Biology lab, showing how he gradually stood straighter, shedding his hair. At the beginning, he looked almost like an Ape, but not quite. There was a piece in-between called the 'Missing Link', but was it missing, or did it simply not exist? 'How can these people not even know where WE come from?' a bemused Kari often asked, frustrated at supposed experts' lack of a clear answer. Some said her ancestors were Apes, some said they just sprung up somewhere, while the Bible told her that her distant relative was a rib. A rib? That one had her slamming the sacred book in disappointed disgust. Maybe life was just a complicated puzzle confined to the earth... Or maybe on planets all throughout the universe, little girls sat in their rooms on a hot summer's day, pondering life and it's reason. That thought just complicated her already uphill task even further. 'I wonder if there's anyone else like me out there?' Karina thought, looking at the sky but thinking far beyond it. It was all too much for her. 'Maybe tomorrow,' the young Karina sighed in defeat, thinking that mysteries were more fun than understanding. '...I think I need a lay down.'
