Awakening.

24 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: today. Along with breathing, there’s a lot of noises. The beeping, the buzzing, the whirring, the purring, the dripping; there were so many noises, my brain simply shut most of them out, instead favoring a voice, rather than anything else. There was the TV, that was seemingly always on, as well as a quite a few familiar programs like Sportscenter. Voices were the only things I heard anymore. I’d gotten used to most of them. There was the man I assumed was the doctor in charge who sounded as if he were middle eastern. There was the sweet nurse, who held my hand as she took my temperature. She smelled fantastic. Her hand was so soft, so safe. I heard the voice of Little Al. Not quite fully matured, but definitely aged since he took over RPW, that’s for sure. He’s been by a few times, talking to me, telling me what’s going on. I appreciate that. Since I can’t see anything, nor can I even pick up the remote to change the channel on the TV, it’s nice knowing what’s going on in the world. He always seems to have a smile in his voice. He could be frowning. He’s confided that he’s seen someone in my position just once before, and after a series of unfortunate circumstances, the man died. So it goes. I heard the voice of Peter Maxwell, both in my head, and beside me. He’d visited a few times. I wasn’t sorry that he came. I listened to Adrenaline. My nurse decided I should hear familiar voices. It was nice to hear Roberts standing up to him. Maxwell, however sounded different. As if he actually was sorry that things went the way they did. I was too. Roberts, of course has been here more than just about anyone else. He’s not used to technology that actually keeps people alive. Many times, he’s told me he’d like nothing more than to take his sword, and slice through every wire in my scalp, every tube in my nose, and the pipe down my throat, keeping my lungs closed. Not to kill me, but to release me. I’d like nothing more than a release. Then, I heard a new voice. It was nearly angelic. I couldn’t just hear her voice, I actually saw her shining words before my eyes. Pictures became clearer. I still couldn’t see; and yet, I could. She came down like an angel. I’d never heard the voice before, nor had I ever heard a voice so sweet, or so loving. I liked her voice. “Jason,” she said. “Jason, I know you’re going to come out of this. We all love you. All of your fans love you. Your friends love you. But most importantly…I love you.” Internally, I smiled. I was hoping she was my new nurse. I needed another nice nurse. Sadly, she was not. She told me all about her life. She was near 40, her husband left her and their 2 kids, Steven and Timothy about 8 months ago, for his secretary in the law firm he worked at. Their divorce was quick, and while she got a large settlement, a few days after, he hit a 250 million dollar lottery jackpot, put all the money in trust, and moved away. She told me about how much she loved him, and how badly she missed him. Steven was just turning 17, and was friends with a guy who knows this kid that went to high school with Little Al. They met on the internet. Timothy is 14, and wants to be a professional wrestler when he grows up, his favorite wrestler is Chris Storm, and the entire family hates him for it. If it was my kid, I’d take him over my knee and beat some sense into him. If I could lift my arms that is. She told me her name was Louise Ann. Louise Ann Murphy. She’d become a fan of mine since I was a preliminary wrestler in the WWE. For those that don’t know, a preliminary wrestler is the guy like Barry Horowitz, “Playboy” Buddy Rose, and “Leapin’” Lanny Poffo. They were the guys that would get beat up by the big names before the main events. Back in 1996, when I was first starting out, we were a dying breed. I was one of the last guys. My claim to fame was a tag victory, teaming with the Brooklyn Brawler against the New Age Outlaws. Just another bit of O’Neil trivia, really. Anyways, she’d met me a few times, but sadly, I couldn’t remember her face. After a while they all run together. There’s so many people to meet, and shake hands with on the road, after a while, it’s just sort of a blur. Names you should remember are names you need to be reminded of. Names you can’t remember, it’s probably best you don’t. It’s just the nature of things. “Jason.” She said again. “I want you to be better. We all want you here, with us. You’re our strength, our pillar. Our rock.” I heard her start to cry, and a tear stream down her face, off her nose and onto my hand. She sniffled, and wiped her eyes. She smiled at me. Instantly, the machines whirring increased. I started to cough. The beeping grew louder. There was quite a bit of commotion in the room. I felt the tube in my throat removed. My eyes were forced open and lights were shined in my eyes. Wires were ripped off my scalp. The tube in my nose was removed. I gasped for breath. The commotion increased as I felt pressure on my chest. I tried to swing my arms, but to no avail. I was stuck to the bed. Reality hit me. My eyes flew open as I looked around the room wildly. I saw faces I didn’t recognize, staring at me, gasping in shock. It looked as my diagnosis was about to change.
