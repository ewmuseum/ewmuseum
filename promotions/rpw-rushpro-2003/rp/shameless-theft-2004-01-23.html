Shameless Theft

23 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless Pete was raised in a modest house in the mighty USA. His favourite past-times were ravaging his own body and tormenting the poor slavemonkey who helped him run RPW. His name was Mat. But Pete never called him that. Pete never rhymed, either, much to the chagrin of everyone not named Peter. It's because he's a wiener. Moving on . . . Nothing gave Pete more pleasure than harassing Mat and making his worst nightmares come true. There. Now isn't that a lovely beginning? 'Oh Pirate-hater,' Pete called affectionately on an otherwise uneventful day several weeks ago, the self-satisfied tone of his voice warning Mat that something fiendish was about to be unveiled. 'Guess what I've found.' 'More parrots to shit in my room?' Mat asked with feigned humour. 'A bunch of goofs willing to pester me on AIM with demands of changing their characters into pirates?' 'Why yes, actually,' Pete answered slowly, caught by surprise. He scratched his head with wonder, then shook the curious look from his face and spawned another evil smile. 'But also something far more aggravating than that.' Mat thought for a minute. 'No way . . .' His eyes suspicious slits, Mat bounded from his bunk and teetered over to the desk they shared. 'If you've coloured the right lens of my glasses black again, I swear . . .' 'Worse than that.' 'Worse?' 'Worse.' 'Inconceivable.' 'How can you be so sure?' Mat sighed. 'Nothing could be worse than this pirate nonsense you've been so caught up in lately. It is absolutely, totally, and in all other ways inconceivable.' He moved back over to his makeshift cot and settled down, laying back and clasping his hands behind his head. Pete shrugged. 'Just out of curiosity, though, why do you ask?' 'Well, haven't you noticed that you're teetering around instead of your usual ambling?' Mat stopped dead and for the first time took a real account of his surroundings. The cabin he shared with Pete was small and unfamiliar, nothing at all like the make-believe office they shared in the make-believe RPW corporate building. Two planks were attached to the wall and covered with tatterdemalion blankets as makeshift beds, and the desk he had run to check was really just a small table with charts of navigation spread across it. The only other object of note was the huge wooden steering wheel hanging on the back of the door, obviously a relic plundered off some ship from an era long past. It was on this apparition that Mat settled his panicked gaze. 'YOU'VE FORCED ME ONTO A PIRATE SHIP!?!' Mat sprang from the bed and fell on his face, his sea-legs utterly useless now that he knew he needed them. The fall did nothing to diminish his fury, and, looking up from the floor like a groundhog disturbed from its hole, he bared his teeth and prepared to scream again. 'I'LL KILL Y--' 'WE'RE NOT ON A PIRATE SHIP!' Pete interrupted at the top of his lungs, pulling back a fist in case Mat took going squirrelly to the next level. Mat's mouth worked soundlessly, then he firmed it to a thin line and got to his knees, spreading his arms out to help him maintain balance. 'I wouldn't do that to you,' Pete said in a quiet voice, trying to mollify his friend. 'Not with your raving hatred of pirates. That would just be cruel. And we both know that I am not cruel,' Pete finished with what could only be described as a cruel smile. Mat was not convinced. 'Then why are you smiling?' 'Because I know something you do not know . . .' 'What's that?' 'I am not left-han--' The words degenerated into a ridiculous fit of coughing as Pete realized his blunder and tried to pretend it never happened. Mat's face took on a light of sick gratification as he crossed his arms over his chest and waited. Finally the snorting died and Pete pounded a fist against his chest, shaking his head at the violence of his lungs' outburst. 'You'll see,' he amended sheepishly. 'Must we butcher the script so?' 'Aye, we must.' Just then a clamour of outraged and terrified shouts came to them from above. Mat glared at Pete; Pete grinned at Mat. 'We're not on a pirate ship, you tool,' Pete exclaimed jubilantly. 'We're being attacked by a pirate ship!' 'You've gotta be fucking kidding me,' Mat moaned, then stumbled six times as he tried to follow Pete out the door and up the stairs to the main deck. The sun was at its zenith when they popped up on deck in the middle of a quaint little sailing vessel, and sure enough, bow to stern with them was a much larger ship touting a menacing black sail. Underneath the standard skull and crossbones in gilded gold script were the words 'Oh yeah, me bitches, we be pirates!' just to avoid any potential confusion. Mat fell to his knees sobbing as rogues and swashbucklers galore flooded onto the ship. The crew of the ship, which from here on out will be called the RPW ship, screamed and slapped their hands against their cheeks and did all kinds of things that scared people do in the movies when they need to look like scared people. One of them, a sorry little excuse for a Boda-look-alike, was actually cowering behind the skirts of a hot little number clutching a teddy bear. Towards this match made in schmoopie heaven strutted a grungy looking pirate with scraggly black hair and a demeanour that simply oozed . . . oozed . . . whatever the opposite of confidence is, this guy oozed it like Magnum Wolf oozed ass. 'Hi there,' Scraggly said to the lass, ignoring the bitch hiding behind her. He ran a hand through his hair and coughed, then did a bunch of other shy motions I can't be bothered to describe. 'I'm new to this stuff, and, uh, yeah, heh.' His eyes moved back and forth like those of a trapped animal. Struggling for some way to keep her attention, he blurted, 'Wanna fuck?' The chick blinked and took a step back, forcing a pained squeal from the man hiding in her dress. Scraggly quickly recovered, though. 'That is to say, uh, what, yeah, um . . .' Having already proven himself a casanova with his words, he did the same with his actions, fumbling a hand out towards her bosom and palming her left tit. His tongue lolled out of his mouth, which had reverted back to its natural state of being: an idiot's leer. Further down the deck of the RPW ship, Mat cried out, 'I knew pirates would bring stupidity to our shows! What kind of arrival is this!? I just knew it!' He didn't care that it was out of place, and nor do I. 'Eeep! Somebody do something!' The girl with two leeches attached to her begged, but no one moved to help. Not from the RPW ship, at least. Then, unbeknownst to them but becoming beknownst to us, a hero emerged on the deck of the pirate ship, a masked man completely clad in black. Descending with the intangible brilliance of a rainbow he swooped onto the RPW deck right behind the scraggly pirate, and in one swift mootion snapped his neck and pitched him overboard. There was no splash, and predictably, no one mourned the loss. The girl with the bear opened her mouth to speak, but no words were forthcoming. 'Begging your pardon, m'lady,' the Man in Black intoned with a bow, his mysteriously accented voice rich and refined. He took her hand in one of his and laced it with a gentle peck as he straightened. 'Good crew are so hard to come by these days, don't you think?' Blushing and fluttering her eyelashes, she was about to respond when that Boda-ish troll leapt up from out of nowhere and planted a big wet one right on her lips. She recoiled in horror, naturally. 'Yesh,' the troll cried in celebration, 'I saved you from that horrible groping man! I am the absolute picture of perfection and courage and hon--' The words were mercifully cut short as he, too, had his neck nonchalantly snapped by the Man in Black. The head ripped clean off the neck, but strangely, didn't fall to the ground. 'I must return to whence I came,' said the head in a monstrous voice that was clearly far too tough to belong to the body it had just abandoned, then it flew off across the sea, undoubtedly searching for a gigantic headless weed-smoking crybaby. Gasping for air, the girl finally gathered enough to speak. 'You killed one of your own men . . .' Her words were belaboured, as if she was trying to puzzle something out as she spoke. 'I myself am often surprised by life's little quirks,' the Man in Black offered with a smile. 'He came here on the good ship Newbie just as I have, but that is as far as the similarity extends. Where he so pathetically failed to make an impressive impact and doomed himself to such an end, I will storm into your lives and take over, leading your now directionless ship to victory!' He then leaned in and whispered for only her to hear (and for you, too, whoever's reading this), 'My victory, that is.' 'What the heck are you on?' Her question was answered by the broad darkness of his back as he turned it to her and addressed the people who were even now being looted by his mates. 'Greetings to the people of the good ship RPW. Before you get alarmed, let me assure you, there is no reason to get alarmed.' He paused for a moment to seek out any possible alarm, then continued good-naturedly once he was satisfied there was none. 'To me you are all boobs, but so long as you're not cancerous or full of silicone, you shan't be lopped off and cast into the sea of talentless despair. Those who deserved such a fate have met it, and as long as there is . . . as long as . . . as there is . . . is . . . dear god, what is that slurping noise?' The whole ship paused to listen, and sure enough, they heard the undeniable sound of someone sucking. The Man in Black turned back to the woman and the decapitated body, and sprawled out on the deck lapping up the blood spilling forth from the body was a deranged looking idiot seemingly bent on tarnishing the good name of masked men and legitimate heels everywhere. Worse than that, the idiot, or better yet, 'el idioso', was relentlessly singing his own praises as he did this ridiculous thing. With one hand on the foil at his belt, the Man in Black approached this mad scene. 'Excuse me, sir,' the Man in Black beckoned, to which El Idioso groaned unintelligibly. 'You wouldn't happen to be a vampire by any chance, would you?' 'Grrnnnnnnhh,' came the response, followed by, 'I not undead, I R BAD GUY~! Grrnnnnnnhh!' 'Not a vampire, you say? Good.' There was a blinding flash as the foil was released from its scabbard, then a miserable gurgling as it plunged straight through El Idioso's torso. 'That should kill you, then,' the Man in Black said matter-of-factly as he pulled the blade out and wiped the blood off on the second lolling tongue he'd had the ill luck of happening upon today. 'Now where was I?' He tapped the point of his sword into El Idioso's eye as another man would tap his chin. 'Ah yes, the matter of why I'm here. As the more astute of you might have guessed, I am a pirate.' Mat's wailing grew louder in the background, but no one cared beyond using the fact to pester him. 'A rather good one by the estimation of my past victims, of which there are many. Cutting to the chase, I am here to liberate you of your gold, and so long as none of you stand in my way, my seizure of it will be the height of that discomfort.' He didn't actually do any of the collecting himself, but sent his lackeys around to do it instead. There was some resistance, but the mob squashed it rather easily. The crew and passengers of the RPW ship watched with a look of collective confusion as the pirates piled their booty directly in the center of the deck. After it was all collected, the two factions stood staring at one another for what seemed an eternity. 'Have none of you ever been waylaid by pirates before?' The Man in Black asked with palpable incredulity. 'Very well, I suppose I'll explain,' he sighed. 'It is a horrible misrepresentation of fact by storytellers that we pirates abandon the most valuable treasure a ship has to offer. I would truly feel a fool if I were to leave you all here knowing th--' A foursome of women march to the center of the deck with their hands on their hips and their panties obviously in a bunch. They glare at the Man in Black with looks that are undoubtedly intended to intimidate, but typically succeed only in assuring those it is levelled at that their PMS is peaking. 'You are a fool to think we would allow you to take us with you, 'pirate',' one of them sneered. 'We're too good to sleep with you,' the second added, then the third chimed in with, 'yeah, bitch,' followed by the fourth with 'we're lesbians.' All of them nodded defiantly as one, determined not to be abducted and made into rape victims. 'Uh, ladies? I don't mean to crush your sweet illusions,' the Man in Black laughed, his voice ripe with amusement, 'but I used the words 'valuable' and 'treasure', not 'bitchy' and 'pseudo-evil idiots'.' The girls tried to get uppity, but he paid them no more mind, and so their evil scowling and posturing was rendered useless. 'The treasure I'm talking about, of course,' he told the rest of those gathered, 'is the ship.' Gasps all around. 'It's mine now.' The Man in Black stroked his sword, obviously hoping it would need to be used. 'Hopefully, you can save me the time and yourself the indignity of having to walk the plank by simply throwing yourself overboard and swimming for shore. Do that now, and I promise no harm will come to you. Do it not, and I'll be forced to tie your hands and . . . Hullo. What have we here?' What he had before him now was a serious looking man in a flowing brown robe. His head was bald and painted, and the robe concealed every other inch of him, including his hands, which were tucked into the opposite sleeves and hung unthreateningly in front of his stomach. His intent with the Man in Black did not bode of good things. 'Ahoy, matey,' the Man in Black greeted warmly. 'You're here to play the part of hero, are you?' The bald man nodded. 'Might I have your name, then?' The bald man stared. 'A pleasure to meet you, nobody,' the Man in Black extended his hand and shook the air when the bald man refused to accept. 'I can't help but notice that you haven't a sword on you. How are you proposing to defeat me, hm?' The bald man lowered himself into what the writer believes is referred to as the horse stance, whereby the martial artist essentially squats down as if taking a crap out of doors. He doesn't say anything, but just nods as if to invite whatever violence the Man in Black might muster. 'What's this?' The Man in Black asks, even more amused by this than the attempted show of force by the four bitches. 'You do realize that I could up and stab you through the heart right now, don't you?' The only response is another nod. 'Wait a second . . . You're one of those monks I'm always reading about, aren't you? Deadly killers who've taken a vow of silence or some such rubbish, right?' Another nod, though this one was grudging. 'Very well, I await your attack.' Both men stand there staring at each other for a good ten seconds, the monk in his horse stance and the Man in Black leaning on his sword. 'Do you seek to defeat me by boring me to sleep? I assure you it will not work . . . I spent the last five years of my life weaning myself off of sleep for just such an occasion.' Springing forth with what promised to begin a rousing spectacle of skill in fighting and military prowess, a melee which would cover the whole ship thrice and allow both men to show off their various abilities, the monk tried to bitchslap the Man in Black. Unfortunately for the monk, though, the Man in Black was still awake, and was still holding his sword. Without pomp or ceremony by either me or him, the Man in Black stabbed the monk in the stomach with the sword before he could get within arm's reach. The monk's face sagged and he stared down at the thin blade coming out of his stomach with obvious disbelief. His mouth dropped open as he brought his gaze back up to meet the Man in Black's, and for the first time in who knows how long, he spoke, his voice bewildered. 'Who are you?' 'No one of consequence.' 'I must know,' the monk pleaded, dropping down to his knees. 'Get used to disappointment.' Satisfied, the Man in Black withdrew the blade and turned to address the masses once again. 'As for the rest of you, get used to swimming. Or don't. I really don't care, just so long as you get off my ship.' 'Wait.' It was the monk again. 'You're not very good at this vow of silence thing, you know.' 'I have a proposition for you . . .' 'I am always listening, though I would have your name first.' 'Jason,' the monk said. 'I'm Jason. And I know where you could make a lot of money . . .' This, at least, seemed to pique the Man in Black's interest. 'Pray tell, how would I do that? And how much work is involved? I wouldn't have to kill too many people, would I?' 'No killing.' The Man in Black's face darkened a little, but the monk rolled on with the good news before his opinion could sour. 'You'd only have to work a couple of hours once per week, and even less sometimes. It's how I make my living, now that I'm not with the cloth anymore.' 'I fear I don't believe you, Jason. What magical profession is this that you speak of?' Despite his disbelief, the Man in Black began helping the former monk to his feet. 'Wrestling. Professional wrestling. I think you'll do fine already, but I could train you in what you don't know.' 'Exactly how much money is involved in this professional wrestling?' Jason leaned in and whispered something in the Man in Black's ear, something good judging by the lightening of his face and the shit-eating grin that appeared there. 'Really?' The monk nodded, so the Man in Black nodded back, cheerful as always. 'Alright, Jason. I've never had a valet before, but we'll try it if you like.' The Man in Black clapped a hand down on Jason's back. 'Get these people off of my new ship, mop the decks, then come tell me more about this wrestling of yours. It sounds rather intriguing, though I can make no promises. I'll most likely kill you in the morning.' So it was that the Man in Black came aboard RPW, and so it was that Mat was made to walk the plank, crying all the way.
