All Of The Truth

24 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Being young allowed us the pleasures of enjoying our youth. None of us were popular or rich, but we were untapped spirits. People always shot us sidelong stares as we walked down the street, because of the choices we made in hair and dress. Seth and I only smiled and shrugged it off, as the black sport coats walked past us, noting our faded jeans and black tee shirts. I didn’t understand the reason we weren’t accepted. I was indifferent towards them, for the fact that getting outraged at their misjudging would only cause a scene. These were the streets of Newport Beach, replace one suit with another and we would get the same reaction, this is how I learned the lesson that everyone was alone. We were a language in the dinner room that represented the lights dimmed. There were moments that I wished we were within the norms of this secret society. Seconds in fleeing moments that one of them would look at me, without the dreary eyes of their elders, but with the eyes of their heart and see me for the person that I was and perhaps offer me a place to call home. A thought that came only in dreams to disappear as eyes were opened wide. Reality slapped me in the face as Seth tugged on the arm of my shirt. In all my years of living in Newport Beach, never an invitation to a party was sent to my direction. Harmonic gatherings past over head faster than jets, my chance to become one of the in crowd never was appointed. At this instance it was all too painfully clear. We shouldn’t have been here; these people’s soul didn’t speak the poet words that we longed to hear. These people didn’t care if we were thrown from the face of the Earth, they would never notice. Conformity was on Seth’s brain that night though; he was nineteen and never been to a party in Newport. Either had I, but I had also never experienced love, it was something I wondered about a lot of the time, but then it slowly faded back into the inner most depths of my soul. Depths that no one could reach unless the wrinkled notebook fell from the sky and even then the twisted means of the words could never be understood; I knew I was better than these posers. Sometimes I wondered the reasons this place lolled itself into the security it had. That night exposed us to the truth about Newport Beach, reassuring the belief that we didn’t belong. In the first instances Seth and I stepped into the palace of Princess Holly, we saw the horror of the things these people called pleasure. White powder floating up their noses into their bloodstreams and tainted drink that would touch their lips and consume their being. Seth looked to me for support and smiled when my approving nod sent him into motion. His presence had been requested at the whim of Tara, the blonde he had a crush on since he was seven. Something told him that he had a chance and maybe he did, the answers weren’t clear to me and I wasn’t resolved on going to look for them. I heard the faded footfalls of Seth as he walked away to go look for his dream. Not much mattered to me at that moment, this was a world that had never exposed itself to me and I was in a state of disgust. It took only a few moments to get past it, to rip out a few pages of the notebook. I had a beer in my hand and it was passing down my throat smoothly. A good time wasn’t that big of a rip on my soul I assured myself and walked outside down to the beach. Sand scratched and tickled my feet all at the same instant. The grit felt like my soul, but it was pushed to the side. “Hello.” It sounded like a female’s voice and I turned on my heel. She stood in front of me, her raven hair hanging down to her shoulders. The pale silver light shone upon her face with angelic effects, my jaw was unhinged at that moment and I just wanted to disappear. “You aren’t from around here are you?” She was just like the rest of them, a realization that took me no time to settle on. “Yes, I’m Matt.” She smiled and it jeered me, I was the superhuman and looked at everything impassively. Nothing ever hit me as hard as her smile. “Well Matt…most people call me Summer. Do you want to take a walk?” “Yes.” Burdens were lifting from me and that was my voice rising. I needed a record of my failure so I could document our love.
