The Underdark: Prelude

5 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/5/04 The Underworld can be a dark and scary place to outsiders, with its mix of murder, theft, and drugs. It’s a black mark on the shining world that society has attempted to create around it. Society lashes out at it, for they can’t understand it – they can’t comprehend the dealings that happen in the dark corners of allies and the shade of the sewers. Yet, without understanding it they look to stop it, hoping that they might be able to shield the future from the horrors that happen under the moonlight. Out of fear they come into a world masked in deception, only to turn and run. They believe that they are doing the just thing, that they are going to save the souls of the inhabitants of the Underdark, but they fail to realise that we have our justice system. All societies do, it’s the only way to keep order – the Underworld’s justice just happens to be a little different. To openly commit murder or wage war on the streets, these are the things that bring the merciless justice of the streets. Not to say that you can’t stick a dagger in your enemies back during a bigger battle, or the confines of a silent ally – this is looked upon in favour, even applauded. Station is the way to avoid chaos, but there’s no real way to escape the chaos. The bloodshed that happens almost daily, it’s just the norm. Everyone is just on the strings on the eight members of the counsel, each a puppet in the master plans of those who they would fight for, hoping beyond all hope that perhaps they might just move up an inch and be a little closer to the top of the ladder. Station is the paradox, having power within the hunger for more power. It is gained through treachery and invites treachery against those who gain it. Those most powerful in the Underworld, watch over their shoulders, defending against the would be daggers looking for their backs. Their deaths usually come from the front. -Matthew Joseph Jackson
