Before Today #01 : After-School Activities

8 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/11/03 As he dropped down into his dominant brown leather chair, Lawrence Wolfenden exhaled audibly. It was now just after 3 in the afternoon, and this was the first time he had been off his feet since stepping out of his Mercedes at 7:41 that morning. With brown hair, greying at the sides, and a pin-striped shirt broken up by braces, he looked like a real-life Gordon Gecko... And he'd the same workload. But while the busy floor that he commanded continued to buzz through his glass door, the family man in Lawrence was now given the time of day. Monday to Friday, without fail, and even if he had to blow-off a meeting, he'd phone his children, Karina and Max, after school. It was all he had of them during his working hours, as he refused to put their photos on his desk instead... If he had nothing around to remind him of them, he HAD to call them to hear their voices, even though their tone was usually a mix of hurried and intruded upon. Hitting speed dial #2, Lawrence swung his feet up onto the uncluttered, mahogany desk and awaited an answer from Karina's cell phone. Tapping his fingers along with the rings, he checked his watch just as Karina answered. 'Hi, Dad,' was her chirpy reply, as if she'd been keeping a close eye... Or ear... On her phone for the last few minutes, knowing the call was coming. That's why he called. If he had a choice each week between hearing his kids in the afternoons, or getting his cheque, the latter wouldn't stand a chance. He'd sacrificed a lot for his work, his career, and he felt guilty about it. This... This just seemed to appease him a little. 'Good day at school?' he enquired, as he did every day, and knowing what answer he'd get back. 'Was okay,' his daughter casually dismissed, knowing far better and much more entertaining ways to spend the day. 'Now heading off to practise...' A lie. 'Do you need picking up from Lacrosse?' Lawrence asked, but she wouldn't need to be picked up... Especially from somewhere she wouldn't be. Karina paused for the briefest of seconds as she thought of an answer. She may be as stubborn as an Ox most days, and annoyingly independent if you're one of her parents while she was still at a young age, but one thing she never liked doing was lying to them. Every time she duped them and sneaked away to a place they'd never approve of, she felt mildly sick. They'd given her so much, it was almost a betrayal... But the old logic of 'But they'd only say no' applied here. 'Er... No, I'll be alright,' she answered, trying to sound as natural as possible, 'I'll probably grab a lift home with Serendipity.' That would be Serendipity, her fictional Lacrosse team mate... Invented when 'Jane' or 'Sarah' sounded a little too easy and false... Who'd doubt a Serendipity? 'Okay, well I'll hopefully see you tonight,' Lawrence sincerely and warmly replied, knowing that his daughter would want to get off now and do her own stuff. 'Take care.' 'Will do,' was Karina's casual response, not really appreciating how much he meant it. Strangely for a parent, Lawrence wouldn't tell Karina he loved her over the phone, he felt it lacked meaning when carried down miles upon miles of phone cables, or rebounded off masts... It was something to be said face to face. Karina, well, she had just passed the age at which she'd say such things to a parent in hearing range of anyone she might know. Looking at Karina, one word you could probably use was 'plain'... Not in an insulting way, but not altogether a compliment. There's a time in someone's life when they blossom, come out of their shell, and the 15 year old Karina Wolfenden was getting towards the end of the road to that moment... But wasn't quite there yet. Her face was make-up free, freckles crossed the bridge of her nose, and her un-startling sandy blonde hair was cut short and tidy, almost boyish. She was the mannequin for what would soon be a fashion display for independence. Someone on their way to Lacrosse practice would likely be expected to be surrounded by a little greenery, and especially some kind of grassy playing surface... But Karina was in an all together more urban, with the afternoon sky obscured sporadically by the towers that bordered her walk. To her family, she was going through the motions, practicing a sport she only chose as a falsehood due to her loathing for it... ...While in reality, she was entering Albany's 'Court Club', where her newest passion was being fostered. Up the steps she skipped, before the doors were opened for her by a polite man who was then leaving, sports bag held over his shoulder. Along the deep red carpet Karina walked, enjoying the smell she always associated with this part of the building, it's scent being linked to enjoyment by her brain. Of course, to the club, her father - Lawrence Wolfenden - supported Karina fully in her membership here... After all, his signature was emblazoned on her membership forms. 'Er... Miss Wolfenden?' enquired one of the receptionists, who had either been waiting for her specifically, or had a good memory for members' names. Karina looked worried... Had they needed to get in contact and phoned her home, getting through to her mother who would never approve? Or worse, Max, who's hang it over her head like the Sword of Damocles, using the knowledge to bargain with. 'Yes...' was her sheepish reply. But, she needn't of worried. 'Kickboxing classes are in Studio 2 this week,' the receptionist politely informer her, pointing behind Karina, 'Just up the stairs there.' Hardly Lacrosse... Much more fun.
