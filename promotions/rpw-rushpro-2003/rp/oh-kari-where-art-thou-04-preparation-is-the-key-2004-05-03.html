Oh Kari, Where Art Thou? #04 : Preparation Is The Key.

3 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/05/04 Yannick Reeves was a hometown boy. Born and raised in Charlotte, North Carolina to a French mother and American father, he had grown up with sport as a stable in his life. The Hornets were the team to cheer on through his early teens. The Panthers then emerged and gave him another team to follow, and another shirt to buy. It was a bonus then, that his mother's homeland were beginning their dominance of world football. But while sports were fine to keep him fit, and to pass a few hours by tossing a ball about, wrestling was his real passion. He was mocked by his father for watching a 'fake' sport. His mother hoped he'd 'grow out' of it. Even his friends gradually stopped watching, and ribbed him about his love of sweaty men in tights. But Yannick didn't need them, he had Ric Flair. He couldn't care less about hearing 'Real American', and he refused to pop for a single 'Suck It!' Yannick only cared for the WHOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!'s, and the sick slap of chopping hand against raw chest. So with RPW's appearance on the Carolina Indy scene, Yannick had found the theatre for his dreams. After coming close to surrender in his seemingly unwinable war to start a career, Yannick shed the extra pounds he was carrying, and got himself back into shape. 5 months later, he was soon to take the first step into a new world. Despite the fact his deeply religious grandmother was aghast that he'd do such a thing on a Sunday, his try-out match had been arranged for Adrenaline. He didn't care if only a handful of apathetic fans were there to watch him perform a good hour before the bell. They could unleash a taunting barrage of profanity his way, and he wouldn't blink an eye. He knew the people who mattered would be watching. So, with 3 days to go, Yannick had booked the weekend off work and set about his preparations. Just the thought of the match would have caused the hairs on the back of his neck to stand up, were they not saturated by, and matted down with, sweat... Just like every other hair on his body. It wouldn't just be a match, it would be like losing his virginity... But better. With perspiration washing salty waves across his frantic body, Yannick tested his body to it's limits. He wasn't going to 'blow up' in his first real match. It was the only way he knew. You want something badly, you train with all you have, right? Surely that was how any RPW star would be preparing for a match, wouldn't it? At that very second, Gideon Poe must have been pummelling a punch--bag as if it represented all that was wrong in the corrupt world, huh? Everyone has their own ways of preparing body and mind. Yannick Reeves has his, and Karina Wolfenden has hers. To say they were alike would be a blatant lie, and to say they were worlds apart would be an understatement. On Sunday, they'd be at the same show, but until then... There was no musty smell, with more than a hint of dried sweat. No dusty air tickling throats and appearing as a mist in the harsh sunlight... And certainly no sense of urgency. 'And up next,' yelled a young, teasing voice through the sharp air. 'The one they call the K-WOLF!!!' There was off piste, their was freestyle, and then there was a huge ramp, painstakingly formed out of the snow until fingers went numb through their gloves. For the young and carefree, a little effort was worth it just to get things right. So, under a topaz blue sky that wasn't blemished by the merest formation of a cloud, those who didn't have to worry about the hustle and bustle of 9-5 living could play. 'C'mon Kari!' a more supportive, female voice called out to her through a cultured French accent. While tourists hit the mundane slopes of France and Switzerland, and their own parents headed to the more exclusive resorts in Austria, the children chose to play in Liechtenstein... For a very simple reason. 'I haven't been there yet,' was the widely accepted explanation. What other reason did they need? 'Better have your American Express in those pockets, K,' an accompanying, Germanic male voice boomed, heavy with Bavarian gruff. 'I'm in training for Oktoberfest!' Simple rules; The best gets the Kudos. The worst gets the beers. No better code of honour in life. Gathered around the intimidating, frozen launch pad they stood, waiting to see what their nomadic American friend could pull off. 'Keep it coming,' challenged the K-Wolf from the top of the steep slope, while she motioned them yapping away with her left hand. While the black and white, padded layers disguised her stream-lined body, her burning emerald eyes still acted as beacons for all who could see her... Even when housed behind goggles. 'Get the cameras ready, amateurs!' With a hopping twist of her hips, her board began it's motion. Mocking the mere principles of friction and all who obeyed it, Karina felt her '3rd foot' shovel through the loose top snow, while the solid layer beneath gave it a supreme surface to exploit. The international cast of 4 began to hush, carefully observing the K-Wolf's acceleration cumulate into a fast, almost reckless pace. 'Can you say train-wreck?' a dismissive, American voice chimed in with, only seeing pure speed and minimal control. 'You'd be surprised,' the Gallic female voice countered, 'she could turn landing on her head into a top-drawer trick... Just wait and see.' What was the worst that could happen? A few broken bones? Been there, done that, worn the sling. Feeling as if the world was sliding out from beneath her, Karina hit the ramp, feeling heavier as every inch of steep ice was passed. Then, with the swirling, icy wind trying to blow her back down to earth, the K-Wolf was launched into the bitter air, her body showing a natural grace in flight that could not be learned. Many people were confused when Karina tried to explain why she spent every Sunday night in Carolina, wrestling for RPW... And if she could only pause time mid-jump, it would be so much easier. Men and women were never meant to fly... Ask Icarus for proof... But there are those amongst the species who are more than willing to gamble their immediate health for the greater prize - exhilaration. Karina Wolfenden didn't want to drive a saloon, so she bought her 'baby', her beloved Ducati 748. She didn't want to jog, so she spent a summer in Lyon and the outskirts of Paris, using the multi-level urban landscapes as Free Running tracks. She didn't want to go Skiing with the rest of her family, so she packed her board and travelled the world. Fast, risky, exciting, and sometimes painful... Wrestling is just another pastime. Understand now? 'Wow,' exclaimed the previously doubting German as the K-Wolf's body spun like a Tornado through the air, all but ripping the snow from the slope and into her slipstream. With no inhibitions, Karina let out a cry of sheer pleasure, as if the air her body was pummelling from it's path was massaging every pleasure centre on her body. She was the girl who could fly, sending gravity home early so she could walk on wind. Pushing her muscles' elasticity to the limit, Wolfenden curled her legs up behind her, almost sitting on the bottom of her board to execute a Flying Squirrel... ...Then reaching her gloved hands back, clamping fingers to edge, and skinning the trick. The awe-inspiring peak in the distance was of little consequence, as was the snow, the pure sky, and the cheering friends. There could have been dusty streets and blossoming, summer-awaiting trees and it wouldn't have mattered. The snow could have been asphalt, and the old but favoured board a Ducati Superbike. Or, the ramp could have been a turnbuckle, and the pleasure-seeking friends the loyal RPW fans. Same girl. Same pursuit. Same reason. 'You didn't say she could do that...' One rotation became two, became two and a half, before the K-Wolf touched down, having to ignore the false landing that the soft, surface snow tried to fool her senses with. Only when she could feel the hard, compacted ice below did her near-orgasmic vocal salute cease, replaced by a much more reserved punch of the air. Nailed it. The smile bridging her ears seemed to strain her pale pink lips, the body's physiology inadequate to relay such pleasure. Momentum was absorbed into the snow, and Kari came to a halt, before just dropping back into the snow, arms stretched out either side. 'Beat...' she sighed, the exhilaration having drained her, '...THAT!' Gloves that combated the chilling air muffled the claps, but the hooting and verbal praise flooded her ice-numbed ears. She'd gotten the kudos, someone else would get the beers. 'I accept the challenge,' the domineering, Germanic voice replied. Not even sitting up, a flushed K-Wolf just gestured with a dismissive right hand while her competitive, Bavarian friend got into position. Karina didn't even need to see what was happening. She heard the edge of Carsten's board slicing through the iced surface, calculated his speed from the sounds. Had Kari sat up and watched on through her wide, emerald eyes, she'd have seen the man begin a twisting flip almost before his board had even left the ramp. A few pitchers of beer wasn't going to dent the wallets of any of the gathered crowd, but their pride was worth more than a whole brewery. His trick was well executed, and everything about it screamed 'textbook'... That was the problem. It was learned, it was studied, but it wasn't instinctive. It wasn't natural. The flip was completed at the millisecond it needed to be, and the twist was faultless. All he had to do was land. Knowing where abouts he was in the chilled air, Karina finally sat up, and looked right at her Bavarian rival. But where as she was taking speed and motion, he was staring at nothing but the white snow below... Even holding off on blinking until he touched down. Carsten wanted to see the landing, not feel it, and seeing that, the K-Wolf knew she need not hunt in her rucksack for her wallet. 'Oh yea-' he went to boast as the nose of his board touched down, only for his focus to then evaporate away. Rather than feel the different textures of the snow, and not bothering with the physics of their differing frictions and speeds, Carsten was sent head-over-heels, rolling down the remainder of the slope while showing off his vast vocabulary of expletives. Karina knew little German, but she'd known Carsten long enough to translate all but one of his profanities. Immediately, the others descended on their fallen friend, and after seeing he was perfectly okay the playful ribbing began. Smiling through it all, Carsten looked to Kari, who shot a playful smile back, exclaimated by a wink. 'Fine, I'm buying,' his gruff voice grunted, before breaking out into a chuckle that was instantly matched by Karina. Springing up from the snow, where her body print was left for others to admire, Kari offered her hand to Carsten, helping pull the German from the snow. 'Next time,' he promised, not needing to add any more details. '...You WON'T end up flat on your face in the powder?' Kari cheekily shot back as the pair followed the others around the ramp and towards their favoured drinking hole. 'Americans,' he scoffed. 'Oh, Carst,' a head-shaking Kari quipped back, patting a gloved hand on his shoulder. 'You of all people should know I belong only to the world.' From inside the glorified shed that housed the nearest bar, it looked a scorcher outside. Looking up at the clear sky and blinding sun, it would be so easy to forget the frozen ground and feet of snow... Well, easy to forget if that wasn't why you were there. 'I hope you realise that's the last of my money gone,' groaned Carsten as he returned to the group's table with 2 pitchers of beer, 5 glasses, and one empty wallet. 'Next time we go somewhere with cheaper drinks.' 'Why?' Manuela, the Gallic member of the circle chimed in. 'Getting ready to lose again already?' The few mocking, pitying groans for Carsten were replaced by warm, friendly laughter as he smirked back at them all, not appreciating being the butt of their jokes. 'I blame the snow,' he continued to protest, as he had most of the way to the bar. 'You lot roughed it up... I go first next time.' The 2 Americans, Karina and Gail, shook their heads in unoffended disbelief at his continued excuses, while Manuela rubbed her thumb and index together, borrowing Tarantino's 'World's smallest violin' line to mock her Bavarian friend. Sitting beside Carsten, Alexandra, the quiet Italian of the group put her arm around him. 'I'm sure you'll all AMAZE us next time,' she assured him through her thick, Roman accent. 'Yeah,' Kari couldn't resist following up with. 'But try and spin more times in the air than you do in the snow.' 'Oh, ha ha,' Carsten groaned in reply, only for Karina to flash her tongue out at him, showing she was just playing. 'So, Kari,' Gail began, deciding it was only fair to take the heat off their defeated German friend for a while. 'Who're you fighting this weekend? The Hulkster? Stone Cold?' To her little group, Karina's wrestling 'career' was always going to be something to rib her about. Not with any spite or malice, but enough to force her bar-wards when the time arose. 'You're still doing that?' Carsten questioned, genuine surprise in his voice. 'I didn't think even at your prissiest that you had THAT much repressed anger in you.' 'Maybe she just has a thing for jocks?' Alexandra entered herself into the discussion with, motioning a macho-laded bicep flex. 'I think I can safely say,' Karina bluntly stated, 'that I'd rather not talk about it right now... or at least until I've got a few more of these down me.' Karina nodded down to her rapidly empting glass. 'Things are getting wayyy too weird there, and they're trying to make it like... Well, the W word.' 'Work?' Carsten queried right away, to a souring moan from all around him. 'Don't say that word to us!' Gail playfully roared, hammering her hands down onto the table while Alexandra punched him half-heartedly on the bicep. 'It is the word of Satan.' So, yeah, work-shy would be a word used to describe them. The thought of being tied down to a job, 9-5, limited holidays, daily timesheets... It scared them. It would be shackling them, clipping their wings. 'Only one way to save this,' Carsten then boldly announced, acting to save events. 'A toast!' As if they were all used to raising their glasses often, a regular feature of their gatherings, the 4 women thrust their right arms up alongside Carsten's. 'To lives of leisure,' he proudly proclaimed, before the multiple chinks of glass blended into one ringing noise. 'To lives of leisure!' they repeated after him, then downed what was left in their glasses, leaving a coating of foam all the way down the sides. With the bubbles still being felt on Karina's tongue, Yannick Reeves collapsed onto the tatty carpet, pouring a cascade of water into his mouth. He swallowed as fast as he could, quenching his arid throat and beginning to replenish the scary amounts he'd sweated out. That which he couldn't force down spilt out from his mouth, running down his already drenched body, and onto the floor. Each breath was a gasp, and each movement had it's own ache. But it would be worth it. Rolling onto his front, Yannick left the makings of a sweat-stamp on the thin, beige carpet, before placing his heavy and throbbing head on the one un-saturated towel in the room. On his makeshift pillow, Yannick closed his eyes, ignoring the salty sting, and for the first time in hours, he relaxed... And felt guilty about it. Maybe they'd pass one another at Adrenaline, possibly exchange a few pleasantries. But one thing would be for sure: Location was the only thing they'd have in common.
