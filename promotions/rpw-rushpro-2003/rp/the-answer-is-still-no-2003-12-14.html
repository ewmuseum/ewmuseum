The Answer Is Still No

14 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/14/03 “The sun will come out tomorrow.” That’s what that little red headed orphan Annie used to sing all the time. I remember I used to sing that song to myself all the time when I was younger in hopes that everything would change and I would live the fairy tale life. However now I’m twenty two and I realize that the sun may come out, but it does not mean things will get better. I’ve seen rain fall on a sunny day, and snow fall when the sun is brighter than ever. Just like any other television character, Annie lived the perfect life, the life I knew I’d never have. But I suppose I should make the best of what I have. Two children who may not be blood related to me, but loved me as if I was their actual mother. Not to mention a successful career as a professional wrestler, and a best friend I knew I could count on no matter what. Things would get better in a matter of time. I would just have to wait and see. Too bad though it more than likely still wouldn’t bring Chris back to my heart. It seemed memories of him lingered all over the place. Now things would only be worse that I would have to see him each week, not acting like himself, or at least the Chris I grew to know and love. Strangely enough I wasn’t worried about my feelings for Chris. Instead, I was worried about how Evan and Christa’s would react to the change in Chris. Ever since the two of them found out that Chris and I were once again working for the same company all they have been asking for is to visit him. But how on earth do I explain that visiting Chris would not be a good idea since he would probably just break their little hearts with his new attitude. It seemed I had some thinking to do. “Rain rain go away, come again another day.” Five year old Evan busted out into song as he took a look outside to see the rain falling from the sky, melting away the snow that remained on the ground. It seemed that his singing was the only thing keeping him occupied yet he seemed as happy as a peach with his song so I let him continue on with his song. “Tessa can we go visit Chris yet?” I looked over to my side and noticed eight year old Christa looking up at me with pleading blue eyes. “Yeah, can we?” Evan’s ears perked up at the mention of Chris’ name as he ended his song and quickly joined in on Christa’s cause. “No we can’t. Chris is very busy today.” Though I felt guilty dropping a complete and utter lie to them it had to be done to avoid the truth which would surely enough leave them in tears. Besides I’d rather lie and see them smile than tell them the truth and watch them cry several rivers worth of tears. “Than can we come with you on Sunday and visit him then?” Christa persisted with her stubbornness. It was unlikely she would stop until she got her way. However, this time she had to learn that not everything in life was going to go her way. “No we aren’t going to visit him on Sunday either. Chris is a very busy man now. He’s much too busy for us to visit.” At least my response was not an outright lie. After all, he was very busy, just not busy with what I would consider work though he made a living off of it before I came along. “We can’t even visit him for a second?” Christa whined in hope that it would annoy me into giving into her. However, I simply shook my head in a no response which caused her head to fall down as she walked away with a pout. I felt horrible keeping them away from Chris, but I wanted to keep them as far away from the evils of the world as I could. After all, had they not been through enough crap in the past year than most people go through in a lifetime? They would just have to live with the answer given and I would have to accept that kids would be kids no matter what.
