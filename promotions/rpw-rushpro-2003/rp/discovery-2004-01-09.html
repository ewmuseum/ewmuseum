Discovery

9 January 2004

Date: January 9th 2004 Usually, nobody is happy in a cemetery. It’s a place of grievance, misery and longing for something impossible. People go there to try and attain something… however, this afternoon, one girl comes to oppose the entire concept. A long black coat sweeps to her knees, hiding slender thighs that are poured into black knee-high platform boots. Her dyed purple hair swings about her face along with a pair of emerald green spherical earrings. Behind a set of wraparound violet sunglasses, a pair of aquamarine eyes stare down at a rectangular lump of marble. “Leonard Griffiths, 1962 – 2001.” She breathes the words in a tongue not her own. The mist rising from the dew-laden grass seems to take shape into the form of a male human. Staring down alongside the girl at the tombstone, the figure seems to drop to his knees. “This is who you were.” The girl whispers, reluctantly. Her eyes dart across curiously to the apparition, who is transfixed on the grave. She lays down a single violet in front of the stone and closes her eyes, bowing her head. “I was only just forty years old…” an ethereal voice sobs. “It was the night of Grandslam, the match before the main event. I was facing Jake Hall, that drunken bastard. He’d been on the bottle again before the match, saying it was for luck. And there was luck involved. Bad luck. As he went for a headscissor from the top rope, he was too intoxicated to land right, and then my neck…” The mist seems to sink back into the ground. Lumi’s eyes jerk wide open for a moment, then return to normal. Once again in a voice that did not belong to her, she speaks in sadness. “Take me away from here.” ~ Earlier that day… ~ Boston Public Library, morning. Lumi shuffled carefully around a stack of books left on the floor towards a set of computer terminals. Dressed rather conservatively in comparison to her usual shades of black, red and purple, she appeared very un-Lumi-like in a red skirt-suit combination and rectangular reading glasses. Moreover, her hair was clipped up at the back of her head, forming a little purple fountain. Indeed, she looked more like an executive than a wrestler. Parking herself into a chair, Lumi’s fingers began to dance over the keys as her eyes ran left and right over the monitor. “Now what exactly am I looking for…?” Lumi said to herself. She had gotten used to saying things as though thinking aloud… it was a convenient way of asking questions to her ghostly companion without making people think she belonged in a straitjacket. “Deaths in the ring. It was recent. Things haven’t changed much since I left, except the music has become almost intolerable and children are much more rude.” A voice replied from in her head. “It occurred here in America, but I can’t remember which state. Nor do I remember the name of my employers.” Lumi closed her eyes, slightly annoyed. As a means of communicating, she typed “What CAN you remember?” into the search engine, deleting it once the voice began again. “The event was high card, but not main event… I think the man I was facing was named Jack, or maybe Jake. But that’s all.” “Jake deaths wrestling” was typed into the search engine. Return. “Hmm.” The voice pondered. “Children killed imitating wrestling superstars, death by overdose, is this the death of wrestling, beloved wrestler dies… no, none of this is right. I died in the ring. I remember… being on the top rope, and then it all went black. “Top rope death wrestling Jake” was the next thing to go in. “No… no… n- wait, go back up. There!” A few clicks of the mouse saw a new page open up, one that made a shrill buzzing noise ring like a trapped fly in the back of Lumi’s head. A picture of a wrestler with short dark messy hair stirred up the rage of the ghost inside the girl. The page was an interview with a wrestler named Jake Hall. “That’s him! He’s the one!” the voice yelled accusingly, causing Lumi to clutch the sides of her head in pain and grunt. “Sorry… but… show me the rest. It’s him, it’s definitely him.” Peter Bradley: Hello everyone, I’m Closed Fist Wrestling’s Peter Bradley. I’ve been given permission to interview veteran wrestler Jake Hall today, so without further ado, the first question I ask is how are you doing? Jake Hall: I’m… pretty damn good. Yourself? PB: I’m fine, thanks. So, what are you doing these days since your retirement? JH: Well, to tell the truth, I’m not fully retired. I still do a show as a guest wrestler every now and then for indie promotions. I guess the urges never leave you. PB: Speaking of urges, how do you answer the rumors of you going back to the bottle? JH: Right in at the deep end, there, huh… well, I wanna scotch those rumors right now, if you’ll pardon the pun. PB: Heh. JH: Seriously though, I’ve not had a drink since New Year’s Eve. It’s been tough but with the help of my friends and my wife, I’ve made it through. I’ve let alcohol ruin my early years, I want to retire and die a sober man. PB: Of course, there was that one incident of Grandslam that obviously had an effect on your career. How was that for you? JH: Uhh… that’s a heavy one right there. Yeah, back then I was buzzing every night with whiskey ‘n’ vodka running through me. I had to be driven to the arena that night. Anyway, the guy I was fighting that night was good ol’ Masked Muscleman. I saw him before the match, we wished each other luck, I offered him a li’l to drink and he declined. So I said, aw, what the hell, more for me. I poured a glass, downed it… poured another, down it… finished up an entire bottle. So then we’re about to finish up and I was gonna do this move I picked up called Down To Earth, kinda like a headscissor from the top rope. PB: Uh-huh… we can give you a break if you need one. JH: No, I’m okay. Just takin’ me back to when it happened. Anyway, so I go for the move… I was drunk… we landed awkwardly and he’s down. I covered him and I was expecting a kickout just before the 3, but he’s staying down. I thought that was kinda screwy, but the show must go on, y’know? So I’m parading about and all, then I heard screaming and when I turned around, I saw blood leaking onto the canvas… and then I realized that I’d broken his neck so bad in the fall that I’d gone and (due to this site being for younger readers, we have to censor this part. Subscribe to read the full interview!) his neck right up. Then I just sunk to my knees and I said “Please God, don’t make me a murderer.” PB: Of course, Masked Muscleman, or Leonard Griffiths as he was known, was dead before they even got him out of the ring. JH: Yeah, that’s the worst part. It was just prolonging the whole thing, carting a corpse off to a hospital just so say he ain’t breathing no more. As soon as I was… physically able to speak, I remember going to my brass and saying “I can’t do this anymore.” And we went to the office and my contract was torn up right in front of my face. I just started crying right there and then… (at this point, Mr. Hall breaks into tears. I decided to give him 5 minutes alone.) “I don’t want to read anymore.” The voice in Lumi’s head growled. Unsure of how to react, Lumi simply closed the page. The buzzing inside grew louder and louder, forcing her to stumble out of her chair. As she fell, the monitor behind her exploded with a loud pop and smoke rose from the inside. The entire library jumped in shock as the relative silence was shattered like the glass of the screen. An attendant rushed over and knelt down beside her, trying to get a look at her face. “What happened!?” he screeched, his head fleeting between the girl and the destroyed monitor. “Uuh… uhhh… it made a really weird noise, some sort of… high pitched whining and then it exploded. I don’t understand what happened…” As the words left her red-painted lips, the other monitors began to make the same noise. The other users hurriedly abandoned their computers as one by one, each monitor exploded, sending shards of glass flying into the floor. The Mexican wave of booms startled everyone, now edging away from the other electrical equipment. “Some sort of freak power surge?” the librarian suggested, looking around as confused as everyone else. “Take me to the nearest cemetery.” The voice demanded ruthlessly. Scared and turned a distinct pale tone, Lumi scrambled to her feet and began to walk to the exit as fast as her feet could carry her. The only trace of her left behind was a pair of rectangular spectacles, held in the trembling hand of the librarian…
