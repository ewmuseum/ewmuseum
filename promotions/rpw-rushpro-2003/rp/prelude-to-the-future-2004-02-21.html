Prelude to the Future.

21 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/02/2004 Fighting through the copious amounts of bodies strewn along her floors and furniture from the night before Carys struggled to her feet, not without a few stumbling a long the way, but she had managed to make it. Her hands begged in front of her face for a light switch, there were enough of them in the room yet several stabs at them in the dark brought no results. The time couldn’t have been before 5 in the morning, less than an hours sleep since the party had ended; yet she felt fine. Seeing the intoxicated beings surrounding her all across the smooth terracotta tiled floor made her glad that she didn’t drink; a smile cast across her face, half from pity and half from smugness. Her next direction was the large silver fridge, thirst from whatever she had been doing taking over her as she gripped the metallic bar handle prior to yanking a bottle of orange from it’s bottom compartment. A gulp tore away her thirst as its contents leaked down her throat. Her hand went from the bottle when she had finished with its contents and towards the wrought iron handle of the door; she swung it open and allowed the violent weather a way into the top floor apartment. The wind blew strands of her hair along her face in no clear pattern, but a frustrated growl had Carys tying her hair behind her head in little time. She welcomed the soft patter of rain across her mostly bare skin as she stood onto the veranda. She coasted over to the railings with a slightly reproachable look, cast directly at the gargoyles that loomed upon the building on the opposite side of the street, as though they had been speaking to her a creak shouts out into the dark. Her body reacted to the creak with a sharp line of goose bumps dancing up her neck. What she saw, in her harsh reality were demons dancing upon the holiest of holy grounds, as their feet drug across the ground their bodies groaned. Of course, what actually existed and creaked were the cogs inside the clock tower, thudding against each other as they left their ever changing message for the world. A nasty voice reared in her head, something that sounded as distant as it was bitter and hateful, you shouldn’t sleep, not while those things are dancing about, mocking you as they still live. God only knows what kind of things they’ll do if you leave them be. ‘Shut up,’ she hissed to herself as her bare hands clutched onto the metal frame of the fence tightly. God, she hated being on her own, especially for more than five minutes, when her imagination grasped the better of herself she did herself absolutely no good at all. A soft sigh parted her lips as she fell down to her ass. Another one of her big fuck-ups in life, feeling to her that she couldn’t do anything right without help from other people, not always the ‘good crowd’ type either. Her own self loathing type of under confidence is what stopped her from going down to the ring by herself every week, it’s what keeps her in a wild lifestyle, and it’s not always a good thing, kids. Carys tried getting back to her feet, unfortunately her body didn’t feel the same need to get back to a vertical base that her mind did, and so a compromise was reached as she crawled along the wet, red flagstone that lined her veranda. Pain, the only thing she could feel as her knees drug over the painful flooring, a slight needle sensation just crept back unpleasantly, the feeling that’s associated with slight bleeding. The puddles, which were filling with the cold, eloquent moonlight, began to form on the cold stone floor as she neared the door that led back to her porch; the Moonlight so strong even to cast a firm shadow beside her. A shadow that made her turn her head in expectant fear, her legs finally rose from the ground with enough force to roll her into the apartment building. SLAM! The door was closed without a second concern given to the creaking of the doors that honestly looked, now, as if they could have been made seventy years ago. Carys couldn’t see out of the door, she thanked heavens for that small mercy as the only thing that stood between Carys’ forehead and the in fact brand new door was a violet strand of hair that was wet through. Dear, what the hell kind of good is this door going to do? Nothing can protect you from those… ‘Kiss my perfect ass, Dick wad.’ She didn’t like what the voice inside her head made her do, hiss like a python and be as angry as bulls charging down a matador. Her fit of anger was promptly interrupted as she saw the plum colored custom Les Paul Studio Plus electric guitar resting on the very edge of her crème colored futon sofa. Something to occupy her mind is exactly what she needed; that’s what she realized when she grasped the neck of the guitar, allowing her hand to cover its eleven strings that spanned up the silver neck. She snatched it away from the careless man who’d so lazily put it down when he tried to make a pass at her, she remembered that. From the look of the position he was laid in that the knee to the balls was still causing him a righteous amount of pain, even in the drunken stupor that lay on him like a blanket. She span in a circle in the body littered room, her eyes darted about as though she was collecting memories from the previous night. Upon the left wall was one firmly imprinted of Alyssa, her dear cousin, tease-dancing to the entire audience before taking her top off and running towards her drum set, that she was still lay at now, and beating away at the drums. As she faced the kitchen arena she saw Alotti snoring on top of the counter. Her head in her arms, and a whiskey bottle in her hands, she didn’t know the suffering she would feel in six hour or so from now. She certainly didn’t last night when she was playing drinking games in the small intermissions between songs. Though, the men she had bested in each of those games would feel the tight knot of hangover around their stomach more. Then there was Erika, who took over the right side of the large room. She sat on a large silver extravagant sofa with pillows that looked like heaven, and two girls on it to match. Something struck Carys as odd, and her eyebrows went up to confirm it, there were no men near Erika. You don’t even know what you’re doing any more. That’s why I’m here, so you can make sense of everything. Are you retarded?Her thought was once again interrupted by the ignorant voice inside her head. ‘If I am, then you are…thick fuck.’ The cocky bastard inside her head had no witty come back for that, which left a satisfied grin across her face. ‘How the hell am I supposed to eat my cereal, now?’ At first Carys doubted that the voice even existed, but as though out of no where Ana was mumbling to herself through tight swollen eyes and with her face looming an inch or two above the bowl of cornflakes. Carys looked over towards the neon clock that no doubt haunted a few of the drunkards sleep; it read 5:28 am. Carys wasn’t far wrong with the guess earlier. ‘There’s milk in the fridge, Ana.’ Carys whispered over to her friend, agent and manager with a soft smile that was returned with a smile that said ‘oh, someone else is awake.’ But, Ana wasn’t too keen on Carys’ answer, ‘What, no Bailey’s?’ she referred to the Irish crème liquor, and beamed hopefully at Carys, who shrugged her shoulders in the ‘I guess it’s still party time for her’ movement, while making appointments with the spirits cabinet. By the time she had returned Ana was snoring into her dry cornflakes, probably with ‘too little an amount of blood in her alcohol stream,’ as she was apt to say. Her foot traced over something as she hobbled back towards the cabinet. Something wet, and disgusting that made her intentions turn immediately towards the telephone that rested near Alotti’s hand. The red button indicated that a message had been left, as it screamed with its loud colors in the dull night aura. She pressed the button, knowing full well something was wrong if someone had actually left her a message… BEEP. “Carys…I think this is your number…” She groaned, hoping that her first instincts were wrong. Ha! You know who this is. “I don’t know why we don’t talk anymore, but I need your help. You’re the only one who can…please.” A choked cry came, from two directions, the phone and Carys. Jade was in trouble, and that was a pile of shit that she didn’t want to touch.
