Oh Kari, Where Art Thou? #06 : The Futility Of Myth.

9 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/5/04 Ever since she first rode down to Carolina and signed up for RUSH Pro Wrestling's less than demanding schedule, Karina Wolfenden had been forced to listen to the Religious ranting of Bruce Shanahan - better known as Violence Jack. She'd never even bothered to find out who, or what, Cthulhu was... But Karina was sure of one thing, she didn't want to know. Even encased in the protective walls of her locker room at RPW's Dale F. Halton Arena, 'Father Shanahan's' words permeated through the brick-work, like a virus spreading through a reluctant host. Ringside's house microphone broadcast his words, which resonated through the very foundations of the building, so that not even covering her ears could keep his preaching at bay. RPW's fans shunned his words, the RPW roster did their best to ignore him... But worryingly, some followed Shanahan, carrying out his every command as if their very souls were at stake. 'Typical,' Karina muttered in disdain whenever she heard Violence Jack order his obedient followers around. 'Easier to give orders, than to do yourself.' It took months for Karina to get used to just having fans. She must have been the only wrestler who felt uncomfortable about having people cheer for her, or chant her name. It wasn't what she was there for, and being an idol scared her more than it gave her pride. She wasn't a role model, and she certainly wasn't a woman who wanted to inspire others. She was herself, and that wasn't always easy to deal with. So, for someone who was often baffled and bemused by life itself, trying to understand the religions of the world tended to be a step to far for the K-Wolf. It had been ever since childhood. Try telling a kid with an unnaturally curious mind to believe in things adults themselves could neither explain or comprehend, and you're just asking for trouble. In some ways, she envied Ethan Knight. He truly believed there was something greater than him, and he'd devoted his life to it... While Karina couldn't even pick a hair colour and stick to it. His faith gave him strength, it gave him a purpose in life. Who knows, his devotion to Cthulhu may have taken him from the depths of despair, and made him a man who was finally happy with himself? Or maybe, just maybe, it was a sham... Milking him for every penny he had. Sometimes, in asking people to take a leap of faith, and transcend thinking of just the mortal realm, you're asking them to give up both reason, and their conscience. 'Why do I have to be here?' the whining child asked of his smartly-dressed mother as he was dragged along by the wrist. 'Dad never comes!' With June came heat and humidity. Throughout the neighbourhood, sprinklers were on, lawnmowers were running, and most families were preparing to enjoy their last day off before the working week crawled back around. But at St. Catherine's church, devotion to more than leisure was being pledged. Marching like animals into the Ark, family's two children walked through the intimidating, heavy doors... Each dressed so neatly in their 'Sunday Best' that it were unnatural. 'Because otherwise you'll go to hell,' the hassled, short-of-patience mother snapped. It may have seemed like a regrettable outburst by a stressed parent who'd had to put up with such whining for the whole of the hot, stuffy, 15 minute car journey... But if the words of the book they were soon to be read to from were to be believed, it was also true. Did the mother really believe her child would burn in hell if he strayed from God's path? If she didn't, and chose to look for a different meaning behind the words, what else would that outlook apply to? Heaven? Christianity? God? 'Now straighten your collar and behave yourself,' the parent ordered, guiding her offspring into the house of God. Mixed into the entering crowd were the 2 Wolfenden siblings - Karina and Max. Since they'd each turned 9, every Sunday they were sent to St. Catherine's, and dropped off by the family's driver. It always amazed Kari that her parents would insist on her attending, yet probably hadn't entered a church since her Christening. It stunk of hypocrisy, which even at a young age, Karina could sniff out from a mile away. However, their lack of attendance became a blessing. Parked down the road, their driver - Maurice - would be taking a nap, and certainly not paying attention to the 2 children. 'Good to see you again,' was repeated with little sincerity. 'I wonder what the sermon will be about this week?' was asked with little interest. 'Come here,' image-conscious mothers would demand, 'wipe your face.' Far from eager to follow them through the door, Karina already knew what she'd be learning, and it had nothing to do with Loaves and Fishes. Looking awkward in a red and white summer dress, a nearly teenage Karina Wolfenden scanned all around her with already worldly emerald eyes. With mousy hair, and a galaxy of subtle freckles bridging her nose, Kari looked far from the woman who would grace RPW's ring with her dynamic presence. When she was sure nobody was paying any attention to her, Kari disappeared in the blink of an eye. Disguised by the rich fauna that bordered the church yard, Karina's light footsteps crossed the last few feet of gravel before touching down on the lush grass. Through gaps in the hedge, her wide eyes watched her brother enter, followed by 2 or 3 more stragglers, before the minister entered. The sigh of relief that followed told just how much she resented beliefs being imposed upon her. In stark contrast, Max seemed to enjoy the weekly visit, although he was more into the stories of smiting than the real message. The Wolfenden siblings were close, as they had to be with such demanding parents, and Max kept Karina's secret safe... Just as she would any of his... Never even holding it over her head as a bargaining chip. 'Freedom,' Kari boasted under her breath, dropping her head back to feel the heat of the morning sun. The first couple of months, Karina was one of those who were herded through the door. Stuck sitting on the uncomfortable pews, and breathing in the stuffy air, she was far from inspired. Nothing made sense to her... Contradictions and mixed messages stuck in Kari's head more than the veiled wisdom. Could a being be both vengeful, and eternally loving? Could even the most evil of men be redeemed? How could such a powerful being possibly be so arrogant? Why would he make himself be known? So people would love him? Fear him? Serve him? Were they even his words? Every week, Karina left with more questions than answers, and with more harm done than wisdom gained. All Karina really knew was what she liked, and what she didn't. She did what she felt was right, was always kind, helpful, and tried her hardest not to judge... Was she a bad person for not walking through those doors every Sunday? Instead of being encased by aged stone and strained glass, the young Wolfenden strolled down the hill from the church, under the cherry blossoms that had scattered pale pink confetti all over the path. Every Saturday afternoon, she'd sneak out and come to St. Catherine's... Not to pray, not even to admire the architecture of the admittedly awe-inspiring building, but to leave her Sunday morning supplies. A blue carrier bag was tucked just behind the best climbing tree that overlooked the sleepy, bendy road, deposited by Karina, and soon to be retrieved. Contorting her awkward body through the barely noticeable gap in the hedgerow, Karina emerged on the other side, where a thin border of lush grass surrounded an abandoned building site. Hurriedly, Karina yanked a navy hooded top from the bag, and pulled it satisfyingly over her summer dress, which she detested having to wear. Smart and uncomfortable shoes were kicked off, and a pair of inviting suede sneakers instantly replaced them. She was beginning to look a little more like herself. As soon as her thin, unmanageable hair had been tied back out of her eyes, all that was needed was for Kari to grab her board. In an almost Enid Blyton way, Karina climbed 2 footings up the wide tree, and reached into the deep hole that gaped in it's side. 'Good morning,' she then beamed as her prized skateboard was pulled out, like a white rabbit from a magician's hat. Hopping back down, the K-Wolf was then free to worship at the alter of fun. If she was truly one of God's children, would he resent Karina for following her heart? All week she was forced to go to school. Afterwards, her parents made her finish all her homework. Nearly every Saturday they went to visit her grandmother... That just left Sunday, and she wasn't about to give half of that up. An omnipotent God would understand that, wouldn't he? It's not as if she was opposing him, or speaking out against him. Karina was just having fun. The miniature city of concrete was her playground... It's geography a mix of hills, steps and pipes. From there, she could see the Church's spire, and the face of the clock that told her when playtime was over. There was nobody to disturb her, and only the muffled sounds of passing traffic to interrupt the loud rumbling of wheels rolling across concrete. What if all her brother was hearing inside had just been made up by men centuries after Jesus' supposed death? All that time, all that money, all that devotion would have been for nothing. Although she was far too young to bet, she wasn't keep on taking that life-altering gamble. Someone like Ethan Knight obviously felt differently, and maybe he was a better person than Karina for it... Or maybe he was just a pawn in a much larger game. A game that the K-Wolf had no intention of playing.
