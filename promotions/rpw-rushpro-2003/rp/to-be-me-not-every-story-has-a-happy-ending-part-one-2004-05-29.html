To Be Me : Not Every Story Has A Happy Ending (Part One)

29 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003 It was the proverbial 'first day of the rest of my life'. Once again, I was an ex-wrestler, not tied down to any binding contracts. All my fears and concerns about returning to the ring last year had been for nothing, as my OSW deal had allowed us to invest a healthy six-figure sum in a high-interest savings account, and we were more than comfortable financially... Especially as we already owned our own home. Sure, I'd never be lighting cigars with $100 bills on what we had, but I never had lavish tastes or aspirations to be rich, I had something money couldn't buy. And so there we were. 'Please say that book isn't what it says on the cover,' I sighed as Eleanor brought the feared object out of her Waterstone's bag. 'The Big Book Of Baby Names' was held up to me, beside Eleanor's beaming smile, mischievous as she knew how much I'd tired of baby name talk. 'Well, we can't keep calling her 'it',' she bluntly replied, with more than a hint of 'DUH!' to her tone. 'Besides, I read that naming your baby when it's still in the womb helps with maternal bonding... Or do you WANT her to hate me?' Life with Eleanor was a balancing act... So much that was great, that I wouldn't give up for anything, mixed in with a healthy dose of wanting to bang my head against a brick wall. 'Since when has 'it' been a 'she'?' I casually enquired as I tore a piece from my Croissant. 'And I think I'm going to 'accidentally' lose a few of those mother-to-be magazines, before they start telling you to read exam papers to your womb to help raise a brighter baby.' It was Monday morning, the morning after my OSW contract came to an end, and the morning after Matt Register had died in the ring. To some, just getting right on with my life may have seemed cold and uncaring, and I wouldn't argue with them. I'd promised Eleanor that when the final bell of the final Slam! had rang, that I wouldn't even mention OSW anymore. It's chiming was the end of my official wrestling career, and the beginning of my new life... As a loving husband and devoted father. I'd even offered to back out of my Universal Title commitments, breaking my ties with the profession fully, but Eleanor had refused... So long as the more than adequate pay-offs went into a proper life insurance policy for us both. All bases were being covered, and our venture into parenthood wasn't being taken lightly... ... Hence her aggravation at my refusal to settle on a name. 'Oh, ha ha,' was her false-smile reply, 'I suppose you just want her to be a boy so you can force 'Christian Jnr.' onto him, huh?' We hadn't been able to do this in months, just sit inside a homely coffee shop on a Monday morning and grab a late breakfast. After Slam! we'd have a rushed journey of taxis, airports, early-morning flights and a 2 hour car journey back to Merlin Bay... The weekly cost of our life of paradise in Barbados. So as opposed to trying to sleep during the inevitable turbulence, we sat by an open fire, watching the shivering world through the windows while knowing we weren't like everyone outside. We didn't have a job to rush to, we didn't have just 45 minutes to cram lunch with each other in to... We could have stayed there all day, knowing interest would have meant we walked out richer than we walked in. 'Nah,” I dismissed, not being able to resist another chance to rib her, “I still prefer ‘it’. 'It Kannon', has a ring to it, don‘t you think?” Playfully, she stuck her tongue out at me, before opening the book. While sipping from a tall glass of orange juice, Ellie having become a vitamin fiend while with child, she flicked through the pages, ‘Ummm’ing and ‘Ahhhh’ing when a name tickled her fancy. “How abou-” she went to suggest, just to see a look of frustration cross my face. As I let out an audible sigh, Ellie just chuckled. “You’re too easy…” We were, in all fairness, doing absolutely nothing… And I liked it. It was how I wanted to live, retired at 28, all the time in the world to be with my family, no immediate money concerns. I didn't even see myself being the slightest bit bored... As how could I be bored around the woman I loved so much? How could I ever be bored when my 1st child was born? I was never one of the guys backstage who valued my spot in the business, who needed to be famous and look good. It was my job, playing the role of 'Xavier Kannon', acting the smart-mouthed hero for all to see. And, to be totally honest, I wasn't going to miss being recognised one bit... The purple singlet would be burned for a start. When we arrived back in Merlin Bay, I'd decided to grow a beard, and finally wash the red dye out of my hair... I wanted to be Christian Alexander Kannon again. Time just passed, and we just let it... The fact that neither of us wore a watch wasn't forgetfulness, nor coincidence. 'I know you're technically unemployed,' commented an unimpressed Eleanor as I settled the bill, 'but do you have to be quite so tight with the tip?' She pulled her winter coat over her slender frame and un-tucked her flowing blonde mane from the collar while I added an extra $2 to the tray. Looking at the cold grey world through the condensation-lined windows, I pulled on my full-length coat, wrapped a black scarf around my neck, then pulled a ski-hat over my red hair. Yes, the wrestling hero didn't want to catch a chill... ...Now there was something Ivan Stanislav could have used against me in our program, the Russian Bear always did like to rib me backstage about my intolerance of the cold. 'So,' I turned to an eager Eleanor and enquired, 'where are you taking my credit card next?' Ever since my sister, Isabelle, moved in with us, her love of Interior Design had rubbed off on Eleanor. Ellie now didn't see a beautifully located family home in Barbados' tranquil Merlin Bay, she saw blank walls to adorn and spaces to fill... I never thought I'd see the day when my wife tried to impose the wisdom of stencilling upon me. That's right, the man who could beat the unbeatable, stand toe-to-toe with 7 foot monsters, gives in to his petite, unassuming wife when it comes to decorating. 'You know the answer to that,' she beamed back at me with a wink as she opened the door, immediately receiving a frosty greeting from the wind, 'everywhere.' Glowing, and filled with enthusiasm, Eleanor bounded out through to door, showing the spirit of a woman who'd just gotten her husband back. Twirling on the spot, she looked up at the grey marbled sky, and as if her smile alone had demanded it... ...It snowed. With unfathomably wide, pale blue eyes, Ellie looked up at the opening heavens and watched the snowflakes fall, observing with majesty as one slowly glided it's way onto the tip of her nose. I could watch her for hours. 'Hurry up, slowcoach,' her youthful and vibrant voice demanded of me, 'there's shopping to d-' Not looking where she was going, the breezy Eleanor bumped into one of the passers by. Bouncing back off him, Eleanor laughed, the show having excited her a little too much. 'Sorry,' she giggled, with a smile that no man could refuse... ...Except the one she collided with. 'Watch where you're going,' he grunted, all of 19 but trying to show bravado beyond his years, 'dumb bitch.' I know I didn't exactly make a career for myself in the most moral, or socially acceptable of careers, but I still had a sense of what was right and what was wrong. Sure, every week I was shown punching, kicking, and hitting people with chairs. I'd sliced my own forehead open with a razorblade so kids all around the world could see my own blood blind me... ...But I wasn't going to stand by and let some kid, who could barely grow stubble, insult my wife. 'Hey, watch you're language,' I demanded, reverting back into my 'promo' voice. Giving him a less than savoury look, Eleanor retreated back behind my shoulder. 'Then keep the bitch on a leash,' he growled, 'and I won't have to, comprende?' Although it's unfair to judge someone by their looks, it was as if he'd deliberately tried to look like trouble. Maybe it's just my understandable bias, but there was something about him that warned people not to get in his way... Like red in nature, the sign of danger. Peeking around my shoulder, Eleanor shot a poisoned look at the man, making it clear what she thought of him. 'Or maybe she just wanted to feel the touch of a real man, huh?' He was goading me, and I knew that, just as I could hear Ellie whispering 'leave it' sharply from over my shoulder. Now, there are people all around the world who's greatest desire is to be famous, to be recognised by people on the street. Budding actresses, aspiring singers, or the new generation of 'It-Girls', all they want is to see their picture in the newspapers, for people to know who they were. I was different, and did my best to differentiate myself from Xavier Kannon... ...I'd been around the business long enough to know that being recognised as a wrestler sometimes led to trouble. Some people couldn’t resist taking a shot. 'Hey,' the pitted-faced man then paused, his brow creasing... Showing a primitive sense of recognition. 'I know you... I know you, man.' His thin, chapped lips formed a taunting smile, while his body language became more threatening, causing Eleanor to start tugging on my arm. 'Let's go,' she again whispered directly, just wanting to get on with her business. 'You're [b]Kannon[/b],' the smirking lout then revealed, saying my surname with as little respect as humanly possible. 'So what're you gonna do, [b]Kannon[/b]? Gonna pretend to hit me, are ya, tough guy? Gonna hit me with a CHAIR?' He was taking pleasure in it, something that worried me. I may be young, but my sense of social responsibility goes beyond my years. I moved away from the States because public order was disintegrating, violent crime was rising, and basic morals weren't even being given the time of day. I moved myself and my wife away to avoid people like him... ...And the day I was to sever all ties with America, I'd met it's ugliest face. 'Not so valiant now, are we?' he scoffed, as if I wasn't proving to be any fun in goading. 'Afraid to fight for real, fag?' I was unimpressed, and I looked it, something that seemed to stoke his fire. In a way, I pitied him, this was going to be something he remembered fondly, something he'd probably laugh about with his friends... Who were unfortunately more than likely to be just like him. 'I'll even give ya the first shot...' All around us, people stopped and watched, but as society had become - none said a word. 'Leave it,' Eleanor once more demanded, contorting my body with her tugging at my left sleeve. 'Listen to your bitch, Kannon... She'll stop ya getting hurt.' I know I'm not the toughest guy on the planet, far from it. Before I stepped into the ring, I don't think I'd even had a proper, legit stand-up fight. Sure, I'd hopped the bar to break up some fights back in New York, but that was hardly a brawl. It's just that there was something about him, his look, his voice, his attitude, that made me want to land one on him... And as most of my opponents could testify, I worked stiff, and they'd taken a full-blooded right hand from me before. I could take him... But it didn't mean I should. 'That's it,' he victoriously boasted as Eleanor led me away, 'hide behind your woman!' He'd won, by the sad rules that governed society. My wife was insulted, I was insulted, even my profession had been insulted... And I'd just walked away. Why? Because it was easier, it was the path of least resistance, no matter my desire to land one on his smirking mouth. I wasn't a valiant fighter. I wasn't Xavier Kannon, the man who would never quit. I wasn't in a wrestling ring. I was a husband, I was soon to be a father... ...And I didn't have to play the hero anymore.
