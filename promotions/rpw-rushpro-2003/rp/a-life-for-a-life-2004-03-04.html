A Life For A Life

4 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless Saturated with macabre resolution, The Dread Pirate Roberts flowed from the alcove into the room, letting his drawn blade lead the way. It was his guiding torch in this overwhelming darkness, his beacon in the gloom. The heavy door clicked softly shut behind him, and in the choking stillness the latch snapping into place had a grim sense of finality, like the lid of a coffin slamming firmly and irrevocably closed. In a very real way this place was a tomb, though for which of its occupants it had been erected was still to be determined. The fluorescent overhead lighting washed everything in a surreal yellow glow, routing would-be shadows before they had the opportunity to form. Against one wall were two steel chairs with scant beige padding, reserved for visitors who wished to offer the solace of a friend to those who found themselves trapped here in desolation. Under different circumstances Roberts would have wrestled one up beside the bed against the opposite wall and flopped into it, as he had countless times before over the past few weeks. That had been his intention up until a moment ago, up until his friend's life had flashed before his eyes. The natural midafternoon light was curtained off from the room by a heavy shade drawn down over the single window, denying dominion to the true source of life. In its place two cumbersome machines artificially sustained the limp form sprawled across the elevated mattress, feeding him life through thin tubes and monitoring the essence of it with so many dangling wires. As a bag hanging ominously above the bed deflated and refilled with rhythmic precision, the chest and abdomen of Jason O'Neil rose and fell in time. A little over two weeks ago on an episode of Adrenaline, during a warehouse brawl slated to pit Jason O'Neil against his arch-enemy Peter Maxwell in a no-holds-barred fight to the finish, O'Neil had almost lost his life. Maxwell had no intentions of engaging him honourably, instead orchestrating an ambush where a dozen of his henchmen would first beat him to oblivion and then drop him headfirst from a catwalk to the cold cement floor twenty feet below. Roberts had discovered the devious plans in time, but even his best efforts couldn't prevent the inevitable. After dispatching the dozen men with his trusty sword, Roberts turned to deal with Maxwell only to discover that Maxwell was already upon him. With a fifteen pound wrench heaved high overhead, he descended on Roberts with the malicious intention of caving in his skull, twisted by an insatiable hate and jealousy only he truly understood. The Man in Black was defenseless; he was doomed. Maxwell's face contorted with a savage scream as he brought the weapon bearing down, knowing that in his own demented way, he had won. At the last moment, as Roberts resigned himself to the cold embrace of death, the ever-watchful Scarecrow exploded onto the scene, crashing into The DPR and knocking him out of harm's way. Jason O'Neil had saved his life, and in so doing laid forfeit his own. The heavy head of the wrench slammed into the side of O'Neil's skull so hard it almost fulminated straight through. The actual result was only slightly better. Blood splattered Maxwell's shocked face as the wrench clattered to the ground. O'Neil lay crumpled beside it, unmoving, half of his skull above the ear gruesomely concave and oozing gore. Maxwell and Roberts had both assumed he was dead. In a state of blind agony and unparalleled rage The DPR pledged himself to retribution. Maxwell tried to flee, but The Man in Black was right on his heels, gaining on him, propelled by the power of justice. He was going to decapitate Maxwell and rejoice in the deed, and then -- only then -- would he mourn his friend. His saviour. O'Neil's weak, breathless cry for help, the culmination of his last remaining strength and a true testament to the potency of his heart and spirit, was the only thing that made him abandon his revenge. That single whimpered word was the greatest thing The Dread Pirate Roberts had ever heard in his life. And the most frightening. It told him that while O'Neil had not yet succumbed to death, he was precariously close to it, and if Roberts hesitated for even a moment he would plunge beyond reach forever. Letting Maxwell escape, Roberts rushed him to the hospital in a blur of disbelieving panic that seemed to last an eternity. The wait for Jason to awake from his coma was immeasurably longer and more painful; every waking moment was filled with a haunting replay of that courageous cry for help. His dreams offered no respite. Over and over he was tortured by that strangled plea, fearing that in spite of his most virtuous efforts he hadn't done enough. Although O'Neil's mouth never moved, that supplication thundered in The DPR's mind now as violently as it ever had. 'Move away from him.' The Man in Black's voice was hollow, but it resonated with unquestionable authority, shrouding the room in its command. His startling azure eyes, swirling like a violent ocean, never wavered as he strode up to the foot of the bed. Every muscle in his body was taut, flexed, and ready; he was a coiled spring ready to explode at a feather's touch. In saving his friend's life, he was death incarnate. Peter Maxwell's dark eyes swept up and regarded him with feigned fawn-like innocence. Looming over O'Neil, he was decked out in a finely tailored black suit and immaculate white shirt, with a fat red tie thrown in for flair. His polished shoes gleamed under the lighting the same as his freshly shaven head, which was bowed as if in prayer. He had the bearing of a man at a funeral. If he didn't move away from O'Neil, Roberts vowed silently, it would be his own. Hanging limp in Maxwell's ringed right hand was a black cord. Roberts didn't need to follow its length to know one end disappeared into the machine at his side and the other into the wall. The Alpha Solution's lips slowly parted, baring his teeth in a savage smile that belied the innocuous cast of the rest of his face. 'I'm just here to pay my respect, Roberts,' he grunted, sneering as he gave voice to the name. His eyes flashed dangerously. 'Now put that sword away. Can't a man pay his last respects in peace?' As The DPR lunged for his throat, the sword leading the way, Maxwell rolled smoothly underneath the strike, yanking the cord as he went. The hum of the machine died a slow, purring death. Maxwell laughed as he danced smoothly to his feet, taunting The Man in Black. 'Come on, pirate,' he called derisively, backing towards the door. 'Let's see what you've got.' Fury consumed Roberts as he brought himself to a halt and spun around, eyes ablaze. Maxwell was halfway between him and the door, chuckling heartlessly and egging him on. Succumbing to the wrath within his soul demanding release, Roberts took two long strides towards vanquishing his foe. He was hell-bent on eradicating the danger and the taunting in one foul blow. Then he came up short as if stricken by a bolt of lightning. 'Help me,' his mind pleaded desperately, but it was O'Neil's voice that called, not his own. Abandoning Maxwell for the moment, he bent and scooped up the thick black wire, his trembling fingers fumbling around for it through his gloves before he finally snatched it up. He crawled over to the wall as he heard the door swing open, cursing himself for letting Maxwell escape again. The only thing that eased the feeling of failure was the knowledge that he was once again doing the right thing by assuring the safety of his friend. He could kill Maxwell anytime; O'Neil's life might not prove as enduring. O'Neil's life was definitely more important. He could feel his heart throbbing in his temples as he struggled to push the plug back into the outlet. He felt as if he was moving through gelatin instead of air. Breathing it, too. 'Help! Help!' Maxwell suddenly called, the words wrought with wild desperation. His voice was accompanied by the sound of several sets of rapidly approaching feet. 'He's trying to kill him! He's mad!' Slamming the cord into place, listening with relief as the machine buzzed back to life, The DPR straightened quickly and spun around to face the rest of the room. Three nurses in hospital green were charging for him, their expressions grave, as Maxwell grinned over their heads. They skidded to a stop when their eyes settled on the sword, then flickered to the machine and back to him again. Out in the hall, Roberts could hear a fourth nurse screaming for help, shrieking that someone needed to call the police. He couldn't help but agree; the police would rob him of his personal satisfaction, but he knew it was the best thing. 'Put down the sword,' the nurse in the middle commanded, holding her arms up in a mollifying gesture. She was watching Roberts as one would a rabid wolf. A rabid wolf prowling around a defenseless babe. 'No one wants to hurt you,' the one on her left cooed soothingly, mimicking the first in tone and action. 'Let's talk this through.' The Man in Black could only gape. Did they really believe he was trying to kill O'Neil? He had just saved his life! Rage blossomed anew at the outrage, the injustice, of it. He glowered at Maxwell, taking an unconscious step toward the man before realising he'd have to cut down the three nurses to get at him. Maxwell let his mouth drop open in mock fright and waggled his fingers at Roberts before smirking triumphantly. The DPR felt his face flush as he tried to summon words that would properly degrade the scoundrel. Nothing he could think of was adequate. 'Come on,' the first nurse beckoned again in a soft voice, gesturing for him to hand over the sword. The whites of her eyes showed all around her irises, which slid from Roberts' face to the sword and back again several times each second. 'Just relax.' The other nurses were fanning out to either side, as if they might flank him and disarm him with surprise on their side. The absurdity of the situation made him want to scream. He thought of sheathing the sword, but guessed in doing so he'd only commit himself to a wrestling match with the three women. If he could just explain himself and make them understand, he knew it would be alright, but at the same time he knew they weren't prepared to actually give him their ear. They'd listen, sure, and agree with everything he said, but they wouldn't digest his words. They definitely wouldn't believe him. They thought they had caught him red-handed. He was backed into a corner. Gritting his teeth, he steeled himself to his only recourse. The tip of his blade whistled as it arced up and lodged itself under the middle nurse's chin, pushing her head up ever so slightly as all three gasped. The other two jumped back, both of their mouths working but unable to form intelligible words. The one in the middle squealed. 'You've got it all wrong,' Roberts panted, his eyes darting from one to the other in rapid succession. 'I'm not trying to kill anyone!' The nurses to either side nodded their frantic understanding, biting their lips and watching the sword at their co-worker's throat with watery eyes. Tears flowed freely down her face. Her squeal had turned into a long, piercing whimper. Maxwell smugly grasped the lapels of his suit and rolled back on his heels, thoroughly enjoying the spectacle. 'I don't want to hurt you.' The Dread Pirate Roberts lowered his sword but kept it poised before him to let them know he would brook no aggression. He met each of their eyes in turn, willing them to recognize and understand his intent. The nurse in the middle collapsed to her knees, sobbing loudly, and the other two swooped in to comfort her. 'I'm going to put my sword away now,' he informed them, his voice smooth and steady, booming to carry over the sound of the wailing. 'Then I'm going to explain what happened.' 'We saw what happened!' one of the comforting nurses erupted. Tears stained her cheeks now as well, and a look of terror washed over her ashen face as it dawned on her that she might be inciting the maniac. 'Question what you saw,' Roberts bellowed. 'I was plugging the machine back in, because he--' he thrust the sword accusingly at Maxwell, who quickly adopted an indignant scowl-- 'unplugged it! He's a murderous scoundrel! Jason is my friend!' They regarded him with obvious and almost sympathetic skepticism, not daring to take their eyes off him long enough to glance at Maxwell. They didn't believe him. Taking a deep breath to calm himself, he pacified his tone. 'I have a sword,' he stated tiredly, shaking it at them. They shrank back as if it was a threat, expecting him to attack at any second, not comprehending his point. 'If I wanted to kill him,' he continued in a soft voice, 'wouldn't I have just stabbed him and been done with it, hmm? Why would I go through the charade of unplugging his life support, especially with someone else in the room with me, then plug it back in?' 'Don't try to push this off on me, psycho,' Maxwell grated, taking an admonishing step toward Roberts. 'This poor man is just lucky I was here to stop you and call for help.' With a quick glance to make sure none of the other eyes were on him, he flashed Roberts a wink. 'That's it,' Roberts muttered. Without another word he advanced on Maxwell, determined to cut him down where he stood, witnesses or not. He wouldn't kill him, he would simply take him to the pain, as he had once threatened the nefarious Humperdink. Surely there was no one more deserving of such a fate than Maxwell. The nurses shrank back as he stalked past them, not daring to make a move as they had so boldly intended at first. The DPR reasoned that they were delirious with fright, which would explain why they couldn't make sense of his words. He heard the heavy clacking of shoes drumming on the tile floor at the same time he heard the authoritative shout. 'FREEZE!' Three men in blue uniforms spilled into the room with guns drawn and trained on The Man in Black's head and heart. They spread out to surround him as best they could, one shouting at Maxwell to get down. Acting frightened, he complied. The DPR stopped five feet away from the crouching Maxwell, sharing his glare evenly between him and the three police officers. If he struck suddenly and without warning, Roberts figured he could lop the bastard's head off before they shot him. But there was no way he could kill them all. Once again, he was backed into a corner. The only difference was that this time he couldn't see a way to escape through his words. 'DROP THE SWORD! NOW!' Roberts hesitated. He couldn't let them drag him out of here, leaving Maxwell behind to finish what he had come to do, what he had started weeks ago in the warehouse. 'NOW!' With a curt nod Roberts began bending slowly to the floor, his sword held out before him, letting them know he wasn't going to make any sudden movements. Letting them think it, at least. Quick as a wink the sword was at Maxwell's throat, indenting the skin at his Adam's apple but not piercing it. 'STOP RIGHT THERE! STOP RIGHT FUCKING THERE!' The DPR could see in their eyes that they were a hair's breadth away from blowing him away. Summoning his calm, he spoke in a hushed voice that seemed to echo through the small room. 'I will surrender.' He felt bile rising in his throat. 'But only if you agree to take this man in with me.' 'DROP THE SWORD! RIGHT! NOW!' Roberts clenched his teeth to keep from shouting, then spoke through them with the greatest restraint. 'Only if you swear to bring him in with me. I'll not leave him here to finish what he started. Not alive.' The threat hung in the cramped room like a rotting corpse. 'It is not my wish to harm anyone, but unless you promise you will not let this man harm my friend, you will force my hand.' He paused for the briefest moment. 'And I will force yours.' He stood there, poised before them with the tip of his sword ready to ram through Maxwell's throat. Though his voice was calm, his arms shook visibly. With the restraint of not killing Maxwell. He had meant what he said. Jason had saved his life, and he would return the favour. Even if in so doing it cost him his own. The officers exchanged a quick look. 'Alright,' the one to his left said, speaking for the first time. His voice was strained, but he had control. His gun pointed directly at Roberts' eye. 'Now drop the weapon.' 'Swear it!' Roberts growled. 'Promise you will not leave him here to kill Jason!' 'I promise. Now . . . drop the sword.' Their eyes locked for a long moment, each measuring and weighing the other, trying to discern whether or not trust was an affordable commodity. Roberts didn't know if he was doing the right thing. Even with the police taking Maxwell into custody there was no guarantee that he wouldn't be able to return and finish the gruesome task. The only thing that assured O'Neil's safety was The Man in Black's sword. It would cost him his life, but that wasn't important. If it wasn't for O'Neil he would have already lost it long ago. He owed it to him. He could lay it down for him here and now and know that in his death his friend was promised life. But O'Neil wouldn't want that. In truth, The Dread Pirate Roberts didn't want that. It would cheapen O'Neil's heroic actions. It would make his suffering for nought. The noble man would also undoubtedly hold himself accountable for Roberts' death, blaming himself, forever feeling the failure The Man in Black felt blossoming in the back of his own mind. But at least Maxwell would be gone. Roberts snapped the blade away from Maxwell's throat and, with a dramatic flourish, slammed it into its scabbard. As he threw his arms up in surrender, the officers charged and tackled him to the ground, manoeuvring him onto his stomache and ramming a knee into the back of his neck to restrain him while they slapped the handcuffs into place. With their attention diverted, Maxwell tried to slip out of the room, but one of the officers stopped him with a sharp warning. He would not escape. 'Thank you,' Roberts wheezed as they hauled him to his feet. Even as they were reading him his rights, informing him he was under arrest, he couldn't help but grin. Jason O'Neil, his friend, would live another day. Unfortunately, so would Peter Maxwell. But The Dread Pirate Roberts would fix that soon enough.
