Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The American Wrestling Council opened on 3 February 2001, staged in its own handbook as the purchase of two recently-closed federations' rosters, WAO and RMW, by the fictional Marquee Entertainment — “while RMW and WAO may be 'dead,' the AWC is not dead.” Its kayfabe leadership paired Tyrone Parker, billed as “The Superstar” and WAO's own former president, with Joshua Orloski, and promised the combined talent of both defunct promotions “multiplied exponentially.”

Its broadcast team named head commentator Paddy Martin, twenty years into the business, alongside color man Wesley Wade, a WAO veteran; ring announcer Jeremy West; and backstage correspondent Tammy Taylor, previously a local Tampa news reporter. Two broadcasts were planned: Stars, Stripes, and Slams, its flagship two-hour show, and an in-development Monday program, After Hours, taped live from Marquee Studios. Four championships were established — the AWC World Heavyweight, Old Glory, Cruiserweight, and World Tag Team titles — and six pay-per-views a year were promised, the first slated for that April.

On 18 February 2001 it opened a dedicated cruiserweight division, seeking sixteen wrestlers at 225 pounds or under to build it from scratch, run by its own co-president as Director of the Cruiserweight Division.

No further document survives past that recruitment call. Whether the AWC's promised pay-per-view schedule or its cruiserweight division ever materialized isn't in the surviving record, and neither is how or when the promotion ended.
