Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Championship Wrestling from Iowa introduced itself in a Handbook posted 10 February 1999, its own owner and president describing a goal to capture what the independent wrestling scene of America was like. Its own card results were weighted 55 percent roleplay to 45 percent strategy, a split it called more honest than most feds' on a stated principle: “In real wrestling, is your talent measured by the way you give interviews? No.”

Roleplays went directly to the owner rather than a public board, packaged into two newsletters, “Shooting from the Hip” on Mondays and “Trash Talk” on Fridays. Discipline ran on its own dry humor: for a handler unhappy with a result, its rules named the options plainly — “I can choose to fire you, suspend you, or just plain job you. :)” — before admitting that last one “will not happen,” with most violations drawing a two-card suspension instead.

A weekly show, WCI Superstars, ran five to ten matches and three or four interviews each Tuesday, mainly from high-school gyms across Iowa. A bi-monthly supercard, Hawkeye Heritage, was billed as the promotion's biggest card of the year, built around an eight-man tournament for the number-one contendership to the Iowa-National Heavyweight Title.

The handbook named a full on-screen crew: play-by-play announcer Greg Davis, billed as a “resident wrestling historian”; color commentator Danny Elliot; ring announcer Ron Sauer; backstage reporter Jake Holman; secondary interviewer Jamey Viers; and five referees, Tim O'Meara, Dan Schuster, Rich Heideman, Dean Rogalla, and Chris Sinclair.

No roster of wrestlers or card result survives beyond this handbook.
