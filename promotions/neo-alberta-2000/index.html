A regional promotion broadcasting from gymnasiums across Alberta and British Columbia, its December 2000 run building a four-way tournament toward the crowning of its own first Heavyweight Champion at a year-end supercard. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

By the first weekend of December 2000, this promotion already had a website, a roster, and a storyline in progress: a weekly post-show bulletin announced the site's relaunch, listed a run of talent arrivals and departures, and previewed two Saturday broadcasts building toward an end-of-year supercard. Nothing in the surviving record marks its actual opening; what survives begins mid-run, already busy with feuds and already counting down to a first champion.

Its broadcasts were staged in small Western Canadian venues, a gymnasium in Canmore, Alberta among them, in front of crowds numbered in the hundreds, and carried a roster mixing homegrown talent with imports: a masked traveller introduced as Sushi Tsunami, a trio of English wrestlers who arrived within weeks of each other, and a returning veteran nearing the end of a thirty-year career. Its color commentary leaned hard into comedy, its interview segments into elaborate short-form sketches, and a handful of episodes broke format entirely for a dedicated talk-show interlude between the matches.

Two storylines dominated the month held here. A masked man calling himself the Black Knight interrupted a championship-eliminator match to declare a new era already begun, vowing to work alone and answer to no one. And a returning wrestler drew a formal challenge from an old tag-team rivalry at a much bigger, unrelated federation, the two promotions' own front offices trading a fax to arrange it, an angle left open at the edge of the surviving record.

The month closed on When Hell Freezes Over, a four-way match for a vacant Heavyweight Championship staged from Kamloops, British Columbia. A fan favorite came through the four-way to claim the strap as the promotion's first champion, ending the broadcast year on the loudest pop its announce table had called all month.
