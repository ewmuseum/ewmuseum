Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This fed opened on 6 May 1997 self-titled Global Fantasy Wrestling, its own operator citing a run of earlier feds behind him as he laid out the terms: a booked promotion running two cards a week, participation required on pain of being jobbed, and every new signing entered straight into a tournament for the World Heavyweight title.

Its first three signings arrived that same day. “Awesome” Mike Austin declared himself “the greatest wrestler in history,” daring anyone to prove otherwise; his bodyguard, Primus, offered one line of his own — “NOTHING CAN MATCH THE POWER OF PRIMUS!!!!” — and “Vicious” Vic Vega delivered a taped nihilist monologue the operator cut short, noting only that “he got much worse.”

Six days later, on 12 May 1997, the promotion was forced to rename itself: “I originally had the name GFW written down but that was taken,” its operator explained, relaunching the identical pitch as the Canadian E-mail Wrestling Federation, now building toward a sixteen-wrestler triple-knockout tournament meant to crown three separate singles champions at once.

Three further signings that same day brought the roster to six: a self-styled wrestling psychologist, Dr. Sigmund E. Xavier, promising to “bring sanity back to this sport”; a messianic newcomer billed only as Prophet, who claimed his arrival was “preordained” to “lead the CEWF out of the darkness into a new era”; and Pulsulating Pepe, who had no comment.

No further record survives past that day.
