Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

MHW's own surviving record opens mid-run, as the MACHRI World Order, self-hosted at callisto.worldonline.nl under president C.J.A. Hoesen. Its earliest held newsletter (30 August 1997) already carries a full set of champions crowned that same Saturday LIVE: “El Perfecto Body” Jacques Castonguay won the World Heavyweight title in a battle royal, Shocker took the BRAWL title in a hardcore battle royal, and Bloody Blue (Curt Vander and Lord Lander) won the World Tag Team title in a round-robin tournament, with the European singles and tag titles both left vacant. Every wrestler and manager named across its record — Domino, Badmuts, “Dildo” Bob, Mark “Sexy” Steelman, Eric “the Crow” Draven, Brain Bond, the Assassing — is an original persona.

It ran two weekly shows, Wednesday CLASH and Saturday LIVE, results decided across three separate wrestling simulators at once, and its third newsletter announced a points system that made roleplay itself — interviews, challenges, in-character actions — “much more important than before” to a wrestler's own standing. A pre-event news post of 23 September 1997 narrated the federation's own DISORDED! pay-per-view in the past tense (Brain Bond newly crowned, Mark Steelman outlasting Castonguay in a two-hour Iron Man match 15 falls to 11, the Assassing gone from the roster) and announced, in the same breath, that the promotion would be renamed MHW — MACHRI Hardcore Wrestling — that Saturday, its European and BRAWL titles unifying into an MHW International title, its two tag titles into an MHW World Tag Team title, and its World Heavyweight title carrying over intact.

The rename card delivered on it: Domino beat “Big” Al Marvelous to become undisputed MHW World Heavyweight Champion, “Dildo” Bob beat Brain Bond for the unified International title, and Bloody Blue beat Hell & Heaven for the unified tag titles. The same broadcast set its next feud in motion: Eric “the Crow” Draven won the world-title contendership by disqualification, then turned on Brain Bond alongside Father John II and The Priest, drawing a “WAR-Games kind of match” against Bond's own Enforcers for the following card. A week later, Domino piledrove and bladed his own tag partner Insanity on camera and dumped him from the ring, the second partner — after the Assassing — he had discarded that way.

The last documents MHW's own record carries, on 9 and 10 October 1997, are recruitment calls urging new wrestlers to debut at the coming Saturday LIVE. No further broadcast, result, or closure notice survives past them; the promotion's own record stops there, its Enforcers-versus-Draven war and Domino's title reign both still open.
