A member territory of the National Wrestling Council, attested by a single fourteen-part January 1999 broadcast crowning a Royal Rumble winner for a shot at the alliance's own Heavyweight Championship. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

SCCW survives in one broadcast: its own second pay-per-view, High Stakes, staged 25 January 1999 out of the Thomas & Mack Arena in Las Vegas as one of the National Wrestling Council's five member territories, a banner of regional federations open since 1997. The broadcast's own ring announcements bill it repeatedly in full as Sin City Championship Wrestling.

The card opened on a grudge match years in the making: “Heartbreaker” Stan Grubb over “Rebel,” two men who had once worked together at Rebel's own earlier promotion, IWCW, with Grubb as its president, before that federation split them apart. Elsewhere on the card, the Knick Man defended the SCCW Television Championship in front of a house stacked with UNLV players, Jason “Blade” Saxton kept the Gamblers Heritage Heavyweight Championship by disqualification, a Missouri Wrestling Alliance-crossover contest for the 1999 MWA Logan Cup went to Jon Atlas, and the National Wrestling Council Tag Team Championship changed hands to a team calling itself Censored, beating the champions from Motor City Wrestling.

The night's two centerpieces tied together. Rocky Storm won the SCCW Heavyweight Championship off Armageddon in a cage match, then the broadcast closed on its own thirty-man Royal Rumble for a February shot at the National Wrestling Council's own Heavyweight Title. A masked newcomer, Kid Crimson, fought down to the final two and eliminated Armageddon to win it, only for SCCW President Donald Vaccaro to challenge the paperwork behind his entry and produce a replacement contract with a signature he didn't recognize as Kid Crimson's own. Kid Crimson pulled off his mask to reveal Rocky Storm, already champion once that same night, now also holding the National Wrestling Council's own title shot for himself.

No earlier or later document of SCCW's own survives; the only other trace of it is a February 2000 announcement naming it, still, as one of the National Wrestling Council's five territories. Whether Rocky Storm's double-dealing stood, whether he ever wrestled for the National Wrestling Council's own Heavyweight Title in February, and how SCCW's own run ended aren't in the surviving record.
