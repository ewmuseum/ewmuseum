Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Vegas Championship Wrestling introduced itself in a founding application dated 13 March 1997 as “a regional league based out of Las Vegas, Nevada,” staged inside a casino it called the Golden Palace, with two weekly cards, no pay-per-views, and occasional supercards. Its two co-founders introduced themselves by citing résumés of work across several earlier e-feds.

Two weeks later its own founder returned to the newsgroup with that application forwarded verbatim, accusing a Niagara Falls e-fed's own announcement, posted three weeks afterward, of copying its casino staging, title structure and card format nearly word for word, and taunting the accused poster directly: “Rip someone else off, 'cause for me…It's VCW on mine!!!”

Its only surviving complete card, Casino Nights, ran 15 April 1997 “between the craps table and the slot machines” of the Golden Palace and opened a Heavyweight Championship tournament: Stevie Phoenix and the Boogieman won their first-round bouts while two further dark matches went unresolved in its record, and the main event's six-man tag, Tony Ruiz, Davey Cormier and Christopher Stonebreaker's Legendary Alliance over Kevin Legend, Victor Vance and Aaron Rhett's Vanity Flare, ended in a brawl across the casino floor.

By 10 May 1997 it was under new management, a fresh president and vice president restating the same casino pitch and the same three titles, but its own two surviving descriptions of its second weekly show, posted the same day, contradict each other on both its name and its night — one calling it “Wednesday Night Warriors,” the other “Tuesday Night Torment.”

Its own name and web address resurface on a January 1999 pay-per-view, New Year's Revolution, and a March 1999 weekly broadcast carrying five further titles. Its Cruiserweight Championship was defended twice on the same card by the same champion; its Western States Championship changed hands after three prior failed challenges by the same wrestler; an Ironman Championship defense was thrown out for interference and rebooked as a steel-cage blowoff; and its Women's Championship stood undefeated through eight matches, its champion left without an opponent when a unification bout fell through.

Whether the 1997 promotion and its 1999 incarnation are one continuous run or a relaunch under the same name and address isn't established in its own record, and no document after 26 March 1999 survives.
