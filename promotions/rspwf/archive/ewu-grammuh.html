Now, I'm sure everyone's heard that you're suppose to use correct grammar in a role-play. My question is why? I believe a lot of people look at correct grammar in a role-play as a determining factors in many things: intelligence, the character, and how good the role-play actually is. The problem with that is, correct grammar, in the form that a lot of people use, is not accurate for the sakes of realism. Bear with me as I'll get to that point in a few moments. People talk about realism in wrestling. They don't want to see outrageous attacks that would kill any normal person, which I've been guilty of writing myself in years past. I've often read that e-wrestling mimics the current trends of wrestling. I can believe that because if you look back to 1996, SCSA clones ran rampant through the world of e-wrestling. I'm not talking about characters based off of SCSA, I'm talking about direct rip-offs. You'll see the same thing today as attacks, angles, and challenges are mimiced from wrestling. Yes, I have seen this. That brings me to the point of correct grammar in flashes. Next time you're watching wrestling, listen to what someone says in an in-ring. You'll notice a very different trend and my reasoning that completely correct grammar isn't neccessary. People don't talk in correct grammar, well most of us at any rate. When someone talks, they use run-ons and fragments as if they were a part of the English language, or whatever your respective language is. However, the way people talk and type/write is a different style. When typing, most people use passive voice instead of the active voice that they use. If you don't understand what I mean. Here's an example. Steve Austin delivered the Stone Cold Stunner to the Undertaker. -That's a sentence in active voice. The Undertaker was hit by the Stone Cold Stunner delievered by Steve Austin. -There's a setence in passive voice. Throughout school, most people learn how to write in passive voice. My reasoning is, let's face it. When a professor or a teacher asks for five page paper, passive voice is the way to go because it uses more words. Thus, you fill up the required length a lot quicker. Though I've also learned that citing a lot of text also helps. :) Anyhow, when people are aiming for realism, why start knocking for correct grammar? Granted, you don't want to see a whole lot of long winded sentences. I don't think someone could say that much without passing out due to a lack of oxygen. We're suppose to imagine (unless you're Janis, but I digress) that this e-wrestling stuff is "real." Then why would it be too far fetched to have the flashes reflect the way our characters would actually talk? IMO, it's not. Granted, part of this essay stems from the fact that I'm a broadcasting major, where I've been told that we write how people would talk. We use short, active voice sentences. Yes, this is a trivial thing. That brings me to the other aspects of grammar. The idea of where to use commas, periods, ect. Most people use the "feels right" method of placing commas. They're looking for a break to take a breath in a long sentence and place it where it "feels right." Yes, I'm a regular practioner of that method. :) The less common mistake is using the right punctuation to finish off a sentence, though I still make those mistakes. Granted, e-wrestling's age is diverse. Not everyone has gone through the same levels of English that others of us have. Yes, there's more to grammar of how to correctly quote someone else and all that yaz, but I don't feel like getting into that. People always tend to knock someone for their lack of grammar or the way they have their character talk. Heck, even the way people describe things get knocked today. Though I'm also a firm believer in the idea that you could use blue eyes instead of using three sentences to give the same basic description. There's also the idea of people who like to use a lot of profanity in role-plays without even attempting to block them out. I can understand a lot of things that get knocked. However, I've read quite a few role-plays that made them that much better because the lack of grammar made the character much more believable. A prime example would be Tex Violence. I'm sure some of you have heard of him, and others are scratching their heads asking, "who?" I'm using Tex, partially because I'm a mark. :) However, looking at his flashes I decided to go ahead and delve into the stuff I listed above. Most of the time, his handler has him talk in active voice. He uses incorrect puncuation and mispelled words to bring across the Texas accent in a clear manner. One of the better qualities is that Tex _lacks_ the extensive vocabulary that his handler has. What impresses me the most is the fact that Tex is a believable character because he talks like an every-day person. He's clear and concise. While Tex doesn't use profanity on a regular basis, he does happen to [BLEEP], or some form, to give the real effect of being censored.
