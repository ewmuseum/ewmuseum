Sims Vs. HJS

EW University
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A while back I was involved in a debate concerning RP feds vs. Simmed feds in which I stated simmed feds were better and defended them bitterly. That was about two years ago and since I have not visited this NG as much as I used to. But when I have visited here on rare occasions, it seems like simmed feds have come more into vogue as fewer and fewer simmed leagues are apologizing for being what they are nowadays. In the past two years, I have tried many RP feds in addition to simmed feds so I feel I have even more experience to offer than I did back then. What I found is this: 1.) Not all simmed feds are better than RP feds. There are some simmed feds that simply take a wrestler's size and what style they use and sim a match from that information. You might as well just throw the dice on that one. 2.) Simmed feds simulate shoot wrestling and RP feds are a battle for the top push. Simmed feds, if the sim is a good one, are supposed to tell you who would win a match if the two wrestlers really did meet in real life. In other words, the sim is "simulating" a real match. Of course, RP fed owners are quick to say that pro wrestling is anything but real, which is true. But, that being the case, what are RP feds trying to measure? Certainly not wrestling ability. It is the ability of a wrestler to talk or set up a character that will be "over." So, ultimately, RP feds simulate somewhat the struggle for getting the top push in a particular fed, since the best characters get the belts. Of course, as I stated before, there are a lot of other things that go into the mix when trying to discern who gets the better pushes in real pro wrestling. Good wrestling, for example, is one of them and RP feds don't measure that. Relationships with the front office, who's married to who, who is making the most money, etc., etc. -- all of these things also play a major role as to who ultimately owns the top belts. For example, Hogan doesn't have the championship because he's a good interviewer, nor is he a good wrestler. He has it because he has a huge salary and is friends with Bischoff. That is also why Diamond Dallas Page and the Steiners are getting pushed in WCW. The interesting thing is, if a e-fedhead decided to give a push to his friends in a league and the other players found out, he would have a rebellion on his hands because handlers would claim "that isn't fair" -- even though that's the way it goes on in "real" wrestling. I have never seen any RP fed take all of these things into consideration. But, when deciding what kind of fed you want, you must decide if you are interested in simulating realistic matches or battling for pushes in a particular league. 3.) Simmed feds have much fewer complaints than RP feds. When a sim tells someone they won or lost, that ends it. But that is not necessarily the case in an RP fed. My experience in RP feds is that there are complaints out the gourd almost every time a card comes out due to the subjectivity involved in judging RPs. And the complaints are numerous -- especially when the obvious better RPer loses. And even when the obvious better RPer wins, there is an immediate protest from the other wrestler about his opponent's RPs being either too long or too short or too many or too few or "not enough quality." Now, this kind of thing is not surprising if you think about it. If you have a good RPer who enters your league and he far outdoes everybody else, that person could control all the titles they go for and nobody would ever be able to beat him if he continued to RP as he is capable of doing. Thus, the outstanding RPer would win every time and potentially destroy the federation because, if a wrestler is judged strictly by RPs/strategies and he kept doing what he was doing at all times, he would never lose and the competition would lose interest. So what are the recourses? One of the things I have seen is the attempted "Clintonizing" of what good roleplaying really is. In other words, if you can't beat the good RPer, then you redefine what good RPing is in terms that you can accomplish and therefore become more competitive. For example, if you can convince a fedhead that character development has nothing to do with wrestling, then you can turn a very positive thing into a negative thing for that RPer. Combine that with the statement that his RPs "are too long" or "too short" or "too many" or "too few" and you can actually beat him with those "I'm gonna beat you, bub!" RPs. 4.) Complaints in RP feds lead to quits. Suppose you have a stable in your RP fed made up of four or five handlers and one of them has a very important match with another member of a stable made up of several different handlers. The fedhead who decides the winner is in trouble either way he goes because, no matter which wrestler he chooses to win, the other stable can threaten whatever they want to do and end up leaving the fed. The subjectivism of RP feds often leads to a lot of problems and instability in these kinds of feds. Of course, some people in simmed feds don't like it that their RP efforts have in many instances little effect on the match outcomes. However, I have seen these same disgruntled handlers come over to RP feds and quit them as well because of the same reason -- they were not being rewarded for their RPing against what they feel were inferior opponents. 5.) In summary, for those in e-feds now or who are looking for e-feds, I would say this: as The Rock says, "Know Your Role." Or know what you are looking for -- and know what you are getting into. If you are interested in shoot results and finding out who would really beat who in a real fight, then simmed feds are the way you should go. If you are looking to battle for the top push in a particular federation, then RP feds are the way you should go -- though be aware that most RP feds are limited in their ability to measure that particular part of the struggle since they focus around mike work -- and if that was the only judge, then Dean Malenko and Lex Luger would be getting zero push in WCW.
