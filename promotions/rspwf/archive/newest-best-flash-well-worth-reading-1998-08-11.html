Newest "Best Flash" - well worth reading

11 August 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello again. I've outdone myself this time, if I do say so. Recently, Creed was arrested after losing his temper a punching a man who turned out to be a cop. His valet (and much more than that) Angel went down to the 15th Precinct where he was held, trying to explain the situation to Det. Sipowitz (yes, THAT
Sipowitz). That should be enough information to make the circumstances of this flash clear to all.
Enjoy the read! Creed -------------------- [A black screen, letters slide in to form the opening graphic during a voice-over.] VO: In the criminal justice system, the people are represented by two seperate but equally important groups:
The police, who investigate crime. And the District Attorneys, who prosecute the offenders. These are there stories. [The screen is filled with the title of:] LAW & ORDER [A graphic appears at the bottom of a black screen: ] 1013 W. 52nd STREET HELL'S KITCHEN BROOKLYN [Fade into a city street, evening time, where a young man in casual slacks and shirt, covered in a jacket, sits on the curb next to a suspect in handcuffs, dressed in black. Two men approach, and the camera turns to identify them as Detectives Brisco and Curtis, Lennie Brisco an order man in his 40s with a gruff exterior, Rey Curtis a younger man with an inquisitive face.]
Brisco: What's the situation here? Man: Officer Mike Mobles, off-duty, from the 2-7. Curtis: What's the charge? Mobles: Assaulting an officer. After I told him I was an officer, he took a swing at me. [The suspect coughs loudly and looks to the side.] Brisco: Who's this guy? Mobles: He's a professional wrestler, visiting with some family. He was out here on the street, obviously upset. Says his name's Creed.
Curtis: Creed what? Creed: Just Creed. [Brisco nods and frowns in disbelief, Curtis rolls his eyes.] Curtis: Good thing you took him down, eh? Mobles: Actually, once I told him he was under arrest, he was pretty cooperative. Guess he didn't want to make it any worse for himself.
[Curtis and Brisco turn to walk away.] Brisco: Mobles is lucky he didn't end up in a double-back strangehold. [Both men laugh as the scene fades, and the opening credits roll.] ----- [Inside a police station, at facing desks, Brisco and Curtis pour over paperwork, and several black video cassettes are stacked on Curtis's side.]
Curtis: I watched some of this stuff last night. Creed is a heavy hitter, comes in swinging and doesn't go down easy. Got a bunch of catch phrases to get the crowd going, too.
Brisco: Catch phrases? Curtis: I guess it's the 'in' thing for wrestlers. Something the fans can identify with. Like he says, "Face the Creed," which is supposed to mean it's a mind game as well as a wrestling match. Or he'll say "End of the
Line." Brisco: I get the idea. So does he have any priors? Curtis: Couple of arrests across the country, but in each case, the charges were dropped. I get the feeling that these were bar fights, and nobody wanted to press charges and possibly get themselves arrested as well.
Brisco: So he's got a history of violence. Curtis: Well, considering what he does for a living, that's not surprising. But at least he's never bitten anyone's ear off. [A middle-aged African-American woman approaches the desks, Lieutenant Van Buren drops some papers stapled together in the space between the two detectives.] Van Buren: Mobles has filed suit for damages, he claims Creed nearly broke his jaw. He wants punitive damages, in the amount of five hundred thousand big ones.
Brisco: That'll bankrupt him. Curtis: I don't think so. Have you been to one of these things lately? I took my nephew down to the Garden last month for his birthday. Twenty bucks a pop. Each. Just to get in the door.
Brisco: Makes sense. I wouldn't want to get beat up every night all over the country for minimum wage.
[A new graphic changes our location to: ] SUPREME COURT TRIAL PART 23 [In a courtroom, viewed through a long panning shot, Creed is seen on the defendant's side, with a lawyer from the public defender's office, Lou
Giovanni, who looks generally unremarkable. To their left, making their case are Assistant District Attorneys McCoy and Ross. Jack McCoy is a man in his late middle-ages, with a face of determination, while Anita Ross is a quiet woman in her thirties. A small plaque on the uppermost ledge shows that the Honorable Rosalynne Lenz presides over this courtroom.]
Lenz: In the case of the People vs. Creed - is this a joke? Giovanni: No, ma'am, it's a legal name change. Lenz: Fine, People vs. Creed, the charges are Assault on a Police Officer, and Assault to Cause Bodily Harm, how does the defendant plead?
Giovanni: Not guilty, Your Honor. Lenz: I'll hear bail statements. McCoy: The defendant regularly travels across the country and has no real ties to this community. Recommend denial of bail.
Giovanni: Your Honor, the defendant travels the country as his livelihood, supporting himself and his fiancee, who *does* have ties to the community.
He has already spent a year in counseling, so he carries responsibility for his actions. And he is willing to post any amount of bail requested.
Lenz: I'll take you up on that offer, counselor. Bail is set at seven hundred and fifty thousand.
[As she slams the gavel down, Creed nods agreement to his representation. The scene changes once more before it is identified as: ] OFFICE OF DISTRICT ATTORNEY ADAM SCHIFF [Seated at his desk is the District Attorney Adam Schiff, his grey hair reflective of his knowledge and experience. McCoy and Ross are in the chairs before him.]
Schiff: And there's been no offer made? McCoy: None. Ross: I spoke with Giovanni earlier, trying to entertain possibilties. It sounds like he isn't even remotely interested in pleading.
Schiff: Hmm. Maybe they've got something up their sleeve. McCoy: I've talked to Mobles, he's got a decent background. Transfered out from LAPD a few years ago after two IAD investigations, claimed he wanted to be closer to his family out here.
Schiff: IAD investigations? Ross: Nothing came of them. Schiff: I can't imagine what this Creed character could have up his sleeve. He punched a cop, he's going down. Get him. [McCoy nods as he and Ross rise to leave the office. We return to the courtroom, where a man in his twenties sits in the witness box. McCoy is on the floor, with a file folder in hand.]
Baliff: State your name. Witness: [name withheld]. McCoy: Mr. [name withheld], you worked as a wrestling promoter, President of the
All-Pro Wrestling company from 1996 to 1997, correct?
[name withheld]: Yes. McCoy: And during that time, you employed the defendant? [name withheld]: Correct. McCoy: Can you give us an idea of his behavior while in your employ? [name withheld]: The man was very violent - but then, that's what the people paid to see. He was certainly entertaining, and profitable - from a businessman's point of view.
McCoy: Was it unusual for him to attack people he didn't know? Giovanni: Objection. Lenz: Overruled. [name withheld]: Well, not exactly. Within the business, sure, that's what it's all about. But outside the world of Pro Wrestling? He was known as a bit of a bar fighter, that's one of the reasons I hired him.
McCoy: So he did fight people out of the ring. [name withheld]: I guess. McCoy: No further questions. Giovanni: [standing up] Did Creed ever attack a security officer? [name withheld]: No. Giovanni: Did he ever attack a fan? [name withheld]: Not to my knowledge. Giovanni: No further questions. [The witness on the stand is now Mike Mobles.] Mobles: So I said, "Hey, look buddy, I'm a police officer, and I'm asking you to quiet down." And that's when he popped me.
McCoy: And you're certain you identified yourself. Mobles: Absolutely. [McCoy sits down as Giovanni stands, walking out on to the floor of the courtroom.] Giovanni: So you're certain you indentified yourself as a police officer? Mobles: [indignant] Of course! Giovanni: Are you aware that if you did not, you would not only be guilty of perjury, but the charges would most likely be dropped from my client?
Mobles: [furrow his brow] Yeah. Giovanni: Have you ever been investigated by Internal Affairs? Mobles: Twice back in California, but nothing ever Giovanni: [cuts him off] Is it true you have a lawsuit filed against the defendant for punative damages, for five hundred grand?
Mobles: Yeah, but Giovanni: Isn't it true that a conviction here would go a long way toward winning your lawsuit?
McCoy: [stands up] Objection, Your Honor! Giovanni: [sits down] No further questions. [Creed is now on the stand.] Giovanni: You realize you're in a spot of trouble here, Mr. Creed. Creed: Yes. Giovanni: A policeman has already testified that you struck him. Creed: Yes he did. Giovanni: That wasn't a smart thing to do, hitting a man who says he's a cop. Creed: I know. I didn't. Giovanni: Pardon me? You didn't hit a police officer? Creed: I didn't hit a man who identified himself as a police officer. Giovanni: Now, sir, I know I'm your attorney, but realistically - how can you expect your word to be taken over the word of an officer of the law.
Creed: I don't. That's why I brought proof. [Giovanni raises a video tape laying on the desk over his head.] Giovanni: Is this your proof? Creed: Yes. As a wrestler, we often film an interview, make a speech, put together a video package, that type of thing. It creates more interest in our matches.
Giovanni: Can you tell us about this one? Creed: Sure. Angel, my fiancee, was in the building visiting her family. I went outside to have a smoke, and the IeWC cameraman showed up to get an interview. So I told him we'd do it right there, outside the apartment building.
Giovanni: I assume you've seen this tape already. Creed: Yes I have, and the cameraman kept the tape rolling while Officer Mobles approached. Giovanni: I'd like to enter this as Defense Exhibit A. [The tape is played, the TV screen used to view it fills the picture.] ----- [Creed flicks the cigarette butt aside...right into a pedestrain walking toward him.] Man: Hey. Buddy. You wanna watch where you throw that? Creed: [head whips around, a sneer forming across his lips] You wanna go f(bleep) yourself?
Man: Look, let's just calm down here, all right? [Creed lunges forward, his right hand shooting out to land flush on the jaw of the bystander. He sinks like a rock.]
[An extended moment later, Creed is still standing over the fallen pedestrian, watching. The sneer slowly melts into a frown. He rolls his eyes, sighs deeply, shakes his head, and reaches out his arm to pull the man back to his wobbly feet.]
Creed: Look. I'm... It's not your fault, not your problem. I'm sorry. Man: [reaching under his jacket] Not as sorry as you're going to be. Creed: [brings his arms up quickly, poised protectively over his chest, yet ready to strike again] Oh yeah?
Man: Yeah. [pulls out a wallet and lets it fall open, the gleaming golden shield reflecting a small amount of light] You're under arrest for assaulting an officer.
----- [The tape is paused on that frame, and the view returns to the courtroom, where all are silent for an extended moment.]
Giovanni: No further questions, Your Honor. [McCoy and Ross exchange meaningful glances as murmurs from the people in attendance flutter through the air. The gavel bangs once, then again.]
[The Assistant D.A.s are now leaving the courthouse, walking down the front steps.] McCoy: Without his credibility, Mobles was useless on the stand. Ross: I've heard he's suspended pending investigation. McCoy: I guess the idea of suing a professional wrestler clouded his judgment. Ross: Odds are he's seen the last of his law enforcement career. McCoy: As Creed would say, it's the "End of the Line." [Fade to black.]
