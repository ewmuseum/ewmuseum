Troy Majors RP opinions are welcome

2 August 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Scene: The screen is black, suddenly the sound of somebody typing away like crazy on a keyboard is heard. Gold script letters forming the words WWO UPDATE fill the screen. The screen explodes and David Rigor is seen sitting behind a wooden desk. He is wearing a white shirt and WWO: Attitude silk tie. Behind
Rigor hang giant posters of the WWO's stars. Eric Draven sitting in the corner with Scotty Davis jumping in the air next to him, Brian Bradley holding the national title and wearing that sadistic smile, Chris Bowenstern adjusting his tie, A black and white picture of Jack Daniels, and a picture of Troy Majors with his arms crossed across his chest and the WWO world title around his waist. Rigor shuffles some papers and begins to speak.]
DAVID RIGOR: Welcome to this edition of WWO update. Last week on Primetime we saw a series of despicable acts that revolved around WWO World Champion Troy
Majors. Recently John Jacobs sat down with the champ at a restaurant for a candid and intense interview. Troy Majors covers everything from Jack Daniels to Chris Bowenstern to Triple XXX.
[A large screen rises up from behind Rigor's desk. The camera zooms in on the screen and it fades to an empty restaurant. It's an old family style, Italian eatery. Chandeliers hang from the high ceiling. All the chairs are turned upside down on the tables and the bartender is packing up indicating the place is closed. The scene shifts to the back end up the restaurant, Troy Majors sits at the table playing with his fork. He is wearing a black T-shirt with a silver logo of a cybernetic head with glowing eyes on the front. Majors has a bandage on his forehead and his right bicep is wrapped in an ACE bandage. An elderly Italian waiter puts a huge plate of pasta down in front of Majors and nods to him. Majors jams his fork into the table hard bending a couple of the prongs and runs his fingers through his short brown hair. Footsteps echo through the empty restaurant and shake Majors from whatever thought just went through his mind. John Jacobs takes a seat across from Majors. John is wearing a pair of black dress pants and a green, silk dress shirt.]
JOHN JACOBS: Good evening Troy. TROY MAJORS: What's so good about it? JACOBS: Do you want me to come back after you're done with your meal? MAJORS: I'm not really that hungry any way. Let's get this over with. JACOBS: Let's cut to the chase. You were scheduled to say something about the death of Jack Daniels on this past Primetime, but for obvious reasons that chance never came. Is there anything you would like to say about Jack to his family? A lot of people are curious as to your feelings. You were his opponent when the horrible tragedy took place.
MAJORS: I'm not going to come out here and cry any crocodile tears or blow smoke up his ass. I'm not going to say anything about how he was a great guy cause I don't think he was. A lot of guys are using this whole thing to get themselves some cheap heat and THAT pisses me off. When I see those rat bastards in Triple XXX coming out here with cardboard cutouts of the man, when
I see Chris Bowenstern using Jack's death to further his personal vendetta against me....it gets me heated. I don't need that crap to get a reaction, so I am not going to spend the rest of my life talking about Jack Daniels. I will say this about the man, he was one of the toughest brawlers this world has ever seen and I think that's exactly how he wants to be remembered. He gave me one hell of a fight and I think that's going to make Jack smile more than anything else anybody could say about him. Jack wherever you are may you find the peace in death, that you could never find in life.
JACOBS: I know you don't want to talk about Jack Daniels, but what happened on
Primetime does need to be addressed.
MAJORS: Bowenstern called me a murderer. He sunk as low as many alive could go by using what happened at Senseless Violence to get under my skin and try and sucker me into a match. Everybody discounts the conspiracy theories but after
Primetime there can be no doubt in anybody's mind just what is going on around here. Triple XXX wants to cloud the issue by talking about some relationship between McCoys and Majors, well I'd really like to know how in the hell Erin covered things up when she has absolutely no power over what goes on in the locker room and on the arena floor. There is no cover up. Jack and I beat the hell out of each other just like we said we would, just like CB wanted us to.
If I wouldn't have rolled out of the way of Jack's dive there would be two funerals going on in wrestling right now.
JACOBS: Will this tragedy have any impact on you? MAJORS: What kind of stupid question is that? When your opponent dies it's going to have an effect. Not the effect you might think though, you see this just fortified in my mind how quick this could all end. I have more guys than ever looking to take me out and if I'm going to go, I'm going to make sure the people I care about are taken care of. There is no way in hell I'm getting taken down before I get my million dollar deal, my pursuit of the contract that the WWO owes me just got a whole lot more serious.
JACOBS: Some people will think that's cold. MAJORS: It's reality, that's what it is. Jack is gone, it wasn't my fault, there is nothing I can do to bring him back. I have to look out for my interests and my family's interests. I told you I'm not going to shed any fake tears. I didn't want the man to die, but the fact is he shouldn't have been out there in the first place. He shouldn't have been wrestling with a blood alcohol level that high, but this is a cut throat business and the draw a Majors vs.
Daniels match had is why he was allowed to compete. Bowenstern didn't kill Jack
Daniels, I didn't kill Jack Daniels, the business killed Jack Daniels and that is all I have to say about it.
JACOBS: Fair enough. What about the world title match against Brady Wilkes? You seemed shocked and shaken when you unmasked him. It was the first time I have ever seen you stop fighting.
MAJORS: If you just looked into the eyes of a guy you were forced to cripple five years earlier, who you never expected to see again I think you'd be a little shocked too. Yeah, it was unexpected, it threw me off for a bit. When I injured Wilkes, when I tore his knee out, it wasn't something I wanted to do.
It was something I was forced to do. I didn't shed a tear over his career ending injury because he brought it on himself, but seeing him back in the ring last week brought back a lot of emotional baggage, a lot of stuff that we went through together that I swept under the carpet. Looking into his eyes I knew I was going to have to do it again to end the match. Bowenstern bought a doctor that cleared him and it cost Brady another major knee injury from what I hear.
The guy is so blinded by bringing me down that he's putting other people at risk to do it. Brady is another guy that had no business in that ring. His knee was shot, Bowenstern didn't care. He brought Wilkes back to get an edge on me, but all this stuff does is get me angrier, makes me want to shred him up.
JACOBS: All the emotional and physical punishment you have taken over the past two weeks has to be taking its toll, how much more can you withstand?
MAJORS: As much as I need to. You see John, I am not a normal human being like you or Rigor or the other guys in the back. When I was seven years old, my mom sent me upstate to live with my grandmother because she wanted to tame a wild child. She hoped some time in tranquility would calm me down. I brought my bike up there with me and one day I was riding up and down the hills. The brakes on the bike weren't the best, in New York City it's not that big of a deal because you put your foot down and stop the bike....in the hills of upstate New York that didn't work so good. I put my foot down and flipped over the handlebars right into the barbed wire surrounding Mr. Johnson's property. As the barbs wrapped around me from head to toe and cut me to pieces I screamed for my mother, but she was hours away. I screamed for my father, but I hadn't seen the ungrateful, drunken bastard in two years, I screamed for my grandmother, but she couldn't hear me. Nobody could help me, nobody ever could. I ripped every last barb out of my body, bled like a pig, picked up my bike and went home. My grandmother cleaned up my wounds and sent me to bed. I couldn't sleep that night, not because of my wounds sticking to the sheets, because something was missing. I went back to Johnson's place and stole some of the pieces of barbed wire that still remained on the ground, put them under my pillow and I slept like a baby. You see John, I welcome pain, I need the pain, I live for the pain, and I'm inspired by the pain. The best part of all is every time I'm in that ring, I think about the pain I have suffered and make sure my opponent suffers worse. Pain motivates men to do different things, some curl up in a ball and beg for mercy, some run away...that's what men like Triple XXX do.
These men will realize the true definition of pain as I cram my fist of hate and contempt right down their throats.
JACOBS: {a little shaken} Every time we get insight into your personal life experiences I want to thank you for keeping most of them private. Speaking of
Triple XXX they haven't given you much respect.
MAJORS: I don't need them to love me, respect me, know where I live or what
I'm about. They want to walk around with their heads up their asses and give speeches longer than the president's state of the union address, fine. It will make each and every FUTURESHOCK I drop on them that much more painful. I'm not out to beat them in matches now John, I don't really care about the end result.
I'm planning on sailing through the WWO in a river of Triple XXX's blood. There will be injuries, there will be broken bones, there will be a massacre and as always, I will be the last man standing. I've sat back and waited on my heels for way too long around here. They say the world champion is the most hunted animal in the jungle....the hunted just became the hunter again. Estacio,
Walavan, Andrew...welcome to the WWO, welcome to the gates of your living hell.
You're excused Jacobs. [Majors shoots Jacobs a glare and Johns jumps up out of his seat knowing full well not to play around with Troy when he gets like this. John rushes out of the restaurant. Majors cracks his knuckles, picks up the bent fork off the table and jabs it into his forearm a few times drawing blood. His nostrils flare out and his expression grows angrier by the second. The old waiter runs over to Majors with a bandage, but pushes him away. Majors stands up, pays his bill and walks out of the restaurant with blood dripping down his arm. FADE]
