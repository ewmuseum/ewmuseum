One year of fedheading

EW University

A Year of Fedheading by [name withheld] Ladies and gentlemen, with myself going through some major life changes starting this weekend..(waits for the wedding march tune), I will be away from the internet and e-wrestling for three weeks. Anyway, I'd been writing the following in my mind for the past couple of weeks, and now have finally gotten all my thoughts together in decent enough format to share them with you. I've been fed-heading the WWO for over a year now, and the following article is my thoughts and opinions on what I've seen as a fedhead, and what i beleve makes a federation work, both as a president and a handler. Please remember that these are my opinions and that they are not intended as flame bait. Besides, I won't be around to read them, and that means that if you try to flame me, the mailbox will get too full to take any more. Other than the above comments this posting is not about humour, it's not about railing against anyone, it's just my honest clean look at what I've done and what I've seen others do, and I hope that some of you can take something out of it. Believe it or not, it's anniversary time again. Those of you who read the joint PPV between the WWO/CWC/NEWA/NLWA/IWL/IWF/WOW, I hope you weren't disappointed. But the War of the Ages 2 marked the big milestone for myself. One year as a federation president for a single organization. It's a label that not too many people can claim, and looking back over everything, it's been a lot of hard work. But the one thing I got out of it was a LOT of fun. I thought at this point, I'd share with you guys some of the things I've learned over the past year, and I hope many of you get as much out of this as I got out of being the center stage of the whole thing. First off, I'm going to give kudos to the federation heads who have reached this milestone. People like Dan, Gerry, Zach, Chris, and so on. People who have run top named federations on the internet. Handlers, those of you who do nothing but complain that they're not giving you a fair shake in the game, take a look at what they do, and what they try to do for you and all the other handlers in your organization. Players for the most part, usually handle no more than 3 characters in a single federation. And most people limit themselves to one or two. That means you have to be creative for only 3 different creations. And believe me, those of you who do it, I'm not taking anything away from you. But remember what a federation officer has to do. He not only has to help you with your storylines, but he also has to help other handlers run stories for their characters. Being creative for 3 is difficult enough, but you're asking someone who has to be creative for 10, 20, maybe even 50 other characters to focus on you, when so many others are doing the same. And Presidents, one thing that I have seen so many people forget when they move up to the front offices. Recognize your handlers. No, don't go out and hand them titles just for being a handler. It doesn't take more than a note or two to say that the handler is doing a great job. Recognize handlers who have worked their way up from bad grammar and spelling and no heat to someone who at least is in the midcard range. To them, that's an accomplishment. And I think you as federation officers should see it as such. Now for a few bits of advice. Don't promise something you can't deliver within reason. Most handlers understand that there is a life off of the internet, and away from this game. They'll understand if you're a bit late with things. But a bit late is not the same as an indefinite postponement. If you can't deliver a product that you've promised, well, maybe you should take a look at what you're promising. At one time, I used to promise two full length written cards a week, with ten matches to a card. But i never had any staff to write matches. That means I did all the writing myself. Twenty full blown matches a week to put out and in a reasonable time frame. It couldn't be done. But I kept trying, and failing. And because I was failing, the product was failing. The product was failing and I was going through burnout. The handlers weren't happy with the delays, and they weren't happy with the finished product. I had to step back and take a look at what I was doing wrong. And I found my mistake and cut back on the total output in a week. A human body can only exert itself for so long before it gives out, and the human mind works the same way as I discovered. But the key was, I found my mistake, I admitted it and I corrected it. Today, all too often, in particular in recent weeks as I've read on RSPW-F, I've seen handlers and presidents get in arguments to say "they were right in their beliefs". Gentlemen and ladies, we all make mistakes. And the best federations are the ones who recognize those mistakes and make up for them. And the best federation presidents are the ones who admit they make mistakes. We may pretend that we are god in this game. That in our universe we are omnipotent and omnipresent. But in reality, we don't know it all, we're not right about everything. And if you make a mistake, 'fess up to it. Admit you made an error, and if you can find a reasonable way to make it up to your handlers, do it. Sometimes, just admitting you make a mistake is more than enough for the players, and when you admit you erred, they'll actually show you a bit of respect. I know the handlers I help orchestrate did, and you know something, they got a lot more respect from me in the process. Next, I've already told you that the federation presidents have to recognize your handlers. Dont' forget to recognize your staffs. I personally would have never been able to keep up what is, in my opinion, the top quality cards and products of recent months without people like [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld] and so on. (And guys I apologize if I didn't mention you but the list is simply too long) And sometime in the past, they've all assisted me in someway or another in running the WWO. It could be as simple as writing a match, producing a card, a magazine, or it could have been as indepth as checking new applicants, planning angles for other characters and so on. Guys, I couldn't have done it without you, and most of you federation heads, I bet you've got a staff that you wouldn't have lasted without. So make sure they know how much they appreciate what you've done. Next, and this is a biggie. You are not in competition with your handlers. Your goal is not to make yourself the focus of why a character won or loss a feud or battle. Your goal is to give two or more handlers the ability to duke it out with one another with one fair and impartial judge. And you must be fair. I've got a great number of friends who I don't think my life would be the same without anymore, but they know that when I'm judging battles, the friendship is momentarily left behind. And if it's someone who I've had problems with recently, well, I will put my anger or dislike of them behind me as well. One of the things that I've disliked about a number of federations is the "no contest" clause. Where if a choice is needed, and the president is unsure, he finds someone to declare the match a "no-contest", as though the players never RP'd for it. Close RP battles I can understand, and necessary rulings to further a feud can be accepted, but somewhere you have to have winners and losers. Some federations I've seen have had week-in week-out battles that just went nowhere, and no winner, and the handlers got bored. That is not a good thing for your fed, it's not good for your federation, and it's not good for you. You took a responsibility to make some tough decisions when you became a federation president, and you need to make those decisions one way or another. And along the line of "responsibility", there is a necessary thing federation presidents must do, and that is "keep your word". A friend informed me of a feud he was in, in which the federation president explained to the handler what he should do to win the contest. The president did everything but guaranteed him a title after the battle. The contest came along, and the president somewhere said to himself apparently "I want to keep this feud going", and changed the outcome to a no-contest. The handler was irate, because he had done everything that was asked of him, and still did not get the recognition for this effort. Even worse, was that the president did not discuss the possibility of continuing the feud with the handlers. Next, try to get as many people involved in the action. I've had a number of solid roleplayers come to my doors looking for a signin sheet, simply because they got forgotten about by other presidents in their current organizations. I should also make a point here, handlers, please remember that we're usually selecting you over a large number of other applicants, and we put effort into convincing you to sign with our organization, so please participate. But back to the point at hand. If it's from some of your weakest players, give them the opportunity to compete at some level. If you can, put them against other players of a similar level, give them a playing field they can compete against. And if they want to test their luck against a bigger star down the road, give them the opportunity. You can only get better if you play against people who are going to make you work at it. One of our handlers, and Papool, I hope you don't mind if I list you by name, has a character who had always been relegated to the background, simply because he didn't have the time to put in a full effort in a solid RP contest. One day, he asked to take on one of our bigger stars, just to see what he could do. And you know what? Papool's effort after that battle increased 100 fold. He probably could have taken on half of the roster and beaten them with the effort he put out. IT just so happened he was taking on one of the half he couldn't beat this time, but he made this superstar work for the win. And Papool has continued to show significant improvement since that battle, and for the first time in quite a few months, he has gotten himself involved in a long term feud/angle. And speaking of improvement, don't be afraid Presidents to help out your handlers. Let them know where they can improve their games. Over one period, I helped a handler with his roleplaying writing. He sent me his roleplays, I told him where things did not look so good in it (spelling, grammar, choppiness, etc.), and I sent it back to him and suggested he do some re-editing. Did I do the re-edits? No. He did all the work, but he had more fun at that time when he was getting help than I think he ever had before in the WWO. Who knows, maybe after I end my federation presidency routine, I'll start a school for e-wrestlers. I"d like to think i have the credentials to do so, and might just share my knowledge with the players even further. Next. Presidents, don't become predictable. Throw a surprise or two in there. If everyone knows what the next thing coming is, why are they going to even try any more? Make an upset title holder on occasion... I did once, and it shocked 90% of the handlers, except for the champion, who knew he was going to lose the belt, and the few people who were in on the angle. Heck, now and then the topic still comes up (and may even become part of another angle in the near future.) And I guess I'll start to wrap things up here. First, I'm going to say, don't knock someone without giving them the opportunity to prove themselves. Now, I know we can't take every handler who wants to put a wrestler in our organization, but don't limit yourself to the big names of this sport. And yes, I'll do some name dropping here. It would be nice if every organization could add someone like a Brody Thunder or Ronnie D or Otto Verhoeven or Creed to their rosters. But in passing over some of your current applicants for this big names, you might miss the next break through star. Some people were born with a gift for writing, while some people i know have worked hard to learn what and how to write things in this sport. If i took a couple of characters from the WWO and showed you their original Roleplays and then showed you what he does now, you'd never believe they were the same person. I took a chance on these people, and it's paid off for both me and them. Just remember that under some of that coal, you might find a diamond just waiting to be found. And finally, a word of advice. I'd like to give some advice to those people out there who want to jump right in and start their own federation. And I know this advice has gone out before, but work your way up. Learn what a federation president has to do. Join another organization as a staff member, and see what those presidents and staffers do in a regular work week. And then slowly add on to your job. Learn everything you can while you can. Sometimes you'll end up getting thrown into situations you're not sure about, but you'll have someone with the know-how to deal with it, and they can help you along. And then use what you learn and then start your own organization, or take over when someone needs you to. And those of you who are trying to start your own organization, but can't get the talent you want. Let the players know what your talent is. Who can vouch for you, who can say this guy has potential as a federation president? And last but not least, i'd like to give you guys a statement that one of the WWO handlers gave to me... "Mark, I don't know how you do it. Putting up with the egos and the problems, and still keep yourself in control of the scene. I would have folded a long time ago." Remember guys, this is a game. This is not work. The moment you start taking this as something that you have to maintain control over all the time, that's the moment you need to step down and back away. I learned right after my case of the burnout, that if you take this game too seriously, you'll never have any of the fun and excitement that I've gotten in the recent months of e-wrestling. One day, when I have nothing left for my character in e-wrestling to do, and I've taken him to the top of the mountain wherever he goes, I am still going to be participating in this contest, because this "fed-heading", is, simply put, one of the funnest things I've ever done, and you should find it the same way. You should enjoy your hobbies, and I...well, thanks to my friends here on RSPW-F, in the WWO, even on IRC (yep, ill let you guys and gals qualify there too), I truly have as much fun running the World Wrestling Organization as I have with anything I've ever done. [name withheld] President of the World Wrestling Organization Handler of christopher stonebreaker and having one hell of a great time doing both!! --
