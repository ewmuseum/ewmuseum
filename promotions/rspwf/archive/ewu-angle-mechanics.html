THE TRI-ANGLE: A GUIDE TO THE MECHANICS OF ANGLES Many of you reading this will be accomplished roleplayers. Many of you do what I`m about to put into plain words without thinking about it. But then there people who really aren`t aware of this...or perhaps don`t do anything about it. Whoever you are...read on. It could be the difference between languishing at the bottom of a fed you don`t enjoy to riding high in a fed that you can`t get enough of. The difference simply is running a good angle, by being a good roleplayer. It`s good roleplayers that make a good fed. What is a good roleplayer? It`s something more than just being able to write decent flashes. It`s about creating angles, and running them effectively. People remember good angles more than they remember good flashes. A good angle I would consider is something that puts your wrestler in a story-line. It doesn`t matter whether it`s original or not. It`s just important to be involved in an angle. And run it successfully. So how can you run an angle successfully? The first thing you need is an idea. Perhaps you`ve seen a character you`d like to feud/team/ally with. Perhaps you have an idea of how you`d like the storyline to run. Second step is to e-mail that person, and the fedhead, with your ideas. Fact is, most people are more than happy to run with angles, and will often provide suggestions for making it better. The important thing is to have THE TRI-ANGLE which is yourself, the persons you wish to run the angle with, and the fedhead. Why is this important? Quite simply, everyone will be focused on running that angle. If you try running an angle without someone`s knowledge, half the time, they won`t realise. I`ve seen it happen too many times. The greatest storylines are those run using the Tri-Angle. And the people who run storylines in this way I have always seen rise to become acknowledged as great. The Hardin`s, Starr`s, Byron`s, Creed`s and Kowalski`s all operate with this method. Why did they get acknowledged as great? Not simply because they wrote good flashes, but because they devised angles with other people that people remember. They ran great storylines. Again, people wonder why the IIWF was so popular. It`s not simply because Daniel put in so much work, it`s because he strived to ensure players had angles and ideas worked out for months ahead. Sure, it doesn`t have to stick to the plan (in fact, it`s better if it develops naturally), but there is a plan there. Out there in the e-wrestling world, there are many good flashers who could go on to become great roleplayers. I hope this goes some way to bringing that about. As for fedheads - make sure your players have access to everyone else`s e-mail address.
