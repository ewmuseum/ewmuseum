[ESSAY] Swearing in Roleplays

24–27 January 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There are quite a few of these "essays" on RSPWF lately, and they've all proven to be pretty helpful to a number of players, I'm sure. Although I've seen essays on how to build a good face/heel, I haven't seen essays on how to maintain them. How they conduct themselves. Here's where this comes into play. Did you ever notice how many guys in e-feds today use the word, "fuck"? It's just a common word now. They don't bother to put in a [BLEEP], they don't make any big deal at all. One question... how do we know when your character is really mad? Swearing has become too common in roleplays, and has lost its effectiveness when wrestlers let themselves "slip" in anger. Don't get me wrong, words like ass, bitch, and bastard are fine. No bleeps, nothing. But when you step into the range of the fucks and shits and sexual references, try to use some control. Ask yourself, "If this were a real federation, would my promoter FIRE me over this interview?" Yes, I believe so. The fact of the matter is, you do NOT need swearing in roleplays to get heat. I'll use two prime examples right now: "Dreamlover" Trey Porter and "The Attack" Zack Malone. Porter is maybe the biggest heel of all-time in e-wrestling, and I have never ONCE heard him swear in an interview. He uses wit, intelligence, and even effective body gestures to get him over. Malone is quickly becoming the most hated man in any fed he enters. I have never once had him swear to get attention. There was a time when he was ripped off big time, and he was really mad, so in the interview, he was ranting and raving, and a put an assorted [BLEEP] in the interview. Much more effective than the actual word. At least it keeps the opponent guessing as to what you called them. :) Just the plain fact is, a page of swearing is not a good roleplay. Not even a fairly good roleplay. I'm glad to see a lot of federations taking it upon themselves to [BLEEP] out interviews by themselves, and the FWLI has even gone so far as to release any handler who swears in interviews. Just control the mouth. It isn't appreciated. -CQ- ------------------------ [27 January 1997 — Robert] I have to agree that profanity should be cut down in flashes. First of all let me say that I don't find the use of words like "fuck" and "shit" offensive. To me, and I am sure to most of my generation, they are just as natural to use in everyday life as any other word. However, there are reasons why I believe swearing should be curtailed in e-wrestling:- *E-wrestling is supposed to reflect real-life wrestling. In order to suspend disbelief, e-feds should generally stay pretty close to their real-life counterparts. In real wrestling, an interviewee who said "fuck" every sentence on live TV would be fired straight off, it just does'nt seem realistic to have e-wrestlers 'effin and blindin' all over the shop... *Swearing is cheap heat. Anybody can badmouth an opponent and call him a "cock-sucking faggot" or whatever, but the insult is hardly original, in fact it is downright boring. One thing I have noticed in some feds that permit excessive swearing is that many of the roleplayers will become nothing more than swearing billboards. There is nothing to their interviews except a long list of profanity, no room for character development, no distinctive personality, just a guy who swears. [name withheld] said in his post that you don't need swearing to get heat. I'll go one step further than that and say that swearing can actually reduce heat. I know how I feel when I see a flash filled with profanity, and I am sure many others know this experience, I don't feel like I want to beat up the character, I just feel bored. Like "oh man, another mediocre wrestler who can't think of anything interesting to say so he just swears." Come on guys, use a bit of wit, imagination and originality huh? Some characters can get away with swearing a lot and still be interesting. For example in the IIWF there is a character called Steve Kowalski who generally gives a great interview and he's got a mouth like a trucker. Even so, the swearing does not really add anything to his interviews (in fact it can be annoying with constant BLEEPS breaking it up) and IMO, he would be just as effective with a lot loss profanity. So in summary, I propose pretty much the same thing as Quigley: that excessive swearing should be restricted to only the most extreme and emotional circumstances (like the Pillman gun incident on RAW), at any other time it just ends up making your character sound lame.
