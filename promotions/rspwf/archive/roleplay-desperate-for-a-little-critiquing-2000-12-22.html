Roleplay, desperate for a little critiquing

22 December 2000
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well here we go, this is my first RP in about a year, and it's for a brand new character I created, "The Dream" Jerome Davis. The RP is designed as a introductory flash and I wanted to get his attitude, appearance, and gimmick accross as best as I could. The RP may be a little ranting, but that's because
I just wrote it while I was sitting here and haven't really edited it all that much. Nonetheless, I'd like to get some critiquing from the fedheds of the more respected federations or respected handlers. Well, seeing that I've babbled long enough, I'll leave you all to my little literary achievement.
[The scene opens into a new, well-equipped gym. There are mirrors lining all the walls, and the various weight machinery is all polished and well maintained. Oddly enough, despite the good condition of the gym, there is only one man working out. Sitting far back in the corner the camera slowly moves towards him, but his back is to the camera, making his identity a mystery.
He's a medium sized young man, probably 6'0" and 230 pounds. He's wearing no shirt and as he does his concentration curls you can see the well defined muscles in his back work to support his arms. The camera pauses for a second as the man continues to lift, but he seems to be oblivious to the camera's existence. The cameraman goes to move forward, when suddenly the man begins to speak, stopping him in his tracks.]
Man: You know, not all of us had it easy, not all of us came from a good family, not all of us had every advantage growing up, and not all of us had everything given to us…
[The man stops lifting, dropping the weight to the floor. He pauses a minute and then turns his head to the camera slowly, exposing his clean shaven face, flashing blue eyes, and a mischievous grin.]
Man: …but then again, some of us did. Some of us were born wealthy, some of us had a father who's a pro wrestling legend, some of us were blessed with natural athletic grace, good looks, and charisma, some of us had only the best training growing up, some of us…
[The man springs to his feet, spinning around to face the camera and show off his perfect physique.]
Man: …get to be me. Wait, I take that back, only I get to be me. Only I get to be the perfect athlete. Only I get to be every promoters dream, and only I get to be the crown prince of pro wrestling. Only I get to be Jerome Davis.
Now in six months time I'm sure I'll be a household name, I'm sure I'll have beaten all of you second rate athletes, and I'm sure I'll sit alone at the top of this federation, and probably this sport. But please don't be angry, because all of this is part of a plan. You see, not too long ago the fates looked down at professional wrestling and saw a sport parched for true talent and bloated with worthless trash masquerading as "superstars". The fates of course found this unsatisfactory, but since a champion of suitable quality didn't grace the planet, they sent me to save the world's greatest sport from its own mediocrity. I guess that makes me a messiah of sorts and I guess that makes you all witnesses to the reclaiming of this sport by the elite. You may feel a little jealousy at first, but in time I'm sure you'll all realize you didn't really own this sport, you were just watching it until its rightful owners returned to restore it to its past glory, and, you guessed it, I'm the first of that reclaiming movement. You see, we're all just actors in the theatre of pro wrestling, it's just that I'm the star and you're all the support players. I guess you can take comfort in knowing that no piece of work can truly be a masterpiece without the supporting cast, however, and likewise I cannot bring glory back to this sport without all you pieces of worthless trash to walk all over. Just think, in 20 years you'll be able to tell your grandkids you got to stand in the same ring as "The Dream", maybe not for very long, but you stood there nonetheless.
[Davis smiles and towels himself off, gracefully stretching out his back as he begins to cool down.]
Davis: Now I'd love to go on and enlighten all you little people further, but
I'm afraid I may have already overloaded that simple bundle of synapses you folks call a brain. In summation, and I know you all love summation, "The
Dream" is here to bring some respectability to this sport and you're all about to get horrible beatings to help bring that glorious event to fruition. You can scoff now, and please do if it'll help you feel more secure, but I guarantee you in six months Jerome Davis will be the king of this sport, not to mention the name on everyone's lips and the poster on every kid's wall. Six short months folks, let the countdown begin…
[Jerome throws his towel onto the ground, walking away as the camera fades out.] -[name withheld] Handler of "The Dream" Jerome Davis Ex-handler of Zeus, "Your favorite pro-wrestling diety" "Whatever floats your boat"
