Nothing can be seen and no shapes can be made out. Suddenly, a glimmer appears sparkling the center of the room. It appears to be a blade made of pure silver. Right now, only the sword can be seen illuminating in the room. You can hear rain begin to fall
in the background. Drizzling at first, then it breaks into a full thunderstorm. There is a crash of lightning and for a brief second the figure can be made out. He is draped in all black cloth cape that covers his entire body. He is wearing a black and white yin yang mask to cover his
face. His hair is dirty blonde and spiked. Only his arm can be seen because it is carrying the silver sword shown below. There is a Japanese inscription on the sword that runs from the top to the bottom of the blade.
It translates into "One that cuts into the very soul". With this the figure can be identified. It is none other than Diehard. A mysterious dark and twisted figure that lurks in the night. A man who plays by his own rules.
Once a relentless hardcore icon, he is now a more calm and spiritual person. He has an unique code of chivalry where he plays judge, jury, and executioner to all of those who he finds to be............"unjust".
Sometimes they are evil down to their very soul. Sometimes they are misguided figures needing a spiritual light to carry them through the darkness. Sometimes they are good and pure. Sometimes no one can understand the path that Diehard chooses or why he chooses it. You see, it is impossible to characterize one who Diehard has decided is "unjust". You must simply pray you are not one of the unlucky ones. There is another crash of lightning. For a split second again Diehard can be seen putting his sword, which for those of you who don't know or haven't figured out is his trusty companion Soultaker, away. Once again the room is pitch black.
The rain continues to fall as the crash of thunder and crackling of the lightning can still be heard.)
Diehard:I have returned my friends. I had left under circumstances that mere mortals such as yourselves could not begin to understand. The mere thought of what I had to do what send men as weak as yourselves running in fear. You would shriek like the insane howl of a banshee's song. But what
I had to do is of my own concern. All that should matter to the rest of you
is that I have returned. This time, I will not need the likes of anyone to do my bidding. First of all, I would like to address my dear friend Ricky
Deathwish. Deathwish, how could one so feeble minded as yourself be running
this federation? All you understand is sheer brutality. You are a wrestler. No better than anyone else here. It was fun using you as my little puppet though Ricky. Remember when I had you destroy Dark Wolf and his WOLF Empire for me Deathwish? That was fun wasn't it. I did promise you a favor in return though.....but oh well. The fact of the matter remains as this Deathwish.....I know how to control you. I understand your most secret desires and your deepest fears. I know what makes you tick. I know how to manipulate you into doing EXACTLY what I wanted you to do. I have no quarrel with you Deathwish. You were merely used as a pawn in a chess game you still do not know exists. Do not dare think that you can do anything to harm me. Strike me down and I will more powerful than before.
It is just a warning to you Deathwish. Keep and past in the past or suffer in the present.
(There is a loud crash of lightning. The bolt glows so that once again
Diehard can be seen. Now, his mask is off and his face for a moment can be made out. It is, of course, the face of Eric Reeves. Eric Reeves, a man who has played the role of several different people in his life. The most famous of these roles is as "The Ripper" Eric Reeves. Quite possibly one of
the most, if not the, most hardcore wrestler to walk the face of the earth. Known for shattering beer bottles over the heads of opponents, throwing them into steel doors, even hanging them off cages there was nothing Reeves would not do. Until one day he accidentally caused the death of another man. But that is a story best left untold. All that matters is the resultant factor was the cause of Reeves' insanity. He became a malicious beast. Caring not
for himself, his opponent, his family or his friends. Only in the destruction of others. Until one day he met a man named Hiro Ishizuku, a
Japanese wrestler/sensei known in Japan simply as "The Great One". It was
Hiro that taught Reeves about honor, chivalry, meditation, and justice.
That is when Eric Reeves died. Like the great phoenix though he was reborn this time. Stronger than ever. That is when Diehard was created. He was given the name Diehard by Ishizuku because "he could not be stopped. To end
him would only bring him back smarter, more focused, and stronger". His face looks different than before though. His rugged good looks seemed to have disappeared. His face seems focused now. Intense. You look into his eyes and see nothing but pain. He is empty and cold.)
Diehard:Now I must deal with the matter at hand. That is non other than you Stephen Storm. Maybe you don't remember me Storm but I surely remember you. When I first entered the IWA, it was you who was found to be "unjust" by I. I had chosen you as my first victim. You were to be a martyr, you should of been honored Storm. It was a blessing that should of been embraced with open arms. Instead, you fled. You were a coward Storm. Driven out by the weakness and fear in your heart. For years, I have waited for the time when
we can meet again Storm. Maybe then, you could of held your own against me for a while trying to avoid your inevitable demise. But let me assure you of something Storm running was the biggest mistake that you could of made.
I am one thousand times more powerful now than I ever was and one million times more powerful than you could ever hope or pray to be Storm. I know you do not believe me Storm. I realize that a concept that great is something above your head. I must admit, it is one even I have trouble grasping. Unfortunately for you Storm, it is true though. No longer will
I
bother beating you for the sheer entertainment of those I fight for, but this one is personal Storm. Avoiding your destiny can only make it worse and now it has caught up to you Storm........there is no escape...................
(There is another crash of lightning) Diehard:This brings me to a end dear friends. I will have more to reveal to you all when the time is right. There are many more I wish to address. Mcbaine, Iceman, Assassin, and let us not forget the clown prince of wrestling Vejita himself. I have big plans for you Vejita. Very big plans.
But let us not worry about this now. For now you should all be celebrating my return. I suggest you all start living life to the fullest. While you still have the time. As for you Stephen I suggest you make peace with all of those who have angered you. Make love to your soon to be widow
Stephanie and pray to whatever god you believe in. Fate is at your doorstep, and he is carrying a razor sharp sword.......

[The sound fades out.] ====================================== MENELAUS - THE BLACK ROSE ====================================== A shadowy figure careens blindly through the driving sleet, barely visible as he flees some unseen enemy, as though pursued by some savage force of nature. The headlights down the interstate do little to illuminate his flight. One lone car, not noticing a sudden curve in the road, swerves, almost too late, to miss this shadow of a man. Almost too late, or so he thought. The figure drops to the ground without warning. The car pulls over to the breakdown lane and Chris Ander, a defense attorney from
New York City, runs to inspect his victim. Untouched by the car, it appears that the exhaustion of the mere thought of collision has caused this man's collapse. Chris, aware of the danger that may lie in store for him, removes from his trunk a blanket, wraps up the nearly frozen man, and coaxes the pathetic, half-conscious creature into the passenger seat of the car. 35 miles outside of town, he turns around and sets his course for the nearest hospital, nearly 45 miles away. Blasting the heat to remove the bitter chill from the early January weather Chris gazes in curiosity and concern at his new guest.
"I've seen a lot of criminals in my time, buddy," he says to his unconscious passenger, "and usually I can spot them a mile away, but you....you my friend, have me completely intrigued"
The man wore a long black coat which looked as much a victim of the weather as the poor man, yet is was obviously more expensive than anything Chris had ever been able to afford. The man wore an expression of utter anguish as he slept, yet something about his eyes betrayed guilt of some unknown crime. The wronged criminal, almost justifiably wronged, yet what crime had he committed, if any? From what was he running? A sudden stirring in the seat next to his shook
Chris from his thoughts. "Thank you" were the first words out of the man's mouth, semi- conscious only, as if he was thanking Chris for more than Chris would ever know, for things Chris had not yet even done. Something suddenly told him that somehow, this man was to change his life.
Awakening fully with a start, the man, panic-stricken, shouted "Who are you? Where am I? Where is Mandy? Where are we going?" His eyes blazed with fear.
"Calm down, you were running along the highway, I thought I hit you, but it turns out that you passed out from shock. You may even have a rather serious case of hypothermia on your hands. I'm taking you to the hospital back in the city."
The dread in the man's eyes multiplied, and he barely mustered the words "please, god, no, not the city." He turned to Chris, eyes pleading, and with fear in his voice to match that in his heart begged "Please, sir,
I
don't know who you are, but I thank you for your kindness. I appreciate your concern, but please, you will do me a much greater service to allow me to go on my own way. If you would let me back out on the highway,
I would be forever grateful." "My concern for you turned me around, if you wish, I know not who you are or why your fear of the city is so great, though my only guess is a crime of some sort, but my destination was a small cottage on Virginia
Beach. If you wish me to carry you along, I will do so, for some distance at least, even if it is only enough to warm you up and dry you off. The
January sleet is bitter, and I care too much about my fellow men to turn you loose in this weather. The only thing I ask in return is your story. I am a defense attorney, so I know the value of not admitting guilt, so tell me only what you feel you can, but tell me something, please."
"Thank you, I have no choice but to accept, for my feet will carry me no farther, and such kindness one does not find every day. Please, tell me your name."
"My name is Chris Ander. I am, as I said, a defense attorney in New York City. Yours?" "Call me Menelaus" "I already know your intent to stay, so I trust that your story will come with time. Sleep now, my friend. Regain your energy, and warm yourself."
Within minutes the mysterious stranger slept soundly, snoring subtly, breathing heavily. Chris focused on the road before him, while his curiosity of this newcomer caused him to consider his own past. Chris, born in a small sector of Boston, almost a suburb within the city, never felt as if he belonged anywhere. At the age of eight he and all his childhood insecurities were packed up and moved to a nondescript town in Southern New Hampshire where the cows outnumbered the people and 8 o'clock at night was considered "late". His parents shoved him into a private school where the teachers worshiped him, and the only difficulty he experienced was in putting up with a dress code. He never met any children his own age, and never cared to. By the time he escaped to college, he saw a high-profile., high-income career as his only hope for happiness, thus his degree in law, and subsequent return to city life on the anonymous streets of New York City. At the age of 27 contentment was not yet his, yet he was convinced that it was simply around the next corner. However, little did he realized that in his search for contentment, complacency had set in, and his only hope for satisfaction in life was a drastic change.
Chris was shaken suddenly from his thoughts as Menelaus was shaken violently from some troubling dream. After a moment of disorientation, however, he showed signs of recalling his whereabouts. Without a word of preparation, the stranger jumped immediately into his tale.
"My parents never loved me, but they tried to pretend they did. I was born in Scarsdale without a want in the world, save the want of attention. My family had a history of wealth that went back further than anyone remembered, for all we know we had ancestors in the mob back in the early 1900's. I was a fussy child, yet my parents soon learned that presents allayed my tantrums and assuaged their guilt. Save my unruly temper which had a tendency to flare up only in extreme circumstances, the tantrums became too much effort, I was a content young adult. I grew up with a warped sense of love, yet somehow, this didn't bother me. I harbor no bitterness towards my parents, as I've heard many unloved children do, yet I hold no affection for them either. They were my caretakers, yet when they died I was not hurt. By then, at the age of
23 I was already long since on my own, already quite nicely settled into the day-to-day routine of my job. Perhaps it was this that taught me the true meaning of passion. For all my wealth, I truly loved my architecture career. There was something about designing the small, cozy houses for people, something about designing their security, that appealed to my missing sense of family and enthralled me.
The first time I ever felt this passion for a member of the human race occurred when I met Helen. She was the most beautiful woman I had ever seen, yet her beauty was simple and pure. She was a temporary secretary at my office building, filling in while the regular one was on maternity leave. She grew up in the Bronx, the daughter of a housewife and a professional drunk. From the time she turned 16 she worked fast food places, and never established any references substantial enough to land her anything better. She never admitted it to me, but I think she slept with the owner of the temp agency so that he would hire her.
Never once did life overwhelm her, though, she took everything and everybody that came and went in stride. She remained a perfect rose amidst the smog of New York City. Her spirit was undauntable. After dating for a mere 5 weeks I proposed, and she accepted wholeheartedly. At least I choose to believe it was wholeheartedness, perhaps it was a sort of melancholy desperation. I adored her with all my heart, and though her background made her ill-suited for high society, her natural grace and elegance allowed her to feign whatever experience she lacked. Only an occasional hint of unconformability indicated that she truly felt out of place. On the surface, no woman born and bred with millions looked more at ease at fancy social gatherings, yet beneath that all, she began to feel suffocated. Ever oblivious, I fell more and more in love with her each moment of our marriage.
After a year and a half our daughter Amanda was born. My little Mandy became the second person in my life I truly loved. However, shortly after her birth, my job suddenly determined that the traveling life was for me, leaving me little choice but to leave my beloved family for a few days every month or so. By the time Mandy was turning five, my excursions were less frequent, yet for longer durations. My poor inquisitive baby did not understand why her daddy left her for so long, but she always greeted me with a bear hug and a glowing smile.
One particular time I returned from a business trip to San Fransisco bearing gifts for both my special girls. For my little darling turning 5 years old, I had a watch with a teddy bear on it. Mandy was always fascinated with my pocket watch, and would play with it for hours, just watching the second hand move, wondering why the minute and hour hand never moved, yet still told the time. Her watch had a small pink butterfly as the second hand, which the poor teddy bear was never able to catch. As
Amanda's eyes lit up, I gave her a kiss on the cheek and wished her a happy birthday as she scampered off to marvel at her new toy. For
Helen, despite all the charms of San Fransisco, what caught my eye was one of the most stereotypical souvenirs one can buy, yet I knew it would mean more to her than any other. The gift was a simple, yet elegant snow globe of the eiffel tower. My little reminder to her that despite the late hours, and despite the distant trips, I had not forgotten my promise to her that one day I would show her Paris. The fairy tale wonder in her eyes upon the mention of this dream of hers was priceless.
Suddenly Mandy ran in with a small stuffed bear. "Where did you get that, sweetie?" I chuckled as she and the bear barreled into my arms. "Mommy's friend gave him to me. His name is teddy. Do you like him, daddy? Teddy looks like the bear in my watch!"
I laughed as her eyes sparkled with excitement. "Yes, Mandy, he's wonderful." I had barely pried her out of my arms and gently set her down on the floor when she was gone again in a blur of birthday joy.
"That was nice of Kate to give her that." My wife's best friend tended to live at my house when I was away, so I had been told. She was almost as much Mandy's mother as Helen was. Many a time I would return from work to find Mandy at the zoo, or the park with "Auntie Katie".
"Yes, it was." Helen replied. Her silence surprised me, but I wrote it off as fatigue and perhaps a little resentment for my being gone.
"At the time an awkward moment or two was easy to dismiss, yet over the next few months, Helen began acting more and more distant. Hoping to surprise her, I began planning a vacation to Paris. I would take the time off from work in early January, and we would leave Mandy with
Kate for the two weeks. My plan was to surprise her on Christmas with the news. As the holiday season neared, Mandy began acting strangely also. One day I returned from work only to be greeted by a hasty hello from Helen, and a quiet and contemplative Mandy. Usually cheerful,
Mandy's sullen behavior left me puzzled. After dinner, she approached me and tugged gently on my sleeve."
"Daddy" she nearly whispered. "Yes sweetie?" "What happens to people when they die?" Shocked by this question, it took me a moment or two to respond. "Why do you want to know that?" I asked carefully. "When I was over auntie Kate's house today, a man on the TV got shot. What happened to him?" Helen and I had always monitored Mandy's television consumption rigorously. While we believed in exposing her to the world, we also knew that some things were not meant for 5-year-old eyes and ears, whereas Kate, having no children of her own, was often less discerning about our child's viewing habits.
"Well, Mandy, nobody really knows what happens to people when they...." "But mommy says the people go to a place called Heaven....where is Heaven?" Not a religious man myself, I decided to simplify matters by agreeing with the story she had heard. Uncertainty never helps a confused child.
"Heaven is far, far away, Mandy, it's in the sky." "But how come I can't see it in the sky? And how far, far away is it? As far as Paris? Mommy always talks ‘bout how far away Paris is."
"Yes, sweetie, heaven is far, far away like Paris is." Her wide-eyed wonder growing about this fantastic place, she began to get excited. "So heaven is IN Paris??? Is that why mommy wants to go there so bad? She always says what a nice place heaven is, and what a nice place Paris is." Then concern crept into her voice. "Daddy, if mommy went to Paris would she have to die?" Her lower lip began to quiver.
Before her fear could give way to tears, I eased her mind. "No, Mandy sweetie, heaven is only a PART of Paris, the rest of Paris you can go to without dying. Don't worry, nothing is going to happen to mommy if she goes to Paris."
Her sigh of relief rivaled most small tidal waves, and she attacked me with one of her bear hugs. "Thank you, daddy, I love you!" Then before
I could answer she was running to her room to play.
After that, Mandy's spirits were better for a few weeks, as were Helen's for the most part. My wife, for the first time in months, walked with more of a bounce in her step and a smile on her face. Then came the phone call. One night in mid-December my boss called me at home. Our biggest client at the time was a wealthy man in his mid-thirties from
Japan. He had made nothing short of a small fortune in his brief 10-year tenure in the United States, and was making the huge investment of his first home. Naturally, as the ranking architect at the firm below the bosses, they had me on the project. The man was difficult, and exacting, but he seemed to like me, making things somewhat easier than I had heard rumored from the 4 other firms he had contacted before. Anyhow, the man had FINALLY decided what he wanted his house to look like, despite the 8 other sketches I had already drawn for him. He wanted a replica his childhood house, inflated and ornamented with Greek columns. The catch was this: he wanted his architect to examine the house thoroughly, meaning pictures would not be sufficient, and he wanted the preliminary sketches by December 28th. His wife's birthday was on the 28th and he wanted to surprise her. Apparently they had lived together in that house for 5 years before they had moved to the
USA and she was growing homesick. My flight was already booked to
Tokyo for December 23rd, and my return flight was scheduled for December 26th. My protests fell upon seemingly deaf ears. "This man is our biggest client, and Tokyo won't wait until after Christmas" my boss argued. Helen's protests gave way to sobs, and after pleading for me to quit my job and my arguing that we could not afford it, I spent the next two nights on the couch downstairs. My temper nearly flared up, but I calmed myself by remembering that she had every right to be upset with me for missing Christmas. After all, I had nearly missed Mandy's birthday. Determined not to leave with her mad at me, I decided to give
Helen her Christmas present early. I also assured her that once I was done with this account, I would begin looking for another job. I had no desire to sacrifice my family for ANY job.
My trip went well, for the most part, I even had the sketches he wanted done ready for him early. On Christmas Eve I called Helen and Mandy to wish them a Merry Christmas, though I decided to surprise them rather than inform them of my early return the next day. Mandy was exuberant because mommy had told her that she could sleep over Auntie
Kate's house the next night. The situation was perfect. I would stop by
Kate's, say hello to my little angel, then go home and spend a romantic evening with my wife.
Frozen, wet and jetlagged, I pulled up to Kate's house. Mandy flung herself in my arms, or rather, my arm, as I unveiled with the other a beautiful handmade doll from Japan. I also had for her a blue silk kimono which she immediately donned and flaunted about the apartment. After every plant and article of furniture had been introduced to "Cindy", her doll, and been shown her new "dress", she gave me one of her famous bear hugs, and sat on my knee to share the tale of her Christmas.
Suddenly, she got very quiet, though. "Daddy...I have a fession" "A CONfession, Mandy?" "Yeah, a fession" I just smiled. "What is your fession, Mandy?" "I was a bad girl last night, daddy. Mommy told me not to tell you so you wouldn't be mad at me, but I have to tell you."
"Sweetie, I wouldn't be mad at you, what is it?" "Last night, I was so excited for Santa Claus to come, and I couldn't get to sleep, and I heard a man laughing downstairs so I went down to peek and see if it was Santa. I was VERY shhhh so he wouldn't hear me, but
I saw Santa all in red on the couch with mommy." Bursting into tears,
Mandy clung to my neck and buried her face in my chest.
Fuming at the possibility I knew I needed to calm this poor, innocent creature down. "Mandy, it's O.K. You didn't do ANYTHING wrong, I'm not mad at you. I snuck down to see Santa Claus when I was 5-years- old too. Don't worry, nothing is going to happen to you. Now you go get ready for bed, and have fun with Auntie Kate. Be good for her, and remember, you didn't do anything wrong. I love you very much, Mandy.
I have to go talk to Mommy now, though." I gave her a kiss on the head, and a hug, feeling as if it was for the last time.
I raced home in a blinding rage. I remember nothing of the ride to my house, though I remember the feeling of my blood boiling. I screamed through the door, and was met with my worst nightmare. My memory of the rest of the scene is vague. I bellowed "GET OUT OF MY LIFE".
When they were gone for good, I ran around the house destroying anything that reminded me of Helen or our life together. My rage maneuvered me more than any conscious thought, as I took off my wedding ring and threw it in the romantic fire the two had burning.
'That's where THEY belong', I remember thinking. I snapped thoroughly when I saw the snow globe. Water and glitter everywhere, I threw what was left of that in the fireplace also. 'The tickets!' I thought. My conciseness kicked in momentarily as I searched my secret hiding place for the plane tickets to Paris. The old shoe box with the baseball cards Helen had bought for me when we were first married lay strewn on the floor of the attic already. Never interested in baseball, I had received them to make me more accessible to her, less "upper class".
What I had thought at the time was a cute gesture to start me on a hobby, I now realize was her way of trying to bring me to the level of her other relationships. I know nothing of her lover, yet need to know no more than he must have been some blue-collar worker who never made her feel out of place. Anyway, the tickets were nowhere to be found, though perhaps I had already destroyed them. Had they been missing already? I would never know. Unable to face the site of the atrocious act, I averted my eyes from our bedroom, and ran out the door. When I regained some semblance of composure I made two phone calls only.
