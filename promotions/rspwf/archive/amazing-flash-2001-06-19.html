Amazing flash.

19 June 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Alright, no matter how good this roleplay is.. I can't take credit for it-- a man by the name of [name withheld] wrote it and I do believe me days are numbered. *sigh*
[SCENE: darkness. The gentle sound of water lapping against concrete embankments can be heard. Slowly the camera appears to become accustomed to the darkness, and the dim, refracted glow of sulphurous street lamps is reflected in the otherwise invisible surface of a large mass of water. The barely visible ripples in the water are disturbed by a gloved hand pulling an object from the water. We hear a voice:]
VOICE: Decay and failure. It hangs in the air like a putrid stench. [The voice is low; guttural, disdainful... and also muffled, as if the mouth is obscured. Despite this, the words are clearly enunciated, slicing through the stillness with a sharp coldness.]
VOICE: Especially here in... London. [The name of the city is said with a peculiar emphasis, scornful perhaps. The figure seems to take a deep breath, as if savouring some aroma carried to him on the breeze.
A gloved hand opens, revealing that the object that was retrieved from the river is, in fact, a decomposing fish, its rancid guts leaking out of a split in its swollen belly. The hand allows the fish to fall back into the water with a small splash.]
VOICE: Most people shrink back from the dying and the dead. Afeared, they stupidly believe that the dead can do them harm.
Yet decay and failure are a part of the great circle of life. Every living thing must yet die. Every living thing is already dying. Once a thing has come to be, it only remains for it not to be so.
Ashes to ashes, if you like. [The voice gives a snort of derision, as if amused by his own turn of phrase. In the distance, the blast of a car's horn, wailing crazily.
The camera pans out. Silhouetted from behind by the harsh, crass glare of an orange street lamp, a figure stands on a narrow, concrete towpath.
It's not possible to make out any details, but the posture has the appearance of pent-up aggression, the body is wide, the arms are thick as tree trunks, and the bearing is very threatening.]
VOICE: But what of those who walk in the twilight between the dead and the living? What of those who fear no man, living or dead? What of those who feed on the failure of others? What of them? What do they fear? [The figure raises his arm slowly, deliberately. There is the sound of a flintwheel being struck, and suddenly there is a small flame -- a cigarette lighter. Yet the figure makes no move to light a cigarette.
Instead, the light offers but a few further glimpses of the man who bears the flame. ...his torso, covered in black, but some kind of symbol on his chest... it is blood red... a skull... a blood red skull emblazoned on his chest... ...he lifts the flame... his face... he has no face! He wears a mask -- a featureless mask. Not even hollows for eyes... it is not possible to discern a single feature.
But we know who this man is -- it is Cadaver.] CADAVER: What... of Cadaver? You have heard of me, perhaps, but none of you have met me. Like a whisper on the wind, or the threat of a distant storm, am I nothing more than a rumour, a figment of the imagination?
[The figure takes a step forward, the figure ever more imposing and threatening.] CADAVER: No, I am not. I am not some form of incarnate cruel and unusual punishment. I am not the devil himself. I am... just a man. [A slight tilt of the head suggests that Cadaver is smirking.] CADAVER: Just a man who goes where devils fear to tread. Just a man who knows no fear. Just a man... who delights in pain and destruction. A man of simple pleasures: another man's screams, another man's agony, another man's distress.
And a _hungry_ man. [The figure advances further on the camera, which now appears to be looking up at him from below. In the harsh glare of the street lamp behind him on the towpath, and the dim flicker of the cigarette lighter's feeble flame, strange shadows play across his barrel chest with its red skull emblem. His empty hand clenches and unclenches, as if holding in some terrible rage.]
CADAVER: But there is plenty of time yet. Let us not get ahead of ourselves. For now, let us ignore the hollow laughter of those who would dismiss me. Many have trodden that path before -- and have rued their rashness in the cold light of the emergency room.
Know only that Cadaver is coming. And nothing -- and nobody -- will stop me. [The camera is jostled and suddenly the figure disappears from view. The camera swings wildly: it becomes clear that we have been standing on a section of towpath alongside the river Thames in one of the areas of
Docklands still awaiting development. One the far side of the river, we glimpse the abandoned Millennium Dome, now a sad and empty relic of a great ideal gone sour. On the near side of the river, hulking great wharf houses with windows smashed and huge illuminated signs proclaiming another waterside apartment development.
But nowhere... nowhere to be seen is Cadaver. And then... drifting in on the breeze comes his voice:] CADAVER: Peryszinski... Sebastian... Erickson. Count the days, for your reckoning will come soon enough.
[Fade.] -[detail withheld]
