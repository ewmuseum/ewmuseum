(ESSAY) E-wrestling from the newbie's point of view

13 March 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is the first essay of mine which I may make a weekly thing of if I find time. Today I will look at e-wrestling from a newbie's point of view. I myself am not new. But a while ago I was. If you are a new player finding RSPWF or a fantasy wrestling federation through Yahoo or who-ever the first thing most do is rush to a fed and send in an application form for a hastily made wrestler. My advice is to first compose a list of about five feds that you might be interested in. Remember, unless you are a superhuman three is the usual maximum roleplaying potential limit. You then scout the five feds. Ask to be put on the mailing list for a week or two. Find out the rules. Most new players then realise that there are in fact thousands of e-wrestling feds out there. You will start to dislike some feds maybe because one is full of perverted sickos or because the language is harsh. This is fine, lots of people feel that they have made the wrong choice in that fed. Fine, quit your character and join another. Eventually start going to places like the IIWF homepage and looking at interviews there. This should guide you, by looking at what others have done you will gain in skill. Roleplaying isn't something you are born with. You develop certain skills by example. Most newbies should either enter a federation for experienced roleplayers or one for newbies. Many feds allow both so there is a wide variety to choose from. Eventually go back to your list and say to yourself, "See, I really shouldn't have joined these feds and I'm glad I didn't." Many new feds close down quickly which is really annoying for players who will have put time and effort into roleplaying. Another dissapointing thing is when you roleplay your butt off and your opponent doesn't even do one interview yet he totally trashes you. It happened to me lots of times but I just left those feds immediately. E-wrestling can be very fun and exciting especially if you can get into the right sort of fed, one that suits your style. Many people drop out of e-wrestling because they let the actions or words of one sicko speak for the entire e-wrestling enviroment. People who do that I can understand. Maybe you like crazy things, try looking at one of the outlandish weird feds with Space Aliens and Barflys. Perhaps you are more of a serious roleplayer and like to win alot. Or maybe you just like using a simulator as you think it is fair. All of these viewpoints make e-wrestling a diverse and changing game. NEXT WEEK: Livening up your characters
