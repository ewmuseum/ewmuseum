Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is among the first three roleplays I have written since I returned to serious e-wrestling after about a two year hiatus from actually participating in the game.
I'm very interested to know what you guys think of the writing ability of a spam troll like me.
www.parodyfed.com [ There are some things in life that simply can't be explained. Why in the world would anybody cheer for a man who carries so much guilt and trauma on his back? Murderer, taking the life of an unarmed man and hiding behind his former badge in order to get away with it. Do people cheer for the fact that he is under the strict employment of a con-artist mobster?
Or maybe they like seeing Kirk Collin's ankle dislocated. ENTER JANESVILLE, WISCONSIN Friday, 12:25 am [ You would think a drive between Madison and Chicago would only take five or six hours. That's not the case when you're driving with a stubborn Tony
Suriconi. One hour on the road after the show, Tony decides that he is fatigued and needs a good night sleep.
[ Scene opens inside car, Tony driving. ] Frank Maddox: Christ man, do something with your transmission, your car sounds like hell. Tony: Did I ask for your f_cking advise wise guy? Frank: Are you cranky because it's passed your bedtime? It's pretty late for someone who works so hard.
[ Momentary silence follows the screeching of the break pedal bring the car to a complete stop. Tony looks at Frank with an agitated face. ]
Tony: What the hell is that supposed to mean! You don't think I'm backstage handling all the politics of that damn group! Nobody would have cheered for you if I hadn't have been hustling the front gate of the show.
I'm the only reason you're popular, so don't start getting cocky with me you damn labor worker.
Frank: Just shut up. We still got three hours to go, hit the gas already. Tony: You punk, you just don't get it do you. You aren't allowed to give me commands; I'm the one at the wheel.
Frank: Then get the hell out and let me drive, buster. I'll get us there in an hour. [ Tony hits the gas. ] Tony: You're a damn idiot, you don't even have a license. You know what, you're an idiot for even assuming I'd let you drive! I got merchandise in the trunk, and if we're pulled over with you at the wheel, your oinking buddy is going to be poking his snout in every crevasse of this car.
Frank: Merchandise? What're you peddling now, child pornography? Tony: THAT'S IT! [ Tony pulls into a motel. ] Tony: I can't take your damn mouth anymore. I'm buying a room for the night, you get to sleep in the car asshole.
Frank: Alright, alright. Quit playing around, get back on the road. Tony: You think I'm playing with you wise guy? [ Tony forcefully yanks the keys out of the ignition, and jumps out of the car without missing a beat. ]
Tony: (from outside, open door) And if you even try to hotwire this piece of trash, I'm going put ten thousand bounty over your head. So don't try anything stupid, you hear?
[ Frank mumbles to himself as Tony slams the door and marches his way into the motel lobby. Fade out. ]
ENTER CHICAGO HEIGHTS, IL J & J Coffee Hut - 11:30am [ Frank and Tony have been driving for the past three and a half hours, and are finally close to their destination. A pit stop at a highway coffee shop isn't going to hurt anyone. Other occupants of the restaurant include truck drivers and residents of that trailer park a few blocks down the road.
Frank and Tony are seated at a corner booth with large windows peering the outside. ] [ A waitress approaches the duo. ] Waitress: Hi there! I'm Delores, are you ready to order yet? Tony: Yeah, get us two cups of coffee, and uh, how good are your pancakes? Waitress: They're finger licking good. Tony: Mm, just like you. Get me a plate of those. [ The waitress smiles at Tony, and fills the empty cups with black coffee and looks over at Frank. ]
Waitress: Anything for you, hun? Frank: No. Just the coffee. Waitress: Alright. Tony: Hey I forgot to tell you, I got a call from the SWP. Frank: Oh yeah? What did they have to say. Tony: Seems like the fans are really digging you. We might be seeing a pay increase in the coming weeks.
Frank: What about that tournament? Tony: That's what I mean. You're going to get a raise if you win this thing. I have a hard time believing it, but people are saying you got a good shot at it.
Frank: That first guy was a joke. Kirk Collins? A cowboy? He fought more like a god damned horse whisperer.
Tony: Well don't be getting cocky. You're going to fighting twice next week. The first fight is with this Triple J guy. He's pretty tough I think.
Frank: Yeah, he's as good as The Game. Mr. Borderline Copyright Infringement just reeks of talent. That's why he hasn't been sued by the
WWE yet. Tony: Just keep the skinny bastard on the ground and you'll be good. After that you fight either that Haaglund guy, or that one guy you punked out two weeks ago.
Frank: Oh, Harlacker. "Hardcore" Harlacker. Tony: (snaps fingers) That's the one. Frank: I don't know who's on his side, or who's against him, what he ate for breakfast, nothing. All I know is that he was laid out like a Thailand massage whore the second I stepped foot in the ring two weeks back. If I did it before, I can do it again.
[ Tony grabs a newspaper from an empty table behind him. ] Frank: Harlacker is going to be chewing on my foot, and eating the canvas for dessert. Tony: (hiding behind paper) You get em, tiger. [ The waitress returns with Tony's pancakes, and we fade out. ] www.parodyfed.com
