Sample RP - Not a free agent but looking for some feedback

15 April 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
***COURTESY OF R-TV*** [Scene opens up in front of a bar on a cold, rainy night. A faded green neon sign above and to the left of the door, tells us the name of the place; The
Thirsty Indian. A cigar shop mannequin of an Indian chief, complete with headdress and moccasins, patiently stands near the entrance to the place, greeting the very few visitors who pass by here. The building looks like a replica of an old fashioned saloon, something you would see in a John Wayne movie. The old building is mostly painted red and gold but the colors look faded and the paint is peeling off. The camera then zooms out to show a man in a black trenchcoat and hat standing in front of the bar. The man in the trenchcoat stands in the pouring rain for a few moments. Then he slowly makes his way towards the door. He pushes the swinging, saloon style doors open]
[We follow the unidentified man inside the bar. The interior of the bar is also painted red and gold, much like the exterior. The first thing you would notice is the place is very dimly lit. Secondly, the clientele here are almost exclusively Native-American. Their ages range from young men in their twenties to older men in their late seventies. Some of the clientele are wearing traditional attire but most are wearing suits and ties or more casual attire such as jeans and T-shirts. None of them look the least bit happy as they instinctively drown themselves in shots of Jack Daniels and various colored cocktails, some of which glow in the dimly lit room. The man whom we followed in here, slowly makes his way towards the bar, his back still turned to the camera. He casually takes a seat by the bar. He doesn't say anything but motions for the bartender to bring him a drink. The bartender, a tribal elder who's missing all his teeth, pours the stranger a shot of Sidewinder™ and then tells him "it's on the house" and goes on with his business. The mystery man picks up his drink and downs it with one gulp, tilting his head back for effect. The man sets the empty glass down on the table and brushes his long, wet hair out of his face. We then instantly recognize him as the one and only Chavez, the insane, drunken Indian and the ARMAGEDDON
Heavyweight champion. The insane one, who has chosen a different drinking for tonight, gazes into the shadows in a moment of reflection. He doesn't seem to be all that drunk tonight but looks rather distracted and distant. Then a hoarse, gruff voice calls out from the dark]
Voice: "Hey, why ain't you drinking kid?" [Chavez slowly turns his head] Chavez: "Dad?" [The shadows give way. A rotund, middle aged, Indian man in a flannel shirt and faded jeans, his dark hair neatly done up in two pig-tails that fall way past his shoulders, sits opposite Chavez. He has an empty bottle of
Sidewinder™ in front of him and an indifferent look on his face] Man: "I told you never to call me that." Chavez: "Sorry." Man: "What brings you here?" Chavez: "I was in town and needed a drink." Man: "Bullshit." Chavez: "I need advice." Man: "Go see your grandpa. Maybe he's got some sacred chicken wings for you or maybe he'll bestow upon you the sacred dental floss. Who knows, he might still be sane after all these years."
[Neither Chavez or the older man look each other in the eye as the speak. The bartender pours out two shots of Sidewinder™ and leaves the bottle on the bar.
The man knocks his drink back immediately. Chavez just gazes at it] Chavez: "Chavez face biggest challenge. Sanction man in steel cage on Monday. Chavez fear for sanity if he lose." [The man grunts his dissaproval as he pours himself another drink] Man: "First of all, you can speak English properly. There's no camera's here, nobody's filming this crap."
[Chavez turns to the camera for an insant and flashes a knowing grin] Chavez: "Oh they are ALWAYS watching." Man: "Second of all, you haven't been SANE since you got discharged from the armed forces. Third of all, since when did you become such a chicken shit?
What happened to you kid? I brought you up on the reservation and I never imagined you'd become such a pathetic bundle of nerves. You were such a little ass kicker back then. You know what your tribal name was?"
Chavez: "Dances With Buffalo?" Man: "No you idiot. It was...It was, damned If i can remember it. But the point is, you've lost it, kid. While your out making movies, stupid parodies, your enemies are gathering on the field."
Chavez: "I have friends to watch my back." Man: "Friends? Who are your friends? That kiwi guy? The man who wears a horse suit? That asylum refugee with the pet Viking? You trust this group of crackpots? Do you?"
Chavez: "Now that you mentioned it." Man: "You're the champ, son. Everybody, and I do mean EVERYBODY is out to get you. You think they hang out with you because they think your...cool or something?"
Chavez: "Well, Horsey says I'm cool." Man: "And I bet you think he wears that suit because it gets chicks? Listen to me, kid. You're losing it and losing it fast. You've let all the fame go to your head. Instead of making another box office bomb, why don't you concentrate on the task at hand?"
Chavez: "Sanction is tough cookie." Man: "You're tougher." Chavez: "Sanction has reputation." Man: "It wasn't Sanction who retired eight men. It wasn't Sanction who handed the Professional his first loss. It wasn't Sanction who become the second
ARMAGEDDON Heavyweight champion. It was you, you idiot. You didn't beat any slouches to get where you are. You earned all those victories."
Chavez: "Sanction is prepared. He has already beaten Chavez." Man: "Bull *beeping* shit, son. He didn't beat you. Your friend Robbie thought he'd put this two cents in the match and that's why you lost."
Chavez: "But Chavez ask Robbie to." Man: "Pay attention, kid. You've got to show that jerk what a real man is. The
U.S. government has been scamming and looting from our people for years. It's time for payback."
Chavez: "Payback? I don't know where to begin. Hey, dad, since when did you become such avid wrestling fan anyway?"
Man: "DON'T CALL ME THAT!!!" Chavez: "Sorry." Man: "Now where was I? Yeah, you've got to show that little runt what Navaho pride is all about. You've got to kick his ass from one end of that domeless steel cage to the other. Pillar to post, pillar to post. Stomp his ass! Navaho pride, son!"
Chavez: "Chavez drink Navaho pride?" [Chavez holds up his still-untouched glass of Sidewinder™] Man: "No! The firewater can help you but it will burn you if you're not careful. You can't rely on it for everything."
Chavez: "So, what are you saying?" Man: "You've got the heart of a wolf but you've let yourself go. Who's going to pay your tab when you're sitting on skid row, living in a box, reminiscing about the time you were somebody? Are you going to let this Sanction man send you there? Are you?"
Chavez: "Sanction is better than Chavez." Man: "Alright I give up. You're on your own kid, but don't come running to me when you're down and out. Good bye, kid."
[The man gets up off his stool and stumbles off into the darkness muttering something about taxes and the government . Chavez just sits there and stares at his drink, a bit dissapointed in how things turned out. He says, to nobody in particular]
Chavez: "What do I have to do?" [We fade out Chavez in a rare moment of reflection, chin resting on his hands, staring into the bottom of the glass as if it were a crystal ball.]
