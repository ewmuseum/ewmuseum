Making a great character

What makes a great character by [name withheld] I was wandering around the newsgroup earlier today, and I've been following the thread for what makes a great e-wrestler. Well, here's what I have tried to use for my own character (Dan "Flash" Kauffman), and some of the things that I've learned over the last two years in e-wrestling. First, and I think this is more important than anything else, you have to be able to have fun with your own character. I've always had the most fun portraying my own self into my character because I was able to use real emotions and ideas in my writing. If you aren't having fun with whoever you created, what's the point? Be creative: I mean, you aren't holding a mic in front of thousands of fans in real time... try some different things. If you find a pattern or a way of roleplaying/interviewing that is fun for you, stick to it. It will probably help you in the long run. Second, you MUST be patient. As hard as this may be to believe, when I started in e-wrestling way back in the Ice Ages (August '95) in a little fed known as the IASW (Internet All-Star Wrestling), my best RP was a 0.4K Paragraph of pointless threats. But I allowed myself and my character to mature as I learned the ropes, and by January 96, I was starting to get a lil' recognition as CFWF International Champion. My roleplaying got better as well, especially when I started using my own life and thoughts in my character. I wasn't born a good roleplayer, I made myself one. Of course, roleplaying is a HUGE part of wrestling. In real wrestling (There's an oxymoron if I've ever seen one...), the best wrestlers and the highest profiled ones are the ones who can keep a viewer's attention. Keeping a reader's attention through an entire roleplay is not easy to do. But I believe that the more compact and detailed an RP uis, the better it can be. (This coming from the guy who is known for his 20K flashes in the IIWF :)) And "shooting" an interview every once in a while is a good way of getting over your character too (But don't overkill). Perhaps the one single thing that kills roleplays worse than anything is bad spelling/punctuation. Now I have been known to make a few typos in my time, but generally, I make sure that the basics of good writing are in my flashes. We've all seen flashes with the various forms of "There, Their, and They're" mixed up, and this drives me insane. And folks, an "I" looks much better than an "i". (This is for you, Greg. :)) Watch capitalization and spelling. You don't have to be perfect, but don't butcher your RP up either. What else... Well, personally, I think angles are a big part of e-wrestling. You have to be able to plan out your character's actions. This comes as part of the maturization of a character, how he acts in situations, if he likes joining stables or is a loner, whether he shows sportsmanship or whacks your brain with a steel pipe, etc. Angles are not the easiest things to come up with, especially the good ones that some people (Ah-Hem... *cough*GREG*cough*...) come up with. I'm not that great an angle planner. But I know enough to at least have some semblance of a plan going into a match. Why make angles and plans? Because it creates __HEAT__ for your character, be it "Face" heat or "Heel" heat. A guy that makes good angles and stays unpredictable always has an aura of heat around him. Super Scott is one of the characters that can draw heat just by showing up somewhere. (Of course, it helps to have a rep like him too...) Mr. Robinson draws heat with his angles and unpredictability. (Damn guy has suckered me twice in two weeks... Greg, your character is very annoying... :)) Good roleplaying, development of angles, and a certain flair for the dramatic will get a character over best. Well, that;'s a start. I'm sure there are many other extras that can make a guy a great e-wrestler, but these are the ones I watch for. - [name withheld]
