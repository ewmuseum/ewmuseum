[Roleplay] William O'Leary [Critique?]

24 April 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Scene takes place as William O'Leary, a man who has supposed to have been serving Johnny "Sexy" Bod, is seen sitting on a long wooden bench outside the
Nixon Nightly Game Room in New York City. The bench is clearly seen as old.
The wood is slightly warped in some places, the wood has been chipped and discolored, and many things have been written across the bench. Some things include "I love Johnny" and "Glenn wuz here." The arms of the bench are made out of a black metal. Possibly steel.
William O’Leary is dressed in his usual sloppy style – Not in the style he dressed when he served Johnny Bod. He is dressed in a pair of black, faded blue jean shorts, a long, dirty and holy white tee shirt, and an old pair of
Adidas tennis shoes.
William O’Leary is seated on the right side of the bench, leaning on the side bar. As the camera settles from a previous shot of the sky onto William
O’Leary, he shifts his weight to where he is sitting in an upright position.
The camera zooms in as he speaks.] William O’Leary: Ahh yes. It feels good to be back in the great state of New
York. You know, after being down south in Louisiana for a while, New York feels a lot different. I mean, first you have the weather. I mean, right now in Louisiana it’s in the low 90’s, but up here in New York, it’s in the 40’s.
Then of course, you have the atmosphere and the people. In Louisiana, there aren’t very many people out this late at night. But the one big thing is the people. In Louisiana, people don’t talk funny. I never have understood why these people in the north talk so funny. I mean, they are __SUPPOSED__ to be like people in the south aren’t they?
[William O’Leary scratches his head. His black hair, which is cut in a crew cut, runs trough his dirty hands and fingertips with ease.]
William O’Leary: But, before I get into talking about people here, my match with the Screwed Ball, or anything else, I must pay my gratitude to the people who died in Littleton, Kentucky. I may be one dirty individual, but I pay my respect when I need to. I must say that it is a shame for some freaking “Hitler-its” to go in and shoot innocent people. You know, I believe that this whole thing may have been caused by Marilyn Manson when he said, “When I die, I want a __HUGE__ funeral, and at the end I want a
__HUGE__ bomb to blow up so everyone will die with me.” If you think about it, it makes a lot of sense. Those Trenchcoat Mafia people did exactly that .
. . they killed themselves along with sixteen other innocent people. [William O’Leary closes his eyes and bows his head for a moment of silence.] William O’Leary: OK . . . now we talk wrestling. You know, the past few weeks, people in UEW and around the wrestling world have begun calling
William O’Leary a “loser.” Now, I don’t like that. When I first came to
UEW, I was called “great.” When I think about it, they were probably sizing that UEW Ultimate Championship belt for the waist of William O’Leary. I mean, I was 3-0 and all of my wins were total blowouts. And then we came to
Gambit, the retired freak. Somehow, he managed to beat me. Next, we came to
Johnny “I’m stupid. I thought O’Leary would serve me.” Bod. Now, that was a win. Not only did he not pin me for three, but I __DID__ pin him. And just to prove myself, I pinned him again on Sunday Night Heat. And then, in my last match, I faced a man I call “The Crippled” Ivan Kolinski. I will admit that he was my toughest opponent, and he did "beat” me. Notice that I use the term loosely. Ivan, let me just say that Russia __NEVER__ “won” the Cold
War . . .
[William O’Leary grins to himself and slowly shifts the weight back onto his arm and leans against the side bar. As he leans on the side bar, he reaches down to the side a brings up a 20 oz bottle of Coca Cola. He slowly untwists the top and holds it in his hand as he drinks from the bottle. Aft his refreshing drink, he twists the top back on and sits back to the side of the bench.]
William O’Leary: You know, I have been in this game room before. There is one game that I really enjoy playing with. The game is called “Trap the
Screwball!” Of course, it is a pinball game, which happens to be my favorite type of game to play. I mean, pinball is a cool game. First, you get to shoot the little ball up and then you try to keep it from falling in the pit.
In this particular game, you try to knock the pinball, which is oddly shaped, into a small hole. When it hits the hole, a cage pops down. If you trap it, you get like some outrageous number of points. I’ve played that game __SO__ many times that I can continually hit it into the trap. I believe I passed the original high score by like 2,000,000 points.
[William O’Leary raises his hands and smiles as if saying, “I finally won something, again!”]
William O’Leary: You know, that’s kinda like what I plan on doing with this guy a face on Anarchy. I believe his name is “Screwball” Danny Jansen. He combines the idiot and ugly traits of Lee Janzen with the stupid and overrated traits of Danny Tartabull together and gets . . . well . . .
himself! [William O’Leary smiles and raises his hands in the air again at his smooth and, to some people, a funny comeback.]
William O’Leary: First, I’m gonna knock him around a little bit just like I do the little pinball. You know, I may run him into a pole or two, sling him against the ropes, clothesline him . . . just a little bit to soften him up.
And then, when he least expects it, I’m gonna trap his ass in the corner and deliver some __HUGE__ blows to the chest. And then, the whole thing comes together when I nail a Servant’s Suplex and pin you for the one, the two, and the three just Like I have to so many other people.
[William O’Leary acts stupid and does his little “Cabbage Patch Dance.”] William O’Leary: Well, it looks like all of that caffeine is finally getting to me. I guess I better get going. I do need to play a few games and then get some sleep. I have been arrested . . . umm . . . twice this week I believe. That __CAN”T__ happen again!
[On that not, William O’Leary stands from the bench and pulls up his blue jean shorts. He turns and walks into the game room. As the glass door slowly closes behind him, the camera zooms out until the game room, the door, and then the picture is no more.]
