Types of players

Player Types by [name withheld] I`ve identified four kinds of player types. The first I call the OMIN-PLAYER. The features of the Omin-player is someone who`ll enter their wrestler into loads of federations (say, five upwards) and be devoted only to one, if any, flashing occassionally, but generally achieving mid-carder status. The second I call the CAREER-PLAYER. This, I believe, is the type that makes up most of the big name wrestlers in the big name feds. Very competitive, determined, and stick with one wrestler for a long time, determined to make that name well known. They`ll probably enjoy being that wrestler, but what is of most importance to them is being in the big leagues, being in the big feuds, winning the big headlines, and getting recognition, either in the rankings or in the RSPWF Top 200`s or whatever. These are really serious, committed, and often very talented players who devote a lot of time to making their wrestler the best that they can. They`ll do everything to give them more realism, and will get to the point where they almost consider them real. Success is everything...they`re going to the top, baby. The third is the GLORY-PLAYER. These players are like the CAREER PLAYERS, except they don`t really have as much talent or determination to make it into the big time. Instead they jump from fed to fed, or set up their own fed, until they get the titles they crave. To these people, titles are everything, even if those titles are nothing. The glory-player comes to a fed, typically saying 'I`ve won 15 world championships in six months". And we all reply, "So?" The fourth...the fourth is covers a rather large area, but is generally players who aren`t bothered about making it 'big'. They`ve found a fed that satisifies their e-wrestling needs, from whether it is playing Hulk Hogan to being the kind of wrestler you`d like to be. Perhaps they once career-players, or omni-players, or maybe they will become career players or omni players. This group, probably the largest group, I call the CASUAL PLAYERS. And the abilitiy within this group will range from the very bad (like, can you even type man?) to players who would probably bring shame on most career wrestlers in terms of their ability. They may take it very seriously, or not at all, but what is important to them is not that they are becoming big-names or winning lots of titles, but they are enjoying what they are involved in. So which are you? I`m not making any comment over which it is better to be. If RSPWF was 100% of either of the four catergories, we`d be in trouble. The Omni-players help fill up space, the career players push forward the boundaries of what it means to be a good player, improving e-wrestling in general. The glory-players are...well, personally I find them good to laugh at, but one day, they could be good career-players. And as for the casual player...this is the pool from which all rise or return eventually, a group which has to exist for all others to exist.
