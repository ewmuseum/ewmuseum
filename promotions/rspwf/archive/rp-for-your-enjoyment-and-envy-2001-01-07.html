By "Drastic" O. Skiller, WIW - http://wIw.8k.com The road trip to Z-Maniac's past, episode I ...Here I go, On the road again, There I go, Turning the page... Only the page is turned in a backward direction, back in time, quantum- leaping, if you will. And the book is the monotonous story of Maniac's life of misery and hopelessness.
The silent mountains on the distant, dimmed horizon, their tops slightly clad with small caps of tainted snow, are fleeing in the opposite direction, as the known Viper (never trust a snake!) speeds along the Interstate 57, cutting the boundless space... and the enormous gulfs of times thought to be buried for good. It's late afternoon, and our luminary, deluding the mankind with false impression of its longevity, is also speeding, in the Western direction, to be swallowed by the peaks of the ancient mountains, and to descend into the realm of Hefestus.
The interior of the car is fully equipped with latest words of electronic gadgets. Latest version of the reliable GPS system, a portable computer with satellite connection to the World Wide Web and some additional, less official networks, a mobile phone/transmitter, connected to a VIP service, promising full reception in any part of the globe. Add to that a small TV set, and you receive some sort of a comprehension of the way Skiller's mind usually works. Always planning ahead, always foreseeing all the possible dangers and obstacles.
Skiller once told a reporter that he can be trapped in his car under an avalanche for days, and survive without breaking a sweat. Always a survivor, a rubber ball bouncing off the hardest of walls.
Finally, we focus on our hero. Skiller, like in most cases we spot him, does not appear to be a fancy dresser. Worn jeans, a jacket carelessly thrown over the hottest item in WIW Merchandise Shop, The Four Aces T- shirt, leather cut-through gloves holding the steering wheel. His grey eyes are centered on the road, his face the essence of calmness. A ringing telephone interrupts his train of thought.
"Yeah?" "Hey, Skiller, it's Bob, from editing room." "Oh, yeah, I've recognized you. What's shaking, Bob?" "Not much. Just received a tape with Maniac's promo, care to waste precious moments of your life for it?"
"Sure thing, Bob. Just hold on, I'll pull over, and you will transmit it to my TV. Can't watch TV and drive, you know."
"Well, actually, there is not much to watch, except for the fungi on his bare feet - yuck! As usual, he just talks and talks, then he makes a dramatic pause, and yaps some more."
"Ah, typical. OK, then just play it for me; hope it will be funnier than the country songs I'm getting on the radio in this region."
Skiller carefully listens to Maniac's transmitted monologue, and a bemused expression spreads on his perfect face.
"God, he just repeats himself. Just shows that he didn't listen to my words at all. Such a disrespect from a fellow colleague! And to think that I absorb every syllable his gap utters, hungrily looking for his pearls of wisdom... and not finding any, naturally. I told him already,
I've never relied on my belts as the proof to my superiority over him;
for that purpose I have my multi-colored skills and the world's recognition of them. You can call it arrogance - and, by the way, you are contaminated with exactly the same sin - but this business has no place for the humble and the modest. If you think that a wrestler should act out of consideration regarding how he will be perceived by his opponents, then you better re-read your manual, "Pro-wrestling For
Dummies", again. You call it ego - I call it self-motivation.
All right, Bob, thank you for your help, I'll take it from here. Now, Man-Man-Maniac, let's go through the basics once again, and I promise to speak slower, so that even you will understand. Lesson number one, Maniac - never show your true emotions to your opponent.
What you think being a fierce rage burning inside of you - and I'm not scared, since your reaction was soooo predictable - is weakness that I can easily exploit. But don't worry, Maniac, you ARE good, no doubt about it - but with my help you will be the best. So, as I said, my apt pupil, exposing your emotions, snapping, losing control is a big no-no in this game you dared to play against me. Never utilize the right lobe of your atrophied brain, whilst it is the left one that should be engaged. 'Hate leads to suffering', you claim? Well, somewhere along that overused "Star Wars" quote is something about anger leading to fear, isn't that so? Nah, of course you don't fear me, or at least you might be dumb, but not too dumb to admit it. What you really fear is a defeat. You've been given too many props since your arrival here, you've been given too much push in the underdog promotions you used to scramble before you made your way into the company number one, World
Internet Wrestling. But again, don't worry, sonny, I'll upgrade you to a higher level, for every top contender deserves a worthy opponent, and
I will create such out of you. You'll thank me later, I know for sure you will. Meanwhile, it's your God-given right to dwell in a total denial of your limits.
By the way, you mind if we drop the childish name-calling, name- twisting? You gotta be better than that... I hope. Calling us The Four
Asses - been there, heard that. After all, speaking of unoriginality, you are the last one to accuse others of such. Maniac? What, just
Maniac? How many hours of hard mental work did it take you to reach that outstanding radio call? It symbolizes your unstable persona?
Couldn't you go for something like Schizophreniac? Maniac-Depressiac? A
Mildly Disturbed Individual With Suicidal Tendencies... -iac? Ah, forget it, I don't know why I even bother giving you points for improvement, since I don't receive even a drop of gratitude in return.
So, anyway, just as I was saying, I can throw at you the same words you threw at me. In your two-dimensional mind, you have already built a prejudiced model of me. Heck, just another fed, another paper champ, another Skiller, another deluded egotist, another notch on my belt, another scratch on the wall of my padded cell, correct? You dismiss me - us, for that matter - so lightly, so carelessly. Did you ever hear me whining, Maniac? Moreover, will YOU whine and complain, should I pull a victory over you? It can happen, you know, nobody is undefeatable. Yes,
I agree, there is a slight chance that on First Blood Main Event you might outwrestle me, I think of all the options and have considered that unlikely alternative as well. That victory will be understandable - for every Bret Hart there is Shawn Michaels, and for every Booker T there's Big Scott Steiner... eh, bad example... Nonetheless! Although you might outwrestle me, it won't come before I'll outsmart you. In fact... I already have. I gave you a hint, I gave you a headstart - but you blew your chance. Too bad that you are being unfolded as such a lousy contender, an unequal chess player. I'm already three steps ahead of you. Oh, well, maybe one day a better challenge to my starved mind will arrive... meanwhile, let's continue messing with your mind.
OK, I think I've arrived to the first stop on my destination course. Shhh, be vewy, vewy quiet, we're hunting Maniac's past." ************************* Like a relict out of the 50s, rising from the dried soil, the last of its kind for hundreds of miles in all directions, an old gas-pump station was increasing on the view of Viper's windshield. With clouds of dust erupting from beneath his wheels, recklessly twirling in the quickly approaching twilight, Skiller pulls over next to a rusty pump, with cobwebs visible on the filling hose. The glass of the meter is cracked, and something tells him that it's not one of those automated pumps that accept credit cards.
Skiller steps out of the vehicle. He notices an old building-slash- convinience-store-slash-restroom-slash-who-cares. The building also suggest that this little middle-of-a-nowhere business establishment has known better times... which was decades ago. In front of the building, slowing rising from a creaking rocking chair, the owner of place notices the potential customer, the only (barely) living soul Skiller has spotted for hours of endless drive.
The gas station owner has a straw hat half-pulled over his dimmed, yellow eyes, wrinkled face partially covered with something that is far from being called a 5 o'clock shade, more like a 5-years beard. His clothes are far from a desperate need of change, more like begging for a mercy-killing. The old guy spits through a lessening row of plaque- infested fangs, his spit, black of chewing tobacco, hits the ground with an audible hiss.
"Howdy, stranger." "Yo, wazzup!" Skiller barely maintains to choke a laughter. South meets
North! "So, how much gas do you need for that fancy car of yours?" "Right, like I'm going to let you squeeze that goat urine into my engine." "Goat urine?! Who talked?" "Ah, I see you got some sense of humour still alive in you, pops. That means we'll get along just fine. Actually, I need some information, and
I know you might be reluctant to provide me with such, so I guess you and a friend of mine, a dead president, mind it, better shake hands right away," and a one-hundred dollar bill passes ownership.
"All righty, what ya need to know?" Skiller reaches toward the passenger seat of his Skiller-mobile, and produces a folder, with the printed letters FBI momentarily being flashed on it. How did he get a grip of such item? Our guess it that it pays off to have friends in high places...
"Since I'm such a straight-forward guy - have you seen this guy? All the tracks lead to this desolated area?" a photo from the file is being shown to the old-timer.
The expression on his face dramatically changes. He slowly shakes his head and takes a step back, the lower jaw almost falls off and starts visibly dribbling. Not a pretty sight to behold on such a glorious evening outdoors.
"I'll take it as a yes, old man. Go ahead, speak." "There are... there are things you smart-ass city boys just don't understand. You don't go searching for such men... especially for this one... He... he..."
"What? Come on, I've heard and seen all the horrors this and other worlds have in store. What did he do? Wiped out an entire population of
Rednecksville, Hillbilly County? Raped all the virgin sheep around here? Stole your morning paper?"
"It's not that... it's not just that. It's what around him... He has... a bad air." "Bad case of hygiene? I thought it's a common trait of the local residents..." "Bad air around him... clinging to him... you can almost FEEL it..." "Oh, an atmosphere, or a bad aura. Yeah, that's the worst kind. OK, so, please, can you be so kind at to pleasantly direct myself to that unpleasant individual. Come on, Uncle Jebediah, I just paid you, don't you go shaking your head on me..."
"OK, listen, I'll give you even a better direction. There is a road inn a bit off the 57. Ask for the owner there... Then you'll know it all...
It will prepare you for the inevitable. Hold on, let me fetch for you a map from the store..."
Skiller, sensing his usual sarcasm seeping away from him, as he got some glimpse of the relevance of the terror that gripped the old man, nevertheless replied in the same cheerful tone:
"Sure, sure, your outdated Confederacy cattle-tracking map will be a good addition to my GPS monitor. Go ahead, I'll wait right here."
But just as the old man stepped out of the store, holding a folded sheet of yellowing paper, all he could hear was a screech of tires, and all he could see is the taillights of the Viper, speeding toward...
