(Critique) RP, William Craven.

6 May 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hey, folks. I've noticed other people coming here and posting rp's to get a critique, so I thought I'd do so too. My flashes have never been viewed by a decent-sized audience and this is (I think) one of my better ones. So, how does it look? Is it good? Is it bad? Do you really want to read this?
Well, if not, you could always make fun of my name like "Homeless" Joe. Heh, Joe's funny. _____________________________________________________________________ The screen fades to black from the commercial break. After a moment the screen blazes white and this word is shown in black. . .
. . .Consequence. . . Then this fades away. Cold, silent darkness reigns for ten seconds solid, seeming almost a dramatic pause from the light and sound of the normal television chaos. From the silence is born a sound. Slowly, laboriously, the chorus of Alice in chains' "Man in the Box" begins. It blasts for a few seconds, drenching the airwaves in sound. As the music fades to background noise, a scene fades in. Craven, sitting, propped against a wooden post. A lush field of green grass, yellow, blue, and white flowers serves as the backdrop. The edge of a large barn, it's red paint stripped away by time is visible on the periphery. Craven himself seems depressed, introverted, and brooding. He acknoledges the camera only with his eyes. Head cast down, his eyes take on the shape of teacups and his pupils almost disappear under the upper eyelid of each eye.
He wears a simple jeans and T-shirt, no shoes, no socks. The shirt is yellow and has illustrations on it designed to make it look like a straightjacket. Craven's arms follow the illustrated sleeves up and over his shoulders, his hands clasped around his neck. His legs are in a lotus position, and after thirty seconds of "Man in the Box" playing as soft background music Craven has still not spoken. The camera moves a bit, as if the cameraman is shifting uncomfortably under Craven's stare. In doing so, a blade is barely exposed to the camera lens, sticking out from the backside of the wooden post. It is a katana. Deeply embedded into the wood, it almost seems that the other end must be sticking in Craven's back. The camera moves around to the side, and Craven's head turns towards it. . .
"I'm not dead, if that's what you're thinking." Craven says quietly. Then with more volume, "I wouldn't have told you to meet me here if I had planned on killing myself.". Quietly again, "Wouldn't use this cheap blade either.".
Craven's hands slip from his neck, his legs from their lotus position, and he sits slumped forward for a moment, his chest against his knees.
Again he speaks with volume, and power in his voice. "You wouldn't be surprised if I was dead, would you? Nobody would be. All that keeps me alive is prescribed barbituates and my doctor's pressuring me." Laughing now. "Heh-he, he may be a quack, but at least he does care.
. ." Seriously now. "No. Nobody would be surprised, and few would care. They'd say, 'Hey, the bald, scarred freak is dead.' or 'Oh, yeah, the abhorred one's gone, let's go get a soda!' big funeral, with no mourners.
It'd be my mom, my stepdad, my ex, and my kid. B.F.D. right? I never expected to be understood, so. . .I guess that it's not really a big deal."
Craven's voice drops in volume and tone, "I'll probably just be cremated anyway. That way nobody will have to look at my ugly self. . ."
Craven falls silent for a moment, clenching his eyes shut and then opening them wide. His gaze is abstracted as he looks to his right, away from camera, at the barn that the camera lens now centers on. . .
"There was a time when I wanted only to hide my head from the slings and arrows of the world. To blind mine eyes from it's evil. I fought my history, I battled my memories, losing again and again. Finally I surrendered. I stepped out into the world with eyes wide open, and I lost my mind. The doctors told me that I was like this from the beginning. They said that it was hereditary, and that I had always needed to be medicated."
Craven's body begins to quiver with laughter, or weeping, this is not clear. His face is turned away and he makes no noise. When he speaks it is in a strained tone. . .
"I had been the blacktop bully on the playground, the thug on the corner, the baddest ass in the motor city, and what finally drove me over the edge. That one straw that broke my camel back was. . ." Craven shakes harder, and chokes on the word. . . "Marriage. . ."
Craven looks back to camera, answering the unasked question, he had been crying, not laughing. His face is contorted, tensed.
"I saw my existance as mundane! College educated, working an office job, scars all over my *bleep*ing face. I didn't blend. I was the round peg among a bunch of squares! I had to do something, find someone. I just chose the wrong person. . .people. About ten of them. . ."
Craven shudders one last time before finally relaxing. He seems instantly too relaxed, flaccid. He looks down, his eyes are downcast as he continues.
"Do you know what it feels like to reach out to the woman you love, who is weeping at your bedside, and find nothing? Craven explodes here,
"Nothing because your GODDAMMED ARM WON'T *BLEEP*ING MOVE!!!?" Quietly again. "My god, I lost my family because I started a fight. My neck was broken for the second *bleep*ing time in my life. I almost died, because of one_small_error in judgement. I'm saying this because my actions have consequences. Consequences." Again Craven screams. "Consequences that I
WANT STEVE *BLEEP*ING STARR TO EXPERIENCE!!!"
Craven grows silent for a moment. From behind the camera steps the huge man 'Riot' that Craven has been seen with recently. Riot says nothing, but stands behind Craven and the post, to the left of the imbedded katana.
He seems to simply be there, company for his buddy Bill. When he speaks again it is in a whisper. . .
"Starr doesn't know consequences yet. He thinks that he and his funnymen can run rampant over the ACWA. He believes that he rules the world. He doesn't seem to realize that all he is is a pretty face from
Seattle. He's a weak little man with luck on his side. Luck and numbers.
Look around, Starr. The numbers are even. I'm training Maxx, he's coming along nicely. Wildy's not nearly as hurt as you think by the Lovelie's attack on him. Look behind me. . ." Craven looks up and back at the huge man Riot. "Seven feet and three hundred-forty pounds of muscle backing me up. The Motor City Madman himself. You're at a disadvantage now, Starr.
Maybe nobody else will bring you to justice, not Jonah, not the law, not god. . ." Craven chuckles and looks down. . . "I no longer believe in the saintly nature of the church. I no longer worship the wise. I no longer believe that the world is fair. Maybe this will change. I mean, things are always changing. I'm a villain, I'm a victim, I'm a sinner, I'm a saint, I don't want your approval, but YOUR LOVE
IS ALL I NEED! Like smoke in the distance, and clouds in the sky, the facts change everyday, and so_do_I. The only thing that remains the same is that
I always win. I won the belt, I'm meeting and beating you, I beat your
Lovelies, I win Starr. . . I win. . ."
Craven now looks back to camera, a very intense expression on his face. . . "I really want Steve to see this. I want him to see what he's getting into. Starr, look at me, look at what I am. Think about this. A wise man once said 'live fast, die young, leave a beautiful corpse.'. I_am_what happens when_you_don't_die. . ."
"Not die. . ." "Never die. . ." The screen fades to black, "Man in the Box" raises in volume again. It plays for about ten seconds, and then all is still.
