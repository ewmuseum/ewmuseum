[Critique] A Poem

8 August 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Ah, a fellow poet. (snicker) In the hopes of furthering the cause of poetry on this newsgroup, I think I'll post a more...well...on topic poem. IT'S A FLASH!
Yup, an old flash, done before I really knew what I was doing. It's
Craven. Please note that the title "Eternal" Is supposed to be in white, set against a black screen, as with many of my "titled" RP's for Craven. I make it clear that the words that follow it are seen and not heard... I dunno, guess I just used to overlook basic stuff like that.
Now, on with the flash! ----- . . .Eternal. . . A black screen. These words flash across the screen and then disappear. "What crawls on its belly," "and lays all men low," "in time?. . ." The scene opens, a junkyard. The camera pans about, seeming to look for something. About the junkyard are demolished bits of buildings, ruined appliances, and the rusted out husks of cars. The camera focuses on one of these automobile bodies. It is an older model Volkswagen Beetle from around
1969. Inside the rusted mass, it's paint long stripped away, is a mannequin. It is dressed in a straight jacket, wears the headdress of a egyptian king, and has a boquet of roses in it's lap. On the door of the car is a pentagram, on the hood is a gas can, an axe, and a statuette of a falcon. This seems almost a religious offering. With an effigy, and odd symbolic objects. The view widens, showing a figure dressed in a trenchcoat that blots out it's body, and having about two feet of blonde hair that covers it's features and thin cloth gloves. This figure is attempting to light a match. As it tries again, and again, failing each time, these words flash upon the screen. After each line fades, the next appears.
"Call it an ending," "call it a start," "I'm in till the end, dear," "'till death do us part. . ." The figure gives up on the match, tossing it aside, and begins on another. The glowing white words continue.
"Call it a devil," "call it a dove," "take it within you," "and give it your love." Again the match fails. Why? No one knows, but again the figure tosses it aside and begins anew.
"Call it a casket," "call it a corpse," "I'm in 'till the end, dear, " "for better, for worse." The match lights! The figure stands, turning towards the car. "Call it a headstone," "call it a grave," "I'll be the master," "and you'll be my slave." The figure removes it's glove, placing the lit match into it's palm. With a gusty breath, the figure blows the match onto the glistening trail of liquid. A kiss of death for the effigy.
"Call it a fun'ral," "call it a fire," "he took all the birds," "and they burned in a pyre." Flame burns up the trail of liquid, setting ablaze the effigy, and the bizarre ritualistic offerings. The figure turns, face exposed now, scars running across it, it is Craven.
"Call it an ending," "call it a start," "I'm in 'till the end, dear," "'till death do us part. . ." Finally, the scene fades, Craven still grinning, backlit by the burning
Beetle. After a second, a period appears, then fades, then another, fhen fades, then a third, and then all is black.
. . . In the darkness Craven finally speaks: "Goodnight, sweet prince. . .". ----- [name withheld]. Handles: William Craven - ACWA, WET, EMWC. Salvatore Cassanini - ACWA.
