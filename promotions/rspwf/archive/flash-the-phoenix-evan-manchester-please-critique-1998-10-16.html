[FLASH] 'The Phoenix' Evan Manchester - Please critique!

16 October 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fellow RSPWF'ites, I would really appreciate it if anyone who has time would critique the following flash. I think I did a pretty good job, but I wanna see what others think. Please direct any comments/questions/critiques/etc to me at:
[detail withheld] . -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= [FLASH] 'The Phoenix' Evan Manchester [FLASH] -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= [Darkness engulfs the screen - no, not a black production screen, but the darkness of the night. We hear footsteps; the sound of feet trudging through the puddles of rain which cover both the Seattle streets and sidewalks. Splash
- splash - splash. The cameraman has just affixed a light to his camera, softly illuminating his path, but only enough for him to see where he is going - no more.The cameraman starts walking down the dark path; his destination, unknown.]
[We keep hearing footsteps and splashes as the PWU cameraman stomps toward a light - a row of lights rather. We see a dumpster, as well as a couple metal trash cans lining the side of this path. The width of this path cannot be more than three or five feet, as we can see both sides, which are defined by brick walls, in the small area lightened by the bulb. The only conclusion we can come to is that we are walking down an alley, and toward the street.]
[As he nears the streetlights, we hear a crash. It is nothing really, just a small alley cat; and he has just knocked over a trash can in his search for food. The cameraman is frightened, but just for a split-second. He seems to be a bit nervous, as anyone would walking the streets of such a dangerous town at this late hour in the night.]
[Finally, to his relief, the cameraman emerges from the alley. He looks around, and sees a diner, which is strangly filled with people, as well as a dark jewerly store and pawn shop. The cameraman looks up at the street sign; we are on the 124th street. The camerman turns to his left, and walks along the sidewalk - his feet still being heard, pattering in the puddles.]
[We see various shops and stores; your basic suburban 'mainstreet'. The cameraman looks up once again. The street sign reads: "Martin St.". The cameraman looks around - seeminly looking for someone. As he is turned toward the other side of the street, he hears something; footsteps. He quickly turns around, and we see a man. He is leaning against the side of the building flipping a coin. We cannot see this man perfectly, but we can see enough of him to recognize him as 'The Phoenix' Evan Manchester.]
[He has on his normal attire, which consists of a pair of blue jean shorts, a light blue tee shirt, and black boots. He also has on a black, leather motorcycle jacket. His hair is long and black, with only a few streaks of blonde. It is parcially in his face, which is rugged and cold looking. He stops flipping the coin, and puts it in his pocket. He than pushes himself off of the building and begins talking. His voice is slow and steady, and very monotoned.]
'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "On October 18th, two truely troubled souls will step into the squared circle and do battle, but only one can emerge vicrorious."
[Evan turns, and begins walking down the dimly lit road. The camerman follows.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "Shame is a man who has had a tough life. From the tragedy on Halloween night, 1978, to the more recent messy breakup of Shame and his girlfriend, Marcie, he has truely had a tough life. It has been said, that on that night, a little over two years ago, when she left him, he lost his soul; that he lost everything. Rumor has it, that when this happened, Shame almost felt that his life was so bad, as to commit suicide. Also rumored is that the voice of his decest mother filled his head, pleading with him not to take his life. Fortunatly, he chose to listen to the voice that filled his head. He has been training, and is seemingly ready for battle."
[Evan opens his jacket, and pulls out a pack of cigarettes, as well as a silver
'Zippo' cigarette lighter. He pulls one of the cigarettes out of the pack and places it between his lips. Next, he flips open the lighter, and ignites his cigarette. He takes a drag and than pulls it from his mouth, holding it by his side as he continues.]
'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "October 18th will truely be a monumental day in the life of the man they call Shame. For he will wrestle a man who has been around this sport since a very young age. I know the ropes, and I know... how to win."
[Evan pauses, taking a drag from his cigarette. He pulls his hair back from his face before continuing.]
'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "Shame, I feel that I have an understanding with you. And although we've never met, I feel as though I can relate. I too have had a troubled life. One thing turned it around, and that was wrestling. I was able to let out my angers in somewhat of an organized manner. This will help you also Shame. So appreciate every second you have in that ring, although, it may be few."
[Evan stops at a street corner. He glimpses both ways, and continues on.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "What you've experienced in your life, will not be even close to comparable to what you will go through on the 18th. I've been called a legend in this sport - a title I both hate and deny - but nevertheless, it's been forced upon me by the PWU. My SunSet Drop is one of the most feared moves in the busniess today, as well it should. I've put out many men with just one move, and can surely put out many more. But it is not always that last move that puts a man out, it's what happens before."
[Evan is now on the other side of the road, he turns to the left and continues.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "Shame, I enjoy pain, in all aspects of the word. Both inflicting it, and, strangly enough, receiving it. Pain has helped me cope - it has always been there for me. Since childhood, I have experienced pain - both in the emotional, and physical aspects."
[Evan gets a slight smile across his face, and takes a drag off his cigarette.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "As I have said, my life has been hard. But I wouldn't change anything about it if I could. If things had not happened has they did, I would not be the same man I am today."
[Evan pauses, and takes a drag off his cigarette. As he continues on, he looks very calm, taking large strides. His head is held high.]
'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "Sure, I could be sitting in a fancy house, with beautiful women hanging all over me, but this is the life I have chose - this is all that I know."
[Evan lets the smoke out of his nose and continues.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "So Shame, let me ask you this. Are you ready to feel the pain, the anger, and the rage of 'The Phoenix'? If so, let's get it on, but if not, I cannot be held responsible for what I will be forced to do to you on the 18th."
[Evan takes another drag off his cigarette and blows the smoke out.] 'THE PHOENIX' EVAN MANCHESTER - "Shame, PWU, I leave you with just two words - take them as you wish...
... Sweet Dreams ..." [Evan stops, and leans up against a lamp post. As he lets his weight hit the post, the light goes out - and the street is dark. We see only the outline of
Evan Manchester and the small spark of his cigarette as the screen fades to black.]
