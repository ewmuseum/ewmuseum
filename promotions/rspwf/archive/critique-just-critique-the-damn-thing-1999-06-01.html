[Critique] Just critique the damn thing.

1 June 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fellow posters, I have not posted a role-play in a long time so I was just checking to see what you guys thought of my role-playing skills as of late. Well, please just critique so I know what the hell I am doing wrong and what I am doing right.
Thank you. NOTE: For best viewing pleasure, maximize this screen. [name withheld]... New signature to be released soon! ----- [Darkness... The screen then fades to a shot of an old apartment. There seems to be no-one living in this home anymore because the windows are boarded up and there is a "For Sale" sign in the front yard.
The front yard is filled with long, uncut, green grass. There is a weight set in the yard as well. The shot of the yard and apartment remains in a still shot as a voice over is heard.]
V : This is where he was brought up. He said his first words in this very home. He took his first steps in this very home. He also witnessed the fall of the Alexander family in this home.
[The still shot then fades to another still shot. This one is of a family sitting at the dinner table, everything seems to be perfectly fine. It seems like a normal family. The mother is at one end of the table, and the father at the other. On both sides of the table are the son and daughter. The shot remains as the voice begins to speak.]
V : Before that night, January 12th, 1983, everything was fine. f you were to ask anybody what they thought of the Alexander family, they would have told you that they were the model citizens of Boston. Always helping the needy, attending church on every Sunday, just like a Brady Bunch with less kids. That was before that night... January 12th, 1983.
[The scene once again fades to a shot of another still photo in which shows the father standing up yelling at the dinner table, he appears to be yelling at the mother. The two little kids are crying in their seats.]
V : What had made this man snap? Why would he take everything that he had been proud of and flush it down the toilet so quickly?
Was it alcohol, drugs, or was it just a bad day at work? Those things were never known but as far as Shane was concerned he didn't care. He seen his father turn violent for the first time ever.
He will never forget the words that were bellowed at him by his father... "You Shane, were a _MISTAKE!_" How does a kid go through life with the feeling that his father hates him and calls him a mistake? It is not that easy.
[The scene now shows you a shot of Shane Alexander in High School. He is standing inside of the men's bathroom. He is being confronted by a bully. The bully seems to be asking Shane for his lunch money. Shane is shaking his head no.]
V : If that day had never happened, Shane might have been able to defend himself. But instead he could not. He got the crap kicked out of him. And there was nothing he could do about it, at least, at that time.
[The scene then shows a shot of Shane Alexander training at a gym with his uncle, Jason Alexander. Jason seems to be yelling at Shane to do one more bench-press. Shane is straining but finally makes it.]
V : Shane's uncle helped him get tough and stick up for himself. He was like the father that Shane never had. But there was one thing that Shane did not know about Jason...
[A shot of Jason sitting in a chair with a bottle of J&B Whiskey in his hands is shown. Jason is clearly drunk out of his mind.]
... He was a drunk! [The scene then fades to black...] T O B E C O N T I N U E D I N P A R T 2 ! ! !
