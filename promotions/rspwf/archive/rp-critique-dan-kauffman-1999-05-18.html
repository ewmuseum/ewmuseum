[RP Critique] Dan Kauffman

18 May 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Oh hell, why not? Below is the first RP I did for a CRIT match with Alex Martinez. Fell free to comment on anything... I may take notice, I may not, but hey,
I'm curious what kind of response I get.
- Dan ----- [Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... Blackness fades away, and a grass field slowly comes into focus... a view littered with fuzzy gray pock marks in the green horizon...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... As the camera continues to focus, our eyes bring in more details... these "pock marks" are not dirt marks after all... they are three-dimensional, rising out of the ground, casting darkened shadows in an early-morning sun. The grass becomes a shade yellower with the added light, but it's no longer the grass on which our eyes focus...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... The yellow-green grass and the gray markings and the darkened shadows are moving, coming onto the screen from the left and exiting on the right. The camera has zoomed in far enough that there are inscriptions... letters... visible on the gray markings... "Jarrett",
"Jenkins", "Joliffe", "Jollis", "Judife" to name a few of the several words... names... on the tombstones passing by...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... The view now shifts backwards, and the paved one-lane road comes into focus, and we discover what that sound is...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... ...Dan Kauffman's footsteps, as he walks along this road. In a white and red Maryland Terrapins hat, a plain white tee-shirt, ordinary khaki pants and two hard-soled boots, Kauffman looks out across the horizon, scanning... contemplating. He is at ease, but there's still a sense of an inner depression, a form of agony in the steps...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack...] DK: You know, Alex, God don't give us many days like this. Trust me, I know. [Kauffman still scans the horizon, but pauses briefly, and the scene
.. the yellows, greens, grays and blacks... stop moving sideways.]
There are a lot of stories here. A lot of untold stories. A lot of hidden dreams, and passions, and memories, and possibilities. And a lot of people who never took advantage of something because they never knew they had something to begin with.
And then there are people knew they had that something. Their stories are written in books, their dreams recorded in the annals of their life's history, their passions and memories left in the hearts of those who cared and loved and sacrificed for them.
[Kauffman now turns towards the camera, a solemn look in his face, his eyes focused, but yet mourning...]
There are hundreds of people here who I can't tell you about. There are four people here who I can. [Kauffman turns away... Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack...] The stories of my Dad are secondhand, I was told most of them as a young child from my Mom. He was, by her somewhat predictably biased account, a major league caliber baseball player. It was his passion, and it brought her to him.
But my Dad loved his country, too. And when the Army called for him, and then when Vietnam called the Army, he went there when I was still six months from birth.
I would never see my father save through the pictures my Mom carried of him. He didn't really look like me... dark hair instead of blond... chisled chin and forehead, instead of a broad top... but my
Mom always told me I had his eyes.
She told me I was to be his eyes for as long as I lived, and I believe my Dad is using them, too.
He is enjoying this morning just as I. [Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... Kauffman doesn't stop, just looks skyward and smiles... before continuing on his journey to a place we can't see...]
And I believe Mom is doing the same thing. Actually, I wouldn't be surprised if my mother is dancing in some private corner of her own in heaven right now. She was spontaneous, so much so that I always worried about her when she'd stay out past seven... being a kid and all, seven was pretty late to me.
Well, one day, she was spontaneous, and went out. And soon, seven came. And passed. Eight, nine and ten flew by, too. Of course, I should have been asleep at this point, but you know kids...
Someone did show up at eleven... but it wasn't mother, it was a big guy with a really shiny medal pinned on his chest.
I was only eleven years old, but even kids know what a big guy with a really shiny medal pinned on his chest means when his or her mother hasn't come home.
I didn't really know Dad, but I knew Mom. And I can tell you, for
18 years after that, I looked back on that day with the most sorrow.
It was hard even to talk with other kids, because they had parents there, and when I needed my parents, I couldn't hold them or talk to them, I couldn't even see them.
But I learned to cope. I prayed a lot, and gradually, I started making friends, and even though it wasn't easy, I made it through. I was already learning the art of survival...
[Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack... Kauffman, in his walk, smiles... more memories recalled...] And then I found the greatest friend of all. I was 18 when I met my wife. She was shy, quiet, reserved, and she must have thought I was the biggest fool to ever roam the earth when she first laid eyes on me. Later, we both agreed she was, in fact, that fool... she did, after all, marry me.
We stayed in the shadows, and we had a great time. She knew about my parents... I can remember the first time she attempted to make my mother's old cheeseburger pie...
[Kauffman laughs...] A bullet couldn't have penetrated the crust. [... Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack...] Then, about five years ago, I started my wrestling career silently. Just as we both wanted it. We could live our peaceful existence, revel in the simple oddities of life, and I could live out a dream of my own childhood, much like my father's baseball.
But my career became an oddity, too. And after a year... then two... that obscurity was gone in my wrestling career. But not in my marriage. Kelly stayed behind the scenes, raising our daughter Tawnya. She let me live out my dream, and was always there for encouragement, even through the down times, both financially and timewise. And I made sure that she and my daughter came first.
[Clack - Clack - Cl................................... Kauffman comes to a stop...] My brithday falls on February 13. And last year, we had something special set up for the three of us.
I was expecting them around five that afternoon. Instead, a big guy with a really shiny medal pinned on his chest showed up. And I didn't even want to survive. What's the point in me being able to see the sunrise when my father can't? What's the point in eating cheeseburger pie when my mother can't cook it? What's the point in a birthday party when my wife and daughter aren't there to celebrate it?
What the point of living when they can't share in it? [Kauffman, who has been looking into the distance for this time, now turns his head towards the camera... his eyes are watery, but he stays composed...]
Alex, I don't tell you these things as a means to gain heat. I don't tell you these things to gain an advantage. I tell you these things because a lot of people for a lot of years have made their gain in this sport by being someone they're not, a creation of theirs or someone elses.
Alex, I want you to understand that there's only one Dan Kauffman, and I am that person. No one owns the copyrights to me. No one gave me a character to portray.
When I wrestle, I try to be the best. That I cannot deny, my competitive spirit makes me try to be the best. But you know what? I don't have to win to be successful. If I can make people think about life, if I can make them aware and perhaps give them a light of understanding in a cruel world, I've already won.
I want people to understand where I've been and what I've seen, what I remember and what I've dreamed. And to hell with promoters, presidents and wrestlers telling me it's not okay.
[Kauffman looks into the horizon again, as the suns rises and the shadows become shorter...]
My mother, father, wife and daughter now live in me. And they will be with me always. That includes in the ring.
My mother was an inspiration. My father a dreamer. My wife was a prayer answered, and my daughter my deepest pride.
I will bring my inspirations, my dreams and my pride to our match. And I know that a prayer will be answered. [Kauffman looks back at the camera, composed...] God don't give us many days like this. Trust me, I know. [... before turning around, and walking away from us, the white tee-shirt clinging tohis back, the khakis bending at the crooks of his knees, his hat forming around his head...
Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack - Clack...]
