A heel example: Before I left the WWA, I was doing a kidnapping/seduction angle [1] with a guy named Dante's Discipline. The man was a complete sociopath that just happened to be bisexual. He killed without a thought. He was selfish. He tortured people for the sheer joy of it. And how did he get away with all this you ask? He was rich and paid off the cops whenever they got too close. Anyone can do an "alternative" character, but that cannot be their only trick. (Bad pun unintended.) There has to be depth to the character and little quirks. Take Tigress for example: She fights both men and women (not picky as long as she does it). However, in the WWO, she was the only woman and fought exclusively men. Instant gimmick, right? Wrong. The "woman in a man's world" gimmick, while kinda new, would grow stale after about a month. Plus it's too obvious. So what did I do? I ignored it completely. I made her almost androgynous in her mannerisms and attitude. She wasn't a seductress or a butched out feminist. She was just someone that enjoyed wrestling and didn't see difference in genders. The fact that she was a woman was cursory to her brashness and dry humor. She dismissed pokes at her sexuality, claiming that it was nothing new to her. So anyway, point is: don't make the gimmick painfully obvious. Make it subtle and it should be really fun. -- Peace! >^_^
