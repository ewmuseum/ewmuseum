[Free Agent] "Ice Storm" Chris Winters

14 July 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Enclosed is a sample roleplay. I will join _one_ fed with Winters, and I will field all offers. This is not really one of my better roleplays, but it's something that I had offhand, so check it out and make me an offer if you're interested.
Winters currently competes in IWF/WOW, where he's yet to wrestle and in GSWP, where he's 10-0-1 and the GSWP Television
Champion. <---> [The camera opens up to a dimly lit office. A small green lamp sits on a large
Oak desk. No faces are seen, the camera zooms to show a large oak bookcase with volumes of books on psychiatric help. An aged hand reaches out and pulls one softly off of the shelf and another hand is seen thumbing through it. The title of the book is seen on the binding. It reads, "Coping With Large
Quantities of Stress". A familiar voice is heard, continuing a conversation after some intricate thought.]
VOICE: ... no, that's not it at all. I've been thinking, and I'm wondering if wrestling is really my calling. The stress is getting to me, Doc, and I'm not sure if the pressure is really worth what I'm getting out of this ...
DOCTOR: It all goes back to this, Chris, what exactly _is it_ that you're getting out of it?
[The camera now pans to the patient's side of the desk, where Chris Winters sits on a comfortable leather reclining chair. He's leaned back slightly. A large pack of ice rests on his head, water dripping down his battle hardened face. His mouth creases into a mild grimace as he peers upward at the ceiling, thinking once more. His short brown hair is not slicked or spiked, unusually, and instead is matted down. Winters looks to be in a tired and overworked position.]
CHRIS WINTERS: I don't even know. It's not the money that drives me to do what I do. I've got enough of that to feed a small country. I guess it'd have to be the lust to be _the man_. I think perhaps I'm caught up in the illusion that I should be the best and settle for nothing less. And that's what scares me. I'll do _anything_ to become more than what I am now. I've proved that before. I'll back down from no challenge, I'll duck no competitor, but the lumps I'm taking -- I don't know if I can handle the stress. I got jumped _twice_ last night, Doc. Twice. Most men don't get jumped twice in twenty years. Last night I got slammed into my car about ten times by Eddie Rue, not to mention cut open with a few vicious right hands. And then I got belted out of nowhere by that jealous and overzealous Mike Douglas.
He can't handle the fact that I'm a better athlete than him. He denies the fact that I can defeat him on my worst day. And there is something about that guy that just makes me sick to the pit of my stomach.
DOCTOR: I'd like to know, Chris, exactly what is it that you don't like about Michael Douglas? Is it perhaps that you are afraid that he may steal your spotlight in the near future?
CHRIS WINTERS: No, that's not it at all. You're looking past the problem and seeing the solution. What I'm telling you isn't getting through. I'm not afraid of Mike Douglas stealing my spotlight.
It's merely the opposite. I'm tired of him trying to take my spotlight and continuously ending up on the short end of the stick.
I want him to realize, one day, that attacking Chris Winters isn't going to make him famous. I've been attacked in a million different ways by a million different people. And once I beat them, then what? They're not humbled, and they just do it again. I _pinned_
Mike Douglas, and I want him to finally admit that I was the better man, and that I will be the better man next time we are scheduled for battle. I'm just sick of being blindsided. My head f[bleep]ing hurts.
DOCTOR: Ease up, Chris. I'm not hear to hurt you, only to help you. Foul language isn't necessary to portray your aggression.
We're starting to stray from the topic. I'm trying to get something out of you, and you're not giving me anything to work with. I want to know _why_ you decided to schedule yourself for a match every week for the next five years. Are you hell bent on destroying your body? Don't you have a family to raise?
CHRIS WINTERS: I scheduled myself for a match every week for the next five years to prove a point. And that point is, that I deserve to be the number one wrestler on this planet. I've worked my ass off for the last ten years of my life, and I don't plan on retiring any time soon. What I want is recognition for my accomplishments, but I --
DOCTOR: How on EARTH do you plan on getting recognition for your accomplishments when all you're doing is fighting an uphill battle?
Listen, Mr. Winters, I don't mean to burst your bubble here but it's quite obvious that the GSWP doesn't plan on putting you up to main event status. That day you're searching for will never come, and the self inflicted pain you're inducing is only going to conclude with your ultimate demise. Your wrestling career will come to a crashing halt. And I'm telling you, if you don't take next week off, you're going to end up beaten mentally and emotionally. And this is coming from a certified doctor.
CHRIS WINTERS: Next week I'm in the ring with "Hate Child" Ethan Love. I have a chance to extract some revenge on a friend of a foe. Eddie Rue has been harassing me week in and week out, and the guy just won't get off my back. If it's a Television Title shot he wants, hell, I'll give him one. I've never been one to dodge competition. But I wish he'd just come right out and
_say_ that he wants a title shot instead of torturing me. I'm not a bad person, I try to be fair and upstanding. And I don't deserve to be treated the way everybody has been treating me lately. I've laid my body and my soul on the line too many times to be rubbed out by a --
DOCTOR: [interrupting] Chris, there's a very simple way to solve the problems that have been arising with Eddie Rue, and both you and I know how you can put an end to that scenario.
Stop prank calling him. It's quite obvious that he's not a very stable man, and what you're doing to him is going to drive him to the point of murdering you in cold blood.
CHRIS WINTERS: [groaning] But there's a problem with that, Doc. DOCTOR: A problem? CHRIS WINTERS: Yeah, a problem. I haven't _made_ any calls to Eddie Rue's house. I don't even have his number. I'm not the type who would do petty things such as prank call, and if I did? I'd admit to it and laugh in his face. I know I can beat anybody in
GSWP right now. I don't _think_ I can, I _know_ I can. So there would be no reason for me to play with this guy's mind when I could just go out there and beat him in the ring. And Ethan Love?
"Hate Child"? He's going to receive just a small sample of what I have to offer. Next week he and I are going to do battle, and it is going to be messy and violent. As each day goes by, I'm weakening mentally and physically, but I'm gaining momentum. The
Vicious Circle has only began to wage war, and if you're expecting me to duck out now and end my career, you're sadly mistaken. I'd love to take a week or so off, but that's just not possible at this point, and next week? I'm going to do the same thing I've done on
Impact for the last two and a half months. I'm going to go out there and _win_. I'm going to score another victory for the
Vicious Circle, and after Ethan Love comes Eddie Rue. Rest isn't an option, Doc. I hope you understand. Thanks for all your 'help', but all this session has done is made my path a little more clear. What you'd like me to do is not plausible, and what I'd like to do is possible. If it means I work my body into a state of near death? So be it. But I'm going to keep winning every week, and I'm going to refuse to weaken. My father, god rest his soul, would love to see me now ...
[Winters gets up from the reclining chair and takes the ice off of his head. This reveals a large open wound, a slit down the middle of his forehead. He turns around and makes his way towards the large oak exit as the camera fades slowly to black.]
