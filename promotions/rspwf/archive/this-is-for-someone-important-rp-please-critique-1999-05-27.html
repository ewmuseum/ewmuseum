This is for Someone Important. (RP, please critique.)

27 May 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well, seeing as I'm ignored when I ask for a critique in_general, I've targeted one person who just gave a_damn_good and accurate review to someone
(Specifically-the Drifter).  That being the person that goes by Someone
Important.        Okay, I'll assume for right now that you'll do it, and base my comments in this request on that. (I hope so, I'm only getting reviews from one person normally.  He likes one in particular, but he knows the character inside and out.)  Basically I want good, well thought-out criticism from someone who, hopefully, can understand the character.
      That's what I've found is my biggest problem, being understood in one RP.  Some people just_don't_get it until they see 3 or more RP's.  With the first one they say "He comes off as a nut" (The character-Craven.) or
"Isn't that just Raven with a C?" (William Craven, Craven not being used as an alias in this case.) So, how does it come off to you?

NICW= "Big Stupid" Tobias Rodland.   ACWA homepage URL: http://welcome.to/ACWA   .          The screen goes black after a commercial break.  A few seconds of dead air and the sound of "Sex type thing" by Stone Temple Pilots fades in, blaringly loud it seems almost the reason for the darkness.  The sound is almost enough to blot out the world.  On and on it plays, and with the lyrics "Here I come, I come,
I come, I come", the darkness ends. . .
     . . .A scene appears.  No fade, just a mass of still images playing in rapid succession.  The images are scenes from the
Craven vs Steve Starr pit match.  One showing Craven being piledriven through a pane of glass.  One with Craven hurling Starr down into the pit.  One with Starr spraying Craven with fire, and so on and so forth until finally the screen comes to rest upon an image of Steve Starr.  His face bearing the cuts from the straight razor attack by Craven.  Starr screams bloody murder while staring into the hand mirror left for him by Craven.
. .      With an explosion of sound the scene goes white.  "Sex type thing" fades to silence and this word fades in in black. . .
. . .Fruit?. . .      Finally a real scene fades in.  It is the farmland that housed the pit match.  In the light of day it is now apparent that this is the land which Craven has used as a backdrop in many of his promotional spots.  The camera pans around showing the rickety barn that held the junk that Craven used in the Pit Match.  It probably holds more, but this is not clear.  The stone slab and field of flowers lay across from the front of the barn.  The slab still bears a reddish tint from Craven's blood.  The wooden post that Craven leaned against when he spoke of consequences, Riot at his side.  Finally, the pit itself.  The abandoned hole that would, should have cradled the foundations of a farmhouse.  Wide and deep it yawns, a parody of the peaceful home that it should have been.  The sides are caved in and it is still filled with the blood-covered junk that was used in the Pit match.  From off camera, laughter is heard.  The camera wheels about to show Craven, dressed only in jeans, kneeling close to the ground, staring intently at something through a magnifying glass. . .
     Craven creeps along, grinning widely while examining the parched earth that surrounds the pit, following a trail with a tenacious glee.  He moves slowly, stirring up gravel and sand, sweeping dried grass aside, and finally he stops.  Pointing, a fecal-matter devouring grimace/grin marring his scarred face even further, he looks to camera. 
Hissing like a child sharing a secret pleasure with a friend, but hiding it from his parents, Craven relays to the camera the source of his elation.
     "See?  See?  Do you see?  Right there!  Closer!  Look!  SHOW THE WORLD!"      The camera moves closer, zooming in on one spot of sand and earth that bares a reddish tint.  "That's blood. 
Starr's blood!"      Then Craven's hand moves, beckoning the camera to follow.  "Then. . .here, he moves on all fours, crawling, trying to get away.  Heh-heh, he tried to leave on his own!  Oh, here, here!"
     Craven bounds to his feet and dances a twisted jig to a set of tiretracks.  "Here, the gurney.  Footprints, that's the paramedic's footprints.  Over here, they loaded him up, and drove him off to the hospital"
     Craven claps his hands and giggles like a schoolgirl.  He hops and follows the tiretracks for a few feet, to a deep furrow in the earth.  "They almost got stuck in the loose earth. 
Oooh, the humor!"  Craven drops to his knees, fondling the earth, picking up a small pebble and rolling it between his thumb and forefinger. 
"Almost, Steve.  You almost went to the hereafter.  You in your grave, alone, unmourned, and unloved.  Ro-zay doesn't give a flying
*BLEEP*ing pig with wings, Mia Farrow wants you only for the mealticket that you are.  Riot?  Don't_make_me_laugh.  He loves money, something I knew from the beginning.  Heh, I just figged that maybe I was paying him enough."
     Suddenly Craven is standing.  He just hops up from his knees to his feet without using his hands.  With a deft motion and a flick of the wrist Craven throws the pebble off into the horizon. 
Cravens mood has quickly swung from one of jubilation to one of quiet pondering.  He looks down at the ground, lips pursed, and seems to consider some deep, philosophical question.
     "Cherries."  Craven spits the word as if spitting a pit from the fruit itself.  After this, however, he is oddly silent.  He scratches his chin, turns from camera to face the sun on the horizon, scratches his armpit, sniffs it, and chuckles.  Some interior joke has caught his attention and he seems to have forgotten the camera.
     Suddenly Craven whips around to face the camera.  A few short steps and his reddish, worn face fills the camera lens.  "Can't you see?  It all revolves around plants and repetition!  Look at it!  Back, back, back to before the founding of our country!  George Washington chopping down that little cherry tree, and telling his father that it was indeed he that took the woody fruit source out of the ground!  Why didn't he get spanked for his confession?  Simple, very simple!  It wasn't a confession at all, my friends, but a threat!  You see, as he told his father that he chopped the tree down, he still had the axe in hand, shaking it excitedly.  He dared daddy, 'SPANK ME
IF YOU DARE OLD MAN'!!!  Daddy didn't whoop the little psycho, and for that the fruit died!"
     Craven turns back to the horizon.  The cameraman shifts uncomfortably in his place.  Muttered comments are heard from the cameraman of "Didn't take his medication." and "I thought that Mr. Washington DID spank George.".  Craven paces in a hyperkinetic fashion to and fro, looking for the words to fill his airtime.
     "Who was it that said 'he that doesn't learn from history is destined to repeat it'?  Carlisle?  Who cares? 
It's true.  The fruity panted funnymen have overlooked what has gone before.  They didn't look in the record books, they didn't check their history.  You see, with the possible exception of Sir Issac Newton being hit on the head with an apple, nuts beat fruits EVERY TIME!"
     Craven circles the cameraman, walking towards the field of flowers.  With a motion of his hand he tells the cameraman to follow him.  It is a short trip, as Craven comes to the stone slab.  A small bunch of uprooted briars, parched and dead, occupies Craven's reddened slab, his altar.
     "Oh, and RO-ZAY!  The Rose.  The fruitiest of the fruity funnymen, and the weakest of the wines!  The one with the least ability and the largest mouth.  This plant is really going out on a limb.  Just thinking of his tender petals makes me go bananas!  Now, I know that bananas have no bones, and I know that you can't get blood from a turnip, but the Rose is one fruity plant that I just want to beat to a bloody pulp and break the bones of! In the end, I'll leave him a vegetable.  You want to fight me?  Oh, you will, funnyman.  I shall test your fruityness, and you will touch my way of life.  First you'll know my scars, and then you'll see that "Prison Sex" is more than just my entrance music!  The prison is the body, and the prisoner my soul.  I WILL BE YOUR BUBBA AND YOU WILL BE MY BITCH!!!"
     Craven lifts the long-dead bush from the stone, once unraveled it is clearly the remnants of a rose bush.  The roses that Craven destroyed in his fit of rage some months ago.  With one exercise of his powerful arms, he shreds the bush, scattering it to the four winds.  His back to the camera, Craven continues his diatribe.    
     "I know the truth, I see it all.  Turnips have no blood and Riot is a coward!  What I did to Starr I must do to all of the funnymen.  The king of Egypt, the flowered fruit, and my_ex-friend, the Bride of Frankenstein-Riot.  Don't believe that I can?  Look at the footage.  Starr's begun his descent.  He's made his way down, into the pit.  Do you remember the stages?  The stages of descent? 
Look back, look back on my words.  Remember Ro-zay, you may have Farrow, you may have Riot, but I have my boys too.  Maxx, Kenny, and me-Bill. 
Me, I'm gonna bite away at your exterior and spit out your bones.  In the end_you'll_be_just_like_Starr. . ."
     Turning back to camera, Craven grins wide without showing the least bit of mirth.  Lines form from his features in the corners of his mouth, eyes, and across his forehead.  Seeming fit to burst, his ice-blue, blootshot eyes glare balefully in two different directions. 
One straight into the camera lens, the other off to one side.  At this time the camera freezes, and zooms in on a still of Craven's face.  "Sex
Type Thing" by Stone Temple Pilots plays loudly again, as an image of the
Rose fades in to replace the one of Craven's face.  This image too fades out soon after to reveal an image of Steve Starr after the Pit Match with the same pattern of scars marring his face that mar Craven's.  Finally the screen fades to black.  "Sex Type Thing" plays loud enough to wake the dead before fading away suddenly, then silence. . .
