[Free Agent] Robert Mula

1 February 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Plain ol' unused Sample flash from Angry Rob, aka Robert Mula. Just looking for a nice, stable fed that plans on being around awhile. If you're interested, well, y'know what to do. Anything else, I can email ya.
[Norfolk can be a busy time during the post-Millenium celebration store rush. With people scurrying to exchange gifts, rushing to blowout sales, one guy riding his bicycle down the street, and running for those afterthought gifts, one could get lost at some of the shopping centers like the one the scene opens with. However, it's still close enough to the holidays to have a store Santa working a sidewalk Salvation Army donation kettle.
While most would never give a second glance, some can notice that this isn't your normal Santa. The demeanor is of a jolly, happy Santa Claus, whereas the soul of our volunteer is that of an angry, spiteful man, better known as Angry
Rob.
Rob's been doing a good job today. He hasn't snapped on anyone, hasn't kicked any curious kiddies, and hasn't tried to kill his volunteer coordinator. As a matter of fact, Rob's been disturbingly quiet. Even as he turns to face the camera, he makes no sound other than that of the bell in his hand.]
Rob: Y'know, I've always loved the holidays. It's the time of giving, not taking. It's a period of wonder, not terror. It's a time where people can, for a short while, forget some of the past memories and come up with a new batch. People can help people who can barely afford the food that must nourish them. They can buy food with the money we take in. Food, clothing, shelter are what they need, not sympathy alone. They won't buy needless things, like luxury soaps or watches or gold...
Rob pauses for a moment, noting the presence of a guy doing tricks on his bike in the parking lot. With a heavy breath, Rob continues his collection duties...
Rob: Gold. The object of many men's desires. In the global economy, gold is the standard of trade. Gold represents wealth. Gold represents success. I think it was a Jack Palance character once said, "There are two important things in life: one is that 'one thing' that everyone has and then there's
GOLD!" But Gold is a metal, and a transition metal at that. Even with an atomic mass of 196.967, and an atomic number of seventy-nine, gold is an fairly useless element. In normal conditions, Gold is cold. Gold is hard, but too soft to be of any efficient use. Gold doesn't conduct electricity very well.
Gold cannot comfort. Gold cannot soothe. Yet somehow, gold is the desire of men. Justin Storm is a man who is after gold. To him, gold represents his ticket to the big time: the World Heavyweight Championship belt. He, like all the others, wants to be the next champion.
[Rob pauses a moment...] Rob: Justin, you are right. Your ambitions are not unique. In the time I have spent, I have met many like yourself. Some have gone on to greater things, others have disappeared into the mediocrity of limbo. They have all made the same mistake: looking past me on their road to stardom. Many times I stand in the way of their ambitious hunger for their title hunt, and many times
I stand triumphant over the souls of such pitiful fools. But a fifty-fifty chance? I think not. Are you so short-sighted that you do not believe you can become like them? Are you so vain that you cannot realize that others, too, have fought hard for what I have earned? If that is what you believe, then you are a fool. An utter, callous fool who belongs with the garbage (motions toward the bike rider, who is riding towards him). Like this freak...
[The bike rider, a young man in his mid-teens, swoops by. The boy grabs a hold of the kettle, and tries to ride off. A split second later, the boy learns that the kettle has been welded to the stand, and the stand has been bolted down to the concrete. The boy, unable to let go of the kettle fast enough, is violently yanked from his bicycle and lands on the ground with a satisfying
"THUD." Unflinching, Rob jerks the boy to his feet and slams him back-first against the brick wall.]
Rob: WHAT THE HELL IS YOUR PROBLEM?!? Stealing from the Salvation Army? How low can you friggin' get!
Boy: I had to! It's the only way I could help pay my heating bill! We don't make much, and it's winter, if ya can't tell!
Rob: Don't lie to me, boy. I can tell when someone's lying to me. Someone put you up to this, now who was it!
Boy: Man, you can't scare me. You ain't nothing but a cheap store Santa! [The boy tries to fight Rob off, violently tugging at Rob's coat. The boy rips open the red coat, believing that there's a padding in the suit. Thing is, there is no padding, it's all Rob. It's not soft, either, more like a hard, tough leather. The boy tugs more, to reveal the front of Rob's trademark green
T-shirt. Emblazoned across the front, plain as daylight, are the words "TIME to DIE!" Recognizing the shirt, the boy's eyes widen into a panic-stricken frenzy.]
Boy: Oh, (censored)!!! Okay, okay! I'll talk! It was the Chicken-Head Crew. They been bullying some of the neighborhood kids into doing their dirty work for them. They tell us if we don't, they come back and break our kneecaps.
Rob: That's better. Y'know I probably should rip your throat out. But then, that wouldn't be in keeping with the holiday season, now would it? No. I'm in a fairly good mood, so I'm gonna give you a second chance. I'm going to tell you what to do, and you're going to listen. You are then going to do exactly what I tell you, UN-DER-STAND?
Boy: Y-y-yessir. Rob: In about three to five minutes from now, a police officer will be passing by on his patrol beat. You are going to go and tell him exactly what you just told me. I'm going to forget about this little incident if you do that. Are we clear?
Boy: Yes, sir. Tell cops everything. Got it. Rob: Gooooood. I'll be watching you. [Rob lets go of the boy, and slowly returns to the stand. He pauses for a moment, maintaining a watchful eye. He then begins talking to the camera again...]
Rob: It's a tragedy. People like him think what they are doing is right. They think that the ends justify the means. Do the reasons give enough cause for them to wrong another? Are you like them, Justin Storm? Is your thirst for the title strong enough to make you step over the backs of the weak?
But, then, maybe I'm not giving you the benefit of the doubt. Perhaps you are different. Perhaps you can learn that the true test of a champion is his ability to stand for what they believe in. You gotta ask yourself one thing:
what do you believe in? Gold? Money? Power? Titles? Victory? Even Vengeance? Or do you prefer the more "virtuous" parts of life, like Justice?! Compassion?! What do you believe in, Justin Storm?!? WHERE DOES YOUR HEART LIE??? [Rob watches as the boy talks to a police officer. A moment later, the boy goes off with the police officer in his squadcar. Rob feigns a smile, but turns and looks dead straight into the camera.]
Rob: To me, these things do not matter. The fight is what drives me. Go ahead, have your cake and eat it too. I accept your challenge, and I will wait in the ring. You are mine, Justin Storm. You will not walk away from our meeting the same.
Whatever it is, Storm, leave your ego in the locker room. You will have no room for petty tricks or nonsense. You will find no fear in me. Fear rules those weak enough to accept it, and I accept NOTHING!!! You want a chance to get a title shot? You have to start with me! C'mon down, Justin Storm, and if you wanna rock... hey, LET'S ROCK!!!
[With that, Rob puts his cap over the camera, plunging the scene into darkness.]
