Free Agent Looking for a fed

29 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hi folks, It's been a long time, but the itch is back and I'm diving into the ring one more time, hopefully without the interruptions of school like in the past.
A lot has changed in e-wrestling since I started in 1995, but I've been lurking for awhile now and I'm starting to get the feel for things again.
I'm including a generic application, something I put together for a fed that closed even before it got off the ground (seems to be the common theme these days *sigh*). Take a look and let me know if there's room in your fed for
The Canadian Savage. I'll be honest right off the start, I'm in this for fun. I'm too old to care if I'm the King of the Intercontinental Hardcore
TV Champion of the Internet anymore. This is just an outlet for some pent up creativity.
I don't have a sample flash. If you like what you've read so far, I'm pretty sure you'll like my stuff when match time rolls around. They always seem to work better when it's based on an upcoming match, or to develop a storyline. I've got time for about a match a week or so, just so you know the kind of commitment I'm able to make. Also, I don't have an inner misogynist, so if the people on your roster get off beating up on women in every single flash, I'll respectfully decline.
Well, enough rambling, let's get down to it. Again, if you like what you see, drop me a line and we'll talk. If not, thanks for the time.
[name withheld] Handler of The Canadian Savage Height: 6'2" (Too tall for the midget division, to short for the NBA. Sometimes this "fantasy" stuff hits a little close to home). Weight: 252 (Close your eyes and picture this fine physical specimen so far. Then remember that those were the first three digits to come into my mind).
Build: Compact. Think Chris Benoit on someone slightly taller. Ethnicity: Caucasian (with a hint of Yugoslavian for some ethnic flare) Hometown: Banff, Alberta, Canada. (Too many wrestlers already claiming to be from Calgary, so we head an hour west. Actually, nobody is born in
Banff, I don't think. But it was either that or Balzac, so you can see why
I picked it).
Appearance: Brown hair that has a tendency to get in his eyes during a match. Clean shaven (although he tends to go a few days between groomings).
Piercing green eyes (I don't know what they pierce, but it sounds good). No tattoos, but lots of scars on his forehead, arms, back and chest due to a career full of rather dangerous and ultimately foolish "novelty"matches.
These tend to open easily during matches (I'm talking Dusty Rhodes "Crimson Mask" easy here). Wears a red wrestling singlet with black shoulder straps and black trim. SAVAGE is written across the back in white script. Entrance Theme: "Eruption" by Van Halen. Wrestling Style: Technical brawler. Emphasis on the Technical. Finisher: The Savage Submission. Finisher Description: Spinning Piledriver from the top turnbuckle! Just kidding. It's a step over toehold facelock, more commonly called an STF.
The only modification I could even come up with for this one is he chickenwings his opponents near arm, and then clamps down. Try it out on friends and loved ones!
5 Favourite Moves: Belly to Belly Superplex (ala Rick Steiner. LOVE that move!) Flying Back Elbow. Running Clothesline (again I'm thinking of my man Steiner here). Triple Northern Lights Suplex (hey, if Benoit can drop somebody three times while the fans eat it up, so can I). Arm Bar (nobody sees it coming).
Gimmick/Defining Character motive: A road weary veteran taking one more run at the big time.
Background: The Canadian Savage began his career in 1994 as a wide-eyed rookie with some amateur credentials. His introduction to the professional game was a harsh reality check (no money, few fans, crummy arenas) but he stuck it out in the rings of western Canada and gradually developed a devoted, although small following of fans. As the months went by, The
Canadian Savage climbed the ladder in both the rankings and in the hearts of the fans.
A match with King Carnage in Vancouver in early 1995 almost ended The Canadian Savage's dreams of wrestling stardom. Wrestling in his first title match, The Canadian Savage fought valiantly against the more experienced champion and was on his way to winning championship gold until a loaded boot to the back of the head ended the match and, very nearly, his career.
Six months later, a little bigger, a little stronger, and a lot less naive,
The Canadian Savage exacted a brutal revenge on King Carnage and claimed his first championship. But like most who seek revenge, The Canadian Savage paid more than just a physical price for his redemption. Although he maintained a legion of fans, and travelled across the country defending his championship, something had changed deep down inside the young star. The cheers didn't resonate as loudly. The wins didn't come as easily. Slowly, match by match, The Canadian Savage was growing to resent the fans for their support. There was no rational explanation for his attitude towards the people who had supported him all along. Perhaps something had been knocked loose by that boot. Perhaps the trials and tribulations of life on the road was getting to him. Or perhaps The Canadian Savage, lured by the money and first class treatment bestowed on a champion, was prepared to do whatever it took to defend his belt.
A few months went by and The Canadian Savage lost his championship in a loser leaves town match. He had signed a contract with one of the Big 10 wrestling leagues in the States, and figured he was on his way to bigger and better things. But fate had something more in store for him than fame, money and championship belts.
On his way to his first house show for his new employer, on a lonely stretch along the back roads of northern Minnesota, The Canadian Savage hit a patch of ice and skidded his rental car into a ditch. After the initial shock and adrenaline rush wore off, he felt a stabbing pain in his right calf. He moved his leg, but his foot refused to follow and fell limply to the side.
Using his cell phone to call for emergency roadside assistance, he was rushed to hospital, where doctors discovered a compound fracture of his
Tibia.
Doctors told The Savage he would walk again, with the assistance of a cane. He began the slow and painful recovery process. There was no talk of wrestling. An injure like the one The Canadian Savage sustained didn't inspire promoters to come running with multi year deals. His physical conditioning before the accident minimized the long term damage to his leg, and eventually he surpassed the orthopaedic surgeons predictions and regained 90% of his pre-accident mobility.
After a number of years away from the ring, a chance encounter with a promoter in a downtown Calgary watering hole reignited the competitive fire that had been all but extinguished in the accident. Through his contacts in the personal training industry, he was able to escalate his rehab, and began wrestling warm-ups with students at a local high school.
Although he will carry the scars of his accident for the rest of his life, he also carries the burning question of "What if?" What if the accident had never happened? How far could he have gone? The Canadian Savage is coming back, against the advice of every medical professional in the western hemisphere, to answer that question.
