Lacking inspiration for a flash?

EW University
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Lacking inspiration for that flash? By [name withheld] Flashes can become so…repetitive. You find yourself writing the same insults, making the same tough macho comments, and basically boring yourself with it. And when you become bored, the people reading it become bored. So here are some tips on how to keep your flashes as fresh as possible, or at least some ideas when you`re lacking ideas. Talk about the previous card, or the upcoming card. Don`t just talk about your match, talk about EVERY match. Be involved in an angle. Usually, as long as you`re in an angle of sorts, there should be some material with which to cut a flash. Delve into your characters life. This is often called a 'character flash', when it has little to do with e-wrestling, but instead offers an insight into what the wrestler does when he isn`t wrestling. So many e-wrestlers appear to be full -time wrestlers, but that is the minority in the real world. Most of the indy league wrestlers have day jobs like a mechanic or a carpenter or teacher or something. Use that. And if you haven`t got that 'alter ego' - create one! Write a flash with someone else. Perhaps use ICQ or IRC or AIM or MIM or whatever. Maybe exchange e-mails back and forth. Either way, a joint flash can often inspire ideas. Plan a story-arc, and base several flashes around that. Maybe visiting a sick relative far away, or travelling to the event or arrested by the police or visiting their family. Whatever. Make it a story, with a beginning, middle and end. People like stories, and that is what you are doing everytime you flash, everytime you wrestle - you`re adding another bit to that characters` story. Make it interesting. Need ideas for a story? Look at newspapers, listen to the lyrics of songs, get ideas from books and TV as well. There`s lots of places. All you need to do is look. Do a 'flash' in the style of a magazine article, giving third person commentary and analysis. This method is exceptionally underused, but particularly effective. Talk about a load of wrestlers in your fed. Give your comments on them, good and bad. Not only is it a good way to do a flash, it can build heat! Put together a summary of everything that has happened to your wrestler recently - a series of flashbacks as it were - setting them in their current storyline. Include match extracts for example. Just look at what you usually write - AND DON`T WRITE ANYTHING LIKE THAT! The easiest way to be original and new is just to avoid doing what you usually do.
