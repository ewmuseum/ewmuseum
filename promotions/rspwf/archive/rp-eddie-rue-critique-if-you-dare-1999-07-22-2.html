> Well, for once I'm actually going to CRITIQUE your roleplay, >instead >of flame you for trying. I must say for starters, that it's a very well done >roleplay. Thanks. >I'm more of a comic heel mark myself, but I handled an eerie figure myself >for >about 2 years of my ewrestling 'career'. Though I don't know much about your >character, it seemed that sometimes you ventured a bit out of character. Some would think so too. But if you didn't pick it up, I ever so subtly said: "Trying to maintain his ominous standpoint. He slowly brings his hand to his chest. Patting it with coven witness to his own multiple-persona behavior."
I said he was trying to maintain an ominous standpoint for starters, I tried to suggest cooly that he could break into laughter, tears, or another emotion at any second. Then him tapping his chest -- and the who part about being coven witness.
If you are a coven witness to your own acts, you are considered to have a multiple-personality disorder. I am not talking about Mic Foley -- where I'd switch into a totally different outfit and character name. But Rue exhibits an unstable personality. It's one of his weaknesses.
I kind of further hoped people would understand what coven witness to your own acts is when I said 'multiple-persona behavior.'
That about sums that up...next part of this critique. > The >only ways I see of solving this are to avoid the areas where you speak out >of >character, or tone the character down a bit. It almost seems as if you're >sitting >on the fence, The fence, eh? Well, since we are talking about fences. I guess a fence surrounds a house, and most houses has a shed...I sort of consider Eddie not as
'I am not the sharpest tool in the shed'. But rather Eddie is in the shed -- looking to find a tool to work with.
>trying to decide whether you would like to go with an eerie, >mysterious >figure as opposed to a tough, worker type of guy. AH! TOUCHDOWN! [Peels the cap off the top of the football and does a celebratory dance -- acting like the football is a cool hawaiian alcoholic beverage.]
I don't always say this on all the applications I apply on. But Eddie Rue is really my old character; Peter Damien. The only difference is that Peter
Damien underwent emotionally scarring beyond which his mind could cope with.
So... "ENTER THE NEW PETER DAMIEN" Peter Damien's old nickname was "The Edge" because he was somewhat pyscho- in his willingness to throw his body on the line. But he was a crafty, smart semi-heel(a heel that gets the crowd to like him unintentionally). Peter sort of was that pervasive comic heel - to a degree. Peters' main thing was he was a manipulative man. He'd take scenarios and blow them out-of-proportion with ease.
Peter Damien's emotionally scarring came when his spouse died on -- of all irony -- Halloween. If you want to throw on some more unneeded irony, his spouse's name was
Diedre. Peter took his nickname "Edge" and cut off the -ge forming "Ed" then tacking on the first 3 letters of his wife's name, which happens to be Die.
"Eddie". Rue means sorrow. And scarring and sorrow can go hand-in-hand a lot. So his initials are "ER"? Nope. Tack on the "Heartless" nickname before and it becomes "H.E.R." In other words -- he subtly did it all for HER...
Then you have his finisher -- Rue de Die(dre). His setup is the Cold
Chamber. Also -- it's Eddie that says that at his old house where he and Diedre lived, at the old oak tree -- he carved a symbol of their love. A heart with
"ER + DM" carved within.
Eddie claims that the heart he carved was his own from within his body, he put it in that tree, claiming that as long as we live together, my heart belongs to 'you'. Well, when Diedre died -- Eddie lost his heart. For it was forever lost in the trunk of that tree.
And that is why he is Heartless. And this my friend -- is how you create a solid gimmick from head-to-toe. Reading on... >I loved how in depth your >descriptions of Rue's body movements and expressions were. They helped >me picture the scene far better than any other I've seen. Yeah. I've been working on that a lot. But the fact that it is the best you've seen disturbs me...Because that would be the first time I am the best at something in e-wrestling; or anything for that matter.
>I liked the way >you >attacked your subject, but I think you could have went a little longer, a >litte more >in depth. When I am done making my point. I am done. I see a lot of role-players. Even the good ones tend to drag on and it decreases the quality in the role-play. >The last bit of advice I have, is unless it's what you're really >going for, >I'd try to stray from the unreal. Saying you're going to cremate your >opponent. True. But if you know the character week-to-week...he will paint a metaphoric picture in the listeners' mind. I guess I could have coorelated cremating his body to leaving an emotional scar.
>Incorporating it metaphorically is one thing, but that's not how it sounded. >Well, >that's all for me. Take this for what you will, and take into conideration >that I'm >just another ewrestler...and it's 3:55 am EST. :D Oops...Oh well. I'll make sure to read the end of the critique next time :) But for whoever reads this post -- it could help to show you a lot about developing a character.
