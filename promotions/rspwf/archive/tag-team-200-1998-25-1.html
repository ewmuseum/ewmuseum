1998 RSPWF Tag Team 200 (25-1)

6 February 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The top 25... 25. Wugline (Rob James & Glenn Hudson) [LWC] -What can be said about Wugline? Bigger Than Hollywood, Bigger Than Life, Bigger Than Your Man. Wugline was, without a doubt, one of the most dominant tag teams of 1998, monopolizing the Tag Titles in LWC for nearly half of the year, a feat made almost superhuman by the strength of roster. Wugline, heading into 1999 as The Nine Inch Males and still packing the LWC Tag Gold, show no signs of slowing down. 24. The Goths (Azreal & Belial) [IWC] -Azrael and Belial, the team of the Goths, along with their silent, and emotionless manager, Lucretia, debuted in the IWC in the spring of '98. After a long and bitter feud with the Best of the Best. They finally overcame their rivals and became the IWC Tag Team champions. They held the titles for 2 months until losing them to the team of Teen Angst. 23. Fracas Boys (Kristoff Johnson & Adrienne Creed) [SCRA] The Fracas Boys had a phenominal year. They really broke out on to the scene in the SCRA, capturing the World Tag Team Titles on numerous occasions. And feuding with some of the bets tag teams in the SCRA. Among them, The Prophets of Rage, The Northpac Coalition, and The Black Watch. It's a new year, so let's see what the new year brings these boys. 22. Prophets of Rage (Derek Rage & Shadow Rage) [IIWF] The Prophets of rage have been labeled by many to be one of the best tag teams in IIWF history. They captured the strap twice, and have fought amongst the best tag teams in the game and won. Who knows what the future holds for this dangerous duo. 21. Baddest Thangs Running (Gunnar Gaines & Caleb Temple) [IIWF] - When two legends of this sport decide to band together, Bad Thangs happen. Gunnar Gaines, a former RSPWF 200 #1, and Caleb Temple, a true ICON of e-Wrestling, put their differences aside and terrorized the IIWF Tag Ranks in the early parts of '98. The fact that the team has been disbanded for the largest part of the year is made insignifigant by their sheer dominance and their win at IIWF Forever over the formidable team of Eddy "Flap" Jacks and Rick Williams. The Baddest Thangs Running may not be running any more, but they sure are staying- in the memories of e-Fans everywhere. 20. Pain & Suffering [IWF\WOW] The current IWF/WOW Tag Team Champions burst on the scene late in the year. Managed by the Mistress Sonia, Pain and his partner Suffering are quite unique. Though Sonia does almost all of their talking outside the ring, the team seems quite proficient at making their statement IN the ring, and an upset victory for the IWF/WOW Tag Team Championships at the end of the year sealed their place on this list. Currently busy trying to fend off the challenges of the Killing Machines, Death & Destruction, and Pain Inc 2000, among others, Pain & Suffering will have to stay sharp to maintain their position in 1999! 19. Power & Honour (Jason Keening & Gabrielle Whitecross) [UEW] The members of Power & Honor are better known as singles wrestlers but "The Era Of Defiance" Gabriel Whitecross and "Screaming Drillbit" Jason Keening have combined to make a tremendous impact in a relatively short time. When Keening won a title shot against the UEW Ultimate Tag Champions, The Outlaws, he formed a team with his old friend Whitecross and they came dangerously close to winning the belts on their first match as a duo! Since then, memorable feuds with the Perfectly Perfect Duo and Circle of Death along with winning the UEW Television Tag Team Championship have meant that Power & Honor is more than just the sum of its parts! 18. Down Boys (Adam Peterson & Dan Oliver) [IIWF, EMWC] The Down Boys proved once again in 1998 to be one of the best atg teams in our sport. They were guided to a short Tag Title reign in the EMWC by their sock manager, Jivy. They also captured the IIWF World Tag Team Title, for a short reign. 17. Rich & Infamous (Hayvin Coules & Vince Valor) [EMWC] Hayvin Coules and Vince Valor's 1998 begain in the MLWO where they were the longest reigning MLWO World Tag Team Champions ever. However, after the MLWO's demise...and Valor's "death" at the hands of Coules, it appeared that the R&I were done for. However, in late September, Hayvin Coules reemerged in the EMWC...and soon after, Vince Valor was "resurrected". The cripple and the angel made an immediate splash on the tag team division, quickly becoming the #1 contenders. At press time, their team was on the rocks, but regardless if they stay together or split, the R&I truly were one of the greatest teams of 1998. 16. Killing Machines (Overkill & Demolisher) [IWF\WOW] Overkill and Demolisher are one of the all-time great tag teams in IWF/WOW history. This year, the duo, managed by [name withheld], not only took the top prize in I/W, but also added several other tag titles to their trophy case. Though they dropped the gold to Pain & Suffering in a triangle match at the end of the year, their 1998 won-loss record was quite impressive, and their title reign was marked by a very memorable feud with the West Coast Connection. The team continues to be the top contenders, and arguably the biggest threats, in the I/W's tag division. Definitely an oft-overlooked team who has earned their spot amongst the sport's elite. However..they managed to win the PCW tag belts the first night they ever wrestled together. PCW closed soon after..so they headed to MLWO..where they became the tag team that held the MLWO World Tag Belts the longest. They also won the Japanese Tag Belts in MLWO..just before it closed. When MLWO closed...Vince Valor was found lying in a pool of his own blood...while Hayvin Coules sat over him...with his head in his hands. It is thought that Coules killed Valor. However...Valor is back from the dead..and "believes"..or would like you to believe that he is an Angel. That's where Valor stands right now. 15. Fire & Ice ("Red Hot" Alex Perkins, "Iceman" Michael Ice) [WWO] Former WWO tag team who returned to the organization in 1998. So far they've had a rough return ever since an early shot at the World Tag Team Titles from Uncivil Disobedience, but along with Rachel Edwards Ice, have seen gold in the organization in the past, as part of the six man tag team champions, and if they ever return to form you can be sure that they are a threat at any given time. 14. West Coast Connection (Mike Johnston & Sean Kroaker) [IWF\WOW] "The Enforcer" Sean Kroaker and "The Wildman" Mike Johnston wrestled all over the place in 1998, in DWI, NEWA, and others, eventually landing in IWF/WOW. Kroaker was originally teamed up with Hightower... but Kroaker turned his back on him, and began teaming with Johnston midway through the year. The team had a very memorable feud with the Killing Machines in the second half of the year, from "helping" the Machines gain the I/W tag titles in late summer, to pinning them in the triangle elimination match in which the Machines lost the titles. The Connection never did manage to gain the titles they coveted, but they did showcase their talent for all to see. 13. Northpac Coalition (Timothy N. Turner & Akira Saito) [SCRA] - The NorthPac Coalition were one of the most explosive teams this year. The Rocketman and his partner, Saito, spent the whole year at the top of the tag team ranks in SCRA, involved in a bloody fued with the MacBeth brothers. Holding the SCRA Tag Titles for a while, The NorthPac's careers soared like a rocket this year, pun heavily intended. 12. Death & Destruction (Perry Neptune & John Achillies) [IWF\WOW] Death and Destruction is one of the better-known, and just plain beter, as shown by this ranking, independant teams in the world. With the always outspoken "Diamond Girl" Heidi Leick as their manager and mouthpiece (though they freely give their opinion on just about anything), Perry "Death" Neptune and John "Destruction" Achilles have been in many places, and excited many fans. The controversial trio have usurped broadcast booths, interjected themselves into matches, and done just about anything that they could get away with. And they've done it with style. A team to watch out for in1999, D&D has a bright future. Assuming Neptune doesn't kill Achilles first. 11. Bad Ass Mutha FU**AS (Michael Spears & Lance Jordan) [TWL] Lance Jordan and Micheal Spears are two of the most intimidating men in the profession. When they first entered the TWL, they earned a great amount of respect. After a brief hiatus, they came back bigger and better than ever. Jordan and Spears are hard-working and had a classic encounter with Sex Sells at the TWL’s recent Pay-Per-View. The love the fans, and that feeling is certainly mutual. Lance and Micheal have had a great past, and an even more promising future. 10. Teen Angst (Vedder & Cobain) [IWE] In 1998, one word describes Teen Angst: success. Compiling a 22-2-4 record in 1998 (losses to Evolution- Chris Douglas/Ethan de Sade and Explosion), this tandem won World Tag Titles in IWC and IeWC... without even trying. These two men went into IeWC and immediately drew The Perfectly Perfect Duo, and then took the titles in a rematch while injuring their manager in July. They moved to IWC, where they took the federation by storm, defeating such teams as The Goths, The Explosion, Team Elite, and others. They won the tag belts from The Goths in August, and kept them until November in a great reign. There has been talk of retirement for Vedder and Cobain, but with these guys, who knows? 9. Objects of Your Desire (Grant D'Arby & Anthony Levert) [TWL] Anthony Levert and Grant D’Arby first started out a long time ago as a small team no one had heard of. But the two remarkable athletes didn’t take long to prove what they were made of. After winning a huge tag team Battle Royal to take the vacant Tag Team Titles, they went on to hold them for the longest time in TWL history - a period spanning over 3 months. These young men are certainly two of the best in the game, and work well together. Their cocky and audacious attitude only contributes further to their immense success. 8. Sex Sells (Jeff Jacob & Matthew Hutchinson) [TWL] Matthew "Dollar Man" Hutchinson and "Simply Irresistable" Jeff Jacob are two of the hottest stars in the sport of professional wrestling. Climbly quickly in the TWL tag ranks, these two have tag gold in their near future. Not only is their charisma unmatched and their talent near untouchable, these two are also great when it comes to actually wrestling. Few can, or have gotten up from "The Nightcap", and the way they’re going, not many will. This team will certainly be a force to be reckoned with in professional wrestling. 7. The Fraternity Boys (Gus Zampitella & Chris Obrien) [EMWC] - e-Wrestling's resident party animals threw a monster bash this year, bashing the heads of both The Eclipse and The Phalanx to capture the EMWC Tag Team Titles twice, for a total of nine months as champions. Combined with a win in SCRA's G-Star Tag Team Tournament, the Frat Boys cemented their place in not only the RSPWF Tag Team 200, but in e-Wrestling history, as well. 6. The Benjamins (Bobby Benjamin & Joe Benjamin) [EMWC] After a relatively uneventful stay in the IIWF before closing, Bobby and Joe proceded to flourish within the EMWC's tag division. After entering the fed in late summer, the Benjamins quickly moved into a top contender position, capturing the EMWC World Tag Team Titles from the Fabulous Ones at Devil's Night in October. Their reign lasted for two months...ending at No Imitations Accepted at the hands of the Rich and Infamous. Currently the titles are vacant, but no one doubts that the Benjis will rise again, and get right back in the title hunt. After all...It's All About The Benjamins...(BENJISMILE) 5. Black Watch (Duncan Macbeth & Andrew Macbeth) [SCRA] -Scotland' Finest are fond of yelling "TOSSERS!" at their opponents. And, this year, it was a wildly appropriate catchphrase. Black Watch spent most of the year tossing the SCRA tag ranks around like rag dolls, and doing much worse things to their heated rivals, The NorthPac Coalition. After a year of unbridled brutality, drunkenness, and Celtic Crossing in the SCRA and a solid showing in the IIWF, The Scots have earned their spot and more. Their recent capture of the FINAL-EVER SCRA Tag Team Titles is just icing on the cake.. er, haggis. 4. Explosion (Fire & Nitro) [UEW] The Explosion started off 1998 as the UEW TV Tag Champions. They would go on to capture those titles two more times during the 1998 calendar year. Lead by their manager Zach Rose, Fire and Nitro were able to defeat such teams as the Fairchild Foundation and the Lethal Leaders. But the most impressive accomplishment came near the the end of 1998 when they defeated the Outlaws for their first ever UEW Tag team title reign. In addition, the Explosion held the UEW TV Tag titles at the same time, a first in the history of the UEW, as well as the IWC Tag title crown. Look for a big year from this team as the challenge such UEW tag team powers as Power and Honor and the Perfectly Perfect Duo for more UEW Tag team gold. 3. The Fabulous Ones (Sho Satsuma & Agito Nakajima) [IIWF, EMWC, G-Pro] "Sweet" Sho Satsuma & "The Universal Heartthrob" Agito Nakajima, with manageress, the always gorgeous Ms. Miki. From Nagoya, Japan, weighing a combined weight of 525 http://www.erols.com/bfrankel/Fabulous.html The Fabulous Ones started their soon to be illustrious e-wrestling careers in the AEWA with Agito wrestling in singles competition. Soon, they emerged in the UWF where they captured their first title, the United States tag team championship, in a four corners match. Not long after that they won the UWF World tag titles by defeating Anarchy, and simultaneously held both titles for a week, until they lost their US titles to the Dogs of War. Later, they would lose the World titles to Sebastian Jericho and Xavier Lee Jackson. This set back was short lived as within one month they had won the AEWA World tag titles from the West Coast Connection. During this time they feuded with the Machines in the IIWF. This feud was looked at as one of the top tag feuds in wrestling, if not THE top tag team feud. They captured the BsWo world tag titles in a two night, five round tournament. About this same time, Mr. Tsuburaya underhandedly negotiated a deal with CIWF’s President Shamus that saw the Fabulous Ones carry the CIWF Canadian National tag titles, until they lost them to the Ronin. They eventually lost the AEWA tag titles to the Firebirds. However, they never lost the BsWo titles, they were stripped of the belts when the promotion changed over to another president. Then, came the Loser-Leave-The-IIWF match with the Machines, that the Fabs won, but in the process lost Agito to injury. This lead to Sho teaming up in the UWF with RSPWF #25, “Madman” Randall Osbourne as Sweet Madness, where they eventually captured the UWF World tag titles. After a two month reign they lost the titles. Agito would return in early September, after winning the “Mr. Universe” title, and compete once again alongside his Fabulous Ones’ partner. They fought in the Gunryo Pro promotion based in Japan as two thirds of a trios team with the Kabuki Kid and won the trios title there. They journeyed to the EMWC where they ended a four match jinx against the Down Boys and won the World tag titles. They eventually lost them to the Benjimens. The same week they rebounded, as they always do, and won the coveted UWF World tag team titles once again, resting them from the waist of the Darknight Express. They would lose them at the beginning of the 1999 year to long time foe Twister. Just recently they captured the G-Pro Combative tag team titles under questionable means. 2. The Perfectly Perfect Duo (Lane Studd & Maxx Studd) [UEW] Some may or may not know, but the year 1998 began and ended an illustrious year for the Perfectly Perfect Duo of Lance and Max Studd. Twin brothers, both fully blooded wrestlers with egos bigger than the state of Texas. Led down the aisle by Sensuous Samantha almost everywhere they went, they began the year in Awesome Simulation Wrestling (ASW) and also American Internet Championship Wrestling (AICW). Capturing the ASW tag team titles in January of 1998 and also the AICW Tag Team Titles on December 27th, 1997, they soared through the tag team scene. They had classic feuds with the Island Rats and the Best of the Best in the ASW and in AICW, had a few good feuds. Yet, while in the AICW, they held onto the tag team championships for six straight months, dropping them to a tag team by the name of Frenzy. As the AICW closed down after that, the Perfectly Perfect Duo exceeded to other leagues...one known as the Deadly Wrestling Organization (DWO). There they became the first ever tag team champions in a match against other greats such as the Outlaws and the Mahmood Brothers. They held the titles until that league closed before entering International Championship Wrestling (ICW) under the guidance of a new manager, Chris Wrigley. Chris Wrigley promised the PPD gold soon. From there they had yet another feud with the Best of the Best, landing a win at an ICW pay per view. From there, they were deemed the number one contenders to the ICW Tag Team Titles, only to grab them from the Island Rats in early December. During all this, the PPD had had an awesome stay in Ultimate E-Wrestling (UEW) with Sensuous Samantha. There, they joined forces with the Ultimate Champion, "Top Dog" Rick Styles and the New Alliance. They went from the bottom of the ranks to the main top while being the first team to defeat the Outlaws in UEW. Only a disqualification win, but looking good on their run there. The PPD soon were up for an Ultimate Tag Team Title shot, but for it to only be in a Heaven and Hell match. This match was against the champions, Explosion, Partners In Crime and Power & Honor. Despite the rocky match, the PPD were able to become champions with a little help of the "other Studd" who was introduced in the past as their triplet, Jack Studd. Today, the PPD are the reigning International Championship Wrestling tag team champions, as well as Ultimate Tag Team Champions in the UEW. They hope to continue to dominate throughout the rest of the year and always stay a step above the rest. If anyone could do it, it's the Perfectly Perfect Duo. And the number one tag team for the year of 1998... 1. The Outlaws (Brent Maverick & Doc Holliday) [UEW, SJPW] The men known by the wrestling names Brent Maverick and Doc Holliday have traveled a long, twisted, winding road to get where they are today. And the road gets more twisted every day. Born Brent Mueller and Matt Holliday, the two men met in the second grade in Tucson, Arizona. They became lifelong friends, who graduated high school, and college together. They attended Arizona State, Maverick on a football scholarship, and Holliday on a wrestling scholarship. Maverick was a decent linebacker, Holliday a decent wrestler, but after college the two men had no options to pursue athletics. They met a promoter by the name of Matthew Zellar in the summer of 1995, while working out at a gym, who gave them an option. Professional wrestling. (OOC: Everything from this point on happened in a paper-and-pencil wrestling roleplaying game... if anyone out there remembers the WWF RPG from Whit Enterprises...that game:) The men who would become The Outlaws burst onto the wrestling scene in September 1995, in a fed called the UWF. They were called the StormTroopers... a terrible soldier gimmick. They promptly lost every match they wrestled for three months. They were the tag team "jobbers" of the league at the time... everyone has to break in somewhere, right? But they were the most talented "jobbers" on the roster, so the same promoter who discovered them soon turned them over to a manager, named Reno Ruby. Ruby gave the team the chance to use the Outlaw names that Holliday had wanted. And from there, they took off... ...but not terribly far. :) The Outlaws were low-mid-carders. They could beat the "jobber" tag teams with ease, but the established tag teams would always win in the end. This went on for about a year, with Ruby giving them a short-term gimmick change into the "Warriors", a street-gang team. That didn't suit the Arizonans well at all, and it was dropped after a month. From February to September 1996, the Outlaws wrestled VERY infrequently, almost dropping out of the sport altogether. However, a manager by the name of Tex James would soon change all of that. Tex and his cohort Charles Magnus were building a powerhouse stable in the UWF, and Tex was doing it with a "youth movement". James was NOTORIOUS for having a "Midas Touch"; taking failed wrestlers and making them into successes. The Outlaws were another success for James. They started slow, but a big feud got them jump-started, and between November 1996 and May 1997, The Outlaws rang up an impressive succession of wins. The once doormats of the league were in title contention! It culminated on the UWF's last card, the Micheal Zellar Sr. Memorial Tag Team Tournament... a 64-team tournament. The Outlaws, placed in the North regional, advanced to the Final Four, before losing to the eventual winners. And then, the UWF was disbanded, and almost all of the wrestlers disappeared into obscurity... (OOC: The e-wrestling part begins here, when I got computer access and found out what e-wrestling was:) In February 1998, Tex James decided to get back into the wrestling business. He called on the two men who he saw the most potential in. Before the month was out, Brent Maverick and Doc Holliday were competing in the Internet Wrestling Coalition. Tex sent a sample of the Outlaws' work to a fed he had seen... Ultimate E-Wrestling. The head of the UEW, Jay Steele, liked what he saw, and gave the team the next available tag team roster spot. From there, it was like magic. The Outlaws went on an INCREDIBLE 1998 campaign... defeating long-time UEW icons Havok, defeating the red-hot Fairchild Foundation (a team that the Outlaws take credit for "cooling off"), and in June, they accomplished the ultimate task. They outlasted seven other teams, directly defeating four of them, in the 1998 UNHOLY WAR match for the UEW Ultimate Tag Team titles. Titles which they dominated almost all year. The first setbacks for The Outlaws occurred about this time. The Outlaws had won the Internet Wrestling Coalition tag titles, but lost them to the Partners In Crime... a team which to this day has always seemed to be the ones with The Outlaws' number. Brent and Doc continued through the year, leaving the IWC in a management dispute, and entering a different IWC... the Insane Wrestling Corporation. Their stay there was brief, highlighted by their first (and only) pinfall win over the Partners In Crime. The Outlaws left that IWC soon after as well. In UEW, another big task approached as The Perfectly Perfect Duo made a major run at The Outlaws' belts. The PPD used a mind game... they spread a lie that they had faced and beaten The Outlaws 3 times (actually, they were taking credit for PIC's wins)... and the flustered Outlaws were convincingly beaten by DQ in the first Outlaws-PPD matchup... the first Outlaws loss in UEW. The score was forcefully evened a month later as the Outlaws beat PPD by DQ, in a match where an actual 3-count against the PPD was called back due to a technicality. The Outlaws went international, joining Super Japan Pro Wrestling. Matches there have been infrequent (OOC: AHEM, Gerry!:) but exciting, and The Outlaws seem like the atmosphere there very much, even if the Japanese people don't return the feeling. Another big moment came as The Outlaws won a ten-team Unholy War in an interfederational event, the JRMC. This was the first time the Outlaws had been invited to such an event, and it was a huge statement for them. Later on at this event, the Outlaws accepted the challenge of the Fracas Boys, and wrestled them to a draw. The beginning of a dynasty? Unfortunately, we may never know. November 30, 1998... a night which will live forever in the memories of the Outlaws. The night where Doc Holliday, for reasons that have not been fully revealed (stay tuned to UEW TV:), walked out on his best friend of twenty years, Brent Maverick, during a title defense against The Explosion. Maverick wrestled The Explosion, essentially by himself, STILL almost won, but no one man can defeat a tag team by himself, and he was defeated. From there, Holliday has gone on a rampage, and Maverick has been near-death... but that's another story. Yes, The Outlaws are still a tag team in SJPW (OOC: if Bret Hart could be a face in Japan and a heel in the U.S., why not?:)... what are the reasons for this seeming contradiction? Perhaps we will find out in 1999... ...but in any event, the year 1998 saw a team come from nowhere, to arrive at the pinnacle of their profession. Who knows WHAT will happen in 1999? We'll have to wait and see. -Jay *IWE Owner *Handler of: King Snake [63-18-2] "Drifter" J.D. Carter [7-7]
