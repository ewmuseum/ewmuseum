[The scene opens up in Washington, DC You see many people walking along a path. Trees, flowers all planted, and the grass cut very nice and evenly, almost like a baseball field. In the background You can see The Lincoln Memorial, and in the distance the Washington Monument. You see an old women walking the path, with flowers in her hand, a piece of paper, and pen. She is wearing a black dress, the women looking to be in her 80's begins to tear as she approaches a great BIG Black wall, with gold and gray writing on it. She approaches the wall, as you begin to hear her thoughts and her feelings.]
Little old Lady: [[Sighing] " Thirty Years ago almost, brave man once said to me. "Don't worry I'll be back" As he walked out the door, with his luggage, and his hat, and never returned into my life again. He was leaving to go and serve his country in what people say, was the most brutal war, a war we didn't have to fight, a war were lives could have been saved, and the every man on this wall died in Vietnam, may god always be with them, and there families, and god bless for what they have done.
[The little old lady begins to walk again as she passes two stones of walls, she approaches a the wall, and steps closer, she kneels and does the sign of the Holy spirit, and the holy ghost, she places the flowers down, and stands back up, as her thoughts are herd again.]
Little Old Lady: It seems as if you broke your promise to me. You swore nothing would go wrong, and you would come back to me safe and sound. You had the choice of not to go, but you [begins to tear] went anyway. I didn't mean a damn thing to you, and so you left me home with two kids, to raise by myself.
Jimmy, i pray overnight, that while i sleep, you come into the room and speak to me, and wake me up and say 'Your home" But every morning when i wake up, your not there, and the only thing i have to look forward to is dreaming again that night, praying to god that you'll come back, that is the only reason why i keep on living, but now at 84 years old, my time is almost up Jimmy, and I will be in heaven with you shortly. But I say to myself every now and then, what if you hadn't gone, would thing be different? You know we lost the war Jimmy, we didn't win, and we wouldn't have won, even if only one life was lost. They say that we won the war, because we drove them off, but over 2000 lives were lost, did we really win? Look at all the names on this wall, so many veterans, so many young men who just didn't even begin to live. Men who were still in high school, it was horrible, and were even lucky we got anyone out of there alive.
It was a brutal war Jimmy, and we won, but we lost at the same time. Lives were lost, familles were lost, it was a mess. It didn't have to be this way, if you just didn't try to save old' Bobby's life. Yeah he made it through the war, he survived, while only losing one leg, after you were shot, a grenade was tossed at you both, that when it hit your stomach, [begins to cry] You died instantly, the grenade ricoshadeded, the explosion that it, and Bobby only lost a leg, Thank God, Betty was crushed, she was forced to take care of the kids, with cripple for a father, there wasn't much she could do, and money was tight, the war department gave some help with the money he received, he received disability, social security but with four kids, it just wasn't enough, they could even make ends meet, so they had to move out of there high ranch house and into an apartment. Can you imagine? Six people in an apartment? I felt so bad, I received money for you, and it helped with the kids, Brian is Married now, and has a little baby girl, his wife name is Susan. She's a lovely girl.
Well, I'll cut to the chase and tell you why 'm here and explaining all this, but finally after all these years, there having an award, dinner ceremony to honor all the men and women who served in the Vietnam war, and I received a letter and your receiving three of them. How I wish you could be there, but all
I can do is pray.
[The little old Lady, stays there for a few minutes praying, as she leaves quietly, but not before taking a piece of paper and putting it on the wall, scratching it with a special marker. You see the name Jimmy Walbert on the piece of paper, the little old lady begins to walk down the path following the
Memorial, that leads all the way to the streets.]
[You then see a man in a wheel chair riding along the same path, that lead to the Veteran Memorial. He has only one leg, wearing a soldiers uniform, looking very sad. While riding in his wheel chair you can notice that he has a paper and marker in his hand, while pushing himself toward the long stoned wall. He passes about two stones, when he comes upon the same wall that the little old lady stood at. Although he just sits there, you can hear his thoughts]
Man in wheel Chair: Jimmy, if your up there, i'd like for you to listen for a few minutes. Yeah it's me Bobby, remember? The last time i saw you, i was holding you on my good leg, asking you to please not die. You saved my life
Jimmy, and i'll always remember that. Thank you Jimmy, if it wasn't for you, i'd a been dead now. You jumped in front of that bullet, then were blown to pieces, all i lost was my leg, or so people think. I lost a best friend. When we both left for the war, we had bee high school enemies, every day of my life, i'd always take your money, pik you up by your underpants, and hurt you badly, but as the years went on, we both got married, and lived down the block from each other, but never realized who each other was. On the plane to go to base, i knew who you were, and i wanted to say hi, but never got the chance. Then when we were paired up, we both knew who we were, althugh we didn't get to talk much at first, because of the hatred between us, we missed out on what could of been a great friendship. None of my friends in high school, would of dared took a bullet for me, but my enemy did? Why did you have to go and do that
Jimmy? You made me feel so guilty for 10 years, day, and night i would think of you. Whats even sadder, is we would ofbeen discharged in two days. Two days, and the war was over for us, we lasted there for 3 years together, and those were the best three years of my life. The minutes, the hours with you, at base, leanin up against each other to keep warm, me and you had going, and after i treated you like shit, all those years ago, you decide to jump in front of a bullet for me, take the hit and get blown to pieces. [Begins to shout and cry]
WHY JIMMY, YOU BASTARD!!! I'll never forgive you, it should of been me, it was my time to go not yours, you didn't have to do what you did, and now, im paying for it, your family is paying for it, but eve though it doesn't show and i can't find the right words to say it, i nver did say it, not even prayers
Thank you Jimmy, you saved my life.
[The man in the wheel chair obviously being, the man that Jimmy saved...Bobby
Keller, a vietnam hero, a man who served in the great loss of a war, coming back to Thank a man, who saved his life. He reaches into the back of his wheel chair and pulls out flowers as he places it down by the wall, he then begins to wheel himself downthe path, putting his hat on, and paying his respects to the rest of the soldiers, he spent time with, served in the war with, before leaving he takes his paper and places it up against the wall by Jimmy's name and take the name with him by scratching the marker on it, and the name revealing onto the paper. He then begins to push himself toward the street]
[The scene zooms back toward the wall again, but this time higher up, then the camera was before. You see an old tree standing about 12 yards awat from the
Memorial, the scene then catches a man standing by the tree, with a very large trench coat. He has a hat on, as he begins to walk toward the wall, as his soul, and his mind isn't herd, but his voice is]
"Mr. Wonderful" Chuck Williams: For the past fourtyfive minutes, i'v stood behind that treet and herd the sorrow, the thoughts of that little old lady, and that poor man in the wheel chair. My heart was with them, and although i know there story, there pain i can't feel. I didn't come here to feel sorry for everyone, and i didn't come here to hear a sob story, but i came here, because this is what we live by today. This is our life, this is our world. We live and die by the code book, and by something called war. We don't like someone, we fight them, we don't like a country, we get our finest men and send them into battle. Not caring if they die or not, not caring if they have a family at home, what there race is, religion. They must fight. Bobby Keller and Jimmy
Walbert, two high school enemies, made there peace during the vietnam war. They decided that it was time to put the past behind them, and to live by thepresent, or at least thats how Jimmy chose to live his life. I'm not trying to sit here and preech on how war is bad, on how lives shouldn't of been loss.
We all know the war shouldn't of happened, we all know that none of it should of ever went down, and over two thousand people died, to serve us, people dind't recieve there money for there loved ones, people were screwed in the end, but given a medal, a flag, and told "i'm sorry" but in this case did sorry cut it? Many would say hell no, but here is my side of the story. Why i'm here is to pay my respects, and to show someone, a man, someone who i used to call "My Friend" How the past, the present, and the Future shouldn't relflect on anything. You see shotgun Messiah, like Jimmy Walbert i would choose to put the past behind us, i would jump in front of a bullet for you, no matter what you say, no matter what you did, because Friendship, is rock solid, you can't break, the only thing can break friendship, are the two who started it, and thats only ifboth choose to. Long as one man chooses to go on as friends, they'll always be friends. It seems as if in our case, we decided to stay friends, but we sure don't actlike friends. You act as if this title around my waist means more than anything in the world to you. You want this title? Is this exactly what you ant Shotgun, your training for this? I don't know, far as i go, i' don't want the title anymore, this isn't what i am fighting for anymore Shotgun, don't believe me?

I'm fighting for Respect, for friendship, i want every man in this world to know, i didn't end the friendship, but it was you that ended it.
