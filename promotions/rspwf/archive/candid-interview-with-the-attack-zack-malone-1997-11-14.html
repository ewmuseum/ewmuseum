Candid Interview with "The Attack" Zack Malone

14 November 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Basically, everything you ever wanted to know about Zack Malone, but weren't interested enough to ask... -CQ- ---------------- [Shawn Peterson is sitting in a green rocking chair, looking comfortable in a red sweater and blue jeans. He has a few notes in his hand and a microphone hooked onto his collar. Sitting across from him on a wooden stool is Zack Malone, who even without the glitz and glamour of his wrestling paraphernalia is still a strikingly handsome man. He's wearing a faded blue pair of jeans, a black Body Shop t-shirt, and a blue denim jacket, with two glasses of water laying on a glass table between the two of them.] SHAWN: Folks, here tonight for the first time that I can recall, we are going to see the man behind the name of "The Attack" Zack Malone. Outside the wrestling rings, outside the arenas, leaving the jokes and the arrogance behind... ZACK: [cuts him off with a smile] Leaving the jokes and arrogance behind? Sorry Peterson, that's no act. I am who I am. SHAWN: That's fine, Zack. I'd still like the peel back the layers, if you will, and get to see the real Zack Malone. Any problems with that? ZACK: Hey, I signed the contract, I'm all yours. SHAWN: Good! First of all, I'd just like to ask you... [Shawn checks his notes]... what's your real name? ZACK: Zachary Thomas Malone. You really peeled back a few layers that time, Peterson. [laughs] SHAWN: Well actually, I recall you getting quite upset at a few men who spelled your name Z-A-C-H. Isn't that your real name? ZACK: Yeah, technically it is, but I like this spelling better. No biggie. SHAWN: Can you bring us back to when you first wanted to get into wrestling? What was, as you like to call yourself, the big attraction? ZACK: Wrestling to me was a good way to get attention in high school. Anyone could be a star on the basketball court or the ice hockey rink, and I was both. But it was a team thing. Everyone got the credit. I wanted nothing more than to stand out, and be great on my own, that's why I was heavily involved in track and field, and also of course, wrestling. SHAWN: Oh yes, track and field. You set quite a number of scholastic records , especially on the 100 meters, did you not? ZACK: Yeah, the 100 meters was my best event. Donovan Bailey and Michael Johnson can fight over who is the World's Fastest Man all they want, but if you want the truth, I am. [smiles] SHAWN: So wrestling was your second love, to track? ZACK: Basically, yeah. The problem with wrestling was, and I'm serious about this, my girlfriends back then used to constantly worry about me hurting my face, and nag me to quit. My track coach wasn't too pleased with seeing me in leglocks all the time either, I imagine, but I didn't care. I was a better track star, but wrestling was definitely in my blood. SHAWN: So, as I understand, you were a good wrestler, not a great one, until you surprised everyone in a city tournament, and moved on to a high profile amateur wrestling meet, which also featured future pro wrestlers like Chris Quigley, Kurt Manning, and Mark Bagwell. Was that when you decided this is what you wanted to do? ZACK: That tournament was a major point in my life. During the whole thing I was riding on a major high of incredible success. I kinda surprised myself by getting into the finals, but when I lost that match and had to take home a silver, I was pretty devastated. What's worse was that I lost to Quigley, I guy who I'd overheard telling a few friends in attendance that day that he'd have no problem winning this thing. I really wanted to beat him when I heard that, but he was almost as good as he thought he was, and he was tough. I think my main goal was to outdo Quigley eventually, to meet him again. We've never gotten the chance yet, although some promoters early on thought it'd be cute to make us allies. SHAWN: It's ironic that you and Chris, as well as quite a number of other future wrestlers, ended up accepting an invitation from Kurt Manning to attend his father's exclusive wrestling school. ZACK: That basement _was_ a Living Hell. I really believe Steve Manning has no conscience. The torture that man would put us through in that basement, you would not believe. I almost packed it in and turned back, but if the others could do it, I'd be damned if I couldn't. The good thing about this school, was that promoters usually looked to Steve first, to provide them with young talent. I was almost immediately signed by a small promotion called the GECW. I came in as a smiling young kid, slapping hands, the whole works. I remember my first interview, I said some things that were pretty arrogant, and the promoter, John LeGrande, slapped my wrist after, saying I was a good guy, and this was not the way to go about getting the fans cheers. I hated it in the GECW, and I don't miss it at all. SHAWN: Then you got your first _big_ break. The WIWF's head promoter John Checkovich came knocking at your door, but there was a catch... ZACK: Yeah, there was a big catch. He had an idea where I'd be in a tag team with another guy, Darren Randall, who went by the gimmicked name Dr. Dangerous. I didn't mind teaming with Randall much though, because the man was an experienced veteran. He knew every trick in the book, and gave a great interview. I credit a lot of my interview skills today to him. It was nice to finally see him again in the NFW and TAEWF, although he was managing, but Craig and Dave looked to be doing great under his wing, much like I did. But yeah, another thing was I was allied with Chris Quigley. So here I was, an athlete who despised the whole team aspect, knowing I couldn't say no because the WIWF was just a huge federation and a great place to be. But the day Check approached me and told me Randall was on his way out, and our tag team would be splitting up, that was one of the greatest moments of my life. Finally I'd get a chance to shine in the singles ranks. My first major singles match on a PPV, I lost to Randall actually, but I beat him in a Loser Leaves Town cage match a few weeks later, and my confidence soared. SHAWN: Shortly after this, you experienced quite a chance in attitude, did you not? ZACK: Not really. I was always the arrogant type, without even trying to be. I think it may go back to when I heard Quigley talking about how he had the tournament wrapped up. I hated it then, but I really wanted to be able to have that much confidence. It eventually happened, and I mustered up enough confidence to start a word war with the biggest name in the business, Trey Porter. This went on for quite a while, until Trey approached me in the locker room, and suggested we talk to Check about letting us team up. I was amazed at how Porter seemed so sure he could sway a promoter in his favour like that, but he did, and during a tag match where I was teamed with Paris, taking on Trey and Kurt Manning, who had shown up a few weeks before, I made my big turn, and I had never felt so great in my life. The crowd was booing me, and hey, don't get me wrong, I don't bask in the boos like some guys, I mean, I'm not a bad guy, I don't go around tying tin cans to puppy dogs tails, but I've always been a firm believer that any publicity is good publicity. I was finally over. SHAWN: You created your name in the WIWF, but it was in the FoPEW that your star began to shine bright. ZACK: No doubt about it. Cameron DeJong, the former head of the FoPEW, before Strake took over, sent me a letter when the WIWF went on what was then thought to be a hiatus, but eventually was the official end. So I signed with FoPEW, thinking I had taken a small step backward from the big lights of the WIWF. I'd never been so wrong in my life. The FoPEW was like a breath of fresh air. It had better quality production, better locker rooms, better security, better referees, better everything. And the management were, for the most part, extremely reasonable. At first I was hovering around mid-card status, but enjoying myself, as I found myself allied with Trey Porter once again. We missed having Kurt with us, as contract problems prevented him from joining FoPEW, but we brought in yet another legend, "Bad Boy" Mark Bagwell, and the three of us have been the core of The Body Shop ever since. SHAWN: You were a mid-carder, but making money, and you were very happy? ZACK: I was happy, yeah. I was winning matches and losing matches, but I felt I was always entertaining, and I always got a good crowd reaction, as having Trey and Mark with me really helped out, not only with getting more crowd noise, but my interview skills went through the roof, and my wrestling abilities soared as well. Right now, I don't believe there is another athlete in the world who can do what I do, who can excite a crowd more, or who can get up in front of thousands of people without feeling the slightest pang of nervousness. I just love the attention. It eventually came to the point where my status had to be re-evaluated. I was too good to be held back any longer, and I fought and beat Chris Andrews for my first title in pro wrestling. Andrews was a great wrestler and a great guy on the mic, I'm sorry he's retired, although I still believe my Superkick on the back of his head had a lot to do with his retiring. SHAWN: Bringing up your Superkick, when you first started, people criticized you for having a common finisher. No imagination. But while they complained, you just kept perfecting it and perfecting it, until your Superkick became absolutely lethal. You actually broke Brian Knight's jaw _and_ his hand with one kick, in your first world title shot. ZACK: [laughs] I totally outclassed Knight in that match, but I admit now I clearly wasn't ready to wear a World Title belt. I made a huge mistake and it cost me, no matter how many excuses I made afterwards, but everyone knew then that I was better than Brian Knight, and they certainly know now. SHAWN: You held that Canadian title for a long time, until Simon Lebec used some treachery to get it from you. You got a rematch in what was to be the last ever FoPEW card, but you only won by count out. Does this bother you? ZACK: Honestly? Yes. I'd say 9 out of 10 people would agree I was the greatest FoPEW Canadian Champion of all time, and it's just too bad I wasn't the title holder at the end of the FoPEW's first run. But I still outdid Lebec until all he could do was walk away. I'm not ashamed of my performance in that match, not by a long shot. I was the better man all along, and he knows that as well as I do. It's probably the reason he went nuts. [laughs] SHAWN: You signed on with NFW after spending some time off from the sport since December. Despite some very exciting offers from "Loop" federations, you chose the NFW. Why? ZACK: Mainly because I wanted the chance to wrestle the guys I was familiar with, but never got a chance to face, like Quigley, or Taylor, or American Enforcer. That World Title tournament win was extremely special, because not only did I beat three great wrestlers, but I beat two guys who will most likely end up in the Hall of Fame in Taylor and Knight. SHAWN: Do you think you'll end up in the Hall of Fame? ZACK: I like to think so. Before the World title win, I wouldn't have said that, but after this, and along with the fact that I'm currently toying with the idea of signing with PCW for some appearances, I'll become much more well known. I rated #20 in the RSPWF 200 last year with only one relatively minor title to my credit. I considered myself a candidate for #1 this year, especially after the NFW World Title victory, but with only 2 matches in the last 6 months to my name, I'll be happy to even qualify. SHAWN: Have you considered permanent retirement? ZACK: [brushes hair out of eyes] I've always said that when it gets to the point where I feel I'm slowing down even the slightest bit, that's when I'll call it quits. I'm 28 years old, and I left off in the prime of my career, and I hope I still am. I don't think I'll hang around in the ring trying to reclaim past glory. I have no financial need to keep wrestling, after Georgia Lucas left me her life savings. SHAWN: That still rates as one of the most incredible stories to date. This woman who was stricken with a terminal disease was an incredible fan of yours, and left you literally millions of dollars. Didn't that humble you a bit? ZACK: Not really. It made me feel like I was doing a helluva job entertaining, for someone to do that for me, even though I blasted the fans whenever I could, they still know who the real entertainers are, and who the slackers are. SHAWN: Can you tell me what has been your most memorable match to date? ZACK: It's a tie between my World Title win over Chris Taylor, and my match with Trey Porter in the FoPEW. It's too bad that had a dusty finish, because I really believe we were putting on the match of our lives, and I still think I had Porter beaten, although with Trey, you can never, ever count him out. SHAWN: Thanks Zack, for this exclusive interview. I certainly hope we see you wrestling again soon. What can we look forward to if Zack Malone does indeed return to the squared circle? ZACK: High ratings, high buyrates, and one damn exciting match after another. [Zack flashes his million dollar smile into the camera, as it zooms out on the two shaking hands, then fades to black...]
