The Ronnie D./Dan Kauffman RP Thread

10 January 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hey guys... Ron, this is finally the thread I said I'd start where we go back and forth in character. Like my true self, I will start this off. Respond in character, and I'll get my rebuttals flowing as fast as I can.
[name withheld], here is your RPing that you wanted. :) Oh, and [name withheld], take notice of this. Your VP is going to war with your newest recruit. Great for the ratings, ya know... :)
Here goes nothing... ----- DK: Ronnie, if you are the icon of professional wrestling, then Cher is the greatest musician in history. And we all know that the latter isn't true, so I guess I'm gonna have to be the one that puts you in your place.
(Fade in on "Hell's Abyss", the basement training center. The dim light in the concrete-block walled room shows several things... the free weights lying with the bench in the near left corner, the punching/heavy bag to the far left, the wrestling mats off to the right... but most noticably, the wrestling ring located in the center. A trophy case, with clear tinted glass protecting the various prizes inside, makes up the back wall... and over it hangs the now-aged gold plated sign... "Hell's Abyss", and under, in small print... "Dan Kauffman, Owner".)
DK: Ronnie, if you think you are _the_ icon of professional wrestling, then you don't even understand the sport you are participating in. Because no one person stands as the icon of this sport. Not Hulk Hogan. Not Ric Flair.
Not Bret Hart. Not Roddy Piper. Not me. Not you. Not any one person. The _icon_ of wrestling, as you like to put it, Ron... lies in all that _is_ wrestling. The entire picture is in fact the icon of wrestling. The rings we wrestle in. The past of our profession. The men that laid the groundwork. The men that will come down the road. The announcers who broadcast our matches to fans near and far. The fans themselves. The matches in and of themselves. _ALL_ of this... is the icon of wrestling.
Because only as the whole can wrestling survive. Ronnie, if you were to die tommorow, it would be a tragic loss. yes, a tragic one. A lot of people would be delighted as hell to see you in your grave, but personally, I don't like seeing anyone die. But get this through your head, Ron... if you were to somehow exit the wrestling world, it wouldn't be that big a loss. Hell, it wouldn't be a loss at all.
_Wrestling_ would go on. Maybe the fans and the wrestlers would remember you, but otherwise, life goes on, Ron. It would be the same if I were to leave. Or if any one person decided to leave. It wouldn't matter that much.
Only when the whole of wrestling... the fans, the announcers, the rings, the venues, the matches, the wrestlers... Only when all of it left... would wrestling die out.
You see, Ron, YOU aren't the ICON of wrestling. The icon of wrestling IS _wrestling_. But Ron, you go about town acting as if you are the hottest thing since honey butter. You like to try to get people to believe that you are the actual reason people watch wrestling. And you know what we all think? We think your ass needs a reality check.
That's where I come in. You see, Ron... I haven't been the most successful wrestler in the world. I haven't won the most titles or garnered the top prizes or gained the most fame. But I never wanted to. All I wanted to ever accomplish was to go out, work my ass off, try my hardest, and earn the respect of the fans and fellow athletes. And I've done that, Ron.
And in doing so, I've earned myself more than I could ever have expected. I've got few friends in this business, but I've got a lot of co-workers who, whether they like me or not, still respect who I am and what I've done.
Well Ron, most of us are getting sick of your repetitive bullshit of how you think you are the icon of wrestling. First off, I've fought more big-time athletes in this business than you could even mention. Forget about whether or not I won or lost, I've been in the ring with some of the best, and I've stood tall. The only thing, Ron, that you've stood tall in is talking too much.
Ron, you like to think that you are the greatest wrestler in this sport today? That's fine. Maybe I can't talk sense into you.
So I'll wrestle it into you, "Icon". (Kauffman turns to go away, but instead, turns back around one more time to deliver...) I just hope you know what you've got coming. (Then he walks off...) -----
