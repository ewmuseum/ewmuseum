[Tricks of the Trade] The year ends, what does 1998 hold?

19 November 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
------------------------ Tricks of the Trade Volume 1 Issue 2 "The future and past of E-Wrestling" ------------------------ As 1997 comes to an end I felt it would be best to talk about the ever going changes in E-Mail Wrestling. I have been doing this for about five years now. I realize that some of you might have been doing this a bit longer than me so it may not make sense depending on how long you have been doing this. ------------------------ The changes ------------------------ Unfortunately things in our sport take a long time to change. I feel that this in part is brought about by us being scattered about rather than being all together in a network of different federations. This slows everything down considerably, but we have seen some small changes to prove that there is hope for a better RSPWF community. So what am I talking about? Well, I'm talking about the almost universal change to a closed flashing system. In the old days everything was done on an open list where members would simply send their flash on their own to the entire fed. Where as now most members are forced to send the flashes to the president of the fed. This isn't much of a major improvement, but it allows the president to keep his list closed which prevents people from stealing members or it prevents un- ruly members from making a scene over a loss or a stupid complaint. Well since this does remain a minor improvement let's have a look at where E-Wrestling came from. ------------------------ The very beginning ------------------------ Well, this is going to be a hard topic to explain because it depends on what server you are on, but for me E-Wrestling starts through two things. Part of it comes from a wrestling fans what to compete in a ring and have the glory of being a champion. The second part would come in revolt against on-line role-playing game services that charge you to compete. An example would be AOL's Grandstand (Keyword: Grandstand or http://www.grandstand.com/ ), they have not only wrestling, but all kinds of sports and they use a RealTime chat version rather than E-Mail. This explains why five years ago a lot of AOL feds where hosting cards in private chat rooms, but as the word past on more interest came a bout from other ISP members so the E-Mail system came about. This is probably as far as I could trace on-line sports. Services like the Grandstand are responsible for it. Like it or not it's going to be impossible to point someone as the creator of E-Wrestling. ------------------------ The Simulator, is it dead? ------------------------ Simulators like WLS and TNM certainly boosted a revolution in E-Wrestling. They provided presidents with an alternative to picking winners or even writing matches, but are they still flowing strong? There's no doubt in my mind that many still use simulators, but the Sim V.S. Booking wars have died down and I think we are heading into an age where the creators of TNM need to change their direction to survive. Most fed heads are looking into writing themselves so the only major advantage the simulator gives is slowly dying. If I were [name withheld], I would take TNM and make it Windows based. You can easily do this with Visual Basic 5.0. Most codes could be transferred or edited with out having to be rewritten entirely. Is this enough? Well a Windows based simulator sounds nice, but it definitely won't be enough. Here are some features that would make the next version of TNM a hit throughout RSPWF. - Feature that automatically adds applications received from website forms or E-Mail into pre-stored text archives. So it should update your roster for you. - It should also schedule tours for you. This feature would be like a monthly calendar. It would start a chain of arenas and create a set of tours throughout the months. - It should handle your rankings after every card. You should simply be able to enter your results and then get a fully updated version of your rankings. - With the ranking feature it could also help decide who should face who in order to help keep the ranking list well scattered and prevent it from having 10 wrestlers rated at number five or three. Unfortunately there are some things that a simulator will never be able to do. These things remain for a fed to handle and it always will. - Writing cards. Unless you are into those boring TNM cards don't bother praying for a future version to write good cards. I though this might be possible, but now that I have learned some programming codes I realize just how complex a process and how huge a program it would be. - Angles, it's up to you. Your simulator will never be able to think up of two wrestlers that would be capable of having an excellent feud. It can simply point it out by choosing statistics from it's roster files. So you would mostly see Face/Heel ideas coming out of the simulator. This makes the simulator into more of a federation organizer. It can handle your rankings and files, but it will never make your writing any more simpler than giving you ideas for moves. So in other words the simulator is heading into a grave. ------------------------ Writing style! Play by Play V.S. IIWF Style ------------------------ The first step here is naming the IIWF Style of writing. I prefer calling it the "Descriptive" style. While Play by Play may seem better and more detailed writing cards in IIWF format is simply better. They are more organized and definitely contain more detail than in the other system of writing. In the next few years I hope to see a change to a stable writing style. I mean that every fed writes in the same format. This could help bring us into a network which we really need. The style I chose is IIWF or Descriptive format. I have my reasons and you might disagree with me, but of course it's only my opinion. ------------------------ Multimedia in our sport ------------------------ Certainly all the advances in technology as played a part in E-Wrestling, but when will it done something huge? The answer might be soon. With ISPs battling for survival over AOL (They now hold 11 Million members.), they might be forced into taking steps in the direction of Steve Case. As most of you don't know the next version of AOL will allow displaying of graphics in E-Mail. This will allow feds to post their logos as headers to cards. It can even help you give out the rankings or announce tournament brackets, but is this enough? Hell no! Most of the big changes have come in the website technology. People are able to create better and bigger sites with new ActiveX controls. Things like Java have allowed feds to set up RealTime chat rooms. Of course this detracts from the actual cards. What we need is some breakthrough stuff. That where my idea comes in, as processors gain more speed, and as modems get even faster that the cable modem speed kings. We will be able to sent out huge files in no time. Unfortunately not every has brand new computers. This means that a major evolution could be as far away as five years, but wait I haven't mentioned my big idea yet. Well it boils down to this. You put all these top of the line things together and you have the capability to download WAV files in seconds. This adds the extra of being able to hear the themes of the wrestlers as you read through their entrance in the cards, but not even this is enough. That's where 3Dfx comes in. 3Dfx is new graphic technology that allows 3D graphics to come in even sharper. My idea is that eventually you will be able to read the card and then download a full 3D version of the card. I'm not talking about what APW Mat Masters is doing, because with all due respect their clips remain a bit to cartoonish for me. I'm referring to graphics in 3D games like Teeken 3 or Mortal Kombat 4, but this is and will remain a tedious task until 3D graphic applications come around and make it easier. That's my big idea, it seems far fetched, but nothing knows what technology can bring us. ------------------------ What is for sure? ------------------------ It's safe to say that for now we must all keep heading in the right direction and that would be incorporating the technologies that we know or that are arriving into what we do best. With time everything will become easier and E-Wrestling will be redefined and take life as a true sport, but no of this is possible with out you. So as 1997 slowly approaches an end think about how you started and where you are heading for a minute and maybe just maybe you'll be the next person like [name withheld] to do something huge for this little sport.
