Managers by [name withheld] Now I will admit, I have never run a manager character. That's kind of hard to believe, I guess, but the key aspect of a manager is *personality* and I could never think up with a character who would draw sufficient heat to justify the manager's role. In real pro-wrestling managers are often given to wrestlers who don't interview well, or bland characters who need to be identified as a heel. Since it's the same person writing for both manager and wrestler, the first doesn't apply to e-wrestling. And if you can create an interesting manager and put him over, you can also create an interesting wrestler and put him over, so the second doesn't really apply either. Although, if you're debuting a new character, attaching an existing manager to him would make sense. What can managers do? Clearly they can interfere in matches, and that is the purpose for which newbies tend to use them. Their reasoning is that by interfering a manager can help your character win, and since you want to win, you should have a manager (and be 700 pounds, and be able to do triple moonsaults, etc.) Managers come in handy in writing interviews. You can do a dialogue between the manager and his protege, and stuff like that, rather than just the protege standing there and talking to the camera. Managers who have a firmly established alignment can help characters do turns. Joining with a heel or face manager makes you a heel or face, rejecting a heel or face manager makes you a face or heel. However, the manager has to be sufficiently over that it's clear that it's the wrestler who's turning, not the manager. What I mean by that is that if Evil Elroy fires his manager Donnie Dull, it might come across as just another example of Evil Elroy treating people like dirt. If Donnie Dull is instead Donnie Diabolical and is an established heel with a distinct personality of his own (as opposed to being Evil Elroy's yes-man or something) then the turn will work better. If you come up with a really good manager character, you can use him as your signature character. For instance [name withheld] has a huge number of characters, but they're (mostly) unified under the Falkner Wrestling Enterprises banner, which features manager Mike Falkner. You should probably not try to create a trademark character when you first enter e-wrestling. Rather, try out a few characters and figure out which ones you're good at playing and like to play. Then give them a larger role as you move into more leagues. When you design a manager to go with a wrestler, one option is to give the wrestler and manager the same gimmick, for instance the wrestler is a soldier and the manager is a general, or the wrestler is a space alien and the manager is a higher-ranking space alien. This is a logical approach, but it has a few drawbacks. First and foremost, a manager should add something to the character, rather than simply repeating what the wrestler already has to say. Also, how well does the "same-gimmick" approach work for multiple characters? That is, how many people can take a whole stable of soldiers or of space aliens and make them all unique and interesting? A manager with a deeper personality is better for managing multiple characters. Basically what I am trying to say is that the "same-gimmick" approach is fine on a small scale and if you put enough depth into the manager (which you should do anyway). I've repeated several times that a manager should have a strong personality, but I haven't really said how to accomplish it. That's probably because I've never run a manager myself, as I said before. Yes, managers can interfere in matches and toss insults around, but they should be able to do more than that (after all, your wrestler can probably toss insults just fine on his own). When you think about creating a manager, think about what he can do for the wrestler. If he's supposed to be a devious guy, he can probably come up with more exciting devious things than just reaching into the ring and tripping people. If he's a coach/mentor type of guy, he can do community work and coach/mentor people outside of e-wrestling. Maybe those of you who have had some success with managers can toss in your two cents here... how did you do it?
