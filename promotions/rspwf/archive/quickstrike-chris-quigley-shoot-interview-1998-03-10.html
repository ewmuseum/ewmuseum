"Quickstrike" Chris Quigley "Shoot" Interview...

10 March 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Like Quigs, a "shoot" interview. Entirely in character, naturally. Marty ========================== [ SCENE : A deserted locker room. In the room sit resident IIWF commentator Tim Dross, and Marty Warnett. Warnett sits, rubbing his wet hair with a towel. ] TD : Folks, I'm here today to give a free, frank and honest interview with IIWF wrestler Marty Warnett. I must hasten to add, all Marty's opinions are his own, and not representative of the IIWF.
MW : Heck, Tim, that's only because limpet-like Osterhout has NO opinions. Flows with the tide, that one. TD : Ahem. Marty, recently the good people watching channel RSPWF have been treated to several interviews. What do you feel about that?
MW : It's a very good way of showing the fans out there that we are real people, not just faceless charismatic performers. Of course, certain people lack the charisma, but what the heck?
TD : Tell us about your start in the sport. MW : Well, I wanted to be a world class goalkeeper, but I suffered a knee injury whilst attempting to beat the World keepy-uppy record. I went travelled the world, and ended up in Canada, where I was trained by a legend in the sport and ingratiated myself into his family.
TD : Really? MW : No. That sounds corny, and a complete rip-off. TD : So, how did you start wrestling? MW : I'd always been a fan of the sport - back in Britain, I remember seeing Davey Boy Smith in his really early days. He was so light, and a real high-flyer, he was at the stage of always taking a pounding, then making a comeback to get the crowd into it, but taking the loss. Bless him.
As time went on, and wrestling went down the pan here in Britain,
I'd
spent a bit of time in the US anyway, and when I saw the advert for the
IIWF training camp, I spent a lot of time, three, maybe four, months there, preparing for an in-ring debut. In that time, we worked on a gimmick for me.
TD : How important are gimmicks today in wrestling? MW : I'd say ... very important. We're out there, looking to put bums on seats, and if people think we look ridiculous, or act completely inapropriately for our characters, we'd get laughed out of the building. One example was the Highwayman; a good idea, but if you think of the legends of
Highwaymen, it's all very swashbuckling, dynamic, frilly shirts, flintlocks, "Money or your life". He wasn't, for whatever reason, getting that side of things done, and got a lot of stick for it.
TD : And you were very vocal about it, too. MW : [ laughing ] Yeah, I did rip into him a bit! I like to wrestle, and I like to be pretty free on the mic, too. Certainly, VP Osterhout has privately told me he likes it when I run my mouth.
TD : Looking back, what can you remember about your first bout? MW : Very little. Hey, I did get nailed, y'know. A certain jobber by the name of Verheoven, I seem to recall. Don't know what he's done in the sport since then. [ laughs again ]
TD : When I look at you then, and look at you now, something's different. What do you think that is?
MW : Too many heavy nights on the road, two back injuries, numerous slanderous remarks from Soundbite, many diseases from LaRue, and several good feuds!
TD : What are your feelings about some of the people you've faced? MW : Ok, let's see. Lord Byron. That guy was really on the ball, cut great interviews, plays the character to a tee, superb technical ability, and able to really raise his opponents game. I'd won the
Intercontinental title before that, and he was the first person I had a protracted series of defenses against. I was sorry I lost the belt, but hey, he never cleanly pinned me, and we took each other to the edge, which was a great boost to my confidence.
Simon LeBec was my first actual series, and we showed a lot of passion and craziness in our bouts. Very egotistical, but then most of the people I face are.
Stetson, it all kinda got intense then faded, the Universal
Powers tried
to legitimately cripple me, which got blocked by the suits, and then they disappeared. Bradley Reed, again, one PPV cycle then he was gone.
TD : Again, it seems that your momentum has dipped after a bright start. MW : Yeah, I kinda wonderd if it was time to move on. I've had plenty of offers from feds elsewhere, but a lot of the problems of projected opponents leaving hasn't been my fault, or the suits, so ...
TD : Marty, one name wasn't mentioned by you before. Chris Quigley. MW : That was for a reason. When Chris speaks of legit heat ... that's true. The Internet rumours were true, he really did attack me backstage over some of the comments I made over him and Troy. He showed his wrestling skill by trying to pull my hair out!
Chris was a comsumate ego-maniac, to the point of really believing he was the greatest and that the IIWF revolved around him. I always saw him as a name wrestler, somebody I could learn from in the ring, and basically improve my stock, yet he never wanted to face me.
Indeed, a previous ex VP mentioned Chris didn't really want the bout as there was nothing in it for him. Yet, a week after the PPV bout, he got the
Intercontinental belt. I guess whinging beats talent any day. So, I pinned Chris, the first "clean" decision against him in the IIWF, yet, no title shots after he got the belt. Petrow would recognise Chris for what he is, with Petrow's duck involvement. Chris is a good wrestler, but not great. The PPV bout showed that - I'd done my best to get the crowd interested in the weeks before, with little work from Chris at all. He then whinges that he "put me over".
Fact is, I already was over with the crowd a long time ago, thanks to Lord Byron, LeBec, winning the Intercontinental belt and so on.
Chris basically ripped his image so badly that the IIWF is currently having to defend itself over an Intellectual Property case. The same style sunglasses, the same style entrance, the same wrestling style, the same inability to cut a coherant, interesting interview.
People fail to realise that Quigley is actually an agent, hence the reason Manning arrived. Now, we could talk about Manning's gimmick, but I refuse to speak ill of the dead. Apparently Chris left in a temper fit when Spreadbury refused to allow him to bring in a limping
Englishman and a non-working, Gingery goateed drunkard. TD : Marty ... MW : No, Tim let me finish. Quigley then has the nerve to run his mouth off about Joe Petrow, one of the most unique individuals in the ring today. Let's see, Chris. Didn't you leave the dubba-eye?
Didn't you insist the commentators made a big deal of your absence? The way in which by failing to turn up for 30 days your contract would be terminated? Wasn't that the time you made your little character change? Came back wearing a lumberjack shirt? Boy, even that was a rip-off - backstage you were known as Big Jerk-off.
TD : Finished? MW : I've talked enough about him. TD : Who would you most like to face in the ring? MW : Many of the IIWF greats that I never got series against - Dan
Kauffman. Jay-dubba Hardin. The Syndicate. Some of the greats. I'd like to face Joe Petrow, too, just to see how crazy we really can make things.
Playboy Ronnie D, if he ever competes in the IIWF. TD : Did you like the more "hardcore" style the IIWF did before the new "Wrestle Clean!" campaign? MW : In a word, no. I'm a wrestler, not a freak. If people out there get their kicks from blood, fine, maybe they should beat themselves to a pulp. Foreign objects tick me off, too. Like, are we supposed to plan backstage "You throw that duck at me, and I'll hit you with this
Jividen rubber doll"? TD : Marty, thanks for your time. MW : Not a problem. I'd just like to thank Billy Shakespeare for voting for me as Best Heel - his eyesight really is bad!
