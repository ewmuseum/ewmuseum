[OPINION] Importance of a finisher

7 January 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
-- A lot of people tend to judge the ability of an e-wrestling character based upon the creativity of their finisher. This is fine for some characters (note I'm talking about characters and not the actual handlers), but others don't really have their finisher play a big part. A perfect example is one of my first characters. Back in May of this year, when I'd only been around e-wrestling for a month or two, I came up with a new character for the UWF (which I'm still in, BTW) who was essentially a Mankind rip-off. I wasn't sure what I wanted his finisher to be, and the pressure was on as his debut was soon approaching, so I just gave him a camel clutch, figuring I'd come up with something better later. Well, the character has changed a lot since then, went through an unmasking and became Brett Greene, whose gimmick is quite different from the original. He evolved into a devious heel with the gimmick that he was ungimmicky. Basically, he was against all these idiots with "superpowers" or who pretended to be ninjas or something, and he just wanted people to wrestle. He stopped having theme music, as he thought it took away attention from pure wrestling. Greene still had that old camel clutch as his finisher too. But he never beat anyone with it. That's not to say he wasn't succesful. Far from it, he had the longest United States title reign in the history of the UWF, but he just never beat anyone with the camel clutch. He was so over as a wrestler, and especially as a screwjob artist, that it was credible for him to win matches with clean pins on lesser opponents, and with cheap shots and then screwjob pins on better opponents. His reputation made it that he didn't really need a finisher at all. In fact, the character was so succesful that I ripped him off of myself, made him a face, and entered him in the IIWF, just because I knew I could roleplay with that type of character. (Sorry, Daniel, if you're reading this but Ronnie Paris is not a purely original idea.) What I'm trying to say here is that if you can put someone over as a pure wrestler, they don't really need a prominent finisher at all. In fact, it's the sign of a weak character (IMO) that your finisher has to be something truly spectacular, unless of course it's a luchador type character who merits the move. ,[name withheld]
