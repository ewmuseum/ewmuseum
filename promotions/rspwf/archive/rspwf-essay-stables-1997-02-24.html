[ESSAY] Stables

24 February 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
First let me say, "Good job!" and "Thank you!" to [name withheld] for doing the RSPWF 200, and congratulations to Gunnar "Grizzly" Gaines for being number one! For those of you who are looking for a new fed to join, I hope you saw the post about The High Impact Wrestling Federation run by [name withheld] ( [detail withheld]@aludra.usc.edu ) and United Championship Wrestling run by [name withheld] ( [detail withheld]@geocities.com ) and company. Now, I am not affiliated with either of these leagues and I have no idea whether they'll be any good or not, but the advertisements show a lot of promise. If you're planning on starting a fed, write an ad like those, and you'll show people that you are committed to doing a quality league. This week I decided to write about stables. This was partly prompted by me seeing "The Nation of Domination" on WWF Challenge a while ago. I don't watch the WWF very often, but their brief appearance did not impress me. It looked like someone (Vince McMahon) through a bunch of jobbers-to-the-stars together and called them a stable. I don't think that works. If a group of wrestlers is not over individually, they won't be over as a stable. But if they *are* over individually, forming a stable could build further heat. A stable is only as strong as its weakest link. Well, I should say weakest *regular* link. If a member of the stable is acknowledged as the "junior" member, then it doesn't matter as much whether he's in the same class as the rest. If the stable has a weak regular member (either in terms of in-ring skills or of heat-drawing skills), he's dragging the reputation of the stable down, and it's largely the reputation and cachet that determines the success of a stable. Generally it's not a good idea to have too many stables in a league. A stable generally tries to represent the "elite" heels or faces, and they can't *all* be elite. However, I remember reading a post from someone (I forget who, sorry) about starting a league where everyone was in a stable of some kind. Sort of like NASCAR drivers, who are sponsored by one of several "racing team" stables. Allowing interfed stables into your league is a "don't" in my book. By interfed stables I mean a stable with lots of members, spread over several leagues. A manager who is in multiple leagues simultaneously is fine, but if you have an interfed stable then basically you have to have guest appearances by members in other feds, or refer to them in flashes at least. This requires other players to know or care about these other characters, and if I'm the fedhead, I don't want to give airtime to someone who isn't in the league anyway. I don't know many players who can run more than a few really different characters at the same time, so I don't like the idea of one player controlling a large stable all in one league. Most leagues don't let each player run more than a couple characters at a time, which takes care of this. Note that I said "different" characters. I think a stable works best when there are some differences between the members. If they're all Steve Austin clones, then it's not much of a stable. They should have a lot in common (alignment, for instance) but if they're too similar then they're redundant. It's generally a bad thing to have copycat wrestlers in the same fed, so think about how bad it is if they're in the same stable. I think it's better for a stable to form in the league than to enter the league pre-formed (unless they're established and coming in from elsewhere). This way stable-formation can be part of an angle and for a reason. Incidentally, I also like the idea of rookies coming into a league *without* a nickname or gimmick and then developing one as they gain experience. I entered rookies Joe Wheeler and Mickey Rogers in VCW a while back, but lost interest in e-wrestling before I could turn them into Joe "Eighteen" Wheeler and Mickey "Flyboy" Rogers. When I sat down to write this essay, I was going to write something about there not being many good face stables. Then I remembered Falkner Wrestling Enterprises (although many of the characters were fairly similar), Ace Diamond's stable (I forget the name... TNT?, but also had a number of similar characters), and Tropical Storm. And that's just in the several handfuls of leagues that I've been involved in, I'm sure there are a lot more. NEXT WEEK: Undecided, there are a couple of things I could write about, but I'm open to suggestions (basically I'm running out of topics).
