Critique - "Concieved on a Bed of Nails"

28 September 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Folks.. ..this is a roleplay done by the National Wrestling Council's "Scorn", one of our most outlandish and talented writers. While I am not him(although I wish to be:)), I was wondering what you folks thought of his work.
For note, he is currently challenging our World Heavyweight Champion, "Bullseye" Shawn Arrows, this week. The message is titled "Concieved on a Bed of Nails". --- ‘My relationship with my father can best be termed a nuclear discipleship. Rather poetic, don’t you think?’ [The river surface is serene and passive, no betraying ripple to even suggest that it flows. The night sky is flecked by stars, an inky screen stabbed by the tips of buildings in the distance. The gray waters are flanked by grassy vanguards, dark inclines which rise to a concrete walkway and a quaint river side path.]
[The moonlight scars the river surface with its blasphemous reflection, an entity of effervescent color against the dismally unremarkable sheen of the river surface. Even the bankings seem to have sacrificed their hue to the night sky, a pallid shade between the rivers’ bleakness and the paths’ insipid concrete.]
‘He educated me well, Shawn, with rigorous attention to detail and with an emphatic desire to ensure that his pupil never forgot his lessons. He succeeded, Shawn.
The pupil forgot nothing.’ [A brief pan round reveals more – a metallic causeway overlooking the pallid waters. The bridge spans the flow, all steel and mind numbing mechanics, a substantial drop parting the river surface and the walkway.]
‘I never forgot the smell of the alcohol on his fetid breath, the scraping of his voice against my ears, the shuffle of his feet against the stairway. I never forget the barren, unforgiving ice of his fist or the solid ruthlessness of his knee. I never forgot callus hands around my throat, rotten wood against my temple, furious voices shattering the solitude of my bedroom. I forgot nothing, Shawn, as my father was such an effective teacher, and I his nuclear disciple, the fledging student to his violence.
The student absorbed his fathers teachings with every…passing…day, Shawn. Not a single sun set before I had shed blood on the floor of my home for countless years. No dawns ever broke before I had tended to the poisonous bruises on my arm or the burning flesh on my skull. No moons ever shone before I had been told how worthless I was.’
[Scorn has breached the railing separating concrete from air. He clings to it with his hands, looming threateningly over the waters surface, his quiet and intense voice slitting the air asunder with its murderous eloquence. His hair hangs downward, the blackest eyes assimilating the drop to the water below.]
‘Inevitably, the day arrived when student surpassed the teacher. For five years my father had not lain hand upon me, had not choked his throttled abuse at me for fear of my retaliation. He had seen the boy who had entered his home grow into a man. Perhaps he was even aware of the walking, organic anomalous result he had helped to collect. Perhaps intimidation overweighed the misguided desire to retrieve the woman whose death throes had preceded the cries of my birth by only a few moments. But whatever the case, his anger one night belittled whatever had restrained him for those previous years. He barreled his fist at me, at my jaw. It broke right there.
My father never did retrieve the ability to walk after that night.’ [Scorn hangs forward, dangling perilously over the waters surface. His eyes widen, the river face defiantly offering no response to his threats.]
‘My first act on this Earth was to take the life of my mother. Before I had breathed my first breath, shed my first tear, choked the first wail from my mouth. Still coated in the organic filth of my mothers insides. Her death occurred simultaneously with my birth. A fact my father constantly reminded me of, a fact he loaded onto tear fraught eyes. He made the long walks to school a misery.
So I took away his ability to walk. Ever again.’ [Scorn drags himself back from the brink, his back hitting the railing behind him. He throws his head back, spidery strands spinning in the breeze that suddenly whips up the waters below.]
‘Gaze down at the river, contemplate my fall, this is how I feel about it all.’
[The head twists, blazing eyes finding us. Scorn snarls, spitting his words with acrid venom.]
‘That would be too easy, wouldn’t it, Shawn? I educated my f---ing self, read every book I could acquire, immersed myself in every philosophical viewpoint, historical source, psychological fact that had been published. It passed the time, the long hours spent alone, despised by my family and abandoned by the friends I had never made. They forgot all about me, Shawn, waving their social hand dismissively as I watched with bewilderment. Too quiet for the popular kids, too unstable for the freaks. Who could I trust, Shawn? No friends in the world, walking down the road, only myself to care for. Only me, Shawn, and no one else. Selfishness to stay alive.
But I awakened, Shawn, saw what you all were. Cattle blocked in by hedges that deceived you. Nailed into the institution of dross, insecure and worthless. A society buried alive, Shawn, in claustrophobic conditions, the air thinning, consigned to a lifetime of lying horizontally on your back in a six feet prison. Hammering futile fists against the lid of your buried prison from the inside, but it was too late for you all. You had dug your own graves, climbed into your own coffins and consigned yourselves to the ground.
Well, I stood above you all on the surface. I took up the shovel and piled the filth back onto the lid of your coffin. I could hear your cries of anguish six feet below me, the gibbering, stifled moans for liberation. I can still hear them today, Shawn. I don’t intend to do anything. My tomb is ready, too, you see. I am in there now, trapped in that airless chamber, only a dead body for company. Banging on that lid. Gasping for breath. All of us are in this position, Shawn. As human beings we are consigned to it, the institution of dross. You are worthless and so am I.’
[Scorn slumps downwards where he stands, dropping into a seated position on the edge of the bridge, his legs dangling over and his heels beating the air. He spreads his arms fully, clinging to the railing, his hair billowing out in the vehement wind.]
‘They can’t take the ghetto out of you, can they not, Shawn? Lower class since you started? Touching, Shawn, truly touching. Compare yourself to me if you wish, Bullseye. Perhaps our pasts do correlate somewhere along the line. But that line is fraught with inconsistencies, Shawn, inconsistencies which are crucial to why Scorn and Shawn Arrows exists on different plains. You bare your soul, relaying to me the horror of friends who tampered with needles and cavorted with the realms of addiction. Fighting on the street, Shawn. A father behind bars and a mother too f---ed up to care about you. Surely this parental pain turned your internal whimpering on your friends, Bullseye? They were the only ones left to trust, weren’t they, Shawn? The only ones willing to embrace you despite your emotional problems. Theirs were probably worse. Your friends,
Shawn. The only ones with whom you felt accepted.’
[With sudden savagery, Scorns skull lashes back, snapping against the railing with injurious speed.]
‘I never HAD any FRIENDS, Shawn! NO WHERE to turn! NO ONE to listen! NOTHING to
JUSTIFY my INSIPID LITTLE EXISTENCE!! The needles and syringes sharpened the road you walked, Shawn, but at least you didn’t walk that f---ING road on your own! Your mother didn’t CARE about you, Shawn, but at least there was one there to be shunned by! Your father sat behind bars, ROTTING AWAY in ANOTHER institution, but I would have given ANYTHING, absolutely ANYTHING to put my father behind the steel of a cell! You CAN’T CONCEIVE what I have known, Shawn!
The LUXURIES you were afforded were NEVER granted to me! And even if they were,
LIFE had NUMBED me SO MUCH that I WOULDN’T EVEN HAVE NOTICED!! I only remember
ONE ASPECT of my childhood, and that was having MY SKULL BEATEN TO A BLOODY
PULP BY MY f---ED UP MADMAN OF A FATHER!!’
[The skull snaps back again, steel beating bone.] [BAM] [Back again.] [BAM.] [Back again.] [BAM] ‘They spent 20 YEARS SMASHING ME TO SHARDS, Shawn! 20 YEARS BATTERING the foundations of my MIND, of ME!! And all the while, NOBODY listened, NOBODY cared, and NOBODY…
[BAM] ‘Did ANYTHING…’ [BAM…snap] ‘About…it…’ [The wind has picked up fully now, lashing back his hair and whipping against the eyes bled dry of tears. A pale hand clutches the railing, a palm finding the back of his skull and fingering the claret viscosity that seeps out of the new orifice there. With blood in his hands, Scorn yanks himself to his feet, toppling apathetically over the railing, glancing back at the waters surface almost regretfully one more time, the wind lashing his skin taunt.]
[Unceremonious fade to black.] -Ross H. Handler of Rico Sabuani Former GCW Glory Champion Current GCW Record - [3-6-1] Handler of "Shawshanked" Shawn Carson Former MCW Tag Team Champion And Former SCCW GH Champion
