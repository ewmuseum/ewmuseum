E-Wrestling Opinions

17 June 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello, I've recently seen a lot of fighting and different opinions expressed on this newsgroup, and while I believe it's good to have people say what's on their minds, I think when people start getting personal, they're taking e-wrestling way too seriously. Then again, after reading this, some of you may think I'm one of those people. I'm not writing this because I believe everyone wants to hear my opinion. I'm writing this because I think a few might want to hear it. Those who don't want to have the option of just not reading the rest of this message, but I believe I do have my right to post it and if people do have something to say about my views, I'd like to hear them, although as some of you know, I might be losing my computer access soon. Anyways, to make all my views clearer, I'll break them up into sections. First of all, a lot of people may not like me in e-wrestling simply because of the fact that I've joined a lot of feds and have not stuck with them and have opened my own e-fed several times, but have not even been able to get it off the ground. I'm not going to make excuses for why I didn't go through with being in the feds or running my own. To those handlers in my feds and those fedheads who I might have left "hanging" (and they know who they are), I apologize. Secondly, as to the opinions brought up by "Alan C." recently, I would have to agree on some of them. I do also feel that some of the people in the "loop" feds and even some outside take this game (and that's what it is) too seriously. Now some, like [name withheld], take it seriously, but people like him are classy enough to not make a big fuss if someone bashes their league. Others, however, simply don't get the fact that part of e-wrestling is losing. The fun in e-wrestling is being a part of a good angle or a good story. It's about being involved and meeting new people. Whether you win or not is important, but it shouldn't be the sole motivating factor behind what you're doing. A few months back, I left one federation and joined a few others of the "high profile" status because I thought I could end up having my character(s) better known by competing in those feds. What I didn't realize is that if you're joining a fed just to be the best, you might not have fun doing so, and consequently, you're not going to be so successful. I had gone into a retirement because of that, but thankfully, it didn't take me long to realize that the leagues that were the funnest weren't always the most high profile ones. It's like what Shawn Michaels said to Bret Hart a while back on Raw. Basically, he said that wrestling had become an obligation to Bret and was no longer fun. Also recently, there's been a lot of talk about e-feds coming and going in the blink of an eye. While I'm not the expert on keeping an e-fed going, just as stated above, I also don't plan on opening my own again when I return to e-wrestling. I think the best plan for anyone interested in running a fed is to become a VP and then once the fedhead becomes bored with running the fed, you can take over. That cuts down on the number of e-feds on the net and all of the confusion and also a lot of the bitching and moaning that some people (like me :) ) do about too many feds. Before I get off my soapbox, although I do plan on returning to e-wrestling, I would like to "name a few names" of people who've helped me during my time and I would like to thank them: [name withheld], Bob Freeman, [name withheld], Rod Knowles, Mike Beeby, Mike Sonby, [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], Matt Strake, Josh Warren, Dan Kauffman, [name withheld], and probably more people that I'm forgetting to mention. Well, that's about all my big mouth has to say. I hope that what I have had to say has gotten through to some people. Thank you, [name withheld] Handler of "Superman" Mike Stewart, American Thunder, & Kyle Hatcher
