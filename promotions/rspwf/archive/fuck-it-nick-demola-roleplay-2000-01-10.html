[FUCK IT] Nick Demola roleplay

10 January 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Have a blast. Scene: Nick decides to come back to wrestling after the car accident that almost made him paralyzed. It's short, but it got the job done in this "indy" fed I was in. It was the climax of the "Demola's Future" angle, where I got tormented and ragged on, but refused to come back to wrestling. This was the turning point.
Enjoy. I think. DEMOLA'S RETURN PART V There is a Sunrise of the Gods on this winter day. The sky is so enlightened in flourescents that even the doldrum ocean has turned an overbearing orange in submission. The sand crackles under the feet of the lone man trapsing it on this chilly near-evening. His face with a calmness like the sea... masking that orange, dying for submission so it can be released.
The brightness of the scene reveals the cracks and lines in the face of this lone man. He rubs his chilled arms defiantly. The one who was forgotten will not allow himself to be defeated, even by this ocean wind.
The wind howls and yells out to all who listen. It howls of lost time and unseen sunrises gone by, and the sadness that comes with those injustices. It yells out to the lone man about his lost time, about lost pride.
The scars on the head, the incisions on the neck. They are still fresh. Still healing. The last remnants of his youth, of his innocence.
The champion stands alone on the vastness, surrounded by a miracle, filled with something to prove.
No more fast cars. His last one remains in a junkyard, left after that slippery summer night. No more fast women. His last one lays in a bed in a far off hospital, clinging to what she has left after that same summer night.
He still holds on also. And with one fell swoop the winds die. The sun relents to the sea, and all goes to normal. The sky and ocean agree upon their blues. The scene is still standing, still holding on to that last gasp of brilliance.
The lone man takes a last breath of the memory of the scene. Now closes his eyes, touching the scar on the back of the neck. Now taking two slow steps. Now opening his eyes and turning back towards the earth, his brown eyes now flaming fervent suns.
