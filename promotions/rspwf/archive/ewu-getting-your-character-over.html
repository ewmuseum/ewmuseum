Getting your character over

EW University

Getting "Over"...A Guide For Players on getting that push by [name withheld], I believe Anybody can create a character in e-wrestling. It doesn't take much thought into creating a character. People in e-wrestling go through an assortment of different characters over the course of their e-wrestling experience. However, it takes a dedicated player to accomplish what every player and handler wants to accomplish. That, my friends, is getting characters "over" Now, the only question is, how? Before we begin, we must get a clear definition of what we mean by getting over. In real wrestling, getting over refers to the state where the wrestler get the fans to actually care about him or her. In other words, when the wrestler walks down that aisle, there must be some reaction from the fans. Whether the fans cheer wildly like they do for The Rock, or boo lustfully like they do for Triple H, if the wrestler is getting a strong reaction from the fans, he is considered "over." However, if the wrestler receives a lukewarm reaction from the fans, such as the reaction Viscera receives, he is considered "not over." Getting over doesn't necessary translate into wins or losses. At the time of this writing, The Rock has been losing quite a few matches lately. However, he is still over. Keep that in mind when you are e-wrestling, because too many people focus too much attention on wins and losses and not enough attention on what is more important, getting over. Now that we know what over is, how do we get over in e-wrestling? In e-wrestling, it's basically the same principle. However, in e-wrestling the reaction isn't cheers or boos. The reaction that you are looking for is to get people to read your material. When people read the roleplay packets, or the cards where your wrestler is wrestling or conducting an interview, your goal in e-wrestling should be for everybody to stop at that point, and carefully read whatever you wrote. It really doesn't matter what they feel about your character. As long as you can get people to read your material, your character is over However, if people tend to skip ahead when reading your material, then your character is not over. This brings us back to the original question? How do we get over? As the handler, you are the main one, in the long run, who is going to determine whether your character gets over or not. If you make an unoriginal character who rambles on and on in roleplays, people will not read your material. Many fedheads will simply delete your application and don't even dignify you with a response. Even if you do get in a fed, you will be the guy that the fedheads will use to put other characters over. Fedheads will think "Hey, I got this new guy that's pretty good. Who can I put him up against to give him some wins under his belt?" Unfortunately, your character's name might be the answer to that question, if you do not do the things necessary. So what should a handler do to get his character over? Here are some steps to help you. 1. CREATE A CHARACTER THAT YOU CAN RELATE TO You are the one that makes people care about your character. Therefore it is important that you create a character that you can relate to. For example, if you are unfamiliar with the culture of Japan as well as the Japanesse Puroresu, you might want to avoid creating a Japanesse character. I once created a tag team for the AICW called "The Rising Suns" These guys were horrible. They never got over and eventually, they were just used to boost the win records of other tag teams. Why? Since I had little knowledge about Japanesse wrestlers, the only thing Japanesse about The Rising Suns was their names. The easiest way to create a character that you can relate to is to create a character similiar to yourself. Some people even go as far as to name the character after themselves. My most successful character to date, The Knick Man, was based on what I believed I would be like if I was a world class basketball player/wrestler. You must be able to relate to your character in order for others to relate to your character. 2. CREATE A CHARACTER THAT IS UNIQUE TO THE FEDERATION One of the common mistakes that I see handlers make all the time is the mistake of creating a character that is similar to a character in the federation already. This could sometimes work and I have seen it work successfully in the past, but if you are new to e-wrestling, I would strongly recommend that you avoid this. It takes a certain skill to do this correctly, and you need some e-wrestling experience in order to pull this off effectively. Here's a common scenario. Let's say the champion of the federation is "Playboy" Scotty D, a arrogant pretty boy that thinks he God's gift to women. Now let's say you come in as "Pretty Boy" Pete Patterson, with the same gimmick as Scotty D. What's the first thing that's going to pop into the minds of the fedheads and the handlers? "This guy is a ripoff of Scotty D. That's not exactly the first impression that you want to make. To avoid all of that, make sure you check out the fed first, see what wrestlers are in there now, and make somebody different. If your wrestler is unique enough, that right there will get people to wonder what your wrestler is all about, and as a result, get people to read your material. Case in point, I wanted to join a Japanesse federation, Neo Japan Puroresu. However, I wasn't exactly the greatest roleplayer for Japanesse characters. Therefore, what I decided to do was use a character called Legion, who was from America, and his gimmick was that he was a sick, twisted, and sadistic individual who simply likes to hurt people and hurt them bad. Since most of the fed consisted of Japanesse characters, my character was very different. At the time of this essay, Legion is one of the top characters in Neo Japan, currently in a heated feud with "Midnight Slayer" Sankou Kirikorosu and now the President of Neo Japan. Legion is "over" because he is different and unique to that federation. That's what you want your character to be. 3. ROLEPLAY CONSISTANTLY STRESSING QUALITY OVER QUANTITY The only way you are going to get people to notice your character is if you roleplay consistantly. How consistent you need to roleplay varies from federation to federation, but the standard is usually once a week. At the same time, although you want to roleplay consistantly, you don't want to go overboard. For example, there is no reason why your fedhead should send out a roleplay packet, and out of 10 roleplays, 7 roleplays are by your character. People will simply get sick and tired of seeing your character and tune out. Remember, it's not how many times people see your character, it's what the character has to say, that people are going to pay attention to. Never feel that you have to create a roleplay a certain size length. When you do that, you tend to ramble and take away from the quality of your roleplay. You should only do a roleplay when your character has something to say, and only continue that roleplay until you get your message across. Keep in mind the type of character that you are roleplaying and stick to that character. Don't create a serious violent character and then have him do a roleplay about being a pretty boy, unless you got a pretty good reason for doing so. 4. ALWAYS MAKE SURE YOUR CHARACTER IS INVOLVED IN ANGLES AND FEUDS This is perhaps the most important step to getting your character "over" Ask any handler in e-wrestling, and they will tell you that what they remember most are good angles and feuds. Take real wrestling for example. What do you remember more? The interview Triple H did before he fought Vince McMahon, or the angle that occurred when Stephenie McMahon helped Triple H beat her father and betrayed her father? Chances are you remember the angle. It's the same way in e-wrestling. Although roleplays are important, that isn't what people are going to remember most about your character. It is the angles and feuds that your character is involved in that people will remember. You can check out "How To Feud" & "Feud Mechanics" by [name withheld] for more information about this, but if you really want your character to get "over" you must ALWAYS make sure that your character is involved in some type of angle or feud. All it takes is ONE killer angle or feud to turn your character into the most read character in the federation. At the same token, all it takes is ONE stupid angle of feud to destory your character. Always remember that angles and feuds are the most important aspects of handling your character, and always put your best work into developing good angles and feuds. Just a reminder, these are the four steps to getting "over" in your e-fed: 1. CREATE A CHARACTER THAT YOU CAN RELATE TO 2. CREATE A CHARACTER THAT IS UNIQUE TO THE FEDERATION 3. ROLEPLAY CONSISTENTLY STRESSING QUALITY OVER QUANTITY 4. ALWAYS MAKE SURE YOUR CHARACTER IS INVOLVED IN ANGLES AND FEUDS If you do these things, I guarantee you that you will be successful in e-wrestling.
