[Critique]Roleplay for William O'Leary

5 April 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Scene takes place as William O’Leary, a man who faces “The Crippler” Ivan
Kolinski on the upcoming Sunday Night Heat, is seen sitting on a black leather couch. The couch is located in the center of a rather large room.
Johnny “Sexy” Bod’s living room to be precise. The walls in the living room are painted Navajo white. Across the walls, there are many pictures. Some of Bod’s few accomplishments and some for pure decoration. As the camera continues to span the walls, we come across a clock. The clock is white inside surrounded by a wooden octagon frame. The clock reads exactly 8:05PM.
Beside the clock sits a rather large picture. The picture shows Johnny Bod raising his hands after he “defeated” William O’Leary at Extra dark. On the bottom right of the picture, there is a small crack in the glass cover of the frame. Various other pictures include pictures of Spice, Bod, and various other UEW superstars.
As the camera finishes spanning the walls, the image switches to William
O’Leary, who is seated on the black leather couch. William O’Leary is dressed in nice clothes, like he has been the past few days. He is dressed in a pair of Ozweego Adidas tennis shoes, Khaki shorts, and a black tee shirt that says
“UEW” on the front in grunge yellow. As you know, these clothes in no way relate to the William O’Leary we have known in the past.
As the camera begins to shift scenes from O’Leary, he begins to shift around on the couch trying to get comfortable. The camera switches from him and hits a scene of a wall we haven’t seen before. The wall is barren by the portion we see. The camera slowly moves down and a Sony big screen television comes into view. On the screen, a logo flashes across the screen that says “Cold War.”]
William O’Leary – Ah yes . . . I remember a little about the Cold War. Of course, being born in 1975, I only remember the stuff that happened towards the end of the Cold War. It just happened that I was flipping through the channels a while ago, and I came across this. With over 300 channels on this television, it isn’t hard to find something to watch.
[The camera switches from the television to a front view of William O’Leary. A smile on his face is seen as the camera quickly switches to a diagonal view of his face. A small glare covers the right side of his face.]
William O’Leary – You know . . . I thought serving Bod would really suck, but it hasn’t been as bad as I expected it to be. I mean, he made me wash his car, but other than that, he hasn’t made me do anything else that a normal person wouldn’t do on an every day basis. Of course, I shouldn’t have lost to him, but I __WILL__ get another shot at him, and it will be __VERY__ soon.
Although getting another shot at him is very important, it is the least on my mind at this time. I mean, I have other matches to think about right now. I have a match with a stupid Russian who needs a Chiropractor on Sunday, and then I will most likely have a match against a man who changes his name and appearance after every match he has, which he losses. Before I have a match with him, I want to confirm the name that he will be using. Will it be
Jessie Muldoon or Doomsday?! Whichever name and appearance you use, you won’t be able to run or hide from me. And when you lose, just remember . . .
you challenged __ME__ to the match, so don’t go whining to Haynes for signing the match. [William O’Leary pushes a small, round button on the side of the couch. As he pushes the button, the bottom of the couch flips out forming a recliner. He relaxes his legs and smiles.]
William O’Leary: But you know, I am confident, which all doctors and therapists say is good. I remember when I was little and had Health class in school. I remember what my teacher always said like it was yesterday. She always said, “Think Positively.” You know, throughout my life, I have never done that. I have always thought negatively of myself and positively of the person I was wrestling . . . since I __DID__ want to serve them. But now, it seems like it’s changed. Now, I am confident, and surprisingly, it feels good. You know, the more I think about it, the more serving people sounds less appealing. I guess we’ll just have to wait and see what happens . . .
who knows? One day, I might be a doctor. [William O’Leary smiles and switches his attention towards the television for a few seconds.]
William O’Leary: You know, the Cold war was going to settle the little differences between the United States of America and Russia, but Russia just wouldn’t do it, they wouldn’t switch from a Communist form of government to a
Democracy. Now, I’m not saying that Russia sucks, but they did kind of make a stupid choice by not following along with the United States and all of the other countries that are switching.
And, since I always side with my country, the United States, I’m going to have to go into my match with “The Crippled” Ivan Kolinski on Sunday Night
Heat with a grudge. Now, people always say that when you have grudges, you are a bad person, but this is actually a good thing for me. Because when I have a grudge against someone, it just makes me more determined to beat them.
So Ivan, I’m going to be coming into our match on Sunday with a grudge. Not only am I going to come in and cripple your ass, but I’m going to come in and prove to you that the United States always, and I mean __ALWAYS__ get what they want.
[William O’Leary scratches his armpits.] William O’Leary: Well, it looks like it’s about time to take a shower again. When I didn’t have a house, I only took a shower about once a week or so, but since I’ve started taking them regularly, it’s got to be where I always start smelling and itching if I don’t take one at least every other day. It must be something to do with my PH level or something. Well, I guess I better go on in the bathroom and shower on down.
[As William O’Leary begins to get up, he shifts over to his right side and grabs the television controller off of the end table and grips it in his right hand. He lifts back up to vertical and then switches the television off. He places the remote control back on the end table and sits back up vertical. He stands and lifts his arms – obviously stretching. The camera fades to black as he slowly walks out of the living room and turns down the hallway.]
