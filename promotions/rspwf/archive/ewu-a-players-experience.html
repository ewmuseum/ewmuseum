MY E-MAIL WRESTLING EXPERIENCE by Milhouse How I Got Started Imagine a guy with enough audacity to offer you the extra room in his spacious downtown Toronto apartment - An apartment he can well afford because due to an inheritance the guy is financially independent. There is no way you can afford the apartment because its the height of the recession and with your Arts degree you can’t find a job. Oh sure you get some work now and then but only enough to pay the rent on your current cubby hole and to keep you on unemployment insurance. So, as part of the deal the guy offers you the smaller room at a fraction of the rent, just as long as you do a few things around the apartment like the dishes, the vacuuming, etc. So in essence you become your friend’s very own butler. You put up with the guys crap for nearly six months. You pick up his socks, put away his CD’s and scrub his toilet and the whole time he treats you like his bitch. The final straw is when the guy develops a school boy crush on an eighteen year old high school girl (much too young for him) "thinking" about attending the University of Toronto. The guy thinks that if she moves in with him he can gradually break down her defenses and get laid. The guy then informs you that he’s kicking you out because he’s renting your room to a chick So what can you do? Your name is not on the lease. It’s essentially his apartment and his stuff... Hmmm...How about convincing the eighteen year old that she shouldn’t move in with the guy because he never washes his sheets (Really, how is a guy going to get laid if he’s hygienically challenged?), then you give away all his best CD’s to whoever happens to drop by the apartment. And then, to really drive him crazy, and as a last act of revenge, you give out the guy’s e-mail address to your cheeky monkey of a friend that just got access the net and is itching to raise some havoc. Hint: I’m was that cheeky monkey of a friend. And the bottom line is you don’t screw with my buds. The guy didn’t know what hit him or had any clue where it came from. Christian penpals, lesbian support groups, gay personals - You name it, he joined it. Including an e-wrestling federation. Some people join because they love wrestling. I joined out of revenge. My First Character It was you people that made me laugh the most. All that bolona about threatening one another. Pretty stupid if you ask me. I mean I can understand what the gay guys were looking to get out of the e-mail. I just couldn’t figure out what you people were doing. So hey, I decided to expand my Internet hobby of screwing around with other people’s lives and start screwing with yours. And what better way to do it than to join up myself. The first thing I needed was a character description and a sample role-play. Being a former closet fan of wrestling, I new what a character was suppose to look like and how he was suppose to talk. Pretty easy if you ask me. The main thing was to remain original. So, I sent in an exact physical description of Vader (even saying my guy looked exactly like Vader), I changed his name to Juggernaut ( After all, who has ever read an X-Men comic?) and everywhere Vader said Vader, I just substituted my character’s name. Vadertime became Juggertime Vaderbomb became Juggerbomb I even gave him a manager Bobby Horton (Bobby Heenan?) You get the drift. Okay, I admit it. Not original. My sample role-play was even worse. It went along the lines of something like... ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- [The scene starts in the **** interview area. Standing pacing back and forth is a four-hundred plus pound man with a scared forehead wearing black tights with the word Jugger written across the chest.] [Jugger] Finally, the Master of the Juggerbomb has come to raise havoc on the ***** Federation. I’ve got one goal and one goal only, that’s the World Gold. And if anyone gets in my way, the big hurt is gonna take them on a guided tour of the house of pain. I’m the biggest, I’m the best, and no one can stop me! Juggernaut - The next heavy weight champion of the world. [Fade-Out] ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ Luckily, the federation at the time was in a down phase meaning they were established but due to a recent shut-down, they were short on players. So, the fedhead decided to give me a break. The American Patriot Okay, I admit it. It was fun. It was fun to insult someone else without really hurting their feelings. And despite lamo flash number one, I wasn’t half bad at coming up with little catch phrases and making up descriptions of what my character was going to do with another character’s spine. But if there is one outstanding feud that put me over with the fedhead and the other players, it’s my feud with a guy called The American Patriot. I’ll describe the scenario. A new player comes to the federation claiming to be a fourteen time world champion. In his last success, he won some fantasy news group tournament proclaiming himself to be the Universal World Champion. i.e.. The champion of all champions. So, not really having a fight for the upcoming PPV I say "Okay, Champ. Put up or shut-up." This is where things became funny. The Patriot’s response - "It’s against the rules!" Apparently, to qualify for a title shot, first I had to win a recognized world title. Next, I had to fill out an application form. Then I would be asked to send in a sample flash. From there I would receive an invitation, and then things will be settled with twenty other guys on a simulator (a program that essentially decides at random what happened and who won the match). And this was all to occur sometime during March of the next year. Needless to say, I didn’t think The Patriot was playing fair because I was a newbie (new guy) and he was just giving me the runaround. And things just got to be a little too ironic when the truth finally came out and it turned out The Patriot was the guy actually organizing the tournament. That’s right! He won his own tournament! Simulator my ass! I was out for revenge. Hit ‘em where it hurts is what I say. He loves his country - Flash top ten lists of why America sucks Call him chicken in character - Call him chicken in real life He flashes two paragraph memos - Flash back two paragraph memos. In short, imitate and mock everything he does. And with all this effort trying to humiliate my opponent, something weird started to happen. Juggernaut actually ceased being a two dimensional Vader-like character who shouted empty threats and rehearsed lines. His hatred of his opponent was actually becoming genuine. And more importantly, Juggernaut was developing a sense of humour. As part of my strategy to tick off my opponent, Juggernaut would walk around America dressed as a ridiculous fat flag calling himself the US Patriot making a fool out of himself everywhere he went. The end result was my flashes were starting to take on a humourous edge and people were starting to like it. I was actually starting to become a little entertaining. The truth is I was actually nervous about the PPV. The Patriot was a far more experienced player than me and had been around e-wrestling since the beginning and, as I thought at the time, was well connected within the e-wrestling establishment. I was making some pretty heated remarks about America that I’m sure a lot of players didn’t appreciate. I had this real bad feeling that I was going to lose to this guy for the simple fact I was being a trouble maker. The thing was, he the last guy in the world I wanted to go down to. Things got way too personal. The result - I won. It turns out the fedhead was an Englishman and booker was a New Zealander with a serious hatred of the United States. It was a one-sided match aimed to humiliate the other player that actually ended up placing me in line to take over as federation champion. That’s right, after one month in the fed and developing a bit of a sense of humour, I was getting promoted. The fedhead was taking a leave of absence and the booker (the current champ) was taking over as fedhead and I was being earmarked for the gold. The Birth of "Mr Sexuality" William Bennett It was time to steal from Vader one last time. I was the World Champ, I was still undefeated and I wanted my character’s ego to inflate beyond belief. In short, I wanted my character to start acting cocky, riding in limousines and wearing expensive suits. I even wanted him to have a real name. But I started thinking realistically. Does Armani make suits for four-hundred and fifty pound fat guy? I wasn’t sure I could pull it off. Thinking back to a WCW Nitro when Ric Flair and Vader had formed an alliance to feud with Hogan, both men showed up to the arena in a stretch limousine. Flair as usual was wearing a real expensive suit. Leon White (Vader) in an attempt to follow Flair’s lead also decided to dress up. Only instead of a tailored suit, he wore a rumpled up old chord sports jacket complete with leather patches on the elbows and a pair of polyester dress pants. I remember laughing at the time thinking "Man, Leon White dresses like crap in real life." A light bulb went off. So will Juggernaut. So, I decided that instead of an old chord jacket, my character would show up to flashes wearing a plaid jacket, yellow pants and a bow tie. He would then start believing himself to be a sex symbol. But instead of gorgeous women falling all over him it would be overweight women between the ages of 50 and 75. And of course like all champions in real life before him, he would start a lame acting career on the side. I began to write scripts of crummy made for TV movies staring Juggernaut. Several come to mind: "Suze Where’s Your Suitcase" Parts 1 & II where Juggernaut co-stars as Gary Coleman’s twin brother. "Under Siege III: Take This Sadam Hussein!" - A Steven Segull rip off "The Body Guard II" where Juggernaut played the delicate singer and Whitney Houston plays the hardened tough bodyguard. Not only were there movies but Juggernaut also began to star on Broadway in tacky shows like "CATS" and the revival of "Annie" where he musically portrays a four-hundred and fifty pound orphan girl. However, I think my best work was when Bennett started appearing on talk shows and infomercials for merchandising, sex lines and my all time favorite the Juggernaut Psychic Pizza Alliance - a scam where he merged a psychic hotline and a pizza delivery service. I even created an entire line of useless Juggernaut sponsored products including Jugger Three-Day Underwear, the Juggernaut Waffle Iron/Backscratcher, and children’s toys such as the Jugger Big O’ Broken Glass. Juggernaut was no longer just Juggernaut, he was now William Bennett. Flashes rarely had anything to do with wrestling other than the occasional mention of an opponent’s name. And the funny part was no one could beat me. It was total William "Juggernaut" Bennett overkill. In the course of this wrestler’s career, I explored his family life, his sex life, his social life, and his insecurities. Juggernaut had a favorite food, a favorite colour, and even a favorite song (Karen Carpenter’s "You Light Up My Life"). William Bennett became a person with an entire history that dictated his behavior both in and out of the ring. And I guess that’s why people liked him. The hard part was keeping him fresh. Bennett had more nick names, gimmicks, and personalities than I can probably remember. These are the ones that I do. They took place over a span of about three years. Juggernaut - The indestructible ring enforcer US Patriot - A comic book superhero that loved America "Mr Sexuality" - The actor/singer/dancer/sex symbol "Mr Saturday Night" - The disco dancing nightmare "Captain Turbo" - Bennett’s crime fighting alter ego eventually arrested for mischief and accused of arson "The Reverend Doctor" - Bennett becomes an evangelist after he finds out that he doesn’t have to pay taxes. "Mr. Sexuality" Milhouse Undefeated for six months, champion for five, I swear I was starting to dress in plaid pants myself. I suddenly became a whiner. I finally lost the belt to a guy who deserved it. He beat me fair and square and it was my first lost. Never the less I still felt I was the lead wrestler in the federation - the guy who participates in and helps to develop the major angles (sort of the role of the fed champ). To do that, in my opinion, was to continue to crush all my opponents in lop sided matches and go on undefeated. The fed head felt the best way to do that was for me to start losing and putting over other players. Suddenly everyone and their cousin was kicking out of my finishing hold, I was being jobbed in tournaments, and being used to make other characters looked good. And I was getting ticked. Finally, I got into a heated debate with the fedhead, who not totally agreeing with my point of view, decided as a favour to allow my character to give the World Champion a severe beating before a short break so I could still stay in the limelight. It was sort of a play back for my character lying down and it was done in order to set up a cliffhanger for the new season. Only in the effort to keep our cliffhanger a surprise for the whole federation, we neglected to tell the World Champ he was about to get stretchered. Can you say "@#$% hit the fan"? The federation literally split down the middle. Half siding with the fed head, the other half siding with the fed champ. There were lectures on federation edict, fed rules, and the rights of the fed head to control angles. You name it, we debated it. I even tried to deflect some of the blame onto myself. After all, it was my inflated ego that started the whole mess and it was my inflated ego that was about to sink the federation. In a temper tantrum, the fed champ eventually decided to quit and it was then I decided my entire approach and attitude to e-wrestling was really getting screwed up. It was about fun. I decided to stop being a prima donna and to start being a nice guy. It’s fake after all! Who cares about who wins or loses? It’s about character development and interacting with other people. You don’t have to be the champ to be popular. It’s just a role or an angle like any other role or angle in the federation. It was then I decided to have a crazed fan take a shotgun and blow William Bennett away. Nobody is indestructible. Bennett was too good. He was too undefeatable. People were sick of him winning all the time and for the sake of the federation it was better off he was dead. The new season started out without a World champion and without a number one contender and things were just as entertaining. Did I eventually bring back Bennett? The truth is yes. When things finally died down and the fed needed an ultra heel or a lead character, just like in the soap operas Bennett miraculously came back to life only to be killed off again when he wasn’t needed. At his last death, Juggernaut turned face for the very first time. I’m not sure if anyone noticed the turn or not. It was more for me than anyone else. Other characters started showing respect for the big guy and I thought it would be nice if he went out one last time with the fans applauding for him. The federation’s last PPV was suddenly stopped during the World Title match with Bennett as a spectator, his career finally over due to an accumulation of injuries, slumped over in a chair dead from an apparent heart attack. He was the last character to have held the WAR! Title having achieved the honour on four separate occasions. The Power House Wrestling Association (PHWA) A novel approach by a veteran player. A small time wrestling league where characters are not world class and they compete every week in a converted Louisville K-Mart. It had Milhouse written all over it. This was going to be damn funny. It was the birth of perhaps my favorite character "Nature Boy" Larry Spikowski. Spikowski to me represents the lovable loser. He’s a former bush league star that considers himself to be a legend of the 70’s. A guy that knows the trade but was never really good enough to fight in the big leagues. In the PHWA, we pick up Spikowski’s life post retirement. He lives in a trailer, has a fifteen year old girlfriend, is the Assistant Manager of a Dairy Queen and hasn’t stepped foot in the ring in eight years. The PHWA represents to him the opportunity to re-live the glory days. Spikowski is perhaps my most widely known character having stints in the PHWA, WAR!, Armageddon, BzWF, and soon RAW. In my opinion, Spikowski is wrestling’s ultimate underdog. In the ring, he’s lousy but outside the ring he has charisma. He’s the poor man’s Ric Flair who hasn’t aged as gracefully as his North Carolina counterpart. The great thing about the PHWA is it was a great place to experiment with character development. Characters were required to have flaws and expected to be small time. This is where I learned to flip flop with a character’s gimmick to keep him interesting so the reader didn’t know what he was going to do next. I did this with a character called Wojo "The Carolina Hulk" hailing from Cleveland Ohio. The guy was a weak-minded alcoholic redneck. One week he was a homophobe accusing his opponents of being gay . The next week, Wojo was suddenly convinced he was a homosexual himself. The redneck suddenly started wearing lipstick, a string of pearls and carrying a purse around town. Finally, on a trip to New York, after being handed a "Jesus Loves Me" pamphlet, Wojo became a born again Christian. Wojo wasn’t a terribly popular character and was mostly used as a jobber in matches to put over other characters. However, his paranoia, lack of intelligence, sexual disfunction, and obsession with Christianity was incorporated into many of the characters I was already developing and had created at a later date. These are some of the characters that I believe Wojo influeneced over the years: William Bennett - A show tune loving wrestling juggernaut. A man with more split personalities than Roseanne and perhaps the most successful wrestler to ever grace the WAR! Wrestling Federation. At his height he was actively recruited by literally dozens of other e-mail federations. "Nature Boy" Larry Spikowski - Member of the International Brotherhood of Nature Boys and the anti-Ric Flair. A middle-aged throw back to the ‘70’s who brings honour to being trailer park trash. ‘The Electric Cowboy" Billy Ray Gant - A closet homosexual with bad knees. Married to a transsexual and played the role of biological father to another popular player. Mamma Cass/"The Destroyer" Cassidy Stevens - Perhaps the ugliest man to ever wear a dress. A former world class wrestler turned transsexual housewife and manager. "Animal" Ray Stevens - Red neck extrodinaire. He’s had the same case of the clap for ten years and wont do anything about it because it keeps him mean. Not to mention that he’s so paranoid he takes a crap with the stall door open just to ensure no one is sneaking up on him. "Butcher" Singh/Big Nude - The Wild Beast Man of the Sudan turns out to be a mini van driving family man from New Jersey that tries to convince everyone that despite his clean living, deep down he’s really a bad guy. Hobbies include playing Led Zeppelin on the bag pipes. The PHWA didn’t last long. And although at times my characters seem utterly ridiculous, to me they seem more real than most of the "normal" characters I see in other federations. . I learned in the PHWA that no character is perfect. If they don’t have a flaw, they aren’t real. Many players like to create characters that directly reflect their own fantasies - Big, strong, rich, good-looking, etc. I mean, who doesn’t have that fantasy? But the truth is given the choice of believing a wrestler has a perfect life and imagining a big fat guy with a sexual preference for hot fudge sundays, I’d go with the "hot fudge" scenario every time. Sure it sounds a bit cartoonish, but at least it’s interesting. Who can stand reading about a person that is perfect in everyway? In reality, if we look at the personal lives of our wrestling heroes, they actually turn out to be a bunch of rejects. The outsiders Kevin Nash and Scott Hall may come off ultra cool but in reality they’re two guys with severe addiction problems who are throwing their lives away. Bret Hart "The Excellence of Execution" and wrestling’s last true hero has turned out to be a bitter and unhappy man. Heck, even "The Rock" Dwayne Johnson is probably deathly afraid of spiders. Soap operas are interesting because people have problems. The same goes for e-wrestling. I try to make my character’s quirks funny. That’s my thing. That’s what I like. However, it doesn’t have to be that way. Character flaws can be tragic, sad, or even inspirational. It just has to be something other people want to read. My Heroes I’ve met all types in e-wrestling. Losers who like to send you personal e-mails telling you their life trajeties, people that actually believed they were their character, and I’ve even had someone make believe they were suicidal (My advice was to pull the blade down on the wrist, not across. That way they would be sure and cut the artery and avoid the tendons.) Funny, I never did hear back from them.... Let’s face it folks, its the Internet and everyone is a weirdo. And despite knowing a lot of people for over five years, I still haven’t told any of them my real name or more importantly, where I live. But out of all the people I’ve met, here’s some that stand out as my influences. These are people that helped me develop as a player. Sorry if I refer to them by character and not by name. "Homer" Tim Simpson - A Kentucky truck driver who’s perhaps the funniest man in e-wrestling and by far the most skilled writer. Tim was one of the first guys I saw who’s characters had weaknesses outside the ring and was able to incorporate humour into flashes. I haven’t heard from Homer in a long time. I hope he’s finally writing that famous novel that’s been flowing though his veins. Lita - Housewife, mother of two and in her spare time an evil masked wrestler named Nomad. In my opinion, the best strategist in e-wrestling. Both her and her husband created and ran the PHWA where I experimented with flashes and character development. [name withheld] - The legendary mastermind of WAR! and E-wrestling University. He’s one of the great fed heads and the man that gave me my start. But I remember him most as the guy who accidentally confessed he likes to slip into leather pants and get Jiggy with it on a Saturday night. Jake-baby, to steal a line from a friend, you made e-wrestling more fun than a pedophile can have in a wave pool. Jake and I have worked in numerous federations and I even wrote for his failed e-magazine "Purple Verse". Southern Man, [name withheld] - The man that was instrumental in my big push to World Champ all those many moons ago. Southern Man is also known at times as Robby "The Body" Douglas. He was a beer swilling "I don’t give a damn" tough guy long before anyone ever heard of Stone Cold Steve Austin and probably the best all round player I have ever seen. On a side note, my New Zealander buddy actually sent me a comic book with a character that reminded him of Juggernaut. Unfortunately, the note said I had to send the book back because it was a gift from his dear departed Grandfather. I live in Canada! It cost me thirty-seven bucks to send that big heavy book all the way back to Kiwi Land! The funny part is the dumbass actually thinks he’s going to be an traffic controller. American Patriot, Will something - My first big feud. I kind of make fun of Will in this but he’s a loyal player and after everything was said and done he turned out to be a pretty good guy. [name withheld], B.Sc - The handler of the famous Tonnage. For years, other players when wanting to get under my skin accused me of ripping him off. We finally ran into one another in Armageddon long after both characters were retired. I only mention him to put on the record that to this day I have never even read a Tonnage flash. Our in ring characters and our style may have been very similar, but unlike Juggernaut, I don’t Tonnage knew how to tap dance. Randy Gant, player name unknown - It bugs me that I forget this handler’s name. We worked together on angles for nearly an entire year. This guy was truly a great player and the scary part is we thought alike. I was the Kevin Nash to his Scott Hall. Although, one day, after seeing a family picture on his web site, I accidentally accused him of dating his sister. Hey, I’d bang her. She was hot. And I swear I thought it was his prom date. I don’t know what happened to ol’ Randy Gant but last I heard, in real life, he was doing a little bit of pro-wrestling on the side. "Stray Cat" Sid Starr, player name unknown - Was the first guy and only one of two players to actually ever pin Juggernaut. He knocked me off my pedestal and for that I thank him. "The Intrepid" Ryan Howard, Ryan Howard - His behavior convinced me that winning had little to do with being a good player. "Too Sweet Extreme Psycho Killer" Phil, player name unknown - The absolute worst player I’ve ever seen in e-wrestling. He wasn’t "TOOooooSweeeet" he was "TOOoooStupid". This guy wrote an incomprehensible five page flash once a day and stalked me for two weeks to become his tag team partner. I was almost fired from Armageddon for making fun of this doofus. Not my proudest moment. If you’re still out there Phil, learn to spell, numbnuts! [name withheld] - Spaghetti Si as he is fondly known was at one time the most hated person in WAR! He did this as a newbie by breaking every rule and code in the sport and ticking off other players on a personal basis. Jake asked me to team up with the guy and work with him. I did so against my better judgment and a dislike of his over use of foul language. Si, once he learned the ropes, turned out to be a pretty decent player going on to win the Armageddon World title as well as many others. More importantly, he also turned out to be an up and coming Fed Head and I’ve had the pleasure of participating in at least two federations that he’s been co-president. It’s guys like Si that are the future of this sport. ?
