<RP> Precious Moments

5 December 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I used this RP to debut in both the ACWA and WWO. It was that good of a debut flash, that I had to use it in both feds. Anyway... here it is!
-------------------- (It is dark and hell is hot... but that has nothing to do with this cold, rainy morning in Detroit. In fact, it's neither dark nor hot. It is more of a grey day, with a steady stream of rain pouring from the sky. As we look around, through the eyes of the camera, we see "The Spirit Of Detroit" standing proudly, as usual. This large statue of a man holding up a sphere has caused many a person to realize just how fortunate they are to live in the city of
Detroit. It brings hope to the people, and it alleviates the fear that many of them feel. And now we look further down, at two oddly dressed females standing in front of the statue. Both are attired in tu-tus and leotards, one wearing all pink, and one wearing all light blue. They are staring upwards at the statue, as we slowly move in on them. Listen...)
Girl in Blue: It's pretty, Bink. (The voice is not a female's. I repeat, the voice is not a female's. We move in closer, and it is revealed that these are not females at all. These are men. Men dressed in tu-tus and leotards. Men staring at the "Spirit of
Detroit", and smiling so broadly that you would expect them to start dancing and frollicking with joy within seconds. And that's just what the man in pink starts doing... dancing and frollicking)
MAN in Pink: It's BEAUTIFUL! I must dance with gay joviality! I much frollick to and fro, thinking of the wonderful man who must have sculpted this! I MUST
DO A JIG! (And he does just that. He jigs like mad, as people walk by either laughing hysterically, or staying as far away from these two seemingly insane men as possible. When he stops, he smiles widely again, courtseys, and then goes back to staring at the statue)
Man In Blue: It really is a pretty statue. Man In Pink: Yeah, it is... (The two men stare at the statue some more, quietly, and then both spin around at the exact same time, with looks of shock on their face)
Man In Pink: WHO ARE YOU?! Man In Blue: WHAT ARE YOU?! Both: AND WHAT ARE YOU DOING HERE?! (Both men stare at the camera, and as if we, watching this, have spoken to them, they smile big wide smiles, and let out a sigh of relief.)
Man In Pink: Hiya, I'm the Binky Fairy. Man In Blue: Hiya, I'm the Winky Fairy. Both: We're Precious Moments. The Binky Fairy: We just signed up to wrestle in the World Wrestling Organization. The Winky Fairy: And we're excited to announce that all the evil in the WWO will, from this day forward, be vanquished from the arenas the WWO inhabits.
TBF: I mean, c'mon. Evil isn't good. Evil is bad. And since we're good, we have to show those people that are bad that bad is evil, and evil is bad, so you shouldn't be evil, because it's bad.
TWF: You see, we're good. Good leads to happiness. And when you're happy, as long as you're not evil, you're good. But if you're evil and happy, then you're EXTRA bad. And that's extra evil. And that's definitely not good.
TBF: We've come to the WWO to make sure the good is good, and the bad is good, too. Think of us as the fairy folk that make everyone happy and gay.
TWF: And you will be happy and gay. Or else we will bink you and make you happy and gay! TBF: And trust me... being binked is NOT a nice thing. TWF: Yeah it is, Binky. TBF: Oh yeah... I guess it is... (Binky and Winky stare at each other for a few seconds, then begin laughing insanely) TBF: I love this city. I love this country. I LOVE THIS WORLD! TWF: It's a lot better than Fairy, that's for sure. TBF: Well... sometimes I miss Fairy, but for the most part, I'm making something of my life. I mean... I'm five years old. I'm getting up there in years. And this is the first time I've been able to make a difference. And it's fun, and silly, and great! I love it!
TWF: Me too, Bink. (Winky looks up at the statue for a minute more, then looks at Binky, a look of enlightenment on his face)
TWF: I was just thinking... I wonder what this statue's called? TBF: I don't know... how about we ask the locals? (Binky and Winky look around, and see a man walking down the street who looks like a janitor. A facist janitor, for that matter. But that's besides the point, he's a janitor nonetheless. Binky and Winky skip arm-in-arm over to him and stop him)
TBF: Excuse me, Mr. Human. I was wondering if you could answer a question for me. Facist Janitor: No. Now leave me alone, you freak. TWF: That's not very nice. All we wanted to know is what you humans call that statue over there?
(Winky points at the statue, and though the man keeps walking, he mutters under his breath, loud enough for Binky and Winky to hear...)
Facist Janitor: That's the "Spirit of Detroit", you dips... (Winky and Binky turn white at the mention of the name of the statue) TWF: Did he say... (gulp) spirit? TBF: As in... ghost? BOTH: RUNAWAY! (Binky and Winky run away, as fast as they can, as we zoom up to the statue again. It stands proudly, unmoved, undescecrated by the many crimes and pain of Detroit. But can even the "Spirit of Detroit" withstand the pure force of stupidity that Precious Moments exudes? Can even Detroit withstand these men that claim to be "fairies"? Can even I set up the scene for Precious Moments, without ridiculing them? We shall find out with time. And time is what we have now... until next time)
