OFFICIAL RSPWF Top 200 (2003)

27 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Special thanks to Dave A, Butch, [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld] and the Ring Syndicate guys (and their uh... imaginative paragraphs) for helping with the top 25 1. Jacob Cross (NEO-Pro) 184 -The most dominant man in the industry for years, Cross went from a second thought in EMWC to a main eventing, killing machine in NEO-Pro. Sweeping through the competition he won their Triple Crown and now leads the House of Cross. Many have compared him to Caleb Temple when Caleb Temple was feared by all... and trust me, there is reason to fear Jacob Cross. 2. Edmond Winston IV (UWF) 133 -EW4 finally won the big one this year, defeating 3 men in the main event of UWF Gold Rush 2003 to take the World Heavyweight Championship. Winston held the belt for the rest of the year, and holds the distinction of putting Michael Augustine out for months with something worth of a Bond villain... an exploding Waterford Crystal replica belt. 3. Ktulu [NEO-P] 120 -The story of the year in many eyes, Ktulu literally came from nowhere to garner an absolutely huge following of fans. The silver haired martial artist is... well... weird, but has flash and heart in the ring. Going toe to toe or hardcore, he's done it all and is still continuing to improve. Frightening. 4. Cobryn (IWA, Ring Syndicate) 116 -Cobryn epitomizes the term "working heel". An incomparable throwback to the days when Stan Hansen, Ric Flair, and Harley Race ruled the world, Cobryn has seen quite the year. In just one year, Cobryn won two world titles (one by outlasting four other men on two consecutive nights), crushed nearly all the competition in his path (Cobryn has only suffered one loss this year, which came from nefarious means), and delivered some of the most heinous acts of verbal assault towards his opponents. Truly, few can compare to this world-class athlete who still has plenty of years left to go in this business. 5. Steve Spector (EMWC, I Slash) 115 -Despite working in both EMWC and I Slash at the same time, perhaps the top face in the history of the game never showed any signs of slowing down, winning I Slash's top trophy and fighting a match of the year candidate against Devon Case. Where does he go from here? Certainly not down! 6. Psicopa (NEO-Pro) 115 -Perhaps THE best Junior Heavyweight ever? The staple of NEO-Pro's cruiser division, Psicopa has finally graduated up the ranks and is not only easily the most popular competitor in the company but one of it's best. 7. Tigerheart Rally Jackson (IWA, Ring Syndicate Australia) 101 -Won the OWE National Heavyweight title towards the beginning of the year defeating Eric Valentine in the tournament finals. Held that title until the fed closed and had wins over Riley Young, Steve Greedy, Ken Morlock, Derek Douglas, and Johnny Glamour among others during the four months he competed in OWE. Won the I-Slash Spotlight Title defeating Cooper Concrete in the tournament final and has held it ever since with successful title defenses over such names as Trace Michaels, Echo Dare, and Chad Allen. Defeated Setzer Van Strife in IWE, avenging a loss from earlier that year. Also avenged a three year old loss to Mike Stryker during the Mid-West Wrestling Alliance's "farewell" card. Won a shot at the world title in only his Ring Syndicate debut. Also held the XGW tag team titles with Ian Bradley earlier in the year and I have no idea how he won those. Hosted a tag team tournament entitled "When We Were Kings 2: We Were Partners (Once and Young)". 8. Super Scott (I Slash) 96 -One half of "Super Cool." Super Scott burst back on to the wrestling scene in the middle of 2002 but he truly hit his stride when Scott hooked up with Johnny "Cool" Coles. Scott, a former EWA champion returned to the spotlight with his constant bashings and verbal outlash that has labelled him a "troublemaker" from day one. Still, Scott excelled in 2003 capturing the titles with Coles and spent the majority of the year fueding with Nate McMannis and Maverick and ended it by handing them their asses at Egos and Icons. Super Scott, if remains focused in 2004 could find himself with the I-Slash heavyweight title by years end. The key though is, if he remains focus and if he still wants it. 9. Hardkore Jonnie Valentine (Ring Syndicate) 89 10. Hannibal Carver (NEO-Pro, GLCW) 88 -The Boston Strangler might have had a quiet year in some people's minds but it was enough to bleed opponent after opponent dry. Competing in brutal matches with a foul mouth to boot, Carver has or is appearing almost everywhere and everywhere he goes leaving a trail of bodies and empty beer cans in his wake. 11. Robert Hunglestein III (IWA) 86 -Robert Hunglestein III is currently living large in his palatial estate with his wife Jennifer and son Hunter. When asked about making the RSPWF Top 25, he refused to take our call. He did however send in a letter stating he's better than all those who are listed above and certainly below his name. He also states he is a rich and very busy person, so stop wasting his time. 12. Quinn Brown [UWF] 83 -The UWF Heavweight Champion at the beginning of the year, Quinn waged a war with Scott "Hotspot" Daniels throughout 2003. Lost the title in a four-way match at Gold Rush, but continued the fued with the reformed Pride and Daniels himself. Still one of the most agile performers in the business, as well as one of the most popular with the fans. 13. Scott Daniels [UWF] 82 -2003 saw the return of the OLD Scott Daniels, as Hotspot showed his true colours when he gave his own daughter his signature move, the Pride Breaker on an episode of SNR just prior to Gold Rush. Since then the scheming, evil, out-for-himself Scott Daniels has turned crowd after crowd against him. Reformed the Pride faction with Tom Landis and Team Canada, and stayed in the world title hunt while continuing to fued with Quinn Brown. Challenged QB to an iron man match at an upcoming PPV, and still determined to win back the championship he feels he rightfully deserves. 14. Michael Augustine [UWF] 80 -The leader of the Youngbloods, Augy was actually out of the ring with an injury for half the year 2003 thanks to a trick exploding crystal belt given to him by Edmond Winston IV. A strong first half of the year in a four way fued for the UWF World Title, and a comeback in the last few months of the year with revenge against EW4 on his mind. 15. Derek Irvin [NEO-P, EMWC] 78 -Oft considered the brightest of the bright up and comers, Derek Irvin wrestled the Match of the Year in NEO-Pro against Psicopa before going down to injury. However, the technical wizard is not forgotten, many spectacular matches in NEO-Pro and GLCW giving fans something to remember him by. 16. Luke Kinsey [EMWC, I-Slash] 71 -The best wrestler to never be World Champion, Luke Kinsey has fought them all. He dominated in RCW... was up for world title contention and an Iron Man match in EMWC... he made about as big a splash as you can in I Slash. Leading EGO Max to a death grip over EMWC including a lengthy run as North American Champion, Kinsey was poised for the top until the dying days came. 17. Tripp Shade [NEO-Pro/I Slash/GLCW] 70 -Super popular babyface in NEO-Pro to hated coward heel in I Slash, Shade isn't just everywhere, he's everything. No matter how he comes off there is one thing you cannot deny... his talent. Arguably the best mat wrestler in the game, Shade brings flair and character unlike very few ever. 18. Rum Barton [SPW/NEO-Pro] 70 -The South Pacific flagship and longtime Jisatsu Champion, Rum Barton brawled his way through the Pacific Ocean, facing all comers. On more then on occassion that man was Raidon Kentaro, the two leaving a trail of blood everywhere they went. Despite losing in one of the best deathmatches of all time at SPW's End Game, the "Hackney Hitman" is far from done. 19. "The Doomsayer" Kyle Lee [UWF, MBC, LEW] 68 -The quintessential breakthrough year, nobody came on to shock more people with what he accomplished than Kyle Lee. Although he was banned from his own MBC when it was resurrected, Lee made up for it with big wins over Michael Augustine and others... then came out of nowhere to take the UWF North American Championship. After years in the midcard, the Doomsayer came to the forefront this year. 20. Johnny "Cool" Coles [I-Slash] 68 -The man who made being cool.... well, cool. Johnny "Cool" Coles comes in at number 20. What a year it was for Johnny "Cool" Coles. After being out of the game since 1997, Johnny Coles returned to the wrestling world at the end of 2002. He hooked up with Super Scott (his long time friend) and the two instantly clicked. It would not be long before Johnny captured gold in the I-Slash. Along side Super (Super Cool) Johnny won the I-Slash tag titles. Super Cool had formed but Johnny's single star status wasn't dying down. After fueding with... well, everybody in 2003 (I think he had a t-shirt for every single superstar in the I-Slash, mocking the HELL out of them) he focused his ridicule on Maverick to the point where Maverick took time off. Possibly the best insult man in the game, Coles has captured IC gold in the Slash and continues to be a superstar there as well as in LVPW. 21. Chris O'Brien [UWF] 67 -Another surprising good year, the multiple time tag team champion began 2003 as half of the UWF World Tag Champions and may end it as the Cruiserweight Champion. A stunning upset over the cruiser champion at the time, Uriah Hawkins, Chris has faced all comers for the lightweight gold. A former MBC World Champion to boot, 2003 has been extremely good for the Drunken Icon. 22. Cyrus Williams (IWA) 67 -When you saw two middle fingers crossed above a wrestlers head after a match, which you saw a lot of times this past year… it was a representation of the lifestyle and attitude of the hardest working man in the business. Cyrus Williams, a name that struck fear into the hearts and psyche of any wrestler who was unfortunate to be penned against him that week. Cyrus didn't need the fans chanting his name when they did, and to be honest he didn't care in the slightest when they stopped... He just did what he's always done, and that's beat people up... bad. A man who completed what was a dream year for any wrestler in 2003... with a record of perfection, yes unbeaten; undefeated in the IPW/IWA, Cyrus Williams rocketed through the ranks and unseated Kilroy Evans to win the Triple Crown Championship in the IWA. Along the way he threw in a ride with the famed "Society of the New Breed" and simply beat anyone the IWA put in his path. Sources say when told he was honored, he replied "Messiah challenged me to a switchblade showdown behind the IPW dumpster." 23. Michael Bonn [UWF] 66 -One of, if not the, most despised UWF Cruiserweight Champions in history. Bonn dominated the division much of 2003, and owns the longest CW reign in UWF history at 365 days. A loss to Uriah Hawkins a year to the day he won the belt aside, Bonn has had a won-loss record unparalleled by just about everybody in the UWF. A fued with Chris O'Brien has ensured Bonn stays within reach of the belt. 24. "Suicidal" Nate McMannis [I-Slash] 65 -After 2002 seen him becoming the I-Slash champion and the most hated man in I-Slash, Nate McMannis spent 2003 being the good guy and getting screwed. The constant struggle between McMannis and the I-Slash uppers was McMannis' real fued in 2003 as he was not able to thrust himself back into the Main Event picture. After dropping the title to Kiljoy, he was no able to step back into the main event picture in the I-Slash. That's not to say the Nate McMannis didn't have an amazing year. McMannis spent the year fueding with Super Cool along side stablemate, Maverick. He was unable to defeat Super Cool at Egos and Icons but that was thanks to a big part by Eric Travers who stabbed McMannis in the back. Currently, fueding with the "Epic" one, Nate McMannis has the drive that brought him the I-Slash title. will he be able to capture it in 2004? Very possible. 25. Eric Travers [I-Slash] 65 -Number 25is being brought to you by Maxim Online, Head and Shoulders, Mazda, Rolex and any other sponsor that is endorsing the man who drops in at said number... "Epic" Eric Travers. If people didn't believe before that Eric Travers was for real, 2003 surely was the answer to them. Travers captured the EWA world title in 2002 and held on to it right into the middle of 2003. During his run, Travers defeated opponents like, Steve Spector, Gunnar Gaines, Caleb Temple and Mack Ensassarol. There is no question to the fact Eric Travers elevated his game in 2003, will Travers' follow up year be just as good? You better believe it. 26. Andrew Karnage (IWA) 61 27.
