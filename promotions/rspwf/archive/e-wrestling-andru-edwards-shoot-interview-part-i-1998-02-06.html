[E-Wrestling]- Andru Edwards Shoot Interview!! <Part I>

6 February 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Jason Hudson]- Hello everyone, I am Jason Hudson, and I would like to personally welcome you all to the very first RSPWF Video Shoot Inter-View. Our first guest, is one of the greatest. This is a man that, after only two years in active competition, has already proven to be one of the best. Tonight, we have "The Franchise"
Andru Edwards on our show. [He turns to Andru] [Jason Hudson]- Thanks for inviting us into your home Andru. Now, you have only been wrestling professionally for about two years, and already you are, as some people might say, a legend. It seems that everywhere you go, you manage to win a title, or some sort of accolade, but lets start at the beginning. What was it that captivated you, and motivated you into becoming a professional wrestler? Was there something special about sports entertainment, or did you know someone in the business?
[Andru Edwards]- Its my pleasure having you here Jason. It is true, I just turned twenty six this past November. I have been wrestling for about two years, and I tell you,
I have loved every bit of it. Hmmm...how did I get started in the business?
Well, I will tell you this. I have been a very big wrestling fan since I was about six years old. I was totally hooked by the age of seven, so much, that I remember about an incident where I started crying because I didnt want to go to church, I wanted to stay home, and watch the Canadian Strongman take on The
Hammer, of course, nowadays that isnt a match I would be caught dead watching, but that is how captivated I was by the business. I believed everything that was said, every move was real, every character acted the way they acted on television, as well as in real life. I was what the company wanted their fans to be, I was certainly a mark. I always cheered for the good guys, and booed the bad guys. As I grew up, I began to realize it was all fantasy, and that is really was just "sports entertainment". The thing is, unlike most people, this just amazed me even more. Usually when people find this out, the stop watching, thinking it is childish, but not me. I wanted to learn more about what goes on behind the scenes. I was eager to find out, and that is what I did. I began to intern for small promotions, helping put the ring together, or helping by being a ring assisstant, referee, anything that would get me backstage. I got to speak to many workers, and find out more about what went on, but I never really though about actually WRESTLING. The thing that got me doing that, was actually another wrestler. You see, there was a wrestler named the Professor of Pain. I was mopping up backstage, and he came over and talked to me for a little while.
I told him how I felt about the industry, and he told me I had more heart than half of those guys in the lockerrom, and that I certainly had the form and shape to compete. He told me that I should consider it, and if I wanted, he would help me out.
[Jason Hudson]- So you took him up on his offer? [Andru Edwards]- Well, I thought about it over the next few days, and I realized that I would
LOVE to become a pro wrestler, and that was my calling. However, Professor of
Pain had jumped leagues, he went overseas to Japan, so I was left hanging. I got in the gym, and immediatly began to perfect my condition. I was always in shape, and always a health nut, but I wanted to have complete discipline before
I started a career. I talked backstage with the promoter of this small league, it was called the JJF, and I told him I wanted to be a part of the roster, and since they were so small on talent, he immediatly let me into his promotion. I got my license from the New York State Athletic Commission, and then I had my first match.
[Jason Hudson]- Who did you face? [Andru Edwards]- The Joker. He was my first opponent, and I was nervous as hell...BEFORE the match...but once my entrance music began to play, which was "Whatta Man" back then, and I stepped through that curtain, the ladies went WILD, the men began jeering me, and I just thought to myself, "This is pretty cool". It seemed like a dream come true. I began to continue walking down the aisle, but this time with confidence. I stepped through those ropes, and raised my hands high above my head, and I felt incredible. Once the bell rang, I just did my thing, I tried to behave, make sure I didnt blow any spots, just so that I would have the promoters from the other leagues see me, and see that I was for real. I won that match, by surprise actually. I was booked to do the job, but I guess the
Joker saw potential in me, and I applied a version of the Japanese armwrench, and he said Uncle. I was shocked, and I was extatic at the same time. When they announced my name over the speakersystem, as the winner, well, there is no words that can describe the feeling that I felt.
[Jason Hudson]- It must have felt good...did you hang around in the JJF? How far did you go? [Andru Edwards]- I stayed in the JJF for a while. Since it was an independant league, I was allowed to work other indies as well. In the JJF, they loved me. I got to face
World Champion, Mike Woods in my fourth week of competition, and on that night,
I realized a dream, because I became a champion. I never really thought it possible, but it came true. I dropped the belt about two weeks later to Woods, and regained it the next day. With that, they had an angle where a man named
Blade would run in on another match between me and Woods, and take us both out, causing me and Woods to ally, and eventually, we won the tag team titles, and I held that right along with the World Title. I also won their King of the Ring tournament. The JJF soon closed though, and I retired my title and went to look for more, and better, competition.
[Jason Hudson]- Where did this take you? [Andru Edwards]- It was now time for me to pay my dues, as they say in this industry. I went to the UWA, which I believe is one of the greatest, if not the greatest league of all time. I went there, and I was way out of my league. I was wrestling against men who have been veterans for at least five years. They were unbelievable, and it was ridiculous. I lost my first three matches there, and I thought that the
JJF was just a fluke, until Bob Hollywood came in, and mailed me a contract. I read it through, and he told me that he thought I was going to go places in this industry. He said he wanted to team me up with "Mean" Mark Bagwell, and he was going to call the team "Simply The Best", and that is where the legacy began. "Heavenly" Heather Kay, a really good friend of mine, decided to join me as my manager, along with Hollywood, and jealously began to brew there. Behind the scenes Hollywood complained that Heather was stealing the show, so the UWA decided to have me and Bagwell break up, and that Bagwell was going to team with "Gangsta" Tommy Gunn, leaving me in that feud, and with me keeping the name Simply The Best. I still lost, but I gave some of the greatest performances you have ever seen. I was about to come out of my shell in the
UWA, and hit main event status, when they decided that they, too, were going to have to fold. They announced their last card, and on it I was to face Tommy
Gunn in the match to end all matches, funny thing is, it was called Out With A
Bang, and the card still hasnt happened yet. It was announced two years ago. I guess they didnt have the money to get the card out. From there I was out of work, and no one wanted to hire me, because I was an unknown. I decided to get together with a few friends, and start a small local promotion, the Fantasy
Wrestling Federation of course, as just a little joke, to maybe get some attention, and show my skills to people for free. The first card had just two matches, and I won the world title against The Knightstalker, who is also a good friend of mine, or was at the time. We all were basically putting ourselves over to be noticed by other bigger leagues, and it happened, because one day I got a call from the promoter for the ICWF...
[Jason Hudson]- That brought you to the Internet Championship Wrestling Federation. Many people say that this is the league that made you a star. This is the place that you went, and where you were challenged, and you made the best of them. You came here, and your talent came out...what do you think?
[Andru Edwards]- I will tell you one thing, if there was no ICWF, I really dont think there would have been an Andru Edwards, or at least, the "Franchise" Andru Edwards that you see today. In the ICWF, they gave me control over my character, and I was allowed to do a lot of things if I wanted to. They also gave me something that not many people have given me in the days before, the put the microphone in my hand. Me and Heather came into this league, and we enjoyed everything.
They treated me with respect and class, and that is something that I am always going to appreciate them for. Now this league was a fresh league, but they were already big. Their champion was a man named The Spartan, real name Troy
Walters. He had been running rampant through the ICWF, and no one could beat this man. I challenged him, and the match was made, and this is truly where I came into my own. Now at the time I was already FWF World Champion, and feuding with The Spartan in the FWF. This was his home turf, and his belt was on the line. We put on one of the greatest performances ever seen in ICWF history, and in the end, he went for his Moonsault, I moved, and I covered, and got the win.
My first GENUINE World Title, and I was on top of the world. It was an honor, and I was again, very thankful to the ICWF for trusting me as their top man. I tried to put on a great show, and each and every time, I did. You would hear the people talking about my matches after the shows, about what a great performance I put on, and I was living a dream.
[Jason Hudson]- And this is actually where you dominated... [Andru Edwards]- It is actually a funny thing, you see, I was scared to be myself, so I was using matierial, old matierial that was used before, such as "Turn out the lights, ITS CHECKOUT TIME!!!", and that is what was getting me over. That is what the promoter wanted me to do, because that is what was working with the fans, but then that promoter had to leave town, and he gave the league up to
David Regan, another close friend of mine, but back then we were on strictly business terms. I was working back then, and I had my stable. I was one half of the world tag team champions, as well as world champion. I forfeited my tag titles a little later on, and began feuding with Scott Flair. That feud built up to a match where my nemesis, Scorpio, who I have been feuding with in almost every other league, came out, and had cost me my title. I began to act more the way I wanted to act, and less like a reproduction of a stale product, and in the table match of the century, I won back my belt. That was one month later.
Then the ICWF was set to fold, but I didnt want to see that. I told David that we could merge the leagues together, the ICWF with my FWF, and that is what was done. From that point on, the FWF skyrocketed in popularity and talent. We held a big card, Heatwave 96, where we combined leagues, and it was a big success, and everyone loved it. We unified all the titles, but kept the name FWF on the belts, and a new era was born.
[Jason Hudson]- So here we are now in the FWF, which is growing rapidly. How did you feel? [Andru Edwards]- Again, it was an amazing feeling. I felt great. A small little promotion that I built, with help from very few people, was now flourishing. We had some of the greatest workers in the game as well. In the main event, I faced Death Wish. I was on team FWF, but I was defending my ICWF World Title in that same match. It ended with me getting a win by disqualification, and the gimmick of the event was, whichever league won 4 matches first, would gain control of the other.
That was the seventh match, and my win played it so that the FWF won, and now had control of the ICWF. We incorporated many of the ICWF customs into the FWF show, including their newsletter, "The Truth, The Whole Truth, and Nothing But
The Truth...", but the writer for that soon had complications and time constraints, so that didnt last long. We had some of the best matches after this, and I got increased help as well with the increased roster. The feuds grew and everything, but we still werent what we would have LIKED to be.
Remember, I was still a worker, and I was wrestling for other leagues while trying to balance wrestling and running the FWF.
[Jason Hudson]- One of these leagues you were wrestling for, the PWA, was also a well known league. As big as an indie could get some would say. What did you think of the competition there?
[Andru Edwards]- They had the talent, and they had the workers, but they didnt have a good end product. This is the league where I was introduced to all the big time names at the time. The Shawn Austins, the Rick Wrights, the Fangs, Tommy Van Dams...they all wrestled here, and they were all damn good. This league had a lot of potential. I remember starting there, and again having to do some jobs, but after a short while, they began to hit me with a push. I beat one of their biggest names, but it was a countout win, because they didnt want to to be too over yet, and I was going into the hunt for the Intercontinental belt, but, as the story goes...
[Jason Hudson]- The league folded... [Andru Edwards]- Exactly. It was getting ridiculous. I decided to search around before joining another league. Around this time, the FWF was having its one year anniversary, which is what almost propelled us into the bigtime, but it wasnt time yet. The
Slammy show was a success, and with it, we got a slew of new members. "Sexy" Rex Harley, Tommy Boyz, and a few others. One of the most notable, had to be [name withheld], a.k.a. Erin McCoys. This was a close friend of mine who I invited to the show, and at the last second she just decided that she wanted to present one, and that she wanted a gimmick. I quickly came up with Exotic Erin and
Erotic Erin, and the rest was history. She went out there, and did her thing as if she was a pro. I was really proud of her, and proud to have her in the FWF.
It was the beginning of a legacy actually. [Jason Hudson]- And Erin became your manager..in a lot of places, actually, almost everywhere you competed, Erin was by your side.
[Andru Edwards]- Yes, Erin was great, and she loved the business. She was also very eager to learn. I taught her the ropes about the business, and she led me down the aisle, and to many titles. One title that she led me to actually, was another one of my greatest moments.
[Jason Hudson]- Are you referring to the Totally Awesome Wrestling? [Andru Edwards]- Definatly. This is one of the greatest moment in my career. Erin led me to the ring in that league, along with one of my versions of Simply The Best. Over there it was Team Extreme, Superstar Troy Martin, and Scott Tallion. They say that we were beginning to take that league over as well. When we first entered there, we werent really anything special. I remember beating their world champion, XERXES The Dark Angel, in one of my first matches there, but it was a non title fight. Something happened over there, I dont really remember what it was, but basically, the title was declared vacant, and they had a thirty two man tournament to declare a winner, and new champion, and my name was thrown into the hat. Now there were some big names in this tournament, and NOBODY, including myself, thought I was going to even have a shot at coming close to winning...nobody except Erin. The night of that card, it was on a Wednesday,
Erin told me something about myself, and what she saw in me. She told me, she knew I could do it, because she sees me from the outside. She sees me without seeing my doubts and my worries, no distractions, and that meant something to me. That night I went out there, and I put on the performance of a lifetime!

[Andru Edwards]- Definatly, Erin has always been eager to be active, and have an active role on camera, as well as off. She didnt just want to be a valet, but she really likes to take part in what is going on, and be part of the show. That is a very good quality that she has, which I admire. At the same time, I also was wrestling for the AAW, and Erin was just as active there as she was here in the TAW. In the AAW I held the Intercontinental Championship on two occasions, and the funny thing is I never lost the belt. The AAW is a league that actually didnt like me much at all, and they couldnt stand the fact that the fans wanted to see me. That is why they gave me their Intercontinental Title, to get the fans in the seats. They threw me into a feud with Attica, who is a man that cant say anything if there isnt foul language involved. I defended my belt against him, and the way I lost it was he had me in his finishing maneuver, an arm hold, and Erin was supposed to throw me her purse so that I could hit him with it, but apparently they changed the scripting, because as soon as the purse hit the canvas, the ref rang the bell, and Attica was declared the new
Intercontinental Champion, reason being that the purse was being treated like a towel would be in boxing.
[Jason Hudson]- And this isnt boxing, what did you do about the situation? [Andru Edwards]- What could I do? I was under contract, so I continued wrestling, and I fought
Attica a few more times, and got DQ wins, or things of that nature, which never meant anything to my career...eventually I won the title back from him, and soon thereafter, the AAW closed down as well, when Ryan Parker decided to put it to rest. Soon though, he opened up the PPW, which in my opinion, was another one of the greatest leagues I have ever competed in...which soon called itself the PPWO. I wont he Global title in that region as well, and it was a title that I was proud to hold as well. It meant a lot, because a lot of my peers began to respect me, saying that I really wasnt a flash in the pan, but that I actually was what I said I was all along. I was Simply The Best, and I was The
Franchise of the industry.
[Jason Hudson]- Let's talk about when you lost that title, because that is one of the angles that you didnt like. You actually hated this angle. Can you tell us why this upset you so much? The fans were captivated by it, and it was interesting...
[Andru Edwards]- Basically Hudson, I felt cheated. I was the proud owner of the Global Title, and I felt happy to have had that belt. My opponent was an arch rival of mine,
Shane Slaughter. This is a man that gloats like no other man, on and off the camera. He defeated me in the PPW once, I remember the match, great match, and he hit me with a superkick for the win. Fine with me, I have no problem doing the job, but this match was totally different. Once again I was not told of things that would go down. I knew that Erin would turn on me in this match, to cause even more intrigue, but I had no idea that I was slated to lose my title, and suffer another loss to Slaughter. This really upset me.
[Jason Hudson]- I realize this, the publications and rumor mills say you were actually thinking of hanging up your tights, and boots. Is there any truth to this?
{Andru Edwards]- Actually Hudson, there is. When this happened, I got totally disgusted with the sport, and everything it stood for, because it was changing, and the way a wrestler like me got respect wasnt mattering anymore. I couldnt just go out there and puton a great show, there had to be more to you than that. You had to have more than great microphone skills, and be a great athlete. You have to have killer instinct and be able to get away with anything and everything you want to get away with. The promoters didnt care about you, all they wanted now was for their league to be the favorite, no matter what it meant. Dishonesty became big in the industry, and I really decided to call it quits. I was sick and tired of it all.
[Jason Hudson]- What changed your mind? [Andru Edwards]- Well, it took a while, but the person who changed my mind, was none other than
Erotic Erin. She called me up, and she told me that if I was going to continue to be a pro wrestler, and if I wanted to go down in history as one of the greatest of all time, then I had to be a WRESTLER. That meant wrestling, no matter what the conditions of wrestling were. The nature of the whole business is like this, this is a very corrupt, political business Jason, and I realized that. In retrospect, I dont think I would have actually retired anyway, but I was very upset with the way things were going for me at that time.
[Jason Hudson]- Say you had retired, what do you think you would have done, and where would you be today? [Andru Edwards]- I wouldnt have left the business altogether I suppose...I guess I would still be just sitting at the FWF Offices in Manhattan, a few blocks down, and running the league. I would have any active or on camera roles I suppose, but like I said, that isnt how it went, so I am still out there on television doing it better than anyone else can..
