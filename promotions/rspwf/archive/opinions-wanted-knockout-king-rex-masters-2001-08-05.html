Real name (optional): Rexall Montgomery Masters. Hometown (in character): Atlantic City, New Jersey. Hometown (out of character--optional): Atlantic City, New Jersey. Age/Birthdate: 22/6-23-1979. Height: 6'5". Weight: 222 pounds. Gimmick/personality: Professional boxer. Blackballed from the big leagues for illegal tactics in the ring. A perfect fit for the catch-as-catch-can stylings of professional wrestling.
Alignment: Heel. Comedic with a big mean streak. Advantages. 1: Hard hitting. Rare is the man that can stand toe-to-toe with the "Knockout King". In the boxing ring, he was one of the biggest punchers in the business. According to many, his power was second only to a young Mike
Tyson.
2: Technique. Handspeed without par, and the ability to place a punch with perfect precision. What's more, he uses both hands, unlike most wrestlers, who only use their rights.
3: Footwork. THE MAN CAN MOVE~! Masters prefers to dodge and dance rather than locking up with an opponent, duck rather than taking a punch (how many wrestlers take punches just because they can? RIGHT HAND! RIGHT HAND!), and, in general, make his opponents look slow and sloppy by comparison.
Disadvantages. 1: Grappling. Beyond the basics, almost nothing. An actual wrestling match with Rex amounts to a comedy act. He gets schooled until he can punch the guy, unless the opponent is similarly unskilled as a wrestler.
2: Showboating. Playing to the crowd has always been Rex' biggest vice. He just loves to get a reaction, whatever it is, from the fans. If they cheer, great, if the boo, even better, just so long as it's LOUD!
3: Respect. No respect for the referee, no respect for his opponent, and no respect for the rules. When in a hard spot in a match, Rex is much more likely to force a disqualification than to allow himself to be beaten sometime later on. The man simply does not care.
Wrestling style(s): Primary: Boxing (Martial arts). Secondary: U.S. Standard Worker. Main wrestling maneuvers (5-20 moves). 1: "BIG LEFT HAND!" An everyday left hook that tends to send an opponent to the mat whenever it hits. Not a knockout blow, but a good opener. And yes, he is a southpaw.
2: Rabbit punches. 3: Mounted punches. Sit on a man's chest, punching his face in a repeating left-right combo. 4: Tornado punch. A full 360 tends to make for a pretty solid blow. Signature move. Also easily countered by a belly to belly or belly to back suplex. 5: Headbutt. 6: Biting. When in trouble against an opponent, this'll tend to turn the tide. 7: Low blow. Let's face it, he'll need this one against actual wrestlers. 8: Powerslam. One of his few actual wrestling moves. Other maneuvers (optional). 1: Right jab. 2: Right cross. 3: Left uppercut. Signature maneuvers (Optional). 1: "Throw Down". Top rope flying forearm. If it weren't clear that the man's a cruiserweight, this'll make it so. When about to hit this one, he mounts the top turnbuckle in a turning leap, ala Rob Van Dam. This one can hit at any time, without warning.
2: Rolling inverted electric chair drop pin. The attacker begins the maneuver in a standing headscissors, as if the victim were about to powerbomb him, but instead, he stands up, gripping the victim's legs, and dangling the victim down over his back. Then, hurling himself forward and down, the attacker spikes his opponent on the mat, then rolls through, holding the legs for the pin. Works as a counter to a powerbomb, piledriver, or similar move.
Finisher(s) (Optional). 1: Hurricane Hellstorm. Flashy in the extreme, the HH is, essentially, a spinning, leaping uppercut. Looking like the Dragon Punch from the Street
Fighter series of video games, it is a versatile, if difficult maneuver that requires the attacker to turn 180 degrees to squat down, then another 180 degrees while leaping up. The end result is an uppercut that starts very low, works like a tornado punch (one of his standard moves) in that the 360 degree spin adds force to the blow, and goes very high, using Rex' leg strength to propel him upwards, and through his opponent's position.
Continuing to spin in air, he looks almost superheroic in his athletecism. Still, c'mon, it's just a punch. Get over it. Entrance (in cut and paste form). History. Born to a black Jamaican father and lily white, blonde New Yorker mom,
Rex Masters grew up in New Jersey. More specifically, Atlantic City. The gambling capital of the Atlantic Coast.
Growing up, all Rex wanted was to be famous. Not much to ask, right? Well, he certainly tried his best. In his quest for fame, somewhere along the line, he discovered Boxing.
And he was a natural. Blessed with a genetic athletecism that transcended his heritage, Rex seemed destined for greatness. His parents marvelled at how he could knock out an 18 year old boy in the PAL boxing leagues when he was only 13. When he went to the nationals at age 15 in the flyweight division, they were utterly flabbergasted...
And when he went pro at age 17, their minds were utterly blown. Conquering the minor leagues, he moved up to the WBA, winning the Bantamweight title. Then, undefeated at age 20, with a record of 24-0-0, he tied the slipknot around the neck of his own career.
In a move that would make Mike Tyson flinch, he clamped his teeth down on an opponent's neck when it seemed like he was about to lose. Then, he kicked the man in the groin. The referee tried to intervene, and Rex swung wildly, breaking the ref's jaw, and almost breaking his neck. Having always been a bad boy, this didn't really come as a surprise to anybody.
Unfortunately, Masters himself got the shock of his life when he was barred from Boxing indefinitely.
So, what was next for Rex? For one thing, he went to the gym, trained like mad, and worked his weight up to around 220. What's more, he met
Johnny Axis, who, at the time, was still trying to be a promoter, wrestling only part time. Johnny stated, bluntly, that Rex' antics would go over well in a wrestling ring, and he should let Johnny train him. And he did.
Then, onto the indy circuit. Plying his trade, the newly created
"Knockout King" found success again. Beating many wrestlers without using even a single wrestling move. With his flamboyance, bad boy charm, and near insane level of arrogance, Masters was kickin' butt and takin' names. Now
... he feels the need to move up to the next level, and truly become a "Pro"
wrestler. Appearance (both physical appearance and ring attire). Rex is a powerful figure, if a little thin for a wrestler. At 6'5", most wrestlers aren't cruiserweights, but he pulls it off nicely. With a bodyfat percentage of something like 0.1%, he cuts one of the meanest shapes you'll ever see.
Above the neck, he's got a neatly trimmed beard (no mustache), green eyes, and long, black dreadlocks. His nostrils flare when he's enraged, and his eyes squint. Great ring presence.
To the ring, Rex wears a red, white, and blue robe with matching top hat. Pretty bizarre, but it certainly gets him attention wherever he competes.
In the ring, he wears red, white, and blue boots, boxing shorts, and gloves. No pads. He's a boxer in the wrestling ring, essentially. The gloves are only about 4 ounces, with a little bit of padding that's more to protect his knuckles than his opponent's head. The palms are open, and the gloves are held on by elastic straps, allowing him to remove them easily, and grapple with them on. Additionally, his wrists and hands are taped, adding to his appearance as a boxer (and making a good, quick weapon to strangle an opponent with).
