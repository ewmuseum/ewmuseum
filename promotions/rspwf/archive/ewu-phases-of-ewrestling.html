Phases of ewrestling

EW University
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello everyone, I was reading over the newsgroup and I thought to myself exactly why so many are frustrated with the fact their characters are not in one of the "upper tier" federations or why they are not ranked on some poll. Seeing that I had acted this way at some point in e-wrestling, I thought I would do my best to explain why people act in such a way. I hate to keep picking on Jeff H., but his thread is what is fresh in my mind at the moment. But let's begin: Complaint #1 -- "I am good enough to be in the EMWC. I could beat most of those guys. It's my time!" In my view, this is pure frustration. Either that, or the handler is beginning (or already has) to develop an ego. This is one thing I see going on with Tim Beck and I really wish he could change his ways. I tell him every time I can, but I just don't want to see him go the same way I went (God, I sound like Dr. Dre). Tim has said to me that he is good enough to be in the EMWC, and yes, he might be. That is only up to Chris Blue. However, when you flash three or four times in five months for the WWO, the dedication and workrate factor doesn't say much for the handler. And based on that, I did not think you were quite ready for the EMWC, Tim. Please take into account the fact that I was released from the EMWC the second time for being inactive, so yes, I know what I am talking about. If you go three weeks or so without doing anything and you are not currenlty involved in anything, you will be shown the door. I found that out the hard way. When a handler says he is good enough to be in some federation, from my experience, they are frustrated with the fact people do not think the same way as him/her about his/her character. Someone brought up an interesting topic the other day when they asked if they marked for their own character. I certainly do not mark for mine. There are times when I like a certain thing he did or a certain phrase he made, but I have never marked for my own character. Then again, I think every flash I do is utter garbage, and most likely it is. But Jeff, I'll use you as the example. If you say you are good enough to be in the EMWC, that is your opinion. However, there are tons of other handlers out there that feel the same way about their own characters. I doubt Chris Blue will sit and listen to everyone say how they deserve to be in his federation. And while EMWC may be your dream federation, I know for a fact Chris Blue has a select few "dream handlers" he would like in the EMWC. I mean, if Chris had the opportunity of taking Jeffrey Drake or a returning Musashi (I know the majority of you are saying, "Who?", but please review the IIWF webpage for info), I am sure Chris would not be giving Musashi's handler a no. There are only so many spots in the league for wrestlers. Also, with a shortage of writers being almost a weekly nuisance, that just means less matches the next week. The smaller number of matches there are, the shorter amount of roster space available. The problem is, there are so many handlers out there that want to come to the EMWC and "make an impact". An often problem seems to be people saying they want to come to the EMWC to "get big". And by my own admission, I admit that being the basis for my first stint in the federation. And look what happened -- I clearly was not ready for the "big time" and was quickly shown why. When someone dubs you a "glorified shitfedder", that doesn't do a whole lot for your morale. That is why I speak on this subject -- all of this is from my own experience. When you think you are ready for what is allegedly the "best fed on the 'net", most likely you are not. I'm not. I still say I'm not ready for the EMWC. Heck, why do you think I have a partner? In my mind, Jason carries me every week and Tucker is the dominant force in Strictly Business. I think that if I were to have applied to the EMWC as a singles wrestler, I would have been denied. But because so many handlers just want to come into the league to get big, there is never, or hardly ever any room for singles wrestlers because there is always someone coming in for an angle or something. But, if two handlers (or even one) would just get together, come up with an angle, and apply to the EMWC as a tag team (which, by the way, Chris needs a few of at the moment), you could perhaps be admitted. And if you do that, look at what may happen -- you could be EMWC Tag Team champions. Did Jason and I ever think we would be the champions? Heck no we didn't. However, we came in with an interesting angle, orchestrated some nice things along the way, and we ended up with the titles after around three or four months. And with me, I came into the EMWC with the Dead Presidents (horrid... horrid, I say) and that later led to me bagging my own partner and going right into the singles division. If people would try things out with a tag team, show they can flash well, perhaps Chris would let you try your luck in the singles division. Yet, there are just so many people that won't the fame and recognition that supposedly comes along with being in the EMWC. They want to be "recognized". The only advice I can give is to wait it out. Do your best in the federations you are already in. Accomplish some things and really mold your character into what you are most comfortable with. If you can get your skills harnessed and the character down to a "T", *then* apply. Don't just apply right when you make a character and expect to get in. It won't happen, generally. But, if you show you can succeed with the character and do some quality work, then ask to be accepted. Chances are it is more likely to happen after taking those precautions. If you apply when your character is just made, the president is going to remember the application as, "Oh yeah, I remember that guy. What a horrible application that was!" The president won't recall your current work perhaps, but will remember what they saw before. It's all about first impressions -- even in e-wrestling. Wait until you feel you have a quality character, then go for it. Complaint #2 -- "Damnit, what the hell? Why didn't I make the Top 200?" Alright, first of all -- I did not make the 1998 Top 200. Former EMWC World champion, "Wild Thing" Kevin Slater did not, either. When I first saw the poll, I was a bit surprised to see that I did not make it. I was a bit angered (much like the way Jeff H. has been acting), but then again that was at the time I was getting booted from the EMWC. However, my character had really not done much. A N.E.W. Cruiserweight championship, but I really had not come into my own yet. As I look back, perhaps I shouldn't have made the 200. Perhaps I should have. Frankly, though, I couldn't care less any longer. Yet, I am the prime example of the fact that things *can* change. I went from not ranked on the '98 200 to #1 on the "Mid-Years". If that tells you anything, it should tell you that if you work hard, the fame and recognition will come, whether you are looking for it or not. After I did not make the 200, I really began to ignore the polls and everything, and just got back into the fun aspect of e-wrestling. I joined INWI when N.E.W. closed because it had many of the handlers I enjoy working with. I joined the INWI for the fun, without a doubt and because Pete invited me. I worked hard for my six-week tenure in the league (okay, except the match against Tony Houston) and I was fortunate enough to be the final INWI Light Heavyweight champion. In WWEW, I worked very hard, as well. I flashed twice for two seperate matches against Fuse, both of which never occured. However, when the third match came around, I continued to work hard. Perhaps because of how I worked in the past, I did win and was WWEW Light Heavyweight champion, and might even be still since the federation seems to be closing. For the NPWA, I flashed week in and out, even though the federation was having major problems which ultimately killed the league. I made it to the finals of the Cruiserweight title tournament as a newcomer to the league because I worked for it. The WWO -- I am where I am there because I work for it. I think I lingered on titles a bit too much there... because titles mean nothing to me anymore, to be honest. Yes, I would like to win one World championship at one time or another, but that does not look to be happening anytime soon since I am only in one federation as a singles character. But back to the whole "ranking" bit -- if Kevin Slater did not make the Top 200, then there is something definitely wrong. This proves the poll is not always a direct reflection of the best e-wrestlers of the year, because Slater would definitely be on my list. Had I taken #1 in the most recent poll or not, I still do not think of myself as a "big name". To me, I am just another e-wrestler who has fun at this and does his best. Whether that makes me "big" is a matter of opinion. I flash the best I can and put everything I can into this game because I feel the more you put in, the more you get out of it. So Jeff, if you want on the 200 (which you made and I did not, I might add), or want higher on the 200, just work for it. Find a federation you enjoy and do your best. That's all anyone can, or does do. In closing, I think many handlers are just going through a phase process -- I know I did. Here is somewhat of a timeline of events, in my opinion (does not apply to all): 1) Discovery of e-wrestling 2) "What is roleplaying?" 3) Roleplaying as real characters 4) Discovery of fake characters 5) First fake character is created 6) Discovery of RSP-WF (and awards) 7) "I want on the 200!" 8) "What is stratting?" 9) "Why did I not make the 200? It's just a popularity contest anyhow." 10) I am good enough to be in [insert federation here]." 11) Reality check -- End of ego (hopefully) 12) E-wrestling for fun 13) Have fun in the feds one is in 14) Place in 200 15) Become "big" (usually without striving for it) Granted, that was not the best list ever, and obviously things will not always go in that order. I think of e-wrestling somewhat in the terms of love, as bad as that may sound. Generally, if one is looking for a lifelong partner or something and they go out looking for that, they usually will not find it. However, the second you stop looking, it happens for you. That is how I explain my success (and popularity?) with my character over the last few months, so if you want to get "big", don't worry about it. Just stop thinking about it. I mean, if you have the same character for years and years, it *will* happen for you. Ben has had Fang for quite some time and with time, you definitely improve. I am not saying Fang was ever bad because I havent' seen a great deal of him, but when you have a character for a long amount of time, obviously people will be subjected to him/her. They will see more of him/her and will remember their name. Your work will be shown to that many more people and when that occurs, the likeliness of placing on polls only increases. But I think I have written my novel for the day, so I will leave everyone with this: "E-Wrestling is like Mike Tyson -- you're a rookie... you get an ego... then you get the crap beat out of you."
