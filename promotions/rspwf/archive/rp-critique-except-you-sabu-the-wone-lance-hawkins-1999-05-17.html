[RP CRITIQUE EXCEPT YOU SABU] "The Wone" Lance Hawkins

17 May 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Okay guys, I know Sabu424 is going to say this sucks. But what the hell some of you actually do have some good things to say, and some good comments. Go ahead and tear it apart, cause I know you are going to. Just be nice, is all I ask.
here it goes, a roleplay from "The Wone" Lance Hawkins =================================================== [The scene opens up in the middle of a poorly lit street. The street lamps are lit but they do not give off much light. It is raining slightly and in the distance the thunder claps loudly, as a storm is approaching. Lightning crashes down, and a large BOOM can be heard. The street is lined with run down homes and a cracked sidewalk runs along side it. A few homeless men sit in their boxes on the side of the street, begging for money as usual.]
[From one of the side streets a man emerges. He is wearing a long black coat. His entire outfit is black. He has long hair which hangs down in front of his face. He looks both ways down the sidewalk. He then turns left and begins to walk down the street. The camera zooms in on him.]
[As he walks the man encounters one of the bums. The bum reaches up and grabs the man's leg.]
Bum- Could you spare some change mister? Just a few cents is all I ask. I would be forever thankful.
The Wone- Please remove your hand from my touser leg. Bum- Just a few cents sir, that is all I ask. The Wone- I said, remove your hand from my trouser leg. It would be a very wise thing for you to listen to me. I do not take kindly to those who disobey me.
[The Bum takes his hand off of the leg of Wone. He moves away from Wone. Wone now bends down and looks the man in the eyes for a moment.]
The Wone-You want me to give you my money, which I have earned. But do you wish to offer me one reason for why I should do such a thing? Let me ask you a few questions. Why do you sit on this street in that box?
Bum-My wife left me, and then I got fired from my job. I lost my house, my car, and everything else that I could possibly lose. I couldn't find a job with no address, and I didn't have a phone, which also looks bad on an application. I tried, I really did. I just couldn't find any work, so I am forced to live on this street in this alley, in this box. Now please mister give me just a few cents, so that I can buy me something to eat.
[The Wone begins to shake his head in disbelief. He pushes his hair out of his face and then stands back up. He peers down into the eyes of the bum once more.]
The Wone--All of these reasons that you give me, they could all of been prevented. Your wife left you, you could of stopped that. You could of treated her more like a woman and a little less like a material possession of yours, that is what she said wasn't it?
Bum--Well [pause] yeah it was. The Wone--You got fired from your job. That I am almost certain you could of done something about. You could of done your job better, or faster. You could of kissed ass a little more than the next guy. There are many things that you could of done to prevent that. Even if there was no reason why you were fired, there still is no excuse. You could find work anywhere. You could go work at one of those fast food restaraunts. You are not forced to do anything. You choose to do what it is that you do, and you choose not to do anything to change it. The answer is no, sir.
Bum--What answer? The Wone--No I will not give you any money. [The Wone begins to walk away. As he does the Bum jumps to his feet.] Bum--[yelling] WAIT!!!! [he lowers his voice] Hey mister, what do you do for a living? Why do you think so poorly of me? What is it that you have against me?
[The Wone turns around and faces the man. He raises his voice slightly.] The Wone--What do I do for a living? I simply compete in the one true sport that this world has to offer. Wrestling. The one true test of ones manhood. The one true test of desire and competance. What I do for a living is something that a simple man such as yourself could never understand. A man as material as you could never understand the true meaning of what goes on inside the ring. I look down on you because of what you are. You are a man that never worked a day in his life, yet he advanced in the world. I on the other hand have had to work for everything that I have ever had. All be it not much, but I worked for it none the less. You, you let it all go down the drain. You sit in your tattered and torn box, begging for money. The problem here sir, is you.
Bum--[pause] Why don't you have gold around your waist then? You are probably nothing but a jobber.
[The Wone comes closer to the Bum. He stands nose to nose with him now. Peering into his eyes he begins to speak once more.]
The Wone--Once again you mention these material things. Is a title belt a true measue of ones worth? I think not you poor fool. You sir, live for the wrong reasons. You sir, suffer for all the wrong reasons, and what you have sir, is not pain and suffering, what you have sir, is a problem that can and should be solved, but your large ego and worthless sense of pride will not let you overcome it.
[He steps away from the bum. And begins to walk away once more. This time, the bum chases after him. Walking along side of him he tries to speak to him once more.]
Bum--How do you know what pain and suffering is? You have never lived in a box on the side of the street have you? You have never had to beg strangers that you dont even know for money have you?
[The Wone does not respond.] Bum--You don't know what it is like to be me. If you could be in my shoes for just one day, you would see what it was like. Then you would know how it feels.
[This strikes a nerve in the body of The Wone. He stops in mid stride and turns to the man. He grabs him by the collar and runs him up against the side of a building. He presses the mans body up against the brick. The Bum cringes in fear. The Wone begins to speak with great emotion.]
The Wone--You may not be able to afford that new car that you wanted so badly. You may not be able to pay your house payment for the month, or get those knicks tickets that you so badly wanted. You may not be able to get that woman down the street to notice you. You may not be able to have all these things that you once had. That is not pain, that is not suffering. How would you like to have never known you mother or father? How would you like to have had no one when you so badly needed it. How would you like to not have one single friend.
How would you like to live with two people who when you "misbehaved" locked you in a dark closet for hours with no water food, and no light. How would you like to be beaten with a weight belt just because you stuck your finger in the icing of your adopted brothers birthday cake.
How would you like to live twenty eight years of your life like this. Try it and then you tell me what pain and suffering is. I have travelled everywhere and I have searched long and hard. For one thing to keep my mind of these things that happened in my past. I found one thing, Wrestling. The one thing that can take me away from all of that. The one thing that I can accel in and make a living for myself. I do not like to think about these things that happened when I was younger, and I would advise you not to make me.
[He drops the man.] The Wone--You sir, have many things ahead of you, I suggest you do not put an end to all of them by upsetting me once more. Get on your feet, and get the hell out of my face.
[The bum hops to his feet and runs out of sight. The camera remains in the same place. The Wone begins to walk further down the street. Where he is going is unknown. Where he came from is now evident. As he walks further and further, less can be seen under the light of the dim street lamps, eventually his outline can no longer be seen, and within minutes, the camera and the scene fade away to nothing.]
