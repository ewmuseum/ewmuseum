Observations by [name withheld] First, I have noticed that some special people like including 'real' stuff in their flashes and angles. You know, "On Sunday night, I'll do what Bret did to McMahon to YOU, PUNK!" Or, "So-and-so received a call from Eric Bischoff last night and hung up on him." Now, while this may prove "loyalty" to a federation, does this add anything to the game? No, I do not think so. At the least, people won't care too much. At the most, people will flame you for intruding on their fantasy world. You see, I am a "conservative" who believes that references to "real world" wrestling happenings are no-nos. Maybe a reference to the Louise Woodward trial here or there is fine. But that is not WRESTLING related. If you must make a comment about on-goings in another federation, make it about some e-wrestling federation. Say something to the effect of, "Eric Helmroy contacted me last night and I told him NO, I am NOT interested in joining your backwater federation." I don't know what it is about me, but I HATE real feds being brought into play. I think the "WCW/WWF is courting so-and-so angle" is arrogant. Also, I saw somewhere that someone's finisher was ranked #1 by a "top wrestling magazine." If you are going to say something like that, make it with an internet e-wrestling magazine and MAKE IT TRUE. Sorry to rant on you guys, I just got a teensy bit angry there... Next, what bugs me about some flashes. First thing that bugs me: Some flashes look pulled straight out of the handler's ass. I can't count the number of scrapped/half-done flashes I have on my hard drive. I can't stand a flash that has NOTHING but a guy sitting there, talking for the sake of hopefully winning a match. What I do is get mentally in character. I have to do some ego-pumping, I have to look in the mirror or something and inspire myself. Then, I get to the computer and I'm cocky as heck and ready to take on the world. If I have a bad day, I won't write up a flash because I'd find it hard to feel self-confident, etc like "Playboy" so I can't accurately portray him. You can really tell which flashes the handler got involved in and those are the ones that stand out. Second thing I hate about some flashes: No description or too much thereof. I hate it when the interview just starts with the guy talking and he goes on for ages and ages in a single paragraph. It just doesn't... um... click with me. I need descriptions. But not too many. I don't need you to set the scene up to much. Just a "Abraham Insain is sitting on the cement floor of the basement. He is leaning against some piping. As he pulls at his wrestling garb, he slowly rocks back and forth, tearing swatches out of his clothes," is fine. And then pause every few lines or so to describe what's going on now. Is he biting the pipes? Has he accidentally torn his skin off? Or is he wimpering now? You seem it doesn't have to be one big paragraph. And a secret: The more descriptions, if formatted properly, the less you have to write! A long "Playboy" flash is 6k or so, but if you notice the way I format them, they look as long as the average 10k flash... And the last thing I hate about some flashes: "Fade to black." It's used to end flashes and all I can say about it is: Ugh! BOOOOOOORING! It must be written 120,000 times a week in the e-wrestling world! You could say, knock the camera man down and go all snowy. Or have them just cut out. Or have the guy run up to the camera and stick his face in it making everything dark so they just cut off. There are so many things you can think of, so why not apply a few? OK, so I've ranted and have complained about "real wrestling" getting involved with "e-wrestling" and the peculiarities of flashes. Now I pass on the torch to you, the butt-monkeys -- er... people -- of RSPWF to continue this thread and give your thoughts on the aforementioned subjects. If you don't, I'll break your legs. I KNOW where you live... --
