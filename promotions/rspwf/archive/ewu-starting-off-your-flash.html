Starting off your flash

EW University
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Okay, it`s a bit of an overstatement to say this will make people read your flashes...although it will make them more likely to read your flashes, and when they do, they`ll make sense of it. This is a short little bit of advice, but could help a lot. A lot of the feds I`ve been in have been quite heavy in terms of the amount of flashes and characters. To be honest, it can be difficult to keep up to date with what is happening - and I`m sure it is the same for people reading mine. What I started to do in one fed, and it working to good effect, was to put a couple of lines before each flash, regardless of whether it was a Fight RP or a Character Development RP. What were those couple of lines? They were... "SO FAR: blah blah blah " You see, e-wrestling, like real wrestling, is very much like a soap opera, and just as a soap opera has a flash back as to what has happened before at the beginning of each episode so new viewers or people who have missed a few episodes can make sense of what is happening, so can e-wrestling flashes. An example... SO FAR: Mogbot has been signed up to face Jupiter John this Friday... Another example: SO FAR: Following the savage attack at the hands of the MirrorMen last weekend, the ever-popular Mogbot goes to the commissioner to demand a match... You see? It`s a matter of setting up the flash. Just those couple of lines will really make the world of difference to your flashes and also for everyone else in the fed. Try it and see what happens. Another way is just to change the introductory part of a flash from description to a third person commentary. So instread of writing... [ The scene: a car parking lot, filled with various cars from a mustang to a pink Toyota that is scratched down the side. On the other side of the car park a couple of youths are making out, but they get up and go and walk past the car park attendent. The sky is covered in whispy cloud covering the moon, and the stars shine through. A plane flies overhead... And so on, and so forth. Like, what the hell has any of that to do with wrestling? Just toooo much detail that I almost always skim over. If it isn`t relevant, drop it. I`ve taken to making the introduction section much more informative about the situation, thus using those important first few sentences to bring everyone up to date briefly as to what is happening with my wrestler, so perhaps they`ll actually take more interest in the character, and maybe even the flash. Here`s one I used in the DWF. Not the best example, but an example. [ Gabriel Demorte. The Total Wrestling Machine. Week after week in DWF he has continued to prove that he is a man prepared to be the fly in everyone`s ointment, to rock the boat, to make everyone sit up and take notice. Just ask Dave Pietka and his bodyguard Mark Candress, who just last week felt the brunt of Demorte`s aggression. He`s a tough man alright. And now he`s with freelance wrestling report Toxic Shock...]] So there you go.
