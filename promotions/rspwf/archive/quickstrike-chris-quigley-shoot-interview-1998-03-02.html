"Quickstrike" Chris Quigley "Shoot" Interview...

2 March 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Firstly, I'd like to say that this is a shoot-style interview with the CHARACTER "Quickstrike" Chris Quigley, and is based upon the notion that e-wrestling is "real life". Please don't take this too seriously, it's just a last ditch effort to entertain some people with the character one more time. ;)
------------ [SCENE: A small, sparsely furnished room. There are only two chairs, and a small table in between them. On the table sits two glasses of water.
In each of the chairs sits roving pro wrestling reporter Shawn Peterson, and "Quickstrike" Chris Quigley, respectively. Peterson is wearing a blue sweater and dress pants, while Quigley wears a pair of faded jeans, a #19 Team Canada jersey with Yzerman printed on back, and his silver wire-rimmed shades. Peterson looks up into the camera with a smile on his face.]
SP: Ladies and gentlemen, this is a thrill indeed. As many of you may have seen a few months ago, I conducted an interview with "The Attack"
Zack Malone, which extended behind the boundaries of kayfabe and all those restraining wires. Tonight, I give you an in-depth look at the man known as "Quickstrike" Chris Quigley. Welcome, Chris!
CQ: How's it going, Shawn? SP: Very well, thank you. First, let's get a bit of background info on yourself. Anything you can think of. Your real name, age, where you grew up... that sort of thing.
CQ: [shrugs] Well, my real name _is_ Chris Quigley. I've never been one to put on a mask in front of the camera. What you see is what you get, to an extent. I'm 32 years old, I was involved in wrestling for about 14 years now, and I was born and grew up in Corner Brook, Newfoundland,
Canada. When I was 16, I moved to Pheonix to train with the famous Manning family in their "Living Hell" basement gynmasium. SP: So, how did you get into wrestling? Were you interested in it at an early age or... CQ: [interupting] Yeah, I was always a fan of wrestling. But to be honest, hockey was always my first priority. When I screwed up my knee, my dream of becoming an NHL goaltender was killed, because a hockey scout wouldn't touch me. Funny, they'd be calling me and promising me big things to come, but as soon as I got hurt, they didn't know me.
Fortunatly, I managed to get Steve Manning, Sr. to take me seriously in my secondary goal to become a wrestler.
SP: A _secondary_ goal?! Wow. For someone who came into wrestling as a second choice, you certainly made a go of it. You're unquestionably one of the biggest names to grace our sport, and certainly one of the most talented. Tell me, was there any pressure to be a more "exciting"
wrestler? To have more aerial skills, or have more of an attitude? Obviously your type of wrestler, that being the technical mat wrestling sort, is a dying breed.
CQ: Not really. Steve Sr. always had a keen eye for the individual's talents. I was in amateur wrestling, and so obviously I used my strengths in that area when I wanted to turn pro. But I've always kept an open mind about things. I've done a few moonsaults in my time. [laughs]
SP: So, after training in "The Living Hell" with such names as Zack Malone, Steve Manning, Kurt Manning, Mark Bagwell... the list goes on and on, you got your start in which Federation?
CQ: It was a small indy promotion called the IWACCL. I spent a few years there, I managed to win a tag title, a 6-man title, and several singles titles including the Federation Championship. It was a great experience. After that, a bigger Federation called the WIWF, headed by
[name withheld] signed me on, but unfortunatly, in the WIWF, where I was known as Chris "Q-Man" Quigley, I reached maybe a little above mid-card status. I hated it entirely. I thought I deserved better, but obviously
I was a little too boring to succeed there. SP: As I remember, you left the Federation and went back to a few minor leagues. CQ: Yeah, I got a phonecall from [name withheld], who was known for having a great eye for talent. The man was great at making stars. He wanted me to come work for him, and I jumped at the chance. Less than 6 months later, I was a Heavyweight Champion again. Ever since I won that title, things improved drastically. People began to take me more seriously, and
I attained a huge confidence boost. SP: What does the name [name withheld] mean to you? CQ: [smiles] [name withheld]. Possibly the hardest working man I've ever seen. I swear to God he managed the TAEWF by himself. He signed the matches, handled the budgeting, merchandising, but it became too much for him. I became the World Champion of the TAEWF, which was my first
_major_ title, after I had already held the Lightweight and North American titles in the Federation. Shortly thereafter, he gave me the opportunity to do some backstage work, which I really enjoyed. I also enjoyed the creative control I had during my time there. [name withheld] and I made the Federation work.
SP: There was a little controversy, as I recall, about your power behind the scenes in the TAEWF, but it seemed you always did what was best for the Federation. How about in other Federations, where you didn't have the same power? How easy were you to handle backstage?
CQ: I think I was always fairly easy to handle. I never demanded control over my matches. I basically did what I was told, and left the decisions up to the main men. The one exception maybe in the FWLI, now the IFWF, where it was terrible. I mean, [name withheld] was the head back then, and although house shows for the Federation were frequent, the television deal wasn't working out. We'd get calls 3:00 in the morning telling us to be at the arena at 7 to do some tapings. We'd get there, and the tapings would be called off. During that time, I spoke out publically, refusing to do interviews at house shows until we were guaranteed some TV time in the Federation. [name withheld] got wind of that, and, well... his exact words were, "Chris Quigley will never be pushed as long as I'm in charge." or something to that effect.
SP: That's tough. You were in the doghouse with the main man. What did you do to solve it?
CQ: [smirks] I always had a friendly relationship with Mark Engel, who had the same kind of power in the FWLI as I had in the TAEWF. So when
Engel was cooking an angle with the Universal Powers, I jumped into the angle to take Engel's side. It gave me the chance to win a major tag team title, and also to work with the Powers again, who I consider to be
_very_ talented. [name withheld] has since taken over the reigns of the FWLI/IFWF, and he's doing one helluva job. SP: Two words: Dan Kauffman. CQ: [rolls his eyes] I've never had a better friend. I've never had a worse enemy. Kauffman was the one who called me up, and told me all about the IIWF. I was hesitant at first, but finally I decided to give
Spreadbury a call. Somehow I managed to squeeze my way in, but for some reason, I always felt like an outside of some sort, invading in their world. God knows I was treated like one.
SP: Problems behind the scenes? CQ: Oh hell, it was like I had the plague. Which is just fine with me, I don't like associating my colleagues with my true friends, so I usually don't hang out much with the other wrestlers anyway. But in the IIWF...
I was one of the most popular all the time, fan-wise, but easily one of the most hated in the locker rooms. Daniel Spreadbury once told me that he'd never seen a guy come in and cause so much legitimate heat. What can I say? I was flattered.
SP: Do you think you deserved the IIWF World Heavyweight Title? CQ: Man... I don't know. Yeah. Yeah, I guess I do. Everytime a match was signed, I was shocked to find out that it wasn't my turn yet. But I really, really wanted to taste the gold. So much in fact, that I dedicated my entire career to it. After I lost to Marty Warnett at
Midsummer Madness, my IIWF career was at a crossroads. I was damn near ready to pack it in, but I was offered a contract extention, with one catch, it was _exclusive_. That meant no more of my time could be spent in other Federations, no matter how small they might've been. I agreed to it. I figured that meant big things were coming. In all fairness, a week later I got the Intercontinental Title. But that's as far as it went.
SP: The heel turn. Did you want to turn heel? CQ: [laughs] Not really. I had always played the shadowy face kinda well. I could act like a heel at times in matches, no problem. But I did like the crowd support, especially because there's such a lack of good babyfaces nowadays. I don't think I was really _pushed_ into turning heel, but it seems as if they set me up in a few situations which made it almost inevitable. Steve Manning Jr. getting signed made it a little easier though. At least I was going into this with a guy who knows how to make the fans spit bullets.
SP: Obviously, this whole situation with Joe Petrow has caused a lot of controversy in our sport. Could you take us back, bit by bit?
CQ: [sighs] Well... Joe and I have always had a bit of heat between us, I don't really know why. When he lost his World Title match, I forget which PPV it was... he bailed on the IIWF. He felt he had nowhere else to go, so he quit. Which is ironic, since that's what made me leave.
But basically, Petrow was talking down on the IIWF during his absence. Anyway, a match was signed for me to take on Steve Kowalski, a guy I love working with. We can both put on a great show. So I was excited about that, when I get this call from Jim...
SP: [interupting] Jim Jividen, then-IIWF Vice President. CQ: Yeah. Anyway, he tells me that I won't be facing Kowalski, because there'll be an attack on him. Instead, he tells me that I'll face a jobber, and I'll lose. He told me it'd be a massive screwjob and I'd get the chance to redeem myself the next week. He didn't tell me _which_ jobber, and he didn't tell me what would exactly happen the next week.
So I agree to it, hell, for the sake of an angle, he didn't tell me _which_ angle, but he said it'll all make sense to me the week after. So, I went along with it, of course. Manning hit me with this portrait or something, and the jobber I was facing, "Majestic" Maurice McArthur, won the title, or so everyone thought.
SP: Were you suspicious at all when you found out McArthur would be your opponent? CQ: I didn't find out until he was walking down the aisle. They told me how the match was going to go, but didn't tell me who my opponent would be. At the time, I thought nothing of it. If I had my choice of a jobber to put over, it'd be Casey C. The kid has talent...
SP: Okay, so then, the next week, you're guaranteed to annhiliate McArthur. Steve Manning was put in your corner, and another jobber, or so you thought, El Super Gecko was to be put in McArthur's.
CQ: Yeah, for starters, it was in the middle of an angle where myself and Manning weren't friends anymore, so it made no sense for him to be in my corner. But I played along anyway, having Manning come out halfway through the match, not coming out _with_ me. So then, after I beat
McArthur, El Super Gecko hits the ring, and grabs me and executes a move. I'm wondering what the _hell_ he's doing, but I've _gotta_ sell.
I'm no cartoon wrestler. I get back up in due time, and get ready to really _stiff_ this guy for trying to get himself over, and I figured he'd get in trouble for it with the IIWF brass as well. All of a sudden, the mask comes off, and Joe Petrow is standing there. You'll notice that on camera, this is the only time "Quickstrike" Chris Quigley showed any amount of shock on his face, and that's because I _was_ shocked. I had no idea he'd be coming back, and I had _no_ idea what I was supposed to do. Was I supposed to run? Get beat up? The ref signalled for me and
Manning to high tail it outta there, but I wasn't about to do that. So I grappled with Petrow instead, until he grabbed a Christmas Tree that
Steve Manning had brought to the ring, which really kinda ticked him off, because that was supposed to be his little... "thing" to put him over as a wild, hardcore wrestler. So we just walked away then.
SP: So you and Petrow sign a match for Snow Brawl, a match which you later find out is a "Submission Match".
CQ: Yeah, I signed the match originally, not knowing who would be asked to job to who. Then when I found out it was a submission match, I figured right then and there that I had the match won. When I found out that wasn't the case, my first instinct was to argue, but instead, I agreed. Although it was at the very moment they had asked me to do what they asked me to do, that I decided my time in the IIWF was winding down. It kinda shocked me. You have Petrow, a guy who has _phenomenal_ talent, don't get me wrong, but the fact is, he bailed on the IIWF when the chips were down, and did nothing but badmouth it while he was gone.
Meanwhile, no matter what the IIWF had done to me or my character, I had kept plugging away, trying to turn bad situations into good ones. Truth is, I'd gotten a lot of warnings to leave the IIWF long before I did, claims that they'd ruin my career, but I never believed it. I still don't think they ruined my career, but they definitely put a damper on it, and I don't think they left me much choice but to leave. The
Prodigal Son was pushed over Ol' Reliable on that night, and I left the arena and did not make contact with any IIWF officials until the next
IIWF Saturday Night when I showed up at the arena 2 minutes before the second hour was going to air. I quickly asked for permission to go out there for a moment, and after assuring them I wouldn't shoot, they had enough trust in me to cancel a match and let me go out and speak my mind. Of course, they never imagined that it'd be my farewell speech.
They were hoping I'd whine, I'd curse, I'd attack Steve Roberts again, do something to grab a few ratings and restore my character a little.
Nah... why bother? It'd only be knocked down again at the next pay per view. SP: It sounds to me like your a little bitter towards the IIWF... CQ: The IIWF for me were the best years, creatively, of my career. Yet as far as success goes, it were among my worst. Hell, when you look at the guys I put over... it's a talented list, really it is, but it's a long list. You've got Warnett, Lebec, James, Petrow, Macbeth... the only match they gave me was the Kauffman match, and that's because they were killing him off at the time. But, y'know, what can you do? As far as professionalism, productivity, imagination... the IIWF is the greatest
Federation I've ever seen. Nothing will change my mind about that. SP: What about the well-known backstage power struggles in the IIWF? CQ: Honestly, I have _no_ idea what went on there. Like I said, I paid very little attention to the backstage politics of it all. Maybe that was my problem. Maybe I should have. Given myself some leverage or something, but I don't work that way. I heard nothing of the IIWF
"politics" until I was leaving the Federation, and only then did Spreadbury clue me into what kind of stuff was going on that I never knew about, and even then it was a vague mention, not really a description, which is fine by me. I heard stories of an IIWF "Clique" as well, but I really don't know much about that either. If there was one, I wasn't in it.
SP: So, a lot of people are dying to know... is the retirement final? CQ: [smiles] Oh... I dunno. You don't wanna say, "Yeah, this is it." Because two days from now, I might be back in the ring. But right now, my motivation, my desire to wrestle is dead. It was fitting for me to lose that match by yelling, "I quit." Because that's what I was doing.
My heart left me, and I didn't give a damn anymore. SP: But that wasn't your final match... CQ: No, I had signed a contract long before I knew I was going to be hanging the boots up, for a 60 Minute Marathon Match in the AEWF, against their World Champion Joe Mama. I figured I'd be losing this match, but I
did it to help promote their AEWF vs. The World PPV. Then, the thing with Petrow happened, and all of a sudden, this Marathon Match was my final match. Kinda fitting, since it was always my style of contest, and
I think a lot of people were surprised to see me come out of that on top, and at the end, I dispatched of the AEWF's top heel stable. It was a helluva send-off, one that I can hardly express to Karol Zielinski how much I appreciated it.
SP: Alright, what was your greatest match? CQ: Geez... it's hard to pick just one. I think Kauffman and I had an amazing match at Ring Wars III. It didn't have any of that hardcore bullshit, just a great wrestling match. The sport misses those, the IIWF in particular. I think the Best Two out of Three Falls Match I had with
"Dreamlover" Trey Porter was another classic. I've still got a few burn marks on my shoulder from that match. And I've always liked the match I had on IIWF Saturday Night against Deathbringer. The drama in that match was unreal.
SP: Indeed. Getting back to your lay-off, was it at all gratifying to see Zack Malone return to wrestling just this past week and defeat a world class competitior like Derek Lankford? Malone was away from wrestling for almost a year, it was impressive to say the least.
CQ: Yeah, well, it lets you know that people don't forget. It also showed that ring rust isn't inevitable, Malone seemed to avoid it pretty well. I think I, like a lot of people, watched that match to see if Zack
Malone still had it, because he was always a _damn_ fine talent. I don't think he lost a step. It was great to see, Malone has always been a nice guy, believe it or not.
SP: Hypothetically, Chris Quigley decides to return to the ring. Do you return to the IIWF?
CQ: Y'see, this is the kinda question I just find hard to answer. The way I see things right now, I'm not returning to wrestling. I've got no interest in returning. I can't really say that if I came back, where I'd go, because it all depends on the situation that I'd return to. Right now, I can pretty clearly state that I'll never set foot in an IIWF ring again, but like I said, that's nowhere _near_ a promise, because things could change tomorrow.
SP: Have you been enjoying Steve Manning's success since you've left the Federation? CQ: I've seen only a couple of the shows since I left, honestly, although I did get a kick out of Manning's whole "Conspiracy Theory" with my being caught up in the ropes when I said "I quit." I never even thought of that, and I guess no one else did either. I'm pretty sure that was a semi-shoot from Steve, but hell, nearly all his interviews are.
SP: Really? What is Steve Manning like, away from the ring? What's with the whole in the wheelchair thing?
CQ: I'll just say, Steve Manning is one helluva actor. He had drug problems, alcohol problems, he still smokes like a chimney, and then goes out into the ring and works his ass off. The wheelchair angle was his idea, and was partially a work. I say partially because Steve _was_ injured by that chairshot from Kurt. It was an accident, and Kurt retired because of it. Steve had some major neck damage and couldn't move his legs, but a few weeks later he got the use of his legs back, but he waited until Kurt's birthday, two months later, to get up out of the chair and hand him his present. I think that was a damn cool thing to do, and it was the first time I've ever seen the tough as nails Manning family have a little group hug. [laughs]
SP: So then Steve Manning had the idea to enter the IIWF in a wheelchair and surprise you and everyone else with his recovery?
CQ: Yeah, it was a nice piece of work. Not many people saw it coming, I don't think. SP: I wanna wrap this up with some word association, since everyone loves this kinda stuff. Firstly... Joe Petrow.
CQ: Good talent. Bad attitude. SP: Daniel Spreadbury. CQ: Usually fair. SP: Jim Jividen. CQ: Great motivational speaker. SP: Matt Strake. CQ: Undeserved bad reputation. SP: Brody Thunder. CQ: In his finest year... I beat him twice. [laughs] Just kidding. I think he may have more talent than even Hardin did.
SP: How about Simon Lebec? CQ: Didn't like him at first, but when I got to know him, the guy is exceptionally funny and talented.
SP: Trey Porter? CQ: Legendary. SP: "Playboy" Ronnie D. CQ: [smiles] Who? SP: Mark Engel. CQ: Carried a Federation on his shoulders. Not an easy task. SP: And finally, Dan Kauffman. CQ: Class act, all the way. SP: Chris, this has been a great experience. I know I, as well as many others, sincerely hope you do decide to return to the squared circle at sometime, but whatever you decide, I wish you the best of luck.
CQ: Thanks, and another thanks to everyone mentioned during this interview, as you've impacted me in one way or another during my career, that's for sure.
SP: Thanks again. [The camera pans away as Quigley and Peterson shake hands, and continue talking. Fade.]
