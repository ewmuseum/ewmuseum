The E-Wrestling Hall of Fame: 1997 Inductees

12 February 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After several months of taking nominations and building a committee, the the e-wrestling Hall of Fame has its latest set of inductees, conviently listed below. Incidently, the Hall of Fame now has a semi-permanent web page: http://www.contrib.andrew.cmu.edu/usr/[detail withheld]/BioWeb/HOF/ The committee consisted of myself, [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], and [name withheld]. (Thanks guys!) Each committee member cast up to twenty votes on the fourty candidates. Every nominee received at least one of the nine votes, but only a select few were named on the majority of the ballots. E-WRESTLING HALL OF FAME INDUCTEES Players [name withheld]: the creator of old-timers Baron Von Klaw, Johann "Great Dane" Svenson, "Mathie Man" Riemann Savage and others, as well as relatively recent characters: VCW's Ugly Jim Study and Johnny Dominant. [name withheld]: the creator of Club Anarchy and others. [name withheld] wrote: "When he sent in stuff for Club Anarchy, it was always worth a read. He had some great and very surreal interviews with them, filled with assorted sybolism what would flip out your high school english teacher. Rick and I used to look forward to him send it stuff because it usually blew everything else out of the water." [name withheld]: the creator of manager Sly Foxx and the tag team Animal Instinct. [name withheld] wrote: "Sly Foxx is one of the greatest heel managers in e-wrestling history and his handling of Animal Instinct and his later characters has always been impressive. Revolutionized extreme angles, and was my "mentor" when I first started." [name withheld]: creator of Wild Hazard (inducted in June), the Frisco Kid and assorted others. [name withheld] wrote: "Matt is consistently one of the most amusing, literate writers out there, bringing in references and a sense of irreverence to everything he does." [name withheld] wrote: "Matt creates some of the most whacked out characters I've seen [and to me that's a good thing], and always remembers that this is supposed to be fun." [name withheld]: creator of Steve the Insane, Intensive Care, and others. [name withheld] wrote: "A lot of people can create characters of a specific type and roleplay them well. Unlike most people, Steve's characters range from the crafty veteran to the diabolical manager to, well... the Insane One, and all done well... Steve can do both comedy and drama incredibly well." Characters Baron Von Klaw ([name withheld]): Von Klaw was a goose-stepping Nazi in the UFWS and UeWF in 1991-2. [name withheld] wrote: "No one (except maybe Club Anarchy) was ever more fun to have in a fed." [name withheld] wrote: "Nazis are a staple stereotype of wrestling. Many, if not most, feds have had such characters at one time or another, but no-one has ever done one better than Scott's. Those of us who have faced it still 'fear ze power uv Ze Klaw!!!'." Barry Bromowitz ([name withheld]): Barry got off to a slow start, but became one of the top heels when he joined the EWF. At the 1994 TWiW Awards he was named "Best Heel" and "Mr. Congeniality". [name withheld] wrote: "Probably my favorite character in all of e-wrestling. Ray's character bits and plotting with this character have always been first-rate. Arrogance, originality, and humor blend to make 'the People's Champion' an unforgettable character." Derek Machismo ([name withheld]): a successful wrestler in ESW and the USeW (including a world championship). [name withheld] wrote: "...Derek has proven to be a force in all federations he's joined." [name withheld] wrote: "More than anyone else I can think of, he's represented the evolution of a character and gimmick." "Fabulous" Eddie Awesome ([name withheld]): had a long and storied career in the UeWF 1991-2. [name withheld] wrote: "...he was in probably my all time favorite angle [the "Captain Marvel" set up of King Crimson]." Fender Marshall ([name withheld]): the manager of Hall of Famers The Headbangers and others. Only the second manager to be inducted into the Hall. [name withheld] wrote: "One of e-wrestling's top managers. The Marshall/Diamond feud was a staple of e-wrestling for longer than some of the feds it took place in, but still managed to stay fresh, interesting and exciting." Grim Weibaq ([name withheld]): held the EWC World Title, feuded with Hall of Famer Brewster Cogburn and held the RRtR interfed title for quite a while. A huge star as a face in EWC with a few guest appearances elsewhere. "Irish Whip" Rick O'Malley ([name withheld]): five time ESW World Champion. [name withheld] wrote: "From what I'd seen of ESW, Rick was one of the quintessential faces. He fought fair, but wasn't boring either." Powerhouse Pruitt ([name withheld]): wrestled in the UFWS, UeWF (where he held the world title twice) and the KWF. Managed by the Hall of Famer Ace Diamond, and married to commentator Misty Raddison. Roker Showtime ([name withheld]): one of the most successful wrestlers of the EWC-EWF era (1994-6) and still going strong. Held both the EWC and EWF World Titles and also the EWF Lightheavyweight Title. [name withheld] called him "the best face left in e-wrestling today." Club Anarchy ([name withheld]): a very entertaining heel team, Club Anarchy won the UeWF Tag Team Titles and fared well in the UFWS as well, winning the titles at least once. Captain Necropolis and Dr. Lethe were known far and wide for their entertaining flashes. Tropical Storm ([name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld]): three of the top face lightheavyweights in ESW (Harry "Head Hunter" Harris, "California Kid" Key Atkinson and L.T. Hurricane) joined to form a rotating tag team. They completely dominated the ESW tag scene, winning the titles and never looking back. [name withheld] wrote: "Brought the idea of a rotating tag-team to the forefront of e-wrestling, and all three were great. I remember they were considered the best at the time." Satan's Little Helpers ([name withheld]): the pre-eminent ladies' tag team, and the only candidate to be named on all nine ballots. [name withheld] wrote: "The definitive ladies tag team. You knew you had arrived when you got in a feud with SLH. We gotta let them in or bad things will happen. Bad things, man." Fedheads [name withheld]: ran the WeWF (the second e-fed on the net) in 1990-2, which although it was not the best fed ever, inspired many others. Returned to fedheading in 1993 with ESW, home to several of the Hall of Famers listed above. [name withheld]: ran the EWF (with [name withheld]) as it rose to prominence in e-wrestling, 1994-present. Contributors [name withheld]: creator of TNM, and an easy choice for the Hall. TNM has become the most popular simulator and has been used by literally hundreds of leagues. A number of candidates were seconded but fell short of the necessary votes: Players: [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld] Characters: 600 Pound Gorilla, "Badboy" Mark Bagwell, "Crimson" Joe Reed, "Desirable" Danny Dynamite, "Dreamlover" Trey Porter, "Executioner" Mark Engel, Generation X, "Hot Stuff" [name withheld], "Iceman" Brian Knight, The Jammer, Flint and Steel, The Iconoclasts, The Original Shadow Assassins, The Savages Fedheads: [name withheld], [name withheld] Contributors: (None) There were also a few who were nominated but not seconded, thus not on the ballot: Players: [name withheld] Characters: Kuwasi "Blackfist" Shakur, Doom and Disaster (EWC/USeW) Fedheads: (None) Contributors: (None) The next vote will probably be in May or so, to get the stars of the period right before the r.s.p-w.f boom in e-wrestling (and remaining old-time greats, etc.). See you then!
