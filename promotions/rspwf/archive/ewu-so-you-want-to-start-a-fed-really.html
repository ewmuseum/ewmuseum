So you want to start a fed? Really?

EW University
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
STARTING AN E-FED If I was starting again, this is how I would develop and run my fed. This is from over 5 years e-wrestling experience. I`m not saying this is for the perfect fed, but this is how I would run my fed if I was starting again. It is not the only way, but I have found it to be very enjoyable and manageable. BEFORE BEFORE YOU START Read this guide. BEFORE YOU START Before you even start a fed, ask yourself, just how serious am I about starting a fed? Have I got the time, the internet access time, the wrestling knowledge and administration skills, plus the ability to write matches, to start up a fed? Am I wanting to start an e-fed so other people can have fun, or so I can have a bit of power? Also, you MUST have a good grasp of the language you are writing in (presumably English). Bad spelling & grammar will drive people away. Good spelling and grammar, especially written in a mature manner, will pull people in. Do you have the time? I`d say, to run a decent fed, you need to put in at least ten to fifteen hours a week, plus the time it takes to write cards and everything else. Running a fed is a LOT of hard work. It may seem like a lot of fun (it is), but like keeping a dog or a rabbit or any pet (except, possibly, a cat) it needs care, time and attention. Do you have a life? If you don`t have a life, running an e-fed will fill the vacuum very nicely thank you very much. If you do have a life, prepare for a lot of conflict. WHAT NEXT? Write a card. Just do it. Write a card. Make up your own wrestlers or perhaps use real ones and write a card. Show to yourself that you can do it. Read it. See if you liked it. If you did, good. Why not post it on the net? If you used made-up characters, it could be the start of your fed. Post regular cards and the people will swarm to your fed. If there is anything players like, it is regular, consistent cards. They go a long way to make a fed good. Slowly but surely the players will replace your own creations. They will become jobbers, and that will be that. While your fed is still quite empty, feud with the players currently in. Make it interesting and exciting for them to be there. Develop strong story lines and angles, help your players develop strong storylines and angles. If you do this, they will be interested and stay with your fed. There are many feds out there run by very talented people, but the cards just come out so infrequently and so unreliably. IMPORTANT - Don`t expect great things straight away. IT WILL TAKE TIME to develop a good fed. Start small, grow big. But I`m getting ahead of myself... PLAYERS Okay, this is just my opinion, but I think up to 32singles wrestlers is more than sufficient. At that number, it`s not too big and it`s not too small. It won`t be too confusing either. It is a managable number. Also, if you are doing 2 cards a week, you should, theoretically be able to managable, readable cards that everyone will be able to get the swing of. In a roster of 32, if 60% are active, you`re doing well. Feds actually need players who aren`t that good, who aren`t that active - it makes the decision making for a fed-head that much easier! I would, personally, no longer bother with tag-teams. Tag-teams basically a different fed altogether. You`re better off putting all your efforts into the singles division. You can still have tag-matches, but with singles teamed up. There would be, theoretically, 8 singles matches per card. I don`t know why, but 8 seems to be about the usual number for matches per card. Not too strenuous I suppose, and it would ensure everyone active gets a match or two each week or two (and the more involved a player is, the more committed he will be to your fed). Hopefully, what will be created is a fed that will keep the majority of players interested. Also, another thing I recommend is allowing only 1 singles wrestler per player. Although at first, allowing them to have two or three would fill up the numbers, the truth is, there is no hurry, and good things will come to those who wait. By allowing one player per wrestler, you`ll get better character development. Furthermore, there is less chance of one of the characters being neglected. I know many handlers who control two or more wrestlers in the same fed, and they do it well, but two other reasons have to be considered (1) The more wrestlers a player has, the more power they have over other wrestlers, using the two as one unit. To be fair to all players, they should all have the same number of wrestlers (2) The more players you have in the fed, the more inter-personal dynamics will play a role. Think about it. Would you prefer a 36 man fed with 18 players or 36? The more players you have, the more chance for different approaches and angles and storylines. Furthermore, don`t be afraid to suggest alterations to players characters when they apply. If you`ve got too many brawlers or tech`s or whatever, or too many giants, get them to change it. A good roleplayer can roleplay with anything. Furthermore, try and get a nice balance of heels and faces, but if necessary, have more heels than faces, becuase heels can feud with each other. Heels also tend to be more interesting. However, it is VITAL to have a well-pushed, major face character - a figurehead for the fed. METHOD OF MATCH RESOLUTION - HJS or SIM I`m exceptionally biased. Although I see the benefits of simmed feds, I prefer HJS because of it`s flexibility. Besides, TNM etc. are probably responsible for half the feds that are formed and flop anyway. Kid comes along, plays TNM or Zeus, thinks WOW! and then thinks again WOW, I can do this with other people. Only to find they haven`t got the time. And people aren`t interested. HJS - Human Judgement System, often refereed to sometimes as "Booking", uses the best computer processor in the world - the human brain. As long as you can trust yourself not to be biased, HJS, where you fairly judge the outcome depending on stats, strats, angles, heat, involvement etc, HJS remains the most flexible system, allowing any type of match, and allowing for absolutely anything to happen. It can include any factor and is quick to programme. And it can make use of something a computer does not have - imagination. Imagination is often the distinuishing thing between HJS and SIM feds. And it is a bloody big difference if you ask me. However, this may seem hypocritical, but sims can be great for creating consistent fighting styles for characters, but that is something I`ll discuss later. FLASH SYSTEM Until late `98 you had a choice of two. There is the Closed Flash System, used very successfully by very successful federations such as the EMWC. Alternativelyvthere was the Open Flash System, used by exciting, fun federations such as ARMAGEDDON. There is also the Message Board System, which I shudder to think about! CFS is great for that 'professional' feel. Basically, everyone sends their flashes/interviews into one person who edits them together. Sometimes commentary is added to give the finished article a "magazine" kind of feel, but more often than not, the flashes are simply edited together. It`s good if you don`t like lots of flashes pouring in to your mail box, but I`ve often found it limiting in the development of feuds. Much more heat can be generated through... OFS - Open Flash System. Basically, everyone has the distribution list and they individually send out their flashes. It`s the system I`m most used to, and gives the fed a 24-hr feel, with stuff happening without the fedhead having to be involved. Sometimes it is difficult to keep up with things and sometimes there are just too many flashes, but generally, this way is the most fun and from what I can tell, develops stronger bonds and familiarity between players. Feds are often more active this way. And that is why I recommend it. But you can have both thanks to www.egroups.com who will produce a mailing list for you that people can subscribe and unsubscribe to by themselves, will send messages out individually or in packets or even read it purely on the web like a message-board, depending on the players preference, as well as storing every single message and giving you, the fedhead, an amazing amount of control. There is no reason to not use it. It is simply the best thing out there for e-wrestling. CARDS Consistency and punctuality are the important things. Keep on getting those cards out on time. And if you post them on the newsgroup regularly, and the card is decent, you could have no better advert for your fed. In a way, RSPWF is a fed for feds. The more you post, the more familiar you`ll become, and with a bit of quality attatched, you`ll become an attractive prospect. I believe two cards a week is a good number. You`re not overdosing, and there is not too long a gap for things to go quiet. Perhaps when you start, one card a week is all that is necessary, but two is good and necessary when you have the right amount of people. A weekend card and a midweek card are recommended. My days of choice would be Tuesday (allowing everyone time over the weekend to send in strats for Sunday evening, write the card over late Sunday/Monday/Early Tues) and Friday (gives people the week to build up to, and gives them the weekend to react) Try and get everyone a match on a card during a two-week period at most. Give more time and attention to matches that have greater significance or importance (grudge matches, titles matches). If people are roleplaying extensively, repay them with a full, well written match. If people are not-roleplaying, don`t waste your time writing a long match for them. It`s a reciprocal process. Equally, if a person is doing well, reflect that in the wrestler`s performance, and again, if they are not participating, the wrestler should perform badly. Advertise the card well in advance, preferably a week (so you give them enough time to send it stats, strats , angles and interviews) for heat to build up between the players and for great anticipation to set in. Perhaps a little background of the match and what the consequences of the match may be for that little extra touch of class and interest for players not involved. Now, what should the cards consist of? It really is up to you. The way I see it, there are six different kind of match write-ups, from the quickest to the longest. 1) SIMPLE RESULT (i.e. Jon beat Bob with a powerbomb) 2) BRIEF MATCH SUMMARY (a couple of lines, up to 250wds saying what happened in the match, like a brief football match report, talking about performances, key moments etc) 3) BMS WITH COMMENTARY (As above, but stop the summary a couple of minutes before the finale and cut to the commentary) 4) BMS/COMMENTARY EXTRA (As above, but instead of commentating at the end, go back and forth between summary and commentary) 5) LIVE - Full commentary, from before the first introduction to beyond the final bell 6) Full match summary - Deep match summary going into the crowd reactions, highpoints etc. (about 500-1000wds) Now, my opinion on this matter is that there is too much pressure to make cards PBP, and about 10000wds long. People scan these cards rather than read them, and all that effort is wasted. I found that a card consisting of Brief or Full Match Summaries worked perfectly. You could describe a match without being repetitive; hype up the crowd reactions; fit in easily any strats or requests and most importantly - it`s easier to read for everyone else in the fed. You`re writing for everyone then. Think about it this way - would you rather read in the paper each morning a blow by blow account of a football match, or a report that summarised it, capturing the important moments? One of the great flaws of e-wrestling is that most people try to present it in a style of a TV-show - the flaw being that e-wrestling is a text-based medium. We should make the most of it`s strengths. Report style cards are easier to write - one writer could easily handle it. If you had a hand, the cards could be done easily - resulting in REGULAR CARDS - the golden goal! Furthermore, by having report style cards, when it comes round to having a SUPERCARD, you can have it in full play by play style, and it will appear to be something special. I had a supercard once every two months, and it really did seem special and 'super' having the PBP. As well as two cards a week, if you can manage it, a weekly or bi-weekly magazine show, with exlcusive interviews, editorials, special articles and a summary of the happenings in the fed, would be a superb touch to the fed. You could also use it to manipulate or encourage feuds, angles and stories. The other thing I would finally recommend with relation to cards is to treat them like chapters of a book, and each book being the build up to a two or three month PPV. So if your PPV is called 'Wrestlewarriors: World Destroyers', that book would be called World Destroyers, with the PPV being the finale, then the next chapter begins. Each card would be a chapter of that book, and I would number them, along with the number of the book. So if you are building up to your first PPV, your cards would be numbered 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4 and so on. If you were building up to your fourth PPV, they would be 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 and so on. Having this numbering system helps the planning out of angles and the time-scale of events. CO-WRITERS Co-writers are sometimes essential to have, and with them, you can have more time to add the extra stuff to your fed, like a magazine show or a web-page. As long as you can edit their work together and the writers are reliable, you are sorted. At the end of the day, you can only rely on yourself however, and the responsibility does like with you. As for co-fedheads, they do help to reduce the burden, but it is confusing for players who don`t know who to send stuff to. It is better to have one central figure who can distribute the necessary stuff to different writers, and who will make standard judgements on all that is going on. By all means have fed-assistants, they will only help to make the fed a better place, but do not expect or ask too much from them - let them be players as much as they can. ADVERTISING YOUR FED Regular cards are the best form of advertising. But if you are looking for more players through more direct advertising, be humble but positive. Over-hyping it is only going to lead to a big fall. Don`t say "This is going to be the best fed ever, join now before it`s too late." Perhaps go for a "I`m looking for players interested in a booked fed that is continually trying to improve." Also, include a statsheet and FAQ with your player request. As a player, I`ve always found that people who say "I`m starting up a fed, write to me for details" rarely get around to doing the fed, whereas people who write a lot about their fed and why you should join it etc. tend to mean business. Never advertise until you are prepared. It is very unpro. WEB-PAGES Free web-pages at www.tripod.com and www.geocities.com and www.xoom.com amongst other. Things to include - Fed logo, rankings, In-depth character sheets, Previous cards & magazine shows, Up-coming matches, News on up-coming PPV`s, Latest news. Things not to include - Lots of large images. Rarely worth it. SUPERCARDS Don`t have too many. One every two to three months should be about right. Start hyping them up about two months in advance. I recommend doing full live commentary to make it really special. CHARACTER SHEETS For a booked fed, as much qualitative description as possible. Obvious things like height and weight, hometown and manager. But stuff like personality, and strengths and weaknesses can really help distinguish a character. The more history a character has, the more real and developed they seem, and they will have more to work from. FEDERATION NAME Something catchy. Don`t necessarily use acronyms. Use the first word perhaps i.e. If your federation is called FIRE WRESTLING FEDERATION, refer to your fed as FIRE. Brand-name is vital - make sure it is distinct and stands out and reflects you fed. Avoid use of the term 'Extreme' or 'Hardcore'. TITLES I would recommend one title per 8 to 10 wrestlers. ANGLES Try to include at least 90% of peoples strats. If you can`t do it, tell them why. If necessary, modify them to fit them. If angles aren`t happening, come up with ideas and suggest them to people, and if necessary, move people into angles by arranging the matches. People like to be in angles and feuds, they just need help. THE BOTTOM LINE Be fair, be in control, be decisive, be patient, be prepared. Emphasise the story, emphasise the character - those are the things that people will be interested in reading. Have fun.
