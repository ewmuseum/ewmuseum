FLASH STRUCTURING by [name withheld] Sometimes it's hard to come up with something to write or even to find inspiration to. Sometimes you suffer writers block. Sometimes you just can't find the right words to put on the screen. There IS a sure-fire method to make sure you can always write a flash. It may not make the most amazing flash, but it'll be guarenteed to get the job done, and be efficient with it. The secret really is in the structure. Each story has a beginning, middle and an end, and there is no reason why a flash has to be much different in principle. And it is a very simple principle. It is a three step process - past, present, future. So what am I talking about? I'm talking about a simple four paragraph flash (and remember, make sure the paragraphs aren't longer than 8lines otherwise people begin skimming what you write) The structure of the flash would be so. [ Intro description sequence. Don't go overboard with this. Remember, you don't need to be in a flashy location - just a simple ' In front of the company logo' would do ] First paragraph of speech. This would talk about 'the past' - i.e. the match just fought at the last card, or the history of you and your opponet(s). [ Next bit of description, a small bit of characterization like a smile, grin, laugh, snarl, drinking - whatever ] Speech block two talks about, yes, you guessed it, the present, which really deals with what you think about the whole situation, and basically your characters reaction. [ Maybe another piece of descriptive text. Not more than a sentence or two if possible ] Finally, by process of elimination, you'll realise this part is talking about 'The future' - which means upcoming match or matches or matches you'd like and exactly what you'd like to do etc. [ Another short bit of descriptive text maybe, if you're feeling extravagant ] And then a short finishing line or two. So let me put this into practice. Let's say I'm a character called John Rose, and at the last card I've lost to Sid Ash. Here's how it could go... [ John Rose stands in front of the FED logo, pacing back and forth, rubbing his hands in clear frustration at what has just happened at WEEKLYCARD. Wearing his trademark purple bathrobe and TopHat, he addresses the audience with his texan slur ] Anyone who was watching Weeklycard last week would have seen exactly what happened. They would have seen Sid Ash pull out that bag of cigarette ash and throw it in my eyes, blinding me, then chokeslam like I've never been chokeslammed before. The ref was as blind as I was and didn't see a thing, but the fans knew, the fans were chanting me on, and I came back fighting, for them, because I didn't want to let them down. Then once again, Ash got help from his buddy, Ethan Rack. Everyone knows what happened next. [ He takes off his hat, revealing the bandages on his head covering up the wounds ] Ethan, before last night you were somebody I considered a friend and I don't know what it was that happened that made you betray me. Were you paid off by Ash or did I do something to upset you? Perhaps you were just getting narked that the fans like me more than you. Either way, you've been avoiding me. Either way, you're going to pay. But this Friday, at WeeklyCard, I;ve got my rematch with Sid Ash, and there is no way, no how that you're going to get the edge over me, because I know your game now, I'm ready for you, and there is nothing you can do to overcome me and all my little Rosettes out there. You know I had you beat last time, which is why you threw that Ash in my face, and this time, I will beat you, for myself, and for the fans. [ He puts his hat back on and gives a serious stare ] And after that, I'm coming for you Ethan. [Fade] See? It's not the longest flash in the world, but it is direct, to the point, gets the message across and is easy for the casual reader to read and get up to speed with what is happening. There are plenty of other ways to apply this formula. The one I have just shown you is a very basic one. However, having the structure in mind should always make writing those flashes for your fights that whole lot easier. You could expand any of the paragraphs, add more paragraphs, have other characters involved such as an interviewer or valet, you can change the location, but do try and keep to the flow of going from talking about the past to the present to the future. Hope this helps! Please note: As I am sure you are well aware, there are many many ways to write flashes. This is just one of them, but it is one that will work for you.
