(RP, Please critique) William Craven.

11 July 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
On a whim I've decided to post 3 William Craven RP's. This is one that i've been told is one of my best. It all circulates around that_one_magic_number. . .
---- The screen is a pure, dark black. A long pause, and then a musical humming is heard. It isn't long before a soloist is heard singing. . .
"Aaaa-maaa-zing Grace, how sweee-eeet the sound. . ." A church choir pipes in with "Can you hear it?" The soloist again, "that saaaaved. . .a wretch. . .liii-iiike me!" Again the choir with a simple "Oooo-oooo-oooo." "Iiiii. . .once waaas lost. . .but noooow aaam found. . .waaaas. . .bliiind. . .buuuutt. . .noooooowww. . .Iiiiiiiiii. . .sssseeeee. . ." Slowing and deepening, the music slowly terminates with a sick grinding sound like an old vilyl record being forcibly ground to a halt. In stark white this word appears in large letters on the screen. . .
. . .Beast. . . The one ominous word lingers for a full ten seconds as the grinding noise continues. . . When the sound ends, so does the darkness. Fade in on the interior of a gothic church. Seemingly empty, this church emanates a certain energy, a feel. The walls a mixture of stained glass and loose stonework, the building itself seems to be hundreds of years old. The church pews are well polished and stained red. Carved designs give them an antiquated look as well. A raised platform of stone with an organ, podium, and bleachers for a choir occupies the wall opposite the camera. High ceilings and marble floors give the correct impression to all that view this place. It is a rich church with an afluent parish, making the words suddenly spoken by someone offscreen all the more chilling. . .
"Six. . .six. . .six. . ." Invisible, impalpable, yet audible. The voice itself is gravelly, grinding like the end of the hymn, and is accompanied by the sound of heavy boots *thump thump thump*ing on the marble floor.
"Six. . .six. . .six. . ." From stage right steps out William Craven, dressed in his Sunday best. A bright white longsleeve pinstripe button up shirt and black slacks seem to highlight the heavy combat boots on his feet and the bright pink scars that crisscross his face. To complete the ensemble is a black suit jacket, hung over one arm and not worn. He appears solemn for a moment before his mask of religion breaks with a jagged smile crossing his cracked face. Craven's bright blue eyes shine as he rasps out his words.
"One. . .magic number that means so much. Six has a definite. . .negative connotation in places like this one. Six. . .six. . .six is the mark of the beast. The childrens rhyme, 'Six, six, seven, seven, go to hell, go to heaven'. . . Six, the evil number. Six, numerology's little scapegoat. True, seven ate nine, but six looms in the shadows always, eternally waiting to swoop down upon all others."
Suddenly the smile fades from Craven's face. Seeming somehow weak, he sits slowly down upon one of the pews, eyes downcast. Almost overcome with emotion, Craven pauses for some time in his husky speech. Tossing the jacket on the back of his seat, and clasping his hands together, he finally continues, voice strained. . .
"The number holds special significance for me. . .the bad seed. . .the devil's own. . . This place most holy. . .most dear. . .so long gone from my memory. . .now revisited. I wait in the shadows, waiting to swoop down upon my opponents. . .but like the number. . .I am misunderstood. . ."
Rested now, Craven stands again, turning from camera, leaving the jacket behind. From the back he seems even more out of place. His head appearing like a carved pumpkin set upon the trunk of a tree. Waiting for something, he pulls back a sleeve to peek at a wristwatch, nods, and turns back towards the camera. He Clears his throat, and when he speaks, Craven's voice is clearer than before.
"Misunderstood and labeled. . .bottled and thrown away. People call a man evil just because he likes to hurt people, but everyone inflicts pain.
You eat meat, you hurt an animal, you toss trash on the ground, you hurt the environment. Now the one will hurt the six, whomever they may be. . ."
Pausing, Craven checks his watch again. Seeming so impatient, one has to wonder what Craven is thinking of. His face has brightened to an extent, his own logic emboldening his point of view.
"If you can imagine a time when the odds were stacked against you, then consider my position. Jesse Love is bringing six against me to hurt me in anticipation of his sons second fight against me. The bribes. . .the bribes you must've given the matchmakers, Jesse. Very impressive. One flaw to your plan, though. You've set me against a gauntlet, not a handicap match.
Bill Craven_is the marathon man. . . In one night I fought Rapier, Ric
Reckless, and competed in a battle royal without even one iota of fatigue.
I_beat Reckless, made him slap the mat, I beat Rapier, only to have our beloved owner overturn the decision, and I had to be dragged from the ring by two men who eliminated themselves in taking me out. . . The only time I lost that night was when I was one among the many. The fact is that I can fight_all_night. The error is yours, the point is mine, Jesse. Your son will fall to me again, and your legacy will_die. . ."
Churchbells ring and the first few parishoners begin to arrive in the church. Craven quickly bends to lift his jacket, slinging it onto his body.
Before the twelfth bell, Craven is ready, looking as good as he could ever look, short of plastic surgery. Turning back towards the camera, Craven makes one parting statement. . .
"One among the many can do much, Jesse, sew the seeds of discord between the walls, and the house will fall. The Love household is fragile, like its inhabitants, and few seeds will be needed to cause its destruction.
It began with your sons defeat, it continues with your six champions, for I am the seventh set against the six. Six, seven, hell, heaven. Heaven beats hell, Jesse. . ."
Pleased with his own recollection, Craven repeats. . . "Heaven beats hell. . ." With this, Craven sits gently down in his seat as the church fills with people. A robed priest steps up behind the podium on the raised platform, the first notes are struck on the organ as the scene fades to black. . .
