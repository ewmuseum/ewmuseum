Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues

20 March 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In article <5gmc5l$4ud$ [detail withheld]@gryphon.phoenix.net >, [name withheld] < [detail withheld]@phoenix.net > wrote: >I would just like to point out some things about play-by-mail leagues >that people have not spoken about yet. I am no fan of the IWA, but others >are doing a much better job than them and I don't think the whole >industry should be painted with their brush. And in regards to PBM vs. >E-mail: > >1.) COST: First of all, play-by-mail is actually *cheaper* than e-mail >leagues. Why? Nothing comes free, and e-mail wrestling isn't free. You >have to pay your ISP. They aren't giving this service to you for nothing. >Thus, to play e-mail wrestling, you've got to pay $30 every month or $20 >every month or whatever. True, with the ISP you get internet access to >other things, but suppose you don't want the other things anyway and just >want to be involved in wrestling? If you look at just PBM wrestling vs. >E-Mail wrestling, PBM is cheaper. You can get by on PBM wrestling with >just $5 to $10 a month while you must plunk down around $20, if not more >just to have access to e-mail. And we all know about the problems >with AOL! Now, you could get juno (free e-mail) but then you don't get >internet access and you have to wait a long, long time to get through the >ads. Isn't your time worth some money too? So in reality, PBM is the >better deal. This is a very valid arguement. In fact I never thought of this. But really,...how many people do you know have internet access only for e-wrestling..and that's the only purpose for their internet accounts? Probably not to many. What you need to do is seperate how much a person uses their acces time for e-wrestling. You have to separate it between other forms of information. Me for example,..use the net to promote my band,...in the future to show dates of shows we are to do,...my resume, my hobbies, e-mailing friends and family.........it becomes so numerous,..that really the amount of money you are paying gets separated between so many different things...that the amount you use for e-wrestling is peanuts. > >2.) EVERY MONTH IS BRAND NEW: In most e-mail sim leagues you choose >attributes for your wrestler and that's it. You have no more control >other than writing flashes. In PBM wrestling, you can change your >strategy each month, You can bounce back from a very bad month to a very >good month if you choose the right strategy. Each month is a brand new >day, unlike in e-mail wrestling where you are frozen with the same >wrestler with the same attributes forever. Now I know that booked >leagues have strategy, but again I don't even see booked feds as true >e-feds because they are based on the amount you write, not on the value >of holds you choose. PBM and sim leagues are a better comparison because >both are free from personal bias from one person to another and don't >depend on how much you write or don't write. But again, in PBM you can >change your strategy every month. > Ok....I have to get on my sim feds vs. booked feds debate by saying,...there are feds out there that are DICE BASED ROLEPLAYING!!! People tend to forget about these,...considering these take the most time to work on. You have to change your stratagy everymatch,...you still have to write up flashes and senerios,...come up with Angles...etc. The varibility of a Sim is there,..but you can say..."Well if X tries for this move,...have Z go for this move "...etc., and actually control what you guy is to do. Biasness is down to a minimum as well. Plus,...you have the access to change any of this (strats, flashes, angles, etc.) in a matter of minutes,...not 2-3 days of mailing time. >3.) EXPERIENCE CAN COUNT: You can truly dominate PBM if you stay around >for awhile. Experience counts in PBM. That's not really the case for >e-mail. Sure, in some feds you do get a few bonus points for experience, >but that's also a weakness because who says you necessarily get better >with experience? In PBM, you can get better with experience after you've >been there for four years. Then again, you can be there for four years >and still be Barry Horowitz. Yes and no. I participated in the IWA for 3 years. Yes,..I did get better after about 7 months and did decent up until the end. But people always managed to do beter than me. Since I've been playing e-wrestling,...I've gotten much better. Even more since I first started in April of 1995. Experience has helped me write better flashes,..write better strats,..and come up with some really cool angles...like an angle I had that was worked for over 6 months. That's something you just can't "jump" into. > >4.) MORE THOUGHT: PBM is more of a chess game where thought and strategy >go into it as opposed to e-mail, where you write a flash and that's it. >Again, booked feds will claim they have strategies, but they are not >judged by computers. They are judged by humans, who are open to being >prejudiced toward people for various reasons. And many judge on >"quality." What's quality to one is juvenile to another. PBM is not as >quick as e-mail, of course, but that's good because it gives you some >time to think and prepare rather than just shoot off a flash and be done >with it. > Again,...I have to say,...try a Dice Based E-Wrestling Fed. >5.) MORE WRESTLER CONTROL: In PBM, you have much more control of your >wrestler. If you want, you can have him wrestle 30, 50 or even 100 >matches in one month against whomever you want him to wrestle. This isn't >true for e-mail. Sometimes you have to wait months to get a shot at a >champ depending on how the schedule is. In PBM, you are guaranteed to get >the match you want and you can have as many matches as you want. I have to whole heartedly disagree. In the IWA,..there were no attributes. Nothing to really make your character stand out as the physical specimen he is. If you weight 500 Lbs. in a PBM game,...so what. In a E-Wrestling game,..here I go again, but...especially in a Dice Based,...weighting 500 Lbs. makes a whole lot of difference. And besides,...sure you may have 30 matches....but who do you think most of those are they going to be against... THE HAMMOND EGGERS...THE JOBBERS!!! The ones with a 3-78 record. And to top it all of,...the e-wrestling is more realistic. "OK,..you want a title shot....you have to fight for it" I have. I had to climb the rakings and get that #1 contender seed before I got my true title shot. And it did take me a month or two to get that. I was much more satisfied when I won the title that way than any, ANY of the titles I won in the 3 years I was in the IWA. > >6.) MORE HANDLERS: In PBM, you are in a situation with 200-300, if not >more, other managers/handlers. In e-mail feds, you usually have between >15-25, if that, because the fedhead doesn't have time to do matches for >200-300 people. Some e-feds have tried to use more writers, but many of >them poop out at the last minute since they have no financial stake in >the matter. Other things come first for e-fed writers and fedheads such >as school, family, work, etc. Thus they don't have time to serve large >amounts of people and often cancel cards or back out at the last minute. >In PBM feds, you have people who are paid to give their full attention to >the leagues and give them their undivided attention and their full time. >This isn't true for e-mail feds, which are done as a hobby. > This much is true. But one thing I experienced in the IWA,...there were a lot of Smacks in there...including myself (For those of you whoever encounterd Substance [name withheld] in the IWA between 1990 and the end of 1993,...hey,..here's my apology...I was a punk. Looking back...I'd even want to get in line to dish out a beating. Sorry.) I had guys who got a hold of me through the mail...get my phone number and prank call me. I'm from near Chicago,..and a guy and his brother would prank call me a few times...and they lived in Detriot!!) Sure,...there are our share of idiots in e-Wrestling,...but far and few from the numbers there were in the IWA. As for the quality and response time...I think more quality goes into e-Wrestling. In PBM,...all you are getting is a few peices of paper,...Titles,..the rankings,...then the matches (...Wrestler X beat Wrestler Z with The XXXX),..and then the comments listed right after that. In E-Wrestling....sure,...not all the feds have that high quality standard. People are busy,..people have lives!!! But when you do find one that delivers quality,...you don't leave it. I'm glad where I'm at,...The GIWF (Global Internet Wrestling Federation.) [name withheld] is the Fed Head Of All Fed Heads!!! Period. Case closed. I also devote my time to our Bi-Weekly Hotline and Huge Archives Web Site. Sure,...sometimes we are a few days behind with things...but you have to expect that. I think it's much more satisfing. >7.) MORE STABILITY: In my experience with e-feds, for every one that >survives you have 20 that don't last more than a month due to the time it >takes to run them properly. And plus you have a lot of 12-year-olds who >run these feds who just aren't mature enough to handle them and fold them >up after you've invested a lot of time in them. This is not true for PBM >feds, which are run by people who understand the importance of stability >and service to the customer. > True,...true,...and VERY TRUE!!! It happens. I can't argue with that. >8.) MORE DETAIL: PBM is much, much more detailed than e-mail feds. E-mail >feds give you a card and not a whole lot more, again because the fedheads >don't have time to go into depth. PBM doesn't give descriptions of each >match because in PBM many times there are more than 2,000 matches a month >that get played due to the fact that people many times ask for lots of >matches. Instead, PBM not only lists results, but gives reviews of the >action, complete standings, descriptions of the wrestlers involved, a >summary of who is feuding with who, call-in radio talk shows, fan >letters, tongue-in-cheek monthly awards, Most beloved/Most despised >lists, etc. The whole shtick is much more detailed and a lot more >attention is given to the business than in e-mail simply because people >who are doing it as a hobby don't have the time to invest like PBM people >do. > Go to www.public.iastate.edu/~[detail withheld] You want detail....take a few minutes...read the Hotline...check out a few old cards....take a look at our last PPV...War Of The Worlds '97. I'm sorry,..but NO PBM game can ever do what a high quality e-wrestling fed can. >9.) MORE ORGANIZED: It takes no time to download a PBM newsletter. A >well-done newsletter gives you everything at one time and anytime you >want to review it you can just pick it up and read it and know where >everything is, as opposed to e-mail where if you want something from >several months ago you have to weed through tons of e-mails -- which >takes time. Or else you have to organize everything yourself -- which >also takes time. > OR let somebody else organize it.....which I have. YES....it does take time,....man,..I still hurt from it,...and I had our Archive site up and running in November. >10.) LARGER TALENT POOL: PBM has access to a lot more people than e-mail >does. Though the internet is growing, there are millions of people who >don't get it and never will. Thus the PBM potential pool of talent and >handlers is 100 percent of the wrestling fans while e-mail wrestling is >limited to just those on the internet, which right now is only 10% to 15% >of the population at the most. > Sorry,...I have to disagre again. Just take a look at some of the people who grace RSPW-F,...look at some of the Fed Heads....look at the e-wrestling Top 200. Talent....we have it growing out our ears. -- Skinny (Substance) [name withheld] Lead Guitarist/Lead Singer for "Stomp" Chad likes and uses Peavey Electronics, Nady Systems, Seymore Duncun Pickups, and Frosty's from "Wendy's" [detail withheld]@iastate.edu http://www.geocities.com/SunsetStrip/8101 http://www.public.iastate.edu/~[detail withheld]/homepage.html
