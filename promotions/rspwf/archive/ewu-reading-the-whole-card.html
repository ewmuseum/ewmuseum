READING THE WHOLE CARD - Some thoughts by Various When I first started E-Wrestling, I'd pay no attention to anything but my own matches... Then I skimmed to my match, and then re-read everything else. And now I start at the beginning and read down to the end. But, that's just in the feds I compete in... I'm on quite a few mailing lists, which I usually skim, or check out "big cards", such as PPV's and/or big weekly shows in depth. I think that if you don't read the other angles and whatnot, you lose out in A LOT of the fun in this game. You also learn quite a bit as well. - Jason Russel As a fed head, read the whole dang thing so as a player you do not fuck up other people's angles, feuds, or storylines, there are so many poor sports in efed's that weeding them out is best by just getting rid of the one's obviously reading just there matches, and if there kicking ass with run ins and such, but loose there match and that is all they focus on, then ditch em too, so many players can really suck, I have had countless dead beats, who make me work my ass off, just so they can drop when they get there first loss, that they deserved to loose for not RP'ing or just thinking that is was in the bag, full card readers fan out to be the only one's worth keeping. I had one dumb fuck vet who couldn't believe a guy beat him in his first match Duh he Role-played and you didn't dumb fuck, he was one that only read his matches and when he didn't win he would always whine !!!!!!!!!!!! Sorry but it's a major piss off, when you make a whole card and half your weak ass, 16 year old fucking moran punk ass kid players, get all whinney and quit! - XWF X-Treme To tell you the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth... it all depends on how good the card is. Now, I know you're thinking... "how do you know how good it is, if you don't read it?" Well, if there's no good storylines involved... if there's no good matches scheduled... and there's no participation in the flashing and in-ringing... the card should suck. But if participation's up... the card is jammed with in-rings and angles... and good matches are scheduled... I'm down for the whole damn card. Just recently, the GSWP held a supercard-type show. And it was one of the best cards I've read in a while. A few things weren't good in it... but overall, I loved the whole thing. It's cards like that, that make e-wrestling fun. - [name withheld] I read the whole thing, from beginning to end. Granted, when I'm short on time, I will skim through at the -very- least catching announcer's comments and looking for anything out of the ordinary other than series' of moves. That's not very often, though, and usually when my time is -that- short I'm in trouble time-wise anyway. I do write matches once in a while as well, and I can honestly say that it doesn't bother me that people don't read them... But I also think it may be something to think about. A trend I would like to see, for the sake of the 'fun factor' is that the normal weekly (or bi-weekly for you hardcore fedheads) cards be taken down to something more along the lines of well written summaries, rather than full matches. Just enough to get the point of the strats, a few moves to get the wrestlers over (if that's the case, otherwise not get them over :P), and to get the angles in there properly. Other than that, and I'm more guilty than anybody, there's no need to try to top the Jerry Lynn/Lance Storm match from Anarchy Rulz every single time out. Save that for Supercards and PPV's :) - Shawn Mihalek I think Jas pretty much hit the nail on the head... pretty much in any of the "big name federations', if you skip a match, you're not going to get some of the comments and jokes that result from teh earlier matches. It was one of the "tricks" I used that enticed people to go back and read earlier matches and events originally. Comparing what's hapenign in the current match to what happened in a previous match (say 2 or 3 matches earlier). But for me, I much prefer to go from top to bottom, simply because the good cards have hints about what happens later on, and later matches make quips and such about earlier matches, and I want to try to be in on every joke that I can. (some of hte insider/handler jokes, I'll never catch... although I may remember a "remark" later on in chats.) For a good example, see the Tiger Claw/Casey James match... all sorts of notes and hints to past fueds, that you'd never catch if you didn't follow the Syndicate. [It took me asking Mat about some of them...cause I completely missed them... (even though I remembered the incidents once he reminded me).] As for in a federation if I was handling.. I learned a LONG time ago to read the entire card... as I passed up a four or five great feuds (at the time, for me), because I missed comments utnil I went back and reread an I/W card AFTER doing an RP based on the outcomes of my _known_ involvements. Of course, certain people (HADES!! Not to name drop *G*).. I swore never read the entire card..cause my challenges would go unanswered time and time again ::) [name withheld] For the life of me ... I'll never understand why people would play in a game that is based on reading and writing and not read the cards. It's something I'll never understand as both a fedhead and a player. The defense of the non-reader is that "it had nothing to do with me" but my question is how do you know if you didn't read it? Also another consideration, by reading other player's material and the cards, you as a player just might be able to pick out something to use in your own roleplays or in future feuds/angles. Sure, I can understand skimming over the card to see if you won your match due to lack of time but in doing so, you just might miss out on a set-up for the match. As a fedhead, I try my best to keep the card entertaining so that readers will want to read it start to finish. You just never know when someone will show up or have something to say about your character ... unless of course, you read it. Just my thoughts ... - Brian
