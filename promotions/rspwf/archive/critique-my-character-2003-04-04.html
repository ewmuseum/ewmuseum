[Critique] My character...

4 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In lew of a sudden tsunami of handlers asking for critiques, I'd thought I'd ride the wave and have people critique something I've written. I'm usually the one tearing others apart, and every once-in-a-while you want a taste of your own medicine. This should be an easy role-play to critique, since there is no background I can give for this role-play or the character, since he was new to the fed and everyone in the fed had little to no knowledge of the character.
---- [An extreme close-up of the upper-left side of a man's face. His gray eyes stand out more than a usual set of eyes; they have the deepest gray center and this gray bleeds to the outer perimeters in lighter and lighter shades.]
??: You know, it's a commentary on the times that the term "old-fashioned" comes before the word "honesty." It's not difficult to be cynical when so many of you lie... about who you are, what you want and are willing to do. The world is filled with people without feelings. Is it any wonder the media praises an exceptional few as "champions"...
...as if it were an emotional marvel of an achievement! And, of course, it's all the more exclusive, because the camera's right on top of them. Most of us don't want that camera on us, because we're too busy covering up our inadequacies with lies. God forbid the spotlight comes on you!
What would you do? Could you still make yourself look genuine? It's that same doubt you have every morning when you look in the mirror. You ask yourself, "Am I Good Enough?" And you get a good look at yourself close-up, so you can answer that question reeeal well. Then you discover close-ups are perilous in that they can distort as well expose a lack of technique; you realize that what you see others have to see too... that your lies don't add up to an even sum. Something's odd, and you know it!
...And so you think about that spotlight being on you, and what you would do. You think about the camera breathing on you and think back further to that morning in the mirror, and you are hesitant to fall back on handy poses and blank stares, but you know that's all you've reeeally got, as your pulse rises, your lungs pant for air and you're suddenly overcome with intense claustrophobia despite the fact you're not reeeally claustrophobic, and you know that everyone watching you can sense there's truly nothing behind the mask.
[Fade to black as a voice calmly says, "Eddie Rue is Coming".]
