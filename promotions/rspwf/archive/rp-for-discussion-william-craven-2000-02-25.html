(RP for discussion) William Craven.

25 February 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wow, been a REAL long time since I've done this, eh? Posting a flash to the RSPWF. Ah, the memories. This one's just run in EMWC's Noise
Pollution. William Craven and his delusions. Whatta kook, I tells ya.
Anywho, what I'm not looking for. 1) Troll-like and sycophantic simplified answers such as "I don't like it, it sucks" and "I like it, it's good". 2) Fed invites (PLS JIN MI FD). 3) To be ignored (PAY ATTENTION TO ME, DAMN YOU ALL!:) What I am looking for is a well-thought out opinion of the work in question (see below), with advice, what's right, what's wrong, etc. I already see a few of the flaws, but I won't cite them myself. Let you all handle that, eh? :)
Anywho, I'm done. Make with the criticism, or something. Moze. ----- [Stark, clear, pure black emerges dominant upon the picture plane. Empty is the screen as a night scene of nothing, part of a signature style made familiar through time and practice, screams a single name without ever uttering a sound. Issuing forth through the tranquil pitch comes the distant beating of a horrible heart, words in sing-song-style accompanying.]
V (Voice): I can see you. Can you see me? [Grainy, rough, familiar, with that oh-so haunting quality, the voice serves to tease the mind as words in white burn into the ebony void.
"'...blest with beauteous wives, they would restrain the one, distain the other. And who doth lead them but a paltry fellow, long kept in
Bretagne at our mother's cost? A milk-sop, one that never in his life felt so much cold as over shoes in snow?'
-- From William Shakespeare's 'Richard III'." The heart is silenced, the words fade, and specks of light flit about the screen as the black becomes a dark and grainy morass; slight static marring the perfect ebony beauty of a night scene of nothing. A heartbeat sounds in the darkness, providing a signature of sorts, spelling out the name of the featured player in the coming segment without a single letter used...
At the bottom of the screen is a digital readout that shows the date and time. "14:53, 1/29/00", meaning 2:53 PM, January 29, in the year 2000.
At the bottom right, the word "record" blinks on and off, stating the obvious. A small "pop" is heard, and a sigh. The world opens up. A brief glimpse of a large hand pulling a cap from the lens of the camera being used can be seen as a scene straight out of the industrial revolution appears. Massive, oversized gears and pistons work diligently to turn an enormous pair of shafts that lead to a huge clock face. Light shines in through a gaping round hole that lets those outside and below see the time for miles around.]
V: Believe in the sanctity of the household. Believe in the consecration of the home as holy ground. Anoint all thy loved ones, and love them true. They will never leave you. So says the sermon, preached by the pastor from his pulpit before the pews. Daddy dearest spoke the truth. Love her...and she'll be yours--mine, forever.
Cleave to thy husband, Lydia...cleave. [Speaking again, and drawing out long the last word spoken, the speaker, growling fervently in that hoarse whisper, identifies himself by proxy.
So desperate to believe, so deluded, so lost is William Craven. Moving to the sill of that massive, round opening goes the camera, apparently nothing more than a handheld VHS model being carted around by the psycho himself. Coming to rest, he leans out, focusing carefully.
Outside of the clock tower is a dismal, gray winter sky hanging low over a field of white. Moving about in play are children, running to and fro between small groupings of parents who huddle together for warmth, sips of coffee and borrowed cigarettes. Angels in snow are made, men are built from three large spheres each, and little balls of snow are thrown over drifts of snow at other snow covered children. Slightly negligent are the parents, who seem more concerned with one another and the ground that they walk on than their offspring.]
WC (William Craven, previously known as "V" and "Voice"): And there she is... [Panning about, the camera view comes to rest on a grouping of three. A small, almost dainty woman, her long and curly hair an almost blinding array of rainbow hues is sitting cross-legged in the snow. She's wrapped up against the cold in a brightly embroidered white jacket from
Guatemala; an item bought at a craft show, perhaps, or picked up from a thrift store.]
WC: Lydia... [Drawing the name out, William zooms in near on the woman he believes to be his wife.] WC: Just imagine, my dear... Twenty years from now. Your hair graying, mine mostly gone, and we sit about the fireplace playing our old home movies. Remembering and laughing at these old times...
Remembering our troubles, no longer anything more than a joke of the past, a misplaced memory.
No more fear of the man that struck you. I made him pay, you know... He came against me before. I defeated him then. He came against you. I made him pay. Against me again... [Silence now, save the raspy breathing of the man that holds the camera. He sighs heavily, and a cloud of steam, William's exhalation, whooshes out from below the lens.]
WC: ...And who's this you have with you? [Panning down slightly, the lens comes to rest upon the second figure. Also on the ground is the woman's nephew, Noel. He's clutching a large cardboard box tightly, perhaps making conversation with the turtle inside. His expression is one of removal from his surroundings. The boy is so absorbed with his new pet, toy, whatever, that he seems to be in another world.]
WC: Junior. Mommy brought you to the park, did she, son? It's been so long. So long since you've seen your daddy. I've missed you. Oh, how you've grown. Almost 13, but I remember when you were born like it was yesterday...
[Pan up and to the right. Standing above the two protectively, eyeing suspiciously everything around him, is Andrew Tucker. The woman, Piper, cranes her neck slightly to say something to him, and Tucker looks down with a grin, his answer causing Piper to go into a fit of giggles.]
WC: Tucker... That's not me, Billy. Don't be afraid of him. Daddy'll be home soon, son... [In spite of the low-hanging, dark clouds, the park is still a fun-filled winter wonderland. These three, however, stand out from the crowd as less than joyful. Piper's gaze on her nephew is confused and sad. Perhaps it's a good thing that she's so distracted, else she might notice the form of her stalker far above.
Noel himself is almost dogged in his determination not to acknowledge his surroundings. Being so removed, it almost seems appropriate that
Craven would assume this to be his son.
Andrew Tucker himself seems weary, with tired eyes and the tightness of tension around his mouth. He's obviously had more than his share of stress via Craven in the last several months.
Sighing, William's voice lowers in volume and tone. He rasps and forces the sound out, his words ringing clearly in spite of the fact that he's choking on his own whispers.]
WC: There was a time when a man could slay his wife with impunity. Drag her to the village square and discipline her for everyone to see. They'd cheer and make merry, all the while pointing and laughing at the woman's pain. This is much like a village square, isn't it, Lydia?
Roads encircle this square of land. People mill about... ...But no, I would never do that to you. Now and forever, I am my wife's protector. No indiscretion of yours can shake my love.
Looking down on you now, I can see so clearly how it would all play out. The advantage of the high ground... I could pick you all off from
here-- But I won't... Because I love you... Feel my love, Lydia. Feel my love... *BONG!* *BONG!* *BONG!* *BONG!* [The clocktower bell sounds as the hour turns. On the fourth stroke of the bell, the heartbeat begins again. Everything fades, and all is gone save the darkness and the beating of that horrible heart. End...]
