[ESSAY] Starting a (booked) e-fed

21 April 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Preface: Having seen the end of two good 'established' e-feds and one promising new one, and being definitely in favour of booked feds, these are my personal thoughts on the starting procedure and issues faced by any aspiring fedhead. If I were to start a fed, these are also the guidelines I would stick to. However, being a working person, I don't have the time needed to open a new federation - the best I can do is write the occasional card for other feds. I will give information that applies to simmed federations as well, however, I will focus primarily on booked federations. Disclaimer: I know that WWF, WCW, Ric Flair, and tons of other names I'll throw around are trademarked and/or copyrighted, and that I am using them without permission. No infringement on existing rights is attempted by using them as examples to show my points) 1) Before deciding to start a federation Before you start an e-wrestling federation, you have to realize there will be tons of work headed your way, and more bitching than many people can stand. There have been literally hundreds of federations that folded within a month from their start, due to the fedhead not having realized how much work it was going to be. Especially in the beginning, less is more. Don't expect everyone who promised to write cards/matches to start doing so immediately. Most writers prefer to know what the wrestlers they write about are like, so as to capture the flavour of the characters. To do this, they'll have to read several cards with those wrestlers and lots of their flashes. So, especially in the first two to three months, be prepared to do all the work yourself. If people chip in during that period, be glad, for it cannot be banked upon. 2) The federation design Once you have decided, yes, you want to bear with all the hassle and thanklessness the job of fedhead involves, it is time to decide on specifics. 2.1) Simmed v/s booked A simmed fed gets rid of at least some of the bitching the losers of the matches are going to do. You can always pass the blame on, 'Hey, I didn't decide, the sim did.' OTOH, booked feds give more of a WCW/WWF feeling, the angles can develop better and the players are more involved in their characters. Besides, the WWF and WCW are booked feds, too :-) Both simmed and booked feds can be separated into two different types again: PC-simmed v/s. diced, and quality/quantity booked v/s angle booked. A quality/quantity booked fed _can_ be compared to, as a notorious poster on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy put it, a writing contest. Deciding matches solely on quality/quantity of roleplays is a valid way of determining, however, you'll have to expect arguments like 'but I wrote more than him' or 'but I did the better roleplays'. Especially in the beginning of a federation, this can be very disruptive. On the other hand, an angle-booked fed dispenses with the 'the referee decides who he thinks flashed better, but he is an idiot for not seeing it my way'-syndrome. Matches are booked for what would most enhance a feud, settle it once and for all or start a feud with the best possible heat. Unless it is of the 'I am the underdog but one day, so help me... I'll beat you' angle, often the wins see-saw back and forth, keeping the interest up as a repeat win on one side is, of course, always possible. 2.2) Real wrestlers or fantansy wrestlers? Using RL wrestlers will have the advantage that you know how they react, but the disadvantage that you are limited to their developments on screen. Of course, once you started, you can ignore things like Bret Hart slamming the USA, SCSA being turned face, Hulk Hogan becoming Hollywood Hogan, DDP finally admitting he is married to Kimberly etc. However, it becomes hard differentiating between the TV wrestlers and those in your fed. Therefore, my advice would be to use 'original wrestlers only'. But that is purely a matter of taste. 2.3) The flashing system. Essentially, there are two types of flash systems. An 'open' system is one in which all participants send their flashes to all other participants. The advantages: a quick system, if your flash doesn't get out it's your own fault, you can react much faster, the count on back-and-forth posts can go up as far as 5-10 flashes a day. A 'closed' system, in contrast, has the participants send their flashes to a central point who collects them and then sends them bunched to the participants. Advantages here are that changes in the participants' rosters can be more easily implemented ('flash central' changes the list and everyone is happy, no big chance of dropouts getting flashes weeks after they decided they wanted out), and that the number of flashes is more regulated - you don't flash ten times for one flash show. In the opening phase of a federation, a closed system is preferrable, even though it means more work for 'flash central'. Dropouts don't discourage the other participants, and no bad blood is generated by a dropout sending, for the sixth time, 'I have dropped out, now, remove me from your mailing lists! Idiots!'. Once a fed is established and the roster doesn't change that much anymore, it's all up to one's personal taste. 2.4) Hardcore? 'Extreme' federations (ECW-style) or 'mainstream' federations (WCW/WWF/AWF)? Well, that is a question of taste, but it is preferrable to be clear on what style you want. Extreme wrestlers/handlers have trouble dealing with mainstream wrestlers/handlers and vice versa. There is no question that handlers can happily have a hardcore wrestler in one fed and a mainstream in another, but saying it helps (also knowing this beforehand will reduce the number of dropouts who don't like the style). 2.5) Special styles? Fans of the lucha/japanese/European/whatever style might disagree, but unless you _know_ you have a stable membership for it, going into any of these directions immediately might be A Bad Idea(tm). Of course, my ideas in point 3) regarding heels/faces apply equally to rudos/tecnicos in Mexico and their counterparts in the other styles. You also might slowly develop your federation into the direction you desire, taking your handlers with you, but to the most participants, U.S.-style wrestling is all they know and therefore what they feel best at. 2.6) Number of titles / participants? Unless you are going for soemthing very special, for a beginning fed I would suggest three titles and about 12-25 participants. That would be two singles (World and a Regional) and one tag teamn title with strong competition. Once the fed is established, you can still experiment with the number of titles and participants. Don't give out too many titles for too few participants, though! A federation in which everyone has a title soon loses the 'Wow, he's a title holder' type sense of wonder. If people work for it, creating memorable feuds and angles, everyone has more fun than if everyone has a title. Think about the current situation in the WCW. Essentially, there are currently _two_ singles titles defended on a regular base - the TV and the Cruiserweight Title. The US Title always was low profile, and since Hollywood Hogan grabbed the World Title, it is pretty much out of the picture, too. Yet, there are still lots of interesting (for the viewer) angles going on - nWo, Benoit-Sullivan, Horesmen breaking up - or not, Sting, Macho Man - DDP and others. The only one of these angles that includes an immediate title connection is the nWo. 2.7) Number of cards Again, think of what we see/get on TV and how it is affected by the number of shows. There aren't that many original shows in WWF, most of their shows just recap other shows' actions. You can't do this, it would be boring. On WCW, there are fewer shows, with more 'new' matches per show. However, even the WCW rarely goes over 15-20 new matches a week. Yet there are feds out there, that have 10+ matches _per_card_. At three cards a week. Plus PPVs every two weeks. With a roster of wrestlers not bigger than WCW, usually less. This leads to saturation. Not every wrestler has to wrestle on every show. It also detracts from angles if wrestlers face each other without reason. If the WCW were to have a matchup Benoit/Booker T, or the WWF had a matchup SCSA/Flash Funk, everyone would say, Why?. It's the same - even more so - on a booked fed. Again, look at the big feds. WCW essentially has two shows a week, Nitro and SNME, with the latter being of low importance. The WWF even has only one main show, RAW. Unless your fed is _much_ bigger than these two, this seems like an advisable rate for cards. PPVs, however, currently suffer from overabundance. There used to be times when a PPV was something special, nowadays we have 3 PPVs in a month. THAT IS TOO MUCH. PPVs should be used sparingly, to close feuds, to start feuds, for _major_ developments. Once a month should be the upper limit. Once every 6-8 weeks sounds even more reasonable to me. 2.8) Naming your fed The last (and least important) thing to think about would be a name for your federation. An acronym that reads as something would be nice (WAR! comes to mind), but most feds don't have such names. Just take care you don't name your fed WCW, WWF, WCeW, eWWF or similar ripoffs. You will be more attractive to the more serious players if you don't. There is nothing wrong with using your own name. One of the best feds I was in (it _was_ simmed, though booking had a big influence, and the cards were written accordingly) was the LWA - Lambie Wrestling Association led by [name withheld]. Matter of fact, I learned that was the reason for the name only after two months... Of course, if your name is Willie Wanker, don't make it the Willie Wanker Federation (WWF), as this again rips off the 'real' WWF. 3) Getting your wrestlers / writers / staff There are three different ways of getting your wrestlers and staff. a) Posting on rsp-wf. This has the advantage of reaching out to all those interested. Disadvantage: Most applicants, you don't know, and there are many who'll drop out immediately again. It helps if you have the answers to 2.1 to 2.8 ready. b) Asking people you know. Disadvantage: Everyone will expect favours. Advantage: you know pretty well what to expect. c) Word-of-mouth. Ask a few friends if they know people from folding feds that might be interested. Disadvantage: maybe these are the people that caused the fed to fold. Adwantage: _If_ you know your friends, you can be pretty sure what you get. An important thing to consider when accepting wrestlers is a healthy mix of faces and heels. 25 heels beating the crap out of each other (or worse yet, out of a single face) can be amusing, but gets boring quickly. And 25 faces (big chance you'd be getting those!) will be equally uninteresting. Finally, don't go overboard on fan reactions. The odd 'cheered heel' (SCSA, Flair) can be refreshing, as can be the odd booed face (Hakushi), but to have this for all faces/heels would be overly stereotyping again. 4) The Grand Opening. Despite what I said about proliferation of cards, the _opening_ of a fed is worthy of a good PPV. If people leave in the first few weeks, it is based on the opening show. The opening has to be something special. The titles need to be given out, usually this is done in a tournament or a Battle-Royal type match (Royal Rumbles seem to be preferred here). If you do this, even if you are running a booked fed, you might want to think of simming this match. At this time, there probably are no really big feuds developing, and people might take umbrage at you choosing someone else over them. Therefore, especially for an opening match, a simmed match sounds much preferable. And if you want to decide the winner for yourself, still claim you were using a sim/dice system, noone can prove you lied here ;-) Also, if you have different titles, you might want to have different matches for them (no 'winner gets world title, runner up gets cyber title'). This way, the secondary title doesn't take that much of a back seat to the main title. Also, having people decide which title they want to go after will often be the first in-character decision they make. Also, it helps to have one match at least end in a regular clean victory, and one in a screwjob. The latter will draw at least some heat from the guy screwed, and maybe from others as well, while the former will show clean matches are possible and there is no way to expect either. Maybe you have another idea, some way to give out the belts that hasn't been done before. Then, by all means, go for it. Just remember: it's your fed and it deserves the best you can do. Flashing before the fed opens: sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't. At least, everyone should know who the other wrestlers are, i.e., minimum of name, size, weight and maybe a few words regarding the gimmick. One of the less-used ways to do so would be the 'press conference' or 'interview show'. 5) The first few weeks This is the real test of your fed and yourself. Don't overdo it, just stay true to yourself. Don't give in to people yelling and screaming because they won no title, but don't be rude either. There certainly will be flames (or at least incendiary remarks), stay cool and put them out again. You'll have enough work cut out for you without getting into a flame war simultaneously. Be prepared to have people drop out immediately. Have a waiting list ready so you can replace the participants easily. You might even consider keeping one or two of the best roleplayers 'on hold' (if they agree to do so), so that they can come in with a bang at a regular card or even at your next PPV. Once you have survived the first few weeks, you'll probably be going strong enough for the fed to outlast you. Most feds that exist for two or more months only fold when the fedhead doesn't have the time anymore or gets completely fed up with e-wrestling. Bets wishes to all.
