[UEW] Flash for William O'Leary

10 January 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
People, critique it.... I don't care. I just decided to post it. [Scene takes place as William O’Leary, the newcomer to UEW, is seen sitting in the back bed of a red truck. The bed of the truck has a large slab of rubber placed in the bottom. Possibly for comfort and/or traction. Through the back of the bed, you can see the back of the driver’s head through the back window.
The man is wearing a red cap, and has gray hair. William O’Leary is sitting in the back right corner. He is dressed in his usually trashy sense. He is wearing a pair of dirt coated shoes, a pair of blue jeans with holes in the knees, and orange shirt that says “Bums will be
Bums” on the back, and has nothing on the front. Also, O’Leary is wearing a red cap that says “Lawson’s Farm” on the front.
As the truck slowly rides down the road, William O’Leary begins shifting his weight back and forth trying to get comfortable. The truck is driving on a desolate road surrounded by tall green pine trees and bushes. As the one-lane road begins to narrow, the truck turns off on the right into a gravel parking lot. The building’s sign reads “Billy Bob’s Feed.” The man exits the truck and is wearing a pair of denim overalls over a white tee shirt. He walks to the bed of the truck, and speaks to O’Leary.]
Man: Listen here boy. I am gonna go in here for a while and get some manure, cow feed, and horse feed. Now, I want you to go ahead and take off walking on down to my farm ‘cause I’m gonna need that there bed. So, you better get walking. That are lots ‘a things I got for ya to do.
[The old man turns from the truck and walks to the glass doors of the store. He opens them, and enters.] William O’Leary: Well, I better haul my @$$ on down to Lawson’s. [O’Leary stands in the bed, walks over to a side, and jumps over it. He lands flat-footed on the gravel, and immediately starts walking towards the road.]
William O’Leary: Well, if I have some spare time on my hands while I’m not serving, I guess I can give a few words to the fans, address a person or two, and talk about some other stuff can’t I?
[O’Leary kicks a rock on the side of the road.] William O’Leary: Well, first of all, I have to compliment on Liam Rogan’s acceptance to be on Freak Show. I must, and I say, I must, talk a little about that. Personally, I liked the scene where you were signing autographs, and the kid and his mom walked up. Thank goodness I saw it. Luckily, I was at someone’s house, and I happened to catch their son watching Freak Show.
Well, anyway, she did have a nice @$$ didn’t she. I don’t blame you one bit for complimenting on it either.
[William O’Leary brings his right hand to his face, and acts as if he’s slappling himself.]
William O’Leary: Buddy… that slap on the face was well worth it too. Now, this is just what I think was going on, but hell, I’m gonna say it right here on this interview…
[William O’Leary drops his head to look at the ground, and a broad smile flashes across his face. He brings his head back up facing forward, and nods his head.]
William O’Leary: I bet you were one horny [BLEEP!]. I mean, with a woman that fine coming up there, you probably couldn’t help yourself. I sure know I couldn’t. But then again, I haven’t been with a woman in…. well….
[William O’Leary begins counting with his fingers. He holds up 3, 4, and then
5 fingers.] William O’Leary: I guess it was 5 years ago. Boy. I remember that day very well. It was with a woman named Julie Thibideaux. Buddy, she was one fine lady that day. We went to eat at a restaurant called Beavers Buffet. It is so clear. Maybe because it was the worst, yet best day of my life.
[William O’Leary looks at the ground looking depressed, and talks.] William O’Leary: First, we went to Stage, a clothing store, and I bought her a couple of dresses and a little something we won’t talk about here. See, in that time, I had money. I was a constructor, and had built the most fascinating building in all of Leesville. So, after we went shopping, we went back to my place, and talked a little. In the time period while we were there, she changed into a new dress. After that, we went to Beavers Buffet, the restaurant I was talking about, and had the most expensive meal on the menu, Steak and Lobster. Well, I must say that it was going great.
[William O’Leary raises his head and rubs his eyes.] William O’Leary: The great went to greater and then worse. Later that night before we left Beavers Buffet, I proposed to Julie and she said yes. We were going to get married. On the way back home, we drove by the building that gave me all I had. It had fallen to the ground. Well, to make a long story short, she dumped me and I was sued for all I had. Now, I am a bum that serves….. but I must say that serving is the best thing I have ever done!
Enough about my saddened past… lets talk about Liam Rogan. Liam, I said it in my first interview, and I’ll say it once again… I WANT TO SERVE YOU!!! Well, one reason for that is that strap you hold. Yes, gold does tarnish. But I’m sure you don’t want it to tarnish do you? Well, I have one solution to that question…. LET ME SERVE YOU!!!
[William O’Leary reaches in his back pocket and gets out a can of polish and a rag to polish with.]
William O’Leary: See… I always come prepared. So Liam… Rogan… whatever I should call you, LET ME SERVE YOU! Well, I must get to Lawson’s before that old hummer does. I must get going.
[William O’Leary picks up his pace and quickly walks down the road. As he is almost out of view, he turns to the right down a dirt road. The camera shifts to the sunny sky as it slowly fades out.]
