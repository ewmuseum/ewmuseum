The Suicidal High Flyers Open Flash

29 April 2000
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Tommy Kain & Ken Stanley are sitting on stools facing a reporter who is sitting on his own stool. The reporter is pretty skinny with glasses, and a caesar haircut with a piece of paper or two in his hand. When you see Tommy, you won't think..."This guy looks like he could bench a ton" but he can hold his own in the ring. He has long straight blonde hair...nice lookin guy. His left eyebrow is pierced and he is wearing a New York Knicks jersey and a pair of denim shorts.
Ken Stanley is probably the stronger of the two men. His body looks a little bit more developed. His straight brown hair goes down to about his ears. He is wearing a gray t-shirt that ends right above his tatoo on left arm of TazManian Devil, the Devil is holding a sign that says "SHF 4 Life".]
Reporter: Tommy, Ken I thank you for joining me this evening. TK: Not a problem. Reporter: You two have been through alot in the world of wrestling, but that all really only took place in one or two different organizations. You two competed in the GCW and the UEW, what do you think the differences between the two companies was?
KS: Well I'd have to say that both were not a tag team wrestling organization really. GCW gave us more of a chance at first, but then when some "popular" teams in the owner's mind came around, we were left behind. We were the longest staying tag team in the GCW, but to be honest we were treated like rookies. We were former tag team champions there, but the way we lost the titles wasn't what some would say was fair. As a matter of fact, it was such a screwjob it should have taken place in Montreal.
TK: That's true. What happened was, a newly formed team between two singles competitors had stolen our tag team belts. The feud was going pretty weak, but non the less it was signed on GCW's pay-per view event. While we were ready to get our tag titles back, we found ourselves wrestling a team by the name of the X-Cops. A truly talented team in their own right, but at no time were we told we would be facing a mystery team instead of the two men we intended on wrestling.
KS: That's right. We lost the match, and lost respect for the owner of that organization very quickly. The UEW wasn't a bad company.
They just had too many wrestlers signed at one time. It was pretty hard to get things done when you've got about 10 tag teams, nine of which wait for the suits to tell them how to work instead of coming up with ideas on their own.
TK: After treading water in the tag circuit in the UEW, we finally decided to get off our asses and make things happen on our own instead of waiting. We ran into the team of Fire & Ice who at the time had titles around their waists. We chose to work with this team because we believed they were getting too much credit for the work they put it inside and outside the ring. They agreed to go to the people in charge with our idea, but simply got lazy when it was time to put it into action.
KS: We quit the UEW right after that, because we didn't feel like sitting around, thinking of ideas to make the tag circuit better and then see our plans not taken into action because people got lazy.
Reporter: You mention the quality or should I say the interest in tag wrestling. Do you think it's a possible fix or a lost cause?
TK: Well, we had this problem in GCW. Tag wrestling just wasn't getting the attention we believed it deserved. It was the match that everybody got up and went to the bathroom during. That pissed us off because we believe tag wrestling if done right is more exciting than any singles match.
KS: It's a real shame to see how tag wrestling has gotten in the wrestling world today. Wrestlers just don't want to be a part of a tag team anymore because of the extra work that goes into it I guess.
Reporter: You two are known for your daring high flying moves, and you've been doing them for about almost two and a half years now, can you think of one spot that you've performed that just stands out?
KS: Actually, I can. We were involved in a hardcore tag team match and there is this one spot where Tommy helps setup our opponent onto a table, I then climbed a twenty foot ladder.
TK: Mind you, this was before the ladder through a table move became popular on television. This was in maybe 1997 or 98.
KS: Right. So there is this great shot of me practically praying for my life at the top of the ladder because I couldn't believe what I was about to do. I then performed the Nightmare Before The Dream on my opponent off the top of the ladder through the table. The crowd went insane.
[A clip of Stanley performing an Off The Top Rope Corckscrew Moonsault
Splash off the top of a ladder through a table is shown, and then we cut back to the interview.]
Reporter: That move is by far one of the most amazing moves I've ever seen, how did you know you could do it?
KS: I have no idea. It's like one of those things where you know you can touch your nose with your tongue, or you can bend your finger all the way back to your wrist... you just know.
Reporter: You guys were on the half year awards in 1999 as number 37
I believe. How did you take the news?
TK: To be honest we were surprised. We knew that we deserved it, and deserved maybe better. But we didn't expect to get any credit at all for our work. To be on the list with some tag teams that we respect was a great honor. However to be on the list ranked higher than some teams that we can beat, and that don't deserve anything they've gotten
... sort of pissed us off.
Reporter: What do you think makes a great tag team? KS: Some people say teamwork, and communication. But the truth is, you've gotta have an owner that understands tag wrestling. They've also gotta know how to get the tag team circuit over, which sadly we haven't seen many owners do.
Reporter: Right now you're not working, any federations contact you or vice versa? TK: We've contacted Nick Demola about working, but received no answer. We haven't really contacted anybody else, we might wait for them to contact us now.
Reporter: In GCW you guys seemed to have a turn around in your characters. At first you guys didn't have an edge as heels, but something happened and you guys got some credit for your work. What caused that change?
KS: Well, we were in GCW for maybe two years total I guess? Maybe less who knows. But for more than half of that time we tried to be the goofballs of the organizations. Because it appeared that laughter made the front office happy in GCW. It wasn't about talent, it was about how hard you laughed at the segments. So we got alot of heat in the locker room for way too long.
TK: So what we did was, we decided to screw with the wrestlers we weren't supposed to screw with. We started a feud with one of the most popular singles wrestlers in the GCW, Viktor Karasopolis ... we were supposed to lay down for this retirement match, but we wouldn't have any part of it. He brought in his friend Tim Dyson, and we had a quality feud which we ended up ending at the ppv with a victory.
KS: We didn't care what the locker room said about us anymore. It was always about pleasing them, but now it was about pleasing ourselves and the fans. No matter what your alliances is ... heel or face, you still have a job to do. And we did it.
Reporter: One last question guys... what's next? TK: Whatever we decide is next. We want to work, but we want to work for an organization that knows how to run a tag team circuit. We want the shot to wrestle tag teams that are supposedly the best. We are up for the challenge, we haven't had the chance to wrestle a tag team that is known through out the wrestling world yet and we are ready to do just that.
KS: We'd also like to walk around with some gold on our waists. Somebody once told us that we need the tag titles to keep our pants up, and you know what it's true. But we want tag titles that mean something, and that are respected by everyone. We wouldn't be in this damn business if we weren't in it for the belts.
Reporter: I wanna thank you guys for your time. I hope to see you guys working in a company that treats you guys as a talented tag team.
TK: No problem. The tag team trendsetters are always willing to speak their mind because we are the tag team of the past, the tag team of the present.
KS: And the tag team of the future. [The camera fades out as they shake hands with the reporter.] [name withheld] [detail withheld]
