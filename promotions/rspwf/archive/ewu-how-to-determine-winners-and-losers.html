How to determine winners and losers

EW University

<><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> HOW TO DETERMINE WINNERS & LOSERS IN E-WRESTLING <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> by [name withheld] This essay is for fedheads of e-wrestling leagues using the Human Judgement System, and simulator feds that use sims only to construct matches, not determine results. One of the things that can really break a fed is bad decision making in terms of who wins or loses matches. Many times I`ve seen in e-wrestling leagues, fedheads have pushed their friends or even worse, themselves, simply because of who they are. In the meantime, people who are giving a lot to the fed, and trying their best to entertain, are made to lose to people who simply don`t deserve it. It is fair enough to expect defeat during your time as an e-wrestler, but all too often, bad calls are made, and people lose. Players don`t mind losing as long as the loss is fair, but if you`ve been churning out angles, flashes and strats only to lose to the feds non-playing brother who is the world champ, for example, that player will not stay. As a result, the fed is weakened. One of the key elements to a successful, enjoyable, long lasting fed is to make sure the results are fair, and that ultimately, those who work the hardest to make sure the fed is fun, entertaining and full of excitement, are rewarded with victories. There`s an old saying - ' You reap what you sow.' That is exactly how it should be for players, and it is the fedheads duty to make sure that the effort that is sown by the players is reaped. So, how does the fedhead, running a Human Judgement System fed, determine who should and should not win? There are four general areas that should determine a players success. They are as follows. [1] ROLEPLAYS. Probably the staple method in which players are judged. In 'real' wrestling, wrestlers that are pushed tend to get more air-time or are involved in plots etc. The decisions made in 'real' wrestling about who gets a push comes from the fedhead or booking committee. In e-wrestling, it is up to the player. The players has to make their own air time (by flashes, interviews etc), and create their own heat. The players that do this succeed, because they are bringing entertainment and interest. Players that provide entertainment are considered valuable by fedheads and as such, should be rewarded with victories and belts to keep them in the fed. By no means should the person who does the most flashes be considered the best. Certainly, the effort they are displaying should be taken into consideration, but mostly, it should be judged by the quality. Each fedhead is different as to what they consider quality - some prefer the tough talking approach, whilst others prefer a comical style. Ideally, as long as the 'flash' sounds realistic to the persons character`s style, it should be considered of quality. Flashes shouldn`t be meaningless babble either. They should be either talking about a past, present or future opponent, or should be a character developing flash, building up the psychology or life of the wrestler, making them appear to be more real, adding more depth. In sub-summary, there is [a] The Fight RP and [b] The Character Development RP. Both are important, and it is a sign of a good player who can do both. Fight RP`s should go some way to creating heat for a match or feud, a Character Development RP`s should go some way to rounding out the characters life, or adding to an angle. Both are essential to a good, well rounder, interesting character. A good flash is something that other people in the fed will read and will be entertained by, either in a comical, dramatic or whatever manner. Remember, as a fedhead, you are basically providing an entertainment service. Reward those in your fed that achieve that for you with via the flash system. [2] STRATEGY. People that provide strategy for a match should also be rewarded, because it shows, in a way, the wrestler himself is prepared for the match and also that the player is really trying. Again, it depends on the fedhead as to how big the strats are that they want, and what deal they go into. The player is active and interested in the fed - in other words, the kind of player you want to keep. The other kind of strat is the Interference strat, whether it is a run-in, or a 'grab the mic mid-match' kind of interference. These create controversy and is thus good for the fed. [3] FED PARTICIPATION. Many feds are a multi-person organization running the league, and also have characters in the fed. If this is the case, if people are writing matches, running web-sites or whatever they are doing to help improve the fed, do not feel slow at rewarding them. If their participation is essential to the running of the fed, keep them. Equally though, if they help write lots of matches, but do not roleplays, for example, you`re going to piss off a lot of people if they win, because as far as they`re concerned, they`ve lost to a jobberoni. [4] ANGLES. What makes a fed interesting is not really the matches. They`re the by-product. What keeps people coming back, keeps them reading, is the angles - the stories - behind the fed, between the characters. Players that work on angles, that get people reading, that generates interest, are perhaps the most valuable, otherwise you might as well be watching WCW Revenge on demo mode. Angles give fights meaning, generates history and conflict...gives the fed a purpose. In short summary, if players contribute something to a fed, they should be rewarded...unless of course, the person they are fighting is contributing more. Other miscellaneous notes about deciding who wins... [1] FEDHEADS AND THEIR CHARACTERS. The only time that the rule [3] concerning fed participation shouldn`t apply is with the fedheads themselves. Even if they are roleplayin the ass off everyone else in the fed, they should not hold a championship. It proves nothing. If a fedhead wants to get around this, I suggest they enter via an alter-ego, but ultimately, it`s super-tough to have total objectivity about your own character. If you want to be a player, go be a player in another fed. If you must have a player in your fed, make sure it is only as a heat-creator. [2] LONG TERM OR SHORT TERM JUDGING? So how do you decide? Do you judge a result on the participation for just that fight, or do you take into account the participation of that player of the past month or so. The only answer to that is that it is a bit of both. It certainly is the area where it gets a bit complicated, and really really have to rely on judgement call. [3] HOW DO YOU KNOW IF YOU`RE GETTING IT RIGHT? There are several ways. First of all, ask the players. Ask them if the champions are worthy, if the rankings are roughly just, who should be doing better...these are the people who you are entertaining, and who are entertaining each other. Do not feel ashamed to use them as a barometer for you decision. The other way is to look at the quality of the fed. If you`ve got a lot of participation, if you`ve got a lot of angles, morale is good. You`re doing something right. Yet another way is to see how many complaints you get. It may be tied in with the first point of this bit, but if you find you`re getting a lot of complaints, or good players dropping out, you`re doing something wrong. [4] THE EXCEPTION. Perhaps there are other exceptions, but there are only two exceptions I can think of to judging fairly in the manner described above. The first is if you are deliberately giving a player a big push for a very good reason, for the good of the fed (i.e. You need to build up a really good face). The second if there has been a strat by another player that would cause a player to win or lose, and is good for the development of an angle, or heat. That`s pretty much it. Bottom line is to be as fair and impartial as you can, and reward those who show commitment and enthusiasm to your fed, for they are the ones who will make it strong. If a player doesn`t participate in any of the ways mentioned, do not be scared to make him lose - it will either send a message and they will start playing, or it is the beginning of bye-bye. I leave you with an extract from the War! Players Guide 2.3 "I used to proclaim that WAR! used the Human Judgement System, trying to determine rationally who won. But who the hell was I kidding? All along it was the JKJS - The [name withheld] Judgement System. It`s not a bias system. It`s a fair, booked system. If we think you`ve worked harder than your opponent, you should get the victory (assuming nothing else happens!) but at the end of the day, it is the fedheads call, so you`re just going to have to accept the verdict. We try our best to get the right results, but on occasions, your opinion will differ from the fedhead. If you lose, you have to accept it. The measure of the man is whether he can come back fighting from defeat. The fact is, sometimes it is too close to call in WAR! because so many of the players produce equally high standards of work. There is no favoritism though, there are only what we hope are fair results. We can do no more than that."
