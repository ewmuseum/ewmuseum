Before I answer some of the comments of this misbegotten soul, let me say that I am finding myself having to deal with questions I have answered previously in this thread. So as to spare the complainers, I will simply direct those comments to previous points. Also, though I would be willing to leave sleeping dogs lie (of course, some of you would love to call them cats since you believe you can call anything whatever you want), I also see claims that I do not have answers to other statements. This is one of those posters, so I issue this warning to all those who hate this sims vs. booked fed debate. The warning -- skip it, do not read it, hit "cancel" or whatever so as to spare yourself of writing profane babblings where you roleplay as the moderator of this group. Now that you have been warned: [detail withheld] (DPage23295) wrote: >In article <54ua9f$ci6$ [detail withheld] >, [name withheld] >< [detail withheld] > writes: > >>Kent < [detail withheld] > wrote: >>>Janis let me state some points of mine. >> >>First of all, learn to spell "scourge." > >Irrelevant criticism. You are the only one that claimed spelling was >important to role playing or this debate. I have noticed that you have a >tendancy to pick on one's spelling, while ignoring the subject of the >statement itself This thread began with criticism of the "I weel beet yew" flash. Notice the misspellings. Thus, not only is the writer criticized for a short flash but for his poor spelling as well. Thus, if booked feds are going to penalize people for poor spelling, their poor spelling should be criticized as well so they can see how it feels. As far as ignoring the statement itself, I do that only when the spelling or grammar is so poor that I cannot make sense out of the post. >>> > >A programmed simulator will favor certain stats. WLS 5.1 favors speed and >conditioning rather heavily. If it does not favor certain stats, all >characters created have 33/33/33 odds of winning/losing/drawing. Who says >that I agree with what the programmer felt was an important stat? Sims >have their own forms of bias. At least it is based on something to do with wrestling, not just jabber. > > >Sims do not wrestle, they compare numbers. Yes, they compare numbers based on wrestling attributes that play roles in wrestling matches. > The sims that are currently >out there cannot acuratly simulate the physical side of wrestling, as it >is too complex. If they are totally hopeless, no one would buy them. They are getting better and better, though. And certainly they do a much better job than writing contests. > I am not saying that booked feds are more realistic than >simmed feds, but I am also not saying that simmed feds are more realistic >than booked. Sim feds give a much more accurate account of who would win a match because they compare and contrast attributes that would play a role in a real match, such as strength, stamina, size, etc. Booked feds stress the mouth. And that has nothing to do with how a wrestling match would turn out. > If you wish to simulate the booking aspect of RL pro >wrestling, then you cannot rely only on a sim. The fact that these feds >simulate the booking aspect of RL pro wrestling is why they are called >"booked" feds and there is no need for any other name. Booked feds only take the mouth into consideration. That also is unrealistic because it does not take into account the jealousies, salaries, egos, etc. that cause a match to be booked the way it is in real wrestling. If you're gonna go the insider route rather than the mark route, the mouth is just the tip of the iceberg. You're gonna have to go a lot deeper than that if you want to emphasize the booked aspect of feds. Not just the mouth. > >>>- Booked feds are like real wrestling just for that reason. They are >>>booked! Vinny Mac and Eric B. don't make their decisions via TNM! >> >>No, they make them out of jealousies, salaries, who their friends are, >>etc. Is that included in your booked fed or is it just a lot of talk? > >Are a wreslter's eating and excercize habits figured into a simmed fed? Uh, no, but stamina and conditioning are. >How about the injuries he sustained in last week's match? Uh, he didn't get hurt. > How about the >fans throwing trash into the ring and making the surface slippery? Not all matches are ECW, you know. Besides, in *real* wrestling, if that happened those people would be escorted the heck out of there. > What >about exposure to common colds and flus? You're stretching it. > How about the altitude of the >city the match takes place in? Duh. Same for both wrestlers. > Your refutation does not stand because a >simulator cannot take every single factor of a physical match into account >any more than the head of booked fed can cover every single aspect of >booking a match. Who said a sim does? Does it have to take everything into account? For someone who just goes by the mouth to do a match, you certainly expect a lot out of a sim! A sim is much, much more scientific than the mouth. It may not take everything into account, but at least there is a simulated match decided by wrestling attributes. > >This depends on if you wish to simulate the booking side of wrestling or >the physical side. Don't tell me booked feds simulate the booking side of wrestling because they don't unless you take the jealousies, egos, contracts, relationships, etc. into account as well. To say you are covering the booked side of wrestling because you emphasize the mouth to decide matches is like saying you have your butt covered because you didn't take all the toilet paper out the last time you wiped. > >>>- Booked feds have more member activity in most cases >> >>Not the feds I am in. > >This is not a good refutation because it specifies only the feds that you >are in. Duhh, you spoke from your experience when you said booked feds have more member activity. You have no empirical evidence to support that. So I spoke from my experience. > You are appling something that occurs in a part to the whole. >This is a common logical fallacy. No, I was just proving to you that your subjective opinion is just that, subjective. > Also, the orriginal statement is >ultimately unprovable. Duh, *you* made the original statement! > >Work and fun are not mutually exclusive concepts. Let me say this. If you think writing a roleplay is hard work, then you should get off your duff and get a job because roleplaying is not hard work at all. If you think it's hard work, then you probably aren't very good at it. Writing should come naturally and it should flow. If you see it as hard work, it means you are struggling with it. And if you are struggling with it, there may be many reasons for it. One may be that booked feds "force" you to roleplay to win. So sometimes you are *forced* to write a long, dull roleplay so you can win a match. It doesn't flow and it isn't easy. Thus, you may see it as "hard work." Blame that on booked feds who run by a version of the university motto: "Publish or perish." In the roleplay leagues, it's "Write or lose." When that happens, you are forced to write and it seems like hard work. I have never felt I was forced to write in the leagues I am in because I never felt I would lose a match if I didn't write a long roleplay. Thus, I write when I want to and the roleplays come easily. I'm not saying they are always great. Some may be stupid. But that's fine. I write for my enjoyment and if the others don't like them, they don't have to read them. > >One should point out that you have made the following arguments repetedly, >with nothing new added since the weekend this argument started: How many ways are there to say a dog is a dog? > >1. Booked feds are not true ewrestling feds. [You do not have the >exclusive right to define ewrestling. The term 'ewrestling', as it has >commonly been used in RSPWF, refers to all forms of feds: booked, simmed, >and otherwise.] This has been discussed earlier on in the thread. > >2. Booked feds are biased and simmed feds are not. [Simmed feds are >biased based upon what the programmer(s) feel are the most important stats >(or sometimes through simple programing errors).] Uh, machines cannot be biased. Human beings can be. Sims may favor one attribute over another, but that is not bias. Bias is when I say, "I like wrestler A but not Wrestler B because he didn't treat me well." The sim doesn't know the name of the wrestler or the handler. It simply decides the matches based on the attributes. > >3. Booked feds are unfair because they remove inactive members. >[Expulsion due to inactivity/not providing support is an accepted standard >in many organizations, both recreational and professional. But not without working with the people first and trying to keep them in the fold. This is sick. > If you do not >pay club dues, you may not take part in club activities. Bad analogy. No money involved in e-wrestling. Booked feds take just one into account, the mouth. The simmed feds take a whoooolllle lot more attributes, characteristics, etc. into account to decide matches than you do in your so-called "booked" feds. >6. Now you may not like it, it may hurt to hear it, and you may get mad to hear me say it, but that doesn't mean it's an insult. If I tell you there's a pimple on your nose, you may get angry and think that I insulted you because you are embarrassed at the fact that there is a zit on your schnoz. But hey, what's there is there. > >Can anyone else think of anything I have missed? After all, I am human >and fallable. Remember that the next time you book a match. >
