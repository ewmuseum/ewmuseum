Roleplay feds

11 February 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
<< I would like some info from some of you that run your own feds. I have been in a certain fed for about 8-9 months now, and am about to quit. Both of the co-presidents have told me many times that I am a good roleplayer, yet in all my time there, I have had only 8 matches (there are at least 5-6 cards a month, usually more) and only 2 wins. This is a roleplay based fed. >> How many matches do the other handlers usually get (if you're in a large fed, you will tend to get fewer matches)? Also, are you actively challenging or waiting to be challenged? << efforts to please the higher ups, I still lose, and lose badly. I have asked them what I might do to correct this. I received replies from both guys, and each said something different. The guy who had held the league the longest said that I was ok and should hold on. The other said go singles. Yet I still lose... >> If you don't think they're being very helpful, let them know it. If you still feel as though you're being shafted, consider joining a different fed. << My questions to all of you: If you have a roleplay based fed, how do you decide who wins? Do you ever keep someone from winning because you don't particularly care for their gimmick? Do you let someone win because you have met them online (ie. chat room, bulletin board, etc.)? And do you think I am right in quitting a fed where I am obviously getting nowhere? Andy >> There are some fed heads that do show favoritism. Usually, thier feds don't last too long, or have a high turnover rate, because the other players realise what's up and drop out. Also, if you aren't having any fun, you should drop out. This is supposed to be fun. If anyone asks you why you dropped out, tell them exactly why you dropped out (but be polite). At the very least, you may get them to think about just how objective they are, or are not, being. ************************** << Even better idea -- don't join any booked feds and just be in sim feds where you won't have to wonder if you're getting jobbed out of subjectivism or not. >> Assuming, of course, the fed head of the simmed league is actually being objective himself. You can adjust push (rather radically) in TNM, and you can give bonuses to wrestlers in WLS. Furthermore, the fed head could just ignore the results and make up whatever outcome he or she wanted. Nothing, other than careful observation of the fed, will let you know if the fed head is truly objective. Deric ************************************************************************** **************************** ** The light at the end of the tunnel is a high-powered ** [name withheld] ** ** sunlamp. You now have skin cancer. Have a ** [detail withheld] ** ** nice day! :-) ** ** ************************************************************************** ****************************
