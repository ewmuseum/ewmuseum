"The Bard" Johnathan Shakespeare Introduction Rp for the GLCW{Criticque please}

13 February 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Greetings to RSPWF, Please could you comment on the role-play which is enclosed below. He is a relatively new creation. Yet I seem to be doing well with him so far. Anyway he has recently been accepted into the GLCW and is looking to compete in one more federation which is a quality federation with my criteria.
<>Full Commentary Cards[ABS or PBP] <>At least open for 4-6 months <>Good decent talent. <>All in all a Good place to be So from that let the offers begin. But first of course, read this sample RP. ================================= "The Bard" Johnathan Shakespeare ================================= [Fades in as a black limousine is seen pulling up outside the GLCW headquarters. It then stops still and then out of the right hand side, a fine Gentleman is seen stepping out of the limo. The Gentleman stands
5'6", has light greyish hair and is dressed in Shakespearian Costume, consisting of a purple velvet tunic, a pair of rather elegent silk trousers and a rather stylish frilled white shirt. The Gentleman then then goes around to the other side of the car, he then opens the limo door and then out steps a rather stylish, yet pugnaceious lady who has black hair and wears leather red pants. Both the gentleman and then Lady then turn towards the cameras and then begin a few comments.]
JS - " 'Tis be it. 'Tis be the place where the light shines brighter then the morning sun and the place where there is nothing but raw arroganace and bliss. Yet cans't thou utter those meaningful words of spoken gestures unto my ruptured heart which bleedeth for mercy?
[The Gentleman then turns to the lady who then in a form of Soliloquie begins to speak.] LJM - "Oh Johnathan, what blackest hour thou needth. O what of that, Oh what ruptures brings us to this place. Cans't this be it? O what of that, oh wounded place. For my heart bleeds and bleed it shall for 'twill be 'twill be."
[The Lady then turns to to the Gentleman, who then continues.] JS - "Oh 'twill be the way. 'twill see what prevailing winds doth bring, oh cast out like the stones of the blackest hearts. O what of that? 'tis deep and sordid place, cans't call it home, twill be the way, for all shall see just what the ancient forefathers of The Bard himself hath spoken. O out, call out unto the names and thou shalt bring forth new light, for the light shall always prevailith like the prevailing winds.
[Pauses] Likewise 'twill be the same here within the GLCW. Some shall come, some shall surpass me by. Sam Willis, Kurt Orr, Luke
Steele. These 'tist, shalt thou surpass me by, for thou shalt all know just why the forefathers of Shakespeare himself hath come. Why thou hath entered into my self esteem and why thou soon shalt know why, Dreams doth come, but men Make dreams!"
[The Gentleman then turns to the cameras for the last time, he then utters this soliloquie.]
JS - "O that 'tis to sullied flesh would melt, thaw, and resolve itself into a dew, or that the everlasting had not fixed.
'Tis place, the GLCW, they shall soon know just why like the flesh thaweth in the winter, the Bard himself hath arrived and hast now a cause to dramatise all, starting with the one who knows nothing but thinketh he seeketh the heat.
Or that the everlasting had not fixed. His cannon 'gainst self- slaughter. Oh God! God! Oh what a rouge and Peasent Slave he is!
Patrick, seeketh the heat. Seeketh the light, for you shalt know why the Bard hath spoken.."
[Fades out as the names "The Bard" Johnathan Shakespeare and Lady Jane McDuff flash across the screen.]
Thank you for reading this.
