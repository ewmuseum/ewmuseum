How to make the fans pop

DISCUSSION OF WHETHER OR NOT THE FANS MATTER IN E-WRESTLING or How to make use of 'THE POP' by Various The fans _do_ matter in e-wrestling... It's true. They _do_ matter. If you put your character over with the crowd in your in-rings... the more powerful of a star you make yourself out to be. For instance... Some guys like to put the crowd reactions into their in-rings. And that's what you _should_ do... but some guys do it very boring-like. Like this: [One Eyed Willy looks out to the crowd. They pop huge.] That seems a little boring to me. If the crowd is popping huge, you should make it look like they're popping huge. Does that make any sense to anyone else? Try this one... I think it should be done more like this: [One Eyed Willy looks out to the crowd. They pop _huge!_] If you ask me, that's a lot better. The exclamation point and underscores make the word stand out a little more. What I do is something like this: [DC]: "You fans really do suck..." [MONSTER HEEL HEAT!!!] That's what I would do. It makes it look like the fans are _really_ into it. But it's all in the way you think about the game. You have to imagine yourselves there in the arena. I do, anyways. Makes it more fun. Don't you think? The crowd reactions _do_ count if you ask me. The idea for this post was brought up in another thread. Someone, I'm not exactly sure who, but they said that there are no fans in e-wrestling. And I couldn't _disagree_ more. If the handlers hate your character and they want to feud with you... you're over. If you make it look like the fans are... you're over. You have to get attention. And one way of getting it, is huge fan reaction. Don't you think? [name withheld] Message 3 of 12 Re: The fans _do_ matter in e-wrestling... I don't believe handlers should write in crowd reactions for their own character. Too often, they're either too unrealistic or too simplistic. I've seen guys type in a generic "you suck" and have him get a monster pop. I've also seen guys who've had their character cut a great promo and had him recieve a lukewarm response. My solution? The fed head can have a third party member of the fed who has nothing to do with the angle or characters and has some free time to write up a reaction to promos depending on his or her opinion on how well everything was delivered. The handler typing up the promo would leave in brackets for crowd reaction from the third party. If the thirty party doesn't like what's typed, he'd type in a mild pop or little crowd reaction. When the handler sees the "crowd reaction" on the card, they'll have an idea what other people are digging about their character's promos and what people aren't getting into. It'll make people improve their promos when they know someone shows very little interest in them. -CJ >) Message 4 of 12 Re: The fans _do_ matter in e-wrestling... I'm going to go with CJ on this one. I can't stand guys who suck, or guys who have been in the fed for like 4 days writing how much the fans love them. Unless of course, you make it clear that the audiance is mostly your family and retarded children who think you're someone else. -- Brock Ali >) Message 5 of 12 Re: The fans _do_ matter in e-wrestling... Another good way to get your wrestler over is good announcer commentary during in-rings and matches. It's always a good idea to go ahead and add your own commentary when you write them and send them in, and let the Pres' edit as needed. As far as the "fans"' reaction, I have seen people make mistake after mistake when dealing with the crowd. You don't get a [HUGE POP] for saying "so-and-so smells like purple putrid piss particles!" on your first interview out, you have to WORK the crowd first. How many times has a wrestler come out for their debut match and gotten [HUGE POP]'s? It's unrealistic and it usually turns me off to a character. I usually try to make it so the crowd isn't into my character per say (unless the intro angle is just THAT good), but I try to at least make it look like their attention has been drawn. Then when the wrestler actually DOES something to earn their reaction, I build on it. It usually takes me about a month (i know that's fast, but ewrestling has faster timelines) before I start putting in any kind of real reaction by the crowd. And by then I can usually guage it by how many other wrestlers in the fed are responding to my character (the REAL crowd heat). As for this discussion, I think it's an interesting one. And I'll digress momentarily to rant about one of my particular pet peeves in e-wrestling. I hate... no, loathe... no, *DETEST* when a writer (be it a player writing his own entrances or a matchwriter) uses the words "pop" or "heat" in a description. To me it is the height of laziness because the writer can't be troubled to use the words "cheer", "boo", "scream", "yell", etc. etc. I abso-FRICKIN'-lutely can't stand seeing the word "pop" in an entrance because to me, it means the author isn't creative enough to use the language to describe the scene for the reader. Now, a previous contributor has said that the audience should be treated as an additional character, getting their own lines. I think there's some validity to that but my personal preference is to have bracketed descriptions to help me, as a reader, form a mental image of what I would be seeing on the TV screen as the "show" unfolds. Usage of the words "pop" or "heat" are insider references, short cuts which do *NOT* properly express what is happening but rather indicate the writer is too lazy to be descriptive. Yes, I know, we're all familiar with the terms and people recognize them. But they're "insider jargon" and they do *NOT* make for interesting reading. OK... off my soapbox for a while... Tyran hates "fades", I hate "pops"... `nuff said. Getting back to the topic at hand, I also agree with the previous statement that handlers can be excessive with giving their characters instant recognition from the crowd when they don't deserve it, whether it be in a positive or negative manner. My personal preference as a fedhead or a matchwriter is to remove control of the audience as a character from the players and place it in the hands of the people who *SHOULD* be in charge of them... the fedhead or the matchwriter. Matchwriters can be lazy too when all they do is cut-and-paste the players' entrances verbatim without editing them in the slightest. I've done it myself when I've been too rushed or lazy to do a good job of it. But if possible, the writer should *ALWAYS* edit the entrances to make them suit the particular atmosphere of the environment. For example, a character making his debut in a federation should *NOT* get a dramatic response from the crowd. So I'll edit those entrances that say that by putting in phrases like "polite cheer" or "warm round of applause". I think the fans *DO* matter and I also think fedheads should spend more time on making sure that the characters get responses from the crowds that are appropriate and *NOT* controlled by the players. However, in the rush and hustle of trying to get cards out on time, this is an element that's too often ignored. Just MNSHO. Patrick-Who-Will-Edit-The-Word-"Pop"-Out-Of-Your-Entrance
