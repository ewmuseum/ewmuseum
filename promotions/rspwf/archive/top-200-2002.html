Simon Solkoff 28. Nate McMannis 27. Billy Rock 26. Despair 25. The Scrayper Harkening back to the days of Simon Ezra, the man known simply as The Scrayper has made an impact like few other rookies. Stapleguns in hand, he made the Grand Isle his hunting ground... and now the EMWC will feel his pain. With his (even more insane?) sister at his side, we have only begun to witness this maniac truly at work. 24. Juan Vasquez Long regarded as vastly underrated by the wrestling community, this is the year Juan Vasquez kicked down the door to stardom. In the now defunct RCW, he had a strangle hold over the River City title as he feuded intensely with a certain "Cold Blooded" Texan and led the rebellious Ego MAX. Rumors persist that when the River City shut down, he was next in line for a title shot. He has since jumped to the two wrestling superpowers, GLCW and EMWC, and just now is finding his niche, leading the RCW invasion in GLCW (ironically) and reforming Ego MAX in EMWC as he makes a bid for the World Title. 23. Derek Irvin Not only is he perhaps the brightest up and comer in wrestling, Derek Irvin is maybe the hardest working. From GLCW to RCW to UWF to EMWC to GWC/MPW to BSCW to NEO, he can be seen almost anywhere at any time. While his stints in EMWC/RCW/GLCW/ UWF are or were short, DDI has made an impact elsewhere. In GWC/MPW he was a top contender, on the verge of a World Title reign. In NEO his stock continues to rise as he climbs to main event status. An amazing technician, Irvin is one to look for in 2003! 22. "Sadistic" Steve Liermann Calling this man underrated would be an understatement. A veteran in the ring, he has only recently begun to draw attention to himself. With the re-vamping of Xtreme Hardcore Championship Wrestling in late 2001, Liermann kicked things into gear in 2002. It was an uphill battle for him for he not only had to prove himself to the fans but he had to prove to his fellow XHCW co-workers that he&#8217;s more than just the most powerful man in the company, but he can back it up in the ring. He proved himself in many ways, one of the biggest was probably the formation of Absolute Power, a collection of XHCW&#8217;s elite. 2002 saw Liermann work an extended program with former tag partner The Juice, driving him out of the XHCW, as well as branding Ramsus Palantiri and shock the world by aligning with longtime rival Adam Simon. Most recently, Liermann has surfaced in WILD, decimating Cruiserweight Champion Rick Hanson, as well as appearing on Superslam II, losing in the first round to the late Spikyjim. 21. Corey Irons It should be no surprise to anyone that Irons made it this high -- after several years of struggling to get noticed, Corey Irons exploded onto the scene in 2002, wrestling under the GIW, GLCW and EMWC banner. Given how Irons seemingly came out of left field in 2002, it will be interesting to see just what Irons is capable of doing in 2003, now that he's proven himself. 20. Robert Kellan The family man from the Grand Isle took perhaps the most verbal abuse of any man in the company. Everyone in the know labelled him a wifebeater just because, but those in the know knew this man to be one of the nicest men in the company. Nice.. but also very brutal in the ring. He is one half of the current GIW Tag Team champions with Harisoto Mashima and is looking to add more hardware soon as he is slated to face Rembrandt for the Television title. Throw in the fact that he was named RSPWF Rookie of the Year and you know he is something special. 2003 can only bring better things for the man from Texas. 19. Simon Underwood Dark, brooding psychos are a pro wrestling cliche but this man is anything but cliche. Truly dangerous, Underwood has terrorized the recently renamed Empire (formerly the EWWF) and looks to go straight to the top leaving a wake of bloody bodies in his path. 18. Rembrandt Wrestling's journeyman, Rembrandt made a tremendous impact in both the River City and Grand Isle in 2002, capturing the GIW Television title in a match of the year candidate against City Jack, nearly sending him into retirement. He captured the Oita Pro tag team titles, going undefeated in a round robin tournement with Bram Black; the Killer from Kyoto is currently making waves in NEO and Oita Pro, as well as returning to the Grand Isle as Television champion. 17. Stevie Scott On the surface, it might seem unusual to see the Hotshot at #17, until you take into account that there were few people who matched Stevie's consistency this year. Having made a brief appearance in the EMWC as well as becoming one of the main players in the GLCW, Stevie Scott was one of the all-around most entertaining people to watch in 2002. As things begin to take shape in the new year, amidst rumors of a reformed Hotshot, we are clearly in store for more of the same Stevietainment in 2003. 16. Johnny Pleasence The Big Bad, was indeed, big and bad for this past year. Feuding with Luke Kinsey, and becoming a member of Redemption, in the EMWC, as well as having a successful final run in the UWF, Pleasence is shaking off the role of being a rookie, and is now looking at the top. Pleasaece, now a member of the GLCW, looks to continue his ways, attempting to put yet another fed, laying in his wake. 15. Shane Destiny Sweet Dreams has all the talent in the world. He showed the Grand Isle that you didn't have to be 6'5" 270lbs. to toss people on their necks and battle with intensity unmatched. He has the heart, determination, drive, and willingness to defeat the odds that'll carry him to bigger and better things in 2003.. as he works the major circuits after being defeated in a Loser Leaves Town match against Jamie Roth in GIW. Beyond that, Destiny's also lit up rings under the RCW, EMWC and GLCW banner, establishing himself as one of the premier performers in the business. 14. Tim Sanders An undeniably strong year for The Priceless One, 2002 saw Tim Sanders win the GLCW's prestigious Powertrip tournement and go on to capture the World title at Between The Devil & The Deep Blue Sea. Sanders has also given an air of legitimacy to the position of GLCW commissioner, a role that often has a high turnover rate in this business. Despite having lost the title at Victory Blues, Sanders is still far & away one of the most valuable players in the GLCW today, with aspirations of a second title run in 2003. 13. Mark Langseth The man that's considered the franchise of the EMWC, yet again, pulled off quite possibly the most shocking turn of events, by ending the reign of terror, of Redemption, and having "Extreme" walk out of the famed War Games match. Langseth had yet another incredible year, feuding mostly with Jake Shaw, winning the tag team belts, and was involved in some of the more classic matches in the history of the EMWC. Yet again, Langseth is the pillar of the EMWC, and a true legend. 12. Tommy James A man who's name has become synonymous with the Great Lakes, 2002 saw "Mr. Workrate" come out of retirement for an extended feud with Chris Courtade, a program that saw the James start out as a hated veteran and finish as the beloved champion of the Great Lakes. James' position on this list should be no shock to anyone, when you consider that he was all but retired at the beginning of the year, and came back to prove to the world that he still had it in him to be one of the very best in the business. 11. Luke Kinsey Kinsey, whom many consider the future, had a stellar year, ending if off by ending one of the most improbable winning streaks ever, and winning the EMWC's North American Title. Kinsey, who had been a cornerstone for the success of the now defunct RCW, showed signs that this position, is only the basement, not the penthouse. In the year 2003, look for Kinsey to climb to top. 10. Steve Spector The longest reigning EMWC World Champion in history, finally achieved his goal. Spector, who defeated three others, in the last Showtime Main Event, climbed to the top of the EMWC, and quite possibly the top of business. A man that rarely gets the recognition he deserves, may finally be getting just that. While many still don't notice the efforts of Spector, those in the know, understand how remarkably consistent he's been over the years. 9. Leviticus Nelson The Grand Isle Terror. The Monster. This seven foot, four hundred pound behemoth tore the roof of the Sanddollar Marina in the Grand Isle since the first night of its insurrection.. casting himself and "Bad Eye" McBaine through a car windshield. From there he scrapped with every big name in the company and downed them all.. his size, anger, and willingness to never say die making him one of the toughest men to ever grace the rings of the Grand Isle. 8. Tony Cameron The current reigning GLCW World champion, 2002 saw Tony Cameron transform from a dark horse contender for what was then considered a minor title into a seemingly unstoppable force and one of, if not the most respected champion to come through any promotion in years. Cameron's thirty minute draw with Chris Courtade, his mauling of Tripp Shade and his performance on all three GLCW PPV events all but earned Tony Cameron a place in wrestling's hall of fame; even in light of upcoming challenges from the likes of Chris Courtade, Tim Sanders, Devon Case and Hannibal Carver, it would be no shock to anyone to see Cameron hold onto his gold for the entire year. 7. Jake Shaw The man many consider the most underrated in the business today, finally begins to get what he deserves. In what can only be considered the most improbable streak over, Shaw went undefeated, for an entire calendar year, taking on all comers, in his quest to gain the respect he's so sorely desired. Shaw, who's moniker of "The Machine", is as intense of a competitor as possible. His nearly year long ties with Mark Langseth, and his stance of tradition, and technical skill, were often lost in the battles of Extreme, in the EMWC. The year 2003 can only get better, as it's clear Shaw's yet to top off...The Machine's got a lot of damage to hand out. 6. Bad Eye McBaine What more can be said about the GIW Heavyweight Champion? He ran through the competition like a hot knife through butter.. defeating Leviticus Nelson at the first ever GIW supercard to capture the title and never relinquished it. At the Nightmare in New Orleans, he downed and seemingly ended the career of Russ Brady and is on track to face City Jack next month. Undefeated and perhaps unstoppable, that is the one they call "Bad Eye" McBaine. 5. Caleb Temple The Legend, plain and simple. What else can you say about a man that's had every possible honor bestowed upon him? Temple, who's battle with Adam Rogers shocked the world, yet again continued to reign over the EMWC with fear, and terror. His feud with Chris Courtade, a feud in which many considered to be one of the greatest of all time, saw the mad man lose in the end, but gain numerous respect in the process. Somethings change over the years, but somethings never change. Caleb Temple is still the man...no matter what year it might be. 4. Chris Courtade He dropped the accent, had a wonderful feud with Caleb Temple (that doesn't get as much respect as it should), and is currently harassing the hell out of GLCW wrestlers while kicking the crap out of them at the same time. So what's next for the Legend Killer? Well, that one's easy: it's time for him to get that all-elusive World Title (one he'll actually acknowledge these days), and 2003 will be the year for him to do so. 3. Devon Case Finally coming out of the shadows that have hidden him in the past, it's clear that Case has finally arrived. In a year that saw him dominate the EMWC, Case came out of middle of the pack, and became the lead horse. Defeating and retiring the legend, Tiger Claw, then turning his attention to the EMWC World Title, Case won, yet lost the belt, in a most controversial match, at Blood, Sweat and Tears. Leading Redemption, finally defeating Jake Shaw, casting aside the doutbers, Devon Case has finally made it, and he won't stop until he's destroyed everything in his path. 2. Hannibal Carver The South Boston Strangler has arrived. Tearing up NEO over the past year, Carver has destroyed many a career on the path to his one goal... the Heavyweight Championship. He is a hardcore as they come, and as his stay in GLCW has proved, can hang with the best of them on the mat. Suplexes, brawling and a can opener... all in a day's work for a man who seemingly came from nowhere to become perhaps the most feared man in wrestling today. 1. The Gremlin There is no denying that 2002 was one hell of a year for The Gremlin. In the EMWC, we saw him lead the charge against Redemption on top of defeating Alex Martinez, Robert Donovan and Chris Courtade to win the 2002 King of the Deathmatch tournement. In the GLCW, The Gremlin dropped the World Title at Dead by Dawn in February, but went on to come out victorious in long programs against Juan Vasquez and Stevie Scott, finishing out the year with the beginning of a best of seven series against Chris Courtade -- and if those matches are any indication of things to come, it's already looking like we could see The Gremlin at the top of this list next time around. The most intense, charismatic and well-rounded competitor in the business today, The Gremlin's connection to the fans and his insatiable need to be the very best in the industry put him a step above the rest this year.
