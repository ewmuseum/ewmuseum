Written by [name withheld] Egos. Like assholes, everyone's got one. It doesn't matter if you're black or white, straight or gay, full-headed or in need of Rogaine, you have some kind of ego. It's more or less an opinion of one's self. Some people have "normal" egos, and they think of themselves as equals with everyone. Some people's egos are too small. They are suicidal (Mainly.). They have virtually o self-respect and think that everyone else around them is better than them. And of course, there are people with large egos. Over-inflated egos. They are what most consider "assholes," "dickwads" and many other creative terms which I shall not delve into. (See: "'Playboy' Ronnie D," Webster's dictionary, pp 666.) There are many of them in the world, especially in the world of professional wrestling. One example of an over-inflated ego in the world of professional-wrestling is "The Heartbreak Kid" Shawn Michaels. Many consider him to be the reason Bret Hart left the WWF. He decides when he loses, how he loses and if he loses. Why? Many consider it to be somehow related to him giving Vincent K. MacMahon, Jr. blowjobs. I consider it to be that he simply has Vinny in his back pocket. But we can all agree that Shawn controls his character. So did Bret Hart, whom also had Vinny Mac in his back pocket, to a large extent. And they both tried to portray their characters how they wanted them portrayed. Why did they portray them as the "icon" of professional-wrestling and a great Canadian hero, respectively? Because of their egos, of course. They both wanted to have what they thought would make them "hip," "cool" and the best thing going. Look what happened. The same thing happens in e-wrestling. After all, like professional wrestlers, we are people and therefore we have egos. We also control a character, much like professional wrestlers. Back to the egos, we, much like professional wrestlers, have a wide variety of them. Big, small, and normal. Like professional wrestlers, our character is mosty what others see. So we, again like professional wrestlers, feel we must protect him. What happens when two or more people try to protect their characters? A collision. Head-on. Not pretty. We end up with terrible story-lines, poor sneak-attacks, and at least one person walking away unhappy from the whole thing because their ego has been deflated. All because so-and-so's character "would never do that." Or because so-and-so's character "wants to whoop his sorry ass!" And we end up with a situation akin to the Bret Hart - Shawn Michaels situation in the WWF -- someone leaving. What was the whole point of this diatribe? To encourage others that even though you may think it will look poorly upon you, and it may at the moment, to lose a match or commit a certain act, in the end your legacy will be if you help make this _game_ enjoyable for others. Not your win/loss record or anything else. The people that participated in this game will remember if they had a good time working with you towards a good story, or if they did not. The point of e-wrestling is to have fun WITH others, at least in my opinion, so why must we have fun at the expense of others? Of course, most of you who haven't a clue who I really am will say, "Who's he to talk about egos?" but I wouldn't expect more. Just keep this in mind when you work out a storyline with someone: It's about having fun WITH others, not AGAINST others. All comments and additions to this are welcomed, you bastards. This anal gripe has not been paid for by anyone because I'm using a freenet, so hah! Actually, you, the Ontario and Quebec taxpayers funded this, because that's where I went to school. Suckers! :)
