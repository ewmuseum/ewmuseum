[ESSAY] What makes a Great E-Wrestler? Here's my POV...

30 June 1997

I read Dan's post, and it contained several good points. The one about grammar, punctuation, and spelling is #1. Very few people have the time to decipher digital hieroglyphics in reading flashes. I just wanted to add a few pointers based on my own experience. 1. Don't be afraid to lose. Having never run a fed, I don't have personal experience on this, but I'd bet that very few things piss a fedhead off more than somebody griping about their wrestler's most recent loss. "How DARE you job me to him!" If you think the fedhead's screwing you over, leave. Life is WAY to short to blow a gasket over such a thing. But don't assume that's what's happening when you lose a match. The best feuds are back and forth exchanges, and if you lose a match, you have more incentive for the rematch. Also, in a booked fed, be willing to lose a match to advance a storyline. A good fed head will appreciate it, and reward you. 2. Don't stress over belts. A good fed doesn't make everyone a champion. When you join a fed, look specifically for a mid-carder, or another up and coming wrestler, to work angles and feuds with. Win, lose, or draw, it will give you experience RP'ing, get both your names out there, and make the fed overall a more entertaining place to be. Belts will come. In fact, they're almost impossible to avoid sometimes. This is, in my opinion, the IIWF's greatest strength -- the willingness of its handlers to participate in non-title feuds, giving its cards several meaningful matches up and down the lineup. The IIWF also wins every award/poll this side of Hogan's bald spot. Coincidence? Maybe, but I doubt it. 3. Avoid genuine heat. I know, I know, there are several wrestlers who have built their reputations on personally insulting other handlers, and maybe it works for them, but I wouldn't recommend it. Get in touch with people you want to run angles with, and work with them. If they're already involved in an angle, let the angle run its course. And always try to resolve disputes with fedheads and other players civilly and privately before going ballistic on RSPWF. Dissing them on RSPWF or flaming them may make you feel better, but to the rest of the world, you're just being a jerk. And nobody wants to join a fed with, or push, a jerk. This is a good thread. Post your own comments/observations. I look forward to reading them. [name withheld]
