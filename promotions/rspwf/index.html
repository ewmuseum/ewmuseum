The rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy newsgroup, running from an April 1995 founding into a final exchange in 2018, and RSPWF.com, the web face it wore from 2001 to 2006: an A–Z federation directory, a classified board for feds and wrestlers seeking each other, a column series on writing the hobby, and the year-end Top 200 awards the community voted on, held here across fifty-two award categories. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy is the community held here, and it had been running for years before its own web face existed — the 1997 edition of its own year-end poll cites a 1996 edition eleven times by rank, and no copy of that 1996 edition has been found. RSPWF.com, the site that face wore from December 2001 to February 2006, is the source of most of what's held: an A–Z directory of federations, a classified board, a column archive, rankings pages, a news desk, and a wall of 88×31 buttons for feds to link each other with — the furniture a newsgroup can't hold.

The directory is the part that reads most like a record, because every fed wrote its own listing. The fields are the same on all 146 of them — full name and acronym, website, owner, description, Keynote Members, Federation Champion, a description of the regular show and one of the pay-per-views — and what varies is how much of itself each fed managed to fill in. Some are complete down to the broadcast night and the arena's own name. Some are a name, a URL and nothing else. One gets as far as “Website:” and stops.

Beside it ran a classified board in four categories — Feds Seeking Wrestlers, Wrestlers Seeking Feds, Feds Seeking Services, Services Seeking Feds — and EW University, a column series on the craft itself. Thirty-four of those columns survive: how to determine winners and losers, how to make the fans pop, how many roleplays are enough, angle mechanics, egos, time management, one year of fedheading. They were already losing their own authorship as they were filed. One opens “I was not the original author of this. Can't remember who was either.” Another, two columns later, “Can't remember who posted this either.”

The Top 200 is the community's own reckoning of itself: ballots counted every January, two hundred wrestlers placed in order, and a paragraph written for the top twenty-five. Seven editions survive, ranking 1997 through 2004, and the compiler's own line changes across them like a hand-off caught in the record. 1998 credits a compiler “to the best of my knowledge”; 1999 reads “(There has been speculation that this was Gil's last year compiling)”; 2000, “Compiled by ??? (Certainly a different style, no write ups)”; 2001, “Compiled by ???”. The write-ups come back in 2003, thanks to a handful of helpers, and the 2004 edition opens on its own compiler counting forty ballots and swearing at the number.

How the site ended isn't in the surviving record. Its own dynamic pages are captured through February 2006 and then stop; the domain later carried squat spam belonging to nobody in this story. The poll outlasted the site: the last two editions held here were posted straight to the newsgroup, in January 2004 and January 2005, to a community that was by then counting itself in dozens rather than hundreds and saying so out loud.

Affiliations

Global Internet Wrestling Federation (GIWF, 1999–2001)

Present in RSPW-F (1995) (1999)
GIWF’s own run on the newsgroup is positively identified at roster level. “[GIWF] WE’RE BACK!!!”, 25 April 1999, sits cleanly after the wing’s own founding of February 1999; on 5 August 1999 a fourteen-thread [giwf]Meet: series introduced its roster one post at a time, naming Commissioner Rosso, Hamish McGrubber, The Cockney Rebel, Lew Spectre & The Phenonenons, and ten more — every character those threads name already appears on GIWF’s own held roster. Recruitment continued on the group into 2001 (“[GIWF] Looking for unknowns...”, 23 June 2000; “GIWF --come join”, 3 June 2001), though GIWF never carded a show there: its own show name, GIWF Global, never appears in the newsgroup’s own index.

Rampage World Wrestling (RWW)

Present in RSPW-F (1995) (2002)
RWW is listed by name in RSPWF’s own 2002 community fed directory, with the fed’s own self-description quoted in full.

Appalachian Valley Wrestling (AVW)

Present in RSPW-F (1995) (2001)
AVW posted its cards, roleplay packets, show notices and recruitment calls to the newsgroup under its [AVW] tag from January to December 2001; its roster's handlers signed their newsgroup posts with the tag throughout the year.

Japan Puroresu Association (JPA)

Present in RSPW-F (1995) (2000-10 to 2001-05)
The Japan Puroresu Association posted its results, Flash Bulletins, Battle START! broadcasts and press releases to the newsgroup under its [JPA] tag from October 2000 to May 2001.
