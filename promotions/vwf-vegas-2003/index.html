A Las Vegas federation that opened with a Wednesday-night card at the MGM Grand in August 2001 and staged its Vegas Violence and Phear Phantastic pay-per-views that first autumn, later running a season-long Dog Days elimination tournament and an annual 12 Days crossover invitational open to wrestlers from other promotions. Its final broadcast, in April 2005, stages an on-air sale and rename of the company. Seven title lineages, 71 shows and 134 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Vegas Wrestling Federation opened on 1 August 2001 with a Warrior Wednesday card from the MGM Grand, its president Rob Beauty announcing that night a one-night tournament for a first 24 Hour Championship, which Sebastian won the following week. Its first autumn added a Triple Threat title, a Tag Team Title Tournament and two pay-per-views, Vegas Violence at Cashman Field and Phear Phantastic at Sam Boyd Stadium, whose ten-man Rage In The Cage put every title on the line inside a two-story cage.

That first year's story was a war for the promotion itself. Rob's brother Ric Beauty, beaten by Kerry Powell in a Loser Leaves Nevada match, came back claiming half the company and set up a Beautiful Wrestling Federation inside it, with a jewelled championship of its own, while Demonic, Blaze and the Real Deal invaded from the defunct RCW. By December 2001 Demonic held the 24 Hour Championship for the invaders.

By 2003 its weekly show came from a venue its announcers called The Joint, mid-way through a season-long Dog Days elimination tournament. Its Birthday Bash of 1 August 2003 marked the promotion's “second birthday” and closed the tournament: Randall Crane won the vacant VWF Heavyweight Championship, and Scott Starring the 24 Hour Championship, the same night.

Its title ladder grew across 2003 and 2004 into Heavyweight, Tag Team, Submission, 24 Hour and Hardcore lineages, defended on weekly Firestorm broadcasts and settled at Vegas Violence and Gauntlet supercards. Each winter it also ran a 12 Days Tournament, staged as an interpromotional invitational; its 2003 running, billed on air as the tournament's “third journey,” drew a surprise late appearance from a self-billed “four time and current SWF Champion,” The Equalizer, returning from retirement to avenge a stablemate, Yancy Kilpatrick, killed off earlier in the arc.

Ownership became a running storyline in its final year: Kingston Cash, installed as majority owner at The Gauntlet 2004, was pushed out in early 2005 when an on-air angle had the IRS seize the promotion over talent funds traced to his offshore account. Its last broadcast, The Gauntlet 2005 (11 April 2005), closes with a returning Jason Storm announcing that he had bought the company, destroying its logo on the jumbotron and replacing it with three new letters as he declared it “going... XTREME.”

Affiliations

Vegas Xtreme Wrestling (VXW)

Successor to VWF (Vegas, 2001–2005) (April–July 2005 (VWF's own last document 11 April 2005; VXW's own earliest surviving broadcast 25 July 2005))
VWF's own last surviving broadcast, The Gauntlet 2005 (11 April 2005), ends its own on-air Gauntlet match with a returning character announcing he has bought the company; the on-screen VWF logo is destroyed and three new letters lower into place: V-X-W. VXW's own commissioner later confirms the same history directly, in the promotion's own words: Vegas Violence "kicked off the VXW, back when we were called the VWF." The identical posting-account address runs both tags without a gap, and a large set of VWF's own 2003–2005 wrestlers — including a VWF Heavyweight Title contender fighting under that same banner as early as 2003 — recur as VXW's own already-held wrestlers from VXW's own earliest surviving broadcast onward.

RAW Carolina

Crossover booking with VWF (Vegas, 2001–2005) (2002)
RAW Carolina and RAW-Vegas co-promoted Ghosts of RAW on Halloween 2002 with RAW-Ohio Valley, and the RAW Tag Team Champions defended on both promotions' cards.
