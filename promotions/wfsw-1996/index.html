A Usenet-native weekly TV-style promotion that crowned its first World Champion in a twenty-man battle royal at its own debut pay-per-view and ran two more title lineages alongside it before its own record breaks off less than four months later. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Fantasy Superstar Wrestling built its whole roster through open recruitment: its own president, in mid-January 1996, complained of “16 heels and only 5 faces,” and a March 1996 post closes the books outright: “we're no longer accepting wrestlers.” Every wrestler in its record came in through a written character-creation sheet; no real wrestler ever appears in it.

Its debut pay-per-view, Gold in the Cold '96, aired 29 January 1996 and crowned its first World Heavyweight Champion in a twenty-man battle royal: Harlequin Tragedy won by diving into the match's own final two men, Pitbull McGraw and Moo “Lonesome Lion” Kame, and eliminating them together, leaving himself the only man standing. The United States Championship, meant to be decided the same night, was punted instead to a rematch between those same two men the following week.

By its 17 April 1996 broadcast, Mark “The Crippler” Baker held the United States Title outright, retaining it over Gatekeeper — who insisted he'd handed Baker the belt in the first place — with a low blow into his own finisher, the Whiplash. The World Tag Team Championship, by then, belonged to the Returners, who defended it against the Harlequins on the promotion's own last surviving broadcast.

Its final posted card previewed a ten-match Showdown '96 supercard for 27 May 1996, built around a tournament and a “Steel Cage War” gimmick match staged by a masked figure calling himself Cobra 4000. No recap of that show, or any further document in the promotion's own hand, survives.
