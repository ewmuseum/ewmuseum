Column: SuperStars Under the Hot-lamp?

1 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
- BIG MONEY WRESTLING - SuperStars Under the Hot-lamp? [name withheld] - BMW Editor Friday, February 1st, 2002 It's nothing new that some of Big Money Wrestling's personalities get baked from time to time, but is the wrestling federation starting to really cook as they kick off in Prime Time Central? Actually, the attention is nothing new, and neither are the annoyances, but as the faces of millions of viewers turn to Big Money Wrestling, as many "fed heads" and recruiters will begin to grab at the heart of BMWF; the SuperStars. Reports of radical recruiters and extreme federation owners trickle in through small reports and freak-magazines. Cases of recruiting-gone-wrong destroy respectable federations, and lead potential top-notch talent to retire. This has little to do with Big Money Wrestling, no, but it has much to do with up-and-coming talent as they ready themselves for the pressure-cooker that is the media. Believe it or not, the intensity has hit members of the BMWF roster as early as November of 2001. Email spammers attack in groups of 10 and 20 in some cases, but worst of all, it's the repetitive invitations that spark the attitudes of some of BMWF's most dangerous. All in all, however, the majority seems untouched by it, and there is no problem to deal with, currently. Big Money's network and website is the cleanest means of communication and remains untouched by outsiders. Where the problem lies is in personal contacts: Messengers, Email, and means of direct communication. I spoke with two of BMWF's Triad on the issue and got the same general feeling. When asked about recent comments on recruiting from other feds, Tycoon responded, "Hmm... It doesn't bother me much, because I believe in the product that Big Money Wrestling delivers." "What ever they offer can't possibly be as good as what we have going." His confidence shows, and with reason. Big Money Wrestling has proven to be one of the most stable wrestling federations around. Quality and stability is not easy to beat and is often the downfall to some of the most well-known federations. "I don't think it will impact our roster much. He can try all he wants, but the beef of our roster knows that we are a top flight fed.", the man said in regards of Donomite's expressed concern for email-spam and repeated invitations to join other feds. Clearly, repeated invitations are annoying and have spawned the debate over this topic. Donomite has spoken out about repeated invitations to members of our roster and taken it personally over repeated emails listing "returning federations". Like a guard dog, he is patrolling the roster and speaking with other owners over the issues. When speaking with Donomite, he seemed to be more out on the level of a wrestler in that he wanted to limit contact information. Realizing that wrestlers have steps they can take to stop invitations, maybe the option of letting wrestlers keep their contact information a secret should be up to them in the future, and most likely will be in their hands. Louis Miranda shows the same concern with recruiters from other feds, but sided with Tycoon on how he felt about it. "I feel that Big Money Wrestling has grown to a level that many 'young' owners will look to build from. They see our writers as some of the best, it's more of a compliment then an insult.", he regarded. When asked about spam-mail and repeated invitations, Louis commented, "That is when the owners step in. If I believe that the writers that have helped make BMW what it is today are being harassed, I'm being harassed. Offering a person a spot in a fed is a compliment, but nagging and spamming? C'mon, we are more professional then that now-a-days. Well at least I am!" Where do you draw the line? Most wrestlers can only compete in a single fed, being legally tied down by their contracts, and thus so, they leave if they think they see a better federation. It can cause a lot of shockwaves when other federations try recruiting the talent of others', and has even caused the best feds to be shut down. Big Money Wrestling isn't in any danger of this, but the talent is truly the biggest part of a fed. The owners can't really draw a line, so it's up to the wrestlers in how they deal with the annoyances of being a SuperStar. This means dealing with the public. Former World Champion, Scott Phillips feels annoyed when other feds try to recruit him. "I'm pretty annoyed when fed-heads try to recruit me by e-mail or AIM.", Scott said, "It's very bothersome when I'm trying to work on things." The thought was passed by him in how to stop such things from happening so often. "Not having my e-mail address available on the roster page, deleting their e-mails, and blocking them on AIM", he suggests, although he doesn't feel the impact from constant recruiting and repeated invitations is a big problem for the fed. "Well, I'm not one to speak for all the other boys, but it's never been so much of a trouble that I would leave a federation due to it." Scott believes, "it's really inescapable if you think about it...you'll be scouted everywhere if you have a fair amount of talent." In the end, he says it's a way of life for veterans. He's dealt with it for the most part of the last 3 years and never seems to escape it. I caught Eiji Jusyhin Fjiwara in time to ask him what he thinks, "It is quite a compliment. I mean, knowing you are being recognized is a good thing. It helps me to concentrate on myself. Not get distracted and all." Recruiting and invitations are flattering, but as we've stated, too much can be too much. I asked him if it becomes a problem when too many federations start trying to recruit him, or when a particular fed invites him more than once? Sometimes a fed will spam with email and repeatedly try to get a wrestler to join. "It does become annoying. I mean I spend about twenty minutes every day sorting through federations trying to sign me. I just delete them in the end. It does actually get quite scary. You get psychopathic fed owners that even add you to their Personal Messenger contacts and attempt to speak personally. I mean it is highly flattering but people just have to learn that their is a line that you do not cross. I have personal contacts on mine and I do not really oblige to being added for hassle. I feel that they're certainly are more downsides then positives to the constant media attention and federation interests", Eiji says. It was then that the most shocking incident was brought to my attention. I asked if Eiji has ever been threatened and how extreme recruiting tactics have been. I've never come across threatening recruiters, but there's a first for everything, apparently. In Eiji's experiences, "Well only recently I was threatened by a certain owner that he would come and hack introvertly the BMW.com site. I found this very disturbing. I used no rudeness or spite. A simple "No." and then this owner went on a rampage, using vulgar language and etceteras. Some people like this do become quite scary. You just do not know what they really can do. Luckily this was only a scare recently. But I would not count out the fact it could happen again. With me winning the Puorese Tournament Wrestling even, I have been put into the spotlight even more so now then ever. Success has certainly hindered my pleasure of wrestling. Some people damper my pleasure of competing in BMW, due to their vulgar comments they make after a simple "No." But these people are normally blocked instantly and deleted." I have never heard of this type of incident happening, but it's painfully apparent that some Federation Owners go far beyond what is acceptable. Eiji concluded, "I try to keep away from the scandals of all the Federations circuits. For instance all the controversial hacking and monopolizing of wrestling, I personally do not agree with." Of everyone that was interviewed on this topic, they all feel that the contact information can be made more difficult to get ahold of, and that is part of BMWF's future plans. The information will be up to the wrestlers to distribute, and thus in their hands if they get into a recruiting trap. To be honest, the contact information is not needed on the SuperStar's websites. The forums allow for all the communication that is needed between wrestlers and staff. With the private message features, everything can be completed through the message board. There is no need to publish contact information on the website, and that's one way to complicate recruiting matters for outsiders. Some believe that invitation spamming and recruiting is not an issue, while others have had some pretty bad experiences, but one thing certain: it's basically up to the wrestler and how he handles it. The bottom line is that being a SuperStar comes with costs, but hopefully this won't be too big of a problem. Ways to avoid issues: - Block people on chat programs - Delete email and block email addresses - Allow your federation to know if someone is harassing you. Help give them a bad name. - Tell all of your friends about the owner and the federation so they can avoid them. Keep in mind that a single invitation from one guy is a compliment, but if they don't take no, then it's more of a problem.
