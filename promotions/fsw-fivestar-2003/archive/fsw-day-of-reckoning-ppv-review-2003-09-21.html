Day of Reckoning PPV Review (2 of 3)

Sep 21, 2003, 12:00:00 AM
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PROMO: Ricard confronts Riggins outside the arena. //GRADE\\: B -- As Darth Vader once said: "Impressive. But you're not a Jedi yet." Or something like that. I like how Ricard's colorful words are edited but NO ONE ELSE'S IN THE WHOLE CARD ARE.
Consistancy, man, consistancy. I didn't take off of this promo for that, since you get the impression the promo writer edited himself.
For that, I am grateful. This promo shows some excellent heat between the two forces and VERY NICELY sets up the main event later in the card. Very well put together.
MATCH: FSW TAG TITLE: America's Least Wanted v. Scarlett/Bratzke v. Youth Gone Wild v. Heaven't Hunted v. Mason Walker (Gauntlet match, with two teams left, match turns into Ladder Match)
(written by Ian) WHEW! Lots going on on this one. Elimination match, with the last two teams fighting it out in a ladder match. Only one "team" is a single person, and he's a big'un. You gotta know he's gonna be the last team in, and will have an inherent advantage in that the other team should be tired. Except for that whole 2 on 1 thing. Anyway,
America's Least Wanted(ALW for short) and Scarlett/Bratzke begins.
And ALW is taken out in short order by Bratzke and Scarlett. No, ALW didn't hit one single move. Youth Gone Wild rushes to the ring and gets immediately more offense than ALW did within the first sentence on the write-up. Some NICE match tempo and flow, with just the right amount of false finishes, and somehow the writer manages to put over
ALL FOUR of the wrestlers in this part of the match. A 450 legdrop by
Extasy (one of Youth Gone Wild) ends the match, but it's with a price, as Bratzke and Scarlett has done their damage, with Heaven's Hunted up next. So, HH (as their friends like to call them) head down with, and you get the distinct impression that these two teams have met before.
Distinctly different styles, with Youth Gone Wild both being aerialists, and HH being heel brawlers. Bunches of high spots, with
EXCELLENT match flow, but the ending was confusing to those who have never read a FSW card before. Youth Gone Wild wins with a top rope doubleteam move, but the move isn't described. With that win, we head to the finale, but Youth Gone Wild is hurt, and Mason is big, bad and
FRESH. The wirter does a good job of highlighting this fact. Walker heads down to the ring with his manager (I think) as the belts are lowered. On to the third section of the match.
GOD, I hate it when play by play or color commentators cuss. Again with the "don't cuss without reason" thing with me, and the heel color-man does so, lowering the rating automatically. Anyway, both of the Youth Gone Wild kids individually launch themselves at the much bigger Mason. He catches each of them, and hurts them. Mason plays off a rib injury from one of the Youths. Yes, the writer uses match psychology. After some EXCEPTIONAL match writing, one of the Youths are incapacitated after a table bumb, and Walker and the other Youth
(Extasy) are left. After some time, they both wind up on the ladder, hanging on to the title belts. The ladder falls away, Walker and
Extasy fall ... each with one of the tag belts. I've honestly believe
I've never seen this ending before. First one with both belts in his hands wins. Walker up first, about to take the win, when the OTHER
Youth (the one incapacitated earlier) hits a very descriptive chairshot, grabs the belt, and collapses. Youth Gone Wild wins the match, and they've gained a fan in a certain fedhead named Grant.
//RATING\\: 3.9 of 5. Would have been higher, if not for the needless cussing from the colorman. Other than that, EXCEPTIONALLY written match. Good flow, good psychology, good use of injuries in the match, just ENOUGH bumps, but not too much, and VERY good use of false finishes to make it interesting.
PROMO: Fire and Ice interrupt Youth Gone Wild's celebration. //GRADE\\: C- -- Fire and Ice are famous (I guess), "Fire" Perkins has a bunch of titles around his waste, and he looks like what Kane should have in the WWE. They cuss alot. They use "insider" terms in their matchs like "push" and "spot machine", because, y'know, that makes the team COOL, or something. They just want the fans to see a
"real" tag. Golly, I'm SOOO impressed. Not. It's fairly cheap when the promo writer has to use insider terms to get his team over.
Uninspired, unimpressive, and really ends the very excellent match writeup on a poor note. PROMO: Keiko Barnett and Remy Gonzales talk about how he's ... God. Maybe. Or perhaps he just really likes himself. //GRADE\\: C+ -- Good job at establishing their character is a short time, but again, it's not a promo one will remember for a long time. Above average, not more than that.
MATCH: Johnny Malibu v. John Shock v. Vandal Gomez (written by Ian) Again, this is a tale of a match that should have come BEFORE the tag title match, because this is most certainly a let-down. This is strange, since both were written by the same guy. Maybe he got tired.
Pace yourself, Ian. Gomez is a fire-obsessed albino, Malibu "jobs" to ringside (I don't usually complain about one or two words being misspelled, but, "JOBS" to ringside? Neat, considering that's what
Malibu does). Shock wins after a uninspired triple threat match by pinning Malibu with a Yakuza Kick.
//RATING\\: 1.9 of 5 stars. Despite the backstory, this match was misplaced, and should have been earlier in the match. YET ANOTHER heel turn. This is getting really repetitive.
