Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Invitational Championships opened as an inter-federation tournament, its founding call of 14 January 1997 asking fed heads to submit wrestlers by name, real name, and e-mail for entry. Entry was restricted to reigning world singles champions from other federations, and the bracket played out online through that February, with first- and second-round results posted to a Tripod-hosted site on 10 and 13 February.

The tournament crowned its first champion on 21 April 1997 in a broadcast staged live from Madison Square Garden, called by commentators Derek McMahon and Robbie “The Genius” Keenan. The Deranged Animaniac, unbeaten through the bracket in his first run in wrestling, met The American Patriot, an eleven-time world champion elsewhere, in a best-of-three-falls final for the Undisputed Heavyweight Championship of the World; Patriot took it two falls to one, the deciding fall at 43:49, in a match long enough that the rest of that night's scheduled card went unplayed outright. The tournament's own organiser presented Patriot the belt in the ring and then broke his own rule on the spot, inviting Animaniac back for the following year's tournament as well as the champion.

The title's own name became a running dispute. In September 1997 the WIC polled its own participants on whether to keep calling it the Undisputed Championship, then dropped the “undisputed” label days later over the same disputes it had just asked about, replacing it with a year-round title defense: whoever held the belt on 1 January earned an automatic berth in that year's tournament, with no title matches outside it. By November, with the field for the 1998 tournament roughly doubling in size, the “Undisputed” name was restored and title defenses were made mandatory on demand, with no grace period between challenges.

The WIC also fell out publicly with a wrestling federation, All-Pro Wrestling. An October 1997 press release from its newly formed governing council barred APW wrestlers from entering and refused to recognize any APW title until the two organizations came to terms; by November the dispute was settled directly between the two feds' own principals, and APW wrestlers were welcomed back on the same eligibility terms as anyone else. That same autumn the WIC added a tag-team tournament, open to any team that had won a world tag title in 1997 or received a special invitation from its own vice president in charge of tag teams, and announced a merger with a separate tournament, folding that event's own December champion into the WIC and scheduling four more feeder tournaments to come.

The 1999 running, WIC99, was cancelled outright; its own organiser blamed the entrants directly — “NOBODY RP'D!” A reformatted WIC2000, announced that November, dropped the champions-only entry requirement altogether and opened the tournament to anyone: a Wild Card Battle Royal down to sixteen wrestlers, then a double-elimination bracket to crown the Undisputed World Champion of E-Wrestling. Its organiser was still recruiting participants as late as 14 December 1999, for a tournament scheduled the following year. Whether it ran is not in the surviving record.
