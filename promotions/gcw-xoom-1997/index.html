Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Championship Wrestling launched in December 1997 under three co-presidents; within a couple of months two of them had dropped out, leaving one to run it alone, take a brief hiatus, and keep it going with the help of volunteer match writers. Its own recruitment pitched a fed that “basis it's decisions on your roleplays, and not your ability to make a character in TNM,” explicitly warning readers not to confuse it with a same-acronym rival's “not being a kiddie partie” pitch.

Its broadcast of 23 July 1999 was announced as a tag match, Ken Stanley and “Sky” Derek Walker against “The Sable Dragon” Viktor Karasopolis and a mystery partner, but Stanley claimed Walker was injured and wrestled Karasopolis alone; an unnamed giant ran in on Karasopolis's behalf, and Tommy Kain, believed sidelined since a car crash, revealed that injury as faked and struck Karasopolis with a crutch before the referee called for the bell and awarded Stanley the match by disqualification. Nothing in the surviving record resolves who the giant was.

The promotion closed in October 1999. It returned in 2004 under new ownership: James Oslo, credited with obtaining the rights in 2003 after cutting ties with an earlier regional promotion, relaunched it as Saturday Night SuperFights on a cable deal, running at least three cards between June and July 2004 out of arenas including a sold-out Bell Centre crowd in Montreal. Nothing later survives.
