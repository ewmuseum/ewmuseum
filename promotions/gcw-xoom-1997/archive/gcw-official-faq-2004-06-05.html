Since then, I have repeatedly tried to start up a new promotion to match the fun that I found with this one. The problems that I have run into have been common. I have tried to impose game rules outside the spectrum that players are willing to participate in. I have tried starting two dice based feds and neither generated enough interest to get going. One was a simple dice-based ruleset and the other went into an extreme amount of roleplaying that people seem to have found overwhelming. Other attempts have just been to strict and too defined from the start to ever go anywhere. So, that's what we won't be doing here. I realize that the promotion is going to grow and change over time. I have been reviewing archives of my two most popular promotions (WCWF & GCW) and realized how alot of things have changed and grown over time. I hope to take what I saw there and combine it with a renewed interest in my most popular e-federation. The GCW will return to what it was before - a simple booked e-federation. No complex rules or requirements; just people looking to have fun for a few hours each week. A LITTLE ABOUT ME I am a 25 year old husband and father of two. I have a six year-old stepson and a twenty-month old daughter and I live in the Florida panhandle. I work as a web programmer and designer for a national communications firm based out of California. My time is limited during the days and evenings but I usually have a few hours at night that are to myself. I don't watch wrestling anymore. I find it flat and not very entertaining. There is no effort, in my eyes. Not like there used to be. Times change, I guess. Anyway, I once did watch wrestling almost religiously and I started e-wrestling in 1996. I ran e-feds from 1996 to 1999. Two, actually. The World Championship Wrestling Federation was a TNM-based simmed wrestling promotion that lasted for a year and a half. It was where I made alot of my initial connections as a fed-head and some of which I still communicate to this day. After that was my "claim to fame" -- GCW (Global Championship Wrestling). GCW was started in December of 1997. When it first started, there were three co-presidents: Nick, Stew, and myself. Stew had to drop out after a month or two and soon so did Nick. Having just run a fed on my own for a year and a half before that, I wasn't really ready to handle a fed by myself again.. or should I say, I didn't really want to. I kept the fed going for a few more months before I took a two month hiatus. The fed came back and I was the solo president, although soon people would start helping by writing matches. The fed continued until October '99 at which point I was no longer having fun and no longer had the desire to run the fed. I closed it down, instead of dragging it to its death. GCW HISTORY In late 1999, Global Championship Wrestling came under extremely unexpected turmoil when suddenly funding was no longer available to pay superstars. Mishandling of corporate funds and sneaky legwork in boardrooms had one of its major backers involved in a telecommunications scam and ultimately in jail. The GCW, although not in on the affairs, was sacked and ruined by the individual's actions and while he was further punished, there was nothing really owed to the GCW or the members of its core audience. [name withheld], who played the President but really was just one of the executive producers and creative forces behind the promotion, also suffered terrible loss when his wife, Elisa, passed away suddenly of undisclosed circumstances. Most say it was a drug overdose of some sort. Fans of the GCW knew Elisa as Krista - the GCW's answer to any woman that ever stepped into the wrestling ring. Battling severe depression over the loss of his wife and his promotion, he ran into a man by the name of James Oslo. Oslo had spoken to Warren about his newfound interest in the world of wrestling and how he was looking to obtain a promotion from middle Georgia and relocate it to Orland. Oslo did so but ran into many troubles in trying to turn the regional promotion into something bigger. He eventually cut his losses with the said promotion, the PWA, and with it went his interest. That is until [name withheld] fell in love again. This time with James Oslo's daughter, Anne. Mark and James starting to see each other on a more regular basis and through many conversations about their past lives as wrestling promoters (of sorts), they began to discuss a way to bring Warren's GCW back to the fold. Mark and Ann married in June 2002. Oslo later obtained rights to the GCW in 2003 and since has been renegotiating and obtaining deals with new stars, a cable network, and areans across the country to bring back this once loved entertainment bonanza. The question is: Who will Oslo be able to bring back? Will the fans love it as much as they did before? Or will Warren and Oslo decide to let this horse rest? Not if they have their way. GCW GENERAL INFORMATION The organization will function in a way comparable to WWF circa mid-90s, and for all comparison purposes, should be the one to use. The fed is run by me, [name withheld], and that's it. I make the calls. I may or may not ask for people's assistance in certain things. Whether they choose to or not is my problem, and it'll stay that way. The fed will be run under booked rules, meaning I make the call on who wins with my own judgement. I really liked the concept of dice wrestling, but this is one of those factors that takes too much of my own time -- and booking it is a fast and effective way to run a fed. Of the three I've run (simmed, dice, and booked), the booked fed went the longest. I am not going to set as many rules on every aspect of the game like I have in the past. The great thing about the GCW was that the rules grew as the promotion did. You will see different things experimented with and we may treat them as special features during different parts of the year. If they are liked enough, we will keep them around. For the most part, if you have a question about something, just ask me and I will provide the question and answer to everyone in the fed. There's no reason to dedicate a section to the titles, as they should be pretty self explanatory. The titles that are up for grabs are the GCW World Heavyweight Championship, the GCW Television Championship, and the GCW Tag Team Championship. I am currently considering how to decide who will start with the belts. Tag teams will be formed from the combination of two singles wrestlers and won't be an exclusive area of competition. In other words, you can partner with another handler to be a tag team and also compete in singles competition. GCW NOTEWORTHY FIGURES These characters are worth pointing out, as they are generally staff members, officials, etc.: James Oslo - Owner & President of GCW Oslo is the heart of GCW, as he pretty much runs everything behind the scenes. You will very rarely see him on the camera, and will probably hear his name a whole lot more. Oslo handles daily operations of GCW which include signing the wrestler's checks, overseeing the product, controlling the product, and being the overall visionary behind the product. He is 45 years old and was raised in Dallas, Texas. [name withheld] - Commissioner of GCW Now 25 and still very young in this business, Mark has seen a thing or two in his time as a driving force behind the GCW. Gone are his days of the Evil Promoter and in its place is the want and drive to make GCW the most successful wrestling program on the planet. Mark will often be seen on programs making rulings where necessary and will otherwise stay out of the limelight. That's where the superstars will be. Tommy Flynn - Commentator Flynn is the main commentator and leader of the two-man team. I hate to say it, but he's your average play-by-play guy. He's excited about everything, he hates it when the heels cheat, and he gets blown away when the good guys win. Flynn has been involved with Oslo since he attempted to move the regional promotion to Florida and is very involved with the product along with his close friend. Flynn is 30 years old and has been involved with wrestling for about eight years on-and-off. Simon Forte - Commentator GCW was fortunate enough to be able to re-obtain the most popular color commentator of its time - Simon Forte. This former wrestler is nothing but bad attitude and the words to go with it. Doom and Gloom is his motto and along with his reverse mohawk haircut, that's exactly what he will stick to. He'll also have lots of fun making Flynn's life as difficult as possible. Ron Nord - Head Referee The man that has earned the reputation of being "Built Nord Tough," Ron Nord is a bald-headed bad man in stripes. He doesn't take anyone's crap, as the Nordinator is there to enforce the rules and make sure that his officials can do the same thing without the wrestlers taking advantage of their position. Nord is an experienced wrestler and referee. Kazuhito Tokeshi - Referee "You think Tokeshi punk bitch? Tokeshi no punk bitch!" Tokeshi is the international connection on the referee squad, and tends to be the one that is sent to task when the match has some sort of special or dangerous stipulation. He is a very deep individual, and takes offense to being called a "punk bitch." Unnamed Referee - Referee Word has it that one of the referee's will be one of the former GCW superstars who is no longer capable enough to compete in the squared circle. Richard Anderson - Ring Announcer "What's he doing with that mic?" Richard Anderson is a very experienced ring announcer, as he served many a year in GCW. I don't know what else to tell you about him. :) Chris Fisher - Security Heading this security team, the man known only as The Shark is quite the formidable security guard. If you cross his barricade, you may just experience the oddness of the Strange Swimming Dance. This, of course, could only be followed with a combination of nightsticks and handcuffs that will have your head swimming for the next week. GCW SCHEDULE The GCW plans on scheduling a show every Saturday night. Deadlines for the show will be the preceeding Thursday night at 11:59 PM. This would, of course, include any strategies or roleplaying for the card that weekend. Shows will generally consist of around six matches - half of which will actually be written out and the other half summarized. This will ensure a show every week that will keep a fast-paced and enjoyable environment while not causing too much strain on myself to keep them written every week. We will also experiment with other shows and formats as the league progresses. I will attempt to schedule PPVs every four or six weeks. They will be completely written out. GCW GUIDELINES So, how does the fed work? Well, like I've said, it's a booked fed. Roleplaying is important, but it doesn't necessarily have to be in the standard "flash" format. Anything from in-rings to backstage attacks are roleplaying, and as long as you're developing the character or the angle, then you should have nothing to worry about. There will not be an "RP Show" or a "Flash Show." All roleplaying will be done on the actual shows with the wrestlers themselves. I plan to do some sort of "ECW Pulp Fiction" like segment (i.e. alot of the guys talking in various locations backstage after an event over the theme from Pulp Fiction). I don't care about how much you curse.. that's up to you. But I'm probably going to edit it with [BLEEPS] so.. if you want to sound like Mike Awesome, that's fine with me. Also, please write like a somewhat coherent person. I know the human race is generally stupid but if you send an RP that looks like a four year old typed it, I'm going to get rid of you for the sake of everyone. "ok u undersatnd kewl!" As far as strategies go, please don't go overboard. You can customize what your wrestler does on the card/in the match to any degree you want -- but I don't want 20k strats for standard cards. That's ridiculous. If you feel you have to send those come PPV time, then I'll understand that. Strategies will have weight come time to deciding who wins. I just don't need 300k to sort through before making decisions. Oh yeah, this goes without saying -- don't sit on your ass. Participate. Otherwise, you're just wasting the time of me and all the other players. If you don't like things that are going on in the GCW, come to me and tell me about it. If you don't like whatever it is that I have to say in return, quit. This is probably the 20th fed starting today. There's lots of other ones out there. The object is to have fun and if you're not - well, that's one of the few thoughts I shouldn't have to finish here.
