Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Fantasy Wrestling Federation announced its return to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 10 May 1999 as “Version 2.0,” styling itself one of the original RSPW e-feds, having run earlier under two other names, and warning off confusion with a rival that had taken up its initials two days before: “Don't be fooled by other feds using the letters.” It called itself “a fully booked e-fed...not something run off a simulator,” back after “a multi-year hiatus” under new leadership, with its fed head marking eleven months back on the job.

Its comeback card, Battleground, aired 16 May 1999 as a night of title unifications. “Flagman” Joe Melillo defended the World Heavyweight Championship against the man he had once beaten for it, Chris “Hit Man” Thomas, the two men billed as former partners in a stable called The Lion Pride; European Champion “The Franchise” Ryan Rankin and North American Champion Vic “The Rebel” Black raced to unify their titles into a new International Championship hidden somewhere in the arena for either man to find; and the Hitmen defended the unified World and International Tag Team Titles, won five weeks earlier, against a multi-fed tag team, Bad Company. The undercard carried the promotion's own oldest grudge to a head, billing Brian Knight against Joe Petrow as “the most intense feud in all the years of this federation.”

The surviving fragment of that broadcast covers the World Heavyweight main event in full: Melillo retained, and Thomas, true to a promise he had made before the bell, offered his hand in a show of sportsmanship the champion accepted in front of the crowd. The broadcast then moved into the Knight – Petrow match, its own commissioner entering the ring to explain the stipulation, at the point the surviving record breaks off.

No document survives past that 16 May 1999 broadcast. Whether the rest of Battleground's own announced unifications came off as planned isn't in the surviving record.
