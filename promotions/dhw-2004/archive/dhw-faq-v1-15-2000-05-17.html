Introduction John-Michael Wood, long-time business man, was spurred on to create a side project. He liked professional wrestling, so he made a few phone calls, and what was born, he called "Dark Horse Wrestling." After a short tenure, Wood passed control to Wasteland. Wasteland worked diligently until the DHW faded into anonymity.
Now, Wood has chosen a successor, in a young, fresh-minded man named Mike
Bellamy. Bellamy has new ideas, and a new "attitude."
2. What to Expect DHW is NOT your typical fed. We don't carry storylines. We don't carry feuds from one show to the next. None. Zilch. Zip.
DHW is what you could call a "character development" fed. You have a new character you want to try out? DHW is where you want to do it. You want to further explore your character? You can do it here, too.
WE ARE AN ORIGINAL, BOOKED FED. Every person who joins DHW is essentially "paid-by-appearance." If you roleplay, you'll end up on the card. If you don't, you don't appear. You might enter a wrestler, and then never show up again. But you might want to at a later date, and we'll still let you in (we'll have you on file). That's the beauty of it!
3. THE RULES: a) Overall: All mail will be sent to [detail withheld] , including wrestler applications, matches, roleplays, and so forth. All material sent into DHW will be credited to their intellectual creator and owner.
-No attacking DHW officials or staff without our consent. -Fixxxer Rule: Please don't kill people in your roleplays or matches. I personally don't believe in that, and so it won't happen here. You want to see people die? Go elsewhere.
-No whining about the outcome of a match. Things happen, okay? -No other wrestlers without their owner's consent (even DHW's "jobber" roster), but nameless jobbers that you create are fine. -Be creative, original, and innovative. -And above all: have fun... it's just a game. b) Handler Rules DHW will allow whatever wrestlers you want. But be forewarned, we don't have much of a budget. We can't support big pyros, a massive screen for videos, or even fancy-colored lighting. We have capacity for a few sparklers, some smoke, and your theme music... that's about it.
As far as roleplays goes, it's all up to you. You don't have to, but it would be nice. If you do, remember: we can't afford much more than the simple stuff.
If you're a free agent using DHW to get noticed, we will make a special note to ensure that is the case.
A note: in the case that there are too many wrestlers appearing for a match with a jobber, you may be placed in a match with another handler. If there are no open spots, then you will be notified that your appearance will be pushed back one week. Guaranteed.
c) Freelance Match Writer Rules You want to prove that you can write a match? Well, here's your shot. You can write a match any way you want. You can use your own wrestlers, or use our jobbers (but okay it first with us). Try to use our announcers, but it's not necessary. It's not not necessary... that's the beauty of it!
You WILL be credited for writing the match. But be forewarned, DHW reserves the right to reject any match and the right to edit a match for the sake of legibility, and whether it's done by match summary, Action Bracket System, or strict Play-By-Play, we have a pretty set standard at what we're looking for. We only accept completed matches.
Props and gimmicks are fine. Tables? Great. Trashcans, blenders, keyboards-
I don't care. Anything goes, but please, be realistic. Please, nothing potentially lethal, like guns, or knives (no "scissors"), or Howitzers.
Don'ts: Lay off of gimmick matches. Don't kill anyone. Don't smash up the set, or annihilate fans at ringside... I don't want to have a mess to clean up. Do not leave the arena!
d) Other matches written Match writers may request to be given "assignments" - particular matches with a certain outcome. Such cases may be when two sets of wrestlers request a match against each other on DHW shows. Such events can happen when DHW has a plurality of wrestlers who wish to appear on a chosen card.
A special note to fedheads: If you want more exposure as well from your most recent show, feel free to send it on over. DHW has no shame in promoting other feds. No... not at all.
4. Tutoring DHW also offers a unique tutoring opportunity to those who want help. If you feel you can't learn from places like:
http://www.ewu.org.uk/ http://www.calnan.com/keenings/keenskool http://www.roughkut.com and would rather have a "hands-on" approach, I'll do what little I can. I can get you started, and maybe get you to hone some skills, but I do suggest the above schools first.
5. Shows: a) Happy Hour Happy Hour is DHW's regular, weekly show each Tuesday. The host, "Shotglass" Jon Lerner, runs Happy Hour from it's initial setting- Lerner's bar, "Happy
Hour." There will be approximately two to four regular matches per Happy Hour show. It's almost cable-access, so please don't be offended by the quality.
b) Supercard Direct-to-Videos What makes you think a small, local promotion like DHW can afford a Pay-Per-View? Well, they can't. They do, however, have special videos of their supercards. Typically contain 4-6 full-length matches.
Upcoming Supercards: Destruction Derby I- July 20, 2000- All Gimmick Matches, the DHW supercard of the year New Year's EVIL- December 31, 2000- basic supercard Armored Core II- March 6, 2001- basic supercard 6. DHW Arenas Norfolk Boathouse- Smallest of the small venues. The roof is so low, that the wrestlers can't jump off the top rope in three of the four corners of the ring.
This often leads to some interesting developments. Capacity is approximately
800 standing room only.
Virginia Beach Civic Center- The roof is much higher and there is much more room. Has a stage. Capacity is about 1500 SRO.
Virginia Beach Pavillion- Capacity (approximately 3500 seated) is about the size of of maybe some of the smaller WCW Thunder venues.
7. DHW Staff a) Administration: DHW President/Booking Committee: Michael Bellamy- Small-time business man, promoter, and booker. He loves what he does, even if he has some unusual quirks...
DHW Vice President: Merlin Coltrane- Former jobber. DHW Founder/Financier/Booking Advisor: John-Michael Wood- Wood Enterprises,
Ltd. is a small, family-run stock firm. The name is all that is known of Mr.
Wood. Only DHW's officials actually know what he looks like... Think of J-M
Wood as DHW's "mysterious benefactor."
b) Hosts: Happy Hour Host- "Shotglass" Jon Lerner- Semi-retired wrestler with twenty-one years of experience. Has occasional matches once in a while. He enjoys being the Happy Hour host, and even does interviews.
Alex Tracer- Play-By-Play man with face tendencies. Pretty much an analyst on identifying just about every move conceived.
"Stainless" Ray Allen Steele- Color announcer with heel tendencies. Once a manager for DHW's first champion, Dark Sky.
c) Officials and Reporters DHW Ring Announcer- Mike Piston- former DHW jobber turned ring announcer DHW Ring Crew Leader: "Big" Josh Black DHW On-the-spot Interviewer/Reporter: Ronald Gaff- Sophomore Journalism Major at nearby Old Dominion University
d) Referees "The Magistrate" Leon Tolchinsky- DHW's Senior Official. The man who calls it down the middle. A former wrestler in his own right, Tolchinsky is more than able to maintain order in his ring. Tolchinsky only DQs in extreme cases, and even then prefers No-Contest decisions.
Miguel De Pinna- A ferocious hardcore brawler up until an unfortunate back injury cost him his career, De Pinna is a man who loves to see excessive amounts of violence, and basically never rules a DQs or No-Contest.
Dan Sterling- A former in-ring announcer turned referee, Dan Sterling is less experienced at his job than the other refs. He sometimes forgets he is the referee and unwittingly lets some rather serious infractions go.
