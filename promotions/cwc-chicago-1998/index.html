Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Chicago Wrestling Corporation opened on 14 November 1998, the same day its own founder handed a previous promotion of his off to a partner to start it. Its own player's guide billed it under the tagline “It's just 'a' plain athletic arrogance” and laid out a whole season of pay-per-views in advance, named months ahead: In Your Face: Judgment Day that November, then Fatal Encounter, Wrestlefest, Lord of the Ring, School's Out! and Summerfest running through the following year.

Its own rules ran a small, tightly kept roster — twenty-four singles wrestlers and six tag teams — and barred both “Out of the World” gimmicks and any wrestler built to copy a real one by name. Results were booked by its own council with the ongoing storylines in mind rather than decided purely by whoever roleplayed hardest that week, and it sanctioned three singles titles, the Skyweight, Cook County and Chicago Championships, plus a Chicago Tag Team Championship, all vacant at the promotion's own founding.

Its first pay-per-view, In Your Face: Judgment Day, ran on Thanksgiving Day 1998 as two eight-man tournaments. Andrew “Flash” Tucker won the Chicago Championship, submitting Jack Vars in the final after outside interference from a group called Outcast by Society; “Flatlander” Duke Putrid took the Cook County Championship from Tommy Grayson in a ladder match. The same broadcast carried a backstage angle in which “The Truth” Tony Shards was found beaten unconscious. The Skyweight and Chicago Tag Team titles, named in the promotion's own founding documents, were never contested in any surviving record of its own.

No closure notice of its own survives; its own last surviving document, a newsletter dated 28 November 1998, still promotes the season ahead.
