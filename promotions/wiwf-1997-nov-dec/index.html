A national-scale WIWF founded in November 1997, whose only surviving broadcast opened with an in-fiction tribute to a rival promotion's fallen wrestler and closed with a subtitled international debut match. Fourteen wrestlers and one tag team are recorded from that broadcast. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The WIWF introduced itself modestly. Its own operator plugged it on 21 November 1997 as “the WIWF (I know, I know...really unoriginal),” a TNM'd fed with no one signed yet, and admitted a week later, “I'll be the first to admit we're not the best...but we'll give you everything we can!” A survey he posted on 2 December laid out what he judged a “good” e-fed to be: original wrestlers over “twelve dozen Stone-Cold Whatevers,” no cussing, no real wrestlers unless roleplayed well, no harassment among handlers, and wrestlers choosing their own moveset rather than being boxed into a fixed “style.”

Its only surviving broadcast, the WIWF Christmas Special of 25 December 1997, staged from the Tad-Smith Coliseum in Oxford, Mississippi, opened with an in-fiction dedication to a rival promotion's own performer, “Sabbath,” framed as having died in an explosion five days earlier. Called by announcers Tony McMoore, Glenn Osbourne, and Randle Cunningham, the card opened with Nymisis beating Alvarez Eventide in a brawl that spilled outside the ring and used a chair and a microphone as weapons, and closed its first half with a vignette introducing the Doom Knight, managed by Lord Kazrick and promoted live from Schaan, Liechtenstein, promising his in-ring debut after an earlier run in a promotion called the UCWF. Its own president appeared on camera during the broadcast to sign “The Power” Mark Exodus to a contract.

The second half saw Rogue beat newcomer Prudence Pride, managed by Vinnie, in thirty-eight seconds after interference from Wasp; Mark Exodus's debut against Desperado ended in a handshake after an accidental referee bump left both men more concerned with the official's safety than the finish; and the show closed on Happy Juice, the tag team of Prozac Barnes and U.W. Horowitz, managed by “Bob, the guy who looks like Stalin,” beating Agony in a bloody handicap-turned-tag brawl. The broadcast ended on a teaser: “Coming in 1998...GENESIS...The WIWF starts here.”

A framing note posted the same day disclaimed any more Usenet posting from its own president unless the response was strong: “Unless I get a lot of response, it will be the only time I ever do this.” No document survives after 25 December 1997, and no trace of the promised 1998 relaunch is in the record.
