"Last Resort" - Shamrock Recorded 7-18-00 Shamrock is walking down some stairs as the camera comes in close to see him. He walks through a swinging door, obviously into the parking lot of GCW offices. His 2001 Ford Mustang Cobra is sitting. As he gets closer to it, he sees Outlaw starting his own footage. He smiles and stands back for a few seconds. Then, slowly, he starts to come in. Some wind blows, making Shamrock frown. He comes in closer, and we can overhear Outlaw. The camera quickly pans to Outlaw. Outlaw: Put the thing on me, will ya? Good Lord, why did I have to interview...HIM? I can't believe this. I can't even stand he's back. What a waste, I could be interviewing Executioner. Shamrock steps up quickly. He's right on Outlaw. Shamrock: Hey, Outlaw! Long time no see! Outlaw: Uh...hey! I was supposed to get an interview with you. You're not gonna....make that thing go fast, right? Shamrock: With this traffic? How could I? Get in. Shamrock climbs in, and Outlaw's face gets close to the camera, obviously talking to the cameraman when he whispers, "Did he hear that?" Outlaw opens the door and puts the seat back so the camera can climb in. We cut to Shamrock beginning to pull out of the building and onto the street. Outlaw: I'm sure you're not all that happy about how your entracne seemed to make few waves. Shamrock: It doesn't bother me. Like I said, I'm not here to scream at them all. I'm here to make sure they know the old ways, because that's what I and GCW were brought up on and they need to know them as well. Outlaw: Your challenge has been accepted, right? Shamrock: I hope so. I'm going to watch LiveWire after the house show. Shamrock turns onto a busy Atlanta street. His eyebrows comes down and he is very alert of the traffic scene as he switches over a few lanes. Outlaw: Uh, where are we headed? Shamrock: You didn't prepare to well for this interview, did you? Outlaw: Hey, I asked you a question, didn't I? Shamrock: We're going to Ireland. Outlaw: That's gonna be tough unless this car can float. Shamrock: Yeah, that's why we're getting on a fairy... Outlaw: Oh. Shamrock shakes his head, and Outlaw leans back, covering the mic on the camera so he can ask the cameraman for questions to ask. His eyes light up. He turns back. Shamrock finally gets a green light he actually makes it through, and speeds the car up towards 70. As he hits the highway, he maintains that speed. Outlaw looks a little nervous. Outlaw: Well...uh...Psyberia has challenged you for SNE. Your response? Shamrock gets very serious. His eyes turn cold, and he doesn't blink throughout the statement. Shamrock: Psyberia, I did nothing to provoke you. I do not accept your challenge. Things can happen in a match, the match turns ugly, and the competitors turn ugly towards eachother. Psyberia, we're both on the same side of the pond right now. Don't push me. Shamrock's eyes remain cold for a little while. He finally blinks. Outlaw: Will you quit that? You look like you're undead or something. Shamrock laughs. Shamrock: Sorry. It's habit. When I get to talking about GCW, the intensity is there. It's hard to avoid. Outlaw: Yeah, well, just watch the road. Have the guys responded since your message to them? Shamrock: No. And I'm not happy about it. Maybe I will have to start taking out the trash. Slash and Johnny have not responded as far as I know. As a matter of fact, other than the guys I respect, no one decided to cut an interview yesterday! What a surprise! These guys aren't serious. I'm beginning to lose my respect for some of them. Johnny MacGrath. I remember Johnny. Johnny, you had such a future. Anybody watched you in the ring and saw that you were something special. Then you dumped Roxy, and it's all sucked since then. Johnny, ever heard the term "getting on the ball"? That's what your career has missed ever since you first walked in that tower with the three big letters on it. Johnny, where are you? Ever since your breakup with Roxy, you've been too busy at Vegas, or too busy on the beach, or too busy doing something else to work. I'm not impressed, Johnny. You had one belt when I left, and guess what? You still only have one. Outlaw looks a little angry. Outlaw: Well Johnny has time to do those things because of his athletic ability! He's a true superstar. Some of these guys try to pass it off, but Johnny's moves are out of pure talent. Shamrock: That's exactaly the problem. Thank you for proving my point, Outlaw! Outlaw: Uh...yeah. Shamrock: Pure talent. Johnny was pure talent when he came in, and he still is pure talent. Roxy tried to polish you and failed. Johnny, when people looked at you and your babyface image, they saw a future World Champion. Now you've changed your image to the wrong side, and how many belts has it got you? Not a one. Not a single one. Johnny, you're still a ball of talent that, if polished, could get somewhere. Work ethic? What work ethic? Outlaw seems frustrated. Outlaw: Well SLash has paid his dues, all right. Shamrock: Yes he has. Slash, you're another guy right there. You fought through he Dojo, you fought through a terrible losing streak, and now you're coming out of it. But you have nothing to show for it. Slash, you're a fantastic wrestler that is one step away. You know what that step is, Outlaw? Outlaw does not seem to be enjoying this. He looks out the window, angry. Shamrock quickly swerves the car and rolls the power window down at the same time. Outlaw leaps out of his skin practically, and screams at Shamrock "YES! WHAT!?" Shamrock laughs. Shamrock: To take the good path, not the bad one. He's set if he'll follow that simple instruction. Outlaw rolls his eyes as they pull up to a toll booth. Shamrock gives the machiene a dollar coin and pulls in. He finds a parking space easily in the midtown Atlanta parking deck, right on the bottom level. He parks the Cobra and gets out, Outlaw following behind. The cameraman struggles to get out of the Cobra's admittidly small backseat. The camera catches up to Shamrock as he climbs into a GCW limo. Outlaw seems like this is regular procedure; Shamrock balks. Shamrock: WOW. When did GCW get this thing? Outlaw: They have two. Shamrock gets in, still shaking his head. He mutters about taking the Cobra instead of the limo. Shamrock: Would have been fine to take the Cobra...no need for this, OUTLAW...I don't want to trouble GCW, I'm sure someone needs this thing... Outlaw: Not really...besides, you requested some time to look over tapes of your opposition, remember? Shamrock: Yeah, but I thought I did that after the house show... Outlaw: Not anymore. Shamrock mutters a few more incoherent words and flips on the TV in the back seat. It starts running clips of MacGrath, especially in his match at SNE. Shamrock watches intently. Papa Roach's "Last Resort" is playing in the background. Shamrock stops and listens. He pauses the tape. Shamrock: Outlaw, this is my last resort. Outlaw: You going to Maui or something? Shamrock: NO...this is my last resort. I've watches everything happen in GCW. I've suggested things for the big suits to do, and they did most of them, but still no help. I've watched and e-mailed some people; no help. This is it. If you can't beat it into them, then they're just hopeless. Shamrock starts the tape again. He wacthes as Johnny loses his composure. Shamrock: Many people always ask me "Sure, you can SAY you have a gameplan, but do you?" I can assure you I do. Johnny, I'm pretty well setup for this one. It seems, even with the weakest of opponents, your lack of polish shows through. Putting your head down to early for a back body drop. I do that sometimes accidentaly too; but now I see some weaknesses. What if I fake a punch, then clothesline you? You never seem to see fakes coming, so I'll nail you with the clothesline as you get ready to absorb a punch. Outlaw: Well Johnny has his own counters, like hitting you with a fricking chair! (Muttering under his breath) Not a bad idea...anyway, even the ever-great Shamrock goes down to a piece of steel. Shamrock: That's right; I do. Everyone does. The problem is, it's a DQ. You're letting me win. Now I don't care what Johnny says. You can't send a message to me. When you beat me with a chair, the only message it sends me is that you're afraid to go one on one. You get nothing out of it with me, Johnny. I think that just about says it. You balk yours and Slash Kapone's chances of getting a win. I don't think that's what you want. Now, you do seem to lose your temper. All of these problems show of that lack of polish, Johnny. Nevertheless, until you grow a little older and a little wiser, you're not going to see it. The limo continues driving to wherever it's going. Outlaw sits around, watching the tape as well. Shamrock continues to point out the litteslist thing. Outlaw seems unimpressed that his favorite guy is getting picked apart on tape. Outlaw: Sure you can pick him apart on tape, but this is your first match back from months of inactivity, and Johnny's as tough as they come. Shamrock: Very true. It's going to be a very tough fight. I've been working double time in the gym, just to make sure I'm ready for this. It's a strange thing to come right back into the heat of things like this. It's gonna be tough, Outlaw, and I'm sure you'll be there to pick at my work all the way. Outlaw grins proudly. Outlaw: Yes, sir! Shamrock's eyes get narrow and he silently slides down the driver's window. He looks at Outlaw, and Outlaw's eyes get a little big. Shamrock: WHAT!? DON'T YOU EVER SAY SOMETHING LIKE THAT TO ME AGAIN! Outlaw: Uh...sorry...I mean, really. you're just.. Shamrock cracks up. The driver chuckles, and slides the window back up. Outlaw seems to be enjoying this interview less and less. Shamrock: Sorry, stealing from Jack Anthony a bit there...anyway. Outlaw, I've been watching tape on your other favorite guy as well. He slides in his homemade tape of Slash Kapone. In it, his most recent match with MMM, as well as some others. Shamrock makes a few comments on both men's ability. The match finishes, and the tape moves on to a different match. Shamrock rewinds. Shamrock: Now. Kapone has some kind of seriousness to himself when he's motivated by something. I know he'll be motivated for this match; after all, look at the two guys he has a chance of pinning. You either pin me or the World Champion. That's all the motivation a wrestler needs. There weren't a lot of weaknesses in this match for Kapone--he hit anything from a flip out of a german suplex to a t-bone. But there's one thing I did notice: Slash doesn't like surprises. That jawbreaker nailed him, and he was out in a hurry. Now, I don't celebrate. I never let someone have that moment to rest. Slash, all you're gonna get from me are a few spinebusters here, a few jawbreakers there. No rest for the weary. Outlaw: Well, we're at the house show. Shamrock: Don't wait up. Outlaw sighs as Shamrock gets out and jogs to some fans, and signs autographs. We cut to after the house show, the limo's lights small spots on the darkness of the small town. Shamrock climbs into the limo. He waits a few minutes. The driver, always a professional, remains completely still, and does nothing more than he is told to. Outlaw finally arrives, and he gets out and opens the door for him. The yellow caution tape that surrounds the employee's cars is really stretching, fans trying to get to Outlaw. Some even go as far as to chant "Out-law! out-law!" Outlaw climbs in the limo, and rolls his eyes. He sits back and rolls down the window to the driver. Outlaw: Move it. Driver: Yes, sir. The limo moves forward, a creeping hulk in a mass of smaller moving objects. FInally, they pull onto the on-ramp and into the mass of asphault called a highway. The limo drives on through the night. Outlaw rolls down his window, the reflection of the inside changing. On the outside, there are two small children in the back of a Ford Focus, looking inside in awe. Outlaw rolls the window up quickly, the motor whirring it's heart out. Outlaw: When do these people go away? Shamrock: It's civilization Outlaw. Get used to it. Outlaw grumbles to himself, and Shamrock turns on the monitor. He turns to a channel instead of video. We can see an antennae rise up from the trunk of the car, and the stations come in. Shamrock hasn't smiled at these luxorious accommodations, but he can't help but grin at this. He immediately flips to LiveWire, passing a channel with "Striptease" playing. Outlaw: Hey! STOP! Shamrock: This isn't playtime, Outlaw. Outlaw mimmicks Shamrock's, "This isn't playtime, Outlaw." Shamrock just shakes his head without turning. He watches the monitor as Johnny MacGrath talks about retiring the old geezers. Shamrock cringes. Outlaw smiles. Outlaw: Sooooo Shamrock, what were you cringing for? Afriad because they took the match? Shamrock: ...not his best work. Outlaw does not seem satisfied. Shamrock: No, Johnny has taken steps, he's a fantastic interview now. Johnny, you talk about retiring the old geezers. That's a tall order, my friend. You managed to retire who? You didn't retire Duke. As a matter of fact, Duke called my house yesterday and left a message. Now me and Duke were never that close, and I still don't like his flamboyant style, but the fact is, Duke was working a house show in Annapolis and pulled his hamstring. He can make appearances, but he's pretty slow and akward in the ring. He's regulated himself to only house shows for a while. Now Duke's a trooper. Johnny, I've never seen you do anything comprable to some of the things he's done. Remember the old saying that if you forget your past then you're doomed to repeat it? I think that's how it goes. It certainly fits for you, Johnny. I was here for every new era that was tossed around. I've seen them all, Johnny, and you've seen a few yourself. Now you're having your own rennovation, your own rebirth. However, you have forgotten your past. You have forgotten that cutting promos in front of the fans, working house shows, and pushing weights in the gym got you your only title, not walking around Vegas with two women on each arm rolling the dice on your fortune. Your mistake; I just hope you correct it. As for Jordan? Yes, he did go out on top. I may not be as fortunate. As a matter of fact, I doubt I will. I don't believe in leaving on top. That's selfishness. That's leaving everyone behind so you can go out looking bigger. Something you would do, Johnny. Not something I would do. So hey, maybe something was right in that talk of yours. Outlaw: Yeah, and Jordan walked when he dunked, too! That bastard would always walk four steps when he hit the hoop. Shamrock: I'm not so sure about that. Outlaw mutters "Yeah, well it's true..." as Shamrock watches Johnny's interview ends. He fast-forwards through Andy Stone and Executioner's interviews with the remote, muttering "I'll watch later." He finally gets to Slash's, and listens intently. He grabs the TiVo remote and rewinds. He watches again, looking closely at the figue deemed "Overlord". He then stops it again. Shamrock: Overlord, huh? Sure. Looks and sounds more like Michael Hayes in a sleeveless white dragger. Slash, you have come a long way. Indeed, I respect you as well, as I have mentioned. Slash, both you and Johnny fixate on the future. The future. The future. Outlaw: They are the future! Shamrock: Unfortunately, they probably are. Slash, you want me to move out of the way. How, exactaly, am I being a roadblock for your career? I'm giving you a shot with a "future Hall of Famer". How, exactaly, is that holding you back? I believe you were the one that agreed to this match, not me. Wow, the "Age of Workers"? I was so in the dark about this new phrase! Thanks for the enlightenment! Slash, I used the phrase in my first interview back, and I've used it in this one as well. It's pretty obvious that I'm up to date on GCW. I do my homework, and I make very calculated decisions. Obviously, I'm considered a dinosaur roaming in a world full of anti-tank weapons. I have to pick and chose my movements carefully. Slash, one of my first movements was to target you. Consider yourself in the jungle, your anti-tank weapon to heavy to carry around in the heat. That anti-tank weapon can represent your good guy, fan loving tactics. You have left it behind because things seem to move faster without it. So now you've found me--perhaps the other way--and what are you going to do? I'm ready, aren't you? Outlaw: Man, enough of this funky analogy stuff...we're almost there. Shamrock: Of course, the main focus in both of the interviews was my age. That I come from the wrong age. In my age, we controlled three guys. My age was the days of the Rock and Austin, of Scorpios in there prime and Kanes. This was not exactaly the age of laziness, you know. Once again, I'm sure you wouldn't know. In both of your rushes to get to the top of the heap in this new age, you both left all the important things behind, such as history. Consider that you're playing king of the hill with guns. You two decided to leave the weapons so when they hit go you can run faster than guys like Overlord, who kept and remembered his past. Of course, in this little analogy the past represents a sub-machiene gun. Despite being able to run faster, it's gonna be aweful hard to dodge those bullets no matter how fast you are. Bottom line, keep the heritage. And since neither of you want to, I'm just gonna walk up the hill and toss both of you off so you'll get it this time. Outlaw: Dude, didn't you see Kung Fu? He dodged bullets! Shamrock: Is the ring Hollywood? Outlaw: Well...er...hey, we're close to arriving at the Ritz! Shamrock fast forwards through all of LiveWire, but seems disappointed to not see anything from Overlord. He frowns. Shamrock: Overlord, as of tonight, I haven't seen anything from you. Now I don't want to jump to conclusions, so I won't. But Overlord, I asked you to carry your weight, and you aren't. I'm thinking about a match at SNE. Thinking, the key word. Because if you sit around and be as lazy as you have been, I'm going to step up and teach you a lesson, that you weren't crowned the World Champion to sit. You best carry your weight. Outlaw: Dissention among the ranks! Shamrock: No. Just a warning. Outlaw: We're almost there, so hurry it along, will ya? Shamrock: Bottom line is this. Fine, I'm old. I already said that, you don't have to restate what I've said. If I'm a ghost compared to you two in-their-prime "workers", I may not be like you, but I have my advantages. Ghosts can disappear, they can pick their spots. Rememer the movie "Ghost"? Remember when he moved things? Well, just consider me that way. My young career died a while back. Over those years I trained and I finally learned how to toss stuff around. Now I'm putting it to use. So Johnny, I'm gonna make you mad, and Slash, I'm gonna hit you when you least expect it. That's what I call a gameplan. With that said, I suppose that means I have a gameplan. How about you? See you Wednesday. With that, they climb out of the limo. Shamrock asks about his car, but notices they are in a hotel next to the deck. Outlaw gets out and moves to the lobby quickly so he doesn't have to do anything or greet anyone. As Shamrock comes in lugging a gym bag, the desk clerk stares. The limo driver walks in and asks for the reserved rooms. The desk clerk immdeiately perks up and says "Shamrock! I'm a big fan! And Outlaw! Wow I can't believe..." She continues to ramble on, Outlaw thumping his fingers on the desk. Shamrock just listens and smiles as LiveWire rolls on to the next taped interview. "Broke" - Executioner The camera shows the outside of a warehouse, the Executioner, dressed in a black, body length leather jacket, with black jeans, and "Death of Superman" t-shirt underneath. He adjusts his shades as the cameraman makes his way across the street. The Executioner opens the door to the warehouse, and motions for the cameraman to enter, closing the door behind him. The screen goes black for a few seconds until a click is heard as a lighter illuminates the Executioner's face. He brings the lighter down onto a cloth on the end of a stick, lighting up the immediate area around them. The Executioner motions for the cameraman to follow him as they make their way through the warehouse. The Executioner stops when they reach a large mound of ropes and metal posts piled against a wall. Ex : I know I shouldn't be here, but rules are meant to be broken, and ironically enough, that is what I want to talk about. Broken things, not rules. You see, I came here, inside one of GCW's warehouses, to talk about things that have been broken. From records, to promises, to bones. Anything can be broken, or shattered, or destroyed. The fact is, that we forget what is broken, what could have been, what SHOULD have been. Things change, and we all move on. For a long time, all of us here have been ignoring the past, and although the past cannot help us, in the ring, ignoring it, can hurt it. Those who forget the past, are doomed to repeat it. So let's take a trip down memory lane, shall we? The Executioner motions for the cameraman to follow him, as they make their way down a hall, filled with various items, most of which unrecognisable, the Executioner sets the torch down on a ringpost, allowing it to stand, without falling over. The Executioner turns, holding a chair. Rusted, and dented, the chair looks as natural as a normal steel chair would. The Executioner swings it a few timed before turning back to the camera. Ex : Ah, the chair. One hit, breaks the rules, and you lose. But, the chair, maybe this one, maybe not, has been involved in a lot over the years. I still remember my first PPV, Holloween Horror. With the coming of Wrestlebowl II, I look back now, to see where I've been. Holloween Horror, the first PPV here in GCW to be held twice. And there, I had my first brush with gold. A four man match, the winner, to face the newly crowned TV champion. That night, four men went it, and one left, the winner. Of those men, I was the first eliminated. Why, because of a chair. And, as I've said many times before, a chair I never got to use. Yes, the ref, making a judgement in error, disqualified me. And the rest is history. I threw the chair in, eliminating the current president of the Dojo Fox. Then, our former World Champion Steiner, was able to defeat a man who disappeared soon after, Colossus. Later on that night, our current World Champion, Overlord, defeated X-Pac, to win the TV title. I still wanted a piece of the gold, so the first bridges of diplomacy between myself and Steiner were formed. I, like always had a plan, but things outside my control thwarted it. Steiner was unable to win the TV title, as a result of interferance, on the part of Johnny MacGrath. And, of course, a chair. Johnny went on to win the TV title, only to evenutally lose it to me. But that, as they say, is history. The Executioner picks up the torch as they continue their way through the warehouse. The Executioner makes his way in front, looking at many different things as they make their way down the hall. He comes into a large area, and makes his way to one wall. Leaning against the wall are two ladders and some scaffolding equipment. The Executioner turns back to the camera, still holding the torch in his hand. Ex : Promises. Many a broken promises have been laid out here in GCW, most of which, no ones fault, but fate. The two potentially greatest matches in GCW, were taken, simply because, the fates were unkind. No scaffolds, no ladders. But this has not been the first time potential greatness has been taken away, due to setbacks. Survivor Match, everyone's chance to get a World Title shot. Except, for me. I was locked up, in the dark dank recesses of that hell hole called Arkham. An asylum. Everyone had their shot a greatness, I was locked up, nice and comfy in my straight jacket. But, that was fine. Later, Frost, the man that won Survivor Match, to go onto to win the World Title. Not from the champion, but from Hoss, after the Champion retired. Then, after Frost was stripped of the title, and retired as well, a tournament was held. The top eight competitors in GCW were to be given a shot. Except for me. Once again, fate was unkind. A bug caused me to withdraw my name from the tournament, only to find out the bug was not very bad, and I COULD have fought. But, instead, Steiner won the tournament, only to later be fired, and the title was taken away. The Executioner once again makes his way down the passages of the warehouse, this time, coming upon a book. He opens it, blowing dust off the pages. He closes it, only to set it back down. He turns to the camera, making sure it was still following. Ex : Ah, Records. Like rules, they were made to be broken. Overlord, and I, well, we have been setting some new records as of late. Overlord, he held the TV title longer than anyone, until I won it, and I broke that record. But, just recently, he won the World Title, and achieved something that I have long sought after. He is a former TV, Georgia and Tag Champion. That means, that he, as World Champion, is the first man to complete the Grand Slam. Once again, I am second. Only one man before me held the Triple Crown of Tag, TV and Georgia titles and that man was X-pac. Only one tag team combination held the Tag Titles twice before THEM accomplished the same fate, and that was the team of Frost and Anthony, Triple Threat. And now, only one man has achived the Grand Slam status, and that is Overlord. But, more records will be made and broken over time, but until then, the record holders get a taste of immortallity. But, nothing, even partnership, is forever. Ah yes, partnership, seemingly the greatest bond that can be made, but also, the most easily broken. Anything can get in the way of two people, gold, women, values. Steiner and I, Forsaken and THEM, Overlord and Shamrock, Overlord and Hoss, Frost and Scorpio, MacGrath and JackHammer. The fact is that no two people can stay together indefinately, it just doesn't happen. Even Johnny and Roxy, or me and the Masked Jobber. Managers, partners, lovers. All grow apart. Time does not heal all wounds, merely pours salt in them, keeping them painful and fresh. We force ourselves to forget, but in the end, we remember. No two people have all the same goals, only similar. And in the end, sometimes too similar. There cannot be two World Champions, so the champion cannot have a friend, not in the business anyway, not for a long time. Look at Frost, we abadonned him, in his time of need. Why? We wanted that piece of Gold. Steiner, I attacked him, just as Overlord attacked Hoss, the same night. Why? For the gold. And now, Overlord attacks all that approach, and wisely so. Shamrock, you don't want to teach, you don't want to help, you want what everyone here wants. You want what you've already HAD twice. You want the World Title. The fact that you deny it, is only a lie to the fans. I don't lie, people just don't like the truth I say. The Executioner makes his way again, now approaching the door. There, he picks up a knee brace, and he merely tosses it to the ground. He picks up some bandages, as well as a broken piece of cast. He reaches into the pile, and grabs something, and his face seems puzzled. He pulls it out, and it is seen that he is holding up a straight jacket. He drops it and bends over, seemingly sick. After a few moments, he gets back up and sits down on a bench, and looks back to the camera, purposely avoiding looking at the jacket. Ex : Where was I. Yes. Bones, another thing that can be broken. Injuries plague wrestlers. Once you get a bad wheel, you have a target on it the rest of your life. Why do you think I had gotten Psyberia into a ladder match. Take out his bad knee, and he can't climb anything, let alone a ladder. Shamrock, now, you aren't just an old wrestler, most likely plagued with some old injuries, but now, just recently, you injured your knee. If, or when, you come back, you know that everyone will be gunning for it. Everyone wants to peg themselves against you. "The man that put Shamrock on the shelf for good." Sounds pretty nice, doesn't it? Its music to the ears of everyone in the back, I can tell you that much. The Executioner makes his way around, avoiding the jacket, and opens the door to the warehouse, putting out the torch in a bucket of water. The Executioner and the cameraman made it out into the fresh air. The Executioner dusted off his jacket before closing the door behind him. He once again turned to the cameraman. Ex : Andy Stone. Mr. Lightning. You want me and MacGrath in a tag match, whenever, it doesn't matter. You got lucky, a lot like Slash, and now you think that you are king of the world. You see, anyone, can beat anyone else, on any given day. But to be able to beat someone whenever you want to, to be able to beat ANYONE you want to. When you can go out, day in and day out, and win, get belts, be in the big leagues, THEN, you will be able to brag. But one win, is nothing. And you like to quote popular singer's, well this one is from the Bloodhound Gang. "I don't care if you don't like me, cause I don't like you, cause you're not like me." So what I'm saying is that Stone, I don't really care who you get as your partner, even if you get the cast of Grease to come in and sing "Grease Lightning" for you, it wouldn't help. Stone, you have to learn that you are rookie, and as such, you need to pay your dues, and know your place. If a beating courtesy of me and Johnny is what you need, that's fine by me. Now, one last thing that I almost forgot about. Slash Kapone. At SNE, I haven't forgot you. This was a little lesson for all those little guys, but it is also a little lesson for you Slash. At SNE, I will not only break you, but the myth that you even deserve to be in a match with that Georgia title, let alone hold it. The fact is that Slash, you've broken not only a rule, and a law, but a commandment. Thou shall not steal ring a bell? That belt is stolen property. The Executioner turns on his heels quickly and begins making his way down the street quickly, hailing a cab as he goes. The cameraman follows him for a little while, standing by as a taxi stops. The Executioner reaches into his pocket and hands the man in the taxi a sheet of yellow paper. Ex : Umm, I need to get to that place. I recently bought something that I will no longer be needing, so I want to see if I can get my money back, it did cost a pretty penny, as you can guess by the name of the place. The Executioner, and the taxi, speed off before the camera can find out what was on the sheet of paper. The cameraman merely scratches his head as Livewire goes to a commercial.
