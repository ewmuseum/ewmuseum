Hall of Fame (April 2004)

22 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The federation’s own Hall of Fame as it stood in April 2004, with its founder’s tribute to each inductee. It opens with Patrick Little, who created ICW.
Hall Of Fame

The GCW Hall of Fame is a place where only the greatest athletes of them all get to put their memories up for the rest of GCW to always remember. These are the greatest stars to ever walk the GCW aisle...let's always remember them.

In chronological order...

Patrick Little Gimmicks: Strider, Big Dawg, Titan Height/Weight: All stood at around 7'2, 299 lbs. Titles Held: ICW World Title (x3), ICW Internet Title (x1), ICW Announcer, ICW President, GCW Vice President, GCW Commissioner
Stables: Four Horsemen, Alliance of Warriors, nWo Wolfpac
Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: To me, it would be a complete disgrace to all that GCW stands for if this man was not voted into the Hall. To put it plainly, if Patrick Little had not made ICW, you would not be reading this page. Patrick created a very fun, somewhat-hardcore federation just as e-feds were taking off in June, 1998. Using the name Big Dawg to run his fed, he also created Strider to become the federation's World Champion. At first, he was somewhat corrupt, but with the guidence of Overkill and his dedication to learning, Patrick created one of the most enjoyable federations on the web. I was introduced into the ICW as my wrestler (Hitman--who I later dropped to Shamrock, as he ran both men during his days in GCW, as well as about 2 others), and began to ascend the rankings. I finally realised that I enjoyed the front office part of e-wrestling more, and that made me create the NWF on July 1, 1998, and begin the run that evolved into what we have today. Patrick made me. As his ICW went under due to time constraints, he graciously came to the now-called NeWA Georgia to make his mark as a wrestler. As both Titan and Strider, Patrick made an impact that isn't noted by the annals of GCW history enough. His stables were fearsome (including such notables as Sephiroth, Kane, Shamrock, Scorpio, and X-Pac), and shockingly, he never won a GCW championship. Soon Patrick quit as a wrestler and became a full-time helper in my front office while he tried to re-start ICW. He had just became Commissioner and was running quite a feud with Mike Hoss and Devastation when he dropped off the map. Mike Hoss also disappeared, and the angle was dropped. Without this angle, Big Dawg fell off the GCW radar. His ICW closed down soon after, and he left the realm of e-fedding, except for a few cameo appearances in the Degenerate World Order (our sister fed that is now closed). It was then on December 30, 1999, that Patrick's life was suddenly taken away from us all. While at a friend's house, Patrick attempted to get on top of his friend's van. Apparently the vehicle was moved before he was set, and he came toppling down, landing on his head. His skull was fractured and he suffered major brain damage. It was only one day later that Patrick Little was pronounced dead, one day before the passing of the century. Patrick was a very religious man, and his funeral was something I will never forget. I didn't cry at the funeral, but I know that I am welling up with tears when I write this now. Patrick was a great friend, and without him, there would be no us. It seems only appropriate that he be inducted into the Hall of Fame that, in essence, he built. If you ever wondered what "RIPPL" means on the end of my posts, it is "Rest In Peace Patrick Little." I'm absolutely sure that he is.

Scorpio/Overkill Gimmicks: Scorpio, Overkill Height/Weight: Overkill unknown; Scorpio 6'4, 201 lbs. Titles Held: ICW Light Heavyweight Title (x2), GCW Cruiserweight Title (x2), GCW Georgia Title (x1), GCW World Title (x1), GCW World Tag Team Titles (x2), ICW Vice President, ICW Announcer, GCW Vice President
Stables: Four Horsemen, Forsaken, Forsaken 2000 Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Boy, this man's accomplishments read like a book, don't they? Scorpio was truly one of the legends of Global Championship Wrestling. Let alone the fact that he was one of the original wrestlers to join Patrick Little's ICW, he also became Patrick's Vice President to help with the rush of interest in the federation. Overkill was a trooper, and if he had ever run his own fed, I am sure it would have been just as big as anything myself or Pat could have made. As the ICW opened, Scorpio generously joined the ranks part-time to help me get off the ground. It was only after ICW folded and the NWF merged with NeWA Georgia that the man known as Scorpio took off. Scorpio was a cruiserweight in size only. A mat technition, Scorpio knew how to control a contest, and usually did. After being a member of the original Four Horsemen and Forsaken, Scorpio and Shamrock came into GCeW as allies. The team of Irish Poison was hard to beat. Soon, however, things would change. Scorpio joined former ICW ally Kane to make Forsaken a reality in the now-GCW. Scorpio then took a back seat to one of the other legends of the game and made his way to the Georgia Title. After The Messiah (Shamrock) defeated Kane at WrestleBowl I to end his career, Forsaken fell apart. Scorpio was not to be outdone, however. Almost completely unscathed by the destruction, he began to campaign for a cruiserweight division. Eventually the title became a reality, and he was one of the first men to feud for it. Scorpio had bigger plans. The formation of Forsaken 2000 would again shift the power of GCW to one group, and Scorpio led a band of atheletes that sounds like a who's who of GCW folklore now: Stephen Frost, Jack Anthony, ....it keeps going. Scorpio eventually captured the biggest prize in GCW, and Forsaken was on top of the world. Then, the two words GCW wrestlers most fear popped up: The Curse. It had him by the throat, and eventually Scorpio would be finished by it. His stable would be taken over by Stephen Frost, who went on to succumb to The Curse himself. In a showing of the true class this man possesed, he came back so he could drop the title fairly and officially retire.

The life of Overkill was also interesting. As Patrick balked at the work involved with making an e-fed work, it was Overkill that kept things running. With both of them working together, ICW would have (and did) survive as long as they wanted it to. Overkill did more matchwriting, card updating, page updating, and fed running in my abscence than any one man should have. He was (up until Executioner) the hardest working Vice President I ever had. When others would try to find a way out, he would take their job and do his too. He was just that kind of guy. No one could question his dedication. Then the conspiracy happened. It was true that at the time, Overkill was Scorpio as well. I didn't see a major problem with it: he was the Vice President, so it wasn't like he had the final word on giving himself the title. Of course, after this, I never mingled with execs-as-wrestlers again. Before Scorpio had even came NEAR winning the World Title (at this point working on pushing his Cruiserweight division), a very jealous wrestler named Insanity decided to take a risk and expose the truth. I had decided to start making "contracts" with wrestlers. I would work an idea over with them, and they would have to follow up on the idea or breech their contract and lose whatever was the eventual goal of the contract. I do this today based on mutual agreement and the plain FACT that if you don't hold your end up, the angle won't work. Back then, however, I was trying to make it sound official. Insanity agreed. After no-showing one of the biggest matches in the angle, I fired him. He got very upset and managed to get Jack Anthony's login password so he could spam the board and try to make the fed leave in a "revolution." Being the political bastards we are, we denied the charges that Overkill was Scorpio. At least, I would have--but Overkill himself said to let him handle it. And handle it he did--the eventual outcome was that Insanity was proven right, but his revolution fizzled when everyone realised the point of it was for him to become World Champion. Another problem eliminated by Overkill. OVERALL, Overkill did more for this fed than any man that has come before or since, and it will probably stay that way for a long, long time.


Sephiroth Height/Weight: 6'3, 225 lbs. Titles Held: ICW World Title (x1), ICW Internet Title (x1), ICW European Title (x1), ICW World Tag Team Title (x1)
Stables: Forsaken, Four Horsemen Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Again, another man that the history of GCW does not do justice to is Sephiroth. Let it be known that Discord was also controlled by Sephiroth and he ran both characters at once, even capturing the Tag Titles with them. Sephiroth was the original leader of the Four Horsemen, even though that incarnation of the Horsemen never stepped foot in a ring together. Sephiroth was the original head of the Forsaken, the stable that took the power from the Strider-led nWo Wolfpac and monopolized ICW. After the ICW merged with the NWF, Sephiroth left. A brief stint in GCW would lead to a mini-feud with HBK, but Seph left, angered by the cheap quantity tactics being used by HBK. What this history doesn't show, however, is that Sephiroth (as Scribe) was the President of the Degenerate World Order. I remember the first time he showed the fed to me, I was shocked. I thought immediately, "I will NEVER be that good." Unfortunately, Scribe's time didn't allow the dWo to continue, and the fed has been shut down and re-opened multiple times--all of the openings successful. I haven't heard from him in a long time, but I remember that he was always a very intelligent person that was wiser than I was when it came to running a fed. When asked why he didn't give the dWo to someone else, he told me "The dWo is my dream. I will never let someone else run my dreams." While his accomplishments happened years ago, Sephiroth was the first to bring the legendary name of "Forsaken" into the GCW realm and was also the first leader of the Four Horsemen. He ran GCW's sister fed for many years. Sephiroth has many qualifications for the Hall of Fame, just in a different sense than the others. Seph didn't lead by the keyboard--he led by the mind.

Kane Height/Weight: 7'5, 310 lbs. Titles Held: NWF Internet Title (x1), GCW World Title (x2), GCW Tag Team Titles (x1) Stables: Forsaken Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Kane was Forsaken. Simple as that. But first, let's begin with ICW. While Kane never held a title in ICW, he was certainly a force. Again, it seems almost eerie that probably the fourth highest wrestler in ICW did not win a title. Even with his tag pertner Mankind, Deathtoll couldn't capture the World Tag Team Championships. As the NWF began, Kane entered he tournament. It was here that he became a star. Beginning to focus on his work in the NWF rather than ICW (where he was one of the original Forsaken members), Kane entered the NWF Inagural Bash Tournament with winning on his mind. He tore through the first round and continued a path of destruction to the World Title match. Stunningly, Shamrock managed to down the monster and win the World Title. Kane would barely even pay attention to the Internet Title he was given as he went after the man that had defeated him. As Hitman retained the title at Haloween Horror 98 against Shamrock, Kane began to make his move. Creating The Man in the Mask, Kane would slowly gather the men he needed to start Forsaken. The stable began around the time Shamrock defeated Hitman to capture his second World Championship reign. With Shamrock the champ and Hitman's career crippled by a car crash, the door was open for Kane to get his revenge. The monster World Title rematch was set for Broken Hearts. There truly were some broken hearts in that arena, as Shamrock laid down for Kane and gave him the World Title. Shamrock became the enforcer of Forsaken, and it looked as if they were unstoppable. For many months, Kane would simply rest on his laurels. Finally, Stone Cold Steve Austin managed to call him out on a mandatory title defense and shock the world by getting the 1-2-3. His title reign was short-lived, as mucho Forsaken interference would get Kane his belt back. As the original WrestleBowl loomed closer, The Messiah began to be a major thorn in Forsaken's side. Kane and Shamrock lost the Tag Team Title to The People's Team, and things were falling apart. Kane would finally fight Messiah in a career match, a match that Messiah won and revealed himself to be Shamrock, showing that Shamrock had only joined to take Forsaken down from the inside. Kane's career was over, but the man has always been remembered as the first monster heel in GCW history and the leader of the myth, the legend known as Forsaken.


Shamrock Height/Weight: 6'8, 268 lbs. Titles Held: NWF World Title (x1), GCW Georgia Title (x3), GCW World Title (x2), GCW World Tag Team Titles (x3)
Stables: Four Horsemen, Forsaken Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Shamrock defines legend. I hear people sometimes make mention of Kane or Sephiroth, but I can't tell you how many times I have heard Shamrock's name bounce around with such quotes like what I heard from Johnny MacGrath once: "I want to be the next Shamrock." A man who never broke the rules, or only broke them with the eventual goal of doing right, Shamrock was always the face of the battle. His career began about halfway through the tenure of ICW. He was an immediate success, and with success came decisions. The battle between the nWo Wolfpac and Forsaken had begun. Forsaken had control of all the major stars except Strider (obviously), Outlaw, Ma$e, and Shamrock. Outlaw and Ma$e answered Forsaken's invitation by pounding Deathtoll into the ground and joining with Strider. Shamrock was the final 'big boy' that hadn't taken sides. Shamrock was brought into ICW by Kane (smell the irony), and therefore gave Kane one "favor" to use. Kane used it, alright, and Shamrock became a member of Forsaken. As Forsaken won the Ultimate WarGame and became the dominant stable of ICW, the fed hit hard times. Shamrock came to my fed with his mentor Kane, and shocked Kane by beating him in the finals of the Inagural Bash Tournament. At this point, GCeW began with the pieces left from the NWF and ICW. Shamrock became a main event mainstay as soon as the fed began, feuding with Hitman. Hitman was a character I had previously controlled in both ICW and the NWF, but with Kane taking time off and me not wanting to run a character and a fed, we needed someone at Shamrock's level to feud with him. Why not Shamrock himself? Shamrock took over the Hitman character and, in essence, ran the entire first three months of main event feuding in GCW by himself. During this time, Shamrock began the second installment of the Four Horsemen. Consisting of Steve Austin, The Rock, Scorpio as enforcer, and himself as leader, Shamrock's group was small in numbers but high in quality. The group eventually managed to destroy Hitman's rival nWo Black and White and make the eventual goal of the World Title coming to the Horsemen a reality. With Shamrock as champion, the Horsemen continued without trouble until Forsaken. Of course, up until the time Forsaken came in, GCW was having some trouble with members. The problem was we were brand new and we didn't have many. Since we were recieving no aid from the NeWA, the fed looked to be in major trouble. For some reason, Shamrock was compelled to help GCW. Working in the same fashion as Overkill (controlling both Overkill and Scorpio) and Big Dawg (controlling both Big Dawg and Strider), Shamrock controlled Shamrock, Hitman, Triple Threat, and one other name that escapes me now. With him running four wrestlers the fed looked larger than it really was, and it helped to gather us some members. After Hitman's "car wreck" (which allowed Shamrock to focus on his main character), Triple Threat was also phased out, as was his very-brief fourth character. Triple Threat had previously had a different handler and had even upset Shamrock for the Georgia Title under that handler's tenure. However, Triple Threat was engaged in angles when his handler disappeared. Shamrock didn't want to see the angles fall apart, so he took control of the extra character. He also had taken Hitman from me, and made one more--I guess just for more members. He never even told me that he had taken over the fouth guy and Triple Threat until much later, when he was ready to leave. GCW had taken off, and he got to finally drop them. His first program with Kane leading up to Broken Hearts was just as fantastic as the first encounter in the NWF. The infamous laying down for Kane to give him the title sent shockwaves through GCW, and made Forsaken back into the dominant (if not more dominant) power that it was in ICW. As Shamrock began to start throwing matches and making "errors" to cost Forsaken victories, The Messiah hit the scene. Messiah was a masked figure sent from somewhere (I guess above) to destroy Kane. So once again back to double duty controlling characters, Shamrock would contiunue screwing up and Messiah continued to wreak havoc on Kane and his Forsaken. Messiah/Kane Career Match at WrestleBowl was signed, and Shamrock began to get back on the right page with Forsaken. The Enforcer of Forsaken was needed to do things right at this point, and he did so. It was all a plan. Shamrock secretly got half of Forsaken to make him the unofficial leader, making Kane's control almost null and void. When The Messiah defeated Kane and eliminated him from GCW, Messiah was unmasked as Shamrock. The half of Forsaken that was following him left Forsaken, and the other half, without a leader, self-destructed. As a matter of fact, only one man remained from that old group...Scorpio. His Forsaken 2000 would become the new dominant stable. With all of the work Shamrock had put in for GCW in the past year, his burnout level was high and he took time off. Since Austin and Rock couldn't hold it together to fight off Forsaken 2000, Shamrock returned to help. Austin, the new leader of the Horsemen, decided not to be so open to giving Shamrock his job back. So Shamrock became a loner, and he would spend the rest of his career that way. After a few victories over Forsaken 2k, Austin offered him the job, but he refused. The Horsemen then became the unwanted helpers of Shamrock. Shamrock even went so far as to tie them up in the back to keep them from helping him through another beating. Just as GCW was gearing up for an Austin vs. Shamrock vs. Scorpio pay per view main event, Scorpio went down at the hands of The Curse. This GREATLY effected Shamrock, and with Scorpio being the final "old man" in GCW to leave other than himself, he felt as if his welcome had been worn out. Stephen Frost took the World Title and Austin began to chase him again. Shamrock would fall off the radar for nearly five months. He played only a minor part role, but refused to "get in the way" of the new guys fighting for the title. He ran a small angle where his father defected to New Forsaken 2000, but the angle was never completed, as Frost succombed to The Curse and Austin left. Shamrock decided to leave as well, unhappy with the new group promoting tons of roleplays over angles and compelling storytelling. After Austin left and Frost was virtually hung by the new 'age of workers', Psyberia and his new workers took control. The lack of angles and basic "roleplay > win" structure worked for them, but top talent looked the other way at the lack of storylines and GCW fell off the mainstream radar. When Psyberia himself, leader of his 'age of workers' went down to The Curse, the movement collapsed and the fed running it almost did, too. It took a long time to convince people that yes, you could still win no matter who you were against, but the CLOSE matches would be swayed by the angles involved (as this is how we run today). After what is considered the "low point" of GCW, Overlord came out as Champion. Without a major opponent to fight at WrestleBowl II (Scotty Steiner had managed to get himself fired), there was only one man I thought to look to: Shamrock. He whined for a while, but eventually understood what needed to be done. Shamrock went in carrying the good name of the Horsemen, and the stipulations to the match were that the Four Horsemen would be ban from GCW if Overlord won. Shamrock was quoted as saying "If the Horsemen die, I die." And so the match took place, with Shamrock losing to Overlord and being removed from GCW. Truly, this man's career was the definition of sacrifice and loyalty. Shamrock was offered many times to join MSCW, and even the IWO/FWLnet, the largest e-wrestling organization on the Internet. He withdrew from all of these offers to stay with GCW, and GCW went from a backwater fed to one that was covered by the various e-wrestling websites because of his devotion to staying with me and GCW. His sacrifices usually blind me to when others sacrifice for GCW, because I always think someone will match what he has done for GCW. I don't think there will ever be a way anyone will. If Shamrock doesn't make the Hall of Fame, there shouldn't be one.

Triple Threat Titles held: NWF Internet Title (1), GCW Georgia Title (2) Inducted: 12-18-99 Career Highlight: Defeating Shamrock for the GCW Georgia Title.  More research will be done on Triple Threat's prestigious career soon.

Unknown Height/Weight: What else?--unknown Titles Held: GCW Georgia Title (x1) Stables: None Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: As Crow put it in one of his columns, Unknown was the original mind bender. As most people even today can remember, the mind games Overlord played with his opposition could sometimes completely overcome his opponent. Unknown's use of black text on a black background annoyed some, but served a purpose always. In fact, Unknown's titles and stables seems irrelevent. Unknown was never interested in stables or titles and sometimes took a beating for it. However, most stables just stayed away from the insane man and hoped he stayed away from them. It was one man--Stone Cold Steve Austin--that would not leave the mind bender alone, leading to what would probably be ranked second only to Kane/Shamrock as the most heated feud in the history of our fed. By the time Broken Hearts came around, Steve Austin was frequently sending me messages saying that if he ever met the man, he would kill him. Steve wasn't the most hardened man, but it took a lot more than a week's worth of roleplays to fry him. With Unknown, it only took 3. Add to this Unknown using a foreign object to take the Georgia Title from the Horsemen member and the fire was raging. Every time Austin would have Unknown, he would escape--be it through a trap door or a zip line swinging him back up to the rafters. His personna drove Austin mad, but unfortunately, this feud fizzled at the end, keeping it from #1. The gigantic steel cage match was set between Austin and Unknown for the Georgia Title, but by Broken Hearts Unknown had disappeared. The character was kept long enough to finish the match, but I know that was one thing Steve always regretted--never getting to finish the feud properly. And where did Unknown go to? I knew him personally, and I don't know. He dropped out of school and his whereabouts today really are unknown. Mind bender to the end.


"Stone Cold" Steve Austin Height/Weight: 6'2, 252 lbs. Titles Held: GCW Georgia Title (x1), GCW World Tag Team Titles (x1), GCW World Title (x1) Stables: Four Horsemen Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Unfortunately, this summary of Stone Cold's career will read more like a book of missed opportunities (mainly not his fault) and blown chances. Steve Austin was simply one of the greats in GCW history, but considering the people that held the belts around him (Kane, Shamrock, Hitman, etc.), Steve almost looks like an upset champion. When Steve entered GCeW, the fed was already started and running poorly. He was one of the first major talents to enter the federation. Austin started his career fighting to get a spot in the Four Horsemen along with his tag team partner, The Rock. When him and Rock made the cut, the fun began. Steve immediately got embroiled with Unknown in the most hateful feud I've ever seen. Most people mutually agreed on the angle--Austin and Unknown would constantly send me different strats, and I finally got to the point to whichever one roleplayed more that week got their strat used. As Austin toiled with Unknown, The People's Team also toiled for the GCW Tag Team Titles. Unfortunately, Austin and Rock were stuck in the shadows of Irish Poison, consisting of Scorpio and Shamrock. Playing second fiddle tag team was OK because of the Georgia Tag Titles. The feud between them, the Freebirds, the brand new team of Extreme Devastation, and X-Pac and Dan Benson (as the Demolition Squad) was going great, but the dropoff in main event tag teams caused the belt to be vacated so the tag team division would allow teams like Austin and Rock to rub elbows with the main eventers. This plan worked, and after the Shamrock defection Steve got his chance. After finally defeating Unknwon to win the Georgia Title (the chance for the most memorable feud in GCW history lost on account of Unknown), Steve got his shot to go after Kane, since Shamrock had left and Scorpio was gone as well. Austin tried to make the Horsemen large, and in fact, we had a Six Horsemen at one time, even if it lasted merely a few weeks. Austin and Shamrock were the obvious advisaries, but Steve knew there was a weakness. He managed to get a shot at the figurehead of Kane via the madatory title defense rule, and took him down. The shock was huge, that this "low-budget Horsemen" had managed to take the strap off Kane! Austin would reign for only about a month, however, as Kane and Forsaken would push their way back into possesion of the title. With Shamrock's elimination of Kane, Mr. Stone Cold was not willing to play second fiddle again. In fact, it turned out to be a bad decision. Austin would lose another chance for the title in a triple threat match with Scorpio and Shamrock (again, Scorpio's fault, not his), and Shamrock left as well. With Steve now by himself to fight Stephen Frost, he did so. But finally, in an opportunity he lost himself, Austin began to disappear from radar. Steve was angry at himself for hurting his ties with Shamrock, and angry that his chance to feud with the legends was gone. Frost took it as victory, and poor old Steve didn't have another run left in him. He faded out without a trace. Without even mentioning the huge World Tag Team Title victory for himself and Rock over the unstoppable rookie force of Mike Hoss and Devastation, it is obvious Austin had a big place in early GCW history. However, the lost opportunities took their toll on his spirit, and he finally quit with a sputter. It is a shame, since a man of his stature should have gone out with a bang. Instead, he just has to hope he makes the Hall of Fame.


Duke Nukem Height/Weight: 6'7, 350 lbs. Titles Held: GCW Georgia Title (x1), GCW Commissioner Stables: None Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: "Hail to the King, baby!" Duke Nukem made his start in GCW after the handler's first character quit. Duke was always virtually a loner, a man who's alliances rested with no one. Duke's main goal was to kick peoples' asses and look cool doing it, a predecessor to the "cool" characters in e-wrestling today. Duke's first few months in GCW were not the most noteworthy, as he fought his way through the card. Eventually he got his name put in lights as Georgia Champion, and reigned there until Mike Hoss knocked him off. The only thing that kept Duke Nukem from becoming a star the likes of Shamrock or Scorpio was time, or the lack thereof. Early in his career, his main focus was on winning, and it showed as his roleplays sent him to the top of the midcard in no time. However, after that loss to Mike Hoss, Duke's career started going south. It really wasn't his fault; real life had just caught up to him, as it does for many of us sometimes. Unfortunately for Duke, instead of taking a lot of time off, he took small doses and refused to take a vacation. Duke would rather see himself as an active, losing member than a premadonna star that picked his spots, much like what Scorpio would do to him later. So his roleplays would tower over his opposition, but when he only got up one against five, he just couldn't match them. So Duke's career got stuck in the midcard mud, and stayed there. When school let out for Christmas of '99, Nukem made his biggest impact. This time in a serious push for the belt, Duke was held off by Scorpio. Scorpio knew that when Duke was on, it was going to take an increadible effort to stop him, and even with the effort that Scorpio could put out, the chances were a mere 50/50 of winning. Duke was on. So instead of fighting Duke straight up, he made Nukem "earn" the opportunity. And so he kept Duke stuck fighting others until Christmas ended, and Duke again lost the time. An eventual few title shots would not prove successful, and Duke's career sputtered out. He disappeared from radar until he made a triumphant return with the HWAC a few months later. Unfortunately, some problems with real life caused Duke to cut that run short, and other than part bits, his career was over. Duke was very original when it came to angles (no one else has blown up the ramp with C4 since him) and that probably helped him with his other persuit...

What isn't mentioned in Duke's career is what made him great: his backstage work. Nukem was one of the few people that cared less about winning and more about the fed as a whole. He'd take time out to work on getting some member of another fed to join us. If that ment losing the roleplay that could have won him the match, so be it. While reading Duke's career it sounds like (to the critical ones out there) a batch of disappointment, but I don't think Duke was disappointed at all. He was the one person that cared more about the wellness of the whole rather than the self. He was one of the members of the infamous "blitzkreig" advertising campaign that made GCW explode. He even got promoted to Commissioner simply because of the massive amount of work that he did, it didn't seem right to not have him on staff. Duke wouldn't be disappointed. Just to see GCW running would make Duke the happiest man in the fed.


Stephen Frost Height/Weight: 6'2, 247 lbs. (trimmed to 220 when the Cruiserweight division began) Titles Held: GCW Cruiserweight Title (x1), GCW Georgia Title (x1), GCW World Tag Team Titles (x2), GCW World Title (x2)
Stables: Forsaken 2000, New Forsaken 2000 Inducted: 11-18-01 Summary: Stephen Frost's career didn't look good at the beginning. What made arguably the biggest star since Scorpio look to be in trouble? He wasn't very good. Really. Stephen Frost started off as just another newbie, which made his stratospheric rise that much better for me, as a fed head, to watch. It was simply great to see a homegrown GCW product make that many strides. He may have been a rookie, but he was a smart one. Frost made an ally early, and that ally would be known as fellow hall of fame candidate Jack Anthony. Together the men used the tag ranks to show their stuff. Scorpio saw a lot that he liked and took them on immediately. Together they rose to the top. Frost got a brush with greatness from Shamrock, who lost the belt to the young star. Frost was bested by Scorpio only one week later, making him hold the dubious distinction of having the shortest title reign in GCW history. Frost regrouped and fought back, eventually gaining a second title shot. Scorpio and Frost fought to a time limit draw. As the emotional moment unfolded, the future was clear: Frost was next in line to hold the honors of being Forsaken leader. When Frost won the belt, he was truly on top of his game. When no one could best him, he even went so far as to fight the Cruiserweight Champion, just so he could have that other singles belt with "World" on it. Frost was a powerhouse and ruled in a time when there were no other titans. However, the young stars were coming up, and soon enough Overlord, Scott Steiner, and Psyberia were knocking on his door. In true GCW fashion, Frost succumbed to the curse. After Psyberia scored a monster non-title upset win, Frost's days were numbered. The new generation had the guts to go after him now, and they did. What they did after was apalling, completely berating Frost's tenure as Champion by using examples from the time they had been there, which was only a portion of his reign. It was enough to make me contemplate retirement for the first time, one of only three such instances since I have run GCW. With his reputation tarnished, Frost wanted to return for a quick 4-6 week run. I was very negative to the idea, as the fed was rebuilding and didn't need someone to squash everyone for a month and then leave again. I have lost contact with Stephen, but I hope he sees this. He was the best homegrown talent in GCW history. Now others were "homegrown," like Shamrock, Scorpio, and Patrick. But they all started at World Title level. Frost started as nothing and made his way to Champion, all in his career at GCW. And that is something that even a President can be proud of.
