GCW History, by the Masked Marauder

GCW History by Kirby, the Masked Marauder

[ Prologue ]

For those of you who do not know me, and that should be a significant amount considering the many years that have passed, my name is Kirby or as GCW has remembered me, the Masked Marauder.   I’m leading the long, long journey towards a complete GCW history in the only way I know: by example.  It is my intention that every GCW President will be able to write about their tenures in office, hopefully creating a history that will be just as vivid and memorable as the events it transcribes.  If anything, it should make for a decent read when the parleys are coming in slow on a Wednesday afternoon.  With that out of the way, I will begin recapping my chapter of GCW, from the original foundations laid in June of 1998 to my bloody mess of a retirement at WrestleBowl III in August, 2001.  The origins of the greatest creation I have ever made or will ever make: Global Championship Wrestling.

[ Introduction ]

Well we all like to stroke our egos by believing that someone wants to know about how we got started, right?  If you don’t, just scroll on down to part one.  But if you want to sit down next to the cozy fireplace and recount with me the tale of a lonesome nerd with not a whole lot to look forward to in life than wrestling, well, you’re in the right place.

My roots with fantasy games in general were not deep at all.  In fact, I had never played a fantasy game until the eighth grade of middle school—and for those doing their math here, that’s only a year before I ran GCW.   Experienced I was not, but what I lacked in understanding I made up for in motivation.  Some friends and I originally created a fantasy war game to play at the lunch table because lets face it, middle school lunch break is not exactly The Cheetah—it gets boring.   So with nothing better to do, we all worked together and created not only this war game, but even some Dungeons and Dragons-style roleplaying games.  It was very enjoyable and yes, we were certainly teased at the lunch table.  But the way I saw it, Ian was on the football team as a lineman so he could kick anyone’s ass if necessary.  Ian would go on to be the short-lived Minion in GCW.  The third member of our little group was Patrick Little—and the Hall of Fame should be pretty self-explanatory for him.

As high school began, we all started to lose contact.  While talking to a friend and fellow wrestling fan of mine, I was told about this new thing he was doing called “fantasy wrestling.”  He said all I had to do was send an e-mail with my wrestler (yeah, we didn’t have fancy application forms then - /end grandpa rant) information and since I knew the President (Patrick), I would be in, no questions asked.  The promotion was brand new, so standards were not high.  At all.  If you could write a semi-coherent two line roleplay, welcome aboard.  The federation was called Internet Championship Wrestling.  The friend that referred me?  Overkill.  And so my journey began.

I tried to keep the introduction short and sweet, so there you go.  I’m sure we all had somewhat similar experiences, and that’s why I thought I would share some background with all of you.  Just trying to let you know that no matter how they act, your administration had roots very similar to your own.

[ Part I: ICW/NWF History ]

If possible, it would not be me beginning this history—it would be Patrick Little.  He created ICW at an unspecified date in June, although all indications point to it being within the first or second weeks of the month.  Overkill had already been in for a while running three characters, and I was quick to assume the role of “Hitman.”  Hitman was not a complicated character.  Simply based on Bret Hart with some edge, Hitman took the better parts of Hart to me (his ability and catchphrases) and discarded the stuff I didn’t like (the stupid pink tights).  Turn him heel, and you’ve pretty much got it.  I remember my first match was against a jobber, and as it works today, he was just a basic drone that couldn’t fight back (my apologies to Satan).  Of course, I was ambitious as hell and got a fellow named “DDP” mad at me by coming out of nowhere and challenging him.  The way I saw it, he could only write two lines.  I would wipe the floor with him and be Internet Champion (secondary champ) before my first seven days on the job were done.  By the time Friday came around, I was anxiously sitting through the day, going over and over through my head the scenario of me winning them both.  I knew I had beat DDP that week in roleplays—of course, that’s what they all say ;-)  I finally got home and jumped online and Overkill told me the show was up!  My fat palms sweating, I read the entire show top to bottom.  Can you tell I was hooked by day one?  I read through and in the second match, Hitman had indeed destroyed the jobber so bad it barely lasted three sentences.  Well, not a whole lot written there, but a win is a win and I’d take it.   As the show continued, I got to the match before the main event World Title bout—the Internet Title defense.  I read the whole match, determined not to peek at the ending.   I’ve never read that fast in my life.   As I finally got to the bottom, a little cheating and one Diamond Cutter later, Hitman was 1-1.  I was stunned.  What the hell was that?  I inquired the next time I saw Patrick online, and he told me very bluntly, “I can’t have a guy walk in on the first day and win the Internet Title.  It would just look bad.”  And a funny thing happened: I completely understood.  You can’t have a guy being groomed to fight the World Champ have an off day and job to a new guy in his first show!  How do you have storylines when they can be destroyed by a one-week fluke?  This was a lesson I never forgot, and I had many storylines that people still talk to me today about because of that lesson.

I suppose now would be a good time to introduce another name from GCW’s past—Shamrock.   Being another wrestling fan and friend of mine personally, I decided to mention this fantasy wrestling thing to him.  Although it was geeky as hell in hindsight to introduce an e-fed to a guy who you had only seen outside of school once, I WAS a geek back then.  It made no difference to me.  I sent the fateful e-mails away and soon enough, Shamrock was entering ICW and quickly becoming the hot “free agent” in the stable war between the established nWo and the emerging Forsaken.  Hitman’s role in the nWo (I had to pick sides, too) was negligible.

ICW built up to its first Pay Per View, the Ultimate WarGame.  But for me, roleplaying was no longer my biggest interest.  I’ve always been a person to love to make things work and get maximum enjoyment out of watching others enjoy my creation.  Patrick was obviously neglecting the fed for reasons I have no clue about, and I got fed up with watching Overkill have to run everything by himself.   Realizing that Patrick might not want to give up power to a guy who had been in e-fedding for 3 weeks too easily, I decided to create my own federation and invite some of the ICW guys to come join as long as they stayed active in both feds—and on July 1, 1998, the National Wrestling Federation—and official GCW history—began.

The NWF was a name I had been using since I was a kid, dreaming up my own fantasy wrestling leagues before the term even existed.  My invitation went over well, as I got Overkill to bring his characters into the NWF, Shamrock to bring—err—Shamrock into the fed, and Patrick to bring the Strider character in.  Along with some other new recruits, we had our NWF Tournament to decide all of the champions.  It was an 8 man tourney, with the winners facing off for the World Title and the semifinal losers competing for the tertiary belt, the Television Title.  This ended in the NWF Inaugural Bash, a big, decently-written Pay Per View supercard with the title matches headlining.  Shamrock defeated Kane and became the first World Champ.  Visions of a Kane/Shamrock blood feud danced in my head, but that was crushed by reality.  Kane was Overkill’s character, and he was stretched too thin between working on ICW, the Ultimate WarGame PPV, and trying to keep up with the NWF.  He bowed out for a while, leaving me with no viable contender to Shamrock’s title.  Well, there was one.  The Excellence of Execution was about to bring it.

Hitman quickly became Shamrock’s major enemy in the NWF, and the first big step in GCW roleplaying history was taken there.  For the first time, we were writing roleplays with introductions, body paragraphs, and conclusions.  It seems like nothing now, but the difference between two page roleplays and two LINE roleplays is unquestionable.  Why did it happen?  Nothing more than competition, I’d say.  We both wanted to prove we were better and when Shamrock began the feud by making a two page roleplay, I knew I had to respond.  It was around this time that ICW had the Ultimate WarGame.  I have read the show at least 5 times and it’s just as much fun to read now as it was then.  Shamrock joined Forsaken for the WarGame match (a triple cage with an electric chair on top—sound familiar?), Strider lost the World Title to Sephiroth, and Forsaken had taken control.  The only problem with this was Forsaken had EVERY good wrestler except two.  Who do they fight?   The two surviving nWo members, Outlaw (yes, that Outlaw) and Ma$e, quickly quit and defected to my NWF because they saw 8 guys just as good as them breathing down their neck.  Forsaken soon followed them into the NWF.  I was fortunate, though, that Overkill decided not to bring his three characters into the NWF and Sephiroth went back to our sister fed, the Degenerate World Order (dWo).  Instead, I just got four solid face midcarders, two solid heel midcarders, and a man (Overkill) who would soon resurface and create an angle which made all the basic “face vs. heel because they both want the belt” stuff I was doing look like WCW in 2000.

As the NWF began running weekly shows, I told more of my closer friends about it and they began to think this whole fantasy wrestling thing was cool, even if they weren’t wrestling fans.  Of course, wrestling was in the midst of the mainstream boom, so everyone at least caught a little bit of the action.  Triple Threat, Dragon, and Cheetah soon joined the ranks and I had myself a substantial little fed.   It was time to build some feuds.

As is always the case, the best way to build some solid feuds is to create some factions, and that I did.  Hitman re-created the nWo in the NWF, bringing back Outlaw and Ma$e and recruiting Dragon and Triple Threat.  Shamrock refused help, valiantly fighting them all off.  His battles were helped at least by mutual allies in the old Forsaken members, who soon lined up to tee off on Outlaw and Ma$e.  The NWF was chugging along very well and I started to look around for ways to promote our league.  If there was one thing I realized very quickly, it was that relying on the same old people could bring enjoyment, but the fed would never reach a top-tier level.  I was not in this fed thing to run a show between old ICW guys and local friends.   The search engines at that point had no categories for “fantasy wrestling,” so every fantasy site just got tossed into the gigantic “pro wrestling” category—not good for attention.   There had to be another way.  I soon stumbled across some e-fed “alliances.”   These were groups of feds that worked independently, but every once in a while worked together to put on huge inter-fed supercards.   The idea was sound, and it promoted wrestlers that were alliance “champions” sliding between promotions regularly to keep the roleplaying amounts strong in each.  Being naive and thinking “how could this possibly have any downsides?” I joined the NWF up with the National e-Wrestling Alliance, or NeWA.  My familiarity with the NWA and its official link on the real NWA website won me over, and I decided not to apply to the National Wrestling Council, or NWC.  Guess which one became more famous?  The next generation of GCW history was about to begin.

[ Part II: NeWA Georgia to GCeW ]

As Part I left off, the NWF was going along decently but in need of advertising. The dinky Angelfire website with the nWo theme midi in the background as you entered (yes, even I have to breathe a sigh of disappointment at that to this day) was not exactly blowing the competition away. We had no history, only a few shows archived, and no blow-away stars to impress the masses. In short, we were at the point that the vast majority of feds close up shop. For me, however, failure was not an option. I am a very, very stubborn person. Even if I absolutely hate to do something, I loathe every moment of it, if I voluntarily started doing it I will refuse to quit until it’s done. Well, I was enjoying the NWF immensely, so you can get a good idea of how motivated I was. Having no life to speak of, I had plenty of time to look around for ways to promote, and the NeWA homepage came across the monitor of my HP Pentium 133MHz. The NeWA separated their federations into regions of the United States. I quickly noticed that there was already a Georgia division. I was disappointed, but already thinking. Maybe the NWF could just be a generic “NeWA South?” Maybe we could just move operations to Huntsville? Shuddering at the thought (sorry, it’s a GA tradition to make fun of AL), I checked out the NeWA Georgia website. I was stunned by the blue background and yellow text. This site…was actually worse than mine. As it turned out, the President was some fella by the name of Ole Anderson and he was currently looking to sell the fed. My jaw dropped. The NWF’s golden opportunity had arrived. I sent an e-mail to Ole (even asking if he knew the real Ole Anderson…sigh, why the hell am I telling people this) and linked him to the NWF as my credentials. ICW had gone down, so there was nothing else for my reputation to sit upon than the young National Wrestling Federation. I was also afraid he actually expected someone to pay MONEY for his monstrosity of a website, so I had to ask about that as well. Well, the fed didn’t cost a thing—and it was mine. The NWF was about to be renamed Georgia Championship e-Wrestling. I told Overkill about it, my second in command at this point (and would stay that way until 2000, when Executioner took the task), and I both agreed this was a great opportunity. I announced the merger and we moved to the GCW website. AND I DIDN’T CHANGE THE COLORS. How anyone managed to read three lines of text without having a seizure I will never know. I quickly found a lot of perks for being in the NeWA. They gave us a message board, no need to set it up. We got free advertising on the NeWA’s main page. We were free to meet and talk to promoters from every division to try and work out a talent exchange. I was psyched. Now, I thought, time to see about all the former GCW employees that I was inheriting with the merger… None. As it turned out, the fed had been a simmed fed only, with no actual roleplayers except Devon Greene, who was controlled by the same handler as Ole. He no longer wanted any part of e-fedding, so my initial talent gain from this move was zero. I asked the NeWA President of the time, whose name I don’t remember and don’t care to, if I could be allowed some time before my champions had to fight around the league since I had gained no new members. The time was granted. I also asked for some of the NeWA champions to come by our promotion and run some programs with Shamrock. They said “just as soon as you establish yourself here.” Apparently, we never did. Back on the wrestling front, Hitman’s feud with Shamrock had grown stale. I had ignored it, mainly because of my work administratively. I knew things needed to be shaken up. So I had Shamrock fight Devon Greene to determine the first Georgia Heavyweight Champion of GCeW’s new era. Shamrock won, and his feud with Hitman over the now-called “Heavyweight Championship” (only World Title allowed was the multi-fed NeWA World strap) was put on the back-burner as the new member of the Black and White, Triple Threat, became Shamrock’s major feuding partner. Meanwhile, Hitman was attacked by a new group: the Dungeon of Darkness. I had told a friend from a neighboring High School about GCeW, and he was hooked immediately. He came in as Afterburn—he would be best-known as X-Pac. He also got a few of his friends from school to join him and create a new faction. Great, I thought—but I quickly found out these guys were for real. Not your normal newbies, the Dungeon of Darkness became a force immediately. Their ability to create multi-paragraph roleplays brought fear to the eyes of all those who opposed them. Being faces, their leader Donovan (who would also be known as HOFer Duke Nukem) challenged Hitman for the World Title. Well I was a little busy arguing with the NeWA. I felt that we should have an NeWA Champion come to our fed before we went to theirs. They wanted Shamrock, and I wanted to see some results from a merger that had thus-far given me nothing I could have taken on my own. They obliged, but by the time this argument was over it was Saturday and I had written only two roleplays as Hitman for the week. Donovan wrote five. I suffered my first dilemma as President: should I have Donovan go over as he deserved and risk alienating my old-guard roleplaying core, or should I have Hitman win and risk losing four top-notch talents? I decided on a compromise: I had Hitman win the match, but by DQ. After the match, I had the nWo and the DoD get into a giant brawl, DoD winning, thus setting Donovan up for a feud against Hitman. After I posted the show, I received a very angry phone call from Afterburn asking why his bud had lost to Hitman. I tried to explain my side of the case, but they were all quite mad about it. Finally, getting into the heat of the moment, I said something along the lines of “you should have lost anyway.” Bye bye, DoD, good to see ya. I had learned a new lesson: don’t ever let the heat of the moment get to you. I had also lost my major feud heading out of the summer. So with the NeWA breathing down my neck, four very talented roleplayers gone, NeWA Georgia entered into the fall months with school beginning and the available roleplaying talent pool dwindling. GCeW’s chances looked grim. But a monster feud (that was basically a shoot) and some brilliant ideas from my Vice President brought GCW into its first Golden Era. The battle with the NeWA had me drained, and fortunately, those close to me realized it. Shamrock volunteered to take over the Hitman character and Overkill decided to take some of the load off me and send his character, Scorpio, into the strange world of the NeWA Official Message Boards. Scorpio had entered into GCW without fanfare and worked his way up the ranks. Overkill loved the idea of a cruiserweight title and decided to challenge the NeWA Light Heavyweight Champion, Juventud Guerrera. He theorized that perhaps instead of pleading for NeWA members to come to GCW, we should just call one out. It worked, and Juventud was booked to defend his Lightweight belt against one of GCW’s own on Sunday Night Fight. Scorpio and I both bought the farm on the “NeWA earned respect” idea, so Scorpio went all-out and absolutely demolished Guerrera in roleplaying, 6 to 1. As I began writing the show, the NeWA contacted me and said that they had been watching the match up and made the final decision on their own belts. And in their honest opinion, Scorpio had done a “great job” but the winner was Juventud. As it was explained to me, he had more important feuds than Scorpio so thus, he was to go over. To say we were pissed would be like saying G.I. Bro was a mildly embarrassing. Scorpio about had a heart attack and was ready to burn the NeWA down (however you do that electronically), but I had a better idea. I said OK, and wrote the show. Juventud won, as promised. However, I had no directions explaining how he won. So I had Scorpio absolutely obliterate Guerrera until the ref called the match. But according to the rules of the NeWA, you could only win the title by pinfall or submission. Scorpio heard this and went ballistic, yanking Juventud off his stretcher and powerbombing him through the said stretcher. That got the ref to change his initial decision and rule it a DQ win for the Light Heavyweight Champ. Well, this didn’t go over very good with the NeWA President, but since he had never said anything about how the match was supposed to go, there wasn’t a whole lot he could do about it. We weren’t stupid; we knew there would be consequences for what we did. But we needed attention and attention we received. There became two decisive camps after this incident: one, the side of the younger NeWA guys who agreed that the old guys kept themselves on top and had got what they deserved. The other side included the “enlisted,” those who had a vested interest in keeping things as they were. I had a lot of talent in my corner, and I knew we had to keep up the pressure on the NeWA. The NeWA Champion of the time, whose name escapes me, was set to defend his World Title at the end of the month. Being an old guard fellow and a great talent, he decided to call out GCW’s best. The best at the time was Hitman (cough Shamrock cough), but Shamrock’s main character wasn’t doing too much. So Shamrock headed to the main NeWA boards and learned how to elevate his game. No longer was “random event opening > speak to someone allegedly important but not really like a secretary > trash talk > fade to black” good enough, as it still seems to be today for many folks. Now, character had to be established. Thematic roleplaying was necessary for a Shamrock win. He learned quickly and began a torrid pace of two roleplays per day for the week before the Pay Per View, while at the same time defending the GCW Title that week as Hitman. The NeWA Champ had “computer problems” two days before the show and had to withdraw. Shamrock was instead saddled with another competitor, the NeWA’s best of the time, and managed to roleplay him to a standstill. Guess who won? You get one try. It was at this point that I got an interesting message from the artists formerly known as the Dungeon of Darkness. They had been wandering the NeWA feds (some of them) trying to find a new fed to fight in and had decided that there weren’t any like GCW. They were back, and they wanted to challenge for the NeWA 6 man tag team titles. I was more than happy to tell them go for it. The NeWA sent me a message thereafter basically saying that GCeW was trying to challenge for too many (read: one) NeWA titles at the time and we needed to chill out. So for some reason…I did. I don’t know why, maybe at the time I thought peace would be better than risking it and losing my spot in the alliance. After all, these guys in other feds who were celebrating our rebellious ways had no commitment to follow GCeW out of the alliance. GCeW stopped causing the NeWA problems and the NeWA simply ignored us for a few weeks. After a few weeks, the champion with computer problems had vanished. A tournament was announced for the vacant NeWA World Championship, and the first 16 men to apply for the tournament were in. Since we had done nothing on the NeWA front for a while, Shamrock went ahead and applied for a seed. 24 hours later, the brackets had been updated, but with no Shamrock. 48 hours later, all 16 slots were filled and Shamrock was not included. I e-mailed the NeWA in protest, and the feud began again. I e-mailed the NeWA Thursday night about Shamrock’s situation. I went out and had dinner with my folks Friday, and returned to have three new messages, all from the NeWA President. The first said that Shamrock hadn’t applied in time for the tournament, and thus was not included. Sounds fishy, but I was willing to accept that on the condition that Shamrock got a shot once the Champ was crowned. I never got the chance to strike the deal. The second e-mail I received stated that because GCW had “real wrestlers” (Shamrock and Hitman) on its roster, it had 24 hours to remove these wrestlers before its contract with the NeWA was terminated. I responded to this e-mail without opening the third, stating that Shamrock had very little in common with Ken Shamrock (since I know the guy who handled Shamrock had never actually seen the real deal before), and Hitman had some things in common with Bret Hart, but the name was not one of them. I then opened the third e-mail. Sent only THREE HOURS after the second, this third e-mail said that our 24 hours were up and GCW had been removed from the NeWA. I was shocked. Not mad, but shocked. I hit the reply button but decided against it—no, I would reply publicly. I posted on the NeWA message board (back before we had usernames and passwords to ban) and said that GCeW had been kicked out, pasted the e-mails I had received, pasted the timestamps, and said that GCeW was no longer Georgia Championship e-Wrestling, it was Global Championship e-Wrestling and that the true World Champion was Hitman. And if anyone in the NeWA had a problem with it, they could come on down to GCeW and see what they’re made of. And just so you know, this name we have been using for so many years now—the Global moniker—I thought up in about 10 seconds while writing the message board post. We already had a cool “GCW” logo and I didn’t have the graphics knowledge to change it. I figured that once I could do graphics, I would change the name to something cooler. Oops. Anyway, I also dared anyone who thought this was unjust to “join the revolution” and leave the NeWA. I had 15 applications the next day. GCeW was going to be just fine. And to note, the administration of the NeWA made things a lot worse for their side by never replying to my message and LEAVING IT UP FOR THREE DAYS before deleting it, so a ton of people saw it. No one challenged Hitman. The DoD was back, Scorpio was a cult hero, Shamrock’s reputation was building, and the NeWA had ended up giving us 15 new members, 10 of which stayed for at least 3 months. I dumped the horrid old Geocities site for a new Xoom site and GCeW began to build towards its first ever Pay Per View event, Halloween Horror.
