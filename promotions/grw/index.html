Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Georgia Regional Wrestling's own surviving record opens already mid-run, with a reigning champion and an established cross-promotional title in place. Its own broadcasts describe it as run out of Southern Championship Wrestling's Georgia region, and a May 1998 title defense carries the cross-tag “[CWC:GRW]” for a title shared with the Continental Wrestling Council — two outside banners its own record answers to without ever losing its own name or its own two weekly cards.

That May 1998 main event, a Scaffold Match for the Southern States Championship, ended in chaos: both “The Submissionist” Angel Reaper and challenger Ian Walker fell from the rig together, and the replay showed Walker landing on top — but the champion was announced “still” the titleholder regardless, to a chorus of boos, before a newcomer ran in and attacked him. That August, GRW staged its own Georgia Cup, a forty-four-man, twenty-two-team tag tournament run alongside a strap match and a lingerie match on the same Heatwave '98 card; “Die Hard” Michael Lewd used the tournament to turn on the promotion's own fans outright, announcing on camera that he was done being “a fan favorite” before entering as “The Iceman” David Smith's partner.

By its next surviving cards, in November 1999, GRW was running under two singles titles: “The Warrior” Logan Gunz as Georgia State Champion and “The Razor” Steve Blades as Atlanta City Champion. Blades retained the Atlanta City title in a bloody rematch against “The Sandman” Jim Green by turning Green's own blinding powder back into his face and finishing him with a superkick.

That same week's Friday Night Showdown opened by memorializing Jacob Moore Sr., billed as the man who had brought in the promotion's own foreign talent and a retired “International Champion” who had once held four titles on four continents at once; his son, Jacob Moore Jr., delivered the eulogy on camera and vowed to finish what his father hadn't. The same card turned dark when Denny Langham, refusing to wrestle his scheduled tag match, struck the promotion's own commissioner and then abducted Gunz's manager, Destiny, driving off with her as the camera caught a license plate reading “LEGEND.”

How that angle resolved, and how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record. Its last surviving document, a December 1999 call for roleplayers, still billed Langham and Blades on the active roster and complained that handlers kept joining and then disappearing.
