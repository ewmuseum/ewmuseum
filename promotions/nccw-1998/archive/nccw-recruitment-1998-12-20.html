NCCW — Recruitment

"[NCCW] At least give it a read..."

20 December 1998
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
New Classic Championship Wrestling. It's based in tradition. Now, I know, I know, some of you are passing this off as just another e-fed. Well, it might just be. I mean, how many e-feds can reach the level of the IIWF's, the SCRA's, the GFWA's. I'll admit, we're not there yet. But, NCCW has one thing that many new feds that post on RSPWF don't have: experience. New Classic Championship Wrestling has been open since late May '98, and has been running nonstop since then. But, if we've been running that long, why would we be posting on RSPWF? Well, quite simply, it's because NCCW has lacked star power. Every great fed has a star, a name that draws in other names. Now, we're not necessarily looking for a star. But if we could get any additional good talent, to mesh with the strong base that we already have going, we might be able to take that next step. Here comes the question... what do you know about running a good fed? Well, to be honest, this is my first successful attempt. Granted, it's only my second attempt, but how many people actually run a good, long-lasting e-fed on their first try? I do, however, know how a good fed is run. I have handled in two of the premiere feds on the net, the SCRA and the GDWA, and I was accepted to the GFWA before it closed. I also had a successful start in the UEW, though I left due to a lack of time on my part. A couple of other big feds I handle in now are the WWO and the AEWA, along with the regional APWA. I've been in e-wrestling for years now. If anybody knows how to run a fed, I think I am qualified. And I'm just the vice president, in title though. In truth, I play more of a co-presidential role. The president of NCCW, [name withheld], also has had significant success in e-wrestling, his claim to fame being "Boardwalk" Bradley Warden. He's helped hold the NCCW together in the early running, when I thought it might fall apart. Then, the names. Yes, we've had a few recognizable names in our fed. "Dominator" Don Cameron, known in the EWA and the NWWF before hand, has retired as possibly the greatest World Champion in NCCW's short history. Among the other names that have seen action in NCCW: "StarFox" Curt Crane, "Bullet" Ricky Revolution, "GQ" Geoffrey Quartz, "Shooting Star" Ryan Douglas, Superstar, Katis, Michael Drexus, Aaron Bagwell, Chris Kanne, and Death and Destruction, just to name a few. Now, this is all fine and dandy, but you want details, right? Well, if you've read this far, I'm sure you do. NCCW is an e-mail only e-fed, and we only accept fantasy wrestlers. This is fantasy wrestling, after all. There are two shows a week: Saturday Matinee, which is the smaller debut-type show, and Wednesday Superstars Live, the big show where title matches and such are held, along with one big weekly flash show, the NCCW Report. Oh, and titles. What would e-wrestling be without titles? We have the World Title, of course, which is held by some of the best NCCW has to offer. Then there's the Cruiserweight Title, which is rated almost as high as the World Title, with a limit of 230 pounds to compete for it. The secondary title is the International Title, which is defended a little more often and is not far below the World Title. We also have a Television Title, defended generally every week on Saturday Matinee, which gives newcomers a chance to build themselves a name. And of course, there are the World Tag Team Titles, held by a team that exemplifies teamwork and coordination. Does this all sound good? Well, then go ahead and fill out this application, or mail us if you have any further questions:
