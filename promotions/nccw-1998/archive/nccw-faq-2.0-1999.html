NCCW FAQ 2.0
Edited by "Krazy Ace" [name withheld]
Last Updated July 1, 1999

This section is a collection of Frequently Asked Questions that I get often enough that it would save time to just put them here. Also note that a lot of things regarding the workings of NCCW are found in the Rules section.

I. What is NCCW?
NCCW, or New Classic Championship Wrestling, is an e-wrestling federation. NCCW has been in existance since May 1998, and doesn't look to be closing it's doors anytime soon. The current co-presidents are the ones who started the whole show, [name withheld] and "KrazyAce" [name withheld], and have always been in control of the federation. We are a closed booked federation, based around a strict closed flashing system. What that means is that, in order to win matches, you MUST flash, or roleplay. Whatever you may call them, you must flash to win. As well, the flashing is limited to a maximum of two per week, one on each of the shows (see below). As well, NCCW is one of the first truly "world" federations, because, although NCCW spends the majority of it's time in the United States and Canada, NCCW doesn't have a "hometown," so to speak. They spend the entire time on the road although New York City holds a special place as the site of the first NCCW event. As well, in NCCW history, wrestlers hailing from Greece, Austria, Germany, Great Britain, and Mexico have joined the large number of American and Canadian wrestlers, truly making NCCW a "world" federation.

II. History
NCCW is also unique in the fact that it frequently makes reference to the many federations that have helped NCCW become the organization that it is today. The NCCW front office, as well as it's wrestlers, respect the people who have helped shape the federation. The main influence is that of the original Classic Championship Wrestling. CCW was an offshoot of the ICW, which was an offshoot from the legendary Dawg Pound Wrestling (DPW). Many of the original wrestlers in NCCW got their starts in the CCW franchise, and still work to keep the name alive today. As well, the CCW commentating crew has returned to NCCW. Another strong influence in the creation of NCCW was the American Wrestling Alliance. Though no wrestler at present has bridged the gap between the AWA and NCCW, many of the ideas the federation has used have carried over, and provide an influence in the front office. As well, the original AWA commentating crew has also come to NCCW. As well, the National World Wrestling Federation has had a major influence on NCCW. The NWWF, which originally went under shortly before NCCW began but later returned to the wrestling world, gave NCCW many of it's top stars. You can read about the NCCW's ancestry at the family tree section of this website!

III. Shows
Tuesday: NCCW Report — Hosted by Craig Simmons and "Cool" Keith Robinson, the Report is the major flash show for NCCW. If you flash for one show a week, this is the one to flash for. The Report provides NCCW's elite with a chance to share their feelings about the going ons in NCCW, as well as giving Simmons and Robinson a chance to give their predictions for the upcoming show.
Wednesday: NCCW Superstars Live — on V-SEN (Virtual Sports Entertainment Network)! The big kahuna. The whole enchilada. Hosted by Todd Fischer, "Suave" Stevie West, and Nathan Roberts, this is where the matches happen. Anywhere from 4-6 matches will be shown in their complete entirety. Title matches are frequent, and it's rare that a show goes by without some sort of major angle taking place. From June 1998 to March 1999 there was a second show on weekends called the "Sunday Matinee," but it has since been dropped. Superstars Live usually runs about 80-85k total, which is about right for a 120-minute show using the "1k per minute" system - the rest would be 32 minutes of ads and the remainder of the time would be used to hype NCCW events and merchandise. NCCW Superstars Live does not air the week OF and the week AFTER a PPV because putting a PPV together is very draining work.
Bi-Weekly: NCCW Update — From news to rankings, title histories to the famed Unofficial Lists, if you want to know it, it's on the NCCW Update. The only unhosted show, this is purely to inform and entertain.
Pay Per Views: are held about every one and a half to two months. While they're not on any set schedule, the pay per views are still fairly easy to predict when they'll be, and the fact that the schedules are not set in stone allows for more flexibility in the schedule. There are six PPVs per calander year.

IV. Titles
NCCW World Heavyweight Title: The big one. The biggest prize in NCCW, the wrestler who holds this title is generally recognized as one of, if not the, best wrestler in NCCW.
NCCW World Cruiserweight Title: Only allowed to be held by wrestlers under 230 pounds, the Cruiserweight Title is a close second to the big title in the eyes of the NCCW booking committee, and is the only other recognized World singles title. To underline its importance, the Cruiserweight Title match at PPVs is always near the top of the card.
NCCW International Title: The secondary heavyweight title. Generally defended more often than the World Titles, but not as often as the Television Title, the International Title is generally used as a stepping stone to one of the World Titles, though holding the title is none too shabby itself.
NCCW World Tag Team Titles: The best team in NCCW holds these titles, which are one of the three recognized NCCW World titles.
NCCW Television Title: The TV Title was created to provide a stepping stone for young wrestlers, but has since become a test of perseverance, as the title is the most often defended title, often defended on a weekly basis, making it a real test to hold onto the title, despite the fact that it's the lowest recognized title in NCCW.

V. How do I roleplay/flash?
NCCW uses a "mail packet" system of flashes, not a board. You send your flashes to the Presidents, and they get put in with other flashes that get sent out in one big e-mail to your home address.

VI. Where do I send RPs and strats to?
Send them to BOTH me ([detail withheld]) and the other President ([detail withheld]). If you send it to both of us it makes things easier to keep track of.

VII. Why don't you have a flash board/guestbook?
Many reasons. The biggest is that my co-booker, the other President, has no internet access so he couldn't view the flashes to help me book and keep track of feuds. Also, there were some bad experiences in the NWWF with its board regarding sabotage. Another problem is the constant wrangling over how many flashes a handler is allowed per day, and how someone who's been away for just a couple of days has to scroll through about 300 flashes to get caught up, which is especially painful for those with slow computers, aggravated by people who used HTML to write their flashes in great big letters. Some people simply can't get on the internet every day, so they're at a disadvantage there. I also don't like guestbooks because 99 percent of the entries are just ads placed by other feds for themselves without permission.
I prefer the mail system because it allows us to present the roleplays in a logical, organized fashion, in the format of a "news" program, dressed up with news and updates as well. Everyone is on a level playing field, given the exact same number of opportunities to flash. Since the flashes and cards are sent by mail, one can participate in NCCW without ever needing to access this homepage. If you miss an RP show you can roleplay with a "live" flash on the Wednesday show.

VIII. Does the NCCW edit roleplays?
Well, we used to but that has kind of fallen by the wayside. That's because we haven't had any recent cases of handlers drastically breaking the flash rules. If I catch a four-letter word you can't say on prime-time TV, I WOULD replace it with a *bleep*. But the handlers have usually been very good about staying within the limits we set on the rules page, which makes our job easier!

IX. How do I get a match?
You can either e-mail the President requesting a match against a specific opponent, or directly challenge that opponent in your flash. We also "force book" opponents together to fill out cards, but give them enough notice for a flash and strategy submission. We might do occasional squash matches where a wrestler on our roster beats a jobber who is not part of the roster. No flash is required for those. Squashes usually happen when brand new wrestlers come to the fed - they start on a winning note, and everyone else gets a look at that wrestler's fighting style.

X. Why wasn't my sneak attack used?
Sometimes we get a whole bunch of sneak attack requests for one match. I can't have them all happen. Sometimes I don't use a sneak attack if I think the match really should have a clean winner (like a title match) or if I don't think it would be very wise for your wrestler to try (if the ring is surrounded by the guy's stable buddies and an attack by just one or two guys would be suicide).

XI. How do I get my own bio page in the roster section?
There isn't any particular way, I just do them when I find the time. If you keep bugging me enough about it I might get around to yours faster, though. :)
