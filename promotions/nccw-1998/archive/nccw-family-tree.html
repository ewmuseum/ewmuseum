NCCW — Family Tree

NCCW Family Tree

captured 7 October 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is the NCCW Family Tree, a tribute page to some of the feds that NCCW draws its lineage from.

AWA (American Wrestling Alliance) - (November 1994-June 1996)
A true originator for it's time, the AWA projected many of the beliefs that future feds such as CCW used, even though they weren't related. Among these was the "classic" edge, a mainstay in most of the feds in NCCW history. The only stars from the AWA to make a name for themselves in the CCW family were CCW Tag Team Champions Fire and Ice. But that doesn't mean the fed lacked star power, as stars such as Tony Foster, Shade Roberts, Chris Michaels, Derrick Michaels, "Crazy" Cale Davies, "Blackjack" Chris Swanson, "Stunning" Scott Stevens, "Prime Time" Scott Dawson, and tag teams such as the afore mentioned Fire and Ice, Fear, and the Continental Connection all continued their careers after the AWA.

CCW (Classic Championship Wrestling) - (December 1996-July 1997)
Classic Championship Wrestling inspired many other feds and was the first appearance of great stars such as Ricky Revolution and Geoffrey Quartz. Other stars not included in the list below included X.Plosion, "Boardwalk" Bradley Warden and Fire and Ice. One leading CCW stable, the Phoenix Squad, has popped up a couple times in NCCW.
CCW Stars who have been in NCCW: Les Canadiens, Don Cameron (as their manager), "GQ" Geoffrey Quartz, "Bullet" Ricky Revolution, Chris Kanne, Devestation Express, "The Fox" Curt Crane (as talk show host), Desmond Harrison, Joe Spear, Johnny Handsome, Murder Incorporated, "Iron Man" Bill Macragge.

SCEW (Supreme Championship E-Wrestling) - (May 1997)
An attempt to continue the CCW tradition, SCEW didn't even complete its first month before it meant it's untimely demise. Among the stars signed to compete in SCEW were CCW alums Chris Kanne, Fire and Ice, and Murder Inc, as well as El Perfecto, who would later become known as "To Excess" Rick Williams of IIWF fame. Unfortunately, the league folded just a couple weeks after opening, leaving people wondering what could have been.

NIFW (New Internet Fantasy Wrestling) - (August 1997)
Sadly, only a footnote in history as NIFW was poised to pick up where the CCW left off, but it shut down literally on the eve of its inaugural PPV. Keeping many of the CCW rules, it already had signed CCW talent such as Curt Crane, and certainly would have attracted more if it had gone through, but NIFW is now remembered only as a great might-have-been.

NWWF - (July 21, 1997-May 5, 1998....Spring 1999 - July 1999)
When the NWWF closed there was a massive migration of talent from there to NCCW. Fed highlights included Don Cameron's victory over Longshoreman, Don's loss to Deuce Demon, Jackal over Katis in the finals of the Ruler of the Ring tournament, and Deuce Demon over "Hotshot" Mike Stewart 2 falls to 1, Longshoreman over Hammer, and Katis over "Koolio" Ken Williams in a cage match. NWWF still maintains a "ghost town" site at www.geocities.com/Colosseum/Loge/9480. NWWF opened again in the spring of 1999 but amalgamated with another federation in July and is once again no more.
NWWF Stars who have been in NCCW: Don Cameron, Katis, British Kid (Matthew Cole), "GQ" Geoffrey Quartz, PhantomLord, DemiGod, Superstar, Brian Johnson, Les Canadiens, Ricky Revolution, "Rock Hard" Mike Wayne, Austrian Powers.

NCCW (New Classic Championship Wrestling) - (since April 1998)
Half a year and still going strong carrying the standard for old-school wrestling!
