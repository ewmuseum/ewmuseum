A 1998 mail-packet e-federation whose own GeoCities site names five titles across a season and a half of dated reign changes, stages a fully written pay-per-view card, and opens its own family tree by naming Classic Championship Wrestling, the American Wrestling Alliance, SCEW, NIFW and the NWWF as the leagues it draws its roster and its "classic" style from. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Classic Championship Wrestling opened in the spring of 1998 as a mail-only fantasy federation, conceding in its own December 1998 recruitment post that it wasn't yet at the level of e-wrestling's biggest names but arguing it had something rarer than star power: “experience.” Its own vice-president, introducing himself as an e-wrestling veteran running the show day to day, credited its own president's wrestling persona, “Boardwalk” Bradley Warden, with holding the promotion together early on.

It styled itself the inheritor of an older tradition. Its own “Family Tree” page traced its “classic” style back through a line of earlier, folded feds and their casts, closing with its own account of itself, half a year old and counting: “still going strong carrying the standard for old-school wrestling!”

It ran two weekly shows — Saturday Matinee for newer talent and Wednesday Superstars Live for the bigger matches — plus a weekly news flash, across five championships: Heavyweight, Cruiserweight, International, Tag Team and Television. Its title histories ran eight pay-per-views by August 1999, opening with Ignition at Madison Square Garden and touring through a card staged in the desert near Roswell, New Mexico for FanFest '98 and another in Birmingham, England for Hallowe'en Horror. Its own tag division turned over constantly and colorfully: the undefeated Coast-to-Coast Connection left the league and forfeited its belts outright after a title defense; New Austrian Powers won the same titles and immediately threw them away, calling their own tournament to remove any doubt of their legitimacy; a masked pair calling themselves the Insiders won the belts at Hallowe'en Horror and unmasked as Les Canadiens.

Lew Spectre, billed “Strictly Business,” won the Television Championship on 14 April 1999 and carried it into the promotion's eighth pay-per-view, Battle in Seattle, on 7 August 1999 — the last of its own fully resulted shows in the surviving record — retaining the title over “Missile” Mike Divine two falls to one, on the same card where “Phenomenon” Tyro Marvel won the Heavyweight Championship.

No closure statement of its own survives. A third-party wrestling column recorded its own president at a press conference as late as 10 December 1999, floating a possible women's division ahead of that year's Fanfest pay-per-view — more than a year and a half after it opened, with no sign yet of closing.

Affiliations

Classic Championship Wrestling (CCW, 1996–2001)

Predecessor to NCCW (1998)
New Classic Championship Wrestling carried on from Classic Championship Wrestling under a new name in 1998; the original Lew Spectre's career runs through both.

National World Wrestling Federation (NWWF, 1997)

Shared continuity with NCCW (1998)
The newer federation's own FAQ credits this federation as a major influence that, after its first closure, gave it many of its own top stars; the newer federation's own tribute page separately names its own highlight results and an eleven-name shared roster, several of them (this federation's own "Dominator" Don Cameron chief among them) already recorded as continuing careers on both federations' own held records.
