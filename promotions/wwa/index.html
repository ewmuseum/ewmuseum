A multi-brand e-fed that ran from a 1997 founding tournament into the 2010s, staged through the on-air decisions of its one kayfabe authority figure, President Warr. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WWA's own World Heavyweight Championship history opens 12/12/97 with a tournament win by Mark Lynch, matching the site's own claimed founding year exactly — though its earliest surviving broadcasts (February through April 1998) run under an on-air “Northeast Championship Wrestling” banner, rebranding to the WWA name mid-1998 (“the second official WWA card,” in its own commentary by that August). By 2003 it was running two house shows at once (Meltdown and Fiesta), each with its own 25+-wrestler roster, plus a numbered PPV series that had reached its 7th installment (“Rage in the Cage VII”) by March 2004 under fixed annual names — Rage in the Cage, Birthday Bash, Labor Day Brawl, March Madness, Independence Day Rumble — recurring year over year for over a decade. Its own operator, credited throughout its own news copy and software footer, staged in-fiction as kayfabe “President Warr,” who at one point strips a champion of the title for going “too far,” and, in 2003, personally retires the World Tag Team Championship (briefly) “in his infinite wisdom.”

For four months in 2004 it ran three brands at once: Meltdown became the Northern Wrestling Conference (NWC), Fiesta became LDA, and both were staged against a new outside rival, the Squared Circle Wrestling Federation — see its own wing for that promotion's story. WWA's own numbered title-history ledger absorbed the rival's belt directly: the SCWF Championship's own reign chain runs continuously back through an earlier “European Championship” naming to a 12 April 2003 first tournament, becoming the SCWF title the same night, 4 March 2004, that the promotion it came from folded and SCWF launched from its ashes. A parallel Extreme Championship opens under “Gen X” billing the same way. The operator folded all three 2004 brands back into one unified “World Wrestling Alliance” banner on 14 July 2004, and, separately, the LDA brand minted its own Diablo Championship that May, first held by Jack Griffiths.

The fed rebuilt on the “eW Locker Room” CMS by 2010, continuing news and results output at a reduced pace through 2013. Its community lived across three separate forum platforms over its run, each a full migration rather than a continuation — an Invision Power Board (active 2003–2006), a Simple Machines Forum on community.wwa-online.com (2010–2016, a real, long-running community with members posting thousands of times over multiple years), and a thinly-populated phpBB revival in 2019, the domain's last confirmed activity before it went dark for good. Editorial content included a recurring in-house prediction column, “The Gamblers Table,” previewing every numbered PPV from 2002 onward. How the fed finally ended isn't stated anywhere in the surviving record — its own last captures (2021) are a handful of leftover roster head-shot images, not a closing post.

Championships

Nine belts survive across the run: the World Heavyweight, World Tag Team, United States, Intercontinental, and Television championships from the fed's own core lineage; the Rookie Championship for its developmental talent; the LDA brand's own Diablo Championship; and two titles whose numbered ledgers carry an earlier name mid-record — the SCWF Championship (opening as the European Championship) and the Extreme Championship (opening as the Gen X Championship). See the full lineup, each belt now its own page with a dated, sourced reign ledger.

The World Tag Team Championship in particular ran through a string of named recurring pairings rather than one-off teams: the Commandant's Own and Anarchy traded the belts through the fed's first two years; Havoc, Mount Olympus's Phobos and Deimos, held it a record six times and were its last-ever champions; the Veteranz, X-Corp, the Cell, the Overlords of the Dark, the Pain Syndicate, the Franchise Players, True Living Colors, and Vain & Vicious each held it in turn across the run. See the full roster for each team's own page.

Editorial

Alongside its news desk, WWA ran two recurring in-house columns: "The Scouting Report," bylined from its own kayfabe Director of Talent position and running roster news, injury updates, and front-office gossip across at least 28 numbered issues; and "The Gamblers Table," a PPV-prediction column bylined by an in-house gambling persona ( “The Gambler”), previewing the fed's own numbered supercards — Independence Day Rumble, Birthday Bash, Rage in the Cage, and March Madness — by name and year from 2002 onward. Both are served under the wing's own show archive, filed as documents rather than cards.
