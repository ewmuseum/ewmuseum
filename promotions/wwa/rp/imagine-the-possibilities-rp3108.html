Imagine The Possibilities...

19 May 2006

By Jonathon Winters

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Impossible - an adjective used often to exaggerate a person’s skills and achievements, sometimes for publicity purposes, in order to sell said achievements to an audience in a much more digestible format. The world of professional wrestling is as guilty as any industry or individual in this regard and I have to admit, to my chagrin, on more than one occasion the WWA public relations machine have used the very same word in a variety of equally decadent sentences in order to transparently exaggerate what I’ve done or what I’m about to do. All publicity-related uses of the word aside, in reality, impossible is just a matter of perspective - only until the achievement in question has actually been accomplished will people submit the term and perhaps utilise a more safe adjective - improbable. For example, imagine, just for a second, that time travel is possible. I know what you’re thinking. You’re sitting there convinced that I’m insane. You believe me to be a crazy man with crazy notions. Maybe you’re also muttering something to the effect of ‘...that’s impossible!’ under your breath, but you continue to listen anyway. That’s a good thing and don‘t worry. I‘m not here to spin a less than convincing yarn about travelling back in time in order to moon Hitler or anything reminiscent of some thinly woven new Bill and Ted script. That‘d be ridiculous and completely untrue. At this moment in our evolution, we cannot claim time travel to be a viable possibility. I’m just asking you to imagine if you could. Imagine travelling to the start of the twentieth century. Imagine speaking with people of that era, perhaps you even let slip a little something about what they can expect in the future. There can be no doubt that people of this century have been a witness to war before. A conflict dubbed the Boer War between the British Empire and the independent Boer republics in South Africa would’ve actually been taking place. Some people might’ve even been around to have witnessed various other conflicts spanning back deep into the previous century. Perhaps The Spanish-American War, The Boxer Rebellion in China or even The American Civil War. Imagine if you were try to explain to them very the notion of the two World Wars hosted within the twentieth century that would almost define and would very definitely change the world forever. Wars so massive in scale that more or less every nation in the world will be involved. Wars so tragic millions upon millions of people die. Wars so devastating weapons of ultimate destruction are crafted in order to be victorious, culminating, of course, in the creation of the Atom Bomb used to completely obliterate the Japanese Cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki effectively bringing the madness to an end. Perhaps once this has all be explained to them, the people of this era would simply ignore you. Perhaps they’d ignorantly dismiss the idea without even attempting understand or accept your explanations. Perhaps they’d simply glare back at you, speculatively at first, trying to decide whether you’re being serious and when they see no humour on your face and they do realise that you’re being deadly serious, they’d mutter, under their breath, words laced with shock and disbelief, ‘...but that’s impossible!’ Now imagine waiting for the people of the earlier twentieth century to adequately recover from this revelation only to compound their shock and confusion by explaining to them about man’s attempt to conquer space, rather than each other. Explain to them how, in approximately sixty-nine years from now, a man-made space craft would land on the moon’s surface, the area previously dubbed the sea of tranquillity to be precise, and from that craft a man would actually set foot upon the, as described by Neil Armstrong himself, powdered charcoal surface. Of course, ever since human beings were first able to think for themselves, they’ve looked up at the moon in awe and wonder, some cultures even attributed the mantle of ‘god’ to Earth’s mighty satellite, so at the turn of the twentieth century it isn’t out the realms of possibility that some of the more forward-thinking humans of the era had actually dreamed of this eventuality at one point or another. However, hitting them with the definite knowledge, perhaps showing them footage or even the infamous sound bites - you know the whole ‘That’s one small step for man...’ speech that encapsulates the entire endeavour that virtually everyone from this era can regurgitate at will - is an entirely different matter. No matter in which way they react to this info-dump, the words ‘...but that’s impossible!’ will more than likely spew from their mouths. All of this information is as a foreign to them as the notion of time travel, an alien invasion or coming face to face with the Loch Ness Monster is now to us. Many of this era have begun to imagine what these impossibilities might be like if we ever experience them, at this point in our existence our minds are simply not ready to comprehend hard, unquestionable eventualities and we never will be able to comprehend them until the human race actually experience such events. On July 25th 2005 I had my first match up within the World Wrestling Alliance, in Old Trafford, England, pitted against a fellow rookie Ken Sendela. I won the match without difficulty, but that‘s beside the point. If you asked any other worker within the WWA locker room at that time whether this cocky newcomer Jonathon Winters would be able to remain undefeated up until February 27th 2006, when I was screwed out of the victory by Khaled, then I’d imagine that the majority, if not all, of them would immediately dismiss the idea as impossible. Well, it happened and I did remain undefeated for all that time. A month before this year’s annual WWA Bourbon Street Brawl, if you asked anybody within the WWA locker room whether they thought a relative newcomer named Proteus would walk out of the Bourbon Street Brawl victorious, the new number one contender to the WWA World Heavyweight title, they would immediately dismiss the idea as impossible. Well, it happened and he did walk away from the event victorious. Now, the next Monday Night Meltdown after Bourbon Street Brawl, I am scheduled to fight this Proteus in one on one competition. If you ask anybody within the WWA locker room whether I will be able to overcome this monster this Monday, they will immediately dismiss the idea as impossible.
