Family Affairs Part II

3 Jul 2003

By Wes Sanders

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The dust settles in a small town on the outskirts of Salt Lake City, as the bus pulls away. Leaving a small figure, hands on hips looking around. He sucks in the air around him and pouts his chest out. Taking in the surroundings the figure picks up the bag lying next to him like a loyal puppy dog and swings it across his back. He walks into a local bar.) (The bar is pretty empty, a couple of men sit at one side of the wooden bar watching TV and making jokes among one another. A young couple share stories together and sit down away from the smoking and laughing men. The figure takes one step on the wooden flooring and everyone looks in his direction.) “…” (The young figure just looks at all the people staring at him and goes to sit on a stool, he drops his bag and hunches up looking carefully up and down on the menu. A young girl comes up to the figure with a smile.) “What can I get you?” “One moment” (The figure had short hair, thick dark skin and a pretty smile, he looks up at the waitress.) “I’ll just take a cup of coffee please” (His Hispanic accent is thick and broad, the words come out of his mouth slowly. As the waitress goes off to make the figures order he watches the TV screen above him. It is playing WWA TV with a promo from Wes Sanders and Wallace Coleman.) (Wes Sanders is in that dark gym holding his head in his hands. Wallace sits in a plastic chair next to him. Wes looks into the distance at nothing.) “My brother, he is a great man, he fought alongside me many times in my earlier days” Wes recollects “He is an organized man, he knows his priorities in life, and he is very focused. I would say he is more focused than me. I remember our last match together.” (Wes looks at Wallace for a second before looking back into the distance.) “Sanders against the El Diablo’s in a no holds bar match. It was a gruesome affair with myself taking the brunt of the blows delivered by the two biggest stars in New Mexico.” “You wrestled in Mexico?” Wallace asks like a school child. “Yes, a long time ago me and my brother fought well, we were regarded as the two best in the indy feds and many high things were predicted. But that night had a terrible ending.” “What happened?” Wallace asked in a slow clam voice “I left my brother in the ring on his own, the boss of the federation ordered me to leave the ring half way through the match. He threatened me with our livelihoods and I couldn’t help it. I fully regret it now but my brother could not and would not understand.” “And what happened” Wallace looks on at Wes “The only thing that could have happened, my brother rejected me, he didn’t wrestle anymore, the love for the sport just drained from his body. I guess I knew that was going to happen sooner or later, he has the gift but never had the will to use it.” “But how can you tell he’s nearby now” “Call it a sixth sense if you will, I just know he’s coming to watch me perform at Independence Day. He is willing me to lose, it’s just a feeling but a feeling I know too well” (The screen fades to black and we look back in the bar when the young figure watches the TV sipping on his coffee. The waitress watches the TV and looks at the young guy.) “You into all this wrestling stuff?” “Yeah I guess you could say so… I find it very intriguing” “My boyfriend watches it all the time, I just don’t get it, seems a bit fake.” “That’s what they want to make you think, but I feel if you were in the ring it would be a little different.” “It’s just like a performance isn’t it?” “Well, I don’t know about that, your either good at it or not. Seems to me that the people who are good at it don’t have the drive, and the people who haven’t got the gift have the drive to kill themselves. I mean look at this.” (The young figure points to the television screen.) “You see this? This is supposed to be the next big thing. The Independence Day Rumble, all the stars will be there, all competing for the biggest prize in their field. But it’s nothing really, the effort they put in and try is for nothing. If they don’t have the gift like many of them don’t then I don’t know why their bothering” (The screen shows pictures of each individual wrestler and their IDR stats. It then features Wes Sanders.) “This guy here has talent, he could be in with a good chance don’t you think.” The Waitress speaks with a naivety that was almost cute. “Him! Well he used to be good and talented, but I’ve seen him at his best and worst, he has the gift. But it’s locked up inside him and only a ghost from the past can stir his instincts and bring out the best. To help him win the rumble.” “And you think someone can make a person perform beyond anyone else.” “Think about it, you got 30 guys in that ring, and say they enter in one at a time. One after the other. The odds are stacked against you, you need someone or something to motivate you that little further. Make you step that extra bit. Make you run that extra mile” (The figure stops and thinks.) “And by god he is going to need it, especially if he wants to get back into the dream.” “You know bunny, you got some wise words about you, a little crazy but wise none the less… What your name” (The camera pans in on a familiar face.) “Chivo.” (Chivo pauses for a second.) “Chivo Sanders.” (With that final comment the screen fades to black on that smiling face.)
