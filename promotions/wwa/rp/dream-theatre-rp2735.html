WWA (1997–2019) Roleplays

Dream Theatre

21 Oct 2005

By The Muppet Man

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Reaching up, growing from unknowable nothingness, come the roots, a red deeper than blood. They are ever-reaching, unceasing. Orange also. And yellow. Joining from the roots, they stretch onwards, upwards, forwards, backwards, beyond perception. A green light, surrounded by, and in perfect harmony with a blue radiance. A deep purple unites the great gathering... all pulsating around a burning indigo, its intensity unmatched anywhere in the material world. Somewhere, deep in the heart of Salt Lake City, Utah, a figure was at unrest. There was nothing particularly out of the ordinary about this figure. He was just like any other; hard working, or at least convinced that was the case, clinging onto what few beautiful things make life worth living, trying to live every day as it came, searching for inspiration, personal growth and self satisfaction. In all practicality, he was a single, insignificant organism, running his own life and dealing with his own problems, happily ignoring the bigger picture. He was an individual, just like everyone else. There was something about that notion that had always got under the Muppet Man's skin. From an early age, he had always been keen on being an individual, separate from everyone else. It made him feel, for lack of a better descripton, special. Even more so, it gave him a plausible reason to believe that he was indeed superior to everyone else. As he grew up, and his intellectual pursuits became grander, he began to grimly acknowledge the truth that he wasn't so different, his place on the outskirts was just the role he had set for himself in society and his own personal world. Unlike most others, he always recognised that the ego isn't everything. He recognised the natural order, and the insignificance of his actions alone. But as the Muppet Man lay in bed in a hotel somewhere, deep in the heart of Salt Lake City, Utah, his subconscious mind twisting with ambiguous and troublesome dreams, that seemed a little unfair. In any case, it was probably the last thing on his mind. He was back in the WWA, constructing and telling his own stories, living his life, and his first match was in the main event against residential legend and psychopath Ian Tense. There was a man with no lack of an ego complex. All Muppet had to do was picture him as an inner demon, or something equally subjective, and it would be so poetically fitting in that way only the most pretentious followers of the left-hand path could appreciate. When travelling through dark woods, comfort isn't what you'd think would come to mind. However, as the Muppet Man walked, following a non-existent path mapped out inside his head, he was as peaceful as he could ever remember. Everything about this forest was calming to him. The cool breeze blowed his hair playfully in and around his eyes, but it was only annoying in a kind of warm way. The bark of the trees, the beautiful and majestic trees, was soft and its topography almost familiar. Here he was free, liberated from everything negative, and united with nature. This went down to even the smallest of details separating him from the rest of the world; his mask, his gloves, and so forth. Muppet delighted in the touch, the sheer atmosphere, of everything around him. In fact, here, the Muppet Man did not exist. That was just a character, a state of mind, an experiment... a game? Here, it was Andrew Scrine, for name's sake, because there was no need for names. Only complete oneness. There was no path, but his direction didn't seem to matter. This was all uncharted territory, but the exploration was the key. While it all looked similar to the naked eye, each section, impossible to distinguish between by sight alone, held differing but equally illuminating knowledge. As he moved forwards and onwards, Andrew felt the feeling of growing excitement in his stomach. It was as if his body was systematically digesting the information. Maybe there was some truth to the power of the solar plexus after all, he thought with what could be conceived as a cynical grin. Subjective metabolism. Interesting. However, as he moved on, an ever increasing doubt was gnawing at Andrew like some kind of poisonous insect. And yes, he had checked for that numerous times. Even here, there was a natural paranoia that would not subside. It was the fact that he didn't really know where he was going. He'd expected he'd find out when he got there, and so far there was nothing to suggest otherwise, but he couldn't help feeling that something was slightly off. Something else helped him shake off these doubts almost instantly, though. Without even realising it at first, he had proceeded into a clearing of sorts. To his left, the forest continued normally. Straight ahead, there was a dark hill, and he couldn't make out just what lay that way. To the right, there was what seemed like a lake. As Andrew moved closer, overcome by wonder and curiosity, he realised that the end was nowhere in sight. The continually deepening blue suggested it just kept going and going, depth wise at least. If it wasn't huge, then this was logistically like no other lake he had ever encountered. Obscuring a large part of his field of vision was a thick fog, which hung only in that direction; just floating there, ominously. Then again, what else was fog really going to do? Casting one last glance over the mysterious lake, he went with his gut on a snap decision, which was a good sign, according to recent 'revelations', and turned around. That little doubt that was left, though, suddenly embodied itself in cry from somewhere close behind him. He almost jumped out of his skin. "Flee not. Not yet, at least." The Muppet Man tossed and turned in his bed. It had been a restless few days for him, and that was carrying through into the night. In fact, he had only arrived in Salt Lake City that very day, or the night before to be exact, and it hadn't been the most pleasant of journeys. As he'd been officially unemployed for the past nine months or so, plane tickets were a bit of a luxury. Obviously, though, financial release was one of the lesser reasons why Muppet was finally returning once and for all to the WWA. Whatever he had occupied himself with, Muppet had never been completely satisfied. There was a hole that the business had ripped out of him when he was forced out by injury, and he had to fill it. The main cause for stress over the past few days, though, had not been physical. It was mainly due to the announcement that on the return he had waited so long to make, he would be facing Ian Tense. To say it was going to be a challenge would be one of those spectacular understatements generally associated with Captain Obvious himself. The Muppet Man couldn't help but harbour a natural fear, a fear that something would go wrong and Ian would snap, a fear that it all might happen again. He had to work to prevent that, and to show that he was well and truly back. Whether it was for the long haul would all be decided on Monday the 24th. Recovering, Scrine turned around and found himself looking directly at a man who had seemingly appeared out of thin air. He was definitely shorter than Andrew, by what look like almost a foot, and he wore a black cloak. It wasn't one of those stereotypical, obscure-everything-but-the-vague-shape-of-the-body black cloaks, in fact all his features were in full view. His face was sort of pale, in an old fashioned way, and he had neat black hair. Black, or a very dark brown. On his head was what looked like a crown constructed of laurel leaves, and an innocent looking key dangled on a chain from his neck. A snake poked its head out from inside of his cloak, looking around inquisitively. Andrew suddenly found all menace associated with snakes to be ridiculous. The man was grinning in a friendly kind of way. "Welcome," he said, giving Andrew time to collect himself. "Uh... hi," said Andrew, at a loss, and not quite sure what to make of his situation. "She looks intimidating, yes," the unidentified man continued with a laugh, obviously referring to the lake, "But there's nothing wrong. I was just about to take a swim." "Yeah," said Andrew, opening up a little, and recovering himself. "It's just the idea of a large, foggy lake in a dark forest like this... however cool it may be." "Heh heh, I know what you mean. This place is fascinating, isn't it?" "Sure is," came Andrew's answer, as he stared out over the now apparent tranquility of the lake with a vacant smile on his face. "What's your name?" "Me? I'm Virgil." Andrew's stomach turned at this name, although he had no idea why. "Mind if I tag along? I could do with a swim, for reasons that are unclear even to me." Virgil smiled jokingly. "Sure you won't find more than you wanted to?" "What?" said Andrew with a start, taken completely by surprise. "I admire your enthusiasm," continued Virgil, not missing a beat. "I'm just not sure you've explored as fully as you can, as fully as you want to, to be able to take this journey." "Journey? What the hell are you talking about? I'm just talking about going for a quick swim." Virgil actually laughed. "Quick? Watch how the fog approachs you... even now, look!" Scrine looked around him, and saw that the fog had indeed extended very far out. It was in danger of entering the forest itself. "What's that key around your neck?" he said, trying to change the subject and get out of this hole. It didn't work. "I think you know," Virgil remarked with a cryptic smile as Andrew looked on. "Lasciate ogne speranza, voi ch'intrate," he added in a full, thick Italian accent. The snake began to coil itself around Virgil's arm, perfectly at home. Andrew Scrine sank backwards, wide eyed, and took a step behind him. He tried to, at least, but his feet wouldn't move. Virgil compelled him. There was a sudden and ear-popping flash of lightning, illuminating for a fraction of a second the hill that he had noticed earlier. All the same, he took it all in. Andrew saw that it was a difficult path up there, with jagged rocks and an overgrowth of wild plants. On the side of the hill was some sort of a grand house, and it was on fire. No, not on fire. Surrounded by flames. Purest red flames. Apparently, they didn't give off any light, as he could no longer see them. "You're trying to flee, but you don't want to," said Virgil analytically. "Will you comply with your instinct, or follow your desire. Curiosity killed the cat, my friend." Despite his deep anxiety, and what was approaching fear, Andrew couldn't stop himself from speaking. "Stupidity killed the cat. Curiosity is only a by-product of ignorance." "If you're not curious, then turn back." Virgil continued. "There's nothing for you here. If you're not curious, that is." Immediately after saying this, Virgil reached into his sleeve and pulled out what appeared to be a long string. Yarn. He dangled it in front of himself, and the snake, still coiling itself, hissed. This seemed to sooth Andrew, and once again, he couldn't put his finger on why. "Follow the yarn, if you will." With one last grin, Virgil turned and began to walk, straight into the lake. He began to submerge, not paying any attention to the water around him, the yarn trailing tantalisingly behind him. Just as Andrew's body began to give in, and follow, there was another roll of thunder, but this time there was no lightning within sight to accompany it. It began to rain. As the first drop gently hit Andrew's brow, everything was back as it was, in a crippling flash. There was Virgil, standing as if nothing had happened, perfectly dry, except for the few drops of rain. The fog was back at what now seemed a safe distance. "Oh well, maybe some other time," Virgil said cheerily. "Looks like it'll be pretty heavy. You'll need to take cover." "Um, yeah," said Andrew, recovering for what seemed the hundredth time. "L - later." With that, he turned and headed hastily back towards the forest. It no longer seemed inviting, though, and the darkness was more apparent than it had ever been. It was as if it was transforming into another place, a place he had resided in for a short while after the 2005 Bourbon Street Brawl, and a place he wished never to return to. He shuddered, feeling no more like Andrew Scrine, and the Muppet Man retreated under the cover of the sheltering trees. Behind him, the snake finished coiling itself around Virgil's arm, nine whole times, and it went softly to sleep. Waking up was a surprisingly gradual process for the Muppet Man, that night. From his own personal experience, the transition between either a very bad or a troublesome dream was always very quick, leaving you disoriented and not knowing quite what to make of anything. That was what made it even more uncomfortable for Muppet as his conscious mind slowly switched itself on, and he began to wrestle for freedom from the duvet that enveloped him. Making his way into a seated position, he felt very faint, and his throat was unbearably dry. Trying to swallow only brought on a greater pain. Getting to his feet, Muppet speedily sorted through the myriad of thoughts that had simultaneously advanced on him. He tried to recall the events of his latest dream, that was looking even more nonsensical to him now than any dream he could recall, but the details were rapidly disappearing, like rain on a still-wet canvas. That was what irritated Muppet most about dreams; they always made perfect sense for whatever reason when you were experiencing them, but then lost all semblance of meaning as you recalled them after waking. Lucid dreaming was always something that had interseted him, but in his mind the effort required outweighed the possible gain. Rubbing his mildly aching spine, which he thought he should really do something about before returning to the ring, the Muppet Man walked over to a nearby mirror and glanced at it. He looked terrible, but that was to be expected. Training would be must in the weeks to come, he accepted with a frown. Muppet had always adapted his ring style to suit his current lifestyle, and training had never much appealed to him, nor had he ever really found the need. He had always been in pretty good shape, but now something definitely had to be done. It wasn't the effort itself, it was the thought of the effort that was the real killer. As much as he tried to put it off from immediate confrontation, though, thoughts of his upcoming battle with Ian Tense were never far from the forefront of his mind. To his honest recollection, the Muppet Man could never remember being so nervous before a match. If it had been anyone else, all doubts would have been long obliterated. You could say that he had nothing to lose, but that wasn't entirely true. In fact, he had one very important thing to lose, and that was his career. Muppet knew very well that Ian Tense had the ability and ruthless streak to end careers, and he'd been keeping in touch with the WWA just enough to know that he was trying to do just that to Denrol. Muppet had crossed Ian on one occasion before, although that was mainly due to his increasing disillusion with the now-disbanded Devil's Own and a set up from his supposed 'friend' Quietus, and the results hadn't been pretty. However much he was over it, the memories of his complete destruction at the hands of Tense's DO would never truly leave him, and it made his stomach turn even now. Brushing past the harmless discomfort in his abdomen, though, Muppet knew he would have to be on top of his game... surpass the top of his game. The idea of having to be that good on his first day back was just as exhilarating as it was intimidating. He walked over to the side of the room, and opened the double doors leading out onto the balcony. As he stepped out onto the balcony and leaned over the railing, not quite lost in thought, he observed the urban beauty of the city that surrounded him. It may not have been the natural world per se, but this was hardly an agressively industrial environment. It was an evolved habitat, as much a genuine part of the world as anywhere else, and the Muppet Man felt a tiny awe creeping up inside him to be looking over this modern ecosystem, working as fluidly as any organism. It had certainly helped to get so lucky with the location of his hotel room, now that he thought about it. The WWA was going through signficant change. The dictatorship had been dissolved, and now the power lay with a much less interfering group. In fact, power was almost a non-issue now. Favour would go to those who earned it, and the WWA was free to be shaped into something new and unknown by those who understood, and those who chose that path. It was a period of advancement and opportunities - of evolution. The Muppet Man couldn't have been more pleased with his timing. It was time to return to the Muppet Man of old, not in ideology but in practicality, it was time to once again be the puppet master, and shape his own fate as he saw fit. And if he had to survive Ian Tense to do that... so be it. However as Muppet tore himself away from the view and went back into the room, intending to get ready to go out and train himself more physically than mentally, he couldn't shake the nagging feeling that someone had beat him to the punch. Perhaps there was something going on, and important details, however small they might seem, were being overlooked. How could someone be pulling his strings, though, when he felt freer that he could ever remember? Around a green heart they align, conditioning, responding, perpetually, holy, divulging everything that is possible and more. But the great convergence is imperfect. There, unnoticed deep in the heart, lies a single spot of black. Designed By [name withheld] | © World Wrestling Alliance 1997 - 2005
