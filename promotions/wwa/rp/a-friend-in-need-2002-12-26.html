A Friend in Need

26 Dec 2002

By Chance

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 26 Dec 2002, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
-The scene opens in a large, smoke filled room. The dim light comes from a series of shoddy looking lamps that hang overhead. The smoke blocks the lights passage through the room, so the light only succeeds in lighting the dirtiness of the smoke rather than lighting the room itself. Even so the light does enough to illumine the room, and so we see what said room contains. Light’s nice like that isn’t it? Even when something tries to block it, intentionally or just by coincidence, it almost always finds a way to filter through, almost always overcomes the difficulties. Just so that we can see. Just to help us. It’s a good friend in that way. Because isn’t that what friends are for, to help us? To overcome any difficulties to try and help us when we most need it? Well that’s what I would’ve thought anyway- -Anyway what the light is or does isn’t all that important right now. More important, and of immediate concern is what the light allows us to see. And what that is, is between ten and twenty old-looking wooden tables (count yourself if you want something more exact), each one occupied by five or so people, most smoking, some eating, almost all drinking, and everyone of them looking at the raised platform at the end of the room. To one side of the room, through the hazy smoke, we see a bar, that while like the table looks old, is in very good repair and is polished to a shine (shows you the importance our society places on drink doesn’t it?). A few people sit on the bar stools in front of the bar, and they too are all looking at the stage at the front of the room. They are looking here, because this is where the thing that is of most interest to them, and to us, is situated. On the stage a band is playing, and at this moment, suddenly the sound that we did not realise was off comes on, and we here the band playing Creed’s “Higher” to their appreciative audience. As we look closer at the band we see that it is “Forlorn Hope”, the band in which Chance is the lead singer. And sure enough there he is singing, wearing a purple silk shirt and loose black trousers, with a white enamelled cross hanging from his neck, he stands in front of his fellow band members, one of which (the lead guitarist) may be recognised by some people as Jarod Crow, former EWA wrestler. It soon becomes apparent that the band are just finishing as they begin to pack away, as soon as the song is finished (they’re quite good)- -The camera follows them backstage, through the surprisingly clean white walls of the corridors in the back of the bar. The band turn out through the door, talking softly amongst themselves as they walk. Outside it is dark, with only a little light from the few lampposts that spread light sporadically around the street. As the band load their equipment onto their van, we zoom in on Jarod Crow and Chance, who appear to be discussing something serious- Chance: Good set tonight, huh? Jarod: Yeah, good set. Sure is cold out, isn’t it. Chance: Yeah, sure is cold out. Jarod: ... Chance: Might be a frost tomorrow. Jarod: Oh yes, might be a frost. -The conversation fizzles out as both men begin to crack up laughing at their insanely boring conversation, it becomes clear they’ve played at that before- Chance: Seriously though there are a couple of things I need to talk to you about. Jarod: I was wondering why the camera was following us. Chance: Yeah, well first off, I want to ask you about Dave Harley. Jarod: What about him? Chance: Well, you two were in the EWA together, you might know more about him than me. Jarod: Not really, he only had a handful of matches in the EWA, and I was sort of busy at the time. The truth is you know more than me, you’ve watched the tapes, you’ve actually wrestled him, there’s nothing about Harley that I could tell you. I couldn’t even tell you what it feels like to take a Harley Bomb ... especially not through a table. Chance: You just had to bring that up. Jarod: Just thought I’d remind you of a reason not to go easy on Harley. The way you treat matches, as just a big gamble, it’s a wonder you win any at all. I think that’s why you lost the title to Enigma, you didn’t realise he thought it was personal, so you treated it like any other match, and look what that got you. Chance: Don’t you worry about that, I have no reason to go easy on Harley, my back still aches a bit, now and then. As for Enigma ... well now I know that it’s personal. That sort of brings me on to the second thing. Jarod: You want my advice on what partner to pick against Enigma? I assume you’re accepting his challenge? Chance: Of course! When was the last time I missed out on a gamble like that? His challenge is officially accepted. But you’re not quite right about wanting your advice on getting a partner, I want you to be my partner. Jarod: Chance, you know I don’t wrestle anymore. It was hard enough giving up the fight in the first place, can you imagine how hard it would be to give up a second time? Chance: I understand that. I know how difficult it was to give up your battle, and I know what you went through trying to carry it on when you weren’t really able. But this is just one match, not even a singles match. Just a single tag match to help out a friend. Isn’t that what friends are for? Helping each other through their different battles? I was there for you when you needed me when the EWA closed, now I’m just asking you to help me fight my battle. You’re my friend, you should do this. Jarod: I’m sorry Chance, but friend or no, I promised myself and Rebecca that I wouldn’t wrestle again. Even if I wanted to wrestle, I couldn’t break my promise to her, she’s been through too much for me for that. Now please just drop it, there are other partners you can choose. Chance: I know there are. There are lot’s of talented guys out there. Lots of good and decent guys out there. But none of them are my friends. I don’t trust any of them, not the way I trust you. If you’re not going to be my partner, then I don’t want one. Jarod: If that’s how it’s going to be, then I wish you luck. Then again, you probably don’t need it. You’ve got to understand that I’d do almost anything else to help you Chance, but wrestling again? That’s too much. Chance: So how about lending some moral support? Jarod: What do you mean? Chance: I want the band to play on Meltdown, if I can arrange it. It’ll be good publicity, and I’d like to have you there, even if you won’t wrestle. Jarod: Sure buddy, we’ll be there. So you still visiting Rebecca and me tomorrow for Christmas? Chance: Just try and keep me away ... -Their voices fade away as they enter the van, and we fade to black-
