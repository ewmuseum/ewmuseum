So, this is how it goes...

1 Mar 2003

By Nicholas Adams

Item 4 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 1 Mar 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The world definitely has its surprises. Many seem to be happy surprises. For example, a birthday party. Another example is when somebody buys the unsuspecting recipient a very good gift...like a signed book by their favorite author, or a limited edition car that they always wanted. Unfortuantely, there are also surprises that aren't so good. They come into being for the sake of being malicious. Other than that, they have no purpose. They are destructive, and often very painful. For example, it was a surprise to the inhabitants of Sodom and Gamorrah when sulfur began to rain from the air, fire began to comsume them, and brimstone destroyed their homes. It was a surprise for the Egyptians when the ten plagues began to ravage their city. It was also a surprise for the German army in World War II when the Russians held Saint Petersburg - then known as Leningrad - and defeated Hitler's forces in a giant rout. Rebellions are always a surprise, and so are assassinations. This destructive form of being completely unexpected is also used by the military. They always try to get "the element of surprise" on their side. Being stealthy and random definitely aids when one wants to make an attack. But Nicholas Adams prefers the style of fighting that is overt, agressive, but NEVER predictable. Which is why he's getting his hair cut in Canada...in a beauty parlor. The shop is rather nice, but except for one of the hair stylists who is wearing some extremely well-coordinated clothing, Adams is the only man there. The woman cutting his hair is carrying on a conversation with him.) Stylist: So, how's your personal life? Adams: What, are you a wrestling reporter that just happens to cut hair? Stylist: Wrestling? What are you talking about? This is what I ask most customers. And for you...well, I'm just sort of curious why you walked into a beauty parlor for a hair cut. Adams: Ah...well, I'll answer the second question first. I was just driving around on my motorcycle, and I knew that my hair was just a bit long...I usually keep it at three inches, but as you already know, it was about at my chin all the way around. So, I just came into the first hair cutting place I saw. Stylist: And as for the parts of your life that matters? Adams: Well, I usually don't do this with people I'm not close to, but...what the hell? I just got back together with a woman I've known for five years. We dated for two, broke up, and haven't seen one another for about a year. Stylist: So why did she call you? And how did she know your number? Adams: I haven't changed houses in three years. Stylist: Really? So, how are you two getting along? Adams: Pretty well, actually. She's with me in Canada, though neither I now she want her to be onscreen. I've seen far too many relationships like that end badly. I'm going to do this one right...hopefully. Stylist: That sounds nice. (The stylist does a small cut, then grabs a towel.) Stylist: Well, I'm almost finished. Nice to hear that your life is going so well. Adams: Yeah. Actually, it's pretty much in my hands now. I was thinking about quitting my career... (The stylist starts to dry Nick's hair with the towel, and as she is doing this...) Stylist: Is there a "but" coming? (The sylist finishes, and as she lifts the towel away from Nick's head, Adams replies.) Adams: Yes, there is. I was going to say, "but it would mean picking between the two most important things in my life." Stylist: Definitely a difficult choice. Adams: Yeah. It's the equivalent of asking a devout...wait, that's a bad analogy. Okay, I've got it. It's like asking a lifetime actor to suddenly up and leave the entire business because he loves pie-eating contests and could be a great champion at that. If he doesn't, he'll never be able to enter a pie-eating contest again... Stylist: Come again? Adams: Okay...it's like asking a hockey player tho throw a game or his family gets hurt. He loves his family, but he's got principles that he follows, and one is that he tries his hardest. What can he do but find a loophole and do both? (The stylist puts away the towel and goes for a comb.) Stylist: I get your point. By the way, how do you want your hair combed? Adams: I don't. Stylist: What? Adams: I don't comb my hair. I figure however it goes after I take a shower and towel it off, that's natural. (The stylist shakes her head, takes off the apron that kept Nick's hair off of his clothes, and then says...) Stylist: Well, in that case, I'm finished. Adams: Thanks. (Nick stands up and walks over to the cashier's desk. He hands the stylist a twenty-dollar Canadian bill, says "Keep the change," grabs his jacket and helmet, puts on both, and then walks out. He puts his keys into the motorcycle, then steps into the seat, revs up the engine and drives off. It was completely unexpected for him to do something as simple as this, but he did. And he's going to do something a bit more interesting...) ABOUT FIFTEEN MINUTES LATER... (Nick's motorcycle is shown driving around the roads, and it's almost a chase scene...in fact, he' being trailed by another motorcycle. It isn't a police bike, but they're still hurtling down the road dangerously fast. Of course, that is pretty much unpredictable, so it's probably just what Adams wants to do. He flies down the streets as if he were insane, and so does the other motorcycle. As the cameras go closer to the trailing motorcycle, we see that the symbols on the helmet resemble those on the mask of a former WWA announcer and current switch interviewer. Nick's motorcycle slows down, and so does the trailing "hog". They stop at a building labelled "Skate Park", and both stop their motorcycles. Nick pulls off his helmet, opens up the storage area of his bike, and extracts a pair of rollerblades. The other man on the motorcycle does the same, but in reverse, and when he removes his helmet, we see the face...or rather, the mask...of Juan Guevesos.) Juan: So, why are we here again? Adams: I just got my hair cut, you came here to surprise me, and I figure I might as well have a little fun while I'm here. Juan: Go figure. So, how much does this cost, anyway? Adams: It's fifteen fifty a person, Canadian. Didn't do the math for US money, and besides, I'm buying. Juan: Good deal. So, what, are you practicing for your match this monday? Adams: Actually, sort of. Juan: Why do you think you were paired with Venny? Adams: Venny's a good man, if you can excuse some of his worse actions an gullibility on his youth. And while he takes a shot at a traitor, I take a shot at the man who wants my title. Of course, he'll never get it. At least, not from me. There are few people that I think deserve the TV title as much as I do. One of them is my partner. But Grunge most definitely isn't. But enough about the match, man. Let's go in and have some fun...maybe even clear our minds a bit. Juan: Sounds good. (Both men take the keys out of their motorcycles, grab their helmets and rollerblades, and go into the building. And so goes this day in the life of Nicholas Adams, a man who believes that being random is one of the few advantages that he has.) FADE OUT
