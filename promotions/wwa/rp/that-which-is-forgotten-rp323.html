That which is Forgotten

31 Jul 2003

By Chance

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I still think this is crazy. How could I have just forgotten 3 days of my life? How could is just be gone, is an absinthe fuelled haze? A shudder goes down my spine, a momentary patch of utter cold that freezes the jelly in my marrow as I think about what I woke up to that morning. Then I feel a flash of total shame and embarrassment, the kind that makes you wish for death just to end the humiliation, at the thought of A-Trains strong, masculine and very hairy arms surrounding my body, with a firm yet tender strength. Eek, what did I just think? Argh, think manly thoughts! Think manly thoughts! I receive a welcome relief from my thoughts when Dave calls back to me from the passenger seat. Dave: Hey, Chance buddy? Are you feeling ok? You’re shivering! I think about how to reply to this, and decide not to. It’s just too complicated to try and explain to my friends. I stop thinking about it and then wonder what Jill had to do with those three days. The days that have been obliterated from my memory, and that’s a funny thing about forgetting, once you’ve forgotten something it’s as if it never existed, there’s a vague feeling that there’s something missing, but even that’s not always there. That’s why forgetting is such a scary thing, even if someone dies they are remembered, but if you forget a memory there’s no way you know it ever existed in the first place, so if you can’t rely of your memory what can you rely on? Dave: Ok guys we’re here, this is Jill’s apartment. Um Chance, should you really be here now, you’ve had a tough couple of weeks, the match at Truth or Consequences, the tag matches on Monday and the Monday before. Your neck hasn’t had any time to heal properly from that sick fall you took Chance: Dave, I couldn’t get this all sorted out before the Independence Day Rumble, and that pissed me off, almost as much as losing the match. I was still wondering about it at Truth or Consequences so there is no way in hell I’m going to leave this hanging around in my head any more, I just can’t deal with having three days of my life missing! Dave: Ok, let’s go in. You coming Mal? Mal: Sure, it’s been a while since Rookie talked to Auntie Jill! Hey is that the X-Mobile in her driveway? I’d been wondering where that got to! Auntie Jill? That’s one I haven’t heard before. Still, I guess Rookie gets around, he does seem to know everybody. Maybe he invited A-Train to the party? Wait, DAMN, best not to think about that! Ok, everything is ok, we’re in the elevator, we’re going up, we’re nearly there, soon we’ll get to the bottom of this. The elevator door is opening and … Chance: HOLY CRAXXT DOWNS THAT HURTS!!! Dave: Chance? What’s wrong man? The pain forces me down onto my knees and feels like a hundred tiny dragons breathing fire inside my skull. Accompanying the pain are images, memories, flooding back to my mind like mistreated girlfriends who just keep thinking I’ll change. I would welcome them back except that they leave a trail of burnt neurons after them, much like the ones I fried with alcohol to send them spinning from my brain in the first place. I stay kneeling on the floor, dissecting the images, trying to make some sort of vague sense out of a traffic cone, a seedy hotel room, and plane tickets to England and Florida. Eventually I manage to piece together a vague outline of one missing day as more and more memories crawl apologetically into my head after the initial explosion. A flashback of fire and agony later and I make my way groggily up to my feet to find Dave and Mal have dragged me into what I assume must be Jill’s apartment. Mal: Are you ok buddy? You looked out cold for a minute there? Dave: What happened? Chance: It’s nothing I just had a flashback of one of the days I missed, a very painful one. Dave: Really? What part did Jill play in it? Chance: She wasn’t in it. Weird. Where is she anyway? Voice: I’m right here handsome. Handsome? That’s … odd. And that’s all I have time to think before a radically changed Jill appears in front of me. If it weren’t for the few wrinkles she might actually pass for quite young. Her formerly gray hair is now a shining platinum blonde with not a hair out of place. And where before it was held in a bun, it is now like a waterfall of liquid fool’s gold. Her typically old person clothes have also changed, she is currently wearing a skirt that while not being short, is a lot shorter than I’ve come to expect from her. She’s also wearing a black blouse that has a fairly low neckline, revealing far more than I want to see on a woman her age. A sliver necklace hangs around her neck, which thankfully takes the focus away her neckline. It is as if an extremely competent fairy god-mother decided it was finally time for Jill to go to the ball a little too late. Chance: Um … hey Jill, you’re looking, uh … different. Jill looks slightly put out by this. I begin to worry about what might have done. Jill: Is that it? You certainly had a lot more to say when we… My God, I think Jill’s actually blushing. The words ‘like’ and ‘school-girl’ could easily describe the kind of blush too. Chance: When we what? Jill: When we, well, you know. She blushes even more and I begin to really worry, Mal and Dave are sniggering in the background. Bloody immature little so-and-so’s. Chance: No Jill, I really don’t know, could you fill me in? God she’s blushing even more. And she seems a little pissed off too. Jill: Well if I must say in front of these two. We… I begin to dread the next words. Jill: …went to the stylists together. I breathe a sigh of prodigious relief. Jill: You encouraged me to get my hair done. Then we went clothes shopping. You even bought me this necklace! Chance: Well, that’s enlightening. Um, why was I taking you to the stylist? Jill: The girl who was with you said you needed to get ready for the party we planned for Mal’s house. She needed to get the red streaks in her hair redone, and she persuaded you to get me a makeover. You said it looked really good. All the colour drains from my face as I pray feverently to God that the girl is not who I think it was, and that it was just someone I met that day. Chance: That girl, she had wavy brown hair right? And blue eyes? With a pale sort of angular, elf-like face? And a black and silver cross around her neck? Jill: Yup, that’s her. Who was she anyway. Oh dear Lord no. Well at least the ticket to England makes sense. Chance: Um, Jill? What else did we do that day? Jill: Well, we all went on a trip in the X-Mobile, covered quite a distance too. Mexico, Canada, you name it we went there. Chance: How did we get from Canada to Mexico in the X-Mobile in under a day? That piece of junk barely even goes gets to the mall in under two hours! Jill: Well actually we had it shipped by plane part of the way. It’s a funny story really … I’ll spare you the details of that journey for another day when all this makes sense, and when I can put it all in chronological order. Until then, this has been one very troubled and confused lucky guy, and you’ve been reading my mind. Good for you.
