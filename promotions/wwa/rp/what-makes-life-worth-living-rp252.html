What Makes Life Worth Living

17 Jul 2003

By Grunge

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
FADE IN (Grunge sits alone at the couch in his Oklahoma home. It’s a brand new house it seems based on the surroundings. It seems to be a music room as there are several guitars set up. In the corner is Grunge’s prize possession, his large drum set. Grunge is writing on a large legal pad.) Grunge: Now what rimes with rancid? (Grunge gets a look on his face as if to say “ah that’s it.” He quickly writes it down as Julie walks in from the other room.) Julie: Still working on that song? Grunge: Yeah well if Sea Prism is going to put out another album we’re going to have to have some new material. (Julie sits over on the couch next to him. She seems to be worried.) Grunge: What? Don’t worry about the band; it’s just a side project. Besides I know we’re not Pearl Jam. Truth be told we’re not even Steely Dan but… Julie: (interrupting) I’m not worried about the band. (Grunge smiles a bit even though Julie is obviously frustrated.) Grunge: I can see that…what’s on your mind? (Julie bites her lip in worry. She’s measuring her words carefully. The concern on her face tells it all.) Julie: It’s your match on Monday… Grunge: (Interrupting) Oh not this again. Julie: It is some cause for alarm. I mean he already injured your throat and what he did to poor Alec… Grunge: Yes and I promised… Julie: (interrupting again) I know you feel you have to do this because of his brother. I know the two of you are friends. But you already have Levi at Labor Day Brawl. Why do you have to do this twice? Being in the ring with that monster some more is just cruel. (Grunge looks off for a moment and then turns back.) Grunge: I like spaghetti Julie. (Julie looks shocked that he would say something like that.) Julie: Well I know that. I am your wife. Grunge: I love art. My favorites are music like James Taylor and Carly Simon. I like comic books and Shakespeare. And of course I love Kevin Smith films. Julie: I remember. I remember when you dragged me to see him at a Comic Con. And I still have the T-Shirt from the James Taylor concert. Grunge: And of course you know I love crossword puzzles… Julie: (interrupting) You leave the books piled everywhere. (Grunge leans over and kisses her on the forehead.) Grunge: And the way you smell like Vanilla and Wildflowers. And of course I pride myself on the fact that the space between my ears is immeasurable. (Julie chuckles.) Julie: You mean filled with empty space. I already know this stuff about you. What’s your point anyway? (Grunge wraps his arm around his wife’s shoulders. Grunge laughs softly and leans back on the couch.) Grunge: My point is that these are the only things worth hanging around in life for. They make life worth living. But if you run away from your problems you’re not really living that much of a life. Julie: Of course Leviathan is a really big problem. Grunge: Yes he is. But if I really value the way we live I’ll find a way to win. You have to face problems head on. Leviathan will learn that when I say rock and roll it’s not just a trendy catchphrase, it’s a way of life. (Julie leans back as well.) Julie: Speaking of rock and roll, play me something. (Grunge smiles and picks up his guitar and begins to strum away. He starts to play the opening cords of Eric Clapton’s “Wonderful Tonight.” This has seemed to do the trick with his wife as she begins to close her eyes and sway to the music. And as she begins to do this we begin to…) FADE OUT
