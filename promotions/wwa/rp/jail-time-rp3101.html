Jail Time

4 May 2006

By Armaan

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“If you’re gonna die, you might as well make it look good.” Armaan opened his eyes slowly, letting them adjust to the light at their own pace. As everything became visible, he was taken aback by his surroundings. This was not his hotel room anymore. The place he was in was no larger than a few feet either side, enough to walk around in, while a set of metal bars sent in some air and light. Apart from the sound of the boy sitting opposite him, everything else was silent. The boy himself was strange – despite being dressed in tattered rags and evidently having been through a rough time, he adorned a cheerful smile. Mischief could be seen in his eyes and, other than a few bruises on his jaw, he was a handsome boy. “Huh? Wha…?” was all Armaan could manage, still confused by what was going on. “I said, you’re gonna kill yourself, so you might as well make it look good.” “Where am I?” Armaan asked, choosing to ignore the boy’s rather cryptic comments. “Looks like a jail cell, or something like that. Though I don’t think they’re ever this dingy.” Armaan got up to his feet and headed towards the metal bars. Shackles attached from his ankles to a big metal post suddenly pulled him back, however, generating a quiet laugh from his companion. Armaan tried to lean forward as much as he could, so as to get a glimpse through the only open space, but everything outside was dark. “How did I get here?” was the next question from the puzzled Indian. The boy shrugged his shoulders but, with Armaan having his back to him, he was forced to answer. “Does it matter? You’re here, let’s leave it at that.” “But I should not be here,” Armaan suddenly blurted out, panic clear in his words, “I was in my hotel room, in bed, I was asleep, how can I be here? This makes no sense to me, I should not be here, I should not have shackles on my feet …” “Woah, woah, my man, take it easy. Haven’t you understood yet? You’re sleeping. All this is just a dream.” Armaan turned his head to face the boy, who glanced back with the same cheerful expression. Shaking his head, Armaan turned back in his desperate attempt to peer through the metal bars. “It’s no use, Mani, just come here and sit down.” Armaan suddenly stopped and moved his body around to face the boy, shock spread on his face. “Mani?” Armaan said slowly. “Mani,” the boy repeated. “That’s what I said.” “Only my mother used to call me Mani…?” Sighing, the boy pointed at the spot on the floor where Armaan was initially seated. More out of perplexity than anything else, Armaan obliged. “Like I said,” the boy continued as Armaan took his seat, “this is a dream. Things don’t have to make sense; I might know things I have no way of knowing. Because they’re all your thoughts.” “But if this is a dream, why like this? Why the jail cell, and shackles on my feet?” The boy lifted up his bottoms to reveal shackles on his own feet. “Atmosphere, I suppose,” he replied, with a smile. There was a long pause, where neither man spoke. The young boy fiddled around playfully with his shackles, and Armaan sat still in contemplation, looking down at the ground. Only the sound of the boy’s shackles disturbed the silence. “I don’t want to kill myself,” Armaan finally said, the first to break the hush. “What?” the boy replied, his attention diverted from the shackles. “You said I want to kill myself. That’s not true.” “It was.” Armaan looked up into the boy’s eyes; his expression had changed from confusion to one of sadness. “Yes, it was. Not anymore.” “How were you gonna do it?” “Do what?” “ It ,” the boy said again, an excited grin on his face, “kill yourself.” “I…I don’t know,” Armaan said uneasily, “I do not really want to talk about it.” “We might as well,” the boy responded, shrugging his shoulders. “Come on, tell me, how were you gonna do it? Seriously, I’m interested, because I can’t imagine you killing yourself. Were you gonna hang yourself?” Armaan refused to reply, feeling almost humiliated by his previous thoughts of suicide. “You haven’t told me your name,” Armaan asked, trying to change the subject. “Oh…yeah, you’re right. Umm…hmmm…a name. Jason.” “Jason,” Armaan repeated, a small smile on his face. “Actually, no, not Jason. Richard.” “Richard?” “Or Cynthia. Cynthia is a great name. My name could be Dominic too, I like that name. Lewis isn’t bad either.” “I don’t understand what you are tryi-“ “Andrew Scrine!” the boy suddenly shouted. “Andrew Scrine. That’s my name. Or Steven Murphy. That’s a great name. Both equally as good.” Armaan’s smile quickly disappeared; in fact, every emotion had drained from his face, leaving a cold, blank stare. “What are you trying to do?” Armaan asked slowly, trying to restrain himself. “Making you confront yourself, man. You were gonna commit suicide because of two guys? Gimme a break.” “You don’t know what has happened to me.” “I do know. Of course I know. You get hit by a car, you get kidnapped, you get beaten up god knows how many times, what else?” “Aren’t those reasons enough?” “Umm, yeah,” the boy replied, rather unconvincingly, “I suppose they are. But you didn’t commit suicide when your mum died. She did die, didn’t she?” The coldness in Armaan’s demeanour increased as his dislike for the young boy did too. “And you didn’t commit suicide when your parents abandoned you.” “Why are you telling me these things?” “I’m just telling you so you can remember them, my man. I’m telling you because you pick some shabby reasons to get upset over. Your parents abandon you, your mum dies, but you mess yourself up because two guys beat you up?” “I have been tortured…my body, my mind.” “Why didn’t you think about killing yourself when your mum died? Wasn’t that torture?” “I had strength then.” “Where’s your strength now?” Armaan couldn’t find an answer for the boy’s question, and uncomfortably began to fiddle with the shackles around his ankles. “Where is your strength, Armaan? Where’s that strength that you’ve had your life? The strength to survive through everything thrown at you. Has it all gone?” “No…I don’t know…” “How are you gonna do anything if you don’t have that strength? You’re gonna wrestle them, aren’t you? How are you gonna do it if you don’t have the strength?” “I’ll find a way.” “No! Don’t find a way, find your strength. Your strength’s there, Armaa-“ “Where?!” “Look for it. Within you.” Armaan suddenly shot up, sweating profusely from his face. He looked around – he was back in his room. He tried hard, but he couldn’t help panting; he was fatigued and exhausted. The alarm clock read 03:51; shaking his head, Armaan lay back onto the bed and pulled the covers over himself. Within me. Designed By [name withheld] | © World Wrestling Alliance 1997 - 2005
