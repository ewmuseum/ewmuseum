Farewells

8 Mar 2003

By Chance

Item 6 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 8 Mar 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Goodbyes are often hard to say, some more than others. The eternal question of the farewell is how do you put it? How do you simply say to someone that you are leaving, that as far as they are concerned you will cease to exist? Often it depends on the circumstances, millions of people say their goodbyes each day, not truly aware of the consequence behind their words, or even of the meaning those words conceal. For each time you say goodbye, you acknowledge that until you meet again, and if you meet again, that person will be effectively simply an echo in your mind, a memory that does not do the real thing justice. And every time you say goodbye, you acknowledge the possibility you may never see that person again. Which is why prolonged absences from people you care about are often so difficult, because when they, or you, walk out of the door, that is when they cease to exist to you in any physical sense, and you can no more see where they are, or how they are feeling, or how they see things, than you can see for certain that the Universe is expanding around us. So what really makes a goodbye hard? The emotional response to the feeling, or possibility of loss. And how strong that response is, often depends on the strength of feeling for the person you are saying goodbye to. And that is what really links in to this particular case, as right now, the person we are watching, is not at all sure why she seems to care so much about this particular farewell. The woman in question is slim, short, with their shapely body displayed to good effect, though seemingly unintentionally, in the formfitting rainbow striped dress that she is wearing. Her hair falls down her back in brown waves, with slight blonde highlights, that seem more natural than dyed, as if bleached by the sun. Currently she is pacing up and down the clean, sterile, white tiled floor of a kitchen, in a house that is not hers, but a house where she is surely welcome. We watch her pace nervously for a moment, and as we watch we notice that this is the girl we have come to know as Lucy, the girl who has featured in Chance’s last couple of promos. Just as we realise this, another woman walks into the room, this women we immediately recognise as Rebecca Crow, the wife of Jarod Crow. Rebecca: You look nervous? Is there a problem? Lucy starts, not realising that Rebecca had entered the room, before calming down and taking a deep breath before answering. Lucy: What? No, I’m not nervous, just a little … edgy. Rebecca: Are you feeling ok? You’re not really the nervous type. Lucy: Oh, no it’s ok I’m fine. Just … Rebecca: Edgy? Lucy: Yeah. Rebecca: Can I ask what about? Lucy: It’s Chance, you … you said he’s finishing his stay tomorrow, and I feel strange. Like I’m not sure how I feel about him. I mean, there’s a part of me that is going to definitely miss him, but there’s a part of me that asks why. He’s not really my type and … well, he just seems so serious, so intense most of the time, it’s odd to be around someone that focused. Rebecca: Hmm … I know what you mean, Jarod was a lot like that when I first met him, but he’s not so bad now. Lucy: But every now and then I see a glimpse of him, when he just lets go. Like when I went to see Daredevil with him, after watching that film he seemed so relaxed, so effortless, he did backflips down the corridor almost as if he was trying to show he could be a hero too! It was as if he suddenly didn’t care, as if he was … He voice trails off and suddenly the scene switches to another room of the same place, where we hear Chance finish Lucy’s sentence for her. Chance: … flying. It was like I was flying Jarod! It was as if everything that tied me down to this earth had lifted, and I was free to soar and swoop like an eagle! It’s being around her Jarod, being around Lucy … it’s soothing, to no longer care what people think, to no longer care about the WWA, or Denrol, or Enigma, for the only thing you give a damn about to be the person who is giving you this bliss! Jarod: You really, really like her don’t you? Chance: Jarod, I’m not sure if I’ve liked anyone this much after so short a time, and I’m not sure how to tell her that! The scene fades out, as both the males and the females are discussing the same thing. When we fade back in the scene has changed, and Rebecca, Lucy and Jarod are seated in the Crow’s living room, while Chance is nowhere to be seen. Until he suddenly bursts through the door seemingly fuming, pacing up and down the side of the room at speed. Rebecca (whispering to Jarod): What’s the matter with him? Jarod: (whispering back): He’s been watching some of the WWA insider tapes, Denrol’s promos. He’s been watching the same few minutes of video for around an hour now! At this Chance suddenly stops pacing and turns to Jarod and the others, saying rather too forcefully: Chance: That’s it; I’m going to the bar, who’s coming? Neither Jarod nor Rebecca move, but we just pick up on Rebecca giving Lucy a little gesture, as if telling her to go. Jarod: If that’s ok Chance, we’ll stay here, early night and all that, but I’m sure Lucy would be glad to go. Lucy uncomfortably gets to her feet, and Chance looks at her for a second, before grabbing his coat and stalking out of the room, tossing Lucy her coat on the way. They walk to the bar in silence, neither person really talking much, other than the occasional word. Both seem slightly uncomfortable with the other for the moment, a cloud seemingly darkening the situation in Chance’s obvious anger at Denrol, and his impending departure. Eventually they reach the bar Chance mentioned and they both walk in. It’s quite a nice looking place, not what you’d call new, but well looked after. Upon entering the bar, Chance walks straight up to the bar and orders a beer and a coke straight away, carrying the drinks over to a table for two in the middle of the bar. Lucy quickly joins him, sitting down on one side of the table remarking on the beer: Lucy: You shouldn’t drink when you’re angry you know, it only makes it worse. Besides you’re more fun before you’ve gotten drunk for some reason. Chance: Don’t worry; I’m not going to be drinking. (He points to the beer) this was for you. With that Chance strides to the front of the bar, where a band are in the middle of playing. Seeing Chance approach, the guitarists make a slight space for him, allowing him to step into the empty slot of lead-singer. The band finishes their current song, which seems to be one of their own as no one in the bar recognised it, and they speak to Chance for a brief moment, before appearing to make a decision. The band begins to play the opening chords to “Drive” by Incubus, and Lucy begins to look on at him intently. In contrast Chance appears to be looking anywhere but at her. The time comes for Chance to sing, and he does, in a good clear voice, singing: “Sometimes, I feel the fear of, Uncertainty, stinging clear, And I, can’t help but ask myself, How much I let the fear, take the wheel and steer.” As he sings Chance’s voice is filled with anger at many things, but right now his dislike is focused on Denrol, who has insulted and attacked Chance at every Chance he’s got. When he finishes this song he looks up, and looks Lucy straight in the eye, and doesn’t look away again. He mumbles a few words to the band, which begin to play, and this time when Chance sings, he is singing only for Lucy. And he is singing: ‘She’s Electric’, after this he sings City of Angels by the Red Hot Chilli Peppers, placing especial emphasis on the first line singing: “Sometimes I feel like I don’t have a partner, Sometimes I feel like my only friend, Is the city I live in, the city of angels, Lonely as I am, together we cry” Chance sings several more songs, all along the same soft tone he began with City of Angels, his eyes never straying from Lucy, almost as if he is serenading her, and we can see that she only has eyes for him. When Chance does break off, he goes to sit back down to Lucy, who stares at Chance for a second in enthrallment, and for a moment they are lost in each others eyes, then Chance leans in briefly and brushes a soft kiss against Lucy’s lips. So what bearing does all of this, and an unwillingness to say goodbye say about my match with Denrol? It shows that there are more important things in life; it shows why even if Denrol wanted me completely gone, there would be something waiting for me if he succeeded. Simply that there is something for me to fight for now, beyond my own interests. There is someone who’s memory will drive me on, even when she is not there, and I have to say goodbye.
