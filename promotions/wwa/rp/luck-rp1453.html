Luck

10 Jun 2004

By Quietus

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The days seemed to just disappear. One moment it was morning; the next, it was already dark. All of a sudden he found himself staring at a Thursday evening, trying to work out what he did for the last few days. The Brawl on Monday had taken more out of him than he had anticipated, so obviously a lot of the time since involved taking it easy and just generally resting. Tuesday and Wednesday were still a blur, and for someone who never drank themselves silly, that was a worry. He’d gone to see the doctor first thing Tuesday, he could recall, told quite bluntly that he was lucky his ribs were only moderately bruised. A few days’ rest and they should be all right – he was lucky, he’d been told. But for Quietus, luck had nothing to do with anything he did. Except of course when he lost – in which case such and such a guy was lucky he escaped, or luck just really wasn’t on his own side that particular evening. Luck - a wonderful excuse, yes, but not a weapon, and definitely, not a helper. He liked to convince himself of that everyday, as he looked in the mirror, or recovered from his cold shower; he was moving forward not through luck, but through sheer brilliance, skill and technique. Luck was ridiculous to even attempt to tie in with the career of Quietus; at least, according to Quietus. Only the weak were lucky. Everyone else preferred the term gifted. Standing on the last step of the stairs in his rented cosy home that had been his for over a week now, Quietus stared into a long rounded mirror. Ignoring the camera completely for the moment, he opened his mouth to reveal his teeth, staring at them in the mirror. Barring them together, he scrutinised them like a dentist, humming and hawing every now and then, pointing to this good tooth here or that bad tooth there. Looking at them through shades in a mirror probably affected his judgement somewhat, though no one was about to stand up and tell Quietus so. If he had all his teeth knocked out, they’d have jumped for joy. As it was, he had too many teeth, and too big a mouth, for many to handle. Satisfied that he had not turned into an ogre just yet, he popped a gum into his mouth, chewing on it cockily as only he could, hands in the side pockets of his leather jacket. Luck – Quietus almost smiled at the idea. If people weren’t green with envy, they had the rub of the green. It was a simple notion his old man had pounded into him over the years. It was as black and white as one could ask for. There simply was no in between. Quietus, ever the theologian and deep-thinker, decided to put his own spin on things, and came to the conclusion that everyone else was either green with envy when they saw him, or had the rub of the green when they defeated him. As for Quietus himself, he was never fond of the colour green to begin with. It reminded him of all things nice – everything that put people in a good mood; fresh-cut grass, hills in the distance, beer bottles. And for every thing that put people in a good mood, Quietus had to find something to cancel it out and dampen proceedings. He was just being himself. If one ever wanted to be put in a bad mood or to be made feel small, he was the person to turn to. He’d make a lottery winner feel like killing themselves. Quietus the extinguisher of all things nice. “You’re lucky.” The words were said so quickly but with such a sharpness to them, that everyone could have heard them quite clearly. He didn’t develop anything just then. Instead, he looked back into the mirror, adjusting his shades with a slight hint of a grin, though he was doing his utmost to look as solemn as possible for as long as he could. “What? Don’t turn around and glance at others around you. Don’t shrug your shoulders and walk on because there’s no way in hell I could be talking to you, sure didn’t your inbred cousin die last week? That’d be ridiculous, and stupid, which I can accept, given your upbringings, but what the hell; don’t do it. It’s pointless turning away or dropping your head in hope that you won’t be seen. Mal Somers wouldn’t be the Intercontinental Champion had it not been for luck. Nate Raine wouldn’t be the LDA owner had it not been for luck. My new dancing-partner Kash wouldn’t be the World Champion had it not been for luck.” He left everything up in the air with another pause, regaining his breath and collecting his thoughts. The wait was unbearable for some, who were probably wondering whether or not to switch stations because of the asshole that was talking here. Quietus bent down low onto one knee and tied the lace of his loafers, as if nothing else in the world mattered right there and then but that. Just as quickly he looked back up, grinning now. “You all depend on luck to see you through to the finish line. You can go three quarters of the way yourself, but at the final few bends you’re stumbling, or falling, or down altogether. That’s where you say to yourself “Ah sure, with a bit of luck I’ll make it...” or something along those lines. Sound familiar? It should, because you’re the air-heads who try to move forward in life with luck by your side, or in your pocket, or in the air you breathe. You get the point. And if you don’t, satisfy yourself with another chocolate cake. Without luck, the majority of you would be the nearly men, those who had the skill or raw talent, but just couldn’t tie it all together and become something great. You’ll say a quick prayer before you go to sleep to your newest computer god, begging it to help you through the next day without being found out for what you truly are; a green-rubber and a fortunate fool. It’s a strain on you every day of your life, hoping and praying and damn well demanding that you can continue to move forward without really, in actuality, being up to it. But what they don’t know won’t kill them, right?” Quietus grinned into the mirror, pushing his hand through the gelled up jet-black hair of his, nodding his head sarcastically, impressed with himself. “Jack Griffiths was found out a month ago to be nothing but a green-rubber, when he became the DIABLO of the LDA. You all know how he did it, the mediums involved, and so on, or if you don’t; you should be watching Barney and Friends repeats. Mal Somers was found out two weeks or so ago to be nothing but a green-rubber above suspicion, when he retained his Intercontinental Title. You all know how he did it. I won’t test your knowledge, because frankly, I don’t really want to address any of you personally. This Monday night, I plan to open people’s eyes to the Kash that no one knows exists. The one that gets by, like you all, nervously hoping and praying to his lucky stars to keep on shining at their brightest. As one of those crazy bastards once said in those old stupid movies; the truth will prevail.” He nodded emphatically and folded his arms, a stiff, forced serious expression spread all over his tanned face. It was all too much and all too funny for the Australian, however, and within moments his laughter dominated. He howled and hooted like no tomorrow as if the funniest joke ever had just been revealed to him and no one else. “Sometimes the truth hurts, you know. I’m sure you found that out when your son got straight Fs in his finals, or your daughter’s obesity became such an issue that curious neighbours wondered if you were about to become Grandparents. For good ol’ Kash, the truth will hit him harder than I ever could. Not that I don’t hurt; just ask Malcolm.” Again he laughed, this time more of a giggle than anything else. Shaking his head like he should be on the stage, he focussed again on the mirror. “Unfortunately, luck, like everything else, comes to an end and runs out. When it does, people feel naked without it and falter, fall and are soon forgotten about as has-beens. While I’m not gunna come out with something crazy like me being the controller of luck, I’m the devil’s own, kids. And if God, or a god, creates luck, then it makes sense that his opposite, i.e. the devil, would cancel it out and bring it to an end. I’m sure that all sounds delicious to Kash, hoping and praying that he gets his hands on me to prove him wrong. But he, like everyone else, should remember something quite simple and straight-forward; he doesn’t control his luck, so it’d make sense to fold up shop and run when he’s still ahead. Don’t worry, though folks; your World Champion is far too brave to ditch you all this Fiesta. He’ll be there, big and brave, but behind it all, small and nervous, hoping to whatever the Wican believes in that it will be luck – his luck – that prevails and brings him through unfound out. Don’t worry buddy, I can keep a secret.” He smiled in a jeering fashion at the mirror, before bending down to a tool box that was situated between his legs and had previously gone unnoticed. With a giggle, he rummaged inside, before coming out with a small hammer, of modest age. Nodding, he held it up, now facing the camera. “I guess my luck’s ran out...” Without warning, he turned around and smashed the mirror with the hammer in a single controlled swing. The glass shattered, and pieces fell onto the floor with an irritating smash. “So at least when he’s beaten, Kash won’t have that excuse to fall back on.” He dropped the hammer back into the box perfectly, having completely forgotten about the mirror, or lack of, now. He tugged at his shades before ripping them off, and, with a wink at the camera, he began whistling, right after he blew his kiss of all things unpleasant in the same direction. Walking off out of sight, the camera focussed on the glass, as we faded to black. As far as Quietus was concerned, after Monday, Kash would just be another one of the many discoveries. Touch wood.
