The Calm Before The Storm

16 Jan 2003

By Havoc

Item 4 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 16 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With a smooth overhead swing, Phobos buried the axe deep within the chunk of wood that rested on the block before him. With a sharp crack, the wood split, falling to either side of his blade. As stoically as ever, Phobos reached to the side pulling another round piece of wood from the ground. With one hand, he placed it on the block, taking his hand away only after he knew that it would remain standing. He hefted the axe in both hands, inhaling deeply. Sweat beaded its way down the crease in his bare chest. With a sudden exhale, his muscles moved into tight, ropy bundles, and the axe fell. The chunk of wood on the block split cleanly to the sides with a report that echoed from the trees around him. Clad only in black jeans and heavy work boots, Phobos set the head of the axe on the ground, resting his hand on the end knob. From his back pocket, he pulled a bandana, patting it across his forehead. As if in rebellion, a rivulet of sweat ran down from his hairline. His gaze panned about the area, taking in the majesty of the trees that comprised the forest. The blue sky, dotted with a few wispy, white clouds, acted as the canvas to the soaring beauty of a screeching hawk overhead. Nearby, a pile of split wood lay as testament to the day’s hewing. The serene hush of the forest brought a smile to his face. These moments of tranquility, so far removed from the heated cacophony of battle, helped him to focus. These rare moments allowed him to collect his thoughts, to plan his strategies. In just over a week, he and his brother, Deimos, would enter into battle in a steel cage. They would pit their bodies, muscle, sinew, and bone, against two other teams in a bloody contest for supremacy. Phobos couldn’t stop the smile that crossed his face. The acrid smell of carnage, the feel of an enemy’s warm blood, and the glorious sounds of the enraged fray returned to him. His eyes closed as he relished the memories. Ares, God of War, had bred him from his first days for magnificent slaughters. He lived for it. He loved it. Watching the fire in an opponent’s eyes slowly die, the sigh of an adversary as he realized his own defeat, these things brought him untold joy. In Calgary, he would again know the elation of forcing defeat upon his foes. But for now, he would enjoy this moment of peace, this calm before the storm. End.
