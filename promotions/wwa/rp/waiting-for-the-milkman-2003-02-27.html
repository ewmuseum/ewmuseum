Waiting for the Milkman

27 Feb 2003

By Guido

Item 6 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 27 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The scene opens to a helplessly messy apartment. The camera pans around, revealing a bizarre assortment of odd little souvenirs and trinkets. A thoroughly creepy poster of Marcel Marceau, the quintessential mime, is on the wall. An even creepier collection of hundreds of early 90's Burger King kids-meal toys rests on a dresser. Slowly, the camera centers in on a framed picture opposite the dresser. It is a photograph of a half-naked Asian man stretching a nylon stocking over his face. The caption reads: "I'm just your friendly neighborhood milkman!!" A smaller caption directly underneath it reads: "The search for truth continues." Suddenly, a person pops up in front of the photograph, and the camera zooms out to get a full shot of his body. Now this guy looks creepy. Like Robin Williams in "One Hour Photo" creepy. With a Captain America t-shirt surmounted by a shabby mop of dark hair hanging over his eyes and a menacing smile, the guy looks like some sort of insane post-grunge super-villain-geek. Who could it be? Why, it's our friend Guido, the Laughing Prophet of Doom. He wipes the grin off his face, clears his throat obnoxiously, and begins to speak.) "If you feel that you have enough Time to reflect on your life, please raise your hand." (The camera pans around, but there is no one in the room except for Funk, who is passed out on the couch, drooling.) "Now, think about how much Time in life is spent waiting. Stuck in traffic, waiting. Waiting for the elevator to come. Waiting for a friend to return a valuable. Being put on hold on the telephone, waiting for someone to pick up on the other end." "Waiting for that obese black women in front of you at Walmart to stop bitching about her damn coupons." (Pause.) "Waiting for love." "Waiting for triumph." "Waiting for whatever God you worship to do anything other than sit there, omniscient and apathetic." "Too much Time is lost in life because of waiting, and lost Time is one of the worst things that can happen to a person. However, I have discovered the solution." (A mischeivious grin spreads across Guido's face as he moseys on over to his claustrophobically small kitchen, and motions to the microwave.) "Just a regular household appliance, right? WRONG! Look here." (The camera zooms in on the microwave buttons and Guido points to a button labeled, "Stop Time".) "No, you are not dreaming. This seemingly mundane piece of consumer electronics allows one to actually STOP the inexorable march of time. I convinced Funk to press it once -- he reported being sucked into a pitch-black void where the sinuous river of time was frozen solid, and "Jump" by Van Halen was being looped infinitely. Woke up the next day in Gumbo, Missouri with no pants and a tree branch duct-taped to his head. True story." "Now, I'm not actually going to press the button now. I'm afraid of the dark and I hate Van Halen. But now that I have this incredible device at my disposal, I have no qualms about losing time by waiting." "Waiting for my WWA debut, live on Saturday Night Fiesta." "Waiting to bring Chuck Wipple to his unprecedented demise." "Waiting." "Training." "Haikuing." "And content with that. Because I am fully prepared for Fiesta in every possible aspect. Saturday night will be no mere Fiesta for my unconfident pal Chuck Wipple. Chuck, you refer to yourself as "The Human Foreign Object", so I will use you accordingly. Although many people admire "hardcore" wrestlers that supposedly don't fear pain and throw themselves around like impaled Barbies, it isn't exactly the best way to win a match. Wrestling is physical poetry -- an art form underappreciated by many; one that takes skill, determination, and precision. Poets that just throw their words around sloppily pale in comparison to ones who choose their language carefully. The same goes for the sport of professional wrestling, Chucky. You will falter at the hands of the Poetic Vigilante, Guido." (Stop time.) (I am just your friendly neighborhood milkman.) (Fade out to an infathomable, pitch-black void where "Jump" by Van Halen is looped infinitely.)
