From where I draw my Strength

1 Feb 2003

By Chance

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 1 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene opens in a fairytale setting, a thatched cottage, with red brick walls, a pretty little garden complete with flowers of bright pinks, icy blues, and crimson reds. And even a little white picket fence separating it from the countryside to all sides. It seems perfect, and with anything that seems perfect, we immediately distrust this setting, why is it the human reaction to immediately distrust anything that seems perfect? Along the twisted path through the garden, paved with grey and brown soft textured stones, we see Chance walking towards the cottage. He is wearing loose white cotton trousers, and a red Hawaiian shirt patterned with dice over a long sleeved white t-shirt with some simple black patterns on the arms. He seems pensive as he walks further down the path, sort of nervous. We can tell by the way he keeps adjusting his clothes and his newly cut and dyed (red) hair, and the way he repeatedly wipes his palms on his trousers as if they were sweating. Eventually he reaches the door and tentatively rings the bell, looking as if he’s dreading what might happen next. What does happen, is that a cute woman in her early twenties opens the door slowly, and stands there, gaping at Chance in stunned surprise. She is pretty, with a round face and a little rosebud of a mouth. Her brown hair cascades down her shoulders like a waterfall, in rough curls, with streaks dyed red, much like all of Chance’s hair is now. She is wearing a close fitting red top, and form-hugging (but not tight) black trousers. She appears to get over her surprise and adjusts her hair slightly. Woman: Sebastian? Is that you? When she speaks her voice is pure ambrosia. Soft, yet subtly strong, it has a ringing tone to it that makes you think of church bells sounding joyfully on a clear spring day. In contrast to this, when Chance speaks his voice is croaky, almost broken with nervousness. Chance: Uh, yes and no Laura, can I come in? The woman who we now know to be Laura leans in towards Chance briefly, brushing her lips across his with a light kiss, before leaning back again and hitting him with a full-fledged slap across his cheek. Chance recoils more out of shock than pain, but we see a vivid red mark beginning to appear on his left cheek nonetheless. He is about to say something angrily when she interrupts him. Laura: Of course you can come in. This way. She leads him off into the hall of the cottage, and takes him through a side door, into what seems to be a living room. She sits down in an armchair, leaving him to seat himself on a sofa, before she speaks again. Laura: So? What do you want? Her voice issues a challenge across the room, just daring Chance to take her up on it. Chance: Look, I know I didn’t leave here under the best of terms last time we saw each other- She interrupts him, scorn heavy in her voice. Laura: That’s an understatement! Chance tries to carry on as if she hadn’t said anything, ignoring the sharp hostility in her tone. Chance: I know, and I’m sorry, but you’re the only one I feel I can talk to about this; you’re the only person who knows me well enough. He pauses, evidently expecting her to say something, but she too is silent. They sit there for a while, the tension between the two of them as effectively separating them as a solid brick wall. Finally it seems that Chance is about to break the deadlock, but just as he opens his mouth, Laura undercuts him. Laura: So now you want to talk. Now you want to discuss things like adults. Now you want my advice. Well, go on then, talk. Chance: All right, I know you’re angry, I would be too, in fact a lot of the time I still am angry with myself, but I had my reasons. We can tell by the strain in his voice that Chance is carefully reigning in his emotions, keeping anger and self-damnation out of his voice. Laura remains silent and Chance continues. Chance: The last of my family had just died, and I was alone, you were all I had left, and it just seemed so perfect that I had you to fall back on, that I thought you’d be taken away from he as well. My God, it felt as if everything I loved was being snatched away from me; can you blame me for wanting to get away from you on my own terms before Fortune took you too? Laura: No, but I can blame you for the way you did it. You think I couldn’t tell what was running through your head, you think I didn’t know you blamed yourself, your luck, for what happened to your dad, and your sisters? You could have just told me you wanted to leave and I would have let you, you could have just said that you wanted to get away from me. You didn’t have to drive me away! If you’d just bloody well told me, then I wouldn’t have made it so hard for you to leave. You didn’t have to leave me with no idea of where you were, where you were going, if you were all right or not. The only way I knew you were okay was because I saw you wrestling! This time it is Chance’s turn to interrupt, although it looks as if Laura has just hurt him quite badly, he still goes on. Chance: Actually that’s what I wanted to talk to you about. If you’ve been watching me wrestling, then you’ll know about the whole thing with Enigma right? -she nods- Then you know what he’s been saying, that he and I are similar, that we both know -he swallows- that we both know that if we want to win on Sunday that we have to make it so that the other man is unable to get up. And right now, I’m beginning to think he’s right. I’m beginning to think that I might actually try to finish things between us once and for all in that cage! I guess what I’m trying to say here is: do you think I could do that? Do you really think I could do that to another human being? Am I a good person? This time, instead of the scorn that had marked her expression before, Laura’s face is now creased with concern for Chance and she gets up and goes to sit down beside him before talking. Laura: Listen, Seb … or should I call you Chance now? Listen to me. Of course you’re a good person, you’re still the same guy I knew once, even if you have changed a bit. I know that it would be impossible for you to deliberately try and take away someone’s health, someone’s career, even if you’re doubting yourself right now, I know. And if I were you, I’d be worried about Jimmi Zion rather than Enigma. He has something to draw strength from that you and Enigma don’t, he’ll be fighting for his wife in there on Sunday, for someone he loves. If you ask me, he has the advantage over both of you there. Chance: You really think that? I know that when we were together was a few years ago, so I wasn’t wrestling, but I know I could draw a lot of strength from you, it’s what helped me get through a very trying time of my life. Can that strength really help you in the ring? Laura: Of course, when you’re fighting for something other than yourself, you always try that extra bit harder, you always have that extra bit of motivation, and love is a great thing to fight for. Believe me, when it seems that he’s down, even if his body doesn’t have the strength, his love might carry him through. Can you say the say the same? Chance’s reply fades away from us as we retreat out of the room, back through the hall of cottage, then out the door and back down the path, where we fade to black.
