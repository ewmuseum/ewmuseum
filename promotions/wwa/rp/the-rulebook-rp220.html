The Rulebook

3 Jul 2003

By "N.A.I." Nicholas Adams

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Nicholas Adams is shown sitting in a library. He is in a padded chair. Behind him lies rows and rows of bookshelves, obviously a part of the library's reference section. In front of him lie six paperback books, each arranged with their writing so that Nick can read them. The covers all have the same basic styling, as far as arrangement. The books are of slightly different sizes, but appear to be mostly equal in page numbers. Nick smiles as he looks down at them, as if he happens to know something that nobody else seems to know.) Adams: Well, my fine opponents, you are probably wondering why I have brought you to watch me in a library, especially when you could be training at the moment. Honestly, I don't care for your needs. You are my rivals, and therefore, you do not care for my needs. But you would do well to listen to what I have to say. Do not just hear me. Take my words well, or you might just make a mistake when you fight me. So, just so you know how this fight is going to occur, let me lay down the rules. (Nick picks up the book the most left and closest to the camera. He opens it and begins to just flip through some of the pages absent-mindedly, not really reading any of them until he finally stops at a certain page. He then seems to read from it.) Adams: Rule number one - People can be made to beleive any lie, either because they want it to be true or because they fear that it is. (He smacks the book shut, grins and sets it down in its original place, then looks at the camera.) Adams: You see, all of you going into the Independence Day Rumble are all lying to yourselves. You want to believe that just because you are in the Rumble, that automatically means that you, above all others, shall automatically win just because you are entered. Unfortunately, for twenty-nine of us, this possibility, this vain hope turned to knowledge, this confidence that we shall win...well, it shall be proven incorrect. Remember that. You can lose, and for twenty-nine of the people participating in the Rumble, that "can" is actually the word "will". (Adams picks up the second book, and then begins flipping through it. He yet again stops at a page and seems to read from it. It's as if he's a man possessed.) Adams: Rule two - the greatest harm can result from the best intentions. (Nick yet again smacks the book shut and places it back on the table. Obviously a calculated move, since he does it fluidly and stiffly at the same time. He then looks yet again directly at the camera.) Adams: Basically, alliances are not always the best thing to hold in a Rumble. Nobody is quite sure what will happen. A little miscommunication, and a move intended to aid an ally might become the thing that finishes them. If two people team together, one might inadvertantly eliminate the other. In all honesty, I might just avoid my friends in the ring at all costs short of eliminating myself, just so I can prevent my best intentions from causing any harm. For those teammates entering the Rumble, I advise you to think on this. (Adams looks at the books, then shakes his head. He is obviously ad-libbing, since the camera man interjects with a, "But," and Adams just shakes his head again.) Adams: I have these memorized. The books were just for effect. Anyway, rule three - passion rules reason. Take that as you will, but when I state the final rule, you will understand that if you do not remember the third rule, the final rule will be meaningless. But remember what passion can do to you if you let it control you. Remember how passion makes a person blind. In fact, also remember this fact. In a kingdom of the blind, a person with only one eye can rule. And if everyone but one man is empassioned in the ring, then that one man who is not blind shall control every other man in that ring absolutely. (Adams crosses his ankles and leans back.) Adams: Rule four has nothing to do with the match, but I think it applies to real life far more than to wrestling. There is magic in forgiveness. Magic to heal. In the forgiveness you grant, and more so in the forgiveness you receive. Actually, this does apply. Let me say that for the people with whom I am amicable, I apologize for what I may have to do to you to win the Independence Day Rumble. I hope you will find it in your heart to forgive me as I will be able to forgive you for what you do in the ring. A title shot is a title shot. (Adams then leans forward, uncrosses his ankles, and stares contemplatively at the books.) Adams: Rule number five - Mind what people do, not only what they say, for deeds will betray a lie. So far, I have not said much about what I shall do in the ring, except that I shall forgive my allies in Youth United and the Extreme Corporation, should we get to fighting. But this I will promise you. Words mean nothing in the ring. If you can talk up a storm, whoop dee doo. Politicians can talk up a storm, but they aren't much in fights. Lawyers are good public speakers, but they're not that good at brawling. So whatever you say, let's just see what you do in the ring. Only then will I respect you. (Nick stands, brushes off his knees, and then pushes his hair back with both hands. He looks directly at the camera lens once again, but this time with a more piercing glare. It is the soulless gaze that he sometimes affects so that his thoughts do not betray his words.) Adams: The sixth and final rule is that the only sovereign you can allow to rule you is reason. If you ignore this rule in the ring, the rest of the rules are pointless. If you do not think in the ring, you are pretty much destined to lose. Your passions shall drive you, yes, but they shall drive you through the ropes and down to ringside. You will not win if you allow your passions to be your true ruler, for the only useful monarch over your actions that humans can generate is reason. But more importantly, you will not win because I am in the ring. (Nick then turns his entire body to face the camera, showing his full body. He's wearing a tight black wifebeater and some loose black jeans. The shirt shows his defined muscles, making him all the more menacing, since his arms have to be at least fifteen inches around each.) Adams: You might ask why my presence in the ring will mean anything. Here's the reason. I have reason on my side. I am only empassioned when I have a single target. In this match, I have many more. And besides, I have one more thing going for me. (Nick pauses, pulls some sunglasses from his jeans pocket, unfolds them with both hands, and slides them onto his face.) Adams: I am the Rainmaker. (Nick walks off, and the camera fades out.)
