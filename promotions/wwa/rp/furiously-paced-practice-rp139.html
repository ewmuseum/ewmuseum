Furiously-Paced Practice

13 Jun 2003

By "N.A.I." Nicholas Adams

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Nicholas Adams is shown sparring with somebody else. Both are wearing martial arts uniforms, though Adams' partner's uniform is red, and both have black belts with Korean writing on the ends. They are obviously in a Tae Kwon Do academy, or at least a sparring arena. Neither man is wearing pads, making the fighting all the more interesting. In the background, "Invisible Kid" by Metallica plays. During the opening tune, both men make feints, generally cut steps and other fake kicks. Suddenly, as the music picks up, they both throw roundhouse kicks that miss to the side. The kicks make them pass one another. They then turn and face one another, and during the notes just before the lyrics, they just bounce. Then, the lyrics hit.) Invisible kid Never see what he did Got stuck where he hid Fallen through the grid (Adams throws a quick side kick, and his opponent deflects it with a quick low block before stepping in with a punch that Adams barely dodges. The two separate with 180-degree steps going backwards.) Invisible kid Got a place of his own Where he'll never be known Inward he's grown (Adams flies in with a jump front kick, but his opponent quickly counters with a high side kick. Adams barely pulls in his feet in time to spin out of the way to attempt a back kick that just goes above his opponent's head.) Invisible kid Never see what he did Got stuck where he hid Fallen through the grid (His opponent does one backflip to get away, then charges in with a run. After three steps, he jumps into the air with a vicious side kick aimed directly at Nick's head. Nick quickly side-steps it and punches his opponent to the ground with a hit to the chest. The music continues to play as the man stays down.) Invisible kid Got a place of his own Where he'll never be known Inward he's grown (The music stops as applause is heard from seemingly nowhere. The camera turns to show some adults and kids who have apparently been watching a demonstration. Adams goes and helps his partner stand. He then turns to the crowd.) Adams: Ladies and gentlemen, thank you for watching. Just remember, the martial arts are not just for impressing somebody. They are for self-defense and personal discipline. We hope that this demonstration has motivated you to take up a class at this academy. Thank you again for coming today, and have a good afternoon. (The small crowd gives yet another round of applause, and then begins to go to the exit. Adams walks over to his sparring partner, who is sweating, but looks like he just had the time of his life.) Adams: You know, that was actually pretty fun, John. John: Sure, Nick. No problem, really. After all, I did say you just had to call in beforehand so that we could get the sparring demo correct. Adams: Yeah. By the way, sorry about the punch. I was a little stiff, and... (John stops him.) John: Stiff? Actually, your punches need a bit more snap. I think that wrestling is beginning to soften you up a bit. Adams: Whoa, John. Sorry to say, but just because we have to protect one another because of the nature of wrestling doesn't mean I've gone soft. I was here a year ago, and I was just as good as I've ever been since I got this belt. John: Well, that's true enough. Still, I don't quite understand why you're wasting your time in Tennessee. Meltdown's in Kentucky. Adams: Well, the WWA was going through here. I figured I might as well come and visit. It's been a while since I've had a chance to use some actual Tae Kwon Do. John: True enough. But how is this going to help you win against Enigma, Grunge, and Dave Harley? (Adams and John go to the bench in the room and sit down, then grab some water bottles, open them, and take a gulp before Adams replies.) Adams: You want an honest reply? I think I just might be slipping, if not in Tae Kwon Do, than in wrestling. I just felt that I needed the discipline re-instilled in me so that I could have a chance. John: In all the time I've known you, I think this is the first time you've admitted that you're not the same guy you were nine years ago. Adams: So what if I'm not? I can still run with the best of them. After all, I am "The Rainmaker". John: Not to me. To me, you're just Nick, a guy who's three years younger than I am, and still searching for his true purpose in life. You do realize that you've spent eight years of your life on wrestling, correct? (Nick sighs, and leans forward.) Adams: Yeah, and I have something to show for it. John: A long career. But in the WWA, you're still a rookie. Adams: Yeah. I'm a rookie that knows more about wrestling that couple other people that entered the WWA at about the same time as I did. John: Okay, now I know you're punking out on Enigma. Adams: You got that right, man. Every time I hear him speak, it's like he's saying, "Look at me, I know the English language." Honestly, sometimes, I wonder whether it's worth wasting the breath on him. John: Really? The why are we talking about him right now? Adams: Because I haven't vented my thoughts with Juan for a while. He's been on the Fiesta circuit, I've been on Meltdown... John: Man, you really DON'T have friends backstage, do you? (Adams stands and begins to walk to the exit.) Adams: Hey, I'm slightly anti-social at work. What can I say? It's just who I am. I'm not going to change just because somebody wants me to. (John stands as well.) John: Nick, that's the philosophy that made Anna walk away from you. (Adams pauses.) Adams: I know. But I can't exactly do anything about that now. See you later, John. (Nick turns around, bows to the US and South Korean flags hanging on the wall, and then puts on some sandals and walks out the front door. John crosses his arms and shakes his head.) John: Good luck, buddy. Sometimes, I think you're really going to need it. (Fade out.)
