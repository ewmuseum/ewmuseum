Memory

18 Jan 2003

By Chance

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 18 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I remember. I remember everything. That is in itself a form of madness they say, to remember everything. If so, then lock me away because I remember it all, right down to the smallest detail. You might too, if you had been there. If you knew what it felt like to be Fortune’s Favoured, Fortune’s Fool. Sometimes I wonder if anyone knows what it feels like. Do I? Do I really know anything? Does anyone? None of those questions really matter, no questions like that really affect how we live our lives, would you stop living your life as you know it on the off chance that one of those questions philosophers ask might be valid? Just a thought or two to think about. But that is not the point of this life, this journey, or even this story. So what is you may ask? The purpose is probably the gravestones in front of me. Even as I stare at them, I unthinkingly twist the coin around my fingers. I feel the cold metal against the skin, and bone in my fingers as I roll it around my index finger with my thumb, and then spin it between my index and middle fingers with the deftest of touches. I do not even think as I watch the coin seamlessly spin, tumble, and whirl around my hand, knowing that if I begin to think about it, it will fall from my grasp as surely as if I were trying to spin water, or an eel between my knuckles. I force myself to stop, and grasp the coin in my hand, knowing even though I cannot see it that it still glitters eerily, and I really do have to force myself. For not the first time, I consider throwing it away. Throwing it into the darkness beyond the graves, into oblivion and annihilation, for some other person to find, for some other person to possess, someone else for it to bring luck to. But it doesn’t work that way. The coin’s just a symbol, a focus of my luck, not the luck itself. The good, or the bad. And as much as sometimes I’d like to get rid of my luck, I know I cannot. No one escapes the favour of Lady Luck, as much as they might like to. For a while I stop thinking these depressingly fond thoughts. Thoughts that I have gone through so many times that they are almost routine now, although I never seem to become bored of thinking them. I look at the gravestones again. And I read the words I know from memory. On the one side, the etching on the stone is slightly faded with the passing of time, still perfectly readable, but clearly faded. The other almost as clear as the day it was written. I know, for I was there. And I remember. The grey stone is lifeless, boring. Not a fitting monument to the people who lie there now. Not a fitting testament to the force of life, the sheer sense of just being alive that they always had. If I could I would build pyramids to rival Giza, temples that surpass the Acropolis. Monuments that are greater, more beautiful, and more alive than any other. But I cannot, so I must be content with these cold, dead chunks of stone. That and the words, I must not forget the words, how could I? -A light drizzle falls across me, each tiny bead of moisture an angel’s caress on my face. I barely feel it, even as my head is turned upwards trying to feel each and every tiny droplet, and my eyes are closed to enhance the feeling. My mind is empty; an imagined flame burned away all my worries, all my cares, leaving only the flame and a void. Then the flame burnt itself out. Thoughts and emotions cannot touch me as I wrap myself in emptiness, anger, guilt, and fear drift across the surface, above the void, but they are not my emotions. Pain still exists but I don’t feel it, it’s happening to someone else. I cannot feel pain here in this place, but a safe prison is still a prison. The thought drifts across the void, nearly shattering it, but I hold myself in, maybe I like being a captive in my own mind. From deep within the abyss of my conscious self, a part of me is still staring at the grey stones in front me. Memorising the words. Burning them into my memory. A light breeze brushes at my long hair, and I absently brush a few strands back behind my ears, and at the same time a tear runs down the cheek of a man who does not seem to be me. It is strange being confined behind the barriers of my own brain, of being held back from some greater understanding by the limits set upon myself. Yet it is also familiar, for across the ages, mankind has always been imprisoned behind what we are capable of understanding, of what we are capable of knowing. Man was held back and only the select few have the understanding of a certain area. Lately I’ve begun to see that I may be one of those few. Wrapped up in the nothingness brought on by the trick of the flame, I can think about this clearly. I can think about luck, how it is not as most people, just good luck, and bad, each separate, each unique. I can see clearly now, how luck is not divisible into different categories, there is simply luck, the single unchangeable force, acting throughout the universe that twists events towards more unlikely outcomes, for better or for worse. I can also realise and accept for a time that I am one of the few who understands this, but also one of the few whose very being is almost a force of chance itself, attracting luck, tempting Fortune to manipulate the events around me. All of this I understand as I stare at the grey stones, and see the individual grains that run through the yet unworn rock, and learn the words. Then, with no warning the fragility of the void shatters into a million pieces. The emotions come flooding back into my head, and any semblance of reason and logic is gone, and reaching for it is like trying to catch water with my fingers. My head drops down, my eyes close for a second, and every molecule of my being grieves, and is covered in the oily taint of guilt, for it was my luck that did it. My luck that caused the stones to be put here, and my luck that caused the words to be written: “Beloved Mother, and doting wife”, “Dearly loved father”, and “adored sisters and loving daughters”. “May they Rest in Peace”- I shiver as I remember, and it has nothing to do with the cold. Dark is rapidly approaching and as I look up I see the orange and gold flames of the day, being chased away by shadows, why is that always the way things happen? Why does shadow always banish the light, if only for a little, while it should be the opposite? Why can people like Enigma and Jackson Kraven and Damian Thorne run riot over those who society would like to believe should defeat them? Enigma, Kraven and Thorne have very nearly crippled myself, Denrol and Kash, and it seems almost as if they will get away with it. And I know that things are not always as they take place in stories. I also know that the HoP have yet to be proven to be less skilled than any of us, if anything their brutality gives them skills we cannot use. And I also know that even while I try to convince myself otherwise the HoP are a dominating force, and it seems unlikely that any one of us divided could bring them down. But that is what I must count on, that Lady Luck will pull on the strings of likeliness and luck will twist events until what is unlikely will be commonplace and achievable. That is what I must count on, for Lady Luck will surely take a hand as she always does. She took a hand of Monday night. She manipulated my match and what happened later. She pulled and tugged at us until I was left alone in the ring, vulnerable for a loss. And then later in the night, just as Matthew Warr said, Lady Luck decided that I should face Denrol. She decided that I should be the one to get to prove myself. Why she would do this I cannot say, but I am grateful to her for the opportunity, I look forwards to running the risks against The Greatest of All Earth Time. I take a break from my thoughts and look at the coin clutched tight in my hand again, a reminder of who and what I am. It is strange that Denrol could think I could simply cast aside what I am. The gift I have been given, the curse I have been blighted with. I know that I cannot give it up as if it were an act I was putting on; this is who and what I am. I will have to make him understand it I suppose. I suppose he will have to feel first hand what it is like to be Fortune’s Favoured, Fortune’s Fool.
