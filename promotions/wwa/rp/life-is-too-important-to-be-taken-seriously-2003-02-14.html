Life is too Important to be Taken Seriously

14 Feb 2003

By Havoc

Item 2 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 14 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In the bitter cold of an evening in Ottawa, figures moved about on the ice, gliding angelically. Despite the frost that assaulted them, they laughed, they played, and they skated. Phobos stood nearby, surveying the swarms of mortals as they blissfully amused themselves. He pulled his trenchcoat tightly about himself. The mortals tried not to stare at the seven-foot god among them, but their efforts were for naught. Tiffani and Deimos slid past him, smiling in their reverie. Tiffani wore a leather jacket to match her leather pants. A ponytail held her soft, blond hair, revealing her sweet face. Her blue eyes shined with joy and laughter. Deimos, his brother, wore jeans and a t-shirt, certainly not fitting attire for the weather. Somehow, it seemed that adverse weather never touched him. In this frozen northern land, Deimos felt no discomfort. Phobos envied him for that as he blew into his hands, trying to warm them. Deimos’ laughter rang out across the park, alongside Tiffani’s. Laughter. Something that Phobos rarely allowed himself. Such frivolity seemed counter-productive. After all, laughter distracted the mind and dulled one’s focus. But then, why did so many people seem to seek it out with such fervor? Why would people pursue this weakness so ardently? On Saturday, in this putrid city, he and his brother would meet The Cell in a wrestling ring, for the glory of the Tag Team Championship Titles. He held no doubts about the outcome. He knew that Lockdown and Leviathan would fight fiercely for the prize. He knew that they would bring every fiber of their beings to bear against him and his brother. But, in the end, none of that would matter. They would find their struggles fruitless, their efforts wasted. Deimos and Tiffani glided to a halt near Phobos. He could see a concern in their eyes, the same concern that everyone always displayed when Phobos refused to join in pointless frolicking. “You really need to learn to loosen up.” Deimos stated. Phobos cocked his head to the side, with a bored expression. “Are you ready for this match with The Cell?” Phobos asked, his voice monotone. “Meh.” Deimos responded, flippantly. “They’re a good team, but they’ve been thrust together by circumstance. Individually, they’re dangerous. Under Cruz’ leadership, they’re brutally effective. But, as an actual team, they lack the cohesiveness to mount a proper, coordinated offense. Boop-boop-be-doo.” Phobos and Tiffani stared at him for a moment. His expression showed no awareness of his last, strange words. “Look,” Tiffani began. “The two of you are the most dominant tag team in the WWA, the most powerful force in the entire Tag Team Division. You’ve crushed opponent after opponent. You’re three time champions! And now, you’ve got the most dominant female in the WWA, by far, in your corner. I don’t see how you can possibly lose this match.” “Most dominant female. I like the sound of that.” Deimos said, with a smile. “So do I.” Tiffani agreed. “There is no other woman in the whole of the WWA that can begin to compare to me. Just like there is no other tag team in the whole of the WWA that can compare to Havoc. Not even the Veteranz stood a chance.” Somehow, Phobos knew that Tiffani's new found arrogance would be trouble. He sighed heavily. “If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.” Deimos stated. Phobos raised a quizzical eyebrow. “Sun Tzu.” Deimos explained. “We know ourselves. We know what we are capable of. We know our limitations. We also know them. We sat with them at the WWAmmies. We’ve studied their matches, we’ve studied their remarks, and we’ve analyzed them in every way possible. We are as prepared as we can possibly be.” “I suppose you’re right.” Phobos conceded, forcing a half-hearted smile. With a deep breath, he excused himself. “I’ll be at the hotel. I want to get some rest.” “All right.” Deimos said. “Just remember: Life is too important to be taken seriously.” Phobos found the statement strange, contradictory. But then, coming from Deimos, why should he be surprised. He turned, walking away into the cold Ottawa night. From behind him, shouted words echoed through the streets, driving a further chill into the hearts of those who heard it. “CRY HAVOC...!” End.
