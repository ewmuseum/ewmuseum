Se7en - From Grace

27 Apr 2006

By Ember

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Three weeks ago I was confused, momentarily, when I opened my eyes and was surrounded by a sea of moving blue. To start with I didn’t actually recall closing my eyes at all, so to have to open them was a little bit confusing enough but the swirls of blue that enveloped me left me feeling so disconcerted that I felt myself falling. The fact that I was already prone was the only ting that saved me from collapsing but my mind didn’t catch up with the physical reality as quickly as I hoped and so I felt like I was falling even though I never actually moved. “Don’t worry buddy, we are nearly there” “… where?” I replied. At least I would have done if I could, and I was sure that I had, but I realised that the word only echoed within the confines of my own head and that it never actually left my mouth. I took a deep breath, wanting desperately to ask the question once more, but the stab of pain that ripped through my chest brought nothing but an inaudible moan of agony. I couldn’t stop the tears the fell slowly down my cheeks, and wouldn’t have tried to even if I could have; not because I wasn’t ashamed of them and the weakness that they so clearly displayed but simply because at the one moment I was completely and utterly focussed on the pain. And the clarity that it brought with it. Through a haze of tears I stared at the small window above me and the flashes of blue that came through it. The ambulance sirens blared out as it rushed along, and for the first time I felt every bump in the road as the stretcher on which I was strapped bounced subtly in answer to the motion. I watched as the paramedic rearranged some tubes above my head, whatever they were connected to just out of sight, and realised that they led to me. I could feel the jagged metal of the intravenous needle buried deep into my arm and tasted the harsh plastic of the mask that cupped my mouth. A moment of panic was quelled as I felt the familiar softness of my own, much more comfortable, mask. My face was still there, the lie that was underneath it still covered. Still hidden. As long as that was there he wouldn’t see me; he wouldn’t find me. “What’s taking so long, Mike?” The paramedic above me looked away from the tubes and machinery that seemed to be part of my body and shouted up to the front of the ambulance, his eyes tight with concern. “Traffic is a bitch” a disembodied voice replied from out of sight, “but we should be there in less than ten minutes.” “I hope so, I am not happy with his breathing sounds.” Another bump in the road brought another jostle of the stretcher below me, and domino like this action brought its own reaction. Pain. If I could have doubled over I would have but as I tried to control the agony I both cursed and praised the straps that held me still. Another bump and another jostle and I felt as if my whole body left the stretcher completely before I started to fall back towards its tender embrace. Fall back … Fall … I nearly screamed aloud as my boot hit him square in the jaw and couldn’t stop the grin from playing over my mouth as I watched Mal Somers fall like a cut down tree. Unconscious before he hit the ground he never saw it coming and I knew there and then that it was all over. The title was going to stay mine and the little usurper to me throne was dealt with once and for all. First I had broken his mind and then his will. It had been easy, far too easy really, and had just involved showing him what a failure he truly was. I mean talking to a broken belt and giving it a name – Rookie – was bad enough but the real insanity was in thinking that he could stand in the same ring as me … or cage in this instance … let alone beat me. So first his mind and will and then his body. I had beaten him completely. Taking a few deep breaths I stared down at the prone figure below me and realised that three seconds wasn’t enough, it would be over too soon, too fast. I had promised that the match would end with a stretcher just like it had done the year before and of course I would live up to my promise. Pinning Mal wouldn’t be enough, I realised, and so I did what I had always known it would come down to. I climbed the cage. The metaphorical mountain had been climbed, the summit reached and from my lofty perch at the top of the WWA’ss World I surveyed my Kingdom. The thousands and thousands of fans in attendance faded away – their faces blurring into one and their voices muting into nothing – as my mind cleared. I had never felt so alive as I did right then. I could have pinned him, I knew that, just as I could choose to climb down and claim my victory as soon as my boots hit the floor. Mal would awaken to find that I had strolled my way to victory and he would know that he never had a chance. That, though, still wasn’t good enough. Standing completely still at the top of the cage I basked in the glare of the halogen spotlight, smiling as I saw the dust particles dance like angles around my head. I looked down on Mal, so far below; so fragile, so tiny, so beaten. It was always going to come to this, it was fate and who was I to argue with that? I felt like I was burning, I could feel the flames rushing up and down my arms as I raised them beside me and smiled in wonder as I saw wings of fire. Like a burning angel I stood and let the worship surround me and with a deep breath – in the perfect moment – I let gravity take me as I threw myself backwards, Godlike in that split second and knowing that there was no-one better, there would be no-one better, I made my choice. … fell. … fell back. “… back?” The words didn’t make sense, where was I, who was speaking? Had I hit Mal, was it over? “Are you with us, are you back?” Finally I focussed on the clear blue eyes of the paramedic as he leant over me, almost shouting in to my face though his voice sounded so very far away. With a small smile I realised that the pain was fading, my chest feeling like it was moving from me and I felt calm as breathing no longer hurt. I blinked once, confused, and then giggled, not caring about the look of shock on the paramedics face as I realised why it no longer hurt to breathe. Simply because I wasn’t anymore. I saw the darkness reaching out for me, and I looked deep into it, seeing the spark so far below – like a light beckoning me onwards – and I smiled once more. I knew that I had a choice, I could fight it, there was always the fight. I made the choice easily, ignoring the words that I barely heard from the paramedic. “Hurry it up, we are losing him!” The darkness beckoned and I made my choice. I fell. - - - - - One week ago “So, how are you feeling?” It was the same question that I had been asked at this time of the morning every day for the past two weeks. I understood that as I was paying top money for the services of the private medical centre that I was entitled to get top services too but I was sick and tired of it all. I was sick and tired of feeling sick and tired. “Like I have a hairline fracture in my chest” I quipped, not even trying to hide the sarcasm from my tone, “and like someone who was clinically dead for just under a minute this time two weeks ago.” With an impeccable professional attitude the matronly nurse ignored my bad temper and simply filled in the sheet that hung in a folder at the end of my bed. “Well,” she said with a straight face and a mater-of-fact tone, “that would probably be because you do have a hairline fracture in your chest and because you were clinically dead for just under one minute.” Turning in my seat I deliberately turned away from her, ignoring her presence, and stared out through the window into the secluded garden that took up the inner courtyard. It still amazed me that such an oasis of verdant green could exist in the middle of the City, but then again I should have been among the first of people to know what can sometimes be hidden away in plain view. A small bird landed on one of the trees and started trilling away a song of its own design and I tried to lose myself in its meaningless chatter. “Did you hear me?” For a second I actually thought that the bird was speaking to me and then I realised that the high-pitched, annoying, chatter was coming from behind me and not from the garden. With a deliberately obvious sigh I turned around in the chair, wincing slightly as my chest protested at the movement, and fixed the person who had become the bane of my life over the past couple of weeks with an ominous stare – or at least I tried to, but with one raised eyebrow the nurse showed how much my bad mood affected her. “That look, the scary Halloween mask none withstanding, isn’t going to work on me young man,” she stated crisply, eyebrow still raised, “I worked for over a decade on the children’s ward so am used to childish behaviour!” Walking over until she stood directly in front of me, but looming over my seated position, she actually made me feel as small as a child. “As I said though,” she continued, hands on ample hips, “I suppose that you will be glad to be getting home tomorrow?” Home. The word itself brought no real emotion to my mind. The apartment that I had bought in the nicer end of the city was just that, a nice apartment. I didn’t have any emotional attachment to it so I didn’t feel as if I could answer honestly that I was looking forward to going there. I most definitely was looking forward to getting out of the hospital but the only thing that would make me happy – the only place that I considered home – wasn’t that apartment. My home was the ring. “… of course you still have a couple of months before you can return to work,” she said, as if reading my mind, “though how you can consider that to be work is beyond me. I mean look at what it did to you!” I sighed, playing over in my head again the countless times that she had pointed out to me that beating other men up for a living was no way to make a living at all. The damage to my body and the near-death experience had been used, again countless times, as defacto weapons in her war of words against my chosen ‘profession’ and me. “I hope that you are not thinking of doing anything stupid?” She asked, concern evident in her voice, as she took my silence for an unspoken challenge. Which of course it was. “The doctor does know what he is talking about you know, and he has told you just what could happen if you step back in the ring too early …” The bird caught my attention once more, and the nurse’s voice faded away into nothing as I watched it hop from branch to branch, seeming to float effortlessly to the top where it opened its beak and sang out a gleeful little melody. Preening itself it perched at the apex of the tree, as high as it could go, untouched and unequalled. Smiling I realised that there was really only two places it could now go. Up or down. … which meant that there was really only one choice, or in effect no choice at all. - - - - - Now Reaching the backstage area I looked around me and found that I was alone. I could still hear the chaos in the background and chuckled as I thought of all those people in the ring at the same time. A short time ago I would have been in the thick of things, I would have been in the forefront, helping to fan the flames of the chaos even more. Those days were gone, at least for now, though. Instead I had turned up with only one agenda and that was to do something that surprised even me. When I had heard that Mal Somers – the man who had beaten me for my title – was facing Damien Thorne I knew that I would have to be at the arena. Mal was many things, including the current champion, but one thing that he wasn’t was sensible. There was no way on Earth that he would be ready for someone like Thorne. No way at all. Knowing that I realised that I would have to get involved. It was one thing for Mal to have a moment of brilliance inside the cage and pin me for the three count – and no matter the truth that I had ended my title reign with a moment of hubris instead of taking the easy way out he had beaten me – but when he had to come face to face with the devil so soon I knew that he would need my help. I couldn’t stop the smile from appearing on my face as I realised how much confusion I would have caused to Mal – and everyone else – by helping him. It even confused me if I were to be honest. I just knew that Mal had shown something great by going through the Hell I had faced him with and that he deserved more than to get beaten by Thorne. Tracer Bullet’s parting words to me when I beat him for the title, about being true to it and the WWA, had played through my head and I hadn’t been able to shake them off. So I made my way to the arena and I watched the match. Mal did a good job, he tried his best, but the odds were against him. Harley was a factor just as I knew he would be but when the referee sent him to the back I actually thought that Mal would be able to do it, that he would be able to beat Thorne. That is when it happened though. I don’t know how he saw me but Thorne looked right up the rampway and caught my eyes. Alone amongst all the people there he knew that I was there, he knew that I was watching. Without saying a word he let me know that the match wasn’t about Mal, it wasn’t even about the title. It was about me. As he lifted Mal up into the air he kept my gaze locked into his own and no matter how I tried I couldn’t make it in time. I pushed my body as hard as I could and I tried to get there in time, I tried to save the title. … to save Mal. I didn’t. As the belt was handed to Thorne he didn’t even look at it, he just kept staring at me. Staring through me. If I could have told Mal that I was trying to help him would he have believed me? If I had told Mal that it wasn’t ever about him, would he have believed me? Would he have cared? I don’t think that he would. He would have called me crazy, deluded or egotistical. The thing is that he didn’t see the look in Thorne’s eyes, he didn’t hear the words from a few weeks before when Throne spoke. Is it crazy to know what Thorne wants? Is it deluded to know what Thorne needs? Is it egotistical to know that Thorne wants to beat the very best to prove that he not only is worthy of the title but that he is worthy of being the best in the WWA? He wants to beat me, but more than that he went after Mal and took that title simply so that he has the chance to do the one thing that no-one else could. Unmask me. Leaning against the wall I listened to the sounds of fighting in the arena, and tried to laugh.
