The Searcher (Part 3)

22 Jan 2003

Author not credited in the source

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 22 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Private Investigator, Dirk Flint is on the case. He's been hired to find a missing man before this weekend. He has already travelled to New Orleans and Bolivia. Now with only a few days to go he is driving his ‘56 Cadillac down the streets of Brooklyn. In Bolivia he picked up to useful clues, a key with the number 7209 on it and a street map of Brooklyn, with 191 Johan Road clearly marked. Apperantly this is the missing man’s mother’s house. His finger follows the road, one more left turn and he’s there, Johan Road. He turns the corner to be met by a street of quaint little wooden cottages. Flint saunters along the road, looking for number 191.) Flint: 187, 189, 191….! (Flint has stopped between houses 193 and 189 but there is no house 191. It has been replaced with a Starbucks. Flint looks around and all the other number are there but 191 is definitely a Starbucks. Flint frowns and goes inside, he walks up to the manager.) Flint: How long has this been here? Manager: That coffee was made about 3 minute ago. Flint: Not the coffee! Manager: The Muffins? Flint: No, this place! Manager: This establishment? Flint: Yes. Manager: I don’t know, about 4 years? Flint: How long have you worked here? Manager: Since it opened. Are you OK? Flint: I’m looking for the lady who used to live here. Manager: I’m afraid that she’s gone. Flint: I guessed that. Can you tell me where she lives? Manager: I’m afraid not. Flint: Come on, Buddy, tell me. It’s going violate your privacy policy too much is it? Manager: That’s not the issue…. Flint: You mean you don’t have her address? Manager: Well, yes but… Flint: Just give it to me then! Manager: OK, it’s the Westfield Cemetery on 12th Street. Flint: She lives in a cemetery?! Manager: Not exactly lives, no. Flint: Ah, so the broad is dead? Manager: I’m afraid so. Flint: Still, might as well look her up while I’m in town. (The image fades out and comes up on the Caddy parked outside the Cemetery office. Flint walks inside and talks to the Caretaker, a man by the name of Jack Barton.) Flint: So does anyone ever come visit this grave? Barton: Well, everyone who comes in has to sign the visitors book so as we know who’s been in and out. I can check the book for you if you like. Flint: Do that. (Barton pulls out a huge leather bound book and starts leafing through the pages.) Barton: It’s all alphabetised, S…S..here we go. Aha, there have been 3 visits to that particular plot, two from a Mrs Daniels of “no fixed abode” and one from a…oh I can’t quite make out that name. Does that look like your guy’s name? (Flint peers at the book, trying to make sense of the swirling signature.) Flint: You know I think it might be, is there an address with that? Barton: Let’s have a look, why yes, yes there is. Kind of, anyway. It’s says Locker 7203, Grand Central Station. Flint: 7203? Interesting… (The picture cuts to Grand Central Station. Dirk Flint is walking along next a vast wall of lockers. Eventually he reaches number 7203 and takes a key from his pocket. He turns the key in the lock and pulls out a shiny leaflet.) Flint: Gentle Hills, Lincoln, Nebraska. Damn, I hate Nebraska! (Fade To Black)
