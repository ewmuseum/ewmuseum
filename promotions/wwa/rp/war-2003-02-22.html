War

22 Feb 2003

By Nicholas Adams

Item 2 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 22 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
War is the most destructive act that humanity can undertake. It destroys hundreds; thousands; millions of lives. It changes nations, and in certain instances, it can change most of the world. But while these are the widely mentioned problems with war, there is another. War is a constant in the course of human history. Once basics such as spoken language, writing, and trade were handled by earlier civilizations, people suddenly began fighting over control of trade, farmland, and other natural resources. Learning war is much like learning how to talk; though a person may not physically be able to do so, they remember the ability and pass it on to their progeny. It is never forgotten. Over years and years, it becomes modified, somehow more civilized. And yet, it's still too rough and complex to actually accomplish anything but something extremely basic. It never advances beyond face value. This is especially true if the same mistakes are repeated. In fact, war is an example on how the entire world is insane. War is a mistake. People make war time and time again, expecting different results. Nobody ever stops and thinks, "Wait, why are we fighting at all? Is this all we are?" Instead, they just finish one battle and look for the next to come along. Unfortunately for himself, Nicholas Adams is the latter. Then again, that's probably because it's an occupational hazard. Regardless of why his life is just going from one battle to the next, at the moment, he has been able to forget all that. The reason is unknown to anybody who hasn't really been keeping track of the latest Insider shows, but it is still easily assumed that he's happy because he either has his Television title once more, or he has found a woman to make him happy. He then begins to speak. Adams: "Life is full of conflict. That's the essence of it. War is conflict. And maybe...just maybe...I can just fight because it's a job. Maybe I could. But instead, I never will. Blobal Warfare is my time to shine. The Rainmaker will arrive, and I will definitely destroy any and all opposition. Well, whatever opposition remains. After all, I have the TV title. What more can I say? The East Coast will win. That's it." Adams keeps walking without saying another word, taking in the Canadian air and buildings surrounding him. It hits him that it's like home, but he says nothing. He just keeps walking, his footsteps falling like singular echoes in the air. FADE OUT
