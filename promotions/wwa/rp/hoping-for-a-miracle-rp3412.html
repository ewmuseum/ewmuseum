Hoping For A Miracle

22 Dec 2006

By The Butler

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was the night before Christmas, nothing was stirring except for a small African boy who could not possibly be anymore excited. He was roughly eight years old, with a chubby ebony face and long black hair that in later years his mother would state made him look like a hippy. Knowing that his mother and father would be asleep he crept down the stairs, wondering what wonders would lie under the Christmas tree. He was an incredibly lucky child, though he did not appreciate how much so at the time. He happened to belong to a family that was fairly well off, as opposed to living in poverty like the majority of his race. Their financial status did not come without it’s trials though, as his father had to work day and night to be able to afford such luxuries for his family. It was for that very reason that the young boy was able to pick such expensive presents to put on his Christmas list, and he was soon to be ever so grateful. Suddenly, he heard a noise. It had come from the living room, where the tree was standing, and he wondered if the burglars had returned. He held his breath, picked up his mother’s mop and sneakily climbed down the stairs. He felt his heart racing as he reached out for the handle and turned it to open the door. He pushed through and was astounded by what he saw. Father Christmas was in his living room! The young boy charged towards the man dressed in red and threw his arms around him. He hugged Santa for all his wealth, his face being buried in the long white beard. However, something was very wrong, Mr. Claus was not returning the embrace. Confused the young boy released his grip and watching the jolly old man place his hand over his heart, his face scrunching up in agony. And just like that, Father Christmas had a heart attack. The little boy screamed and his mother and father raced down the stairs to check on their little boy. They saw Father Christmas laying on the floor, unmoving and immediately the father rushed out of the house to fetch the local doctor. An hour later, the illusion of Father Christmas was dead, and so was the little boy’s grandfather. The Butler wiped a tear from his eye as he gazed at the tall Christmas tree which stood in the square. He had closed his shop, The Magic Box, early for the day so that he could prepare for his upcoming match at the Birthday Bash. Christmas Eve, the big one. The Butler turned away from the tree and walked home. For the first time since his decision to enter his master into the Brawl, his mind turned to something other than the match. He thought of his grandfather, the great warm hearted man that had told him stories of Knights and Dragons, of Cowboys and Indians and of war time experiences. The man who died pretending to be someone else. The Butler had never celebrated Christmas since that fateful day, he had no reason to do so, and he wondered if he would have reason to celebrate on Christmas day in the year 2006. The day after. He no longer believed in Christmas miracles. He had grown up a lot since that day his grandfather died, he had seen the reality of the world from a very young age and had learned to accept it. But, as his grandfather had often told him, where there was life there was always hope. Hope. The one emotion that everyone in the Brawl would no doubt have, hope to win titles and to become contenders. The hope to be called the best in the world, the hope to make an impact, the hope for one’s best friend to return to the world. Where there was hope, there was will. Where there was will, there was fight. Where there was fight, there was reason. Where there was reason, God allowed miracles. For the first time since the age of eight, the Butler would hope, would prey, would fight would dream of, and perhaps, just this once, God would allow the first miracle in over two thousand years.
