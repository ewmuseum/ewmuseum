Thank god he's busy

25 Feb 2003

By The Other Guy

Item 2 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 25 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
|The scene opens inside of a workout room, where machines, benches, tredmills and other various equipment of shape, design and use exist for men to excell in their physical states. Lying on his back, shirtless with only a pair of shoes to cover his skin besides a pair of plain shorts, is a rather ordinary man. At a regular pace he presses the barbell in his hands up and lets it down. There is maybe one hundred and fifty pounds on the bar, two hundred at most. A clatter of metal on metal accompanies the bar as it is set in the cradles and the athlete sits up, sweat beaded on his body as if he'd just interrupted a powerful excersize routine.| Man: It is I, John Doe, once again. After my prolonged absence I am sure many of you are wondering where I stand in things. |His voice draws on, emotionlessly and gratingly slowly.| Damn, what was his name?: In such an absence as mine I am sure I am on the tongues of all the men of the WWA as they wonder what would cause such silence. I can already hear the whispers of men who seek to solve the riddle for themselves. I hear the unasked questions that will soon be asked of all of you. 'Where did he go?' and 'Why did he go?' and 'What has he got planned?'. I hear these questions follow me through a room like a camera. Everyone wants to know. Everyone needs to know. Everyone runs around, begging to know. I understand it is the responsibility of the famous to take care for the worries of others, but this fame is too much even for me to handle. To have others need to know my life as if what I ate for breakfast would change the world forever, why it is almost too much for me to understand. |The man wipes his forehead with his forearm, though by this time most of the sweat has long since left his face. After all, a man doesn't sweat forever, and with as long as he took to speak these few words, there was more than enough time to see all the sweating stop. He stands and crosses to a bench.| Um... him: The truth is that I have been negotiating certain points on my WWA contract. You see, in spite of the desires of everyone to know what is on my mind, the WWA has failed to send to me a cameraman for some weeks. Worse, now that one has been sent, they have forgotten with it an interviewer. But like the charismatic man I am, I can continue in spite of such mistakes. |The man lifts and sips a bottle of water.| That guy on the screen: So now I shall tell you what I have been about in these past few weeks. I have been seeking the WWA's office to finally book me in the guaranteed matches. How could they forget such a thing you ask? I do not entirely know, something about lost paperwork and misfiling on the part of the lesser staff. But that time is past, as is the forgetting of the WWA, for I have solved the problems myself. |The guy smiles, a normal smile that doesn't do anything for the averageness of him.| He's a wrestler, right?: I suggested to the office that I wrestle with someone large, someone like Tracer Bullet. They suggested the White Ninja. I suggested Jackson Kraven, and they responded with Joey Edwards. I asked for Kash, they offered me some immemorable guy, a man so pathetic the WWA themselves could not recall his name. My interest was piqued by the last offer, because the short file on the man showed him to be a good match for a man of my calibur. We were not only about the same size, but we shared similar styles of wrestling. But they were unsure of who this man was, and said they would find out so the two of us might be booked in a match were it possible. |He stands, his averageness showing in the lacking definition of his muscles.| The man needs a nametag: I understand that not all wrestlers can have a reputation of great esteem. If every wrestler was famous, a headliner, then there would be no headliners among them. And I know that among those underdogs, there are those few gifted souls whose abilities in the ring far, far exceed their skills in the interview. I understand and accept that these souls are destined to, in spite of their great skills, eternal anonymity. But once all wrestlers were nobodies, and so I have no problem granting these men a chance to prove themselves. Everyone needs to get that opportunity, the chance to fight someone of renown so that they may reach great heights themselves. I gladly offer this chance to whom the WWA plans now to book with me, for with such short notice they can hardly be expected to produce Tracer Bullet or Denrol. |The man throws on a shirt and prepares to leave the room.| It's not like nametags are a bad thing: So the WWA can send me their sheep, and I shall accept it for the time, but soon everyone will not only know the name of John Doe, they shall fear seeing it across from their own in the listings. |Thankfully the man leaves.|
