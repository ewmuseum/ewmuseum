BSB #5: Surviving The Suffering

4 May 2006

By Danny Vista

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
How the hell did it happen? He stood in the middle of Times Square, watching as the chaos unfolded around him. He could do nothing to stop it, and yet he knew that he had done everything he could to cause it. It was a strange feeling when you knew that the world was coming to an abrupt stop, yet at the same time you realised that you couldn’t do anything about it. It wasn’t so much fear as it was disappointment. The buildings around him were collapsing under their own pressure, crumbling to the ground whilst sending debris everywhere, but for some unknown reason, it was all missing him. The billowing clouds of smoke surrounded him and, whilst everybody else was on the floor choking, he stood alone unaffected, watching as everybody else suffered at his own feet. Woman and children were screaming for help and for their fathers to come to them. He didn’t realise just how much trouble he had created for himself, and he certainly didn’t realise how it would affect everybody else. He could do nothing to stop it, and so began to pinch himself on the arm, but to no avail. You could hear, and almost feel, the vibrations from the helicopters hovering above the destruction as three huge buildings to his left collapsed in one big heap, causing the ground to shake violently, finally sending him to his knees. At this moment, he remembered a piece of music he had listened to once entitled ‘O Verona’, and the music seemed to match this scene. He felt guilty, but he couldn’t get away from the stirred trouble, and so contemplated by burying his head into the palms of his hands. He squinted his eyes; the dust was even starting to get to him. What would everybody on the outside think? The most famous ‘block’ in the United States of America was suddenly buckling under its own pressure, but he knew it would be portrayed as a terrorist attack. However, he didn’t have a guilty conscience for no reason. He knew that he had caused it. He couldn’t remember how, but it was definitely his fault. Somehow, he had done something that had set off a chain reaction and collapsed the entire area. He finally mustered up the courage to look up from his hands, and saw the rescue helicopters throwing down ropes for anybody to grab onto. If they saved just a few lives, then they considered it a success, given the dangerous circumstances. Something else he noticed frightened him even more. The clouds of smoke surrounding him momentarily made him think that the Bible prophecy had been fulfilled. He thought that the sky was falling down on Earth, signalling the end was nigh. It was a scary thought: so scary in fact that he couldn’t bear to watch any longer. He gulped, rising back to his feet no matter how much the ground was vibrating eerily similar to that of an earthquake. If he was to survive this, he couldn’t just kneel down and pray. He had to do something to get away. But, in a moment’s confusion, he began wondering if it was worth it: he contemplated the possibility of ending it as quick as possible by standing directly next to the large building on his left; a building which was sure to collapse next. As the tear of relief dripped down the side of his face, he took the first few tentative steps towards his freedom. Towards the end. He reached the building and, as if by some sort of magic, he looked up into the air and saw the faint signs that it was starting to crash and burn. He didn’t want to see it, so he simply closed his eyes and waited for the end. But, although his eyelids were being ripped away, his eyes suddenly opened and in front of him he saw a figure form from the clouds of smoke surrounding him. “Danny…” He took a deep look at the figure, initially not recognising who it was. But, as he squinted and tried to realise just who the figure was, he was shocked. “Get out, Danny…” Get out? How could he do such a thing? He was in the middle of dozens of collapsing skyscrapers, and to him it was just unlucky that he hadn’t been hit fatally yet. With the figure distraction not working, he closed his eyes and held out his arms as he heard (and felt) the vibrations getting closer. He knew that, as the Bible had predicted, the end was nigh. “Get out…” --------------- He growled. It used to be about succeeding exceedingly, but now it was just a matter of getting through it without being hurt and/or even killed. Vista was now a man of slight experience. He’s bruised men, cut men, injured men and, on a few rare occasions, had even shattered their bones. But, though he had a minor bit of expertise in that area, even he had never been in such a dangerous match as he was about to be. He’d been getting metaphorical dreams as he slept for the past few weeks, and of course by now he was getting rather sick of them. But, out of all his dreams, he knew that this would be the one to stick with him as he went on the street and as he came off. And, unlike with the other dreams, this one had seemed so much more real. Inside the dream he had felt himself getting a lot more involved into the situation, and then to top it off he had woken up with sweat all over, dripping down his body as if a bucket of water had been poured over his bed. It was now approximately 8 hours before the brawl began, and it was around this time that everybody, including Danny Vista, started to feel the butterflies floating in his stomach, patiently waiting to get the better of him. If I can do this, I know that I’m worthy of my title. He sat back on his leather chair, remote in hand as he flicked on the Sky Sports channel, only to see a build up show to the brawl. It showed Dave Harley’s victory over Jonathan Winters at Meltdown last week, touting both of them as potential victors of the brawl. It showed clips of Khaled breaking Jonathan Winters’ undefeated streak by ‘cheating’, building up the suspense of the US title match. But, most surprisingly to Vista, they showed clips of Danny Vista’s different victories, touting him as an underdog for the brawl and somebody to look out for. This shocked Vista, but it certainly made him a lot more confident. Maybe I can do this. Maybe it is actually possible. But, moments later, Vista sank back into his chair in disappointment, but to anybody around him it would be somewhat confusing as to why this is. His emotions were coming from his mind at that moment, and of course nobody else was able to delve into there. But ‘maybe’ is never good enough. And, unexpectedly to him anyway, he began to slowly realise the significance of the dream he had remembered. The brawl. It was the similarities between the dream and the brawl that scared him, and it soon became obvious to him just what this dream represented. The chaos was engulfing him in both situations. Tearing away at his mind and body, just waiting for the opportunity to suck him in and then deliver a blow. He eventually gave in, sincerely wanting to just get out of the disasters that were unfolding around him. And then without warning it hit, and he was gone. ------ He approached the street with a certain aspect of hesitance; after all, who would want to visit the place where he would no doubt end up being bruised, bloodied and battered to shreds. But Laura had said it was a good idea, and of course he simply couldn’t say no to her. From the top of the street, the set scene looked surprisingly similar to that of a Texan shootout movie. It was the site of the future battle between men that would be horrendously dangerous. He could picture the stereotypical ball of tumbleweed floating by, and he could picture one of his fellow competitors standing at the other end of the street he could barely see from here, flexing their knuckles so as to prepare for the fight as Vista did the same. In any other sort of fight, Vista would try and be as sportsmanlike as possible. That means no lying, no cheating and no double crossing. But this was a street fight. And street fights had different set of rules to that of a wrestling ring. You had to be twice as aggressive, twice as determined, twice as patient and, perhaps most importantly of all, twice as ruthless. He could try and be a good guy as much as he wanted, but even he knew when he had to drop that act and simply do his wrestling thing. He walked slowly from the top end of the street to the bottom. Looking around, he could clearly see everything and anything that could possibly be used to his advantage: to Vista, early analysis of the battlefield was vital in order to try and get as much as possible out of it. He stood still for a moment, listening to the sheer, loud silence that stood before him as he looked around. He didn’t particularly want to be here; it was all her idea. She felt it would give him a knowledgeable advantage against the others, but Vista knew that, in a brawl like this, there’s no room for tactics. It’s just a fight. He looked at the dirty state the street was in. Was this from prior brawls that had taken place here, or was it simply the citizens’ fault? Either way, after Sunday, it was going to be even worse. He wished he could prevent it, but he couldn’t do it: nobody could. And that’s all there was to it. And now, in the same fashion that he had been forced to come to this wretched battlefield, he now had to go. Just as he leant against a metal fence located towards the bottom of the road he felt a vibration in his pocket, and he knew it was Laura telling him it was about time he got back.
