Nearly Normal *BSB*

1 Jun 2004

By Quietus

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
When you never know what the weather’s going to do next, it’s best to make use of the sun, or at least, the warmth. Otherwise you just end up cursing the rain when it comes, and cursing yourself for letting the rain come and destroy that day you had such great plans for. While Quietus wouldn’t go to such drastic lengths as to curse himself – always finding someone else to blame – he still had the same idea. Which explained why he lay out on the roof of his car – a five-series silver rented BMW – taking the term of “sun roof” to a new level. Ever the black sheep or dark horse, he appreciated the dull sky and moderate breezes. Weather like this put him in a good mood because he knew that behind everyone else’s happy faces was a rage that, in the middle of summer, the sun had to go on walk-about. He wasn’t that odd; he liked the sun. But he idolised the bringer of all things bad [for others]; whatever or whoever it was. And today was no different in the steady, no day’s different life of Quietus. The wind blew through his hair every now and then and tried to wangle its way in through his leather jacket, just to make him feel cold and let him know who was in charge. In retaliation, he’d zip the jacket up that little bit more, just to show it that no force could outwit the myth that had come to flesh – Ross Irwin II. Usually when you lie on top of a car, wearing shades to cover your eyes from the grey sky, you’re susceptible to sniggers, insults and general raised eyebrows. The art of Quietus’ existence wasn’t that he was any better than anyone else – he just simply talked you into thinking that. No theologian too intelligent, no bum too pitiful; if he thought you were laughing, or even looking, he’d all but kill you with words. The odd passer-by did in fact give him an odd stare or glance while walking speedily by, but it was still working hours, so many were hanging out in offices still, oblivious to Quietus’ idiocy which in turn, through some logic that no one but he could work out, led to his supremacy. These were working hours for Quietus as well. But when others stared at paperwork all day; Quietus shouted insults at people. When others were driving from town to town selling a new toothpaste; Quietus sniggered at people. And Quietus made more money than most of them. He could eat in restaurants they could only look at. He could drink in bars they had only heard stories about. And if he found people as intelligent, as witty, as interesting, and as dismissive as he himself, he would probably do those things. But when you mould yourself on perfection – nothing else can cut it. The footsteps of a man in his mid-thirties, clean shaven and dressed quite smartly, didn’t quite catch Quietus’ attention. It wasn’t that they weren’t loud enough, but Quietus clung to the belief that if you look at people long enough, in whatever way, they may get the idea you respect them. And so he remained in his position, facing upwards to the sky, chewing on gum that had long since lost its taste. When the footsteps stopped right in front of his car, he still didn’t look up. And for a split second, though it seemed like an eternity and a half, there was absolute silence. The man, reaching into his pocket, darted his eyes from Quietus to the car, back to Quietus, and then around the street. The wind shook several beech trees above him and the Australian, and other than that, all was quiet. “Excuse me?" The man mustered the courage and determination to break the silence. Yes, it was more awkward that he had imagined; especially due to the fact that a six foot six inch man who had earned the reputation of “the Heartless” was lying on top of a vehicle, presumably his own. “Yeah?” Quietus responded, not turning around, not moving at all. “My name is Mart Nixon, and I’m a –“ “Mart?” There was dead silence again, as Quietus finally turned around onto his stomach, facing down at Nixon with a goofy smile. “Did your parents let you choose your own name?” Mart took a deep breath and said nothing. By no means was he a small individual. At thirty-four, he was still in relatively impeccable condition. He’d travelled far and wide to find this guy, and there was no way he was going to walk away having simply got insults out of him. “My name is Mart Nixon,” he began again, through clenched teeth “and I’m a sports agent. I spent a lot of my time in Europe and was an agent to some top football and swimming stars. I’ve decided to concentrate on something new, know what I mean, and –“ “And you want an autograph to sell so you can feed your kids?" The agent glared up at Quietus, waiting for the big man to either break out in laughter or apologise for interrupting with something so personally disrespectful. Neither came. Instead, the Devil’s Own member lay, arms wide open, waiting for something else to be said. “I’ve decided, after extensive training, to advertise solely as a wrestling agent now. My goal isn’t to go after the stars, but those who are only beginning to shine.” It was Quietus’ turn to glare. “And why are you here, in that case?” Nixon was becoming increasingly pissed off with Quietus’ disrespect, and so he blurted out his last sentence without fully realising what an ignorantly proud individual this wrestler was. He cleared his throat in answer to the question, and rubbed his palms together. “For example, I could find raw talent, and mould them into someone that’s camera friendly, has a good relationship with the media, will know the right choices, and can get the best deals...” “Why are you here ?” Quietus repeated; the smile no longer on his face, and certainly not in his eyes. “Well, you’re still new; you’re not – let’s face it – the most popular wrestler to ever get into the WWA, are you? Plus, I’d bet they drew your contract up half-heartedly with more discrepancies than there should be, simply because you’re a rookie.” If a bomb had gone off at the wall near the end of the cul-de-sac, Quietus wouldn’t have heard it. If it had started lashing out of the heavens, Quietus wouldn’t have felt it. If the car had begun to roll down the road with him still lying on it, Quietus wouldn’t have noticed. He usually would smile when people were scowling at him, or insulting him, or giving out in general – but for the first time in as long as he could remember – he was speechless. “Let me ask you something, Nix,” he finally managed to blurt out, “when was the last time you had a client?” “Well, I, uh, I’ve been researching for the past few months, so that wouldn’t be a fair or accurate fact...” “I’ll let you in on a little secret, if you promise to keep it to yourself, yeah? I’m bigger, stronger and certainly smarter than you, so take heed. Never try to talk to an asshole who doesn’t care if your name’s Frank or Zulu. Because if you do, you’ll more than likely get your head bitten off. Now, if you don’t mind, or, even if you do, shoo. I’m sunbathing, minus the sun, or the bathing.” Mart, who had handled himself quite laudably up to that point, wasn’t satisfied with simply walking away. He’d flown from Warsaw, non-stop, all the way to Kennedy Airport, to find out his future client, was in another damn state. Walking away now would not only mean defeat, it would also mean he had wasted a day and a half in pursuit of a person who didn’t deserve to be in flesh. And Mart, not one to back down from a verbal confrontation, wasn’t going to settle for that. “You could be the next thing, Ross.” Quietus looked up, a little bemused at the fact that ol’ Mart was still hanging around. Quietus turned back on his back and looked up at the sky. “Who says I’m not already?” Nixon played a different sort of card, really running out of things. “Well, you could be ‘it’. Listen, I’m sure you know there’s a Brawl coming up next week and while you’re not in it, you could scout the talent to see what you could be up against and take it from –“ “Listen to me, Mar. Tracer Bullet reckons three goofs who between ‘em don’t comprehend that walking into walls isn’t good for them, are the best the LDA has. You know what that’s called? It’s called lack of awareness. I don’t know, maybe he’s so old he’s suffering from Alzheimer’s and doesn’t know what’s going on anymore. But if he thinks I’m gunna give him the satisfaction of watching that Brawl from a television set somewhere, to, as you stupidly suggested “scout the talent”, he’s older than I thought.” “Why not try to take one of X-Corp out? Like Mal this evening? Beat him up real bad and he’ll be forced to withdraw? Maybe you could get into the match that way...” “You don’t get it, Nix; I don’t want to be in the match. It’s ridiculous getting all sweaty in swimming trunks fighting people on a street.” “You did it before in a Mall, and you won a Title from it.” Quietus turned around again on the car. Mar had done some research! But he didn’t flinch while turning. The last thing he wanted was for Mart Nixon to think that Quietus was impressed by his fat head and slick voice. “That was different; that was when I was a small guy in a group of sharks. It was the only way to prove a point that I was...” His voice trailed off. Not for the first time in their confrontation, there was dead silence. Mart almost shouted at Quietus to finish his sentence, but thought better of it. He seemed to be getting more out of Quietus that any trained psychologist or interrogator could. “...better,” Quietus concluded, a faraway look spread over his face. He nodded to himself, forgetting that there was someone beside him who had triggered such a thought. If there was one thing in the whole world that Quietus loved, it was proving himself to be better so that he could laugh at everyone else for falling short. Mart had touched a soft spot; Quietus was nearly normal. Nearly.
