Tense, Ian. 12th September 1978 – 1st June 2003. R.I.P

26 May 2003

By Kash

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Born in the waning years of the 1970’s, Ian Tense was always an intelligent man. He was also a great athlete, an NCAA All-American at 197lb class, and a former WWA TV, US and World Champion. A thinker, a player, the man was capable of amazing genius and cunning. He was part of a group known as The Fraternity with Troy Walcott, another WWA legend. Tense quickly gained the TV title, and within a short time had progressed to the United States title by beating Shane Dorian. Using this title to propel him to the higher echelons of the sport, he eventually captured the WWA World Title in the Birthday Brawl of 2002. He held that belt to his death. The night that his life was taken was a sad day for the wrestling world. Too often have the good died young, and this was no exception. The leader of a group of men known as Devils Own, Tense was on top of the wrestling world. The reigning WWA World Champion, and he was about to defend that title in a standard one-on-one encounter. It was not to be so standard. After a long fought battle, where both men traded blood and sweat, they reached the zenith. Both men trading blows and neither were budging an inch. Devils Own had gotten involved, but their interference had done nothing to aid Tense. He was in the fight of his career. Tense achieved such heights by using a move called the Ian-Tensifier. A move invented in Mexico by Gory Guerrero, it is highly dangerous, involving dropping men directly on their heads. Tense had become the master of this move, and had used it to such great success that it guaranteed victory for Tense every time he could apply it. The very moment that Tense passed from this world was a moment at the hands of his own move. After applying the Ian-Tensifer to Kash, Kash had managed to reverse the hold. Tense was fighting back till the time Kash walked up the turnbuckles, Tense still trapped in his own move. With a scream that could be described as a death knell, Kash leapt from the top rope to the arena floor and drove Ian through the announce table. That was the moment that Tense lost more than his title. The prone body of Tense folded in half at the end of that match, and the wrestling world lost another young legend. Tense is survived by a family in Nashville. The WWA extends the greatest condolences to the family of this great athlete.
