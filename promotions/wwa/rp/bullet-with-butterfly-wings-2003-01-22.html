Bullet with Butterfly Wings

22 Jan 2003

By Grunge

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 22 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
FADE IN (The loud noises of Smashing Pumpkins "Bullet with Butterfly Wings" echos throughout the empty green room backstage. We pull into Grunge who is sitting near a CD player. He looks up at the camera and smiles.) Grunge-Did I listen to pop music because I was miserable or was I miserable because I listened to pop music? (Grunge hits the off switch on the CD player. The silence is deafening. Grunge pauses before continuing.) Grunge-As you can see Payne your not the only one who can quote movies. For those who don't know that's from the book and movie High Fidelity. A very good book and excellent movie although they did change the setting from Britian to Chicago. There's always differences between books and their movies. The point. The point is music. There is something inherently captivating about a man or a woman cutting open emotional wounds that have long been scarred over and putting them to disc. Every neauance of the music, every strain of the voice. (Grunge looks intense as he states this.) Grunge-I, like most people who share a similar disposition to mine, can chart major life events through a steady progression of albums, cassettes, and compact discs. The first record that i ever purchased on my own, the first one that really allowed me to see how emotionally powerful modern music can be was the The Joshua Tree by U2. I saved my allowance and bought the cassette shortly after it came out in the summer of 1987. Eventually i bought the cd. This was the record that taught me that people can sing beautifully of pain, love, joy and life. This is obviously a great deal with what has to do with my life but what does it have to do with wrestling? (Grunge laughs.) Grunge-I'm getting to that. Music is a soundtrack to life. I hope your paying attention to this Payne. You too Nick, Shaman. (Grunge picks up the CD, he opens and read the words.) Grunge-"Despite all my rage I'm still just a rat in a cage." That's going to be all of us Sunday Feburary Second. It's the song that will define our encounter. The line about Rage fits. I can't stand you Payne. Neither can anybody else who will be in there with us. You have what's mine. What should be mine. (Grunge closes down for a moment before continuing.) Grunge-(Yells) It should be mine damn you! (A voice comes from off screen.) Mike-Well what about Shaman and Nick Adams. I'm sure they think the same thing. (Grunge looks up from his thinking.) Grunge-Adams, you feel you were robbed out of your title. I can respect that. Shaman you've been gone for awhile after the loss of the Horseman. What better way to work your way back to the top with a win at the biggest pay-per-view of the year. We all want that title. The difference is that I NEED that title. (Grunge is intense. ) Grunge-I'm listening to this song right now, unfolding deeper meanings within the song, and thus within me. Am i trying to become inspired? Am I trying to find a key to victory, a key to understanding my state and I way to beat all of you? (Grunge cracks a smile again.) Grunge-No. I'm already inspired. Inspired to win. It's about evolving through the art that we create in that ring. It's about dealing with my emotions. It's about dealing with my fears of that cage. (Grunge places the headphones over his ears. He breathes in.) Grunge-All i can do is remember to breath, sit, and listen...headphones on. Lights out. The music washing over me in an ebb through this dark room as I drift away. My headphones still clamped to my head. (Grunge closes his eyes and breathes in.) Grunge-The music fading out. (Grunge hits play on the boombox.) Grunge- My dreams fading in...
