*BSB* Demolish The Built

4 Jun 2004

By Quietus

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The heavy trudge of footsteps over a gravel surface opened the scene before the camera can come to life. As it finally did, two sets of feet are shown, one in a pair of hiking boots, the other in a much smarter pair of going-to-mass shoes. The camera moved up to show us the smiling face of Quietus, beside a man who can be described only as a moderate slob, sporting a sweat-covered tattered t-shirt, faded beyond a distinguishable colour, and navy jeans, having probably been bought twenty years prior. Quietus, in stark contrast, with his leather jacket, gelled back tidy hair and shades, looked like a pillar of contentment, as if the loss at the hands of Mal Somers was now a million miles behind him. It wasn’t even close; but that was for another day, or at least, another thinking period. With the sun shining down on them in punishment, the two come to the silent and not-discussed agreement of retiring at a nearby bench. Quietus took one side, and with a grunt, his comrade took the other so that they would face each other. The Australian continued to munch away on the gum, grinning at the camera every now and then. The other man, however, looked nervous around the filming, and continuously moved back and forth on the old bench, never focussing on the lens, avoiding it like the plague. With a sigh that had its own story, Quietus pushed a hand through his hair, contemplating things, oblivious now to the man’s presence. Mal Somers may have beaten him and retained his Intercontinental Title, but Quietus was under the impression that it was Mal who came out worse from the match; Bona-Time only helping matters. With a bit of luck, ol’ Mal wouldn’t be up to the Bourbon Brawl, and who better to replace him than his old pal Quietus? Always looking out for the health and safety of others. What was really getting to him was the chance of Mal actually making it to the Brawl. If that was to be the case, how the hell could he enter? On last consultation, unless invited, a representative of one federation could not simply enter another. He’d have to talk to Andy later, just to sort things out. Finally he turned back to the man, who was by now a nervous wreck, digging into his fist with his front two teeth like a dog gnawing a bone. Realisation that Quietus was looking at him does not come immediately, and when it does, he seems somewhat embarrassed by the situation. Instead of doing something noble like trying to calm the poor guy down or let him know how to handle things, Quietus just shakes his head and giggles loudly and distinctly. The man says nothing, but frowns. The wind brought a sense of compromise between hot and cold, though, with one glance at the man’s t-shirt, one could have sworn it was desert-like conditions. The man, burly, in an overweight sense, began coughing and spluttering and gasping, as if he were about to die. With the commotion, which even saw him holding his chest for a moment, Quietus began to whistle. The whistling was completely off any logical tune, but it told the man that whatever was wrong with him, the man in his company didn’t care. And so the coughing ceased, thankfully with the man’s life still intact. “Welcome to another LDA interview, with your interviewer, the man you love, Quietus.” He smiled at the camera stupidly like any amateur interviewer would. Just as quickly he held up a fake microphone, moving it from his lips to the man’s, and then back to his own. This seemed wonderfully hilarious in Quietus’ own head, though the man didn’t risk returning the laughter, for fear of keeling over and dying. “Beside me today, on this very special interview, is building contractor...” Quietus’ voice trails off, waiting for a prompt for the man’s name. The man seems more concerned with his own condition than any stupid interview, but as Quietus slammed his fist onto the bench, he looked up, almost in despair. “My name’s Charlie Inglewood.” “Building contractor Charlie Inglewood, is that right?” “Yes sir.” “Tell me, Charles, if that’s all right for me to call you that –“ “Actually I prefer -“ “Excellent. So Charles, tell me, as well as, of course, the whole LDA audience from around the globe watching this very special interview on their black and white televisions, what your job entails.” The man hesitated, put out by Quietus’ forwardness and sarcasm. He looked up at Quietus, he now waiting for a prompt of any sort. His terrible chest condition was now long forgotten about. He frowned, almost pleading in his eyes for something, before sitting back and shooting a nervous glance at the camera. “Well, Mr. Irwin – “ “Mr. Irwin, the second.” “Right. As a buildin’ contractor, I supervise not only the buildin’ of houses, but also the demolition of the stated. For example, a good client may sign my company up to destroy their old house they got in a will or somethin’, and give us details of a new mansion they want. Back in the day I used to be amongst my men workin’, but then I guess – “ “McDonald’s came to town, Mr. Inglewood?” Before the man could respond with anything whatsoever, Quietus unzipped his leather jacket to reveal a full three-piece suit underneath, looking like a real rookie interviewer. He flashes another fake, but meaningfully fake smile at the camera, before looking back down at his imaginary notes and holding up his imaginary microphone. “And can you describe to us the feeling – damn it, the rush – of knocking a house down to the ground? Can you do that, Mr. Inglewood? Oh, can you?” “Well, sure, I guess. The, uh, the first thing you gotta do is make sure you have clearance and all. This part of town don’t like places just goin’ down, ya know? Tonnes of memories go down with it, so we gotta keep folk happy. So that usually takes some time with the local higher-ups or whatever you wanna call ‘em. Then, we gotta decide what equipment is needed. Now, usually what I do is – “ “The demolition, Mr. Inglewood?” Poor Charles Inglewood apparently didn’t catch on to Quietus’ sarcasm and blatant disregard for his reputation and name, or if he did, he certainly could take it on the chin. With another roar of a cough, he pulled himself into the seat, nodded apologetically at the ever-smiling, ever-nodding Quietus, and seemed to relax somewhat. This wasn’t so bad after all. Hurry up, you fat bastard, Quietus almost shouted. “Uh huh, the demolition. Okay, sure. It really is as straight-forward as it sounds. You get in a few hard-hittin’ pieces of steal, and they’ll go through a house so quick most folk wouldn’t have the chance to say goodbye to the ol’ place.” Quietus nodded, before completely turning his back on Inglewood, facing the camera, while blocking the man out. “They wouldn’t even have a chance to say goodbye. It’s always the way, isn’t it? In all walks of life, brain-deads, you’ll come across the great things that just disappear in the blink of an eye without warning and without returning. Like Charlie so...tastefully...said, it happens so quickly and so suddenly, that most will be so busy doing something else, they’ll miss the last moments. You know, there are careers that are just like what have been described. Brilliant, still promising, steady; knocked down in an instant and demolished for good. If something’s broken, it’s easier to replace it than try to build it again, right Charlie?” Charles spluttered, taken aback by the interest in him again. “Well, yes sir. We never use the same stuff we use from the ol’ place if we’re buildin’ somethin’ from scratch. If you’re gunna make somethin’ new, you’re gunna make it with new stuff. At least that’s what I do.” The man’s attempt at humour to break the seriousness of the situation from his position failed to work. Quietus glared at him, delighted to humiliate a local so carelessly and without doing an awful lot. “Of course, why would you use the same stuff? Why would anyone? If something’s able to be felled, surely it’s not good enough to be built back up again. Let’s say - theoretically speaking - that the WWA lost one of its top stars in the morning. Say, just for the hell of it, he was eaten by thorn bushes, okay? The thorn bushes were real bad, and they broke his spine or something, yeah? Everyone okay? So, would the blood-suckers – excuse me, the WWA board of directors – look for two wooden legs and a new spine and stick them onto the guy? If you’re still awake; I’m sure you’ve come to the conclusion of; of course not. They’ll search the streets for another yabo to take over and become the next pin-up boy. As my old man always said, well says, since apparently, he survived his last heart attack; never take anything for granted. Not your career, not your money, not your talent, not your place in a certain Brawl that’s on everyone’s lips. Because you’ll never know when it’s going to be taken away from you, legally, of course.” Quietus smirked, and turned back around to Inglewood, the building contractor. His nerves seemed to be under some sort of control at this point, though he was still edgy when Quietus leaned forward and stuck the fake microphone in between the two of them. “And tell us, Mr. Inglewood, the joy you feel when a house has been built and you see a brand new family moving in to live happily ever after – until divorce?” “I don’t think about it too much, mind you, but I know this one great couple, live couple miles east from me. My company built a new house for ‘em way out in the country a few years back, and I still go out every now and then to make sure they’re enjoyin’ themselves and everythin’s okay. It sure is nice to see such fine people enjoying your work, your creation I guess.” Quietus spun back around again, the fake nodding ceasing and the invisible microphone disappearing out of his hands. “There’s no greater gift than giving someone a start. There’s nothing more someone could do for someone else than shove them onto their path by sacrificing their own trip around. While I don’t expect favours from anyone, since, for some odd reason, not many people seem to like me; I do expect the odd person to call in with back trouble, or the case of the runs, or something along those lines. And when you do, and yeah, I said when, they’ll be others – me – just waiting to jump on board and take the last seat. Your seat. So strap in tightly, hold on to the bars in front of you for dear life, because this rollercoaster’s going up and down and around and around until someone falls off, so I can jump on and have me some fun.” With a nod to Inglewood in confirmation that his torture has ended, Quietus got to his feet, groaning and stretching loudly, for the North Pole inhabitants to hear. Charlie stood up in pursuit, looking awkward as he approached Quietus. “Just wonderin’ sir, what all that was about? You weren’t real specific earlier.” Quietus nodded his head, but held up his cell phone, walking off away from the building contractor slowly. “I’ll be with you in a second,” he offered, leaving out the ‘fat man’ part in case some people got onto to Bridge or whoever about slander. The camera faded out with images of Quietus on his cell phone, fading to static, but with the audio still on for everyone to hear. “Yeah, hello? Uh huh, this is, uh, Dwayne Somers, I’m, uh, Mal’s half-cousin. I’m just ringing to see what his condition’s like? Hope he won’t be taking any chances like going into that stupid fight next Monday...” Always looking out for the health and safety of others. Quietus, the Mother Teresa of the twenty-first century.
