The Ambush

16 Mar 2004

By The Muppet Man

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Muppet Man leaned back in the front seat of his tattered old white van, steering rather carelessly with one hand. There were no flashy cars for him. He didn't like that sort of lifestyle, and because of it he was rather good at avoiding the press. To him, they were just more enemies. Everyone was opposition, there were no friends in this cruel world, but he had learned to deal with that over time. He had been studying deeply into all things related to combat; the way of the warrior, anicent Japanese techniques etc, but there was only so much he could learn from books. Plus, he needed a break. He was travelling. Where to? Not even he knew that. All he knew was that travelling cleared his mind, he enjoyed it. As his car sped down the long, barren stretch of road, he sighed a sigh of relief. He was finally out in the open, not cooped up inside an old house reading for hours on end. That wasn't what The Muppet Man did. It was unnatural. The sun beamed down with a heat rarely seen in Britain, smiling on the occupants. Not liking the heat too much, he decided to take a short break. He pulled over to a petrol station on the left of the road and stepped out of the van. It was an ordinary petrol station, nothing special at all. Yet, something didn't feel right. There were two cars other than his parked there. One was a sleek, shiny black Mercedes, the other a large black van. He relaxed, dropping his head, but slowly he raised it again, staring with shock at the three letters on the side of the van... 'WWA' "Ah crap," he began, and turned to go back to his car. Barring his path was WWA interviewer Wallace Coleman. He'd sprung the trap on himself. Cursing his own stupidity, and sort of admiring the thought the WWA interviewer had put into this ambush, he stared at Wallace. Coleman beamed back at him, a cocky grin on his face, quite proud of himself. "What do you want?" he said, knowing the answer. "What do you think? I want to speak with you about your match and Rage in the Cage, and possibly other things," Wallace replied in an annoying voice, still glowing with triumph. Seeing the look on Muppet's face, he added "why, don't you enjoy a good interview?" The Muppet Man wasn't in the mood for this. He sighed and went to turn, but Wallace was already on him. "What are your immediate thoughts on the subject?" "What subject?" Muppet replied, trying to find a way out other than decking the guy and making a run for it. "Your match with Tommy Starr, of course!" The Muppet Man was really tempted to shout out his real feelings on the matters at hand... "What do I think of Tommy Starr? That arrogant b*stard! Does he think he can beat me? I'll show him true pain. And as for you, you nosy little swine, you better clear off sharpish or you'll find that the same fate awaits you as Tommy Starr." He smiled to himself, thinking that would only give him a bad image, never mind the rather bland and unthoughtful words he used. He prefered to think his answers over carefully, making the interviewer's life as hard as possible. Finally, he setteld on something. "Tommy Starr is an interesting character. He is obviously a very talented (small giggle) worker, but he's in way over his head. He was standing on the brink of no man's land, but now he's flung himself face first into it. Nothing awaits him now. He has no future." Wallace smiled, happy he was getting a good response. Poor fool, he thought. However, Muppet Man was fully aware that he was simply being used to add zeros to Coleman's paycheck. His time would come soon enough. "Interesting. Any messages for Shaman, who will have a shot at the World Title?" "No." He thought a flat answer would be best. There was absolutely nothing he had to say to Shaman. Starr was his only focus. "So what about Rage in the Cage? What do you think of the card?" "I don't." Wallace was a little taken aback. "You...what?" "You heard me: I don't." "So you're trying to tell me you have no thoughts about any other matches other than your own? You're trying to tell me that you're that selfish?" Muppet grinned. It was working. "On the contrary, Mr Coleman. What I think is of no value, only what is true. Maybe you should look into that, you might become something even minutely useful." Wallace froze. He was not expecting the sudden change of tact. "So..." "What is your purpose, Wallace? What were you put on this Earth to do? To leech success off others? I don't think so. That is not how people are made. Well!?" Wallace tried to speak, but no sound came out of his mouth. That question had him stumped. It was delightful irony, Wallace thought in anger, the interviewed had the interviewer stumped. He had given him a question he couldn't answer. "If you cannot answer that, then I conclude this: you have no purpose. That's right. There is no reason for you to exist. You're just like Tommy Starr..." Pushing Wallace out of the way, The Muppet Man stood directly in front of the camera. He started straight at it and extended his arm, pointing directly at the audience. "Are you better yet, Starr? I truly hope so. I don't want you to have any excuses after I defeat you at Rage in the Cage. There, I will show you why you were never meant to exist. You are a mistake, Tommy, you were never meant to happen, along with the rest of your filthy kind. You are useless. You are disposable. And, on Sunday, you will be disposed of!" He spat at the camera, full of malice. Stepping into his van, he sped off into the distance as the picture faded out. There were the footsteps of someone supposedly running after the van, and then came a heavy Mexican accent. "Hey! Get back here! You have to pay, you know! Filthy cabron..."
