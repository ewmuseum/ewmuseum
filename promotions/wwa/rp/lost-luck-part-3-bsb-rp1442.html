Lost Luck, Part 3 *BSB*

4 Jun 2004

By Chance

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
When I walked in the door the doormen welcomed me back, even asked where I’d been. Waitresses smile at me, and the a few gamblers give a wave, others look at me in disbelief, only knowing me from television and stories. These people know me, and I know them. Even the ones I’m only just seeing for the first time, because they’re part of this place, and this place is as much of a home as I can remember. This place always was my favourite casino, maybe because it was never all that popular. The neon lights outside have long ago been broken by vandals, or maybe just a strong wind, and tourists avoid it; it isn’t nearly as flashy as they’ve come to expect. But it still has its faithful following, which I was once a part of. This was where I first saw the WWA on a TV screen, where I first had my dream to gamble more than money. That’s a pastime I’ve been neglecting, but tonight I’m going to change that. If not, then I'll just enjoy being back in a place I love. When I hand my briefcase over to the cashier, asking for chips, they stare in surprise. They ask me if I know how much money there is in here, so I give them the withdrawal slip they gave me at the bank. They give me a big stack of chips, all marked $1,000. Feeling slightly flippant I toss $1,000 dollars to my favourite waitress (still here), and ask for a G&T on the rocks. My drink comes and I take a long refreshing sip, adjust the collar of my Hawaiian shirt, and strut over to a roulette wheel. The croupier smiles in recognition as I place my chips and drink down on the table and survey my kingdom: 37 numbers, and a bunch of other options I know like the back of my hand. A few other people at the table I don’t recognise whisper to each other, apparently the whizz-kid wrestler/gambler is a bit of a legend in his home casino, how about that? Deciding to have some fun I toss my head dramatically, flipping my hair to one side, and cockily place my entire huge stack of chips down on one choice. This is it, 50/50, a coin toss, one spin of the wheel to see if Fortune still favours her son, her soldier. It’s all on red, a gamblers choice, because only idiots pick a single number, Fortune may not favour the brave, but she doesn’t care for idiots either. I know that if I lose, then I lose everything, but come out a man, my own man. Like Kipling said: “If you can make one heap of all your winnings And risk it all on one turn of pitch-and-toss, And lose, and start again at your beginnings And never breath a word about your loss; …Then you’ll be a man, my son.” And that’s all well and good. But if I win then I leave in the comforting knowledge that I’m still lucky, so really this is a win-win situation. Win, I can get on with preparing for Bourbon Street Brawl by rediscovering my luck and having fun in my favourite Casino, knowing Lady Luck is smiling on me. If I lose then I lose the money, but I can say I risked it all, and walked out able to laugh about it. Win-win. It’s just that in one of the win’s I come out with a lot less money. Suddenly, before the wheel spins an image pops into my head, a small metal ball landing on a small black square, with the number 13 on it. I ignore it. I’ve made my choice, I’ve decided to keep the same odds as a coin toss, this way the odds are even. The croupier accepts all bets and spins the wheel and an involuntary grin spreads itself out on my face. The ball and my stomach both jump up and down, and spin round and round, I feel slightly dizzy with excitement. Electricity tingles all over my body, running in through my feet and fingertips, through the pores in my skin, from the air in this place that is saturated with the hopes and thrills of a thousand gamblers. Pure enjoyment flashes before me in the whirlwind of the ball’s movement, I close my eyes and enjoy the pureness of this elemental feeling, enjoy the thrill of Chance. The ball slows and stops and for a moment it looks like it has landed on red, and my hurt jumps, but the image of 13 black flashes before my eyes again and the ball gives one final jump and lands there. On 13 black, on the square I saw it would land on. Suddenly the electricity is gone and I am just an ordinary man. I dig my hand into my pocket and close may palm hard around the one coin I have left. On my way out I no longer strut, no longer grin my crooked grin. I just walk. Next to the door is a row of slot machines. I wearily take my shining coin out of my pocket and twist it around between my fingers one last time, but I fumble and drop it, and it lands on its edge. Angrily I kick the machine and retrieve my coin, what I once thought was the symbol of my luck. Just as I once thought I was the personification of that luck. I let the coin fall one more time into the slot machine and almost don’t bother to pull the lever. As I pull I see signs line up inside my head and see the word jackpot flash. The slots spin and time seems to slow as one after the other they each stop moving. I stare at the in disbelief. Identical symbols on the slots. Bells ring and the “JACKPOT!!!” sign flashes on and off. The electricity floods back in, jolting my grin into place, and the flow does not stop until I feel aflame with luck. Scooping coins from the machine I put one into each slot machine in the row and go down one by one pulling the levers. Before I pull each one a tiny glowing JACKPOT burns itself into my mind’s retina, my visual memory. True to form every machine spills forth a flood of shiny new coins. My grin now fixed, my every step becoming so flamboyant I move in the parody of a strut I go back to the first machine do a little jump and a step and hit it on the side. One more silver coin falls to the bottom, sliding down and landing with a tinkle that I would recognise anywhere. I bend over and see it landed on its side. That’s the funny thing about Fortune, she doesn’t give you anything for free, you can’t belong to her, unless you lose everything else first. Fortune's Fool is back b*itches, and he cannot wait for Bourbon Street! The editing guys let me play around with the camera and the scene now cuts so thatt the next thing we all see is me, sitting in a snazzy little office somewhere, signing a piece of paper that reads “lease” at the top. As the screen fades I put in a clever little image of workmen refitting a shabby old casino, and putting up some blazing new neon letters that read: THE LAST CHANCE CASINO Oh boy do I love this place.
