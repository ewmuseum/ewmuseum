The Inner Struggle: Part 2 *BSB

4 Jun 2004

By The Muppet Man

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wake up. All was black. There was nothing. Only this voice. Wake up! Suddenly, the blackness began to change. Slowly but surely, the metamorphasis began. It was all blurry. No...vague shapes could be made out. Black and white. Red and yellow then came to be. Green, blue, every colour in the rainbow. "Wake up! Wake up!" The Muppet Man sat up abruptly, breathing heavily. There was a group of people around him as he lay on a hard surface. But where? Ah yes. New Orleans. It was all coming back to him now. One of the peope appeared to be a doctor, and he was the one who was calling out. The otehrs were just a mass of faceless nobodies, unimportant. The doctor spoke again. "What happened?" Muppet Man had an exreme sense of deja vu. In the crowd, he noticed the old man who had helped him before. Of course. He had collapsed in that alleyway. But why? "Speak to me," the doctor persisted. "What happened?" It was an effort to talk, but Muppet Man managed it. "I...I...don't know." He told the truth. The old man stepped out from the crowd. "It happened before as well. He told me he was fine." The doctor looked concerned, and the Muppet Man was silent. It was all coming back, slowly, as if being filtered through bit by bit. Yes. He remembered now. The searing pain. The voice. The horrible, mocking voice. Yet the voice spoke the truth. "Come on. We need to get you to a hospital," spoke the doctor in a soothing voice. Yet the Muppet Man shook his head. He wasn't going to hospital. There was nothing physically wrong with him, and he knew it. He slowly got to his feet. "I'm fine. Leave me be." The doctor protested. "But you're not! You've collapsed twice!" "I'm fine! You have to trust me! I'm fine!" He spoke this with such lucidity and authority that the doctor backed down slightly. But he still protested. "It is in your best interests for you to come back to the hospital with me. We just want to check you over, confirm that you're okay." "No! You must leave me be. I have some very important business to attend to." "But you're in no condition..." "Trust me. I must." The doctor argued no further. "Okay. But at least take my card, so you can come to hospital if something happens again." The Muppet Man willingly obliged. The crowd began to part, and the doctor bid him farewell and left. Once again, it was only him and the old man. They stared at each other for a couple of seconds before the Muppet Man broke eye contact and turned. He needed to work things out before the Bourbon Street Brawl. He had to. --------------------------------------------------- It had been a long night, full of nightmares of being run over by monkeys in wheelchairs and such. But the voice remained. All through the night it taunted him, haunted him. It would not leave him be. It was like a demon coming to destroy his very soul. The Devil taking back what was his. He was relieved when morning came. He sat up in his bed in his hotel room, sweating slightly. He ran both his hands through his hair, his brain throbbing with questions. Yet he knew that the voice was still there, dorment in the back on his mind. "I really need to get out," he muttered through clenched teeth. He got to his feet, and immediately noticed that he was still in his clothes from last night. "Ah crap," he muttered. Not bothering to change, he set off. It was still very early in the morning, and the streets of New Orleans were far from packed. The Muppet Man liked it like that. Less people to barge past. Less people oggling at his mask. Less...people. He walked down the pavement, but knocked shoulders with a large, muscular man. He turned out to be Mexican. "Hey! Watch your step, cabron!" Muppet ignored him, continuing to walk. He was not in the mood. He noticed an old fashioned newpaper booth on the side of the pavement, so he decided to catch up on the news. Maybe it would take his mind off the situation. He slotted 50 cents in, and out popped a paper. He turned to the back; the sports section. The headline shocked him. 'Wrestling Star Collapses in Street' All of a sudden, a drop of water smeared the ink in the article. A tear. A tear of desperation. For the Muppet Man was desperate. Desperate for something, anything to calm him, to show him how to continue, how to carry on, because he could not. He could not continue. Not like this. Not while knowing that he would soon stumble and fall, and this time, there would be no one to help him back up again. He was on his own. He took the paper, screwing it up and tossing it straight into the road. But he did not care. What would he do? He did not know. Yet he knew one thing. The Bourbon Street Brawl was out of the question now. He couldn't compete, let alone think of winning. He needed to get away. But where could he go? He was alone. He had nowhere to go. He began to ran. He ran down the street at top speed. Where was he going? He didn't know. He narrowly dodged a car speeding past, and dodged left into an alleyway. The alleyway. There was a vibrating from inside Muppet Man's coat. His phone. Out of instinct he reached inside and retrieved it. A text message. It simply said this: 'Remember the plan. DO.' Muppet Man sat down on a box. The box. He was breathing normally. He was...okay. But there it was. That stinging pain in his temple. "F*cking leave me alone!" But the voice was somehow...different. My friend, your time is now. The pain ceased. Muppet Man stopped clutching his temple. He stood up. You must rise up and snatch your prize! He looked up at the overcast clouds above. And you will, you will. The voice spoke the truth. He knew. Devil's Own will help you. He was focused. But in the end you must overcome even them. He was ready. You can do it. He could do it. You will do it. He would do it. Your time is now. It was his time. Your time is now. The Muppet Man turned, smashing open the door to the right of him. He entered, and the scene faded to black. Your time is now... "Not you again! Stupid cabron..." SMACK! "...Ouch."
