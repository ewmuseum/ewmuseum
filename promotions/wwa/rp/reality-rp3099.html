Reality

4 May 2006

By The Muppet Man

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It's been an interesting six months. Seven months? Something like that. Too long. There's certainly a lot to talk about; Vik demonstrated that pretty well last week. Hell, I could barely sit through that. Still, there's a certain something about seeing everything you've tried to do, everything you've succeeded in accomplishing, and all your failures too, the fruitless results of all your hard work, summed up in a four or five minute slideshow with some commentary. It really puts it all into perspective... I started out trying to make everything better. No, that's not true. I started out trying to survive. And I guess that's how things still are. It's come full circle. Sort of fitting, really. A bit like these airy, uplifted reflectionary comments, the ones that don't really go anywhere or have much of a point. It's been the worst time of my life, and in another way, the best. Whatever the experience, it's sure been one hell of an experience. You'd think I'd have more to say, though, wouldn't you? ---- "Is that all you have to say?" The Muppet Man looked at the man in front of him, Shijo Takeichi, quizzically, as if the answer to this question was perfectly clear to even the most imperceptive of individuals. He nodded slowly, offering a quick smile, before beginning to turn away. It was a momentous action, because the task that lay straight ahead of him - or behind him, I guess you could say, at this point - was the biggest ever. In another way, though, the outcome was perhaps irrelevant, and there was no pressure at all. "Wait," Takeichi said, putting his hand on Muppet's shoulder before could turn fully around. The wrestler offered no resistance. This man had been training the Muppet, body and mind, since before Rage in the Cage. It hadn't really been that long, but the events that had followed seemed to increase the time by a great degree. He'd been fired for a brief period, during which his silence wasn't exactly compulsory, but he'd decided to make no comments anyway. In fact, the Muppet Man had been completely silent behind the scenes since his preparation to face Armaan in the cage. It was getting really difficult, and he didn't have anything to say, let alone anyone to listen. Takeichi had decided, and Muppet agreed, that the training would finish about a week before his match preceding the Bourbon Street Brawl. Muppet liked this guy, who realised that he had to get out on his own and absorb some life, some energy, before he went into this. Training to a rigid program and then going straight into a match was never a good idea; your spirit was still clouded, still recovering. He'd said 'thanks'. That was all, and that was all Muppet thought was necessary. The gratitude was clear, it didn't require any overblown gestures. For the first time since the training had begun, Muppet didn't feel like he understood his 'mentor'. In reality, he was less of a mentor than a partner. Takeichi had on multiple occasions revealed to Muppet that he was also training himself, learning new perspectives and just generally new things. He couldn't claim any seniority. This was the main reason for Muppet's confusion. They weren't friends, per se, that was clear. So, what was the problem? "There's one more thing, before you go," Takeichi said with a serious look on his face. "One more... lesson, you could say." The Muppet Man remained silent. "No, it's not a lesson," he continued, perhaps half to himself. "An experiment? A test. Something like that." "Okay," Muppet responded. He didn't know what this could be about - what area of training hadn't they covered? - but he was willing to go along with this one last thing. Still, he found his patience waning. However calm he appeared, he'd been on edge, perhaps even dangerous, since losing at Rage in the Cage. "Then let's go back," Takeichi said. He quickly turned, walking backwards through the room, and Muppet followed. They were in the basement of this property Takeichi had managed to get his hands on. It was a run down old hell hole, but big enough, as long as you didn't mind fighting off the roaches every now and then. Some might say it builds character. They'd be wrong, but it's a nice excuse. Set up in this basement was the cheapest wrestling ring that wouldn't break under a high impact move, and they'd been training with it for the last month. Takeichi slid straight into the ring, favouring his ribs. Muppet noted this as he entered; pictures of their last and final encounter, not half an hour ago, popped up fresh in his mind. He could still see the sledgehammers and the two-by-fours scattered around on the outside. In the spirit of Bourbon Street, they'd got a little carried away. The Muppet Man knew that come his match with his two adversaries, though, this wouldn't be of any help. He'd just been sort of tense. "Right..." muttered Takeichi as he managed to get up to his feet. He appeared to be making this up on the spot. "How about... two out of three falls, two minute rounds." "That's ridiculous," Muppet said straight. "You're in absolutely no condition." "I know that perfectly well," Takeichi said with a smile. It was clearly faux. "There'll be one slight difference, though." "A handicap?" Muppet suggested. "That's a bit better, but I still don't see how this is an 'experiment'." "Simple. All you have to do... is take off your mask." The silence that followed this comment, this bold suggestion, said more than most words could. "I'm afraid I can't do that," Muppet said, in a tone of voice that sent a shiver down Takeichi's spine. "What's the big deal? I've seen you without your mask plenty of times, but I noticed you always fight with it on. I don't get that, and I think it's a block you have to overcome." At this, the Muppet Man simply turned around. Who was he to talk about mental blocks? How could he judge him like some generic old masked wrestler? Didn't he understand anything? Muppet sighed, calming himself. He knew he was being unreasonable. He didn't turn back around as he spoke, but at least he said something. "You're right," he said. "There is. It's not something I can just push through, though. Why do you think I'm so laid back about this match? I know you've noticed, you've even said it to me a couple of times." "I don't follow you." "It's just..." Muppet started. He stopped, sighing again. "Look, all three of us guys have gone through great changes with this whole thing, but it's strange. I hate their f**king guts more than anything in the world, but after I get out of there, I think I could just as easily take this mask off and be done with it as sit down with those guys and have a beer." "What?" Takeichi still didn't quite get it. "I feel like I'm a blank slate. I've been so screwed up by this whole thing that the slate's been wiped clean, and now all that's left to shape me is this Sunday night. After that, maybe I'll be able to take this off... and maybe I'll be able to put this all behind me." Following the trend, this was met only by silence. The Muppet Man sighed one last time, stepping through the ropes. As he was leaving, he heard Takeichi call after him. "Hey." Muppet turned his head back. "I said this was a test, after all. Heh. Even though I didn't know the answer myself, I think I can safely say you passed with flying colours." "Let's hope I can do the same on Sunday." "I thought you said it was irrelevant," Takeichi pointed out. "Hey, I'm the f**ked up one here. Cut me some slack." Shijo Takeichi stepped out of the ring, taking a moment to nurse his ribs, but he masked the pain well and walked up towards the Muppet Man. "Want to grab a beer?" "Yeah, or fifteen. And some vodka," said Muppet, already on his way out. "If we're going to say goodbye, then it might as well be when we can't even remember our own names." "You really think you could share a beer with those guys... after all this?" "I don't know. And to be honest, that scares me just a little." ---- A blank slate. Tabula Rasa. That's all that's left. Thanks to you, Steven Murphy. Thanks to you too, Armaan Singhania. So... Sunday seals my fate. Shapes it. That's a lot of pressure on you two guys, but, heh... I trust you. Perhaps we'll see the end of this grotesque visage. This is my only message to you all. I know you'll see this as inadequate. It's... not enough, perhaps, considering what's gone before. I feel I should have more to say, too. But I don't. Deep sigh, and smile for the cameras. Nothing to lose, and no regrets. Can you say the same? Good luck.
