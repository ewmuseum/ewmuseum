Just Not Cricket

24 Jul 2003

By Bonafide

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(It is an idyllic day in England. The sun is shining, the sky is blue and we are at The Oval watching Leicestershire vs Surrey in cricket. Behind him are sat too elderly gents. They are dressed in plaid shirts, pale trousers and floppy white hats. They have a basket of egg and cress sandwiches and a bottle of red wine, typical elderly cricketers. Harold is 74, has glasses and has been married for 52 years. He is a retired estate agent and he drives a Bentley. His companion is Albert who is 76, he used to be something big in the city, has been married for 48 years and he also drives a Bentley.) Harold: Lovely day for it, isn't it? Albert: You think so? I was hoping for a little cloud cover, don't see the need for all this direct sunlight! Harold: No, it's not the British way, is it? Albert: Too right, old chap, not the British way at all. (The two gents return to watching the cricket, as usual not much is happening.) Albert: Not much happening, is there? Harold: No, not really. Nottinghamshire are really digging in. Reminiscent of old Gatting. Albert: I think you mean Atherton, old chap. Harold: I know what I mean! And I mean Boycott. Albert: Ah, yes, Geoff Boycott, now there was a batsman. Harold: None of this hitting it for six or four, he knew the meaning of defence! Albert: Yes, a great player, I remember him playing against India in 1977, he was in for 2 whole days and only played a stroke 6 times! That was a truly great innings! Harold: Don't make them like Boycott anymore. Albert: They certainly don't. (The sip their wine and watch the match. The Leicestershire batsman plays a marvellous right hook and the ball flys into the air and into the stands across the pitch for six.) Harold: You'd never have seen Boycott do that! Albert: No class about that at all, Defence is what that young lad needs to learn. You can't go hitting sixes all over the place in a Test Match! Harold: What his name, that fellow? Albert: That is Aftab Habib. Harold: Never had all these foreign players in my day! Albert: Habib is English. Harold: No? Well, fancy that! Must be a northern name, Yorkshire most likely. Albert: Quite, quite. (Habib swings and the next ball and the ball smashes away his wicket. The man in the front row claps loudly.) Harold: Bravo! Good bowling, nicely done. Albert: Boycott would never have got out like that! Harold: So true. No, batsman in our day were far better, so much more class about their game. Albert: True of all sports though, old chap. No gents in football now, no-one like Nobby Stiles or Bobby Charlton, just thugs and ruffians! (Albert tries some egg sandwich and speaks as he chews. He spits cress over the man in row in front as he does so.) Albert: And wrestling is not what it used to be! My grandson, young Jordan, tells me that they have ladders and tables, more like a DIY show than wrestling! Harold: Very true, and you used to have great British wrestlers like Big Daddy and Giant Haystacks, now who do we have to represent this great nation? James Curzon! (The man in front looks back.) Albert: Oh yes, the cowardly one! (The man in the front row stands up and turns round. Albert and Harold realise it is James Curzon.) Albert: Ah...! Curzon: NOBODY CALLS ME CHICKEN! Harold: But, you see....! Curzon: WAAARRRGGHHH! (Curzon grabs the bottle of wine and smashes it over Harold's head. Then he grabs an egg sanwich and rubs it into Albert's face before pushing him off his seat.) Curzon: I am not a coward! Everyone is out to get me, I am just protecting myself! (The two elderly men roll on the floor trying to wipe all the food and drink off themselves. Curzon turns to the camera.) Curzon: I am a great wrestler, the Geoff Boycott of the wrestling world! I have championship credentials, I am a bonafide champion or at least I will be on Fiesta when I defeat Dave Harley. (Curzon is shaking with rage.) This is just not cricket! Everyone hates me, everyone is trying to hold me back! Well I'll show them, I prove that I can be a winner! Dave Harley had better watch out because James Curzon is coming! (Curzon runs up the steps and trips over. He looks around embarassed.) Curzon: Yeah, Dave Harley beware! (Curzon looks around and runs out of the stadium, leaving Albert and Harold in a pile of their own picnic.) Harold: Geoff Boycott would never have done that!
