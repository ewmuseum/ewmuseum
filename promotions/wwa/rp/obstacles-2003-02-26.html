Obstacles

26 Feb 2003

By Chance

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 26 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A dark street. Lampposts shine down upon the cracked and crooked pavement, showing the flaws therein. Showing the holes within the manmade material that without the illuminating light would remain in the dark, unnoticed. Until someone tripped over them of course, as someone does, and that person go sprawling onto their face, only to roll up to their feet and begin brushing the dust from their black and white Hawaiian shirt and rubbing at the grazes on their palms and face. In fact Chance’s pride appears to be what is hurt the most as he looks sheepishly at the woman walking along next to him. She doesn’t appear overly sympathetic as it seems she is in fact laughing at him. This girl appears to be the same one who appeared in the last Chance promo we saw, the one called Lucy. She is wearing a rainbow coloured striped top, and trousers which appear to be a patchwork of more colours than are even contained in the rainbow. For such a cold time of year, in a place as cold as Canada, the attire of both her and Chance seems entirely out of place, especially for night. Chance walks on after his fall still rubbing his grazed palms. Chance: “Ow!” It is fairly obvious he isn’t badly hurt, in fact as we see a bit closer we see that it’s barely even a bruise, not the sort of thing that a wrestler should even feel. Lucy appears to pick up on this as well, speaking to Chance in a playful, and quite sarcastic tone. Lucy: “Ah, diddums! I thought wrestlers were supposed to be big strong and tough. I was at least thinking you’d be one of the three! Then again, wrestlers are supposed to have good body control, and you just showed otherwise.” Chance takes a visible effort to collect himself and try to be casual before answering. Chance: “Meh, it’s not my fault, I blame the city council for the bad state of the pavement there. So you could say that the mayor tripped me. Think I should go beat him up?” Lucy laughs a fact that seems to shock Chance, disarming him slightly, taking away some of the edge in his expression so that he seems more relaxed. In fact even in this slightly edgy expression, he still seems more relaxed than we’ve seen him in almost any of his other promos when he always seemed very intense. Lucy: “No, I don’t think that will be necessary. You managed to recover well though.” Chance: “I thought so.” Lucy: “Modest too.” Chance: “Well when you’re as great as I am, you can’t help but be modest, it just comes naturally.” Lucy: “Actually that roll things gives me the sense that you were once a gymnast Mr Wrestler?” Chance’s voice becomes sombre again as he is assailed suddenly by painful memories, memories he has tried to leave behind. Chance (faltering slightly): “My dad insisted that all of us should do lots of gymnastics when I was young, like mum did. My sisters were always better at it than me though.” Chance’s voice begins to break under the strain of emotion, and he stops. Lucy obviously notices, but covers it up, seeing that this is something he is not ready to talk about to her. They walk on in silence for a second before they strike up the conversation again, Chance seeming to become more and more at ease, as Lucy gradually disarms him. We follow their path down the streets all of which are dark, but not all of which are as dangerous as the first one seemed to be. At least neither of them fall over on this stretch of the journey. The streets are still dark however, and it is still bitterly cold, and as neither Chance nor Lucy are dressed for the cold they seem to subconsciously move closer to each other, drawn by each others body heat, each atom of their beings seeming to attract each other as they move closer and closer until they are almost touching, and instinctively Chance slips an arm around Lucy’s shoulder, just as she slips an arm around his waste. The moves are completely instinctive to both of them, they barely seem aware that they’ve done it, and as such there is a remarkable closeness about this moment that seems only vaguely romantic, lacking the purpose to really seem a romantic gesture, rather it’s a mutual caring on each others parts, an empathy that made them both realise the other was cold, and attempt to protect them accordingly. As they walk on the lampposts still shine, but now they seem to cast pools of liquid gold down into the noticeably fewer cracks in the pavement, as if the light is filling in the gaps, making the paving slabs complete once more. The camera moves in close enough again so that we can once again here them talking. As it does so, we see Lucy lean in closer towards Chance speaking softly to him. Lucy: “Do you think it’s about time to talk about wrestling now? We’re almost there.” Chance: “And there really wouldn’t be much point in having brought him along if we didn’t. Of course I could always amuse you both instead by tripping on a crack in the pavement and falling on my arse again.” Lucy: “No, I don’t think you should damage yourself any more than you did in that last match. I think you still have bruises on your neck from that choke, that guy Kash did on you, and what looks like a bite mark?” Her tone is playful and teasing, but it hides a kind of anxiety when she mentions the bite mark. Chance: “I was hoping that didn’t show any more. Kash is really starting to worry me you know.” Lucy (even more teasing than before): “So a guy gave you that? Maybe he thought you were cute?” Chance shivers and proceeds to almost trip on another unseen crack in the pavement, but Lucy’s support stops him from tumbling over. Chance: “Bloody cracks! Explain to me why it is that even those little cracks in the pavement are beginning to seem like Denrol.” Lucy: “And you accuse me on being random!” Chance: “You mean you don’t see the similarities? I fail to see them coming, I fail to see whatever light may illuminate them and give me warning, and so I reach them and they bloody well plant me on my arse. Like Denrol did. I really wish I’d been able to see that coming.” Lucy: “You’d be able to if you were psychic.” Chance: “Unfortunately not everyone has that particular ability, least of all me.” Lucy: “Just lazy if you ask me!” Chance chuckles as he considers this, then his face becomes serious, as he obviously wants to discuss work again. Chance: “Maybe I was lazy now that I think about it.” Lucy: “So you are going to become psychic?” Chance: “I mean about Denrol as you well know, maybe he’s right and I didn’t show him enough respect, maybe I was too lazy to. I knew I had respect for him and the upper tier of superstars and all those who had established themselves …” Lucy: “But?” Chance: “But maybe I just didn’t show it enough. Maybe I was so wrapped up in trying to climb the ladder that I did fail to recognise the skills of those above me. Maybe I was trying to take the easy way, and simply try to replace them in the fans eyes instead of creating my own identity, my own legacy. I just don’t know any more.” Lucy: “Can I just say something about you?” Chance: “Sure, I don’t see why you need to ask.” Lucy: “You don’t seem like the kind of person who would do that. You don’t seem like the kind of person who would deliberately disrespect someone. To be honest it seems like this Denrol is disrespecting you. Now can we stop talking about wrestling we’re almost there.” Chance: “Sure, I think that’s enough about wrestling for one promo, should I send the cameraman back?” Lucy: “No, let him come with us, he might enjoy it.” Chance: “You’re right, it would seem rude to send him back really.” Lucy: “Told you, you were a nice guy!” It soon becomes apparent that their destination is in fact a cinema. Up in lights at the entrance are the names of the films on show, which have been filtered out, and posters of the films litter the walls, advertising the ultimate narcissistic form of escapism. Think about it, humans through film and TV have invented a way of escaping from real life by staring at pictures of themselves all day. Irony or what? Lucy goes up to the front of the cinema to get tickets, while Chance and the cameraman remain behind, waiting for her outside. Chance: “Hey, would you mind if I did a short vignette style thing here?” The cameraman shakes his head in a ‘no’. Chance: “Denrol, if you’re watching this, and I hope you are, then you just saw me compare you to the cracks in the pavement that tripped me up, and brought me down to the floor, and that’s true. Because of the darkness surrounding you I failed to see that you were a crack in my way, I didn’t see you as an obstacle because your hate for Denrol shrouded you from me. Just as it shrouded the cracks in you, just as my feelings hid the cracks that were appearing in me. I didn’t see that you were falling apart, just as I didn’t realise that I might have been. But now I see clearly, just like those street lamps allow me to see the cracks, so does that girl allow me to see the cracks in myself, she lights them up, and just as the light from those streetlamps appeared to do, she fills those cracks, she makes me a complete person. And now that I am complete, I can see things as they truly are, I can see that I have to prove myself to you and to everyone. And I can see that I shouldn’t be angry at you for what you did. If anything I should pity you.” Chance motions for the cameraman to turn off the camera and we fade to black.
