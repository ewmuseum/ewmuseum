From the view of a Falcon

11 Jan 2003

By Chance

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 11 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
-The scene opens, and we look down to see the ground far below us. Suddenly we dip our heads, lower our wings and we rush towards it, diving straight down, our stomachs lost far behind. Just as it seems we are going to be dashed on the rocks below us, we swoop upwards, air rushing to meet us, ruffling our feathers as it pushes beneath our wings, pushing us upwards, and in that moment, gravity loses all power and we are weightless. The glory of flight gives us the feeling of instant exultation, up here, nothing affects us that lives on the ground, nothing can. We fly through the air, swirls and eddies of wind try to blow us off course, we surrender to the direction they want to take us, and by surrendering, we control them. By submitting to the whims of the air, we rule it. We soar high above the concerns of mere mortals and realise that this is why humans try to emulate our gift of flight, why they strive to attain what only we have, and cannot give- -Below we see the humans, hundreds of tiny little creatures, like bugs, insignificant, trivial to those of us with higher concerns. We this is why humans try to fly, to be free of their planets constraints, to no longer be tied down to fate and destiny, to be earthbound no more. To be free. Free to soar, free to dive, free to glide as we do now. This is a freedom that humans and other earthbound will never know, but they still try, still struggle vainly on. There are those humans who fly through the air, only to fall. I see one below me now. For days now he has tried to fly out here, tried to jump from heights only to fall, as he inevitably will, only to fall as destiny meant all earthbound to fall. But still he tries and tries, and for a moment each day he can fly, and he knows our freedom. We see him down there now; climbing into his platform with four posts, and ropes between. Again and again he flies off, each time doing something slightly different. But each time with the same result. Every now and then he lands awkwardly, clutching at one of his ungainly legs. But always he gets up, trying to simulate us, trying to copy our motions, but compared to us he is clumsy, inept, he has not the grace or the precision to fly as we do. Still, we must admire the determination of the one the humans call Chance- -We plunge downwards towards the ground again; this time as we reach the corner of the four-posted platform (a wrestling ring if you didn't get it before) we spread our wings and flap to decrease our speed. We land gently on one of the posts, and sit there watching as the one called Chance tries to fly. After another failed attempt, he clutches at his bulky leg again, and slowly pulls himself up, inspecting us- "It's alright for you guys. You can fly all the time, you don't need to practice" -He's wrong of course, even birds need to practice, it's just that our practice looks the same as when we aren't practising, so you can't tell the difference- "You don't have to sweat and toil and cause yourselves pain, just to prepare for the few moments a week when you become more than what you are. To become a higher being for a couple of seconds. You don't have to half kill yourself just to feel the wind rushing against your face, you don't fight others to prove you're good at it." -Again he's wrong. We do fight to prove who’s the better flyer, how vain these humans are to think they are truly unique, that we cannot be like them. How very vain to think that we do not also fight to prove our worth- “And if you do, you do it by choice, not for a living. Lately I’ve been wondering if it is truly my choice to fight, and if I could stop if I wanted to. If Matt Warr would let me do it that is. But we do have one thing in common at least: we both have to fly. You probably think I’m clumsy, making these feeble attempts to do as you do naturally. But I do what I must, to fight whom I must. And on Saturday that will be Dave Harley. A man who certainly doesn’t have to fly.” -A second human, the one humans would call Jarod Crow joins the one called Chance. Although it appears to us that Chance does not notice him, we know he can feel his presence, why he does not start when Crow talks- Jarod: “You do know that thing can’t understand you right?” Chance: “Sometimes I wonder … it almost seems to have a knowing expression sometimes.” Jarod: “And I thought I was the one with the history of mental imbalance. You’ve come out here every day for the past week. To a ring, in the middle of a field, in the middle of nowhere, to practise flying off that turnbuckle again and again. I’m your best friend and I don’t even know why.” Chance: “Because I have to be ready. I have to be able to fly. Like that bird. If I’m going to beat Harley, and if we’re going to beat Enigma and Zion I need to be able to fly like I’ve never flown before. Just like that Falcon has to fly, so do I. I know that you don’t have to fly. You’ve put effort into learning how to fight without flying. But I can’t do that, and I wouldn’t if I could. Do you now how it feels to fly like I do? It’s amazing. It’s as if all the pressures that used to weigh you down are gone. As if the pressure that pushes down on your head, keeping the thoughts in is suddenly lifted. There’s nothing holding you down and you’re free. That feeling is addictive Jarod, I’m not sure I could stop if I wanted to.” -We are impressed by this one’s grasp of what it feels like to fly. For an earthbound we realise that he is remarkably aware of his own being to be able to so clearly define what he feels when flying. What we feel almost all the time. It is strange, for to him the brief moment when he flies is a gift, while to us, the moment when we are forced to remain on the ground is a curse- Jarod: “I think you’re wrong you know. You don’t have to fly, I’ve seen you in the ring, seen what you’re capable of. You could learn to fight without flying, just as I did, just put on a bit of muscle and do some extra training. It’s got to be better than this. Sure you fly through the air just like that falcon does, for a few seconds at least, before gravity pulls you down. Chance I’m worried about you, you half kill yourself in every match, and now you have two matches in a week against Harley and Enigma. Two people who have both injured you. Tell me I have no reason to worry, please.” Chance: “You don’t need to worry about me. Gravity pulls us all down eventually. From the highest star, to the lowest jobber all of us will fall from grace eventually, despite our vain attempts to stay at the top. Tracer Bullet, Jackson Kraven, they are stars now, but in twenty years … they may not even be remembered. So don’t worry about me, Harley and Enigma will have to catch me before they injure me again. Whether on Meltdown or Fiesta, they still have to catch me” -The one called Crow looks worried for his brother earthbound. He should be. All who try to emulate us, the airborne, fall. Sometimes they fall and are not hurt; sometimes they fall and do not rise. My kind still remember Icarus, who with his wings of feather, wood and wax tried to match our glory. He too fell, as will this one- Jarod: “And I’m sure they’ll never be able to catch you in a wrestling match. At least you know Harley won’t be the only threat on Fiesta. Enigma hates you too much not to interfere; I could feel that as the chair smashed into your knee … just as much as I felt the chair myself. I want to stop that happening again, so I’ll be in your corner on Fiesta, I won’t let Enigma hurt you again my friend.” -So he is one of them. We have ones such as this Jarod in our race. Those who feel the pain of the sufferers. These earthbounds’ struggles become ever more interesting. But we have listened enough, so we leave the Victim Jarod, and the not quite earthbound Chance to their struggle, while we soar away, free again in flight-
