Choices

14 Feb 2003

By Chance

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 14 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
We walk down a busy street, eddies of people brushing past us, and bumping into us as we go. We force our way through the maelstrom of bodies and the currents and waves of humanity rolling up against us, trying to reach a certain point ahead. Through this onslaught of commonality we battle against the flow, trying to move onwards, trying to get in front of the next person, fighting to get ahead at another's expense, not caring what we do to others along the way, not caring who we hurt or how. Just trying to get ahead in this great rat race we call life, through the crowds of men and women, just attempting to make our way down our set paths, to get where we need to get to be. Of course it does not have to be this way. We do not have to fight, and struggle, and claw our way onwards. We could accept that life is not about the rat race, not about getting one up on your neighbour, and simply make our way as best we can, letting those who need to get by, go. We have the choice to avoid those who would bump into us, to take the time to step aside, to not care if we get to where we are going five minutes early or late, so long as we get there. In some places in the world it is like that, in some cities and towns and villages in Brazil nobody wears a watch, and so time has to meaning, and when you stop thinking of time as constantly slipping past you, and accept that it is simply there, then you will not care how long a task takes you, or if you must leave a place in which you wish to be at any given time, or if you must sit, not thinking, not moving, not waiting for anything, but just sit for hours on end. Or you might not, and you may just carry on with the race as you have always done, the choice is yours. And that's the point of all this, the idea that you do have a choice, and that you always have a choice. And today it is our choice to be here, in this crowded street, fighting through the sea of angry shoppers, trying to achieve what we set out to achieve. And through the drifts and flows of people we eventually see it: a man with short hair, dyed a dark red, wearing a bright red Hawaiian shirt patterned with white flowers, and loose white trousers, standing in front of a small dingy stall that must have once been many bright vivid colours, blues and reds and greens, but now is faded. Behind the stall is a man whose description is of negligible importance, who is casually shuffling a deck of cards while the man in the Hawaiian shirt, who we know as Chance, watches on intently, absently spinning his coin round his knuckles as he does. The man with the cards stops shuffling and removes three cards from the deck and places them upwards on the stall, we see that they are the Queen of Hearts, the seven of clubs and the three of spades. Chance looks at them carefully, then nods, and the other man turns them over then begins to speak. Man: "You know the rules, you place a bet, then I mix up the cards, if you pick the queen you win double your money, if it's any other card you lose it all. Ready?" Chance nods and puts a fifty down on the stall and the man begins to move the cards, slowly at first, then faster and faster, moving the cards over and round each other so that we can barely keep track of which card is the queen. However, just as we're about to lost sight of it in the blur of the cards, the man slows them down, and leaves the cards facing downwards, in a row. From the close attention which we paid, we can see that the card on the far right is the queen, and we are relieved when Chance picks that one seemingly at random after almost picking one of the others. The man turns the card up and reveals the Queen of Hearts for everyone to see. He tosses Chance his winnings. Man: "Well done! Here's your money, care for another try, I'm sure I'll beat you this time!" Chance nods again, putting the two fifties the man just gave him down on the table as the man begins to move the cards again. This time the cards move much faster than the last, moving literally faster than the eye can see, so that we have completely lost track of which card is which. The man stops the cards again and waits for Chance to take his pick, and Chance simply closes his eyes and puts his hand down on the table, picking the middle card, then turning it over with his eyes closed to reveal again the Queen of Hearts. Man: "Well, I'm impressed, think you can do it again?" Chance tosses down the full two hundred the man just handed to him before speaking: Chance: "How about I make this a little more difficult for myself?" With that he closes his eyes and waits as the man again moves the cards around, still moving them as fast as he can in case Chance is peeking. Eventually he finishes and taps Chance on the shoulder, Chance opens his eyes and immediately places one hand on a card, again the one in the middle. The man turns the card over and is shocked to see that Chance has won again! He gives Chance his money and then goes as if to call someone else to take his place, but Chance removes four more fifties from his wallet and throws them down on the stall with the money the man just gave him saying: Chance: "Just one more, and how about we leave this one totally to fate? -he holds up his coin- I'll close my eyes then when you're finished I'll flip this coin above the cards and whichever one it lands on, I'll pick, and if it doesn't land on any, you keep the money." The man smiles hungrily, his eyes never leaving the money as he nods to Chance, who closes his eyes again. This time while the man is moving the cards, one of them comes close to falling off the table and the man just scoops it up with his palm, bringing the card suspiciously near his sleeve. He taps Chance on the shoulder to open his eyes and Chance raises the coin, looking at the man who nods. Chance flips the coin and it spins over and over in the air, tumbling down to right in the middle of one of the cards with a metallic ting. It landed on it's edge. The man goes to pick up the coin from the card, but Chance moves faster snatching the coin away, before gesturing to the card which the man flips over to again reveal the Queen of Hearts. The man looks shocked and he immediately turns over the other two cards to see the seven of clubs and three of spades just as they should be. The man looks even more shocked and his hand shoots out to grab the money from the table, yelling out as he does: Man: "Lousy cheat, give me that!" But Chance's hand is there first catching the man's in a vice-like grips, twisting his wrist around so that the man grimaces in pain. From the man's upturned wrist we can then see a card protruding from the man's sleeve which Chance plucks out and shows to the crowd that had gathered, before saying softly, almost playfully to the man: Chance: "Next time you try to cheat someone my friend, I would advise you to make sure you can get your little trick right. Or maybe you were just unlucky, either way, I don't think you should try that again." Leaving the man to face the wrath of the crowd that had gathered, including a few policemen, Chance walks off down an empty side-street where we follow him. There, no longer surrounded by inquisitive people, Chance begins to talk. "Choices. We all have them, we all have to make them. So what makes a good or a bad choice? Well that man's choice to gamble against me was in material terms a bad one, as my luck won me his money. And his choice to cheat was definitely a bad one, for one thing he's probably going to be arrested, and for another he had the misfortune to get it wrong and leave the Queen on the table. Lady Luck has a way of arranging games of Chance like that to my favour. And recently I too had a choice to make, I had to choose two men to fight alongside me on Monday Night Meltdown against the WWA Franchise Players: Tracer Bullet, Shaman, and of course Denrol. And just as I have, Denrol too has made his choices. He chose to fight for his wife and his own hate to gain revenge on Kraven in the Cell in the Frozen Hell, and after the match he chose to let his hate destroy Kraven. Denrol chose to attack me at RITC, Denrol chose to turn his back on the fans, and Denrol chose to challenge me and two other men to a match on Meltdown. And so I made my choices, and I have picked the two men that I want standing by my side. Of course I'm not going to tell you who I picked, I want that to be a surprise. I will say this only: Denrol, you have with you the WWA Franchise Players, the people who make up the history, the very past of the WWA as we know it. Three men who have been with the WWA through thick and thin, and have all done everything they had to do. Whereas I chose to build around myself the WWA team of the future, the people who will be the superstars of the next generation of the WWA, and if the past tries to get in our way, you will find out why people are saying I am going to be the next rookie to break out into the big leagues. And they will find out why I am Fortune's Favoured, Fortune's Fool." Fade to black
