I am Not a Trained Monkey Anymore

2 Dec 2004

By Grunge

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This morning I was blessed with nothing to do except mull over the fact that I will have to compete this weekend in the Birthday Brawl. I have already finished my physical training. The mental training though… And. I played my acoustic guitar and sang. The cubby in which I have my computer/home office has really decent reflective qualities. And since I’ve found out about my acceptance into the match, I've pretty much lived here. My neighbor is Bill the turtle. But yes. I played. I sang. "This is the room one afternoon I knew I could love you. And from above you - how I sank into your soul. Into that secret place that no one dares to go." And "And one day we will die, and our ashes will fly from the aeroplane over the sea. But for now we are young let us lay in the sun and count every beautiful thing we can see. Love to be - In the arms of all I'm keeping here with me." And "I want to sing the beauties found in death to pain the ghosts that bind our walls. I want to sunset life before the point is moot. And I want to fall the way that love does." And "Twenty years ago I set out to find the circus. Twenty years now and I realize that's all that's left. I was a race car driver smiling back at you through my rearview. Now you're here waking up while I'm catching my breath." And...the fact that no neighbors were around liberated my lungs allowing me to sing out and nearly in tune. My audience, Bill the turtle, seemed appreciative. Though, I'm sure he really only enjoyed that last bit of lyric - a veiled reference to "The Tortoise and the Hare". I remember gathering in various garages around southeastern Oklahoma with my friends Ronnie and Robert. Jamming out to songs by Weezer, Everclear, Better Than Ezra, Smashing Pumpkins, The Beatles, Pearl Jam, The Velvet Underground, The Presidents of the United States of America, the Stones...I remember bringing in Cliff, Cody and Magilla...and jamming out to Sublime, Red Hot Chili Peppers, Incubus, Deftones, in addition to the others. One mystical summer, I remember setting up mics and amps in Robert and Danny's parent's living room and jamming the original tunes that the brotherhood had written: Me, Joe, Danny, and Robert. I remember going back to Joe and Danny's apartment to record our demos - recording acoustic guitar parts in the closet and vocals in the shower. I remember voicing my frustration with Danny's ego by singing "we are all just trained monkeys" at the end of one of our songs, while playing a Paul McCartney-esque bassline over the handclaps. I remember the night that I wrote the music for "Green", and convincing Danny to keep his second verse because those words made the song so much better than it would have been otherwise. I remember the night that it all came crashing down in a gloriously mad moment of artistic self-righteousness. Danny saying "The crap you're writing sucks, so I'll write all the songs dammit!" He then took his guitar into the other room to sulk, brood and act like a tortured singer/songwriter. I remember early the next morning, packing up my amp, my Les Paul, and my Alvarez acoustic taking them home, removing the strings from both guitars and the bridge from my Les Paul. And I remember not touching those instruments for nearly two years. I remember Joe saying he'd never play in a band again, which was a shame because he was the least pretentious and most unassumingly talented of the entire group. Eventually, after Danny moved away, Joe and I would hang out in his livingroom with his Beatles songbook, trying to imitate the masters. So, I put the strings back on my guitar. But it's not been the same since that summer. When I write tunes, I don't write words. When I write words, I don't write tunes. I leave that to my more talented members of the band Sea Prism. And the point to this is that eventually all things change. No where is this more apparent than in the Birthday Brawl. The winners of the brawl last year may not even come close to succeeding this year. It’s open, the most democratic match in all wrestling. Craven Moore and Tracer Bullet have the same chance of winning as Odium and Joey Edwards. If we fail, if I fail, I have no one to blame but myself. I am not a trained monkey anymore. I will stand on my own feet without anyone’s help. Not my former stable Youth United, not my former tag-team partner Liam Jenson, not even my wife. I will stand. Rock and Roll.
