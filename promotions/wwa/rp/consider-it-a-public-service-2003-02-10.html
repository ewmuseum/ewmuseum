Consider it a Public Service

10 Feb 2003

By Jason Wainwright

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 10 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Jason Wainwright is seen in a dimly lit corner of a gym, punching feverishly at a blue speedbag. It almost looks like one of those training montages from a sports related movie. The light of the moon shines through the window just so that it illuminates the inspired movement of Wainwright's arms striking their target. After a few moments of striking the bag. Wainwright stops. His arms fall to his side, beaded with sweat. To say he is weary is to belittle the apparent jet lag. He throws a towel over his shoulder after making very rudimentary attempts at wiping the sweat from his brow; he then walks over to the wall and turns on the overhead light. We see exactly the extent of which Wainwright;s training has manifested. There are free weights strewn about the floor. Excerise equipment standing idly but unkempt.) Wainwright: I've been here since nine in the morning. The owner shuts this place down at eight o clock. I figure that was about three and a half hours ago. (Wainwright walks over in frame of the camera and sets down on the weight bench. He reaches down at his feet for his sports bottle which he brings up to his lips.) Wainwright: So that equals out to be fourteen and a half hours spent training for an opponent that I, apparently, am not taking seriously. And thats news to both me and my cardiovascular system. You see Shaman while you were busy making a retort to my attack by copping the very idea I used against you, Albeit copping it in such a way that it came off as a thinly veiled attack from a man who was legitimately riled by my lil' satire, I have been training for our encounter rigorously. I have watched every match you have wrestled in the last three years. But, of course, I am not taking this too seriously. (Wainwright drops the sports bottle and gets to his feet. He starts walking towards the camera with an increasing hostility, as if the cameraman were The Shaman himself.) Wainwright: I mean, I've only been sitting at home for the last year watching the WWA and paying for the pay per views out of pocket and analyzing every single motion of every single man that has graced that ring because I have no earthly inclination to get back in that squared circle and reclaim it as MY dominion. I have trained vigorously every day since the one after the 3/5 falls match with Dorion because I don't give a damn about a my future in this business. Shaman, when you say that I don't take you seriously, you flatter yourself. You flatter yourself because you somehow believe that you are exempt from being a stop on my journey to the top. I can't afford not to take you seriously Shaman. Which makes you a very unlucky man. Because you have to come to terms with the fact that every fiber of my being is dedicated to defeating you. And maybe... maybe it's easier for you to believe that I won't be going at you with all the strength in my body. Maybe that helps you sleep at night. But I'm telling you now that those preconceptions are misdirected at best and that you will never be more aware of how honest to goodness sincerely I am taking this match as you will be when you are staring up at the ring lights after being dropped on your head via Jason Wainwright. So I had a little fun at your expense, you said it yourself, the temptation to do so is undeniable. I mean, you're so damn lost in your own self-righteous little world that poking fun of you is about as difficult as poking fun of Michael Jackson during his alcoholic housewife phase. But you'll notice that my little tirade seemed to jar you a little bit there Shaman. I mean I haven't seen you around any gyms lately... I haven't seen you locked in a room studying old tapes of my career... However, I DO seem to recall you making foundationless personal attacks in an effort to save face with the watching public. And as you were doing that... I've been here, training. (Wainwright is face to face with the camera. His eyes are the only thing fully in the frame. And the same fire that has always burned therein, burns still.) Wainwright: Shaman, I'm telling you this now. However accustomed you are to be a sick twisted freak willing to do anything to claim victory will not be enough to defeat me. I've been in the ring with psychos. Hell I used to be one. And uncontrollable rage be damned against a determined man. So all your psychotic melodrama doesn't mean *bleep* ...and it isn't going to save you. Not now. Shaman my friend... (The scene cuts to black.) ...You're in deeper than you'll ever know.
