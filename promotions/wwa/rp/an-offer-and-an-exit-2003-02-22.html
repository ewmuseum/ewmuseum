An Offer and An Exit.

22 Feb 2003

By Denrol & Tracer Bullet

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 22 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
DAY 10 - 10:37 a.m. (We can see the two cats, Extreme and Light Heavyweight, pacing up and down in front of a door. You can hear Tracer Bullet on the telephone in the background.) Light Heavyweight: Remind me again why we are pacing like this? Extreme: I am pacing because I am restless. You are pacing because you saw me pacing and thought it looked like fun. So in short, you are pacing because you are an idiot. Light Heavyweight: Now I remember why US hates you. So who do you think Tracer is talking to? Extreme: As if you could deny that you're pacing because you thought it looked like fun. But I'll give you the benefit of the doubt and say that you're not an idiot, just easily amused. Though being easily amused is a sure sign of... look, just never mind. And how am I supposed to know who he's talking to? I'm not exactly fluent in human, let alone fluent enough to know what the hell he's talking about just from listening to one side of the conversation. Light Heavyweight: Conv... convert? Concord? Conman? Huh? (Extreme visibly winces.) Extreme: Conversation. A discourse between two indivi... (Extreme looks at Light Heavyweight staring at him blankly.) Extreme: Where two people talk back and forth. Kind of like what we're doing now. We are having a conversation. (Light Heavyweight just stares at Extreme.) Light Heavyweight: I think you are an ass. (Light Heavyweight turns and leaves.) Extreme: Don't know what his problem is... I'm a cat. Where he got the idea I was a donkey, I don't know... (The camera pans up to the human level as Denrol walks by and joins Tracer, who is still on the phone.) TB: Hold on a sec, okay? (Tracer cups his hand over the phone and speaks to Denrol.) TB: Yes? What can I help you with? I'm on the phone with my bookie, for crying out loud. Denrol: Gambling is a serious addiction and one you shouldn get treatment for before it's too late. TB: Actually, he was wondering why I haven't been in contact with him for the past several months. And granted, I really should have bet on the Super Bowl... but considering I would have gone with the Raiders, I'm glad I didn't. Denrol: That reminds, you got a call yesterday while you were out. Forgot to tell you. TB: Eh? Denrol: Yeah, some guy with a hick accent, I didn't catch his name, said something about you being a hoss and that someone would be stopping by after 10 AM today to talk to you. TB: Calling me a... oh Jesus, he didn't say I had "educated feet," did he? Denrol: No, I kinda zoned after he said hoss, because I was wondering what a hoss was. Is is releated to a mule? TB: Only half, really. Denrol: Anyway he than mumbled something about don't telll any marks on the net aboutthis and hung up. (Tracer puts his head in his hands just as the doorbell rings.) TB: Please don't tell me that's who I think it is... Denrol: Must be the guy he was talking about. Want to get that, or should I? TB: Well, if the phone call was for me, then I should probably get it... (Tracer hangs up and walks slowly to the front door, the camera following him as he goes. Whomever it is must be a bit inpatient, because they knock again. He than speaks through the door.) Voice: Mr. LeBeaux? TB: If you're going to be that impatient, I don't really want to talk to you. Voice: I'm sorry sir, but I am in a bit of a rush, the boss wants to get this done as quickly as possible. TB: Fine. I'll let you in so I can say no quickly. (Tracer opens the door. At the door is a kid, couldn't be more than 22, standing with a big grin on his face. he is dressed in a suit and tie through they look quite rumbled. In his hand is a briefcase with a couple of scratches on it. The man offers his hand.) Man: Hello there Mr.LeBeaux. I'm Brian Chandler and it's a pleasure to meet you. TB: Before I shake your hand, state your business. Who sent you? BC: I believe my boss spoke to you yesterday. Mr. Ross is quite a busy man and he apologizes that he can't come himself. But I am here to speak for him. We have a great offer for you. TB: (firmly) Let me say it again... state... your business. And when I said who sent you, I need more than "Mr. Ross." Not to mention the fact that no one spoke to me yesterday. This lummox behind me answered it and just gave me the message a few minutes ago. (Denrol waves.) BC: Oh my, this isn't good. Okay, my boss is the Vice President of Talent Relations with World Wrestling Entertainment. I'm here to present an offer for you to sign with WWE. We are aware you are in contract talks with the World Wrestling Alliance at the moment and we would like you to join our team. TB: Not interested. BC: Mr. LeBeaux, I beg to please let me at least present to you the details of our offer. We have a storyline already setup just for you. TB: If it involves Triple H, you're getting a smack in the mouth for even being here. BC: I understand there is some dislike for Triple H in the wrestling community, but that is another matter. No, we already learned our lesson from Scott Steiner. We will not throw anyone right into a main event anymore. TB: All right, just because I'm curious, let's hear it. That's not to say that I'm interested in signing with you whatsoever, mind. (Tracer leads Brian down the long hallway to the main living quarters and motions for him to sit on an ottoman.) BC: Thank you. You have a lovely home. (Denrol follows them, but just leans in the doorway to watch. Tracer and Brian sit down.) TB: I know what my home looks like. What I don't know is the supposed "plan" you guys have for me. Spit it out. BC: Okay. This plan requires a rapid response from you, since we want it to start at our pay-per-view on Sunday. We announce that you've signed with WWE. We put it all over the website, we announce it on Heat before the show, we even are ready to insert some full color ads in newspapers to get the word out. TB: That tells me nothing. You've done similar things before. Continue. BC: You come out to a lot of fanfare on the show. You have an in ring promo telling everyone how you can't wait to get into the ring and that you'll announce which brand you're going to sign with later in the show. The commentators will hype up that question all night. TB: I thought you said you learned your lesson with Steiner? BC: Just wait. This is a good one believe me. Near the end, we go to the backstage to get your announcement, but the camera crew finds you out cold in your locker room. Someone attacked you in the back. EMTs wake you up and someone asks you what happened. You tell them you don't remember. You actually don't remember anything at all, not even your name. You see the potenial here, right? You wander back and forth between the shows, trying to find out who attacked you and who you really are. You remember how to wrestle, but that's all you remember. For the first program, we'll provide a hint that Paul Heyman may know, so you feud with the Big Show. (Tracer stands up and beckons toward the door.) TB: Well, thanks for dropping by. BC: What is wrong Mr. LeBeaux? Surely you can see the opportunity here. You can work some real money programs with this. It'll be huge. Just think of the merchandise. "Who KOed TB?" TB: Think of the similarities to what one of my personal wrestling role models did about ten years ago. Think of how it will look if a supposed "big name" is beaten so badly on his first night that he can't remember who he is. And most importantly... think about the kind of matches the Big Show has... (Tracer shivers.) TB: Honestly, why you people won't let him just be his friendly, good-natured, funny-as-hell self is beyond me. BC: No one is going to remember what someone did ten years ago Hell, the creative team doesn't bother to keep track of what happened 10 weeks ago. You don't want the big show, how about A-Train? (Tracer closes his eyes and appears to be counting backward from ten.) BC: And this is just Plan A. Plan B is a super program to Wrestlemania with Triple H TB: Out. BC: So you would of course lose in the end, but come on, you're just a former WWA guy. How much can you expect? But listen, you really need to stop and consider this. This is a huge chance for you. TB: I said "Out." BC: Really Trent, do you want to spend your life as a nobody in a nothing federation? TB: Better to be a star in a "nothing federation" than to work with Triple H, let alone lose to him. But if you really want your boss's future son-in-law to be crippled, go ahead. Continue pursuing a contract signing. Just know that no matter what kind of disciplinary action is taken against me by the likes of you, I will be welcomed as a hero anywhere else. BC: Just because Triple H is a better worker than 99% of the wrestling world, doesn't mean you should hate him. TB: And just by those words alone, I should do the same to you. Rather, I will now forcibly evacuate you from the premises... (Tracer grabs the back of Brian's suit jacket and drags him to his feet.) BC: You're making a big mistake Trent. (Denrol chuckles and moves out of the way.) TB: Somehow I doubt it. I've seen what you do with ACTUAL talent in your league. Does the name "Chris Jericho, jobber to the stars" ring a bell? BC: Jericho got his chance and he dropped the ball. (Tracer brgins to pull the guy to the door.) TB: Sure sure, that's what you want everyone to think. But we all know better. It's that 'roid-popping, ego-tripping, talent-screwing, boss's-daughter-f***ing, swelled-headed prick that knocked the ball from his hands. And I'm not talking about Steiner. BC: You haven't even looked at the numbers we're talking about yet. (Tracer opens the front door and throws the unwelcome guest onto the lawn.) TB: You want numbers? How about number one? (Tracer gives him the bird.) BC: You're going to regret this Trent. Our offer won't be as generous next time. TB: You think having to put up with LeVesque's posturing and backstage politics is generous? I'd much sooner go to NWA if I was going to leave the WWA at all. And let me add that I'm not. BC: Trent, we ARE sport entertainment, period. TB: That's true. You are sports entertainment, period. But you know what? BC: Yes? TB: We... are WRESTLING... (Tracer slams the door shut. There's some applause from Denrol.) TB: What's that for? Denrol: Nice man. I was wondering how long you would going to stand for that BS. TB: I wasn't standing for it to begin with. But you know my rules about being courteous to people who are actually inside my house. But once he started praising that damned egomaniac... Denrol: Right. Hey it could have been worse. TB: HOW? Denrol: I don't know, give me a week and I may have something. (Tracer laughs.) TB: Yeah, that's what I thought. And listen, pitching that guy onto my lawn gave me an idea... Denrol: You want to go to the batting cages? TB: Not as such. It's just that I realized you've been here longer than the week you said you would be. Your house should be painted, or fumigated, or repaired or whatever by now, yes? Denrol: Well yeah, but you know I think I should keep an eye on the new HOP, make sure everything runs smoothly TB: But nothing. You've got a big match coming up Monday night that I'm NOT your partner for. You should prepare for it somewhere else. I let you linger around a few days longer because of Alexandra, but I do think it's high time you packed up. Denrol: Fine, we'll go. Not that I'm worried about this Global Warfare thing. My team has it in the bag. TB: With two members of the House of Pain and that do-nothing Kash on your team? Pff... if you say so. Frankly, I've got my money on the South. World Champion, number one contender, and two big waves of the future, both of which have beaten me before. And the one gave you a damn good run for your money as well. Denrol: Grunge & Eddie, the wave of the future? Give me a break. I'm surprised those two bozos can figure out how to put on pants. TB: You're entitled to your opinion. But remember that you were already a veteran when I showed up, too. Denrol: No I wasn't. I was still in my rookie year. TB: Rookie year, sure. But only just. Denrol: You just won't want to admit you're getting older. TB: Sure I do. Every day, I get closer to retiring. And that could be a real breath of fresh air, actually. Denrol: What, you don't want to do this anymore? TB: Sometimes I wonder. It would be so much easier to just let it all go sometimes, wouldn't it? To walk away without looking back. Besides, just the fact that the WWE was going out of their way to recruit me makes me wonder if I haven't lost all the talent I ever had. Denrol: Well you can't quit. TB: I know. I've never even tried, and I already know that. Why, you ask? Because I've seen you try to quit time and time again, and it hasn't lasted once. Denrol: Not what I meant, but gee thanks. TB: Any time. Denrol: I meant you can't quit because I haven't beaten you yet. TB: All the more reason I should bugger off quickly, I should think. After all, it'll be really shameful if I leave after losing to YOU. (Tracer grins sarcastically.) Denrol: One of these days I'm going to get sick of your crap and just beat you up. TB: Hey, don't hand me that after I've had to put up with all your bullshit for over a week. Jesus, I still can't believe you tried to sell my car... and set my kitchen on fire... (Denrol rolls his eyes.) Denrol: You and that stupid car. (Denrol turns and leaves the room. Tracer shrugs.) TB: Like I said, sentimental value. But you know, it could have been a lot worse. The first words out of my mouth that day could have been, "Dude, where's my car?" (Tracer takes off in the same direction as Denrol.) Tyler: Christ, those two never let me get a word in edgewise... (Fade to black.)
