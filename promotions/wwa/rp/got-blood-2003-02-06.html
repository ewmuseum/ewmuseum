GOT BLOOD?

6 Feb 2003

By Jason Wainwright

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 6 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Jason Wainwright walks down the street under the mask of evening. The roads aren't yet empty but the sidewalks are barren. Rising just above the skyline in the hoary background is the Space Needle, conspicuous in it's mere presence. Jason Wainwright is home. He walks eyes affixed to the cold concrete below his feet, his hands plunged into the homely warmth of his father's old pilot's jacket. He looks up, and speaks.) Wainwright: You know, normally people with any measure of celebrity can't even walk down the street in their hometown without being accompanied by a mob of screaming fans begging for an autograph, and yet… somehow, I manage to take my evening walks relatively undisturbed. Oh occasionally I'll get the impressionable teenage girl who thinks, from behind, I resemble Wes Sanders, despite being two inches taller and outweighing him about thirty pounds, but other than that I have relatively little interaction with the fans. They DO tell me I'm big in prison. Apparently, rapists and murderers think of me as some sort of demi-God. I used to get a few fan letters from guys like Paco Gunther in Kansas who beat little girls and wore their scalps like scarves in the springtime. For a while, I found it interesting, now it worries me and I have those letters filtered out before reaching my mailbox. You see, I'm tired of being considered a sick, heartless freak. I'm sick of being thought of as a soulless mercenary. People see me and they see a ghost of a man who never lived. But what people don't understand is that to be as misguided and impassioned with hate as I once was, requires a heart and a soul larger than most people can fathom. It requires an intense desire that a normal man cannot retain within himself. And such a desire can make a man dangerous and determined and, under the right circumstances, unspeakably evil. But it can swing the other way as well. As horrible a person as I have the capacity for being, the capacity is just as large for me to do good in my life. I suppose no one can understand that without being shown it first. I suppose the lives I've crippled and the career's I've ended cannot be peaceably asked to believe that I can be anything other than a twisted freak. And I am not asking the men in the WWA to wipe away their preconceptions and welcome me back with open arms. I am, however, asking for the opportunity to show them and I am asking for them to be open-minded enough to accept the possibility that a man can change. My quest begins next week on Fiesta. And it begins with The Shaman. Shaman, you seem to have made a name for yourself with this 'Got Blood?' campaign. (Jason Wainwright's hands emerge from his pockets. He brings with his right hand a sewing needle. He hold his left index finger parallel with the camera and gently pricks it with the needle. As we watch a crimson drop of blood festers from the wound and falls to the sidewalk below.) And I'm here to tell you Shaman that I HAVE got blood. I've felt it pouring into my eyes from open wounds on my forehead. I've felt it seep from lacerations on my back and on my chest. From my arms and legs. I have tasted it's bitter flavor as it fills up my mouth. I have watched it drip from my brow onto both the mat and the men who brought it forth. I HAVE got blood. I've got it running through my body as we speak. Protecting me from the Seattle cold, feeding me. Oh, I've got blood all right. It is what fuels the adrenaline that rushes through my body as I stand behind the curtain, ready to step into the ring. It is what regulates my heart to keep it from exploding with anticipation as I stare intently on my opponent across the squared circle. And Shaman, I have no doubts that you intend to bleed me for every drop of that blood. You intend to taste this very same blood I am speaking of. And you very well might. But I have my doubts as to whether or not you are ready to taste of my blood. The blood that runs through my veins has caused more heartache and done more damage than you can imagine. The responsibility that comes with tasting this blood is more than I believe you can bear. Ask Shane Dorion, he tasted this blood and it turned him into an animal. For those few minutes after feeling its corrosive bitterness on his tongue, it's caustic acidity, Shane Dorion was less than human. And yet, as inflamed as he was by the blood running through my veins, he still failed against me. Because his heart and his soul did not have the capacity to hold the desire that accompanies this blood. So Shaman, I am telling you that I have blood. But the question to be asked is… do you really want it? (Jason Wainwright slides his hands back into his pockets and turns 180 degrees away from the camera and begins walking away into the murky dusk.) ~Fade to Black~
