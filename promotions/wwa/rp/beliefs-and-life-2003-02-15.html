Beliefs and life

15 Feb 2003

By Nicholas Adams

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 15 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Some people say that life is just a continuous series of events that are interrupted by periods of unconsciousness and repetition of certain behaviors for the first hour after this period of sleep and the final hour before this selfsame period. Others say that that's a load of bull, and that life is actually not continuous at all. Instead, they say that life centers on a certain destiny, and if a person does not try to disobey it, then their lives become a list of small and pointless adventures that lead them to that destiny. Nicholas Adams is not a man that believes in either theory. Instead, he refers to life as getting whatever you can while your mind functions, your heart beats, your lungs pump air, and your muscles can move your body. He admires people with Muscular Dystrophy and Lou Gehrig's disease because some of these people try to make the best of their disabilities and keep living their life to the fullest. For Nicholas Adams, that kind of life is one he envies. He knows that it's a sin, but he lost his faith in God months ago. These people live their lives to the fullest. In the meanwhile, he just walks around, doing his job, talking about what he's going to do in front of millions of people on a television audience. And for some reason, he finds that he's missing something. True, the previous day was Valentine's Day, and he had broken up with his girlfriend of two months the week before. It would have given any other man cause enough to go to a local bar and get themselves plastered enough to the point that they didn't remember their own name. Nick isn't such a man. Instead, he is the kind to ignore that kind of emotional pain. Of course, he still misses his girlfriend. He put in a personal advertisement for the Valentine's Day issue of the recent "Meltdown Magazine". Yet he shows none of these thoughts. Instead, he sits in his office, working on a laptop computer with the WWA logo on it. What he is doing is not apparent, but it doesn't really matter. He's stoic, as usual. His face, which tends to be as it is now, give away nothing except that he's concentrating on his work. Suddenly, Adams stands up and walks out of his office and into the dark basement that is over his head. He has to catch a quick ride to Ottawa, and Adams figures it would take him a while. The scene is sort of eerie. Adams turns back to his office, grabs a flashlight, and then turns off the office light. The man that calls himself the Rainmaker then turns around and turns on the flashlight. The entire place is extremely dark. The beam of light makes it even worse. With the presence of the light, the darkness seems doubly dark. With something to contrast with it, it seems as if the entire world, save for that one small ray, is in an eternal night. Nick looks at his watch, and ironically, in this artificial midnight, his watch says that it is 1205 hours. Apparently, he has a military watch. The heating system sounds off in the distant reaches of this subterranean prison. The sound makes the room seem a bit more eerie. Some smoke goes through the beam, making it seem as if Nick is walking through a misty marsh during a new moon. Adams just keeps walking, his shoes making only the slightest of sounds from their contact with the building's solid foundation. Finally, he reaches the stairs, shines the flashlight up the darkened rise, and begins to climb. After about a minute, Nick comes to the main level. He closes the basement door behind him, makes sure that it's locked, and walks past the front desk. The administrator notices Nick.) "Happy belated Valentine's Day," she says cheerfully. Nick pauses, turns around, and replies, "Thank you. You too." (Nick then turns away from the administrator and continues out of the front day, thinking to himself, ~Too bad I'm not Catholic, and I don't really like the commercialization of love~. Nick goes to the parking lot across the street. Of course, it's a cold day where the WWA Tower is located, but that doesn't really seem to faze Adams. In fact, his attire is only some motorcycle pants, a leather jacket, gloves, and a knitted cap. As he walks up to the parking lot and becomes more purposeful in his direction, it becomes apparent why he is wearing this. He is approaching a Harley Davidson Fat Boy classic motorcycle. It's red, white, and blue, painted in waves from front to back in that order, though it isn't made to resemble the US flag as much as it appears to be colors that Adams chose for a paint job. He pulls a key chain from his pocket, presses a button, then steps onto the bike and puts his key into the ignition. After putting on his helmet, he fires up the 1.5 liter engine, revs it a couple times, switches into gear, and exits the parking lot, speeding along a road to the industrial area of town.) ABOUT 30 MINUTES LATER... Of course, Nick knows where he's going. After all, with the rising prices of gasoline, one cannot afford to get lost, even if you make $180,000 dollars a year just for going down to a wrestling ring, taking and giving physical punishment, and tolerating everything that goes on backstage. Of course, a person can still afford to go on pleasure trips. This is apparently where Nicholas Adams is going. He arrives at a warehouse that seems to have been abandoned for a while. But there seem to be a lot of cars and motorcycles parked near the garage. After Nick parks his bike and takes off his helmet, he takes out an electric guitar that wasn't previously visible. He then walks into the warehouse door and lets it shut behind him. Inside, there's an underground band playing. They're dressed up like Jimmy Eat World, and they're just finishing a song titled, "If You Don't, Don't". The final chord plays, and the crowd goes wild. The lead singer looks around and grabs the microphone out of its stand. The fans go crazy again, but quiet down just enough just so that they can hear him. The singer looks around at the crowd, then looks straight at Nicholas Adams and says, "Well, ladies and gentlemen, he may be an unexpected guest, but he's one hell of a guitarist! I invited him to come today to listen, but he apparently came prepared!" The audience cheers, expecting something that will rock their world. "Ladies and gentlemen," the lead singer starts, "Now joining the Cautioners, a tribute band to Jimmy Eat World, you know him from the World Wrestling Alliance, and though he doesn't have his own band..." the man continues. The fans sigh, having built up energy thinking that they were about to see Grunge. The front man keeps going. "As I said, though he doesn't have his own group, he definitely can rock! His name is...NICHOLAS ADAMS!" The fans break out into a loud cheer once more, realizing that it was rare for a man that is to appear on a show that same night to appear at an underground band concert. Nick walks up, carrying his home-built guitar, and the fans cheer. Nick sets up, plugs in to the same equipment that the lead guitarist, who is not the lead singer, is using. They then go back and talk to one another before playing the first chords of "Bleed American". The lead singer and Adams sing the main song, with the rest of the band adding their voices when appropriate. "I'm not alone cause the TV's on yeah. I'm not crazy cause I take the right pills everyday. And rest, clean your conscious, Clear your thoughts with speyside, with your grain. Clean your conscious, clear your thoughts with speyside. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Tune in and we can get the last call. Our lives, our coal. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Sign up it's the picket line or the parade. Our lives. I'm not alone cause the TV's on yeah. I'm not crazy cause I take the right pills everyday. And rest, clean your conscious, Clear your thoughts with speyside and your grain. Clean your conscious, clear your thoughts with speyside. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Tune in and we can get the last call. Our lives, our coal. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Sign up it's the picket line or the parade Our lives, our coal. (I bled the) greed from my arm. Won't they give it a rest now?" Adams and the lead guitarist go into a huge improvised guitar solos. These aren't included in the actual Jimmy Eat World album, but as a non-profit tribute band, they basically can do whatever they want. After the crowd goes insane, Nick and the lead singer finish off the song. "Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Tune in and we can get the last call. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt, our hearts littering the topsoil. Sign up it's the picket line or the parade. Our lives, our coal. Our hearts littering the topsoil. Tune in and we can get the last call. Salt, sweat, sugar on the asphalt, our hearts littering the topsoil. Sign up it's the picket line or the parade. Our lives." The crowd goes crazy in appreciation for Nick's participation with the concert. He bows, then turns out to the crowd and grabs a microphone. "Ladies and gentlemen!" Adams says. "I know that you like to rock, and I know that you must be interested in the WWA. After all, you live in WWA Headquarters!" The fans cheer loudly. "And you know what? Grunge thinks that he can just go all out against me and expect to win! He expects to rock my world!" Adams shouts. "But every single one of you knows that I am the only person in the next of my matches that can truly rock, both inside and outside the ring!" The fans cheer yet again, and Nick shakes hands with each of the members of the tribute band before getting back on the microphone. "Thank you for having me here, and thanks to the band for letting me do this! I have to go to Ottawa so that I make it for Fiesta, but I know that deep down, you will be watching me go down the ramp on that show and prove to the world that I deserve the Television title!" the Rainmaker shouts. With that, he gives the international sign for rock and walks off the stage, giving out high fives and a couple autographs as he exits. After walking out the door, He leans against the garage door of the warehouse. He does it silently so that nobody inside the warehouse can hear him. He then looks out and around the buildings surrounding him. "To think that there are people willing to come all the way out here just to see a band. No wonder people say the music business is alive and well. Sort of like how I'm alive and well, despite the threats and musings of Grunge. That man is definitely going to find and interesting day ahead of him. I'm going to fight him on Fiesta, and regardless of whether I win or lose, I'm fighting Grunge again, TV title or not. Seems sort of redundant. Sort of like me repeated matches with Shaman. They're all variations of the same thing. He and I are in a ring, either as partners or opponents, and that's it. Still, Grunge cannot be underestimated. The man definitely deserves credit for his musical recognition and ability. And the fact that he follows the belief of John Donne, which states that no man is an island, points to his intelligence. Good thing he's the same size as I am. This should be an interesting couple of days for me..." With that, Nicholas Adams, a man who does not believe in destiny or continuity, just living life to the fullest, walks over to his motorcycle, slides on his jacket, puts on his helmet, and walks towards his motorcycle. After storing his guitar, he revs up the guitar and drives off into the distance, still thinking about what to do next.
