What's more worrying than Global Warfare and Denrol ... a girl

22 Feb 2003

By Chance

Item 6 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 22 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The scene opens in a cosy setting. A small fire crackles gently in the old oak fireplace, complete with a blackened iron poker to one side. Everything in the room seems in place, nothing jars, everything is in perfect accordance with everything else in the room, everything has its own place and its own space. The plush armchairs, the fancy sofa, the even fancier flat screen plasma TV, they all seem to be perfectly comfortable, and perfectly in place where they are. But something here is out of place. Or rather someone. Of the three people in this room, only one doesn’t seem to belong, both in the way he is dressed, and in the way he acts. His bright red Hawaiian shirt and loose white trousers clash with the sombre decorations of the room, while his right foot taps nervously on the floor, making a kind of rustle whenever it brushes the hardwood floor that grates on the serenity of the scene in front of us. In fact Chance has all the symptoms of someone who is very nervous indeed. Sprawled in his armchair, we can see the sweat on his palms from here, and every other moment he glances quickly towards the door, as if waiting for something or someone to come in, then shifts his gaze back to the fireplace and watches the logs crackle and burn under their heat. In the sofa to the right of Chance and his armchair lie two people, a man and a woman, cuddled up together and not saying a word, simply enjoying the feeling of having a warm, tender body pressed up against your own. They too seem in sync with the rest of the room, and by the rings on their fingers we can guess that they are married. As our view shifts slightly we begin to see their faces, we don’t recognise the woman, but we recognise the man as Jarod Crow, Chance’s best friend, guitarist in Chance’s band “Forlorn Hope”, and former EWA Euromerican Champion. He’s also a man we haven’t seen since Enigma injured him in his guest appearance as Chance’s partner on Meltdown about a month ago. From this we realise that the woman is his wife, Rebecca Crow. Rebecca glances towards Chance and gives a slight chuckle, pointing towards him for her husband to see, and Jarod smiles affectionately at Chance’s discomfort. Then Rebecca begins to speak in a playful, teasing kind of voice. Rebecca: “Ah, look at him, he’s all nervous.” Chance looks up from one of his frequent glances towards the door and glares at Rebecca and Jarod for a moment, then speaks very seriously, obviously slightly hurt by her joviality. Chance: “You think this is funny? In a few moments a crazy woman is going to walk through that door, and make me seem like a complete idiot! I didn’t ask for this! All I wanted was a quiet weekend with my friends seeing as the WWA was performing in Canada and this is where you guys vacation. I didn’t bargain for the fact that you were bringing a total psycho with you!” v Jarod and Rebecca look at each other knowingly, sharing a slight grin at Chance’s tirade. Jarod: “Oh come on, she’s not that bad. You have to admit she’s funny, in a crazy sort of way.” Chance: “That’s the problem, she’s unlike any person I’ve ever met! She’s totally compulsive, she’s happy all the time, she ever seems to take anything seriously and she comes up with the most random comments you have ever heard for no apparent reason!” Again Jarod and his wife share a knowing look, as those who are in a relationship are wont to do when seeing a matchmaking opportunity. Jarod: “You know, if I were you I’d be worried about your career right now. You’ve got global warfare coming up soon, plus that six-man tag against the Franchise Players in a week, you’ve got your work cut out for you.” Chance: “Listen, Global Warfare I can handle. The Franchise Players I can handle. The Greatest of all frigging Earth Time I can bloody well handle! That’s all just wrestling, all I have to do is go out there and fight them, which I know I can do, and Lady Luck will look after me! In a match as chaotic as Global Warfare, my luck is bound to pull through, and Denrol, well, he needs to learn that respect is a two way thing. He lost my respect when he attacked me, but I guess he thinks I never earned his, so that’s what I’ve got to do. But with her … I don’t know where I stand, she has me totally wrong-footed every time I go to say something to her.” Rebecca: “I don’t know what all this fuss is over Denrol, we’ve been watching his more recent promos with Tracer Bullet, and it doesn’t seem like he’s gone off the deep end like everybody keeps saying.” Chance: “I know, I saw them too. And that makes things a hell of a lot harder for me.” Jarod: “How so?” Chance: “Well, before, when I thought he had totally lost it, that his anger had pushed him over the edge, and he was out to get all rookies, well I knew where I stood and what I had to do, everything seemed so simple, so in place, I knew that I had to simply beat the bad guy, show him that I did respect him, and that I deserve his respect. But now it’s harder. Now I know it’s all just about respect, and up until RITC I had genuine respect for the guy, for all the top guys! But I’ve lost that respect somewhere, and it’s a lot harder to justify that now that I know he can still be a decent guy.” Jarod: “So in a way it’s a good thing you have Lucy to distract you from all that.” Chance: “Believe me, I’d rather confront a hundred Denrols, or worse, Jackson Kraven, than face her again. She’s just so totally strange, so unreserved, so …” Rebecca: “So unlike Laura?” Chance: “I wish you wouldn’t keep bringing her up.” Jarod: “What do you expect us to do buddy? You said yourself that she was the love of yourself, the person you judged all other women against. Well, it seems like you’re still doing that.” Rebecca: “And I for one think it’s sweet that you’re getting so nervous about a woman you dislike so much.” Chance shoots Jarod a warning look as if to say “how much did you tell her” and Jarod simply grins back conspiratorially before adding: Jarod: “A woman you described as being: ‘totally perfect in her craziness’” Chance (spluttering): “Damn it, you little … you promised you wouldn’t say anything!” At that moment the object of their discussion walks in the door, equally out of sync with her surroundings as Chance is in dress, she is wearing a short tie dyed purple dress over denim shorts. She is quite pretty, and moves in a way that is so full of life and energy that she almost glows, although that could be a side-effect of her excessively bright dress. Chance takes one look at her and immediately stops slouching in the chair, drawing his back up straight, if possible making him look even more awkward than before. Jarod and Rebecca smile to each other again, as the girl who was called Lucy if memory serves sits down in the chair beside him before speaking out in a resonant, musical voice, heavy with Leicestershire accent: Lucy: “Wouldn’t say anything about what?” The scene begins a slow fade to black, and we just pick up Chance mumbling something under his breath that only we pick up: “I’d rather face the whole bloody House of Pain …”
