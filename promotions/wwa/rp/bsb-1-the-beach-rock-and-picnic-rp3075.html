BSB #1: The Beach Rock and Picnic

26 Apr 2006

By Danny Vista

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Time: 5:59.42pm Location: The Vista Outing The shining, red light spread across the sky as if the Lord himself was spreading a message of peace. The sheer quietening power this light had was phenomenal. At first, it was so distracting I didn’t even recognize what it was; a thought I first had was that it might even have been a UFO of some sort. Then I looked at the glowing circular figure in the centre of it all and I knew what it was; the Sun. I was shocked that something that far away could cause something so naturally beautiful so close to us. And yet, it was happening. We were all sat on a grassy hill for our monthly family get-together. Most families would merely sneer at such a thing, but in my family it was tradition that we came to this hill for an evening of picnicking, followed by a talk about what we had all been doing with our lives. Up until now, I didn’t even bother coming to these things; I thought they were stupid, pointless and, perhaps most of all in a personal sense, embarrassing. The picnicking was fine; everybody were just talk about anything and everything that had nothing to do with Danny. The problem was when the customary ‘what we had been doing with our lives’ talks came along. I would sit down and tell everybody that I had been looking around for a job but had not yet been successful. That would be followed by a simple ‘good for you, Danny’ and then they were off. It was the most degrading thing I ever went through. But, times have changed since then. Now, I can tell everybody exactly what I’d been doing and I would say it a lot. Putting it how my Auntie Melissa once said, I would shove it much further up my family’s arses than their noses were. But, as it turned out, this night obviously didn’t turn at as well as I had expected it to. Things went wrong, as they always tend to when it comes to my personal life. This was the first time I’d been to one of our family get-togethers since I had joined the WWA, and certainly been the first time since winning the Television Title, let alone retaining it successfully on more than one occasion. To me, it proved that I was not a one trick pony; I could (and will) keep this title for a long time. And, unsurprisingly, I had no problem telling this to my stubborn family. At one point I was particular proud; my Great something Tony came up to me and, with an ‘I expect nothing here’ kind of look on his face and said, “So, Danny, what have you been up to lately?” Then he simply sighed and tapped his foot. And, as anybody would in this situation, I made the most of it. “Well, not much really. I joined the WWA, wrestled five matches and haven’t lost in any of them. I won the Television Championship and have now held it for three weeks, longer after Bourbon Street Brawl. Of course, compared to your month of advising people financially, I can hardly compete with you. My paycheque is at least three times the size of yours but, in the end, that isn’t what matters, is it?” At this point, I didn’t even bother trying to hide the laughs. I had my head in the clouds: to be more successful than anybody else in my family was a cruelly satisfying feeling. Of course, what goes up must always come down and, in this case, it came down with a mighty crash. “So, erm…you’re paid to fight?” My relative had brought up a point that I had never really considered. I had always thought that being this respected in the wrestling world had meant everybody else would respect me as well; I hadn’t considered those who weren’t wrestling fans. Do I have as good a career as I thought? Am I respected, or is it all just something built up in my imagination? I clearly had some thinking to do. I bowed my head in a slight shame; my confidence had been wiped away in a mere few seconds. I had only ever started wrestling to impress my family, and it turns out they’re not even impressed with that, no matter how much I’m earning. Time: 8:01:24pm Location: The Beach Rock In Devon, the place where Vista was brought up, the Beach Rock was legendary among the local folk. The Beach Rock was first washed ashore in 1806 when a mighty storm supposedly raged on and on for three nights straight; it was then that the strength was gathered to push this huge boulder onto the beach. It was approximately 11 feet high and about 10 feet wide. But the strange thing is nothing to do with this. The rock that was washed ashore was, apart from a few grazes and bumps, a perfect spherical shape. To the side of it were a few smaller boulders, about the height of Vista’s lower leg, which were all the same sort of shape. How this happened is unknown, and yet it is also irrelevant. Approximately fifty years ago, citizens who had sat atop the rock for a few minutes had recorded feelings of inner peace, relaxation and stress relief, whilst another person said that “all of their troubles just seemed to wash away within minutes of sitting or laying on the rock.” Since then, the rock had become a famous tourist attraction, perhaps one of the most famous of Devon. Many people have laid down on the rock, merely hoping for these same sort of feelings and, before long, it was well known as the place people came to sit when they were feeling some sort of stress or anxiety. And, unsurprisingly enough, this is why Vista came. He sat right at the edge, looking on the verge of slipping off whilst he rested his head against the arm, which in turn was resting against his thigh. He rubbed his nose, before lying back and putting his hands behind his head. He spoke, but the sentence sounded more like a long, drawn out sigh than anything else. “Why are they never satisfied?” He drew out the sentence and, as the waves rubbed against the bottom of the rock beneath him, it sounded as if the words were relaxingly melting into the cold, dark sea that lay ahead. It was this that actually did make him relax; it was soothing. Something about the view of the dead, lifeless ocean made Danny Vista think seriously. Without this huge rock, he would be struggling for his life in the depths of the sea, nobody in sight to do anything about it. And, for some reason, Vista began to interpret the sea as his family. Both were just sitting there. They were watching, listening and waiting for a time they could bear down on him, wanting him to feel the full force of their wrath. But, then Vista began to wonder, what was his rock? Was it his recent girl Laura? Was it the money he was earning? Of course, after thinking about it, Vista knew that these were both preposterous ideas for that question. In a moment’s realisation, Vista knew that the only thing holding him up from the drowning sea was the WWA. The undefeated career, the Bourbon Street Brawl opportunity and, most of all, the Television Championship. After BSB, it would be four weeks he would have held it, and he was damn proud of that too. Andy McMirra. Silence. Belial Kahn. Nick Adams. Kyle James. They were the members of Danny Vista’s current hit list, and just whispering them out to the horizon made his spine curl with delight. Come Bourbon Street Brawl, he wanted to see how many more people could be added to this list. As unlikely as it seemed, Danny Vista thought he had an outside chance of getting somewhere in this brawl, no matter what the columns, the articles and the magazines may say. That moment, he pictured himself pinning the last person in the Bourbon Street Brawl, pictured himself rising to his feet and pictured himself taking the opportunity of the title shot, winning the World Heavyweight Championship. And yet, the voices kept appearing in Vista’s head saying, “It’s impossible.” But Vista knew different. Impossible is different to improbable and, when the situation is improbable, there’s always a chance, no matter how small. Vista smiled to himself in the knowledge that there was a chance of victory, no matter how small it was. He walked across the unstable, wooden bridge that led from the side path to the Beach Rock and, as he walked away, he needn’t even have bothered saying goodbye to his family. As he so rightly thought earlier, they were the sea trying to drown him, and what he really cared about was the rock trying to keep him up. The WWA.
