Chapter 2: The principle of the offensive “To secure ourselves against defeat lies in our own hands, but the opportunity of defeating the enemy is provided by the enemy himself. Thus the good fighter is able to secure himself against defeat, but cannot make certain of defeating the enemy.” – Sun Tzu The second step in a great battle plan, Chance is to decide on the way to attack your enemy. You must see your enemy. I can see you in my sights Chance. You are going to feel the wrath of a methodical plot to attack. Chance, I am a superior warrior than you. As such, I will make certain that you cannot defeat me. Chance, you may have beaten me before in our other battles, but that was in the past. I have defeated you before, but just like my losses, those are in the past and are meaningless in this battle. The only thing that those battles used to show, was to show that there were mistakes made by myself. I have learned from them. For you see that is what makes me a stronger and more intelligent man. Chance, I shall attack your weaknesses. I have exposed them before and I shall do it once again. Chance, you are weak. You may not believe that fact, but you are not as strong as you think. You are not as strong as me. You cannot defeat me. “Security against defeat implies defensive tactics; ability to defeat the enemy means taking the offensive. Standing on the defensive indicates insufficient strength; attacking, a superabundance of strength.” – Sun Tzu I shall not be on the defensive Chance. In football, they say defense wins championships, however in battle, being on the defensive can only contain the damage that has been inflicted. Chance, you will try and contain the damage, but there is no way you can realistically do it. I will be able to take everything that you throw at me and multiply that by one hundred and that’s how much you can throw at me and still not be able to beat men. Chance, mark my words, I will defend myself admirably and beat you. Nothing Can Stop Me To be continued…
