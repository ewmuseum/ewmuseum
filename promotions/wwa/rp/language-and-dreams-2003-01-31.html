Language and Dreams

31 Jan 2003

By Grunge

Item 3 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 31 Jan 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
FADE IN (Grunge is at home watching TV. He fastforwards to one part of the film.) NARRATOR ...and it is in the humble opinion of this narrator that this is not just "Something That Happened." This cannot be "One of those things..." This, please, cannot be that. And for what I would like to say, I can't. This Was Not Just A Matter Of Chance. Ohhhh. These strange things happen all the time. Grunge-Magnolia. I watched both of your Insiders. I listened to you spout of meaningless buzzwords Shaman and watched you Adams, a man who stinks of desperation. I started drifting during the Insider, as I tend to do. When I drift off, I drift off into a deeper examination of the discussion that is taking place. It's just a tendency that I have. I began to think about how arbitrary language is; how two words which are strikingly similar could have totally different meanings. It's all in the context and how the word is pronounced. (Grunge goes to the kitchen and pours a cup of water.) Grunge-The greatest challenge in learning a language is developing an ear for it. One must, in order to attain true understanding, hear the inflections and stresses of native speakers. If ever there could be hope of a universal language, it would be through cinema. The rules of language apply to film, true. However this medium, rather than using inflection and stresses in voice or written word, uses visual referants and significants; key symbols that interact with synapses in the viewer's brain to elicit understanding. Understanding, in many cases, could be brought about without vocalization of conflict. It is for this reason that I feel cinema could still produce viable silent films. If not film then wrestling. Both show language, tell a story through action. That's why I have a passion for it. Not to hurt people like you Shaman or you Payne. And not like you Adams....well whatever it is you do. (Grunge downs his drink.) Grunge-"I think we dream so we don't have to apart so long. If we're in each other's dreams, we can play together all night long." -- Calvin and Hobbes. Heh. I love that damn cat. We all dream boys. I may be the dreamer here but Sunday my dreams will come true. Rage in The Cage and TV title is my first step and it will happen. (Grunge laughs.) Grunge-And you have Grunge's guarentee. FADE OUT
