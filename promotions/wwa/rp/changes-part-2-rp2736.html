Changes Part 2

21 Oct 2005

By Jonathon Winters

Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Alienated So it happened. I was filmed providing morale support to a woman that I respect and owe my life to. There’s nothing I can do about it now and given the opportunity I would do it again in a heartbeat. I have nothing to be ashamed of and I have done nothing wrong, yet I hear rumblings from my various sources within the WWA hierarchy that some action will be taken as a resultant of what happened. Apparently, they’re upset because I appeared on another promotions television programme. I don’t really understand what the problem is. I didn’t speak badly about the company, I didn’t speak badly about any particular wrestler and I sure as hell didn’t trash the WWA title live on AWC Fresh! Now I hear from the WWA locker room that I am to be labelled a traitor. A traitor? What in the hell for? What did I do to deserve such an extravagant mantle? That is, of course, beating the hell out of a good number of them. Whatever their reason, whatever their problem is with me I cannot say that I care much about keeping the status quo. I never have done and will not begin to do so to cater for some wrestlers petty egos and feelings. I don’t particularly need anyone else’s approval. I don’t particularly need anyone at all. I’m a survivor, always have been. The Meeting I followed Alexa into her father’s office and admired the man’s taste in décor. She had us sat in front of the desk, despite her father’s current absence. I was quite surprised to receive the telephone call from Alexa that morning, informing me that her father (and employer), Cornelius Stakiri wanted to meet with me to discuss a few things. After desperately trying to separate myself from my former employer, or more specifically my former employer’s right hand woman, I embraced the offer much to his surprise I was later told. Perhaps the news of my uncooperative nature had spread to outside of the wrestling industry. That suited me just fine. It put me more in demand and on some levels I enjoyed it. “Sorry I’m late,” the averagely built man, said entering the room. I guessed it was Cornelius, despite not being formally introduced. I could tell just by briefly glancing at the material that his suit was too expensive for it not to be him. Besides, he walked into the office as though he owned. A very good, if overly obvious, sign in my book. “The meeting with some of the minor shareholders in the company went on for ever.” “Not a problem,” I told him and meant it. He grinned, slumping down into his rather luxurious and, judging by the expressed plastered across his face, comfortable leather reclining chair. “The worst about it is, a decision still wasn’t reached!” “So,” I said, wanting to get directly to business. “Alexa here tells me that you wanted to discuss something with me…” Cornelius’ grin widened. “Right down to the heart of the matter, I like that.” “I had a feeling you would,” I responded, desperately wishing that he’d just get on with it. “My girl here,” Cornelius began, smiling fondly at Alexa, “tells me that you’re unemployed now.” I glared at Alexa, who was now blushing. “I’m sorry,” she began. “I couldn’t help but overhear your conversation.” I wasn’t too concerned that she’d actually heard the conversation, it was just boggling my mind how she heard Aeryn and I talking about the matter without my sensing her presence in the nearby area. Weird. “It’s nothing,” I told her, then turned back to Stakiri. “And…?” “And, I want to offer you a job,” Cornelius stated. I could quite believe my ears for a second. “ A job? I already have a job?” “Ah,” he said. “The professional wrestling thing. You know you’ve got my daughter all worked up about that. She wants me to have someone teach her.” I turned to her and smiled this time. “Really? You want to be a pro-wrestler?” “Yeah,” she confirmed. “It looks fun.” If she only knew the truth. “That’s great.” “Basically Mr Winters I want to make use of your ‘extra’ talents,” Stakiri explained. “Really?” I asked him. “Perhaps my ‘extra’ talents aren’t up for grabs.” “Fair enough,” Cornelius said. “I have a meeting in – uh – five minutes ago, so I’d better head off, but you think about the offer and get back to me. Alexa, will you show Mr. Winters out?” Alexa stood and escorted me out of the room. Revelation Corlius extracted an highly illegal Cuban cigar from the designated draw of his desk and when he was sure that Winters had gotten into the elevator and left the floor, he pressed the buzzer under his desk. Almost immediately a woman entered the room, with a very stern expression spread across her face. “Aeryn,” he greeted. “Hello Cornelius,” Aeryn greeted in return, her voice matching her icy expression with complete perfection. “Do you thing he knows?” He questioned, taking on the demeanour of a giddy school boy. “No,” she told him. “If he thought you were and have been his employer all along, he would have killed you already.” “It’s like that is it?” He asked, not really surprised by this nugget of information. “Why did you do it Cornelius?” Aeryn asked, cutting to the chase. “Why did I do what?” Cornelius asked, looking genuinely confused by the question. “Why did you get Winters to the roof top by claiming that I was in danger?” She asked him. “It was the only way to get him there,” Cornelius explained. “That the truth?” Aeryn questioned. “Of course it is,” Cornelius reassured her. “Why, don’t you believe me?” Designed By [name withheld] | © World Wrestling Alliance 1997 - 2005
