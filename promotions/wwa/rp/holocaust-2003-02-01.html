Holocaust

1 Feb 2003

By Chance

Item 1 of the fed's own "WWA Insider" issue dated 1 Feb 2003, a compiled multi-author RP digest.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The fire burns, we see it flare, and we hear the crackle of wood splintering under the heat. Fire that burns, fire that blisters, fire that scalds, fire that cleanses. Fire that dances, sooty red and orange flames flickering in front of our eyes, never seeming to move, never seeming to stay still. Fire that seems as if it has no substance, as if it were a flat two dimensional illusion, fire that punishes any who think that may be all there is to it’s hellish inferno. The fire continues to burn angrily, hungrily, feeding on the dead wood, and lighting up the night with its ire. Fire that will cleanse away evil, purge the world of all that is wrong, yet this fire is capable of doing great evil, just as much as it has the power to destroy it. We move away and we begin to see the dark Calgary night sky, lit up by the myriad of stars. A million pinpricks each burning with a fire of its own, that lights up a tiny portion of the pitch-black canvas that is the sky. Surrounding the fire on all sides, lie trees of all shapes and sizes, a fine white powder of snow resting on the branches, while a thick white blanket covers the ground, reflecting the light of the fire and the stars back up towards the sky, radiating a sense of utter peace and calm. We spin round to the side a little, and seated in front of the backdrop of serene nature, we see Chance, sitting on a log, rapped in a blanket to protect from the cruel bite of the Calgary weather. He is doing nothing other than stare at the fire, watching it’s twists and turns as it shimmers in front of him, the heat blurring the air above it into a sort of haze, while absently twisting his coin round in a similar manner, so that the coin catches a sinister orange glint from the fire. The camera moves to the side to see a tent set up to one side, a paraffin lantern hanging from it on one side, unlit. It becomes clear that Chance is camping, but why? What could he hope to gain from it so close to his match at Rage in the Cage? “Fire means so many things. It is a symbol.” Chance’s voice is slow and rhythmical, almost melodic, as if he’s thinking his thoughts out loud. “But a symbol of what? Of anger? Of rage? Of passion? I have plenty of those. Anger at Enigma for everything his has done to me, and others, anger so deep I could drown in it, anger so powerful it threatens to sweep me away like a twig in a tidal wave. But I have to hold it in, if I don’t I become worse than he is, worse than what he is. The House of Pain, they live in a sea of burning anger, they writhe in it, they seethe in it, and they enjoy it. But I cannot, I must not, I must keep it walled in, I must hold back the flood of fiery fury, or I risk losing all, losing my control, my humanity, risk losing Lady Luck. How’s that for irony, I cannot take the one risk that could rid me of Enigma forever because if I do, I risk too much, I risk my luck itself. When I would risk anything else, I would not risk that. You say I’m like you Enigma? You say I know that the only way to win is to beat you until you cannot get up? You’re right, I do know that. I know that if I want to end our feud, and take my title for my own, then I have to beat you to within an inch of your life, then beat you some more. But the difference between us, is that when it comes to take that killing blow, the one shot that could end the match, and the other man’s career and livelihood, you know what you would do, you know that you would end it, there and then, without a second’s hesitation. I on the other hand, I have no idea what I would do and that scares me.” Chance breaks off, and looks into the fire again, smiling whimsically, the smile slightly lopsided and almost out of place considering what he has just said, the insane tint that the fire gives to his light blue eyes only makes the effect all the more eerie. At least we hope the tint comes from the fire. “Rage? Oh I have plenty of that too, and unlike my anger, I don’t think I’ll keep that locked up, I don’t think I’ll spare you the depth of my fury on that account. Or maybe I will, it is yet another thing I’m not sure of. They say that rage is blind, that when you are enraged you cannot see things. But maybe that is just for us normal people, maybe that’s just for the people who don’t have the same experience as The House of Pain seem to have with their rage. I’ve watched them, I’ve watched Thorne and Kraven and I’ve felt Enigma direct their rage at a single target with precise control, so that only the person they target suffers. The problem there is that they tend to be fairly indiscriminate about their targets. How can you stand it Enigma? How can you stand it that your leader attacked an innocent woman, and her unborn child? How can you live with yourself knowing that he hoped to take a life that has not even been brought into the world yet? Just watching it made me sick, because I wasn’t able to do anything about it, I couldn’t have stopped Kraven, and if I were to face him now I probably still wouldn’t be able to, but I can stop you Enigma, and inside of that cage, you and Jimmi Zion will both feel the rage that I keep locked up.” Again Chance pauses, his eyes never having left the fire, and we can still see the insane glint of fire reflected in his eyes, which seem to bore a whole down through the fire, down into the Earth. “As for passion? My one passion was once to risk. And what better setting than in that cage, where I can risk my very body, and not just that, but risk the bodies of two other men, each in the match with only one passion, to win the belts. Don’t be deceived that is my passion as well, but not my only passion, and my desire to gamble may be the deciding factor in this match. For while the sole guiding principal in both Enigma, and Jimmi Zion will be to win the match, by any means possible, and that is where I have the advantage, because while I am here to win, there are other things guiding me, and my passion for risk means that while they try to escape the cage, and escape the combined wrath of three men, all at boiling point, that is when Lady Luck will guide my hand in the path that will strike them down, for their single-mindedness will blind them, while my dual purpose shall set me free.” Chance looks away from the fire for the first time, and turns his piercing gaze onto the camera, looking straight down the lens, right into our eyes. “Anger, rage, and passion. That is not why I am here, for fire is said to have another property, fire is said to cleanse, to destroy both good and evil, for better or for worse. That is how the word Holocaust came into being: trial by fire, because the fire would cleanse the evil and leave the good. Then the word took on a new meaning, when Hitler decided that the world would be better without Jews and decided to cleanse them, to purge the world. And because the fire was used to do this, does that make the flames evil. No, it makes Hitler evil, because men are evil, not the tools they use. So when Enigma decided that the WWA would be a better place without me, that makes him evil. And when Kraven decided the WWA would be a better place if he got rid of Denrol and Mrs Korth, that makes him evil.” Chance takes one more pause before taking a quick and violent kick at the fire. Red hot embers scatter everywhere, hissing in the snow. Chance whirls to face the camera again and stares daggers at us. “So on Sunday at Rage in the Cage Enigma, who knows? Maybe my fire will create a Holocaust for you!” Fade to red hot flames, burning beneath a night sky.
