"The Behemoth" Jim Blair Basic Information Nicknames Real Name | James Travis Blair Height | 6'3" Weight | 358 Date of Birth | 09/01/1985 Home Town | Kansas City, MO Theme | "I Need Peace" - Frank Black and the Catholics More Details Appearance Appearing less like a man than a 3 foot wide fire hydrant, Blair redefines thick, from his bulging neck to his massive arms, he's packed with bulky, huge muscle ... it's also more than clear that his workout routine aims at raw power, not vanity ... or stamina. If he would cut about 50 pounds of fat, it's probable that he could repackage himself and become an all around better performer, but instead he keeps that weight ... so that he can pack on additional muscle. He has coal-black hair indicative of his Irish ancestry, with soft, world-worn green eyes, that almost appear beady when contrasted with his huge neck. He wears the hair slick back and usually has a full black beard to cover up the scars of past match types he is not proud of. His arms have numerous colorful tattoos, 9 in total, but not big enough to be full sleeves. It's said that each of these tattoos represents a sacrifice or hardship his family faced. The one on his right shoulder is a Kanji symbol meaning "Inner Peace". Ring Attire He wears a simple traditional black double armed wrestling singlet with a wine colored outline. On his right leg is a picture of a full compass, the 360 degrees representing his massive size. He wears black elbow and knee pads (with the same wine colored stripe as his singlet) but rather than wearing wrestling boots, he wears ankle high black heavy toed work boots ... as traditional boots don't fit comfortably over his massive calves. Personality The Behemoth can best be described as world-weary, and mature beyond his years. The massive burdens he puts on himself and his worry that he's failing as a father figure (doing the right thing by earning a living for his orphaned brothers, raising them wrong by hurting ... and getting hurt ... for a living) gives him something of a guilt complex, not that he lets it be crippling. He's analytical, cold, passive and calculating up to his breaking points, at which point he becomes alarmingly aggressive. He values loyalty and family to a point that they become Achilles' heels for him. History Jim Blair grew up upper middle classed in the American midwest, the oldest of four brothers, and he excelled both athletically and scholastically. A two time state winning shotputter and all-city football player, he received a full football scholarship to a prestigious university. During his freshman year, tragedy struck in the form of an automobile accident that claimed his only living parent, his father. Now the legal guardian of his 3 younger brothers, Jim was forced to withdraw from school to provide for them, not willing to let them become part of the foster care system. Needing more money than a typical 18 year old with no college degree can make to support a family of four, he self-trained his large powerful frame to take on the wrestling rings and mixed martial arts circuits, which at the time were a mish-mash of street fighters and strictly for show martial artists, without weight classes or, in most cases, rules. He concentrated his anger, frustration, and stress, into increasingly challenging feats of strength, believing that it symbolized his ability to move mountains for those he cared about. And so a 300 pound football lineman turned into a 360 pound musclebound monster, substituting frustration and pure power for technical skill. The typical life he wanted, a good education, a beautiful wife, children of his own, he sacrificed it all to become a showman and a fighter, bloodying himself around the globe in small towns and big cities alike, giving up on his dreams so that the brothers he loved could pursue theirs. It's been a long time now, and the places for men like Jim Blair are scarce. MMA's evolution has left him behind, and now just the world of wrestling, with it's avarice and arrogance, is where Jim finds the days as he starts to enter the far side of his peak. But even though he would not say so, or even give you an answer if asked, he continues. Because deep down inside, he likes punishing those for whom things have come too easily. And he's pretty damn good at it. Alignment | Face Manager Allies Rivals Awards Other Awards Wrestling Style | High Impact Brawler Moveset Trademark (Rare Alternate Finisher): The Three-Sixty Description: Top Rope Flying Body Splash. The name of the move does not refer to any sort of rotation, but rather Jim Blair's immense girth. Who says he doesn't have a sense of humor? Trademark: Mass Times Acceleration Description: Diving Shoulderblock. The Behemoth doesn't leave his feet too often, but when he does, it can be to devastating effect. He frequently uses this to counter a rushing attack or off of an Irish Whip. Early moves: Basic Brawling Techniques (Overhand punches and stomps) Forearm Smash European Uppercut Body Block One-Armed Body Slam Front Release Drop Suplex Test of Strength Hip Throw Back Body Drop Giant Headbutt Late Match: Gorilla Press Drop Short-Arm Clothesline One-Handed Choke Toss Variations on the Belly-Back Suplex Jumping Knee Drop Lifting Choke Hold Tilt-a-Whirl Shoulderbreaker One-Handed Powerbomb (Non-Super Heavyweights Only) Finisher | The Death Wish Finisher Description Bearhug Jumping Belly to Belly Front Slam. Blair uses his tremendous upper body strength to squeeze the breath out of his opponent, before forcibly throwing them to the mat, his immense weight crashing down on top. The move can be finished in a pinning predicament. He prefers to perform the move one of two ways - either by lifting them directly off the ground from their backs while they lie prone, or by catching them mid-air, countering a high risk maneuver. This way, the air is forcefully pushed from their lungs instantly. He has to jump for full effect if the opponent is significantly taller than him.
