The Scouting Report - Issue 4

24 Sep 2003

Bylined Joe Stock - Director of Talent, in the fed's own kayfabe voice.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Welcome everyone to edition four of The Scouting Report. Sorry for the lack of a report last week, but sometimes you just have too much going on at once. I have no plans for stuff like that to happen often. A lot is going on in the WWA and let's get right into it. -The WWA has added some great new talent. The Green Man is a one of a king character. It'll be interesting to see him develop. Ramsey Kaine is a very cocky guy, but he can add a lot of flash to the Cruiserweight Division. I wasn't sure about Axel Steinberg when he was signed, but he has shown some great effort and I wouldn't be surprised if he does well in the WWA. Justin Bourne is also a fine addition to the Cruiserweight Division and I expect him in the title hunt soon. Finally of course there is Jim Black, someone who has climbed to the top of the WWA before. The question is, the Don isn't around anymore, so he has to do it all on his own. Should be fun to watch. -While we're on the subject of talent. Let's switch to talent on their way out. Extreme Impact of course, has already been fired on Meltdown, through I don't fully agree with the way they went. -There is a number of people who may be cut very soon if they don't get their act together. To name some names: Gohan, The Regulators, and Kade Williams. -There are some injuries to report. Kevin Kane tore his shoulder during a house show and thus we had to go with the attack angle by Edmunds on Meltdown. He'll be out about three weeks. -Ian Tense has requested time off and thus suffered the beatdown he got last week. I have no idea when Tense will return to action, but we hope to see him back soon. -Edward Layman broke his thumb sparring the other day and will be out till the week of the twenty-fifth of August. The rookie has shown get potenial and we hope to see him back soon. -Onto Meltdown. Overall I wasn't very happy with the show. While I think everyone tried their best, several bad ideas got used and resulted in a poor show. -Kevin Kane could have had a better match, but he was suffering an injury and it was clear he was in pain the entire time. -The Chase Williams/EkoSyde match was just too short. I think the two should have been given more time. I'm wondering where this Se7en/EkoSyde thing is going. Williams is still looking good and next week gets a shot at Rookie gold. -I was left very confused by the ending of the Payne/LeTrav match. Since when does a 5 count signal a knockout? And doesn't one man getting up end the count? The two tried, but stupid booking ruined the match. A small screwup resulted in the clipping of this match for American Television. -Kaine's entrance was done very well and establishes him as a top Cruiserweight heel. I hope he along with a number of others, can give us some great matches in the future. -Payne may be the first real great superstar to be in the Horsemen of 2002. Now how do we get rid of Magician? -I won't go into the awfulness that was the Strip Match. The tag division is in trouble as it is. That crap just brings it down further. The celebrity involvement was very poorly done and just added to the stupidity. -This Trapper/We The People angle is going nowhere. Trapper is feuding with a nobody and than steals the entrance of a superstar he's not even remotely involved with. If there were a feud involved, this would make sense. Right now it just looks like a waste of time. If Trapper isn't going to do anything useful with We The People, than he should just let them be. -What could have been a great Extreme Match turned out a dud. The two major problems was Thorne's constant involvement for every fall, and the ending. Less is more in the case of interference and the Extreme Match shows that. The third fall was a very bad idea. I only got negative repsponse from the ending and I even received a request from Eddie that the agent involved not be used in his matches anymore. I don't say I blame Eddie for his feelings. -The main event also was the result of bad booking. I still can't get a solid answer as to where this angle is going, but the fans were not pleased that the promised World Title Match did not happen. Plus it seems no one could decided whether this was a number one contender or number two contender match. Is Tracer Bullet still getting his shot? When is Denrol's shot? Confused fans lead to pissed off fans. The finish also was poorly done. While I may not be a fan of Denrol's work, he is a face, so why would he use tights to get a pinfall? Why should have Shane Dorion, who worked the hardest the last week to promote this match, be the one getting pinned? -I have to give this week's show thumbs down. The workrate was good, the booking was just plain dumb. Hopefully things can get together and we can get back to great shows leading to Labor Day Brawl. -There have been a number of questions asked about the Fiesta lineup for this week. As most people know, Cruz is in charge of the booking and the lineup he put out looks like it was pulled out of a hat. Almost no angles or feuds are pushed here. At least we don't have another World Title Match. Kraven/Moore was another great from both men, but it didn't belong on our second tier show. -The lineup for Meltdown is also disappointing. It's a show with exactly one headliner, two superstars, and two midcarders. The rest of the group is undercarders. While I'm all for pushing the guys who need more TV time, Fiesta is suppose to be the show of the undercarders, not our flagship. -In other news, some updates on current and former WWA superstars. -David Wattsoner and Ungentle Giant did appear on Meltdown, but there is no plans for them to return to action. Giant was brought in for a one time thing, but he seems happy at his home in California. David Wattsoner does a good amount of work backstage and helps with training some of the rookies. -John Sharpe, former partner of Trapper, has been working as a trainer also. There is talk he wants to return to action soon. -Some concerns over the fitness of the Texas Longhorns. Both old and not quite able to match some of the younger guys for speed. However they are committed to the WWA and are really pushing their physical limitations. -I can't revel much, but a former WWA superstar who had a big time feud has been stopping by my office to work out a deal. I think we are weeks away from it, but you never can tell with these things. -I expect Death to be reveled at Labor Day Brawl. The addition of this guy to the roster is good news for the WWA. -While the Seventh Layer is looking to become a good heel stable, we haven't seen any face stables lately. That may change. Hmmmm. -While there has been issues with Insider broadcasts, that should be fixed soon and lately that show has been doing very well. Keep up the good work. -For everyone who knows about my big project, it's coming along slowly. Figure September as the start date. Well that's it for this edition. See you next week with more from underneath the New York Yankees baseball cap. Joe Stock Director of Talent
