Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

SBS Wrestling opened on 3 February 1997, self-billed as “a new fed started by a couple of experienced writers,” UK-hosted and booked on roleplay quality, with two cards a week and a magazine show rounding them up. By May its roster was capped league-wide at five singles wrestlers and two tag teams.

Its Monday Madness broadcasts opened on a running joke about their own timeslot, a cold open picking up mid-overtime from a fictional ice hockey broadcast before cutting to the show live from Salt Lake City. Its own roster split along faction lines, chiefly the Apocalyptic Alliance — Zach “Movin' Man” Zager and “Uptown” Johnny Durante among them — against a looser field of rivals holding its titles.

In May 1997, accused in public of copying the IIWF's own website, its president answered within hours with an unreserved admission: “I'm not going to deny it, there is the odd bit here and there… Theft no, a bit of plagiarism maybe.” The same thread drew an unprompted character reference from a former SBS handler by then running his own promotion, who called it “a damn fine fed… I handled there for a brief time… and loved it.”

Its 27 April Brave New World pay-per-view put the World Championship on the line, 'Cajun Power' Rene DuBois defending against Zager, and the match dissolved into a mass brawl: DuBois unmasked a mystery interferer as Eric Righteous, both factions poured to ringside, and a returning Lance Morrison chokeslammed Zager to save DuBois before the bout was ruled a no-contest with DuBois still champion. The same card had Spawn pin Zager for the Television Championship, and Spawn's new stable, the Untouchables, announced themselves already holding half the promotion's gold.

Scorpion took the World Championship from DuBois that June, the change itself unrecorded, and was still champion, retaining by disqualification against Durante on 9 June, when the promotion's own record runs out. Its Tag Team titles were stripped from their holders, Vengeance and Retribution, by President Marshall around the same time, with no new champion ever recorded.

Its last surviving document, 4 August 1997, still calls for new players, “especially Tag Teams,” after “running for over six months.” No document of its own survives past it; a third-party character biography that November already speaks of it wholly in the past tense.
