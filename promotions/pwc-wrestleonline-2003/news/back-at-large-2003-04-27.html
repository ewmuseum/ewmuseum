27 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Former PWC Number One Contender Angelo Deville appeared in court Friday morning before the Honourable Judge Hearn, hoping to be released on his own recognizance for the alleged murder of Jason O’Neil. Looking like Penance must beneath the mask, Deville was horribly bruised and swollen, and the classy mobster looked completely out of countenance in his plastic orange prison fatigues. The matter was much more serious than a beauty contest, however, with the lead prosecutor Rob Murphy almost spitting at Deville. “Exotortion. Blackmail. Larceny. Racketeering. I fear that is only the prologue to Mr. Deville’s sordid tale.” Murphy went on for quite some time as to Deville’s criminal nature, outlining how the charge of murder against him was air-tight, in spite of losing the main body of evidence. During the hearing it came out that the police had received an incriminating video tape show Angelo Deville as the culprit in O’Neil’s murder, but that the tape had since been mysteriously destroyed by fire. The State shrugged this off, saying they still had mountains of other evidence, but Justice Hearn was sceptical, saying it was only his personal curiosity that kept this case from being thrown out. For his part, Deville sold out the wrestling industry, telling the court he and Jason O’Neil had been the best of friends. He even went so far as to put Ron Starr on the spot, saying it was an angle at least partly orchestrated by him. Angelo held nothing back, stating, “Even standing here now I hope that this is just a dream I will awake from. I don’t want to be a mobster, or break the law, I just want to put on a show where people can believe for a few short hours that I am. Up until now, it has served me grandly.” He cast a short, meaningful glance at the prosecutor. “Up until now, everyone has understood that wrestling is fake. “I am not a murderer,” he continued, winding down his long and compelling speech. “That is why there is no evidence proving me one. If the police want to do a real investigation into this matter, they would be well advised to actually look for Jason O’Neil, instead of searching only for a way to turn our show into a horrible reality. Find Jason O’Neil, and ask him. Ask Ron Starr.” Deville raised his hands helplessly, looking for something brilliant to define the most simple of ideas. “I’m just a wrestler.” Surprisingly, Justice Hearn granted Deville his bail, ruling that while he did not necessarily believe a word of what Angelo said, the case against him was too weak to justify awaiting trial behind bars. He set Deville’s bail for $500 000 American, which was paid within half an hour. Two hours later, Angelo walked out of the court and into the sunlight, basking in its glow for the first time since Capitol Punishment. Figuring he would be in a good mood, and wanting to speak more of what he alluded to in court, we approached him for a word. He sneered at us, then snatched our camera away and broke it on the ground in a fit of calm rage. The words he shared are not quotable here, so I’ll just suffice it to say that he had no comment. Though Starr and company also had no comment, Angelo is slated to appear live on PWC TV later this evening, perhaps as a murderer if not a wrestler.
