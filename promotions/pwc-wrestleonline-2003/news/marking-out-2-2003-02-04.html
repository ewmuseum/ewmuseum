PWC — Column

Marking Out #2

4 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello everyone and welcome to the second edition of Marking Out. Well, what a difference a week makes, eh? Last week at this time, people were talking about guys like Jimmy Nguyen and Porky Bunson as contenders for the main event scene in PWC. Little did they know that The Ole Bookerman, Ron Starr, had the likes of Clay Vendetta, Angelo Deville, Jason O'Neil and Sebastian Toomes hidden up his sleeves. The next two-plus months leading up to Capitol Punishment should be interesting to say the least, as any one of those four men would make an idea first PWC World Heavyweight Champion. But will any of those four men actually wear the belt? I suppose it depends on the mood and the appetite of the "Masked Monster" who decided to crash the main event last week. I dunno who that guy/monster/thing is, but he is certainly someone not to be taken lightly. There are many other aspects of the first show I liked a lot. It was good to see Gideon Poe in PWC, and Hoyt Williams looks to be the real deal indeed. I don't know if Karina Wolfenden can wrestle, but I am intrigued to what will unfold between her and Magnum Wolf in the days to come. It's always good to see Jake Keeton back in action, but I have to tell you that I found myself shaking my head after his spineless display against Rancid on Saturday night. Looks like he learned a trick or two from his old pal Stewart Hix. Let's hope we see know sign of Hix around here anytime soon. Warpath appears to be both a monster in body and mindset. This guy has real issues and lets hope he gets some help before he really does harm to himself or others. Its too bad about Jay Phoenix' physical condition. He is a great in ring talent when healthy, and I, for one, hope that Jay Phoenix still has days in the ring ahead of him. All signs point to the contrary, unfortunately. Of all the things that happened Saturday night, however, none could possibly eclipse the unmasking of the Phantom Booker. Its ashame that once he did unmask that I saw a man with no soul who would probably sell his own mother to succeed once again in this industry. Let's just hope he comes to his senses before someone gets seriously hurt, or he runs the company into the ground. There are no injuries to report this week. Both PWC and WGTW TV-48 officials are pleased with the Philadelphia regional ratings analysis that came out Monday afternoon. We got out of the chute at a stronger-than-expected pace, and I see no reason why PWC can not continue its forward momentum. That's all for this week folks. I'll be back next week with another edition of Marking Out!
