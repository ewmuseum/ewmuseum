PWC — Column

The Minor League

23 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
by Gregory Burke Hello PWC faithful! This is ace journalist Gregory Burke reporting from a hotel room just outside of Memphis, Tennessee. I’ve just attended yet another action packed house show put on by the developmental promotion known as the Primetime Wrestling Academy and am now putting my skills as a writer to use by bringing you, the fans, the first edition of a column I like to call The Minor League. The Minor League’s concept is simple. I go to shows put on by the PWA and observe the talent showcased on them. Then I write about said talent and give you all a glimpse of the wrestlers that you may be seeing very soon on episodes of PWC Television. Shawn Stewart is THE man to beat when it comes to the PWA these days considering the fact that he is the reigning Coming Storm Champion. He has been consistent both inside of the ring and when cutting promos, while still managing to keep a positive outlook on life itself. He has yet to comment on the federation that he would like to go to once his stint in the developmental organization is though, but I’ve heard through the grapevine that the Pro Wrestling Circuit is one of his top prospects. Stewart has proven show after show that he is a force in the ring. Couple that with his outstanding attitude and we may be seeing more from him in the future. The second free agent on my list for the first edition of this column is a man who goes by the name of Clark Carlisle. He is held in very high regard by prestigious members of the world of professional wrestling and portrays his character perfectly. Many feel that his open conceit and brashness rivals that of PWC main player “Switchblade” Sebastian Toomes. Of course, only time will tell if Clark can actually prove to be as great as the World Championship Contender. On the last televised PWA event, Rave, Carlisle went head to head with Shawn Stewart in a match for Shawn’s Coming Storm Championship. Unfortunately, Carlisle suffered one of his only losses to Stewart in an extremely grueling contest, his dreams of gold fading away for the time being. Rumor has it that Clark may have the opportunity to compete for more prestigious titles in the ranks of the PWC, but anything definite has yet to be made public. I’ll be sure to update you on Carlisle’s situation in the columns to come. The last, but certainly not least, competitor on my list of those that may one day be gracing the ranks of the Pro Wrestling Circuit is Rupert Roberts Jr. or RRJ as he is affectionately referred to by fans. RRJ is a former high school football player and is obviously a physical force, but his ability to wrestle is definitely in question, which may be the reason that he hasn’t been signed to a PWC contract already. His father, Rupert Roberts Sr., serves as his manager and has reportedly been in non-stop negotiations with PWC officials regarding the signing of his son. It is apparent to this reporter and many others that if RRJ actually sees the light of day on PWC TV, his father will be acting as his mouthpiece and thus won’t be far behind. Rupert Junior’s ability to cut good promos is undisputed at this point, but as I mentioned earlier, his talent as a professional wrestler is up for debate. His performance in his first match, a battle royal with several other new PWA wrestlers, will most likely play in whether or not we see “The Investment” in a PWC ring. In other news, I have personally spoken with high ranking PWA officials and they have expressed great happiness with former Academy wrestling Paula Michelle Smith a/k/a P.M.S and her work in the PWC. “Ms. Smith is in a position right now that many currently involved with our promotion dream about on a nightly basis.” One unnamed staff member told me. “She should be paid attention to be each and every PWA member.” As of press time, P.M.S was being considered for PWA graduation, which is one of its highest honors. In related news, Jonathan Drachen has supposedly been promoted to a bigger federation. What federation it is hasn’t been revealed as of yet, but PWC was no doubt considered. I’d like to thank you all for reading the first edition of The Minor League and would also like to tell you to please be on the look out for these superstars and my next column. Coming Soon: Information regarding Stone Burns, Mephisto Grimes, and Silvio Rochelli. Until the next time, this has been The Minor League and Gregory Burke.
