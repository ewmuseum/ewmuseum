Spotlight: Gideon Poe

30 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
with Jason Maddux I stepped into a dimly lit room, obviously one of the grungier looking suites inside of the hotel that my first interviewee was staying in, and wiped my brow slowly, the nervousness evident on my face. I had never been this nervous about giving an interview before and rightfully so. It was my job, after all. Yet, there was something about this night and this assignment that gave me chills. Was it the fact that I was stepping away from my comfort zone and doing an interview that wouldn’t be televised? Or could it be the fact that I was about to speak to a feared member of the Pro Wrestling Circuit, face-to-face, and completely alone? I didn’t know. All that I knew, as I sat down directly in front of Gideon Poe and extended my hand in a showing of friendship, was that there was no turning back. I, Jason Maddux, had to do this interview and turn in to my superiors what you’re now reading. Poe shook my hand and smirked as I spoke to him. Maddux: “Hello Gideon and thank you for taking time out of your schedule to speak with me for the first ever Pro Wrestling Circuit wrestler spotlight! How are you feeling this evening?” Gideon Poe: “Calm and content for the first time in a long time, Jason. How about you?” Maddux: “I’m great, actually, just trying to make a dollar by doing these interviews. Thank you for asking.” Poe: “If that’s all they’re paying you I’d quit now.” Maddux: “No need to. The thrill of interviewing witty professional wrestlers like you is payment enough for me.” Poe: “The novelty will wear off soon, don’t worry.” Maddux: “Let’s hope it doesn’t for the sake of everyone that wants a chance to be questioned by yours truly. Now, would you mind if we got on with the actual interview? I’d rather not have hair as gray as your manager’s by the time we finish.” Poe: “Point well taken.” Maddux: “Good.” I couldn’t help but ask myself at that moment if all PWC wrestlers were going to be this sarcastic and evasive. Little did I know that things were about to get way deeper. Maddux: “I know that your first major federation was RUSH Wrestling, which was on a slightly larger scale then PWC. Has the change affected you at all?” Poe: “RUSH it was a constant struggle to prove myself, to “earn my keep” so to speak. That's why everything had no limits. That's why me and Hunt went through a German Rules ladder match at Decimation. That's why me and Mordred came close to sealing each other up in body bags before our 25th birthdays. Now in PWC, that need and drive isn't there so much. It's just a case of elevating yourself to the highest possible peak and making every one of those small dollars we earn stretch as far as possible. So yeah, it was a hard adjustment, but I didn't really have time to dwell and think about the consequences of it.” Maddux: “You, like every other professional wrestler, probably began your career in independent promotions. Do you feel that coming to the PWC has brought you back to your roots or do you miss the spotlight?” Poe: “I was more of an underground martial arts guy when I was still green around the collar. But yeah, there isn't much of a difference between the two. You arrive at the small venue, sweat and bleed, collect your pay check and move onto the next town. As far as the spotlight goes I don't really think I've had it long enough to say whether I miss it or not. Yet when I did have small segments of it in RUSH, it didn't suit my taste. I do what I need to do, end of story. I don't want my face splashed all over magazines and the internet because of it. So yeah, you could say PWC has brought me back to my routes, and that is one of the few good points of the fed.” Maddux: “Jeremiah O’Barr is your constant companion and a huge fan favorite here. What exactly is your relationship with him?” Poe: “In a nutshell? When I was ready to travel back to America after training in China and Japan for a large portion of my young adult life, the old wench poisoned me just so he could obtain my services as a martial artist. Now he provides me with a small serum every month or so. Hard to believe? Sure it is, I have trouble convincing myself of it, but that's just the harsh reality of it. Some call it a gimmick...I call it my burden...my life.” Maddux: “Do you feel that you’d be able to succeed without him?” Poe: “Yes and no I guess. Believe it or not, I do occasionally feel sorry for the guy. He does have his compassionate side, and in his own way I guess he does care for me. But what he's done to me is still no excuse. Yes, I think I could have made it without him. Why? Because at the first sign of danger he high tails it as far as his small body will take him. He's an overprotective fool, hence his dislike for Tanya. Yet at the end of the day, he's played a large factor in a lot of the things that have got Poe here today. So to answer it honestly, yea I would have got here without him, but maybe just not as fast. At the end of the day being poisoned by an old fool gets peoples attention.” Maddux: “Now, it has been brutally obvious to every PWC fan that Warpath is out to make your life a living hell. Why do you think he holds such malice towards you?” Poe: “’Cause I put his ass through a table on PWC 1. I think that was a big hint to him that I didn’t want us to be best pals. Other than that obvious reason, I honestly don't know. Maybe it's because I have everything he doesn't. Yea I know what you're thinking, what could I possibly have that someone would want. Well I don't think feud is about material possessions. Instead it's about life long ones. I've got Roland, the creature he seems hell bent on taking from me. He's already imposed himself onto Tanya, and he's already taken his swings at Roland. That was where the mind games stopped. If he wants to get violent and physical, then fair enough. Yet if he wants to get personal with me, then so be it. But once you cross that line with me, there is no forgiveness, and there is no turning back. Just one big downward spiral, and come Capital Punishment I intend to send Warpath down that spiral once and for all.” Maddux: “Don’t you think that you’re underestimating him just a bit?” Poe: “Not anymore. I'll be the first to admit I did. Even through the animal rights activists bullshit he was pulling I underestimated what was going through his head. I mean any 14 year old teenager can fake an animal abuse call right? But not anybody can do what he did to the fans, the security guard, to Tanya, and to Roland. That's when I stopped underestimating him. That's when I finally sat up and took more notice. There hasn't been a minute since that I haven't beaten myself up for not seeing what was coming. It was my own ignorance that allowed Tanya and Roland to suffer. Others may tell you different, especially Tanya. She'll tell you that there was nothing that could have stopped what happened that night. But there was, I was the one who could have sat up sooner and taken him a bit more seriously. So yeah, I underestimated him, and yeah, by God I paid the price. Yet no longer am I turning a blind eye to him. 'Path wants my attention, well mission accomplished kiddo', you got it.” Maddux: “Is there anyone else aside from Warpath that you have your eye on? Why or why not?” Poe: “There's an old saying in this industry. Don't Trust Anyone. Well it's true. You don't. There are only a few I trust, and that's where it stops. So yeah, I've got eyes in the back of m head. As far as I'm concerned every member of this roster could be plotting my demise. You can call it paranoia, I just call it being aware and prepared. It's a lesson you don't want to have to learn the hard way.” Maddux: “What are your long term plans concerning the Pro Wrestling Circuit?” Poe: “To get through each day at a time so far and to get some gold eventually. Not so much for respect, but title holders tend to have a little more cash come their way, anyone can work that out. To prove all my doubters wrong, whether you hear of them or not, they're out there. To be honest I haven't really thought about, you along with the rest will just have to wait and see how things turn out. Right now your guess is as good as mine.” Maddux: “Now for the last question. The world, especially every female PWC fan, wants to know, Gideon. Is that a roll of socks in your tights or are you hiding the true ‘Avatar of Death’ from us?” Poe: “Depends on who’s asking and what you think the true ‘Avatar of Death’ is.” Maddux: “C’mon man! You know what I’m talking about! Don’t play coy with me, Poe!” He laughed as he answered. Poe: “You’d have to direct that question to Tonya. She has dibs on it.” Maddux: “Ok. I’ll be sure to do that in the near future. Until then, however, I’d like to thank you again for letting me get inside your head and for speaking so openly. It’s much appreciated.” Poe: “Anytime.”
