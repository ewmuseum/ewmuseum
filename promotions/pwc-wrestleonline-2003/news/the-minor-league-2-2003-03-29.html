PWC — Column

The Minor League #2

29 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
by Gregory Burke The Primetime Wrestling Academy stormed into Atlanta this week for a series of house shows and one power packed edition of Tuesday Night Rave! I, being the ace columnist and reporter that I am, was in attendance and observed each moment in order to give you, the loyal reader and PWC fanatic, the down low on the developing PWA superstars that you may be seeing on your televisions screens very soon. Welcome again to The Minor League! I’m Gregory Burke, as if you didn’t know that, and I’m back for the second time to deliver the goods as promised. Let’s get on with it, shall we? Each and every Academy wrestler was backstage for this week’s edition of Rave, but unfortunately not all of them were able to compete. Those who were able, however, did and put on stellar matches. PWA officials were rumored to have been very happy with the show, with the same going for scouts who were backstage browsing for talent. For those of you who missed the show, here is a small recap: The winner of the opening match, which was a New Kids on the Block Battle Royal, was a man who is no stranger to this column, Rupert Roberts Jr. He showed off his athletic prowess and ability in this match, which is a weekly mainstay on Rave and was designed in order to show off the newer PWA talent. It came down to RRJ and Travis Kyle, with Roberts finally getting the edge over “The Dark Magician” and leaving the gymnasium with his first in-ring victory. The other men involved were John Gargano, Mephisto Grimes, and SDS, who were all applauded on their tremendous efforts. The next contest was between Donovan Weed and Miller Magnum, with Magnum taking home the win. This match experienced large amounts of build-up on the house show cards leading up to Rave, with Weed winning the majority of those altercations. He was the favorite going into the match, but Magnum shocked everyone by slamming Weed to the ground with a beautifully executed powerbomb and pinning Donovan with a back bridge shortly thereafter. Magnum has said that he intends on remaining successful as a singles competitor following this win, while Donovan Weed has reportedly brought in a partner and is about to unleash Team Weed on the PWA. The follow-up to Weed and Magnum was supposed to be Christopher Camero going up against Brad DeriX, but was cancelled due to a no show on both men’s parts. Officials and wrestlers alike were disgusted. “We’re sitting back here wishing that we had matches and those two fuckers had one and wasted it?” One anonymous wrestler stated. “They should be shot.” Camero versus DeriX was given a suitable replacement, however, in the form of Stone Burns, who I will be reporting on in edition number three, and Tyler Preston, whose main goal as a wrestler is to entertain the people. They put on a wonderful match, so wonderful in fact that I feel it was the best of the night, and left the crowd on the edges of their folding chairs by the time Preston got the win. Burns showed his true colors despite the fact that he lost, however, and got tons of heat due to his consistent use of closed fists punches and bitch slaps. Preston combated with a witty attitude and a good presence, which makes both of these men great additions to any federation’s roster in my expert opinion. I just like the fans that were in attendance for this edition of Rave, am really looking forward to matches featuring these two men in the future. The final match, which was heralded as a clash of future giants in the sports entertainment industry, was between Clark Carlisle and Jack Murphy and was a bit of a disappointment in the eyes of this columnist. I expected Carlisle, a man that I picked as a future world champion in the first edition of this very column, to come off of his loss against Coming Storm Champion Shawn Stewart and show Murphy a thing or two. The end result of this match was the exact opposite of my expectations, with Murphy landing a Shooting Star Press, which was easily the highlight of what was supposed to be a main event, and beating Clark in the center of the ring. Murphy may be taking Clark’s place as my future superstar if Carlisle keeps this type of work up. I mean two losses in a row? That’s simply beastly. All in all it was a good show, but I have one piece of advice for the PWA bookers. Keep the dead weight, that means you Carlisle, off the show and off the roster! Before I let you all go, I’d like to issue my congratulations to Haley Hilton and Matt Jackson, both of whom were signed by Primetime Championship Wrestling! I’d also like to issue a warning to Ron Starr and the Pro Wrestling Circuit’s staff… …STOP LETTING PCW STEAL TALENT FROM UNDER YOUR NOSES! Until the next time, this is The Minor League and Gregory Burke, signing off.
