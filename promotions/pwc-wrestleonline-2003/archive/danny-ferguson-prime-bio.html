El Spiffy! Biography Nicknames Spiff Star, The Spifster, You, That Little Bastard, etc. Hometown Guadalajara, Mexico Birthday 5/5/82 (allegedly) Height 5'8 Weight 165 Biography El Spiffy! is what many classical theorists would explain as an accident. Or rather, a series of accidents. As far back as chronology and temperance will allow us to follow, El Spiffy, then sans the obligatory punctuation, began as a scheme by professional wrestler “Smart Ass” Matt Register. If it isn’t painfully clear, this was long prior to Register’s eventual turn under his own moniker. At the time of his dabbling in the fine art of lucha libre, Register was unable to find a job as himself, and was instead making the independent rounds as a manager. In an attempt to land some in-ring work, he struck some deals with promoters, honed up his lucha skills, and bought himself a mask. And as I’m sure you can guess, there was little if any thought put into the name. With a wing and a prayer, El Spiffy was, er, born. It was a decent gig, or at least it was if “dressing up as a luchador to work shows because you can’t show your face and draw crowds” would fit into your own personal reading of the word decent. Under the hood, Register, or Spiffy as the case may be, won a handful of titles in independent feds, and found moderate success based on the crowds hating his stereotypical ‘Mexican’ behavior. When he had finally escaped the monotony of managerial status, and was able to move on, he threw down the mask and became his own man. That is, until a severe concussion during a match in Mexico brought the Spiffster back from the dead, in a bit of what we in the business like to call “multiple personality disorder.” Not really official, more like having your brains scrambled and thinking you’re Robert E. Lee for a while. A “phase.” Except in a perfectly sane man as opposed to a 13-year-old who watches Nickelodeon well past the recommended age limit they put on programming. But I digress. Register took another nasty head shot and it knocked the sense back into him. I guess collectively it would make his brains comparable to Steve Young’s, but he’s not going to be in our story much longer, so his instability and health problems aren’t important. El Spiffy also added the ! to become El Spiffy! sometime around this point. No real reason was given, but hey, it does look kinda cool, right? I mean, look at it. El Spiffy!. Electrifying. Register lost his job and turned to the hood as he entered the PWA. The Academy made students sign exclusivity contracts, and at the time, Register was using his own name to make the rounds of tournaments, including the exclusive Wrestling God 3 competition. Meanwhile, as El Spiffy!, he was hired by Jeremy Howard’s RUSH wrestling. Somewhere along the line he gained the services of a down-and-out pair off announcers, Juan Carlos Smith and Julio Diaz Perez O’Neil. The two have been attached to El Spiffy!, wittingly or not, ever since. And that’s all we really know about them. Through his prowess in tournaments, Register landed a spot in OSW’s Most Extreme Team competition with Jeffrey Wayne. He no longer needed El Spiffy! to get by, so he dropped the mask like an inexperienced figure skater. However, before RUSH was notified of El Spiffy!’s departure, a mysterious phone call to Matt Register proposed a deal. A sale of El Spiffy!, mask, tights, character. A new man under the mask. It was the Del Wilkes Gambit. Or if you’re exceptionally daring, the Tiger Mask Principle. No one was notified of the change. El Spiffy! suddenly shrank and looked more pale. That was that. No one asked questions. Especially not when this new little guy became some sort of punishment-absorbing suicidal luchador badass. For some reason, the new Spiffster caught on, and caught on well enough to win the RUSH Syndicate title. He held the belt for a record 106 days, shattering any previous reigns, and securing himself a spot in history as the company closed not long after his loss of the strap. He disappeared upon being fired from RUSH by then-owner Xavier Kannon. No one had seen or head from the Spiffy! for a long, long time. Many considered that to be for the better. We would all be so lucky. No one knows where he went, and no one knows what he was doing. All that seems to be prevalent is that he's back, and he's in the Pro Wrestling Circuit. Other Feds CHW, EHCW, WHWL, UCW, All-Asia Promotions, RUSH Wrestling Other Fed Awards CHW Hardcore Heavyweight Champion, EHCW Internet Champion, EHCW Tag Team Champion, All-Asia Deathmatch Champion, RUSH Syndicate Champion (longest-reigning) Wrestling Style Luchacidal: Traditional lucha libre high-flying and technical, mized with American high-risk, high impact flying. Finisher Move Spiffy! Elbow Finisher Description Spinning corkscrew elbowdrop from the top rope, or somewhere high. Finisher Setup Move Chihuahua headlock Setup Description El Spiffy tosses his opponent into the ropes near a turnbuckle. He follows them in, hopping on the turnbuckle in a springboard and grabbing the back of their head, then kicking backwards, sending the opponent face-first into the mat in position for the Sp Other Moves Traditional luchadore stuff (planchas, missile dropkicks, springboards, etc.) and a plethora of DDTs (he can hit a DDT from anywhere, with many positions and variations) The Border Jump: A reckless somersault senton, usually off the highest point possible. Not technique involved, he just jumps into the air, kicking his legs and paddling widly with his arms, eventually making a somersault and landing back-first. Normally he'll do this in long matches, when he's incredibly tired. It will usually knock the wind out of him as well, so he'll usually end up lying on his opponent afterwards for a possible pinfall) Alternate Finisher: Spiffy!
