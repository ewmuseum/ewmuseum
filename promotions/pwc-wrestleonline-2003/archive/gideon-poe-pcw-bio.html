Gideon Poe Biography Nicknames The Shade; The Avatar Of Death;The Harvester Of Macabre; The Anti-Hero; The Sadistic Paladin: Atrocity's Son Hometown Bangor, Maine. Now resides in Salem, New Jersey. Birthday 10/02/80 AD Height 6'4 Weight 230 Biography They say that at several point's in our lives there comes a time when we all come to a crossroads. Our lives will change for the better or worse depending on whatever road we walk down. They say we should think wisely and carefully on what path we choose to continue down in the so called precious gift people will you have you believe is called life. Well my crossroads were a little different than to what you would expect them would be. In the year of 1999, I was nothing more than a young man, seeking knowledge from the world I inhabited, I came to a crossroads. I choose to take the path that would lead me to Tokyo. I had been studying martial arts ever since I could remember. I was a hot tempered child in my early years, a short fuse and a violet temper to match. Yet even at such a young age I was wise enough to know I disliked the feeling of uncontrollable rage, I would much rather be in control of it. Thus began my exploration through martial arts. That was one of the many things that enticed me about martial arts, the control, the organisation, the beauty of it. It was poetry in motion for me. Soon after taking up karate, my temper slowly but surely became controlled. I was able to lock and unlock it with ease, but only when needs be, and that was rare. Yet the more and more I studied martial arts, the more I craved it. America had taught me lots, but there was not enough there to satisfy my needs. Martial arts became my own personal drug, and my dealer of that drug, America, was running out of supplies for my addiction. There was one place where I knew I could constantly get my fix, the one place that endorsed my drug of choice as much as I did......... Japan. I came to my first set of crossroads at the age of 19. I won't bore you with the various roads/options I had the choice of taking, because the decision on which road to travel was already decided upon by me. I would travel to Tokyo to gain the knowledge I so desperately wanted. So, at the tender age of 19, with just enough money to get a plane ticket to Tokyo and arriving there with pretty much just the clothes on my back, my quest for enlightenment began. I was already deadly fluent in Muay Thai, Savate, Tai Kwan Do, and nearly every Karate and Gonfu known of. Various different sensei's has passed their wisdom onto me, they fuelled me with everything they had and I absorbed and learned it quickly and fluently until I had absorbed every last drop. Yet the pool of knowledge that was America was close to a drought, while Japan's was overflowing, and I was there ready to jump in at the deep end head first. My first objective was to find a suitable dojo upon arriving at Tokyo. Accommodation and employment were trailing in second place. I could handle living on the streets or some cramp infested apartment, and I was willing to work at a dead end worthless long hour job. Yet obtaining the skill I so dearly yearned for was something I would never settle second best for. This may have been the reason after 6 months of being in Tokyo I had still yet to find a worthy sensei and a substantial dojo. I did however now have, as I predicted, a small cramp roach infested apartment in Tokyo. I often spoke of my problems to the cockroaches late at night. I paid for my accommodation and supplies by teaching at a small dojo in the centre of Tokyo. It wasn't to my standard to learn in, but I was more than willing to pass the wisdom I had attained onto others. Yet the other sensei's there found it hard to take a 19 year old seriously. Little did they know I knew more than they would ever likely learn about the art's. It wasn't until one of the sensei's challenged my understanding and knowledge of martial arts that my luck finally changed. Dylan Chambers was a bronze medallist at the 96 Olympics in Kung Fu for USA. Yet his Olympic career was cut short when he failed a drug test. He made ends meat by owning the dojo I worked in, and my assumption for the reason he hired me was to feel better about himself. He obviously thought I knew hardly anything about the arts, and had used me as his object of occasional abuse for months. It wasn't until he challenged my ability, not for the first time, that I challenged his own, and his authority over me in the dojo. He grew hostile, a sign of his own lack of knowledge letting his emotions get the better of him, and challenged me in front of all the pupils. I kept control of my emotions, and kindly accepted. 90 seconds later Dylan Chambers was laying face first on the floor wondering where 4 of his teeth were and trying hopelessly to catch his breath. Yet, giving credit when credit is due, the bronze medallist refused to stay down, and I refused to back down. After 5 minutes, my own hybrid style out did his Kung Fu, and Dylan was being rushed to the hospital with a collapsed lung and a broken jaw. I could have gone further, but in the end it was me who stopped the fight and called for the ambulance personally. Afterwards, Ricky Epson, one of the other teachers at the dojo , and one who had always respected my ability approached me. He informed me of a place only few knew of, he only knew of it himself from a friend of a friend, but they both swore it was true. The location - a derelict Shaolin Temple in the Chiu Lin Mountains of China which was originally torn down by the Chinese government upon them discovering that the monks who taught martial arts there were far too dangerous and should not continue in their teachings. The event - a one time event that happened only ever 25 years, hosted by the same monks who originally taught there. The prize - A once in a lifetime opportunity to train with the monks and other sensei's across the world. Since various other establishments of teaching ancient and deadly martial arts were outlawed, various other teachers were left without pupils. This opportunity would allow the winner to eventually have private tuition from various sensei's, each an expert in their martial arts field. Ricky Epson informed me he had been selected, but had refused. Informing me that the risk of death was high there. Ricky had a family, a wife and two young kids, the risk was too high for him. So he offered me his spot. I mean what did I have to lose? My apartment with the cockroaches? As nice as it was to have one sided conversations with them, I'd take this opportunity Ricky had presented me with over them any day. I gladly accepted, and so my name was then cemented in the annals of the tournament. I was going to China. Stowing away on boats wasn't a skill I had ever learnt, but I soon did when I had to do it to get to China. Once there, the sheer atmosphere of the country overwhelmed with opportunity and a spiritual aura much like Japan first did when I arrived there. Reaching the Chiu Lin Mountains was tougher than I first imagined. The mountain was a straight uphill hike, and I wasted no time sightseeing while in China. My mind was focused on winning the tournament. No invitations were needed to enter the competition, once you were invited and aware of the location that was enough. I soon found out once I seen how dangerous the mountain was and how derelict the Monastery was, no one in their right mind would come here without some dark hidden agenda. The atmosphere in the outdoor area of the Monastery was incredibly tense. There were no questions asked, no forms to fill out. If you were here to fight then you were instantly recognisable. They were aware that I, Gideon Poe, had taken Ricky Epson's place, and no doubt they were expecting something special from me being a replacement. The high chief monk , who I was informed was called Kwomasera watched over all the battles with his three advisors, who were unidentified. Very few words were spoken that night, people were merely pointed at, signalling they would do battle, and two battles would commence at a time, monitored by Kwomasera's advisers. It seemed like an eternity before Kwomasera pointed emotionlessly at me, but I welcomed his challenge. So the battles ensued, blood, sweat, tears were shed without a second thought, and that was just the pain I suffered through, and yeah I got lucky a few times, a good few times to be exact, but I managed to pull myself through each duel. The next thing I know there were just two men left standing, and through all the miracles and small mercies granted by whatever God you believe in, I was one of them. My final opponent, a European, a little older than me, 20 say. He proved tough, and was hard to keep down. Truth be told he kept me down for most of the fight. He was an expert in French Kickboxing and Muay Thai also, and a lot of respect was assimilated between us. Yet we both knew how this battle was going to have to end, and we both were unsure if we were willing to go that far to ensure victory. I came to the quick decision that I was prepared. How I came to such a rash and brutal decision so quickly back then remains a blue to me to this day. Breaking the young man's neck was my example of proving just how far I was ready to go. How far I had come as the victor of this gruelling tournament. My first death.... my first kill.... it hadn't been the first of that night, but it certainly had been the most noticeable. I didn't like the thought of being branded a murdered even then, but now, well, I guess it falls on def ears. I emerged battered, bruised, bloody and tired..... but the winner nonetheless. It was a surreal feeling, full of honour and pride. I felt like I had etched myself into something eternally special. I felt proud..... it is an experience I hardly ever feel now. So, to the winner goes the spoils as they say, and to me they indeed did. Kwomasera himself bowed to me, and congratulated me on my victory. He informed me in more detail of what my prize was to be.... Every sensei he had was at my disposal. I could use as many and as little as I wished and they were mine to use as long as I wanted. I would be their only pupil, and I would use the opportunity wisely. I had just got my one big fix for my drug, and I was ready to OD in it. So it began.... While in China, I learnt various arts from various talented sensei's who welcomed me as their sole pupil with enthusiasm. These people were about t turn me into an ultimate fighting and deadly machine. What I was about to learn, was something very few ever would. I learnt Hwarang-Do - an ancient Korean martial art ( that teaches many combat techniques, including 'supernatural abilities.' It is a way of life and honour; it's title translating to 'The Way of Flowering Knights/Manhood.' It was eventually outlawed when the official religion of Korea changed (1392 AD to 1910 AD) and is much like Tae Kwan Do. Upon travelling to the Shino Temples, I was taught the Style of Kagura Chizuru, an ancient martial art of Shinto shrine maidens. Kempo - 'The Way of the Fist' a form of martial arts practiced as early as the seventh century in China by Shaolin Buddhist monks. It primarily uses the fists for fighting. Kopo-ken - a deadly Japanese close combat style. A fighter using this style will rapidly move in close and deliver quick blows to vulnerable spots on the human body, inflicting painful and sometimes bone-breaking damage. I travelled back to Japan finally, where I continued my craving for knowledge. I was already a walking human tool of strife, but I controlled this well. When there, I used more sensei's at my disposal to lean Kyokugen-Ryu Karate along with developing and controlling use of my positive energy, otherwise known as my aura. Along with Blood Flow Atemi - In this attack the fighter strikes at one of his opponent's vital points, temporarily stopping the flow of blood to an area of the body. I even learnt the thought to be mythological and dead art of Tien- Hsueh otherwise known as 'Touch Mastery'. This teaches one to execute various body strikes which are used to paralyse opponents with intense pain, knock out, and kill. Many of the sensei's who taught me this held high positions in underground secret societies. This proved to be the most gruelling and cruel aspect of my time learning the arts of the lost styles, but it was worth it. At the still young age of 22. I was finally content and satisfied with my education in the arts, and decided to bring to it to an end, still at the time contemplating a return in the near future. At this young age I was already developed enough to kill a single person in one poetic swift motion. That scared me to the bone then, it scared me even more that I wanted so dearly to learn it to it's fullest. The educaton in the arts soon became a matter of protection for myself. I was under no doubt that after entering such a shaded tournament and winning it certain people would be after my head. I needed to be ready, needed to be ready for if and when the game of cat and mouse finally started to get swinging in full motion. The question on my mind now though, was ' what next?' I was a highly evolved combat machine, I needed to put it to good use in an honourable way. Then so came my decision to visit America once again, if only for a brief period of time. Yet things took a dramatic turn for the worse...... On my last night in Tokyo while heading back to my apartment, something caught my attention. A moan, or a cry. Someone was calling for me, someone needed help. I looked further into the darkness surrounding me that night, locating the sound of the withered cries of and old voice which eventually led me to a small alleyway. A small petit old man, his head resting loosely against his chest, he was reaching out to me with his arm. He was helpless, and suffering from something, possibly an attack, I didn't know for sure. Yet I took it upon myself to help the man. 'Help my son' He requested. I still remember those three words. I grabbed his outstretched hand, and that was when I felt it. The short sharp unexpected stinging pain, hit and anxious, burning as it ran through me. A small dark, that's all it was, driven into my outstretched arm from his other concealed arm. He looked up at me as he drive it into me, his eyes full of menace and hate. 'Your mine now boy.....' he spoke as I began to lose consciousness. 'Your mine now...' Waking up, I found myself in a small shack, lit by old scented candles with the dancing of shadows keeping me company, along with that wretched old evil. It still troubles me to this day just to think back to it, and I'm not going to spend too long going into the dark details of it now. Then again neither did he when he explained my situation to me, he was blunt and to the point. His name was Jeremiah O'Barr. He said he had infected me with a rare poison he called the Orchi Doku. He explained without his help I would not find the antidote, though he swore to give it to me when I completed his many vast objectives. Until then he would provide me with a small antidote each month. He explained how he was one of the renegade monks who was part of the old Monastery in China where I won the gruelling brutal tournament. He said he had been scouting me ever since then, he said he needed a warrior, he told me I was the one. Depression sunk in just like the poison sunk into my body that night, swirling through my mind as the poison did in my blood. I was trapped in an invisible prison, the toy to a dark puppet master. Soon my whole outlook on life changed as I travelled with this elderly evil of a man across the globe, completing his objectives, his shady deals. Suddenly the world wasn't such a grand place to be anymore. I had trained myself to become a finely tuned combat machine, and now for what? To put it to use for this old man? That seems to be one of the main uses for it these days. So now, with that poison running through me and my heart and soul turning more black and putrid every day because of it, I seek for some small drops of purity to coarse through my sick twisted mind and body. Doing what seems good and just seems to provide me with those small drops of purity I so long for. Yet know I'm also longing for the anarchy and violence that elderly evil has brought me up on. With suicide as the ultimate sign of weakness, I seek death day in and day out, the closer I feel to it the more independent and free I feel. So now I am here, we are here. He has his own reasons for entering this industry, as I have mine. What are they? Why don't you take a guess, chances are your idea of what I'm going to do is here is a lot more logical than mine. Then again, there is no logic left in my life, only the need for salvation, and the harmful yet truthful motto of... Victims... aren't we all?
