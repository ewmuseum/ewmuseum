Clay Vendetta Biography Nicknames 'Classic' Hometown Miami, FL Birthday March 31, 1974 Height 5' 11' Weight 229 Fed Titles -none- Fed Awards T-16, WrestleOnline Rankings (Jan/Feb '03) Biography Born in March of 1974 as Clayton Battaglia, Clay Vendetta was part of a rich Italian family living in upstate New York. Clay was always a spoiled kid; but athletic, and quickly became a bully amongst the weaker kids, often stealing lunch money just so he could throw it in the trash can and laugh. The smaller kids were afraid of him, and the threats of having his parents sue were enough to scare away the larger ones. Vendetta got into amateur wrestling at an early age and led his high school to three conference championships in the four years he was at the school. Wrestling for FSU, Clay was quickly overmatched. This wasn't a result of his lack of skill so much as his temper, which got him ejected from a team record five bouts in his first season, after which he was asked not to return. After a public shouting match with the coach, he stormed off the squad, lighting his singlet on fire. Though he didn't wrestle, he graduated the school with a degree in communications (though most of the four years were spent partying: his GPA barely squeaking through the graduation requirements). As a college student, Clay often used his large (though short) frame as a bouncer for local clubs and establishments, a career he'd keep as a second job after graduation. With his degree in tow, Clay moved to Miami, working odd jobs but never finding major work. Still, he made enough money to get by working the clubs on South Beach. Battaglia quickly grew restless for competition, though. After dropping the wrestling team in college he had taken basic martial arts (from which he was expelled by the sensai once more for his total disregard for the other students) and joined an underground fighting club. With his new name, Clay Vendetta, he quickly made a reputation for himself as a ruthless fighter with a great grasp of submission tactics. Clayton grew up watching professional wrestling, admiring the work of many of the legends of the time, but wasn't involved with the business until being introduced to the IWA's Tommy Silver one night at a club. Silver, the IWA's Continental champion at the time, offered a bodyguard position to Clay with a hefty salary, and Vendetta quickly accepted. He began work as Silver's ring escort; but when the promoter saw him dispatch some of Silver's opposition he was offered a contract on top of the personal services contract from Silver and began work in the IWA. Silver gave Vendetta the Continental title so he could take a stab at the World Title, and Vendetta successfully defended the title fourteen times before dropping it to Taylor 'The Icebreaker' Post in a steel cage. In the meantime, Vendetta was instrumental in getting Silver the IWA World Championship, interfering in a title match against Nightfall so Silver could win the belt. The combined egos of Silver and Vendetta became too much to stay together for long after Vendetta felt he owned a part of the championship; and soon Vendetta cost Silver the title by laying him out mid-match. The fans quickly got behind Silver, but in a triple threat match soon after, Vendetta won the IWA World Championship, making Silver tap out. Contract squabbles followed, and the promoter fired Vendetta in a very public split. Clay bounced through three indy feds afterwards, never gaining much of a foothold with his reputation until he hit the NDWF, a larger indy fed in the Southeast. The NDWF was heavily into a new age style: hardcore and high flying, and it disgusted the very traditional Vendetta. Christening himself 'Classic' Clay Vendetta, CCV went on a tear, wining their World Title within six months while ending two careers in the process and injuring three others. His submissions-based style and arrogant attitude won over no fans but did endear himself to one of the valets, the beautiful Niki Weathers, who ditched her boyfriend to accompany Vendetta. 2002 Soon Vendetta had become a feared name among many of the indy feds along the East Coast, and the Jane Reagan, OSW owner, found out about Vendetta on a scouting mission. Offering him a lucrative contract (the largest of his career), she signed Vendetta and Weathers to be charter members on the OSW roster. After a series of low-profile feuds, Vendetta made a name for himself by demolishing an invading Team CCW, ending the career of former star Griff Truxell, and helping to put The Cult, a much-feared stable, out of business. A partnership with 'Old School' Dave Gibson produced Vendetta's first golden results, a run with the tag titles, and catapulted him into fan favorite status. At OSW's Road To Ruins, though, Gibson turned on Vendetta, costing the Throwbacks the titles, and launched a Gibson/Vendetta feud that was ugly and vicious. The feud culminated in a double-submission draw at OSW/NSW Resolution; however, Vendetta got his revenge on Gibson after the match by plastering him with one of the most vicious chairshots in OSW history; a shot that earned him an indefinite suspension from owner Jane Reagan. Livid with the status of OSW, Vendetta sought to gain his revenge on the corrupt owner Reagan and entered into secret negotations with the PWC owner; with the help of his lawyers, he was able to loophole out of his OSW contract and sign with PWC. 'Classic' Clay Vendetta's purpose in PWC is two-fold: one, to get back to the roots of professional wrestling; and two, show the world exactly what he's capable of when a promoter has finally been willing to give him the ball and run with it. Wrestling pundits expect no less than greatness from the 'Classic' one here in PWC; and many an opponent will soon find out what it's like to 'show up... suit up... and TAP OUT.' Vendetta can pull the move off from the other side, but it tends to be weaker on the left; on his right, it's nearly unbreakable.) Set-Up Move: Tomahawk Drop - Named after the traditional 'Tomahawk Chop' chant at Vendetta's alma mater, FSU, Vendetta takes the opponent high into the air, then snaps a 180 rotation as he drives the opponent head-first into the canvas, brainbuster-style. South Beach Suplex - Vendetta hooks the leg and executes an inverted fisherman's suplex. Often, it's used to set-up and float-over into an STF. Classic Anklelock - Ever the perfectionist, Vendetta makes sure to be in prime position on an opponent's back before executing his flawless variant on the anklelock (the extra weight on the back helps to prevent an opponent making the ropes). The Miami Vice - Another unique submission hold, it's a modified camel-clutch. The key differences in the hold is that Vendetta applies the camel clutch with a double underhook of the arms. Also, instead of sitting onto the wrestler's back, Vendetta leans back all the way and applies the move standing on the back of the opponent's shins, trapping the opponent upright. Chair-assisted screwdriver submission - Not a 'signature' move per se, and not commonly used, but a move invented by Vendetta that he'll use in situations where the rulebook has been thrown out. Vendetta takes a steel chair and traps one of the opponent's legs in it while he's face-up on the mat. Then, twisting the leg (and chair) over the opposing leg, he locks a Liontamer, pulling back on the chair to twist the legs and leaning back to apply pressure on the neck.
