Clark Carlisle Biography Nicknames 'The Pride of the PWC' - 'Attorney to the Stars' - 'The World's Leading Entrepreneur' - 'Unparalleled Businessman' - 'Master Manipulator' Hometown Hartford, Connecticut Birthday March 31, 1978 Height 6'5 Weight 235 Fed Awards Winner of the premiere Last Man Standing tournament Biography For as far back as I can remember, I’ve been commended for the plethora of intellect, power, and funds at my disposal. Maybe it’s resentment or adoration that compels various individuals to do so, but for whatever the reason people find me...attractive if you will. They are perpetually enthralled with my superiority, perhaps because they are under the illusion that they can identify with me. Don’t bother looking back over the previous statement. Yes, the very idea is laughable. And so, even supposing that fact that I hate the notion of sounding clichè by your low standards, I will begin my own long awaited narrative in the manner that so many have undeservingly done before me. Once upon a time... Two love-struck kids consciously (this might be hard for you to understand, this means they were both willing...unheard of nowadays, right?) reached an agreement to tie the knot. However, the smitten couple had their share of obstacles to dodge before they could do so legally. The ardent duo did manage to sidestep the regulations by the State of Connecticut by means that I won’t disclose at this particular point in time, after all lawyers are trained to argue, not reveal. And that, no doubt contributes to why I’m such an upstanding officer of the legal profession today. Ah, but I digress...after marriage (yet another foreign concept, I hope this isn’t to much for you to stomach) the Carlisles’ gave a quiet home birth to their only foreseeable glimmer of hope, Clark Stanton Carlisle. Both my parents, Valerie and Charles Carlisle are significant thereafter, only because of my annual celebration of birth being a constant reminder of their intentions for me and of how modest they were in those intentions. My actual birthday was March 31st, 1978 be sure to note it on your calendars. Oh, I almost forgot...Valerie and Charles pushed their remarkable luck over the edge when 3 years later they borne Cameron Melissa Carlisle, my sister and in the succeeding 3 years my brother, Dylan Julius Carlisle. I will touch on the minute roles they both play in my life later on. Growing up as the son of the wealthy, and still on the rise Charles Carlisle, I was, fittingly, asserted into a dominant position among his peers early on. Even after being a grade at my father’s suggestion, I had an apparent difference in the matured outlook that I had on life, compared to those of the other children. I grasped the elementary concepts very easily, and as a result understood perfectly well that the other children hated me on account of this. Therefore, I did what the average artless, six year old would and withdrew from my classmates as the “weird kid” that was unexplainably shy, introverted, and seemingly at one with his privacy. What’s more those simpletons gave me a chronic case of “cooties”, and the adamant disapproval of my father, which I suppose led to my pragmatic incapacity to fail as an adult. But because this obviously didn’t sit well with my father Charles, he addressed me, his first born very directly in the matter. In fact, I can remember his exact words while he put on my tie that morning before school: “There’s no way my son’s gonna be the sappy, overemotional woman of the family!” He barked at me. “Now you’re gonna make some friends today and by God, you’re gonna like it; because if you come back here without even one...” I recollect his face trembling here for effect. “...then I’m sorry son, but you’re gonna hafta find another place to live, indefinitely.” Though sarcastic in his approach, my father’s words rested in the cusps of my ears throughout the day. And eventually during my recess interlude between classes, I gathered up the courage and approached the loudest, rudest, most obnoxious bully in my class and in a sudden fit of brilliance....I pushed my lips to hers and commenced drooling, thus producing the sloppiest and juiciest five seconds of both of our lives. I was obligated to sluuuuuuuurrrrp like so in order to speak clearly before speaking to this bully, and fortuitously for you, I’m able to evoke the contents of this conversation also. “Now you gotta be my friend Lorelei, or I’m gonna tell everybody that I gave you my cooties!” I mocked at my most vicious tormenter. She gasped “Not uh!” She shrieked. “Yeah huh!” I shouted back to her. “Not uh!” “Yeah huh!” “Not u” ...Any fool could see where this is headed. As soon as our profound dispute concluded, Lorelei Tolerton and I became best friends in every sense of the word, though we never again mentioned our first “kiss” together because of her existing current sexual preferences. However, this droll specimen remains an important piece of my replete life. For now I am back in grammar school, and the contradiction of my former self. I was the popular yet intelligent jock, if that’s even conceivable. For years I was the center of attention of every adolescent at Stratford Elementary, and the envy of others. Yet, as I progressed into my pre-teenage years the single most unlikely event transpired. My younger siblings and I at our respective ages of 11, 8, and 5 lost our father (then 27) to what was revealed recently as cancer. Doctors considered it a great mystery as to how he kept this a secret from his own family and friends, but I for one know how easy it is to harbor fascination and ire from the ones closest to oneself. I conducted the funeral on behalf of my father, the affair was heavily publicized and I’m done grieving for now. As though my father was still living, I proceeded through Junior High and High school with the same self-confidence, respectability, and shrewdness that I’d learned from my mentor. I gained the respect of my classmates via large varieties of Prep League football, basketball, and lacrosse regional and all state honors; I gained the respect of my teachers by means of uninterrupted annual distinctions of High Honor Roll. All the while, I continued to be he brain-dead (though smarter than most of them) jock to the geeks that I took advanced classes with, and the pretty boy Einstein prodigy to my friends, the other jocks. I was always known for being up for a good time at Common Ground High School, but still I proved dependable in academic matters, as well as personal ones. When my longtime friend Lorelei came out to my in High School I managed to keep her secret long throughout. Furthermore, not only did I bring my weak mother, sister, and brother through these difficult times, but I also delegated the responsibilities that were formerly my father’s. I gave ownership of the Carlisle, Cohen, and Associates law firm to its senior partner, Stuart Cohen and installed a non-profit commitment to each of my father’s charities. Though once I matured and grew even more business sense, I quickly removed these silly liabilities. Alas, my glory days at Common Ground High School came to a close in the year 1996. I was named Valedictorian of my class (would you expect any less?) and went on to attend Yale’s School of Law. While there, during my sophomore year I was blind sighted by, feasibly, the most gorgeous woman alive, (Trust me, I don't settle for just anyone) her name, I discovered, was Rae Francis de Costa Martinez however, I made certain, it wouldn’t be for much longer. I could go through the particulars of our marriage: the climaxes and our inevitable decline, but I’ll save that for an ABC special. I will say, that my wife Rae and I met at a mock case in which Yale’s then highest scoring student (Clark Carlisle) was put on trial against Harvard’s current top student (Rae Martinez). My bride-to-be would win the case although we both ended up “in chambers” so to speak that night. Rae and I were married November 14th, 1998, and a year later brought my son, Bradley Clark Alexander Carlisle into being. (Born August 12th, 1999) As we shift into the present, Rae (now 26) and I (now 24) have separated and share joint custody of our son Bradley. Though they’ve all been thrown from everyplace with any part of the Carlisle name on the lease, I still talk to them on occasion (rest assured it’s in good spirits, on my behalf at least) and still intend on raising my son to be just like his father...when I feel like it. I did indeed graduate from Yale’s Law School (Class of 2000 JD summa cum laude) and now preside over the Carlisle, Cohen, and Associates law firm as Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. All of this in addition to remaining an active member of the American Bar Association and the Law Society. Generally speaking, I’ve put my money where my mouth is in the past, and only gained more money because of it. ‘Unparalleled Businessman’, ‘The World’s Leading Entrepreneur’ you better believe these aren’t just labels that sound good attached to the name ‘Clark Carlisle‘. Now I know what you morons are going to ask with broken profanity next: Why the hell does this guy wrestle? Well, colleagues and patrons of the greatest beneficiary in North America and possibly the world, Clark Carlisle, your answer is simple. I deserve more money for my unflagging effort and intend to use my natural athleticism to its full potential. By joining the Primetime Wrestling Academy in January of 2003 I did just that. My immeasurable contribution to the PWA’s enterprise was so great that I gained honors as its Top Student for the weeks of 3/5/03 until 3/24/03. Thusly, as I reached the pinnacle of my Primetime Wrestling Academy fame I was hired by another highly regarded businessman, Ron Starr. I now act as legal council for the Pro Wrestling Circuit as well as a crucial member of its roster. More to the point, I am now free from the anxiety of needing and hating all of the attention I get as the son of a late, wealthy, and successful man. The world will forever recognize the name Clark Carlisle as one of conquest, wisdom, and prosperity. And given my callous background within my line of work, I don't suspect having any trouble doing so.
