Reverberations

5 February 2003

Date: 05.02.03 “That is, like, so not you” Sounded the easily recognisable Californian tones of Sarah, only friend of Sam Alotti in the known world. I say it was Sarah, but I cannot be sure, due to not actually being able see her yet. She could be anything really, a three eyed green thing, a transvestite, or even, I shudder to think, Mo Mowlam. Californians all sound the same to me. “I know, dammit, I know! It just won’t seem to work today!” Alotti growled angrily, her midland English tones grabbing a menacing tone she very rarely used. She knew today would be a bad day; it was already spelling itself out in her eyes. The face greeting us in the gold-framed mirror was one of beauty unexpected. Sam glowed with the aura of someone who spends far too much time staring at herself in the mirror. Her eyes seemed to radiate a beauty only deemed imaginable in the depths of the teenage boys mind and her skin was prepared to a level where her light tan was complimented perfectly by the make-up. The only problem seemed to be her hair. The brush in her hands whirls with a precision and accuracy that would have blurred slightly to the untrained eye, flicking hair to the left and right in a vain attempt to get it to settle. “Just give up girl, its so not happening today.” The Californian Sarah piped up again, still hidden from view behind the flailing Sam at the mirror. The tone of deep boredom in her voice indicated this had been going on for some time now. “It will, I just need some, argh, time” She bites back a shout as she tugs the brush through a large knot. Some days you just knew that it wouldn’t happen for you, these are generally known as weekdays in the Alotti household. It’s hard to remember everything sailing smoothly since Sarah let in the young English woman stay while she found her bearings in the big city. “You’ve had like, twenty minutes, the mark of a stylist is like so totally knowing when to give up” It was hard to hide twenty minutes of boredom, even if it did involve your best friend. “Well then I would have asked one if I had the money, wouldn’t I?” She snapped back bitterly. Indeed, she did not have enough money to see a stylist; she barely had enough to pay for the water and the brush she had used to brush it. Time had been hard on her since she had stopped the Indy wrestling. Jobs had avoided her as most would avoid the bubonic plague and she had been stuck boarding with her friend Sarah until she found another company to work for. Places were hard to come by in the wrestling world, but she had still received several offers for her female talents. It seemed being a woman was a godsend in this industry of male domination. People such as Chryste had proven that women are much more talented than they had been given dues, but still they had not quite made it out of the woodwork. After rejection from several companies for not actually being able to wrestle, she had changed dramatically. Her job handing leaflets to hurrying shoppers had bought her a cheap-ass gym membership and she had learnt how to hip toss with the best of them. Now, it seemed, was the time for her to finally shine on the world scene. The TV beckoned her like blood to a shark and she was about to go for the kill. “I give up!” she shouts while flinging the brush at the neat blue wallpaper “How am I supposed to make it big in the televised world if my hair is floating like a balloon!” Rising to her feet, the strong beam of light filtered in from the partially drawn curtains cast a shadow across half of her face, illuminating the angry scowl to its most effective best. Scowling isn’t something she does often and it triggers an emotion so strong that she wants to hurt stuff. Most people would have accustomed themselves to this a long time ago, while going through the teenage era, but Sam was a happy teenager, rare as they are. She never really got used to anger because she was such a happy person. She turned slowly on her heels and paced across the room. The thin blue carpet under her feet scrunched slightly as it was not properly tacked at the edges. Everything in the room was blue. Sam hated blue, yet she couldn’t really critique it while not even being able to afford to change it. Maybe that would come later. “Hey, like calm down babe” Suggested the ever sensible, but somewhat dim, Sarah “You got, like, ages yet girl” She flicked back her blonde, slightly generic hair and watched Sam pacing the room. Some people wanted fame and other obsessed, she thought. Sam was definitely the latter. “It’s okay for you, being at the bottom is alright for some, but not for me! I want gold, glitz, glamour!” She waved her hands around vigorously as she stared up into her own little world. You would be surprised how often this happened to Sam, the call was too strong. “Hold up” Sarah glares at the now pacing figure of Sam “You think I LIKE not having any money?” Sam could be so infuriatingly inconsiderate! It must be that English blood. “No, not that you don’t like not having any money, just that you well, don’t do much about it” Sam could feel it turning against her. She never meant to offend Sarah. She very rarely tried to offend anyone. Things just always seemed to go the wrong way. “I SO work hard! You think this place pays for itself?” “Okay! Ok, I’m sorry! Just calm down!” Sam paced to the small kitchen and flicked at the gleaming stainless steel tap, which began spewing water. She opened a plastic cupboard above her head and removed a small glass. She filled it with water and headed back into the other room with a deep sigh. Sam getting a message across without making an arse out of herself was as likely as getting blood from a stone or touching your elbows together behind your back. Sarah still glared into the mirror hatefully. She had a hard time getting anything to work, especially her hair, and Sam pitied her for it. It can’t be easy to settle for mediocrity. “Why didn’t you just suck him off?” blurts Sarah. The deep resentment in her voice indicated that she was still unhappy with Sam “I can’t believe you just asked me that” “I mean, he’s not that bad looking is he?” Sarah carries on without listening to the Sam at all. She seemed stuck into the path of thought. “What are you saying?” Sam begins to fret, this was unlike Sarah and she had never seen this side of her before. So cool, calm and focused. “We could have been out of here. You would so have made millions of dollars and you stick to you principals, like, like, like… I don’t know!” Sarah clambers to her feet and fights towards the door, slamming it so violently that the reverberations echo down the hall. “I’m… Selfish?”
