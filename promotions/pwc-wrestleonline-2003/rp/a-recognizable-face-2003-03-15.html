A Recognizable Face?

15 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/15/03 A group of young boys huddle together at the end of the aisle, whispering and pointing to each other. Each has on a winter coat of some sort, a few with gloves on their hands. The corner drug store is busy this time of day, with people stopping in on their way home from work. Several unknown people pass in front of the camera as it remains focused on the young boys, their faces bright with astonishment and excitement. Their voices begin to carry somewhat, and we pick up their words. “I know that’s him, you dork!” exclaims one of the young boys. He has short brown hair and some light freckles on his face. “I saw him on television the other night.” “You’re so full of it,” butts in another boy, this one with shaggy blonde hair and round spectacles on his face. “Your parents don’t let you stay up late enough to watch that.” The first boy frowns and slaps the blonde headed boy on the arm. “ So what! I snuck downstairs and watched after they went to bed, and I saw him on the show.” A third boy pipes up, uncertainty in his voice. “Are you sure? He doesn’t look like much in person.” His tightly cropped black hair is spiked on top, and his face contorts into a quizzical expression. “Go up and ask him!” goads the fourth and final boy. His red hair looks like someone cut around a bowl to style it, and his freckles are bright in color. The last three boys all agree that the first should go over to this mystery person, giving him a gentle nudge. He whips his head around and gives his three companions a sharp glare, then turns and slowly makes his way down the aisle. He passes the aspirin and pain medication, then arrives at the Ace bandages and wraps. He looks up at someone in front of him with an innocent face. “Excuse me mister,” he says, then looks back at his friends. All three of them are looking on, their eyes fixated and unblinking at the situation unfolding in front of them. “Are you that Jim Axtell guy off of the wrestling show?” The camera pans back to see who the boy is talking to, and it just so happens to be ‘The Axphyxiator’ himself, Jim Axtell. He looks down at the boy, surprised that he was recognized. “Yeah, kid,” replies Jim kindly. “What’s your name?” The boy smirks and turns around to his friends, pumping his fist at his side. His three friends look at each other and big smiles emerge across their lips, then they come running down the aisle. They come to a stop at the side of their friend, all four looking up in awe of Jim. The first boy finally responds. “My name is Richard, and I’m a huge wrestling fan!” The exuberance in the young boy’s voice is refreshing to a tired and beaten Axtell. “Nice to meet you, Richard,” says Jim. “Who are your friends here?” “Well…” starts Richard, but he’s interrupted by his friends as they all call out their names at the same time. “JohnnyMikeyDavey!!” “Whoa, whoa!” chuckles Jim. “One at a time!” The blonde boy is the first to get his name out. “I’m Mikey!” Jim smiles warmly. “Hello, Mikey.” “And I’m Johnny,” says the black haired boy. “Hello,” Jim nods. “And I take it that you’re Davey?” The red headed kid nods his head emphatically. “Yessir!” “I saw you on television this last Saturday night! What did you do to make that humongous guy so mad at you?” asks Richard innocently. Axtell kneels down so that he’s face to face with the kids. “Let me tell you something, boys,” starts Jim. “As you grow up you’ll find that there are some people that just don’t care about anyone else. Those people like to be mean and hurt other people for no good reason at all. That…humongous guy…is one of those people. Those kinds of people are bad, and it’s up to people like me to stand up to them.” The boys listen wide-eyed, taking in every word Jim says. “So you gonna beat that Penance up?” asks Mikey anxiously. Axtell chuckles, messing Mikey’s hair up with is hand. “I don’t know about beating him up,” Jim says as he stands back up. “But I sure plan on giving him something to think about the next time he decides to pick on someone. Now you kid better run along, I’m sure your parents are wondering where you’re at.” The kids smile and start off down the aisle, offering encouragement and farewells to Jim. He waves to the kids and watches them disappear around the end of the aisle. From behind Jim a hand wraps around his waist, a tender touch that Jim knows all too well. He smiles warmly as his wife, Kami, moves around from behind Jim to in front of him. “Well hello there, stranger,” coos Jim. Kami looks up at Jim with a playful smile. “Hi, sweetie.” “Did you find what you needed to get?” questions Jim. “Umm-hmm…” responds Kami, revealing something she had been hiding behind her back. She raises it to her husbands eye level, and Jim studies it for a second before realizing what it is. “That’s a…” he starts, but cannot finish. “I know it is, Jim.” Kami wiggles the home pregnancy test in front of her husbands face like a carrot in front of a rabbit. “I haven’t had one yet this month.” “So you might be….” Jim can’t speak full sentences at the moment. The blonde woman smiles and hugs her husband. The look of shock is frozen on Jim’s face as he embraces his wife. As they part, he looks down at her with a serious expression. “This means you’re gonna have to stay home from now on when I go to wrestle.” The caring in Jim’s voice is accompanied by sternness. “I can’t wrestle and worry about you at the same time.” “But Jim!” Kami objects. Jim raises his hands and turns his head away. “This is not up for discussion, Kam. This is something I’ve been thinking about for a while anyway, but the fact that you might be pregnant cements it. You stay home from now on.” Kami opens her mouth to argue some more, but as she looks in her husbands eyes she realizes that it’s a lost cause. “Okay, honey, if that’s what you want.” Jim grabs a few ace bandages and a tube of SportsCreme, then ushers his wife toward the registers. “Let’s go find out what that thing has to say.”
