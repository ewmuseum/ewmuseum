Concerns and Bridges

10 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03-10-03 Promo Title: Concerns and Bridges Date Made: 03-10-03 Location: Along the banks of a man-made creek ‘Of course I watched my debut again. In fact, I needed to watch the tape quite a few times. There was something truly wonderful about the symphony of pain that I composed Saturday night. I replayed it in my head many times, smiling at it, loving toward it, pleased and just plain tickled at my little creation. Two measly, stupid women suffered, all thanks to me. This is what I traveled all the way across country for: the pain of others. For a while, their misery contented me. I felt healed, renewed, and happy… but soon I was starting to feel empty again. Try as I might, by lunch the next day, I could no longer draw the same rush and pleasure from the experience. The images in my head were starting to get fuzzy and gray. Like every good dream, the pictures were rapidly fading, and with them, my appeased emotions. I practically had to track down a tape, which I literally needed to put into a VCR. I fast-forwarded straight to my part, hit ‘play,’ and was instantly rewarded with gratification. Even now, I can hear the VCR purr as Samantha Alotti’s head drives into the mat. It is blessedly clear in my mind again: Karina crawling pitifully to make sure her friend is all right. If I close my eyes and think hard enough, I can even hear that tool, Mark Malloy. “I have no clue who those two were…” Well, now you know. You all know. I am Paula Michelle Smith. Perhaps you know a thing or two about me, but believe it when I say: you will learn more. The Tool also commented that my actions were not exactly popular. I’ve never tried to be popular; I did what I did for nobody else except me, and for nobody’s reasons save my own. Love me or hate me… we all die in the end.’ Dawn starts to throw light down upon on a row of cookie-cutter houses that line a completely undistinguished road. Beyond the borders of their backyards, undeveloped land runs down a hill, into the very extreme borders of a forest. The trees, while remarkably clumped together in some areas, are generally well spaced from their companions. Last night’s rains have given the foliage a newfound zeal for the green of life, but they have also helped create the fog that covers the ground like a smothering pillow. About a hundred yards past where the trees begin, a man made creek slices through the land, a jagged, gaping wound inflicted upon nature. At one area along its banks, a flashlight manages to part the fog. The small halo of light quickly passes over three backpacks laying in the drying mud, pass over tall grasses matted down with clumsy footsteps, and finally flicker across the still water. It quickly retreats back to solid land, where three young boys, their skin all flushed red with effort, struggle to last together what appears to be a raft. The boys all appear to be around thirteen or fourteen years old, and they are all dressed alike: black sneakers, blue jeans, and a ‘Hartwood Junior High’ white, Polo T-Shirt. It must be some sort of school uniform. “No, Marcus, you fucking idiot,” the largest one says as he points to a knot-in-progress, “you’re tying it all wrong.” “Oh, sorry, Greg,” Marcus responds, his voice dripping with sarcasm, “I wasn’t a fucking girl scout like you were.” The other boy, by far the smallest of the group, stands a few feet away, fidgeting while he holds the flashlight with shaking hands. “G-guys… why is it so foggy?” he offers weakly as his eyes dart around nervously. “I’m scared… and, and I f-feel like something’s watchin’ us.” Marcus looks up, a humored, taunting smile on his face. “Shut up, baby.” “I-I’m not a baby!” Greg turns around and looks the small boy up and down. “Yeah you are, Tom. Now shut up, and point the fucking flashlight where it’ll help us.” Tom quickly does as he is told. The light weakly illuminates a shoddily constructed piece of handiwork: some clearly questionable pieces of wood have been lashed together with thin rope, and there are some very visible gaps in the raft. Greg and Marcus, however, both look insanely proud at their creation. They slowly push it towards the water. “Now, don’t let go of it,” Greg warns. “I fucking know!” Marcus says, his screechy voice breaking ever so slightly. As they push the ‘raft’ further into the water, and closer to the far bank, it slowly becomes obvious that their fabrication is actually supposed to be a bridge. They struggle to get it out of the water, and onto the far bank. Their combined strength is just enough to lift their end so that it is out of the stream as well. The bridge just in place, and Greg is just getting ready to try it out for the first time, when a sharp female voice cuts through the early morning air. “BOYS! THE SCHOOL BUS IS ALMOST HERE!” Greg sighs loudly, throws his arms up in the air, and rolls his eyes. “Aw, man!” Marcus makes sure the bridge is in place, and then turns away, walks over to and then collects his backpack. “Come on, dude, we’ll come right back here after school.” Greg stares at the bridge, while behind him, Tom shuts off the flashlight and gladly follows after Marcus. “Yeah,” Greg says quietly, to nobody in particular. Reluctantly, he turns and follows his friends. After a few seconds later, a little further down the bank, the shadows of a human form start to take shape in the fog. Slowly, the fog parts for a figure of darkness: black hair ripples down from the being’s crown, falling around narrowed, hate-filled eyes, which dominate a face that is completed with a petite nose and thin, cruel lips. A black jacket is unzipped, falling away before a black T-Shirt that bears a red Anarchy symbol. Black jeans fall down around thin legs, just as clenched fists drop to the girl’s sides. ‘Figures. I always do my best thinking while walking… alone. And here, I thought I had found a nice, secluded spot, but no, I have to stumble across the Three Stooges, and then I get to enjoy the pleasure of their insipid profanity and insults. Men, in general, are bad, but boys are particularly worthless.’ Paula starts towards their bridge. Wisps of fog curl around her feet, even as the clouds back away from the young girl. She comes to a stop once again right before the bridge. She looks down at it contemptuously, puts a boot on the wooden surface, and prepares to kick it into the water. ‘What a pity. And they worked so hard on it, too…’ Suddenly, she pulls her foot back. A sick smile crosses her face, and her dainty hand quickly darts into her pocket. A split second later it resurfaces, a cell phone in its grasp. She flicks her wrist, which opens the phone, and then quickly dials a number. As it starts to ring, the noise actually loud within the morning’s predominant silence, she puts it to her ear. A moment later: “Yeah, it’s me. Look, I need you here, and I need you to bring a few things…” ‘While we are waiting for my unsightly assistant to show up, I need to give serious, honest thought to a few issues. Now that I am here, they are going to start talking. They are going to start examining me for any weakness. I am short. Sure, okay. But, one could argue that I am at the perfect height to make some of the men around here truly sorry if they mess with me. I am small. I am thin. I suppose the outside observer would assume I am quite breakable… but I know the truth. I have endured my share of pain and lived through it all. I think I can take whatever punishment they try to dish out. And I will get to prove myself soon enough. Ariath is my strength, but he will not always be around. I cannot fight strong, but I can fight fast, and smart. I will have to be smarter than my opponents, or I could very well not stand a chance. It’s not like I spent a lot of time learning any wrestling skills, either. But no matter what anybody says, or whatever questions they want to ask: I belong here, I deserve to be here, and I will just plain hurt anybody who gets in my way. … Yeah, that all sounds good enough.’ Paula shivers slightly as a soft breeze kicks up. Leaves in the trees, unseen beyond the morning’s haze, rustle and shift about quite noisily. The water at Paula’s feet begins moving in small waves. She coughs quietly into a small fist, and then quickly zips her jacket up. Her hands then fall loosely back to her sides, and she returns to thinking deeply and staring down at the stream. About twenty minutes later, the mist and fog slowly vomit up another human: Ariath. He walks along the bank of the brook, his head lowered so that his dirty blonde hair masks his face. A long, black trench coat licks the ground as it bounces off his calves in perfect rhythm with his large steps. His stained white T-Shirt makes another appearance; it has plastered itself to his upper-body, even as some of it has hidden away, tucked in to ratty blue jeans. In his left hand, the Puppet holds a book of matches, and his right hand contains the handle to an obviously full can of gas. Paula slowly pulls her eyes away from the water, and looks towards Ariath. “Took you long enough,” she says spitefully. The man lowers his head, and then holds forward the gas can and the lighter, trying desperately to show that he did a good job. Paula rewards him with a quick, humorless smile, and then simply points to the bridge; somehow, the command is understood. Ariath goes to the bridge, uncaps the gas, and starts pouring it onto the young boys’ creation. Glug, glug, glug. “I wasn’t going to involve the dumb bitch,” Paula says, thinking aloud. “I was mostly after Alotti. But come to find out, the hooker goes running her mouth. I guess she was taking a break from posing naked for nudie magazines, or selling her body, or whatever she’s doing to try and raise money. She threatened me.” She watches Ariath as the man slowly and carefully dispenses the gasoline all over the bridge. “I said,” Paula’s voice angrily stabs towards her follower, “she threatened me.” Ariath looks up, and the gasoline starts pouring onto his foot. He reaches up with his free hand, to push the hair away from his face, and then tilts his head to the side. After a second, he realizes what is happened, and he pulls the can up so that it stops pouring. He just stares at Paula for a long moment. “She said that I need to make sure not to mess with her friend,” Paula says quietly, her eyes narrowing menacingly. “She said that she will back up her friends, no matter what the cost.” Paula closes her eyes, and licks her lips. “Well?” Paula asks a moment later, her voice suddenly quiet. “Do you think we should test that statement?” Her eyes slowly open, and study Ariath, who nods ever so slightly. “I was thinking the same thing,” she responds, her tone cold. “Go back to what you were doing.” Ariath hesitates for a moment, and then starts pouring the gasoline again. “Now, as for Sa-man-tha,” Paula says, each syllable of the woman’s name oozing with more venom than the last, “we will have to come up with something truly special. We… like, totally, uhm, kicked her ass the other… you know, night.” Paula rolls her eyes as she scoffs loudly. “I swear to God, I’ve never listened to somebody more obnoxious in my life. While it would be fun to beat her senseless again, and we might just do it for kicks, if the mood strikes me…” Ariath nods as Paula speaks, and when the can is empty, he tosses it onto the bank and takes a few steps back. PMS also retreats a couple feet. The large man tears open the book of matches. “… I think we should have a little bit more fun than just that.” Ariath rips a match away from its friends; minute, barely visible splinters fly away as the now lonely match screams it protest. The man jams its head into the sandpaper strips, and then roughly and violently drags it across the coarse surface. It sparks up, and Ariath tosses it onto the bridge. The young boys’ work quickly goes up in flames. Paula watches the pyre, a twisted, satisfied look on her face. “If I’m really lucky,” Paula muses, “Sam will bring Sarah along. She is entertaining, in that ‘laughing at the fat pig’ sort of way.” Ariath, who almost looks guilty, watches as parts of the bridge crumble and fall into the creek. When the fire meets the water, tendrils of smoke shoot up from the brook’s surface, and the steam hisses like dying snakes. Next to him, Paula keeps talking. “How does the phrase go?” she asks rhetorically. “Something along the lines of: you can’t cross bridges that you burn. Bridges, friends, fans… who needs them?” She turns, and starts walking away from the creek. Ariath quickly follows after her. The fog quickly swallows them. The last portions of the bridge collapse into the water.
