The Biggest Night in PWC History [17]

13 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Apr. 13 The rain seemed to pound down amongst the industrial lands just outside of the Sovereign Bank Arena. A young man, around his early twenties, slowly pulled the beige BMW to the right side of the building. Oval headlights blinding the security guard as he turned in the compact booth, cap tilted slightly over sky opticals as he leaned out of the sliding window. A few quiet words exchanging between the two... 'You got a VIP Card?' 'You know it.' The guardsman's limb retrieved the thin crimson card, exchanging few thoughts and before handing the man back the thin plastic. He gave him a nod slowly as digit compressed against onyx button, lifting the gate before the vehicle. The BMW quickly then entered the lot, trailing down a single row to the orange zone, before pulling the car into an adjacent spot. Closing the door behind him, he jogged carefully up the steps into the VIP entrance, newspaper held above his head. Dressed within a blue windbreaker suit that came to his knee's; the rain seemed to slide from the material as he came to the entrance, leading him to throw the soaked paper to the side. Solo revolving door circled and pulled him into the building, florescent lit hallways effected his eyes momentarily, before he continued down the narrow path. Before that rusted metal elevator shaft stood two onyx clad men, bound by their gucci suits of such liking that would tell most people of the riches they've received from Ron Starr. Their facades seemed masked with the lack of expression upon them, however they were paid not to smile; they were paid for their lips not to move. Held within duel limbs were those heavy machines of mass destruction, pump shotguns. Attention held straight forward, no moves, no motions, no mistakes ... not even a whisper; the man slowly stopped before them. He knew they'd have special interest in him. After all, they had the pump shotguns for what he assumed was to make sure he stayed within his boundaries. Cranium adjusted upwards to the towering men as he gave a somewhat echoed chuckle. Frail nails scratching at the top of his skull as he seemed quite mad, quite disassociated. Slowly he pulled out the same crimson plastic card, extending limb towards the center of the two. The right soldier's head canted downwards, looking to the actions made by the man, then slowly readjusted his head back to attention, no words, no movements. The man's mind was now a puddle of cream corn, puzzled at the actions took by the two guards he pulled the card back, in same fluid movements flipping it over. Quickly shaking his head as he whispered those words of idiocy to himself, he looked back up to the guards, exclaiming 'I'm not here to kill him.' But he was. Synchronized movements pushed the two soldiers out from the entrance of the shaft, stepping to the side as well. The man sighed, letting out some much needed tension as he did so, and stepped into the shaft. The floor was slightly rotted, rusty, metal, and dusty, he noticed, as he knew the bottom of his shoes would end up just as filthy. He closed his ears and squinted as the awful creaking sound of the shaft going down tortured him in a way he had never been before. Finally, after several minutes, the shaft hit the floor. The man stumbled as the impact alone was quite shaky, before the doors slid open. Bright lights blinded him as he was suddenly thrown into the crowd... That man once considered himself of great importance, considered himself among the elite of the wrestlers, staff and faculty he had just become a member of; But in that moment he realized he was so wrong, as he looked around at the hundreds of faceless men and women-just like him in society. What was so special about him? He pondered that question to the bitter end as he watched the people take their seats. The blue stocking on his head left much to the imagination of the few people that had actually payed attention to the others around them. He realized the answer to his question quickly: He wasn't special. Not in the least. Looking up, to the adjacent side of the elevator shaft was a long hole in the wall, almost as if a balcony. Indeed it was the 'executive box' for this wrestling extravaganza, a long table resembling one of the last supper lying just within the center. Coiling around the back and ends of that table were six men, all seem to be unique and different from one another; whether it be nationality or style. The one man that stood out the most, the man fit almost to be a monster from the pages of tall tales ... slowly raised that silver and crystal glass to the crowd... a low roar of cheers ringing out... Ron Starr would address the crowd before the event continued. All this man wanted to do was watch. He could wait until the cage dropped before he killed him.
