Oh My God...!

20 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/20/03 'Thank you and you may be seated,' a smiling church pastors says, unfolding his hands. Rows upon rows of overdressed, emotional churchgoers look on as the pastor, who already has his jacket off and is sweating immensely paces across a carpeted stage. He goes on, 'I've got a question,” he hesitates, 'what are you, without God?' Those seated all answer to themselves, and a brief murmur resounds throughout the relatively small church. 'Well,' The pastor continues, 'take this as an example.' He walks to the opposite side of the stage, reaches to the ground, which is hidden by a few potted plants. He stands up with a golf club in hand. 'This golf club represents the relationship between you and God,' the pastor says, looking at the club, wiping off the shaft with a handkerchief. The small crowd all look around at each other, confused, awaiting an explanation. The pastor flips the club over, holding the head of the driver in his hand. He puts his other hand on the shaft and begins to unscrew the head. Once the golf club is in two pieces, he places the head down on the ground, next to a ball that he had placed there earlier. 'You see,' he explains, 'that head, is the representation of you, the sinner. He goes on, 'Alone, you can't do much to that ball. Sure, you can hit the ball, but not very far. That isn't going to do much for you golf game, or, your life. Not without the shaft, God.' The onlookers finally see where their pastor is going with all of this. The pastor picks the club head up off the ground and reattaches it to the shaft. 'You, or that golf club head, can't do much without God, without that shaft guiding, leading, and putting power into you.' The pastor continues on, but the scene begins to shift. Out and open window and across the street to a small hotel, Warpath stands outside the door of his hotel room, a one floored building, probably barely enough room for half of the PWC competitors. 'One thing I don't understand with Mr. Hoyt Williams is how for someone who is so dependent on an imaginary being, he thinks he has all this power.' Warpath says, staring at nothing. 'I never bought into all this, 'Greater Being', and afterlife shit. Relying on something you cannot see, touch, smell, or experience is a sign of weakness. Hoyt's devotion to this figment of the imaginations of millions is a weakness, one of his many.' Warpath begins to walk down a recently paved sidewalk, under the overhanging roof of the hotel. Hands in the pockets of his dark blue jeans, hair tied back and head down, he strolls alone to nowhere in particular. Speaking as he walks, 'Why should a grown man rely on an invisible friend to solve life's greatest problems? I mean, it’s okay to get things off you chest in prayer when you are a child, but as an independent man, no, you are much too old to still be playing make believe.' Warpath sits at the nearest seat, a white lawn chair in front of a neighboring room. 'I see no reason to confess my many wrongdoings in silence to an imaginary being. It’s really stupid, when you think about it. What am I without God? I am strong, I am self dependent, I am mentally tough, I am the opposite of one Hoyt Williams.' He closes. So here, the first look into the private world of Warpath. His religious beliefs or lack there of, are revealed as we go deeper and deeper into the man and monster.
