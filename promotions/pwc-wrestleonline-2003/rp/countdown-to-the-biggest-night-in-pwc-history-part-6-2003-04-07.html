Countdown to the Biggest Night in PWC History (Part 6)

7 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/7/03 ”Are you sure you don’t want to come along to the doctor, Kam?” “Oh, I would but the girls are getting together in half an hour and I promised them that I would be there. Is that alright?” There is a short pause, then a somewhat somber voice answers. “No….it’s no problem. Go ahead and have fun, babe. You deserve it.” The sound of kissing is heard. “You’re the best, Jim!” Next we hear a rustle and the sound of keys, followed by a shutting door. A deep sigh brings the next words. “I love you, too.” The scene opens up in Dr. Stevenson’s office at the local sports medicine clinic. A nervous Jim Axtell sits upright on the examination table, his face full of worry. He is alone at the moment, left to wonder what his prognosis will be. The former OSW security guard-turned-wrestler wears a pair of black mesh basketball shorts and a red Nebraska Cornhusker hooded sweatshirt. All around the office are photos and posters of horrendous injuries, ranging from shoulder separations to spinal injuries to broken bones of all kinds. The scenery almost makes ‘The Axphyxiator’ cringe, causing him to divert his eyes quickly to the floor. “I got a bad feeling about this one,” says Jim quietly to himself. Jim scans to his legs and focuses on his swollen and injured knee. The area around the knee appears to be about the size of a cantaloupe, puffy and bloated with fluid. There is some slight bruising as well, giving the skin a yellowish-purple tint. Axtell extends the leg out, bringing a painful wince to Jim’s face. His teeth grit together as he relaxes the leg once again, letting it dangle off the table. After a few more moments of tense waiting, the door to the office slowly opens inward. From the other side Dr. Stevenson appears, wearing a white lab coat with a shirt and tie underneath and tan dress slacks. His salt and pepper hair gives away his early fifties age, as do the slim bifocals that rest on the end of his nose. “Hello again, Mr. Axtell,” says the doctor with a smile on his face. He closes the door behind him and walks over to a nearby chair, Jim’s medical records clamped in his hand. “Sorry to keep you waiting so long, but it’s rather busy here today.” Axtell smiles halfheartedly out of the side of his mouth. “All’s forgiven if you give me a clean bill of health, doc.” Dr. Stevenson chuckles as he places Jim’s records on the counter next to him. “If only things were that easy.” The doctor flips open the file and flips past the first few pages, coming to rest on an x-ray. Jim’s shoulders sulk as Dr. Stevenson turns and peers over his glasses, the look in his eyes saying more that words ever could. “This x-ray doesn’t look good, Jim.” Dr. Stevenson pulls the glasses off his face and folds them up, placing them carefully in his coat’s breast pocket. “H-how bad is not good, doc?” questions Jim hesitantly. “You certainly have a poor history with injuries to your knees. You’ve had both of them reconstructed already and you’re only twenty eight years old.” The tone of the doctor’s voice is somewhat sympathetic. “You’re going to be a mess when you get older.” “I feel like a mess now,” jokes Jim as he tries to lighten the mood. “I hate to see what I’m gonna be like when I get older.” The doctor smiles, but only briefly. “In all seriousness, Jim, your current knee injury is close to career threatening.” The last statement nearly takes the breath from Jim’s lungs as it hits him in the chest like a baseball bat. His stomach sinks to what feels like his toes as he absorbs the doctor’s words. “But,” Dr. Stevenson continues. “You got really lucky this time.” Jim’s stomach returns to its rightful place in his belly and he is able to take a breath. “I did?” “Indeed you did. You have severe sprains of all your knee ligaments like the doctors at the medical center diagnosed.” The doctor pauses for effect. “However, that’s all you have. There is no evidence of fraying or tearing in any of your ligaments or tendons, so your knee will heal nicely with time.” “So what are we looking at here? Three, four days….maybe a week tops?” The relief in Jim’s voice is abundantly apparent. The doctor is not so optimistic. “I wish I could be that hopeful, but you’ll need to rest at least four weeks for a full recovery.” “Four weeks?” Jim asks, dismayed. “I have a match this weekend that I have to wrestle in. There are so many people that are counting on me for this. Are you sure about this prognosis, doc? I mean, four weeks?” The doctor sighs heavily, shaking his head slowly from side to side. “That is a best case scenario, Jim. Most people take six to eight weeks to recuperate from an injury like this. Although your ligaments are not torn, they are severely stretched out. Your knee is loose and full of fluid. Any missed steps in the ring and you’ll blow that knee completely out. I cannot clear you medically to wrestle with a clean conscious.” Jim is taken aback as he sits there, unable to muster any words. The doctor carefully closes Jim’s medical file and swivels in his chair, fully facing his patient. His eyes show sympathy for Axtell as he speaks. “At least you get to walk away from this relatively unscathed, Jim. Many others aren’t so lucky.” The words seem to fall upon deaf ears as Jim sulks. He stares down at his knee, then up at the doctor. “I gotta wrestle this week, doc.” “I’ve already faxed the medical report over to the PWC offices, Jim. I’m sorry.” Dr. Stevenson stands and offers his hand to Jim. “I’ll schedule you for a follow up in a few weeks to check your progress. In the meantime I’ll have a nurse come in and drain the fluid from that knee.” Jim takes the doctors hand, shaking it respectfully. “Thanks for the honesty, doc.” Dr. Stevenson nods and exits the room, leaving a devastated Jim Axtell in the room by himself again. He looks down at his swollen knee and curses aloud in frustration. “SHIT!”
