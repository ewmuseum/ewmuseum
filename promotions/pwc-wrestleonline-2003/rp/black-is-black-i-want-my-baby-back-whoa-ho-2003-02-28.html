Black is black I want my baby back whoa ho

28 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/28/03 Even with the sun beaming down on the New Jersey Park the cold wind cancels the warmth right out. It’s a bitter cold probably a gift from the Canadian air stream that swoops down through the northeast. You can see the wind as it rustles though the trees that litter the park in an unorganized manner. Sitting on a wooden bench that’s been carved up with initials worse then a Thanksgiving Day turkey is Hoyt Williams. He sits on the bench dressed in khaki pants, a white dress shirt, with a black tie, and a navy style winter coat that keeps him warm. The park is a perfect place to people watch and too try and save. The element of the park, range from the homeless who live there to the rich joggers passing through. The homeless are nothing more then a wall fixture to the joggers who pass by with out ever looking into a face. While the homeless watch the joggers as a form of entertainment to take their warn out minds off of their decaying lives. It doesn’t take a man with a good Christian raising or an eight-grade education to observe that the poverty line is draw by the color of your skin. The homeless souls of the park are mainly old black men lost in addiction that was given to them by their government while they were fighting for a war that wasn’t right. With pride and desire all but gone the only thing they are left with is an old army jacket stained with the vomit and whiskey of lives gone wrong. Hoyt sits and watches a younger man panhandle to a lady walking her dog. Is he a victim of his own race born into poverty with no chance of ever getting his head above the water that fills the ocean of wealth? Was he born to a mother with an addiction to crack that was passed through the umbilical cord to him taking away any of his chance at a normal life?? Or was he a good kid who joined a gang falling victim to the devils street love. “Hey Man” Shouts Hoyt to the young black man bugging the frightened lady. The misguided youth immediately drops his plea to the cold-hearted bitch and her dog. You look down on a prostitute for selling sex; a bum for losing faith and hope; an addict for addiction; yet nobody faults the wealthy for lack of compassion. The young man dressed in yesterday’s misfortunes hobbles over to Hoyt sitting on the bench. “Say man can you help a brother out?” Asks the homeless man in an uneducated urban twang. The first thing Hoyt notices is the stench emitting from the body of wayward stranger. How can you help a man if you can’t get near him with out gagging? Hoyt stops to wonder if Jesus ever had to deal with any malodorous ones. Hoyt holds his breath and looks the man in his eyes. “You looking for help man?” Responds Hoyt staring into the dark man’s brown eyes. The man adjusts his filthy Raiders jackets as he smiles at Hoyt. Even though the filth, the smell, and the lost dreams the man’s smile is still warm. It radiates like the heat from an oven cooking soft cookies on a cold night. He places his hand out with his palm up asking for anything to help. Jesus used to make a similar pose with both hands out and palms up to offer support. One arm makes all the difference just as three nails or two bombs have through the coarse of history. “Yea dude I need some food yo.” Responds the hungry stranger. Does one choose not to learn the English language or does his surroundings prevent him from doing so Hoyt ponders. Although it’s not his job to teach those below him to talk right just to save them. Hoyt opts not to make any corrections in the mans destructive language. “I can’t offer you money, food, or booze, but I can give you the gift of God.” Hoyt responds in a quite tone. The mans face goes from radiant to rage like somebody slipped a plate of shit under his nose. He shakes his head at Hoyt in rage but doesn’t dare strike the man twice his size. He just waves his hands in a brush off motion as he continues down the path looking for a handout. “You see these people don’t want to be helped. They like living like the scum of the earth and choose to do so. I can only offer my help but I can’t force it. This Saturday I’m going to show my less fortunate colored opponent what living right is all about. I’m going to bring the body and he’ll supply the blood and I’ll leave the Harlem audience asking themselves what would Hoyt do.” Mutters Hoyt to himself as he gets up to go jog some more. =w=
