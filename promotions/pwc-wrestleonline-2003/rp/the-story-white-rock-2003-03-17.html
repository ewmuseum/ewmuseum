The Story: White Rock

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 17, 2003 Sunday, February 17, 2002: White Rock, New Mexico White Rock, New Mexico. Population: 6192. That was population as listed in the 2000 census, at least. If she would have had to guess, Julianne Seger would have ventured an estimate far lower. Squinting her eyes to block out the glare from the sun, she peers down Grand Canyon Drive, the town’s main road. For being one of the focal points of the community, it was rather barren for three o’clock in the afternoon. Then again, she thought, maybe her perspective was merely thrown off from years of city living. Rural New Mexico was worlds away from downtown Chicago, after all. Yet no amount of rationalization could seem to prevent the eerie feeling from slinking up and down her spine. Cars were parked on the sides of the street with no signs of motorists. Storefronts utterly devoid of consumer activity. This type of social idleness wasn’t normal. It couldn’t be. Could it? Christ, it was like something out of a fucking horror movie... It had been a hectic day and a half since she disembarked from her flight at the Santa Fe Municipal Airport. Eager to get cracking on her story, she had made a beeline to the nearest library and set herself to pouring through the newsreels of the past five years of local newspapers, looking for any mention of Solomon’s radical sect she could find. Her search turned up dismal, with only a few minor mentions, and the large portion of them being related to the trial proceedings that took place four years ago. After eight hours of work, she came out with faint hopes and only a few promising directions to go in. The scrap of paper she had in her pocket seemed to be her only relevant lead, short of stalking out to the compound itself. This particular lead had taken her from the bustling city of Santa Fe out to what could by all means be called the wasteland that was White Rock. It was amazing what a change could be seen as she drove the sixty miles west from Santa Fe in her rental car. The further out she got from the city, the less and less activity she witnessed in the towns she passed. Obviously their small size played some part in their idleness, but she had an inkling suspicion in the back of her mind that this trend in inactivity had something to do with Solomon’s cult. White Rock was about thirty miles south of the compound where Solomon had set up shop and was the nearest decently sized town in the vicinity of the place. For reasons known only to him, the so called prophet had chosen one of the most desolate areas of New Mexico to construct his base of operations. Yet somehow she felt that its isolation was anything but a detriment to the effect it had on the surrounding countryside. In fact, she felt as if she was nearing the apex of some great force that had sent a terrible ripple effect tearing through the countryside, ripping people up out of their lives and taking them away. As silly as that sounded in her head, she felt that she could hold onto that as the truth. There was something fishy going on…she was sure of it… Shaking the willies off, Julie reaches into her pocket, fishing around before pulling out a crumpled piece of paper. Bringing it out into the light, she runs her hand over it in an effort to smooth out the wrinkles. She squints even harder in an effort to make out her scribbled handwriting. After nearly thirty seconds of twisting and turning the paper, her face running a gamut of expressions as she stares intently at it, she finally nods and shoves the scrap back into her jeans. “2050 Grand Canyon Drive...” she mutters to herself. “It should be around here somewhere.” This was the address of Amanda Rogers, mother of Alyssa, one of the girls involved in the court case, which she found by cross listing the names in White Rock directory. The families of the other two girls had moved away in recent years, most likely unable to handle living in an area filled with so many memories of their lost daughters. This left Amanda as the only viable source from the court case…at least the only one with any real human relevancy. Really, what reader would want to hear testimony from a judge or attorney, when they could get real emotion straight from its source? Exploitation of human emotion is what professional journalism was all about. If nothing else, they had drilled that into Julie’s head in her years of study. After all, facts didn’t move the presses…no, it was the raw bits of unadulterated passion, ripped from the air, transplanted and transferred so easily onto paper. Those are what sold the magazines and fueled the media frenzies of years past, and things weren’t likely to change any time in the near future, and most likely not in the distant one either. Julie was well aware that such uninhibited exploitation of people was disgusting and immoral…she wasn’t that naïve. She knew first hand what it was like to be on the opposite end of the sword. But some things had to be done for the sake of the story. Especially this story, which could very well turn out to be the defining point of her career. She would do anything to make it big. She had suffered so much, spent too many a night crying herself to sleep. This wasn’t an opportunity to be squandered. She couldn’t go back a failure. She would write the best damn interest story the industry had ever seen. And when she won her Pulitzer, she’d use it to bitch-smack Zanders right across his smug face. That thought puts a smile on her face. Thoughts of the eeriness of the vacant town vanishing from her head, she continues searching for the address on her slip of paper, imagining just how sweet her vengeance will be…
