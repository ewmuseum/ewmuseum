PWC TV Commercial 9

10 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Originally aired on WGTW TV-48 in Philadelphia, PA at 4:23pm EST Friday, January 10th. Sublime In the light and warmth of an open-air campfire, Dave Dream Strider caressed a baby’s smooth head with long, patient fingers. It was the very fist time he had set eyes upon his grandson and already he sensed a tempest brewing. The possibility that someone might look upon something so overtly vulnerable with anything other than compassion and care confounded Dream Strider. And yet, it pained him greatly knowing that this child’s own flesh and blood did exactly that. “What manner of person would not love his own flesh and blood unconditionally?” He lamented. Heady vapors poured from a smoking pipe dangling between his rickety teeth. Surveying the tiny body through the pall, Dream Strider noted that he possessed the sallow skin of a ‘white-man’ and eyes resembling splinters of highly polished jade - physical traits inconsistent with his own Apache heritage - but there was no less love found in the gaze of his own coal-blacks because of those differences. He accepted this child wholeheartedly and unquestionably, half-breed or not. Imparting advice was an important part of the naming ritual, and while he had been passing on his advice for as long as he could remember, this particular occasion worried him moreso than any other. Taking a miniature hand in his own, Dream Strider’s murmur was barely audible over the low pop of cracking wood. “Warriors covet strength above all other attributes. You will need to stand tall and firm, Oneida Elohi, as the brave grizzly bear does. Study him carefully and completely. He sets the example. He is the ultimate warrior.” The infant gripped one of his fingers. It was an unconscious reflex action that Dream Strider hoped for and received. He sensed, more than physically felt, inherent strength somewhere deep within his grandson, and he was pleased. “Equally important is stealth and wit,” Dream Strider continued. “By outmaneuvering and outsmarting his rivals the clever fox remains one step ahead at all times. You must strive to be the same, and you will need possession of all of your wits to stand any chance.” Dream Strider drew from his pipe and blew a thin flume of smoke over his grandson in blessing. It was a ceremonial act borne out of local tradition. “Exploit every advantage. Grab every opportunity. Don’t ever expect quarter yourself, and be relentless in the hunt…” he said, before pausing mid-sentence to unhitch something from around his neck. Brought down into the light proper one could then see that it was a choker of simple design with the likeness of a wolf’s head at its center: an antique in fact, although only Dream Strider knew its true age, and significance. Having momentarily lost his train of though, the old man had to backtrack, “Yes, relentless in the hunt… like your brethren, the persistent and merciless wolf,” and added as an afterthought, “May he protect and guide you, always.” Dream Strider kissed the bauble and secreted it between folds in the baby’s blanket. The choker was far too large for his grandson to wear at this stage. He made a mental note to inform Eve of the gift. She would keep it hidden until her son was of age. “Understand that your survival is dependent on acquiring all these attributes,” Dream Strider stated with a greater conviction than his low whispering previously afforded. Then he bowed his head in finality and began to chant an old age tune to end the naming ceremony. While this was a formal occasion his affection for the child was understandable but clearly he held some concerns, and those concerns could be found in the most unlikely source: Oneida Elohi’s own father, Jurgen. According to his daughter, Jurgen was an unrelenting and cruel taskmaster who would demand nothing short of perfection from his son. To say that Eve feared the man was an understatement. What she called ‘the madness’ surfaced in Jurgen all the time, and that was certainly something to fear. It was with this in mind that Dream Strider had spoken so apprehensively. “Already you have much to live up to. The weight of expectation lies heavily upon your shoulders, and I sense that it will be a far greater responsibility to bear than any physical burden,” Dream Strider offered as parting advice. Raising his arms high, he offered his grandson to the night as an appeasement to fate, and broke into a long, sonorous wail. A female voice chimed in, and Eve stepped forward to join her father in challenge. Barely past two months, and his ordeal had already begun. *** “Feeble! That was a pathetic attempt!” A baritone voice barked out. Jurgen Helmstaedt rapped the stock end of his baton on the ground, signaling for Sublime to start over. Although he had been stateside for over twenty years, he still spoke in that thick, halting accent endemic to all German-born people. Jurgen was an imposing man despite having the better part of fifty years behind him. His fierce hawk-like nose, severe crew cut, and the hateful glint of his beady eyes ensured that other regulars to The Factory routinely avoided him. “Again, dammit! You will not leave until I am satisfied,” he barked again, impatient with the young man inside the ring. Sublime struggled to lift his spent frame off the canvas, but it was no use. His body - supple, youthful and physically strong as it was - couldn’t compete with his father’s incessant demands. Jurgen was merciless, a troll, and Sublime had nothing left to give. His failing arms collapsed under him and he slumped to the floor in defeat, resigning himself completely to what he knew would come next. How did he know? It happened every day. Some days worse than others, but it always happened. “Get the fuck up! I have not finished with you yet!” Came the expected reaction. Jurgen pulled himself onto the apron and threw his baton at Sublime’s prone body. Striking him squarely in the ribs, Sublime winced in pain, but it was nothing compared to the fatigue he felt right now. In an instant his father was standing over him. Sublime looked up and saw an all too familiar sight. Something he had seen many, many times before. The madness. He couldn’t explain it any better, all Sublime knew is that it made his father lose control. “Do what you like.” Sublime mouthed under his breath. He refused to move. “I quit. I fucking quit.” Perhaps Sublime did not intend to be heard. Deep down he knew he didn’t mean it. Wrestling was his life. It was the only thing he truly loved, apart from his mother. Jurgen merely grinned triumphantly. ‘Oh no, boy. A Helmstaedt never quits,” he replied. “Never.” “In fact, you have just started, because tomorrow… we go to New Jersey… and you finally get to earn your keep” That caused more pain than any physical torture his father could m?te out to him. It meant he would be separated from his mother and her family, Sublime’s only comfort, indefinitely. Jurgen kept on grinning, unrepentant. “Welcome to the world of wrestling, rookie.”
