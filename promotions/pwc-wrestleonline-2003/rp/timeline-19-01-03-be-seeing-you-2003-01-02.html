Timeline - 19/01/03 : Be Seeing You

2 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Teaser Commercial Our shot fades in on a clear, crisp blue sky that isn’t blemished by a single remnant of a cloud. The sound of silence is all that inhabits the scene until a tinny trace of music is heard, only the very highest end, with the tune just recognisable as L7’s anti-capitalist anthem “Pretend We’re Dead”. Using the crackly music as a beacon, the camera descends from the wash of blue to show it intruded upon by a snow-topped mountain scene where figures like ants in the distance descend the slopes and the senses are refreshed with the sounds of ski edges through snow and the random chatter of humanity. ‘When we pretend that we’re dead...’ continues the faint backing tune as the middle and lower layers slowly fade in to make the song complete, and turning to the right we see a ski lodge, laying on one of the picnic tables the source of the music, singing along to her mini-disc player. A young woman, an aura of early-20’s about her is flat on the table, fingers drumming on her thigh while tapping her right foot into thin air along to the stuttering bass of the music. ‘They can't hear a word we've said...’ she sings softly under her breath from her bright pink lips, the words sending a fine mist into the freezing air from warm lungs. With a green, black and white ski-hat on, as well as a pair of orange tinted sunglasses we see very little of her face, only the ends of her shiny brown hair tufting from under the woollen hat and 2 eyebrow piercings glistening in the low sun. Sensing someone approach, her head turns to face the camera but her body remains spread out on the wooden table. ‘Oh, you.’ her well pronounced voice speaks with an apathetic tone, almost as if moving her lips was too much of an exertion. ‘I suppose you want me to find some faux-natural way on introducing myself, right?’. With her orange gloved hands, she plucks her earphones from her ears by sharply tugging at the wires, then slides her silver frames up to show a pair of startling green eyes. ‘Karina Wolfenden, here for my pleasure and not yours.’ she speaks into the camera before a teasing wink crosses her left eye. ‘Talk about a change of scene, huh?’ Karina’s now less apathetic voice continues as she stares straight up at the vast expanse of cold blue above her. ‘Goodbye snow, goodbye blue skies. Hello Philly, hello PWC.’ is added in quite poetic fashion as the young woman adjusts her hat, revealing a blonde flash at the front of her hair. ‘Not what “Daddy” would have wanted for his only daughter.’ is spoken in a mixture of dismissive humour and disgust, the disgust being especially saved for “Daddy”. Still, her eyes stay away from the camera, as if she doesn’t want it to intrude on her rest. ‘The next time you see me, let’s hope I’m not flat on my back again.’ Karina quips with jest before putting her earphones back in and sliding her orange sunglasses down onto the bridge of her nose once more. With a gloved finger fumbling the volume dial, the tinny music gets louder once more as what little attention Karina was paying to the camera evaporates as she once again retreats into herself, prompting the camera to roll off back to the blue sky and fade out to the sound of crackly guitars and skis cutting through soft snow.
