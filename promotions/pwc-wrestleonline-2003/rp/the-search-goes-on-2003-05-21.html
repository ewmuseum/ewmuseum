The Search Goes On

21 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday Charcoal clouds rolled ominously across the sunless midday sky, grumbling from time to time in needless reminder to those below that this respite from the downpour would not last long. A few establishments had the foresight to perch canopies above their entrances and dining patios, but for the most part, every inch of the quiet street bore the darkened slickness of recent rain. Tiny streams bubbled fitfully along either curb, providing the calming sounds of running and splashing water as they made makeshift waterfalls of the sewer grates. Sighing, Angelo sloshed his polished Italian loafers onto the pavement, rose smoothly from his black Cadillac Deville, and slammed the door hard behind him as he straightened. The glare he gave the near-empty town around him seemed fixed to scorch the land dry. Normally he liked the comforting aroma that followed and preceded a storm, but at the moment he didn’t feel much like being calmed, or appreciating any of the little things. The air of nonchalance went no deeper than the skin, and even that was a stretch. Things were not going his way. Even when I come to Ari-fucking-zona there’s a cloud over my head. What the hell happened to my luck? It was harmless rhetoric, some he certainly didn’t expect a response to, so when it came, a chill not from the damp air made him wince and shiver. It wouldn’t be so bad if it wasn’t in his own mind, mocking him. Maybe it’s karma, a voice strangely like his own chortled. In his head. Without any prompting or control from him. He waited for more, rigid as if preparing for a physical blow. That small voice had plagued him constantly of late, wriggling its way into his thoughts and spurring dissension within his brain. He didn’t know where it came from, or what it wanted, but he could never get rid of it for long. He had tried... Mercy, how he had tried. A car whizzed by, lifting water from the street like a wave in its wake, and muddy liquid splattered across Deville’s suddenly not-so-immaculate white dress slacks. Snapping out of his daze, he struggled to unclench fists he couldn’t remember making and ran a trembling hand over his slicked blonde hair, then rubbed it over his face as a great peal of thunder cackled at him from the sky. The thought occurred to him, not for the first time, that he was probably going mad. You don’t have time for that right now! Focus, you fuckin’ half-witted shitfucker! He grinned in spite of the words; it was his voice. He would deal with the other later. He had only been to Arizona twice before, and both times strictly for business. Cowboys, archaic religious views, and inbreeding notwithstanding, the desert could hide things you didn’t want people to know about. The grin grew; southerners didn’t like him referring to their lands as a desert, but as far as he was concerned the only endearing characteristic about it was the unsearchable expanses of sand. He always made a point of dropping by, and if he had his way, this trip would prove no different. He just had to find a... companion... to go with him. Glancing down the road the way he had come, out of the corner of his eye, he made note of the two clean-shaven men watching him from a white Cutlass Supreme. Maybe I’ll have more than one body to haul this time. Twitching his eyes in the other direction, he only hoped they weren’t stupid enough to move yet. The grin washed from his face as he sighed again, and he straightened his suit perfunctorily before marching across the street to the second target in his search. Magnum Wolf’s home had proved fruitless, not to mention ramshackle and seemingly unlived-in for a year, so he figured this was his next best bet for finding the fool. Striding up to a monolithic building that somehow managed to be dwarfed by those around it, Angelo whipped open the glass door and smiled as bells tinkled overhead to announce his arrival. He didn’t fancy having a flowery conversation with anyone employed at Big Burt’s Foot Apparel, and perhaps if one of the monkeys came to meet him instead of him having to track one down, he could be out of there that much sooner. The overwhelming smell of cotton offering the only greeting quickly disabused him of that notion. After circling the large store twice, Deville waited at the counter, fuming at the racks and bins laced with socks in every fabric and style imaginable – the whole goddamned store was full of them, and only them. What kind of a place sells only– He cut himself off, shaking his head with resigned disgust. Tucson, Arizona, the place that had excreted Magnum Wolf, must be capable of spawning all manner of idiocy. Knowing to expect it didn’t make the wait any less sufferable, though. Thin eyebrows suddenly climbed his forehead as he spotted a small bell on the counter; grinning, he placed his index finger on the trigger and pushed it down, emitting a sharp ting, then again, and again, without relent. Fifty or so rings later, he heard a toilet flush, and a moment later a door twenty paces off to his left opened out of the wall, producing a gangly man hastily tucking his plain white-collared shirt into too-tight blue denim jeans. He waved at Angelo – who continued ringing the bell with still-mounting impatience – and twitched his thick handlebar moustache irritably, then ever-so-slowly began swaggering over to the counter. The man even had the audacity to stop and straighten a pair of socks on the way. It was all Angelo could do not to storm over and strangle him. When he finally arrived, his hand closed over Angelo’s on the bell, bringing an end to the chiming. “How may I help you, sir?” The smile on his face went nowhere near his eyes, and the derision on “sir” could’ve balanced scales with a mountain. “First,” Angelo bit off dryly, icy blue eyes cutting into widening brown, “you can wipe that insolent smile off your fuckin’ face.” The shopkeeper’s hand recoiled as if burned, and he eyed Angelo askance, mouth hanging open, before he could catch and stop himself. “Then you can stop gaping at me like a fuckin’ fish out of water and apologise for wasting my time. You think I have all day to stand around waiting for a braindead sock peddler? Some of us actually have a life to attend to, and wouldn’t waste a minute more than necessary in a rundown shithole like this.” As the tirade wound down, the man’s face had gone from shock to outrage to anger, where it stood now, flushing his face with colour. Deville felt a momentary stab of satisfaction; the man was bristling with rage. Clearing his throat audibly, the man flourished a mocking bow. “Please forgive me, godfather.” The man straightened, smirking now. “If I had any idea my meagre store had been graced with the patronage of one as high and mighty as yourself...” His face was suddenly as dark as the clouds outside. “...I’d have run out the back, locked the doors, and burned it down around your fat head! Get the hell out of my store! Before I throw you out!” Waving his arms as if to make himself seem larger than the 260-pound Angelo, he stormed around the counter with a glower fit to strip paint. Deville’s face remained smooth. As smooth as it had been while he was berating the man, at least. A shriek of girlish pain wracked the thin clerk as Deville snatched one of his arms from the air and twisted it sharply around, driving the man to his knees and spinning him completely. It continued even after he fell, as Angelo didn’t let up on the pressure, but faded to silence when the Deville’s other hand found the point where clavicle met shoulder and dug in hard. It wasn’t as if the man wasn’t trying to scream bloody murder, he was simply in too much pain to scrape out even the smallest sound. “What was that?” Deville’s voice was a glacier. “You were saying something about touching my person?” He allowed himself a small smile; talking like that while you were manhandling someone always added insult to injury. The man’s response was a frail attempt at prying the hand from the its nest deep within the pressure point. Angelo tightened the grip, and he took the hint, holding his hand as far away from his own body as possible, in surrender. Deville maintained the hold. “My head seems to have undergone hideous changes recently, thanks to posers I have no control over, but surely you didn’t mean to imply it was fat... Or that you’d try to kill me.” He paused as sobs began wracking the clerk, now trembling in his grip but trying with all of his will to stay still; any movement on his part would double the pain. “Did you?” The head shook so emphatically that Angelo actually barked a laugh. “Oh really? So you’re willing to apologise and stop acting like a fucking clown, then?” The nodding was a moment longer in coming, but it was twice as vigorous when it did. Angelo released him and he flopped to the ground, helped in no small part by Deville’s foot on the back of his neck. “Tell me what you know of Magnum Wolf.” Deville knew a great deal about the man himself, with his interest originally piqued almost a year ago in FSW, but it wasn’t defining characteristics he was probing for. He wanted to know how often Wolf came here, and when the shop-keep expected him to return. He would kill for the information. And I’ll kill once I have it, too. Murdering Magnum Wolf seemed to be his only option now, since the fool had not only ratted him out, but actually taunted him with it and lorded it over his head, threatening to do so again! A dead man tells no tales, he laughed without mirth. A dead man plays no games. Deville would die before indulging Wolf in his games. “W-who?” The question came out between sobs, a wretched squeak bearing no resemblance whatsoever to the arrogant tone the man had originally adopted. He tried to turn his head around to give Angelo an imploring look, but a bit more pressure on his neck quickly made him think better of it. “Aaron Roberts,” Angelo grated, but already hope was beginning to fade. Knowing Wolf for a pompous ass, Deville knew that if the man would recognise his real name, he would also recognise his alias; Mags couldn’t go three sentences without boasting about his “dazzling” career in professional wrestling. He had to try, though. “I d-don’t know anyone by that name!” Thankfully, he doesn’t try to turn around this time, contenting himself with humping the floor. “You just need to think a little harder... I know he comes here often. He must.” The man tried to shake his head, protesting, but Deville twisted his foot and the man’s spine cracked, a sickening sound like bubblewrap being popped. “The man has an extreme sock fetish, and with how hard he grinds the damned things, he must go through a dozen pairs a week. You seem to have the market cornered around here, so he must come to you... Often.” “Impossible! My socks are top of the line! Guaranteed for a year!” Indignance crested his voice despite his vulnerable position, instinctively defending his product and livelihood. The man had spunk, at least, Angelo would give him that. “Walk, run, or crawl, these socks’ll get you your money’s worth! “He doesn’t wear them on his feet!” The shout is exclamated by a vicious stomp to the back of the head. Blood spurted from the man’s nose and mouth, oozing into a widening puddle on the floor. “He fucks them! Constantly! Stuffs them down his pants and pretends its his package!” Angelo blinked suddenly, realising he was shouting, and stopped the rapid stomping on the man’s head. Wolf definitely had him off-balance, but if he thought Angelo was going to take it lying down he had a whole world of surprised hurt orbiting his way. A few fragments of broken teeth swam through the ocean of blood now. Needless to say, the clerk could only muster pained gurgling. Goddamnit! Angelo’s mind raged, at the same time that wretched alien voice piped up with What the hell do you think you’re doing?! Deville stared at the gun in his hand, unable to remember pulling it from his jacket, and a shudder wracked his body. Slowly he lowered it from aiming at the broken man’s head, and the world around him tilted. What the fuck is happening to me? Get out of my head! That last was for the voice, and it mocked him with its silence; it wasn’t gone, just quiet. “Tell anyone I was here...” His voice was stone, cold and compassionless. He was surprised it did not shake. “And I’ll kill you.” Replacing the gun, he turned and bolted from the store. If he was anyone else, it would be described as fleeing. No amount of running would shed him of that reproachful voice, but he ran anyway.
