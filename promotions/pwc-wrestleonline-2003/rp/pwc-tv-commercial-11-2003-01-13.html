PWC TV Commercial 11

13 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Originally aired on WGTW TV-48 in Philadelphia, PA at 10:55am EST Monday, January 13th. This was it. He didn’t think he would have ever gotten this far, but he had. His head felt like it was spinning incredibly fast and butterflies fluttered in his stomach. He remembered the day he first got into this business, the day he reluctantly stepped into this profession. A tiny advertisement in a newspaper and a friend that was eager to get him a job… got him to where he was now headed. It wasn’t anything real big, just a small local organization. He slowly paced down the small Philadelphia Street, his arms swaying slightly with each step. He couldn’t help but feel the need to burst out in one powerful scream. Not only had he gotten good at what he was doing he had also had begun to enjoy it. He was actually looking forward to getting this started, something he’d never thought he’d be doing. His eyes moved up toward the night sky; incredibly dark purple clouds hid everything that could be seen on any clear night. “Rain”. He hated rain; he hated rain with a passion. It always ruined things and hardly ever did any good. Always lowered people’s spirits too. It wasn’t going to bother him today though. He wasn’t going to let it bring him down. He continued to press forward as the honk of a car horn echoed in the distance. He began to sort through the mess of thoughts that were locked away in his head, trying to make sense of things. He hoped he could make this deal work. He hoped he could make a name for himself with this. But he knew that it would be almost impossible to do so. There were so many other people that were twice as good as he was, maybe even three times as good. He hardly had a chance, but it wouldn’t hurt to try. Or would it? There were so many other people who actually lived to do this kind of stuff. They ate, drank and slept wrestling. He didn’t even want to try out at first. It started out as being a means of a paycheck to go along with the delivery job he already had. He hardly deserved this chance. He began to think of all the people who were more deserving of the opportunity he had received. Hundreds of people of that would kill to do this and try to everyday. But he had just stumbled into it; he knew nothing of it in the beginning. But that’s why there was an academy. Being enrolled in the academy not only taught him the physical aspects of the sport, it also taught him the mental aspects, which are just as important as anything else was. He jammed his hands deep into his pockets as he rounded a corner that lead down a street branching off from the main drive. His eyes began to burn the slightest bit; he hadn’t slept in two days. He couldn’t decide if he was overly excited or just incredibly nervous. But maybe it was both though. He was beginning to wonder if he had insomnia. The past two nights he had just laid there on the couch with the remote in hand, watching boring infomercials for slow-roast cookers and self–proclaimed super juicers. Not the kind of thing your average person would do, but he couldn’t help it. There are some pretty weird shows on late night television, nonetheless. Every so often a random car would move past him as he paced down the sidewalk, seeming to ignore everything that was going on around him. Which wasn’t much. He suddenly remembers that he was asked to prepare a statement, stating what he was planning to accomplish in this organization. His mind began to dwell on that as he gradually sorted through his thoughts. It was a good question, didn’t even know if he knew the answer to it. What did he want to accomplish? He guessed he just wanted to become known and not just disappear as failure, pr to possibly work his way up to being a respectable force. Maybe even winning sometimes but he figured that might be asking too much. He didn’t really know. Even those goals were going to a big hard to accomplish; it was only a local organization that was just starting out. It defiantly takes a bit for things like that to get rolling. Hardly ever do they get any bigger than they already were, so he was told. He suddenly stopped walking, looking down and to his right. Lying propped up against the side of the building was an extremely dirty looking, middle aged man. A brown, knit, cap was pulled down over his ears and the outermost or the three winter coats he wore had large holes and dark stains on its light blue fabric. His eyes were closed as he slept peacefully, his arms folded across his chest. His eyes moved down toward a small paper cup that sat upright between the homeless man’s feet, as this type of thing wasn’t an unusual site in Philadelphia. He didn’t know why but for some reason he felt sympathy for the man. He had always ignored them but this time he felt the need to donate to the man. He slowly removed his left hand from his pocket, keeping it clenched into a fist. After he extended it outward, he bent down and dropped a small handful of change into the cup. The man didn’t even stir; he must have been in a deep sleep. His mind began to think again as he began to walk along again. Perhaps he was too nice; he’d always been bit of a pacifist. Now that he thought of it that wouldn’t go over well at all in this place. It may have gotten him through the academy but this wasn’t going to be like the academy. Everyone already knew everything, most knew more than himself. This was going to be his big test; it would either make or break him.
