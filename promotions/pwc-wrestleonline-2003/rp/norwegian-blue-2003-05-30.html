Norwegian Blue

30 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th May 2003 A quiet family pet shop. Very small in size, on the corner of the street. A distinct smell of animals fills the cramped pet shop. You can taste the bland bits of bird food. The cats are meowing, the puppies are piping, the birds are chirping. Behind the counter stands a medium sized man in a long brown scruffy trench coat, a scruffy red T-shirt and a pair of scruffy jeans. He’s got a scruffy mullet sitting on his head and a scruffy beard lining his chin. He’s looking at some sort of chart on the grubby counter surface when the bell that rings when the door is open. In walks a very angry looking Eric Sagar, in his hand he holds a white wire cage with the door open. In it lies a motionless parrot. It’s a lovely colour; aqua blue feathers and a sky blue line down it’s back. Yellow breast and curly beak. The short stumpy preacher is wearing his usual suit. 'I wish to register a compliant.' Eric Sagar grumbles with his shoulders hunched up to his ears. The man behind the counter doesn’t respond. 'Excuse me, miss.' 'What do you mean calling me miss?' The man is quick to his own defence. Eric Sagar squints his eyes at this man in obscurity. 'Umm… Sorry, I’ve got a cold. Anyway, I’d like to register a complaint.' 'I’m sorry, we’re closing.' The man quickly stops 'The Fat Preacher.' 'Never mind that, my boy. I wish to complain about… this!' Sagar bellows holding up the cage. 'A Parrot I purchased from this very establishment half an hour ago.' 'Ah yes, the Norwegian Blue, marvellous animal. What’s wrong with it?' He asks, trying to play down the very unhealthy looking bird. 'I’ll tell you what’s wrong with it, my boy. It’s dead!' Eric Sagar grumbles in a harsh manner. 'No, no, no.' The man stammers, trying to think of something to say. 'It’s resting… look.' 'Look, my boy, I know a dead parrot when I see one.' Sagar glares at the motionless bird. 'And I’m looking at one right now.' 'Nawwww, that’s not dead. It’s resting.' The man insists. 'RESTING!?' Eric Sagar barks in disbelief the man is defending this rather stiff, stationary and pungent bird is ‘resting.’ 'Yeah, resting. Remarkable bird the Norwegian Blue. Beautiful plumage.' The man hypes up the dead bird. 'Plumage does not enter into it, my boy.' Eric Sagar glares at the man like he did the parrot. 'This parrot is stone dead.' 'No.' The man tries to convince 'The Fat Preacher.' 'It’s resting, honest.' Eric Sagar cannot believe this boy is trying to convince him that this very, very, very dead parrot is alive. 'All right then.' Eric Sagar bellows. 'If it is resting. I will wake it up.' The man blinks as Eric Sagar brings the entrance to the cage (where the birds head is hanging out) to his face. 'HELLO POLLY!' Eric Sagar roars at the parrot. He stops dodges his head back and takes another look, it’s not moving. He brings his head closer again. 'I’VE GOT A NICE FRESH BOWL OF FOOD WAITING FOR YOU IF YOU WAKE UP MR. PARROT!' In an act of desperation, with cat like quickness the man nudges the cage. 'Look! There! it moved!' The man insists with an enthusiastic batch of excitement in his tone. 'No it didn’t. You pushed it!' Eric Sagar yelps. What sort of moron is he dealing with? 'I didn’t!' 'YES YOU DID!' Fed up of this man’s antics, Eric Sagar removes the parrot from his cage and drops the cage on the floor. 'HEEEEEELLLLLLLLOOOOOOO POLLY!!!' he yells at it, holding it up to his mouth like it was a microphone. 'WAKEY! WAKEY!' he bellows, as he bangs the parrot corpse against the surface of the counter, the intense stiffness of the parrot makes a banging sound. 'RISE AND SHINE! IT’S YOUR 9 O’CLOCK ALARM CALL!' he booms again, banging it’s inflexible body against the counter. It has not effected the dead parrots stance at all. The man blinks, he can’t believe what a crazy individual he is dealing with. Eric Sagar gets the parrot, hurls it up in the air and it lands on the stiff wooden floor with a clank. Eric Sagar looks down at this bird which remains in the exact same position and expression it was in when he bought it. 'Now that is what I call a dead parrot.' Eric Sagar asserts. 'No. It’s just stunned.' The man claims. 'STUNNED!?' The Preacher clamours. 'Yeah, you stunned him just as he was waking up.' The man demands. 'STUNNED!?' 'Norwegian Blues stun easily!' The man preaches to the preacher. 'I’ve had about enough of this.' Eric Sagar sighs. 'That parrot is definitely deceased. And when I bought it you told me it’s lack of movement was because it was tired and utterly shattered after a long squawk.' Eric Sagar roars. 'Well… he’s… pining for the fjord.' The man makes another wild claim. Eric Sagar squints his eyes in amazement, he leans forward and takes a long good luck at this man. Does he show any signs of mental retardation he asks himself. Eric Sagar looks around, he really is not believing this. 'PINING… FOR… THE… FJORD!?' Eric Sagar hoots in sheer doubt that he’s not dreaming. 'WHAT KIND OF TALK IS THAT!? Look why did it fall flat on it’s back the moment I got it home?' Eric Sagar challenges him, leaning ever closer into him. 'Well the Norwegian Blue prefers napping on it’s back. Beautiful plumage—' 'Look, I took the liberty of examining that parrot. And I discovered that the only reason it had been stood on it’s perch in the first place was because it was nailed there.' '…' the man blinks, he must think of a response. 'Yeah… well.. of course it was nailed there.' 'Oh?' 'Y… Yeah, because if it wasn’t. ZOOM! It’d have been off!' The man claims making a flying movement with his hands. 'Look.' Eric Sagar crouches down to pick up the Norwegian Blue. 'This parrot wouldn’t move if you put 4000 volts through it.' He places it on the counter. 'It has demised.' 'No. It’s pining.' 'IT IS NOT PINING! IT’S PASSED ON! THIS PARROT IS NO MORE! IT HAS CEASED TO BE! IT HAS EXPIRED AND GONE TO SEE ITS MAKER! THIS IS A LATE PARROT! IT’S A STIFF! IT’S TOOK IT’S LAST BREATH OF LIFE! IT RESTS IN PEACE! IF YOU HADN’T NAILED IT TO ITS PERCH IT WOULD BE PUSHING UP THE DAISES! IT’S RAN UP THE CURTAIN AND JOINED THE CHOIR INVISIBLE!' He picks up the dead bird and slams it once more down on the desk. 'THIS IS AN EX-PARROT!' 'Well, I’d better replace it then.' The man moans, brushing the parrot corpse off the counter to the floor. He looks around in the back. 'Honestly, you have to argue until you’re blue in the mouth to get something done around here.' Eric sighs to himself. 'Sorry man, I’ve had a look. No parrots.' The man comes back shaking his head. 'I see…' Eric Sagar sighs. 'We’ve got a slug.' The man boasts for some strange reason. 'Does it talk?' Eric Sagar asks, knowing the answer. 'Not really.' The man taps his fingers against the desk. 'Then it wont do.' Eric Sagar barks. 'I’ll give you your money back then.' (Note: This is my own adaptation of Monty Python’s Flying Circus’ 'Dead Parrot' sketch, made into transcript by me and then altered to be a roleplay.)
