...Angiegol?

30 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday “Why does it mock us, precious?” Rocking back and forth in a small, dark room is a horribly disfigured creature, clutching something in its grubby hands as if clinging to its salvation. Small, irregular eyes gleam pitifully, peering down at the object with palpable longing and desire. Shining even more brightly out of that ugly, rectangular face is a line of perfectly white teeth, exposed forever by lips pulled back in a wincing, painful grimace. “We be’s nice to them if they be’s nice to us,” the strangled voice moans again, increasing in pitch the entire time as if on the point of breaking down into sobs. The newly born Angiegol lets out a long, inhuman wail, its elongated neck stretching back as far as possible, so the head is facing in the opposite direction and the scream reverberates off the wall behind it. The echoing trauma goes on for several long moments after the original cry desists, a symbolic representation of the world’s outrage and disgust for its loss. In the eery silence that follows, the Smeagol-parody’s mouth curls back rhythmically in a single-repeated curse, uttered too quietly to be picked up by the human ear. The retarded gaze never twitches from the object in its hands, and as it brings it up with its gangly, stringbean arms, the light catches it enough to make out a small 8x10 of PWC Superstar Angelo Deville, standing regally and smugly in all his good-looking splendour. “We wants it,” the creature hisses, blinking and shrinking back from the picture as if staring directly into the sun’s brilliance. Despite the pain, it can’t help but stare longingly. Needfully. “We needs it!” The amendment has more gusto, and one of the hands shakily moves from the picture to scratch one of the many scars marring the once beautiful visage. “We weren’t always like this.” The cry is mournful enough to leak tears from a stone, the fond remembrance in that tiny voice compelling someone – anyone! – to action. “And we won’t be now, precious... Oh no! Not us, precious.” A solitary tear falls from one of those squinting eyes and splatters on the photograph. “We wants it back,” it strangles impotently, unable to help itself. “We needs it back!” Trembling irately from ugly head to hairy toe, Angiegol thrusts the picture up into the air like a trophy, a prize to be cherished. “GIVES US BACK OUR POSER!”
