Maintaining Focus

25 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday The hole was an extremely quiet place, but no one who ever visited would define that silence as peaceful. The overwhelming emptiness and solitude assailed Deville’s ears and mind like a marching band banging away inside his head. No rhythm touched the noise, however, and music could never be so coarse. Crackling like static in the desert, the all-encompassing buzz had only subsided whenever Deville slipped onto a conscious thought, and even then it was like a dial rotating slowly over idle radiowaves. “This is nutbar,” Deville muttered, basking in the brief silence. Curling into himself and hugging his knees to his chest as if to keep together, he realised only vaguely that he had parroted the same thing many, many times before, all within this eternal darkness. Angelo had lost count of the hours hunched whittling away in this cell, and even the days. He only knew that he had received a total of four meals, all pushed through a rusted metal latch that nearly peeled his skin from his bones every time it screeched open. The guard – or whoever was bringing him his food – never uttered a single word to him, no matter how loudly Deville shouted or how nasty his threats became. Once in a millennium, it seemed, a rectangular bar of blinding whiteness would open in the empty space before him, and food was delivered with only smell and taste to betray what it was. It was never something good, or even anything he could identify, but it sustained him. Those four feasts were the only link he had had to anything but this never-ending night. It had been a long time since the last, and Angelo was licking his lips ravenously before he could stop himself, or even think of a reason to. Stop it, fool! As soon as you start appreciating and anticipating what they give you, you’re dead! That angry, belittling voice was his only companion in this world of darkness aside from his own. It had ranted on and on almost since the beginning, telling him what to do if he wanted to keep a grip on things, if he wanted to avoid going crazy down here. Deville ultimately heeded all of the advice, but only after puzzling out – aloud – different reasons to choose that route. He wouldn’t dignify the voice’s condescending air by letting it know it was right. But it seemed to know anyhow, much to his disappointment. He stopped glaring suddenly, realizing what had been going through his head, and laughter rippled along his body in cold waves. “Christ! Am I mad already?” As he chuckled softly, the voice smugly confirmed that indeed he was, snickering that it had always been just a matter of time. He rolled onto his side, eyes clamped shut, and jiggled with unbridled mirth, laughing soundlessly at the ceiling he couldn’t even see. “Mad as a hatter!” He crowed, doubling himself up twice as much. After a while – Deville had stopped thinking in terms of time, since it did not seem to exist here – the laughter died to amused snickering, and then simply to heavy breathing. For a long time all that could be heard was the sound of his breaths, growing slowly but surely more pronounced, picking up steam with the fading of the happiness. What had been so damned funny, anyhow? Ignoring the voice, Angelo rose painfully to his knees, sapping his body of strength it didn’t even have anymore. He had felt his face and body, both with his hands and with the nerves still intact within, and he knew he would shudder if he saw himself. Permitting he could recognise his own face, of course. That didn’t explain having the strength of a newborn child, however; it felt as if he was trying to move through gelatin. He had taken worse beatings than those the pampered guards and police constables had seen fit to “restrain” him with. It had to be something else. Perhaps sitting in the dark, thinking and working completely alone, took more out of a man than Deville had ever imagined. He tried to stand, gritting his teeth, and slumped to the floor after only a few moments effort, exhausted. Maybe it was the betrayal weighing him down. “They’re the mad ones.” Coming between breaths, the words were placed as carefully and smoothly as pieces on a chess board, revealing little if any of their underlying meaning. The idea of it shocked him still; unprovoked treason from those he would have died to save. He never would have thought it of them, would have killed any who laid the accusation at their feet, yet it was the only possible answer. They were the only ones who knew for certain, the only ones with proof. In his greatest moment of stupidity, he had foolishly believed he could trust them. He didn’t see the dagger until it was planted firmly in his chest, and sometimes, even now, he still tried to deny it. “But they turned on me...” Deville tensed his muscles, probing for strength, trying to build it, then let them sag pitifully. “They must have.” Kathryn and Erica would pay for inflicting this on him, and he had already thought of several dozen ingenious ways of prolonging the payment. Howling and screaming wretchedly, they would beg on their knees for his forgiveness, for his mercy, and he knew it would never come. He would... He would... He still couldn’t believe they had done it. Was his judgement of character so wrong? What? You? The voice laughed scornfully, then danced out of harm’s way as Deville tried to crush it. Don’t be ridiculous, Angelo my boy. You always pick the winners out of the crowd. Say, weren’t Kathryn and Erica the same women who turned on Cassius Deamont in much the same way, the very second a larger, much better prize came into view? He would have denied it... If he could. And let’s not forget your good buddy Salvatore... Smug now, the tone mocked in and of itself. How many times has he tried to see you buried now? And don’t tell me you’re still pretending he didn’t kill your– Angelo muted the voice. He didn’t know how to do it when he did, he only knew that he had to shut it up. Had to conquer it before it conquered him. He... swallowed... it. Somehow. Shuddering, he tried to force out the memory of swallowing part of his soul. “I know it wasn’t Salvatore who ratted me out.” The distraction was blatant, but it worked; anger flared within him, firing a tiny shot of adrenaline into his blood. He was feeling nauseous from what he had done to that voice in his head, and sweat sprouted freely on his forehead. Why did he feel like this? No matter. “He couldn’t afford to get rid of me... I’m too valuable an asset to discard without reason.” He didn’t like the road he was going down, but it was away from whatever it was he had done, so he pursued it. “It had to be Kathryn and Erika.” Pain swirled through his head, and he had to put all of his meagre strength into retaining consciousness. It had to be them. “Who else would have–“ Light suddenly sliced into the room, a thick vertical shaft that ripped apart the darkness without warning, like a spectral doorway into another dimension. A tiny whimper wracked Angelo as he flinched back, using both filth-covered arms to shield his eyes, kicking at the floor with his heels until he backed into the wall behind him, and not even stopping then. He was nearly all black from rolling around in the dirt of the hole, and his normally golden blonde hair framed an unshaven face bruised and swollen so badly it barely looked human. Shadows materialised in the glowing bar, and then he was being attacked, hauled roughly to his feet and manhandled by cold claws. He wanted to fight back, but even if he wasn’t blind from the scathing brightness, those pincers were the only things keeping him on his feet. “Come on, Deville,” a sneer rippled along the barrier of helplessness coursing through him, and he felt his left arm jerked roughly for emphasis. “Time to go get cleaned up. You’ve got a big date this morning.” ~><>...<><~ The back of the paddy wagon stank like a horrible combination of musty old socks and ditry sweat, but Deville felt remarkably refreshed. Showered, shaved, and sitting gauntly in the now-oversized orange prison suit, he looked like Jack Halloween with his swollen, discoloured head, sickly pale skin, and already withered body, belying his relatively short stay in the hole. He looked like a hollow, cracked-out version of himself. Friday, he muttered, his own voice familiar in his head. Almost an entire week. It had seemed like an eternity. Whatever happened, Angelo had no intention of going back there. He certainly would not live the rest of his life and die there. Less than one week, and they mean to expand it into my entire life. He laughed under his breath, a sound tinged with ruefulness now instead of insanity. He had to get out of this, today, and wipe the situation clean before it evolved beyond a simple mess. He had a plan, but it hinged on a piece he himself could not directly play, a defense he had triggered a long time ago in preparation of an occasion just like this. He hoped his friends remained so, and kept the pact alive. If they did, he could escape. It was too much to ask for, but he plodded on anyhow. They took care of it... They had to. Now I just have to do my part... Remain calm. Despite being fed a decent meal before his departure, the Deville still felt weak as a kitten. He didn’t believe he had any choice whether he stayed calm or not. Shivering against the cold steel around him, it didn’t stop him from dwelling on it, though. The ear who turns deaf to the loudest shouting will crane to hear a whisper. He didn’t know where the quote came from, but it rang true to him. Above all, he must remember tact. No matter what happens, he must remain calm and at ease, even when teetering on the brink of explosion. He hoped it wouldn’t come, but if it did, he meant to be prepared. You know what to say, and how to say it. Now just go in there and say it. You’ll be back in your own home in time for dinner. And finishing... other... business. Turning his mind from that, Angelo began going over his speech, giving as much time to how he would present it as to what he would actually say. Finesse would be needed. Tact. He wouldn’t even entertain the possibility that his friends had failed him, refusing to think of it even as the caged van pulled to a slow stop and he heard the doors sliding open. If they had failed him, Deville would likely rot away in other cages for the rest of his life. ~><>...<><~ Little larger than an NFL endzone, the courtroom was jam-packed with people, some with note and sketch pads. The polished oak walls were tastefully decorated with framed pictures of retired justices, smiling judiciously, and an immaculately pressed American flag behind a sheet of glass. Directly beneath the flag towered the judge’s bench, thrusting majestically above the rest of the room to survey and weigh all within it. A wizened old man with a neatly combed dome of white hair occupied it now, just settling into his throne, tucking his robe underneath him like a skirt. Straightening, he turned bright green eyes, piercing and alert, on all those standing in attendence, glistening with power and righteousness from within his aged and wrinkled face. After a long and somewhat uncomfortable moment, he folded his hands before him on the pulpit, and the bailiff invited everyone to sit down. Deville sat quickly and smoothly, but not hurriedly, his nerves grating as the bunny suit swishes and crinkles. Why the hell couldn’t I just keep wearing my suit? Squashing the anger before it could root, he smoothed his face to calmness and turned beseeching eyes onto the Honourable Justice Hearn, who looked through him without the faintest glimmer of an expression. Deville sat just off to the man’s right, at a little table separate from the bleachers lined behind him, as if this were some kind of sporting event. Flanking his other side was the prosecutor for the state, a portly, bald, ostrich of a man with a twitching and pretentious air. Telling himself to relax, and that his plan would work, he glanced placidly around the courtroom at those gathered to watch this display, seeing more faces than he would’ve liked. More notepads and sketchpads, too. He shrugged it off as something beyond his control, and tried to focus in on what was happening. His case had already been called, and one of the women assembled immediately before the judge was already typing furiously, albeit calmly, as the suit-wearing ostrich rose from his seat and gave his honour a curt bow and a thank you. Deville despised the professional hypocrite even before he opened his mouth, but when his soothingly musical voice began singing his tune, it ballooned up into full-blown hatred. Not a single muscle on his face twitched, though; he watched coolly and impassively. “It saddens me deeply to appear before your honour this morning,” he begins regally, voice tinged with anxiety that touches no other part of him. “Our investigation of the man brought before the court today, Angelo Deville, has shown the state – and any else who care to look for it – that we are dealing with one of the most maliciously cold and callous individuals I have ever seen.” A few whispered murmurs flicker through the crowd, but Judge Hearn only gives the slightest twitch of an eyebrow. Deville, sitting alone, didn’t object. He knew it would begin like this, and expected it to only grow worse. What the man said wasn’t what had Angelo secretly worried, it was what he had had up his sleeve... And whether he still did or not. The rapid clacking of keys filled the brief silence permitted by the prosecution’s deep breath. “Exotortion. Blackmail. Larceny. Racketeering.” The man was staring directly at Deville now, his face a mask of cold hatred and disbelief. Each word came out with a razor’s edge, accusation and condemnation rolled into one. “I fear that is only the prologue to Mr. Deville’s sordid tale. “The main body is murder, and that is what brings us here today. Specifically, the murder of a former co-worker and long-time enemy of the accused: Jason O’Neil. Mr. Deville kidnapped him, bound him, and tossed him off the Brooklyn Bridge to the numbingly cold water rushing below. Our investigation has routed out the depths to which Deville will sink simply for spite, and has shown me personally that the man not only lacks a conscience, but a soul as well. With time, this indisputable fact will be as clear to the court as it is to the state.” Only now did the man look away from Angelo, who had met his gaze with a tinge of a smile the entire time. Turning to the judge, he swept his arms up and unfolded them to either side, as if presenting an armload of treasure from a raid. The judge looked on, neither impressed, scandalised, or anything else that would betray having emotions. “He has murdered many times before, only there was never anything substantial enough to warrant an arrest. He was always too cautious; too clever to be caught. With Mr. O’Neil he slipped up, though, and last week the police received video tape surveillance of the heinous attack and capture less than half an hour before PWC TV aired O’Neil’s body being tossed over the railing by an unidentifiable man. Using computers, we have identified him.” His outstretched arms swept over to Angelo now, both aiming rigid index fingers at his chest like pistols. Icy shock swept through Deville, almost shattering his outward calm. The pig wasn’t lying... They have a tape. “It was Angelo Deville,” the champion of the law spat, twisting his mouth cruelly. “The diabolical man with the audacity to come before your honour this morning seeking bail. The state begs your honour to deny that request, to keep this manipulative killer locked away from the public, so that he cannot main any other innocent men.” He pauses to shake his head, snuffling and pretending to be choked up. “If it pleases your honour, allow the killing to stop and the long-delayed atonement to begin.” Muttering thanks in a choking voice that never touched his eyes, the prosecutor sat down in his chair and poured himself a glass of water. Spying him, Deville did the same, pouring from the crystal pitcher on the table into both glasses before him. One had been meant for his lawyer, but as soon as Angelo found a phone this morning, he had fired the man without waiting to hear his lawyerly excuses. He’s lucky I don’t kill him. Deville tried not to brood as he waited for Judge Hearn to call on him. “Touching,” Hearn sighed in a tired and bemused voice, staring down at a litter of papers and shuffling through them from time to time. “Unfortunately, little brother,” he looked up at the prosecution, his gaze set to nail him to that desk. “I’m reading here that your mountains of evidence mysteriously disappeared during the night. That honourable state skipped over that minor detail in your delightful opening, so perhaps now would be a good time to set the record straight.” He cocked his head bird-like, shooting a single glance at Deville before rivetting his eyes back on the man he had addressed. Journalists scribbled furiously on notepads, looking up eagerly from time to time to measure the scene with their eyes. Deville appeared calm, but inside fireworks were going off. They came through! Yes! I’m free! Angelo had almost given up on his friends, thinking their pact would be easily forgotten with Deville staring at life behind bars. But they had come through; they had destroyed the evidence against him. Or so he hoped. The prosecutor climbed grudgingly back to his feet, clearing his throat and staring down at his own stack of papers. “Well, uh, it’s not exactly as cut and dry as your honour makes it out to be,” fumbling for words to save him, the man cringed visibly as he forced the next sentence out. “There were fires... Deliberate fires, obviously set by Deville’s cronies, that destroyed all of the videotape evidence. Three separate rooms, in two different buildings, suffered spontaneous fires during the same night, and it appears that the only real damage was to our case against Mr. Deville.” Angelo smiled, but not too widely. He tried to make his look pitying, as if he believed there had never been any tapes in the first place. In a way, the judge mirrored that look. “But we’re still alright,” the state hurried on, feeling the case fleeing from him. “We still have the statements and confessions we managed to procure.” What statements? For one brief shining moment, Deville felt himself clearing the Kathryn and Erica of any and all guilt concerning this. He had never bought them a video camera, afterall, and even if he did it’d take a long time to teach them to use it accurately enough for a murder trial. But statements? Statements had to be them. The problem was whether or not the slimy prosecutor was lying. If he was, he would simply remand the beginning of everything until he could squeeze out a couple of statements, regardless of who they were from. A foul taste crept into Deville’s mouth as he thought of the tactics of The Law. Hell, at least we have honour. Hanging his head as if he could feel the thought, the prosecution sat heavily, not looking at Deville or the judge, but pretending to be very eager to scour the papers, as if hoping to find his dignity. “Very well,” the justice muttered, sounding for all the world as if he believed it as much as Deville did. Things were looking good for the Deville right now, and the moment of truth was upon him. He not only had to convince this man – not to mention the public – that he was innocent, but that the charges against him were downright ludicrous. He figured he’s got that handled. Staring at the judge, he is all too conscious of his disfigured face, and he can only hope it does not take away from his influence. “Mr. Deville, are we still waiting on your lawyer, or would you like to see this hearing postponed until Monday?” Meeting that gaze stare for stare, Deville rose slowly to his feet and proffered a grateful smile, trying to seem imperial and respectable in spite of his orange plastic suit. “If your honour has no objection, I would appreciate being permitted to represent myself.” Deville smiles at the clarity and assuredness of his voice, lording out across the courtroom as if a microphone had been pressed to his lips. Everyone in attendance gasped in unison, then frantic whisperings filled the air, shrouding the court in anarchy. The gavel came pounding down quickly and authoritatively, and Hearn shouted for order. Everyone obediently settled. Shaking his head as if he should have expected as much in this circus, Hearn waved his hand and sighed a resigned “Very well.” Deville inclined his head with a grateful smile, “Thank you, your honour.” The room went deathly silent, every eye trained on Angelo, every pen poised to scribble his words for print. Here goes... Ready to recite what he had spent countless hours trying to rehearse, Deville’s mind went blank. He had remembered it as well as his own face, but trying to call it up now yielded only a reflection of how his faced now looked. He never choked, hated choking, but it was happening now. And it had happened all too much in the past month. Improvising, he hung on to the pathetic semblance of a plan that remained. “I can’t begin to explain the horror I feel at being dragged here in chains like an animal. Nor what I felt during the course of the last week.” He let his cuffed hands rise unconsciously to his face, telling more than any account of the events could, then spotted and lowered them just as quickly, apparently out of countenance enough to have not noticed their ascent. “The criminality of it shames me deeply, as it would anyone who could pry into its dark depths.” Lose yourself in tact... Succinct and tactful will set you free. The room was still choking in its silence, with the reporters touching their pens as lightly as possible and everyone else on equally high alert. “I am not a murderer.” A commanding statement of fact. “I am a wrestler.” The prosecutor looked ready to leap up and strangle him, but Judge Hearn stared on as a man watching paint dry. Let him. “We put on shows for teenagers and young adults, purely scripted and choreographed plays. From the wrestling in the ring to the questionable storylines surrounding the battle inside it, we are nothing more or less than actors trying to please an audience.” Let them choke on that. Deville frowned at the bench of the prosecution, staring untold hurt and outrage. “Jason O’Neil was and is one of my closest friends within the business. We worked together in FSW and leapt at the chance to do so again in the PWC. We got along extremely well, and the audience really loved our chemistry when we set it against one another.” He let his voice go dry, “some bought into it a little too hard.” The judge gives a blank look to the prosecution, then meets Angelo’s eyes as they’re turned up on him. “Jason O’Neil, my friend, wanted out of the spotlight. He told me he was tired of it, and wanted to live a more peaceful life, out of the public eye. He and I got together with Ron Starr, who is our boss in this soap opera, and devised a way for him to be taken out of the picture with the maximal amount of controversy.” People were whispering behind him now, a scandalised sound, but Angelo nor the judge paid it any mind. “We dreamed up a way to cap our relationship on screen, a fitting end to the story of a mobster and the man who sought to set him free. It was a script. A storyline. “There is no doubt in my mind that Jason O’Neil is alive, living peacefully and luxuriously on a tropical island somewhere, blissfully unaware of the media circus the Trenton police department and the state of New Jersey have turned this into. I am sure he would think it funny,” Deville allowed himself to muse, then made his voice like iron. “Until he found out how I have been treated as a result. As a result of our game... Our aspiration to entertain the audience.” Deville sighed and shook his head, looking down at his trembling hands before him, a broken man. “Even standing here now I hope that this is just a dream I will awake from. I don’t want to be a mobster, or break the law, I just want to put on a show where people can believe for a few short hours that I am. Up until now, it has served me grandly.” Deville cast a short, meaningful glance at the prosecutor. “Up until now, everyone has understood that wrestling is fake.” “I am not a murderer,” Angelo says again with even more conviction. “That is why there is no evidence proving me one. If the police want to do a real investigation into this matter, they would be well advised to actually look for Jason O’Neil, instead of searching only for a way to turn our show into a horrible reality. Find Jason O’Neil, and ask him. Ask Ron Starr.” Deville raises his hands helplessly. “I’m just a wrestler.” Deville waits until Justice Hearn nods, then thanks him quietly and sits down, making himself look up at the man who would determine his fate. This has to work... I can’t live my life out under these stooges. The prosecutor was halfway to standing again, his mouth open to begin calling Angelo down once more, when the judge irritably waved him down. His gavel banged loudly to instill order in those gathered, and he narrowed his eyes down at the prosecution and Angelo, staring at them each in turn. “I have heard enough.” His small mouth barely moved when he spoke, but the voice was loud and booming, and those the gavel did not silence shut their mouths at its commanding tone. “The state has presented a compelling recital about how Mr. Deville is a murderous criminal, but compelling as it is, the foundation on which it is built could not hold him for shoplifting. Without evidence that mysteriously disappears at the least opportune time, the case against this man is little more than hollow stone-throwing.” The prosecutor gaped like a fish out of water, but Hearn no-sold it, still glaring judiciously at both of them. “On the other hand,” he continued, letting his gaze settle on Angelo. “I know too much of Angelo Deville to buy his silly song and dance about being a poor wrestler just trying to make his way.” Deville looks properly indignant, but Hearn just shakes his head. “It is no secret that Deville’s fantasy life is startlingly similar to his real one, and speculation abounds among the Law with regard to how deep he is into his father’s old life.” I’m not getting out, Deville’s mind whined sourly, and he couldn’t stop his hands from clenching into enraged fists. How dare the man even mention his father? His mind whirled. Things were not going according to plan. “Deville’s plea of innocence demands I be as stupid as the prosecution’s plea to hang him.” Judge Hearn folds his hands on top of the pulpit and turns back to the prosecutor. “But the onus of evidence is not on Mr. Deville; he does not have to prove his innocence. He only has to defend it.” Another muffled gasp escapes the crowd. Yes! “If you’re going to accuse someone of a crime, not to mention hold and question them illegally, you’d better have some hard evidence to back it up. The state is lucky I do not throw this entire thing out on its ear, and it is lawfully retarded if it thinks I can hold this man for the years it would take to go to trial without even the proof under which it supposedly arrested him.” The prosecutor glowered down at his desk, refusing to meet the judge’s gaze. “I’m not going to throw it out, however,” the judge glares at Deville, disliking the arrogant smile already pasted across the accused’s face. “Because there is enough of a reason to continue the investigation. I hope the police do look for Mr. O’Neil, and I hope that they do question Ron Starr as to the legitimacy of your testimony.” Shut up and set me free, old man. Deville can’t stop grinning. I can’t believe this actually worked. Even in his exuberance, though, the thought that it could have been prevented entirely won’t cease seeping into his mind. Don’t worry about that... We’ll make sure it never happens again. “In the meantime, I see no cause to keep Deville locked away from society, or prevent him from his livelihood.” The way he said livelihood left it open to a double-meaning, as did the blank stare, but Deville didn’t care. “Bail is set at $500 000 dollars, and I want to see you all back here on June 15th. By then,” he glances at the prosecution, “you’d better have a case to make.” The gavel slammed down even as the judge was rising from his seat, ignoring the amused “thank you” Deville shouted to his back. The bailiff came over to escort him to his cell while the paperwork was filled out, but Deville followed happily, even clapping the man on the shoulder. He was free. And the first part of the plan had been pulled off. I’m free. His blue eyes gleamed brightly as he left the now uproarious courtroom, a man with something to live for. Now I can fix all of this shit before things get out of hand.
