PWC TV Commercial 3

1 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Originally aired on WGTW TV-48 in Philadelphia, PA at 11:59 am EST Wednesday, January 1st. Production is set and filming about to begin on a television commercial for the new PWC. Hoyt Williams stands impatiently waiting for this monumental moment in his budding career. For as much training as he has gone through nothing could overcome that initial feeling of stage fright that batters his stomach right now. It feels as if the Bears of 85' are doing the shuffle on his stomach's lining. The room Hoyt stands in is nothing more then a garage for trucks. The walls are a cold stone gray with no windows. A few shelves on the side and some space heaters to battle the frigid New Jersey winter is really all that's in the room. It's an ingenious rental for an upstart federation. The rent is cheap and the room is spacious. Plus the fact that it's in an industrial section of the city noise is not a factor. When the backdrop and lights that presently fill the room for the making of commercials are not here. The ring in which the wrestlers train in fills the void and the studio becomes the training facility. A smile forced upon by pride emerges on Hoyt's face as he realizes that when his father was the same age as he is right now, old Duke was still playing football. Wrestling wasn't even a thought yet for the Malice Man. Already one step better then his father in his mind, Hoyt still wrestles with the shadows of greatness that have plagued his entire life. Just like a prostitute deals with crabs, Hoyt does the same with comparison to somebody he barley knows. "Hoyt" a slightly balding production assistant calls out as he fires a glance at the spaced out wrestler. "Hoyt" repeats the man. Feeling a bit uncomfortable being the only person standing around in his black wrestling tights Hoyt's zoned out. The 6'6", 320 pound Williams snaps out of his day dream like an alarm sounded in his mind. The room is full of about five men ranging in ages from 20 to midlife crisis. They are all dressed in casual wear and are handling the production and the shoot. They act like this commercial is the fourth installment in the Godfather franchise as they shoot for perfection. Hoyt stands out like a black guy at a David Duke presidential rally as he towers over these professional men. His body is that of an Adonis ripped from the pages of Greek mythology. His head is shaved like he just stepped out of Fort Biggs. Other then mass size he looks like any other 21-year-old kid equipped with the required gold loop earring in his left ear and barbwire tattoo circulating his right arm. "Yea?" Hoyt responds with a cocky attitude. The production assistant sprays Hoyt down with oil to add a shiny glare to his define body, which bulges strength. "These things don't film themselves lets get a move on" claps the equally attitude laced producer. A remake of Dusty Springfield's "Son Of Preacher Man" starts to play. The new version includes Hoyt's name in the lyrics and the sound is a fusion of punk and rock recorded by a local New Jersey garage band also looking for their first break. A strobe light flashes against the black backdrop as Hoyt stands their looking authoritative with his military like shaved head. The producer then flashes Hoyt the go sign by pointing an index finger at the young aspiring star. "You asked for your own personal Jesus while I have arrived to pay for your sins." Shots Hoyt as the strobes reflect off his body, and the music plays hard, "Pro Wrestling Circuit is where I will dominate." The screen flashes the word "dominate" in bright yellow with a strobe effect behind it. Sweat appears on the brow of the novice professional as he continues to speak with conviction and vigor in his voice. "From Lou Thez and Bruno Sammartino, to Savage and Hogan, all the way to Stanislav and Kannon, I have studied the greats and I'm destine for greatness." rambles Hoyt in standard English with a slight Midwestern "a" accent. The screen again flashes words in bright yellow this time they read "destine for greatness". The producer works his magic from behind the camera adding the effect as the spot is cut. "I understand where this sport has been and I will bring it to the future. I am the future. I come from a tarnished tradition that I will correct and right the Williams name." Continues the arrogant offspring of Duke Williams. The producer adds his magic with the word "tradition" branded onto the screen and into our minds. "Your Personal Jesus has arrived and I can assure you the sport will never be the same again. I'm here to take it beyond where it's been. Put grandma to bed cus things are about to get raunchy in the ProWrestling Circuit. Resurrection begins January 2003" Yells Hoyt; as poignant as he can be. The producer cues the end as Hoyt wipes his brow with a towel thrown at him by the balding fat man. All the producer has left to do is add the PWC logo with a beating heart sounder and effect behind it. "Thanks" "Anytime killer, anytime." Responds Hoyt as he struts away to the small bathroom to change. The thought of a new beginning and the evolution of the sport lingers in the room as Hoyt exits to the john.
