Bushwhacked!

10 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“You don’t know nuthin’ ‘bout ‘Nam, man!” the veteran snarled. Sebastian Toomes curled his upper lip in derision, setting down the glass of MGD on the warped bar, and looked around the VFW. It was nearly empty of people, except the perpetually bemused woman behind the bar with the lunch-lady hair and the angry Vietnam veteran. “Look, mister. I aint got anything against Vietnam vets, man! I mean, I was one of them!” he says above the Hank Williams Jr. playing on the Victrola jukebox. “What? Shit, son, you musta not even grown in yer daddy’s balls by the time the war was over! I was THERE, man! ’68! 3rd battalion of the 187th Infantry Regiment! What unit were you in, Mister ‘I-was-in-‘Nam’?” the fifty-ish man yelled, stopping to steady his hand which held the tallboy of beer. “I was there, man… With my buddy, Chris Campbell… 69th…uh…69th Sexual Forces!” Toomes blurts out. “You were a Green Beret? Oh, the shit’s getting deep now!” the man scoffs. “Not SPECIAL forces, man!” Toomes says patiently, holding up a hand. “SEXUAL Forces! We were there to pacify the…civilian resistance! Lemme tell ya a war story!” The bar becomes wavy as Toomes goes back in time… The scene is that of a steamy rainforest, the humid undergrowth creating a carpet of mist. The words “Va Gi Na, Poontang Valley, 1969” appear on the screen. Toomes, in a tight black tank top and stylish Hi-Tec boots and baggy Tommy Hilfiger khaki pants, is holding an M-16, and has a belt of machine gun ammunition strapped around his torso. He appears out of the mist, two stripes of boot polish underneath his eyes, like a football player, his eyes set and determined. “C’mon, Campbell! The Viet-Dong resistance camp must be… ahead! Or something.” Behind him, struggling with a backpack filled with what looks like a beer keg strapped to it, labeled ‘K-Y Jelly EXTREME’ in black military stencil, appears Chris Campbell, in similar garb. “Yes, sir!” Toomes looks over Campbell grimly. “Is our secret weapon at maximum viscosity?” Campbell snaps a salute with his left hand. “SIR YES SIR!” Toomes nods. “Right. Let’s move out!” The two make their way into the misty jungle… And then, inexplicably, the mist gives way to what appears to be a gazebo with a bubbling hot tub, a wet bar, and a small Frigidaire. Toomes narrows his eyes. “Campbell! Set up our secret weapon! I want fields of lubrication here…” Toomes points in different directions with his assault rifle, continuing. “Here… and here! GET MOVING, SOLDIER!” “SIR YES SIR!” Campbell flops down on the wooden deck around the hot tub, and scrambles to get the keg of K-Y jelly ready. Toomes looks at the roiling hot tub suspiciously. “Hmmm… Intelligence indicates that a Viet-Dong cell must be around here somewhere…” Nodding grimly, he reaches down to his belt, where somehow, an olive-drab can of beer labeled ‘M-500 Panty-Dropper’ attached itself. Toomes unhooks it, and pulls the tab off like pulling the pin on a grenade. “PARTY IN THE HOLE!” he screams, and the camera cuts back to Campbell, who ducks. The can of beer disappears into the roiling hot tub, and out of the bubbling waters, three Vietnamese women rise up out of the water, all dressed in skimpy black bathing suits and wide Oriental hats like you see Asian people wear. They hold assault rifles and pose sexily. The one in the middle points the assault rifle while cocking out her hips, while a generic guitar riff plays, ala Bill and Ted’s Excellent Adventure. “Campbell! Get ready! We’ve been… AM-BUSCHED!” Toomes yells, and holds his assault rifle at the ready. “You American Boy Toy! Me soooooooo HORNY! Me love you LONG time! We never give up Viet-Dong secrets!” she yells. Toomes looks back to Campbell. “CAMPBELL! DEPLOY THE COMBAT THONG!” Campbell nods once, and undoes his belt, dropping his pants to reveal a camouflage thong. He pumps his hips at the Viet-Dong women. “LET’S ROCK!” he screams, ala Vasquez in Aliens. Toomes leaps into the hot tub, and the women begin to run their hands all over his body. “Oh! American Cock so BIG!” one screams in delight, while the one on the right is pulled out by Campbell. Campbell straddles her, and pins her arms down. “Talk, lady! Tell us the Viet-Dong’s plans!” he yells. “Me never tell! Me TOOOOO horny to tell!” she says seductively. “I guess I’ll just have to FUCK it out of you then!” he growls, then proceeds to kiss her sloppily. “Alright, ladies! I’m making a penetration of the rear area here!” Toomes says grimly, as one Viet-Dong woman bends over, hiking down her bikini bottom as she does so. The remaining Viet-Dong woman makes out with the bent over woman as Toomes drops his pants. The sounds of machine gun fire are heard in the background as Toomes is shown from the side, the point of entry on the woman and his waist are blurred. He slaps her ass and calls out to Campbell, who has his Viet-Dong woman’s legs in the air. “War is hell, isn’t it, Campbell?” The scene becomes blurry as we return to the VFW. The veteran has already left, while the bartender shakes her head in disgust. “Yeah… Good times…” he says dreamily. The camera cuts over to Chris Campbell, who walks in, wearing a PWC shirt and baggy jeans. “Hey, Sebastian… Are you ready to go? Starr wants us to go scouting for talent around here!” Toomes sighs. “Ah, yeah… I was just here telling this one Vietnam vet one of our war stories.” “Uh… Dude, Vietnam was over long before we were born.” “HEY! WHO’S THE FIRST EVER OSW CHAMPION HERE?! If I say we were in Vietnam, WE WERE IN FUCKING VIETNAM!” Toomes yells, and drinks the rest of his beer in a gulp. Campbell sighs. “Dude, I’m double-parked. Let’s get a move on before the cops tow my car… I still don’t know why you wanted to come down here anyway…” Toomes looks at him, exasperated. “Hey, I was here scouting talent! Y’know, Road Dogg was a former Marine. I was thinking that maybe there might be guys who mighta been in a war sometime here, and maybe they knew how to wrestle!” “Dude. People who come to the VFW are all old crusty dudes with nothing better to do with their lives except drink cheap beer and reminisce about killing people. There ain’t gonna be anything Starr is looking for in here.” Toomes throws a couple of dollar bills on the counter. “Hey, you never know. This is Jersey, after all… This state, hell, anything can happen. I mean, c’mon, Jason O’Neil got a job as a wrestler in my promotion here! The possibilities are endless! It’s like a dimensional rift where the myriad permutations of reality are subject to change on a moment’s notice!” Campbell shakes his head, tired of driving Toomes around all day. “Fine. Dimension rift. I’m going back to the car.” Toomes rolls his eyes, and laughs, shaking his head at the bartender. “Kids these days… I’d like to see the look on his face when I make the big discovery! Oh yeah! Who’ll be laughing then, huh?” The bartender spits a rotted tooth out onto the bar. “Git outta here, never come back.” Toomes looks at her for a moment, then pulls on his coat. “Hmm. Good idea. Any repeated tellings of my Vietnam war stories just don’t have the magic of the first time, huh?” “I’ll say. This is the fourth time this week.” She begins coughing, and hacks out something brown and slimy onto the counter, and wipes it up with the same rag she used to clean the glasses. “Then I’d see how you may have been disappointed by hearing it so many times. I know, you long for the magic you felt the first time you heard it, right?” he says, slipping off his bar stool. The camera cuts back to the woman, who is cleaning a glass with the same rag she used to clean up her lung butter, and she merely glares at him. “Right. See ya tomorrow!” FIN
