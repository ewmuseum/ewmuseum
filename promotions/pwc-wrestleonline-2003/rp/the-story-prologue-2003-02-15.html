The Story: Prologue

15 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-15-03 Friday, February 15, 2002: Chicago, Illinois Massaging her eyelids with her right thumb and forefinger, Julianne Seger tries to block out her headache. The day after Valentine’s Day has always been a tough one for her, and this year’s raging hangover certainly doesn’t help matters any. Then again, neither does having to get up at 6 AM to go to work on two and a half hours of sleep. Now sitting with her hand cupped over her face in her boss’ office, she really wishes she would have stopped after the eighth shot of tequila last night. “Goddamn, Julie, you look like shit!” spouts the balding man in business attire on the other side of the desk, shaking his head in disdain. “I know, I know…” she mumbles back with a sigh. Brushing her shoulder length curly brown hair out of her face, she looks up at him for the first time with her bloodshot eyes. “I had a rough night.” “That’s no excuse for this shit,” he yells, standing up and slamming his fist on the desk. A picture frame on the edge falls to the floor with a clatter, but he pays it no attention, pointing a finger at Julie in accusation. “Runny make-up, messed up hair…and what the fuck is up with those slacks? Didn’t I tell you not to come in here without a skirt anymore? You know how I love seeing those fine ass legs!” “Fuck off, Michael…” Cupping her face in her hands once more, Julie’s thoughts finally focus…focus on how much she despises this man. Michael Zanders, heir to the empire that is Zanders Publishing. If it wasn’t the backbone of the Chicago publishing industry then it at least was making up a good number of its vertebrae. With eight magazines, seventeen journals, and the seediest newspaper in the city on its current agenda, altogether bringing in billions of dollars every year, it could be labeled a media juggernaut in every sense of the word. And all of this would fall into his hands very shortly…the little prick. His grandfather, Martin Zanders, was a legend in the field, his tactics still emulated to this day. His father, Kenneth, is the current CEO, on par if not surpassing the clan patriarch in genius. And Michael…well he was an incompetent, womanizing piece of shit. Currently the editor-in-chief of the most respected publication in the company’s fleet, an insider’s news magazine named The Truth, he was now merely biding his time until his father stepped down and retired. More likely than not, the corporate legacy would be destroyed with him in the drivers seat, but Julie didn’t care. She owed nothing to this man. Except maybe a violent homicide… “Whatever happened to the sexy little tart I hired? You used to at least try and look pretty.” Zanders says derisively, breaking her line of thought. He is now regarding her with that smug, controlling look that always seems to be plastered across his face. It makes her nauseous to even look at him. “You are such an asshole…” “Hey, don’t you fucking talk to me like that, Julie. You better show me some goddamned respect,” he responds, his self-righteous tone almost cutting in quality. “You know, you’re lucky you’ve still got a job here. Usually when I’m done having my way with someone, I toss them aside like the piece of garbage they are.” Julie can feel the tears welling up in her eyes. That last comment hurt, and it hurt a lot. She had been so stupid, so fucking naïve. Ever since she had decided to go to school for journalism, it had been her dream to get a job working on the The Truth, and when Michael had taken an interest in her she felt that she was on her way to the top. But she had thought he was impressed by her writing…she had no clue what he really wanted out of her. When he finally laid it all out on the table and asked for it, she had been so confused. She had wanted to pull away when he grabbed her around her waist and pulled her toward him, but…but… She didn’t really want to go back into all of that. Not now. Holding back her tears, she suppresses the painful memories, those same memories that drove her into a pit of despair last night, all alone, at the bar down the street from her apartment. She had to retain a professional stance, no matter how big of an asshole he was. This job was too important to her to throw away in a fit of emotional rage. Besides, the whole situation had already been resolved. Bending over, Julie picks the picture frame up off the ground and sets it back on Michael’s desk. Inside of it is a picture of a tuxedo clad Zanders standing next to a moderately attractive blond woman in a wedding dress. That would be Mrs. Zanders. The picture now brought to his attention, Michael straightens his tie uncomfortably, before gritting his teeth and glaring at Julie. She manages a weak smile before another jolt from her headache wipes it off her face. Massaging her temples painfully, she decides to move this conversation along toward its point, whatever the hell that may be. “Is there an actual reason you called me into your office, or do you just enjoy the awkwardness of my company?” His glare fading away, Michael turns his attention to searching through some files on his desk. Pulling out the one he was looking for, he slides it across to where Julie is sitting. Opening it up, she begins rifling through the papers contained within. “Jacob Solomon?” she reads off the front sheet of paper in a puzzled tone. “What’s the deal with this guy?” “The deal with that guy is that he’s going to play an integral role in your next story,” Zanders says, returning to his chair and reclining backward. He spins it around and throws his feet up on his desk, pursing his fingers as he watches Julie skim the print-outs in the folder. “And my next story is...?” she asks, pulling out a sheet of paper with the black and white headshots of three attractive young girls and examining it with a confused gaze. “Is this going to be an actual REAL story? Not just some crap thing about the new public works playground being built in Caprini Green or Northwestern Hospital renovations?” “Oh yeah, it’s a real story alright. I know I say some cruel things sometimes, but really, I can’t think of anyone who has earned this assignment more than you,” Michael says, sounding truly sincere. “Here’s the run down. That Solomon character is the leader of some fucked up cult out in northern New Mexico. Real freaky-deaky stuff. The state and local authorities have been trying to shut them down for years, but no luck. They’re all in accordance with statutes concerning freedom of religion and all that shit.” “Damn that Bill of Rights…” Julie mutters sarcastically under her breath, drawing a brief angry stare from Zanders before he continues. “Well anyway, the whole thing’s been growing bigger and bigger each year. They’ve been pulling people in from all over the area, and even outside of it. And from all walks of life, too. You’d think it’d just be the depressed runaway teenagers and Goths and shit like that, but no, you got businessmen who quit their jobs, housewives who left their husbands…hell, there’s even a converted ex-nun,” he says, shaking his head in disbelief. “There’s like more than two hundred people living on that damn commune now, and a lot of people around there are starting to get worried.” “Who are the girls?” Julie interrupts, waving the sheet of paper in the air for Zanders to see. His eyes focus on it for a second before it seems to register with him. “Oh, them. Yeah, there was a scandal over those three about four years back. Apparently they ran off and joined this cult without informing anyone of their plans. Obviously their parents rather upset with this turn of events, so they tried to bring suit against Solomon on grounds of kidnapping, among other things.” “Oh yeah, yeah…I think I remember hearing something about that on the news,” Julie responds with a nod. “He was acquitted, right?” “Yeah, they didn’t have enough evidence to convict him of anything. The girls were over eighteen, so they were free to do as they wished. The parents were heartbroken, but what can you do?” Zanders lifts his hands in the air in a sign of helplessness, before reaching into his pocket for a pack of cigarettes. Fishing one out, he sticks it between his lips and pulls out a lighter. Lighting up, he inhales deeply, savoring it, before letting out a puff of smoke with a relaxed sigh. “You still haven’t answered my question,” Julie says, shutting the file and setting it on her lap. “What am I going to be writing about?” “You, Julianne Seger, are catching the next flight to Santa Fe to do a feature story on Solomon’s cult,” Zanders says with mock enthusiasm, before settling down to a more serious tone. “They’re just too big for the media to just ignore anymore, and you know how people love fucked-up shit like this. We’re going to be the first to get to them and bring notice to a national spotlight. This story is going to be HUGE!” “Well what do you want me to do?” Julie says, her feelings of anger toward Zanders dissipating, being replaced by excitement at the opportunity being laid at her feet. “What do I want you to do? Fucking everything you can!” Zanders exclaims, before taking another puff from his cigarette. “Talk to people from around the area and get their take on the whole situation. Find the family of these girls and talk to them. Use them to put an emotional spin on the whole thing. Talk to the cult members yourself, if you can. Hell, I wouldn’t even rule out trying to get an interview with Solomon himself. Anything you can do to make this story fucking gold. You know what the people want. You’re good for more than just a decent fuck.” “I…I…” Julie stutters, startled by the closing comment. Setting her jaw, she regains her composure. “You’re damn right, I am. You’re going to get the best damn story you’ve ever had, Michael.” This was it, her big chance to make a name for herself in the business. She was going to be more than just the girl Michael Zanders had his way with. She was going to be a bonafide professional journalist. This was her stepping stone. A quality story on this cult and the people affected by it would allow her to begin climbing her way to elite status in the industy. She could do it. Yeah! “Alright then. That’s what I like to hear,” Zanders says with a smile, tapping the ashes off the end of his cigarette into an ashtray. “Go talk to Cindy. She’ll give you the details and hook you up with the plane ticket. I believe there’s a flight that leaves tomorrow morning at nine.” “Well you can bet your ass that I’ll be on it. I won’t disappoint you, Michael.” Getting up excitedly, Julie grabs the folder and holds it tightly to her chest. Her bloodshot eyes are now shining brightly, and one would be hard-pressed to even recognize signs of her hangover at all. She is almost giddy as she nods goodbye to Zanders and heads out the door. Finally…a chance. It was all she could ever hope for. Maybe life didn’t suck that much after all... =============================== As the door shuts behind the departing Julie, Michael spins his chair around to look out the window, a smile spreading over his face. He lets out a chuckle to himself as he pulls out a cell phone out from his jacket, pressing a button to speed dial. Letting another puff from a hit off his cigarette, he waits as the phone rings three times before someone on the other side picks up. The voice is muffled and unintelligible, but Zanders’ comments are clear as day. “We are good to go. She’s picking up her plane ticket right now.” “The deal’s still on, correct?” “Well I just want to be sure there’ll be no funny business.” “Ok then, be sure to keep in touch with me on updates.” “Good luck.” “Of course you don’t need luck.” “Bye.” Clicking the phone off and sliding it back in his pocket, Zanders is beaming happily. He really was a genius, at times. Laying the bait was just too simple for a girl this easy to manipulate… That bitch would get what’s coming to her. He may have been forced to keep her on staff to keep her from ratting him out to his wife, but that was all going to end very shortly. He couldn’t have a threat like that lurking in the background, no matter what kind of working agreement he may have made with her. It was like keeping around a ticking bomb that’s just waiting to go off. His career was too important, and he was too close to getting power for some slut to fuck everything up with a media scandal. Things would be resolved very shortly. He really hoped Julie was excited about her big story, because if everything went according to plan, her career as a journalist will have just about come to its close…
