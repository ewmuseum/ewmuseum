The Andrea Norton Saga, Part 3: Burning Anger

29 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-29-03 Do not breath, girl. Just stop breathing. My commands fail. Subsequently, the sickening smell of gasoline floods my nostrils, invading my head before finally diving deep into my body, where it takes my stomach and uses it to play kickball. Burying her face into the crook of her elbow, the young girl tries to cough but can’t even muster enough oxygen for that. She immediately begins to panic, her eyes going wide, her legs shaking so frantically that she almost loses her balance. A large hand gently falls onto her shoulder, steadying her. And for once, she seems grateful for the contact. Her eyes still threatening to bulge out of her painted porcelain face, she desperately fiddles with some small object obscured from vision by her tiny, quivering hands. Finally, she succeeds. As she drags the head of a match across the rough sandpaper, a crackle fills the small room. It immediately gives way to a whoosh, as the head of the match erupts in flames, and then quickly sets about the task of trying to eat itself away. Paula lets go of the match. As she and her pillar of stability quickly make for the nearest exit, the match slowly, slowly, slowly circles towards the ground… It splashes into a puddle of grimy, brownish liquid, and flame – given new strength – instantly roar up from the ground. Reflections of yellow, orange, and red splash across the room’s white walls, as if a mad painter angrily abandoned gutted his paint buckets there. Devils, thin little wisps of ghastly smoke, rise towards the ceiling, hungrily seeking freedom and flesh. Now that I think more carefully, I should have waited. Obviously, doing this the very next day will be suspicious. But it does not matter. She claimed a victory over me… a small success, but intolerable nonetheless. Nothing comes free, and she will be made to learn this lesson. **** **** Andrea Norton, looking as ragged as ever, pushes stringy, dirty, disheveled blonde hair away from her face, and tries to force all of her concentration on the road in front of her. ‘Cars, machines without faces, little protected areas where humans could further isolate themselves even when they are supposed to be out in public…’ Her mind finally wanders back into the here and now, just in time for her to swerve away from the curb, and back into the center of her own lane. Somebody behind and to her left honks, but the noise seems strangely muted and disconnected. The owner of the horn comes to her left, glares over at her, and gives the middle-aged mother a very unflattering gesture. It doesn’t seem important. Some nagging suspicion keeps nibbling at the back of her mind, taking a little piece of security here, stripping away just a hint of sanctuary there. Her weary mind fails to snag the offender, however. Despite her best efforts, the cancer eludes her, only to bite away elsewhere. “This whole damn situation is going to be the death of me,” she muses aloud, her normally sunny voice sounding rough and uneven as it bounces around the cab of her car, only to assault its own master. Sighing, Andrea takes a moment to check herself out in the rearview mirror. “Good God girl,” she whispers, “you look like shit. I guess spending a few weeks locked up in a mental institution will do that to you.” Eventually, her gaze drops down to her passenger seat, where a thin stack of papers peacefully rests. Amongst the stack floats the court order that will subject her to the presence of a social worker thrice weekly. “Damn Jonathon for doing this to me,” she hisses, “and damn that little girl, too.” She finally turns down a side street, and immediately sees emergency vehicles, as well as a gathering throng slack-jawed rubber-neckers, congregated on some house down towards the end of the avenue. And for the first time, she notices the column of smoke slowly rising up into the sky. “Oh my God,” she breathes. “Somebody’s house is on fire…” As she gets closer, her horror escalates. A lump of guts and acid leaps up from her abdomen, taking control of her throat, as she realizes just whose house is ablaze. She slams the pedal down, the bottom of her foot practically punching a whole through it, and he car reacts, lurching forward awkwardly. As her house comes roaring closer, the woman leans forward over her steering wheel. Two houses down from her own, she finally slams on the breaks. Before the vehicle even comes to a complete stop, Andrea throws the gear into ‘park’ and leaps from the car. Without pausing to gather her keys, or at the very least shut her door, Norton charges towards her home. A barrier of police tape tries to impede her progress, but she simply ducks under it. Before taking two steps onto her carefully manicured lawn, a police officer and a fire fighter swarm around here. “This is my house!” she shrieks, and they quickly unhand her. “This is your house, ma’am?” the police officer asks before sharing a quick glance with the firefighter. “I just said that!” Andrea screams as tears start welling at the corners of her eyes. The police officer slowly reaches forward, gently placing a hand on her forearm. Thinking that he means to lead her somewhere, Andrea takes a step forward – and almost falls flat on her face as the cop spins her around. He firmly bends her arm back, and then brings her other arm back. As an ominous ratcheting sound flows over her shoulder, joining with the smoldering of her own house to produce a twisted sound that chills her to the bone, Andrea feels cold metal slowly constrict around her wrists like a cruel, robotic snake. “What’s going on?” she demands, trying to look at the officer over her shoulder. “You have the right to remain silent,” he begins, obviously reciting the mantra from memory. “Anything you say will be used against you in the court of law…” **** **** The dented Honda Accord cruises down the Seattle highways, a grim-faced Ariath tensely operating the steering. To his right, strewn across the center console, two black masks with two pair of matching gloves – one pair much smaller than its companions – slowly bend, fall, and wrap around to form a tight ball of ebony cotton. Beyond the evidence, Paula sits in the passenger seat, her dirt-smudged face pressed against the window. Lost in thought, the young girl remembers. The key was right where Jonathon said it would be, hidden in the bushes next to the front door. The gate to the back door was unlocked, just as Ariath reported. The backdoor fell quickly before the key, granting us unfettered access to Andrea’s home. Time was ticking away quickly, as the large clock in the dining room graciously reminded us. We hustled into the living room, and I turned towards my underling. “Ariath,” I said, my tone already forming a question, “what in this room is most valuable?” Like the fool that he is, he just stared at me for a moment. “What is the most expensive?” I asked impatiently. “Also, what does she value most?” He started pointing: a painting on the wall, a picture of some unidentifiable beach blown up to poster size, and then – the jackpot – a massive amount of trophies and certificates with Christopher’s name plastered all over them. “Okay, pet,” I said. “I do not have time to repeat myself, so listen carefully. Take these things, as well as this coffee table, and a few items from Christopher’s room, and take them to the shed in the back yard. Understand?” Nodding, he hurried away. Somehow, he managed to accomplish his task perfectly. “Everybody knows,” Paula whispers as she slowly pushes herself away from the window, “that when somebody burns their own house down, they almost always safe those things they find valuable.” Smiling, she turns towards Ariath. The man quickly returns her look before refocusing his attention on the road. Both the mistress and the puppet smile cruelly. **** **** The police officer that had, earlier in the day, arrested Andrea Norton, slowly exits his cruiser. After he slowly disentangles himself from the seatbelt and rises to his full height, the officer rudely slams the door shut. He reaches up and tears sunglasses off his face, revealing striking blue eyes the shade of melting ice. The man slowly rubs his hand down his square jaw, finally striking at his well-trimmed goatee. Giving himself a once over, he finds his uniform to be in near-perfect order. He readjusts the gun on his hip ever so slightly, and then tilts his badge, labeled ‘Wells,’ a degree to the left. Finally, he is satisfied with his appearance. Looking around the ramshackle parking lot warily, Wells makes his way towards the less-than-reputable-looking diner. The man walks stiffly, like a soldier, but his strides are long, and he reaches the restaurant in short order. Wells pulls open the door, and stalks into the eatery. His eyes scan the patrons, finally falling on a lonely man in the back left corner of the room. Sauntering over to the man’s table, he quickly sits across from him without invitation. “Michael Walker,” Wells says, no hint of friendliness in his voice. “Off’cer Wells,” the dirty looking man replies as he slowly lets go of a half-butter English Muffin. Leaning back in his old, creaky chair, Michael appraises the cop with a humored look. “Ain’t ya on the wrong side o’ da road, crimedog?” Wells leans forward, his eyes narrowing menacingly. “I’m not here to listen to your asinine chit-chat. I have a suspect in an arson case who says that she was with you at the time.” Walker feigns stupidity for a moment, his large nose crinkling and his blue eyes adopting a mock-thoughtful tint, and then proceeds to make a big show of suddenly remembering. “Oh right, Paula. She’s uh nice gurl,” he says as he waves his arms around. Grunting, Wells looks away, obviously assimilating this statement. Finally, his cold, hard gaze returns to Michael. “I don’t trust you, you God damn moron criminal,” he concludes. Putting a grubby hand to his chest, as though wounded, Walker calmly replies: “Dere’s udder witnesses, Off’cer. And sum’dem even have uh clean record.” Looking at the man as though he wanted to spit on him, Wells quickly rises from the table. As his chair scoots backwards, a harsh screeching sound fills the diner, but the officer seems to not hear it. “Fine,” he says through gritted teeth. “And I’m keeping my eye on you, Walker.” He crisply turns on his heel and marches out. A moment later, Walker slowly rises from his table. Reaching into the pocket of his ratty jeans, he produces a few crumpled dollar bills, which he drops onto the table. He slowly slumps out of the diner. Once he gets outside, he immediately hooks to the right, and takes another quick turn into an alley. A few steps into the alley, his progress is rudely cut short as an envelope drops at his feet. “I trust you were a good little boy?” a harsh, hissing voice attacks him from the shadows. He slowly stoops and collects the envelope. Bouncing it up and down in his hand, carefully weighing his contents, he shoots an amused look at his benefactor. “And I trust all uh da money’s here?” Paula Michelle Smith steps forward from the shadows, her fangs showing. “Do you dare insult me?” Holding up his free hand and taking a step back, Walker quickly makes amends. “Of course not. But ya know, if Johnny and I did na go way back, I might have charged ya double.” “Whatever,” Paula hisses. “Now get on your way.” Nodding, Michael stuffs the envelope inside his plaid denim over shirt, and then continues on down the alley. Hatred and loathing in her eyes, the young girl watches him leave. As he gets further and further away, her posture relaxes. “And may I never see your ugly face again,” she whispers.
