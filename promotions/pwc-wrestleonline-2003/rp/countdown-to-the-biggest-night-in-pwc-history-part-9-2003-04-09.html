Countdown to The Biggest Night in PWC History (Part 9)

9 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/09/03 It’s a support the troops rally in down town New Jersey as a crowd of supporters fill the streets with signs and pictures of loved one’s serving there country. It’s a brisk day forcing most of the supporters to cling closely to their coats. A week ago it was seventy degrees now its more like forty with a icy wind blowing hard , almost like God is punishing the men and women for their lock of animosity towards the war. Standing in the back wearing a long black trench coat is Hoyt Williams. Nothing h upsets him more then those hippie humps who disrespect our fine Christian president and his efforts to eradicate the Jesus hating war monger. Sudamn could possibly be the devil himself reborn to rise again. Thank God for strong willed men like our President thinks Hoyt. An elderly man dressed in his VFW show suit catches Hoyt’s eye. His right hand clothed with a white glove holds an American flag firm and proud. In his left hand that same style white glove grips a wooden cane that aids the man in his proud stance. His face is worn from years of service to his government with a few wrinkles produced from a few years he lived in a bottle. His veteran’s hat shows pins that indicate his involvement in the Korean war and the Vietnam conflict. A conflict that put a little shrapnel in his leg and thirty years later still causes him to wake with a cold sweet every night. His tale will never be told and his service to his country will end with a flag covered casket and a few kind words from friends and his VFW chums. But today he stands proud not just for his service but for those brave men and women who aren’t coming home and those who do come home but never leave as imagines replay in their mind night after night like a bad dream that was a reality. The old mans shaggy white beard blows in the cold wind that doesn’t phase him. Once a life filled with women, whiskey, and wonder he’s left with three ex-wives who never understood his only love was his country, diabetes brought on from years of hard drinking trying to erase portraits of pain forced on him by patriotism, and understanding of his fate. Hoyt’s thoughts shift from the man, the flag, and the rally to selfish indulgence. His match with Jake Keeton is this Saturday night and is the lone match on the card. God has given him the guidance straight to the main event in less then three months. Ok so he’s not head lining the Pay Per View but in Hoyt’s egotistical megalomaniac mind this is the next best thing. The whole world tuning in to view Hoyt beating the hell out of Keeton in front of all the Hoyt Witnesses. Nothing is better then that. Hoyt is the offspring of a demon who’s been adopted by God himself. Hoyt feels he should be on the Pay Per View to at the very least spread the word of the lord to the heathen fans. I mean who is better then the only wrestler who could turn water into wine just for the hell of it. Hoyt shifts his eyes back to the old flag-bearing veteran as he begins to feel selfish bitching about not being on the Pay Per View when our men and women are fighting a war. But since Hoyt is God’s adopted son he’s allowed to be selfish every now and then. Hoyt leans up against a wall to watch the service which include a prayer from him.
