Timeline - 05/03/03 : Kiss The Rain

15 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/05/03 Karina Wolfenden is sodden, for hours she has been walking through the stormy night, her slender frame pelted with the relentless downfall. The pink flash that makes up her fringe has lost it's brightness, now darkened and matted down across her furrowed brow... The rest of her usually shiny mane has suffered the same fate, the precipitation having knocked the last bit of volume from it so that it looks as limp and stringy as the wire connecting her earphones to the Discman in her pocket. The CD finished playing hours ago, not that she's noticed, for Karina's ears went to sleep when she should of... Hours ago. Weighted down by a waterlogged black hooded sweatshirt, Karina just walks... Aimlessly. She's probably walked around this corner, across this street more than once tonight, but as long as she's walking she isn't sitting at home... Thinking... Regretting. She doesn't look alive... Deathly pale and showing no emotion, no reaction to her surroundings. Karina might as well not be there at all, although that would sound a pretty good deal to the dejected Wolfenden right now. In her switched off state, Karina hasn't even noticed that her suede sneakers are drenched, squelching with every step... But still, she doesn't even side-step to avoid the inch deep puddles that have formed on the un-even sidewalk. At 3-30am nobody is there to bother her, there's none of the hustle and bustle that their was when she set off from her apartment. Hundreds of people who'd now be at home, deep in slumber beside the one they love, after having kissed their children goodnight... Happy families. What would Karina do when she awakened from this zombie-like state and went home? Probably just drink. There'd be no husband, no children... No family whatsoever. In a city of thousands she was solitary. She'd never needed others, never relied on anyone else before... So why did she feel so sedated? Why were her senses barely registering? Why did her food have no taste, the humid air no scent? The sparse traffic no sound? Why did she feel like this? It seemed Karina had underestimated what having 'Wolfenden' after her Christian name meant. It was more than just a surname, it meant that she had a family, it meant every person in her life who shared that name would be there for her... But she threw it all away for wrestling... And at this moment in time, if she could bare to think about it, she'd regret it. Her stride is finally broken as she stops on the curb, her unmistakable green eyes lazily scanning left to right from under her matted down fringe. But the sights they see do not make it to Karina's brain, the only daughter of Lawrence Wolfenden just stepping out into the road... Where a pair of headlights illuminate her sodden body. The tyres screech as the car brakes, wet road reducing the friction, thus the stopping distance. Through the windshield, the driver's face says it all as his body is thrust back into the driver's seat, his vehicle slowing down around him... He thinks he's going to hit her. His eyes close, preparing for the worst... Scarily, he seems to care more than Karina. Alive by 6 inches. Maybe if 20 minutes more of the torrential, monsoon-like downpour had fallen before she crossed the street, that would have been the last we'd have ever seen of Karina Wolfenden. The drama is lost on her though, her stride not even broken as she makes it to the opposite sidewalk. 'Lunatic!' yells the driver, furious that his brief moment of intense and concentrated stress wasn't even met with the slightest reaction by the woman he thought he had killed. His clenched fist hammers the horn, the intrusive sound looking like it might wake Karina from her internal solitude... But it fails. Worry and care wasted, he hits the accelerator and speeds off, taking the memory of 'The nut he nearly ran over' with him. The handful of people who have business at this early hour just stand and stare, forming their own opinions of the woman encased in the wet hoody and cargo pants. 'Probably a Junkie.' they might ponder, or just take her as a homeless runaway sleeping in a doorway. Funny when you consider that a few days earlier, the woman they're passing off as a nut-job was co-heir to a fortune they won't make in their lifetime. Looks can be deceiving, feelings even more so. Karina Wolfenden is certainly one of those you can't judge by appearances... In fact, you can't judge her at all, you'd only drive yourself mad.
