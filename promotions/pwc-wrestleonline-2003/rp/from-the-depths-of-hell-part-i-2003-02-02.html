From the Depths of Hell...(Part I)

2 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/02/03 Jason O’Neil read the sign on the outside of the joint. “The Blue Note” the blue neon sign read. Slowly, he walked to the large wooden door, and grabbed the brass handle, opening the door. His senses were flooded with the smell of smoke, the darkness of the room, and the sound of sweet jazz coming from the inside of the club. The slow “tse-tse-tsetse” of the snare drum and occasional ching of the cymbal belied the slow pluck of a stand up bass. The twinkling of the ivory keys of the piano, complimented the slow wail of the trumpet, which was perfectly in time with the guitar. He walked into the dark, smoky room, getting looks from most of the people inside. Even at 12 AM, the club was pretty full. Jason stood, looking for a seat. A barmaid walked up to him, and smiled a big smile. She was short, and black, with a large round body, and laugh lines around her eyes. She seemed to glow with happiness, as she smiled at the white man who had entered the club. “Hey, mon. Are ya lost?” She said to him. “No, I’m looking for a seat.” Jason replied with a smile. She looked at him obviously surprised that this young white man was here to listen to some Jazz. She returned the smile, and took his arm, leading him to the back of the club past a few stress damaged tables to the more fashionable back section, where the booths were round, the tables shiny, and the lights low. She motioned him to take a seat at the large booth, where an older black man with sunglasses on sat dressed to the nines in a black suit and tie. Jason sat as he was told. “Who’s this?” the man said in a low, raspy, smoke filled voice, looking in the general area of Jason. Jason extended his hand to the man, who didn’t move. He looked puzzled. “Maurice, this is…?” the waitress replied, looking at Jason for his name. “Jason.” “Aye. This is Jason. He’s going t’be sharin’ your booth with you t’day.” The man extended his hand to Jason coming a bit short. Jason moved his hand into his, and smiled at him. “It’ll be my pleasure. What are you drinking?” “25 year old scotch. A double.” Jason swallowed hard at that. That could easily cost him over 100 dollars just for two shots of scotch. That would be a lot of money to spend on a veritable stranger. “You old liar! He’s drinking Jim Beam. Would you like to buy him one?” The old man laughed and pinched. That was a relief. 100 dollars for a shot would kill what money he had put aside for the week. Jason nodded, and asked for a beer; whatever’s local and good. The waitress smiled, and waddled away. Jason sat back, and listened to the music. Jazz had always relaxed him, and more than anything, he needed to relax. He’d had a tense night the night before, and wanted nothing more than to relax. He sat back, and closed his eyes, letting out his first relaxing sigh since he’d gotten to the Garden State. “Something troubling you, son?” the man across the table said. He looked over at Jason, with a semi-toothless grin on his older face. He was thin; rail thin to be exact. His face, wrinkled and worn, reminded him of his grandfather before he passed away. He smiled a warm, genuine smile, and seemed to look in the direction of O’Neil. “Nothing, really. Just new around here, having trouble trying to find my way around. Typical newcomer stuff.” O’Neil replied, hiding his true thoughts. “Son, let me tell you something,” the old man said, lifting a shaky ashen hand to his dark sunglasses. He lifted the glasses of his eyes, to show his cataract-ridden pupils, and almost shut irises. He smiled again. “Just because I can’t see, don’t mean I can’t see.” Jason laughed at the man, and smiled again. The song ended and O’Neil clapped along with the old man. The band excused themselves for a 10, and a few people clapped. He sighed again, and looked at the old man. “I don’t need to trouble you with my problems, Mister…?” “Call me Maurice. That’s what the fellas call me. And that’s what my friends call me.” “Ok…Maurice.” Jason smiled again. “Now, what’s troubling you son?” “Just really new to the area, I’m tired, I’m lost, I don’t really know anyone other than one or two of the guys I’m here with, and even them, I don’t really care much for.” “What’s wrong with these fellas?” “They’re shady characters. Not the type of guy a God-fearing man like myself should be associating with, especially when on the road.” “Good reason.” “I think so. I mean, the thing is, I left Ohio for the first time since my wife died a few years ago, and while I’ve been back…I still miss her.” “I know what you mean. I still miss my Maribelle. It’s gotta be…whew…15 years this April. Something like that. Anyways, this woman was my prefect mate. She had everything and did everything for me. She loved me, and not just in the way she showed it to me. She showed everybody just how she felt for me. That Maribelle…she was a special woman.” “Sorry to hear she’s passed, Maurice.” “Ahhh…It’s no bother really. She was sick for a long, long time. Right now, she’s in a better place. After she died I felt alone, but then, I found a few guys around who’d been through the same thing. We talk, play chess, and do all sorts of stuff together. But the most important part is that we’re there for each other. That’s what you need, son. How old of a man are you, youngster?” “I’ll be 29 next month.” “Look at you. You have the rest of your life in front of you. I’ve lived 86 years, and haven’t regretted a day, even when Maribelle died. And do you know why?” Jason looked into his eyes, although cloudy, were clearer than he’d looked into in a long time. “Why?” “Because I never let the Deville get the best of me.” “Wait…did you just say…Deville?” “Devil.” “It sounded like Deville.” “No, I said devil. At least, I think I did. 86 years is a lot to remember, youngster.” “Call me Jason.” “Ok, Jason.” Suddenly, it rang out like a child’s wail in the night. Like a lovers call among thousands of screaming voices. Suddenly, Jason picked his head up, and heard the voice of an angel. She started singing in a low, exotic tone, yet she was no where to be seen. Her voice had the depth of Moreno, the Range of Carey, and the soulful bellow of Fitzgerald. A leg kicked out from behind the red curtain that lined the back of the stage. It’s foot, dressed in a delicious red heel, accentuated the calf muscle, as the pointed toes reached out and pointed far, far away. The knee flexed, while the voice continued to sing. Captivated, Jason stared at the stage, while slowly in his mind; the band began to play just for him. An arm poked out this time, moving up and down, slowly flailing, and pointing. The cherry red nail polish on the fingers of the hand contrasted beautifully with the pale tone of her creamy, white, naked flesh. The curtain flew open, and he saw her silhouette. He swore up and down, that Aphrodite, the Greek god of Beauty must have been reborn in her. The shimmer of her sequined dress accentuated her supple curves. She pulled her hands down her taut body, teasing the audience who cat called her. She thrust her hips sideways, shaking her moneymaker slowly, while slow, deep sinful wails escaped her lips. She sang about love lost, that was sure, and yet her voice…it sounded hardened. It sounded like maybe she had lost someone that she loved. The lights turned on, and she turned around, immediately making accidental eye contact with the man watching her more than anyone else. His eyes, transfixed upon her shimmering pools of radiance. She was young, definitely younger than he was. Their eyes spoke volumes to each other. She faltered, just a little through the song, after making such a cosmic connection. She tore her eyes away, albeit briefly. She walked the stage, putting on a show for most of the regulars, sitting up from, cat calling to her. “Hey, buddy? Jason? You listening?” A voice came through the magic. “Huh?” Jason replied, still not taking his eyes off the goddess on stage. “Oh. Yeah.” “You lying son of a…” Maurice laughed. He raised his glass in the air. “To love” “Yeah. Love.” O’Neil toasted and took a long drink. It was more than a toast to love. It was a toast to life. It was a toast to death. A toast that embraced his two extremes in one night. Utter and bitter hatred for Angelo Deville, and now, he’d given his soul to a woman whom he may never speak to. Whoever lost his heart in San Francisco, had never been to Jersey.
