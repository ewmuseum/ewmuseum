To America's Lovable Losers: The Chicago Cubs.

6 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/03 (In memory of Steve Goodman) When he was a boy they were his pride and joy but now they only bring him fatigue. If you want to trace Hoyt’s bitterness towards losing you don’t have to look much farther then his hometown baseball team the Chicago Cubs. Year after year….after year…after year….after year….after year….after year….they have done nothing but broken young men’s dreams. Like a Sammy Sosa dropped routine fly ball they have left nothing but a bitter taste in the mouths of wind bitten dreamers on Waveland Avenue. Hoyt is finishing up his nightly missionary work as he walks down the boardwalk in Atlantic City. He is wearing a black suit with a thin black tie reminiscent of the Blues Brother’s threads. The only thing besides his massive size that makes him stand out is the blue and red Cubs hat that sits atop his crew cut hairdo. “Cubs win…Cubs win…Cubs win…Cubs win!!!!” Screams a passer bye in his best Harry Carry 4 cases down voice. Hoyt gives the stranger a warm smile and a thumbs up as the man in his sixty’s walks bye with out saying anything else. Hoyt then wonders if the man was mocking his beloved Cubs or giving him a cheer of support. Hoyt’s father, Duke, is a die hard Sox fan and although their success record isn’t much better then the Cubs that never stopped Duke from trashing them. Not only does Hoyt despise his father’s sinful ways, or the fact that he is standing in his shadow, but also the fact the he was so cruel to his favorite team. “He will burn in hell!!” Hoyt mutters under his breath. Hoyt’s made a promise to himself never to fall down to his father’s level. One cold windy night laying under the Addison redline EL stop out side of Wrigley field, while waiting for the 151 bus, Hoyt also promised never to accept losing like the Cubs have. This Saturday he has an opportunity to put him in a position to win a title. The last time the Cubs won a National League pennant, was the year we dropped the bomb on Japan. “What a joke.” Hoyt says out loud, speaking only to himself as he continues his stroll back to his apartment. Dusty Baker is a good start, but that’s all it, is a start. Hoyt’s not going to let himself get into a position of being buried and having to start over. He’s going to do it right the first time and that’s the only way. The game plan is in motion of saving souls and punishing sinners on a stage that reaches thousands. What a treat it would be to be TV champion. With God in his corner and the Karma of cheering on the Cubs for years Hoyt has all the supernatural elements covered. What Would Hoyt Do??? Well he’d win the match and thank himself and Jesus. That’s the Williams way and Saturday he’ll show the world that. =w=
