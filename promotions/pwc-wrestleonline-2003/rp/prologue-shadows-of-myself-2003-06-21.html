Prologue: Shadows of Myself

21 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 21, 2003 December 26, 1996 The smoke filled room did nothing to hide the scum of the Earth that sat before me. They were all recent graduates from Praylor University, a small private college outside of Manchester. Nothing more than a conglomeration of wasted talent that couldn’t find their ways out of a paper bag if they wanted to. But that was life. In every walk of reality you find yourself with the haves and have nots. We were part of that elite group that somehow straddled the middle, and I fucking hated it. I stared at my friend, Robbie Hemming, who was sprawled on the floor of my apartment like a murder victim. In his hand was the culprit – a large bottle of champagne that was probably spiked with a harder liquor. Oh, he was quite fond of the drink. It was one of the reasons he woke up in the morning, and the reason he almost didn’t graduate. Failing three of your four classes in your senior year can do that. We’re losers. We’re all just a bunch of jokes that will never make it in the real world. At least I realized the fate that awaited us at the end of that dark tunnel ahead of us. They were all too drunk to come to that awful realization. They had degrees now, but those were only good for hanging up and looking at. They weren’t going to use them. Another body fell to the floor from the sofa I was sitting at. I looked over to make sure there was no vomit coming out from his mouth and once I was sure the man was going to live, I got up from my seat and headed to the kitchen. The light was dim and beginning to short out. I would need to change it soon. Creak. Creak. Creak. Yeah, and the fucking floor needed to be fixed. It came with the territory, since I lived in rat infested, poor excuse for a flat. It was all I could afford, and I refused to take mum’s money. I was a man, after all, and a man does as he pleases, as long as he’s standing on his own two feet. I chugged a carton of milk, somehow forgetting the sour aftertaste that it leaked down my throat. It expired a week ago, yet you’re still drinking it. You sure as hell don’t look like you used to go to Oxford. “Pssh, like I need to spend the forty grand on a travesty of a school like that,” I murmured under my breath, taking another swig of tainted milk. I got into the school with the fact that my mother, one Miss Eleanor Hall, was a very prominent diplomat for the British government. International liaison to the United States, I believe her title was. Well, before she turned a complete one-eighty and fell into a drug laden depression. That was my family for you, alright. Esteemed, proud, and then ready to chuck it back into the shitter when the opportunity arose. I turned back to my now very drunk friends, who were all passed out. The sad part of all of this was that I was one of them. I’m the one that left Oxford, the hot head that couldn’t argue calmly to any one professor in any department, and decided to head off to find my own fortune elsewhere. You took a step down. Hell, a BIG step down. You just ruined your life. “Debatable,” I whispered, remembering all of the wonderful memories I had from Praylor. The parties, the endless nights we spent conversing about absolutely nothing, and the women – oh god, the beautiful, gorgeous women. A smile came over my face as I remembered each one of their beautiful faces. Not one escaped the vacuum which was my subconscious, but then I came to the horrible realization that… …that it was over. What was my life now? One night of drunk paradise, a hangover the next morning, and then sobering up the remainder of the week in order to do it again the next weekend? That’s not a life. Not for long, at least. I slumped down into a chair in the kitchen, watching the flies buzz around me overhead. They smelled something they liked. Has everything gotten that shitty all of a sudden? A card sat on the table next to a dinner plate that hadn’t been cleared. I set aside the plate and took the card in my hands, chuckling at the picture of a very fat Santa Claus trying to fit his gargantuan ass down a chimney. Bloody Americans. I opened the card, only to see a note from my father. He wished me a good holiday and hoped I was doing well. Hope you take me up on my offer and visit me over here in the States. I love you, Andrew. – Dad Kind words, but I wasn’t Anthony anymore. “Arson Zanders,” I said out loud, as I stood up from the table and made my way out of the apartment. Leave the past behind and look to the future. It’s the only way.
