Confessions of a German: Part 1 - Authority Figures

25 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25th May 2003 This peice was orginally written in German. It has been checked and translated by experts into English, for the benefit of a predominatly English-speaking audience. I think it's slightly humourous when you're supposedly forced into doing something. Most of the time you were going to do it anyway, but when the other party beleives that they are getting personal pleasure from making you do a particular task...you just have to look at them and think 'heh, you're an idiot' For example, your parents. Many children are forced into ordinary, daily chores that take up time that they'd rather be doing playing in the street with their pals. When I was young, my brothers, sister and I were often handed daily chores and while the others complained about it until they were blue in the face...I just got on with it. I didn't particularly enjoy it nor did I want to do it...but why complain? You'll only delay the inevitable. My father often had personal satisfaction from sending his children to take out certain jobs. These weren't illegal tasks like sewing clothes for seven straight hours, but nonetheless they were tedious and lasted a lifetime when your were 12 years old. They were your usual run-of-the-mill mowing the lawn, tidying the cellar/attic etc, etc. His favourite part was actually telling us what we had to do, the squinting eyes and a slight smirk gave me enough to beleive that he was loving this. He was a busy man himself, but he always made time to make sure that his three children were notsat on their behinds through the holidays and getting in the way of our homemaker Mother. That's where I got the work ethic and energy that I thrive upon today. Another example is the school teacher. Even though you love your parents, you sometimes hate them for making your life a misery with those 'pointless jobs that they could themselves'. Course, you never said anything...you didn't want to fan the flames. The teacher was different. They loved to be hated and they don't particulary care what you think of them. They wanna do their job and get paid...some familiar? Again, I didn't enjoy school and I didn't exactly enjoy all the Monday morning tests that were shoved in our faces upon entering the classroom nor did I greet the sentiments of working in the stuffy classroom while the sun shone brightly outside. But I never complained, I just got on with what I was supposed to do. I wasn't a brownnoser or a bookworm, I just didn't see the point in complaing and - as I said before - delaying the inevitable. Now when you leave your education behind, you'll take up employment - and this is where the fun begins. Every single boss of yours- or person in authority - lives on a permanent power trip. They run the company or they're given some authority and you can kiss their human exsistance goodbye. They feed on making other lives miserable for thier own gain. The line-manager makes his work force do over time in the factory, so that he gets all the credit from the suits on the board for the double amount of stock available. Bosses - and I'm using the layman's term here, I'm not no graduate from some big university in Boston, Mass - take personal pleasure, even more so than parents and teachers, in forcing his/her employees to undertake a task that they don't want to do to. It maybe in their contract, but the fact that authority figure makes a point out of the job at hand makes them an even bigger crevis than they were before. But when they force somebody to do something that isn't exactly a hardship to that someone - you have to take a step back, look the authority figure and say 'heh, you're an idiot' Not that I'm looking at somebody who is close to home or anything...
