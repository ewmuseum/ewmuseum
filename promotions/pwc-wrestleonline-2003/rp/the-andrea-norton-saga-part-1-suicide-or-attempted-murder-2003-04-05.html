The Andrea Norton Saga, Part 1: Suicide or attempted murder?

5 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-05-03 (This takes place after the events of “Twisted Justice 2,” which can be found in the UTD roleplay forum.) “Somehow,” Paula says, her voice weighed down with guilt, “I feel responsible.” Across the table, a plain-clothes detective stares at her as silence starts to dominate the room. After a few seconds, he leans forward to furiously scribble notes onto a small legal pad. When finished, he looks back up at the young girl. With a wave of his hands, and a broad, comforting smile, he says: “Could you just expand on that comment a little bit?” “Of course,” Paula answers quietly as she stares down at the dingy off-white tabletop. She starts to speak, but chokes up. Reaching a pale, shivering hand up, she wipes tears away from her puffy, swollen eyes. “I… I-I’m so sorry,” she says, about ready to cry. The detective puts down his pen, and gives her a sympathetic look. “No,” he says kindly, “take your time, dear.” As Paula slowly buries her face into her hands, and her shoulders start rising and falling in pitiful little jerks, the man slowly rises to his feet. “Do you need anything, Miss Smith? Water… donuts… a break?” Trying to be as strong as possible, Paula quickly gets control of herself. She slowly looks up, her mascara smeared with tears, and little beads of water hanging off the tips of her nose and chin. The young girl holds up a frail hand. “Thank you, sir, but I will be okay.” He sits back down. “I suppose,” Paula begins slowly, “that I feel responsible because after our conversation, she tried to kill herself.” “You can’t blame yourself,” he responds quickly, his voice soothing. “I can’t tell you too much, obviously, but you should know that she had this set up before you got there. It was just bad timing on your part.” “No!” Paula says sharply, the tears coming back in force. She closes her eyes tightly, and starts pulling at her unkempt, stringy hair. Trying to fight back the urge to cry, she manages to get out: “I knew she was unhappy… I could have been nicer… not so confrontational… I should have known…” As she stops talking and crumples into the table, only her soft sobs provide the room with any noise. The detective gives her another pitying look. “You can’t blame yourself,” he repeats, his voice barely more than a whisper. ____________________________ Later that day… Sitting in the center of a different table, many miles away, a tape recorder quietly hums to itself. “Okay, Andrea,” a wizened male voice begins hesitantly, “I know you don’t like me, and you don’t want to talk to me, but the courts appointed me as your psychiatrist. The sooner we make some progress, the sooner you get to go home. How does that sound?” Andrea, who looks more exhausted and disheveled than ever, slowly crosses her arms, and throws the man a dirty look. Unphased, he continues. “I’m very concerned with your new-found obsession with this young girl.” The old man leans forward. A mop of gray hair falls about his face and shoulders. He pushes his thick, wire-rimmed glasses down to the bridge of his large nose, so that he can look at his patient over them. His kind, glittering green eyes appraise her gently, but she only throws hostility back at him. “Obsession?” she screeches angrily. “The damn little bitch tried to kill me!” She stands up violently, her chair falling back onto the cold, concrete floor with a loud clatter. She gestures around her, pointing out the prison cell-like room. Andrea Norton then grabs the white hospital gown that barely covers her body, and tugs at it vehemently. “She tries to fucking kill me, but everyone thinks I tried to kill myself!” “Well,” the man says calmly, putting on his most diplomatic mask, “the ground up drugs were found in the food and drink you made for yourself.” “She put it there,” Andrea claims, her tone bitter. “Perhaps,” he says. “But how? You yourself told the police that she wasn’t out of your sight for more than a minute, tops.” “That’s long enough for her to try and poison me.” Sighing, the old man folds his hands on the table, and continues giving her a kind, even look. “But not enough time for her to get upstairs and crush the pills in the upstairs bathroom,” he says matter-of-factly. “Bullshit!” “What’s ‘bullshit’ about it, Andrea?” he asked, his voice warm. “The bottle of pills was found on the counter of your bathroom, upstairs – where, by your own admission, Paula never went. Next to the pills was a pill cutter, with residue of the drug still on it. I don’t know what else to say, except”- Cutting him off, Andrea bursts out. “You think I tried to kill myself? You think I’m some sort of crazy bitch, like her?” Finally, the man’s patience seems to have died. He reaches up and sternly pushes the glasses back into place, gathers all of his stuff, and rises from his chair. “The facts and evidence do not support your claims, Mrs. Norton,” he says harshly. “The young girl was questioned, and even given a polygraph. She had nothing to do with this. And the fact that you had documents on your computer stating how much you hated your life and wished it would end… Well…” Andrea lowers her head, and lets out a long sigh. “I didn’t try to kill myself,” she says quietly. Shaking his head, the man starts walking out of the room. “From what I can tell you are delusional. But if you won’t speak to me, I can’t help you.” Just as he is about to leave the room, Andrea rushes around the table and grabs him by the arm. “Where are you going?” she demands, her voice and face both full of panic. “I’m going into the courtroom,” he answers softly, “and telling the judge of your condition.” “No!” Andrea screams, and quickly breaks down into tears. “What about my baby? My darling Christopher?” The man tries to pull away from her. “I suggest you worry about yourself first.” Finally, he manages to shake her grasp. With a disgusted look, he brushes off the sleeve of his shirt, and leaves the room in a rush. In his wake, a devastated woman falls to her knees and starts crying uncontrollably. ____________________________ “Damnit, Ariath,” Paula hisses, “stop glaring at me.” The pair walks side-by-side out of a courtroom. In the background lawyers and the judge are all packing up their stuff, getting ready to leave. Both Paula and her sidekick are dressed up: she is in an expensive-looking black blouse, with a dark blue dress, and Ariath wears a gray business suit. They get to the door, and stop. Paula stares at it for a long second, and then turns to Ariath. “Well, open it already,” she commands. He just stares at her, his upper lip curling slightly, and his eyes narrowed hatefully. “I have had just about enough of you this week,” she whispers, obviously irate. “You do not seem to understand what just happened here. The custody hearing has been pushed back because your ex-wife is unfit to appear in court. Do you honestly think they will grant her sole custody after all of this?” She stops talking as a lawyer passes by. With a polite nod to the young girl, he simply walks right past, opens the door, and disappears. Only after the door has slowly creaked closed once again does Paula dare speak more. “You said you wanted to keep your son,” she whispers angrily. “The courts will not take him from you now, so you should be thanking me.” “Not like this,” Ariath says, his voice equally heated. “I didn’t want it like this.” He turns away from her, forcefully pulls the door open, and then stomps out of the courtroom. Paula glares at him as he goes, murder in her eyes.
