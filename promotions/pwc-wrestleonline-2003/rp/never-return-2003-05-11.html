Never Return

11 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th May 2003 The curtains rustled slightly in the moonlight, but it wasn’t that keeping Alexander Miyagawa awake this night. He lies there, the upper half of his naked body uncovered by his bed linen, as he looks to the woman lying next to him with a smile on his face. It’s another of those warm nights in California, but the woman doesn’t seem to mind, it’s rather surprising when generally she grew up in a climate where it hardly ever reached a freezing point at night, but she seems have placed that all behind her now. A slight gurgling from somewhere else in the room is heard, and Miyagawa slips out of bed, only a pair of EW24-7 boxer shorts keeping his modesty in check, he stands and runs a quick hand over his head, only remembering upon touch that there is no longer any hair there to greeting him, just an embezzlement of star tissue in certain places. A thought passes his mind concerning the fact that at least it’s no metal skullcap, and with that he seems released from that thought. He walks over towards the side of the room where a small cot is placed and in it, a young child, no more than six months old wriggles restless in the warm California heat. Alexander pulls the covers away from the baby and takes it up in his arms, holding it gently and making soothing noises to it, to try to calm it gently. “Shush Chris, time to sleep” says the retired wrestler to his young son. “I’ll tell you what, you go to sleep and in the morning, we’ll go to the zoo, how about that? You’ll like the zoo; I always did when I was a kid.” The young child, Christopher Miyagawa snuggles in his father’s muscular arms and seems to sooth itself in the father-son bonding. Alex places his son back down in the cot and the young boy soon drifts off to the land of nod; admittedly a lot quicker than most nights. Alexander walks quietly out of the room, going down the hallway into the bathroom. He turns the cold water faucet on and splashes his face with the water that runs out of the tap to try to cool himself down. “I have money, I have fame, and I have family. How could I be any happier.” He says to himself in the bathroom mirror. “That reminds me, I’d best go get some groceries in later… ‘Uncle’ Chris and family are coming around for dinner.” He walks back through to the bedroom and slips back under the bed sheets placing an arm around the naked body of his wife. “Talking to yourself in the bathroom mirror again, dear?” she says to him without turning around in a slightly thick Russian accent. “Da” he says, joking mocking the accent, like he usually does. She smiles and rolls over to face him, their faces a few inches away. “Having pleasant dreams, Alexander?” she asks him, smiling happily. Alex nods, also happily, “Only of you my sweet. After all, I have everything I could ever want from life. I no longer need to work, I am respected and what more, I have you and Christopher. Family is the most important thing in life. She looks confused for a second before responding at last, “But you don’t have family anymore, Alexander.” This in turn makes Alexander rather confused, “Huh?” is his only possible response to that previous statement; not his finest hour in the line of arguments with his wife. Suddenly he sees in her hand an ice pick; but it is all too late as she plunges it into his chest, over and over again. Blood spurts everywhere, over her naked body, over his naked body and all over the white silk bed sheets. She keeps plunging it into his chest, and he does nothing to stop her, because he will not hit his wife, no matter what. Suddenly she finally strikes gold and embeds it right in his heart. “Goodbye Alexander. Never return.” She says to her husband. Suddenly Alexander sits up and all in not what it was. The room is different; the cot is missing. He still wears the same clothes and the same bed sheets, but it is defiantly not the same room, for the decorating is all changed. He looks beside himself in the bed for his wife, but he is alone. Forever alone. So alone that he dreams that she showed mercy and simply murdered him instead of walking out with his child in her arms, to never return.
