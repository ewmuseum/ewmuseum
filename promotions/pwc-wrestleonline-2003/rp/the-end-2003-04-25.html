The End

25 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday The soft, rumbling hum of the Cadillac Deville’s v12 was the only outward noise to be heard within as it hurtled lithely down the highway, snaking between lanes to pass those with less confidence in their driving abilities. With the radio off and the windows rolled up, the car’s interior had the air of a bubble in a waterfall. Sitting behind the wheel with Kathryn at his side and Erica lounging across the back seat, Deville was content with the lack of noise. There was enough of it raging through his head. Trying to ignore the voice inside his head as much as his two companions, Deville drove dangerously, doing roughly twice the speed limit. His head still looked like a badly damaged grapefruit, but at least he was back in his comfortable Armani, and his hair seemed lazily happy with being reintroduced to gel. Troubled blue eyes scanned the road ahead and behind him frantically, knowing the speed was too much, the dangers approaching too fast, but not caring. Anything to get his mind off of what he must do. Maybe a cop will come along and stop me, and maybe I won’t have to– Stop it! What the hell am I thinking? The voice he had found in the hole had come back with a vengeance, masquerading as his own and poisoning his planning with doubt. It was a new phenomenon for Deville, having doubt, and he wasn’t taking to it well. There’s no way to avoid what must be done! It’s either them or me! What the hell is wrong with me?! Pushing the pedal down the last millimetre to the floor, the engine roared with newfound life, plunging the needle of the speedometer straight down. Kathryn cast a worried glance back at Erica, who shared her concern, then reached out and placed a comforting hand on Angelo’s forearm. He jerked back as if bitten, then frowned up into her worried eyes before he could stop himself. “Since we’re going to the show so early,” Kathryn began softly, treating Deville as delicately as priceless china. “Do we really need to try and kill ourselves on the way there?” Brushing unseen lint from her white blouse, she kept her other hand on Angelo’s arm, massaging slowly. It’d save me a lot of trouble, Deville laughed bitterly, before the other voice kicked in, remarkably like his own. Please don’t talk... Please. It’s hard enough as it is. He scowled at that thought, and wondered where the hell it came from, but he supposed it was true enough. As much as he wanted to talk to them, to hear them giggle and laugh and scold, he couldn’t bear to hear their voices. Couldn’t abide their friendly chiding. “Angelo? Are you alright?” Realising he had been staring at her the whole time, Deville swivelled his head back to the road just in time to avoid smashing into a pickup truck from behind. No, I’m not fucking alright! I just spent a week in jail, lost my shot at the gold, and must face down murder charges in a court of law! All thanks to the two people I should have been able to trust... The only people I thought I could trust! Instead of that, he shrugs and answers, “I’m just a little shaken from the hole.” The sympathetic smile and nod she gave him was almost enough to make him pull over and finish it right there. Why would they do it? Why? A week of dark solitude and meditation had not provided the answer, and he couldn’t imagine it coming now. There was no reason that he could fathom. But then, he had never pretended to understand women, regardless of how well he thought he manipulated them. He had resolved not to think about it, just to do what must be done, but he was finding that about as easy waltzing on the moon. “We’re sorry, Angie,” Erica’s voice wafting up from the back startled him, and for a moment he could only gape openly at the road, taking the apology for a confession. “I can’t imagine what that would’ve been like, and I’m sure Kathryn can’t either.” The other woman shook her head vigorously in agreement. Not a confession, then... Just false sympathy. “You can talk to us about it, you know...” Her voice was low and understanding, sheepish and hopeful. “You can trust us.” Again the temptation to pull over was overwhelming, and again he barely managed to stifle it. He must do this according to plan. Whenever he veered from his plans lately, shit always hit the fan. Not this time. This time it would go off without a hitch, and he’d be free again. Lost for how to respond without spitting, he merely glanced at her pleading face in the rearview mirror. She sighed resignedly. “Angelo,” her voice was more stern now, almost motherly, “you’re not helping yourself by keeping all of it locked away inside. Or us. We want to help you, and we will if you’ll only let us.” Help me to what? The electric chair? He remained silent, and both girls shifted uncomfortably. He could smell their shampoo, and their perfumes, and underneath it all the scent that was uniquely theirs. He had loved that smell, once... Now he thought it would make him throw up. Only a few more miles... Then it’ll all be over. The strange voice inside didn’t know if he could last that long, though, and the girls certainly weren’t making it any easier. They both looked and acted as innocent as lambs. Lambs in wolf-coats... They must have done it. They had to have. He leaned on the horn at two cars side-by-side up ahead, blocking the lane. No one else could have. But you don’t know that, the voice scolded, slipping up. It was lecturing now, and as a result Angelo could separate it from his conscious thought. What proof do you have? Can you really do this on a hunch? You shut your mouth, he bit back. What the hell are you trying to do, anyhow? You’re going to ruin all of this! You’re going to ruin ME! The voice, still his, only laughed scornfully. You’re ruining yourself. This needs cold consideration, not heated revenge. Horror-stricken, he realised the voice wasn’t going to let him do what must be done, even as he spotted his offramp. Switching lanes and slamming on the brakes, he continued to fight it, pushing himself toward the inevitable. “Angelo, relax!” Kathryn’s voice tearing him apart, tinged with fear for his safety as much as her own. But that was impossible. “You don’t have to–“ “Did anyone ever tell you two you talk too fucking much?” They both recoiled at the threat in his voice, raised against them in anger for the first time in months. He cringed, thinking of the double meaning for those words, and scolded himself to shut up before he gave away the fact that he knew. Sullenly, they both turned their attention to the lush evergreens whipping by to either side, Erica seemingly on the brink of tears. Let her fuckin’ cry, then! Stupid bitch! Did she weep for the tears I shed locked away from my life? From myself? Did she weep during her statement to the police? For all Deville cared, she could cry herself straight into Satan’s arms. Yet he still felt a compulsion to go back there and comfort her. Because you know she didn’t do it... Deep down, you know. He tried to stifle the voice again, but it slipped out of his grasp like a fish covered in grease. That’s why I’m here. Get. The. Fuck. Out. Of. My. Head. Amazingly, it relented. Now surrounded on both sides by tangled wilderness, Deville drove the pseudo-highway carefully, looking for the hidden path that must be coming up any moment. The trail that would lead him to his salvation. And your ultimate damnation. Deville laughed at the voice, mocking it. You think I give a shit about being damned by God? I gave that up a long time ago... Rose above it a long time ago. For a moment Deville rejoiced in thinking he knew more than the voice, that he could defeat it. It lasted only a moment. I’m not talking about him, stupid... I meant that you’d damn yourself. Cranking hard on the wheel as he almost whipped by his turnoff, Deville decided he’s find out about that when he found out, and not dwell on it a moment before. Better safe than sorry, he quoted to the voice, to which it sniffed indignantly. “Where are we going?” Erica demanded softly, turning in her seat to look back at the highway, her leather skirt riding up her thighs. “Shut up!” Deville snapped before he could stop himself, then gave her an abashed look. What the hell are you doing? “I’m sorry... I’m just tense. I have a cottage back here, and I need to get something from it.” Making a show of shrugging off telling her to shut up, Erica smiled coyly. She winked seductively. “Something I’ll like?” Her voice was a purr, and Angelo felt a stirring within him to turn around and forget about this. He squashed it ruthlessly. “You’ll see,” was all the comment he’d give, even as they continued pleading and insinuating it must be something for them. It was, but he didn’t want to tell them that. Let them stew, like they let me, was almost run over by, You don’t have to do this... Give them a chance. A ramshackle cottage came into view above a small rise, flanked on three sides by jungle-esque undergrowth and trees. Signs warning of “private property” and “trespassers will be tortured, raped, then shot” were all the defenses he had to keep people away. Well, all of the normal defenses. The ultimate one, the one he had come to use, would leave anyone who hadn’t heeded the others dead. He had had the cottage built for exactly that purpose. Slowing to a stop immediately before it, Deville cut the engine and left the keys in the ignition. You’re taking this too far! You’re making a big mistake! Sitting in the seat with his eyes closed, Deville tried to remember how he had gotten rid of the voice before, tried to duplicate it. It screamed hoarsely as he grabbed hold of it with the teeth of his mind, then everything went blessedly silent as he swallowed it again. A feeling of filth filled him, but also relief. Am I going to have to put up with this for the rest of my life? He knew all too well that this had never happened before, and wondered idly if he was going crazy. An irritating chiming sound sliced through the thought as Kathryn opened her door, followed immediately by Erica’s, and both women spilled out onto the hard-packed dirt covered with tiny plants. Wincing, Deville followed. Just about done, he told himself, then waited angrily for the voice to pipe up. It didn’t. It was gone again. Thankfully. “This is so exciting!” Bubbling, Erica bounded up to the door with Kathryn hot on her heels. They actually thought this was Angelo’s idea of something kinky. Oh well... Better for them if they do. “Hurry up, Angelo!” Dragging himself up to the door, he fished a single key from his pocket and inserted it into the lock. He smiled as he felt resistance – no nasty surprises to ruin things, then – meaning the door was still locked from the inside, then snapped it open and pushed the door in. Kathryn and Erica raced in before he could remove the key, and he followed them in, shaking his head amusedly in spite of himself. He made a point of leaving the door open as he entered. The cottage was a single room, sparsely furnished with a couple of dressers and a kingsize bed. There were no windows to be seen anywhere, and even the sink and cupboards somehow seemed wrong. The floor yielded less than even hardwood should, and despite the light flickering on at Deville’s touch, the atmosphere was dark and gloomy. Ignoring it, Kathryn and Erica scampered over to the bed and piled on, hugging and wrestling each other. They tusselled under Angelo’s lustful eye for several moments, then stopped, panting, and turned large, hungry eyes on him. They didn’t say anything, but both mouths curled up into delighted smiles. Angelo took a step forward, then stopped, fidgeting with his suit jacket. “Girls...” His voice was choked and quiet. “Please...” What the hell are you doing? This isn’t in the plan! Just do it! His mouth went on as if it had a mind of its own. “Please... Before we do this... I just have to know.” They both looked at him cockeyed. In fact, if he could’ve looked at himself, he probably would’ve bore the same expression. “Why did you do it?” His mind screamed at him that he was screwing everything up, but he forced himself to stand there before them, waiting for an answer. He needed an answer badly. Needed to know what went wrong. They shared a confused look, then looked up at him wryly. “Why did we do what?” Kathryn asked, at the same time that Erica cooed, assuredly, “Because we love you.” They patted the bed encouragingly, but Deville looked down at the floor and muttered to himself. “That’s what I thought,” he told them finally, resignedly. “Uh...” He felt around at his breast pocket for a second, seemingly lost, the made himself meet their gazes. “I left something in the car... Wait here while I go get it.” They looked on the verge of coming with him, but he winked secretively and they were suddenly keen on waiting there, entwining their arms and lips around each other. Deville stalked out of the cottage in a hurry, hardly able to breathe, and shut the door behind him. Sagging against it, he lectured himself about being cold... He knew he needed to be cold and ruthlessness, like he used to be. But at the same time, he knew that having to think about it and force himself wasn’t right. Why won’t it come naturally anymore? Shaking his head as if to shatter every thought in his mind, Deville turned to face the cottage and backed away from it slowly, pulling a black rectangular device from his pants pocket as he did. It had a tiny antenna, which he pulled up, and a small silver switch protruding from one of the wider sides. He pressed his fingers around the cold metal, and thanked everything he could think of that he would not hear their terrified screams. Only what they deserve, he lectured himself when he realised he was hesitating. And exactly what I built this place for. The cottage itself he had visited only twice, once upon its completion and another time when something similar to this had happened. The crew he had sent to clean it up said that the cage had worked exactly as it was supposed to. Deville grimaced. It wasn’t really a cottage at all, but a coffin, or an incinerator. The floor, ceiling, and walls were all reinforced with steel, and the door – the last defense spoken of earlier – locked automatically from the outside whenever it was closed. Once you were inside, unless you held the key hiding in Deville’s glove box, you were trapped. When he flicked the switch, and the fires erupted from the cupboards, there would be no escape. “You burned me,” Deville managed to choke out, his voice failing at being cold and emotionless. He flicked the switch. “Now I burn you.” There was no sign outwardly of what he had done, nothing to betray that the inside of the cabin was now a raging inferno with two women trapped helplessly inside. Deville stood there for a moment, staring blankly at the door as memories of the girls rushed through him, scalding him like boiling water and freezing his blood to ice. A wretched desire to go and save them almost overwhelmed his resolve, and he had to fight it with picturing them in the police station, telling his sworn enemies about what he had told only them. “This is the price of betrayal,” Deville whispered, sagging to his knees, dropping the remote control in the dust beside him. A picture came up in his head of Kathryn and Erica clawing futilely at the door, struggling with the handle as they were flayed alive, all the while screaming that they hadn’t done it. But he knew they had. They had to have done it. Repeating that to himself over and over again, he slumped forward so his head rested in the dirt, and he wept like a child.
