What Must Be Done

28 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday Getting back into the swing of things is easier than I thought. Leaning against a dark wooden beam wider than Angelo is broad, the thought skitters across his mind like a smooth stone cast across a calm pond. Crystal blue eyes scan the area unconsciously, monitoring his surroundings out of instinct and habit, his angular face an implacable mask divulging neither his thoughts or feelings. Dust kicked up from walking along the dirty floor mars the shine of his leather dress shoes, and even manages to dampen the pure white cuffs of his pants, but beyond that he appears as serene and immaculate as a man attending his own funeral. He isn't alone in the large abandoned building, though he stands directly in the centre. Above him the ceiling is domed, without a ceiling to hide the rotting rafters arching across its expanse like ribs. There is only the one column, steadfast in its resolve to keep everything erect, but the rest of the floor is by no means empty. Halfway between Deville and the wooden wall to his right, roughly twenty feet away, is a plywood crate large enough to hold a bear. Directly behind the box, and seeming to dwarf its importance, is a crane-ish apparatus with a tube-like cylinder hanging in place of the boom, directly above the top. On the crate's other side is a lever sticking out of the floor itself and seeming to serve no particular purpose. Standing beside it with a rusty yellow remote control clutched in both hands is Johnny, Deville's long-time friend and the reason for his return to the Family. Johnny turns towards him and there eyes meet, and it takes considerable effort for Angelo not to scowl with regret. Well... Deville second-guesses himself, concentrating on keeping his face composed. Some things are easy, at least. With a conscious effort to keep his hand from shaking, he produces a cigar from his breast pocket and plops it between gritted teeth. 'Think it's time yet?' Johnny calls like an impatient child, and not for the first time. Angelo consumes himself with flicking a match against the wood and then lighting the cancer-stick, relaxing only when a billowing plume of smoke envelopes his head entirely. He puffs furiously, desperate to escape and float away like the acrid tendrils around him. Damn that Salvatore! What kind of a loyalty test is this?! It's fucking ridiculous! 'Let's relax just a little while longer,' Deville whispers instead, angrily fluttering the flame from the match and tossing it to the grimy ground. He walks slowly towards Johnny, eyes downcast, not needing to see the disappointment in his face to know it's there. 'Just give me a few more minutes,' he amends absent-mindedly, and the briefly confused look from Johnny is lost on him. Maybe we can do it another way, he clutches anxiously, hoping, perhaps I can... But he knows there isn't, and he knows he can't. This isn't a simple test of loyalty, and Deville grudgingly concedes that it was never given as a reason at all. There are other compelling reasons... Unarguable reasons why it must happen. As if that isn't bad enough, he knows he can't even assign any of the blame on the boss. He brought it on himself... He cringes, no longer thinking of Salvatore. He knew – he knows – the consequences. Even with that thought, as he comes up beside Johnny and raises his head, his eyes stay locked firmly on the crate. 'Think he's fuckin' scared in there?' Johnny asks brightly, his disappointment of a moment before vanished without a trace. He kicks the crate for emphasis and a muffled whine seeps through the thin wood. 'Wouldn't you be?' Deville responds rhetorically, his voice as emotionless and one-dimensional as his gaze, and immediately regrets the question. How the hell can this be the first major assignment of my return? 'Of course, dumbass,' Johnny smirks, shaking his head. 'What kind of a goddamned question is that, anyhow?' 'Nevermind.' The word comes out as a quick mumble. Get ahold of yourself! You know it has to be done! But knowing doesn't alleviate the pain. Knowing doesn't destroy the history. 'Are you alright, Angie?' Johnny asks with a tinge of concern, scepticism only someone who knows him could harbour. Concern only a friend could own. Their relationship has never been good, and they've been at odds more than anything else, but an affinity grew. A strange friendship, perhaps, but a strong one nonetheless. 'You're acting like a flippin' stoolie.' He barks an uncomfortable laugh, and Angelo fixes him with a steady glare. A tugboat toots in its melancholy way somewhere off in the distance, and Deville flashes a wry grin as he remembers old wives tales about that moan signalling death. Perhaps more than wives tales afterall. 'I'm fine,' he grates, snatching at the remote control and missing as Johnny cradles it like a child. 'Let's get this over with.' The sigh is involuntary, but Johnny misses it in his excitement to be at the task once more. Working one of the small knobs with child-like glee, Johnny laughs triumphantly as a hum sounds from the crane and a grey sludge begins excreting from the tube into the crate. Anguished cries sound from within. Johnny allows a good deal of the goop to drain into the box, and the two men share the time in silence. Finally, hesitantly, Johnny turns the dial back and the stream of wet cement tapers off and dies, halting to a steady drip like thick oatmeal falling off a spoon. Exuberant in his revelry, Johnny carelessly discards the remote and gets up on tiptoe to fumble with the clasps holding the side of the crate facing them together. He grunts and strains only once, then holds a thick metal nail in his hands as the upper half of that wooden sheet falls outward, narrowly missing the pair. Staring out at them wild-eyed, covered in cement and looking like an evergreen in mid-winter, is a giant of a man with a black ski-mask engulfing his head. Cement surrounds him cleanly up to his waist, glistening with wet, but Deville knows that up to his mid-thigh the cement has already hardened, and there's no turning back for him now. The monster's hands are bound behind his back with handcuffs that Angelo and Johnny found on his person when they captured him, but he still writhes and struggles to escape his cage, as if there is any hope of his survival now. Johnny sneers. 'Look at this pathetic son of a bitch,' he whips the heavy metal nail into the crate, and it connects dully with the cement-covered head. The man groans but doesn't flinch, not wanting to close his eyes for a second, even though it means witnessing his doom unfold. 'Not such a big man now, are you?' Johnny spits with distaste, straight into the mask. 'Of course he's not,' Deville snickers half-heartedly. 'He's only tough when he's picking on those incapable of putting up a fight. He should've known better than to fuck with us.' Angelo shakes his head, glaring up at the big man. 'You hear that, asshole? You should've stayed in the bush leagues, instead of trying to come up where we play and take shit over, hoping your size -- your name -- would be enough to get you to the top. It takes a lot more than that, pal.' Johnny walks over to the lever and grabs it with one hand, grinning at the caged man all the while. 'What good does being 500 pounds do you now? All it's going to do is make you sink that much faster.' Muffled protests begin pouring forth from the giant, but the two men have no ears for such things. 'You should've come in respectfully,' Deville lectures him calmly, 'paid your dues. Realised where it is that you stand, what it is that's expected.' He gives Johnny a nod. You should've too, man. 'Now it's too late,' Deville says with unfeigned ruefulness as he reaches into his breast pocket. Too late for all of us. There is an anguished scream as Johnny reefs on the lever and the floor beneath the crate falls away. It disappears through the hole immediately and anti-climatically, and half a second later a huge splash is heard, the drops of its wake flying up through the hole and turning little spheres of dirt to mud as they land. Johnny bounds forth and peers into the gaping pit, down to the harsh death ripples that are the last sign of that man's existence. 'This is great!' He exclaims, bent almost double over the hole. 'It's so fuckin' good to be back doin' this, Angie! You and I, running this shit again! Overshadowing the undeserving before they even realise they are! This owns!' The words get more excited and feverish by the second, and with each rise in tempo Deville shudders a little harder. Just pretend it's already done... He sealed his own fate. 'Dumb son of a bitch should've known better, eh Angie?' Johnny continues beaming, finally standing up and beginning to turn around. 'He shouldn't have fucked with your plan–' the words die as the men come face to face and a look of absolute betrayal grips Johnny's entire being. His mouth hangs open in confused protest as Deville raises the pistol up between his eyes and and holds his gaze, trying to impress the pain what he would do must cause him. What I have to do. 'Neither should you,' Deville grinds coldly, putting the exclamation on his words with a gunshot that echoes the mourning hatred of the action, even now as familiar to him as walking down the road or picking up a newspaper. Johnny jerks back from the blow and collapses into the hole, dead before he hits the water, and Angelo lowers the gun with a shaking hand. He didn't want to do it, but Johnny left him no other choice. No other choice! He shouts, futilely trying to drown out the small voice inside that knows otherwise. He liked Johnny, enjoyed his company, but the man had strayed from the path... Turned his back on the family. It had to be done. ... It has to be done. And Deville, his friend, has the burden of doing it.
