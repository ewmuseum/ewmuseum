Hidden Motives

16 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday 'It's a bullshit rule,' Deville grates, glaring at Johnny from the corner of his eye as they march down a wide hallway with gilded lanterns lining the walls. Dozens of half-shadows dance around them in the eerie light, and there is choking silence all around them, as if something in the air is actually devouring even the sound of their footfalls on the plush red carpeting. In whispers men refer to this passage as the Tunnel of Doom, and the many men who have died after walking its ghoulish length would testify to the accuracy of such a moniker. Johnny barks a laugh, the same laugh he's given the other dozen times Deville muttered the same complaint, but here it sounds hollow. 'I still can't believe it happened myself, Angie, but shit man, you know the rules and you lost, fair and square.' Flickering flames all around give his smile a jack-o-lantern quality, all deep shadows and harsh lights. Another bout of laughter suddenly doubles him over, forcing him to hobble along with his hands clutched at his gut, bent like some fiendish sidekick. 'After clearing the table, too!' he howls, then tries to straighten and fails, 'how the hell do you lose after clearing the table?' Angelo doesn't share his delight at the memory of losing their pool game, and sneers loudly to let the man know it over his cackling. He kicks Johnny in the ankle as they come to a large set of doors, intricately lined with gold along their panels, and two grim-faced imps smile up at them from where the doorknobs should be. Grabbing one and squeezing its mouth shut, Deville shakes his head in bemused wonder as the door swings open with a screech to wake the dead. What a strange fucker, Deville mumbles as he does whenever he enters the personal chambers of Michelangelo Salvatore. Don Salvatore. Or at least that's what Deville called him up until three months ago, when a falling out over legal trouble and subsequent attempts on Deville's life chased him out of this very office. Out of this life. Or so the arrogant son of a bitch thinks, Deville smiles inside, knowing better, but as he steps into the cavernous office he smooths his face to cold determination. Despite vowing never to come back, Johnny appeared on his doorstep several hours before inviting him to a chat with the Don, of Salvatore's behalf. After refusing bluntly and arguing for a bit, Deville placed his fate in the soft velvet of his pool table, agreeing to come if and only if Johnny could defeat him in a game. The man won, obviously, and never even fired a shot; after clearing the table from the break, Deville sewered while banking the 8 into the side. The loss of a simple wager is what brings Angelo back for the first time in months, but as he strides purposefully into the room, looming over him exactly as he remembers it, his demeanour is not that of a defeated man. On the contrary, he seems proud and almost noble... Except for the smirk he casts at the new faces scattered about the room, eyeing him with tangible suspicion. Who the fuck are these mooks? In reality there are only three people in the room besides Salvatore and the newcomers, but two of those faces are foreign to Deville, and that in itself is a testament to how much things have changed in his absence. The third man is an old friend of Angelo's, and though he tries to hide the fact, he's overjoyed that the man is here now; in fact, he's staring at the man as if his lord and saviour. Salvatore's eyes are wide with shock as he leans back in his chair and folds his hands over his lap, features almost indistinguishable in the shadows. The room is far from dark, but with the huge bay window directly behind the man, and the late afternoon sun silhouetting him through it, his expressions are next to impossible to make out. The rest of the room is bright and clearly defined. Deville knows the man does it on purpose, to intimidate all before him and to shroud his thoughts, to hide the knife he yearns to jam in your back. The bitterness in that almost rocks the composure from Deville's placid face. 'You wanted to see me,' Deville bites off, making it a statement rather than a query, his blue eyes flashing with outrage that this man should still command his actions. It takes a great effort to keep his hands at his side, to not strangle the life out of this man who once ordered him dead, but there they stay, solid and unmoving like the rest of his body, tense with the exercise of appearing relaxed. The two unknowns share a look, as if deciding whether or not they should forcibly get rid of the intruder, and without sparing them a moment's glance Deville begins mulling over how they will die. 'Do I?' Salvatore rumbles in his throaty voice, asking himself as much as anyone else. His great belly shakes with a humourless laugh, and the watch hanging out of his waist-pocket jingles happily. The wild grey and black hair on his poking out at odd angles from his head looks like flames leaping from a fire, and Deville marvels at how much the Don as let himself go. He peers around at everyone as if seeking their counsel, but those dark eyes see nothing. When he speaks again his words are biting, 'I have no memory of calling for you, Angelo.' Deville involuntarily whips around on Johnny, who is making great pains to study the marble fireplace off to their left, as if he'd never seen the like before, doing everything in his power to avoid that withering scowl. Deville purses his lips as the new attendants grin knowingly, then adjusts his tie and inclines his head ever so slightly in Salvatore's direction. Without a word he spins on his heel and begins stalking from the room, anger colouring where composure had reigned. He's halfway to the door before Salvatore's voice calls out again, ringing like a deep bell, 'Wait!' Deville stops in mid-stride and turns back, eyebrow arched. In the darkness he can make out a small smile creeping over the Don's face, and looks of horror growing on those of the new talent. 'That isn't to say we don't want you back with us, Angie. Just because I didn't send doesn't mean I shouldn't have.' That's as close to an apology as the man will ever give, Angelo thinks, and more of one than he's likely ever given, even to his mother. He walks back towards the Buick-sized desk and offers the meekest smile he can conjure. 'I'm glad to hear it,' Deville offers, then fails to stop himself saying, 'so you no longer think me a rat, then?' A coughing fit seizes Johnny, and the closer of the new men, the one with white hair, takes a threatening step towards him. Deville doesn't notice, and neither does Salavtore, staring up at him with familiar shock. It's been a long time since anyone has had the audacity to show such blatant disrespect, and if the truth is to be known, a small part of him actually likes the challenge. Deville's old friend tries to warn him with guarded looks. 'You know as well as anyone that chances cannot be taken, Angelo,' Salvatore says with extreme patience, explaining basic rules of life to a child. 'I never considered myself a risk,' he retorts, again keeping his arms at his sides only with great pains. Son of a bitch! That is the reason! The shock in his mind almost leaks onto his face. Angelo had always thought Salvatore discovered too much about his owns plans, his own ambitions, and was after him to preserve himself. Maybe he doesn't know... Neither did I, Salvatore says slowly, quietly, and suddenly Deville isn't so sure he knows anything of what the man thinks. 'Neither did I,' he repeats, this time in a dreamy voice meant only for himself. 'But you are one, I see that now,' he snaps out of his dream suddenly, and the air catches in Deville's throat. Maybe coming here wasn't such a good idea. Johnny begins to protest on Angelo's behalf, ready to swear on his life for the man's loyalty. Angelo is pleased when Salvatore runs him over before he can; enough of his friends have died already. 'One I mean to cast down on our enemies,' Salvatore elaborates, grinning broadly. 'We've made many since you left. If I didn't know better, I'd swear they were at bay before simply because you were around and they feared such an altercation.' Almost right, Deville thinks smugly. He's about to say something humble when the white-haired man strides forward and places a cautionary hand on the Don's shoulder. Everyone looks at him in shock, even the Don, but he is looking straight at Angelo with palpable contempt. The Crimson Angel returns the look with increasing fervour, and eventually the man drops his eyes to the Don, then starts and removes his hand with a mumbled apology. His suit jacket is scandalously unbuttoned, and hands on hips thrust the coat back beyond his hips, like a gunfighter declaring his wares to the world. There is an untamed sexuality about the man, with his eternal leer and long flowing hair, completely white despite his fledgling years. He seems to exude confidence and there is an air about him that says he means to get his way, by hook or crook, like a car salesman. Amazingly, he is almost identical to Angelo in height and build, something almost unheard of this high in the heirarchy. 'Remember what we spoke of,' he says smoothly for the Don alone, speaking as if to an equal. 'His boorish tactics will ruin everything.' Deville isn't the only one goggling at the man as if seeing a fish flying above the trees. 'And you remember your place!' Salvatore roars, casting his arm back to deliver a backhand. Whitey skitters back to his place before the blow can fall, flushed and glaring at Deville as if it is all his fault. Angelo winks at him, then narrows his eyes at the little blonde man who goes to soothe him, the other unknown, short and statuesque and business-like. These two are going to be trouble, obviously... They mean to be, at least. Puffing with outrage and impatience, Salvatore rises slowly from his chair and walks around the desk to stare up into Deville's eyes. He meets the look evenly, but it is a challenge even for him. The stink of whiskey is heavy on the air when the Don speaks again, halting and angry now. 'We need you back. Now. But things have changed. You're going to be working with other people on this.' He narrows his eyes at the speculation rampant in Deville's. 'Not above them; with them.' Salvatore amends. Deville casts a disparaging look at the two men huddled together and whispering in voices too low to be heard. 'Them?' He asks, the disgust more than evident in his voice. Salvatore grimaces and nods. 'If you want back in,' Salvatore gestures, taking a step backward, 'these are the terms.' The two men stop whispering and dart their gazes to Angelo, hatred beaming from their bodies, willing Deville to refuse or call down the boss' wrath. Every man is holding his breath, clinging to the silence with paralysed suspense. Slowly, Deville reaches out to the Don, and the two new main men flinch as if struck. Seizing Salvatore's hand gently in both of his own, Deville meets the man's eyes and whispers with conviction, 'it is my duty and my honour to serve the Family.' Johnny and his old friend grin as he pulls the hand to his mouth and kisses the heavy ruby of the ring on the third finger. Salvatore himself looks shocked, but pleased as well; it has been a long time since someone has actually displayed knowledge of the ceremonies, much less the respect to carry them forward. He smiles at Angelo warmly, and Deville mirrors his look, still deliberating on how to kill him and his two new friends. He didn't want to serve Salvatore; he wanted to kill him. And deep down he knew that this was the best way to do it. Sometimes the war could only be won by losing a couple of small battles on the way... Sometimes it is better to hide your hand than show it, almost always in fact... Sometimes you have to sewer on purpose.
