Momentary Exorcism of 2GVC

22 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03-22-03 The time was ticking away, slower than it ever was. The day was still young but the early morning low temperatures hinted at similar conditions later in the day. I stood at the large window in my apartment, the sun sprinkling it’s rays onto only a small portion of Atlantic City, the remainder caught and hidden in the dark and dismal shadows. Civilians struggled to reach their destination, the pain visible in their face as the tough winds dragged umbrellas, coats and hair in an updraft. Yet, I felt my arm with my left hand. It was bare, covered only by a thin layer of dark hair. Despite the poor conditions outside, the interior of my run-down apartment block was visibly warmer. Above the rusty radiators, a haze of extreme heat caused the distortion of visual images. Only the sound of a Spanish radio station and the snoring of my father, Jose, could be heard within the apartment. I walked away from the window and sat down on a reclining leather armchair. I lay back on this and stretched my legs. In only a few hours I’d be making the hike to Wildwood, New Jersey for this week’s PWC TV. Since joining PWC, I had been a lot more relaxed. A huge burden of pressure had been removed from my shoulders after making the grade in PWA. However, within the past few weeks’ things have been becoming more and more stressful and it’s all thanks to two people – 2nd Generation Viet Cong! ‘I can’t wait to get my hands on that Chandler Tsonda tonight’ I thought into myself. I had never held so much hatred for someone so much in my life as I did for Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda. Not only had they tried to destroy my friend’s, Jim Axtell, career but they caused me to be paranoid for the first time in my life. I reached over to the table beside the reclining chair and grabbed a cardboard box. I opened the top of it and removed the foil packaging. I reached my finger into the box and took a cigarette out. I placed it between my trembling lips and placed a flame to the end of it. I casually took a draw and I suddenly became more relaxed. I hadn’t smoked since I quit in December of 2002 but I needed one more than ever. The words they had allegedly said about planning something special for me this evening made me delusional with paranoia. Nonetheless, I knew that I would have to box up these feelings and not let Jim Axtell, my father or 2GVC know about them. If even 2GVC found out, they would no doubt use it as a tool to damage me as much as they wanted. If Jose, my father, or Jim Axtell found out they’d probably stop me from going tonight. I had to do something… anything! I gathered my thoughts and feelings and packed them away. I quickly scribbled a note for Jose. ‘Be back soon, - Tota’ I left the apartment. The cigarette was slowly taking minutes of my life as I quickly inhaled and exhaled the toxic smoke. I walked down the corridor of the run-down apartment block that I resided in. There were stains on the wall and a thick concoction of dust and dirt lining the carpet. Nonetheless, I treaded along the carpet and exited the apartment block via a rusty set of automatic sliding doors. Only part of the skyline was touched by the sun’s radioactive rays as the wind troubled the leaves on the trees and played with my long, blonde dreadlocks. The area around my apartment block had various trees and fauna lined up, adding the only visual relief from the eyesore of an urban area that I lived in. A final draw of the cigarette made it’s way through my trachea and into my lungs as I flicked it away, the ash jetting around the air. It was still early morning – the illuminous digital characters on my watch burned the time into my pupil... ‘7:42’. Only a few people walked along the sidewalk facing my apartment block so I decided this was a perfect opportunity to, not only enjoy myself, but relive a few childhood moments. I scanned the surrounding area with my light blue eyes and found a suitable target – a large hardwood tree, about 20 foot tall and with small shrubs and ferns situated at the base of it’s trunk. ‘PERFECT!’ I said into myself. Without hesitation, I sprinted towards the tree and leaped up onto its lowest branch, a small yet thick bark surrounding it. To my luck, it supported my weight and was a good location for me to scale the tree. Back in my hometown and birthplace, Buenos Aires in Argentina, tree climbing had been a huge hobby and way to pass the time when Jose was on the road with his wrestling federation. The wind still blew and lifted a few light branches and twigs. My black boots managed to excel myself even higher as I jumped athletically and swung on a thick branch a few meters above me. I kept swinging until I lifted myself up and onto the branch. Here I took a break from climbing the tree and sat down. The wet branch was probably leaving a brown stain along the leg of my white pants but it didn’t bother me. The view was outstanding – despite a few skyscrapers blocking my view a stunning park was visible. Not many cars occupied the roads and it made for a good silence moment of reflection. The aim of this exercise was to pass the time, yes, but it also had another meaning. Ever since I had the unfortunate task of meeting Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda, they have stalked my dreams, raped my conscious and tortured my thoughts. For the first time in weeks, my mind was clear of my job and my rivals. Wrestling was a career and a hobby for me but it sure as hell wasn’t supposed to dominate my entire personal life, which it had done so. I remained seated on the branch watching the world pass by right in front of my eyes until the early afternoon. Albeit for 4 hours, but the time I spent up in the peak of the tree was relaxing, exciting and enjoyable – something that I cannot say are characteristics of a lot of things. I left the height of the tree at around noon. In a few hours I would be facing Chandler Tsonda in probably the biggest match of my career. 2GVC may have a few surprises up their sleeves but if I could overcome these surprises, I knew it’d only be a matter of time until I could break free from the frightening grip that 2GVC had on my life. I wanted 2GVC out of my life… but somehow I knew that they wouldn’t leave it for quite a while!
