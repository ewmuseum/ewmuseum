There's no place like home...

5 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/05/03 The red vacancy sign pulses on and off outside the window, an irritating reminder to Kami Axtell that she’s not at home anymore. As she stands at the window of her hotel room wearing a light blue sweat suit, she looks down at the street below. The clutter and trash that litters the ground is disgusting to her, and the stench is nearly unbearable. Downtown Atlantic City isn’t as glamorous as The Boardwalk….not by a long shot. “Jim, how long are we going to have to stay in this place?” she asks, calling out to her husband. We stay focused on her, but hear Jim Axtell’s muffled voice coming from behind the bathroom door. “Just a little while longer,” he assures her. “Let me get a little money saved up and then I promise that I’ll get you out of here.” Kami’s shoulders sulk at the news. Her face grows long as she reaches over and pulls the drapes shut. The paisley decorated curtains are dingy and have the odor of stale smoke in them. Kami wrinkles her nose at the scent and quickly turns away. We get our first glimpse of the hotel room that the couple is staying in. To say that it was decorated at all would probably be an overstatement. There are no pictures hanging on the walls that used to be white, had it not been for cigarette smoke turning them a dull and dirty yellow hue. To the right is the pre-fab dresser and mirror, which happens to be cracked, and to the left is the bed and night table. The comforter on the bed is a rather forgettable color of burnt orange, and the white sheets are threadbare and nearly see-through. Kami scans the room, her eyes taking in the less than cozy accommodations. A tear wells up in her right eye, then escapes down her cheek. Her chin quivers from the heartache she feels. “You know something?” bellows Jim as he pulls open the bathroom door. Kami quickly wipes her cheek with her shirt sleeve and takes in a deep breath, putting on the best smile she can muster. “What’s that, honey?” “I was just thinking…” His thought is erased from his memory as he exits the bathroom in a pair of jogging pants and looks into his wife’s eyes. He sees the longing and misery held within them, and it hurts him to the core. “Thinking what?” asks Kami in a shaky voice, trying to fight back the tears. The normally jubilant face of Jim Axtell is now filled with pain for his wife’s suffering. His eyes gaze at her tenderly as he walks across the room and stops in front of his wife. “I’m so sorry, boo. I know how much this is hurting you.” Jim reaches out and grabs his wife, pulling her in close to his body. She buries her face in his bare chest and lets out all the sadness she had been holding inside. “I’m sorry, too,” she sobs. “I told you I would be supportive of you but all I can think of is home.” The tears really begin to flow now, and all Jim can do is try to provide comfort. He caresses her head, combing her hair with his fingers. He rests his chin on the top of her head and takes in a deep breath, fighting back tears himself. “I know, honey….I know. I miss home, too.” Jim pauses for a moment, trying to think of the right words to say. “I didn’t want to have things be this way, but this is my last shot at being a success in this sport. I just couldn’t give up after such a short beginning. You know that’s not me, Kam.” Kami sniffles and wipes her eyes again with her shirt sleeve, then looks up at her husband. “I know that, and I didn’t want you to quit. I’ve always supported you in everything you’ve done, and you have done the same for me. I love you with all my heart, Jim, and there’s nothing I wouldn’t do for you.” She forces a smile through the pain as she continues. “I just need to get used to things as they are now. I was comfortable back home, and now that comfort is gone. It’ll just take some time.” Jim gently kisses his wife on the forehead, then wipes the last of the tears from her face. He smiles down at her, a sparkle in his eyes. “You are the greatest thing to ever happen to me, babe. Knowing that you are hurting this way is killing me.” Kami tries to look away from Jim but he brings her head back up with his finger. “I’ll do what it takes to make things right with you. Just let me get on my feet here and things will take care of themselves. I’ve never let you down before, have I?” Kami shakes her head no. The couple embraces in a kiss and then a warm hug. “Now let’s get some sleep. I have to get to the gym early in the morning so I can get some training in before my match this weekend,” says Jim. Kami smiles again, this time a genuine smile. The scene fades out as the couple embraces one more time.
