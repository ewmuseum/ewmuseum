Countdown to the Biggest Night in PWC History (Part 8)

8 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-08-03 I had almost forgot, until a letter from Charlotte – my bestest friend in the whole wide world – arrived earlier today: this is prom week. Since we first started high school, we went to every school dance together, and earlier this year we had promised each other that prom would be no different. Unfortunately, I shall have to go back on my word. If anybody had asked, I would have told them how stupid the prom is… but I am going to miss seeing my Charlotte. For, while she is looking radiant and gorgeous, and some lucky guy is slow dancing with her, I will be fighting. The whole width of a massive country separates our bodies, but I am with her in heart and spirit. Hundreds of cars choke up a packed New York City street. A blur of bodies blankets the sidewalks; a mass of designer clothes, briefcases, and hair, where only occasionally something as defined as a leg or a hand or a head pops up for but a second before being swallowed once more. The buildings that surround this scene rise high into the sky, each one adorned with billboards and advertisements. Supermodels in underwear, young people in trendy clothing, and flashing neon signs tower high above the streets. A few of the buildings are offices, but the majority are stores. Outside one of the ritzier clothing stores, a young girl has her face pressed up against the glass, her arms raised so that her palms, also mashed to the window, flank her head in perfect symmetry. On the other side of the pane, the focus of her wide, hungry eyes adorns a shapely mannequin. It is a dark velvet Armani dress, covered with glittering sequins; the garment is of flawless design, and the young girl can picture it highlighting her body, even as the slit up the side of the dress shows off her attractive legs. The young girl reluctantly pulls herself away from the storefront, and slowly walks over to the entrance. With a great effort, she pulls open the heavy door, and enters. Racks upon racks of expensive, luxurious clothing of all varieties stretch as far as her little sapphire eyes can see. Rich, important-looking women weave in and around the stands, and a literal army of salespeople, all dressed in black shirts and khaki pants, casually mingle with the clientele. After looking around for a second, the young girl glances down at her own outfit: an almost non-existent, trashy black T-Shirt, with an ‘A.F.I’ patch hand-sewn onto it; a short skirt with a dark plaid pattern; and black, knee-high army-style boots. She suddenly feels underdressed and out of place. Nevertheless, she bravely makes her way up to the register, and uses the counter to pull herself up to her tiptoes. On the other side, a middle-aged woman with fake blonde hair, glasses, and a gaudy nose ring stares down at her, a dubious, self-righteous look on her face. “Can I help you?” the employee asks, her voice tainted with a slight tinge of condescension. The young girl stares up at her elder, while – without a word – she reaches down into her cleavage, and produces a small roll of dollar bills, all rubber banded together. She casually tosses the roll onto the counter, and as it lands, we can see that at least two of the bills have Benjamin Franklin on them. A much softer, more congenial mask immediately molds itself to the employee’s face. When she speaks again, her tone is much more helpful: “How may we help you today, young ma’am?” The young girl sneers at the lady, and her glare has a hard, spiteful edge. “Just ‘Paula’ is fine,” she says, her voice matching the unpleasant smile on her face. “Okay,” the servant replies, obviously knowing she has screwed up, “what do you need?” “I want to try on that dress in the window,” Paula says matter-of-factly. The woman starts to come out from behind the counter, her arm already extended to indicate that Paula should lead the way. The young girl, however, doesn’t move. “But I do not want your help,” she says coldly. “Get me somebody else.” Her adversary stops in mid-stride, and then just stares down at her, her face full of dismay. “O-of course, Paula,” she says, nervous, “I’ll get Jessica to come right over.” “Yes,” the wrestler says, obviously annoyed, “you go do that.” The woman disappears, and a moment later, a younger brunette makes her way over. Jessica has a friendly air about her: her short hair is gelled and perky, her brown eyes sparkle happily, and a wide, genuine smile spreads across her cute face. A tape measure hangs around her neck, which she adjusts for comfort just before approaching Paula. Jessica goes down to one knee, and extends a elegant, well-managed hand to the young girl. Paula hesitantly takes it, and finds it comforting and warm; she finally allows a small smile to slip into her features. “I’ve been told that you want to try on that purple dress up front,” Jessica says as she rises back to her full height, which is a foot and change taller than her customer. “Yes.” “That’s the most expensive dress in the store,” Jessica says. Her tone isn't demeaning, but impressed. She carefully takes the roll of money off the counter, stashes it away, and then pats the pocket twice. “Why don’t I look after this, so you can concentrate on that dress?” “That sounds very nice,” Paula admits. Jessica starts leading her towards the front of the store. Okay… so some people are not completely annoying… sometimes. Jessica comes to a halt in front of the rack that has the dresses, and quickly turns to give Paula a critical look. “What size are you, hon?” she asks. Blushing, Paula answers: “Usually a one or two.” Nodding, Jessica smiles. “I’ll grab both of those, and a three as well. I think that will be way too big… but no sense in making two trips.” Giggling, Paula nods her agreement, and then the pair is off towards the back of the store. When they get to the fitting room, Jessica politely turns around as Paula changes into the dress. When Paula says it is alright once again, the employee turns around, and quickly whips the tape measure off of her neck. “That looks stunning, sweetheart,” Jessica says, and she is not lying. The dress fits Paula very well. “We usually give the dresses a few final adjustments, you know, to make them perfect… but it doesn’t look like this will take much work at all.” Blushing, and allowing herself to be completely feminine for the first time in a long time, Paula giggles. Jessica, meanwhile, starts up with her tape measure. “Arms up,” the employee says in a friendly tone. She starts making small talk while she works: “Why are you buying a dress? What’s the special occasion?” There is no special occasion. I am alone. “Prom is this weekend,” Paula says. Jessica’s face lights up, and she gives her most mischievous grin. “You got a date?” she asks, an ‘ooh-la-la’ tone to her voice. Not exactly… “Yes.” Jessica’s eyebrows rise and fall suggestively. “Is he cute?” He…? Closing her eyes, and fighting back tears, Paula whispers: “Gorgeous.” Jessica takes a step back, and admires Paula – and the dress – one last time. “Well, honey,” she says, “he better have one hell of a fashion sense to keep up with his date... You know, I remember my prom…” Paula looks away, and drifts off as Jessica reminisces. Normal sixteen year old girls are getting ready for ‘the biggest night of their lives.’ They are getting dresses, they are getting their hair done up all fancy, and they are practicing putting on make-up in their mirrors. All across the country, regular girls my age are teasing their dates, giggling with their friends, and making all their final preparations. And the best girl out of them all… by far the most beautiful, but also the nicest and smartest as well… that wonderful girl will have to go to prom without her best friend. What the hell am I doing here? I should be at home… I should be picking out this dress with Charlotte… we should be getting our hair done – I even have this cute little idea for her perfect curls… we should be taking pictures together… We should be talking about sex and romance. Instead, I am dealing with a moronic man, his psychotic ex-wife, a looming custody hearing, some bastards in an office trying to make me into a sell-out, and an important wrestling match. I suppose that is my ‘big dance’ to prepare for. This weekend, I will tango with Samantha Alotti, obviously not the ideal dancing partner… And while I would rather be with my best friend, I know I can enjoy hurting the miserable, ditzy bitch. Normal girls all over the country are getting ready for the most important night of their lives, and I suppose that I am no different. Paula looks to her left, and finds herself staring back at her from a mirror. She really does look good. “Okay,” Paula says softly, sadness in her voice. “I will take it.”
