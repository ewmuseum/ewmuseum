Star Gazing

2 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/03/03 And if she needed the proof, then there it was in pure multicolour. Female dominance was a factor, and for that point one supremely underrated. Karina and Sam had done a good job in beating men nearly twice their size and making it look not only convincing but as if it was the only feasible outcome. Now she had to put out a good performance against Karina for the second time and maybe even get the match to finish to this one. She was looking towards the stars, literally in fact. The cold night air swept through her neatly combed auburn hair and she pursed her pink lips against the rapidly cooling temperatures. Her pale skin reflected the moonlight like a mystical heroine from some sort of romantic love poetry, and she basked in its frosty glow. The dark sky was devoid of anything that could possibly obscure the view of the beaming full moon. So beautiful. And, as she looked longingly into the endless abyss of eternity, fate smiled back on the little girl from England. Things could only go up from here. Well, that’s what she told herself anyway. Sam was an optimist to the extremity of the word and followed its meaning with all the heart and soul the little girl could muster. She loved to laugh and joke about what fate had in store for her, and when you’re in a good mood, why would you want to look to the downside? You don’t, do you? Nope. She often wondered what the world was like for a pessimist, those foreboding dark clouds of depression looming overhead wherever you went like some overly attached pet dog or something. If the glass was half empty, then why wasn’t it being downed for another refill? Life moves in confusing patterns, leaving so many behind, at the side of the road waiting for the next passer by. She shivered and rubbed her neat arms against her slim sides. The thin blue sweater was not enough to protect her from the sharp incisors of the cold breeze and she trampled her booted feet on the luscious green grass that stretched as far in front of her as the human eye could see. Where was she? She didn’t know. She just started to walk, following the glowing beauty of the moon like the yellow brick road, dreaming that something good would come at the end of it. As of yet, the only good of her walk had been that she was slowly becoming more accustomed to the dropping temperatures. Even back in England she wouldn’t have gotten this cold, her parents would have made sure she was wrapped up to the upper limits. Did she miss them? I don’t know, she didn’t say anything about them, like ever. Maybe she has hidden depths; things she hides from the public. Surely everyone does, don’t they? Well, if she does then she doesn’t let on, it just isn’t an everyday part of Sam Alotti. So she smiled, and everything was happy again.
