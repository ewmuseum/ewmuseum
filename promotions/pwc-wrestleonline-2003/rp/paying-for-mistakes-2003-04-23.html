Paying for Mistakes

23 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday Drifting in and out of consciousness with each and every blink, struggling to stay awake by instinct alone, Deville sagged back into the ramrod straight wooden chair with a groan of despair. Averting his eyes from what must be the blazing sun, hovering above him within arm’s reach, all he could summon forth to beat back the incessant barrage of questions came out as a jumbled mess. ...Questions? What questions? Who would be questioning him? Numb almost to the point of etherealness, he willed himself upright and forced his eyes to try and focus somewhere other than the blinding light, but it consumed his vision entirely, bludgeoning his eyes shut and his mind closed. Pressing the weight of the world up with his eyelids, he almost begged himself to stop, pleading for nothing more than sleep. Just a minute or two to rest, enough time to make his brain stop trying to break free of his skull. Sleep. Nice, peaceful, sleep. “And then what happened?!” The muted voice seemed to come from somewhere else, perhaps the bottom of a lake, and it echoed hauntingly in Angelo’s mind without any meaning attached. The stiff backhand across his face reverberated with the ringing already there, but it snapped him wide awake at the same time. Rage boiled inside the void he had locked himself inside, skittering along on patience long used up and abused beyond recognition. “He asked you a question, slimeball!” another voice chimed in, clearer this time. Pain seared through Angelo’s arm as he tried to stop the next blow, pulling painfully against the handcuffs stapling his hands behind the chair. The impact of the slap was nothing beside it, but Deville still snarled and tried to retaliate. It was the principle that counted. “I already told you assholes,” Deville muttered, trying to make it a shout but lacking the strength to raise his voice above a whisper. Bloody bubbles erupted from his mouth and trickled down his chin, pouring both from split lips and teeth jarred loose. His nose was in no better condition, and the purplish-black marring his right eye foretold of dangerous swelling to come. His words came as a resigned pant, and required more will then he figured he still held, but he forced them out anyhow. “A million times...” Surely it had been that much by now. How long had it been since he asked for his lawyer? “I don’t know what happened to Jason O’Neil.” Bemused laughter cascaded down on his rumpled body, as it had countless times before when he told them the same. The two officers, both in casual brown slacks and matching white-collared shirts – with the sleeves rolled up, of course – had heard the same story before, but bought it no more now than when they started hours before. The big one – Deville didn’t know their names, as he stopped trying to remember after the third shift took over – reached down and grabbed Deville by the tie, his hand squelching in the wetness and sprouting blood through the cracks of his fingers, and pulled Deville’s beaten head closer to his own. Any resistance he may have attempted at one point had long been wrung out of him. “Don’t give us that shit, Angie,” the interrogator slurred, making a point of showering Deville’s face with spittle. “We know you were involved... Shit, man! We told you more than twenty-four hours ago that we have cold, hard evidence!” So that’s how long it’s been, Deville’s mind winced, not with surprise, but the absence of it. A full day in this room with this flock of assholes... All because someone leaked something. All because someone wants to die. With an exasperated curse, the man released Deville with such force that the chair tipped over backward, crushing his forearms between the thin wood and the floor. As Angelo tried re-orienting himself, four hands suddenly had him by the collar. A sense of weightlessness overwhelmed him momentarily, then he was sitting upright once more, cold fury roiling beneath his skin like a volcano. Fury not at this continued treatment, but the person ultimately responsible for it. Deville knew who it was... Knows who it is... But the name danced along the fringe of his mind, teasing him with knowledge just beyond grasp. He knew he’d remember if given time, but... But these fucking pigs won’t give me any! “Who is your witness?!” Enough of this shit... This wasn’t going to go anywhere unless he made it. “Who told you I killed him?” The intended shouts assailed the duo with all of the intimidation of a friendly morning greeting, but Deville glowered with his one good eye nonetheless. “Who?! Who?!” He gibbered on, repeating the question dramatically, until one of the officer’s fists caught him square on the jaw... The one with the rings. All Deville remembered of being unconscious was waking from it, still in this dingy little room, still being loomed over by two men he meant to see dishonourably discharged from the force. Not as a result of their illegal treatment of him, of course, but through other avenues, well-travelled and time-honoured roads many seemed to be forgetting he knew. ...If he could only remember their names. If he could only discern the name of the one who caused it all. Death will be too good for him, when I find him... He will beg for death, and I will not allow it. Not for a very long time. Appalled at the sudden grin beaming up at them from Deville’s haggard face, the officer with all the rings on his hand slugged him again, snapping him from his daydream, and shouted in his face at the top of his lungs. “We ask the questions here, murderer, not you!” Another right-hand to punctuate that last, to smash the revelation into his head. Deville had expected as much; only a fool would have given him a name. Hell, only a fool would respond at all. “Our witness is foolproof,” the guard gloated suddenly, full of smug self-assurance. “The camera never lies.” He and his partner exchanged a secretive look, but Deville missed it in the blinding light. It had been a long time since he had seen anything but. So they have videotape evidence, do they? Managing to sneer inside his head, Deville fought the urge to give in to the laughter rising in his belly. Only a fool would have given him anything, he knew... But just as strongly engraved in his mind, only a fool would swallow what a cop tried to feed. “Now why don’t you tell us again, from the beginning and honestly this time, what happened on the night in question.” That was the other officer speaking to him now, trying to play the rational and softhanded cop. Deville sighed loudly, then raised his arms in submission before the other could hit him. “Alright.” His voice a defeated murmur, Deville lacked the strength to even lift his head to address them properly. Defiantly. “Alright... I’ll talk.” He missed the surprised glance between them, sloshing his head along his bloodied chest, trying to shake it in resignation. “Saturday night was a cold one, and I desperately needed to warm up.” The words came fluidly, flowing from his tongue almost happily, as if releasing them after so long lifted his spirits. “I had no match to wrestle for Starr and the PWC, so I pretty much had nothing important to do.' He forced himself to look up, to smile at them. 'Boy, was I glad your mother was home, you snivelling little bitch, or I’d have–“ Deville only felt the first strike of the beating, but he knew it would be a long one. As consciousness deserted him, he could only feel pleased that his will had held out. That, and he had just remembered the name. Or names, rather. But there would be time to deal with them another time... Right now all he needed was sleep. ~><>...<><~ Angelo sat alone in the cafeteria, leaning forward on his elbows and staring vacantly at the brown plastic tray and the pseudo-food slopped across it. An oversized spoon and an extraordinarily flimsy butter knife lay untouched beside the unnameable stew and stale bread, and a small white puddle had grown around the filthy paper cup, spillover from one of his many stumbles on the way to his seat. His head still throbbed from his interrogation and subsequent beating, and when he rolled his hands to massage inflamed temples, the plastic orange jumpsuit they had given him for clothing crinkled irritably. No one sat at the glorified picnic table Deville occupied, but the mess hall teemed with loud and hungry inmates, some already seated according to race or creed, but most still carousing in a single-file line against the wall opposite the Crimson Angel. He was facing them, with his back as close to the wall as possible. He knew he was paranoid, but chided that a little paranoia might’ve kept him out of here in the first place. A few had noticed him and commented slyly to another out of the side of their mouths, but otherwise he was undisturbed. No one could’ve possibly known. A statement of fact his mind just wasn’t buying. Would the police really haul him in on nothing more than suspicion? They had already questioned him about O’Neil immediately after the mysterious disappearance, and cleared him of reasonable suspicion. They had bought his alibi, then. But now they don’t... Now they know. He quickly squashed that line of thinking. It did him no good. They’ll only believe I’m innocent if I believe I’m innocent. He paused momentarily, trying to ignore the buzz of activity around him and the whirldwind of static throbbing within. Of course I didn’t kill O’Neil... Why would I want to kill O’Neil? He was such good sport. He repeated this over and over again until he believed it. He did not kill Jason O'Neil. But the police had bought whatever they were told, and here he sat, still waiting on word from his lawyer. His original lawyer had been on vacation when Deville was arrested, and had left no forwarding number on his answering machine. Deville didn’t have time for that. Hopefully this new one would prove himself more eager for the Deville’s hard-earned money. Yet here I rot... All because someone opened their mouth. It grated all the more for knowing that he shared part of that blame. You shouldn’t have told them! The lecture was one he had heard all morning, since being dropped off nearly thirty-six hours after he was dragged away from Capitol Punishment, hauled off like a wild animal. I shouldn’t have told them – the agreement was firm – but they definitely shouldn’t have betrayed me. Where do they get off ratting me out? Who the fuck do they think they’re dealing with? He killed that line of thinking as well. He could do nothing here, and here seemed to be where he would be living for the next little while. He supposed a phone call could be made, but for this... For this he would want to dispense the justice personally. They would die for what they did, and it would be by his hand. And nothing beyond what they deserve. A slight tingling in the back of his neck warned him to look up just as a hulking figure, squeezed into the same outfit as Deville so that his arms had to struggle out of the sleeves, approached his table and offered a less-than-comforting grin. Like an overgrown Tom Selleck, the man sported a moustache bushier than most people’s heads, thick and brown and curled into his mouth. Despite his extreme musculature, he also carried an impressive gut bought of laziness and this shitty food. The man was definitely a lifer; Deville knew it as surely as he knew he wanted nothing to do with him. “Mind if I sit down, meat?” A guttural voice peeled from beneath the huge moustache, and the man started moving to sit before Angelo could respond. “That seat’s taken.” Deville’s voice had regained some of its former majesty, but there was still a definite rasp. The intruder stopped halfway to sitting, gawking at Deville with his eyebrows at his hairline, and studied him for a longer moment before barking laughter and sitting down with a heavy thud that sent the table lurching. Deville inhaled deeply. “I said–“ “I heard what you said,” the larger man interrupted, keeping his voice low and conspiratorial. He was stroking that mouse on his upper lip and smiling wryly. The man was not going to leave him alone. “But I heard those cries for help before, and a lot louder.” He winked and nodded toward Deville’s demolished face. “You look like someone who could use a friend.” Angelo scowled. He couldn’t think of anything else to do. What the hell was wrong with this idiot? Offering him protection? “Do you have any idea who I am?” The man’s smile only broadened. “They call me The Pumper,” that rumbling voice teased, and his eyes drifted purposefully down towards his package. “And I’ll be your friend.” A bloated yellow tongue flicked across his lips and moustache, and a large-knuckled hand reached across the table to Deville’s. He brought his eyes back up to meet Angelo's and winked, a suggestion, or perhaps an invitation. Whatever it was, Deville would rather die than abide it. Sweeping up the plastic knife from his tray, Angelo darted across the table like a viper and stabbed it directly into the smiling man’s eye. Its flimsy body bent away uselessly as soon as it made contact with his rapidly closing eyelid, but the sudden attack was enough to send the would-be-pursuer flailing backward off the bench, shrieking like a little girl. He landed hard on his back, arms still swinging wildly, and Deville was right on top of him, pummelling the face that was all moustache with both hands, wailing like a windmill in a hurricane. Excited shouts broke up all around the two as Deville pulped the man’s head, followed by the sounds of men jumping up and down, then whistles blowing. Almost instantly – far too soon, at least, as far as Deville was concerned – several pairs of hands were prying Deville away from the now-bloody mess, tackling him to the ground and restraining his fury. Then those same hands were pummelling him, and the light faded slowly away. ~><>...<><~ “Capitol Punishment.” In utter darkness the words echoed ominously. His own voice sounded strange with this overwhelming sheet of night pressed against him. It came back to him distantly, as if spouting from another’s mouth, but even it was better than the hollow sound of his thoughts, making him so light-headed that he almost forgot he had a body. Much better to speak, and be heard, and feel the life in him and around him. He awoke this latest time to find himself stripped naked, swabbed in black air instead of his bunny-suit. The hole, he reasoned, trying futilely to crush the swarming memories of stories heard about time spent here. Time that seemed to stretch on forever, robbing a man of his sanity. His only defense was concentrating on the other robbers he had recently discovered, talking to himself to stave off the crushing weight of loneliness as long as possible. “Capitol Punishment.” The words repeated still sounded no better to him, like a ghost’s voice mocking his former glory. “That is what you’ve stolen from me.” His tone reaked of bitterness, but not the withdrawn and surrendering kind. “I could’ve been the PWC’s very first World Champion. So soon after losing the Universal Title to Kriegman, I would’ve recaptured everything. Been back on top.” It was silly to think of such things when you were facing murder charges, wasting away naked in a dark cell, but those things were important to Angelo. As important as anything else, he put his pride on the line in everything he did, and success had become a part of his personality. “My freedom.” Something brushed by his leg, one of his furry new roommates, and for a moment the musty stench of his new home almost brought bile scathing up from his stomach. “I must live here now, as a dog. Told what to do, punished for disobedience. And even if I manage to escape, to get out of this somehow, I’m tarnished forever. Under big brother’s eye even more than before.” He laughed mirthlessly, mocking himself. “My pride and my freedom.” Both were like childhood memories now, fondly remembered but forever out of reach. “Gone.” Only that was not exactly true. One was gone for good, taken away from him as soon as he allowed the cuffs to be put in place. “Stolen.” But the other he could still control. Even if his freedom could never be regained entirely, his pride remained his to command. The knowledge of what he had to do to restore it gnawed at the back of his mind, and this time the derisive laughter held a disturbing amount of amusement. “All because I became too trusting.” There would be none of that now. It was obvious that no one could be trusted anymore. “Too soft. And naive.” It was time to get serious. He had created problems, and he would face up to them, like a man. A mistake is not a mistake so long as it is learned from... And no one can prove the mistake ever existed if it is erased. Whistling an old tune into the cold cloak around him, he decided he’d had enough talking for the time being. He had spoken enough; he had said far too much. Now he was paying for it. For a long time he had thought himself without a weakness, completely invulnerable, but now it was obvious that such arrogance by itself was weakness enough. He had grown too complacent, and slipping up was not far behind. But that was over with now. The mistake was made and learned, and now he was ready to move forward again. Ruthlessness will become me once more. I’ll never be in this position again, ever, for anybody. The whistling grew more intense, and his eyes narrowed slightly, trying to focus on the far wall. They were beginning to adust to the dark. All that stands in my way is erasing the mistake. He already knew what he needed to do for that, and as soon as his lawyer deigned to make an appearance, the first part would be set in motion. He would be out of here by week’s end, if everything went according to plan, and there was no doubt in his mind that it would. He always liked planning, because it pitted mind against mind, an no one knew who had done better until it was too late to change. Ironically, he had forgotten the cardinal rule, to never leak your plans, and to never speak of how victory was achieved once it was. He still couldn’t believe he had entrusted his secrets to anyone, but there the truth was, weighing down on him with the threat of life in prison. He had learned his lesson, though. For the first and last time. The reminder of it throbbed all over his body, and he would never allow himself to forget again. Now all that remained was to erase the mistake. Switching from humming to singing, he chanted the first thread of his song, Sympathy for the Devil, “Please allow me to introduce myself...” He would avenge the betrayal, and in so doing destroy the evidence his folly had ever existed. As soon as he got himself free of this accursed prison, for what they had done to him, Kathryn and Erica would die a horrible death.
