The Andrea Norton Saga, Part 2: Custody Hearing

28 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-28-03 Breathe, girl, just breathe. You have played this perfectly so far. Every round thus far, you have won. At every turn, you have been smarter, better, more prepared. She helped that moron Brooklyn Blade. She gave him the most potent weapon of all: information. But she paid the price, as did he. ‘Karma’ placed her in a hospital bed, and an unfortunate lack of talent forced Mister Blade out of the community. You tried to reason with her. You tried to do what was best for her. But she refused to listen. Miss Norton would not consider her best interests – and now she has been exposed as the irrational, emotional, feeble creature that she truly is. The Courts have to see that; they would be blind otherwise. They have to recognize that she is unfit to parent her own child. You asked her to drop her quest for sole custody, Paula. She was stubborn. You then asked her to postpone the court date until a more convenient time. Again, she pushed back. But in the end, due to ‘unforeseen circumstances,’ the hearing was inevitably rescheduled. Miss Norton has suffered for her annoying antagonism… but not enough. You must admit, in the end, this is all you want: her pain. Getting custody of the child is just a minor detail. She tried to be your enemy, and you will stop until she is crushed. Nothing will crush her quicker, or with more ruthlessness, than the loss of her child. Look at Jonathon, sitting next to you. Even in a nice suit, even freshly showered and with his hair done up nicely, he still looks like trash. God, he was a failure as a human being, and thus far he has been nothing different as a wrestler. Despite the fact that he screwed up every chance that he had with Christopher, he loves his son dearly. Nothing would crush him quicker, or with more ruthlessness, than the loss of his child… No, no, Paula. Focus. She is the enemy. And while ‘Ariath’ will eventually become obsolete, he still has some hint of worth. Miss Andrea Norton will be defeated today. All her crimes against me will be paid in full. There is no other reasonable choice… So why do you feel so nervous, Paula? I know why: because I always get the butterflies before going on stage. The curtain rises… It is show time. The gavel raps loudly, and as the judge takes his seat, the gallery follows suit, producing a communal “swoosh” as dozens of nice, dressy garments slide across dozens of comfortable-looking wooden chairs in unison. “Case number A34-987, Andrea Norton versus Jonathon Norton,” the judge reads off the front of a thin folder, his tone and demeanor screaming boredom. He slowly looks up from the file, casting furtive glances at both the prosecution and the defense. Finally, his eyes flick to the gallery where, amongst others, a young boy dressed in jeans and a Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtle T-Shirt sits next to a prim, professional looking lady with glasses and an expensive suit. “From what I read,” the judge begins, his voice already droning, “we are here to discuss the custody of Christopher Norton.” Paula looks over to her right as a young, gangly man rises up from his desk. “That is correct, your honor,” the man says in his high-pitched, effeminate voice. “Milton Bennett, representing Miss Norton.” He straightens the jacket of his suit jacket, trying to puff out his chest and seem important. Nodding stiffly, obviously just going through the motions, the judge then turns towards Paula’s table. The attractive, dark-skinned lady to Smith’s direct left rises as well. “Heather Reed, your honor,” she says, her soft, silken voice somehow packed with strength and presence. She stands there, motionless, not bothering to mess with her dark blue business suit, and yet she still seems much more noble than her counterpart. “I represent Mister Norton, and his associate, Miss Smith,” Heather reports. Maybe it is the way she stands. This woman exudes confidence. I am just thankful that she agreed to take on this case for cheaper than her normal fare. The judge bobs his head in Heather’s direction, but gives the lady a smile. Finally, he shuffles through the papers within the folder, musing aloud as he gives one page after another nothing more than a cursory glance. “Somebody correct me if I am wrong,” he says, “but the parties already have a custody agreement. From what I read here, Mister Norton is allowed visitation rights at any time he wishes, under the condition that they do not leave his ex-wife’s home.” He glances over the top of the folder, taking in both the prosecution and the defense parties without actually focusing on anybody. “What exactly has gone wrong with this arrangement?” His eyes finally meander over to Bennett, who quickly steps forward. Buttoning up his jacket, as though the simple, pompous act directly demonstrated aristocracy and intelligence, he starts talking, all the while making over-elaborate and completely unnecessary hand motions. “Well, Jonathon Norton has not explicitly broken any part of the custody agreement,” he begins as the judge, seemingly resigned to a long tirade from the showy lawyer, settles into his chair. “I mean that only in the respect that he never took Christopher away from my client’s house. However, in the past year, he has not once come to visit his son. As a matter of fact, the only time he did come to my client’s house was only to harbor a severely beaten young women,” he says as he points over to Paula. She fixes a disgusted look on her face, but the young man ignores her. Jackass. Waving his hands in small circles, Bennett continues without losing a step. “While that fact hardly justifies a contention for sole custody, would the court also consider that Mister Norton was recently arrested in connection with drug charges, that he also has a detailed history of drug and alcohol abuse, as well as violence, and – finally - that he very well may have a connection with the recent attempt on my client’s life.” “Objection!” Heather says heatedly, rising from her chair. Sighing, the judge readjusts, rising to his full height. He motions the lawyers towards him as he bark out two words: “Side bar!” Reed and Bennett approach the bench. Covering his microphone, the judge leans towards the two of them. “Problem, counsel?” he whispers, addressing Heather. “Yes, your honor,” she responds, her tone equal to his. “He just accused my client of attempted murder.” “Oh, come on,” Bennett hisses, “call a spade a spade.” “He was cleared as a suspect,” Reed responds evenly, not lowering to his level of antagonism. “Go with that then, counsel,” the judge orders, cutting in. “Present that officially, and let’s get on with this.” As the lawyers step away from the bench, the judge once again leans back into his plush, comfortable chair. “Objection overruled,” he states, for the record. Turning on Bennett, his eyes narrowing slightly, he asks: “Anything further?” “Not as of now, your honor.” You could have just said ‘no,’ retard. A relieved look on his face, the judge turns towards Heather. “The floor is yours.” The prosecutor-turned-Paula’s-lawyer steps forward, gracefully walks around her desk, and slowly approaches the bench. “First off,” she says, her voice honey, “the allegations against my client are not only untrue, they are downright slanderous. My client, in fact both of my clients, have been questioned as to any connection with Miss Norton’s recent hospital trip, and both have been cleared of any wrong-doing. Finally, the authorities ruled that she had tried to commit suicide, and shut the case at that.” Nice. She turns towards Bennett now, directly addressing her adversary. Their shared tension is immediately palpable, even though Heather tries to cover her loathing for the man. “Furthermore,” Reed says, her voice much more even than her body language, “while my client was indeed recently arrested, all charges were dropped against him; as I would know, since – as prosecutor – I made that particular decision.” Also nice. “On top of that,” Reed says, obviously building towards the end of her opening, “while my client does have a history of drug and alcohol abuse, as well as violent misdemeanors… all of these occurrences happened in his youth, before he turned eighteen, therefore making them inaccessible in this case.” How true. The judge leans forward. “I beg to differ, Miss Reed,” he says, his tone almost apologetic. “For the purposes of this case, his record as a minor will be open for discussion, since they may affect his ability to parent.” Smirking, Bennett fits a mask of smugness onto his face. Seeming a little deflated, Heather slowly walks back towards her desk, and slowly, elegantly sits down next to Paula. Keeping her posture perfect, careful to defend her facial features against any emotion, Reed tries – and succeeds – to appear unaffected by the judge’s words. “Do you have any witnesses to call, Miss Reed?” the judge asks as he steeples his fingers in front of his face. “Yes, your honor,” she responds, “the defense calls Paula Michelle Smith.” Giving the judge a fake, bubbly grin, Paula hops out of her seat, and slowly walks towards the witness stand. Quickly, the bailiff approaches, Bible in hand. “Put your left hand on the Bible,” he instructs, “and raise your right hand.” Paula does as she is told. I hate you, cop… you ugly figurehead of authority. If my role did not require my obedience, I would gladly slap you down with my ‘raised hand.’ “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, so help you God?” After a moment’s hesitation from Paula, the judge leans towards her. With infinite impatience, he asks: “Well, young lady?” Slowly, Paula looks up to the judge. Her lower lip quivering, doubt flooding her eyes, she slowly ventures an almost silent question: “Do I have to swear to God?” Shaking his head, the judge gives the bailiff nothing but a quick look, yet he knows what to do. “Do you swear to tell the truth,” he asks, “and nothing but the truth?” “I do,” the young girl says, and as she nods, her black locks bounce along with her head. What is a little lie between two humans? Absolutely nothing. There is no need to bring God into this. Heather rises once again, slowly walks around the desk, and casually approaches Paula. “Miss Smith,” she says softly, “I just have a few questions. First, how long have you known Jonathon Norton?” “Between a month and two months.” “And, in that time, have you seen him do drugs or imbibe alcohol?” “No, ma’am,” Paula says. So far, so true. “In that time, has he done anything that would cause you to believe he would not be a good father?” You mean besides helping me blackmail Miss Norton? Other than supplying me with a means to enter her house undetected, so I could prepare everything for her little hospital trip? Or would you be looking for what he does for a living, which would be fighting? And how he cheats to win, and how I have conditioned him to thirst for his opponent’s suffering and pain? Perhaps you would refer to the untimely injury of one Samantha Alotti? “Of course not,” Paula answers with a straight face. “And you have had two conversations with Miss Norton, correct?” Heather asks. “Yes.” “And how would you characterize Miss Norton?” her lawyer asks. She is a selfish, horrid bitch who thinks that she is something special. But you cannot say this. Downplay her faults. Do not show aggression. She has the legal burden here, not us. “She seemed a little moody, somewhat emotional. She was very uncaring towards her ex-husband,” Paula says. Nodding, Heather asks her last question. “How do you assess Miss Norton’s abilities as a parent?” Sighing, Paula gives Andrea a long, pitying look. “She always seems tired, and does not seem to be extremely connected with her son. I know she leaves him in a day care quite a bit. I suppose it is the best she can do.” Do not show aggression. Rely on the perceptions of her current mental state, and you shall win the day. As she walks back to her chair, Heather informs the court, “I have no further questions.” Bennett is immediately there, popping up like a venomous snake. “Do you have any personal bias towards my client?” Paula hesitates, and finally decides to come clean. “I am not particularly fond of her.” “Well, isn’t that a bold understatement.” “Objection!” Reed calls out. “Sustained,” the judge mutters. Stepping forward as though he were a soldier advancing towards enemy territory, Bennett fires off another question: “The day before my client ended up in the hospital, you paid her a visit, did you not?” “Yes,” Paula says, trying to swallow her newfound hatred for this man. “What were you trying to accomplish with that visit?” “I was trying to convince her to not seek full custody of her son,” Paula answers. “Why?” Bennett asks, leaning forward, his hands planted firmly on the table in front of Paula. “Because I do not think Mister Norton deserves to lose his child,” Paula responds. “And when she did not agree to this, what did you do then?” “I wanted her to reschedule the court date, to a time more convenient for Mister Norton and myself.” Grinning smugly, Bennett inches closer. “And when that, too, failed, what did you do then?” “I left.” “You mean to say that, you flew all the way across the nation in order to get what you wanted, but you just ‘gave up’?” “Objection!” Reed calls out again, heralding another “Sustained” from the judge. Looking at Paula dubiously, Bennett spins on his heel. “No further questions.” The judge motions Paula back towards her original chair. “You may step down.” He looks over towards the prosecution, his eyes flirting over Andrea. Finally, he settles down on Bennett, and gives the lawyer an anxious glare. “Do you have any witnesses, counsel?” he asks. “Yes, your honor,” Bennett replies. “Prosecution calls Miss Andrea Norton.” Andrea goes over, sits in the witness chair, and takes her oath. Bennett steps forward, but has a friendly air about him; the hostility he showed towards Paula has apparently melted away and disappeared. “Jonathon Norton has not be the best father for your Christopher, has he, Miss Norton?” Andrea shakes her head. “No. He never visits Christopher, and when he used to, they would just sit at the kitchen table for a few hours. The past two Christmases, he hasn’t even bother to send a card; just like Chris’ birthdays. Him just popping up whenever and wherever it suits him is very damaging to my son.” “But are you any better of a parent?” Bennett asks, his tone friendly. Though he’s trying to play devil’s advocate, he’s obviously lobbing softballs. “I mean, you have been detained at a clinic for weeks now, and the doctors are all trying to fit a label of ‘mentally unstable’ on you. Did you try to commit suicide, Miss Norton?” “No I did not,” the middle-aged woman retorts with all her conviction. “I think that he and his little girlfriend tried to kill me.” “Objection!” Heather says, gesturing angrily towards Andrea. “Over-ruled,” the judge says. “You may continue, Miss Norton.” “Do you have any proof to support this claim?” Andrea gulps. “Not exactly. But I didn’t try to kill myself. I care about my son too much to leave him alone.” Bennett puts on a mockery of confusion. “But how did ground up pills find their way into your breakfast and coffee?” “They were at my house while that food was sitting on the table,” Andrea answers as she glances over at Paula. “I left her alone for a couple minutes, which would be enough time to plant the drugs.” “But, how do you explain how ground up pills were found upstairs in your bathroom, right next to a pill crusher?” “I don’t know,” Andrea says, and then thinks for a moment. “I never go into that bathroom, though – I use the one connected to my bedroom. And Jonathon knows where I keep a key outside my house, so they could have gotten into my house without breaking and entering… and planted it.” Nodding, Bennett bows to her slightly. “No further questions, your honor.” Rising from her chair, but making no move towards the witness, Heather starts asking her questions. “You didn’t move your key after you divorced my client?” “Objection!” Bennett calls. “Sustained,” the judge says. “Keep your questions on this case, Miss Reed.” “Withdrawn. Miss Norton – did you tell this story to the police?” “Of course.” “Did they take it seriously?” “They did at first…” Andrea says, and she already seems to know what the next question will be. Her shoulders sag, defeated. “Then what happened?” “They searched my house,” Andrea replies, her voice heavy, as tears begin to well up in her eyes, “and found insufficient physical evidence to establish that a crime had been committed.” “Did they find any evidence to link my clients to the incident?” “No,” Andrea whispers, and then buries her face into her hands. You damn right, bitch. We would never get busted by a moron like you. “No further questions,” Heather says as she sits down. Silence falls over the courtroom. The judge mulls things over for a little while, and then starts talking. “Obviously, we have a situation where both parents may be unfit to raise a child,” he says, his pacing slow and measured. “And while I want to believe Miss Norton when she claims that she didn’t try to commit suicide, legally I cannot hold neither Mister Norton nor Miss Smith liable for anything.” He leans forward. “Given Miss Norton’s current situation with mental health clinics, I cannot grant her sole custody of Christopher. However, given Mister Norton’s history with crime and substance abuse… as well as his recent plea bargain to avoid jail-time… I also cannot justify extending or sustaining his visitation rights. It is the ruling of this court that Mister Norton’s visitation hours are to be restricted to weekends only; furthermore, a social worker must be present at these meetings, and that same social worker shall check in with Miss Norton’s thrice weekly. If there is nothing further,” he says, raising the gavel. What… the… hell?… “Wait!” Paula yelps, jumping to her feet. The judge looks down at her, a shade of anger coursing through his eyes. “Yes,” he says sternly, his tone dripping with irritation. “Because of our commitment to the Pro Wrestling Circuit, we will be unable to visit the boy on weekends,” Paula says, protesting. Unsympathetic, the judge says: “If he cares about his son then, he can get a new job,” before rapping the gavel. As the judge exits, Ariath melts into the table, covering his head with his arms. The sounds of stifled crying emanate from any small opening in his defense. Paula, shocked, just stares forward. Presently, a hand brushes across her shoulder, rousing her from her trance. Looking up, she finds Heather Reed standing next to her. “Miss Smith,” Heather says softly, “we need to discuss the case against your mother. Her trial is coming up soon…” Life is becoming a never-ending stream of problems. Before this problem even has a solution, we must move on to the next. Nodding absently, Paula slowly rises from her chair. As her and Reed slowly leave the room, discussing strategy for their next case the entire time, Ariath crumples into the desk as much as possible. Andrea and her lawyer leave, without casting him a second’s glance. Alone, Ariath finally allows himself to cry.
