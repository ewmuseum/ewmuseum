The long ride home...

5 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/4-5/03 Swerving in and out of traffic in his ’87 Oldsmobile, Warpath’s frustration flows through every pore on his body. After being beaten, boo’d, and then beaten again, there is no question why he is so angry. He grips the steering wheel tight and leans forward in his chair, staring hard to the road. PWC’s opening show just went down, and nothing seemed to go Warpath’s way. After an intense, but brief battle with Duke’s son, Hoyt Williams, he got shocked with a low blow to pin combination. Boos followed, and Warpath snapped on some fans coming down on him too hard. He couldn’t even to that right, as he was stopped by a “lights out” attacker. Soon found to be none other than Gideon Poe. As the traffic eases up, Warpath does the same. He loosens his grip on the wheel and rests back in his seat a bit. He takes a deep breath, but is cut short by a red light. He screeches to a halt, takes his hands off the wheel, and props himself up in his chair. “I jus-,” Warpath begins to speak, but stops. He then stares off into space for a moment, gathering his thoughts. “I just want everyone to know something, everyone being the fans, the ownership, and my fellow wrestlers. I want you all to know, right now, why I hate you all.” Warpath pauses for a moment, and then continues, “In the coming weeks, you will all see a side of me that you will most likely come to despise. Everything I do will have you squirming in your chairs. I will send your children home crying, crying because they just saw their hero get mutilated right in front of their very eyes. I will be the reason your neighbor wont let his kids watch wresting on TV. After a while, you will find that I am not the type of man you love to hate, I will be the one you hate to love. The reason being, I will be the man that keeps YOU coming back, I will have the wrestling fans wanting more, but telling me to stop. People like me are the heart and soul of any wrestling enterprise. Who really turns on the TV to see their favorite good guy stand in the middle of the ring with nothing to say, yet telling you everything?” The light turns to green, and Warpath begins to drive again. Continuing, “Without someone like me to sneak your favorite champion, or abduct his wife, or hit him with a car, you have no show, no entertainment. Let me tell you, though. It didn’t have to be like this. When I went out there last night, standing up to a big bully, I lost due to his cheap maneuver. I went out there and tried to fight the good fight, and all I got was boos. You think I am going to fight for you now? That’s a joke.” Warpath speeds out in front of the car in front of him and continues driving down the road aggressively. After about 5 more minutes of silent, uneventful driving, Warpath arrives at his home in Keyport, NJ. Not so far from where the first show took place, he drove himself. He pulls into a driveway just big enough for his car, turns it off, and gets out. Warpath’s house is not big, one floor, basement, about 3 rooms long, two deep. It’s white with one door centered, and surrounded by a window on each side. ‘Path approaches the door and a dog is heard barking and scratching inside. Warpath unlocks the door and the dog silences, the door opens and he enters. “Fuckin’ Moses!” Warpath yells as he picks his foot up from a pile of dog piss, “Out!” The 90 pound, 6 month old pit bull runs to the back of the house and out a doggie door, leading to the backyard. Warpath makes his way to his white-tile kitchen and grabs some paper towels to clean the mess. He leaves the towels on the pee and walks to his bedroom where he sits on a big, brown, torn up chair that faces a TV. Before turning the TV on, he thinks out loud. “Poe…Gideon Poe. Of all the people to make me look bad at my PWC debut. I mean, I give the man credit, well; I give him the credit that I feel he deserves, which isn’t too much. Well, we’ll see how much credit he deserves after our tag match this week. That is, if this Keeton can hold his own in the ring with Poe’s partner.” Warpath thinks aloud, smugly. Warpath turns on his 19” TV and slumps back in his chair. Maybe on the inside he is ready for his upcoming match, but on the outside, he is showing no signs of life…
