High Expectations

16 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02-16-03 I had arrived in my new home. A short and silent taxi journey to the apartment complex had finally finished as Jose and I stepped out to face the suburban housing that was neatly positioned in front of us. The building was at least ten storeys tall but looked a little dilapidated, with a few windows boarded up and the grey brick emitting a shabby look. Despite its location in the suburbs of Atlantic City, New Jersey, some scattered flowers, fauna, trees and wildlife were visibly dotted around the outskirts of the building. As the birds chirped their harmonic and peaceful whistles, I turned to face my father, Jose Salvador. He was holding his two black, plastic suitcases that caused his biceps on both arms to tense. A purple circle held it’s grip around both of his pupils and red lightning was visible within his pupil – the long plane journey from Los Angeles to New Jersey had certainly take it’s toll on him. His darkly painted, thin, fine hair was combed straight forward on this occasion and black stubble was contending with its grey counterpart on the lower part of his face. Suddenly, his dark green eyes met my voyeuristic light blue eyes. “Hurry up, Tota. I need some sleep.” he said, signalling for me to walk ahead. I approached the entrance of the complex and was greeted by the automatic opening of the sliding doors. As they scraped along its rails, an obnoxious screeching noise was coherent. Nonetheless, we ignored its somewhat ‘run-down’ appearance and proceeded to the desk. The young secretary was on-hand to help us with our every need. “Yes gentleman, how can I be of service?” she asked, her luscious red lips separating and showing her moist, pink tongue. Clearing my throat, I informed her that we had booked an apartment in advance. “Ah yes, the Salvadors. Here are your keys. Your apartment is number 30. Have a nice day.” she said as she handed over the keys. I removed the key from the grasp my lips had had on it and flicked my dark blonde dreadlocks to behind my shoulders as I put the key in the lock, turned the key and twisted the rusty door handle. The door swung open, immediately causing a stench of sweat and alcohol to diffuse in our direction. I fought the smell, grabbed my suitcase and entered the room. Jose followed closely behind. “Welcome to your new home!” I chuckled as I removed dust from atop the wooden surfaces and the windowsill. “Not quite as attractive as the one we used to own.” Jose pointed out - that was clearly obvious. There were two single beds placed within the apartment, both in the same room. They were dressed with white bed sheets that had a few irremovable dark stains decorated on it. The sound of a run-down air conditioner’s engine echoed throughout the apartment. The apartment was dressed with bright, yellow walls, blue curtains with yellow stitching lining them and green carpet. Luckily enough, the window of our apartment had not been removed and remained intact. Located three storeys high, a small road and the grounds of the complex were all that was visible. As we unpacked our suitcases that lay on the bed, Jose and I reminisced of my time in the Primetime Wrestling Academy. We talked about how nervous I was when I was fighting for the title and of how I freaked out when my brother, Pablo Salvador, showed up to psyche me up for a match. “He never was a good wrestling manager…” Jose started with the hint of a laugh in his voice. “Yeah, but somehow he got me through that night.” I sighed. “I sure will miss him while we’re up here.” I added, causing the mood to fall to the sombre level momentarily. “Well, look at it this way, Tota. We are in New Jersey, we are in the Pro Wrestling Circuit. Pablo is living the life he always dreamed of – in the music industry. Everyone is happy!”. My father had never spoken a truer word. “You know, I really appreciate all of this, dad.” I said, exerting a lot of emotion. “You came to USA just for me. Just to help my wrestling career. Without you I wouldn’t be here. I wouldn’t have made it to the most promising and up-and-coming federation. I want you to know it means the world to me...” I continued before I was interrupted my Jose. He placed a solitary finger on my lip and placed his hand on my leg in a playful manner, “Tota, I already know that!” he exclaimed before wrapping his arms around me and embracing in a masculine hug. As the final item of clothing was removed from the suitcases, Jose tipped me on my broad shoulder. He pointed at the interior of his black, plastic suitcase that was empty other than a videocassette. “Fancy watching it?” Jose asked me. I was confused. On the entrance into our new home, he hurried to the apartment to get some rest. Now, he wanted to watch a movie? “Dad, you need some rest. We travelled for almost a full day to get here.” I said, hopefully dragging whatever Jose felt out into the open. “I’m getting some sleep now. I want you to watch this while I rest, though.” Jose politely demanded. I wouldn’t turn it down. I had to see what this tape possessed that was so important. “Oh, and before I fall asleep, remember one thing… you have a lot to live up to.” he said as he crawled beneath the sheets and closed his eyes. ’I have a lot to live up to? What does that mean?’ I asked myself. I couldn’t figure out what he had meant. Whether it was to live up to his past success or that PWC expect me to propel them to the top. Nonetheless, equipped with the tape in hand and the statement in mind, I approached the Television-Video combination set. I grabbed a nearby cloth and rubbed the dust and remnants of dirt from the television screen and pushed the videocassette into the slot. As the cassette penetrated the gap, I turned the television on and pressed the ‘Play’ button. For several minutes, I sat dreary-eyed in front of the screen waiting patiently for the contents of the tape to be shown. The screen remained blank for this prolonged period of time before cheaply made home footage was projected from the tape onto the screen. As the seconds on the tape rolled by, I realised just exactly what it was – my PWA title victory. I watched closely at how I was wrestling and came to the conclusion that I did have what it takes to be a wrestler in the PWC. My eyes filled up with emotion, tears and love as the video rolled on and showed the images of me pinning the opponent. As I raised the belt above my head, a single tear fell from both my welled up eyes. As the footage came to an end, a voice could be heard coming from the speakers of the television. It sounded a lot like Jose. “That’s my son! That’s my son up there!” the voice shouted. It definitely was Jose’s voice on the video. The tape finished and I switched the plugs off at the mains. I jumped back onto my bed and held my hands behind my head. Suddenly, the last sentence Jose had spoke before falling asleep shot back into my mind… ’Tota, you have a lot to live up to.’ It echoed throughout my head for a minute or so before I finally understood what Jose had meant. “I can live up to the expectations. I can!” I spoke out loud. As my eyes became infected with the need to sleep, I saw that Jose was not only wide-awake but was smiling at the way in which I had reacted to the video. I couldn’t stop myself from falling into dreamland, though, as my future suddenly became more important that my past will ever be. PWC here I come...
