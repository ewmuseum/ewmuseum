Payne And Anguish

6 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-6-03 The scene opens on a busy freeway, one mired in the snowy logjam of cars that has become part of this winter’s signature in the Northeast United States. No cars whiz by, as standstill traffic is noticeable as far as the human eye can see. Children coming back from school or daycare, mothers racing home to make dinner, husbands heading back to their nagging wives, they’re just a few of the people that make up this patchwork of every kind of person…all waiting on the sallow route, frustrated with everyone else on the road. In the midst of this giant gridlock of what seems to be every car in the Northern Hemisphere, a large Greyhound bus stands like a titan amongst the other snowfall-covered automobiles. This vehicle isn’t particularly distinctive, going at the same measured pace as the rest of the cars. However, the one reason that this bus is of somewhat noteworthiness is its occupants. As the shot cuts into the bus, we see an elderly woman fiddling with her handbag, a business man chatting on his fancy cell phone, a mother with a young child sleeping up against her…and then we cut to the prize passenger of them all: Chandler Tsonda. The PWC star seems to be enjoying his headphones thoroughly, although their sound can’t be heard. Connected to these and the source of music is an older date Sony CD and AM/FM player. Tsonda seems near sleep, the traffic not affecting him as the bus heads presumably for Atlantic City. The murmur of voices on the bus is not affecting the ex-model, who looks perfectly content, as he’s nudged on the shoulder. He pulls of his earphone, looking at the nudger with disdain. “What in the world could be so important as to interrupt my beauty sleep,” snipes Tsonda at the man in the next seat who can now be seen in the camera. He’s a short man of Caucasian descent, looking about four inches shorter than Chandler, but dressed in sharp business attire, tapping away at a calculator and scratching numbers into an old-worn notepad. “I’ve got tag title shots on the line in two days, needing my sleep, yet you disturb me for something you most likely could’ve done your own incompetent self…I swear, good help is hard to find since they closed the border.” In a neutral voice as seems to fit him, the short man replies, “Well, it appears that our finances are a bit, how do I say…bungled?” This brings a sigh from Tsonda, who is delayed further from his music. He looks over, glaring at the short finances man, and listening to the extent of the “problem.” “I have no visual aid for this, but I suppose layman’s terms are enough in this situation,” quips the knowledgable figure, going over a few numbers, double-checking as he speaks. “Well, of your current liquid wealth, a lot has gone into this wrestling project of yours. We’re looking at a loss this month in our range of ‘High.’” Of course the mention of his money brings the wandering gaze of Tsonda back upon the man to his left. “Yeah, sure…so, what’s it mean…uhhh…?” Chandler’s nonchalance about this matter seems not to alarm the man, whose name he has of course just forgotten. “Payne, sir. Payne Reuter. On the matter of your finances, I would say we’re looking at a five-figure loss, due to your constant travel, lodging, new medical staff payroll, and the list seems to go on.” Frowning, Tsonda thinks for a moment, then decides to find out exactly what this means for him. “Five figures? 15,000 or so?” Payne Reuter grimaces, looking down at his figures once again and weakly smiling back up at Tsonda as he mutters, “Beh…nint….doll….” Tsonda, of course, can not hear the man’s voice at such a low leve. “Sorry?” Taking a deep breath and looking around at the snow falling outside, Reuter keeps his voice neutral, “Ballpark estimate right now says ninety-thousand dollars, sir.” “WHAT?!” Tsonda almost screams, waking up any sleeping passengers on the bus, many who shoot dirty looks back towards him. “I’m afraid so, sir. Until this project of yours matures and becomes a big-time name, we may be suffering the same type of financial set-backs for the next twelve-to-sixteen months.” Clenching a fist, Chandler is barely able to contain his rage towards basically everything in his life. “Fine, paycuts for everyone on staff. Fire that guy who’s always at my house.” Keeping a neutral voice and trying his best not to encur Tsonda’s wrath, Payne replies softly, “That’s your cousin, sir.” This brings only another sound of frustration from the Model Citizen. Trying to provide some light in the situation, Payne looks down at his figures and attempts to give hope, “Sir, I’ll try to find some way around these problems as we continue to work on them.” Swearing under his breath, Tsonda is not in the mood for conversation but now is too riled up to go to the dream world where he was ever so serenely heading before Reuter’s interruption. He places his earphones back on his ears and fumes silently, looking out the window at the albino freeway, a sea of white as the Honda and Ford caravels navigate through. The glare of Tsonda and the fiddling hands of Payne Reuter are the last scene we see before the feed abruptly cuts to black.
