Timeline - 18/01/00 : Out Of Place

21 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/03/03 The Wolfenden residence in Albany where Karina grew up is about as far away as you can get from the buildings she now inhabits on Saturday night. Looking from just inside the front door, the hallway that greets the guest is as awe inspiring as it is formidable. ‘Welcome,’ says Lawrence Wolfenden as he goes through the motions to greet one of his guests. Dressed in a beautifully cut and perfectly fitting tuxedo, his false sincerity is flawless. ‘Let Elise take your coat, get a drink...’. He ushers them through the arched doorway and into another grand room, the high ceiling housing a chandelier that shimmers above the assembled guests. Tuxedos, cocktail dresses and champagne flutes seem to be the order of the day for his guests. The conversations look about a deep as the puddles left from a light drizzle. All they’ll remember is what college the other couple’s son or daughter got into, they’re there to compare children. ‘So, is little Kari making it back?’ asks the guest to Lawrence, trying to make nice with the obligatory enquiring about the kids. Prompted by the man’s question, Lawrence checks his watch. The wrinkling on his forehead indicates that his daughter is likely late. ‘She was due to fly back this morning, or so she said.’ he answers, realising she should be here by now. ‘I don’t think she would miss her grandmother’s 75th birthday for the world.’. Despite the assured tone in his voice, Lawrence’s eyes are now drawn to the face of his Rolex. Flashing false smiles at those who try to talk to him, quickly excusing himself from all of them, the Wolfenden patriarch gets back to the hallway where his face is growing the same colour as the ruby red carpet he stands on. Running a finger inside his collar to give himself a bit of breathing room, he whips his Scotch off the sideboard and knocks it back, casually placing the drinking vessel back on the table. No longer needing to look at his watch, the imposing grandfather clock that stands opposite chimes for 8 o’clock. ‘Where are you, Karina?’ he forces out through his teeth. To Lawrence, the Wolfenden family name is everything, it’s what he’s built up from scratch. If tonight was really about his mother-in-law’s birthday, it would be an intimate family affair, rather than the spectacle he is laying on tonight. He won’t tolerate tardiness from his daughter. The muffled metallic clunking of a key in the Yale lock attracts his attention, perking him up as the thick, black front door is slowly opened. The hand that pushes it houses multicolour nails. Hurriedly stumbling through the door with a rucksack nearly as large as herself on her back is Karina Wolfenden. Upon seeing his daughter, who’s dropping her bag free from her shoulders to much relief, Lawrence doesn’t greet her affectionately. His eyes go straight for her hair, dyed pink with red streaks. In a house full of designer dresses and smart tuxedos, the only daughter of Lawrence stands up straight wearing bright orange cargo pants and a long sleeve black t-shirt that houses a yellow happy face smilie. ‘What are you trying to do to me?’ he whispers angrily while almost forcing his daughter back out the door. ‘You aren’t coming in looking like that, we have guests!’. Karina can’t quite believe what is going on and tries to explain to him. However, by now she’s standing outside the door at the top of the stone steps that lead up to the front door. ‘My flight was delayed, I only got in an hour ago.’. She points to her chunky digital watch to hammer the point across but doesn’t get any sympathy from her unreasonable father. ‘You knew about this weeks ago, yet you still chose to go off gallivanting in the snow.’ comes right back at her. Despite the door being pulled to behind him, Lawrence still whispers harshly, not wanting to make a scene for those inside to enjoy. ‘And now you turn up looking like THAT?’. Frustrated, Karina just wants a relaxing bath. ‘I’m here aren’t I?’ she fires back with, pointing to herself to back up the obvious. ‘I made it back for Grandma’s birthday.’. Stepping forward, he almost forces her back down the steps. ‘I’m not letting you in dressed like that,’ he decrees, as if he’s the bouncer of his own home and his daughter is breaking the dress code. Pointing back inside, he continues to angrily talk under his breath. ‘I have some VERY important people inside, and I won’t have you...’ Even in his anger, Lawrence debates whether to say it. Then he does. ‘I won’t have you embarrass me.’. There it was, she was an embarrassment. The colour of her hair, her different outlook, the way she dressed. If she didn’t already know she was out of place in the Wolfenden family, she did now. Denied entry to her own maternal grandmother’s birthday so not to attract odd looks from people she doesn’t know, and her father probably even doesn’t like. ‘Fine....’ Karina shrugs him off with, leaving her rucksack on the doorstep. ‘Enjoy your party. I’ll try not to let anyone see me as I walk down the street, wouldn’t want to show you up or anything.’ she adds, jogging down the steps and turning her back on her father. The embarrassment walking away from the father she embarrasses. It wouldn’t be the last time she did it either.
