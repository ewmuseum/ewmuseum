The Ride

20 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/20/03 Hoyt sits at a desk in his small apartment typing like mad on his typewriter. He is working on his book, which he figures will out sell the bible as the new top religious reference book after he finishes his missionaries. Right now he’s typing about his battles with the warriors know as the religious zealots know as the Mick’s. “I have the taste for some crackers” Hoyt mumbles to himself as he stops typing. Hoyt gets up and goes to the counter looking for his holy blessed “body of Christ” crackers. He’s disappointed to find he’s all out of the “Body of Christ” crackers and has to settle for animal crackers instead. As he bites the head off of monkey he wonders to himself if vegetarian’s are allowed to eat animal cracker or if that’s unethical. “Flipping hippie crap” Hoyt says as he shakes his head in disgust. Lots of things have been on his mind since those lucky charm eating potato hillbillies showed up. Their false prophecy and actions is enough to make the pope stand up and cuss while smoking a joint on the john. They get under Hoyt’s skin much like that rabbit does to Mr. Fudd. “Lord please punish there souls when I’d done with them” Hoyt yells while staring up to the heavens. Hoyt takes a moment to look himself in the mirror admiring his perfect body. The pain it is bare perfection would be too much of a burden for any man but Hoyt being the son of God makes matters worse. He’s over come the shadow of his hillbilly father Duke and is the first and only PWC TV Champion. Some would want to rest there but not Hoyt who’s gung ho on taking out all the other demons in the PWC till he’s the last man standing. “Damn do I look good!” Hoyt comments as he jiggles his pecks. Hoyt wonders over to the window to glare out at the imperfections walking out on the streets. The homeless, the blacks, the Irish, the Jews, the hookers, all of life’s freeloaders and leeches. Hoyt opens the window and sprays some Lysol out the window to keep the germs back. “Maybe Hitler wasn’t such a bad guy.” Hoyt says with a smile partly in truth and partly in kidding. Hoyt realizes he has to rally the troops and begin the movement for religious law. He needs to find a congregation large enough to distribute his pamphlets on the key’s to heaven. An army of pure plumb perfect soldiers who’s only deity is Hoyt Williams. “Time to make it happen” Hoyt says as he stares out the window with a twitch in his lip.
