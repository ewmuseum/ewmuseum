Countdown to the Biggest Night in PWC History (Part 11)

11 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-11-03 The morning had just broken on this day and the sunbeams stretched the entire way from the sun to the interior of my cramped apartment. The bright blast of sunshine had awoken me from comatose at the hour of 11am. I pulled the thin yet tight bedcovers from above me and rose to my feet. My white t-shirt dangled over a pair of darkly coloured boxer shorts and I rubbed my eyes to free them of any visible ‘sleep dust’. As I did so, I quickly saw the reflection of a letter sitting on my chest of drawers. The letter, from the ‘desk of Ron Starr’, detailed whom I would be facing at Capitol Punishment – Jimmy Nguyen. Despite already knowing this beforehand, the name, both mentally and literally, knocked me off my feet. I sat back down on my bed and thought about the task ahead of me. The countdown to the biggest night in PWC history was well underway and in only two nights, I would be facing Jimmy Nguyen. Words could not describe the anger, frustration and resentment I held towards both Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda. They were already flying high in these categories but managed to make themselves stand out even more via dirty and low tactics. That is, the kidnapping of my father. Jose was still sleeping, his light snoring coherent through the doors of our rooms. All was quiet other than that. My heart wept for my dad. He had been through an awful lot since I joined PWC and the past few weeks have certainly emphasised this. He was kidnapped, tortured and ridiculed by 2nd Generation Viet Cong and in his mind these images would never disperse. I began thinking about all the things that 2GVC have managed to do to Jose, my good friend, Jim Axtell and myself over the past few weeks. “Just you wait, Jimmy, just you wait” I said out loud. Psychologically, I had already begun to battle with Jimmy Nguyen. The fight ran its course as I quickly grabbed a white dress shirt and black formal pants. I slipped them over my night-time clothing, lifted the letter in question and left my bedroom. Without even a second thought, I left my apartment and walked down to the ground floor. Here, I walked along the corridor and knocked on room number ‘47’. This was the residence of a man whom I met at a PWC TV show in the Boardwalk Hall in Atlantic City. I had talked with him on several occasions but this time I was looking for a bit of relaxation. I predicted that he’d be the one to help out. Knock Knock! The door swung open. Marc stood before me, his six foot plus frame overpowering the doorway. His bleached blond hair was brushed straight forward and his green eyes immediately connected with mine. He struck a smile, instantly, and slapped my hand. “How you been doing, motherfucka?” His profanity still shocked me but I responded nonetheless. “I’m a bit tense about the showdown. Yourself?” I hoped that he would somehow pick up on and help to release the tension. “Not bad, Tota. Step on in” Marc said, swinging his arm towards the interior of his apartment. I took a seat on a comfortable red leather chair and immediately exhaled a large amount of air. The stress of 2GVC was slowly but surely piling on top of my shoulders. “Nervous about your biggest match, then?” Marc seemed intent on finding the origin of my problems. “Nervous, excited, stressed. Emotions are running amok on me. One thing I’m sure of, though, is that 2GVC need a lesson taught to them this Sunday” I answered uneasily. Marc was still on his feet, his tall structure walking around the apartment. He lifted an aged mug of coffee and offered it my way. I turned down the offer and he finally sat down. “Now, if I remember rightly, you were exactly this way on March 8th. You were in a tag match with Jim against 2GVC for the number one contendership of the Tag Team Titles. You weren’t coping the best that night but you got through it and you performed well” he said, obviously sweetening me up with the twisted truth. Sad thing is I fell for it. “So tell me, how are you going to rid these demons from your mind and just get on with kick some Nguyen ass – like we all know you will?” I listened to Marc’s question, trying to answer it in my own mind. “Uh… I guess I’ll just go about my normal life and rest my thoughts elsewhere” I declared to myself and to a nodding fan and friend. He was affirming his agreement for the idea. “You think completely removing them from my mind is the best way to combat the pre-match nerves?” My question was scraping away at Marc’s experience as a wrestling manager in indy federations around New Jersey. “From what I’ve seen… no fucking doubt! That way your thoughts won't eat away at your confidence.” There was my answer. Riiiing Riiiing! An alarm clock somewhere in Marc’s apartment rung, to mark noon. Immediately after realising this, I got to my feet and thanked Marc for his help. “Can’t thank you enough, Marc. I won’t forget it” I told him, shaking his hand. “Don’t mention it, superstar. Knock Nguyen a few times for me Sunday' he said laughing and sending a single wink my way. I excused myself from his apartment and headed back up to my own apartment. On the way, images of 2GVC swirled around my mind and began to almost drive me crazy. I took a deep breath and followed the ‘instructions’ that Marc had set out to help me. Within a matter of seconds, any and all doubts, nerves and thoughts about Capitol Punishment had been flushed out of my brain and translocated elsewhere. Wherever they went, I didn’t care much for. For the first time since receiving the information of my match, I was relaxed, calm and actually not even thinking about the match. I guess this was my way of mentally preparing for the match. I swung my own door open and was met by a confused Jose. “Where were you?” he asked. “Oh, just had to sort something out” I said, laughter closely following. I went back to my own room and removed the letter from my pocket. Putting a flame to it, it immediately burst into flames. Before the flame reached my hand, I threw it out of the window, ash scattering in the afternoon wind and drifting away. The biggest wrestling match of my career wasn’t far away but, thanks to Marc, I would have no worries… no worries at all about the match.
