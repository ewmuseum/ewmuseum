Five Simple Questions [3]: When Am I?

25 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 25th, 2003 My father turns me on and off as if I were a switch, meaning that I can be activated at any time and at any place. If he wants me to compete in order to completely obliterate an opponent of my so-called family, the very same family that has never been there for me when I needed it the most, I do. On the completely opposite hand, however, I can be directed to calm down and just stay out of his way. Care for an example? Just turn on your fucking televisions and you’ll find one. You’ll see me looming behind him in all of his glory, white suit and all, watching, obeying, and listening intently as he speaks on things he truthfully knows nothing about. He acts as if he knows me and the way I think. He speaks to you as if you were the lowest of the low and assumes that I feel that way. In reality, which is a realm that may as well not exist in the world of professional wrestling, however, the only person I view as low is him. I cannot say anything about it and he knows that. That, of course, is why he does it. By shutting me off, he can say and do whatever he wants with the freedom of knowing that I won’t object. By doing that, he builds up my anger and allows it to stew inside of me. This is when he turns me on, making me take out that anger and frustration, anger and frustration that really is being saved for him, on an undeserving human. When am I? There is no exact time, date, or place. There is just him and the fact that I am there whenever he wants me to be.
