Welcoming The New

10 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday 'So I'm with you now, am I?' His voice wrought with contempt, Angelo Deville greets the newcomer with a sneer and pushes out the chair opposite him, the grunting protest of its leg across the checkerboard tile floor a mirror of his own reservation. His white suit swishes like a calming breeze as he crosses his arms over his barrel chest, tilting his head back slightly to give the man his full regard. 'Aren't I lucky,' Deville states in a dry monotone, not a question. The other man ignores his mock enthusiasm, nodding curtly as the chair is pushed out and seating himself with delicate care. Externally, he is similar to Deville, though notably shorter and slimmer, with close-cropped blonde hair and a stunning Armani suit. His face is ruddy and rounded, deprived of being handsome by a large nose and dulled cheekbones. He smiles disingenuously, revealing capped white teeth, and folds his hands serenely on the table in front of him, perfectly placed in its centre. A waitress scurries over and offers him something to drink, but he declines with a slight shake of his head. 'Mr. Deville,' the man's deep voice says the name carefully, as if unsure how it tastes on his tongue. He pauses and glances around the small diner, with its posters of rock stars on the walls and its padded green-backed chairs. Besides the pair there is only a young couple sharing a soda and a team of waitresses behind the counter, bubbling with idle conversation. He slowly brings his eyes back to Deville's and holds them there, challenging. 'Interesting spot you chose for us to meet. I wouldn't have guessed this to be your... Niche.' 'It isn't,' he replies coolly, matching the stare. 'I just couldn't have one of Salvatore's uncouth henchmen defiling my hallowed restaurant, let alone my home.' His voice is grating, but he contradicts the sound with a sudden smile that is more a baring of teeth. 'You'll forgive my bluntness. I don't mince words with men I mean to see dead.' The smile grows in spite of the words, and Deville flashes a wink. The man breaks eye contact and frowns down at the table between them for a long moment, considering how to proceed. Crossing his arms to match Deville's pose, the man sighs ruefully and makes eye contact once more. 'In fairness, I wasn't exactly thrilled to hear I'd be working with you, either. You have a reputation, you understand,' he clarifies quickly when Angelo's mouth tightens. 'But Salvatore says you're the best at what we do, and that's certainly what we need.' 'Indeed.' There is an excruciating silence as the man waits for Deville to continue, but he only sits there staring with his menacing blue eyes, a cat watching a mouse. 'I just don't understand the animosity towards me,' he explains, spreading his hands wide. 'We only met once in the boss' office, and I'm certain you've not heard ill of me. What possible problem could you have with me?' 'You're filler,' Deville answers simply, as if declaring water is wet. 'You're only in this lofty position because there was no one better to fill the role. You're not a part of the Family due to your skill or any great things you've done, but out of simple necessity for numbers and bodies.' He pauses momentarily to allow that to sink in, then adds, 'you're only here because Salvatore is a greedy fool. You won't last.' 'You'll be surprised by how... talented... I am, Mr. Crimson Angel, in due time. As you know, Don Salvatore has decided that you and I together must eliminate this new threat to our prosperity. I plan on following the order of the Don – or the fool, as you call him – whether I like it or not, and I expect you to do the same. I will see to it that you do the same,' his voice and eyes seize up and become like huge boulders, rough and immovable. 'Or I will see you gone.' Deville laughs for a time, full laughter rich with amusement coming straight from his belly. 'You're going to take me out?' He manages between guffaws, rising to his feet. The man rises with him, poised to fight. 'You're going to stand over my dead body?' The laughter subsides to chuckling as Deville produces a crisp bill from his inner pocket and lays it on the table. 'That's priceless, Mr. Battaglia. Priceless.' Battaglia flinches as Deville reaches towards him, then glares as he pats his cheek with an open palm like a child. 'Hell,' Deville considers as he moves past him towards the door, 'that was classic.' 'Where the hell are you going? We have things to discuss! Plans to make!' Battaglia protests, moving to intercept Deville. The couple haven't noticed them yet, too enamoured with eachother to care about anything else, but all the waitresses are making a deliberate point of not looking at them, fussing with cleaning and straightening things that don't need it in the least. Deville spins around on his heel and grabs Battaglia by the scruff of the neck, then pulls him very close so his whisper can be heard. 'If you want to survive the day, you'll learn a little tact. Watch your mouth, and watch your step. I'm not letting you screw this over for me, and I'll die before I let you stand in my way. I have other things to attend to right now, but I'll be in touch.' There is no indication to whether he means that in a good way or bad, and he doesn't elaborate. 'Don't worry,' Deville says with sudden cheeriness, 'I can find you.' Leaving Battaglia standing dumbstruck, Deville whips around again and disappears from the café, still saving the question he really wanted to ask. ~><>...<><~ 'Who the fuck are you working for?' Deville demands in a crisp voice, and the question is emphasised by an even crisper smacking sound as his backhand finds the youth's grubby face, not for the first time. The air is musty in the barn, and hay litters the floor everywhere, hiding the rotting planks and dirt underneath. A couple of full stacks wait behind Deville up against a wall, and several chickens rummage through the filth on the floor, scratching and bobbing and fighting amongst themselves. 'Who told you to give my name?' Another vicious backhand, followed immediately by a shuddering whine. Seated before him in rickety wooden chairs are two young men in black suits, bruises and blood covering their faces and rendering them almost unrecognisable. The collars of their suits are thick with wet, and their shoes lay in haphazard positions several yards away, exposing mangled and bloody toes that stain the hay and the hammer at their feet alike. The man who Deville just struck wobbles uneasily in his chair, held up only by the ropes pinning him there, both of his eyes swelled shut. The other has his one good eye – though red and bloodshot, it still opens, and is wider than it has ever been before – locked on Deville, following every motion the man makes with extreme caution. 'Don't want to answer, Marco?' Deville snarls, smashing a right hand straight into the blind man's mouth, chipping teeth and knocking the chair over backwards. Deville has been at this for a while now, preferring to do this stuff himself, and his suit jacket is hung immaculately on a hook on the barn's red doors. Sweat has begun seeping through the armpits of his once-white shirt, and dust and blood cover him in unorganised swirls, splatters, and waves, like a child's finger-painting gone wrong. 'Or are you Steve? I forget the names you gave Vendetta last week... All I keep remembering is you telling him I hired you.' The man whose chair is still upright flinches hard, since Deville has flown into even more of an uproar every time he mentions that. This incident is no exception, as Deville suddenly laces him with an uppercut that spills him to the ground beside his friend, then mounts his chest and begins working on sealing his good eye shut. He takes it with only a few pained moans and sobs; both have long since given up screaming for help. They know there will be none. 'Why...!' Whack! 'My...!' Whack! 'Name?!' Whack! Cursing at their continued refusal to cooperate, Deville lunges to his feet and begins pulling them back up. For a long time there is only the sounds of their ragged breathing and the scurrying of the chickens as Deville considers them with a hand on his chin, as if deliberating on what to have for dinner, or who to bet on in the Superbowl. 'Don't feel like talking, eh...' Deville says this quietly, to himself, as he walks over to his coat. He fumbles around inside it for a couple of seconds, then storms back over to the man and plants his pistol up against the first one's lips. 'Who are you working for?' Deville demands, cocking the gun. The man moans but doesn't say anything. Grimacing at the stubbornness, Deville forces the gun into the man's mouth a full three inches, making him gag. 'Who paid you to give my name?' His voice is sinister with malice, and his index finger begins tightening around the trigger. 'Huck hoo!' The wounded man chokes around the gun, forcing the 'fuck you' out with the last of his strength. Grinning, Deville pulls the trigger, turning his head away at the last second to avoid getting any debris on his face. There is a deafening roar as the gun fires and the sounds ricochet and reverberate through the large open barn. The man's head explodes like a piece of fresh fruit, and his friend goes into a half seizure on seeing it. Deville turns back to the decapitated body and pushes the chair over roughly with one leg, then turns to the other. I hope mine are this loyal when it comes right down to it, Deville allows himself to drift, giving the survivor time to dwell on his position as Deville places the grimy gun to his mouth. And this smart. Does this one know he's already dead? Let's find out. 'A name,' Deville implores calmly, then pushes the gun against the man's face harder to draw his attention away from the corpse of his friend. 'Give me a name and you don't end up like him. Keep protecting whoever it is that hired you, and your head'll explode like a water balloon. The choice is yours.' The man's lips tremble, and his eye darts from Deville to the corpse and back again, a deer caught in the headlights. 'Or even a reason why,' Angelo continues calmly as he pushes the gun into the mouth, bashing it past clenched teeth, all the while keeping his eyes emotionless as a doll's. 'Tell me who is doing this, and why, and you have my word that you'll walk away. Last chance.' Deville gives him a full five count, watching with agitation as the man's breathing increases and he prepares for the bullet. Uttering an oath, Deville cocks the hammer again and presses the gun hard into his mouth, ready to do away with his last clue. 'WAIT!' The word is clear and crisp, filled with panic, even around the barrel of the gun. 'WAIT!' Deville withdraws with a look of scepticism, but inside he is revelling. 'What?' Angelo asks, eyes narrowed suspiciously. 'I'll tell you, man,' he sobs, blood coming from his mouth and eyes as he does. 'Just stop! Please! I'll talk!' Half an hour later, with his suit jacket slung over his shoulder, Deville walks casually out of the barn and closes the door silently behind him. As a small fire begins to burn in the loft, the sound of the chickens skittering through the hay is the only thing to be heard, even breathing. ~><>...<><~ 'Find out what he eats, at what time, and with who. I want to know who cleans his teeth, who checks his moles, and who cleans his house. How often he's getting it... Where he hangs out... Who he knows... Who he knew. Who he hung out with in high school, college... Where he's worked. If the sonofabitch coughs, I want to know everything about it, including where he might've caught the cold. Capiche?' Pacing in the darkness of his den, Deville's eyes glow down at the phone blinking happily on his desk. Shapeless shadows creep up and loom all around him, threatening to eat up anything that is alive and well, and Deville somehow blends in. Well, not quite. More like lords over... The darkness is somehow subservient to him, bending away from his shadow, a blackness darker than night. The shadows almost cringe when he moves. 'Alright, you've got it, Angie,' a guttural voice emanates from the phone's speaker, low and quiet with confidence. 'A full inquiry on Mr. Clayton Battaglia will be yours by Friday, sooner if luck is with us. Anyone else?' 'The full Battaglia Family, as it seems there're connections I never dreamed of. Find out everything you can about them.' Deville walks over to the desk and places his hands on the edge, supporting him as he leans forward, gritting his teeth together. 'The whole Battaglia Family is mine. Clayton especially.'
