Timeline - 28/01/03 : It's All In The Landing

2 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/02/03 ‘Here goes...’ says the well-pronounced, sultry voice of Karina Wolfenden, green eyes looking through her tinted sunglasses that do nothing to filter the intensity from her stare. ‘...Well, hopefully not nothing.’. Her voice has the un-canny ability to jest and joke without a change of tone, her young face’s expressions doing all the talking for her as she hops into the air, twisting her hips and landing. The sound of fresh, white snow crumpling under her frame is heard before lack of friction takes over and she heads off down the slope, her bunched hair blowing behind her to mark her path above the board’s track. The very air she’s passing through tries to resist her descent, pushing her orange cargo pants hard against the front of her thighs as the rest of the baggy material flaps behind her, bending from side to side with every turn of her body as she builds up speed. Despite the bitterly cold wind’s full onslaught against her face, Karina’s pink lips smile This is her drug, her vice, her pastime and her love. No pill can give her this, no man can recreate the feeling... Or no woman for that matter. All around the world, rich professionals are paying hundreds of dollars to sit on a couch and spill their hearts about how stressed they are making the very money they spend to cope with making it. If only they had this too. Who needs therapy when your staring down at the jump, lining your feet up as your body hurtles towards it? No roll-cage, no anti-lock breaks, no traction control... Only a board separating you from the snow, and nothing separating you from the air. ‘Hmmm, seems I have a crowd to please.’ just makes it from her lips before being ripped from the air around her mouth to dissipate into her slipstream. Standing around by the jump, a small crowd has gathered, those who don’t have anytime for the generic lay-out of the pistes. They want to see the people who love to board. Seeing the people there, trekked out into the cold to appreciate her art, Karina knows that it doesn’t just apply to the mountain. She is in her element both on the slope and in the ring, loyalists come to watch her for both, she enjoys both.... So really, is there a difference? ‘Yup,’ she quickly says, talking through her concentration to answer her own thoughts. ‘Here I don’t have a guy to break my fall...’ she adds through gritted teeth, her knees bent, body twisted to the right and locked ridged. It’s too late to correct her path, she’s committed herself and it’s time to go through with it. The jump might as well have a PWC logo on, have a rope pass through it and be attached to the ringpost. The sound the board makes cutting through the snow changes as it hits the upward slope, it’s locked on to the jump, Karina’s body coiled like a Cobra, just awaiting the right time to release itself. 10 feet to the edge becomes 5 feet becomes 2 feet becomes 1 foot. Crouched becomes ready, becomes springing, becomes launching, becomes uncoiling. ‘WHOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO YEAH!!!!!!!’. Gone is the reserved tone, the well pronounced words and the sultry pronunciation. Hey lungs cast out a scream of pure joy into the calm alpine air as her head thrusts back, eyes closed for a few seconds to take in the feeling as opposed to the view. On the ground below, the necks of those standing by the ramp strain themselves to look up at the young woman twisting 360 degrees, her board held up behind her back and being grabbed with both hands to complete the circle of woman and board. “Whooooooooa!!!” is the best way to sum up the collective sound they make as Karina gracefully screams through the air above them. ‘Better land this one...’ runs through her head as Karina’s eyes open once more to see the white expanse growing larger before her. Fingers unclasping from the board, her legs swing back down, cutting through the air’s resistance to take up station below her, knees bent again awaiting contact with the snow. ‘Houston,’ she mutters in a less confident voice than we’re used to from her, ‘We have a problem...’. The distance between her board and snow fights a raging battle against the time she needs to prepare for the landing, caught up in the euphoria of the flight she has overlooked the landing just a little to long. Plummeting through the air, her board doesn’t land flat and instead the head clips the snow, knocking Karina off balance and instead of the smooth crunch of a perfect landing we hear the dullest of thuds as the front embeds into the snow, slowing it down to allowing the tail-end to overtake it.... Taking Karina with it in the process. ‘Ugh.’ is the only way the sound being knocked from her lungs as her body hits the fresh show can be described before she tumbles over herself, just like her tail tumbled over the front of the board. Those watching on gasp as they see the less than perfect landing, the young woman on the board expelled from it. Her body is jolted by each knock against the snow, compacting under her until it offers no more give and just leaves her to bounce off it. Then, laying on her back and looking straight up, she sees only blue. Her eyelids fight to stay apart as a moan expels steam into her view from her mouth. ‘That didn’t.... Quite..... Go to plan.’ she comments, the words giving away the aching she feels through her whole body as the sound of feet running through the snow towards her are heard. The sound of something closer embedding themselves in the snow is then heard as she digs her hands in, pushing her shaken body up as the clear blue sky slides above us to give way to the snow and the interruptions in it where she landed. Sitting up and shaking the snow from her hair, her eyes, now no longer dulled by her sunglasses only hunt for her board, seeing the speck in the white powder where it sits and letting out a sigh of relief like a mother who thought her child had drifted out of sight, before turning back to see them stand the other side. ‘Ah well, second time lucky.’ is then spoken with enthusiasm as Karina hops up out of the snow and shakes it off her, loosening her limbs once again before she walks towards her board, only thinking about how to land the next time. She can take her knocks, but she’ll keep trying. Just by looking at her, you can tell by her body language and un-dissuaded optimism, that this time.... ...She’s going to land it.
