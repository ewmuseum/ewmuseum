The End...And The Invitation

9 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: After....Before...Somewhere In There I just thought I’d say… Good riddance. You came, you sucked, you went. You proved to your American fans that, unlike the Vietnamese war where your fathers killed my fathers heartlessly. It was about more than that with you, ol’ Porkman. Jimmy and I, we’re not just warriors for our people, we’re PWC’s future. You stood in the way and like the metaphorical roadblock; you got quite unmetaphorically run over. Penance took you out, did you a favor. You didn’t wanna run from the fury of a Second Generation. PWA’s greatest shining light…muffled out by a silly little man in a muzzle. Jimmy owned you in PWA and now stands alone, as he should. You mean little and/or nothing to me. Embrace death, because life will only be pain. So, as another famous Porky said…. “Th-tha-th-that’s all….folks!” I have no special introduction to me. I’m what you see, exactly how you see it. I’ve never been a talker. Sure, you go ahead and yell “Pussy” because my hair has been frosted before a show. Call me a fag because I wear the hottest clothes out there. It simply means that when I stand above you, I’ll be in threads you’d kill to own. But I understand the jealousy, I really do. There’s not one fan out there who, no matter how you play the cards, wouldn’t trade places with me. I went from the glamour to the gutter. I went from the most connected industry in America, with the most beautiful people in the world…to an upstart competitive world and…Sebastian Toomes. I lived in a world ruled by the passive genius. The aggressor was no more than a toy. I remember Starr calling me up. I didn’t know much about the guy, or that his name was Starr, he just addressed himself as “Ron.” He gave me a call during last summer, talking about some sort of promotion that was going to re-define sports entertainment. You know what? They all say that. Everyone offers a re-definition of the sport or industry and it’s allbullshit. Every steaming lie that flies from the mouths of greedy CEO’s is a lie, but in the modeling world, you live off those words. I love living off lies, because it pays a hell of a lot more than working in packed cardboard boxes for your dinner. Starr called me with the intention of signing an older guy, with some ‘fire’ to him who could provide a spark for his young generation of stars. “We need that certain feel to the roster and you provide something we’ve got nowhere else.” Shiiit….no, really? A model in the wrestling world? No, I’ve seen that all the time. Especially one who’s got a past of being an asshole to every and anyone who doesn’t like him or disagrees with him. It’s just a fact. A pretty guy has a lot more to lose getting his face busted in by Vendetta, O’Neill, Hoyt, or Axtell. Why else do you think guys like Duke Williams and Ivan Stanislav make it to the top of their respective worlds? “Well, why in the hell would you want an….attractive male in your little wrestling show?” I could’ve hoped for a better response, but I suppose I’d thought Ron was a little more of a naïve guy as I talked to him. “Wrestling show? Maybe you’re not who I need. Forg-” I wanted to cut him off right there, as I coughed into the phone. I scratched my chin, thinking about exactly how I could make my point from a hotel room somewhere in Eugene, Oregon. “No bullshit. I want a contract and you won’t use me as a stepping stone for your new guys. I don’t know a thing about this wrestling stuff, but it’s all fake anyway, right?” I suppose by this point I was asking for a verbal ass-blasting over the phone, but something stopped the guy from hanging up on a thirty-one year old with only a history of brawling. “You go to summer workout with the rest of the applicants or you keep your ass working on commercials for whatever queer shit you swindle.” I growled under my breath, because when you’re used to getting your way AND being the best, being told you’re not the best and that you’ll work your ass off like everyone else is not fun. Now, it was my turn to be ready to hang up. “The hell did you call for, if you’re going to give me the same chance as some curtain-jerking wet-in-the-feet joke of a worker?” I was now trying to impress him with any wrestling vocabulary I’d picked up from what I knew. It wasn’t much, but I honestly though I was going to be the best. Just what I’d always been. The response was indirect and followed by the click of a phone being hung up. “Workouts or nothing.” I thought for a long ass time about keeping up with my lonely TV dinners in my apartment back in Milwaukee. I guess you can live like that. I can live like that, because I don’t enjoy human companionship, but I do love the melodic sound of my own voice. I love watching myself on TV. I’m a power-whore and I’ll admit it. I love every second of it. What was my choice, with the ultimatum left to me by Mr. [name withheld], one of pro wrestling’s most powerful men? I’m here…aren’t I? Answer your question? Good.
