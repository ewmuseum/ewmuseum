No Rest From The Wicked

22 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02-22-03 It was a fine day, in terms of weather, for the time of year. The cool, maritime breezes had halted, releasing the population from the torment of treacherous weather. The sun was slightly hidden by tall, alluring evergreen tress, which sheltered the area where I stood and cast a long shadow onto the grey concrete that lay directly below my feet. The sky was clear, with only a few clouds threatening to disturb the light blue pattern. The sky, however, was slightly tinted in the vivid and attractive colour of orange. The entire scene was affected in some way by this tint. I titled the sunglasses that lay atop my long, thin nose – the sunglasses, which had cast the orange shade of the image. As a warm, gentle wind blew my direction, I catapulted an amount of saliva into the air that finally collided with the ground. The collision of the natural liquid upon the concrete was the only sound that I could hear as I kept my black, designer boots glued to the same position on the sidewalk. This was a distant land I was exploring - a far out, foreign, at least foreign to me, landscape. The smell of thick, toxic smoke was soon inhaled through my short, narrow nostrils as it drifted from a small fire from somewhere in the great beyond. The smoke drifted into my eyes, instantly burning them and shocking them. I spun my neck to avoid myself further damage from the smoke, my dark blonde dreadlocks swinging as its roots tightly grasped my head. I lifted my black boots from the ground, reluctant to leave the attractive scenery. The smoke left me no option, though, and I tossed my haversack, hung it over my broad shoulder and made tracks towards the old, dusty and overused road that led into a small town. The sand that lined the road played a risky and dangerous game with my footing, as I struggled to keep balance on the sand-laden road. There was no sidewalk and I immediately concluded that this town was not only old but was seemingly segregated from the remainder of modern cities and towns that make the USA. I asked myself a troublesome question at that point in time - ’Where am I?’. I quickly scanned for the answer in my mind before my vision shifted to a sign that read ‘Welcome to Ol’ Kentucky’. By now, I was starting to worry. I was supposed to be in New Jersey, wrestling with PWC… Destroying Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda, not in some small old Western town! My curious and inquisitive side had other ideas, though. It soon drowned out my thoughts of PWC with entering this town and searching around. Despite feeling somewhat tired from the powerful effects of the sun, I fought the urge and ventured further down the cobblestone path. “Well, howdy stranger!” said an old fashioned woman who was sitting on a chair outside of the old house. She was middle-aged and had quite poor skin. Her brown hair was tied up and she was decorated with a dress and a plain, white apron. I nodded my head towards her and extended and waved my left hand. This was the first sign of life in the small town. A few houses soon began to line up alongside the sandy and dusty road as the quality of, and the width of, started to increase. The wooden shudders were closed on the majority of these very old fashioned houses. I predicted the reason was that it was still early morning. My right hand immediately shot down to my left wrist to check for the presence of a wristwatch. It felt the cold steel and timepiece before my head reacted and almost unstoppably was pushed down so that I could view the time. Strange thing was – I wasn’t wearing a watch. I suddenly froze in my footsteps, the sun now shining more brightly and with a lot more intensity. My eye’s squinted despite the glass lens and metal frame that protected my light, blue eyes in the form of sunglasses. I had stopped to unveil the mystery surrounding the watch before the sun became too much for my tanned skin and suave complexion to survive. I walked over to the nearest shelter, all the time my backpack straining my back and working up a sweat. The wooden shelter that was above my head created the sign for the western American bar that had horses tied to the front and came complete with the two wooden, swinging doors at the entrance. My nerves began to kick in fully. ’Tell me where I am, and tell me NOW!’ I shouted at my brain repeatedly. It was as if I was in a state of comatose and couldn’t control my actions or my thoughts. In no time, I had walked past the strange-looking cowboys that sat at tables drinking beer. I was there, though - I HAD TO BE! How can I order a beer if I’m not there? Events suddenly seemed to occur a lot faster as I finished a tall, cold pint glass of beer in almost no time. The sound of a cowboy’s voice drifted towards me and hit my ear. “Come join us, brother.” Once again, without control, I was seated next to an arrangement of three cowboys playing a game of poker over a wooden table. This truly was bizarre. Another glass of beer made its way towards our table and more importantly, towards myself. I never drank enough to get drunk and it was the same on this occasion. “What’s those spectacles?” the oldest cowboy asked. The question shocked my through the lack of proper grammar. I had to reply as the gun-slinging westerner eyed me up. “These are sunglasses, got them in downtown Atlantic City,” I replied, actually using my brain this time. “Atlantic City? What you be doing all this far away from New Jersey?” he asked almost immediately, leaving me no time to think an excuse to avoid punishment. This resulted in my brain firing back a random statement – “Well, I love the USA.” Impressed by my answer, the cowboys didn’t bother to question my dreadlocked hair, my modern clothes or even my thick, strong Hispanic accent. “You love the US of A then?” the inquisitive and now, friendly American asked. I simply nodded my head in response to his question, in return slamming my tangled and thick hair on the table. The cowboy opened up a dusty leather wallet of his, revealing a piece of stitched handwork on it. The flowing red, white and blue threads created a small version of the USA flag. Beside this flag, though, was a picture. A picture that made me jump when I first laid eyes on it. Located next to the flag on the wallet was a small passport photo with a young man who looked strikingly similar to Jimmy Nguyen. My heart jumped a skip as I thought about Nguyen’s disrespect towards the flag, the country and it’s inhabitants. Taking a second glance at the wallet verified my first impression of the picture – it was Jimmy Nguyen, right down to the jet-black hair and the gently narrowing eyes! “How can you show so much disrespect for your own country by having Jimmy Nguyen, the epitome of all things anti-American placed beside the USA flag?!” I asked him, overflowing with emotion. I owed everything to the USA. The land of hope and glory had passed its tricks onto a migrant, helping my wrestling career. Suddenly the scene changed to the run-down arena that PWC TV is held in. It’s mat has a dark brown stain around the edge as I watch on from a distance. Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda are situated in the ring and have their arms raised high in the air in joy and celebration. The circling and constant spinning of 2GVC faces quickly followed this. “AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!” I gave in. The two faces had expressed so much anger in me that I couldn’t take it any longer. I jumped forward in fright and released a loud shout. To my surprise, the scene had changed once again – this time it would remain for quite some time. An orange tint coated the blue walls, forming a horrid colour and a small piece of sleep dust fell from in between my eyes. I heard a voice echoing into my room from beyond – “Are you okay in there, son?” my father asked. I breathed a huge sigh of relief. I was in my apartment now but fully aware of what had just happened. “It was all a dream then? Excuse me, a nightmare?” I asked my father, hoping his previous experiences in the wrestling world would come in handy. His black hair was spiked and his skin a lot tanner than mine was. He nodded his head, causing his hair to slightly wave. With my full debut coming up soon, my nerves were off the chart. In a soft tone of voice, Jose explained the circumstances to me. “Tota, nerves are common. You have a big performance to make this Saturday night and you’re frightened. You’re frightened that you’ll not perform as well as you’d like to and that PWC will no longer show interest. Everyone is nervous before a big time like this in their lives.” As Jose’s words slowly started to materialise and make sense in my head, I was reminded of the dream I had. ’This Saturday I’ll have to prove not only to the PWC front office but to myself that I have what it takes’ I thought into myself. My job, my hobby had now started to infect my personal and daily life. Jimmy Nguyen and his partner, Chandler Tsonda, had appeared in and haunted my dreams. What’s next? 2GVC raping my conscience? 'I'll have to sort this out... and fast!' I said with my Hispanic accent seeping through to emphasise some words. I held so much hatred for 2GVC that it was starting to become personal. 'With Jim Axtell alongside, this should be a breeze...' I said, restoring my faith and destroying any doubts I had about crumbling the 2GVC 'empire'. The final showdown will be settled soon enough...
