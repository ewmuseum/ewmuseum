Nothing to Lose

31 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: day after yesterday Newborn eyes embrace the swirling darkness with nothing but instinct to guide, reaching for strength and familiarity in an unknown world, like a baby clinging protectively to its mother. Breathing comes slowly and wretchedly, yet the feeling of necessity and life prevalent in the action assures a fertile mind that it is something good to grab hold of, something to sustain. Floating along in a cloud of blackness with the acrid and overwhelming power to consume, a feeling of unbridled invulnerability settles in. What is this place? the mind probes gently, all serene calm. He can’t remember ever being here before, or anywhere else, but for some reason the realisation doesn’t feel strange. Who am I? The thought comes unprovoked, opening a window into forgetfulness that should chill and burn him to his very bone... To his very soul. Yet the calm remains, complacent in its apathy to not know. Beneath the stifling and somehow comforting ignorance is a feeling of loss, but of what there isn’t even the smallest clue. There is no memory of life before now, of identity before this, so how could there have possibly been anything to lose? Despite this solid reasoning, the feeling remains, itching at the back of his mind like the beginning of a headache. Instinctively he reaches a hand up to scratch the itch away, and comes to a dead stop, inhaling sharply, as if remembering his body for the first time. Vague memories of having a body once, in a life long ago, begin to batter the wall his mind has set up to protect itself. Protect me from what? No answer seems to be forthcoming, so he lets the thought skim along the darkness along with the other disjointed memories and questions he cannot make sense of. He continues his journey through the darkness, moving without willing his body to do so, and the certainty that he was a man – is a man – settles itself firmly. A shiver wracks him suddenly as it becomes more. A dangerous man. I’m a dangerous man. Is that why I’m here? The solemnity and isolation of his Universe increase tenfold, and the darkness recedes to reveal craggy rock floors of sinuous maroon and black, parched from fires not that long extinguished. Tendrils of smoke reach up for a ceiling beyond vision, and glassy black walls to either side reflect it all with eery precision, appearing not as mirrors at all but windows into identical worlds. His mind takes in all in hungrily, the sights jerking memory back into focus, clearing his head of the fog that made the darkness seem a sunny day. With extreme caution he allows himself to move before one of the shiny black walls, reaching out to touch the man standing there extending an arm to him; reaching out to touch himself, he realises suddenly, awe digging its way into his body. Crystal blue eyes stare out of a sharp and angular face, two small ponds of the purest spring water nestled in a slab of granite. His mouth twitches slightly with the recognition, and, discovering he can move it at will, he twists it up into a smile showing perfectly white teeth as he runs a hand self-consciously through his immaculately combed blonde hair, pushing it straight back over his head. Seemingly carved of the same stone as his face, his body stands before him majestically, rippling and corded with muscles meant to do great work, naked despite the sudden cold, stripped of everything. “I am Angelo Deville,” he declares in a richly booming voice that surprises him, and the testing smile becomes a wry grin. “I am the Deville.” The not-knowing that was so familiar before is now a foreign enemy, something beyond understanding and reason. How could I not know who I am? How could anyone not know who I am? Still staring at the mirror, he flexes each muscle of his body individually, beginning with his legs and flowing up his entire body, rippling and expanding and contracting with menace. “Those who have forgotten will soon know again.” The growl shatters the mirror before him as much as his fist smashing into it, and he turns away to continue the path he had been following, that sense of incompleteness plaguing him even as an avalanche of memories blankets his mind. A sickly satisfied smile splits his face. I’ve taken over corporations... I’ve punished governments and made them dance like puppets on strings... Cheated my way into tournaments... Fixed competitions... I’ve made people run screaming with nothing more than a look or a name... I’ve ended people’s careers... Ended people’s lives. I’m a made man in the mafia, the son of a deceased Don, biding my time carefully under the man who had him killed. Memories of Don Salvatore ruffling his hair as a child and laughing at the park fill his mind, followed closely by memories of his father’s death, and the man’s falsely comforting words. His unsuspecting father’s former best friend moving into their home temporarily, trying to put the moves on his mother, foolishly believing Angelo too young and naive to figure out what happened. To see what was going on. He grimaces painfully and pushes the thoughts aside... Will the killing ever end? Does he want it to? I’m also a wrestler, a number one contender in the PWC, waiting for Capitol Punishment to assert my championship dominance. His mind conjures images of Clay Vendetta and Sebastian Toomes, trying their damnedest to screw him out of what he deserves, and the rueful grin is back. Frankie Bones’ dark face appears even darker behind the shroud of defeat, fading away to reveal that of Inphino Blitz, then Kimbusa, then a flurry of faces that Deville recognises as victims he cannot name beyond that. Their failure propels his success, and his mind finally concocts an image of him lording over the cosmos with a golden belt clutched triumphantly in his fist, all of them beneath him, subjects of his domain. His Universe. “I’m the Universal Champion.” Everything belongs to him, and he belongs to everything, a symbiotic existence that gives unmatched power and energy to both sides. There is a burden that comes along with such responsibility, a tome of duty heavier than a mountain forever seeking to crush him, with new and newer bodies always climbing aboard, trying to make him stumble. He maintains his stranglehold on the Universe out of blazing necessity, thinking – knowing – it is his, stating it as all he owns. The substantial feeling that something is missing begins to burn and pulse within him, threatening to burst him open if it isn’t revealed soon, but a brief reprieve is offered as shadowy figures come into view up ahead, moving slowly and with a severe lack of coordination. Maybe I’m not alone afterall, Deville allows regretfully, squinting to get a better look. But it doesn’t make sense... What possible company can the Deville keep? As he approaches the figures become more distinct, and Deville notes oddly that there are four of them, all brandishing shovels as if clutching their salvation. Refusing to budge from their important work, they acknowledge him only slightly, seeing that he is there but paying no more mind than that. The largest of the group, the Samoan, heaves up a heaping shovelful of the rotting maroon refuse and plants it directly in front of the only female in the group, a dangerous and intriguing woman wrapped in a swirling veil of violet. Without hesitation beyond the slowness of her movements, she scoops it up with her shovel and sends it back to the familiar-looking Samoan, who unabashedly begins the cycle again. The two men behind them are doing much the same. “What the hell are you doing?” Angelo demands incredulously, certain he knows who they are but unable to put a finger on their names. The Samoan raises his head to stare at Deville with eyes that belong on a doll, vacant and unseeing, yet overall unaware of their own ignorance. There is a wet sucking sound as he plants the shovel into the ground and leans on it, weighing Deville carefully. “Welcome,” he says in a low voice, inclining his head slightly so his auburn ponytail swishes from side to side. “I have been expecting you. I knew you would come eventually... To learn what we all must.” The words are loud but hollow, almost insubstantial, dying on the air as soon as they leave his mouth. Deville must crane his neck to make them out clearly. Sneering, Deville looks from one to the next with unmistakable disgust. “You? What could I possibly learn from you?” Grinning as if he expected no other answer, the Samoan leans forward intently, beckoning Deville closer to him. Eyeing him with the air of a coiled cobra, Deville does, and the man whispers portentously. “It is time for you to learn what it is to be a wrestler.” Angelo jerks back with a coarse laugh, half-pointing at the Samoan and his shovel with disbelief. “I already know what it is about, and I am the best there is in the business.” Who does this idiot think he is, afterall, lecturing the Universal Champion on how to succeed at wrestling. “Do you now?” The Samoan asks with a smile that does not touch his eyes, and his three counterparts all jiggle their shoulders with restrained laughter. “Pray tell, rookie... What is wrestling about?” “Wrestling is about overcoming your opponent,” Deville lectures coolly, eyeing the four of them askance, doubting they know the first thing about any of it. “It is about proving you are the best by defeating the best... Taking what they’ve got, countering it, and dazzling them with what you yourself possess.” The four shake their heads solemnly, and it is the Samoan that speaks again. “Wrong.” “Wrong?” “Wrong,” he repeats with more conviction. “Wrestling is about this.” Mechanically, he picks the shovel up and grates another pile of the dirt onto it, then splashes it over to the woman. She returns it to him just as mechanically, and the uselessly pointless process repeats, as it doubtlessly will for time without end. “What the hell does that have to do with wrestling? How the hell can that defeat anyone?” Certain that they’re lunatics now, Deville reaches up to his chest to grab his Deville Special Magnum, forgetting it is not there. “This,” the Samoan boasts proudly, “builds character. That is all you need in wrestling... This is what we all strive to do.” Incredulous, Deville explodes. “You’re shovelling SHIT! Do you not realise that? Not only are you shovelling it, but you’re recycling it between the two of you! You plop a load over to her, and she sends it back in just the same way! How the hell is this wrestling?” He takes an intimidating step forward, shaking an admonishing finger under the Samoan’s nose, ignoring the sense of loss. “I know all about wrestling... Hell, I’ve reinvented wrestling.” “Then how did you lose?” Deville rocks back on his heels, his Universe spinning rapidly, like a madhouse carousel gone crazy. The elusive memory of jobbing his title to Alan Kriegman sobers him up, and everything else about his life follows behind in a bland line. He draws himself back to the loss, searching for a reason why, scouring for an idea how. He went up against the Evil Supergenius, Alan Kriegman, gave him everything he had, and walked away the loser. The bitterness burns his black heart to cinder, and rubbery legs no longer support his weight, dropping him to the ground in a heap. The four continue shovelling their bullshit, oblivious. “This is our world, Deville, and in it you play by our rules.” Deville tries to glare up at the arrogant asshole, but his head is too heavy with shame to lift. How he didn’t see it coming is beyond him, but now that it’s here he cannot bear even its memory. “You want to win?” There is mocking laughter in the voice, and Deville painfully forces his eyes up to meet the man, to glare at him with murderous rage. The Samoan shrugs and dumps another pile over to the glittery woman. “If you want to win, Deville...” Those doll’s eyes are surely laughing now. “If you want to win, all you have to do is pick up a shovel.” Before Angelo can tear him a new one for being such an idiot, there is a blinding and singing flash of fire, hot enough and bright enough to inspire jealousy in the sun. The four continue their shovelling, ignoring the apparition, but Deville keeps his drying eyes locked on the fading smoke. Stepping out of it triumphantly, with a glowing sphere that is everything clutched in one miserable hand, Satan himself grins down at Deville with a mouth wide enough to devour him and the earth beneath him entire. “Welcome to my Universe, Deville.” The evil supergenius cackles derisively, and his four puppets chime in in unison, pointing at the kneeling Deville with great mirth. “NOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!” Deville starts awake in his own bed, at home, with every memory intact and a beautiful woman snuggled into him on either side. Neither seems to have stirred during his nightmare, but his entire body oozes with sweat from the experience. He holds his head in both heads and shakes softly for a long moment. “Just a dream,” he tries to comfort himself, knowing full well that it wasn’t. He lost his title – his Universe – to Alan Kriegman, clutched in the underbelly of OSE. What was his is now gone, whoring itself out shamelessly to another man. The loss cripples him, but at the same time sets him free. “Only a shit-shovelling fool would think I lost because of something I didn't do... And only their mother would dare believe this makes me any less dangerous,” Deville grates into his hands, knowing his time to be put to pasture is still a long, long time off. “This makes me more dangerous than I’ve ever been before.” Sinking back against the soaked pillow, he folds his arms nonchalantly behind his head and stares determinedly at the ceiling, and at something beyond, content with what is gone, anxious for what is yet to come. “Now that I’ve lost, what do I have to lose?”
