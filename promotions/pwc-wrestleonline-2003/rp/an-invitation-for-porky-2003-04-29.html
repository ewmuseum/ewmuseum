An Invitation for Porky

29 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/29/03 The Atlantic City Medical Center is a place that Jim Axtell has reluctantly, but voluntarily, frequented over the last few months. His friend Porky Jack Bunson is a patient here, crippled at the hands of the Masked Monster, Penance. Jim hates hospitals, and he hates to see his friend in the condition he is in, but he still comes to lend support and friendship to Porky. It’s been a little while since he’s been able to visit Porky, with the pay per view and his own injuries to take care of, not to mention a pregnant wife to tend to. That’s where we are now, inside the medical center, on the rehabilitation floor. Jim moves down the bright corridor slowly, limping badly but not using the crutches prescribed to him by his doctor. He has a duffle bag slung over his shoulder which swings back and forth in no sort of real pattern due to his unorthodox gait. “Sometimes these doctor’s don’t know what the hell they’re talking about,” Jim thinks to himself. His knee injury needed to be worked out, in his own humble-but-not-medically-professional opinion. The best way to do that was walk on it, no matter how much pain it put him in. This wasn’t a time for him to think about his own injuries, however. As he pushes open the double doors leading into the physical rehabilitation room he spots his friend right away, doing bicep curls in his wheelchair. Jim smiles as he gathers himself with a deep sigh, then heads over to meet Porky. Porky Jack Bunson, the down-home boy from Chicken Lick, Kentucky, strains with effort as he works the curl bar up and down. His arms look to be in phenomenal shape, the muscles rippling with definition. Porky lowers the bar once more and tries to bring it back up, struggling mightily. As Jim limps across the room, he calls out to Porky. “One more, Porky! You can do it!” Jim’s voice echoes in the near empty room. A couple of nurses turn to look at who is hollering, but they quickly return to their work when they see the familiar source. Porky grits his teeth and musters the strength to bring the bar up to his forehead one last time. With an exhale of accomplishment Porky props his elbows on his wheelchair to rest. He breathes heavily as Jim arrives and grabs the bar from him, setting it on the floor. Porky looks up at Jim and a warm smile crosses his lips. “Well, well…if it ain’t mah boy Jimmy Axtell!” says Porky, glad to see his friend. “It’s been a few weeks since ya been up here ta see me. I was beginnin’ ta wonder if ya weren’t findin’ better things ta do with yer time.” “Come on now, Porky,” says Jim, frowning. “You know I couldn’t forget about ya. I had a few things going on the last few weeks here that have kept me away, but I came up the first chance I got.” The two men shake hands and exchange smiles. “I know ya been busy, pardner,” Porky admits. “I read all ‘boutcha in the newspapers ‘round here. I’m damn proud a ya fer beaten that masked lunatic and winnin’ that P dubya C Title.” Jim smiles big and then starts to dig in his duffle bag. “Speaking of the PWC Title…” Jim continues to rifle through the bag until he gets to the bottom, where he pulls out a black velvet cloth that is obviously protecting something. “Take a look.” Porky takes the object from Jim’s extended hand and rests it in his lap. He offers a quick, unsure glance at Jim, who motions for Porky to unwrap the cloth. Porky’s hands quiver a bit as he unfurls the velvet to reveal the shiny new PWC World Heavyweight Title belt that Jim won at Capitol Punishment. Porky whistles as he looks over the belt. “I gotta say that this is the might purdiest thing I dun ever seen. Mucho congratso on the win. You sure do deserve it.” The genuineness of Porky’s words humbles Jim even more. “I did it for you, Porky,” says Jim. “You were the driving force behind the whole thing. I just couldn’t let that animal get away with what he did to you. It took me a while, and a lot of getting the hell beaten out of me, but I made them pay.” “That ya did, pardner,” Porky says proudly. Jim looks over his friend with quickly blinking eyes and changes the subject in order to keep from breaking down. “So how are you doin’? I see you’ve been working out the guns,” Jim says, pointing at Porky’s arms. “Awe shucks, them old things?” deflects Porky. “I’ve gotta have strong arms in order ta try and walk again. Mah legs ain’t gon’ be as strong when I first start, so mah upper body’s gon’ hafta do most of the work.” Jim hesitates responding, not wanting to discourage his friend. He feels the need to be realistic, though. He feels he owes it to his friend. “Is that something you’re gonna be able to do, Porky?” asks Jim sincerely. “I know that the doctors have said that you won’t be able to, and I don’t want you to get your hopes up if it doesn’t work out. I’ve seen you come crashing down already, I don’t want to see it again.” Porky’s voice takes a slightly combative tone, but it’s understandable. “I’m gonna walk again, Jim! You mark my words!” Porky says, his voice raising a bit. “I got me a damn good therapist workin’ with me, and he says I got just as good a chance of walkin’ again as anyone else. He’s a miracle, I tell ya.” Porky points down at his feet with conviction. “Look! I can move mah toes around now after workin’ with this new doc for a few weeks. Before that I couldn’t even do that. He’s helpin’, Jim.” Jim looks in Porky’s eyes and sees the desire within them. “I’m happy for you, Porky,” Jim says warmly. “If this new doctor can get you walking around again then I’m all for it! Where is he so I can meet him and thank him?” Porky waves his hand at Jim. “He’s gone already, pardner. He’s only here a hour or two a day, but it’s just amazin’ what we get done in that short a time. He’s an angel or somethin’, Jim.” Jim pats Porky on the shoulder and smiles. “Everyone needs one of those, huh.” Jim pauses and then gets an enlightened expression on his face. “Say, why don’t you come to the show this weekend? A lot of the guys have been asking about you and would sure like to see you. Whaddaya say?” Porky grimaces a bit, then shakes his head back and forth. “I don’t think so, not this weekend at least. I just don’t want the boys ta see me like this. Ya understand, don’t ya?” “I certainly do,” Jim says, nodding his head. This time Porky quickly changes the subject in order to conceal his sadness. “So how’s that purty wife of yers?” Porky asks. “She startin’ ta show yet?” Jim shrugs his shoulders, throwing Porky off balance a bit. “To tell you the truth, I don’t know how she’s really doing,” says Jim somberly. “She’s barely home anymore and when she is she’s constantly talking about this woman’s group she joined and this so-called ‘savior’ there who has ‘saved her sanity’. It’s starting to get out of control, but I need to let her do it because it makes her happy. I’m not around enough due to us touring all the time, and she needs something to do. I can’t have her at the shows anymore because of Solomon and Penance, especially now that she’s pregnant.” There is an uneasy silence as Jim stares at the floor and Porky searches for the right words. Finally the stalemate is broken by ‘Hard Luck’ Porky Bunson. “I tell ya what, Jim,” Porky starts. “She just needs a bit of space fer now. After some time she’ll come ‘round ta knowin’ what side her bread’s buttered on. When that little’n starts runnin’ round and bitin’ yer ankles, everthin’ll seem just fine.” “I know, Porky. I know.” Jim moves around behind Porky’s wheelchair and begins to push it. “Let’s get you back to your room so you can get some rest. It looks like you had a pretty intense workout. Maybe you can get one of the nurses to give you a sponge bath or something.” Jim chuckles as Porky moans pleasurably. “Now wouldn’t that just beat all?” Porky says, trying to keep the saliva from running out of his mouth and dripping on Jim’s PWC World Heavyweight Title belt. Jim pushes Porky out of the room and they disappear down the hallway.
