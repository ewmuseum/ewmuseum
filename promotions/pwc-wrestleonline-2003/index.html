A Wrestleonline federation whose weekly PWC TV ran on Saturday nights from 1 February to 14 June 2003, with two pay-per-views, Capitol Punishment on 13 April and Last Man Standing on 15 June, and three championships, the World Heavyweight, Tag Team and Television titles. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro Wrestling Circuit announced itself as television. Its own history page says that in December 2002 WGTW TV-48, a small station out of Philadelphia, announced a weekly program for Saturday evenings from 10 pm to midnight beginning 1 February 2003; the debut was staged that night in the Esby Gymnasium at Rowan University in Glassboro, New Jersey, before about 1,300 people chanting the initials. The turn of the night was its owner. The Phantom Booker, shown on the arena screens as the masked man who built Primetime Championship Wrestling and Old School Wrestling, took off his mask on camera, told the crowd to call him Ron Starr, and tried to hand the first World Heavyweight Championship to Old School Wrestling’s first champion without a match. Three wrestlers from other federations cut him off; the four met in the main event, and it ended when a masked giant flattened them all.

Every card was a broadcast written down. The same two commentators shout through all 21 of them, Mark Malloy playing it straight and Bang Davenport cutting across him with backwoods gags; on most, a ring announcer reads each entrant’s hometown and weight; and between matches a roving camera follows the roster through hallways, parking lots and cafeterias, with rows of dots marking a commercial break for Spam or the incredible, edible egg. The weekly was staged from college gyms, an armory, convention halls and theatres, most of them in southern New Jersey, and the second pay-per-view from an arena in Hershey, Pennsylvania.

The belts were handed around quickly. Penance, the masked giant, won the Tag Team Championship alone on 8 February, walking late into a battle royal of nine teams and clearing the ring of everyone left in it, and Jimmy Nguyen and Chandler Tsonda, billed as 2GVC, took it from him on 5 April in a boiler room match. Jim Axtell, the “Working Class Hero,” beat Penance to become the first World Heavyweight Champion at Capitol Punishment on 13 April, the first pay-per-view, at the Sovereign Bank Arena in Trenton, and Hoyt Williams became the first Television Champion on 3 May.

The second pay-per-view, Last Man Standing at the Hershey Park Arena on 15 June 2003, closed a 32-entrant tournament for a title shot that had run on PWC TV since May: Clark Carlisle, the owner’s lawyer, beat an injured Karina Wolfenden in the final. Axtell then held off Penance and Jacob Solomon in a handicap match, and Carlisle beat him down with a chair while the owner looked on, then raised Carlisle’s arm and pointed at his waist.

The card promised a PWC TV for the Saturday after, and a news item of 22 June says that week’s show was preempted and will air the Saturday following. None survives. The last roleplay is dated 21 June and the last news item 25 June, and no closing notice survives. Its history page, dated about January 2003, begins: “There is no history of the Pro Wrestling Circuit...not just yet.”
