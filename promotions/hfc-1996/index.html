A February 1996 blended real-and-fantasy-wrestler federation that ran its first full card within its first week of existence, billing real wrestlers like Vader and Ric Flair alongside invented characters under a house No-DQ, No-countout style. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Hardcore Fighting Conference opened on 3 February 1996, its own operator, Brien, pitching it as “a league for real and fantasy wrestlers alike,” each member carrying up to three wrestlers — a single and a tag team, or two singles — run on whichever engine, WLS or TNM, he felt like using that day. Its house rule bound every match: no disqualification, no countout, tag bouts fought two out of three falls, and World Title matches settled only by three consecutive falls. Three championships were up for grabs at founding — World, International, and Tag Team — with more promised if the roster grew.

Its first card, “Saturday Night War” on 10 February, billed itself “Bigger than WCW, Better than WWF, More EXTREME than ECW and Better Fighting than the UFC,” called by Bruce Beck and Jesse “The Body” Ventura. It opened on a stable, Syko Stable, managed by Bull Nakano, introducing Psycho Sid, the 1-2-3 Kid, and Mark Sherrick as a trio out to “destroy” the field.

Sherrick beat the Sex Junkie after the Junkie's own valet, Tantalizing Tatiana, mistakenly struck her own man trying to hit Sherrick with her purse. Sabu pinned Ric Flair with a top-rope moonsault. And Vader dominated Zeus until an uninvited Arn Anderson — unsigned to the promotion, by the announcers' own surprise — interfered from ringside, letting Zeus finish him with a spinebuster and leaving the broadcast asking live whether Zeus and Anderson were now allied.

The Sex Junkie's own follow-up interview, five days later, waved off the loss as Tatiana's mistake, promised a rematch with Sherrick, and declared himself “the GREATEST wrestler in the HFC” ahead of its coming Title Tournament.

No further card survives. How that qualifying schedule resolved into champions, or how the promotion ended, is not in the surviving record.
