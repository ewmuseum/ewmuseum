[Does AMCW have a special arena?] Yes, it's the AMCW Hall, located in Queens, New York City. We however move all around the USA including big arena venues and High Schools.
[What are the AMCW Television Programs?] The AMCW has three television programs each week. They can be viewed all over the world on specialized channels, check your local cable guide for information.
1. AMCW WIRED Wired will be broadcasted live from special venues all over the US. Matches and Flashes will both be appearing on the broadcast. This is the show that angles are showcased on and titles are defended. Hosted by Dave Van Ryan and
Adam Rotten, we present Two of the best hours of TV every Saturday Night. 2. AMCW Ringside This is the secondary show in AMCW, which airs on Wednesdays. The show is hosted by Lance Caruso, who gives you a weekly dose of Taped House Show
Matches, and also gives wrestlers a chance to mouth off before WIRED on Saturday. Lance also gives you a run down for WIRED live, as well as build ups, and the whole nine.
3. AMCW Ring Report Hosted by Rick Storm, this show, which airs on Mondays, is an update of all the happenings, including Rankings, News & Rumors, This Weeks TV Report, and alot more. (Occasionally, there will be special guest wrestlers.)

[What Gold can Wrestlers expect to have around their waists?] The AMCW holds Four Championship belts currently. The Heavyweight Belt is obviously the number one title in the AMCW today. If you own this, you are the top dawg, without a doubt. There is no weight limit, so anybody can old this. Mostly defend on Pay-Per-Views, but you may catch it defended on TV shows occasionally.
The Juniorweight Championship is twelve pounds of gold, and is precious to any youngster under the Two Hundred and Thirty-five Pounds (235) mark. This belt is used for wrestlers who like to give the fans a show, and take it to the sky.
The Television Championship is for wrestlers who like to go on TV and strut their stuff. There is no weight limit, and if you own this, you are the King Of
TV. Many people in the past have held TV Belts, just look at the history, and in AMCW, you can hold this belt too.
The AMCW only has one belt for the Tag Teams, that being the Tag Team Belts which are for the Top Dawgs in AMCW's Tag Team Division. It's basically the same as the Heavyweight Belt, only there are two of them :) The belts can usually be found around the guys who've put on a show, and proved that they are the best.

[What rules do you have to abide by in the AMCW?] Thanks to [name withheld] for allowing me to use this portion of his IIWF Players Guide. IIWF Forver... How to set up matches If you wish to challenge another wrestler to a match, you may do so through the Ringside Report (See TV Shows.). However, in order to ensure that the match is booked, and to confirm the card on which it will appear, you must contact the
President with your challenge and ask for the match to be booked. How to set up angles The best angles are those which involve more than one handler working together to form a coherent, exciting storyline with a few twists. If you would like your wrestler to be programmed with somebody else in the AMCW, try and conceive of an innovative way to get the attention of the wrestler in question, and it never hurts to contact the handler of the prospective ally or enemy directly.
All the handlers' email addresses will be available after the month of May or you can email the President and he will send you a list.
Ground Rules For Role-playing Nobody wants to stop handlers from having fun in the AMCW, but it's important that the following ground rules are understood and taken into consideration out of respect both for the wishes of the fed head and other handlers:
Don't: Don't ask for your wrestler to hospitalize another wrestler. Sneak attacks are fine, but attacks with barbed wire baseball bats or double-barreled shotguns will not be accepted. It's unfair on the handler of the victim to have his wrestler laid up in hospital and miss out on action in the ring without his consent.
Don't attack announcers in your flashes. Unless you have the express permission of the fedhead, don't have your wrestler brutalize any of the AMCW's staff, with the exception of the numerous jobbers, although you should still respect the above rule about hospitalization.
Don't use excessive profanity. Often it is realistic and within reason for wrestlers to use obscene language in moments of extreme emotion. However, words stronger than "ass", "bastard", "hell", "crap", "asshole", or "bloody" will usually be edited out of flashes, because such language is contrary to what is acceptable in everyday speech and on the majority of television programs. It is still possible to express anger or discontent without recourse to a string of
four-letter words. Don't submit three long flashes a week. The primary forum for flashes is the weekly Ringside report, which gives every wrestler in the league a chance to be heard. Short flashes will be accepted for the Monday show (Ring Report.) and the weekly card, but only if they fulfill one or both of these two criteria:
firstly, they should build heat for current storylines, and not simply babble on badmouthing every other wrestler in the league; and secondly, they should relate to the wrestler's match on that card (if the wrestler does not have a match, the flash is unlikely to be used in any case, and will be held over until the next Ringside report).
Don't take things too seriously. Every once in a while, some handlers seem to forget that fantasy wrestling is only a game, and heat generated between characters can rub off into real life. If you are unhappy with the way things are going, send a message to the fedhead explaining your concerns. You will always be met with a prompt and reasonable response.
Don't pass on the mailing list. Most AMCW handlers might not appreciate receiving junk mail, or invitations to join other leagues. Kindly refrain from spamming the AMCW's mailing list.
Do: Do take an active interest in your wrestler. Your wrestler is more likely to succeed if you follow his progress and not only submit flashes, but also keep the fed head apprised of developments in his character, successes elsewhere and so on.
Do send in strategies for individual matches. One of the most effective and useful forms of roleplaying can be sending in a strategy for a particular opponent. You might be surprised at how much of an effect this can have on the match; strategies give the writers a more detailed impression of the character, and lead to a more "realistic" depiction in the ring.
Do ask for matches. As the rosters become more and more crowded, the only way to ensure that your character is noticed is to ask the fedhead to book matches for you. If you sit back and expect to be given title shots or a push, you'll be disappointed. Activity is essential.
What makes a good flash? There are no rules about what makes a good flash, but several guidelines should be considered:
Keep the angle interesting to all members of the AMCW and develop your character(s). Remember that many people read the reports. The best way to ensure future feuds is by making sure that others know about and are interested in your wrestler(s).
Be creative. Experiment with new settings to add to the personality of your wrestler(s). Longer flashes are not necessarily better flashes. Make sure your wrestler(s) get his/their point across and do not repeat it endlessly. Overkill forces readers to skip flashes.
Flash regularly to maintain interest in angles and your wrestler(s). Handlers will be required to submit at least one item, be it a promo, interview or news item, for each of their wrestlers at least once a week. Consistent roleplays is essential.
Keep flashes free of profanity and swearing. Think of the AMCW as sports entertainment and bear in mind the limits under which actual wrestlers must operate on television. Strong language, if submitted to the report, will be edited.
