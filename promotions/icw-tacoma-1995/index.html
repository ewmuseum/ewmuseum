A federation describing itself as the original ICW, running from Tacoma, Washington from October 1995. A complete pay-per-view survives whole, crowning a first ICW World Tag Team Championship and renaming its vacated International title as the North American Championship, before the promotion stopped and restarted repeatedly through 1996-97 and was renamed PCWF in December 1997. Thirty-two wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Internet Championship Wrestling opened out of Tacoma, Washington in October 1995. By 20 November it was accepting new entries against a roster of about twenty-five, aiming for forty to fifty, running two weekly cards, “Monday Night Extreme” and “Thursday Night's Main Event,” and capping each handler at three wrestlers, two of which could combine into a tag team. Ten days later, on 30 November, its founder, self-styled “Bad Boy,” closed entries outright — the roster had blown past its own goal to eighty.

The promotion stopped and restarted more than once across 1996 and 1997. By October 1997 it was advertising itself as “the ORIGINAL ICW (debuted in 1995)” with “over 80 cards in its history,” naming alumni like “Nature Boy” Todd Newkirk and tag teams including Damage Incorporated, the Ghetto Assassins, the Harlem Knights and Zulu Nation.

Its one complete surviving card, “Season Beating's,” aired 17 January 1997. Boxoffice Bound beat Damage Incorporated in a tournament final to become the first ICW World Tag Team Champions; “Total Package” Peter Bell beat “The Shocker” Brian Anthony for the promotion's own International Championship, renamed on the spot as the North American Championship; and Magus beat Todd Newkirk in the main event's ICW World Heavyweight Championship match. The same broadcast saw President Clark present Curt Crane the fed's own 1996 Wrestler of the Year award on air, and advertised a follow-up pay-per-view, “Clash of the Titans,” never otherwise attested in the surviving record.

“Playboy” Ronnie D debuted at ICW Coliseum tapings on 1 November 1997, using his first interview to declare himself, in the promotion's own words, “the icon.”

That December, the same operator renamed the promotion PCWF; a wrestler on the new broadcast summed up the change on air: “All of the ICW.... Um... It's PCWF now... Heh.... Another name change??” The Internet Championship Wrestling's own record under that name ends there.

Successor to ICW (1995–97) (1997-12)
One continuous operation under a new name: PCWF is ICW Tacoma’s own December 1997 rename, under the same operator, confirmed from primary sources on both sides. Two rival readings — an intermediate rename to IICW, and a March–September 1997 rename to NICWF — were checked against the record and contradicted.
