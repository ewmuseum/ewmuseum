Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fantasy Internet Wrestling Federation opened on 23 November 1996, recruiting “a few more wrestlers” for original characters only, with two cards a week and two further special shows for predictions and interviews.

Its own pay-per-view, Championship Night, ran on 5 December, and the recap that followed called it “a blast” — “filled with excitement” — and invited a vice-president to join, though the show's own results log did not survive extraction intact.

By 27 December a plainer post, simply titled “The FIWF,” acknowledged the crowded field directly: “I know that there are zillions of Feds, and this one is probably just one of many more to come, but WE ARE GOOD.” It described itself as “partially simulator/partially booked,” open to any “original, picky” roleplayer, and reported it had “recently 'cleaned house,' and are now getting things running smoothly.”

The next day's rules-and-officers notice recounted one angle by name: a wrestler who arrived billing himself as a legend elsewhere was, in the promotion's own words, “Wacked so hard that after his First Match, He is now injured for the next 3 cards.” The same post set its running structure — a weekly Friday Night Superstars, a fortnightly Monday Morning Supercard, a bi-monthly pay-per-view, a roster capped at 15–20 singles wrestlers and 6–8 tag teams, and a fortnightly newsletter — and closed out the year with two co-presidents and a vice-president running the league.

No card, roster, or title-lineage document survives beyond that notice. How the promotion continued past December 1996, or how it ended, is not in the surviving record.
