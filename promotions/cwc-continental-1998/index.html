Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Continental Wrestling Council described itself, by April 1998, as seven booked regions under one promotion, “like the old 70's and 80's in the NWA,” every wrestler original and free to choose which region to join. That same recruitment post said the council had been “growin strong for eight months,” placing its own start around August 1997; a separate account, given by one region's own on-air commissioner closing an earlier card, traces the same alliance further back, to August 1996 — the two accounts aren't reconciled in the surviving record.

A January 1998 rankings-and-roster bulletin gives the fullest structural snapshot to survive: a Southern region and a Mid-Atlantic region, each with its own Heavyweight and Tag Team champions, a full ranked ladder beneath them, and a standing house rule that any interview, showcase match, or strategy filed late meant its wrestler would job. That week's Mid-Atlantic showcase ended in a battle royal for the region's vacant Heavyweight title, won by “The Crippler” Rex Stone, who outlasted a decorated-veteran gimmick, a masked 310-pound monster, and two more before pinning the promotion's former Florida champion — his stablemates Doom and Volcano greeting him at ringside, a hint their villainous faction was reforming.

A joint Southern-and-Georgia supercard that March, “Southern Justice,” staged at the Georgia Dome in Atlanta, restructured the promotion on-air. Chief executive Matthew Musgrave announced the Southern region's two titles moving to Georgia, and Commissioner Kay declared the newly titleless champions' belts vacant before unveiling three brand-new championships for a renamed Mid-South region — naming that night, in the same breath, as the Southern region's own last card before the changeover, with a promise to recruit a replacement promotion for the vacated Southern slot.

By its eighth national newsletter, 1 June 1998, the council had built toward its first National Heavyweight Championship: a sixteen-man bracket crossing every region, alongside a National Tag Team and National Television title match, all set for a pay-per-view called “ONE” — “because folks, there can be only ONE Champion” — scheduled for 27 June 1998, with weekly member chats held on a dedicated IRC channel in the meantime.

No document later than that newsletter survives. Whether the tournament crowned a first National Champion, or the pay-per-view even happened, isn't in the surviving record.
