A Usenet e-fed, renamed from the E-Mail Wrestling Circuit to the Extreme Mail Wrestling Council under the same operator, running weekly televised cards for three championships out of Las Vegas from 1995 to 2003. Its flagship broadcast, a twenty-man battle royal night, survives here with forty-two wrestlers. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Extreme Mail Wrestling Council began life in April 1995 as the E-Mail Wrestling Circuit, trained out of a New York City school it called the Palace of Pain, and carried the same operator and the same acronym into its June 1996 rebrand: “welcome to the world of e-wrestling's best — the EXTREME MAIL WRESTLING COUNCIL, better known as the EMWC.” It ran weekly televised cards for a growing title picture — a World Heavyweight, World Television, North American, World Tag Team and Junior Heavyweight Championship — and made its most visible home in Las Vegas.

Its flagship broadcast, “An Evening To The Extreme” from the MGM Grand Garden on 26 January 1998, opened on fan sections the show itself named — the Greeks, the Dirt Dogs, the Creed Army — and ran a twenty-man battle royal won by the Inkinator for a same-night shot at World Heavyweight Champion Gabrielle Whitecross; the surviving broadcast breaks off before that title match's own finish. The same card saw Pietro Sandini and Dirt Dog Unique Allah each retrieve only their own belt in a double ladder match, so neither title changed hands, and a World Tag Team title match between the Fraternity Boys and Mass Pandemonium thrown out after a mass brawl. On-air president Chris Blue suspended the wrestler Creed live for refusing to fight back, and was suplexed for it after taunting Creed about his late mother.

By January 1999 the promotion had built a new home base, the Palace of Pain on Hollywood's Sunset Strip, and kept touring — a stop that same month played Indianapolis's Market Square Arena — while its own on-air president waved through new admissions like the tag team the Midnight Crew, Chris, Tommy Wilson and Julio Rodriguez. That April's “An Evening To The Extreme” changed two titles: “The Attack” Zack Malone won the North American Championship by pinfall after the assigned referee was knocked unconscious, and Andrew Sterling and Dan Thomas took the World Tag Team Championship from TORA and Akuma. “Crimson” Joe Reed retained the World Heavyweight Championship, and “Righteous” Richard Blue won the Junior Heavyweight title in a steel cage match the promotion's own returning former president ordered started even after champion Youth Gone Wild was jumped and left unable to defend himself before the bell.

Later cards survive only as archival replays inside the promotion's own later broadcasts: Showtime VIII (30 September 2001, Texas Stadium) ended with “Blackheart” Casey James strangling Caleb Temple unconscious with barbed wire wrapped around his own hand, a finish that cost James two fingers; an undated Frozen Ghost Death Match, “Rumble In Sin City,” had Tiger Claw force Simon Ezra to fight back only after Ezra's valet, Snow White, was taken hostage backstage and rescued by Andrew Sterling and Dan Thomas, before Claw won it outright; the same night, a returning Gabrielle Whitecross joined Bobby Taylor and Jack Greene to confront the stable High Society at ringside.

On 1 August 2003 the promotion's own president, Christopher Blue, closed it outright, dating its run at “the past eight years or so” and thanking the contributors who had written for it: “It's just… it's time, guys.” He signed off “Stay Extreme, kids.” The months that followed built toward a farewell reunion show, Showtime X: Eternally Extreme, announced for 28 December 2003 with venue bids weighed, past champions and stipulations like the Woodshed and WarGames floated for a return, and a growing list of returning names confirmed week to week.

The last surviving EMWC document is a build update dated 15 November 2003, five weeks ahead of the announced show. Whether Showtime X: Eternally Extreme was ever actually broadcast on that date, and what became of the several championship changes the promotion's own later updates mention in passing without a dated card to confirm them, is not settled in the surviving record.
