Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Elite Wrestling Foundation ran for about six weeks. It put its own top belt up as a tournament before it had a champion at all: the first round opened its first Immortality, on Sunday 9 March 2003, live from a sold-out Ralph Wilson Stadium, and the final closed WrestleFest at the Joe Louis Arena three weeks later. Turbulence beat “The Turnbuckle” Jay there and was handed the belt as, in its own commentator’s words, the very first EWF Champion.

Its rules were unusually specific about the shape of a year it never got. Immortality would air Sundays with two lower-card matches, three mid-card, an upper-card match and a main event; a pay-per-view would come every month in the third week; the pay-per-view names would repeat annually, so July’s WrestleFest 2003 would be July’s WrestleFest 2004. The same page names four championships the fed never crowned anyone for, while the champions page names three quite different ones it did.

The newsdesk is the most human thing it left. One week the results were late because there were no results writers; another because a father’s internet was down all Sunday. The last entry says the results for 13 April are up and the card will follow. How it ended isn’t in the surviving record: a card for a pay-per-view on 27 April 2003 went up with a roleplay limit and a deadline, and nothing after it survives.

Storylines

Jay Krazy's crusade Written by AI, 2026

At the first-ever Immortality, J beat John Dumas in the tournament's opening round and then shoved Baz around the ring in the afterglow — until "The Turnbuckle" Jay Krazy ran in unannounced and threw J over the top rope for it. Backstage the same night, Blood Man confronted Jay Krazy over the interference in the fed's own bar; the two brawled until police arrived and hauled them both away, and Baz used the arrest to postpone their scheduled match rather than run it a man short.

The rematch never happened. Instead, at WrestleFest, Blood Man and J won the vacant Tag Team Championship by eliminating John Dumas and Chaos — pinning Dumas with a chair Blood Man swung behind the referee's back — and were toasting the win with beers when Jay Krazy ran back in to attack them, joined by Dumas and Chaos for a three-on-two beatdown. Hours later, Jay Krazy challenged Turbulence for the vacant Universal Championship in the tournament final. The referee called it for Jay Krazy by disqualification when Turbulence hit him with a chair, but Baz overruled the call from his own office by video feed and ordered the match to continue with no disqualification at all; Turbulence busted Jay Krazy open on the Spanish announce table and finished him with a metal baseball bat to become the fed's first-ever champion. Dumas ran in one last time as the show closed, dropping Turbulence with his own Sudden Stop — then draped the new champion's own belt over Jay Krazy's unconscious body instead of Turbulence's shoulder.
