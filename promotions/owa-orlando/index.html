A Florida e-fed that filed itself as a publicly traded company, crowned six champions inside eighteen months, and left its first pay-per-view carded in full with only two of its eight outcomes ever written down. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Orlando Wrestling Alliance filed itself, on its own wiki, as an American publicly traded, privately controlled entertainment company trading as OWA Wrestling — founded February 2016, headquartered in Tampa, Florida, area served Orlando. Mason Wolfe is named its founder, owner and president; Aaron Strome its chief executive, Dominic Duvalle its vice-president, and Daniel and Mariella Riley the general managers of its only show.

That show was Anarchy. It debuted 2 March 2016 and crowned the first OWA Heavyweight Champion on its own first night — Tyler Chandler, out of an open battle royal — with Clarissa Evans crowned the first Women's Champion the same evening. A week later, on 9 March 2016, the federation booked Chandler's first singles title defense and two fatal four-ways to name contenders, and then went dark. It came back in January 2017 with a live event that crowned three more champions and named a fourth contender, moved Anarchy to Mondays and then to Thursdays, and unveiled a United Kingdom Championship with a thirty-two-man tournament to fill it. None of that show survives as its own document either — nor do the four further episodes the record can still date and partly reconstruct from wrestlers' own later bios: the 23 January restart, where Angelita Henderson and Chris Dunn both won their way into title shots; 30 January, where a Champion vs. Champion match was interrupted by the debuting Hunter Gage laying out Chandler; 9 February, where Adam Dunn won a Universal #1 contendership; and 23 February, where Ethan Williams beat Gage in his own debut.

Six championships are named in its own record — Heavyweight, Women's, Intercontinental, Universal, Tag Team and United Kingdom — and four of them carry a title history. None of those histories runs past two reigns on the wiki's own page; the Tag Team and United Kingdom pages never got past the words “coming soon.”

Rage in the Cage, held 26 February 2017 at the Amway Center in Orlando, was the federation's first pay-per-view, and every match on it was fought inside a steel cage. The card survives in full, seven matches deep plus a pre-show, and is structured whole below. No results page was ever written for it, and six of its eight outcomes are lost, but two survive in wrestlers' own later bios — Brett Edgar's own bio card records him and Shane Miller winning the first OWA Tag Team Championship on the kickoff, and Angelita Henderson's own bio records her taking the Women's Championship from Erin Spencer-Wolfe in the card's second match, a title change the federation's own championship ledger — last revised before the show — never caught up to. The card's own next-event field points at a Rage in the Cage (2018) that has no page.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. The wiki's articles stop in November 2017, and more than half the wrestlers the federation billed on its own roster never got past a page reading “coming soon.”

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a cold read of every served document — the roster and title pages, the show-history article, the Rage in the Cage card, and every wrestler's own bio — rather than assembled from the championship pages alone.

The Dunn brothers Written by AI, 2026

Adam Dunn and Chris Dunn chased two different titles through the federation's own record without either ever winning one. Adam came up short against Tyler Chandler twice for the Heavyweight Championship in OWA's first two shows, then lost the January 2017 three-way #1 contenders match that made Alex Madsen the next contender — a match his own brother Chris also lost. Weeks later Chris won a Four Way Elimination for a shot at the Intercontinental Championship by superkicking Adam by accident and eliminating him from the match, then lost the title itself to Mason Wolfe inside a cage at Rage in the Cage. Adam's own consolation was a Universal #1 contendership won at the 9 February 2017 Anarchy, a shot the card's own text records him also losing.

Angelita Henderson's rise Written by AI, 2026

Angelita Henderson won her way to the OWA Women's Championship in two steps the wiki's own championship page never recorded. On the 23 January 2017 Anarchy she beat Clarissa Evans and Lexie Banks in a three-way match to become the next contender, then took the title itself from Erin Spencer-Wolfe in a steel cage on the second match of Rage in the Cage — a title change the federation's own ledger, last revised before the show, never caught up to and is recovered here only from Henderson's own later bio.

Hunter Gage's debut Written by AI, 2026

Hunter Gage entered the record as an attacker before he ever wrestled: on the 30 January 2017 Anarchy he laid out Tyler Chandler mid-match during a Champion vs. Champion bout between Chandler and Owen Grady, and Chandler demanded the fight that followed. Gage's own in-ring debut came three weeks later against Ethan Williams in a Pick Your Poison match Chandler himself picked him for — a match Gage lost off a Chandler distraction — before the two finally met for the Heavyweight Championship inside a cage at Rage in the Cage, a match the surviving record never rules on.

Made in Detroit's title win Written by AI, 2026

Brett Edgar and Shane Miller, high-school friends who signed together as Made in Detroit, are the only pairing in the federation's own record to win a title on the one pay-per-view it ran: a triple-threat tornado cage match against Jake Anderson & Duke Knight and the Connolly Bros on the Rage in the Cage kickoff, recovered only because Edgar's own bio card happened to state the result.
