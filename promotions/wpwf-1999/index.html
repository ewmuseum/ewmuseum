Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Professional Wrestling Federation published a corrected version of its own handbook on 24 April 1999, an occasion its own introduction used to pitch itself against the hobby's bad habits — federations that closed without warning, ran late, or simply jobbed the affair — and to promise wrestlers a long-term home instead.

Its on-screen front office was built on rivalry: president Gabriel Skinner, who wanted good matches regardless of who won, and vice-president and commissioner Rick Raymond, who loved money more than wrestling and never saw eye to eye with him. The two split their flagship show, Saturday Night Style, into two contrasting hour-long halves, one run by each man, with Rick Raymond's own Style Championship defended there every week by his own decree.

A full staff of commentators, reporters, referees and ring announcers filled out the promotion on paper — face commentator Marvin Samuels and heel commentator “The Icon” Shawn Steele; in-ring host Bernard Fedderwinkle; rival roving reporters Jade Nightingale and Cherry Divine, each loyal to a different president; referees Norman Watts, Antonio Rivera and Sam Danson; and a trio of ring announcers, Bob Bobson, Jim Jimson and Rob Robson, whose operatic training let them sing the anthem at pay-per-views. Five championships were on offer — the Style, Tag Team, Extreme, Intercontinental and WPWF World titles — across a calendar of six annual pay-per-views running from New Year's Revolution in January to the Day of Reckoning in November.

Behind the fiction, the handbook credited a real roleplay manager, angle manager and overall adjudicator running the promotion day to day; a further post, five weeks later on 31 May 1999, pleaded for match writers after two of the promotion's three real co-presidents had left. By that account WPWF had already run its first card, calling it more than a success.

No card result, roster, or title change survives to confirm it, and how or whether the WPWF outlasted that plea is not known.
