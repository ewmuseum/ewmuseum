Pacific Coast Wrestling Rules ---- NUMBER OF CHARACTERS Each handler will be allowed to handler no more than three active wrestlers at a time, this does not include managers. We've put a limit at three to keep things balanced with no one person who's got five guys in a stable from running amok. Occasionally we understand that a manager may become involved in a wrestling match, and those occasions will be dealt with on a one-by-one basis. ROLEPLAYING Like any booked fed, PACIFIC COAST WRESTLING decisions most of the time are based on who's doing the best job roleplaying their character. There are two schools of thought when it comes to roleplaying -- quantity and quality. Here in the PCW we're looking for a melding of both: QUANTITY: To be successful in PACIFIC COAST WRESTLING you are going to need to be seen/heard on a regular basis. That means you have to commit at least three roleplays a week. We fully understand that there are weeks when real life can stand in the way and occasionally falling under the three roleplays a week won't get you booted out, but it can significantly drop your stock. If something major happens outside the world of e-wrestling simply let us know that way we don't job you out due to non-participation. QUALITY: Here in PACIFIC COAST WRESTLING posting ten three-line interviews a week won't get you too far. We'd appreciate your enthusiasm, and you will surely win some matches this way, but you won't win any titles. Take some time. Set up the scene of the interview. Say something other than 'I'm going to beat you so bad it's going to hurt alot!'. Ok, that may be an exaggeration, but the guy posting 3-4 HIGH QUALITY roleplays a week is going to beat the ten three-line guys nine times out of ten. If you think you could use a little bit of help with your roleplaying Vice-President [name withheld] is aboard to help out with just such things, so don't be afraid to ask. Even the best roleplayers could use a little advice. There are some definite do's and don't's when it comes to roleplaying, and it will be Vice-President [name withheld]'s job to 'police' such actions. Breaking a rule once isn't going to get you kicked out of PACIFIC COAST WRESTLING, but repeatedly breaking of rules will get you jobbed like no tomorrow and eventually can lead to expulsion. Things to stay away from: OVER-USE OF SWEARING: We don't want to cut out ALL swearing, but if can't be said on RAW don't say it here. If it's a part of the character use such useful phrases such as "fu*#ing a$$hole". It's implied and nobody is offended. RACIST REMARKS: Will not be tolerated. This will get you jobbed and a stiff reprimand from the front office. Don't tell us 'it's a part of the character/angle' because we don't want those type of characters or angles here. MULTIPLE MISSPELLINGS: It happens, but if you do it repeatedly it just looks bad. This won't necessarily get you jobbed, but a 'word of advice' will come from Devin. Obviously, exceptions will be made if the spelling is a reflection of how your character actually talks. Eg- ICE CUBE: Yo, weez gonna go sliz-am some fo-ties. OUT-OF-CHARACTER COMMENTS: If you're throwing a simple 'sorry for the non-participation, been gone for a week' at the beginning or the end of an interview that's alright, but a full-blown OOC comment directed at a handler or the front office will get you booted, no questions asked. And don't try to tell us it was a 'shoot interview' because we don't buy it. USING OTHER CHARACTERS ON YOUR INTERVIEWS: Unless you have permission to do so, don't. You'll get jobbed. Jobbed badly. Just as a closing note to all of this roleplaying talk, giving 'on card' interviews is a good way to get what you're saying across to the entire fed, and we here in the front office look highly upon these when it comes time to deciding who will be champion. You still need to participate on the guestbook, but these interviews can give you definite 'brownie points'. STRATS/SNEAK ATTACKS We've taken the time to put a document for match strats and sneak attacks up on the web, so we encourage you to take advantage of both: STRATS: In a tough match to decide, if one guy sends in a strat and another guy doesn't we'd be more likely to give the win to the guy who sent one in. In a match that should be a squash, if the underdog sends in a fantastic stat and the favorite sends in nothing, there just might be an upset on the PCW horizon. In short -- send 'em in for all your matches. SNEAK ATTACKS: First off, understand that just because you send in a sneak attack it doesn't mean that it will necessarily be used. But also understand that it isn't going to happen if you don't send one in. For a sneak attack to happen there needs to be two things: (1) most importantly, a reason -- there must be a history or a history in the making; and (2) impress us -- hitting a guy with a chair after coming down the aisle isn't going to cut it; the more description and originality the better. And lastly, you can't kill a guy. So don't ask. LOOSING A MATCH You're going to loose. Sometimes other factors other than roleplaying will dictate who wins and looses. Sometimes when you loose it's so the front office can see how you deal with a loss or even a loosing streak. Handle yourself well in a loss will definitely get you far. Handle yourself poorly..... expect to loose again. We understand that sometimes you're going to be upset with a loss, and an e-mail to the front office asking why can be a good way to vent. But don't do this every time you loose, and handle yourself with maturity when you do it. Pose an honest question and you will receive an honest answer. But if you come screaming at us that you 'JOBBED FOR NO REASON DAMNIT!' then you will job for a reason in your next match and you'll know exactly why. JOBBING Throughout these rules the word 'job' is used several times, and let us take a few lines to explain what that means: when you job you loose BADLY, and usually for a reason. A loss is not a job. A job is when you get it handed to you in record time. The Golden Rules There's two of 'em: (1) Have fun. This is just a game and not real life. Remember that. (2) Respect other handlers. If you deserve to have your time in the sun, you will. Don't attack somebody because they're on top and you never have been because that person is there for a reason. They've earned it. If you've been on top and fallen don't attack the handler at the top -- you had your turn, now it's their's. Just remember that everybody deserves their turn, get in line and wait for yours. ---- The PCW Front Office ---- ©1998 PCW, a Punk Ass Production.|Mail PCW [image: setstats]
