A GeoCities e-fed that booked its own wrestling world tours — a Pacific Coast Highway Tour and a Coastal Tour, complete with roommate pairings and random on-the-road events — and named a three-wrestler stable for the real hip-hop group Westside Connection, down to sharing its entrance song. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pacific Coast Wrestling opened on GeoCities space in 1998 with a twenty-nine-wrestler roster and an unusually detailed operating plan for a small e-fed: a rotating six-week television schedule (Coastal Report, Primetime, Main Event, Tuesday Trifecta, Friday Night Freight Train) built around four championships, all four of which were decided in one sitting at the fed's own mega pay-per-view, "One Night in Hollywood," on 16 August 1998 — Hale won the Heavyweight title in an eight-man tournament, Daft Vader the Cruiserweight in an eight-man gauntlet, Colossus the Coastal title in a battle royal, and the stable the Westside Connection the Tag Team titles in a four-team ladder match.

The fed's own signature idea was the road trip: two competing, named touring formats that took its roster away from its regular schedule entirely. Eight wrestlers went out on the Pacific Coast Highway Tour, paired off as roommates required to tag together and give at least one joint interview, subject to random Oregon Trail-style misfortune along the way; everyone else went on the Coastal Tour, culminating in a two-day "Coastal Carnival" that fed a challenger to the reigning Coastal Champion. Both tours ended in tournaments — the Freight Train and the Carnival — that the fed's own rules called the real measuring stick of a wrestler's run.

Three of its own wrestlers — DW, Ice Cube, and Mack 10, the tag-title-winning Westside Connection — take both their names and their shared entrance music from the real hip-hop group of the same name, a borrowing the roster page never explains and never needs to.

The fed's own results index bills twenty separate cards by name and date, from a 4 July 1998 Main Event through a 21 September 1998 Monday Night Special, and its own rankings page runs current through 17 September — by which point Boche is billed the Coastal Champion, a title change from Colossus's own August win that the surviving record never shows happening. None of the fed's own actual results pages survive in this capture; what survives is the paperwork around them — the roster, the rules, the schedule, and the title ledger — and nothing dated after 21 September 1998.

The schedule page itself goes further than the results index for three of those twenty cards, billing full matchups rather than a bare name and date: an eight-match Coastal Report for 20 September, all four PCW championships defended plus three dark matches on the Monday Night Special for 21 September, and an eight-man Friday Night Freight Train tournament field for 25 September. None of the three carries a result — only who was billed — but together they name seventeen wrestlers and a tag team, the Stone Crushers, absent from the roster page itself.
