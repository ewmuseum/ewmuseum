Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Electronic Action Packed Wrestling formed on 4 July 1997 as a merger of two co-presidents' own prior federations: one had previously run a fed that “folded due to the lack of help,” the other had run a promotion that, in its own words, “also was great but folded once again due to the lack of help.” Together the two promised something plainer this time — not the longest cards or the longest pay-per-views, but, in the promotion's own slogan, “Action Packed, to the max.”

Its own constitution, circulated a week later, barred “Out of the World” gimmicks by name (“no plumbers or clowns”) and anyone styled after a real performer (“no Stone Cold's, Nature Boy's, or Canadian Crippler's”), capped the roster at 24 singles wrestlers and 8 tag teams, and structured four titles: World Heavyweight, National Heavyweight, Flyweight, and a World Tag Team championship. Its own masthead named its broadcast presenters as explicitly fictional: co-presidents Ed Liss and Lawrence Allen, based out of a fictional EAPW Towers in Seattle, commentator Doug Jones, correspondent Vince Beard, and ring announcer Joeboo.

Its debut card, over a month later, aired under an alternate on-air brand, Action Packed Online Wrestling, styled “Wednesday Wallop” rather than the “Sunday Showdown” the promotion had named as its actual weekly show. The opening match, billed for the promotion's own combined National Heavyweight title, started as a brawl in the locker room before the bell ever rang: reigning champion Pipe Bomb dominated challenger TK Maniac all the way to the ring, only for Maniac to counter a corner charge into a back drop over the top rope and finish him moments later with a legdrop, taking the title in an upset.

No document later than that debut card survives. Whether “Sunday Showdown,” the show the promotion had actually named as its main weekly card, ever aired isn't in the surviving record.
