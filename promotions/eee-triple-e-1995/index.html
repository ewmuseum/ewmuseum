Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme E-mail E-Wrestling introduced itself on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 1 November 1995 with twenty-six open spots and a pitch aimed squarely at an ECW-era crowd: “no whimps or chics, sorry!” It promised tournaments for World, International, Continental and Television titles, plus a World Tag Team tournament, and billed itself as the first fed to allow two “superpower teams” modeled on the Horsemen and the Million Dollar Corporation — one heel, one face.

By 9 November the roster the operator had planned at thirty wrestlers had grown to forty-eight, and he put out an open call for help writing matches, warning he might have to cut shows or cut wrestlers without it. He signed his posts across the run as PsYcHo, and later under a fuller “Sir Psycho Sexy” signature.

EEE's studio show, “No-Action Zone,” premiered 5 November under hosts Toad Pettingzoo and Dok Hayes and laid out all four title brackets at once: Psycho against “Hurricane” Bobby Russo led the Continental field; the Majestics, Scotty Kage and Chris Pillon, managed by the Gypsy, entered the tag tournament as its top seed against the Midnight Rockers, X and the Detroit Bad Boys; Super D, the Masked Marauder, Scarlet Dagger and Bates Motel headlined the International bracket; and the World title field paired the Professional, Canadian Canuck, the Specialist and Apocalypse against Stan Manson, Cactus Jackson, Lonestar and the Prince of Pain. All four finals were slated for the same card, a five-man-team “Survivor Sequence.”

The same episode carried the newly-signed Stan “The Lariat” Manson ambushing reporter Jeffrey Schiavone at his car, spitting chewing tobacco on Schiavone's shoe and vowing to win the World Championship after being “run outta more federations than toads have warts.”

On 8 December PsYcHo announced EEE “officially for sale. For a cost of $0,” thanking the members who had offered to help write matches and setting a shutdown date of 16 December if no one took the offer. Four days later a newer community member, signing only “Roe,” wrote back asking to take the reins, admitting he would need help just to build and run a homepage. What became of that offer, or of EEE itself, is not in the surviving record.
