Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Alliance of Wrestling Federations opened its own first broadcast with four announcers, each introducing themselves as the sitting announcer of a separate member federation. Its own matches are billed "interfederational," and an on-air rule ties title eligibility to standing "in all it's leagues" — a formal umbrella, not a loose cross-promotion.

Two supra-fed titles were contested on the alliance's own first event, each built by unifying member feds' own champions. Blade, V.I.G., The Charm, and Golden One — each entering as their own member fed's reigning television champion — fought over the AOWF Television Championship in a match the held broadcast cuts off before declaring a winner. Nightshade won a scored four-way main event over Shame, Savage Warrior, and Chaos to become the first-ever AOWF World Heavyweight Champion, introduced by his new manager, Dot.

By its own second event, the alliance had grown to include at least three further member federations — JWA, BEW, and the already-held Pioneer Wrestling Association (PWA), confirmed by name-and-bio match against three of its own billed wrestlers. AOWF_2 opened with every title declared vacant and staged a King of Extreme Tournament (Crypt Killer, Shattered Dreams, Schoolhouse, and Scaffold matches) alongside a nine-category 1999 awards show and a new Ladder Match for the AOWF Television Championship, won by Tommy Riley.
