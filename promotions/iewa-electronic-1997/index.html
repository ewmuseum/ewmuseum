Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Electronic Wrestling Association had been running since September 1997, by its own account, when its recruitment posts began circulating on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy that winter, touting “great talent such as the Prophets of Rage, the Harlequins, Medusa Rage, the Siberian Express, 'Beautiful' Brad Brilliance,” and more, and pointing newcomers to a homepage at members.tripod.com.

Its flagship show, Monday Night Mania, opened to Guns N' Roses' “Welcome to the Jungle” over a clip package of prior cards, and its 8 December 1997 broadcast played from a packed Great Western Forum in Los Angeles, dance routines set to the Rocky Horror Picture Show theme, and crowd signs joking that its own president was a bigger threat than the real wrestling world's Vince McMahon or Eric Bischoff. A three-person broadcast team called the action, and the show closed on a main event pitting the reigning World Women's Heavyweight Champion, Napualokelani, against an impressive newcomer, Jessica Starbird.

Six championships ran through the promotion by that broadcast: the World Women's Heavyweight, Television, World Tag Team, Women's Skyweight, Women's Intercontinental, and Women's Tag Team titles. That same card saw the tag team Hysteria turn back a returning former champion, the Japan Connection, to the outrage of a debuting duo calling themselves the Disciples of Rage, who challenged the champions to a cage match on the spot.

The Starbird rematch, held 19 December 1997, ended in a disqualification: Jun Shiratori attacked Starbird before the bell, and Napualokelani threw salt in her face mid-match to keep the belt, prompting a run-in from an allied fed's own champion, the GDWA's Officer May Order, to see Starbird safely out of the ring. The title stayed with Napualokelani on the finish. The promotion staged its own touring pay-per-view calendar across 1998 as far afield as Disneyworld, Rome, Egypt, and Quebec.

Its own women's division styled itself as an open, cross-fed invitational: an October 1997 battle royal for the same World Women's Heavyweight title drew entrants sent in by allied feds SJPW, GDWA, and the Modern-Day Wrestling Alliance, each cleared through their own presidents to compete.

A community notice of 17 February 1999 reported that the fed's own head had shut the promotion down on 8 February 1999, citing a busy schedule and a lack of match writers, after issuing an ultimatum a week earlier that he needed help running it or would close it. A wrap-up or tribute card was floated as a possibility but is not attested as ever having run; the surviving record ends with that notice.
