A February-to-June 1997 Usenet federation whose Saturday Night broadcast defended three titles, before its own operator went on to open a successor promotion that June. Three documents survive; seven wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Extreme Global Wrestling Federation announced itself on 18 February 1997 as “the most extreme fed on the net,” carrying six singles titles and six tag titles and claiming, in the same breath, to have “been established for almost a year” already. It billed itself as booked and roleplay-driven, open only to “serious” players willing to write four roleplays a week, and ran every submission through its own homepage rather than by mail, “so you can check out the homepage and roleplay in the same place.”

A March newsletter titled “THE DYNASTY HAS ARRIVED” restated the same pitch — booked, original characters only, “a very well thought out character as well as thought out original roleplays” required to succeed — and called itself “The Dynasty of all E-Feds,” a line its own Saturday Night broadcast repeated as its copyright tagline that same week.

That broadcast, on 8 March 1997, opened with an in-fiction teaser for a coming pay-per-view, Armegedon, and ran two of the federation's own title matches: the Studlys took the Rocky Mountain Championship from the Irish Inmates in a tag match wrecked by run-ins from three different outside parties, and Senator Johnson beat Ares for a Rocky Mountain title of his own after a rival, Rainman, ran in and showed him a photograph of his kidnapped girlfriend, Miss Liberty. The same card carried a friend-versus-friend match between Blaro and G-Funk that ended with G-Funk grabbing the microphone in tears and quitting the promotion on camera.

The same operator's own founding post for a successor promotion, on 12 June 1997, refers to “the EGWF and the WWCWF” in the past tense — the only trace the surviving record holds of how or when the Extreme Global Wrestling Federation actually closed.
