A Bucknell-run, real-wrestler-portrayal TNM e-fed whose own first house show ran the first round and semifinals of a nine-man World Title tournament, crowning Dr. Doom champion by default after the other finalist was written out with an in-fiction injury. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Total Championship Wrestling opened on 22 October 1996 with a pitch built against its own genre: “Tired of all the feds using real wrestlers that simply paste TNM's output onto the page? Then join TCW... I plan on writing some great commentary to go with the match.” It launched with three titles of its own — the World, World Tag Team and North American Championships — two weekly broadcasts, Tuesday Night Extreme and Saturday Night Fever, and a monthly pay-per-view already named for its first outing: Triangles of Terror.

Its own first Saturday Night Fever, on 9 November, opened before a claimed 5,700 fans at its own arena, the Pyramid, with a World Title tournament: Dr. Doom, managed by Maniac Bob, beat Fabio Erotic in the opener with brass knuckles, then outlasted Jack the Ripper in the semifinal. On the other side of the bracket, Masked Bulldog beat Scoult by battering him with a roadsign after an Atomic Bulldog off the ring apron onto the concrete floor, but the finish left him with a legitimate-in-fiction laryngeal injury that forced him out of the scheduled final. Its own president, Dan Fisher, awarded the vacant title to Dr. Doom outright rather than delay the show, with a rematch against a healthy Masked Bulldog ordered for the following week.

The North American Championship was settled by an eighteen-man battle royal on Tuesday Night Extreme (19 November), won by Titanacus over the Flashmaster. The Tag Team Championship changed hands three times in the promotion's first month, passing between Southern Thunder and the Dallas Connection — members of a rival faction, the Palace of Power, calling on interference from their own valet, Jessica, more than once along the way.

All three titles were defended on Triangles of Terror itself, 1 December 1996 at the Bryce Jordan Center: Titanacus retained the North American belt over the Flashmaster with the Moscow Spike; Southern Thunder took the Tag Team Championship back from the Dallas Connection; and Dr. Doom kept the World Title by disqualification over Brandon Charm. The card's own namesake event closed the night — a nine-man gauntlet of gimmick matches decided by spinning a wheel of stipulations, dog collar and dungeon matches among them — won outright by Playboy Tony Flint, who earned a World Title shot at a January pay-per-view billed as a Battle Bowl tournament in Philadelphia.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its surviving documents run from that October founding through the 1 December pay-per-view; the promised Battle Bowl and the Doom–Flint title match survive only as an announcement.
