A real-roster fed of the summer of 2002 that billed WWF and WCW performers under their own names with Vince McMahon as its announced owner, ran three weekly Raws from 8 July with all seven of its titles filled on the first night, and kept its roster turning over through public hirings and firings and an nWo stable in the tag title chase. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Angle Based Fed opened on 26 June 2002 with a post announcing Vince McMahon as its “100% owner”, and its own application page set one rule: real wrestlers only, with no roleplay limits, one show a week on Monday night, and each applicant expected to work out their own angles, “the sillier the better”.

Its news desk, which claimed to have stolen the owner’s fax machine, filled the first days with signings: a starting roster of forty-five names and a tag team, from Hulk Hogan, the Undertaker and Triple H to Mick Foley and Rico. On 30 June a Cruiserweight Title and a Women’s Title joined its other belts, which had all begun vacant.

Its first Raw, from Madison Square Garden on 8 July, filled all seven: battle royals of eleven, seven, twelve and fourteen men for the Euro, Cruiserweight, Continental and World Titles, won by Dusty Rhodes, Rico, Kane and Hulk Hogan, and four-way matches for the Women’s, Hardcore and Tag Team belts, won by Stephanie McMahon, The Sandman, and Edge and Christian. Ted Dibiase joined Vince McMahon’s re-formed Corporation the same night.

Two more Raws followed, on 15 July and, from the Savvis Center in St. Louis, on 22 July. Randy Savage took the Continental Title from Kane and Goldust the Euro Title from Dusty Rhodes on the second; The Outsiders took the Tag Team Titles from Edge and Christian on the third, the night its news post billed as the nWo’s chance at them; Hulk Hogan held the World Title through both. The rest of the air time went to an eight-man tournament for a Euro Title shot, a retirement farewell, La Parka’s arrival and a run of comic sketches in which Rico tried to restyle him.

Between the shows the roster turned over by public notice: hirings and retirings on 8 July, five names fired or resigned on 17 July, and fourteen released after the third Raw with hirings promised within 24 hours.

Its surviving news runs from 26 June to 22 July 2002. A card for a Raw of 29 July at the Pepsi Center in Denver survives beside it — Mick Foley defending the Hardcore Title in a Hell in a Cell, Rico the Cruiserweight Title, The Outsiders the Tag Team Titles, and a main event for the number-one contendership to the World Title — but no write-up of that night and no closing notice do, and how the federation ended isn’t in the surviving record.
