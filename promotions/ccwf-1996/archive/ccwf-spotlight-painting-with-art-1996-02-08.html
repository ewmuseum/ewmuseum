CCWF Spotlight: Painting with Art

8 February 1996
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(Elevator Music begins...) (Camera fades in to an "Art Studio". A man with a gotee, sunglasses, a black beret and a blue smock, is painting a mural on the wall. It's two wrestler's engaged in combat, but for heads, both wrestlers have large yellow happy faces.) Announcer: And now it's time for "Painting with Art". (Music fades away. There are murmurs from the crowd, as no one really knows what to think) Art (Faces audience): Oh, hello. Just working on my lastest masterpiece. I call it "Vitamin Q". Well, welcome to "Painting with Art", and that's me, Art D'eco. "Painting with Art" is a show where I have a chance to chat with a superstar of the CCWF. And since tonight is our first ever show, I have a very special guest. Would everyone please extend a warm round of appaluse to the current CCWF TV Champion, SKY WALKER! ("Cars" by Gary Numan starts up. SW, clad in a sequined silver bodysuit with scarlet leggings and sleeves, plus his scarlet mask, enters with the TV belt. He pauses in front of "Vitamin Q" as the song fades out, and steps back a step or two to get a broader view of the painting.) Art: Well, Mr. Walker, may I call you Mr. Walker? SW: I think "Sky" will suffice. Art: Okay, Sky then, I just have to say, it's an honour to have the first ever TV Champion on my first ever episode of Painting with Art. SW: I appreciate being invited on your program. I know there's no shortage of CCWF stars who would love the opportunity to get up here and say whatever's on their minds. Art: For those of you who do not know, Sky Walker is a relative newcomer to the sport. At least, I assume he is, since I can't see under the mask. He's wrestled briefly in the now-defunct IWS and in the recent World E-Wrestling Tournament, but he seems to have come into his own here in the CCWF. Mr. Walker, do you feel your relative lack of experience you bring with you into the CCWF puts you at some disadvantage against your competition? SW: It does worry me that I may not know all of the tricks that some of the veterans have earned over the years, but there really isn't anything that I can do about that right now. There's no way to gain that experience apart from getting in the ring and hooking with as many different types of opponents as I can. That's why I feel so fortunate to have won the TV Title, because I don't know I'm facing on any given night. Therefore I can't pick and choose my opposition; I' m forced to face types of opponents I haven't faced yet. Art: Your first title defense, against Hasa, was a very close match. Do you find it is difficult to prepare yourself for a title defense against a random opponent? SW: As long as I hold this belt, I really can't prepare for my opposition the same way other people can. But since I'm one of the smaller guys around, I generally assume that my opponent will be more powerful, slower, and more likely to tire early. So at least I can go into most matches with at least a basic strategy. As for Hasa, I was relieved to come away with the win, but it really didn't end the way I'd like. Art: Mm-hmm. Yes, well, Sensei Yamamoto attributes Hasa's defeat to outside interference during the match. How do you feel about outside interference? Is it a big problem in the CCWF as Sensei Yamamoto suggests? SW: It seems to be a problem wherever I go. Magus, Moo Kame, these guys have been at ringside for every single match I've had. I don't know what the purpose is, really. I've done it when they fight, mostly to let them know that they aren't going to intimidate me in this way. But when Magus showed up last time, there was also Hasa to consider. In my opinion, Hasa was an innocent bystander. I know there was no blatant interference, nor was there any contact, but it obviously broke his concentration. I will stand before you now and say in all honesty that if Hasa hadn't been so distracted, the CCWF would have a new TV Champion. I don't like what happened, and if I have any influence with the officers of the CCWF, I will exert it to ensure that Hasa gets another chance. Art: Now, Mr. Walker, even before winning the TV Championship at King of The Mountain, you've had various run-ins with three individuals, Moo Kame, Steve "The Slasher" Latini, and Marcus Magus. What's in the future, with respect to these three individuals? SW: I don't know what Magus' problem is, but it'll be solved soon enough.As for the Slasher, he and I have something of a history. The last time I appeared on an interview program like this, the Slasher showed up, after I'd already taken a rabbit punch, and gave a few kicks in the ribs while I was down. A match was signed, but the league went under a week before our first scheduled meeting. I've waited for an opportunity to get revenge ever since. Moo Kame, on the other hand,has something that I want. In the World E-wrestling Tournament, he beat me fair and square, 1-2-3 in the middle of the ring. I've wanted a rematch ever since, to prove myself his equal. Instead, it somehow degenerated into this game of watching each other's matches from much too close. Right now, I feel like Moo is the champion and I'm the challenger, and I won't be satisfied until the opportunity comes to meet him one more time. Unlike the other two, Moo Kame is a man I can respect, and I don't feel any animosity toward him. It's just a case of two men who think they are the best young technical wrestler around today. We can't both be the best at what we do, but I hope our competition with each other isn't tarnished by the kind of anger and contempt that fuels my situations with Latini and Magus. Moo Kame, as competitors or partners, you and I can revolutionize this sport. I promise you that any match between us will 100% a clean, pure, athletic contest, move for move, my aerial assault vs. your martial arts. No tricks, no third parties, just the two best technical fighters the CCWF has seen putting on 4* matches. Art: Mr. Sky Walker, thank you very much for being on "Painting with Art".It has been a real pleasure talking with you. SW: Thanks for giving me the chance to say what I needed to say. Art: Ladies and gentlemen, let's hear a big round of appaluse for a gentleman and an athlete, Mr. SKY WALKER! (Huge Pop from the crowd as"Cars" by Gary Numan starts again) Art: (Over the roar of crowd) Join us next week on "Painting with Art" when I talk to Sensei Yamamoto and the Golden Dragons. (To Sky Walker, as music, lights, and interview fadeout) Have you seen my new Masterpiece? Yes, I am very proud of this one...
