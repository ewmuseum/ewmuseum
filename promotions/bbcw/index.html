Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Big Balla Championship Wrestling's surviving record opens with a 19 September 1997 call for an artist to draw a gallery of its wrestlers, followed three days later by a pitch for “good roleplayers for original wrestlers.” By 1 October it had a schedule: two weekly broadcasts, Whup Ass Wednesday Night and Drive By Saturday Night, plus a monthly pay-per-view, running out of arenas in Arizona, Michigan, and Illinois.

It staged its own rivalry with the bigger promotions literally. At Kliq Kollision 97 that November, two chartered buses of BBCW fans pulled up outside a sold-out WCW World War 3 in Auburn Hills, Michigan, and “President Davis” climbed onto the roof of one to make the pitch on camera — “THEE Alternative” to “the Old Boys” — before a helicopter dropped BBCW merchandise over the crowd and the chant “BBCW! BBCW!” carried them into the arena.

That same card settled a stable war that had grown out of a feud between two founding factions, the Castaways and the Tourniquet, into a mass battle royal drawing in a dozen more crews at once — No Limit Souljahs, 3WO, Mo Thugs, IFS, Da Posse, and two separate Wildcard teams among them — fought out across four rings simultaneously.

A month after its first card, back at the arena in Phoenix where it began, Trick or Treat to a Beatdown crowned the promotion's first World Heavyweight Champion in a ten-man Beatdown Match, on a card that also settled its World Tag Team, United States, United States Tag Team, Television, and Intercontinental championships in the same night.

Its last surviving trace is a passing mention dated 29 December 1997. No closure document survives.
