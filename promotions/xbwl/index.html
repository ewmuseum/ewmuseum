A long-running Usenet e-fed, renamed from an earlier USWL, running three weekly cards and quarterly pay-per-views behind a five-title championship ladder with its own last-ten-matches ranking system. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

XBWL traces its own history back to the USWL, opened on AOL and renamed within its first year under a motto that answered its own joke: “we don't claim to be extreme, we don't claim to be brutal, we claim to be X-TREMELY BRUTAL!” Its own two surviving founding accounts, a June 1999 recruitment notice and a September 1999 handbook, disagree on the exact founding year but agree on the story: the same league under an earlier name, carrying members who had been there, in the handbook's own words, “since day one.”

That handbook set out a booked league, not a simulated one — “If you want to win your matches you HAVE to roleplay! A simulator will NOT help you win a match in this league except for the battle royal card” — ranked wrestlers on their own last ten matches so newcomers wouldn't be buried under veterans' own win totals, and ran three cards a week (a Tuesday results sheet, a Thursday battle-royal special feeding Title Points toward a shot, and a Saturday sheet) around four major and eight minor pay-per-views a year. Its title ladder climbed from the North American Championship, which “all new members shoot for,” through the International and Hardcore titles to the World Championship, closed to anyone who hadn't held one of the two beneath it. Its own listed staff split real from “fake”: on screen, Awesome Australian sat as president and Mad Jester as commissioner, with referees A.P. Enis and Richard Cranium and commentary from The Wrestling Doctor and Johnny Diamond.

On 6 October 1999 Legacy, self-billed as “the most hated man in XBWL history,” captured the World Heavyweight Championship for a fifth time, ending Matt “Da Mac” Austin's reign with a finisher the promotion's own news report credited with two prior careers, TNT “Nick Stevenson” and ORION. Three days later North American Champion Chris Calhoun, 4-1 and heading into a rematch, cut a defiant promo insisting he'd beaten his challengers before and would again.

The surviving record runs unbroken to that point, with no closing notice anywhere in it and “Mean” Mark Mease — a self-described former World, United States and Internet Champion and former XBWL president — still posting to the newsgroup that same week. How the promotion carried on from there, and how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
