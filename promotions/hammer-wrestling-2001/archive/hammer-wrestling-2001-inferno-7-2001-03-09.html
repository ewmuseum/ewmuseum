Non-taped House Show* OLD OLVERA'S POOL HALL & LOUNGE Petersburg, Virginia * This show is considered a "non-taped house show." In other words, all matches appearing on this card are in a summarized format. House Shows are merely rankings builders -- rewards to players who've participated and worked. The regular cards will have fully written out matches and count a lot more towards a player's record.
Additional Note: the "jobbers" on Inferno are often "borrowed" from other independent federations. Their purpose is to either help the match along for the new talent or just get practice for themselves.
Further Note: any sort of "angle", "feud", or "alliance" that may appear to be formulated by the Hammer booking committee is
ONLY a suggestion. If you disagree with it and don't like it, then it won't stick around past this summarized event. If you do like it or say nothing, it'll stick around and may even leak into the regular cards. This is merely a way of you visualizing a fedhead's suggested angle, feud or alliance.
************* ** RESULTS ** ************* Old Olvera's was not one of the best wrestling enviroments. The biker/trucker crowd seemed a bit resilient to the show, and there'd been quite afew viewers which actually wanted to get in and participate. The chaos included plenty of thrown trash, a few airborne bottles, and a couple of fights breaking out.
Despite their unsavory audience, Hammer did their best to put on a good act. JAKE BISHOP p. TIMMY "THE REJECT" THOMPSON (7:06) -> Catching a fairly good response from the viewing attendants, Bishop came out fairly focused. His opponent for the match was mat technician Timmy
"The Reject" Thompson. -> The huge (and more broad than tall) Bishop flaunted his strength and stamina advantage early during the match. For a great deal of Bishop's brawl, the same focused outlook which he'd originally carried with him to the ring seemed to stick with him. However, after a cheap eyerake followed by a dropkick from Thompson drew some fierce heat from the fans, that cool demeanor shattered and out came a new face for strongman Jake
Bishop: an angry face. -> Bishop got _violent_. He started fighting back stiff and fierce, putting all 350+ pounds of his body into each thrown punch, each suplex, each hold. By the time Bishop had gotten around to capping a resolution on the bout, Thompson looked two dropkicks away from being a dead man.
-> Thompson's body bounced off the mat following a excrutiating and sadistic burning hammer finisher; Bishop had dropped him with an inverted death valley driver, his "Texas Justice". To clarify his win, Bishop merely set his hand over top of Thompson's chest and got a doubtless three-count. Afterwards, he spit on
Thompson, already leaving him crushed like a rotten egg. But Bishop's message to those who had made themselves obstacles in his ongoing Hammer path to fame and fortune was made quite clear: "Don't mess with Jake Bishop."
ANTHONY "THE PREACHER" GIOVANNI s. "THE GOLDEN CHILD" JOHNNY MIDAS (4:20) -> Anthony "The Preacher" Giovanni, in his first match since injuring Hawaiian Crippler a week ago, returned to go head-to-head with one of Hammer's quieter stars, "The Golden Child" Johnny Midas.
Midas also looked set for the match-up, but somewhat taken aback by Giovanni's religious aura.
-> The match moved generally back and forth, both men trading some fast grapples and one or two brawling moves. Although Midas did show a blatant weakness to
Giovanni's fists and feet. Overall, the contest was fairly even, yet it did receive a few jeers and one or two chants of "Boring!"
-> As the match was coming to a close, Midas attempted to lock on a frontface lock, preparing Giovanni for his Golden Touch DDT. However, Giovanni was able to break free with a double-leg takedown and then a
Boston Crab submission. Midas tapped out swiftly and seemed to be screaming with intense pain.
-> When the hold was finally released, Giovanni headed back towards the dressing rooms, praying and thanking his Lord for the generous gift of a wrestling victory.
However, Midas didn't appear to be doing so hot himself. Sitting in the ring, he'd been howling about an injury to his hamstrings. With some assistance from emergency medical technicians, Midas was taken back, obviously injured during the match-up. This would make the second person in a span of two weeks hurt while battling
Anthony Giovanni. "PLAYBOY" AIDAN MADISON p. "PROSTHETIC HEAD" JIMMY MIX (2:27) -> "Playboy" Aidan Madison, new face with Hammer and potentially close to signing a long-term contract with the company, seemed as if he knew what he was doing -- either that, or he's just a little too cocksure for his own good. His first opponent with Hammer was "Prosthetic
Head" Jimmy Mix, a bit of an extreme/hardcore-style wrestler.
-> Madison represented his high-flying background properly, wowing the gradually drunker and drunker audience with springboards, top-ropes, and plenty of other eye-popping high-spots. Madison's relatively small physique obviously allowed him to manuever easily around Mix. Mix, unable to get his hands on Madison if even for a second, found himself severly outmatched. In essence, Mix's extreme style of brawling had been completely ripped from his grasps by the small and speedy "Playboy" Aidan Madison.
-> There was only a little bit of toying around on Madison's part before he squashed Mix down to the mat and followed up with his 360 somersault splash from the top-rope, "The Crash
Landing." With an upturned nose, Madison left the arena the victor, scoring his first Hammer victory and even a handful of jeers.
DAMIAN RIOT p. THE GROUCH (1:09) -> The second debut of the evening was of the 6'11" monster from England, Damian Riot. Riot, obviously one of the largest men that Hammer Wrestling had ever seen, put on an impressive -- _more_ than impressive showing with his first combatant, The Grouch, a 5'8" tall grumpy
Russian.
-> Most of the match revolved around Riot carelessly slapping the foot-shorter Grouch about, almost like a child would mistreat their favorite doll. Every punch tossed at the
Grouch seemed to hit him like a semi-truck. And Riot's arms were like a pair of bulky pythons, constricting its prey into submissive death each time The Grouch would find himself in Riot's deadly grasps.
-> Riot put The Grouch down with one last punch, then lifted him up. He did this to, if anything, add insult to injury. The English punk-rocker scored his official victory by hammering The Grouch with his suplex piledriver, "The Queen is Dead" and then achieving a glorius set of three canvas slaps.
-> On a side note: for just a moment, as Riot was laughing his way around the "contest" (to use the term so loosely since this was _beyond_ the definition of a squash), his eye seemed caught by Hammer's ring announceress, Sherry
Michelle. Post-match, Riot could be seen speaking to his manager, Nathan Rancid, throwing a few ever-so-slight glances at the punk-rock princess, Michelle.
**************** ** MAIN EVENT ** **************** "BAD BOY" BOBBY KAMAKAZEE p. TIM MERCURY (7:11) -> Oddly enough, this was a fairly clean match. No interruptions, no cliffhanger endings; it was just a straight-up match between two tough men. Bobby Kamakazee, one of Hammer's more recent additions, went toe-to-toe with the mantower, Tim Mercury, a man who'd been making some serious contreversy in Hammer as of late.
-> It couldn't have possibly been any more evenly matched between those two opponent. Both wrestlers could hold their own when pitted against any aspect of the fighting;
whether it be trying to overpower each other, or just brawling, they were in the same boat together. Even grappling techniques seemed to be of an equal knowledgable ownership with Mercury and Kamakazee.
-> Things came to a climatic halt in the match when Kamakazee was able to slip away from Mercury's grasps and power the big man down, using what little of his own energy he had to apply a sleeperhold. Even with his size and strength, Mercury seemed unable to resist the dizzying hold, faltering to his knees, and collapsing.
The ball finally fell into Kamakazee's court for the gamepoint in his favor. -> Once Mercury had powered out of Kamakazee's submission, he got a jaw full of superkick -- the Kamakakazee Kick. The finisher itself took nearly everything out of Bobby Kamakazee, but Mercury was out like a light. The next thing he knew, Kamakazee was rolling over top of him for the one-two-three victory.
-> After the bell rang and he'd been declared the winner, Kamakazee tried to shake Mercury's hand. Unfortunately,
Mercury seemed somewhat distraught by his loss, brushing
"Bad Boy's" hand away, then slipping out of the ring.
Mercury pushed into the audience and melted from view, adding more mystery to his questionable presence with
Hammer Wrestling. But despite his opponent's unsportsman like attitude, Kamakazee received a halfway decent pop and left very pleased with his big Hammer accomplishment.
