Non-taped House Show* PARKSEY BROTHERS' FISH WAREHOUSE Norfolk, Virginia * This show is considered a "non-taped house show." In other words, all matches appearing on this card are in a summarized format. House Shows are merely rankings builders -- rewards to players who've participated and worked. The regular cards will have fully written out matches and count a lot more towards a player's record.
Additional Note: the "jobbers" on Inferno are often "borrowed" from other independent federations. Their purpose is to either help the match along for the new talent or just get practice for themselves.
************* ** RESULTS ** ************* The Germans have invaded! Hammer Wrestling got a chance to practice with some of Europe's best stars; specifically, from Germany's popular independent fed, UNK! They fought before a pretty decent sized crowd, although, there were a whole lot more cheers for the
Germans. Roughly a crowd of 300 were in attendance, most of which were UNK fans.
VINNIE "THE ENFORCER" CARLUCCI d. "SPIEL MIT MIR" BURKE BURKO (6:07) * UNK Intercontinental Championship Title Bout -> Burke Burko, the UNK Intercontinental champ, met some serious difficulty when going head to head with resident
Italian tough guy, Vinnie "The Enforcer" Carlucci. The fans were really behind Burko, but were amazed with
Carlucci who played the crowd up fairly well during the match. -> At the start of the bout, Burko informed the audience (in _very_ broken English) that he'd be putting his title on the line for this match. Burko told the fans that since Carlucci was Italian, he may as well be treated with the honor of a European title; for Italy, ya know?
-> Carlucci fought with his best, but in the end, as he was applying his "Sleeping With The Fishies" inverted frontface lock submission hold (Dragon Sleeper), Helmut
Gordon, owner of UNK Wrestling, hit Carlucci in the back with a chair. Gordon and Burko then proceeded to give
Carlucci a "mafia" style beat down. Gordon told the fans that UNK's titles were staying in Europe, and that "yankee trash like Carlucci won't be getting their greasy paws on it!"
-> But Carlucci wasn't down for too long! Coming from the back, came owners Dave Risk and Will Truk, along with head Hammer official, Marc Merciless to a massive crowd response! The trio cleared the ring with the help of
Carlucci's manager, Mike "The Muzzle" Simms. Afterwards,
Risk and Truk left ringside, discussing matters and laughing over the Germans' near loss. They had no doubts over who was the better man.
"COOLER THAN YOU" MARK MARSHALL p. HANS SEHNSUCHT (8:59) -> "Cooler Than You" Mark Marshall made another grandstanding appearance in Hammer Wrestling, going face to face with
UNK megastar, Hans Sehnsuct. Marshall really got under the
European fans' skins as he opened the bout with yet again announcing himself and making lowblow comments and cracks about Europeans -- focusing mostly on the bodily hair of the women and their dislike for bathing.
-> Sehnsucht did fairly well, and overall, it was a terrific watch. But the German favorite soon found himself dominated by the slick and cunning Mark Marshall whose speedy style of wrestling broke down Sehnsucht's slower brawling techniques.
-> After missing a fairly pivotal spot, Sehnsucht fell victim to Marshall's split-legged moonsault, "Cool Stuff" and then a three-count. Showboating and mocking the audience, Marshall left the arena with an immense downpour of dissapproval hovering over his inflated head like a black rain cloud.
"BAD BOY" BOBBY KAMAKAZEE p. TIER (5:00) -> A new face to Hammer Wrestling, "Bad Boy" Bobby Kamakazee, went toe to toe with Tier, a wiley German high-flyer. Kamakazee, who remained fairly focused during the match-up
(and quite intimidating) did well, getting a fairly decent response from the fans.
-> Tier swiftly showed fluxuations in his ability to keep up with Kamakazee and soon fell under. Kamakazee drawed out the match a bit to play up on Tier's faltering stamina, showing a cocky side to himself as well as the menacing mindset.
-> Kamakazee put the finishing touches on the match after he snuck in a dropkick to Tier's gut while the German was blindly leaping from a top-turnbuckle. Stumbling back to his feet, Tier then got a jawfull of Kamakazee Kick
(superkick) putting him down for Kamakazee's first 1-2-3 victory in Hammer Wrestling. ADAM W. RICHARDS s. "DAS ENGEL" VIKTOR PRITZ (2:01) -> The second newcommer for the evening, Adam W. Richards, an older veteran to wrestling, really surprised everyone. Richards, although somewhat odd and gullible, definitely showed his potential when crushing "Das Engel" Viktor Pritz.
-> Richards, the older wrestler, looking to be slower and more methodical, actually used a amalgam of power moves and martial arts. The quick exchange of blows and eye-candy combos deeply impressed all those viewing.
-> Richards toppled Pritz quickly using a rolling cross armbar tackle called the Juji-Gatame. In no time, Pritz was smacking the mat, screaming to get out scoring a submission win for Adam Richards. Afterwards, Richards hopped out of the ring and ran out... before the ring announcer could even announce him as the victor! Some fans watching claimed he was shouting that he left something backstage which he had to fetch. Whether that's true or not, remains to be seen.
**************** ** MAIN EVENT ** **************** JAKE BISHOP & TIM MERCURY* p. METRO & BOB* (7:18) -> Huge match! Definitely catching a dynamic fan response, even from those unfamiliar with Hammer's slow paced return to the world of wrestling. At the beginning of the show, Metro and Bob came out and challenged Bishop to find a partner to take them both on. Accepting, Bishop disappeared most of the evening. At the start of the match, it looked as if
Bishop had failed in finding a partner! However, as Bishop began to do battle with his nemesis Metro and his ridiculous lackey, Bob, in the ring, he was joined by a mysterious man
-- a HUGE man! -> It was later discovered that the man helping Bishop's name was Tim Mercury; Mercury, a huge powerhouse, easily out performanced the opponents Metro and Bob. Combined with the
300 lbs Jake Bishop, former "World's Strongest Man"
competitor, the two tore their opponents up using sheer muscle over actual ability. -> Metro held his own fairly well, though using his wits and ability to brawl to run circles around his slower, less intelligent opponents. But Metro could only last for so long.
He finally ran out of steam after five minutes of battling two different men. Thus, he turned towards Bob...
-> After tagging in his unusual, tiny accomplice, Bob, he pretty much signed the deal on losing this match. Bob attempted to smash Mercury with a chair, but Mercury was able to wrangle the weapon away, remove it from the ring, and decimate Bob with his inverted suplex into a through- legs reverse piledriver finisher he calls "Mercury Rising."
It was then one-two-three over Bob and a well-earned victory for Jake Bishop and mystery partner, Tim Mercury.
-> Metro and Bob quickly left the scene, not wanting to deal with the duo anymore than they had to. Metro could be seen shoving Bob back up the aisle, obviously angry at his partner's inability to get the job done.
-> Without another word, Tim Mercury, the man who'd assisted Bishop, vanished without a trace. Bishop, though pleased with the victory, looked somewhat distraught by the sudden abandonment. Mysterious Mercury!
