Non-taped House Show* HOUSTON AND COLE WAREHOUSE 5A Richmond, Virginia * This show is considered a "non-taped house show." In other words, all matches appearing on this card are in a summarized format. House Shows are merely rankings builders -- rewards to players who've participated and worked. The regular cards will have fully written out matches and count a lot more towards a player's record.
Additional Note: the "jobbers" on Inferno are often "borrowed" from other independent federations. Their purpose is to either help the match along for the new talent or just get practice for themselves.
Further Note: any sort of "angle", "feud", or "alliance" that may appear to be formulated by the Hammer booking committee is
ONLY a suggestion. If you disagree with it and don't like it, then it won't stick around past this summarized event. If you do like it or say nothing, it'll stick around and may even leak into the regular cards. This is merely a way of you visualizing a fedhead's suggested angle, feud or alliance.
************* ** RESULTS ** ************* For an event in a warehouse, this was an incredible show put on by
Hammer Wrestling. And for once, Hammer had a crowd with average ages a bit higher than thirteen years. This warehouse, nestled on the edge of the James River in downtown Richmond, hosted a large group of older people -- twenty-five to thirty -- with quite a few of the younger, dedicated Hammer Fans still present. Spirits were high and the matches were absolutely spectacular. Lucky #10 for
Inferno?
HOOK WATKINS p. RICH WILKES (4:06) -> "The Hobo With The Mojo", Hook Watkins, returned to a fairly decent crowd response. However, his lack of bodily care or sanitation definitely did numbers on how well he was received. Watkins went up against
Rich Wilkes, a young local Richmonder looking to bust into the business. -> Unfortunately for Wilkes, the only busting he'd be doing was his arse. Watkins, the Hammer King of Stink, impressed many by dominating the match-up with his awkward style of sloppy-yet-effective brawling and suicidal extreme moves. Wilkes, who looked as if he had to apply a resthold every two seconds to rethink his battle strategy, quickly fell victim to his opponent's stiff in-ring ability.
-> In the end, Wilkes -- overpowered by not only Watkins hidden fighting talents; but also, by his incredible stench -- was showing that he still had a long way to go as a young wrestler. Watkins put him down with a scoop slam, and then finished him off with his
chest-stomp manuever, "The Food Stamp." -> Post-match, Watkins, exitting the arena (and sniffing his armpit as he went) had nearly bumped into the ring announceress, Sherry Michelle. Michelle brushed him off, obviously not wanting to deal with the homeless man anymore than she had to. Watkins, however, seemed too drunk to care either which way.
"BAD BOY" BOBBY KAMAKAZEE p. "BALLS OF SATAN" JACK VANCE (2:46) -> Poor ol' Jack. It just hasn't been that good of a run for the veteran wrestler since Hammer's landmark return. "Bad Boy" Bobby Kamakazee, big new star with Hammer Wrestling, definitely didn't help Jack's record any on this edition of Inferno. -> Kamakazee, one of Hammer's most knowledgeable athletes, impressed the masses once again by showing the folks in attendance that he knew all the ropes; whether it be grappling, suplexes, brawling, or high-flying, Kamakazee was like a virtual textbook of moves. Vance, the old school brawler, did his best at maintaining a defense.
But in the end, Kamakazee's offense was far too powerful. -> A jawful of Kamakazee Kick (a superkick) is what finally grounded the old-timer, allowing a swift three count victory. Kamakazee left to a unanimous cheer.
"THE GOLDEN CHILD" JOHNNY MIDAS p. "PLAYBOY" AIDAN MADISON (5:45) -> Back from the dead apparently, Johnny Midas really displayed to the Hammer Fans that he could pose a serious threat to Vinnie "The Enforcer" Carlucci on the upcoming Sledge. Midas' opponent was one of
Hammer's potential stars, "Playboy" Aidan Madison, another young athlete looking to make a name for himself within the company.
-> This was obviously the high-spot match of the night. As both men were light heavyweight technicians, there were quite a few leaps, jumps, hops, and dives -- pop after pop for these two, and it looked to be close numerous times. There were many nearfalls.
-> Finally, though, it was Midas who got the upperhand. Following a harsh dropkick to the face, Madison got dropped with Midas' spinning inverted DDT, which he calls, "The Golden Touch DDT." After that it was the one-two-three and a well fought win for Johnny
Midas. ADAM W. RICHARDS s. "GREENBLOODED" SHAUN MURPHY (4:08) -> Mister Senility himself, Adam W. Richards, emerged to another great crowd reception, even considering that half way down the aisle he forgot what he was doing and had to be reminded by a nearby security guy. His opponent was the Irish boxer, Shaun
Murphy. -> It was probably Murphy's fault as to why he didn't fair well in this match-up -- most of his offense relied on punching Adam W. Richards in the head. Since most of Richards' brain had been long gone, trying to damage it further was just fruitless.
Richards, a martial artist/brawler himself, easily returned the favor by bashing Murphy with a plethora of combos, holds, and tackles.
-> Wrapping the match up, Richards applied his cross-armbar finisher, the Juji-Gatame, making the Irishman quickly submit. -> After Murphy had departed the arena, Richards stood up and shouted, "My laundary!" He began to run out of the ring, but by the time he'd gotten down to the floor, he once again forgot about what he was doing. Scratching his head, Richards leisurely returned to the backstage area.
**************** ** MAIN EVENT ** **************** THE GOOD OLD BOYS ("JUMPING" JIMMY BURTON & BOSEPHUS McCLAINE*) p. BOB & METRO* (5:07) -> Coming out to their usual chorus of boos, Metro and Bob made sure to play up on all the fans, trying to get the folks to hate the two even more than they already did. Hammer newcomers, The Good
Old Boys' "Jumping" Jimmy Burton and Bosephus McClaine also seemed to be trying their hardest to get the fans angry with them. The two villainous teams squared off together in the ring.
-> McClaine and Burton were very lucky to get Bob into the ring right off the bat. Burton started things out by manhandling the little twerp, throwing Bob around easily with his mat techniques and grapples.
And whenever Burton would get bored, he'd tag in big Bo' McClaine to come in and pick up on the fun. -> By the time Metro finally got tagged in, Bob was about a couple headbutts away from being legally dead. Metro came in to face Burton, but Burton tried desperately to get McClaine in. For a good period of time, Metro had Burton in the ring, doing exceptionally well. When McClaine finally got back into the ring,
Metro's energy had been spent and he couldn't handle the larger man. -> In the end, Metro tried to tag in Bob, only to find that Bob had run off. Getting his hands around on Metro, McClaine then broke him down with his spinning powerslam, "The Stud Buster." It was then a surprising one-two-three victory for the redneck pairing after that.
-> As the Good Old Boys were taking their leave, Metro could be seen growling something about a certain worthless partner deserving what he'd gotten from Jake
