Non-taped House Show* BRIGG'S POOLHOUSE, BAR, AND STAGE Richmond, Virginia * This show is considered a "non-taped house show." In other words, all matches appearing on this card are in a summarized format. House Shows are merely rankings builders -- rewards to players who've participated and worked. The regular cards will have fully written out matches and count a lot more towards a player's record.
Additional Note: the "jobbers" on Inferno are often "borrowed" from other independent federations. Their purpose is to either help the match along for the new talent or just get practice for themselves.
Further Note: any sort of "angle", "feud", or "alliance" that may appear to be formulated by the Hammer booking committee is
ONLY a suggestion. If you disagree with it and don't like it, then it won't stick around past this summarized event. If you do like it or say nothing, it'll stick around and may even leak into the regular cards. This is merely a way of you visualizing a fedhead's suggested angle, feud or alliance.
************* ** RESULTS ** ************* Pretty good show put on by Hammer. Part of the success was due to the heavy quantities of alcohol which the viewers had been consuming while watching, but nonetheless, the Hammer stars put on a fairly decent show. They are no doubt getting prepared for their big debut on Sledge, and it could show.
"COOLER THAN YOU" MARK MARSHALL p. BOB (1:02) -> Recent roster addition and all-around cocky guy, "Cooler Than You" Mark Marshall went head-to-head with Metro's dastardly partner-in-crime, Bob. Bob, who emerged to a
HUGE heel pop, seemed to be more than ready to face Marshall. Marshall (who started the match off with announcing himself, again) looked rather smug with what he had before him as well.
-> It's almost needless to say that the 150 pounder Bob had his patootie handed to him. As soon as Bob tried to get on his first grapple, Marshall was on him like stink on crap and was breaking him down like Marky Mark and a funky jam! Bashing Bob with high-flying spot after high-flying spot, Marshall then got in a very lax and patronizing savate kick, pulled Bob up to the top- turnbuckle and dropped off with a top-rope DDT. One, two, three -- ding ding ding! That was that.
-> Post-match, as Marshall was gently brushing the unconscious Bob outside the ring with his foot, Metro waltzed down to ringside. Instead of attacking Marshall as many fans thought would happen, Metro took Bob by the ear and dragged him on back to the locker rooms.
Metro could be overheard heavily berating the guy for his recent losses. PHREEK p. TACO THE MIDGET & MISTER SUPERSTAR* (7:06) -> Taco is one angry midget! Phreek came out to do a bit of playing up on the audience, but was interrupted by the three foot tall Mexican terror, Taco the Midget. Taco challenged the green-skinned Phreek to a rematch! The fans just laughed and Phreek rejected the request. Taco then asked that had he a tag-partner, would Phreek then fight him? Hammer's resident troll gladly accepted and out came Taco's partner: a 7'1" 350 lbs giant named
Mister Superstar! Zoinks! -> Mister Superstar was the man in the ring most of the bout, fighting back Phreek with, of course, a large array of horrendously sloppy power moves. Being much, much faster than Superstar, Phreek was easily able to get around him and use his speed to his advantage, relying mostly on his martial arts and shoot fighting skills. He was even able to get a fairly significant belly-to-belly suplex on the massive opponent, wowing not only the fans, but Mister
Superstar himself!
-> Towards the end, the gloves were off and Mister Superstar was resorting to crude tactics; one of these moves, which caught a lot of crowd laughs, was actually tossing his partner, Taco at Phreek. Phreek was able to dodge the midget missile, and get around to hit a double-arm DDT on Superstar. Rolling over him, he scored a fantastic pop for his David and Goliath-like victory.
-> After the bell rang, Dave the Dwarf and Bob the Short ran out and tried to get some vengeance on their fellow vertically handicapped friend and his titan of a partner.
Phreek was able to easily dispatch the 3x3 gang and escape without a single scratch on his olive colored epidermis. "This won't be the last you hear of us!"
squeakily stated Taco, shaking his shrimp fist at Hammer's green oddity. TIM MERCURY p. "THE BEUTIFUL PERSON" TIPPY DIEDRE (2:57) -> What a way to start a match! Tippy Diedre had been waiting in the ring for a couple of minutes, with no sign of her opponent. It looked almost like she'd win by forfiet. But suddenly, out of the crowd came the 6'8"
Mercury dressed as a security official! As one can imagine, Diedre was very surprised to see a member of the
Hammer security force clothesline her down in the center of the ring!
-> Diedre, a female high-flyer, had her wings removed quite fast in the match. Mercury focused most of his attack on keeping her down and on the mat. Whenever it looked like she was about to get up, Mercury would stomp her right back down. At some points, his brutality even got a few boos from the beer-guzzling fans.
-> Finally, when Diedre had taken enough, almost getting NO MOVES in on Mercury, Mercury lifted her into a spinning powerslam and dropped her hard -- a move which he calls "Liquid Mercury". From there it was a quick pin and a swiftly snatched victory.
-> As Mercury was leaving the ring with Diedre downed and looking out, Danzig's slow-rhythmed, industrial song "Deep" came on over the house speakers (which were actually half-way decent for once!). Along with the song, came Hammerhead Official, Marc Merciless to quite a pop (mostly because he looked like he'd downed a few _hundred_ beers before arriving). Merciless'
presence, which had always been somewhat of an eerie, looming shadow with Hammer's stars, seemed to have no affect on Mercury. Despite, Merciless said simply to Mercury, "I don't like you" and then not a word more.
"BAD BOY" BOBBY KAMAKAZEE* & ADAM W. RICHARDS p. WORMBOY* & TOURNIQUET (5:11) -> Richards and Kamakazee, two of Hammer's most recent additions to the talent roster, made somewhat of a fashionable team. Pairing together against Wormboy and Tourniquet, a very unusual grouping, Richards and Kamakazee showed that this tag team match was about respect.
-> Bobby Kamakazee, a relatively tough all-rounder wrestler wowed the audience with his wide range of moves. Moreso, Adam Richards impressed them with his martial arts style of fighting, despite the man's considerable age. Together, the two swept their smaller opponents off their toes, pretty much knocking them around the entire match.
-> Kamakazee was able to finish things up with his Kamakazee Kick (superkick) and then pinning Wormboy to get the victory for the duo. When the bell rang,
Kamakazee went to shake his partner's hand... but found it a little too late! Richards was already running back to the dressing rooms, going on about something, ranting and raving like a crazy fool! This naturally left Kamakazee very, very bewildered.
**************** ** MAIN EVENT ** **************** JAY REIL d. JAKE BISHOP (8:39) -> Terriffic match for the evening's festivities! High-flying Jay Reil took on the incredibly strong Jake Bishop in a match that was all about respect! There was a fairly equal crowd reaction for both superstars upon their entries, and it was relatively balanced throughout the contest.
-> Back and back forth the match went, both men getting their second winds, third winds, fourth winds, and a ton more winds; it could have seriously gone any which way. When Reil would megacombo Bishop down like a bad habit, Bishop would come back by tossing Reil halfway across the ring. And when
Bishop would look like he had Reil in his grasps, Reil would slip away, and like a mongoose on speed, attack Bishop from almost a hundred directions all at once! And so forth.
-> Much to the disappointment of those in attendance, things didn't end cleanly. Coming from the back came none other than the mysterious Tim Mercury!
Mercury charged down to the ring, slid in, and started beating the unholy heck out of Jay Reil! Immediately, the referee called for the bell and declared Reil the winner by disqualification.
-> Bishop was _FURIOUS_. Upon learning of his loss, he was right in the face of Tim Mercury, barking at him, wanting to know what in the world he was doing interrupting the match-up; it was obvious that Bishop hadn't planned the run-in at all. However, Mercury's reply to Bishop's enraged inquiries was quite simple:
a boot to Bishop's gut followed by a teeth-shattering DDT! -> The fans, going off on Mercury, weren't booing for too awful long; "Deep" by Danzig rocked the house again and out came Merciless, shirtless and carrying his infamous steel bo-staff, "The Final Word."
Mercury booked it like a rat on fire, shoving his way to the exits. The last that could be seen of the two was Mercury making his way outside the building and into the streets with Merciless chasing close behind, weilding the five-foot long, stainless steel, brain-bashing stick like a madman with nothing left to lose.
-> But why did Tim Mercury attack Jake Bishop, whom he'd previously sided with only two days past to fight Metro and Bob?
