Non-taped House Show* FLYNT E. WILSON HIGH SCHOOL GYMNASIUM Norfolk, Virginia * This show is considered a "non-taped house show." In other words, all matches appearing on this card are in a summarized format. House Shows are merely rankings builders -- rewards to players who've participated and worked. The regular cards will have fully written out matches and count a lot more towards a player's record.
Additional Note: the "jobbers" on Inferno are often "borrowed" from other independent federations. Their purpose is to either help the match along for the new talent or just get practice for themselves.
Further Note: any sort of "angle", "feud", or "alliance" that may appear to be formulated by the Hammer booking committee is
ONLY a suggestion. If you disagree with it and don't like it, then it won't stick around past this summarized event. If you do like it or say nothing, it'll stick around and may even leak into the regular cards. This is merely a way of you visualizing a fedhead's suggested angle, feud or alliance.
************* ** RESULTS ** ************* Coming fresh off their recent Sledge redebut, Hammer had a whole slue of new talent to offer to fans. However, the fanbase didn't seem to shift much. Only 150-200 hundred fans were in attendance at Flynt E. Wilson high school, most of which were preadolescants under the ages of sixteen.
Hammer's new talent showed well. THE GOOD OLD BOYS (BOSEPHUS MacCLAINE & "JUMPING" JIMMY BURTON* p. DEATH'S DOOR (R.E. REDRUM* & JENN O. CYDE) (4:08) -> Recently prospected tag team talent, The Good Old Boys, a pair coming out of Little Rock, Arkansas, made their official debut with Hammer Wrestling. This redneck team comprised of "Jumping" Jimmy Burton, a technical wrestler, and the big Bosephus MacClaine, went head-to- head with Death's Door, R.E. Redrum and Jenn O. Cyde.
-> Starting off the match was Burton. Burton, a crafty mat technician locked things up quickly with Jenn O. Cyde; Cyde, being more of a high-flyer, found herself grounded early in the match. Utilizing various leglocks, restholds, and even a few suplexes, Burton was able to keep the skilled young woman away from the turnbuckles and ropes, thus, handicapping her.
-> MacClaine entered the match-up later, and was used to frequently clear the ring. The 319 pound redneck got a few jeers with his dirty style of brawling, especially after spitting some of his chewing tobacco juice into Redrum's eye. Each time after MacClaine wiped the floor with Redrum and Cyde, he tagged back in Burton, letting his partner finish them up.
-> Closing things up, Burton smashed Redrum face first into the mat with his finisher, "Brush With Greatness", a full nelson into a Russian Legsweep. From there, the duo nabbed a too sweet one-two-three victory.
-> Throughout most of this match-up, Burton and MacClaine seemed to be trying their best to get under the small crowd's skin. MacClaine really came off as the villain with his bully'ish attitude and vulgar behaviour.
However, Burton seemed mostly focused when he fought, keeping his mind on the match instead of the fans.
He mostly got jeered whenever he'd cowardly retreat from the ring, letting his larger partner do the difficult work instead of facing them himself.
"THE GOLDEN CHILD" JOHNNY MIDAS p. BOB (1:11) -> What an unbelievably fast match this was! Johnny Midas, recently returning to Hammer after an injury from Anthony "The Preacher" Giovanni, showed well.
His opponent was Bob, the skinny, out-of-shape lackey most famous for being seen with Metro and making up rumours about Jake Bishop.
-> Although Midas played the crowd up as a bit of a heel, it was Bob who definitely found himself on the receiving end of the crowd's dislike. Midas scored tons of cheers as he easily slapped Bob around the ring, using mostly his speed and unmatched technical ability to squash his ridiculous opponent.
-> The victory came swift after Bob missed a high-spot, his only offensive move the entire match (which he just utterly ruined). Midas applied a front facelock and then impacted Bob headfirst into the mat with his
"Golden Touch DDT" finisher. -> Following the contest, Metro came out, getting quite a bit of heat himself. But before Metro could even reach the ring, Jake Bishop came from behind with a chain, smashing Metro in the back with it. Bishop then began to ruthlessly beat Metro with the chain until he could be pulled away by security and other officials, including assistance from Bobby Kamakazee,
Jay Reil, Ewan Milton, and oddly enough, Tim Mercury, whom Bishop shoved away, proclaiming, "I don't want your God damn help!" Bishop then marched back to where the dressing rooms had been set-up, alone and angry.
DAMIAN RIOT p. "THE EXERCISIST" REAGAN McDEAL (3:22) -> With Nathan Rancid close behind, Damian Riot of the quickly growing infamous group, "The Underground", emerged to quite a few boos. However, during his ring introduction from Sherry Michelle, fans laughed when she introduced him as "Dumbian Full-o'-Shit".
Obviously, Riot and Rancid weren't too amused by her blatant disrespect. -> Riot's opponent was an over flambouyant fitness guru by the name of "The Exercisist" Reagan McDeil. McDeal, sporting vertically striped blue and white shorts, a pink tanktop, and a bit of an afro, spent most of his time trying to get Riot to "Stretch!
Stretch! Work those muscles!" Riot, not much into getting in shape, frequently replied with large fists to McDeil's face.
-> After brutalizing McDeal in the ring, the seven foot mute, Riot, blew Sherry Michelle a kiss, then slammed McDeal with his suplex into a piledriver manuever, "The Queen is Dead". He got a three-count victory. -> Responding to Riot's show of affection, Sherry addressed McDeal as "the guy who didn't win", then added, "but the _loser_ of this match... is Dumbian
Full-o'-Shit!" Riot left the ring looking rather distraught as his partner, Rancid, sent plenty of curse-words and other nasty comments her way -- a few of which even made Riot slap him on the back of the head! Michelle, if anything, was unaffected by the two, but her dislike was more than apparent.
"CALIFORNIA" CHRIS MORGAN p. TOM TOM VOODOO (5:05) -> Another new face to Hammer Wrestling, "California" Chris Morgan arrived to some anxious fans. This young, new talent out of Los Angeles looked cocksure and ready to fight. His opponent was a strange
African American wrestler named Tom Tom Voodoo. -> Morgan's technical ability and ring strategy is what really won him over with the fans. Displaying some high-impact spots, as well as a vast array of suplexes, submissions, and even one or two flying moves, Morgan definitely had this match in his hands from the opening bell. Although Voodoo's cheating/ martial arts mix of fighting kept him busy, Morgan was able to squeeze out plenty of offense.
-> The icing on this cake was put on by Morgan after he set Voodoo up for his springboard single-arm DDT dropper entitled, "The Tantilizer." Morgan then got a well earned one-two-three victory and even a few cheers from the fans. However, for the most part,
Morgan did not acknowledge them. He left the arena area nearly as quiet and focused as he came.
**************** ** MAIN EVENT ** **************** EWAN MILTON* & ADAM W. RICHARDS p. VINNIE "THE ENFORCER" CARLUCCI & ANTHONY "THE PREACHER" GIOVANNI* (8:08) -> Of all the odd matches so far in Hammer Wrestling, this one was by far the strangest. Since Ted Bruce was unable to book matches, the booking had been left up to Dave Risk. Risk, who seemingly just pulled a couple names out of a hat, chose newcommer
Ewan Milton, and the senile Adam W. Richards to team up against Vinnie Carlucci and Anthony Giovanni.
-> Milton and Richards got along decently enough. The big Scot, Milton, seemed somewhat unnerved by his partner's eccentric behavoiur, and was forced to carry a good portion of this match solo. The help that Richards did contribute, though, was gratefully accepted by Milton.
-> Carlucci and Giovanni, however, definitely didn't want to work together and flaunted this notion. Mike Simms, Carlucci's neanderthal-looking atache, tried to offer Giovanni money at the start of things, but Giovanni swiftly declined, accenting his rejections with fists to Simms' face. This lead to an early brawl between the two and, eventually, seperation.
-> As the match progressed, Giovanni and Carlucci did basically all right tagging back and forth. The unusual pairing had an understanding that, in order to succeed, they had to work together. But after a few minutes, their mutual agreement disipated and once again it was back to bickering and brawling.
-> At the end, Giovanni was busy aruging with Carlucci at the apron. Giovanni hit Carlucci in the face, knocking him off and to the outside, getting Giovanni quite a bit of crowd response; however, his partner- punching distracted him long enough for Ewan Milton to get around behind and nail him with his powerful short lariat finisher, "The Miner's Strike." From there,
Milton received the pinfall win for his own makeshift pairing. -> As soon as the final decision had been made, Carlucci was back in the ring with Simms, stomping the hell out of Giovanni. Milton attemped to jump in to help, but a well-placed chairshot by Simms hastily yanked him from the picture. Meanwhile, Richards was wandering off through the audience, not remembering where he was again.
-> Rushing out to save the day came, once more, Tim Mercury. Mercury levelled both Simms and Carlucci, clearing the ring of the villains. After the two New
Yorkers angrily departed, Mercury tried to assist Giovanni to his feet; Giovanni shoved him away and scowled, growling something about sinners and saints.
Milton, still fresh with Hammer, didn't really seem to mind Mercury helping out and shook the man's hand.
Everyone then went their own seperate ways, leaving quite a bit of mystery hovering over the entire situation.
*********** ** NOTES ** *********** Sledge came. Sledge saw. Sledge conquered. Sledge is oot and aboot and Hammer's rolling right along fairly smoothly! So far, I've gotten a great response on it, with plenty of "awesome cards" and "really great stuffs".
Needless to say, I am very, very happy with the outcome. Everyone who participated in any way, shape, or form on Sledge should pat themselves on the back. Heck, I was just piecing the thing together -- you're all the guys that wrote the thing, so good job! Next week, I hope Sledge is even better!
"Stop! Hammer Time" magazine (the update) will be out tomorrow. There's going to be some schedule changes as well as other info concerning how things'll be done. I'm going to make a very erratic schedule for Sledge. I'm thinking about doing a ten-day deal in which you guys get four days to get roleplays, strats, and junk in, and I get six days to write it. That way, everyone wins.
However, if you guys would rather have it weekly -- which would mean that I write for five days and you guys only have two days to do stuff -- then just tell me. Otherwise, it's going to be the 10-day thing. That should work out pretty good. Only having three days to put out 300k is just awful on me, so I need the extra time to work.
Also, I want to see some big angles going on. If you have an idea for angle -- ANY IDEA -- let me know. Currently, there's already some huge stuff going on in Hammer, if you haven't noticed. If you want to get in on any of it, just let me know and I'll glady work something out with you.
Well, that's about it for me. I hope everyone had fun last week and enjoyed the card. Keep on sending in those roleplays, and keep making Hammer yours!
