Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Hammer Wrestling returned to the newsgroup in the last week of February 2001, its three wrestler-owners staging a press conference to announce a comeback from a collapse two years earlier, when most of its roster had walked out over money. The relaunch opened with one signee and a handbook, a small closed federation of house shows and a weekly flagship broadcast run out of gymnasiums and a warehouse around Richmond, Virginia.

Ten house shows followed across three weeks, building a roster of debutants, veterans held over from the earlier company, and a rotating cast of jobbers on loan from other independent federations. A green-skinned oddity, a boastful newcomer with a lackey at his side, a Catholic wrestler feuding with a New Yorker rumoured to keep mob company, and a giant lifting weights alone at two in the morning all found their way onto the promotion's cards inside a month.

The federation's flagship broadcast returned live on 14 March 2001, opening an eight-man tournament for its long-vacant title with four first-round bouts and closing on an unsolved backstage assault against its own head booker. A second round was already being written when the surviving record ends four days later.

No later broadcast survives. The promotion's own magazine had already begun to track feuds, alliances, and a season-long mystery over a masked benefactor's letter left in a parking lot — threads the held run leaves open.
