A WWF/WCW-real-wrestler-portrayal card-report fed running a Friday Prime Time Wrestling hotline show and pay-per-view-style tournament cards, closed by mid-1995 and relaunched a year later by the same self-disclosed operator. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Internet Wrestling Organisation was already running by May 1995, staging real, contemporary WWF wrestlers — Diesel, Shawn Michaels, “Rowdy” Roddy Piper, Sid Vicious, Alundra Blayze, the Bushwhackers, Public Enemy — under their own names alongside at least one invented champion of its own, Magnificent Mimi. Results and angles reached fans through a call-in “IWO Hotline” ahead of its weekly Prime Time Wrestling card.

The 25 May 1995 Hotline alone carried Mimi, the new Women's Champion, teasing a new submission hold learned from a secret mentor; Diesel, the Internet Champion, challenging Chris Benoit to a casket steel-cage match; and Sid Vicious revealing Dan Spivey as his mystery partner to reform the Skyscrapers and challenge for the Tag Team Championship, while Alundra Blayze goaded Sister Sherrie into an “Italian death match” for a shot at Mimi's title.

A new operator took over the promotion by 6 June 1995 and reopened claims on its full roster — Diesel, Piper, Michaels, Hakushi, Virgil, Jerry “The King” Lawler and the Honky Tonk Man among the singles men, the Royal Family and the Bushwhackers in tag, Sunny, Donna, Mimi and Medusa among the women, and Ted DiBiase, Hurly Burly and Mrs. Philips as managers — allowing the same performer to be claimed under more than one ring name.

“Royal Ring '95,” 1 August 1995, ran an eight-man King of the Ring-style eliminator alongside a full undercard: Roddy Piper against Shawn Michaels for the United States Title in a lumberjack match, the Bushwhackers defending the Tag Team Championship against the Legion of Doom in a steel cage ladder match, and the Ultimate Warrior taking the Intercontinental Championship from Mr. Perfect through a three-part “Ultimate Challenge” — an arm-wrestling contest, a game of Jeopardy hosted by Alex Trebek, and a match — while Ted DiBiase's Corporation ran interference elsewhere on the card.

The promotion closed down for “almost a year” before the same operator relaunched it, “Back with a Vengeance,” on 28 May 1996, announcing a Royal Rumble card to decide its champions on 15 June 1996. No document survives after that announcement, and whether the Rumble itself was ever staged is not in the record.
