Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The United States Wrestling Federation introduced itself on 10 July 1997 through a Creation Packet FAQ, co-run at founding by two co-presidents (one credited as CEO, the other as writer) and billing itself as a place where “major stars of e-wrestling will come here just because they like it and they like to cause trouble.” Its on-air staff cast three broadcast characters modeled openly on real commentators: Ed McMicheals, “a Vince McMahon type guy”; Steve Ross, “a Jim Ross type commentator”; and Adrian Jackson, “like Jerry Lawler, but not afraid to get into a fight.” Its FAQ named three titles — the USWF Heavyweight, Transcontinental and Tag-Team Championships — while a companion page describing the fed listed its belts differently, as a “World Heavyweight Championship,” an “American Wrestling Championship Championship” and a “World Championship Wrestling Tag Team Championship” — the latter two copying real championship names outright, word for word, and repeating “Championship” twice over.

A 15 July recruitment drive sought wrestlers for a sixteen-man World Heavyweight Title tournament, whose loser would receive a new Transcontinental Title, and a four-team tournament to crown its Tag-Team Titles, while its own signup rules turned away real-star knockoffs by name: “No Scott Nash's or Kevin Hall's or Hunter Hearst Hall's of anything like that. WE WILL NOT TAKE THEM!!”

By a 21 July status post the fed was down to a single president, had grown its Wednesday Midweek Mayhem show to five matches, replaced its Saturday flash show with a news magazine called Ring Report, added an open-mic Tuesday Interview Show, and had recruited eleven singles wrestlers and one tag team — five short of the sixteen its own tournament required. Its planned pay-per-view calendar had also shifted since founding, its September date renamed from “WrestleKade” to “USWF Hardcore.” A reader replied on the newsgroup to challenge its three-thousand-word roleplay minimum directly: “why not count quality instead of quantity? you are just forcing your players to hand in crap.”

No card, result, or closing notice survives; the promotion's own record ends there, still recruiting toward its first tournament.
