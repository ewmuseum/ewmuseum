A forum-hosted federation on the fwrestling.com hub that opened its doors in the final days of 2003 and ran a weekly Aggression card into 2016, its own Corporate sub-forum recording two changes of ownership, the founding of its Television Championship and its Onslaught house-show brand. 101 shows, 210 wrestlers and nine annual award categories survive. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Empire Pro Wrestling announced itself on 29 December 2003 and launched on 10 January 2004, its lineup posted to its own forum under a promoter billed on screen as Miller. The very first match on record pitted Mike Diamond against Karl “The Dragon” Brown, one half of what would become the promotion's own Animezing Dragons; the main event that night was “the Ego Buster” Dan Ryan against a touring wrestler billed as Beast. A few shows later, Dan Ryan revealed on screen that he had bankrolled Empire Pro from the start and was its true owner — a reveal that also marks a real change of hands behind the scenes, since booking control passed to him within the fed's first year and stayed there for the next ten.

Under Dan Ryan the promotion grew a weekly card, Aggression, and its own house-show brand: Empire Pro Onslaught, launched 25 October 2005 as a card for characters “who may not be booked on a particular show or PPV, or for new handlers to help them get their feet wet,” its first bill pairing Mike Diamond against “Da Bomb” Mike Evers, “Phenomenal” Frankie Scott against The Sergeant, and Jack Owyns against Boogie Smallz. A Television Championship followed in the spring of 2005, opened to any character willing to sign in for it; ten did, including Priest, X-Ecutioner, Damian Stone, and Karla Starr.

Dan Ryan announced, on 6 March 2014, that he was stepping down as booker after a decade in the role, having overseen “75 editions of Aggression, 16 PPV cards, and 7 editions of Onslaught” and naming Wrestleverse V as the last show he would put together himself. The decision followed a January 2014 scare in which a run of poorly attended cards had him threaten to “close up shop” outright before talking the roster back from it. The handover did not hold: through the spring of 2014 a successor struggled to gather the roster for Wrestleverse V, a final deadline of 30 June 2014 was set and missed, and on that date Dan Ryan wrote simply, “I think we're done here.”

Wrestleverse V itself was never finished. On 9 February 2016, with the promotion already closed, Dan Ryan posted a results sheet for it anyway, declaring it “canon”: Impulse as the new and final World Champion, Rezin unifying the Intercontinental and Television Championships as the final champion of both, and Aaron Jones and Eli Flair as the final World Tag Team Champions. The last trace of the promotion in the surviving record is a November 2016 request, addressed to Dan Ryan, to expand its Hall of Fame — left unanswered.
