Where Have All The Cameras Gone?

30 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Joey Melton collapsed onto a black leather sofa that, ironically, was two feet short of fitting him. The better half of the Tag Team champions’ legs overshot an arm, denying his heels immediate relief from the extra weight they carried in the Main Event.

Even as his middle-aged body cried from an ass kicking not twenty minutes old, and in general, years of excessive living, Melton smiled. A speck of dried blood, presumably his, grabbed his tongue, leaving the champ a taste of how bad he looked.

Aching feet drunkenly hung over one side, Melton imagined how silly the scene must appear to the Gods watching above. Eyeing their prized possession behind the secrecy of cloud cover, or the heavens if you’re more susceptible to believing that, and nodding in divine approval.

It said so much about the man. He’s always dwarfed his surroundings. Retreated in the silence, and safety of an executive’s office in the Nashville Arena, life again played the smaller hand. At his worst, when he’d beat a production assistant for finding him with a camera, Joey was larger than the legacy he’s worn himself out by creating.

Melton thanks the creators for Cruise’s absence. He disagrees with the critics who say he’s too old to carry Cameron. Forty is a number, nothing more. In fact, Melton doesn’t believe he’s carrying Cruise at all. He’s green though, as witnessed in the ring again tonight. Their inexperience together is no excuse; Melton’s never made one after any match in his career. Accepting the realities of the business is how you win. And Joey understands if he and Cameron are to keep the tag belts and make bigger money as a duo, they have to improve. Trust easier, and put forth mutual investment in the other’s success. He let Cameron take more punishment tonight that he should have, and likewise, Cruise f*cked up the middle portion of the match.

The tension was unspoken, but it’s there, and when the cut over his forehead still hasn’t stopped bleeding, there wasn’t a need to overstate the obvious. Cruise hitting the bricks with Mercedes was the right call. Constructive criticism will have its day tomorrow, or whenever the hell Melton’s body stops screaming. A good bottle of wine, and a tight piece of ass has sped up the recovery process for the past some-odd years. Some guys believe in acupuncture, or a two hundred dollar massage. To each their own, and for Joey sex and a healthy hangover have worked wonders, and by rough estimates added four more titles to his legendary name.

But he gave both up for the tanned, mile-long legs impatiently arched in the doorway to his corporate locker room.

“Just what the hell do you think you’re doing?”

Melton’s conscience, in the form of Lindsay Troy, just came a-calling.

She had debated whether or not to even stop by Melton’s locker room after the scene he caused at the end of War Games, but she couldn’t have fought the inevitable for too long. Melton would have found her one way or another.

It always happened that way.

When Melton first mentioned joining Empire Pro, Troy thought he was joking. Then he got it into his head that the name “Joey Melton” would sell out even the standing-room only seats.

It was the ego talking. Funny how the ego failed to tell the rest of Melton’s psyche that Troy was also in EPW.

The man was shocked. It was obvious.

Melton was even more shocked that Troy actually took it to him in the ring not even forty minutes earlier.

If Joey had ventured to her locker room, he would have seen the after-affects of fWo Hardcore Title defenses and one particular match with the four hundred pound wrecking machine known as Nick Brandish.

She had brought Brandish on herself, but to save a fallen friend Troy had let her mouth make the match. In all reality she should be healing and not competing, but Troy’s Italian stubbornness and dedication to the business by default don’t morally allow her to take time off unless she’s in a hospital. She works through the pain, just like everyone else. No coddling or special treatment for her, she won’t allow it. As a woman, she can’t show weakness. These injuries were weaknesses that she couldn’t afford to show, and couldn’t afford to let anyone in on.

Except Dan Ryan, and their confidence in each other was so great that they could tell each other anything with the knowledge that the information would go no further than between the two of them. Certain people, like Marcus Wescott, missed this point as it went flying over their head.

But there she stood in Melton’s doorway with her gym back slung over her shoulder, leaning against the entrance and trying not to give off the appearance of an injured, conflicted woman. Even though she went about forty-five minutes in the War Games match, she still looked damn good. She traded in her ring gear for a black mini skirt that fell only a quarter of a way down her thighs and a black and pink ribbon-tie corset top.

It was a good thing Melton was lying down and selling his head wound like an infomercial sells comfort bedding at 3AM or else he would have collapsed after one look at the woman standing before him. If there was one thing Melton was good at, it was giving the appearance of a worn-out grappler who almost let the element of two cages get the better of him.

Almost.

“You’re not applying enough pressure on that cut, Joey,” Troy said as she took a step inside the room, shutting the door behind her and dropping her bag onto the carpet. “You DO want to stop bleeding sometime tonight, right?”

"Oh, NOW you're concerned for my well-being,” Melton sarcastically snorted.

Growing up without much of a mother, Joey matured faster than he should have and essentially raised his brother Shea, and sister Teri. A sob story in the making, but Melton never stuck around for the ending. He married at sixteen, and colored responsibility for the birds.

There was a time when he could take care of himself though. When did he become dependent on women to heal even cuts that barely break skin? If this is what marriage does to men: subtly blindfolds and spins them in circles until they stop, lost in a state of regression, Joey won’t be issuing his name for a ringing endorsement.

"Stop being so dramatic," Lindsay deadpans as she walks over and swats Melton's hand away from his head. She presses down hard on the wound.

Managing Melton’s like strapping a two-pound box of sugar around your stomach to get a handle on what to expect during pregnancy. She can eat all she wants, is in bed by ten, and wonders what the hell she was thinking in the first place. As an added bonus, Joey’s unappreciative sarcasm allows Troy a glimpse of the teenage years. If there are any prime years in raising a child, she missed the cut with Melton. Don’t think she’s not bitter.

"Damn! Hey, if you try a little harder you could get me to pass out.“ Joey swats at Troy’s hand. She smells like strawberries. His favorite. “Please, use both hands, get full leverage. I’m ready for this night to end.”

"Not having to hear you talk wouldn't be so bad." She smirked slightly as she sits on the arm of the couch closest to Melton’s head. "Alas, I do have a conscience somewhere inside of me that says 'Save this poor man from loss of blood.'"

"Really?” Troy’s conscience was breaking news. “I'm hating that you didn't find it when we were in the ring. The last few pints I've lost had your name on them."

A year with Joey saddled around her waist left Troy intently seeking her charge out when he entered the fracas. She threw more than her share of potatoes inside the cage tonight, but damnit, he deserved even more. Is it too early in their relationship to admit she had the time of her life inflicting a measure of pain?

"I'm sorry that you didn't take the time to read the roster sheet when you signed the contract. Maybe this'll change the tune you've been singing about me for the past twelve months.” Lindsay’s full pink lips stayed a moment longer on the last two words. Joey’s never acknowledged her skill in the ring with a degree of sincerity to her liking. Just another reason for the hard shots in-ring, “I can fight, Joey. I've been fighting for nine years. It's what they pay me to do, and I make no distinction between friend, foe, or annoying managerial charge when I'm inside the ropes."

He loves the crack in the wall openings Lindsay presents on a silver platter. Melton’s sure she does it intentionally, which only adds fuel to the raging fire known as his sexual frustration. "I've been trying to pay you to get inside the ropes for a year now."

He laughs, but pays instantly. He struggles to a sitting position and holds his arm out.

"Help me up, maybe if I'm standing the feeling will return to my..."

"You're going to sit right there.” Troy interrupts mainly to censor Melton. Have to stop him when he’s sober, otherwise he’ll make sailors blush. “I'm not going to have you falling on your ass because you're feeling faint."

"I'm either faint or frustrated when I'm with you. I'd kill for some middle ground."

"If I give you an inch you'll take it a mile, Joey. Nothing ever comes easy, especially when I have to deal with you on a daily basis."

"Deal? So it's a chore to manage me?” He hasn’t listened well since the First Grade. “And you're doing a hell of a job with that. No, babe, I'm the one that deserves a medal for letting you tag along."

"Let’s not forget who’s offering the charity here. The only medal you deserve is one that says you're lucky to be alive. If it weren't for me, Calvin would have booked you to be Eddy Love's downtrodden sidekick. As humbling as that would have been, you'd be out on the street because Cal's momma is so high on Eddy Love that I have to wonder just how much of Eddy Jr. she's smoking on a daily basis."

Troy lets go of the bloody towel and throws it down in Melton's lap.

"I don't need your sh*t right now, Joey. Maybe you'll swallow your pride and show some gratitude, but that'd be asking too much wouldn't it?"

Lindsay’s displeasure vocally raps off the walls, and the echo berates Melton once more. There’s a line of how far he can go with her. Rarely, is it crossed.

Joey tilts his head back, burying it in the black leather and starts laughing uncontrollably.

"Look at you. Acting like you're mad at me. That's cute."

"You're such a prick, Joey."

She turns to go, but Melton grabs her hand and yanks her down on the couch beside him.

"Why'd you kiss him?" He asks with sudden sincerity.

Troy raises an eyebrow. Even though it shouldn’t, Melton’s sudden straightforwardness caught her by surprise. "Why did you care so much that you kissed me?"

"Well, if you were giving them out..." Melton smiles. "Who says I care, anyway?"

"Don't play me like that, Joey. I think after knowing you for as long as I have, I can read you fairly well by now."

She tosses her hair over her shoulder and shrugs. "I kissed Christian to piss off Marcus. It was a spur-of-the-moment decision. That's all."

He runs his left hand softly through the back of her hair. Her hair reminds him of a warm safe place, where as a child he’d hide. And Joey would pray for the thunder and rain to quietly pass him by.

He fights a smile, that damn song has been stuck in his head for weeks. Really, any global problem that’s arisen in the last ten years can be traced back to Axl dissolving the original lineup of GNR.

Maybe not: Troy was waiting for a rebuttal.

"I continually piss you off. Why doesn't that deserve a kiss?"

Troy touches the tip of Melton's nose with her index finger and smiles mischievously.

"Because I'm not using you to get back at a man whose life I seek to ruin...am I?"

"Sure, put conditions on awkward moments." Melton grins and points to his forehead. "What do you think? Am I going to make it?"

Troy runs her finger over the gash, which by this point has stopped bleeding. Not that it was too serious to start. The man needs constantly babying. That he’s growing on her presents moral problems…problems that will need explaining when a certain someone comes back.

If he comes back.

If he ever comes back.

"I think you're going to be just fine."

“You know, I'm a champion now. The bylaws in EPW say I'm entitled to take certain chances without being reprimanded."

She rolls her eyes. "Certain chances like putting your lips on mine and not expecting a repercussion of some sort?"

"So long as you've read the fine print." Joey leans in and softly kisses Troy.

She doesn’t fight him again. Second time in fifty some-odd minutes: maybe she’s too tired, a War Games is brutal after all. It wasn’t of New Frontier magnitude, but the match was brutal nonetheless. Men have died in similar circumstances in other promotions, she’s sure of it. The night was long, and Troy’s effort was hard. Maybe if it was just a singles match she’d conjure the energy to slap Joey, and rightly so. But, those damn steel cages. Those damn blue eyes and that damn smile of his left her so weak that she’s tenderly kissing Melton back.

"I,” Troy breaks from Joey, her face awash in red. She clears her throat. “I…don't think I was told of that rule modification."

He’s silent for the first time since Clinton left office. He’ll never utter another word if that’s the reward.

“How long have you wanted to do that?” she asks.

Joey’s face reddens as well, and he steadfastly answers, “For a year.”

Lindsay’s eyes squint happily. This admittance of his is a sure sign of a small victory for her. Her lips remain parted. Melton’s taken less as a sign of encouragement. He slowly paws at her right hand, interlocking their fingers together.

“What’s this?” Troy drops her face an inch closer to Melton’s. “A test of strength?”

“I owe you one. I held back earlier.”

“Naturally.” Troy laughs softly, balling up Melton’s theory and tossing it behind them, where their pasts of reasonable denials lie ready to finally give way.

A sharp knock on the door breaks the moment.

“What’d you say?”

“Huh?”

A second, harder knock wakes Troy.

“Joey...”

A third, Melton dives in for another kiss. Monks don’t even honor celibacy as religiously as he has: Twelve months. She’ll be worth it; some things are just instinctive.

She puts a hand on his chest, keeping the distance. Interruptions prove to be a mainstay for the both of them.

“Joey!”

“What?”

“Door!”

“Well,” he reaches for his composure. “Get it.”

Troy mutters under her breath, unfolds off the couch and answers.

Brandon Jacobs greets her, apologetically by posture.

“Well, if it isn't the traitor.”

Jacobs hangs his head, “Sorry.”

“Fool me once, shame on you. Fool me twice, shame on me. You and Kenny deserve each other. Ever think of moving to Massachusetts?

“I'm a Red Sox fan, I was born in Massachusetts. Grew up in Swansea. But I came here for another reason, our car has four flat tires and I was wondering if Marx and I could get a rid back to the hotel. There’s hardly anyone still here, and I’ll be damned if I have to ride in the same car as John Doe. In return we'll pick up Melton's bar tab for the night.

“Well then, you fulfill the residency requirement. You can buy a ring now. Anyways, I think you're going to have to do better than the offer you just made, since I hold the car keys.”

She grins. Foreplay’s left Troy playful. Melton’s soul offers a blood-curling scream. So close to reaping his rewards. Poor bastard.

“OK, I'll pick up yours too, but I'm going to have to rob a bank to pay off Melton's as it is. That million isn't tax free.”

“You’re not a bad person, Brandon,” Troy said with a smile. “Just a little misguided is all.”

“That’s a relief to know.” Jacobs returned the smile.

“Just give me a minute or two to get the Invalid’s things together and I’ll meet you and Jon by the exit.”
