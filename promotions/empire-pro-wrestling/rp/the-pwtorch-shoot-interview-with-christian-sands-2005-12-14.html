The PWTorch Shoot Interview With Christian Sands

14 December 2005

THE PWTORCH SHOOT INTERVIEW WITH CHRISTIAN SANDS

--------

PP: Hello, everybody, I'm Pete Penzler of the Pro Wrestling Torch newsletter and this is a PWTorch shoot interview. We have with us today a man who made his name in Global X-Treme Wrestling and became one of the hottest new talents on the FWrestling circuit until personal tragedy struck. We're privileged to be speaking today with Christian Sands. How are you today, Mr. Sands?

SANDS: All things considered, moderately decent.

PP: You don't mind if we get right down to questions, do you?

SANDS: Your show, your call.

PP: Great, great. Care to tell us what first got you interested in wrestling?

SANDS: Probably not what you'd expect. I got hooked on wrestling long before there WAS a CSWA. There used to be a federation called the Great Canadian Wrestling Circuit that toured all around the country and had a TV show airing on Saturdays, guys like Greg Gallant, Lou Koch, Jean Patrice, Newfie Eddie Bright, incredible pool of talent for the time. So I had a cold one Saturday in about '83 and I turned on the TV and I remember to this day it was Jean Patrice and Lou Koch going at it, and I thought to myself... "This is really cool." So from that day back in '83 on I was a huge wrestling fan.

PP: You said you got hooked on the business before there was a CSWA. Did you ever become a fan of the CSWA?

SANDS: Not really. Some of the guys I liked, the rest I didn't. I was more into the indy scene at the time, guys like Jean Rabesque and Terry Laroche. I always liked the athleticism more than the gimmicks and ****.

PP: How did you end up breaking into the business?

SANDS: Dumb luck.

PP: Dumb luck?

SANDS: I was 21 and out at a show in Barrie when Dynamite Ron Manillo, that's the promoter for Northern Ontario Wrestling, Ron Manillo spotted me and he said to me, 'Kid, you've got a good look, ever thought of training to become a wrestler, I run this wrestling school here,' and I was like, '...Whoa.' So the first Sunday of the next month I packed my **** and headed to Dynamite Ron Manillo's training school in a farmhouse outside Barrie, and that started me off.

PP: Where did you wrestle before breaking into the big leagues?

SANDS: Just for NOW for the first couple years. I was working on an engineering degree and selling beds part-time on the sides, but every NOW show Aurelius Bane would be there.

PP: Aurelius Bane?

SANDS: The first gimmick they gave me. Curled my hair Roman-style and billed me as a sort of gladiator. In retrospect it was pretty ****ing lame.

PP: And how long did you keep that gimmick?

SANDS: Until I was 23 and I broke my left hand wrestling Alex Williams in Thunder Bay. After I came back from rehab they repackaged me under my own name. That's where Mr. Skull Head started, by the way. You'd never believe it, but it was a huge hit on the indies.

PP: Seriously?

SANDS: Yeah, it was like, what the hell, here's this guy who talks to a skull, bashes guys with a skull, and people just ate it up. It was crazy.

PP: In 1999 you broke into the American and started to make waves in earnest. How did you feel about that?

SANDS: Well, uh, honestly I didn't really realize I was making myself visible until around mid-2000 when Alex Williams showed me the latest 123Wrestling newsletter and was like, 'Hey Christian, did you know all the dirt sheets are hanging from your dick these days?' I remember reading it and it said something like, 'Indy wrestling golden boy Christian Sands has developed a huge fanbase, and many are speculating that it's only a matter of time until one of the big leagues calls him up.' I was like, '...Whoa.'

PP: So you didn't realize that you'd developed so wide a fanbase?

SANDS: I knew I was able to get over with live crowds, but I didn't know the smarks had, uh, latched onto me so tight.

PP: So what then?

SANDS: I sort of shrugged and kept doing what I'd been doing, since it was obviously working. The, uh, Deep South Wrestling was so high on me they gave me a solid world title run in 2001. I was content, I was making a lot of money on the indies, I was injury-free, and if I got a call, cool.

PP: How did you get involved with Global X-Treme Wrestling?

SANDS: Through Jim Hosselhoff, actually. It was at a Cascadia Wrestling Circuit show in San Jose that I main evented with, uh, Candyman. I was coming out the back to get to my car and go home when Jim came up to me and he said to me, 'Hey, I'm Jim Hosselhoff from GXW, I want to talk to you about something.' So we went out to Applebee's for wings and he set me up a meeting with Chad Dupree.

PP: Speaking of Chad Dupree, what're your thoughts on him?

SANDS: Chad... think of the best guy you can think of. Then double that. It still can't do Chad justice. Just the most generous guy you could ever meet, the kind of guy who'd listen to you and always be there when you needed to talk or share a story or two. Incredibly creative writer, incredibly ingenious promoter, loved this business, loved his family more than anything... more importantly, one of the best friends I've ever made in this business. I still miss him.

PP: I think we both agree that the world lost a great man in Chad.

SANDS: Yeah.

PP: In any case... what was it like breaking into GXW?

SANDS: Different. We tried the crazy Skull Head gimmick at first, and it was getting heat but me, Chad, and Dan Ryan talked about it at one point and we started thinking, it'd get me to the midcard but it wasn't main event. So we decided to start shifting me more towards the legit athlete gimmick, which is how my big push around King of the Cage got started.

PP: Which ended with your brief feud with Devon Sands.

SANDS: You know what's funny?

PP: What's that?

SANDS: He's not even my real brother. His name's Joe Gershwin and he wrestles for an indy in Wisconsin. They picked him out 'cause he looked like me. Honestly, I thought he was a piece of ****. Smelled like **** half the time and had a disgusting habit of picking his ass backstage.

PP: Is that why you went on hiatus after Fallout?

SANDS: Part of it. The other half was because I was getting pissed off with asshats like Reuben Fasco and Mystical Illusions. I ended up reaming out Fasco backstage and Zieba got on my case for it, so I walked out.

PP: I recall hearing about heat between you and Zieba, but I didn't know Reuben Fasco was involved.

SANDS: ****, I hated that guy. Just the most annoying ****sucker you could ever meet. No talent, no mic skills, acted like a four-year-old in the locker room, kept peeking at guys' ****s when they showered.

PP: Really?

SANDS: From what I remember, yeah. Just an utter tool.

PP: In any case. What prompted you to return to the business?

SANDS: J. Allan Bolich made me an offer. He wanted to get a reasonably big name on board for Major Championship Wrestling, so I figured, what the hell. I was a bit dubious at first, but it turned out alright and I got a chance to work with some great guys. Karl Brown really stands out here.

PP: What're your thoughts on Karl Brown?

SANDS: Great kid, destined for big things. When I first met him he was very respectful and eager to learn, and from what I saw he had talent out the ass. Me and Bolich actually talked about it and I said to him, 'Here, I'm gonna put Karl over, get him some momentum and get a good push behind him.' He sort of struggled what with MCW and NWL closing, but I think he's coming into his own now. I want to see him grab a world title run one of these days, but all in due time.

PP: After MCW closed down you moved on to EPW, where you reportedly had some issues early on.

SANDS: Yeah, I thought the booking team was screwing me. I kept getting these booking where I'd end up looking weak or cheap and I was thinking to myself, 'Someone's trying to ****ing bury me.' So I *****ed them out for it and they cleared it up for me.

PP: Rumor has it that you got creative control after the booking team changed and used it to book yourself to win the EPW title.

SANDS: That's a pile of ****, Pete. One guy left the booking committee and they hired J. Allan Bolich as a consultant, but that happened after the bookings were set in stone. Mills wanted me to go over, so I went over. And I don't know where this creative control crap came in. I got creative control in MCW, but not in EPW.

PP: So there were no politics involved on your end?

SANDS: None at all.

PP: What're your thoughts on your feud with Beast?

SANDS: Probably the best feud I've had. Marcus is a great guy to work with, great interview, great wrestler, easy to work a match around. A lot of guys his size I have to carry to a decent match, but Marcus I, uh, Marcus you don't have to carry. He's got such a strong presence in the ring that the crowd can't help but get into his matches.

PP: Your interactions with Beast led directly to your teaming with Lindsay Troy. Tell us about that.

SANDS: When I first met Lindsay it was in GXW for Battleground Britain and I thought to myself, 'This girl can't be as good as everyone says she is.' I ate those words, Pete. A lot of guys dog Lindsay for being a woman wrestler, but I'll say right now she's one of the five best wrestlers in the world by my judgment and one of the best promos in the business bar none. Wrestling her is a great experience, and tagging with her was awesome. I can't say enough good things about her.

PP: Rumor has it that you had some heat with members of the EPW roster regarding Troy.

SANDS: Yeah, some of 'em thought Troy shouldn't get a push 'cause she's a woman. I found that pretty lame. But that's actually where the Dis angle came from, too. Creative were like, 'I bet we'd make everyone **** themselves if they found out this unstoppable mystery man is Lindsay Troy.' So that's where Dis came from.

PP: You were in on the Dis angle?

SANDS: Yeah. I helped a bit with it. That's the only time I got involved in creative, and then only because I was her tag partner. I helped make sure we could work Troy's tag schedule in with the Dis angle.

PP: After Russian Roulette you vanished for some time before returning briefly, only to step back again. What prompted your first hiatus?

SANDS: I took a bump off Jecht and got a bad concussion. Dan thought I should rehab it.

PP: And the second?

SANDS: My sister died... Katie. She had lung cancer.

PP: My condolences.

SANDS: Thanks. I appreciate it.

PP: Mm... a lot of critics feel that your match with Jean Rabesque at Battleground Britain was the best of your career. What're your opinions?

SANDS: I thought the match was awesome, but working with Rabesque sort of lost its novelty. I was a fan when I was a teenager, but working with him up close you really get a sense of how old-fashioned he is. He hasn't really changed his style a lot to adapt for the times, so I had to carry him through some of the high spots. The guy's the best there is on the mat, but fans don't bite for just mat anymore. With my style I do a lot of mat, but I've got a load of suplexes and power moves to draw pops. Jean does pure mat better than I do, but my style gets more heat on me.

PP: Where did Rabesquedor the Borinator come from?

SANDS: *laughs* Lindsay. She made up the song. I drew the picture. I don't think Jean has ever forgiven us for that. *laughs* RABESQUEDOOOOOOR!!!

PP: *laughs* That joke will never die.

SANDS: Never.

PP: Anyway. Time for a little word association. Dynamite Ray Manillo.

SANDS: Opened a huge can of worms.

PP: Alex Williams.

SANDS: Most underrated guy in the business, nobody does mixed mat and high spots better.

PP: Dean Matthews.

SANDS: Best friend.

PP: Reuben Fasco.

SANDS: Gerbil-stuffer.

PP: Chad Dupree.

SANDS: Angel made flesh.

PP: Dan Ryan.

SANDS: Helped me make it big.

PP: John Miller.

SANDS: Millertime.

PP: Karl Brown.

SANDS: Future world champion.

PP: Lindsay Troy.

SANDS: Good kisser.

PP: Beast.

SANDS: Deserves another world title run.

PP: Jean Rabesque.

SANDS: RABESQUEDOOOOOOOOOOOOR!!!

PP: *laughs* World's Finest Wrestling.

SANDS: Not my style, but a fun show.

PP: Empire Pro Wrestling.

SANDS: Best show on TV by far.

PP: Global X-treme Wrestling.

SANDS: Revolutionary.

PP: Christian Sands.

SANDS: Jaded bastard.

PP: I think that's all we've got for now. Thanks for joining us, Christian.

SANDS: My pleasure, Pete.

--Fade--
