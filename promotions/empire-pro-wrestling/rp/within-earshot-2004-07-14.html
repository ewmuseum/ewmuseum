Within Earshot

14 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“What a night.”

Marx cast the words into the Hilton lobby’s climate controlled air, dangling the subjectivity of his statement as live bait to anyone who thought enough of themselves to say otherwise. Two years into the sport, and he was headlining. If the FWI 2003 Rookie-of-the-year sighed, and told you the evening was a drain, his expectations were to be taken at face value. Vicariously experienced by the under privileged within earshot.

But, it’d be at least two hours before the grappler would fully be relaxed enough to sleep. He needed to wind down, verbally abuse a game third party until exhaustion waited by the door like an eager puppy for its master.

Who was the lucky winner tonight to counter his statement with tales of their own dull existence? Jonathon knew somewhere in the world, in a distant corner he’d only read about in “Reader’s Digest”, that literally nothing was happening. Lives were floating as harmlessly along as a small piece of tree bark in the Mississippi River. Clock in, clock out: carefully avoiding the pinch to disrupt the quiet rhymtic beating of your pulse. But, was that Nashville? Could he count on berating some poor hick in order to sleep? The city seemed to pace with excitement earlier, when the town was lit up from his presence.

What are these people like in the darkness? I.E., before he came charging in on a white horse.

“You did run the pool this afternoon before leaving?”

“I ran two actually,” Jacobs said, matching Marx’s pace over the gray carpet. Brandon’s sugar rush played his lips like a puppet. Pulling the reigns far enough back, that a helpless smile stretched from ear-to-ear. Apparently when sleep won’t be an option for another four hours, Jon is a funny bastard.

“And who’s the lucky winner chosen to draw me a bath?” Marx casually dropped his gym bag at the front desk, like a tired six year-old on Halloween, weary from a night of celebrated panhandling. Carrying the goods the last block home was too much to ask. The weight of the world can’t be much heavier.

The drop left a confused receptionist to anticipate the meaning. Was she supposed to scoop it up and lag behind? Call for one of her co-workers patiently rolling up a joint in the break room, or was Marx in fact unaware of his deposit?

She could show some initiative by running it to him, but who’d anchor the desk? Just last week a friend left her post for two minutes, and missed Kenny Rogers. The Gambler, in the flesh, but a pee break cost her the chance meaning. What if Marx called his own shot? And the bag was no more than David Lee Roth circa 1984, on stage, marking a woman in the crowd for roadies to surf through and pull back to the showers. What’s a girl to do?

Serving the public is quite maddening, really. This is why we shoot horses before they grow to complicate our lives even more.

“A fine Hispanic member of the staff.”

“A woman?” The elevator call button caved under the pressure of Marx’s right index finger. “I don’t want a male making my nightly preparations. Not after the incident we had in Berlin.”

“Why am I still paying for that? A woman, bloody hell, it’s a woman!” Jacobs receded through the open elevator doors with his best friend. “Well...”

“I told you, make them shave prior to laying odds.”

“I’m sorry, I was running late and there was a blonde...”

Marx sighed as the pair disappeared behind steel doors, “It’s always a blonde with you.”

“Laura?” Joey Melton causally asked the receptionist politely kicking at Marx’s bag. Nametags make all minimum-wage help so accessible. In Joey’s world, within ten years they’ll include a complete sexual history and a travel version of “Jeopardy.” Nano-technology: building a better tomorrow. “Is there a bomb scare or something?”

“Oh. I—-heard ticking.”

“Seriously?” War Games, physically beating Melton, and then all but having an orgasm for him twice. What else could Lindsay Troy take in one evening? What was next? What did the Gods who gave Achilles immortal strength have in store? Anything short of being transported into a live-action board-game, would come as a disappointment.

A bomb. Brilliant.

“It could just be a clock,” Laura countered, grasping more than anything. She has a cousin who works for a Crime Scene Investigative unit in Mobile. There’s water cooler talk their last case will be featured on “CSI: New York.” Inspired by true events; for seven bucks an hour that’s the help you get.

“That’s Jon’s bag, Joey. Come on.” Troy throws the bag over her shoulder, exhaustedly eying Laura.

“Want me to carry that?” Melton charmingly extended his left hand.

Troy dropped her head, and smiled. “Sur---“

But Melton’s gone, power walking to the elevator. Years of being waited on have left him unable to fully commit. This business of living for others first is a real ball breaker.

Troy shakes her head; some things just don't change.

"Would it be mean of me to hope that he trips and winds up with some dirt on his face?"

Laura shrugged her shoulders. "The man does have impeccable skin..."

Troy laughed. "Right. Maybe one day I'll put a dirty filter in his water container...he needs to have a good practical joke pulled on him."

"I heard that you're the only woman that can make him break out."

"If that's my claim to fame," Troy started with a roll of her eyes, "then Neutrogena had better hire me to develop super industrial strength acne medicine."

“I find chemists soooo cute,” Laura gushed, hopping back behind the counter.

Troy nodded; deciding a leap off the tenth floor balcony is a more suitable response. Melton’s said in the past, small talk with the poor takes your shine away forever. Lindsay’s only now willing to listen.

The man’s also confessed to stalking Diana Ross in his youth. Troy’s lost sleep over debating whether Joey’s more dangerous sober or, the way he spent much of his thirties: Drunk and sliding down a staircase banister ass naked for kicks.

She keeps coming back to Rock, Paper, Scissors to decide. Two weeks ago, Lindsay caught an episode of “Law and Order: SVU” and squinted as if listening to a Spanish Game Show on UNIVISION. He’s killing precious brain cells. When it was just his, fine. But now, she’s asking Alaina to explain hydrogen fuel cell technology. Please God, let this all be a sick, sick dream.

“You’re going to hurt yourself.” Joey repositioned the bag’s strap over Troy’s right shoulder. “Keep square and balanced. Like that.”

"You just asked me a minute ago if you could carry it for me...now you're giving me pointers on how to do it myself?" She rolls her eyes. He's like a little kid, she swears.

“Living in a house with you, I’ve become an expert on doing it myself.”

She smirks. That slow, sexy smirk that Melton's fallen in love with. "Point taken."

The elevator doors reopened, and Jacobs stuck out his right hand, swiping Marx’s bag off Troy’s shoulders.

“Apparently subtly doesn’t work.”

Brandon scoffed, laying a warning to Melton and Troy. If you want anything done around here, try an open fist, electroshock treatment, or the false promise of a door prize for entering a pool.

The steel twin doors began to close for a kiss when Troy reached to interrupt with her left arm. She could use a hot bath herself, the news, and a safe ending to what had otherwise been a potentially damaging evening.

"There's room in that elevator car for the both of us." Now is not the time for the public in the Hilton Hotel to become privy to the intimacy the two shared earlier.

Joey lightly incepts her wrist, pulling her arm down. Jacobs was cut off, and the elevator slowly shot off to reunite he and Marx.

“Melton!” Troy hit the button with her free hand, welcoming Jacobs to their scene, again.

“Wow that was fast. How’d you two beat me up?”

Lindsay sarcastically laughed and took a step to board. “Sorry.”

Melton gently pulls Troy back to him, as Jacobs looks on.

"Joey...I'm tired, I'm hungry, and I want room service." She pushes off from him and gives entering the elevator a fresh try. Success this time, or she’ll spontaneously combust. Seriously...

Think fast, Speed Racer...

Melton grabs Troy by the arm, pins her against the wall with one arm, and with the other reaches in and hits the "close door" button. Finally sending an exhausted Brandon on his way.

“He’s right. Subtly doesn’t work.”

She looks at him, wearily. Right now, she wants to fall asleep in his arms. Two months ago she wouldn't dare tell him that, but Melton's slowly taking apart the fence she built around herself.

"Joey...I don't..."

“Want to be alone tonight.” He playfully kicks at her feet. “Me either.”

Troy laughs softly, placing her hand on Melton's chest and kicking his feet right back. "You're a piece of work, you know that?"

“I didn’t think anyone actually said, ‘piece of work’ anymore,” he verbally jabbed at Troy, instinctively reaching for her hips with his hands. “I thought I was the old man here. Amazing.”

"Don't kid yourself, Joey...you're still an old man." She ran her index finger along his hairline. "I think I see a wrinkle."

He shrieked, partly for show and half from fear. It’s one thing to see Marlon Brandon age, assimilate an increasing amount of open space, and become Norma Desmond’s character from “Sunset Boulevard”: Quite another to have it happen to you.

Truth be told, it’s not aging skin that haunts him, but rather the idea that one day Melton will have Troy’s assistants throw Big Macs over a security fence and refuse to look him in the eye. His mother went crazy, his ex-wife went crazy: it’s merely a matter of time before he follows their introduction into the slap ass loony Hall Of Fame.

He wants desperately to freeze this moment. Where sanity is still a friend, albeit often an unreliable one, and Lindsay is a foot from him. Receptive to his advances, ignorant of why a host of other men in her life would be better choices than the EPW Tag Champion.

“You’re a cruel woman.”

She laughed, in spite of herself. This whole situation was just wrong...she wasn't supposed to be melting from Joey's touch, or secretly wishing for more kisses from the man who is making a run at breaking Wilt Chamberlin's sex record. She was waiting for someone...who may never come back. Every second of every day that goes by, Troy loses a little bit more hope. All of that lost hope is privately being invested on Melton coming to his senses and leaving his old life behind to make a new one...with her?

“I just wanted to say, thank you.” Troy’s hand was involuntarily brushing strands of light blonde hair out of Melton’s eyes. “If I’ve never said it. You’ve done more for me than anyone ever has.”

He wanted to wink at her, but couldn’t. Sincerity was new to Melton, and he had a hard time softening the blows.

“Except for Randalls, but that’s chemically speaking, you understand.”

She was having a difficult time not appearing shocked at Joey's admittance. Struggle was nothing new to her. "No, you've never said it."

She bit her bottom lip and laughed softly. "I'll be sure to tell Mike that he's the exception to the rule..."

He hit the “Up” button on the wall panel and the doors instantly opened. “Now I have. You’re free to go Miss Troy.”

“Unless...” Troy hadn’t moved, but Joey’s voice was perfectly preemptive. “You want to make up for kicking my ass in-ring tonight, and buy me dinner.”

A coy smile crossed her lips. "You got paid this week...you can buy your own dinner." She started to enter the elevator.

He grabbed her right hand, reached behind her legs and scooped Troy off the floor and into his arms. “You talk too much.” Melton winked, as he started to carry her past Laura.

"Joey!" An exclamation of protest was futile, Melton had an idea and he was too far-gone for his mind to be changed.

Her skirt was much too short.

She draped her arm across her legs; that would do the trick for now.

"Damnit....Melton! Where are you taking me?" Needed to keep up appearances in the public eye, anyway.

“With me.”
