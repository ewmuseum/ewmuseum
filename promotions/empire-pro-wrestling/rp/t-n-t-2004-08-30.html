"Enjoy your meal, Mr.---"

Melton extended his left hand abruptly, palm facing the hostess who seated he and Troy not thirty minutes earlier, shushing her empathetically.

"Oh, I'm sorry."

Where was Troy now that Joey was showing a measure of control to block out the distraction of the opposite sex? She's under a complete misapprehension if she thinks Melton's weak twenty-four, seven. Ninety percent of the time, the man's a rock--controlling his whims and frequency of his erections tighter than God does the weather.

She caught the man at his worst, and by the time Joey's able to track her, he'll have a proven excuse at the ready.

"Ass," replied the hostess with an air of finality as she stalked away from the table. It was late. She shouldn't have to deal with this.

Joey paid her no mind. Troy's disgusted retreat is a blip on the map of martial bliss. Even armed with a map where "X" marks the spot, hiccups are expected. This is nothing; an overreaction, and if she wasn't twice as fast as Melton he'd have her by the arms, explaining just that.

Where the hell was she?

Troy hated admitting when someone proved her wrong, and the realization that Sands was right about Melton was a bitter pill for her to swallow. Her astonishment at Joey's earlier confessions of feelings for her wasn't out of the ordinary, but what was out of the ordinary was that she was willing to believe Melton, no matter how unorthodox his proclamations of affection were.

Jack wabbits.

Who honestly says something like that?

It was a breakthrough nonetheless, however momentarily it was. Rays of sunlight had begun to seep through the cracks in the wall that was the entire being of Lindsay Troy, and for a moment she was willing to get her hands dirty and strip down the fortress brick by brick.

Sands's bevy of skanky whores, basking in the glow of Joey Melton and relishing his arms around their shoulders, changed that.

She wasn't about to stick around to hear whatever lame-ass excuse Melton could conjure up in a span of five seconds. Given the conversation she had with Sands in the lounge area of the restaurant, Troy was embarrassed and humiliated upon returning to the table. Keeping her cool wasn't a trait that Lindsay had inherited from her mother. She had her father's knack for losing her temper, and twenty-seven years of living hadn't changed her one bit in that regard.

She'd be twenty-eight in a few weeks. One would think that she would have learned by now that men are, and always will be, incompetent jackasses who don't know their head from their ass ninety percent of the time. Prime example: Joey Melton.

Troy stormed through the restaurant to the front doors, flinging them open in an act of simmering rage. Tugging her skirt down as far as it would go, she stalked across the parking lot to the parked Navigator and fished through her purse for the keys. Her fumbling almost caused her cell phone to fall out onto the ground, but Troy caught it and quickly prevented a second disaster from happening. She put it back into the purse and, having found the keys tucked into an inside pocket, Lindsay disengaged the car alarm and opened the door. After climbing into the driver's seat she slammed the door with a loud BANG, jammed the key into the ignition, and turned it.

CLICK.

Zuh?

Troy tried again.

CLICK.

“Aw, monkey...”

Troy rested her head on the steering wheel and sighed. What else could possibly go wrong...?

“Get on the ground, now!”

A strong hand grabbed Melton’s left wrist and imposed its will, slinging the former CSWA World Champion to the pavement outside of Sunset Grill’s glass entrance.

“Hands behind your head! I said, hands behind your head!”

Joey’s nose skidded across the cement floor of Nashville, bringing him to the Tennesseians’ level for the first time since driving across state lines. He grabbed the back of his head with two hands.

“What the---“

He cracked his neck to the right, running his bottom lip over a ragged, discolored wad of gum, imbedded into the sidewalk: part of the Southern charm that’s been written about for ages. A moral victory for losing the Civil War; “Racist sonsof*****es, but damnit if they don’t have manners, and faint traces of class.” Joey’s disposition confirmed his suspicions. The Cops were finally busting him for his sins eight years ago in Chicago, when Melton romanced the Lieutenant Governor’s thirteen year-old adopted daughter; albeit unwittingly.

“You know as well as I do,” he shot as confidently as a man can be, face deep into Nashville color, “there’s a statue of limitations.”

A World Champion, wrestler of his generation, freedom fighter, and now the confused man caught in a prostitution sting, trying to convince a handful of Cops and FOX television cameras that he didn’t agree to pay for oral sex, Melton’s worn many hats.

“Shut up, sir. And stay right there, relax your arm! Just relax, man.”

The tag champ’s left arm sloppily pulled behind his back, where the right followed shortly, the worst case scenario of Melton’s life just came to pass. He knew the night would eventually whip back around to bite him in the ass.

“Two things, one she was his, and I stress this, adopted daughter. Secondly, we didn’t do anything!”

A host of uniforms barrel inside the Grill, weapons drawn as two flatfoots circle Melton, seemingly under control, but if the body stirred an inch, nightsticks would freely join the fray. Cops train for this, but ninety percent of the job is as mundane and routine as flossing your teeth to stave off gum disease. Traffic stops, and teaching grade school kids to stay no to drugs: ordinarily an easy day at the office. Never takes much to impress the wee ones, but the wife’s a nagging *****, and the novel you’ve been writing under the table for five years just got rejected for the tenth time. Maybe there’s a better way to handle the stress and disappointment than showing up to school hung slap over, but it wasn’t fun for you the first go around. At least with blood shot eyes, and a fading buzz a public education seems more fulfilling. The job’s a bore, and in six years on the beat not a soul has said, “Thanks, I appreciate what you do.” It’s the nights like this, when you’re on the move, wishing you’d eaten lighter, that you long for a reason to beat the piss out of a bad guy, or just an innocent black man. Whatever the scene calls for, for your chest to swell and the uniform to feel like it should.

“She was Ukrainian. You know those girls look ten years older.”

“Crap!” A steady stream of Nashville’s finest leak out of the Grill.

“The robbery’s next door. The alarm was from the Jeweler’s across the street.”

“Damn viruses have the computers all screwed up.”

Melton’s ear is pinned to the earth like a Native American listening for a herd of Buffaloes, hopeful his tribe’s closer to feasting than it was a day ago. The thunder’s off in the distance now, but Joey remains handcuffed and lying uncomfortably on his stomach.

“Hello?”

“I have God to thank for this, clearly.”

Joey slightly turns to see his hostess kneeling eagerly beside his head.

“While I do have some history getting out of these on my own, mind giving me a hand?”

She smiles devilishly, and looks Melton over. “Don’t remember me do you?”

Gun shots fire from the Jewelers, but Joey’s more worried over the gleam in the woman’s eye. He nervously scouts the nametag, and pours on the charm. “Jennifer?”

“My turn to have fun now...”

Troy was no mechanic, but if she had to take a guess as to why the Navigator wouldn’t start it was for one of two reasons:

1. Transmission was shot.
2. Alternator died.

Murphy’s Law strikes again.

Rain pitter-pattered onto the windshield, and all she could do was sit in the leather driver’s seat and wait for a cab to take her back to the hotel and Triple A to take the dead car back to the dealership.

Who knows how long Triple A would take? Troy reclined the seat back and put her arms behind her head to wait.

And wait…

And wait…

And…

Rain was falling in buckets now, and the cab still hadn’t come. Troy exited the Navigator in an effort to track one down on the main stretch of road.

Quite the stupid move on her part; she had found an umbrella in the trunk, but the sharp wind associated with the rain and quickly turned the lifesaving device inside out.

Again, Troy was left to fend for herself…alone.

At this point, there was no turning back without getting drenched. She was too far away from the Navigator to quickly sprint back and avoid a bone-soaking chill. Pressing on was a much better option.

Turning a corner, Troy found shelter under the awning of a jewelry store. The soft glow of security lights illuminated her body but provided no warmth.

Her clothes stuck to her, the natural curves of her slender body accented with the wetness of the material. She shivered. Summers in Nashville were usually warm and sticky, but on this night the rain cooled things significantly. Troy felt like a wet dog as she hugged herself to stop the shaking of her skin and muscles.

The faceplate of her watch told her it was past midnight, and no cabs had passed by since she left the safety and comfort of the Lincoln.

She wouldn’t have had this problem if Melton hadn’t screwed things up back at the restaurant. Murphy’s Law loved to follow Troy around; it was like an annoying 5-year old doted on and spoiled by its parents, yet still clung to one’s skin like an attention-starved vampire bat. Hunger gripped at her insides, causing sharp pains in the region of her stomach.

By this time, her food was sitting at her place at the table across from Melton and was being picked apart by Christian’s Harpies. Troy ran a hand through her hair and sighed. For everything genetics had bestowed upon her--a sharp intellect, a razor-sharp sense of humor, a pretty face, a body which she worked hard to keep looking the way it did--she always had the worst luck with men.

Irony is a cruel, cruel thing.

Melton is a cruel, cruel man--a man with no goals in life except to enter the Guinness Book of World Records as the man who slept with the most women in his lifetime. Why she bothers with him, she’ll never understand.

She’ll also never understand why she’s fallen for him.

Melton’s rude, obnoxious, and horribly out of touch with reality, but there’s something about him that draws her in. It’s one of life’s unsolved mysteries. Robert Stack would have a field day trying to figure out this relationship.

Footsteps rapidly approached in the distance, and up the street was the first sign of a cab Troy’s seen all night.

She jumped out from underneath her temporary shelter and waved her arm frantically. The cab sped up…

…The footsteps quickened.

Troy smiled, a safe haven with the added benefit of heat was exactly what she needed.

…The footsteps broke into a run, and a voice called her name in the distance.

“Troy! Troy!”

The cab pulled up to the curb and slowed to a stop. Troy hurried to the back passenger’s side door.

“Troy! TROY!!!”

She looked up.

Melton.

Running at full speed through puddles and poor drainage. He had somehow found a coat, and it billowed out from behind him.

The look on his face; she had never seen it before. He looked worried, genuinely concerned, and his eyes shone with a long forgotten passion that only she could bring out.

Joey ran up to the cab and wrapped Troy up in his arms before shrugging the coat off his shoulders and placing it over hers.

“You look wet.”

Troy wrapped the coat around her body. “Go back to dinner, Joey. I’d hate to be the reason your good time was interrupted.”

She tried to push past him and get a grip on the door handle, but Melton stepped in front of her.

“Troy…I’m sorry. I was an idiot back there. I—“ Melton sighs, and pushed a strand of wet curly hair out of Troy’s eyes. “Tell me I didn’t lose you. Please…”

“Why do you care about me all of a sudden?” She took a step back. “You’ve never given a damn about anyone but yourself since I met you, and even before I met you. I don’t know why I let you trick me into managing you in the first place. I don’t know why I let you into my house. I don’t know why I go along with half the cockamamie things you do. And most of all…” She paused. “…most of all I don’t know why I love you.”

“Cockamamie?” Melton grinned, before leaning in slowly and softly kissing Troy’s rain soaked lips.

She offered no fight, no protest, and no single sign of struggle. They embraced as the rain came down harder from the heavens, and the cabbie rolled down his window and said…

Tap, tap, tap.

Lindsay awoke with a start and sat up. A tow truck and a cab were parked in front of the Navigator, and the Triple A road crewmember stood outside in the rain, rapping on the driver’s side window.

“Ok, ok. I’m coming. Give me…give me a second.”

When the blindfold was removed from Melton’s deep blue eyes, he found himself in a cellar; a cramped, musky room with stone walls and twelve-foot high shelves of unmarked liquids. As is the case with most superheroes, he’d gotten himself in a pinch, and lies, in mercy, on an ice-cold metal table, in the secret laboratory of an evil genius.

“Cammy?”

Cruise was evil, but a genius? A stretch even Melton doubted as the plea rolled off his pale, dry lips. His arms were cuffed behind his head, and he’d been forcibly stripped down to his “Fonzie” boxers.

This nightmare began when Melton jumped the shark with a bucket of whores. Sure, they’re well groomed Southern women who harbor only the best intentions, but they’re the sort of girls who float over money and a free ride out of town, hoping their scent attracts a life away from Nashville and the good Christian way of life that’s raised three generations of women in their family.

Lindsay wasn’t like that. She may enjoy a good role-play--being spanked and told she’s a naughty girl--but in real time where safe words don’t exist, Troy most certainly wasn’t an easy score. She’s a ball breaker who’s had Melton icing his for the last year.

Joey’s Queen was a woman you don’t throw back in the sea and then tend to your net on the other side. Troy’s a keeper; a woman you rush to marry, and spend a lifetime working to make happy. Melton knew this. He proposed an hour ago! Was Lindsay right? She bombards Joey with a steady jab of remarks about the tag champion being a five year-old child. Is he really a little **** in a candy store that wants to eat until he makes himself sick?

“You can practically see the wheels spinning. How sweet.”

Jennifer, diabolical looking in a white lab coat and plastic gloves, approaches carefully.

“I hope you don’t mind, but I changed into something more comfortable.”

“No,” Melton shook his head, the cold from the table numbing his body. Thankfully he couldn’t tell if he was aroused by the latest turn of events. “Why should I?”

“I don’t know why I expected you to remember me. At your age, I’m sure you do well to recall what you had for dinner the night before.”

Being kidnapped, and raped (Melton was hoping here) is one thing. But cheap shots about his age? Evil with a capital “E.” Now there was no doubting she had grand designs to blow up the city, or worse, entire eastern seaboard. A nuclear weapon? A series of smaller, but equally as lethal explosions? Or did the b*tch secretly launch a satellite that could cause a large-scale earthquake at the mad turn of a dial?

“I know what you’re thinking.”

Ah, good. An answer to the man’s question; she really is quite special.

“Who the hell is this woman, and why is she planning to clinically have my child?”

“Uh, no,” he offered, his body betraying him with wood. What hope was left that he’d pull through unmoved was lost at the thought of falling prey to a weird, sexual scheme. Whoever this woman was, understood what made him tick more than any other dame in his life had. Maybe if Troy dove this deep into Melton’s psyche, he wouldn’t be chasing women the second her back is turned. “But okay, let’s start there…”

"Joey, I can't believe you're feeding me this bullsh*t."

"Bullsh*t? I've been a developing “Primetime 20/20” segment for the last two hours! And I haven't gotten to the end yet,” his lips smack together like the winning kid on ‘Show and Tell’ day. Melton brought boldface lies, naturally. “It's the best part."

"And you're not going to, either."

Troy stood at the door of her suite with the door slightly ajar due to the top slide-lock being engaged. Through that tiny opening, she could see Melton was drenched, defeated, and desperate.

"I don't want to talk to you. So get away from my room, go to your own, and get some sleep."

"Lindsay, come on."

"Don't you 'Lindsay, come on' me, Joseph," she snapped back nastily. It was the first time she used his full first name in months, and she only did when she was beyond the point of pissed-off. Managing Melton was like enrolling in a “Dealing With Your Abusive Teen” seminar. Joey’s faults taught her what high-notes to hit, verbally, and when. "You've ruined my night with your gigolo-pimp escapades. You're a complete and total f*ck-up, and I don't want to deal with you right now. So leave me the hell alone. I'm starving and I want to find some peace of mind that you can't give me."

The force knocked her back a step, and when she advanced forward to try and shut the door again, she was met by the battering ram named Joseph Curtis Melton. He had managed to exert enough force to break the lock off the threshold of the entranceway to the suite and swing the door freely open (he can play a jukebox by drilling a wall with his bare fist, too.)

The momentum carried Melton into the suite where he tripped on one of Troy's high-heeled shoes. He stumbled and fell, knocking Troy to the ground as well. The door behind him silently swung shut and automatically locked.
Troy scrambled to her feet and looked down at Melton who was struggling to get to his. Joey's rain-soaked clothes clung to his skin and weighed him down considerably.

He rose, finally, and looked at Troy in her pink tank top and pink satin pants. Her hair was messily thrown on top of her head with curly tendrils falling down to frame her face. To the apologetic eyes probing for a crack in Troy’s defenses, she’s never looked more beautiful.

“Get out!” Troy pointed toward the door. “Get out right now!”

”What?” He was stunned by Troy’s immediate recovery. How many hotel doors has the man run through, only to get what he wants on the other side? It’s a game, ‘Good Cop/Bad Cop’ for the 21st Century. Lindsay was worn by the night and the meticulous nature of Melton’s charm. Any other woman would have been pinned right there, but Troy’s instinctively defensive. When she’s giving in, Joey’s two hands aren’t enough to untie her emotional ropes. Bondage. The reason Melton pushes forward. “No! You tell me what I've done! I'm tired of this cat and mouse game.”

”OUT!”

”I just ran through a door, Lindsay, and that’s not the only pain I’ve encountered tonight!”

”Oh yeah, I forgot you impregnated an old flame from one of your Cruise Adventures earlier!”

”I never said old flame,” and she accuses him of not listening. The double standard’s murder on his complexion and as he ages, his pores will be less tolerant of stress. “You had the door locked; how else was I supposed to get in here?”

A fair question that Troy dismisses with a sarcastic shift in posture, ”Climb a ladder? Serenade me through the window? I'm sure you can make some more BS up if you thought hard enough.”

”I wasn't...”

”Joey...”

She’s too angry to move, and she lacks the mental ability to bend the room and feed the door into her hands. An easy way to beat your head into wood never presents itself; there’s always work to be done, little flags popping up on the walk over that say, “He’s just a child, and knows no better.”

Joey raises his hand to ask a question, but Troy’s too far into self-interrogation to call on him. Just as well. It’s too bad Troy’s mother has been dead for twenty-five years, or else Melton would have tracked her down to see what levels of insanity Lindsay’ll reach as she hits middle-age.

”I...don't know what I was thinking.” She turns to Joey. “You've driven me to a breakdown!”

”I'm not taking full credit for that!” Melton quips defensively. Don’t hang that albatross on his neck until he can observe another family member and know what to expect.

”I need a drink.” She fishes into a bowl of ice and cools the back of her neck. Her heels are ten feet away, lined against the North wall. She’d have to be in them to click and pine for home. “Why are you still here?”

Because he can’t leave; he won’t ever be able to as long as he’s breathing, and besides…Troy hasn’t filed for court ordered protection. Yet. ”Why didn't you answer?”

”Answer what?!”

”I asked for your hand in marriage.”

”Shut up,” she warned him not to bring up the last couple of hours. “I don't believe that you were actually serious when you said that."

"How could you possibly say that?"

"Because you've bitten the hand that's fed you too many times!” Troy started on her rampage again. This time, there was no stopping her Bruce Banner alter ego from smashing the Sexual All-American into teeny, tiny, humbled bits. “You've never once...never ONCE had any faith in me and my performances in this business. You've had a free ride since NFW started and I've never seen any forms of appreciation from you. All you've done is come down on me, play the Eddy Love card and call me a whore, and take all the help I've given you and cast it aside like a cheap prostitute after you've blown your load in her.”

She took a breath; a momentary reprieve from her tirade? Think again… “You don't love me, Joey, because if you did you wouldn't dump all this **** in my lap and act like an ungrateful bastard. I don't believe you're sincere about your feelings for me for one second; it's just a notion that you've gotten into your head to justify the way you've acted the past forty one years of your life, that your slate will be miraculously wiped clean and you can start all over again."

Melton slowly drags what’s left of his ass off the bed, and eyes the door. He wants to walk out, but owes her some semblance of the truth. Joey’s speechless. Not because he wants to be, or thinks Troy needs to blow off more steam, but because she’s right. Her words--every letter--and the way they shot off her wicked, pink lips cut worse than any wound he’s ever taken in or out of the ring.

It was a calculated speech, one that she’d probably written mentally a hundred times, maybe hoping it’d never come time to release the daggers into the light. But the cat’s out of the bag. Lindsay’s study of Melton is finished, and the exam score was a disgrace.

“What do you want from me?” he asks softly, without a clue.

Troy sighs, and slumps her shoulders. "All I ever wanted was for you to take me seriously, to show me that you cared about me and what I've done for you. And now that you have, I don't know whether to believe you or not.” She looks directly into Melton’s eyes, and whispers, “I fear that it might just be too late."

Was it? Was there ever a chance? Whether the past forty-one years of his life need excusing is another matter. He’s no Angel, but then neither is a woman who slept with a tag partner just to play him in the end. Joey thought Troy understood him better than anyone, hoped she was the one woman who’d look past his faults, and the pratfalls he encountered while working to overcome them.

It’s not about forgiveness in his eyes, or whether the tin cup in his chest hides a beating full-blooded heart. The card on the table between them, unturned by fated, is a colossal misread of their situations and whether Joey could ever convince Troy to accept him and grow together.

“I am sorry, Troy.” Joey stalks towards the door. “For everything, and that includes you not knowing who the hell I really am.”

He opens the door angrily to leave, but Troy closes to ensure one last thought; a tiny morsel to hold Joey over and to make him understand everything that she’s held dear since the two of them first met. "I've spent the last two years of my life waiting for someone, and I’ve finally come to the conclusion that I don’t want to wait anymore. I put that chapter of my life aside. It’s done, over. I thought I could change you; I thought I could get you to come around and treat me like a human being and not an object of your sexual fantasies. But it was a fool’s hope. So go on…leave, now, and let me be alone in my stupidity."

Melton allows himself one last glance at her, at the love of his life he might have pushed too far away, shutting the door once he’s gone.

Joey’s shoulders sunk into the hallway wallpaper, his conscience and mind waiting for his legs to catch up. He cast his eyes down to the carpet, and he could have sworn he heard faint, soft crying from inside.
