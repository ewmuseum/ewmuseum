Empire Pro Wrestling — Roleplay

WON Live with Dave Meltzer... special guest, JA!

21 – 22 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TH — 06-21-04, 10:41 AM (Note, this is kinda weird to explain... it's IC but OOC in that JA is talking out of kayfabe but it's still not me talking, it's the JA character, if you catch my drift: first of four parts)

Dave Meltzer: Hello, welcome to Wrestling Observer Live. I’m Dave Meltzer, and along side me tonight, as always, is Bryan Alvarez.

BA: Evening.

DM: Tonight, we have a very special guest with us. Two-time A1E Cyber Champion and, for my money, the best wrestler in the business not to hold any version of the World Championship, Jericoholic Anonymous.

JA: Thanks Dave, you flatter me too much.

DM: Heh, well, let’s not get into a lovefest here. Anyway, jumping right out of the gates, the biggest wrestling news of the week was your surprise signing with the fledgling Empire Pro Wrestling, probably the biggest American free agent signing of the year so far. What made you decide to get back in the ring again full-time, especially for a fed that’s fairly new?

JA: Well Dave, when I got my start in the big time, it was with another promotion that was fairly new as well, and we know how that turned out.

DM: Point taken.

JA: Anyway, I chose Empire Pro because I knew a few people over there already, but there wasn’t too much familiarity, outside of Lindsay Troy and Beast. I thought it would be a perfect opportunity to start again, especially for a promotion that has so much promise. Empire Pro reminds me in some ways of A1E when it started.

BA: Well, Empire doesn’t have the financial backing that the Ruggerio Brothers lent to A1E. Are you a bit worried about that, since it’s a little more frequent for feds under the FW blanket to fold?

JA: You have to remember a few things, Bryan. For one, there are many more promotions under that blanket than there were with the Ruggerios. And even then, I think the survival rate is about the same, seeing that the Ruggerios only funded three promotions in their history as financial backers. Of course, the one they helped back cost minimal money, anyway, but still, two-thirds of all the feds under Gerry and John’s backing folded.

DM: Right. Getting off the subject of money, what was your final factor for deciding Empire Pro, over, say a return to A1E or another more high profile fed?

JA: Well, in my one match return to A1E, I realized that a lot of the things that made me leave in the first place were still there, and I didn’t want to get into the whole “dead-end job” mentality. I’m a wrestler, and wrestling should be something you want to get up and do every morning. And a few of the guys in the locker room were really ice-cold towards me. I won’t mention names, because that’s not my style. However, two of the people who weren’t giving me the cold shoulder were Dan Ryan and of course, Lindsay.

DM: Yeah, your business relationship with Lindsay has been well documented.

JA: Yeah, she talks to me a lot about a lot of wrestling related stuff. But the conversation I had with Ryan kinda tilted me towards looking into Empire above everything else I had on the table.

BA: Speaking of those other offers, let me run them down. As reported in the WON, you were offered contracts for All-Japan, the WWE, Zero-One, and you were also contacted by Jeff Jarrett for appearing on TNA.

JA: Yeah, Jarrett was practically begging me to come aboard at one point, but I was still too injured. With the WWE, that was totally different, because I never really considered them. Even though I had an in there with Jericho and Benoit, Vince McMahon was dead set on me dropping my mask and wrestling on Velocity to prove my chops. The latter, I can somewhat understand, but he was asking me to drop my identity.

BA: Well, it’s common knowledge that Vince doesn’t think very highly at all of A1E.

JA: That’s his prerogative, and while A1E, I feel, has been a bit stagnant lately, they still put on a product every two weeks that puts Raw and Smackdown to utter shame.

DM: We’ll get back to your thoughts on A1E and the WWE, past and present, a bit later. First, I want to continue talking about Empire Pro. Now, you said you had talked to Dan Ryan. What exactly did he say that drew you in?

JA: Well, he basically just told me about how he likes running a promotion without actually being in it, and I asked him what promotion it was, and he was more than willing to describe it. It wasn’t anything big, and I wasn’t really as interested in the place until I started looking at some of the older shows and witnessing the stuff that these guys have to offer.

BA: How were the negotiations? Can you describe them?

JA: Heh, there were hardly any, Bryan. Both the guys who run the fed and myself were happy with me being there. Besides, I didn’t really need money. I saved a lot of what I made from some of my big A1E purses and parlayed that into other financial success.

DM: So would you say you’re the John Bradshaw Layfield of A1E?

JA: Ha, oh my, don’t insult me like that, Dave.

DM: Heh, well, aside from his shortcomings in the ring, he’s very shrewd financially.

JA: Yeah, yeah, but I think we both took separate paths to financial stability. He’s, like, an expert in the stock market. I just saved, saved and saved some more, and I had brokers at Smith-Barney take care of the investments I had. But back to what you were saying, I certainly wasn’t in it for the money, and their first offer was respectable. The downside guarantee was a little less than what I was making in A1E before I left, but I was coming off a period where I was only working indie matches against established circuit stars. Sure, some people came out to see the Anglo Luchador, but a lot of the guys who were at the ROH shows I worked were hardcore fans of ROH. Plus all the injury questions…

DM: I thought you dispelled those in that match against Euclid.

JA: Yeah, but not everyone is a believer that one match is a great indicator of whether you’re healed or not. And despite my personal problems with Euclid and everything outside of the ring, inside, he’s still a good worker. It’s not like I was in there against… against a John Bradshaw Layfield. Haha.

DM: Well, with your JBL ripping, that’s another good reason for you to stay away from the WWE.

JA: Yeah.

BA: Anyway, you mentioned that you’re joining Empire Pro because it reminded you of A1E when you started there. There were a lot of talented workers in A1E back in late 2000, early 2001, and they included but aren’t limited to: Xavier Storm, Liquid Snake, Canadian Hitman, Beast, Nemesys, Torment and Hayt. Aside from the obvious common factor in Beast, what other workers in Empire Pro give you that sort of feeling?

JA: Well, Beast is obviously still on top of his game and a big reason why I’m reminded of old-school A1E. Also, Lindz is there, and I’ve seen her work from her days when she was wrestling my little brother out in USCW. Other than that, I like Christian Sands, a guy who’s a great worker for his size. Some other guys over there have a lot of potential that I saw glimpses of in A1E, like Jon Marx and Cam Cruise. For whatever reason, they didn’t pan out in A1E, but there’s a lot to be said for the differences in atmosphere.

DM: Yeah, the differences in the Ruggerio feds and in the ones under the FW blanket are noticeable. One of those differences is that the FW feds seem to meld as one sometimes. It’s like they’re all related, and in previous interviews we’ve done with you and other wrestling journalists have done with you, you aren’t a fan of that. How do you plan on dealing with those kinds of situations?

JA: Well, it’s all about adaptation, Dave. I don’t want to come into their house and tell them how to run things. Does that mean I’m going to be living on my past name and accomplishments? No, hell, I’m even sort of changing my name…

DM: Well, you are just abbreviating it.

JA: Yeah, but I think I can start anew and stuff like that. The thing is, if they want to trumpet whatever successes they had in CSWA when they’re out there cutting promos, that’s their prerogative, but I think we can do our best to tell a story, even if I don’t share the history they do in other FW–blanket feds.

DM: Speaking of the blanket of FW feds, it sometimes gives off the image that it’s very, I don’t know to put this, cliquish within that circle of promotions. Did that ever concern you before you signed the deal?

JA: No, not at all. Wherever you go, Dave, you’re always going to have things perceived as cliquish. You should know that in all your years, I mean, I don’t even think Jericho is fully ingratiated into the WWE locker room yet. But still, it’s all how you make of it, and the guys I’ve talked to so far have been friendly and conducive for doing good business. That’s all I want to do, good business.

DM: I think that’s all anyone wants. Anyway, it’s time for a short commercial break. When we come back, we’ll have more with the Anglo Luchador. Stay tuned.

TH — 06-21-04, 02:04 PM DM: Alright, we’re back, and we’re speaking with Jericoholic Anonymous, formerly of A1E and MBE and currently of Empire Pro Wrestling. Now, JA, I’d like to focus on the years you spent with A1E and MBE, really, the first taste of the big time that you saw and we’re also going to be taking some callers as we normally do on the show. Let’s go in reverse chronological order, starting with your most recent match in A1E, the challenge against Euclid.

JA: Alright.

DM: Now, from what I hear, there was a lot of friction in getting that match into the ring. What exactly went down?

JA: Well, you are right, there was a lot of friction. I’ll take a lot of the blame for that, because I should have known what I was getting into. Now, onto the specifics, I had been on the indie scene for a bit, working some ROH dates here, some dates in Canada there, some hometown dates in between, and I felt like I was ready to get back in the ring full-time. Now, I called up the A1E offices and expressed interest to them in doing a date or two, maybe as a feeler, but mostly as a showcase for them and for other prospective feds to get a look at me and to show that I still indeed had it. I didn’t really want any old opponent either, I wanted to finish up some loose ends I had left from the clean split in the storylines I had with Euclid, and I kept pestering and pestering and pestering for it to get done. It got to the point where the show after Vengeance, there was a spot open for me to wrestle him and I pretty much lobbied everyone involved – wrestlers, suits, talent guys – to get this match for this date. And then at the booking meeting which I was in on the conference call for my match that I had wanted, Euclid just went off on me, telling me I was doing things the wrong way and you don’t just come in and challenge the champ and that I was quoted in your newsletter and in the Torch that I wasn’t coming back to A1E and all this other stuff… but that he was going to accept the challenge anyway, and then he insisted it be for the title, which I was against. That was the main source of friction, and it was well-known with everyone else in the company.

DM: We did report on that, that you rubbed a few folks the wrong way.

JA: Yeah, and I hope it wasn’t anything big. I’m not in this business to make enemies, but I’m also not in it for the sole purpose of laying down for people. If I wasn’t so insistent, I probably would not have been in the ring and put on the show with Euclid that a lot of people were buzzing about. I would have been drawn in for a long, extended angle, which was something I didn’t want. I wanted the instant gratification, you know?

BA: People are turned off by that, especially in a business where you have to pay your dues. Did you ever think that maybe your dues ran out?

JA: Well, I yeah, and like I said, I should have known. But the feeling was too exciting to pass up, you know? I’ll admit I was being a bit selfish, but really, I came in for the express purpose of toting my wares for perhaps A1E or another company. I wasn’t demanding to be put over. In fact, I wanted it to be non-title for the match to be more exciting so that the near falls would a lot more compelling. I say I’m coming in for one night only, and I’m given a title shot. Everyone expects Euclid to win, and rightly so. Now, if it’s a non-title match, then you don’t know who is going to win. So when I get the two count, the fans in the stands and at home think I have a chance at winning the match, and when Euclid goes over, it only builds him up stronger.

DM: That’s certainly reasonable, but the argument that it’s a non-title match may give off the impression that you came in demanding to go over.

JA: Well, that wouldn’t be the case at all. All I have left to say about the issue is that despite the tensions, I went in and worked a match that I thought was one of the best in my career, and that it helped further an angle within the fed, which I’m also proud that they used my input in that.

DM: Okay then, now going back a little bit further, you were on the roster of the venerable MBE before it closed its doors. You joined soon before that happened. Did it feel like you were jumping on a sinking ship?

JA: No, not at the time. Sure, MBE was struggling, but I thought that myself and Priest could have helped them out and slowly helped them come back to prominence. In fact, the only reason why I didn’t immediately go on hiatus after my departure from A1E was that I thought that I could offer something good for MBE in my hobbled state. I guess I was in for a rude awakening when the doors got shut with the abruptness they did.

BA: What did you think about the way they closed their doors?

JA: Well, I have no comment about how it went down, as I was very peeved at the time. However, maybe it was the best thing to happen to the company, seeing that most of the stars like Hida and Maggot and Spoiler and Freakfish were all gone. The people who did remain were too little and too late. Even if I, or Priest, or El Tremendo or Kanna or Freakshow had become ascendant and became the next big star in wrestling, there weren’t enough people on the roster to put on a show each week. Guys on the roster were becoming apathetic, and it was a shame to see a fed that had had so much heritage and dignity carry on like that.

DM: Alright, I think it’s time for our first caller, John from Charlotte… alright John, you’re on the air.

John: Thanks Dave, first time caller, long time listener. Anyway, JA, my question for you is, what did y’all think about MBE, considering you worked for what amounted to the competition during your heyday.

JA: Well, I always respected MBE and the players involved. One of the reasons why I went to A1E over MBE at first was that Bob Ryder was still the major financial backer, and I didn’t want to deal with him. At first, I was a bit leery after hearing all the rumors, but they were just that, rumors. Guys like Spoiler, Freakfish, they were great workers and great people to talk to. It’s a shame their legacy faded out instead of continued on, but in this business, those are the breaks sometimes.

DM: Alright, now, you left A1E on acrimonious terms to say the very least. What were the reasons you left and why it was so bitter?

JA: Well, I’m not getting into specifics. I’m not going to air all that dirty laundry, but I was becoming unhappy with A1E and its direction. It was increasingly feeling like a place that wasn’t a second home, but a place that was just a droning, mindless job. A lot of that was on me, as I kept chugging on because I felt I had something to prove. Every week I went in with this giant chip on my shoulder, and I felt like trash every week. It wasn’t fun. And everything with that Tag Title match was just the final straw for me. I couldn’t go on like that. It was getting to the point where I’d ignore the injuries I’d suffer just to prove people wrong.

DM: Alright, we have another caller, this time, Dylan from Saddle Brook, NJ. Dylan, you got the Anglo Luchador.

Dylan: Hey, I was just curious to what those nagging injuries were. I don’t think they were ever clarified.

JA: Oh yeah, well, I started developing some lower back pain in the three way match with Nemesys and Slambo the Clown, the one in which I lost the Cyber Title, and it just got worse and worse. It came to the point where before the non-title match with Big Dog, they were shooting cortisone into my back… that’s the real reason why Priest wrestled Big Dog the week after. My back was killing me. I rested it enough – that was one thing that the two week A1E TV schedule was good for – so that it felt good enough for me to get in the ring with that tag match, and even then, I was able to let Priest do most of the heavy lifting in that match. I was able to perform at nearly full capacity against Dog for the Title, but I aggravated the injury in that match. It just kept getting worse and worse, and finally, I just quit for awhile to let it heal.

DM: Alright, we have another caller, this time, our old friend Chris Cassidy from Boston is on the line. Chris, how are you doing?

CJC: I’m wicked awesome, Dave. Anyway, I got a question faw the Anglo Luchadaw. Now, I remembah some of the things you were tawkin’ to me about yaw gripes with Beast, and you mention the acrimony you had with uddah guys in the company. Could you elaborate on that?

JA: Well, Chris, about Beast, yeah, our problems were well documented, but we’ve since reconciled. In fact, Beast and I have actually been on friendly terms lately, and we’ve been talking, very preliminarily about working together in Empire Pro. I must stress the very preliminarily though. Yeah, we’ve had our rough patches, but it was nothing that couldn’t be settled civilly. About the other people I had heat with, well, I actually do not want to get into that. Once again, I don’t like airing a lot of dirty laundry, and now that I think about it, what I said about the behind-the-scenes stuff with the last match I was in might have been a bit too much. But really, the heat I had with others I consider to be water under the bridge. If they have problems with me, it’s on them to come to me and iron them out.

CJC: Way to dodge the question theyah.

JA: Oh man, don’t get me started on you.

CJC: Haha, take care guys.

JA: Dave, do you always let him get free reign on the show like this?

DM: Well, he is our best customer. Anyway, I know you have plenty of positive memories from A1E. What are some of your best, candid memories?

JA: Well, I think the match I had with Hayt, the one where we made it a flaming sarcophagus match, I mean, we just kept one-upping each other before finally, we were both like, “whoa” at the match we had set for ourselves. I mean we just kept on thinking of ways where we’d out-extreme each other, and it was fitting since we were embroiled in a feud at the time. My one regret with that was that we didn’t save that for a pay-per-view, but I think both Hayt and myself would consider that one of our finest moments.

BA: Speaking of fine moments, where on earth did you ever get the idea to go to Dudleyville?

JA: Hah, well, Hitman had proposed the idea of having our match with Cannibal and Metal be tables, ladders and chairs, so I just ran with it. I mean, at the time, my big gimmick was gimmick infringement, so what would be a better place to go for tables than Dudleyville? Sure, it was borderline, but so was my ring name, really. We just took a production crew out to some bumblescum locale and had a field day. That was probably the most fun I’ve had in the business outside a ring.

DM: One more question, if there’s one person who you’ll be inextricably linked to, it’s gotta be Liquid Snake. Have you kept up with him?

JA: Yeah, I talk to him occasionally. Not as much as I’d like to, but we both have our schedules. He’s doing his thing, training guys in Ted Betley’s old school, and I’m still on the road. Of course, he’s still a great friend and was an even better in-ring rival, and I’m glad he was able to come over and be a part of the build for my match with Euclid.

DM: Alright, well, we’re going to take another break. When we come back, more questions for the Anglo Luchador on this very special edition of WON Live.

TH — 06-22-04, 09:52 AM DM: Alright, we’re back with Jericoholic Anonymous, EPW’s newest superstar, and now, we’re going to delve back into your formative years, ask you about the Hart Dungeon and your upbringing.

JA: Always a pleasure to look back on the young days.

DM: What was it like growing up in a wrestling household?

JA: Well, it sure stoked my flames for the business, I’ll tell you that. I spent a lot of my summers on the road just watching my dad. It was a good thing since he was mostly in the mid-Atlantic territory, so he was never far from home. I had time for friends, but I never really had very many close friends outside my family.

DM: So basically that’s why you’re so close with your brothers.

JA: Well yeah, there were five of us on the road, and we either grew to love each other or hate each other. I mean, me, Benoitholic and Yokoholic all clicked, and Angleholic was always the agitator, the ballbreaker. Chavoholic was just on his own all the time, kinda like in his own little world. We all started training young, and we’d end up going and training after school. It wasn’t your normal childhood.

DM: Do you think it made you crazy or anti-social just being around wrestlers all the time?

JA: Ha, Dave, I don’t think I was ever not crazy. But I don’t think I was ever anti-social either. When I got to high school, it took awhile for me to adjust, but I always had weekends off, and because of my training, I always had a good body, so I had my share of chicks. I eventually learned how to interact with the non-carnies out there.

BA: Alright, now you graduated high school and then almost right after the ceremony, you left straight for Calgary. How did you get your way into the Hart Dungeon?

JA: Well, my dad had a few contacts. He didn’t know Stu Hart, but he knew people who knew people who… well you get the picture. He made a few calls, and then he told me like the day after I graduated that his present was that he arranged for me to go to Calgary and train in the Hart Family Dungeon. I was really surprised, but it was a pleasant surprise. Anyway, my dad was trying to get plane tickets, but I figured since I wasn’t going to college, I wanted to take part in one of the great college traditions before I went.

DM: Binge drinking?

JA: Haha, no, no, I went on a road trip. The week after he told me, I went out and scrounged up some cash and bought an old minivan and loaded it up. Then, me and like two of my buddies I was training with just headed west.

DM: And how was that?

JA: Well, it was probably the best month of my life. We stopped everywhere… Graceland, the Arch, all the different little greasy spoon diners and dive bars… we even got served at a few of them. We met so many people just from stopping at different places. We slept in the van some nights and others we had the nerve to go up to different houses and see if they’d let us board up there for the night, since we didn’t really have money to stay in even motels. We finally made it to Calgary sometime in the first week of August, which was about a month ahead of when Stu was expecting us.

BA: Now, how was the training?

JA: Well, all the stories you hear about the brutality and just the sheer intensity of the training are absolutely, 100 percent true.

DM: Alright, we have a caller, it’s Mikey from Saskatchewan, Mike, how are you doing?

Mike: I’m alright Dave. Now, JA, who all trained with you down in the Dungeon?

JA: Well, we had a few regulars. Teddy Hart was one of them, man I could have told you he would have turned out to be a conceited jackass then. Harry Smith, the Bulldog’s son, trained with us too. Some guys who didn’t quite pan out were there, and we always had guests. Chris Benoit, Chris Jericho, Lance Storm and even Bret and Owen Hart made appearances every once in awhile. I’m telling you, at the time, meeting Bret Hart was the biggest thrill of my life, and he’s a classy guy.

Mike: One more question, since you brought up Owen, did you attend his funeral?

JA: Yeah… it… it was pretty hard for me, being I was just this trainee when I met him, and I had only met Owen once or twice. He seemed like a really nice guy too. He came back one day, I think it just a few weeks before he was going to wrestle Rocky for the IC title, and was just showing us some things about how to stay nimble and maintain dexterity. I was thinking to myself, man, I wish he could come back again and again, but he never got the chance to before I left. I remember me and a few guys were watching Over the Edge on break from a Japanese tour and… it was just, I can’t explain the feeling, like you just got punched in the stomach. But I couldn’t imagine how badly Stu and Helen felt, I mean, I felt really badly for them.

DM: It was hard for everyone.

JA: Yeah, it was a needless death too, but… (pause)

DM: It’s okay. (pause)… Alright, now you made your debut in Stampede about halfway through your training, your professional debut. You also didn’t really make a whole lot of friends there.

JA: Heh, yeah, imagine me, a green rook going in, and believe you me, I was sloppy when I first got into a Stampede ring. I think I tagged Bruce Hart with an enziguiri that was a little too stiff, and after the show, he nearly took my head off, blindsided me. I got up woozy, and he was going to plant me again, but by then everyone started to hold him back and I was like, screw this. I nearly quit then, but I ended up talking to another green kid from England.

BA: Is this your way of telling us your story about meeting up with Snake?

JA: Heh, yes, it is. From then on, we palled around, and eventually, we started tagging together. We ran our course after breaking up and feuding, but we kinda stayed in touch when we both left Stampede.

DM: Alright, after then, you became a veritable globetrotter. Japan, Mexico, Portland, Smoky Mountain…

JA: Oh man, those were the days. Always on airplanes, getting from card to card. Most of the time, I didn’t have time to do anything else but train and wrestle. Most of the guys I was travelling with would go out, but I was always so consumed with the business. One time, we were in Monterrey, and the guys were all going out to a titty bar. I was like, nah, I’m going to go meet up with Mil Mascaras. Well, I got there, and he’s like “where’s everyone else” and I told him where they went. He was incredulous and he was telling me that I should have gone with them or I was going to burn out. I didn’t take his advice until way later though, about midway through ’99, the same Japanese tour that we were on when Owen died. I had just wrestled one of the worst, absolute worst matches in my career against Professor Tremendous in PbPro, and I just felt so bad, but I just didn’t feel like wrestling anymore at all. So I went up to Chaz’s room and I told him I was sorry for sandbagging him. He told me he noticed I was really burning out for awhile, and then that’s when I realized I needed to go home.

BA: Alright, at that point, you had left Japan, about June of 1999, and you didn’t make your debut for Liberty Bell Wrestling until August. What did you do between then?

JA: Heh, well I went to a few titty bars.

DM: (laughs)

JA: I just relaxed, did some light weight training, recharged my batteries. I slept in my old room, met up with some of the old gang from high school who were on break from college. They were all like juniors and seniors, and some of them even followed my career and they were talking about the classics I had with Blue Panther or with Hida Yakamo, or even with my dream tag match where I teamed with Chris Jericho. It was neat.

DM: Alright, for about eleven months, you were living at home, wrestling for LBW. How much of a culture shock was it to go from going around, wrestling in premiere foreign promotions and top independent feds here to wrestling in a smallish hometown fed?

JA: Well, to tell you the truth, Dave, it wasn’t much of a culture shock at all. Maybe if I had gone to Chicago or New England, it might have, but Philly is home to me. Aside from Calgary, which I consider my second home, every day I was away from home, I was a bit wistful. But LBW was just what I needed. They had some good opponents for me, like Captain Suleimon, and I could just be myself.

DM: Alright, another caller, this one is Todd from State College, PA. Todd, you’re on the air.

TY: Thanks Dave. Now, JA, I remember between LBW and A1E, you went on to compete in the Canadian Tag Team Tournament. That 2000 tournament was probably the best I recall seeing. What was going through your mind having to carry your greener, younger brother throughout?

JA: Well, Benoitholic held up his end very well. He’ll admit that I did a lot of heavy lifting, but for someone who was on the national and international stage for the first time, he did a hell of a job. I know he didn’t stiff anyone like I did in my first match.

TY: Hehe, well you had a few opponents. Which ones were your favorites to work with?

JA: Well, Furnas and LaFon in the semi-finals was a good match. I thought we almost blew up in one spot where they were going for a double back body drop and nearly dropped me on my neck, but we rebounded nicely. My favorite opponents, though, were in the round robin phase, the third match, it was me and Benny against the Canadian Loonie and Webweaver. Now, they’re both Canadian, but the tournament that year was held at the Saddledome. So being that Calgary loves its Dungeon students, we got an insane pop from the crowd, and it even surprised our opponents, who also got a nice pop. So we went out and worked our asses off, and it was an inspired match, I mean, I had never seen three other guys in a match with me work so hard. It was just my favorite match I was ever in before I got to A1E.

DM: Alright, thanks for calling, Todd, and we’re going to take our last commercial break. When we come back, we’ll have some final questions and final callers for Jericoholic, so hang in there.

TH — 06-22-04, 11:16 AM DM: Alright, we’re back to conclude our interview with Hart Dungeon alumni Jericoholic Anonymous. JA, let’s tie up some loose ends, and some questions about odd things and opinions.

JA: Sounds good to me.

DM: First up, how did you end up getting your ring name?

JA: Well, I don’t think anyone who knows me doesn’t know about my rabid fandom of Chris Jericho. He’s actually the one guy who visited the Dungeon frequently that I latched onto. He was my sponsor, my mentor, and I went on trips to watch him wrestle. The week before I debuted in Stampede, he came around and I was gushing to him about stuff he was doing, and he turns to me and says, “Tom, you’re a veritable Jerichoholic,” and then a couple of minutes later, Stu comes in and asks me what my ring name is going to be for my debut. I had been thinking about a lot of names, a lot of corny ones like Captain Philadelphia and even Tommysterious, but just as a spur of the moment I said “Jerichoholic Anonymous.” And he asked me if I was serious, and I thought about for a minute and it just sounded right.

BA: Why is it that you’ve been misspelling it for the last seven years?

JA: Because Stu misspelled it and it was never fixed. I thought it added character.

DM: Alright, we have another caller, this time, it’s Brandon from South Carolina. Brandon, how are you?

Brandon: I’m alright. I wanna get JA’s thoughts on some of the big angles of the last few years, like the Monday Night Wars ‘n stuff like that.

JA: Oh man, the Monday Night Wars… those were a trip, if just to see what schlock WCW would bring out next. I have to give Vince credit, that when he’s really pushed, he responds well, but WCW was just… ugh, I was embarrassed for Chris to have to have worked there and be as misused as he was. I’m surprised he didn’t join the New World Order. I think everyone did at some point.

BA: Yeah, that was certainly one of the most overbooked angles of all time.

JA: I’m convinced that if WCW was still around, they’d still be running that angle. You know, it kinda died, then came back, then died then came back in sort of intermittent cycles. I’m guessing that they’d still try to rely on celebrities and other non-wrestling personalities to get over. I mean, I can almost hear it now (in Tony Schiavone voice) “Welcome wrestling fans to the greatest night in the history of our sport. Tonight Smarty Jones will appear, and… oh my, it’s that music. THE NEW WORLD ORDER is coming to the ring! And… oh my God, it’s Smarty Jones, and he’s got an nWo banner under his saddle… SMARTY JONES HAS JOINED THE NEW WORLD ORDER!” Hell, I think everyone except Ric Flair was in the nWo at some point. What a wasteful angle.

BA: Yeah, it was such a waste that Flair never joined.

JA: HAHA! That’s good.

DM: Alright, joking aside now, who were some of your favorite personalities aside from the usual suspects of Snake, Maggot, Mikey f’n W and the people you were really associated with in A1E and MBE?

JA: For one, Pharaoh was one guy I thought never got his due. He had some injury problems, but if there was a guy on the microphone who was more natural than he was, I haven’t seen him yet. He exuded charisma, and I thought that they made a mistake by not pulling the trigger on a money Beast/Pharaoh feud. Another guy I really enjoy is this Vreck Stone character. It’s a little offbeat….

BA: Some would say sacrilegious to wrestling on the whole.

JA: Well yeah, but you’re talking about a business that’s seen a guy in an ape suit come in and star in a promotion. I mean, Robocop, Shockmaster, the Gobbledygooker, wrestling trashmen… this is still a carny business at heart, and I think that Vreck’s methods are great because he doesn’t even think about bringing the zombies to the ring, ya know? I mean, this guy is a brilliant storyteller. I just hope he comes back sooner rather than too late.

DM: Alright, we have another caller, Fran from the Big Apple, you’re on WON Live.

NYGirl: Hi guys! I love the show… anyway, this question for Jericoholic. You were talking briefly about some of the FW guys earlier making jumps to A1E and how they weren’t successful. We know that you did speak up at one point in the locker room during the big wave with Doc Silver and all those guys. Did you really hold a lot of animosity towards them like was speculated?

BA: Just a reminder for those who forgot, back when Doc Silver was the Cyber Champion, JA, you made that speech about using insider terms in promos in the locker room.

JA: Yeah, I remember that vividly, and people were just accusing me of being against the FW guys from the start. That’s kinda ironic now seeing that I’m going over into their territory, but I never, ever really harbored long-lasting animosity towards them. Doc Silver did rub me wrong, because he was so vocal, and because of the shady stuff he was doing in the shadows, but the other guys never really did anything grudgeworthy. I stood by what I said then, and I still stand by it now, but it’s really live and let live in this business. You have to lead by example, not lead by *****ing and moaning. I guess I might have done some things different then, but I still think the sort of outcry I incited was good for the company on the whole. It’s a shame that most of the FW guys ended up leaving after that, because it might have been something different for the promotion on the whole, but that’s life. But A1E still has the best of the best in its own ranks, and it still has some FW spillover with Dan Ryan, whom I think is coming into his own very well.

DM: There have been some grumblings that MBE could make a comeback someday.

JA: Heh, could be any more vague and cryptic, Dave?

DM: You know that’s my style.

JA: True.

DM: Anyway, if MBE ever resurfaces, would you consider going back?

JA: Well, that’s a possibility. It depends on how much of a workload I have with Empire Pro. I know some guys can do the work for two different promotions thing, but I remember when I did that way back in my globetrotting days. Certainly, if they can get a strong roster together, I would love to go back, especially if it meant facing off against guys like Spoiler, Freakfish, Hida, PILE or Paco.

BA: Speaking of Empire Pro, what’s your outlook for your stay there. What do you think you’ll accomplish there?

JA: Well, I don’t even know whom I’m wrestling in my first match there yet, so I can’t really say.

BA: Is it going to be something you’ll look into as a permanent thing, or just an intermediate before something else comes calling?

JA: Well, if it were just intermediate, I’d probably just have gone in, made some challenges to Beast and hotshotted an angle with him. I don’t want to do that, I want a fresh look, a new beginning. I mean, I’m tired of being opportunistic. I just want to settle down and find the one promotion I can call my home on the road.

DM: Alright, one final question. Now, I know you said before you’d never step into their ring again, and you broke that with your last match. Now, you’ve implied that that would have been your last match in an A1E ring, but will it really?

JA: Haha, I knew you’d ask that to close this interview, Dave. What I can say now is never say never. Right now, A1E is not in my immediate future, but somewhere along the line, if I feel that it’s the right choice for me, I’ll go back. I don’t know if that will ever happen, but the desire will always be there to win the A1E World Championship. Since I stepped foot into that company in October of 2000, my goal was to hold that title, and unfortunately, I didn’t reach that. I would be lying to you if I told you that the A1E Championship doesn’t mean anything to me anymore. However, right now, I don’t know if going back would be worth the indigestion.

DM: Alright, thank you very much for your time.

JA: Dave, it was my pleasure.

DM: Alright, that’s it for WON Live this week. Tune in next week when our guest will be Teddy Hart.
