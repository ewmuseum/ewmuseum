Thin Line Between Love and Hate

6 – 10 July 2004

QueenOfTheRing — 07-06-04, 01:01 AM Marx and Jacobs turned back to their rental for a sobering grasp at closure. Feet imbedded in the pavement of Nashville Arena’s VIP parking lot, waiting for inspiration. Like Cowboys over the head of a fallen horse, hoping the other understands the shared silence is his cue to shoot the animal or properly eulogize it.

Who was the poet between them?

“I don’t know how our rental got four flat tires...” Jacobs stepped to the plate first, solidifying his reputation as the more courageous of the two.

“I do, that’s what happens when you let these people out of their cages.”

Marx nodded toward a security fence lined with drunken well-wishers, and thirty-five year-old men who steal their mother’s social security checks for entertainment expenses and the last third of the month’s rent.

“Damn you people,” Jonathon dismissively waved, as if erasing their section of humanity like an Etch-A-Sketch board. “Go back to your shanties!”

The poor, and shameless help maintain society’s balance: though often it seems to Marx’s untrained eyes they’re just occupying prime space his dogs could walk and poop on.

“Oh come on,” Lindsay Troy interjects. The defense lawyer in her takes control when she can. It’s not easy being a brilliant mind in a gorgeous body, enslaved to an unhealthy competitive spirit. “They’re not that bad.”

Leave it to a woman to see the big picture, and understand how the world operates. But, Lindsay would be the first to tell you: most of EPW’s regulars are SOL when Microsoft begins mass producing Robots that will relieve the burden of low-level jobs from the public’s hands.

“Right, there’s at least one or two fit for public viewing,” Jacobs said, leading the caravan from the crime scene and to Troy’s black Lincoln Navigator parked close by.

Joey Melton lagged behind, letting his peers create some distance between him. He eyed the work of a deranged fan, and coyly smiled. At forty he knows the name Melton looks anorexic in comparison to the weight it carried five years ago. Time takes much, even the obsessive actions of societal deviants. He’s a talker now, still with obvious skill in the ring, but Joey stares at Marx’s flaccid wheels and drifts to the pleasure spots in his mind. The moments when he was hot enough to draw such ire. When he had ten year-old Hornet fans in tears, and grown men stalking the shadows with crowbars, reassuring themselves it was right to punish Melton for being an example to their kids and women, they could never live up to.

They laugh at the former CSWA World Champion now. Book Alaskan cruises; giddy over Internet rumors that Melton will set sail with them, wrestling mutated humans for a free vacation and sizable appearance fee. It’s been too long since he walked to his rental expecting a reaction. Leaving the stage is a reflex, a bored movement he remembers only from sheer repetition.

His habitat threatened by over commercialization and younger predators who haven’t forgotten that Joey blew off an autograph request on their birthday, or shagged their mothers backstage while they were sent scurrying to retrieve a life-sized poster of the adulterer himself. Sometimes, Melton was amazed he fit so much into his week. Tonight, time drags on like a dull blade. It has for a year. The spotlight’s cooling, and Joey knows he’s in danger of waking up one morning to find he doesn’t exist at all.

Why then is he happier than he ever has been in his life?

The gray area of his career has become prime real estate. He turns from the rental wearing a genuine smile, detached from the days when he cared only for number one. Melton’s walking in the opposite direction of the automobile that delivered him to the show.

“Shotgun,” Jacobs calls, before Melton absent-mindedly nudges him to the back door and hops in.

He was riding next to the woman who’s guided his career back to within striking distance of the top. To the woman who he lo--...

La...

La...

Lov...

...

Troy slams the driver’s side door, jarring the full house, and starts the Navigator’s engine.

“Flight 125 now, mercifully departing.” Lindsay bellows with mock intensity. Arm on the back of the passenger side headrest, head flung back to pilot the reverse into open road. Troy’s eyes however, never move off Melton.

She could ask, but won’t. Ten minutes earlier she feared an inability to say goodbye. The resolve to spurn Joey’s advances were lost, and at the worst of times. When she felt her knees weakening from a second kiss. He could have done anything to the Queen, and she wouldn’t have fought it. Not when it was the best she’s felt in a long, long time. He was hitching an unneeded ride, and Troy wanted to pull out before he recognized the keys digging into his left thigh.

“LINDSAY!”

Beep!

She nervously slams on the breaks as a car swerves behind the Lincoln to avoid ripping off the rear fender.

“What’s going on up there?”

She buries her face in her hands and pushes the nervous energy from her body. “Damnit.”

In the rear mirror Troy catches a man flipping her off, salt on the wound. “I’m friggin’ trying to back up here. Christ.”

“If you try a little harder, Lindsay, you could probably get us killed,” Melton said, his focus out the window. He hasn’t once looked at her since climbing in.

“No kidding. I don’t wish to die here, not now. These fools would probably cut and sell my body on Ebay.”

“Yeah, what they said.”

“Ugh. Shut it, all of you.” How does he do it? Find the words to crawl under her skin, whenever he sees fit. She’s insane for dreaming of him. Really, what woman with proper wits about her would put herself into a position to fall prey to Melton’s charms?

“Nice and easy,” Marx instructs as the Navigator pulls out and sets off on the ten mile trek to their hotel.

Joey runs his hands through his hair, and steals a glimpse at the beautiful driver, currently on edge: A hint of the woman, who he lo--ves.

#

“Hey, we’re in Nashville, lets turn on some country music,” said Jacobs as the ride was ground to a halt in traffic.

Marx choked. He may not survive the evening, a fact he’s privately making peace with. “While you are at it, do you want to drive around in a bright orange car with a confederate flag on the roof?

“No, don't be silly, but if Melton could jump some cars on the way to hotel that would be cool.”

Joey smiled. He did it on a dare in Phoenix in 1994. This is the b*tch of his legacy. Everywhere he goes now, grown men and women clamor to be there when Fonze jumps the shark again.

“I'd like a nice quiet safe ride thank you.”

“Listen you two, I'm the one who's driving, and as long as I'm behind the wheel there will be NO country music.

Brandon sulks. She could disappear off the face of the Earth, and no one would miss her.

“Thank you Lindsay, last car ride I was on with him I had to listen to an hour and a half of Toby Keith.”

“At least I have better taste than you do...”

“What's wrong with a little Mozart? Classical music has been shown to stimulate brain waves.”

“As much as that might help Joey's cause, I'm not listening to Classical music either. Unless, of course, you want me to fall asleep.”

She said the last line with a mocking tone, but there’s a quiet panic between the three men. How worse could she drive with both eyes closed? And if a tree fell in a forest...

Lindsay begins fiddling with the pre-set buttons. The lights on the touch pad keeping her interest: worked as a child, works now. Some girls just don’t need much for entertainment. Look at her present company.

“Damnit, nothing good is coming in.”

Joey swats her hand away and hits the tune button. Stopping on 100.1 FM, when a familiar voice calls his name.

If professional gamblers could bottle his luck...

The other three heads in the car groan.

“Joey, no!” Troy cries.

“Look my wrists are bleeding and I didn’t touch them,” Jacobs quipped.

Melton ignores the cries of the commoners without taste, and softly begins to sing with Rod Stewart.

“I know your window and I know it’s late
I know your stairs and your doorway
I walk down your street and past your gate
I stand by the light of the four way
And watch them as they fall, oh baby
They all having their heart attacks
They stay at the carnival
But they’ll never win you back

Troy, Marx, and Jacobs sit in stunned silence as Melton belts out the lyrics. The light up ahead turns red and Troy slows the car to a stop.

“Will I see you tonight on a downtown train
Every night, every night it’s just the same
You leave me lonely
Will I see you tonight on a downtown train
All my dreams, all my dreams fall like rain
On a downtown train.

Joey’s voice fades with Rod’s as a proud DJ cuts in.

“So, Lindz...what happens when he's actually drunk?” Marx asks. Was he prepared for the answer? Probably not.

“The fact that he know the lyrics, by heart, to that song scares me deeply.”

“That’s about as close to an orgasm as I’ve been in twelve months,” Melton states matter-of-factly.

“Ew,” Marx, Jacobs, and Troy collectively share their disgust.

The light turns green and Troy grips the steering wheel as she sends the car abruptly forward.

“Who is Rod Stewart?” Jonathan whispers to Jacobs.

“I don't know, he was before my time,” Brandon whispers in response. “Probably some Bon Jovi wannabe.”

“Rod Stewart is Joey's personal Jesus, in case you couldn't figure it out by his lifestyle.” It was all she could do to keep from laughing at Melton’s singing disaster.

Up ahead, a sign for a 7-11 glows brightly. Jacobs grabs the back of Troy's seat and points.

“Mommy...err...Lindsay pull over. I haven't had a slurpee in ages.”

“Sugar is the work of the devil. Besides, you always drink those things way too fast and get brain freeze.” Jonathan, always conscious of young Brandon’s drinking habits, chides.

“That was when I was younger, I am much more mature now. I know the art of drinking a slurpee.”

“Fine, do yourself in.”

Troy laughs to herself as she pulls the Navigator into the 7-11 parking lot. Jacobs jumps out of the backseat and runs inside. Marx rolls down the window.

“Hey! Grab me a Soap Opera Digest!”

Jacobs is already inside, and doesn't hear him. In the front seat, Melton and Troy burst out laughing.

“Hey Jon,” Troy faces the back and says, “Brandon couldn't even keep a simple Survivor alliance. You really expect him to remember your Soaps Magazine? You'd better go in there with him.”

When you’re right, you’re right.

“You two kids be good.”

He exits the car, leaving Troy and Melton sitting side-by-side. Alone.

#
#

Thirty minutes ago in a similar situation they were close to making a mistake of serious proportions. Or, rewarding their mutual attraction: could go either way, thus they dance around the thin line between love and hate.

A bug flies on the windshield and inches across the glass.

Troy flips on the wipers and the bug escapes in the nick of time.

It flies back.

Troy’s left hand stretches for the switch again.

Melton looks at his watch. Do they have to special order slurpees now?

“You’re awfully quiet,” Lindsay suggests as she waits for the insect to return.

“Am I? Sorry.”

“Need a lift back to the arena to pick up your car?” She laughs. Any sound out of her mouth tonight plays soft and sweet to Melton’s ears. He’s quiet so he can listen. Can he say that without being laughed out of the car?

Joey grins, “I didn’t forget.”

“You didn’t?” Lindsay shakes her head easily. “Can’t admit to anything can you?”

“I’m not in denial,” he reassured. “Well, not about that anyway. I just wanted to ride with you. I’ll walk back if need be.”

Troy’s neck lightens; she struggles to keep it up right. “That’s,” she clears her throat, “a waste of gas money. You should have drove.”

She nods her head confidently. That was that, her point was made. Very well, too, she might add. Lindsay’s eyes searched the windshield in vain. She was left to deal with this alone, ciao to distractions, baby.

“I’ll pay the gas money. That’s not a problem. I’ll return every cent you’ve spent on me in the past year.” Melton was no longer avoiding eye contact. He couldn’t if he tried.

“I,” Troy stutters. “Didn’t mean it that way. I was just---.”

“I’d pay rent just to keep living with you, if I had to.”

Lindsay gently tugs on her skirt. It’s much too short right now.

“Well, you can’t…” She motions with her hand as if they’ve been instructed to finish the sentence. “…keep living with me.”

“Who says?”

“Me! What about independence, room to stretch your feet, and a bathroom decorated by your own touch.” Play to his fashion sense, good. “Every guy’s dream right? Who doesn’t want to live alone? We should all live alone. Ten acres of land between us. That’s what I say, anyway.” Lindsay laughs nervously. She sounded like an idiot.

“Eh.”

“Eh, what Joey? Come on, after I just got done eloquently speaking for five minutes. Do better than ‘eh’ okay?”

“I don’t want to live alone.”

Better.

“Have for too long. After a while you’ll just be roaming those acres of land looking for contact, and a friend. Someone to make love to, and see places of the world you’ve only seen by yourself.”

The windshield’s attacked again; this time Troy could give a f*ck.

“Like where?”

“I was just saying.”

“Oh. Yeah. Joey...”

He throws his left arm around her seat.

“You can’t live with me. I’m sorry. No can do.”

“Okay.” He shrugs it off. “I’ll move out then.”

Lindsay sits up suddenly, straightening her posture. “Forever. You know, permanently speaking. Right now...” her voice hits an unplanned high note. “You’re fine. A week from now, six months, who’s to say how long?”

Joey laughs, and Troy uses the break to cringe. Are Marx and Jacobs lying dead somewhere in the store?

Melton looks past Troy, outside her driver’s window.

“Good grief,” he points. “Check that out, hurry.”

“Riight.” Lindsay smiles and scoffs at Melton.

“I’m serious!”

“I turn around, and when I see nothing and turn back you’re poised to kiss me.”

“What? Lindsay, look!”

“Jeez Melton, I think I saw this on an episode of USA High once.”

“I knew you watched the midnight reruns!”

Troy damns herself for the slip.

“Lindz, in all seriousness...”

Finally, Troy looks out her window to see Cameron Cruise pushing his car into a parking space, wife Mercedes embarrassingly seated in the driver’s seat.

“That poor bastard,” muses Troy. She turns to laugh at Joey, and then turns back to the scene. “For once he’s carrying someone else.”

Proud of her joke Troy spins around to face Melton and is caught flush with his lips.

“mmmm...oooey....”

Melton leans back, leading Troy’s face closer to his side of the car. Their lips part, Troy opens her eyes and after a second to recover, slaps her charge across the face.

“For my peace of mind,” she explains before throwing her arms around Melton’s neck.

“Right. Brilliant, then.”

He kisses her heavily this time, arms reaching around her back to pull her into his lap. With his right hand Melton fishes near the door, and is able to recline the seat in a flash. Maneuvering Troy directly over his body, Melton’s hands push back her hair.

“Do we have time for this?”

“Shut up.”

Troy runs her hands over Melton’s chest, before reaching for the ribbon on the front of her top.

#
#

“I can’t believe they ran out of Cherry slurpees.” Jacobs took a long sip from a blue straw while Marx had his nose inside his soap magazine.

“I can’t believe they had Jennifer’s plane crash,” Marx said with piqued interest. “And Bo finding out about Patrick’s past…”

“Hey, look at my tongue.”

Marx paused to look. “It’s quite blue. So are your lips.”

Jacobs wiped his mouth with the back of his sleeve and looked over to the black Navigator.

“Am I imagining things, or did you notice it on the way here to?”

Marx folded the magazine and nodded. “There are less signals at a four way stop light. How couldn’t I notice it? Hopefully the two lovebirds will be honest with themselves. Life is far too short.”

“He’s so much older than her though,” Jacobs matter-of-factly stated.

“Tony Randalls was seventy-five when he found the woman of his dreams. You’re never to old for love.” Marx added before humming “As Love Goes By” by Jimmy Durante.

Jacobs looked at Marx then back to the Navigator. “Think we should give them more time to be alone?”

“No. If we wait any longer, we’re going to have to wait for their son to graduate college. Let’s go and feign cluelessness.”

#
#

The windows weren’t foggy, but they were tinted. Marx and Jacobs weren’t able to see in, but Troy could see out.

The approaching footsteps on crunchy gravel sent off a warning signal.

“Joey! Get up, they’re coming back.”

“Goddammit…”

He sat up, grabbed her by the back of her head, and kissed her hotly once more. A soft murmur and a returned kiss later, Troy broke away albeit unwantingly.

“Act like nothing happened.”

“You know I can’t do that.”

“You can pretend to be Eddy Love’s friend, I think you can act like we were sitting and having a casual conversation.”

Troy expertly maneuvered her way around the shifter that was in-between the driver’s and passenger’s seat. One adjusted skirt and hair flip later, Troy looked just as good as she did before she gave in to Melton’s advances.

Melton’s seat was back in its upright and locked position but Joey was having a much more difficult time putting the short, intimate moment with Troy to the back of his mind.

#
#

The Navigator’s back doors swing open, and bickering to the bitter end Jacobs and Marx fold into the Lincoln.

“Have you got the heat on?” Jacobs asks.

“What?” Lindsay defensively responds, retying the pink ribbon into a bow. She forgot she had untied it. “Don’t be an ass.”

“I won’t.” Jacobs offered a smile. “Thanks again for the gracious lift.”

Troy looks at Melton whose face is covered in sweat. “Well, let’s complete this joy ride shall we?”

She cranks the ignition and puts the vehicle in reverse, turning around once more to safely pilot out of the parking space.

Troy hits the gas, and the Navigator jumps forward clipping a man walking past.

“GEEZ!”

“That’s not my fault, he came out of NOWHERE!” Troy throws her hands into the air.

Melton leans forward, noticeably out of breath. “Is that Cruise?”

“I saw nothing, haul ass Lindsay.” Marx, ever the voice of reason, says.

And as their black Lincoln Navigator speeds from the scene, Cameron Cruise struggles to his feet.

TSiegel — 07-10-04, 10:15 PM "So when are they going to get here?" asked Mercedes, leaning her head out of the window.

"Depending on traffic, they ought to get here within an hour or so." Cruise replied, irritated.

Despite thinking himself to have a decent performance in the cage, Cruise was so tired from zig-zagging back and forth between the Eastern part of the States, that he even left his own car at home and used a rental car (on EPW's buck), to help with travel arrangements.

Unfortunately, the 1980-something Town Car that Dan Ryan had given him had finally had given out on him, after sputtering and letting out 'Cartoon-like' sounds coming into the stadium. Little did Cruise know that he had been had by Ryan, who had been peering from around the corner, almost doubled over in laughter. It was then that Cruise vowed that he would never leave the choice of rental cars up to someone else but not before promising the same to Mercedes, hours before on the side of the interstate.

Those same thoughts appeared again, as he leaned on the left side of the car, next to Mercedes.

Cruise couldn't wait for the year to pass, for the opportune time for him to lie back in relaxation without cosequently paying for it: No bookings, no appearances....just his wife and him taking quality time together to do nothing at home. His brother Carter had even moved out, finding a spot over in Red Bank, New Jersey, a spot which was logical to him because it got himself a place on the East part of the States, plus it wasn't too far that Carter couldn't meet up for business.

It was a blessing however, that the current bookings in CSWA were on hold; it was all he could do to just make it on time between the NFW season and his obligations in Empire Pro. The last thing he would need was Merritt *****ing at him if he were to arrive ten minutes before curtain.

As it is however, as much painstakingly obvious that he hadn't enjoyed some of his partner's off-duty antics, Cruise had actually learned from Melton, something he'd never thought he would accomplish at all in his career. Not only that, but while he was learning....he was on top of the world, representing one-half of best Tag-Team in the business, with the Championship belts to prove it.

Would there be....SHOULD there be....any reason to complain?

Mercedes Cellphone rings, as she answer's the voice on the opposite end changes her grimace to suprise.

"Yes, Karla I heard about it too, tragic....it's not over??....Well what's the point of....no, I don't agree...." Mercedes continued her conversation, but Cameron ceased to listen.

It was obvious that Mercedes didn't like to wait. This business had been good to her as her to him, but for the success that has come to the both of them, Carter coming back into Cameron's life, and Cruise with a belt to boot, for her to have to halt time like this for a tow truck to help them was unacceptable.

Cruise peers back into the car for a second over to Mercedes, who is still on the phone. Nudging her shoulder, she pauses, looking up at Cam.

"I'm heading over to the seven-eleven, you want anything?"

She shakes her head no, smiling. Leaning in for a kiss on the cheek and a ruffle of his hair, she turns back to her conversation. Running his own hand through his hair, he sets out to cross the parking lot. As he nears the store, a black Lincoln Navigator backs out of a space, and jumps forward towards the exit of the parking lot. Cruise, unbeknownst the speed of the Navigator and lost in thought, moves out of the way but gets clipped regardless. Brushing himself off, he struggles to his feet to take after the bandit, but invain as the Navigator speeds off. Cruise shakes his fist angrily at the vehicle getting away.
