DBrunkGXW — 06-13-06, 10:15 AM Everyone,

I'd like some discussion on something. The people who I've been able to talk to online lately can tell you that I'm frustrated by the pace of our shows lately. I've got a few matchwriters doing a bang up job and returning matches to me quickly, but that's about it. I'm not angry about that. People have lives just like I do. That's just life. But it's not only that. The last show we put up to me was about one third the quality I expect of myself for shows in EPW and it's just not good enough.

There are going to have to be some drastic changes I'm afraid, or else we're going to suffer the same fate that CSWA met over the last year or so (just now putting shows out again) or WFW, which is stagnant as well. In fact, the only feds running actively and consistently right now that I can see are NFW and A1E. Our strength here in EPW has always been the storytelling, grounded in reality and in wrestling and innovative and with enough twists to make it a hell of a fun place to be. But, it's hard to make that a staple of the fed if we're doing a show every month or two. It's not good enough for my own personal standards that I hold myself to.

So then, what to change? I have some ideas. I've had some ideas thrown at me, and I have see things incorporated in other feds that have helped.

Should we do more Onslaught cards? Shorter Aggression shows with more wrestler interviews and vignettes? Or something different than either, more along what P*Japan does where you roleplay privately sent to me, it gets released as a promo package or as part of the show with short form, but fully descriptive write-ups? Should we try an amalgam of what CSWA is doing or what EMWA used to do and go to a format of roleplays showing up on cards only, having our shows appear on-screen just like a real life wrestling show would, with matches and wrestlers cutting promos between matches and segments on the show itself?

Gimme some discussion. The alternative to this is that we rededicate ourselves to full writeups with a quicker turnaround. But I want to know what everyone is thinking. One show every two months is not an option.

Biron BBS-Your idea about having Onslaught written in short form and Agression (the flagship show) written in long form.

That is actually a good idea. I remember in GXW (and Dave can back me up on this) when we had our cards, our version of Onslaught went to the short-form/summary format while Revolution and X-Perience were in regular full formats.

Onslaught, for the most part should (in my view) fullfill two goals...

1) Develope new talent...
2) Hype Agression and even presents feuds and whatnot...

Onslaught should be temporary, but it should be used as a way to help get new characters introduced into the EPW and get them some heat. For the most part Onslaught shouldn't be a very long card, so you can get actually get away with writing the actual matches in short form.

Besides the short form summary can still have some adequete amount of detail (just enough to help paint a good picture) and at the same time not be so overwelmingly like a chore to write.

Agreesion is your bread n butter flagship show. It should be used to hype and further build existing feuds for the upcoming ppv that is on deck.

I would even say that the steps for each show could go as follows (again just a suggestion and nothing more)

1) Onslaught-to develope new talent and build hype for Agression. (You can get away with writing the matches in short-form/summary) It should be your Sunday Night Heat/Velocity show or be even done more like OVW but televised.

2) Agression-To fuel existing feuds and hype the upcoming ppv. It can be written in long form, but you can cheat on this and say like not every match has to be written long. Some of them can be written in short form. (for instance if two handlers were unable to rp for that week for whatever life reason-that match would be written in short form)

3) The PPVs-Everything that is done on Onslaught and Agression should ultimately lead to the ppv. That is our overall goal right? Everything leads and ends with that, then it all starts over again.

I think that this could be deemed as ONE CYCLE. Onslaught, Agression, PPV.

Another idea of what you could do is...

Instead of doing a cycle formala like:

Onslaught
Agression
Onslaught
Agression
Onslaught
Agression
PPV

You could actually shorten the cycles down to this simple but long lengthy formula:

Onslaught
Agression
PPV

But I know what you are thinking how does that allow time to rp and hype that particular feud?

One idea could actually be longer rp sessions and don't limit them to just rps, but allow people more time for writing segments and whatnot.

Now I am not even say that this has to be a permanent change, it could be a temporary one until you evaluate to see if it works for the EPW.

Another idea I would have is instead of Onslaught be the EPW's SNH or Velocity. Instead what if Onslaught was EPW's "televised house show schedule"

Remember that in wrestling such as WWE and now TNA trying to intergrate the house show tour schedule. That is actually change the dynamic of EPW quite a bit; however, it could have some interesting results if done right.

Think about it, in real life wrestling, a wrestler is not always on television, he/she also spends time on the road touring from venue to venue (I.E. house shows)

House shows are just as important as a televised show or ppv because they are like a "test" for feds like WWE to see if something is over, how fans react to certain superstars, and respond to angles.

So the EPW could actually make the Onslaught have a lot more emphasis but it still wouldn't overshadow Agression or the ppvs.

It would also bring more of a real life into the EPW and show that the superstars do more than just come on camera once a week or once a month work a match and then go back behind the scenes waiting for their next que to enter.

Yes efedding is just for fun, and there is a fine line between fantasy and reality, but in the past and so forth I myself have always attempted (some with and most times without success) tried to bring fantasy and reality as close to together as possible. The object of fantasy pro wrestling is to make your character appear as close to real and seem almost like he a real character.

This could be a way to do this.

Dave, I have known you since EWI, Nthwa, and GXW/GWE and you have always tried to be a realistic as possible even though it is still a hobby of fantasy for you. Well this could be an idea for you to play around with and see if it works for EPW.

Your best writers and everybody's best should be on the PPV. Agression would be written long, but you don't neccessarily have to make it a ppv caliber match everytime your write it.

Ben EpyonMarx — 06-13-06, 04:18 PM There's one reason why cards aren't published more regularly.

The match writers attitude.

Now, before anyone bites my head off, I don't mean that in a bad way. Put simply, we have lives away from the FWC, and I know there've been times when I've had to delay a match to write an essay, or deal with a tonne of personal crap, or help someone else with something, or whatever. This is a hobby, so I get around to it when I've got time - which is the attitude I think is healthiest, because if real life needs to take up that time, that's the way it has to be. My attitude is "I'll do the match when I've got time".

The question, in my mind, isn't how to up the pace of the cards, but how to make sure the writers have time to write them. That's something that we can't really solve here, because it's down to the individual how they use their time. You can change the schedule to Onslaught/Aggression/Onslaught/PPV or whatever, but if the matchwriters for Aggression and the PPV have to take more time, then they'll take more time. The only thing that could be done would be to either have one person writing almost all of each show like we did when EPW first started, or like happens in MCW and New ERA, or switch the matches round and keep in constant touch with matchwriters to make sure they're able to do the matches. I know there've been times when at the time I said I could write a match, I could do it, but when the assignments are sent out a few days later, something else has come up.

If you want a couple of suggestions, find out what every person who's willing to write a match actually enjoys writing - what kinds of in-ring styles they themselves enjoy. Then, ask over the last two days of the RP period who's up for writing a match. On the day of the results, or maybe the day after, make sure all the matchwriters have their assignments. Give them three days, then ask how it's coming along and if they think they'll make the deadline. Matchwriters, be honest if you can't and tell Dave or Tom so the match can be reassigned. Also, maybe say how long the match has to be either time or page-wise to give an indication of how much work is needed.

To conclude, the problem isn't with the cycle from what I see, it's with the matchwriting taking too long - something that'll happen regardless what changes are made to the cycle or the way it's booked or whatever. If matchwriting becomes more enjoyable, people are more likely to get down to it quickly, and if they're chased up intermittently and have a deadline and an idea of length, I personally see things being turned around quicker.

And I just thought I'd reiterate what a few different people have already stated, since it's not just my opinion. Cutting down matches to what needs to be told is not a bad idea, but if 'heat' is something to consider, the importance of long-form matches can't be overlooked, either. A good compromise could be a longer short-form match, as was stated: a page or so long, so the story is told, and heat is retained by those who end up on the losing end of the match. At the same time, you aren't draining yourself to write the match. Doing most of the matches in this format, then long-forming one match on each show sounds good, too. That way, it 'highlights' one match each show, making it stand out as a truer 'Main Event,' even. For handlers, I'd think it also gives even more incentive to RP well, to reach that 'marquee' slot of the card.

In my opinion, though, the issue of 'heat' isn't as important in the match as it is with the overall angle/feud that the character is involved in. Even if someone writes a small piece of an angle or storyline, that pushes it forward, for every show, they will retain or build their heat. It can be something as simple as a short interview or promo spot; an attack; being attacked. Having the announcers or match summary being the primary event that someone depends on for their character's 'heat' is-- again, just in my opinion-- not the right way to go. I feel submitting even the smallest angle for a show, as long as it progresses what's going on with your character, is a much better idea.

People worry about how long it takes to write angles for shows, and it deters them because they want everything they submit for a show to make a big impact. I know it's deterred me many times in the past, and I ended up not writing any angle for the show as a result. That isn't smart. Think of this, too: the more 'pieces' you add to the story each show, as an angle, the easier it is for your character to maintain their heat, because it'll be having your character participate on the show in a way that, it seems, a minority of people do in feds. Not to put down anyone... I just think there's a lack of activity in how much participation there is outside of matches. I'm not excluding myself from that, either, but I'm definitely trying to consistently submit angles now, even when my character isn't in a match for that show. Another reason is, concerning matches, it takes pressure off the cardwriters to make the characters seem 'even,' as was stated above, before putting one over the other. Not that all matches should be squashes; far from it. All I'm saying here is: if one of my characters has been actively, consistently involved in shows through angles I've submitted, continuarlly building heat, losing a match won't be a 'hindrance.' Although I don't really believe all the talk of losing hurting characters. But for anyone who does, think of this as a good way of counteracting that. I just like to add more to the storylines and feuds.
