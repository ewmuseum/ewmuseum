A genuinely email-run fed — its own eGroups.com mailing list, not its GeoCities site, was the actual venue — that lived exactly twenty-two months by its own count, January 1998 to October 1999, across eight titles and a roster its own profile page named at thirty before the full show-by-show crawl multiplied that cast many times over. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Email Wrestling Alliance ran two years almost to the day — its own closing notice reads "EWA January 2, 1998 - October 26, 1999 R.I.P." — genuinely conducted over its own eGroups.com mailing list ("ewa2000"), with a GeoCities site serving as the results and roster storefront rather than the venue itself.

All eight of its own titles are now served and structured, each with its own fully dated lineage table. The Scott Cummings to Mortis World Heavyweight chain still anchors the set, but the European, Hardcore, Intercontinental, Light Heavyweight, Television, United States Heavyweight and World Tag Team titles each carry their own fifteen-plus-entry ledgers, several of them — like the World Heavyweight belt — briefly outliving what their own notes call the fed's end via a late-1999 revival chain.

The fed's own first-ever card, Clash Of The Champions I (2 January 1998, the MGM Grand, Las Vegas), and its own first PPV, EWA Ground Zero (14 February 1998, the Superdome, New Orleans), are both held and structured, staging its own roster of original wrestlers alongside guest turns under real-wrestler names and gimmicks — Kane, "The Rock" Rocky Maivia, Chris Benoit, Ric Flair, and a Faarooq who simply never showed, costing the Nation of Domination their jobs on-air. The remainder of the fed's own show-shaped corpus — 121 dated weekly shows, nine further PPVs, twelve further Clash Of The Champions specials — stays entirely unmined, honestly counted rather than thinned.
