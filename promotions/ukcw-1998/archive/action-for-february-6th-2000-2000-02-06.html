_____________________________________________________________________ \ / \ Celebrations Galore / / (Pre show events) \ /___________________________________________________________________\ Since this was the first ever ACTION, UKCW laid on a few special events to keep the fans entertained. The doors to the UKCW arena opened a couple of hours early to allow the throng of fans waiting patiently inside access. Inside they found the superstars of UKCW waiting, with pens at the ready, for one huge autograph session. I managed to get autographed pictures of each and every superstar in
UKCW (envy me! envy me!), and I even managed to wangle a photo of the new ring announcer, Victoria Gemmell. Man, she is one hell of a fox.
Especially in that dress... excuse me while I stop drooling. Also present were the ringside commentary team, Rebecca Jamieson,
Barry Jordan and Lord Sebastian Salisbury. They were very cool, willing to sign autographs and pose with the fans for photographs.
Except Sebastian, who was a total arsehole throughout. I don't know if he's genuinely a piece of crap or like one of those actors who stays
"in character" all the time. Net result's the same, though; he came over as a total SOB.
Surrounded by four hefty looking security guards, a glass case in the centre of the foyer held some very interesting bits of gold. Yep, the
UKCW Heavyweight belt, UKCW Television belt and UKCW Tag Team belts were on display. They'd been polished to perfection, every inch gleaming. I wonder how well they'll hold up when the wrestlers start hitting each other with them? :)
OK, with the events over with we found our seats in the arena itself. I've got to say something about the arena, because it's worth saying... WOW! For a place we veteran fans used to call "The pit" it's been polished up a treat. The decor's your basic black, with the seats gleaming chrome. Every single bit of metal shines so brightly it's almost painful to watch when the light hits it. The place is so black and chrome it almost looks like something out of a seventies sci-fi movie. Up by the head of the aisle they had the traditional big screen, which when I first saw it had a rotating, spinning UKCW logo on it. I've no idea what it's called, though. UKCWTron sounds...
wrong, somehow. Around 7:50pm the big boss man himself, Michael Carlisle, walked into the ring with a microphone. He thanked everyone for coming and said that he and his colleagues were determined to make this the best two hours of wrestling we'd ever seen. He reminded us about the usual rules, putting special (and well deserved) emphasis on not using laser pointers. He then said he hoped we had fun tonight, and left. Short and to the point - I've always liked Carlisle.
____________________________________________________________________ \ / \ EIGHT PM / / (The show begins!) \ /___________________________________________________________________\ And then, at 8pm sharp, the ACTION music started blaring across the arena PA system. If this was to get the crowd pumped up it worked.
What had previously been a crowd quietly mumbling to itself in anticipation turned into an applauding, screaming bunch of maniacs.
Me included. Like I said, there's nothing quite like being at an event yourself. As fireworks started exploding from the head of the aisle and laser beams started sweeping above the arena Victoria Gemmell slinkily entered the ring. My god, I'm not sure if she was wearing a dress or a large belt. Why can't more ring announcers dress like her? :)
She briefly ran through the card for the night, including going through the complicated rules for the UKCW Heavyweight Tournament and the tag match. As she spoke flashes were going off all around me. I guess Victoria's worth a few photos. Personally, I was saving my camera for the action later tonight.

"Survival Of The Fittest" contestants. Told you it was the names!

Andrew "Flash" Tucker... The Anti-Gravity Kid... Master Michael Darque... Jamie Kidd... There was a suitably loud pop for each of these names, with Jamie's seeming to come out on top in terms of popularity.
