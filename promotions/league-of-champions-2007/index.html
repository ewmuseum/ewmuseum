Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

League of Champions is a fan federation's own promotion, booked with real professional wrestlers under their own names. Its pages put Ricky Steamboat in the owner's chair, bill it as the place 'Where Pride, Honor, and Glory is Earned', call its weekly show GLORY and its first pay-per-view PRIDE, and run belts of its own (the LoC World Heavyweight, United States, World Tag Team, Elite and Pure Wrestling championships, and a Sweet N' Sour International tag title) with champions of its own. It portrays no real company; its wrestlers are its own portrayals of real people, and its belts are never the real ones.

Its first held card is GLORY from Detroit on 10 March 2007, with the fans waiting to see the next era of professional wrestling begin and Steamboat entering the ring; from 29 April 2007 its MAYHEM was staged with Solid Gold Wrestling. Its own champions page says the league shut down on 24 June 2007 'due to the Benoit tragedy' and vacated its titles; the last held pages from that run are a tournament night in Mexico City on 14 June and a front page billing The Final Showdown for 23 June. The fed reopened on 6 December 2008 with GLORY from Boston, where Steamboat returned their titles to Bryan Danielson, the New Hart Foundation and CM Punk, 'restarting the reign as if it never ended'. Chris Jericho won the World Heavyweight title on Honor and Glory on 27 December 2008 and held it through its tour of Japan to Tokyo Turmoil on 28 February 2009, which his stable The Standard won in a ten-man elimination.

GLORY came back on 23 April 2010 with Chapter One from Charlotte: the same name and tagline, Steamboat in the ring to explain the concept of LOC, Bobby Cruise announcing and Maria interviewing. This time its belts were crowned anew. Chapter One promised 'the first LOC Heavyweight Champion' and a 'first-ever LOC Elite Champion' and crowned the Kings of Wrestling as its Tag Team Champions; CM Punk won the Heavyweight title over Samoa Joe on Chapter Two in Nashville on 30 April, and Edge the Elite title on Chapter Three in Atlanta on 7 May, the last held card.

What survives is 46 pages: 38 show reports, three front pages, two champions pages and three roster pages. Solid Gold Wrestling, Japanese All Pro Wrestling, Next Generation Wrestling and Maximum Velocity Wrestling are named on its pages as partner feds and have no wing here; their wrestlers appear on this record only as the pages present them. How it ended is not in the surviving record.
