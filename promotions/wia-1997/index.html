Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Wrestling Internet Association (W.I.A.) opened on 28 May 1997 with a short post describing itself plainly: “a roleplaying federation for original wrestlers only,” looking for “average to expert roleplayers who are in less than two other leagues,” an Angelfire homepage given in full. That first post was signed not by a president but by a manager persona speaking on the promotion's behalf.

A president took over the byline from June onward, opening a new-members drive on 8 June and, by 18 August, marking what he called the promotion's “third week” of operation — a count that sits oddly against the founding post ten weeks earlier, and the surviving record does not explain the gap. The same August post described W.I.A. as a small, “Jobber free” promotion that had already run at least one pay-per-view.

Its own heavyweight champion, calling himself Spangler, addressed the newsgroup directly on 21 September: “My Name is spangler, I am The WIA heavyweight champion… if you a good roleplayer, Sooner or late you will get beat by the greatest e-wrestler.”

By 28 October, W.I.A.'s president was recruiting specifically for tag teams, naming one title and five teams already on the roster and promising newcomers “a good chance of being a top tag team in this fed.”

No card, full roster, or title-history document survives beyond these five postings. How long W.I.A. ran past October 1997, and how it ended, is not recorded.
