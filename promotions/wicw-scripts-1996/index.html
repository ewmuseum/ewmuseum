A September 1996 real-wrestler-portrayal e-fed simmed on TNM by its own self-signed president, whose two surviving weekly show scripts and a Clash of the Champions PPV announcement are the fullest single-week record recovered for either of the two unconnected federations sharing the bare acronym WICW that month. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WICW opened on 6 September 1996 with a short post from its own president, calling it “a small league that was started on IRC Chat with a few friends” and pitching a real-wrestler-only format: “This is a Real Wrestlers League, No Creations.” It ran on the TNM simulator, five or six members strong at the time of that post.

Its first-ever card, Monday Extreme, called by Jim Ross and Bobby “The Brain” Heenan, decided every vacant championship in one night. Vader won the TV Title over the Ultimate Warrior and Stone Cold Steve Austin; Bret Hart won the Intercontinental Title over Sid and Mr. Perfect; Sabu won the Cruiserweight Title over Chris Benoit and Dean Malenko; Doink the Clown won the US Title over Ric Flair and Hollywood Hulk Hogan; the Road Warriors beat Sting and Lex Luger for the Tag Team Titles; and Shawn Michaels won the World Heavyweight Title, pinning the Undertaker after the Undertaker had eliminated Macho Man Randy Savage.

Its second card, Friday Fury, turned over one belt: Jake “The Snake” Roberts beat Doink the Clown for the US Title in what its own broadcast called a major upset, while Vader retained the TV Title against the Ultimate Warrior and a six-man main event pitting Bret Hart, Sting and Lex Luger against Hunter Hearst Helmsley and the Road Warriors went to a time-limit draw.

The same day, its own president announced the promotion's first pay-per-view, Clash of the Champions I, from Madison Square Garden: a four-way random-elimination match among Mr. Perfect, Sid, the Undertaker and Sting to set a triangle-match final for the right to face Shawn Michaels for the World Title inside a cage, alongside a full undercard running the Road Warriors against the Horsemen for the Tag Team Titles, Bret Hart defending the Intercontinental Title against Hunter Hearst Helmsley, and six further singles and tag matches.

How Clash of the Champions I played out isn't in the surviving record — no further card, result, or closure notice survives beyond this announcement.
