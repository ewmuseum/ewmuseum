Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Damage Wrestling Federation announced itself on 16 June 1997 with little more than a name and an address: “Great new E-Wrestling Fed. Check it out! Hurry and join!”, self-hosted on an Angelfire homepage.

Its opening night came a month later, 16 July, on its own weekly show “Saturday Night Fever,” called by announcers Steve Williams and Terry Branch. Jeff Jacobs beat Tom E. Gunn in what the broadcast itself called “the first pin anywhere match of Damage history,” finishing him with a move billed “Lights Out”; Crimson Mask then beat 100 Proof, whose own finisher, “The Proof Lock,” nearly closed the match out before a masked man blindsided him with a steel chair behind the referee's back.

Three days later, Blood Bash '97 ran the promotion's full roster through a rumble staged at the United Center in Chicago, with a stipulation announced up front: the winner could choose either a new Blood Bash Title or a shot at reigning World Champion Tom E. Gunn. General Glove won it, eliminating Armageddon last with his own finisher, the “Glove Sleeper,” and chose the title shot over the belt — leaving the Blood Bash Title itself never awarded to anyone in the promotion's own surviving record.

Glove cashed that shot in on the next surviving card, 4 August, at the Carolina Coliseum: a two-out-of-three-falls match in which Gunn took the first fall with a piledriver before Glove rallied, helped mid-match by a man in white robes who showed him a bloodied white glove from ringside, to win the next two falls and the World Championship.

No document later than that title change survives. What became of the promotion, or whether General Glove's reign outlasted this one match, isn't in the surviving record.
