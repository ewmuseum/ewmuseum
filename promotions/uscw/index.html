A struggling fed that changed hands mid-1997 after its president was removed from AOL, reorganized under a booking committee in early 1998, and by summer was running weekly cards across five titles under a self-billed owner. Twenty-one wrestlers and two tag teams are recorded across two full 1998 cards. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

United States Championship Wrestling was already established and struggling by two undated 1997 posts: its Vice President wrote to announce that “the president was kicked off of AOL” and that the fed was rebuilding around three weekly shows and a Monday newsletter, while a further post that year admitted the fed “is not doing too well” and looked for a merger partner, and its owner separately tried to sell it outright, “looking for one or more people to come in and take it over.”

It relaunched on 25 March 1998, “after a two month hiatus for regrouping and restructuring,” moving away from what its own relaunch post called dependence on “just one president” toward “an executive and booking committee, a match writing team, a webmaster and a newsletter editor.”

Its Battle Bowl: May Day '98 card, staged from a Virginia Beach boardwalk on 4 May 1998, decided four of its titles in one night. Abyss took the Television Title from Shining Path in the fed's first-ever weapons-and-cage match; “Shotgun” Shawn Evans won the Light Heavyweight Title in a three-way bout settled by a run-in blow rather than a fall between the two wrestlers left standing; Da Lench Mob's Seagram, wrestling the tag match alone after his own partner was a no-show “by special request,” pinned Dana Czech to take the Tag Team Titles; and Viper won the Heavyweight Title from Jacob Yoder, while the Marauder Chris Evans won the card's own battle royal for $50,000 and a future Heavyweight Title shot. Afterward, its own owner had an old ally, Daniel Dare, ambush the newly crowned Viper in the owner's own hotel penthouse, smashing him through a window with the title belt itself — settling, on the air, a rivalry the two men had carried in from another promotion.

At its Midweek Madness card on 17 July 1998, David Dobouis ended Scott Dakota's undefeated six-match reign to win the North American Title, and Mike Adams submitted Tony Dylan for the Television Title the same night.

A further magazine issue and a run of near-daily story updates and weekly broadcasts survive collected but describe cards its own record doesn't otherwise carry in full. How the promotion ended is not in its surviving record.
