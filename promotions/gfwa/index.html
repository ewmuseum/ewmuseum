Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Grapplemaster's Fantasy Wrestling Association traces its own founding to 30 July 1996, as a simulated (TNM-run) league. Its own president announced the shift to a fully booked promotion on 4 April 1997 — “Until April 4th, 1997 we were the epitomy of simmed leagues. It was at that point that I opted to convert to booked” — after time spent in an e-wrestling IRC channel, and only then began posting the fed's own cards to the newsgroup.

Applicants sent in one singles wrestler or one tag team on a detailed creation form — gimmick, look, style, finisher, fifteen or more signature moves — and by the spring of 1997 the promotion was, in its own words, negotiating to become a regional affiliate of a larger alliance. Its card ran on two weekly shows, Wednesday Massacre and Sunday (later Friday) Night Slam Masters, with supercards including A Nightmare in Newton and Terror in the Triangle.

That July supercard, staged on a beach at Elbow Beach, Bermuda, closed out an eight-man tournament for its own vacant World Title, stripped a month earlier from Greg Knight for injury. The final, billed as a Winner-Must-Reach-Shore match, pitted fifth seed Hazard against sixth seed Randall Lewis III in a brawl that spilled off a wooden plank into the ocean itself — underwater grappling, a canister of gasoline poured across the water and set alight, both men diving through the flames — before Hazard sabotaged the plank itself under his opponent and became the new GFWA World Champion.

The same card was interrupted, unannounced, by a champion from a rival promotion that had partly funded the event: a masked, silent figure who turned out to be the EWA's own Caleb Temple, drawn out earlier that night by an open on-air challenge and left, through the whole World Title match, simply watching from the plank.

By June 1997 its president was introducing the fed to the wider newsgroup for the first time, promising to post its shows there “immediately,” and its own roster fed into the newsgroup's 1997 awards ballot that followed.

No closure document survives; a title-history credit for its own Eastern Heritage Championship, signed by a former handler, is the only trace of the promotion still standing as late as 1998. How its own supercards continued past November 1997, or how it ended, is not in the surviving record.
