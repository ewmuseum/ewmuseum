Flash Frenzy (3/29/00) (Part 1 of 3)

30 March 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
1- "Pure Chaos" Brandon Franklin 2- The Football Hooligans 3- "The Natural" Adam Rogers 4- Humpty Dumpty 5- The Big Chill ============================================================== "Pure Chaos" Brandon Franklin ============================================================== [The Scene opens up as a green picture. That's about all. It almost looks like astro-turf, but there are little pieces of paper, and dirt on it, so it couldn't be. As the camera zooms out we see a hole in the "turf" for
Pool balls. The Camera zooms out faster as we streaks of smoke coming from the right side. Now, we are at a front view of the Pool Table as a light hangs down from the ceiling just above the table. Then, we see the man who won his debut match with the NeWF over "The Extreme Warrior" War Child -
"Pure Chaos" Brandon Franklin.]
[Brandon has a cigar in his mouth as he puffs away on it, and a black Tee
Shirt on. The front of the Tee Shirt has nothing on it. Brandon turns to flick the ash off of his cigar into an ash tray, and we can see the back of his Tee Shirt - it reads "Pure Chaos" in blue lettering. Brandon also has on some jeans and black millitary boots. Brandon begins as he shoots the three ball into the right corner pocket.]
Brandon Franklin: "Another night in the books, and another win for the legend that is - Brandon Franklin. I told him I was going to shut his mouth, be he didn't listen."
[Brandon walks around the table, and small block of blue chalk from the edge of the table. Brandon sets the chalk on the top of his stick, and moves it around, chalking up the pool stick top. Brandon looks up, holding the stick up in his right hand.]
Brandon Franklin: "War Child - I watched your second interview, and I couldn't figure out whether or not you were the man you said you were in the first one. You were a whole new man - War Child, and I didn't appreciate a damn word you said! War Child, I must've hit a few of your buttons, because I know I wasn't the only one who sensed a change of attitude in that second interview. We all heard it, War Child. We heard your frustration and anger. You proved to everyone that you were a flash in the pan, and that's all you have ever been. Insulting people is what
I've done my whole career, and I'm not about to stop when it comes to insulting you. Whehter I insult them in my interview, or simply - in the ring as you found out, it's done. I don't insult you to make me big a
'bigger man' I insult you because I know it pisses you off, and takes your focus off of what is at hand."
[Brandon bends over once again, aiming his pool stick at the cue ball ready to hit in another ball.]
Brandon Franklin: "I've had that Hummer..." [Brandon hits in a yellow ball, and looks up, puffing his cigarette. Brandon rests the cue on the table.] Brandon Franklin: "For Three Years. Same Red Hummer. Now you want to start fights about Hummers, and about how I'm a 'copy cat'. Good choice of words by the way - really made you sound like a man."
[Brandon chuckles.] Brandon Franklin: "I don't immitate anyone! I don't have to immitate people - because I know how to get the job done. And obviously, you don't! Originality is something you haven't come across, I can tell. Everything
I said - you just turned it around and reversed it into your own little catch phrase so you could waste more Television Time. Tell me, is that really original? Now who's the Intimidator and the Immitater? I'll let you sleep on that."
[Brandon shakes his head in disbelief.] Brandon Franklin: "If you are a man starting a war - then you better go get your tanks, and airplanes, because our little feud - isn't over! It's just beginning! So, strap on tight, Soldier, because you're in for a long ride here in the NeWF!"
[Brandon picks up his pool stick after setting his cigar on the ashtray. Brandon goes for the last ball - the eight ball, and hits it in the left corner pocket. Brandon sets his pool stick back on the rack, and turns back towards the camera.]
Brandon Franklin: "This Saturday I'm going to take on yet another opponent. A man who's respected in almost every Wrestling Organization that he signs the dotted line for. A man who seems to be pretty popular with the fans - 'Superstar' Sean Cage."
[Brandon breaks into a sudden laugh.] Brandon Franklin: "Is THIS a joke?" [Brandon laughs a little more, and gains his composure.] Brandon Franklin: "Sean Cage as a 'Superstar'? Give me a break! Sure the guy has done a couple things to get his name posted on Billboards, and sure he has some good wrestling talent, but if we were on a ladder and I was on the top rung - Sean Cage wouldn't even be on the bottom. Hell, he'd still be trying to figure out how to climb the ladder, without falling."
[Brandon picks up his cigar and takes another puff.] Brandon Franklin: "Ya know, Sean. I just got done celebrating a victory, and I don't think I'm done yet. Nope - I like Celebrating, so therefore
I'm going to keep my little streak alive. I've already won my debut match,
I have a win under my belt. Now, this is your debut match. Going up against a guy who has already made his mark, isn't going to be easy for you, I'm sure. Realize this, I don't have anything against you, but once I step into that ring, I have respect for nobody that's on the opposing side!"
[Brandon sets his cigar back down on the ashtray, as it rests in the small slits.] Brandon Franklin: "You do have some advantages over me, like your height and weight, but that shouldn't be a problem. It shouldn't be a problem becase of the fact that when you step into the ring with Brandon Franklin, it doesn't matter if your Eight Feet tall, and you weigh Six Hundred pounds, I'll still find a way to take you down. I'm pretty versatile,
Sean, and if you look at my past, you'll realize that. Not only can I Out muscle someone, but I can run circles around them if I want to."
[Brandon pauses.] Brandon Franklin: "The only man at a Disadvantage is you, Sean Cage. Mentally, you're at a disadvantage. Hearing the good things about 'Pure
Chaos' and nearly pissing your pants. I know it's happened to you, Sean.
It happens to all my opponents before they go 'One on One' with me. They ask around, see if they can learn a little about me, and it turns up that they're afraid to fight me. So, don't ask around too much, Sean, otherwise those fans may be dissapointed when you don't show up. As a matter of fact, you better keep working on those free throws, and somehow find a miracle to get you into the National Basketball Association again, before this match. Because - I may just feel like ending your career! And, if I don't..."
[Another pause as Brandon picks up his cigar.] Brandon Franklin: "Consider yourself lucky!" [Brandon puts his cigar out in his hand, as we hear a sizzling noise. The
Camera fades.] ============================================================== The Football Hooligans ============================================================== [Scene opens in the locker room after the recent Indipendant Pro Wrestling house show. Smithy is sitting on a bench with a towel rapped around his waist. He has just stepped out of the shower and his hair is soaked.
Moisture drips down his impressive physique as he turns to his partner and best friend, Dazza.]
Smithy: That was a good work out boy. Dazza: Yeah but we have gotta expect more of a challenge from the teams in the NeWF. Smithy: Tell me about it. The thing is though that we are the best thing is tag team wrestling since sliced bread, we ain't got much more to prove.
Dazza: Well we make our TV debuts next Monday against the Psycho Twins. Smithy: The Psycho Twins? Who the hell are they?. [There is a knock at the door and Tracy, the Hooligans' manager, enters the room waving a peice of paper.]
Tracy: Have you guys seen this? Dazza: What is it? Tracy: The line up for Saturday nights house show. Smithy: Are we on? Tracy: Yeah, but we're facing some antique furniture. Dazza: What are you on about girl? Tracy: We're taking on a couple of wanna be strippers. Smithy: Well they'd better keep their bloody clothes on when they're in the ring against us.
Tracy: Apparently they think they're some kind of studs. Dazza: What, from Sheffield? You have gotta be joking. Sheffield is a crap-hole. Tracy: Yeah well whatever. It doesn't really matter who they are or where they come from because this is gonna be our big debut. We have to show this fledgling federation that we are the best. So when you guys step into that rin on Saturday I don't want you to take it easy. I don't want you to ease up on these guys. I want you to go all out to show this fed what we are all about.
[Scene fades to grey as the two men continue to get changed.] ============================================================== "The Natural" Adam Rogers ============================================================== [Backstage, Convention Center in Indianapolis, Indiana. Our scene opens with a shot down a long, empty hallway. There is some trash scattered around on the ground, probably remnants from some crew members working back there during Mayhem. Without warning, we see a dull reflection off a dark-colored metal door that swings open into the hallway. Walking out from the open space is a muscular, blond-haired figure that we quickly identify as 'The Natural' Adam Rogers.]
[Adam begins walking down the hallway toward the camera, seemingly ready to head out for the night. His blond hair is combed neatly against his head, still damp from the shower he just took. He wears a white Led Zeppelin t-shirt, accompanied by a faded blue pair of Levi's. He holds a red duffel bag in his right hand, slinging it over his shoulder to lighten the load somewhat. Walking alone, 'The Natural' heads for a set of glass doors leading into the parking lot...]
[And a sea of awaiting fans. As Adam walks outside into the cool night air, he is greeted by cheers from the numerous fans who have waited to get an up-close look at the wrestlers, while standing behind a chain-link fence for the wrestler's safety. Adam looks up, somewhat surprised by the greeting, but is quick to acknowledge the crowd with a smile and a wave.
He walks over toward the fence, making the most of this opportunity to interact with the fans. Of course, the fans are just waiting to talk to him, as they generally are with any wrestler.]
Fan #1: "Hey Adam, great match out there tonight!" Fan #2: "Yeah, you really showed Collins, didn't you?" [Adam smiles at the fans' comments. These guys aren't quite your 'smart' fans.] AR: "Things went pretty well out there, yes. But that's just one match,
I've still got a lot more to prove still."
[While Adam is talking, some fans are trying to get an autograph, shoving a
Mayhem program and a pen through one of the holes in the fence. Still in stride, Adam takes the program and signs, then slides it back through the fence.]
Fan #3: "Nah, you handed it to Collins tonight. He can't even hold your strap." [That's not a real great visual image.] AR: "Yeah, well, Collins talks a good fight, but the place it really counts is between those ropes, and that's where I get the job done. Plus, it really helped to have all of you behind me out there."
[The fans all begin saying different things, basically happy that Adam acknowledged them for their support. Adam glances down at his watch, then looks back at the crowd.]
AR: "Thanks for coming out tonight, hope you'll all come on back when we hit Indy again. I've got to get out of here, though."
[Before the fans can really beg him to stay, Adam waves and heads toward a few parked cars off in the distance. As he walks away, the collective moan if disappointment is heard from the remaining fans. But Adam has a schedule to keep, he has places to be.]
AR: "I always hate leaving before I get to talk to everyone like that, but some things just can't be helped. You know, I get a lot of flack for catering to the fans and playing by the rules. Some call it a sign of weakness, others say it's just plain stupid. It's those people who don't understand what this sport is really all about."
[Adam comes to a red Lexus, circa 1998. He removes a set of keys from his pocket and pushes a button on the remote, making the lid to the trunk pop open. He continues as he tosses the red bag into the open trunk.]
AR: "Ever since I can remember, I was a part of this sport. Back before it aired on prime time television, back before the world's largest arenas sold out for wrestling events. I've seen this sport go from high school gyms and national guard armories to the Silverdome, the Gund Arena, the Pepsi
Center... but even through all the changes, one thing remains the same...
the fans." [Adam gently pushes the lid of the trunk down, latching it into place. He turns and leans back on the bumper of the Lexus.]
AR: "They're the ones who buy our merchandise, who buy the tickets to watch us compete. Without the fans, we'd still be hitting the local gyms six nights a week. But thanks to them, we make more money, we have more notariety. So of course, I pay attention to them. Of course I cater to them. You see, I remember where this sport was, and we're a _lot_ better off now, thanks to each and every person who loves what we do. But why don't I get off my soapbox, and refocus my attention on the next task at hand... 'The Extreme Warrior' War Child."
[Still leaning back onto his car, 'The Natural' crosses his arms together, unintentionally flexing his large biceps in the process.]
AR: "See, right off the bat, we have a problem. Your name says it all... 'Extreme Warrior'. Without even seeing you play with barbed wire like a toy, I know we're going to be at opposite ends of the wrestling spectrum.
And if you want to know what I think about all this extreme wrestling business, well... I think it's a bunch of garbage. See, what you do, that's not wrestling... that's street fighting. I doubt you know the difference between an armbar and a salad bar. What kind of athleticism does it take to hit people with chairs, to wrap barbed wire around your fists? What do you prove by bringing in the kitchen sink to beat your opponent with? All that says to me is, you can't get the job done on your own."
[Pretty bold statement there, seeing as War Child is a pretty tough guy in his own right. But Adam is quite confident in his own abilities, as he shows through his words and his voice.]
AR: "Earlier tonight, you stood in the middle of that ring, and told me to watch your match with Brandon Franklin; so, being the nice guy I am, I did just that. And Child, I sure hope you can bring more to the table next
Monday than you did tonight, because it's going to take a _hell_ of a lot more than that to beat me. For starters, as I showed tonight and will show you next week, I am the most technically sound wrestler in the game today.
And when I get those great fans behind me, cheering me on, there's _nothing_ that can stop me... _no one_ who can keep me down, and that includes you, War Child. You need evidence of that? Go talk to Brian
Collins. Take a good look at the tapes from tonight. You'll see all you need to know."
[Shifting his weight away from the car, Adam moves slightly toward the camera. He slowly, very slowly, begins making his way around the car to the driver's side.]
AR: "The fact is, War Child, you're in for the match of your _life_ next
Monday night. I'm not here to win championships alone... I'm here to continue to make my name known _worldwide_ as one of the best this sport has to offer. And to do that, I have to go through every single challenge placed in my way, and that includes you, tough guy. So Monday night, why don't you leave the barbed wire in your bag, the chairs sitting upright, the tables under the ring. Let's just do this like they did it when this sport began a century ago. Let's do this..."
[With his focus still fixed on the camera, Adam reaches down and opens the driver side door. He steps half-way into the car before finishing the sentence...]
AR: "THE NATURAL WAY!" [With that, Adam completes his entry into the car, shutting the door with a
'thud'. We hear the V-8 engine roar to life, as the camera begins its fade to black.]

Sheesh... just read on gentle reader) Alas, it is all a cheap setup, complete with faux snow, cute little penguin beanie babies (Don't laugh, in 5 years Humpty Dumpty could become a frigging millionaire!) and a melting HD in the background.]
[HD Stares intently into the camera, or more rather how intense can a
Barney wanabee get.] Humpty Dumpty: The Big Chill... Listen up, this message is for you. [He reaches in into his coat & takes out a thick, though crumpled piece of paper. Unfolding it, it contains one of the most remarkable renditions of
TBC that the world had ever seen. Carthatic in expression, indescibable in beauty - below is the ACIII version of the drawing...]

[Try to look fierce... & ends up failing as scene fades to static.] ============================================================== The Big Chill ============================================================== (Scene opens in a kitchen. Behind an island in the middle of the kitchen,
The Big Chill is dicing onions. To his left, there are some peppers and tomatoes. To his right, there is a carton of eggs.)
TBC: Sometimes symbolism goes a long way. (TBC puts down the knife and flips the lid of the carton of eggs open. He takes an egg, cracks it on the side of a bowl and pours the contents into the bowl.)
TBC: I never thought so in the past. I thought the guys standing on the mountains, trying to outdo each other's soliloquies was a silly thing to do, but maybe I've changed my mind. Just a little. You still won't find me reciting some haiku or laughing about days past, but sometimes...maybe not too often, but sometimes...I'll do something with some sort of meaning.
(TBC looks up and flashes his evil grin at the camera. TBC takes another egg, extends his arm out and drops the egg onto the floor.)
TBC: Whoops. Did I miss the bowl? Did an egg break? Will I be able to put it back together again? Will anyone?
(TBC chuckles again.) TBC: Pretty clever, doncha think? (TBC walks out from behind his cooking station.) TBC: Enough of this. I think we all know what I am getting at. You see, this coming Saturday night, in the God forsaken town of Martinsville,
Indiana...ugh, a Saturday night spent in Martinsville, Indiana...the NeWF will be introduced to the T, the B and the C! April 1st. What better day for the joke of the NeWF to meet his end. That's right. It'll be April
Fool's Day, and you're the fool, Dumpty. A fool for letting yourself get into that kind of shape and a greater fool for getting yourself into this type of situation. But, maybe you've discovered the follies of your ways.
No one has heard a word coming out of that pie hole you call a mouth. No one has seen hide nor hair of the fat man. We all know you can't be hiding that disgusting, fat body of yours. I look at it this way. You're either somewhere sweating to the oldies or you called up Ryder, rented a truck and had some drive you to some remote hide-away in the mountains to avoid your fate! Hell, you're probably still trying to stop shaking since you found out that you're going to be my first victim here. In any case, I could care less. If you show up on Saturday night, there will be only one thing that you need to know. The simple fact is that the T, the B and the C is going to go through you like a hot knife through...
(TBC chuckles). TBC: ...lard. But you know that, don't you. That's why you're hiding. (TBC pauses momentarily.) TBC: Maybe it's because you really don't know who I am. Any wrestler worth their salt has heard of the Big Chill, but maybe you're the exception. It's simple actually. Unfamiliarity breeds fear. Let me try to give you a little more insight. If there is one thing you need to know about TBC is, its that you're not going to find a more egotistical wrestler, in the ring or out of it, out there! There you go. That's all you need to know. You might be asking yourself, how can this guy admit this and why is he saying this? Cuz
I am one THE best thing in wrestling today. My skills are on a level that only certain wrestlers ever achieve and after looking around the NeWF, ain't none of them here. I have taken more lives than cancer and ended more careers than old age. When I'm in the ring, I'm tend to be more offensive than Tet'.
(TBC chuckles.) TBC: There. Feel any better, Dumpty. Didn't think so. Don't worry, though, come Sunday morning, you're not going to feel anything at all. Why?
Because, on Saturday, you're stepping to the T, the B and the C, which means that night, you're going to D, I, E!
(Scene fades to black.)
