The successor promotion built by the same creative circle after BEW's own closure -- a stable-turned-corporation staging its own annual "Night of Armageddon," billed as proving its own dominance first over a single federation, then over the wider AOWF community. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Masters of Armageddon began, per its own served history pages, as an in-fiction stable founded within the WCIWA in September 1997, and toured several other federations before finding a home in BEW in 1999 — see "BEW," this same site's own earlier-era wing, for that PPV's own record. Once BEW closed, the same circle continued under the M.o.A's own banner as "MoA Corp," a self-described corporation with named sub-divisions (a UW division and a PWA division) and its own roster of wrestlers competing for titles across many federations simultaneously.

The fed's own held corpus documents its own second annual PPV, Night of Armageddon 2 (2000), billed as the follow-up to a first Night of Armageddon that "prove[d] their dominance over a single federation" — this year proving it "to the entire AOWF community" instead. No results survive for this second event in the held corpus.

A dated narrative segment, "One Last Dance" (11 April 1999), follows directly from BEW's own Night of Armageddon: Flesh, hospitalized after that show, is visited by a former ally and then euthanized at his own request by Styx, the M.o.A's founder, in a scripted father-son farewell — an in-fiction storyline death, checked and confirmed fictional, not a real memorial.

The death of Flesh Written by AI, 2026

Flesh, the M.o.A's own AOWF Hall of Famer, is left dying of injuries from two Fallout finishers in the show's own untitled main event at BEW's own Night of Armageddon (20 March 1999), where Kevin Merrix beat him — no title changed hands in that bout; the fed's own BEW World Heavyweight Title was won earlier the same night by Jamie Williams. Three weeks later, at the New York Methodist Hospital, a former M.o.A ally, Vigilance, is summoned to Flesh's bedside — the two on opposite sides of the M.o.A/BEW feud since Vigilance's own departure from the stable — and reconciles with him: Flesh apologizes for wronging him, and Vigilance leaves accepting it, still calling him a friend. Flesh's own student, Angel of Extreme (billed "Mass Carnage" in the segment's own stage directions), is present throughout, and is charged with carrying Flesh's own legacy — and his unfinished feud with Merrix — into a federation of his own: "you are the Angel, the ruler... in your home you must begin your own crown." Styx, the M.o.A's own founder, arrives last and dismisses Angel of Extreme from the room. At Flesh's own request, Styx shoots him, staged as a father-son farewell ("A love I could only show for a son. May you rest forever... My son"). An epilogue follows two homicide detectives, Frank Ragland and Jason Miles, investigating the death — Styx's own fingerprints are the only physical evidence recovered, no weapon is found, and the case goes nowhere on the held record's own last word.

Affiliations

BEW (1999)

Predecessor to MoA (1999) (1999)
BEW’s own held broadcast (Night of Armageddon, 20 March 1999) frames the show as “a joint venture” between BEW and the M.o.A, with BEW’s own President Merrix calling the show. A separate document states plainly that “the BEW did eventually close” afterward. The same creative circle continued as “MoA Corp,” running Night of Armageddon 2 (2000) independently, its own promotional copy billing it as proving dominance “over a single federation” the year before and “the entire AOWF community” this year. Recorded as two distinct promotions — BEW carried its own independent institution (President, five-title system, venue, commentary team) — rather than one renamed, since no purchase or acquisition language appears in the sources.
