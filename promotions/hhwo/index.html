Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Hometown Heroes Wrestling Organization was born in Cleveland, Ohio in July 1998, run out of a fictional “HHWO Towers” on a closed flash-packet system and a weekly card, Friday Night Frenzy. Its own founding Handbook billed a roster that included “Playboy” Ronnie D., “The Showstopper” Simon Lebec, “Super Rad” Billy Kane, Creed, Billy Sexton, Mad Goat and Lawrence Williams.

Its first pay-per-view, Legends 98 (30 August 1998, Sun Devil Stadium, Arizona), crowned the promotion's first World and United States Champions in three-way matches, and closed its undercard with a Steel Cage/Weapons/No DQ three-way the fed itself billed as deciding who could “truly call himself 'King of Extreme.'”

The promotion's own president announced his retirement on 8 October 1998, five months in, saying only that he had “lost interest in handling anyone, as well as running a fed” without elaborating further. HHWO nonetheless returned five weeks later for a second-era show, Rebirth (15 November 1998, Gund Arena, Cleveland), restaging its own World Title as a four-man tag-team ladder match and an eight-man tournament and introducing a North American Title, contested there in a four-corners match.

HHWO's own July 1999 return handbook admitted plainly that the promotion had “closed down” barely six months after founding, just before it was due to appear at IIeW '99. It came back regardless, replacing its old World and United States Titles with a National Title and a Rock and Roll Title “dedicated to the city of HHWO's origin, Cleveland, OH.”

That comeback show, Warpath (15 July 1999), opened to a sold-out Gund Arena where the cameras caught a fan's handmade sign reading “R.I.P. Simon Lebec” — the same wrestler who had lifted the promotion's first World Title a year earlier — and promised an announcement on the still-vacant National and Rock and Roll Titles.

What that announcement was, and how the promotion's second return played out beyond its own Warpath broadcasts, isn't in the surviving record.
