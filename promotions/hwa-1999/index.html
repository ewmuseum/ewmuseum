Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Hardcore Wrestling Alliance relaunched on 19 January 1999 for a third time under the same operator's own account: a first run begun in July 1998 that closed over financial problems and members' own personal issues, and a second, more successful run the same operator nonetheless shut down himself before a planned pay-per-view. Billing this attempt “the Revolution,” its founder wrote that “out of the dust that other federations have left behind, comes a Revolution. A Revolution that will not die.”

Its own founding letter doubled as an extensive roleplay-writing rulebook, walking new members through content, length, and structure — a beginning setting the scene, a body with a clear point, a conclusion — alongside house rules: no using another member's wrestler without permission, at least two roleplays a week, and cursing permitted but never required.

It planned a weekly Saturday show, Suicidal Saturday Night, and twelve pay-per-views a year, four of them — in February, July, and November — bigger than the rest. Its World Heavyweight Championship was to be crowned through a battle royal on its first card narrowing the roster to sixteen survivors, who would then contest a single-elimination tournament at a March pay-per-view billed “The Night the Belt Changed Hands”; its International World Heavyweight Championship would go to the winner of a three-way dance among the best-recorded wrestlers left out of that tournament's own final; its Extreme Championship would be handed out on the first card itself to whichever applicant best fit the part.

No independent card, match, or result document survives to confirm any of it ran. Two days later, a similarly-initialed federation's own co-president publicly stated that the two operations had no affiliation and had been unaware of each other's existence.
