A Seattle-area federation that opened its first card at a sold-out Safeco Field with a world title tournament and a chair-shot streetfight for its first championship. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Wide Stadium Wrestling announced itself on 15 November 1999 as an e-mail-run federation, promising a level playing field to anyone who signed up: “I'm not putting any of my friends or my own characters into the fed beacuse that would make things unfair.” Its own president laid out a weekly slate — Midweek Madness on Wednesdays and a Saturday broadcast, plus a lighter Monday house show called Rushhour, summarized in one e-mail rather than fully written.

Four championships were planned around it: a World Title, to be decided at a first pay-per-view, Christmas Carnage, on 20 December; an Intercontinental Title, its tournament opening 17 November and running to the same card; a Streetfight Title, to be settled 20 November in a Streetfight Royal Rumble — a battle royal with weapons scattered around the ring; and World Tag Team Titles, drawn at random from 24 November and decided at Christmas Carnage in a four-team, Survivor Series–style elimination match.

Its opening broadcast, Saturday Night ShockWave, aired 20 November 1999 from a sold-out Safeco Field, called by Mike Moter and Ty Schultz with Ian Myers announcing. The World Title tournament's first round ran three ways: “The Outlaw” Ronnie Black beat Pain after setting a steel chair on fire and hitting him with it, sending Pain to the hospital; Jason Jones beat Scott Masters with a top-rope finisher he called the Nice Thing; and Jimmy “Dynamite” Davenport advanced by no contest after attacking his scheduled opponent, Mike O'Neil, with a baseball bat backstage before their match could start.

The night's Streetfight main event turned into a building-wide brawl among Black, Jones and Davenport — through a women's restroom, a printer room, the street outside, the football field and up into the stands — eliminating Davenport along the way before Black pinned Jones with the title belt itself wedged between them to become the first WWSW Streetfight Champion. He held the belt aloft for only a moment: Pain, discharged from the hospital, ran back down with another burning chair, dropped Black with it, and closed the broadcast holding the Streetfight Championship himself.

No further broadcast survives; whether Christmas Carnage or any of its four championship matches ever ran isn't in the surviving record.
