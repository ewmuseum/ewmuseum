Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

FoPEW was already a fully structured, multi-division promotion by the time of its earliest surviving broadcast, Hurricane Havoc, on 29 September 1996, run in a standing regional alliance with SJPW whose own champions automatically ranked among FoPEW's own top five contenders.

Five singles championships were in play across a 37-man ranked roster by an official rankings sheet dated 29 October 1996 — World Heavyweight, World Lightheavyweight, United States Heavyweight, World Television, and Canadian Heavyweight — alongside World, United States, and Canadian Tag Team titles, contested among stables that included the Dangerous Alliance, the Body Shop, and Sudden Impact.

“Iceman” Brian Knight held the World Heavyweight Championship across all four surviving broadcasts, defending it in the main event of Clash of the Superstars 4 on 24 November 1996 as part of an eight-man tag match, allied for the night with Dangerous Alliance members Blitz Krieg, Taavu, and Kiiva against a rival four-man team; Knight's own side won. The United States and Canadian Heavyweight Championships changed hands repeatedly across the same three shows, ending in a dispute over “Dreamlover” Trey Porter's claim to hold both belts at once, before Chris Edwards took the US title from him at Clash of the Superstars 4.

No founding document survives, and nothing survives past that same 24 November 1996 supercard; how FoPEW's own regional alliance with SJPW began, and when the promotion first opened, are not established by its surviving record.
