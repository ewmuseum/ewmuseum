Round 4: Mrs H. Bracket Results

31 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JONATHON WINTERS V RICH ROLLINS V JAYCE BRADLEY V PADDY O'SHEA Judge 1 Jonathon Winters #1 - Everybody's Fool Part One: Nightmares The RP is itself very well written. Everything is highly detailed, and details the character of Winters extremely well for the first RP of his I've read. Well written or not, though, the RP has a very mediocre flow to it. It's just too detailed; for every few interesting tidbits thrown in (his use of the iPod, for instance), it just goes on and on in every instance. It was interesting for the first third of the RP, but eventually it got to the point where I just started skimming past all of the meaningless filler to get to the actual content of the RP. Story was good, though I dislike the overused nightmare/dream-sequence tool, and leaves a decent set-up for the next RP. Good RP, but it would have been much better if it wasn't for the unneccessarily wordy additions that seemed descriptive just for the sake of being descriptive. Rich "The Renegade" Rollins #1 - The Honeysuckle Conspiracy: Part 1 Excellent RP with none of the problems I saw in Winters'. It was a solid and concise story from beginning to end, without anything unneccessary dragging it down. Very well written, entertaining story, and perfectly focused on wrestling, just the way I like it. The only complaint is that, outside of a brief mention, his opponents didn't factor into it at all, and it would have been better suited for a match with Deville. Jayce Bradley #1 - Reality Finally Settles In Not a bad RP, but not particularly great, either. It seemed to be split half-and-half between a common wrestling-oriented RP and an old Stephen King novel. It might have been better if I had any clue why that split was taking place, or what if anything at all the wrestler sitting on his couch trash-talking his opponents through an inner-monologue had to do with a psychopath murdering a child, but I don't have that clue, and it wasn't explained, so I'm left simply being a bit annoyed by it. The serial-killer storyline was aggravating and poorly explained, and the wrestling storyline, while not bad, wasn't particularly intriguing or different. Not bad by any stretch, but more description of what's going on and less assumption that the judge had read your previous work would have helped it a lot. Paddy O'Shea #1 - The Fall of the Mighty I'm very iffy on this RP, mostly because of the subject matter, and on my own prefence past that. Technically, it's a well written and interesting story, and I have no problem with it past that. A story based on this subject matter so intensively can't just have it ignored in the judging, though; well written or not, the storyline comes off as simplistic, if not arrogantly so. The British are the bad guys, the Irish are the good guys,poor Paddy O'Shea was grabbed for no reason, brutalized and terrorized by the real bad guys. A person's welcome to their opinions all they want, but as a story, it was simply annoying and aggravating. It took a complicated issue and simplified it to the lowest common denominator, which grates on my nerves and takes away a lot from an otherwise decent beginning to a story of a prisoner on one end, and alcoholism/attempted 'resurrection' on the other end of the story. I don't like basing judging on a judgment of the content of the RP, but such is the risk you run purposefully choosing content like this, and I believe a good writer should be able to tell a good story without having to simplify it this much. And for my own personal preference, the complete absense of wrestling in a wrestling-based tournament also knocks the story down a level or two. Technically a good and interesting story, but with two very large strikes against it. Jonathon Winters #2 - Everybody's Fool Part 2: Questionable A bit more interesting than the first RP, but otherwise, leaves me with about the same opinion as the first. Very well written, well fleshed out story, unneccessarily wordy at points, and missing any level of connection to wrestling, unforunately. Still a very strong RP. Paddy O'Shea #2 - The Rise of the Small The comments for his first RP apply here as well. Very well written and emotional RP, but still unfortunately very simplistic content and absolutely devoid of relevence. I'm still hitting the wall where I really enjoy the characterization of Paddy throughout, but really dislike the simplistic story the characterization is portrayed through. Rich "The Renegade" Rollins #2 - The Honeysuckle Conspiracy: Part II Another excellent story. Not as enjoyable as the first one, but close. More relevance to the match at hand, which was a plus, but the overused (especially in this bracket/round) dream-sequence works as the only real minus from it. Everything about the final scene, as the Wrestler of the Year trophy is melted down, leaves me smiling. Rollins continues to blend excellent writing with a relevance to wrestling that so few others seem capable of doing, and which is very nice to see. Jayce Bradley #2 - Just Another Night: Ready to Lose it All The connection between the wrestler preparing for his match and the socipathic mass-murderer still irks me a bit. The third dream-sequence I've read for this match doesn't help, either, and has begun to really aggravate me; there really doesn't seem to be a more overdone or annoying plot device anymore, but I can't get too annoyed at him when so many others this round have done the same. Neverthless, still a solid-to-good RP with nothing spectacular about it. I'd be a lot more complimentary about it as a normal RP, but for a fourth round tournament RP, it comes off lacking. Overall: Plenty of excellent writers in the round, but as I said, Rollins is the only one that manages to blend excellent writing with excellent relevance. The combination of the two easily puts him at the top of the pack. Winters and O'Shea follow him, with excellent writing but nonexistent relevance. For second, I've changed my mind twice since reading all of the RPs involved. My problems with O'Shea's simplistic content nonwithstanding, his RPs were more enjoyable and emotional, and he barely slips past Winters, whose lack of flow in the story was my biggest problem. Bradley takes last, with consistently well written and relevant RPs, but not excelling at either nearly enough to go past the other three. #1 Rich "The Renegade" Rollins #2 Paddy O'Shea #3 Jonathon Winters #4 Jayce Bradley Judge 2 Awesome effort by everybody in this bracket. It's a shame I have to pick two guys to bow out, because I think anybody in this bracket could make it through to the final if they were placed elsewhere in the field. Anyway, here we go: Jonathan Winters Good story, building on what you've done in previous rounds. The writing overall is good, although there are several bits throughout where the verb tense jumps between past and present, which sort of bothered me a little bit. In your first RP I felt like the first half or so was a rather generic "talk about the tournament" bit that didn't really fit in with the rest of the story. It wasn't bad, it just seemed extraneous. The second RP was much better and I like the story you have going, with Winters on mind-blocking drugs and the recurring nightmares. Although Jayce Bradley did have a good point in his RP, where he discredited you for conveniently remembering how to wrestle despite the drug-induced amnesia. If I missed somethere where you may have explained about that, then I apologize... although it is a bit of a stretch in any case. Overall, some good work here. Rich Rollins BAHAHAHAHA! You, sir, have created pure gold. The PTC Awards Ceremony was a brilliant idea, featuring some characters and legends of the past and making fun of one or two. Then "accepting" the award on behalf of Deville, that rules. In your second RP, you returned to the story you've been doing throughout the tournament, the "Frank must KILL~! teh Rollins" thing. Nicely done in that scene, and I literally went "Ooooooh" when you ended with Frank digging up the grave of Rollins' mother. Can't wait to see what becomes of that. And the PWN shop? Probably the most genius thing I've seen in this tournament so far. I laughed my ass off. Melting down Deville's trophy into PWN brass knuckles... sweet, sweet poetic justice. I enjoyed this thoroughly. My only advice is not to rely too heavily on the whole PTC Wrestler of the Year bit in later rounds, because it's a theme that might wear thin. You hit it perfectly this round, though. I applaud you. Jayce Bradley I found myself nodding in agreement during the beginning of your first RP when you talked about how Bradley sort of slipped under the radar into this match. This is certainly an uphill battle for you, going against some very good talent. I can't say your first RP particularly grabbed my attention as much as I had hoped, because to me it seemed basically to be a tournament analysis RP with some bits of storyline thrown in, kind of along the same lines as Winters only a bit more so. The second RP was stronger than the first I thought, with some good emotional interaction between Pride, his wife, and Braun. The dream at the beginning was kinda cliche, but in all fairness it seemed like everyone in this bracket used it this round (except O'Shea) so I don't fault you too much for that. Nice twist at the end with Braun being Bradley's father, although I still was left wondering where you were trying to take your story at the end. All in all some good writing, but against these other guys it may not have been enough to get you through. Paddy O'Shea And so the feckin' story of poor Paddy O'Shea's unjust arrest continues! Bless me Blarney Stone! *ahem* Sorry. In both RPs you opened with a short poem, something that is a common motif of your tournament work so far if I remember. I thought you did an excellent job relating both poems to your RPs as overarching themes. You had some good, vivid descriptions of Paddy's surroundings and the turmoil he was being put through, so well done there. The middle segment of the first RP with Danny O'Shea did seem kinda lengthy though, and at parts I found my mind sort of wandering off. Nice intensity at the end when Paddy is forced to view pictures of the people killed in the bomb blast. The second RP though felt just like more of the same, Paddy being beaten by the people who had taken him in and trying to break him. Except for the end where they take his father away and leave him in the carpark (by which I felt a tinge of genuine outrage, OUTRAGE~! as Uber Judge would say), this RP just seemed a bit more bland than the first. I actually found myself thinking you could have done better. However, this was still good work, and I now have a very tough decision to make. Decision Well, my big conundrum here is who gets second place. In my mind, there is a clear winner for this match, so I'll do my first place pick... uh, first. Everybody made a solid effort, but I think you can all tell from my comments that there's one man whose work I enjoyed head and shoulders above the rest. He made me laugh and kept my full attention throughout both pieces, and I look forward to seeing his work in the bracket final. Congrats, Rich Rollins. First Place: Rich Rollins. Alright, now for the other three. The race for second is tight, and these picks sure as hell don't come easily. In fact, I hate you bastards for making this so hard! In my mind -- and it pains me to have to say this about any of you three -- I felt like Jayce Bradley's work just wasn't quite good enough to keep himself in contention. The story is alright, but I felt that there was overall just too much "tournament analysis" through his RPs, giving it a generic feel that, at this point in the tournament, is detrimental to anyone's hopes of advancing. I'm sorry, man... I really don't like putting you in 4th, but unfortunately I have to put someone there. Give yourself a pat on the back though, for making the sweet sixteen of what's probably the most difficult field in a PTC tournament ever. I tip my hat to you. Fourth Place: Jayce Bradley. And that brings us down to Winters and O'Shea. Guys, you made it really, really hard for me to pick the runner-up in this match. Winters' work was good, but while I was reading it I felt like it was going to be in the middle of the pack. I thought O'Shea had a strong start, then kinda dropped off before having a quick heart-wrenching end. I read O'Shea's last and pretty much felt it was between him and Winters for second as I was reading, and I kept going back and forth in my mind as to who would get it. There were both postivies and negatives to each person's work. Good stories on both sides. Decent writing. Both left me wondering what's going to happen next. But then each story had its weaknesses... How does Winters remember how to wrestle? And how in God's name is O'Shea supposed to wrestle with the condition he's in? I also felt my mind wandering away from the stories on occasion, but to my recollection it was in equal amounts for both of you. So, I finally came to my decision. I chose the person whose work I only enjoyed just slightly more than the other's. You both should take a bow for making this choice so difficult. My advice to whichever of you goes to the bracket final... step up your game. Avoid unnecessary descriptions, proof your writing carefully, and don't make your stories too complicated or unbelievable (not saying you did, just make sure you stick to your guns). Rollins will be a tough cookie to beat. Alright, enough chit-chat. My third-place vote goes to... ... ... ... ... ...Jonathon Winters. Which means I pick Paddy O'Shea for second place. Summary 1st: Rich Rollins 2nd: Paddy O'Shea 3rd: Jonathon Winters 4th: Jayce Bradley Awesome job, guys. Once again, I feel for whoever doesn't go through to the bracket final, but unfortunately that's the ever-present necessary evil of these tournaments. I applaud all of you for making this far and for doing the maximum number of RPs this round. You should all be very proud. Congratulations! Judge 3 The Fall of the Mighty - by Paddy O'Shea I'll be honest and say this is the first I've read from you since round two, so I'm not 100% Sure if this RP was a continuation from the past round. That being said, it was a great piece of writing. I'll assume it was a sequal but even my lack of knowledge concerning the rest of the series doesn't ruin this, or even effect the quality for me, the reader. You have a knack for description and it really helped you out here. It's obvious why you've gotten to this stage of the tournament, and the only gripe I have would be the slight, and I mean SLIGHT (Only two or three) Grammar errors. Really looking forward to #2 Here. The Rise of the Small - by Paddy O'Shea Just as good as the last even if the torture scenes were a little dragged out. This piece did good work closing the story for now, but still keeping it open for the next round, and the ending makes me want more. Reality Finally Settles In - by Jayce Bradley The beginning narrative isn't bad, but it's repetetive. I find myself reading the same thing in different places to start. I also complained about this with a different guy last round, but you don't have to use your characters name so much. 'He' will suffice in a lot of these situations, rather than 'Jayce'. I find myself reading 'Jayce' more than 5 times each paragraph, which interrupts the RPs flow alot. After going through the rest, the Bradley chapters seem pretty basic. The Kidnapping chapters make no sense to me at this time, I see no link as of yet, or any relevance. Thats all I can say about #1. Just Another Night: Ready To Lose It All - by Jayce Bradley Some of the question have been answered, but by the time they are I'm bored. Part 1 One have given a little more info on the connection between Russel and Jayce, it would have helped immensley. Was it a bad piece? No. It didn't leave me wanting more, but it did leave me with a lot of questions. Everybody's Fool Part One: Nightmares - by Jonathen Winters To start out, the writing is good. You do over-describe a lot though (IE How many gigs Winters I-Pod holds) but other than that and the slow, rather painful conversation with Vik it wasn't bad. It got better and better as I continued reading, but it seemed a little unrealistic. He stops on instinct? Everybody's Fool Part Two: Questionable Reality - by Jonathen Winters It tied a few of the loose ends up, but it could have been a little better. Some of the dialogue bored me but no major problems with this set. I enjoyed the ending and it did hook me slightly, even if some of it was rather confusing, like this entire amnesia angle. The Honeysuckle Conspiracy: Part I - by Rich Rollins Easily the best written piece of the brackett in my mind. I've read all the RP's in one sitting so everything is pretty fresh, but this was great. Totally smearing Deville for his mock dissapearence, but without breaking character.. The Honeysuckle Consiracy: Part II - by Rich Rollins I didn't enjoy this as much as I did the last, but it was still good. I haven't read all your GTT5 work but I got a good idea about what you've done in the first three rounds, which added a lot to the story. And Finally, the Judgement: #1: 'The Renegade' Rich Rollins Very close. Nearly slipped a coin, but decided going over the RPs once more would be the noble thing to do since it's round four. #2: Paddy O'Shea Great work, Paddy. Had your stuff flowed a bit better you would have gotten the nod. If my picks reflect the picks of other judges I can't wait for the brackett final, a lot of people will say Rollins is the favorite (And he is, technically) but it's closer than you could EVER imagine. Really #3: Jonathen Winters I found myself getting lost during your submissions a lot, maybe it was my understanding of your series, but nevertheless it happened. You get the three spot because for the most part, the pieces were fine. They just weren't the best in the round. #4: Jayce Bradley You have nothing to be ashamed of, here. Nothing at all. You were just outwritten this round, happens to all of us at some point. RESULT Lowest Score = Highest Value Rich "The Renegade Rollins wins with 3 points. Paddy O'Shea second with 6 points. Jonathon Winters third with 9 points. Jayce Bradley fourth with 12 points. Rich "The Renegade" Rollins and Paddy O'Shea advance to the Mrs H. bracket final. Brackets
