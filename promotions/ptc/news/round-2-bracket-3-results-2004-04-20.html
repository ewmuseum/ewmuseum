Round 2 Bracket 3 Results

20 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Cockjockey (XUW) v Coricidon (4WW) Judge 1 I judged Coricidon in Round 1 and was blown away, so I hoped I'd get to judge the this matchup. Sadly, unlike Round 1, this one left me feeling let down for two reasons. One because while I like it when players make an attempt to acknowledge what's going on around them, it started with what was essentially a forced analysis of a small cross-section of the tournament, which actually left me feeling indignant that the cliffhanger from last round was being interrupted with superfluous crap that I wasn't interested in in this context. Two because while VERY well written, the rest of it seemed rushed and going through the motions rather than having a serious crack at winning this match. I can see some planning going in there with a start and an end point in mind, but the depth from last time just wasn't there. Mind you, this negative slant on it certainly wouldn't stop me reading other work for the same character, as the writer is by no means a flake because of one armchair critic like me. This is my first exposure to The Cockjockey. I guess the pint-sized player is an acquired taste, because I had trouble getting into it. I'm also wondering, is Cardboard Chase related to Mr. Hat, or maybe Mr. Twigg? My difficulty here is weighing up disappointment with Coricidon's RP compared to a large inability to relate to Cockjockey's RPs. I'm going to give the pick to Cockjockey because I did get some laughs out of it, and I too want a cat called Fatbadger one day, even though I hate the stinking little leechers. Sorry to both guys for not putting more into this and giving you both better feedback. Judge 2 A well told story but it was hard to notice any relevance between the roleplay and the match-up. The harsh reality in my opinion is that this is a knockout tournament, and angle's such as Coricidon was hitting out at in her roleplay aren't going to progress as far as they would in a normal fed-based situation. It is unfortunate because this roleplay is a emotionally hard hitting read and as much as I would like to send it through I don't feel it did enough to justify eliminating The Cockjockey. For pure comical value it will be hard to find a more entertaining competitor in PTC than The Cockjockey. His roleplays have me in stitches from start to finish which is a rare feature these days. It was a very tough call.. but for me the defining factor had to be: Officer: ... right, what's your REAL name? Fatbadger: Uh... Fatrick J. McBadgerson! Genius... pure genius! The Cockjockey def. Coricidon Judge 3 Cockjockey: Good content, told a decent story. I'm not sure if he was trying to be funny, but...it really wasn't all that funny. It seemed a bit contrived not only having one person that talked with only sign language, but another just just had dialogue consisting of "...". It seems that most, if not all of the XUW RPs are more multi-character dependant, instead of one character showing their own emotions, feelings and desires. Corcidion: Only posted one RP, but it was decent, and character driven. I've seen a few things from the character previously, and they're for the most part the same. Somewhat dark, somewhat depressing, and somewhat kinda...blah. That said, there was excellent content, and we see a side of a character that most are willing to reveal: Rather than play the prolific hardass, hell bent on winning a title because of some injustice done to them, Corky chooses to show a different side. The Call: Corcidion takes this. Granted, the RP count was 2-1, but in the end, quality should beat out quantity. While the CJ wasn't necesarrily bad, in a character driven tournament, it was anything but. Winner Cockjockey (XUW) defeats Coricidon (4WW) 2 votes to 1. Ethan Knight (RPW) v Raijin (FA) Judge 1 Raijin vs. Ethan Knight Raijin RP #1 Pros: - Awesome writing. Great description and dialogue, the whole thing flowed really well and was a treat to read. - Characterization. More was revealed about Raijin, the God of lightning, yet there is still an air of mystery about him at the same time. A nice mix. - The RP dealt with his last-round loss well, yet he explained how he is still in the tournament very well. - I liked how he dealt with his opponent, bringing up how he worshipped what Raijin called as a false God, and how that would bring his undoing. Cons: - There wasn't a really bad thing about this RP, although I will say that by the end of the RP he was reinforcing the same point over and over, so it did get a little repetitive, but that's only because I'm nitpicking. A great RP. Raijin RP #2 Pros: - Outstanding use of language. This writer always uses some very descriptive words that most others use in RP's to try and make themselves look more intelligent, but with this sort of character, it is very believable and a nice change from most characters. - Nice intertwining of the story with Knight once more, explaining how Knight is trying to alienate those who Raijin was sent down to earth to look over. - All the conventions that were good about the first RP are consistent with the second RP as well, like great writing, good use of characterization, etc. Cons: - The only thing negative about this RP was that it was a bit of the same old, same old. No other characters were used, it was just Raijin talking. For a character such as Raijin, you wouldn't expect a bevy of supporting characters or even a different storyline like many of the other ecclectic characters in the tournament, however a change of pace would have been nice. Ethan Knight RP #1 Pros: - Great introduction. I wasn't able to read his first round RP's, so I was a bit hesitant going in because I wasn't sure if I'd be up to speed on the character or not. But in the first few paragraphs it introduced who he is concisely, without going overboard. I also really liked the little dig at Raijin at the end with the mock Japanese, I laughed at that. - Interesting dialogue. At first there was interaction between Stryker and Craven and we weren't sure what they were talking about, but as it progressed it was evident that they were focusing on Knight and his relation to them. - Great characterization. Like Raijin, Knight is able to convey his character excellently. From the descriptive violence on Jago to his thoughts in narrative form, we get a great feel as to what the character represents, something often missed in most RP's. He had some nice quips at Raijin as well. Cons: - The only criticism that took away from the effectiveness of this RP was the changing from narration to dialogue to narration again. It didn't really flow all that well, however in saying that, I can see why the handler did this because the attack on Jago was only revealed halfway through the RP, which led onto the end part. Again it's nitpicking, and it was a great RP with hardly any faults. Ethan Knight RP #2 Pros: - It was shorter than the first RP which some may seem as a negative, but I thought that it definitely had more impact as it was more concise and got straight to the point. - Talking about the equilibrium between Knight and Raijin and how they were at different ends of the spectrum was a great point. It must have been fate that these two faced off in this tournament, because they've got two very contrasting characters who have almost opposite beliefs, and both men utilized this in their RP's. Cons: - I tried hard to find something bad about this RP, but I honestly couldn't. I almost felt stupid for not being anywhere near as intelligent as this writer, as it's a great character pulled off with fantastic writing. The Call: OK, I thought about this decision for quite a while. There weren't a lot of things wrong with either RP's whatsoever, so it was a very hard choice. Both handlers have very interesting characters with a lot of depth, even after only reading a few RP's from each of them. That comes down to the standard of writing, as again, both were fantastic in this department. From dialogue to description, it was all written very well and it flowed tremendously. In the end though, I gave this victory to Ethan Knight His RP's were more varied than Raijin's, and that's the main thing that stuck in my mind. Raijin's RP's were both the same - even though they were very good - where as Knight's RP's were from a variety of different points of views including himself and Stryker/Craven, and they were just a bit more interesting than Raijin's in the end. I will give Raijin a lot of credit though, as he drew a very tough opponent and would most likely beat most others in any other bracket with this standard of work. Judge 2 Ethan Knight - The story is good except there was a little lack of the match till the end. Overall the RP was good, but the lack of match killed it for me. Raijin - I loved the story and style of Raijin's work. The story was there as was the focusing of the match, which made it that much more enjoyable. Winner: Raijin, I was able to get more into his work that I could for Knight. I think what helped Raijin here is the fact it seemed like Knight was forcing the RP to be long. Judge 3 Ethan Knight #1 Rite of Passage Saga: The Floodgates Open -- Okay... got to say pretty good stuff. Pros: Being one that has run a similar character before, this is top notch for this type of genre. I like his style. The man can write in all ways, describe the scene, action and get things moving. He's obviously studied his opponent and really goes for the juggular here. Cons: A slight bit long winded in the opening and the closing stuff. Talks well above his intended audience which can cost you a bit of style points. However, he has control of the english language. Ethan Knight #2 Rite of Passage Saga: A Time For Reflection -- NICE! Pros: Again showing that he can write and use the language. A wordsmythe to a good degree. Very good. Although I liked the opening part in the first one, this one went right to the story part. The use of his opponents words against him is usually something I don't like to see on a regular basis, but for this match, he made it work and fit it in rather well. Cons: Again, his target audience may get lost. Raijin #1 Wrath of the True Gods -- Not too shabby. Pros: A shot at the feelings of a man who got a buy in due to a no show. I like how he works that into the storyline here. Very good, very eloquant. He obviously read his opponents rp and bio and knew where to strike. Cons: Unfortunatly all trash talk. Not much action or story. Raijin #2 The Crushing Fist of Heaven -- Pros: Still well written. He knows his character and how to get him across. Doesn't bother with a lot of drival and backstory at all. Goes right for it and gets his message across. A firm showing that length and size in a roleplay is not everything. Cons: Unfortunatly, still going for pretty much straight trash talk. That's not bad per se, but the similarities in both roleplays brings them down a bit. Not a bad roleplay, a very well written roleplay, but by far, not a superior roleplay. Overall: Not a bad pair up here with the 'avatar' of an old world god, and a man who worships the great old ones. Although I have to say that a somewhat rehashed Raiden from Mortal Combat (sorry) compared to the follower of Cthulu or whichever one Ethan is supposed to be in league with, Ethan takes that one hands down. Ethan continues to tell a story and to talk about the tourney at the same time. Winner Pick: Ethan Knight. Winner Ethan Knight (RPW) defeats Raijin (FA) 2 votes to 1 CW1 brackets
