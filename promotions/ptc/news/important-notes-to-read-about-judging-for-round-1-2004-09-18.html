Important notes to read about judging for Round 1

18 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Round 1 Judging System and bouncy ball to follow: - The are 18 judges for Round 1. Most of them graciously responded to the call for volunteers, some of them I invited before I put that post up, the rest I told them they were judging, no further questions asked :D - The assumption was that each match would have one judge. - Each judge was given the number of matches that they said they would be able to comfortably do within a reasonable time after RP period expiry. - Each judge was given additional "emergency" matches, so that if their assignments no-showed, they could still be submitting picks for something in the event of no-shows. The emergency matches that were given were being judged by other volunteers. - With the number of no-shows being 44, some matches therefore received two picks. - Some of the two pick matches were split decisions. - Tie-breaker judges were/are being found for these matches. That's all under control. So, in summary, to decide the winner, some matches have one judge, some have two, and where the two were split, those have three. If you were in a match that had one judge and you lost it, that was how it was meant to be. You're not actually getting cheated out of something. The matches were there were two and sometimes three judges were lucky beneficiaries of the system I developed to make sure judging got done quickly rather than take chances on people being late with their picks or people no-showing, and that some judges put extra time in and did their emergency matches as well as their original matches. Hopefully that explains adequately why there isn't an even distribution of judges. If anyone has any questions, feel free to ask. Judging Comments: There will be judging comments. They were not required to be a complete assessment of the roleplay down to the nth degree (spelling, punctuation etc), but a line or two about what tipped the decision. In some cases the response has been as simple as "I liked player x's work more than player y's", while other judges have gone into more detail than that. Anything over one or two sentences is an added extra, as that is all that was asked for from them.
