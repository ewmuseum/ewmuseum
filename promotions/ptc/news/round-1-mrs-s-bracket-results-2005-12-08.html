GTT5 — News archive

Round 1: Mrs S. Bracket Results

8 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
HOYT WILLIAMS V MIKAEL LUNDGARD Good night sinner and God bless you by Hoyt Williams The story started off with a good introduction to his character, and made me get to know him fairly well. The first little section was well-written and he showed me the character. Overall throughout the roleplay he used his sense of comedy, politics, and good writing skills to really show the character off. In the end it let me knowing what the character wanted to accomplish and also what he thinks of himself/his opponent. It was fairly entertaining, and only lost me the odd sentence in his rants. Johan by Mikael Lundgard It started off with a good historical introduction to Johan, and gradually led into more off of that. I found that the led up was to introduce a character that was further injured and permanently out of the wrestling game for good now. I feel the writing piece was fairly original but didn't really tell me much about Mikael Lundgard the man...just a bit about one past event. A bit more about the character would've helped him get over better. Result: Hoyt Williams d Mikael Lundgard Williams wrote a hell of a piece, and definitely deserves the win. The way he wrote it it showed his character well, and hopefully he continues at this pace in further rounds. LOGAN CAINE V DAMIEN RYAN All I've Lost by Damien Ryan Not bad at all. Easy to read. I like the inner thoughts. The ending was a bit meh for my liking, it didn’t really add much. Beginning and an End by Logan Caine Attempted to have a bit more purpose and story to it than Ryan’s, but overall the finish product was a bit bland. A running narrative that doesn’t vary in pace or delivery that could have been much better if it included something to make me think. Result: Damien Ryan d Logan Caine Not much in it really, Ryan's inner thoughts provided some pace change variety in places. KADEN CALIX V AXEL ACTION The life and death of a martyr… by Axel Action Some nice burns on Calix. Made me laugh a couple of times. This is disjointed... like a few smaller RP's of different styles built into a larger one, without the required clear deliniation or continuity. I'm not into the whole grab a random pedestrian thing and conduct what is essentially an in-ring promo on them. Deception for a Reverie, Part I: Fantasy Becomes Reality by Kaden Calix Quite well written, but not a particularly original storyline. This is how it is done: http://community.primetimecentral.net/showthread.php?t=14737 Result: Kaden Calix over Axel Action Had Axel Action been consistent, I'd have seriously considered giving this to him. Calix's was servicable, but he can do a lot better. THE REAL DEAL V VANGELUS OLSIG Devour / Destroy by The Real Deal Real Deal: Flowed and kicked along nicely, a few spelling gripes here and there, but nothing drastic. I enjoyed the story, although a few points were raised in my head. Example, Why would after a drunken conversation in a pub would you let a known drunk manage your career and sign you up for GTT5? It was also a rather bizarre and strange series of events. Nothing drastic, but just seemed to bounce from location to location a bit. Could have handled that better and slowed it down a bit. other than that, a decent effort. Will need to tighten up if you want to advance further though. Tales of a Forgotten Tomorrow by Vangelus Olsig Nice story. Whilst it got off to a slow start, it provided some nice insight into the characters background and way of living, which is good as I've never read you before. It wasn't until David was found beaten in the locker that I really got gripped though, and that was right at the end! Left me quite the cliffhanger to whet my appetite! Result: Vangelus Olsig d. The Real Deal Tough decision. Both have ongoing stories I'd like to follow. I have to go with Olsig though. The Real Deals kinda had me jumping around, while Olsig's was well thought through and timed well. MICHIRU KAIOU V ARCHER Homework by Michiru Kaiou We have some training, an interview and talk about the tournament. References to “you” make me feel rather uneasy, as I’m not sure if you are trying to physically involve me in what is going on, or whether it’s a quirk of your writing style to do it that way. Can’t get used to that at all. After reading this twice, I’m not really sure what the purpose of this is supposed to be, other than to establish that you want to meet Tessa Windsor in the finals. Consulting the hype post didn’t really add any additional meaning to it, other than reinforcing the Tessa Windsor bit. I can’t really see the point in doing that, as you are in a match against Archer, and looking over him or anyone else and wasting RP real estate on future opponents won’t do you any favors. The Last Day of the Rest of my Life by Archer At first I thought quoting of lyrics and nothing else in the first hype was a bit lazy, but in retrospect it fits perfectly into what you are trying to do here. I suspect several people will write their characters into this garden variety situation, but few will do it in such an engaging manner, and even fewer will be able to make me care. If I had to say anything negative, it'd be that there's maybe 2% too much description, or selling how down/low/pathetic/lonely Archer is. Nothing specific leaps out at me as not belonging there, but just that overall it feels that little bit too much in his first outing. Its not a bad place to be in though, I've seen far worse examples of this. I've beaten a lot of people up for not giving me anything to look forward to in the next round. This piece doesn't leave anything specific either, but I'll go out on a limb and say that it doesn't need to do that. In saying that, there's nothing here that tells me you will run out of ideas too soon. Result: Archer defeats Michiru Kaioi Convincingly. CHRISTIAN ZENITH V DANIELLE NORTHSKY Do you really know anything?' by Danielle Northsky From the beginning she introduces the match up between her and Zenith and at which stature her character is said to stand, well from the fans perspective. Following that they introduce the history of her as a child, her family, and even tells us as to why she has certain characteristics, such as her wings. It's a good lead in that gives the information to new readers. As the story progressed it continued to show the character of Danielle very well, and showed some good insight to the coming match. It was an entertaining piece of writing, and had me into it the whole way. Cheating The Reaper (Part 1: Homecoming) by Christian Zenith We are introduced to Zenith right away after the article about a murder in Chicago. It's a good introduction that already shows some conflict with the character. His past is revealed and a true crime investigation has begun, where no one has believed that he's innocent, well besides those that are outside of his stable. It gave a good look of Zenith and his past, and slightly introduced his entourage to the reader. It was a good read and left you with the urge to see what's next. Result: Christian Zenith d Danielle Northsky I'm going with Zenith on this one. It was a hell of a close match, I had to re-read the roleplays a few times cause you two were having me both stumped. But I say Zenith because of his solid story telling, and great introduction to his character. Good job by Northsky too though, it was a great piece you wrote. SHAUN STYLES V DAWN MCFARLANE Too Long by Shaun Styles: A very contained piece. Started very slowly, and when talk of guns and what not were mentioned I hoped it would pick up in pace. it kind of kicked along, and was decently written, but there was no hook to pull me in. Not a great deal wrong, but just lacked some zest. Uninvited by Dawn McFarlane: Read it through, and enjoyed it. Can't honestly say it really stood out at me, but it was decent. Provided some info into a character I haven't read before, and managed to pick up on some backstory. A well told tale, I'm sure that this will be a good base for her to work from. Result: Dawn McFarlane d. Shaun Styles Overall: McFarlane's, whilst not a standout piece of the round for me, was good. And it left me on a bit of a cliff for the next installment. I never really got into Styles' piece, and it didn't have much zing factor. DIGITAL MORTALITY V SUPERNOVA Going in Alone by Supernova Started off extremely well, had potential to be pretty good, but lost it towards the end, almost as if you only had two parts of a three part story. A New Day, A New Suffering by Digital Mortality The base ingredients of this piece are a bit of a mixture between something I'd expect to see on the Shooting the Breeze forum in a D&M thread and a character roleplay. Nice work in blending them together to give us something enjoyable to read. In saying that, I'll make the personal observation that this was a little too narrative-heavy. In future rounds, I suggest you look at avoiding so directly making the reader feel like they are being preached at. That feeling in a judge may cost you a match if it is close, and later rounds will indeed be close. You can certainly explore the same ideas, but put the content more into the story than in imparting them in the same manner as here. Result: Digital Mortality over Supernova This could have been a lot closer if Supernova had tied up some loose ends. There doesn’t need to be so many minor detail questions left floating in the air leaving the reader asking “but what about”. SHADOW V MASQUERADE The First Dance by Masquerade Loved the introduction. It felt just like the “Previously on... (insert TV show name here)”, but with depth to the drama. Once or twice it felt like you were being wordy for the sake of it, though – a trend continued throughout. Don’t get me wrong, your use of language is, for the most part, spectacularly good, yet your success is not using “said” as a descriptor even once in the piece tells me that you purposely strayed from it rather than writing a natural flow. I think I’d like to know how the mask looks, feels, moulds, flexes. It’s so important to the character and the story yet we hear very little about what it’s actually like. Please go with some continuity on your use of ellipsis for a pause. The convention is three dots; you started with five, but on occasion used four, or seven... it catches the eye more than the text, which shouldn’t be desirable for something so bland as a break in speech. You did well to weave the details of your match into the story, but this wasn’t always seamless. The throwing in of your opponent’s name or the word “wrestling” seemed, on one or two occasions, quite a jump from the dreamy world into which I’d settled, snapping me back to the reality of judging the piece rather than letting my mind wander through your writing. Overall, though, an exemplary piece of work which should see you through... unless Shadow is even better. *goes to read Shadow’s roleplay* Happily Ever After by Shadow I love your style of narration, I really do. Sentences like “Time, though, like many other things that we use to measure our existence, is much more fluid than we give it credit for.” are just golden. One or two things irked me (as ever!): if the storyteller has been around for so long, why is the Czech Republic referred to by its relatively recent new name? If memories are of a past long ago, as it seems, then surely the country would be referred to by its old name? I'm not going to count this against you, since my point could be invalid depending on what you intended, but I just wanted to point it out. As with the first section, I'm enjoying your style in part two. The non-speech-marked-speech is a great way to dance around with the narrative and you use it really nicely. “It is a shame that my grandmother was such a liar.” Brilliant. Absolutely brilliant. You give her an entire section to tell her story and then dismiss it in one line. Attitude, brutal honesty. Excellently done. But even more so than Masquerade, wrestling is nothing but an afterthought. The mentions of Global Wrestling seem awkward and clumsy. Talking about the McFarlane estate and so on seems nothing more than name-dropping to claim the relevance card. Result: Masquerade d Shadow While both pieces of writing were exceptional, I'm awarding the win to Masquerade, BY A HAIR. Shadow’s piece was beautiful, but eventually I tired of the history and wanted to see just a little action. KILLEAN SIRRAJIN V MICHAEL BENSON Comfortably Numb by Killean Sirrajin What a twist! While I've never read him before, it was quite apparent that the effects of his fathers departure had played havoc on him. Thats good writing. It started a little slowly. The intro and getting to the bar dragged a bit, I can see what you were trying to achieve though. And the guy leaving that patch... Classic. Want to see the repercussions now! Birth of Malformation by Michael Benson Right into the action. Nothing like a gun in the face to start things with a bang! Different intro. After that I got a bit lost though. I couldn't really follow any kind of plot, while it was a nice piece and covered some nice points about the character, it seemed to be a bit aimless. Result: Killean Sirrajin d. Michael Benson Benson wrote very well, but the material wasn't as strong as Sirrajin's. Sirrajin's left me wanting to read on while Bensons I just thought "Thats nice" and moved on. GREG JACKSON V SLEDGE Wrestling… or E-fedding??? by Greg Jackson (FA) I'm still trying to decide if I enjoyed this piece. Don’t get me wrong, it was nicely written, but that was it... nice. Relying on an “original” (William Gwynn has done it well for years) e-fedder gimmick that screams out at me “SO LAST YEAR!” means that not much really happens. Your parody of the e-fed world is lazy and doesn’t raise a smile. Your work is littered with nearly-jokes that don’t actually end up being funny. This piece reminded me of the work of two writers who I won’t mention by name. One is a high-profile member of the PTC community who’s been around numerous promotions. The other is a low-profile guy who hosts the AWC website and over-uses the word “numerous”. What characterises the work of both these admittedly easy to identify writers is that they have lots of great ideas, but somehow between brain and keyboard the humour and dynamism is lost. I think the same can be said for you. As I mentioned at the beginning, a nice enough piece of work, but it’s not going to linger in the memory. I'm left with a big round “meh” in the speech bubble alongside my head. Who am I? by Sledge My first point is a pedantic one, but your roleplay would have been significantly better had it been proof-read. On many occasions, you typed the wrong word: “even” instead of “if”, “Sledge his” instead of “Sledge’s”, and so on. My second point might again seem choosy, but it bugs the hell out of me when people keep switching erroneously between the past and present tenses. Don’t you have a clear idea of when this is taking place? Your notion of “travel” towards the shop is cheaply done. It’s the equivalent of people breaking up a long block of speech with “He takes another drag of his cigarette” back in 2001. You’re not describing anything about where he is, what it’s like. It’s just A Journey. What is he doing? Crossing the road? Running over the tarmac? Struggling through the snow? I have no clue, since all he’s doing, the narrative tells me, is “continuing his travel”. I like the way you tend to repeat yourself in short, stubby sentences. It gives character to your writing. “A lady is getting robbed. Robbed by a kid.” I can just imagine Angelo Deville saying that with a loud “WHAT?” from the crowd in between. (This is a compliment...) Do you really mean “guerilla”? What about Sledge’s appearance gives the impression of his being a revolutionary? I surmise that you meant “gorilla”. I'm not sure about Sledge anymore now that he’s started speaking. Initially I was very happy of your development of Sledge as a logical, loveable, simple man – further strengthened by the interaction with the puppy. A logical, loveable, simple man. Who wants beer. But now he’s suddenly gone all philosophical and a little cliché, and I'm already 20% less interested. It seems to me that this would have worked better without him speaking at all – just helping the lady out then walking off without a word to get beer. Your characterization of Jimmy is very weak compared to that of Sledge. If you hadn’t told us he was Korean and resorted to feeble stereotype (“choppy choppy” the dog) we wouldn’t have had a clue. He talks perfect English and has a Westernised name. You can help yourself here. Trash talk at the end: weak and unnecessary. But I'm putting you through. Yes, your piece had its flaws. Yes, Greg Jackson’s handler seems a more accomplished writer. But I actually care about Sledge. You both gave me a mundane everyday situation in your roleplays, yet Sledge’s had a purpose, and that was to answer the admittedly cliché “Who am I?” posed at the beginning. Having read Sledge’s piece, I felt that, although I would have handled some parts differently had I been the author, I had learnt something about the character and gained from reading the roleplay. Greg Jackson, on the other hand... it’s a bit “so what?”. So for me, Sledge is through to round two.
