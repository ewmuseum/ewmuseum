Round 3 Results - Tigger

23 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SAIJ V IBLIS Judge 1 Saij: Saij's story is compelling right now. And has a few surprises who only see the 'fark' and 'midgets' in it all. It's obvious to see how he is a former Global champion. Very well written, and very well executed. However, a bit dry also. The guest appearnace by Plucky was good, but it was not great. Ibilis: He can set a scene, that's for sure. The character is intersting also. A few small things thoug. sometimes he leaves dialogue without the quotes around it. And the mentioning of a real world wrestler in our fantasy world(s) is a turn off for me. However, what he wrote kept me wanting to read more. Although the death toll for GTT6 continues to increase. In light of it all, I'm more intrigued by Ibilis' character then Saij. I think we should dabble into the unknown a bit more. Pick: Iblis Judge 2 Flower in the sun A very strong first roleplay and kept me reading with anticipation something many writers have failed to do in this competition. However at times description seems to be for namesake rather then effect. Despite this small whim I would have to say this was a very good roleplay with very little to pick at. It was flaunt in it's pace, was systematic and drew the reader in with a energetic vocabulary. 2nd roleplay When I first saw roleplay number 2 it began like a classic roleplay number 2, rushed, unthoughtful and dragging with quick, snappy and pointless conversation. How I was wrong because by the 2nd half of the roleplay an intelligent, well written piece had unpacked itself. Well done. Roleplay 1 Was very smart. I liked it Saij put across an exceptional story, three simple stories interwoven with great effect. Saij puts across all of the saga's characters with relative success to both the non consistent and the continuous reader. Roleplay 2 Again Saij manages to avoid second roleplay syndrome with a thriller of a roleplay. However it felt a bit like an episode of Lost. A flesh back and the present twined together to progress the story surrounding Saij. Pick: Saij on a 60/40 split. His two roleplays where more complex and compelling then those of Iblis Judge 3 Iblis Flower in the Sun Curious, I think, is the first word that came to mind when I was trying to figure out exactly how I would describe this piece if I had to pick one word. The writing style is undeniably good- extremely engaging and descriptive. It's got that je ne sais quoi that makes it extremely easy to get into the character's head, understand things through his eyes. I have to say that I didn't exactly expect the ending to the dream to come in that form, but it just fit so well with the character that had been laid out that it pleased me as a reader to see it play out that way. I've done a lot to list pros, so I suppose in that vein, I should find some cons. They're a lot harder to put together for me- I could say, on the one hand, that the writing style is pretty simple, pretty uncomplicated, but that works so well with the character in the dream that it's hardly something to complain about. The only other thing I could really say in negative is that I don't think I totally 'got' the RP until I went and read Iblis' biography. Granted, I probably should have done that anyway, just to have some sense of what I was looking at, but it might be a 'turnoff' in a way to have to do some background legwork to really 'see' what it is that you're reading. Really, though, this first piece as a whole is, as I said, terriffic for me. It's very interesting, very engaging, and has that wicked little twist at the end that I find keeps me wanting to read more. Cut to the Bone I find that I don't really have to know who the opening conversation is between to find that rather engaging, also. It's a very humorous way to start things out; I love the sarcasm and wordplay- it sort of helps me start to pick Jin out. I get a growing sense of who he is, and what he's like. The way the story continues on... hm. I'm at a loss to come up with just a few words to explain it. In a sense, the character revelation is a little too obvious for my taste, having been undertaken with the definite purpose of being just that. However, that doesn't mean that the character revelation is not an interesting story, that leaves plenty of story still to be told. The thing that saves it from becoming trite is, I think, the understated way in which all this is done. We don't have to go into detail and talk about how event X when Jin was five years old affected him for the rest of his life. We don't have to have flashbacks, or nightmares of childhood, or memories, or whatever. This is just straight-up living in the present with all those scars intact- and plenty of them scars that go unexplained. For that reason, I very much like what I'm getting here- I don't feel like it's everything that's been rolled out immediately, with nothing left to talk about as far as this character goes. I'm interested in reading more- that's something really awesome in a situation like this, where a close decision can come down to something like whether or not the person reading wants to see more. Saij The Royal Family of Sing Usually, I will admit, it takes something special to get me interested in a comedy piece- at least I presume that's what this is, given that we're already talking about inflatable penises (peni?) . But... it's got something going for it- the fact that there's a young child introduced into this already gives it more than you might normally get out of something that's just going for straight zany. That, and I get a sense of realism out of how Saij is interacting with her at the beginning of the piece. I like that. Having read the whole thing as I come back to this commentary, I also like the way the scenes were split up- none of them ended up running on too long and losing my interest, nor were they so short that the story started sounding schizophrenic. I'm not quite sure what I expected from this piece- I mean, given that the duck was there, I guess I expected something a little more wacky. The fact that it had that extremely bittersweet ending was something that I found surprising, in a good way. I'm eager to see how or if it will play out in the second RP for this round. Pieces Hmm... Again, about the same comments apply to this one as to the first. It's a really, really interesting mix of brief moments of comedy (much briefer than the first) and these moments where I just feel extremely sorry for Saij. You make it pretty easy, with the way the story's told, for the reader to identify with what's happened to him. I think part of that is the choice to have his parents tell the story about Leanne instead of him, which gets enough distance from the person who was actually IN the story that it's easier to feel for him- sometimes, at least for me, being too close to the situation is just a little overwhelming, but the way this is told is perfect. The ending... wow. Yeah. It's pretty overwhelming as a reader, honestly, because as you're reading, you're just hoping that Saij will find some ingenious way to duck Brenda and Graham. The ending is pretty interesting too, in that I'm really wondering what happened there- obviously, if Saij is going to continue on in the tournament, it's going to be a little hard for him to be dead, so the question of what's going on here is one that would definitely have me interested in seeing more of this. Pick: This is an exceedingly difficult pick for me to make, because I think these writers are very evenly matched, and they both have a gift for telling the story in a way that makes me eager to see how it plays out. For that reason, I hate picking against either of them, knowing that whoever goes out will pretty much be ending their story here. I think that, as a whole, Saij has tackled a little bit more complex of a story, and taken a little bit more space and time to develop it accordingly. Still, though, length isn't much of a consideration here, because the introduction to Iblis was just so quirky and character specific that it doesn't matter that it's shorter than Saij's pieces. I get a good sense of character from both of them. I enjoyed the way both writers go with what they're good at- Saij blending that pathos and humor in a way that's just so true to life, and Iblis using his considerable talent for first person writing to draw in the reader. Like I said, I really hate that I have to make a call like this at all, but it would be a rather boring tournament if I could call for a draw on every close match. In this case, the call is going to come down to, essentially, personal preference- which character I get a better sense of, which storyline I'd like to see play out for another round. In that vein, I'm going to go with Iblis. Both writers did an excellent job, but Iblis had that added oomph that I really don't know what's going to happen in his story, which makes it that much more compelling an idea to see more of it. Pick: Iblis Result - Iblis d Saij, 2 votes to 1 JASON SNOW V GARBAGE BAG JOHNNY Judge 1 Jason Snow: Excellent work. The way you wrote these two pieces negates that need, though; your work was easy to get into and hard to get out of. All of your characters are alive -- some more than others, admittedly -- with their own personalities and situations, and I really liked that. For serious, you hit the right notes on just about everything. Tension was thick (see: Snow v. fingers) and the reasons and consequences were believable, too. It wasn't done just because. Potential romance in a few areas. And it was hard not to squirm at the end of "Hell." You picked up right where you left off from "The Tank." This is shaping up to be another amazing series. Garbage Bag Johnny: I want to apologize right from the start for having you at a disadvantage: I haven't been following your work like I have been for your opponent's, so I was sort of lost throughout these pieces. Reading them, though, I can tell that you're a very competent writer; it's clear that you're worthy of AWC's top crown. I enjoyed how you put in Nova and then your own fed's Dash; it was pretty humorous. As far as the plot is concerned, I'm not picking much up on it. I understand that this Julius Jones (fuck him and the Dallas Cowboys) is after the guys. To win, there needs to be more. When I compare your two pieces to Snow's, I'm a bit underwhelmed by your content. Pick: Snow to move on. Judge 2 Garbage Bag Johnny RP 1 - Part 4 of Series An interesting story no doubt, but the style of writing seemed very unrealistic from a first-person point of view. The best way I can put it is that it seemed more like the type of writing you’d find in a third-person story, converted into first-person. I just see the story as one where I’m supposed to see your character sitting in a chair retelling this to someone and I don’t realistically see him using ‘staccato’ and ‘unenthusiastically beckoned’ in the retelling. Loved the details you used, I could visualize the story well and that is always very important. I felt you cut the ending off very quickly and abruptly but overall I enjoyed the work. RP 2 - Part 5 of Series Since it’s the second part of a single round’s rps I won’t complain much about the start but I do feel it started without much detail. If you weren’t assuming that the reader had read your earlier piece you would likely have lost them within the first line. Similar issues about the first-person perspective and the positives of your detail-telling ability apply to this rp, as they did to the first. This one seemed to get off roughly but smoothed out into a very good story. The way you had the farmer bury Nova and for the specific reasons turned me off to the story a bit but the way you ended the rp was very well done. Creatively the story gets an A, realistically it gets a C…let’s middle it at a B. Well done. Jason Snow RP 1 - Track 3 I…I…cripe this is long. I really hope you win this whole thing or that you just love writing because I’d hate to see such a brilliant piece of writing go to waste. There were parts that got a bit confusing and I’ve never been a fan of the song in an rp route to writing but in the overall it didn’t hurt your work much. I liked the broken up segments for each person but I got into moments where I didn’t know what was happening because you skipped around to so many different segments. The writing itself was spot on and I won’t harp on the few grammatical errors in this one as you had issues with the posting of the rp. Overall it was good but I think it could have been better if you had written more for the quality of each segment over the quantity of the rp…even if you didn’t mean to, moments in the reading made me take pause, glance at the scrollbar and wonder how much longer I had. RP 2 - Track 4 The Emily and Alyssa portions of this one really appealed to me, the rest were on the same above-average standard as your previous rp but those two really got my interest. I felt the quality of the piece was better and the length seemed to suit this one a bit more than your previous one had. I honestly don’t have much negative to say about your stuff beyond what I stated in your first rp. I think if you continue at the route you’re going you’ll reach an opponent who will probably crush you quality wise by simply accepting that it is less important to match you length wise. I believe the size of the rps was just a coincidence from the fact you had so many parts but I do believe there will be a limit to how far you advance with the style you use. Pick: Jason Snow Result - Jason Snow d Garbage Bag Johnny, 2 votes to 0 SUMMARY - Iblis d Saij, 2 votes to 1 - Jason Snow d Garbage Bag Johnny, 2 votes to 0 BRACKET FINAL - Iblis v Jason Snow BRACKET JUDGES Brett Andrew (Langsuyar) Kat RJ Jamie Tigger
