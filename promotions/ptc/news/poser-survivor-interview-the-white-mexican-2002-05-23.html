Poser Survivor interview: The White Mexican

23 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Unrealsk8er talks to White Mexican, one of the Poser Survivor contestants, about how he got into Poser, realism, and where e-wrestling art was heading. Lewis J.: Hi, I'm Lewis J. and I'm here to interview another of the contestants of the Poser Survivor contest, this time its Jeff S., better known as the White Mexican. Lewis J.: Hello Jeff The White Mexican: Hello Lewis Lewis J.: So tell me Jeff, when did you first get into poser and what made you decide to do so? The White Mexican: Well to be honest poser was the main reason I got where I was today in efedding. I started out in a little efed on a wrestling forum that I frequented and then it closed down and I joined another fed called ecw, the e being for electronic. Anyways there I found about these cool images that you could get for your wrestler. I applied at PCW and went to PWA with hopes of one day getting to PCW to get a poser. I fell in love with writing more so and efedding became more about the actual RPing aspect. So then the opportunity arose to get poser from my dad's school, and I jumped at it. Did my first P4 poser and along the way I was to poser work. Lewis J.: And how long ago was all of this? The White Mexican: It was around June/July the time I made it into PCW. Lewis J.: Ok, Cool. Now you've been highly spoken of by all of my past interviewees; in fact you have been mentioned by all of them as the one whom they think will win the Survivor contest, why do you think this is? The White Mexican: My work in OSW, I knew it would be a great opportunity to get myself known. Which I should not be getting all the praise for seeing as I have only made about half of those posers. Miya has done a couple but Frankie Bones should be getting the same praise I have as he and I both do the majority of the poser work for OSW. I do not think I am the best in the contest at all. In fact I'm a little surprised to see that I've been dubbed a favorite. I'll say one thing though I wouldn't be half as good as I am if not for Miya who helped a lot when I was starting out and Ron who is always critical of my work. Lewis J.: What do you feel the strengths and weaknesses of your work are? The White Mexican: My strengths would have to be my attention to detail and my ability to work quickly. I also have a pretty good trick to get more variations out of one texture that helps for a more unique look through out. My weaknesses is my lighting, it's not bad but it's no where near the level it should be at. Lewis J.: Fair enough, and out of your opponents, who do you feel has the best chance of winning? The White Mexican: It's a toss up, that's what I love about the survivor format. There are no favorites, people seem to think Craig and myself are favorites but all that is, is a target on our chest. If both of us last to the merge then if people do think we are the best then the second we lose a challenge we are likely gone. It's all a mind game there are no favorites. Lewis J.: yeah, I guess so; and what do you look for in a good poser image? The White Mexican: Realism, if I look at an image and at least for a split second question myself if it's a picture or a poser, it turns me off. Lighting, texture work, the pose are all contributing factors to that look. The day I create an image that people can't tell if it's picture or poser is the day I will finally be satisfied with my work fully. Lewis J.: Yeah, I think thats what every artist strives for. It seems that as this standard of realism rises, as does the time needed to produce them; will this lead to a lot of artists leaving the trade? The White Mexican: Those with out patience will leave before they even make a decent image. Not all things are easy and poser is no exception. Yes with Michael and now Michael 2 it's alot easier then it was before but I don't really think that the standard raising will lead to more artists leaving but in turn lead to more trying poser and those impatient will leave and those willing to spend the time to learn will stay which is a good thing. Lewis J.: So do you think Michael and now Michael 2 has a lot to do with the rising quality of poser images? And what do you think of the accusations that 'anybody can make a good poser with Michael'? The White Mexican: This will be a long winded semi-rant and I was hoping you would ask this question Lewis. Back before I started poser there was P4 and that's what people used. There weren't any fancy clothes, no easy way to make one guy look different from the next. Then Michael came along and this was around the time I started poser. It took me a while before I got Michael so I started solely on P4 and athletic man and every image looked like the same guy in a different outfit. Michael came into the picture with all the musclebound variations and if you had one of them you could finally do a guy that looked like a wrestler. Their were still limitations with facial morphs and it was a talent if you could disguise the "Michael look". Now Michael 2 has arrived and it seems those that get poser get him and use him to start with. Once you learn the basics about adding texture maps and transmaps anyone can do a decent looking poser. Play with the dials a bit, distort his face and you can easily create a unique character. Is that a bad thing? Definitely not I think the more people that can use poser the better and this definitely helps them along the way. Now however easier it is to start with using Michael it seems like it takes that much more to get at the level that the artists in this contest are at. There is less of a learning curve so to say. When we started it was P4 --> Michael --> and now Michael 2. Along that road you learnt the proper techniques and if you were good enough, tricks to make your image stand out above the rest, and those techniques used on Michael 2 with all his morphs makes for a great image. Now with Michael 2 being the first figure most artists use the learning curve is steeper and either they catch on quick or give up, but it's alot easier to do a decent image now then it was. Lewis J.: Wow, a very opinionative and worthy statement there, all you poser newcomers be sure to read that J. And what do you think the release of poser 5 (sometime this year or early 2003) will bring to the world of ewrestling poser? we saw the standard drastically rise after the releases of Michael 1 and 2, can the same be predicted for poser 5 or is it too hard to say? The White Mexican: I wish I could say it will help raise the standard again but with out knowing the changes in store I don't have the slightest clue. If they fix up the animation aspect of the program then some animations could come in the near future, other than that I don't see anything that would have a major aspect on the ewrestling aspect of it Lewis J.: Where do you see yourself and poser in a years time? The White Mexican: I hoped to revolutionize poser in efeds... heh... seriously I have no clue, I don't plan that far ahead. Lewis J.: In many people's opinions you already have revolutionized poser in efeds to an extent; doing new things with the program. Namely your work with arenas and such; what gave you the idea to do so? The White Mexican: I'm flattered that it would be considered revolutionary but it was hardly the first time it's been done before. I saw other artists and their 3d modeled arenas and said, hey I can do that and eventually I did. Lewis J.: as and artist and employee, do you generally prefer full artist freedom or do you like having a detailed description and sticking to it? The White Mexican: I've feared that question since I saw you ask it in the first interview. I guess it really depends what kind of mood I am in as times I rather have everything and do it so it matches the description and their image of their wrestler to a tee. Other times I like to get out there and be creative. Corg is an image that was followed to the letter of the description and it's one of my favorites. El Drago and his managers were vague with lots of freedom and also are one of my favorites as well. Lewis J.: yeah, both are great posers if I may say so :-). What do you think the next step forward for poser artists is? Do you think its pushing realism to the limit or is something like wrestler animation more likely to come first and to be a bigger step in the advancement of Poser work? The White Mexican: Animations will depend on two things, the first being how improved the feature is in Poser 5, if it remains the same as 4 it's not going to be used, because the time involved and crappyiness of the animating features. The second being hosting space and bandwidth issues. As many feds have their own domain names and hosting now it's unrealistic to have a roster of 20-30 or even in PCW's case 40-50 and have a 30 second animation for each wrestler. It will kill bandwidth and there just isn't enough room. Unless Efedding becomes professional I don't see animations happening at all on a grand scale. Lewis J.: So you think realism is probably the only way forward? or are there other advancements that you can think of? The White Mexican: There will always be advancements be it in realism or some other manor. The question is when and by who and that I have no answer for. Lewis J.: I suppose so, well thank you very much for your time and the best of luck in the competition! The White Mexican: Thank you Lewis
