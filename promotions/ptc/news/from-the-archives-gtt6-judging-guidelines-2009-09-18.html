From the Archives -- GTT6 Judging Guidelines

18 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
FROM GTT6 -- I figured it's likely as poignant today as it was then, so, here it is again. There's been a lot of talk about judging recently. So, under advisement from [name withheld], I've been asked to post a list of criteria for the judges to follow very, very, very closely to avoid any sort of conflict. Please follow them to the letter, in the event you're asked to judge, or you volunteer to judge. RULE A - Friends Shouldn't Judge Friends. It's true. If you're a friend of one of the competitors, it's probably a good idea to tell [name withheld] that, so he can give you a different match. This way, there's absolutely no perception of favoritism toward someone. However, if there are two of your friends in a match (like, Rollins vs. Sage in round 1), and you believe you can be objective in your judging (better word choice?), then you're more than welcome to do so. RULE B - Lest Ye Be Not Judged By Thine Enemies Likewise, if there's someone you truly dislike or despise, chances are you should just pass the judging to someone else. I definately wouldn't judge a Noah Hanson match, because I doubt I'd want to be put in a moron coma. Still, I publicly dislike him, and there's no way I would be fair, even if his opponent no-showed. At the end of the day, it's better to simply pass the buck to someone that will do the writer a service by looking at the work objectionably. RULE C - Hate, Hate, Hate, Hate. Again, if you have a problem with one of the known judges, please ask for them NOT to judge your match. This should not be a problem, but, if you think for any reason that there could be a judge out to get you, please PM [name withheld], post haste. This will avoid any conflict later. On to the actual guidelines themselves... GUIDELINE #1 - Forget the Rest. Forget the work you just read by Rich Rollins, that you feel is the pinnacle of all of what an RP is, and should be. Just because you think the Bears are a better team than the Patriots, doesn't mean that you bring that into a Patriots/Jets game. You're reading Wrestler X vs. Wrestler Y. Wrestler D and Wrestler V have no impact on what you're reading, no matter what. It's a contest between two writers, and thus, should be treated as such. Also, forget any and all previous work by each character that you've read. Regardless of you being a fan for years of both people, you simply need to focus on the task at hand: Judging this round, based on the writing at hand. GUIDELINE #1a - Uhm, Did You Actually READ What I Wrote? This has come up as well. Two people write their asses off, and get comments that don't exactly fit their story. If you're going to make comments about parts of the story, you'd better show that you read the story. I realize that introduction stories are difficult, since a lot of characters will be thrown at you, and that there will be difficulties understanding just why one character is there when he really shouldn't be. Your job isn't to second guess what's going on. Have faith that it will come, or else they're not going to last very long. It's as simple as that. GUIDELINE #2 - You Attract More Flies With Honey... Judges have been quick to pull the critical stick in the past. No more. As judges, we cannot help anyone improve, if all we're going to do is say something along the lines of "God, this sucked. I couldn't even read it, it was that horrible, you steaming pile of ass." As a rule of thumb, do as your mother (should have) told you when you were young; If you have nothing good to say, say nothing. That will be much more telling than tearing someone's work to shreds, because you didn't enjoy it. I am absolutely guilty of this in the past, and I have been called on it, numerous times. It simply doesn't need to be done any longer. If you're going to post something negative, do it constructively. So, instead of: Jesus, that was a festering bag of shit, Wrestler Y. I'm not sure why you even think you know how to write. Ever hear of a spellchecker, pusbag? Try something like... I have to say that while the intent was good, I think you should either use Word's spellcheck, or, pass it off to a friend to edit for you. There were some glaring errors that should have been corrected, and could have made the story much better. GUIDELINE #3 - Simplicity is Key... As judges, we shouldn't take hours at a time to write thesis quality arguements, picking about a roleplay. We should be brief, simple, show understanding of what we've read, and give a reason or two on why Wrestler A was better than Wrestler B. That's it. No more giant paragraphs summing up roleplays, no more huge discussions on the value of a character plot device as it pertains to the story at hand, and no more debating over the merit of how one character you saw back in 1945 did the gimmick better. Forget all that. Example: Wrestler A's writing was a technical masterpeice. As I read, I really felt what the character felt, and I think that went a long way in making Wrestler A's story advance over Wrestler B. Wrestler B simply took too long to tell his story, but, the story was good for what it was. Wrestler A just did it better, and he advances. That's it. You don't need anymore than that. GUIDELINE #4 - But, Not Too Simple... Ben M. (The Uber Judge/Rook Black) told me a year or so ago, he took part in a tournament somewhere else. His judging feedback to him was as follows: Great Story. Well Written. I hated it.. I don't get it myself, honestly. If you think someone had a great story, and you think it was well written, why did you hate it? This goes back to the previous guideline #1a: Show proficiency in what you write. I can tell you this for sure: You will get PMs asking you to explain yourself. You need to answer them, and show examples. If you're not going to bother actually reading the stories at hand, do not bother judging. Period. That may sound harsh, but, people put a lot of time and effort into their work. The least you can do as a judge is be ready with specific reasons on why they have not done what you feel they need to advance. GUIDELINE #5 - No One Shall Ever Go Alone. In the event that you recieve a PM that's borderline or outright accusatory, saying that you wouldn't know your ass from a hole in the ground, or something along those lines, pass it along to an administrator. Instantly. Don't reply, don't fight back. [name withheld], RJ, and myself will have your back, regardless. If you're thinking of sending a PM like that; Don't. You lost. The judge more than likely has a good reason for it. If you disagree, you disagree, and you leave it at that. You have no right to question a judges integrity without any proof, or without knowing why you lost in the first place. If you feel that you were unjustly given a loss, a PM to [name withheld] or myself can put you on the right track. If you're going to run to your buddies on AIM, and say how much we judges suck, and how worthless GTT is, feel free to do so. Blow off some steam. Get angry. Get passionate. Hate our guts. And then come back, and judge. If you think we're doing something wrong, then by all means, show us how to fix it. We're obviously here for that reason, and we're going to listen to what every participating member of this community has to say. GUIDELINE #6 - Above All Else... Have fun with it. Seriously. Don't be all Pete and think that if you make shitty remarks about every RP that you're going to get over. You're not going to. I'm a known asshole, and I've been called out for judging comments as recently as last month. And I was wrong in my perception and the way I worded my comments, absolutely. I held people to a standard I shouldn't have, and I've apologized for that. I'm now going to have fun with judging, and not take it so seriously, thinking that I'm judging the works of the next Gene Wolfe, Kurt Vonnegut or Dalton Trumbo. It's what needs to happen to ensure that GTT and PTC as a whole stay as they are. Questions? Comments? Concerns? Something you'd like to see added? Feel free to reply below. This should be ever changing, and it will never be a perfect system. Understand that with the human element, and a limited amount of judges, we're never going to get every result, absolutely right. But, we're going to do our best to ensure that we do.
