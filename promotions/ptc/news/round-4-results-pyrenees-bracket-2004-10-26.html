Round 4 Results: Pyrenees Bracket

26 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dave Harley d Dallas Reeves (2 votes to 1) to advance. Tarrant advances. Bracket final match: Dave Harley vs Tarrant Judging Comments Dave Harley vs Dallas Reeves Harley First off, your rp was funny at some points, but at others it seemed like the comedy was being forced into the readers and ended up not being funny at all. I didn't find many mistakes on a grammatical sense, but I think some of the comedy really hurt you when compared to your opponent. Also, I know this may sound a little weird, but when I read an rp or a book or whatever I do not like it when authors censor themselves. To write sh*t in an rp looks bad for the flow of the roleplay and in an aesthetic sense. Other than that I enjoyed the rps and I really enjoyed when Harley went looking for the personal assistant to Vegas because he wanted the coupld of hundred bucks back and when he said that it was a ploy by Dallas Reeves. I have read better rps, but these ones weren't bad at all. Reeves I really enjoyed all three roleplays. I enjoyed all the characters in the rps, especially Beastor. You did a good job with the comedy through the three roleplays. There was not a time when I was reading them that I wasn't laughing. I didn't find many mistakes, so I have to say that I really enjoyed Dallas' rps. Pick: Dallas Reeves Before I start, I'll note that I've never read any of either competitor's work before this Round. So the comments herein are based only on your RPs for Round 4. Dave Harley RP 1: Similar, Yet Worlds Apart Now, on the one hand, I'm glad that you gave me some of the history behind what you were talking about. But at the same time, I think you may have done too much of it, and it ended up taking some of the focus away from GTT and putting it on WWA. For example – and I'm putting this a bit more bluntly than I actually feel, to get the point across – Why, in the scope of GTT4, do I care about Harley's feud with Craven Moore? The history with Mal Somers, you used in your trash-talk, but the Craven Moore mention was just superfluous information, that didn't really add anything to the piece but a few words. Nice touch with the cameraman falling asleep. It allowed a bit of humour (always good in my opinion), and also allowed you to expose some of Harley's personality. Be careful about inside jokes, like your cut on Matt W. (who I can only assume is in a leadership role of WWA). The clear problem with inside jokes is that they only make sense to people who are actually inside. In a forum such as this tournament, a good number of your readers (myself included) aren't inside, so the joke ends up lost on much of your audience. A fairly solid trash-talk piece, and I'm happy to see that people are still using the trash-talk format, as it often gets a bad rep these days. RP 2: Covert Operations I don't know if you've used Jill in previous Tournament pieces, but either way, I'm glad you told us who she was, right off the bat. There's little worse when you're coming into an RP without any prior knowledge than trying to figure out who the people you're reading about are. I like the relationship between Jill and Dave, as it is a great way to expose things about Harley, both in actions and in dialogue. For example, the joke about Burger King Kids Meals says a lot about Harley's personality. As a side note, while I liked the joke about writing a book, to be fair, one could always dictate a book, without knowing how to read. For example, Dave could make Jill – as his secretary – take down an entire book by dictation. Thank you for explaining why Jill knows about NGW. Supporting characters who just happen to know everything about the wrestling world for no particular reason, beyond convenience is a bit unreal, and for me, annoying. All it took to avoid that was a single sentence. Unfortunately, one such character showed up in the form of the cab driver at the end – and just so you could make an inside joke. At least it gave something of a reason for the mention of Craven Moore last round (because it would have been completely lost on me without that mention), but still, I could have done without it. With all of the people that are "household names" in pop culture alone, a very similar joke could have been made without the "inside" factor. Jill quitting and going to Vegas totally came out of nowhere, and I literally did a double-take when I saw it. I liked how you built to it (repeating the morning scene from the beginning, but without Jill there) but the sudden explanation of her absence was a bit odd for me. I realize it was meant as a shocker, but still, it just felt off to me. Knowing that there's a continuation, I hope to see something more substantial on it there. All that said, I also liked how Harley jumped to the conspiracy theory that his opponent and/or NGW was behind it all, both for the humour and the character exposition. RP 3: The Strip I'm not going to lie to you, I was a bit disappointed by this piece. I'm all for antics and hijinks, and the piece was fun... But the randomness of it - coming off of a fairly solid trash-talk RP and a second with good character exposition and potential for something really interesting – took away from it for me. Made me feel a bit like the build-up of the Jill/Dave relationship in RP 2 was wasted, given that what you'd written previously had me expecting something different, and less off-the-wall. When I read of Harley asking for, and immediately getting Rick the cabbie's keys, I was prepared to ask you "Why in the world would he do that?" But there are far too many other similar questions that tend to arise from the randomness of the rest of the piece, so I won't go down that road. Perhaps I should have expected what I got here, given the utter randomness of Jill's disappearance in the first place. But again, the rest of what you'd written this Round up to that point (and even the start of this 3rd piece, up to the aforementioned key thing) didn't really lend itself to that kind of haphazard development and conclusion. Dallas Reeves RP 1: Quick intro of your supporting characters to start is good news. Not a whole lot going on here until they reach the farmhouse. A little random conversation, giving readers a bit about the three characters, followed by a unrelated phone call (thankfully explained by the "acting gigs" reference later), and a bit on Reeves in GTT4. Then the strangeness begins. Beastor eating a (presumably live, or extremely recently live) chicken... And then, while it's absurd, I'll admit, I laughed at Frankie uttering "Guard…cow," while under the iron hoof of the farm animal turned security guard. If this isn't indicative of your usual style (I'll find out in the next two RPs, I'm sure), then this, let's call it "quirkiness," is at least good evidence for the "motley crew of miscreants" line later. Good balance in the piece, getting into some wrestler/manager conversation about the upcoming match, though it's beginning was somewhat abrupt. That is, it pretty well came out of nowhere, and didn't flow well from the events directly preceding (Reeves moving to smother Trudeau). RP 2: This was a cute piece, which ended up tying together nicely, under the "over-achieving" banner. The cowboy bit was clichéd as all hell, but it wouldn't have been funny if it weren't. As I said about Harley's first RP, I'm glad to see the trash-talk happening here. I enjoyed this one more than the first, simply because it was, in the final analysis, less random and the part that was somewhat random served more of a purpose. RP 3: Again, good quick intros of your characters. Someone who only reads this RP will not have to scramble to figure out who's who and what the relationships are. Though, for those that do read all of your stuff (or even just this Round's RPs), you might consider working a bit more variety into how you make these introductions, so they don't get bored reading, effectively, the same thing in multiple pieces. Beyond that, this piece just seemed to stagnate, somehow. Beyond going into Harley's apparent Hardcore tendencies – something that I, as new to Harley didn't know anything of from reading his work - there was nothing really new here. The interactions between characters were consistent with the previous piece which is good, but there wasn't any further development or much extension beyond what was already done in the first. The tournament relevant dialogue seemed to mostly reiterate what had already been said. Conclusion My favourite part of Dave's RPs was in his second, juxtaposing Harley with and without Jill. The events were set up well, and the section without Jill was a good way to clearly exemplify their relationship. My favourite part of Jer's was also in his second (the two second RPs were the strongest, to me in this match), because it was funny without being mostly random like the first and last pieces, because the humourous section tied together well with the trash-talk later. My main issue with Dave's RPs was that his third piece was a disappointing end to what I felt was a build with good potential. My main issue with Jer's was that he didn't really seem to go anywhere, in story or in character development. I think Dave's good was a bit better and Jer's bad was a bit worse. The biggest difference is that while the end was a bit disappointing (based on what could have been), at least Harley went somewhere, while Reeves seemed to be in a bit of rut. Pick: Dave Harley Reeves: Great roleplays as usual, a good set of series all together. They had a good amount of length and story to them, but I think there was some filler in it which I find shouldn't have played a part, ie. some comments made throughout the roleplay that aren't relevant with getting to the point. All and All however, everything was well written Harley: 3 terrific roleplays, each of which had a good flow, the story spanded over all three roleplays. They had interesting twists and had shown some things about Harley's character that may not have been known about before. Easy for a first time reader to follow along with. Pick: Harley Tarrant McClancy resigned, but some comments for Tarrant, nonetheless: RP 1: A New Unexpected Chapter Begins It's nitpicky, I know, but a few grammar issues hampered readability a touch: - The dreaded homonym mix-up popped up a couple of times ("your" versus "you're"). It'll never get caught by a Word spell check and probably won't get caught by a grammar check either, so it comes down to proofreading. The small mistakes are often the hardest to catch, unfortunately, but they also tend to be the easiest to make. - There were a number of paragraph/sentences that ran on a bit, and probably would have done well being split. For example, the paragraph/sentence beginning with "Turning around Tarrant continued walking..." and the paragraph/sentence directly following it. My thinking is, anytime you've got a single sentence that runs 3+ lines (and/or an entire paragraph), it can probably do with a split into at least two smaller sentences. - In a few spots a word seemed to be missing from a sentence. It was fairly clear what you were saying, but it took a second to mentally add the word, which took away from the flow of the piece a bit. For example: "at least it would be to a man whom I have a deep respect." seemed to be missing a "for," before "whom." Beyond those relatively minor issues... I liked the intro, as a way to set the scene. It was confusing at first, but the realization that it was a rehearsal made it click. I liked the not-so-subtle commentary on the evolution of the game ("Who would have thought that wrestling stars and match winners would come down to who could bring in the most money and hype, and NOT actual wrestling skill eh?"). Thank you for telling me who Amber was right as she appeared. Having not read any of your work previous to this, that quick sentence was enough to eliminate any "Who the hell is this woman?" thoughts from happening. I find it's always a good bet to do something like that, so a reader can jump in anywhere and not be confused. I like the gimmick drop, and the way it was done, citing the aforementioned evolution of the game. I've done a few gimmick drops myself, so I appreciate that angle. My only criticism on it is that I think it might have been more powerful somehow. As I said before, I haven't read any of your other RPs, so I don't know if there was more build to this drop, so based on this RP alone, it came off as a bit sudden. I'm hoping to see some more exploration of the emotions involved in the drop when I read your next RP. Leaving something that's presumably been at the core of your character a long time has a lot of potential, and I hope it's not wasted. RP 2: The future is Chris Tarrant Thank you for not disappointing me. I was hoping for some more exploration of the gimmick drop, and got it right away. Then got more throughout the piece, exploring the fear involved in such a dramatic change, and explaining more of the reasons for it. Back on the nitpicky train, similar grammar issues in this one, particularly the run-on sentences. Another couple of pesky homonym mix-ups popped in here as well ("to" versus "too," "strait" vs. "straight"). Also cropping up once in a while in both RPs was missing letters from words (examples: "I had to get use to" missing a "d" on "used" [paragraph 3 of this RP]; "A few ours later" missing the "h" on "hours" [first paragraph of third section of this RP]). Kudos again, for telling me who Amber was right away, and adding that she's married to Tarrant - something that I didn't get from the first RP, and needed for this one. That makes the massage bit in the first RP make even more sense. I liked the infusion of humour. ("Sorry about that, I was choking on your nuts.") Even something small like that adds a fair bit to the enjoyment of the piece. I personally always try to put a touch of humour into my stuff, and always appreciate it when I see it in others' work. I'm glad you mentioned that the Magazine was about to go to print, and would be out in a few days. The first thought I had when I read about Tarrant going to a magazine interview was that it would be less effective (nearly pointless), if the magazine wouldn't be out for a while. All in all, a good follow up and expansion on the gimmick drop. Conclusion You've got a solid base to build from after this Round's pieces. I hope you don't mind all the nitpicking, and that something I've said helps. Good luck in the Bracket Finals! Pyrenees
