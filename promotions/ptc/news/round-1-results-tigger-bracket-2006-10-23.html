Round 1 Results - Tigger Bracket

23 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
ELY BRAXTON V DANNY VISTA Judge 1 Whoa, first of all you need to proofread these things. It was absolutely littered with errors, to the point that I had trouble reading it at some points. The story itself is better than Vista's. In my opinion, had you concentrated more on the main thing you're writing about this would be an easy decision. However, in terms of actual writing, he bested you. So that makes this slightly more difficult. Very close match. In the end, even though it was more difficult to read, Braxton produced a better piece. Pick: Ely Braxton Judge 2 Head to head, Vista takes the match. Vista's story was good, as was Ely's. Vista's however, was more one complete story from front to back. Ely's seemed almost as if it were two stories intertwined in a looser fashion than what probably should have been. Had it been tightened up a little bit, the result could have been very, very different. Unfortunately, it's not. Pick: Danny Vista Judge 3 Ely Braxton: Enjoyed the balance of the rp. The breaks made it a bit annoying at times. Story seemed a bit broken, but good meat to the story. Danny Vista: The story was good, but needed development a bit more and more reasons as to why someone would do what they do. Perhaps play on Vista's paranoia a bit more. Good rp though. Pick: Ely Braxton, just. Result - Ely Braxton d Danny Vista, 2 votes to 1 SAMUEL STEVENS V SAIJ Judge 1 Saij tells the better story. Stevens wrote well, but, the story didn't draw me in, and make me want to read more of it, I guess. Whereas Saij has an everyman quality to him, Stevens doesn't do much to endear the reader. Technically both peices were very sound. Pick: Saij Judge 2 Saij: I liked the story and how you put into words how Saij was feeling. I don't have much to nitpick (though I would have much preferred the vibrating cell as an act for his kid, but I suppose it's the character, yeah?). Solid effort. Samuel Stevens: Be honest with you here: I didn't care for it much. That's my usual response to "future" pieces, but that, all the characters and mentions of characters, and what struck me as forced, unnatural dialogue killed any enjoyment I could have gotten from it. I'll give you one thing, though: I thought the ending was kinda clever. Pick: Saij Result - Saij d Samuel Stevens, 2 votes to 0 TOKEN WEED V SCOTTYWOOD Judge 1 Token Weed: It was a little rough around its edges, succumbing to a few careless grammatical errors. It struck me as very tA, though, and I could pick more positive than negative, so good job. Scottywood: Maybe it's the difference between our senses of humor, but I didn't get anything from this piece at all. Some careless errors in your piece, too. If you want to advance in a tournament like the GTT, you need to up your game. Pick: Token Weed. Judge 2 Token Weed You encounter the drug thing really well. I've had some of the same experiences, but it's also interesting to see it from another perspective. Beyond that, though, there isn't a lot in this RP that I haven't seen, and it's not written in any style that I enjoy. It's actually quite jargon, as if you've just started writing. You really need to read over your work, or better yet, have a friend do it. Authors see things in un-clear writing that readers don't. Scottywood Comedy needs to be funny, and this isn't funny. Very bland opening, very bland dialogue, very stereotypical punchlines...it's just not funny, and it looks like that's what you're banking off of. While your opponents grammar was ugly, yours wasn't bad, but the language was so simple that it didn't even matter. Way too simple. This is a high-level competition. I don't think you're ready to be RPing in this tournament...read some of the better RPs and get an idea of why the script style is dying out, or at least why it is for simple language. Pick: Token Weed barely squeaks by. Judge 3 Short and sweet; long and short. Token Weed takes this, soundly. His prose, while a bit forced, read much better than Scottywood's script format. The rest is moot, because as judges, we're supposed to be nice now. Pick: Token Weed. Result - Token Weed d Scottywood, 3 votes to 0 FRED GREST V GARBAGE BAG POPPA JOHNNY Judge 1 Two contrasting stories here. GBJ lived up to the hype with a long, expositional RP. Grest chose to take the short route, and told a decent story. Unfortunately, Grest had a few tense issues, and a few other issues that needed a bit closer proofreading that could have helped more. Pick: Garbage Bag Johnny. Judge 2 Fred Grest Basic plot that didn't move to take a fresh spin on the "pro wrestling failure decides to give the business another go" idea. The piece felt mechanical, like the events were following a prescribed sequence in a race to get to the end. The intent was there, but glaring structural errors in the writing made for distractions. GBJ Nice use of descriptions, especially in the first few paragraphs, and backstory later on in the piece. It tended to run on the long-ish side, but in this case the length was beneficial to the storytelling without being too verbose. I'll be interested to see how the characters interact with one another in subsequent writings. Pick: GBJ Judge 3 Garbage Bag Johnny Excellent story. I loved the beginning where he explains being taught about fighting by his father, through to Adam Dick being his savior from a serious beating in some bar, all the way through to the end. An excellent read that I really enjoyed. Fred Grest I actually liked the idea of him thinking he had to walk away and now being a video store clerk after being beaten numerous times by the upper tier stars of the companies he was in. I think this idea had a lot of merit and could have been used to perfection. It was a very interesting beginning and reason for him to be there. Solid work. While I feel like Grest had something going for him here in his piece it simply wasn’t good enough to derail GBJ who had a fantastic story in his piece. Great job to both but GBJ wins this one and moves on to the next round. Pick: GBJ Result Garbage Bag Johnny d Fred Grest, 3 votes to 0 JASON SNOW V THE SENTINEL Judge 1 Memento by The Sentinel: I really don't have anything bad to say about it. I got to know the character, good writing, and I'm a fan of the 'Snow' Metaphor you used. Track 1: "Died" by Jason Snow: A lot of hype surrounding this debut, and you easily lived up to it. One of the best RP's I've read all round, and if you keep this up, I can see you getting far. Wasn't a difficult decision. Nothing wrong with Sentinel's here, but Snow's piece was on another level. Pick: Jason Snow Judge 2 This judgement will be 234,342 words shorter than The Sentinel and Snow's entire stories. I liked how each were written, but, Snow's was a little better. I liked the dialogue, but, Snow was a little better. I liked how each story unfurled, but, Snow's story was a little better... Can you see where this is going? Sentinel, you write well, and if I didn't know better, I could tell you've been doing this for a long time, like the character. But, Snow was better and different. There was nothing you could have done better, you simply were beaten by a better, hungrier man. And even that might not have worked. Pick: Jason Snow. Judge 3 Jason Snow Story was easy to follow and broke down several questions you began to have reading. Only dislike was a songs lyrics. Seemed to be filler, but above and beyond that the rp was very good. The Sentinal The story was coming around once it was read a few times. The breaks made it sometimes confusing to follow without any lead ins. Oherwise pretty much a good story. Pick: Jason Snow Result Jason Snow d The Sentinel, 3 votes to 0 BRYAN STORMS V THE REAL DEAL Judge 1 Storms chose to tell a bit, while Real Deal decided to just give a little glimpse into what could be his story. Problem was, there was absolutely nothing of substance in Real Deal's story, and there could have been a lot more. Storms wrote a typical "day in the life, oh my god, I'm going to GTT!" story. And it was good, for what it was. Pick: Bryan Storms Judge 2 The Real Deal Not a bad piece but it simply left to much to be desired. What you had seemed to be pretty good but it just didn’t seem to really give the reader, in this case myself, enough to really be excited about it. Storms I actually really dug the beginning and was honestly getting quite into the whole meeting thing and really thought you were going with something interesting. I was a bit disappointed when you quickly jumped from that and simply had him already in his hotel room. Then you continued to jump around a bit too much for me. He was at the meeting, at home, in bed, next day, calling this person and then that and it was done just a tad too fast and without any real description or time between them. Other than that it was a pretty solid piece that had it’s moments in which I was able to get into the character and his story. Storms wins this one pretty easily. I don’t know how good The Real Deal could be if he gave a serious effort but this one seemed like he just didn’t give it much thought or care. Storms has some stuff in his characters history that could be interesting if he continues to flesh it out as much as possible. Good luck in the future rounds Storms. Pick: Bryan Storms Judge 3 Bryan Storms The story is written fairly well, but Im easily lost with it. Im not sure where you're going...you talk about a book, but it seems to drop within moments. A drug addiction...and nothing into it? I know nothing about Bryan Storms, and this RP didn't help too much either. It would've been nice to get more of an insight into the character. Regardless, though, it was a decent RP that I had an OK time reading. The Real Deal It's tough to say anything about this RP because...well...there's nothing to it. What is The Real Deal? Why should I Care about anything he does? You have a lot of little meaningless bits of information that are strung together to make a hype for last year's competition. Pick: Bryan Storms, but he needs to improve in the second round. Result - Bryan Storms d The Real Deal, 3 votes to 0 SUMMARY - Ely Braxton d Danny Vista, 2 votes to 1 - Saij d Samuel Stevens, 2 votes to 0 - Token Weed d Scottywood, 3 votes to 0 - Garbage Bag Johnny d Fred Grest, 3 votes to 0 - Jason Snow d The Sentinel, 3 votes to 0 - Bryan Storms d The Real Deal, 3 votes to 0 - Plucky The Cockfighting Duck d The Eagle via noshow - Brandon Youngblood d The Low Priest via forfeit. BRACKET JUDGES - Pete - Max (FUSE guy) - Andy (Shaman) - Andrew (Langsuyar) - Sam - Lindz - Dean Tigger
