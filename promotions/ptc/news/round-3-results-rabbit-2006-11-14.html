Round 3 Results - Rabbit

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TIGERA VS STEVE WATSON - CPA Judge 1 Tigera 1 Pros: - Strong mood created well at the start. - Some teething troubles with language at the start, but once it got into a flow it was very easy and pleasant to read. - Interesting twist at the end! That really, really rescued the roleplay, as if it had been a normal court case then it was horrifically implausible... but as it is not, you get a plus, as courts of the immortals are fair game! Cons: - Very repetitive use of words near the start - Not enough background given on the murders. How did she kill 47 people? That's a lot of bodies. - If I had a dollar for every time someone quoted Sun Tzu at the start of a roleplay... Undecideds: - Amnesia, court case, murder. Seen a LOT in these tournament things. Can be good, can be bad. Jury is out after piece 1. Watson 1 Pros: - Quite similar to Tigera's first in that it started with some clunky words and sentences, but then suddenly took off and became much better. - Hooked my interest early. Hope that the incident with the hit and run will be explained. - “Au revoir punchable monkey” made me laugh - A good air of mystery, and a good turn at the end to get our man off the hook. Cons: - Some technical issues with word use, grammar, run-on sentences and the like, which spoiled the flow a little. I can give further details if needed, but it's mostly just proof-reading needed. - Whilst the story was reintroduced, I still feel like I'm missing a few bits of key information that would let the story make more sense. I know I added an air of mystery as a pro, but it sometimes verges on my stumbling around in the dark. It's a fine line... Halftime, and it could currently go either way. Watson 2 Pros: - Good opening - Good relevance to the tournament - Very easy to read, bar a couple of technical issues as before Cons: - Slight contradiction from the first piece in that he didn't seem to care so much about the tournament then, and he does now - And the ending, whilst a good (though kinda obvious) move for the story to take, leaves our 'hero' wit a gun to his back and there being a fair old chance of him not making it to the tournament at all. So, I guess he survives and all, but I would have preferred a little closure. Maybe even the girl mentioning that he had to go and wrestle like everything was normal... I dunno Tigera 2 Pros: - Good tie in to the tournament, and I liked how you worked in the bye in the previous round. Double points for that as wrestling after gunshot wounds would have set off my implausibility sensor more than I would like. It still skirts close to the line, but stays the right side of it - Flows very well, with fewer errors than the previous piece - Enjoyable to read Cons: - From what I've read in the roleplay so far, I disagree with God's decision. 47 is a lot of dead people, so more explanation should be given if you want the reader to actually feel any sympathy with the character - What were the jury doing there in the first place if the only important person was the judge? We even got to hear about their perceived feelings, yet they served no purpose - Slightly cheesy ending about destiny - Not much made of God's 'second chance' at this point SUMMARY This is very hard. I'm really not sure who to pick. Both had good and bad points, though in both cases the good outweighed the bad. I think the writers would both fit into a bracket final with no problem, and could even get through to the Final Six, so well done for that. Given that I liked both of them, I'll have to look at what I didn't like about them to maybe decide. And here it is hard to separate them too. I don't think either presented enough of the history of their character. A little more info on the hit-and-run and the expense account would have worked wonders for Watson, and more story about how Tigera apparently killed 47 people in self defense would have laid to rest my main problem with that story. While both writers struggled occasionally with typos and writing errors, they weren't too bad. Tigera would edge this one, and I think that her overall writing skill in terms of description and eloquence is above that of Watson... yet Watson claws back to a level footing because his character seems a little more real to me. That seems strange to be saying it, since he seems to be living in the movie/book 'The Firm', but I still kinda liked him. My mind keeps on going back to the inconsistencies. Tigera's story just doesn't have enough information in it for me. She was shot by, apparently, her father. She also shot him, but chances are he, too, survived. What I don't get is where all the death and killing went on. The 47 number wasn't disputed in the 'court case', but the only abuse she seems to have suffered was when she was a lot younger. Did she kill them all then, or, as the story suggests via the appearance of Iashi, hunt them down in later life to extract revenge? The problem is that I just don't know, and any evidence that got her pardoned and returned to life was glossed over in the missing two hours. That, combined with the switch from jury to no jury midway through it, hurt the pieces quite a lot, because Mya is quite clearly meant to be seen as a sympathetic character, and yet she seems a little too good at killing people who wronged her. I don't doubt that some, most or all of the dead were not looking out for her best interests, but there you go. As such, I give the win to Steve Watson by the narrowest of margins. Pick: Steve Watson - CPA Judge 2 Steve Watson Roleplay 1 Pros - Good story. - Lots of information. Story is moving forward. Cons - Too many technical errors for this stage of the game. Roleplay 2 Pros - Great continuation of the last roleplay - The ending is superb. Didn't see that coming at first. Cons - The technical errors hurt you here. Big time. Tigera Roleplay 1 Pros - Good writing. You write rather well seriously. - Good ambience. You really describe the scene you want. Cons - Never get into the story really. Just not interested. - Technical errors throughout the piece. Roleplay 2 Pros - The same as before. Good writing and good ambience. Cons - The same as before as well. The two pieces were very similiar in that regard. Poor technical piece, and I just never found myself caring about the story. Pick: Steve Watson, CPA Judge 3 Steve Watson: I'm breaking one of the self-imposed sins of judging here, but, being that these are now the 4th and 5th roleplays I've read from you, you still haven't gone over your use of there/their/they're, to/too/two, you're/your. Yes, I'm a grammar nazi, and yes, I dislike it. Just read more carefully, please! (Note that this does not factor in judgement. I just wanted to point it out.) The story is well written and it's interesting. I've said before that this isn't just another 'wrestler moonlighting as something else' story, and thankfully, you've evolved it past being just that. I really don't have a bad thing to say about this story. While not perfect, it's very well done. Tigera: Aside from a few minor errors, the story is fairly well done as well. It may have been better that I'd read the other stories to get into it, but, since that means nothing this round, I'll say that I was pleasantly surprised with the story. Pick: Steve Watson - CPA Simply, it was the better story. Both had small technical issues that I won't speak of under penalty of nad punching, but, Watson pulled me in, and I think could be a dark horse to take this entire tournament. RESULT - Steve Watson - CPA d Tigera, 3 votes to 0 EMBER VS XIAN PHILLIPSE Judge 1 Ember While I enjoyed it, it didn't sing to me as I'd expected it would have, and it didn't grab me and suck me in. I'm completely disregarding your forum message. Perhaps if your mind was into it, it could have been better, and more on par with what you intended. Still, it does well for what it is. In stark contrast to Phillipse's pieces, your language is in parts a bit over the top. I do enjoy language, and I do enjoy how you fit words together, not simply because you can, but, because you know what they mean, and how to fit them in without sounding pretentious. Xian Phillipse Your imagery is fairly well done. Your dialogue, however, feels like something translated from some manga or anime and dropped in for the sake of it. It's shallow, and seems almost dropped in for the sake of being there, rather than adding to the story, it detracts. I have no doubt that with another judge, this story would be more interesting, but, it failed to capture me, unfortunately. Don't read the above as a harsh criticism. The story is well written, and your use of language isn't flowery for the sake of simply being so. It seems as though you write as you speak, and thus, know what you're talking about, and exactly how to say it. Pick: Ember It came down to the use of language and story, for me. I could possibly have gotten into Ember's, had I read from the beginning, however, Phillipse failed to capture my attention or desire to read other parts of his story. Judge 2 Ember 1 Pros: - Good vocabulary, and more importantly, good use of it - Very interesting story! Whilst murder has been done to death in this setting, the apparently psychotic Ember makes a powerful figure that interests me a good deal more than I expected it to. - A short piece, but it didn't seem to let it down at all. In fact, it almost seemed distilled, like the anger and hatred Ember holds for his dead brother. Cons: - Despite what I said above, right from the start the sentences get overly complicated when striving for eloquence. It doesn't happen too often in the piece as a whole, but be careful of long sentences. - The written words of Ember do not seem to be the way I would expect someone to write things down in diary, letter or any written form. The “What was his name? Russo?” is something surely Ember would have thought, before only writing down the actual name. It just didn't sit right with me. (That said, you seemed to get the style down fairly quickly after that instance). - If he's writing in some crazy code that nobody can read, then it should probably have been mentioned when we, the readers, were 'shown' him writing letters on the page. I hope the second piece lets us in on his 'code'. - The final line was a little corny. I'll forgive it if the brother is somehow not dead though! I must say, that was really quite good. Xian Phillipse 1 Pros: - The slyly knowing narrator has been done a fair few times, but when it is done well, as it is here, it can be very effective in setting the mood of the piece. - It reads very well. A couple of minor errors here and there, but nothing that really detracts from the writing Cons: - Not a tremendous amount goes on for a great deal of the roleplay. Undecideds: - The infatuation/love of the young XP seems quite silly, and I hope this is intentional, going for melodramatic parallels to Romeo and Juliet to point out that they're just a couple of kids, rather than to try and make the reader think that their love is something epic. It's going to be close, is this one. Ember 2 Pros: The scene with the cops reads surprising well. I say that because most scenes involving police procedure usually come off as incredibly fake. This is still a little cliché, perhaps, but it is written well and I enjoyed it. The meaning of the dream sequence was nicely done. I enjoyed the confusion. Maybe those drugs helped. Interesting ending. Jay Phoenix coming back from the dead? Leaves me wanting to see the next bit. Cons: I rolled my eyes at the EDH reference. Was that for a bet or something? Ditto for the Seymour one, and basically any part of the roleplay which involved someone winking and slapping in a horrible 'in joke' And the XP reference seemed clumsy too, though it tied in well with the 'coming back' idea XP 2 Pros: I smirked at DDR being good at... DDR Excellent standard of writing Interesting mystery I actually liked how the characters all started talking about apparitions and angels and similar stuff without skipping a beat. It amused me in a good way Good relevance Cons: Initially a little confusingn fairness this is a minor niggle, as all becomes clear Angel and Demona? Hmmm... It's looking like the stuff about XP's feelings for Angel were meant to be taken seriously, which is a slight disappointment as it seemed a little overblown in the first instance Another tricky one, and the Bracket Final is going to be hotly contested between whoever progresses from both of these matches I have judged. Some really excellent writing in these pieces, though they are coming from very different angles... though in the end they're not a million miles from one another in several thematic instances. Who to put through? Both writers conveyed the history of their character and re-introduced him to the reader. Both wrote well. Both told a story with fantastical elements, about someone possibly coming back to life. Both wrote about characters who seem to be losing their grip on situations they thought were under their control. But both cannot win. So I'm giving my pick to the writer who caught me up the most with emotion. All other things being roughly equal, I thought the surprising vehemence of Ember's letter and his palpable fear of the future at the end gives him a slight edge over Phillipse and the return of his lost love. At the end of the day, whilst I would very much like to read the next chapter of both stories, if I could only read one I'd like to see Ember's. So I pick him, though Phillipse ran him exceedingly close, and I enjoyed his pieces a lot. Pick: Ember Judge 3 I picked Ember in this match up. I'd give you more really indepth feedback but to be honest there isn't a lot for me to say. I simply liked what Ember had to sell more than his opponent. Pick: Ember RESULT Ember d Xian Phillipse, 3 votes to 0 SUMMARY - Steve Watson - CPA d Tigera, 3 votes to 0 - Ember d Xian Phillipse, 3 votes to 0 BRACKET JUDGES Andy Pete Craig Dean BRACKET FINAL - Steve Watson - CPA vs Ember Rabbit
