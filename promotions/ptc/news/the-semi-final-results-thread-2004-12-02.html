THE Semi Final Results thread....

2 December 2004

Latch Key Kid vs Dr. Curiosity Judging Comments Judge 1: I'm not going to pull any punches here. You both suck. I was greatly offended by the miserable tripe that the both of you authored and then tried to pass off as writing. The indignity of it all is an insult to this tournament. Therefore, I have decided that you both lose, and my vote goes to that Sheep fellow who posts at the end of every round. Winner - Sheep Oh, I have to actually pick someone? Bah, fine. All kidding aside, I am familar with both of these characters and it's fun to be able to judge this match. Doc and LKK are both fabulous writers and deserving of their spots in the semi-finals. I could sit here and write all day about what these guys did well and not so well, but I prefer just to get straight to the point. Both series were well-written, and both had some very nice surprise twists at the end, which I can totally appreciate. However, I found Doc's story to be entertaining throughout the entire series, and I was really drawn in the whole time. LKK's story, while good, seemed kinda dragged out at points, and I found my mind wandering off. LKK did a hell of a job though, and he definitely has done some great work. With that, I give the nod to Doc for the win. Winner - Dr. Curiosity Judge 2: Dr. Curiosity ---------- RP #1: A strange form of control A well-written, good flowing RP as usual from the Doc. I especially like the way that Dr. C’s RPs always have an original storyline to them, there is never anything used that has already been done. Good job on this RP. Rating: 9.0/10 RP #2: In the air tonight Another fantastic RP. This one simply had a comedic line everywhere you looked, very hilarious and enjoyable to read. And towards the end with Collette and the gun, no comedy, but it was truly fantastic stuff. Left me wanting to know what happened in the next RP so much. Rating: 9.8/10 RP #3: Breaking The Formula What can I say? I am pretty much, absolutely speechless. This roleplay was one of the best I’ve ever read in my opinion. A great follow-on from the second one, a great ending to a story, the characters are written incredibly well. For this roleplay, I feel I am obliged to mark high. Very high. Rating: 10/10 Dr. Curiosity’s overall rating: 28.8/30 Latch Key Kid RP #1: Cruising Over The Causeway Solid RP and effort. Quite a good read, and the story was well-written. Watch out for writing a lot of description, it gets a little bit boring after a while, but not enough to affect the RP really. Rating: 9/10 RP #2: Lending a Helping hand Another solid RP. The story and the flashbacks in this RP were entertaining, and I enjoyed this RP more than I did his first. Well done on this RP. Rating: 9.5/10 RP #3: The Truth Shall Set You Free Liked this one a lot, the best of the three LKK RPs. The story was good, the twist with Frank was good, and the ending was good. Overall a very good RP. Rating: 9.7/10 Two very good sets of roleplays. But there can only be one winner: Dr. Curiosity’s overall score: 28.8/30 Latch Key Kid’s overall score: 28.2/30 Pick: Dr. Curiosity Judge 3 The Latch Key Kid versus Doctor Curiosity LKK RP 1: Whoa! I really liked The Latch Key Kid-character. He’s in a way very stupid, but brave at the same time. Although it started off a bit too slow in my opinion, it was undeniably a very good roleplay. Also, a bit more relevance wouldn’t hurt your roleplay either. 8/10 LKK RP 2: Another good roleplay, really solid. The plot was very well thought out. Everything was super about this roleplay, save a few grammar and spelling errors. But even that couldn’t prevent this from becoming one of the most terrific roleplays. 8.7/10 LKK RP 3: This is definitely the crown jewel to a very good series. The only thing that really bothered me about this series as a whole, was a bit lack of relevance. You mentioned wrestling here and there, but it wasn’t your main theme. Nonetheless, this is just as good as your second roleplay: 8.7/10 Doc C RP 1: This had it all in my book. Doctor Curiosity blew me away here. I read this thing from the beginning to the end without stopping once. I really liked this roleplay. I wasn’t too familiar with Doctor Curiosity before, but he sure is a good roleplayer. 9/10 Doc C RP 2: A great climax at the end, although some things could easily be predicted in the roleplay, it was still good. Once again, Doc C proved why he is a GTT4 Semi-Finalist. 9/10 Doc C RP 3: What a way to end the best trilogy of the tournament! I was blown away by the brilliant way all pieces of the puzzle were revealed. I’m still amazed at the quality of the roleplays, the character and the actual writing. An exceptional high rating (at least compared to my standard) for you: 9.5/10 All in all, both men are really good roleplayers. You have both showed an extraordinary amount of talent, but only one man can win. All the luck in the world to both of you. My Pick: Doctor Curiosity Judge 4 Liking both sets of work from talented writers, left a difficult decision to judge. Some of the pacing with the Latch Key Kid's work felt a little slow. Also, by the opening paragraph of the third work, the revelation of Frank being connected didn't feel like a surprise at all when it hit. In all, we learned a variety of important things about LKK, but it felt that this work could have been condensed into a shorter format. While everything had it's place, the language, the way of telling us this story just failed to "grab" my interest. Doc's work showed us a curious turn of events, killing his wife, and sending Eegor to jail. The surprise of Eegor making his move like that was hinted towards, but not so much that it didn't feel like a genuine surprise. All in all, comparing the chain of events that took place in both stories, it felt like Doc's was better constructed. Pick: Doc Judge 5 Dr C – A strange form of control A few small grammatical problems (such as repetition) but didn’t really take away from the RP as a whole. The tougher side of the Doctor was shown/hinted at here so that it was obvious that he was not just a joke. Not very much about LKK but enough to act as an opening gambit here while the continuing storyline of Doctor Curiosity progressed. Not the best Dr C RP in this tourney by a long shot as there seemed to be ‘something’ missing. LKK – Cruising over the causeway Again a few niggling grammatical issues but again not enough to make it unbearable at all. Something that would have made this RP better, or easier to get into, would have been a reintroduction (or a reaffirmation) of who Katherine is and what her relationship is to Brad/LKK. The flashback to his childhood and perceived failing of his friend was a nice touch, and was the analogy of the causeway/bridge and wrestling/GTT4. The jump to the conversation with Frank was jarring and didn’t seem to flow or feel at all natural; it dragged a little. Lack of any real relevance here as well. Dr C – In the air tonight This was much better than the first one in terms of pace, flow and writing which in turn lead to the enjoyment factor rising. In fact unlike the first two RPs that I read I didn’t stop to take notes while reading this one at all. Nice nod towards LKK again with the flashback as well as reinforcing Dr C’s experience in other tournaments. This lends itself towards showing that Dr C is a wrestler as well as a story driven character. The humour was subtle enough so as to not feel forced, but evident enough so as to give a good flavour throughout the RP. The bad-guyesque dialogue especially shone through in this one. Back on top form with this RP and even finished with a typical cliff-hanger ending! Dr C – Breaking the formula Relief. I knew that Dr C couldn’t be shot by Collette (I mean he is in a tournament!!!) but I believed the characters so much that I was left waiting to see what happened. If Dr C had slipped out of character in shock when he realised that Collette had tried to kill him it would have had more impact, as it is though maybe it is simply that there is no more Chris and only the Dr C persona survives. GRIN. OK, I wish and I get. Andy must have read my mind as Dr C reverts to Chris. Nice touch to have this personality show through and have Dr C put to one side almost as a discarded item of clothing, but even nicer when Dr C comes back on top so to speak to enable Chris to cope. A poignant moment as the realisation dawns on me that this is actually quite sad and that Chris/Dr C is the victim of a tortured and fractured psyche and not just a comedic character. Making an efed character believable enough that the reader feels pity is a great achievement! All masks are briefly dropped as the tragedy unfolds. Eogor briefly lives up to his name and rampages across the top of the castle like a reject from a B movie as he disposes of Collette. Chris (not Dr C) tries in vain to save her. End of the day Paul took Collette’s life, gave up his own, and changed Chris. All for his brother’s sake and in a vain attempt at redemption. This was so good that for a split second, for the briefest of moments, I forgot that I was reading a RP and was sucked into the characters’ World completely. Ending achieved a full circle as Dr C vows to not let his brother down, and in some ways picks up Eogor’s fallen mantle. This was the best of the three RPs from Doctor Curiosity and had some inspired moments mixed with real emotion. The three RPs were like a rising crescendo. LKK – Lending a helping hand Another well-written piece and the flashback links very nicely with the Dr C RP about Tim and the lemonade stall. Some very nice dialogue between LKK and Katherine, especially the interchange between the two where Katherine finishes off LKK’s sentence about his mother with her apology. Again little to no relevance to the opponent apart from lip service mention, and while the RP was technically well written I didn’t see the actual point to it, where it was going or what it was trying to say. LKK – The truth shall set you free “Nothing had been easy” is how LKK’s life was described in this RP, but the examples of his past life actually don’t back this up at all. He is shown to have had a loving mother, a sister who gave him money when he needed it, good friends in and out of wrestling, a successful career, and of course Katherine. That niggled me slightly as it just didn’t feel right at all, it was as if the writer wanted us to feel sorry for LKK or give him some angst in his life, but didn’t follow it up in the actual writing. The RP was again (like all three to be honest) very well written technically, and had a wealth of information, but it also felt stilted and forced at the same time. The same went for the twist in the tale which while a little interesting didn’t feel at all believable or ‘real’ in the context of the storyline itself. Synopsis The RPs in this round showed that both handlers know how to write. There is no denying that both Dr C and LKK are technically talented writers. In fact in some parts I found that technically LKK was the stronger of the two (there was some nice information about Brad and his home location that I didn’t know and that was supplied well). However the biggest difference between the two was the emotion inherent when reading their work. I found LKK to be a rather stilted read overall with the characters not being as well defined as I wanted. I didn’t get a strong feeling of who LKK was; is he a childlike savant, is he a confident wrestler, is he a troubled teenager with a hard background? I still don’t know (and as I know of his character from XUW where I always thought that he was a naïve super-babyface that is worrying). The overall storyline he put forward had good ideas and intentions; the use of flashback and analogy was well handled, but the central premise and story of the lemonade stall left me scratching my head and wondering why. It just didn’t ‘work’ for me, I didn’t find it believable enough in terms of characterisation. Doctor Curiosity on the other hand got me caught up in the characters themselves and the story of their lives. This was basically a Greek tragedy rolled up and pretending to be an efed RP. You had the siblings with a love / hate relationship, you had the love story between Collette and Chris, the betrayal with Paul, the fractured psyche that led to Dr C’s creation, the redemption of Paul through Eogor, and finally the tragedy itself of Collette’s death. On top of that you have a mad scientist, a liar in the desert and inventions that wouldn’t look out of place in a Road Runner cartoon with the label ACME stuck on them. So how is it that I found THIS storyline more believable? Simple, the characters lived and breathed, they fought and argued, they were absurd and scary at the same time. I believed in and cared about THEM. End of the day taht simply led to Dr C being the more enjoyable read, and that is what this is based on I suppose. Bottom line is that while LKK had a great run of good luck to get to the bracket finals of this tournament, his luck just ran out. I believed (and still believe) that anyone is beatable on any given day, but today was not Doctor C’s day to be beaten. Pick: Doctor Curiosity Judge 6 DOCTOR CURIOSITY “A Strange Form of Control.” The role-play starts off with a nice visual of not only the castle and its obscure location, but also Eegor’s toiling and the Doctor’s wardrobe. Careful wording seems to be the choice of action here as each emotion and adjective seems cleverly picked for utmost success. A few times it seems like the good doctor is trying too hard to make his language flow. Understandably so considering the level of competition at this point. However, his language and storytelling is compelling and works wonders when it’s used to lead into something humorous as the first scene indicates. “Perhaps both, Eegor. Perhaps both…” The Doctor definitely has a way with words, and I could feel myself repeating the dialogue over and over in my head. The interaction between Eegor and Curiosity is nothing short of splendid, and couple with the abridged life history of the Doctor, Andy gives us a solid opening to this series of role-plays. The bulk of the role-play starts off where the other series ended. He’s mixing in his last round antics with a summary of the most important and compelling parts of last round with a little introduction to the new series. What stands out the most is that the scene with Collette reminiscing in the flashbacks of the torch to her face seemed a bit overly stressed. Perhaps not so much to stress the importance of the the event, but to explain the doctor’s change of demeanor. Afterwards we pick up on the ongoing struggle between Collette and Curiosity. Over the rounds their relationship has gone from naive ness to brutal game of can you top this? The rp doesn’t neglect the mentions of the tournament, spoiling on his win over Moraco and providing a brief glimpse at LKK. However, the bulk of the rp seems centered around the story. Definitely not a weak point at all. Just when you did you’re in for a seriously moody role-play filled with despair and revenge, the doctor throws a scene at you like the last. The flow of the language is like butter, and the dialogue is easy to read along with and sometimes mimic. He never lets up on his ambience, and reverts the rp back to the GTT form. His line of LKK’s opponents mysteriously disappearing was hilarious, although I do wish he had elaborated more on that aspect. Doctor’s pot of gold was always making fun of the theme in tourneys, last time it was making fun of ransoms. This time it’s totally left field as he’s evolving the doctor with every rp. “In the Air Tonight” …starts off with the same kind of beautiful narration and ambience that his first role-play had. You can almost picture every single detail the doctor is going through as he tries to fix his inventions. As always, Andy leads you in with a great writing and allusions before dropping the bomb. The dialogue between Eegor and Doc is nicely written, but moreso than that it paves the way for something spectacular to come. At least it seems so that way. A lot of writers tend to leave gaps and teases, called cliffhangers. I’ve seen Andy do this before, without actually concluding the mystery he invited. Which, if not looked upon, can be a bad thing. But, for now, I’ll just concentrate on the now. Yes, The Lemonade stand reference was golden too. Continuing with the story, Andy reverts back to that goofball Doctor we all know and love. The flashback to the lemonade stand was nearly flawless, with the characterization taken to extreme effect. It was also downright hilarious, and the spirited oddball doctor was back it seems, if only for a moment. Like I said before, Andy has a knack of throwing you off guard. We're finally coming down to the end and the rp and writing really begins to lift off the cyber page. Heavy stress is put on ambience and emotion with Andy teasing us with every sentence as to what the big news is. That's the mark of a true writer. A great storyteller. I can envision the entire seen in my head, moreso the facial expressions as she begins to understand the truth. Finally, the part where she breaks is gold, as you can almost feel sorry for her. I admit, I thought the surprise would be something else, but this works. It also makes a lot of sense. He lost his deals to that wheelchair guy. He’s been unstable. He went to the utt, but lost, he went to the fwo survivor, but lost. He's never had a big payday deal and it makes sense. The rp ends in a big climax and I await in anticipation the third and final installment. The Doctor definitely has a way with words... "Breaking the Formula" Wasting no time, Andy starts off with an audio clue. We begin the third installment with our hearts thumping to the sound of an empty gun. The narration that follows is nicely written, but even better is the mixture of moods that Andy has mastered this round. The mood is gloomy, but he still finds a way to add in humor. After the gun incident, it appears that the Doctor has won. He's won his battle against Collette and follows her through the desert. In a sense, it's like he's almost won the GTT as well. Everything ends here. The games. The charades. Everything. However, just as it seems to be ending, Collette won't give up. Perhaps like LKK won't give up. I always thought Andy was a great character development handler, and this scene proved it. He was able to write the Doc as two people. One the wily, goofy, and failure proned doctor we all know with failed experiments, and the mastermind that was far more devious than clownish. Again, the mood is set, and once again Andy throws in bits of comedy. The ending is nothing short of splendid, and it seems that Andy has come to terms with Curiosity. The game is over; all that remains is to concentrate on the match. If only it would be as easy as a lemonade stand... THE LATCH KEY KID “Cruising’ Over the Causeway” Man, I feel sorry for the LKK. After reading Doc’s rps, I’m expecting him to totally wow me. I’ll just pretend I didn’t hear of Doctor Curiosity… LKK starts off a little differently than the Doc. He doesn’t jump into an audio narration of the storyline, but rather into an informative narration of who the LKK is and a schooling of sorts. The language is written well, not so much intoxicating, but effective enough to get the message across. The rp begins to pick up nicely with LKK already in a struggle. This time he’s going to save his friend. Kinda like how the Doc is playing it between brothers. The dialogue between Katherine and LKK seems pretty mechanical at this point. That’s not the strong point of the rp, but narration around it and the story that follows is. We’re introduced to a little flashback of why LKK just had to do this. Okay so we know he’s come a long way, and LKK does it without struggling or oddly interrupting the flow of the rp. The next segment of the rp deviates away from the story and instead focuses on dealing with the development of the character. No, an explanation of the character. He’s already made it this far, and it’s now that he’s realizing the effects of wrestling on his world. His next match with Doctor Curiosity is going to be an important all, guaranteed. Now we are getting to the good stuff and LKK gives us an example at what kind of story he can tell at this stage in the game. The scene at the coffee shop is nicely written, flows smoothly, and his use of words is pretty effective. The dialogue seems a bit off hand, if not mechanical, and at first it seems like LKK totally forgot about his plot. Wasn’t LKK supposed to go in and help Frank, not fight with him? Well, LKK does a nice job of portraying his character in a different light. He’s unusual at best. Naïve. Kind hearted. Stubborn, and a hell of a lemonade stand businessmen. By the scene’s end we learn what the problem is and as expected LKK sets out to rid the problem. The best moments of the entire scene and perhaps the entire rp is how real and serious the writer makes the lemonade stand seem. Sure, its not much, and not for a grown man, but the writer gives new life to it. “Sidewalk Café” Second starts off with a narrative explanation of the theme soon to follow. It gives us a little insight into what goes on in the mind of LKK. He has a friend, and he is in need. The story picks off after LKK already sees Frank and sees that Frank wants no help. No matter, Frank will get help whether he asks for it or not. The scene with Katherine also focuses on dealing with the Doctor’s shenanigans. It seems everyone has been paying attention. We’re treated to another flashback, the writing and dialogue remains the same. At least now we know why he chose that spot. At this point it seems pretty moot to point. Now we are back in the present and Katherine feels sorry for Brad. He really did have a tough childhood, some days. We’re being given a clearer picture of LKK now and with each segment and flashback we understand LKK better and why he’s such a great person. The writing is on par as always, and after the incident we know a little bit about LKK, the kid. It ends in Katherine changing her mind about LKK’s plans. LKK tries a little too hard to explain that her answer will be unexpected, but I think he prolongs it too much. Yes, we all expected her answer would change, but sometimes shorter interruptions work better. “The Truth Shall Set You Free.” …And another informative narration shall set the stage for this rp. LKK continues with that trend, something I’ve seen many handlers do. However, he does it effectively, and the briefness of it makes the reader want to dive into the rp right away. Speaking of the rp, the first scene is pretty much like the others. The writing hasn’t proved and there are some blotched spots of grammar here and there. The dialogue continues to be mechanical at best. But, more important to consider, is the fact that the explanations for actions seem a bit forced here. It seems like LKK is trying to fit in too much information. I’m liking what the LKK is doing here, to an extent. These constant flashback memories and explanations tend to lead away from the story, but they also provide a bit of background on LKK. Which is understandable considering the fact that he has had little time and opportunity to showcase his character given the byes and no-shows he’s received. It seems like he’s trying to jam in as much LKK into the rps as he can, and I for one am thankful for it. And I’m going to go right into the pick, but I will point out a very important line in this rp. “You’ve been concentrating too much on me and not enough on Doctor Curiosity and that’s a bad thing.” DRUM ROLL… Alright, after reading both rps and viewing my analysis, I have made my decision. I’m picking Doctor Curiosity to advance and here’s why…. First and foremost, it seems like everything the LKK did well in his rps, Andy did better. In so many areas that I don’t know where to begin. The LKK did the right thing in trying to reintroduce the history of his character. However, his flashback interruptions were exactly that: interruptions. A lot of times I felt myself skimming through them just to get back to the story. With Doc’s rps however, I felt myself at a constant pace always wanting more. The level of character handling was also different between the two. I mentioned often before that LKK’s dialogue seemed mechanical. It was. I could hardly tell the emotion of the character without having to read words like “angrily, nervously, antsy.” With the Doctor, the handling was so much good that I could literally picture the evolvement of emotions going on through his characters. Moreso, I could find myself repeating the dialogue in my head. Both styles were very different, but I found the Doc’s style more bait and hook. LKK went into a prolonged informative role, and the Doc played on moods. He constantly changed the mood around from deadly serious to jokingly funny. That kept me entertained and that kept me wanting to read more. On a pure writing level, Andy just edges LKK and that gives his rp better substance. Finally, looking at the storylines and plot, I find the Doc’s plight more enjoyable and suspenseful. Andy had all the ingredients of a great story – suspense, intrigue, mood, character, and conclusion. LKK did admirably well, but I thought the Doc was one rung, maybe several, higher than him this round. I give LKK a great hand and I admit, as I always have, that XUW is a place to be feared. Wow, if Shadow and Blake Wolfson had time to rp, we might have had 5/6 Xuwers in the Semis. Regardless of what ifs, LKK proved his worth, and it’s a shame he had to be matched up with Doctor Curiosity. Especially at a stage where Doc seemed to be peaking. The Doctor totally impressed me this round. I can’t wait to see what he has in store for the next round, should he advance. I still think his first round role-play was the best of the tournament, but this might have been the best series of the tournament. PICK: Doctor Curiosity RESULT: Dr. Curiosity d The Latch Key Kid (6 votes to 0) to advance to the final round of GTT4, to face Exceptional Dave Hurst and Craig Goat, winner take all. Finals
