Round 2: Mrs H. Bracket Results

23 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
BLAISE "HAZARD" MCCLANCY V PETE THE PG TIPS CHIMP Judge 1 Pete the PG Tips Chimp Evil Tea Syndicate! I didn't believe so much could be achieved with an animal as the central character. Quality burns all over the place. Having Timmy blame the McClancy Clan for all of Pete's woes in order to psych him up added depth to the surrounding slapstick. That's exacty what you'll need to keep doing in order to advance much further, as it will help keep the gimmick fresh. This made me laugh... a lot. Little Timmy: "But … what about your opponent, Pete?" Pete the PG Tips Chimp: "Ooo?" Little Timmy: "Didn’t they tell you? You’re facing Blaise McClancy!" Pete the PG Tips Chimp: "Pft." Blaise "Hazard" McClancy Convictions I quite liked the courtroom scene at the start, however after that the sequence between Dani and Ukyo seemed pretty meaningless and I lost the focus I had, and by the time Blaise came back into the picture and started talking about Global and 4WW issues, it was too late to get me interested again to the same level. I understand that those things were talked about to demonstrate the convictions theme, but it didn't work for me. Overall, I think there were a shade too many characters in there to deliver the idea as concisely as would have worked for me. It makes it harder to care about any one of them when they are all spread relatively thinly. I did care about Blaise though during the courtroom scene, and the ending was a nice touch, quite significant. Pick: Pete the PG Tips Chimp The pace of McClancy's work seemed more suited to a two RP set, with convictions as one theme and something else as the second. The Chimp's submission was perfectly suited for a one shot wonder, which probably helped him in this case, as I'm still to be won over on the later-round-making longevity of the gimmick. Judge 2 The second battle of the Admins, and coincedentally, RJ has been involved in both of them. Either he's a popular target for administrative abuse, and should get pushed on principle, or Ryan-O is a bully, and begged and pleaded with Argyle for this match. Whichever the case, let's see if RJ evens his record up at 1-1 in these things. We begin with a tiny glimpse into McClancy. Fantastic. I was clueless about him, but, I know he's a bit blue collar, so to speak, which makes him okay in my book. As Blaise sits in court, waiting for the inevitable favorable verdict, again, we get a glimpse into his past, which was well done. I enjoyed the conversation between Northsky and Ukyo. They seemed as if they were old friends or at least trying to be, and we were simply dropping in for a glimpse on what could be a normal day of the McClancy household. Okay. I understand that Sean has an accent. I've read a few of his things before, and I get it. But, reading his dialogue is painful. Just making a comment about his irish brougue is plenty. Emoting every word as a literal translation makes my head hurt. That aside, the second part of the story falls into a bit of mediocrity. I suppose I need to know the connection between Anderson-McClancy, McClancy, Northsky, Ukyo, and the Irish lad to understand deeper, which I unfortunately do not. I think this story needed a second RP to flesh more of it out, or at least a bit more of an explaination of how everything fits together. As it is, I feel a bit hung up on not understanding the story about the Order, or anything like that. And now, the monkey that took the world by storm, Pete, the PG Tips Chimp. ... ...testicle on the counter? ...kitty staring contest? ...Dad gets a hooker? The mindgames of Timmy are amazing for a simple 12 year old boy, ready to drop testicles of his very own. The story is mired in it's ridiculousness, bogged down by it's mindless and inane chatter between Timmy and Pete. That's also the best part about it, the clever jokes, and comedy woven tightly through the storyline of what quite obviously the handler was going through. The balance between overly ridiculous and marginal ridiculousness gets beaten, ripped off the ground, and thrown way into "What the fuck?!" land. And that's a good thing. Nice cut on a former PTC fed that will remain nameless. I've gotten into enough trouble with them, thanks to signatures. So, does the Irishman lose a testicle? Does the Chimp get his ass whooped and join Brick in the shitty people McClancy's beaten category? I hate it when it's comedy vs. serious RPs. I have a soft part for comedy, and tend to lean towards it when rendering a decision. After going through both with a fine toothed comb, I'm going to have to give it to The Chimp. All in all, both were well written, and it was fairly close a few tiny mistakes in grammar and spelling gave it to Petey. Content and storyline were on par with each other, despite having clearly different messages and mediums behind them. Pick: Pete the PG Tips Chimp. Judge 3 When I read the first Pete the PG Tips Chimp roleplay, I have to admit that I was skeptical that this gimmik could last more than a few RP's, however this round I was happily proven wrong. The writing is laugh-out-loud-while-snorting-coffee-out-your-nose funny. It's fresh, it's not cookie-cutter writing, and it stands out because of this. McClancy, on the other hand, while well writen doesn't really stand out from the crowd. In my opinion, it needed to 'go' further, it started off very strong, yet the ending seemed to crumble. Pick: The Chimp gets the win. Result: Pete the PG Tips Chimp d Blaise "Hazard" McClancy 3 votes to 0 DOREEN PEPPERPOT V BRIAN ARSON Brian Arson – Losing Hope, Parts I & II I had never read Arson’s work before, so I have to give him credit for holding my attention through the two pieces. Sometimes that’s difficult to do when you read an unfamiliar character. Both pieces were well-written, and on the whole, I enjoyed how you paced both of them out. The first piece hit the ground running, but it got hung up for me in the airport security scene. That bit with the pen stuck out oddly from what it felt like you were generally going for with both works. It was silly, and unbelievable (airport security blows, but come on), which would have been fine if similar tones were found in other parts of your works, but they weren’t. They were, in essence, two relatively serious rps with this one wacky interlude that just didn’t fit. Nevertheless, I moved on, and forgot about it. I liked the college statement segments in the second piece, because they sounded authentic, but I wasn’t sure what their purpose was. Brian was kicked out of college for drugs. We know Brian is involved with drug cartels now. Is it just backstory? I appreciated the fact that you used the excess time in the limo before the meeting to address GTT5, even if the trash-talking was a little uninspired. My only problem with the end was that I could have used a little more explanation as to Brian’s involvement with the West Coast drug scene, other than the fact that he’s apparently something like a Don. Don Arson. Hehehe. *ahem* On the whole, two solid works from Brian Arson. Doreen Pepperpot – The Lost Art of Communication, Parts I & II It doesn’t even feel like there was a First-Round piece for Doreen. The author eased me into the characters in the first piece in the opening statement (of sorts) about communication. This theme (and the title of both works) was constantly reiterated in both pieces, through changing narratives and, in the second piece, the literal inability of the characters to connect with one another. The author held to the message without sounding repetitive, and that’s extremely hard to do. Also, Brian Arson as the crush of Doreen’s sister was possibly the most creative way to portray an opponent I’ve ever seen. If I could critique something, I’d say that you could have fleshed out Coach’s character a little more in terms of the accident. I didn’t get a clear picture of when exactly it happened. Also, I liked the allusions to the son, but what happened? Is his son not involved with wrestling anymore? This might be addressed in future installments. Result: Doreen Pepperpot d. Brian Arson Pretty cleanly. Arson did a good job with his pieces. I know I critiqued a lot of things in his pieces, but they were well-written and I enjoyed them. Unfortunately, he was outmatched this round, as Doreen’s pieces connected tightly with the theme she was pushing forward, developed her characters, and still managed to tie in her opponent in a commendable fashion. Look out for Pepperpot, fellas…it wouldn’t be the first time a lady has kicked major ass in one of these things. JASON WARR V PADDY O'SHEA Judge 1 All right, two rps to one, let’s see here. On one hand Jason Warr presents to us nothing more than a not so subtle form of conscious streaming. Okay, so he got passed the first round, it wasn’t that hard. Well, not for him at least. We aren’t really introduced into anything more in these two pieces. All they seem to be is just Warr taking one definition and riding it. So, he’s the underdog so what? France is always an underdog. There are glimpses of anecdotes of why things in the past would help him rise to the occasion here. But, Warr fails miserably in making those little seeds sprout. I was about to give him the nod here when he showed a bit of promise in the second. But he ended that abruptly without any closure. These series turned out to be nothing more than someone trying to use narrative to streamline his story over to the reader. By contrasts, Paddy’s narrative actually drives two-fold here: one to maintain his stance in this tournament and the second to plant the seeds for later. Unlike Warr, Paddy actually manages to concoct different ingredients and make his story more enticing. Everything in his roleplay just flowed better from the dialect, to the narrative, to the seeds being planted and sprouting in the end. This is closure folks!! These were two different styles at battle with one another and in the end I decided that only one of them managed to grip the reader. That was Paddy O’Shea. Yeah, he only did one rp….but he still managed to out-flex Warr. O’shea simply posted a better quality. Pick: Paddy O'Shea Judge 2 Jason: I like the tone set for this rp. His story did a good job of portraying the strength of the underdog and was executed well. However, at the same I found myself wanting more..I am not sure what..perhaps a bit more detail? Something to make me connect more with the character as a whole. The same goes for the second installment of his rp, the flashback was good and kept the reader wondering just enough to keep them reading...the sudden ending of this just kind of left me feeling...eh. Paddy: Lonely Soldier... Wow just...wow. This story not only kept me interested but had me connecting not only to the main character but to the supporting character as well. A writer who can do this is gold in my book. I didn't even notice the length upon finishing this. Luckily for me I have an irish/gaelic translator and was able to translate was said. I would suggest putting translation somewhere, perhaps in foot notes or something if the sayings have an important impact on the story itself. Granted some may not care..but for me, knowing what was said gives me a better understanding of everything. Pick: O'Shea d Warr Warr is a talented writer with a few rough edges in my book. If he polishs it up with detail and more character development he will be a deadly force in tournies. Paddy had me from the beginning..and drew me deeper with each paragraph. He goes on to win here. Great job to both handlers. Warr has nothing to be ashamed of here. Result: Paddy O'Shea d Jason Warr, 2 votes to 0 JACK MURPHY V STEVEN MURPHY Steven Murphy For the first RP, I'm inclined to say that I'm not quite sure whether the writing style is avant garde, or sloppy. I appreciate the way that it plays with the narrative, but at the same time, it seems to me that it's done in a way that de-emphasizes what Murphy is saying- and since what Murphy is saying is the most important part, that's not necessarily a good thing. I don't dislike this piece, per se- there are parts that are particularly good, with a sort of stream-of-consciousness feel (the repetition, and whatnot), and I think the addition of the psychology citations was particularly clever. However, I also feel that the style is a little bit halting and jerky in this first piece, which makes it difficult to get into the story. The second one I think is better, not only for story purposes, but because I'm also more used to the style that they are written in. The story in this one is tighter, and, I think, more interesting. I've not much to say about it other than that- the style still had the same effects as it did before, but with the tighter story, it wasn't so noticeable. The two pieces together make for a good effort, although to me, the sudden wrapping up of the story by him already finding out that WWA is behind Frank helping him kick his drug problems is a bit anti-climactic. Jack Murphy The first RP is written in what I'd call a nice 'open' style. The writer isn't shying away from describing everything he needs to describe, and he isn't overdoing it either. That said, there are times when the story drags just a bit, and I feel that the wrestling interview, while a good inclusion, takes the focus away from what's supposed to be the centerpiece of this series (the script). The script, while the center of the first and last parts of the RP, seems rather secondary when put next to the interview, which takes up a lot of time and resources in the RP, and seemingly for not much reason. It's not that I dislike the story- it's played out pretty well- I just feel that the focus was lost a bit around the middle of the piece, but the second piece could easily make up for that. The second piece I like better than the first, mostly because I feel like we've moved past awkward inclusions of wrestling, and are really digging into the story of the character. The writing style suits this sort of thing much better, I think, and overall, the story of the piece is well done, and fairly enjoyable, though a bit anti-climactic at the end. In deciding between these two, what I'm basically looking at is two writers who are pretty good at telling their stories in appropriate ways, from disassociated drug-addict to moody, thoughtful older wrestler searching for his place. I enjoyed both series from both writers, though as I noted in my judging, both are lacking in places. I do feel like this is a pretty close race, as, while Steven Murphy's piece isn't told as well, it's more interesting to me. Jack Murphy's is better written, but seems to drag in places, and lose its focus just a bit. It's a difficult call. Pressed to make it, though, I'm going to pick Steven Murphy for the win. His writing is less skillful than Jack Murphy, but more interesting, and he's adapted some stylistic innovations that suit the story he's trying to tell remarkably well. Jack Murphy's no slouch here- but his story does not provide the necessary hooks to keep me interested enough to put him through to the next round. Pick: Steven Murphy TERESA TOMAS V JONATHON WINTERS Judge 1 Winters vs Teresa Tomas Winters - these two works justify why you where put through into round two despite losing to Almasy in round one. I liked the twist at the end, but must say it left me with more questions than it answered. Teresa - Your work was very 'light' similar to the opening chapter of a novel. It told me enough to want to read more, but it didn't tell me enough for it to stand alone, even together. For both writers, the writing was tight, although I spotted one or two mistakes, but nothing major. Pick: Winters the win. Judge 2 Teresa Tomas Since these pieces are fairly brief, I'll examine them both together instead of taking the time to look at them one by one. I'd like to say that overall, I really appreciate the crispness of the style. This writer has definitely learned the art of sparing the reader details he doesn't need to know. The writing is good, the story is good, but unfortunately, length may play into this if Winters gives more than Tomas did. I really feel like despite the skill of Tomas, there was still a lot of room to expand on this story, and that may wind up hurting her in the long run. Jonathon Winters The first piece of this series, I feel, spends a lot of time dancing around the point. To clarify, there's a lot of repetition and going over things again and again that sort of makes the story seem like it is circling around and around without really going anywhere. I found it hard to keep my attention on what was going on for a good portion of the RP because of this. The second piece is a big improvement upon the first. The story is definitely tighter and more interesting here, though I actually feel that it's complicated enough that it could have easily unwound over several more RP's than just two, or even that some of the material from this piece could have been used in the first piece to greater effect. The best part about this, however, is that there's a really powerful hook for the continuation of the story. Between these two, there is, on the one hand, two pieces that were good, but probably a good bit shorter than they could have/should have been. Tomas gave me enough to convince me that she's a good writer, however. Winters, the second RP was by far better than the first, and I felt like the first RP took so much time to wind up that by the time it was over, it was fairly disappointing. I think he did make up for that with the second piece. Like other calls I've made in PTC, this one's close. Despite the fact that it may seem strange, however, I'm going to go with Tomas for this one, because the writing, while shorter than I felt it could have/should have been... it kept my interest. Winters' first RP interfered with my interest in his story. Pick: Teresa Tomas Judge 3 Disregarding the subject matter, there are many similarities between these two pieces. Tomas edges Winters for writing quality, hers was easier to read, more concise and other than the opening paragraph, didn't have any sentences with poor structure or spelling that made me cringe. Winters edges Tomas on storyline. Tomas gives us a lot of great description and makes me feel quite good about the character, but this is at the expense of anything significant happening. You have your key points - cousin in trouble, Tomas to come to the rescue but for a price... but not much else - and this is over the span of two RP's. Winters provides us with more action and also reasons for it, some intrigue, as well as a more titilating closure, although I will say that the plausibility of the rescue sequence was missing in action. Tomas, while certainly having the tools to do quite well, left me unfulfilled this round... Winters gave me enough, although not near his best, and skirting dangerously closely around the edges of bad cliches you see in reruns of Walker Texas Ranger. Pick: Winters Result: Jonathon Winters d Teresa Tomas, 2 votes to 1 JAYCE BRADLEY V JOE MILLER Judge 1 Joe Miller: This really needed a part two. I mean really needed a part two. I would've given the match if it had a part two. Jayce Bradley: Like the dossier reference and a few of the mystery/detectve story touches. Great RP. Had me hooked from the beginning to end. Overall: I like the Sci-Fi/mystery based RP's. Joe Miller really needed a part two otherwise this match may have been his. But Jayce's look like it came straight out of Sci-Fi mystery novel. I'm giving this one to Jayce. Pick: Jayce Bradley Judge 2 Jayce Bradley v Joe Miller Bradley -- It appears that CSI/ME RP's are the in thing this year.
