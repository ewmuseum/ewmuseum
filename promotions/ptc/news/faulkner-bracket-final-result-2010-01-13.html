FAULKNER BRACKET FINAL RESULT

13 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Here we go... Judge One - Aaron I think I have to confess something here, so everything is on the level; I read this match last thinking it was sort of an afterthought. I m sure plenty of people are sitting their from the outsider positioning ready to say “Lane Stevens wins by default” and to be honest, I think it is impossible not to buy into the hype behind such a character. I had read Impulse a little bit, and I know Russo’s writing from over the years; there would be times it felt wooden and that it just got dragged down because I don’t think he knows his audience. So Pete has those elements to contend with. Then again, I’ve read absolute jack shit of Lane Stevens, mostly because of timing making it difficult. Even so, I woke up and put my judging cap on. The past and the future doesn’t matter, what does is what is here. They said Angelo Deville was unbeatable too before Dave Hurst knocked him out. Does Impulse win the rematch, or does Stevens stake his claim to dominance? Title: Sins of the Father: Intertwined Featuring: Impulse I’m not going to complain about the errors because I didn’t see many of them. And the be honest, in the beginning, I looked to the scroll bar to figure out how much more I was ‘going to have to slog through’. These critiques for this round are probably going to surprise people, but the critical element in these roleplays is captivating storytelling. There are any number of unquantifiable elements that go into why a judge picks one piece over the other. There’s no reason to shit where you eat unless there are issues with tenor and tone. That being said? I don’t know what it is about this piece in particular, but at the end of it, I couldn’t help but nod my head. This is good. And out of the many I have read of this tournament, it’s near the top of the pile, maybe even in the top five. I don’t think it is overstated, or saturated in style; it’s subtle, it’s disarming, and it is filled to the brim with charm that you find almost nowhere else. This is the balance of really intriguing storytelling that doesn’t need exploding cars or guns in people’s faces to get over; I think that Ivy’s descent into the seedy underbelly of the remnants of The Asylum made for a jarring tempo shift, and the backdrop of Knox and Rosie is so magical just in their presence. Like…seriously, you satisfied me from all ends, and we’re afraid to give the audience the treat of seeing Rosie stand up for herself by punching Randall’s father for his bullshit comments. This is good. This is really good. I’m a fan, and there isn’t enough I can say about this piece. Title: The Girl: People Like Us Featuring: Lane Stevens Hey, um, I think you put ‘want’ instead of ‘went’ in the scene with Darren right before the bell hammer. I don’t think I can find other things like that, mostly because at a certain point, you get lulled into such a strong sense by the prose that you don’t even care, which is a kind way of saying to yet another person that I didn’t find errors in what you wrote. You’ve heard it from a lot of people, but I’m a fan. This is great, with slivers contained within that hit with ferocity that it doesn’t catch me until I have to think about it later. I talked about Justin and how much of a pro he is, and the fact is, this is professional style writing right here as well. Great intrigue, well constructed characters. This is really fantastic. I think my only gripe might be the just odd placement of wrestling into the piece. The title belt and the actual promo he cuts feel so...I don’t want to call it labored because I don’t think it is. I think it is unnecessary to what you are doing. And that is me having to find something to gripe about. Just great writing here, Ben. Title: Sins of the Father: Home Featuring: Impulse Again, Pete, you’re making this rather difficult for me, especially given that this is supposed to be the slaughter that is actually brought up in the promo (which is a nice touch to getting into the mindset of why Randall needs to know more about Stevens). This is a story that takes place in the middle of New York City, and I think the touches and details and the oddballness of the trappings all work in your favor. I mean, as a story, I think my complaints are that bringing Roderick in to just tell Randall that Stevens is a crony is not enough information for Knox to construct a gameplan to wrestle against Lane. I mean, the surroundings to it were all leading to a big reveal, or perhaps a sliver that will give Randall all he needs to conquer the River Rat, and it just sort of doesn’t get there. Overall, though, another very solid performance. Title: The Girl: (The Death of) Dreams Featuring: Lane Stevens Alright, I drank a beer as I was reading this, and given your penchant for drunkness, perhaps this was an intelligent choice. I don’t know what to say outside of to once again gush about what you’ve done, but this one is different. You trust your audience to fill in the blanks in your construction, especially with the manner in which Darren connects with Regan’s dreams. I thought the question at the end of the piece was quite intelligent, and shifts who the audience might sympathize with. Lane is a one liner machine, and I think the exchange in the airplane about the title belt and not letting the kid touch it was outright silly. He’s a prick, and he was a prick in the first piece, and I don’t think you do the disservice of making us actually think Lane is a good guy who is just wearing a mask. At the same time, when everything is said and done, I had issues with the piece. The whole torturing of Darren was going someplace, I felt, especially in connection with the dreams and the cowboy cult and all that. By the end, I’m not going to lie; I didn’t know what to make of everything you presented. Again, another standout piece. JUDGEMENT Alright, time for nut cutting time, and I want to tell you that there has been some talk of this rematch not being proper. That Impulse wasn’t worthy of being here and that Lane shouldn’t have to fight someone he’d already fought over again. The issue? Those people probably aren’t going to read your (great) roleplays, so those people don’t understand why this match is taking place, let alone why it is, in my opinion, the toughest to decide. Where are my problems? I don’t have many, and I enjoyed both pieces. I gave them my dedication and time and read every single word; there was nary a flaw, and fundamentally speaking, while these two are different, they both are exceptional in how they construct their pieces. What it comes down to is the reasoning behind what is presented. The Stevens pieces are extremely well written and have a flair most others might not be able to interject. With Impulse, I think it is quite a bit of charm. I...hmm...I could say something about a coin flip. I have to think long and hard on this one. I don’t want to send anyone home, or put them closer to being sent home. It isn’t going to come down to technical flaws, and it isn’t going to come down to mistakes. This one is almost completely arbitrary, and involves what I enjoyed more as a story. And even if I get down to that, it’s tough. I thought there was charm to what Impulse wrote. And with Lane, I sensed such great strength and character...I have flipped back and forth all day on this. I have flipped all day on what I want to see continue. Who do I believe won. This isn’t a rout. This is a hell of a bracket final. I’m going to point to something with Stevens which has me scratching my head to the utmost; the promo in the first roleplay. Why? Why does Lane Stevens cut the promo on Darren in that chair? Some might say that there is something to relevancy, but you actually have hit any of that sort of criteria. You do a cute thing with that title belt in the second roleplay, and with the backdrop of a wrestling interview, I think it works. But before beating a man with a hammer, he’s cutting a wrestling promo on Lance Marshall and Impulse. I am sure that there are people that will read this and say “but Aaron, Lane is all about mocking life and situations, so it fits his character”. In the roleplays with Impulse, Desade being the owner of SCCW is false. Stevens as a lackey is not satisfactory reason for Roderick being there, as it doesn’t give Impulse a window into the soul like he seems to need on his opponents to construct a game plan. I am sure that there are people that will read this and say “but Aaron, this is all Impulse needs to make a gameplan”. So many people are telling me to go with my gut, and that is the problem. My head and gut are split. And both know the reasons and I could keep putting this off before arriving to a conclusion. For the last half hour or so, though, one thing has stayed constant through all of this goddamn agony. I trust my gut. The winner of the Faulkner Bracket is Impulse. The charm wins me over, and when I had to get to what I liked better, the promo Lane cuts on Darren just...it doesn’t read organic to me. It feels like it is there to take a dig, and I am fine with it when it is on the plane ride with that little girl, but I’m not fine when it feels like it pulls away my focus from what is a really intense scene where I have no idea what is going to happen. Judge Two - Andy Impulse 1 Straight away I really liked the style of this writing. The first person perspective works really well, and you seem very comfortable with your characters. The slight overlap in the switch over makes me comfortable, and I know something's about to go down. I like the differentiation you have between the voices of your characters, especially between Know and McGinnis. Whilst some of the latter's scenes go by in a blur of names and backstory, there's something about her voice that keeps me interested. I like Knox's sections too, though whilst they do a good job of portraying the character, the day-to-day life isn't quite as interesting as the contrasted drama. That said, it does pick up towards the end, and I find the start and the end were the best parts of the Randall/Rosie sections. What McGinnis is up to I'm not sure, but it's intriguing. A good start, sir. Stevens 1 Like your opponent, a good start. You draw us in gently as readers, and the style is such that everything seems familiar (in a good way) even though this is the first work of your that I've actually read. Everything just feels 'right' in the first scene, and even with my raised eyebrows about the arrival of Clinton Sage, the dialogue and mannerisms just continue to hold me within the world you're writing about. I’m always wary of dream sequences, personally. But this does have a bit of intrigue in it that keeps me interested, and some cool imagery. Lane Stevens is a marvelous asshole. His reaction to Sage’s threats and seriousness is just perfect. I do hope something horrible happens to him at some point during this, just to see his reaction. The rest goes on well, and I’m quite drawn in to the character and the set-up. I don’t get every little nuance, perhaps, as this is part of a larger story, but there’s a lot happening and it’s all entertaining. Another good start. This is a great match thus far. Impulse 2 Again you bring us in easily and your writing style is measured, realistic, and combines normal-life with extended drama really well. All the voices in this piece are good, but they are, at times, perhaps not distinct enough from each other. Rosie seems almost the same as Knox, with the same kind of asides noted. Whilst this could be acknowledged as them being a couple and well suited, it didn’t feel quite as distinct as I’d perhaps have liked. That said, when Roderick arrives and you switch back to Impulse’s view on things, he does get a little more verbose, and in a good way, so perhaps I was being harsh there. The rest of it all reads well, and I enjoyed reading it without taking notes. I think you did a great job in explaining the motivations of Impulse and having him actually progress in life as a part of this. You also weaved in the wrestling business throughout and made it very real. If there was a weak point, I’d say it was in the treatment of Lane Stevens, strangely. Whilst getting Ivy to go into the lions’ den and get this guy and bring him to give Impulse some info was a cool journey, the guy hardly said anything and then bolted. I don’t think that’s going to give Knox much of an edge, to be honest. Given that it took up a lot of space, it was a little anti-climactic. The rest was great though. Stevens 2 Interesting opening, and this is going to be a tight match. Your prose is very good, and your tone is pitch perfect. The one thing about your timeline is that the prologue and epilogues being where they are… they confuse matters. Everyone seems to be doing the time-switching thing these days. Stevens is just funny. He’s an absolute friggin’ nutcase, but in a jolly way you can’t help but enjoy. His conversation with Darren is a highlight. The flight scene is good, or rather Stevens’s part is. Not so keen on Cozen’s part. I’m not familiar with her, and she was kind of annoying. And whilst I enjoyed reading every last word throughout this, I’m not really certain what happened in the end. I’m not sure if I needed to have read previous parts, but I’m not sure what the great victory is, or even what the Cowboy fella was up to. Perhaps it’s an ongoing thing… perhaps I’m an idiot, but it left me a little puzzled. DECISION TIME Oooh, it’s a close one. Both writers wrote excellently, and Impulse certainly stepped up to the plate against he man who’d previously eliminated him. It’s hard to pick a winner, and the sentimental side of me wants to see Impulse go through just because it’d be a great story. Alas, on balance, I just enjoyed Lane Stevens that little bit more. His character sets the (metaphorical) page alight, and whilst parts of Impulse’s story resounded slightly stronger with me, and whilst his characterizations were excellent, Lane’s extra little spark just tips him over the edge in my book. Winner – Lane Stevens (just) Judge Three - Trent Impulse RP 1 Here are some of my observations on style, language, structure, tone: One thing I like about this roleplay is the consistent character dialect. Each character has their own way of talking, and that is consistent throughout the piece. It's not easy to adapt different voices within one voice, but the author manages it. That said, the dialogue doesn't always succeed at keeping me interested in the characters and their actions. Miss Ivy's whole “shrouded in mystery” business about what she's going to pull off has a narrative triteness about it. I would say almost every writer in e-wrestling is guilty of this on some level, and it's difficult to avoid, but it's still something I noticed. While I like that you've removed the quotation marks from the narrator's dialogue, there are times when the meaning is convoluted or at least unclear and ambiguous. For example, in the first section when the author writes, “I’m a big boy, I said to myself, as I watched her on the opposite end of the bar, I can handle the truth, but I love you for trying to protect me.” This could use something more than what's there - I had an idea of who the pronouns refer to on the first read, but it's not as clear as I think it should be. Also, there are instances when punctuation could have been used more effectively when the characters are talking. When Knox's Mom says, “It’s just I know it always takes you a few days to get back on your feet after you’ve spent the night in the hospital, you know I worry about you,” it seems to me like there should be a stronger break where the comma is. As it stands, the sentence doesn't flow very well as something the Mom would say. This is a strong piece so yes, my nitpicking may be a result of trying to find flaws. And I hate to be a stickler, but there are some tense changes. I noticed them because I have been guilty of it too, and as hard as it is for me to admit as a writer, they can be distracting. I like the shifts in perspective, though, especially when the timing is right. I'm particularly thinking of the transition from Knox's POV to Rosie's, right after he says, "Somehow, I don't think Rosie believed me." It adds a sense of foreboding to the scene. A part of the strength of a writer depends on how well he or she can keep the reader's attention, and the author of this piece does that well in moments like these. Comments on characters, story, and theme: I particularly enjoy the father/son dynamic. The conflict between Randall and Knox is executed well. However, I'm not sure if it is intended, but the ending of the last scene is slightly comic. That's both a compliment and a criticism. On the one hand, it's funny; on the other, the tone of Rosie punching the father is at odds with the earlier part of the scene. Nonetheless, the author succeeded at making me want to read the next part of his story. The characters in this story are interesting, and the author describes them in an informative way. I like this part about Miss Ivy's phone: “The first four never changed. My brother Brian, Eli’s wife Angel, the diner, and the bar. All the other speed dials on my cell phone were in constant flux.” It gives me a good idea of what she's like and where her priorities lie. The author manages this well throughout the piece. I like that the writer has incorporated the main character's job as a wrestler into this piece. The author never loses sight of the fact that his main character is a wrestler, and weaves it well into the drama. However, I must admit that I found the plot involving the family much more interesting than “the plot” to uncover Lane Stevens's dirty laundry. It just didn't grab me in the same way that the stuff with Rosie, Knox, and Knox's family did. Impulse RP 2: A lot of my comments from the first roleplay apply here. I think the writer does a good job of continuing the conflicts and plots that were established in the first piece. Rosie and the Dad's strained relationship is very interesting and I think the writer pulls it off well, despite the danger of falling into cliche (it doesn't). I predicted that they would be unable to reconcile their differences, so I was pleasantly surprised with how the relationship developed. I think that the Dad is written much better in this piece – he seems more human and as a reader I can understand him better. I think this piece ended better than the first as well, and the flaws are minor. Overall, good piece. Lane Stevens RP 1: This may seem obvious to everyone who's familiar with a Stevens roleplay, but the best part about a Lane Stevens roleplay is Lane Stevens himself. I want to hate him, and there are times that I do, but ultimately he's a character that this piece makes me want to read more about. The characters here are very well written, especially those that are unique to this author's roleplays. Their roles are well-defined and are maintained throughout both roleplays. There are very few flaws. The prose is tight. The dialogue is funny and there is hardly a sentence out of place. The one thing I don't like is when the writer directly addresses the reader. Across both roleplays the author might have done it twice, so it's not a big deal – it just seems a bit lazy to me, especially when it comes from what I can tell is a very diligent writer. I like what the writer has done by jumping from different times while maintaining linearity. It's obvious that it is done intentionally, and attention to detail is something that is important for me as a reader. This attention is shown in multiple ways, and it definitely added to both pieces. Lane Stevens RP 2 Everything that is good about the writer's first piece goes for the second piece as well. I really like that Regan is centralized in this part. Darren also has an interesting story, and I like that you kept his ending unresolved. I'm not sure if the reader is suppose to fill in the gaps or if it's something that will be explored in a later roleplay, but nonetheless it had me interested. The sections on Darren are very enjoyable to read in this second piece. DECISION What I found interesting about these writers was the parts of their stories that I consider “character pieces”. I liked that both writers incorporated their careers into their stories, however, I found myself more engaged in the parts that didn't involve SCCW-related meta-angles. Thankfully, I didn't feel alienated from these parts – just didn't enjoy them as much. By the end of each two-roleplay series I was more than satisfied with what I had read, however, I'm giving Lane Stevens the edge. His story is funny and well-plotted, and the characters are damn near alive, something that made me enjoy my time in his world immensely. Good job to both handlers. Judge Four - Reyes I won't be breaking up my analysis because after reading both your pieces I felt that there wasn't going to be anything different I wanted to add between pieces, and because the transition from the first piece to the second piece was flawless and overall this was one of the best pieces I have read thus far. First and foremost, I absolutely loved the style you chose here. Initially, I was a bit skeptic when you alternated between dialogue with no quotes, and dialogue with quotes. It was easy to differentiate who was speaking, but at the start, I felt that you needed to be careful with how you do that. My advice would have been that it was better to stick to one form. But then I read on and I realized that everytime the scene changed, the narrator changed. I fell in love with having the narrator speak without quotations. It was very easy to follow along, very easy to enjoy the story, and it really, really made this a fun read. I am a sucker for down to earth stories, and this was it. Everything was nicely written. The characters all seem very authentic, very well developed. I think having the characters narrate their own story really did an outstanding job in making them seem real to the reader. You also do an equally outstanding job making the world they are in as detailed as possible. And maybe it's unfair that I live in NY, grew up in Williamsburg, and know exactly what you are talking about. Or maybe it's the fact that there is so much effort put into the characters' surroundings. I had no issue with the writing, definitely no issue with the characters. I loved the story here, mainly the focus on RK's life with Rosie and his parents. Like I said earlier, this was a nice down to earth story and I like those. If I had one small complaint, it's that I felt that there needed to be more done in regards to the initial question you posted: How can Impulse defeat Lane Stevens a second time around? And it just seemed a bit underwhelming that after McGinnis goes through the trouble of finding the accountant, that Impulse doesn't really get anything out of him. But that's really just a small footnote in an otherwise great story. LANE STEVENS There are two things that you do perhaps better than anyone else: Characters and dialogue. And reading these two pieces, I've found a third: story telling. You write in such a cool, riveting tone. You create characters so real, dialogue so intense, that it almost seems like I'm not reading a story, but actually watching it happen. The narrative is so smooth, and I love your style of making the story advance in a circular method, vice the straight on linear we are so accustomed to seeing. Focusing first on your characterization, you take developing and introducing characters to a whole new level. I thought the introduction of Miles was one of the best I've ever read. Most people introduce a new character, or reintroduce, by dropping a bit of kackground information, hoping that the reader understands everything there is to know about him/her. Yet you do it differently. You don't info dump so much as you have that character interact with his surroundings. As anyone would know, you get to know someone by directly interacting with them, not hearsay. I loved how you introduce Miles' character with that exchange with the waitress. And the bit about the tattoo? Priceless. You are very, very good at dropping subtle hints like that, subtle little clues that something is about to happen. Or in your case, something already happened that we just don't know about yet. You have, I think, the most authentic dialogue this round. The dialect is so unique to every single character, that it creates this very real world. Although, I do have to stop myself there, and say that Miles sounds a lot like Lane Stevens would. I guess that's why they're childhood friends? In any case, it's very easy to spot the differences between characters in their dialect. You make it easy for the reader to understand who's the gullible sidekick, the clever loan shark, the scared woman in distress, or the plain sick fuck with no boundaries. Your lines are spot on, I would have to say my favorite was "Huh, never really though of it that way." And the third piece I've come away with from all this is that you have a unique and wonderful way of advancing the story. I am sure most of us are used to, and see in this tournament, the linear way of storytelling. That is something happens to start it, something happens to lead to it, and all sequential scenes all add on to it, until we reach a resolution. But I love your circular way of doing things. I like how at times, you give us the ending, and then later on go back to the beginning. You jump back and forth between scenes and times, but you do it so effortlessly and smoothly, that it just reads right. THE VERDICT OF DOOM Match of the round. This match was so fucking close, that I don't think even Moses could have split the vote here. I loved both stories a lot, and for the longest time I couldn't decide. I mean how the fuck do you choose when you love both sides and didn't have anything bad to say about either one? By finding out what you liked the most. I loved the style that Impulse decided to use here with the constant change in narrator. I loved how real his characters were, and how detailed he was in their surroundings. I love his down to earth story, and it gave him a sort of underdog feel to it. But he came out strong, really strong. However... I just think that Lane Stevens writes in a much cooler and riveting tone. I think that where Impulse creates solid, emotional characters, that Lane creates characters that you feel you already know, because they are developed so intensely. Where Impulse creates a wonderful world with his story, I think Lane Stevens creates one that's so intense, full of intrigue, and just a bit more exciting. Impulse's story was simple and down to earth; Lane Steven's story was riveting and full of surprises. I am saddened that I had to make a decision here. The Winner: Lane Stevens Which means... THE FAULKNER BRACKET FINAL CHAMPION IS LANE STEVENS (3-1) ...and that has to be a contender for the closest match of the tournament thus far. Major props to both handlers for a classic match. Faulkner
