SHAKESPEARE BRACKET FINAL RESULT

13 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Here we go, ladies and gents... Judge One - Reyes LANCE MARSHALL Roleplay 1 I really liked the writing with this one, it had a sort of stream of consciousness feel to it. It was simple in the good way; it is much easier to visualize your world than Violence Jack's, and that's a big strong point in your favor. The writing is pretty much spot except for a few nagging issues: I think you have a formula, and I'm sorry if I'm late to that party, I've only been following your week since GTT7. But it seems that you rely too much on advancing scenes only if there is some sort of mystery, or cliffhanger to them. And I think that you are just way overdoing that. Sometimes you just need to tell a story with an ending, let things be. Watch your word choices carefully. I found it odd that you could spell out Hell, but you couldn't spell out G-d. There is a bit of repition going on, such as the constant use of Hell, to start sentences. The story is a bit slow paced because there really isn't much going on. And before I get to the part I disliked, I'm going to focus on the part I really liked. For the most part, the characters here are the strong point. The way you write their situation really makes them come to life, really gives them depth. There's a lot of emotional investment in them, and that's why this piece was just so enjoyable. On the flipside, I fear that the Voice is becoming too one dimensional, too cartoony. It seems like the Voice really has nothing else to do, that there really isn't anything else about him that we should know. Remember what I said about too many cliff hangers? Here is where there is a big problem in that: You created the Voice as this really evil guy who likes playing with boys. And you give him nothing else, not a background story, not a hint of future things, nothing really. Your sole piece of information is repeating a mantra along the lines of his employers being very pleased with what he is doing. And at this point, it reads not like something to look forward to, but more of a stall on the story on your part. There is way too much "something cool is going to happen next", and not enough actual building up to it. You see nothing really happened in the story here; Sure, there was some nice character development, but the overall story was just lacking. Roleplay 2 Please be more careful with your word choices. You didn't have anything outlandishly wrong, but at times I felt that you could have been more specific. Try to refrain from saying things like "it was undescribable, or among other things." Find a way to describe it, list specifically what it was that was being packed. But a warning, don't over do specifics. You just have to give the reader enough to create the world. I thought the dialogue seemed a bit weaker in this piece. The main culprit was Alanna, who at times seemed a bit childish. It's just a minor detail, and I think it's mainly because of the emotion you are trying to put behind her based on her incident with the Voice. Speaking of whom, I found it odd that you kept referring to him as the Voice. At the start of this story, I can understand that. No one knows who or what he is. But now he's actually presented himself to the Marshalls, now we have eyewitness testimony of who he is. He needs to be developed more. Yes, you give him a name at the end. It's a nice start. As for that ending... I'm sorry, but it epitomized the two things I found greatly wrong with these two pieces. 1. The voice is one dimensional. There is nothing else interesting about this guy, because there is nothing else. 2. Too many teasers. You rely on having some sort of "cool stuff will happen next" routine to go from scene to scene, story to story. And at this point, it's becoming some what of a chore. It seems everytime that the Voice leaves the story, it's because he's going to his underground lab to plot some shit like some kind of evil scientist. It worked before when he attacked Alanna, but now it just seems outlandish. It's like your simply creating a scene that goes "wtf I can't believe he just did that" solely for the purpose of establishing some sort of cliff hanger. VIOLENCE JACK Roleplay 1 This is the bracket finals and I've put a lot of expectations on everyone still in this tournament. Because of that, I've ended up being a bit harsher than I was last round. These pieces were very entertaining, but as you might have guessed, there was room for improvement. I'm going to start out this critique by saying that, first and foremost, the writing here if phenomenal. The narrative is very chilling, creating a gloomy, omnious atmosphere. At times, it reads a bit elitist, like you're trying to establish that Bruce Shanahan's world as superior to the current world he's in. This is strengthened by the fact that the dialogue through this piece is pretty much uniform. There really isn't any difference in the dialect in any of the characters - henchmen, Bruce, Maury, etc. At first I assumed the reason for that in the case of the henchmen was that in creating a cult-like organization, uniformity is a vital aspect. But reading Mizakawa, for instance, talk in the same kind of voice as ther others, kind of gives me the feel that the characters here are just being quickly drawn up, rather than worked on. I'll readdress this point later on. There are some great lines here, however. And I think that's the primary reason I still smitten with that aspect of this piece. The story is fast paced; it's enjoyable and flows smoothly. At this point, I only really see two faults. First is that there isn't enough Bruce in here. Sometimes I feel it can be a bad thing when you start to overshadow your primary character. Your opponent did the same thing, but I'll get to that later. Secondly, since it's fast paced, when you introduce new characters, or switch scenes, there isn't enough to get a solid grasp on the characters. I think you did a good job with the background info dump that you did in certain places, such as Mizakawa, but I think at parts the story moved on too quickly for the characters to develop. And as a minor note, you need to watch carefully about consistency in your story. In the interrogation scene, you started off saying that the inmate was a level 4 inmate can couldn't be told information about the outside world. However, the next time we are bought back to that scene, you tell him of the Vatican bombing. (Of course, he most likely already knew that, but it wasn't made obvious). Again, it's just a minor note, but they can add up if you are not careful. My overall thoughts are this was a very entertaining story that was written superbly. Despite some very clever lines, and the chilling aura of the narrative, I felt that sometimes the characterization was too narrow, and the dialogue seemed too universal for my tastes. But all in all, a solid effort here to start off. Roleplay 2 I thought your second piece was much better. For starters, the narrative evolved from a chilling, elitist atmosphere, full of gloom and despair, to an overwhelming one with intrigue and mystery. I really liked that in this piece there is more interaction among the characters. I'm one of those who champion character development over story and likes to see the characters advance the plot, rather than action scenes. Speaking of characters, I thought you did a solid job here. And I think the reason is that in the second piece it's Bruce's clan that is focus point here. And I'm starting to suspect that your writing increases greatly when you focus on Sect of Black Wisdom, vice it's antagonists, such as Gabriel and Maury. For one thing, the interaction among them (Such as Bruce and Ethan) is much deeper, and the writing smoother. The dialogue is also spot on. I'm very much into any scene directly involving the Sect. And I think if you can carry that to the other scenes, you can write something outstanding. There's a lot of hinting done here and it works. I think you've piqued my interest just enough to see where this goes. It's definitely s stark contrast from your first. As a side note, I was curious about something: My impression, based on the story and the accompanying narrative, is that the Old Ones is supposed to be the superior religion, maybe even the real religion. And I just found it interesting that although you attack Christian beliefs openly, you also have a lot of events happening that are based on Christain beliefs. It's just a minor note, but I figured that one was trying to establish a new superior religion, that it would probably work better if the competition was absent. THE VERDICT OF DOOM I thought this was a pretty close match, because honestly, I felt a bit underwhelmed by both pieces. Both of these guys are tremendous writers, both wrote really entertaining stories, and both have their strong points. For Justin it's his writing that appears so cryptic, and for the intrigue that he peppers in. The Sect is just so beautifully written and there were plenty of clever lines. For Marshall, it's his ability to instill emotion and believe in his characters, his storytelling, and his smooth narrative makes it easier to picture his world; his story. On the flipside, VJ lacks a bit when he isn't dealing with the Sect directly, and at times the secondary characters all read the same. The story is fast paced and the characters are seldomly given a chance to interact and develop. By contrast, Marshall makes a fatal mistake in now making the Voice too one dimensional and relying too much on the teaser aspect of writing. I was not pleased with the fact that the main character (Violence Jack, Lance Marshall) in both stories were pretty much absent. The good news for VJ is that in his case, the secondary scenes all evolved on Bruce, all led up to him. By contrast, for Marshall, it seemed like the secondary scenes were meant to only reinforce how evil the Voice was, which we already knew. In the end, I believe Violence Jack wrote the better overall product. The Winner: Violence Jack Judge Two - Aaron SHAKESPEARE BRACKET FINAL Alright, I’m going to do this; read one person’s piece and then read the other. I will also say I have read most of Justin’s work in GTT leading to this and that I have only read the first piece of Dan’s. That being said, we are supposed to judge on this round, and as such, feel it safe to say that I am not going to try and ask stupid questions out of either writer as a reason for why one loses over the other. Title: Act V: A Tango Upon Broken Legs; Shanahan’s Wager Featuring: Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan Alright, I’ve said it on multiple occasions with you and it stands true to this piece; you write in a Clancy style. What I haven’t said is you write in a very gothic style, and by that, I mean the narrative, even though it is third person, is measured and introspective to the moment and the entire dark and brooding underlay works to posit a twisted sense of morality. And while this might seem like a great praising wide-sweep for the sake of appearances, I also must tell you the issues with such comparisons; the level of connectedness and the amount of differentiation between characters is almost nonexistent, and the entire story hinges on whether or not you can deliver a story that makes audience members question the dark otherness and its relativity to the constructed norm. I think your sensibilities appeal to me in what you are saying about religion and the actual level of difference between the schools of thought (that there really isn’t any). I will also state that, outside of a few times with Whitaker. I say this because the trappings of your story have one tenor this entire piece, but when Whitaker goes off at the end of the first section, I actually had to clear my eyes because it popped off like someone speaking in an unhinged manner. I think so much outside of this is sterilized to the point where the portion of the roleplay you feature from another piece you had written previously comes off almost the same as any other; that’s not necessarily a good thing, especially since that section is supposed to be more action oriented. You’ve lulled the audience into a mindset, and the thing to remember is that for instances like that, you want to hit the audience over the head and grab them by guts. Or, with you genre, to give the audience something so sinister yet reads so real that we can’t shake the image. Overall, this was a good piece of writing, and while I state that there is only one tenor throughout, that one tenor is rich in the details and has a way of pontificating a scene that carries real weight behind it. I’m not asking you to fundamentally change what you are writing to come up with a piece of writing that tries to please everyone; what I want you to do is keep doing what you are doing. Write what you are strong at. There are elements of characters questioning their motivations (well, Maury), but the rest, as you sort of put it in your piece about Gabriel; they are sentinels. Title: Chapter Five: Players On The Board Featuring: Lance Marshall You don’t write as cleanly as Justin. I think your initial paragraph could be peered down quite a bit rather than ambling around the point you are wishing to make. I don’t know if I can compare you to a writer I’ve read professionally either, so yeah. In wrestling, this is called stringing along your audience...get them thinking one thing. I’m about to launch into a tirade of issues you have Dan, and Christ, you have no business being here. What you lack in Justin’s construction, you overwhelm with subject matter and delivery. Which isn’t to say that your prose construction is somehow faulty wire; Justin is very professional in how he writes, and he plays to his strengths. You play to your strengths by knowing how to set an engaging scene and by constructing a punch that Justin’s style might lack. And as I say this all, I understand I might be getting myself in hot water with both of you. You write two different styles of writing, and already I am seeing things I like in each piece that make this not necessarily an argument over which person wrote better, but who in the fuck deserves to move on in a close match. I had to move away from my computer a few times as I was reading this piece, mostly because I felt powerless as I was reading. There is something so twisted and so sinister is the casualness of The Voice that hits me hard, and the surroundings you put Alanna in remove any sort of response that is going to satisfy the audience that this twisted motherfucker is going to get what he deserves. Maybe you have one tenor as well (I think you shift from the introduction to the rest of the piece), but the fact is, that whole piece was this all too efficient psycho-mindfuck to someone who is truly helpless that it is impossible for me not to be shook up a bit by what is being presented. I think, one a piece to piece standing, this one has a hefty punch, and jarred me pretty well. Title: Act VI: The Songs That Dead Men Sing, The Sights The Damned May See Featuring: Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan Once again, I could repeat myself as to what you have written as it seems everything I have said stays true. Your prose is fantastic and you know what you are writing. The ending with Kerry was what I had been waiting for from you; great visual and visceral descriptions of the unexplained macabre. This steeps what you are writing in the lore, and at the same time gives the audience a haunting visual to contend with. The connotations of what you are writing about are also worth noting, as I understand that the effort in your crafting isn’t just surface level. I don’t have a lot to complain about in this, really, as I believe this is a solid outing in both pieces that does you some form of justice. You’re a fantastic writer, Justin, and even with all my literary backing, I can’t pick apart what you are doing on a systematic level but on one of resonance with audience, which again, is not something you can achieve with the style of story. It would be like asking for Gran Torino to have a scene involving Clint Eastwood crying just to show his vulnerability. I know I am brief here, but I very good piece here, and I think it provides some intrigue going forward, as this world is obviously getting swallowed up into the maw of Hell. Title: Chapter Six: Making Moves Featuring: Lance Marshall I was extremely praiseworthy of your first piece in that it completely had me on pins and needles the entire time, and I felt that the writing was tight throughout. I think that, without a locus to focus the emotion and powerlessness that you excelled at in your first piece, you lose focus. It isn’t that I want more of Lance; you made an active choice to feature other characters prominently and believe you do a good job in doing so. I think the dream sequence is unnecessary to your point (especially since round pieces are essentially companions), and especially with the way things were in the previous piece, Alanna’s ability to settle down is rather…alarming. She has seen the face of the man who molested her son, and if that isn’t enough, the same one who threatened her with a blade and groped her. Not remembering his face is one thing, but I know if I were in her situation, I would be shivering and shitting bricks, especially because she does not have to uphold this pillar of strength façade anymore. I think the Cthulhu plushie is a cute thing and believe that something has to be done about this in the next round. Either of you. Someone has to. Overall, I think that this is a pretty good piece, though I think your gambit with the ending, while causing intrigue, also puts you in a position where the reasoning is perplexing. JUDGEMENT Alright, I’ve read both of your pieces and can see why you are both here. I wanted to read this one first because I wanted the time to pine over this in case it was as close as I believed it would be. After one roleplay, I was stuck; I stand by my assessment of Justin, and believe that he knows what he is doing so well that it is almost impossible to top him craft wise because he’s top shelf. Dan met him, and overcame him in breathtaking fashion in a piece that was cripplingly hopeless and brutal all the same. I think that once he got into the scene with Alanna and The Voice, Dan was jugging and felt that Justin’s one tenor wasn’t going to be enough to top Dan if he hit him with this sort of heavy material in his second piece. The second piece had a reversal of expectations; Justin needed to make ground, and did so with another solid outing. His prose was crisp, and he ended with a visual which typifies the genre he is writing. As he is moving forward, the spiral is in full bore, and there are chinks in the armor on both sides in his macabre tale. I don’t think Justin cranked a home run out of the park, or that he slam dunked Dan, but it set the stage for Dan to have a chance to answer. His second roleplay lacked the sharpness his first did, and after the dream sequence, the tenor of what Dan was writing became more playful in nature, moving into a direction where Lance and Alanna are a nice happy family and he just happens to be a wrestler. There is concern on the part of Lance, but at the same time, his actual topic matter was moving away from the strength of the first roleplay until we reached an ending that sort of left me scratching my head. Now I know Dan is working for the future of his series, but having The Voice turn himself in isn’t what provokes this reaction; you’ve actually moved away from The Voice the entire second roleplay, and the balance seems to shift in the way you construct your second roleplay. Even so, the statement needs to be made; the second roleplay is a solid showing. Which brings us to the decision making process. I think Justin’s the better writer from a construction standpoint, but I think Dan’s slivers of emotion are powerful enough to offset that. When I look at the pieces, I think Dan’s first is the best piece out of the four. It is so well executed that it’s memorable in the way it tortures Alanna and the audience with the manner in which it progresses. It is gut wrenching and efficient and anyone who wants to try this style should read this piece and take notes because Dan knows how to do it amongst the best. When I think of Justin’s two pieces combined, I think that the writing is well executed, and from a craft standpoint, people should look at this if they want to see how to set up their work. I’m thinking long and hard on this, measuring this decision. I hate doing this. Dan and Justin are both tremendous talents, and while others will find faults in their overall performances from whichever way they wish to slice it, I think that both are tremendous and budding writers. Neither man is here by accident, and it is in this situations where you realize that you literally hold the keys to a monumental decision I have Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan to win Shakespeare. I think that, out of the roleplays posted, Dan wrote the best and worst of the four, and had it been first roleplay against first roleplay, Lance Marshall wins the bracket, but there are too many bumps in his second roleplay for me to not look at the body of work. Justin wrote in one tenor throughout, and that one tenor is a buzzsaw. Judge Three - Andy Lance Marshall 1 A good opening section, though I think the first paragraph is worded little clumsily. The Voice is such an excellent villain. I, however, like him when he's more in his calm, cold, mocking voice. He's in such control that it seems jarring for him to call her a bitch and brag about his erection. Perhaps that's his weakness, and what makes him more evil than you'd otherwise think, but I still find myself entertained by his wicked ways. That probably says something terrible about my psyche! Ot much else I can say here. I don't need any background or anything like that, I can just sit back and be entertained by a sadist being evil. Fun times. I'd like to see some involvement of Mr Marshall himself in the second one though. So he's talking shit. Man, philosophers can be RUBBISH! Anyway, moving on to the actual piece itself... I wasn't massively sold on the first section, to be honest. I think that whilst you write very well, it seems a little by-the-numbers in terms of the prose used. Science man disbelieves, and Acolyte type speaks of the demise of the world in grandiose terms. I just feel a bit of deja vu. Let's see how it progresses. The scene with the Yakuza boss was good, though I'm not sure how I feel about the use of a recycled section of roleplay (even though you cite your source). Towards the end the voices all blur together, as well. The second scene with Whitaker seems a little strange. He almost seems to be helping the man of science, telling him what he knows of VJ, etc. That strikes me as odd, and his tone of voice changes rapidly. Whilst this can convey madness or a lack of coherent thought, it just doesn't seem right to me. I like the voice speaking to Bruce though. That's a good part of the story, showing that he has enemies that even he does not know about, and who seem to have one over on him. So, I have mixed feeling on this roleplay. Obviously you write well and yadda yadda, but it's not grabbed me as much as I hoped. Lance Marshall 2 This one takes a while for me to get interested in it too much. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan 2 Your opening section makes me wonder about how far we can go in this little game of ours. If VJ wins GTT7 will he end the world, or have it all bowing down to Cthulhu? If so, WTF do we do with a GTT8 (if one happens)? Have everyone try to overthrow the Old Ones? I'm not gonna go marking down anything for this, just a thought I had. The questioning of Maury was done with a light touch. You're left with suspicions of your own, and perhaps he's doing things he's not aware of, but you can't be sure and it leaves you on edge. As you're such a good write, poorly chosen (IMO) words really stand out. Lady Sterling's “posh” tastes is one of them. C'mon, you can do better than such a commonplace and colloquial word as 'posh'. I have just two real criticisms for this piece. 1 – You've spread your net a little wide, and in doing so have encompassed things too thinly. You've got a myriad characters, and new ones to the uninitiated being introduced all the way through to the end. I can't really feel great emotional attachment to them, and their actions don't seem as important to me as perhaps you'd hope (that said, Kerry was an intriguing figure). 2 – I know we're in the middle of a story, but it really seemed like a middle section. Whilst things happened that could be considered 'a big deal' (the death at the end, the destruction of the manor), it never felt like a climax. DECISION TIME There was a lot of good stuff here by both writers, and it's a close decision in the end. In fact, it's a decision I wasn't expecting to give after the first half, as The Voice's torture scene was excellent. Bruce Shanahan and his minions and the quality of the horror writing in Justin's second piece really ramped up my interest in his second piece, whereas Ben's second felt flat for me until the ending. So it's a tough call. I'm very interested to see where Ben goes with this story and this cliffhanger, but for my money I'll have to follow it in SCCW. I think Justin's overall quality JUST edged this one. But it was devilishly close, and both authors could have stolen it. My victor = Violence Jack Which means... THE SHAKESPEARE BRACKET CHAMPION IS VIOLENCE JACK (3-0) Though it's worth noting that the 3-0 was by no means a walkover, as I hope the judging comments showed. That was a helluva close match. Shakespeare
