Intermediate: Painting Hair-White Mexican

10 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
How to paint realistic hair over a Poser render in Photoshop or Paint Shop Pro, in eight steps. In this tutorial you will learn how to paint hair on an image. This is the basic technique used to enhance existing hair. It doesn’t work great on a bald image that will come later on. It is assumed that a general knowledge of Photoshop or PSP has been obtained so simple features such as the smudge and layer effects are not explained, if you need that go to the Photoshop tutorial. Good luck. Original Image Create a new layer and begin to paint with the 3pt brush in strokes that hair would realistically go. Use the hair that's there as a layout to follow and go along fairly generously. Step 1 Now hide that layer and create another one. select a middle color from the hair and repeat but this time use the 1pt brush. Step 2 Turn on all the layers and take a look at what you've got. It should be starting to look realistic but we're not done yet. Step 3 Hair has highlights in it so you need to add those two. Select the lighter shade of what you were working with, create a new layer and with the 1pt brush again, go along all the hair that is seen coming the same direction. You'll notice with this that there is a strand from of the bangs that sweeps left and a strang that comes from above the ear down accross the back hair so DON'T paint those area in the proper direction, go with the general flow. Step 4 Now take the smudge tool and set it to 75% and 9pt and smudge only that top layer. Go with the general flow that you just painted. Step 5 On that same layer, take the paint brush tool again and this time paint the two areas we didn't do right. Going in the direction the hair would flow. The reason for this is that this hair is in front of the hair behind so it should have the appearance that it sits in front. This will do that. Step 6 Now once again take the smudge tool and proceed to smudge just the two areas you repainted in those directions. Step 7 Now all the hard part is done. Click on the first layer of hair we painted and the little drop down box that changes the layer type we want to change that from normal to lighten. Click on the second layer we made and do the same thing. Finally click on the third layer and set it on Color Burn and change the opacity to 60% Step 8 Now for the final step, duplicate the top layer and change the opacity to this one back to 100% and the layer style to lighten. Finished Product
