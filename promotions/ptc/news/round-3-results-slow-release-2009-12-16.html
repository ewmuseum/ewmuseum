Jared Sykes PIERRE JARED SYKES As an opening disclaimer I would like to point out that with me not being as in love with SCCW as most of PTC seems to be right now, I'm coming to this pretty fresh. Unfortunately I don't feel that you do enough to draw me - a new reader - into the clearly intricate web that weaves together the SCCW characters, rather assuming that I already cared about them (you need to make me care). For example, a lot of both your roleplays seem to revolve around an attack by Lane (Stevens?) on Jared Sykes, but I don't know about this attack, and it took a while for me to figure out what was being referred to. "Positively Fourth Street" The opening segment is too dialogue-heavy, and drags. It reads like a prosaic, and not particularly interesting, on-show segment; that it is both too long and not dramatic enough to actually be one doesn't necessarily qualify it as a good scene in a roleplay. Because your focus is on dialogue (with which I don't think the characters were differentiated enough) you fail to develop Jared as the story's main character; all I know is that he seems very scared of Alexandra, and he repeatedly shifts his position against the car (given that there was little narrative breaking up the dialogue, couldn't this narrative have been more interesting than "He shifted his position against the car, now turning his whole body to face her", regardless of what it implies about his attitude towards Alexandra?). Furthermore your approach to which characters get prominence is sometimes off; for example, the way you introduce Mr. Hawke suggests he's going to be significant, but then he completely disappears. The second part is completely different, as you pay far more attention to introducing and describing the characters (perhaps because they're new?) and it's all the better for it. I was worried about the relevance to your wider story but you tugged it back well at the end and added some intrigue about who this George is and what he wants with Jared. I also liked the next part with Mervin, whose hesitancy was really nicely characterised, and I liked how you explored an often ignored facet of the Secondary Character in E-Wrestling (life on hold to follow you around, etc. etc.) - that was pretty neat. But my problem with this roleplay is it doesn't necessarily form a coherent whole. Yes, segment one part-explains segment three, but where does the middle segment come into all of it? The requirement this round was for two roleplays - which can't just be taken to mean one long roleplay split in two. A roleplay needs to tell a story on its own, and this instead was a splicing together of three snapshots of Jared's life that left me with the feeling of having read something promising but unsatisfying. "Everything Beautiful" Roleplay number two I found far more compelling. Initially I felt bewildered; I don't know where, particularly with these SCCW intertwined storylines, one draws the boundary between "whose" roleplay it is, but the characterisation of Alexandra Pierce seemed in many ways much more important and poignant than that of Sykes: while I really enjoyed the way you wrote their encounter, I couldn't help but wonder why this was specifically being submitted as a roleplay by/about Jared Sykes. Then the end to that scene changed my mind, as in two immaculate paragraphs you swerved me to the realisation that this really had been about Jared's own feelings all along, and this was masterfully done, with sparse reference to them beforehand, and a beautiful final sentence. Moreover your cast of secondary characters is more appealing in the second piece. The bar and shooting scenes are well written, though I couldn't help but feel they needed spacing out - I would have preferred an interspersing of your two stories, rather than telling the Amy/Alexandra story and then telling the George story. Regardless you again write well, and this time less disjointedly. IMPULSE "Book 1.5-2" A bit jumpy on the tenses in parts, I had to read things several times over at points to try and figure out whether "the now" was past or present. A read-through would also have fixed slight flaws like your hyphens and dashes showing up the same, and BB code not working, and the apparent continuity error on how many years ago your character graduated (pedantic maybe, but I rate attention to detail). I like the way you write, though; you have a dynamic kind of flair that keeps everything rolling nicely off the screen and I immediately felt absorbed. Plus, these days I'm more and more a sucker for wrestling-centric roleplays, so maybe you lucked out on having me as a judge, or maybe you just did well on dropping anecdotes here and there that believably (this is too, too rare) painted a picture of a man whose wrestling weaves into his life and is not just a sideshow (I liked: "My arms were still sore from the match last night, but that’s what you get, so I didn’t pay it any mind"). That said, I was not a fan of how the roleplay ended. If the second one goes on to follow straight on from here, I'll feel you cheated me given that the first was merely setup; if not, I'll feel you cheated me by not delivering on what you'd built up. Either way it isn't going to be a positive reaction which says to me you shot yourself in the foot. "Book 3: He Said, She Said" Moreso than in the first piece I found myself reading dialogue that I didn't necessarily believe ("usually when I’m in New York it’s for just a day or two, so there’s bills, there’s laundry, there’s catching up on the goings-on of the neighborhood"). Yet I liked the voice you gave to Ros(i)e: her first portion was probably one of my favourites of the roleplay which is testament to your ability to shape likeable and interesting secondaries (both Rose and Max), especially little flaws like the asthma which are touches few take the time to add to secondary characters. The more I read of your piece, the more I enjoyed it. DECISION Ultimately there is not a lot between you. Both of you wrote stories firmly rooted in the gritty world of real life, which in a PTC often full of assassins and vampires was refreshing for me. Both of you had flaws and either of you could easily go on to tell a compelling story that I would enjoy the rest of. So my decision has to come down to fairly cruel differentiation by one factor that I'm going to pinpoint: at point in Jared Sykes' roleplays, particularly the early parts of the first one, I felt bored - like I was waiting for something to happen. By contrast, Impulse's roleplays were perfectly paced, unfolding into my head in a perhaps straightforward but nonetheless compelling way. In some ways Impulse's handler wrote things more simply, but the output was effective in that I felt equally animated by and genuinely interested in all three of his main characters. Jared Sykes' roleplays were too fragmented for me to get the same feeling.
