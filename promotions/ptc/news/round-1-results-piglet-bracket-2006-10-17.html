Round 1 Results - Piglet Bracket

17 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
ANDY M. V JACOB MCKAIL Judge 1 Andy M.: I liked it. You described the debauchery well. Quick and to-the-point. The only thing I didn't care for was the dual perspectives. I think you could have convinced us through Andy's point of view that the girl thought he was a pig. Jacob McKail: Firstly, I hate you. I should disqualify you due to exceeding the maximum word count I just now imposed. But you're a good writer. You've got that to your credit. Along with my wishing you chilled out a bit for the opening roleplay, there's my wishing you dove to the point quicker than you do. I'm really not prejudice against longer pieces, but I found myself sort of drifting away from the story. It didn't hold me like a longer piece needs to. Pick: I have to go with Andy. It held my attention and kept me interested. Judge 2 Andy M.: Solid start. I became pretty interested in seeing what Andy was doing lately and how he came to be hung over in the hotel room. The only problem I really had besides a few small spelling errors was that this was a typical story that I’ve seen written a million times. Guy gets drunk, girl get drunk, they sleep together, guy wakes up and has no idea who she is and can’t recall all the events of the evening. Solid piece of writing just nothing that reaches out and shakes me saying this is an awesome piece. Don’t take that as a bad thing cause the piece was well written and a solid read. The fact that Andy is upset with himself over the way he has allowed himself to become is something that I think you can really do some very interesting work with. Seeing the ups and downs of trying to change his life, as well as fight off the back injury, are some things that I think if you work into future work properly could be very interesting and entertaining reading. Jacob Mckail: This starts off pretty good. I’m not one who usually likes to see people open their tournament roleplays with a death, or someone dying, as there is really no reason for me to care at this point. I don’t know the person and thus it makes it impossible to really attach myself. However there is enough of a hook here to interest me in seeing how you continue with it. The only issue here is that you drew an opponent also struggling with inner demons and it would appear that you’re both on your “roads to recovery”. To me it’s the same story, with obviously different reasons, and some twists and maybe a little deeper problem with Mckail. All in all this is a good read, entertaining enough to keep me going from start to finish, and it does it’s job, for the most part, to keep me hooked on wanting to see where this story progresses to. Good work by you as well as Andy. I’m going with Mckail here. Both guys did a solid job but in the end the Mckail story had more intrigue in it and there was plenty more depth and reason given in it for me wanting to follow the story. Andy should be proud of the job he did as I did in fact like his piece. Congrats to Mckail. Pick: Jacob McKail. Judge 3 I liked Andy's piece, it was self-contained, short and sweet, and very well written. Not a lull to be found. McKail's piece took some considerable effort to read. There was one or two places where the grammar police would be choking on their wheaties, however he managed to incorporate a nice blend of history and current events. I could probably make a watertight case for putting either of these guys through, but only one can. Ultimately, I'm wanting more of the hallucination, because I think its got potential. Pick: McKail Props to Andy for entertaining me so well. Result Jacob McKail d Andy M., 2 votes to 1 BRUCE 'VIOLENCE JACK' SHANAHAN V TCHU Judge 1 Tchu (Juggling Loaded Guns): Hmm... the story here is interesting. It was something to read, that is for sure. A few things such as "blowing her knows" instead of "blowing her nose" crept in, again something an electronic spellchecker would not catch, but still a very good read overall. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan (Herald of the Damned): One word: Damn. This man went through many that some say he shouldn't have last year. But this was something that showed how and why he did it. Last year's masked man known as "Masquerade" comes in swinging. I was a bit taken aback when it looked like in the rp he was basically saying "sorry we won't really face off" but then the hook gets thrown that slams it home for their home federation of PRIME and it all comes together to fit into place. Both: Two tournement juggernauts if you read the tale of the tape here, but one has to be eliminated. Both put up a good showing and had their own reservations about the tourney, and were able to fit everything else in and give the tourney enough attention that it was a concern of theirs. However in the end, I have to say.... one person did keep my interest more. Pick: VJ Judge 2 Violence Jack: I really loved this roleplay. Right from the start where it’s in the words of his doctor at the asylum it hooked me instantly. I like how it eluded to past events, giving just a taste, but not allowing too much information right from the start. This is just the kind of roleplay I love to see cause it ties in all the elements I like. It plays off the past, gives some character development, ties into the current situations for the character, and is just generally written very nicely. I liked the idea of VJ simply manipulating the entire series of events, right down to putting in his GTT contract that Tully could take his place for the matches. Gives you the clear feeling that VJ has been, once again, pulling the strings all along and the being locked up is all part of his plan. The description from the doctor at the start of the roleplay was excellent. You really got the feeling that the doctor genuinely fears what VJ is capable of but at the same time has no choice but to be in awe of him and his intelligence. Excellent job here and it was a real joy to judge this match up. Tchu: Another extremely nice read. I really enjoyed this roleplay as well. I loved how the conflict between keeping his promise to his wife and being legally obligated to participate in this tournament are used to literally tear Matt apart emotionally. You can tell he clearly could care less about competing and knowing if he is one of the best in PTC yet he knows that if he doesn’t he is putting his family in a very tough situation financially. Matt W. is in the ultimate no win situation for himself and I think it is very well written. It makes you feel for him the entire time. Great job. I now loathe the two of you immensely for a couple of reasons. Number one you made this pick the hardest one I’ve had to choose between in the times I’ve judged here. Both of you guys wrote great stories that I really enjoyed and would REALLY love to see both of you continue one. This is one of those times that it’s just so damn hard to choose that I’ll likely kick myself later for not going the other way, and then flip flop back to agreeing with my decision. Great work by both of you and it was a blast. Never the less I have to pick a winner and I’m going to go with…VJ in the closest of margins. Seriously if there are play ins for this deal and Tchu doesn't get one it's a damn crime. Pick: VJ Result Violence Jack d Tchu, 2 votes to 0 RICH "THE RENEGADE" ROLLINS v CLINTON SAGE Judge 1 In spite of rollins writing some great stuff, in particular the poligymous storyline that reminded me of "Big Love" and the introduction of Rollin's older sister, the Sage story was stronger in ease of acquiring all the backstory and the sinister subtext in the characters of the main players, Hollinger and Sage. A close battle, but that fascinating nuance made all the difference. Pick: Clinton Sage Judge 2 CAVIAT: To those reading this judgement, I am friends with both Andrew and Ryan. As such, I am judging this match because of this fact. There is no bias going into the match judgement, and I want that to be known before anyone complains that I am judging for two friends. Thank you. Clinton Sage: Ryan, you moved away from the formula, and it fucking worked. Holy shit, my friend. This is exactly what people have said you're capable of all these years, and yet, you fell into that goddamn rut, using the same formula, because it worked, for the most part. The story, in and of itself is simple in it's execution. We know (now) that Hollinger is Spartan. We know that Sage is looking for him, so now, we as readers can assume that that's exactly what the story is going to culimate in. And yet, the two of them are separated by so much, it seems impossible that it could ever even happen. Great story, Ryan. Rich Rollins: If there's something that could take away from this great peice of writing, it's the asides that are peppered in that take the piss out of PTC, Deville, and the rest of us. Yeah, I know it's the character, and yeah, I know it's what Rollins does. But, they don't fit into the flow of the story. At all. That said, the story was well done. Rollins hasn't skipped a beat, despite what the "rusty" aside said at the beginning. I can tell that you've got plans for this storyline in GTT6, the same as you did when you started GTT5. The pieces seem to all be in place, depending on the judgement. Overall: When it comes to the technical side, Ryan beats Andrew. Hands down. His story was more consistent, and when I said to Andrew that those asides could be his downfall, I meant it. They really do. When it comes to language and imagery, Andrew beats Ryan. The problem with Ryan is that his dialogue is basic. Neither Hollinger or Sage have any sort of quality in their speech that makes me think that they're even different characters, or just simply regular people. Rollins, however, is distinct. You could read a quote and have to guess who it's from, and you'd know it was Rollins. When it comes to potential, and how the story left me feeling and looking forward to a future story from either, this is a draw. Both were endearing, and both had a hook that left you wondering what happened next. I can't give a plus or a minus to either. For every category where Ryan beats Andrew, Andrew comes back and beats Ryan. So, this is going to have to come down to personal preference, and nothing else. There's really no other way to decide it. In a story where Clinton Sage was supposed to be the antagonist/protagonist, he seems to be more of a backseat character, rather than the one who's there to add to the story of Nathan Hollinger, rather than the other way around. I think Ryan took a huge risk, portraying the story this way, starting off with more to do to with Hollinger than he did with Sage. The ultimate downfall of Rich Rollins, however, is absolutely those asides thrown into the story. I realize that Rollins is by his very nature, a dickhead. But, the asides broke the story up too much, and it almost seemed as if they were from Andrew, disguised as Rollins, rather than the true feelings of Rollins himself. Maybe I'm reading a bit too much into it, but, that's how they came across for me. So, I've lollygagged about it enough. The winner of this matchup, in my eyes is... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... Clinton Sage. Fuck, that was hard. Judge 3 If you want a close match, this is it. Both charactes have given me teasers to things that I want more of - polygamy in the family and stigmata. The only reason I have to pick against either of them is that the interjections between paragraphs from Rollins, while giving me the intended nice warm "OOC/Hype board" glow, took my focus away from what I wanted to be paying attention to. Pick: Clinton Sage Result Clinton Sage d Rich "The Renegade" Rollins, 3 votes to 0 JOHN COVEL V DUSK Judge 1 Other things being close to equal, John Covel had a much stronger use of dialogue in his story, enough to make the difference in his favor. Pick: John Covel Judge 2 John Covel (Internal Passion: The Decision): The writing here was good. I just wish it had more substance for me to grasp onto. I've read some of his work before and it was up to par with it. I just didn't quite get why he'd concentrate on Covel's baseball background and wanting to get back into it for a wrestling tourney. The entertainment value was just not there for me. Based on what was there to 'chew on', it ended at the right moment. Dusk (Chasing Dreams: The Heart of Life [Part One]): Okay the constructive part... don't repeat yourself and make sure you're not making the reader get tired while reading the passage. A comma is generally meant to take a breath but to continue on, while a period is a point to take a small break and absorb what was said. Other then that, there was a lot more substance to this to add to a storyline. It had a slow start but picked up from there, which was good. I wanted to keep reading. I have to say that the tools are there, but learn to use them. By all means, find someone to proof read for any future rounds though. Sometimes you don't catch it yourself and that is not something an electronic spelling or grammar checker will catch either. Based on the entertainment value and the story, I have to give the nod to Dusk here. Pick: Dusk Judge 3 John Covel: I enjoyed your work, but wished that there was something more substancial to it. It was like eating a salad with 'lite' dressing on it, I just wished it had more, body. Dusk Where John lacked body, yours was perhaps too substantial. Your writing style is a bit hard to follow sometimes, I found it repeatitive and hard to read. Pick: Covel the win. Result John Covel d Dusk, 2 votes to 1 RON MEXICO V TRASHCAN MAN Judge 1 While Ron Mexico's use of language is marginally stronger than that of the Trashcan Man, Trash's storyline, even using devices as MPD and the struggle to reach his family, is more compelling enough to pull in his favor. Pick: Trashcan Man Judge 2 Ron Mexico (Young Urban Proffesional): I don't know, it was hard to stay on track reading it, so the writer was obviously not staying on track writing it. Very little mentions about the tourney and wrestling are not always bad, but when the rest of it seems to be just 'there' to be 'there' then it can be quite distracting to read. I read it once, and tried reading it a second time and other things were able to pull me away from it. There was just too much going on that wasn't really giving me anything to digest as to who this character was and what he was about. I started this not knowing anything about Ron Mexico and he's still an enigma to me. Traschcan Man (Airports are fun!): I'm impressed. The innerdialogue between the "different characters" was spot on for what we saw. Although this was by far not as long as his opponents, this is where the key comes in on the quality of the writing. Some minor spelling and grammar mistakes that most electronic means would have missed as flagging, but nothing more then that at issue. Pick: Trashcan Man to advance. Result Trashcan Man d Ron Mexico, 2 votes to 0 SEMORE SMOKE v SLEDGE Judge 1 Semore Smoke (A second chance ends in disaster?): I can honestly say that I don't remember the Semore Smoke from yester-year, and this was a pretty good surprise for me. I've always heard a lot of the hype, but this was a pretty good read. There were some minor things, very minor with formatting and syntax, but for the most part not to the point where anyone but an English Teacher would have raked it over the coals. I liked the take on having the draft and that actually used in character and in a way that was feasible. My one hang up is we already add 1 to the death count for this years GTT rping pool. Sledge (The Past Haunts): Okay, add about 4? 5? Who knows to the death toll. However one big glaring issue comes up. And that is the complete change in the middle of the rp when it jumps to the present in how it's written. The flow completely goes away, as it goes from well written prose to suddenly script format with brackets to denote non-speech. The story completely gets lost at that point, it's almost as if two seperate things were kitbashed together. The styles between the two halves are that different. Final Verdict: Based on the individual merits and observations, my nod has to go to Semore Smoke. Pick: Semore Smoke Judge 2 Semore Smoke: I already like the premise...a TRUE FA trying to prove himself. Keeps me reading already. Some awkward sentences and grammar. The ending was good...until this line: "Don’t rush it kid, this is serious. I hope for your sake you’re being honest. It’d be a shame for you to go down for something like this after coming so close to success." Overall it was a good RP, without a doubt, kept me interested and wanting another. Sledge The RP was ok. The thing is, though, the Semore Smoke RP was just...better. Semore gets the win. Pick: Semore Smoke Result Semore Smoke d Sledge, 2 votes to 0 SUMMARY - Jacob McKail d Andy M., 2 votes to 1 - Violence Jack d Tchu, 2 votes to 0 - Clinton Sage d Rich "The Renegade" Rollins, 3 votes to 0 - John Covel d Dusk, 2 votes to 1 - Trashcan Man d Ron Mexico, 2 votes to 0 - Semore Smoke d Sledge, 2 votes to 0 - Jesse Jamester & The Handler both forfeit.
