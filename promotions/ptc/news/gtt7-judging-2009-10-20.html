GTT7 Judging

20 October 2009

Screw it. Here's all of them. Quick results at the bottom for those who want the dirty fast. Two seeds eliminated. KING Suzanne Larsson vs Syko Arsonist -- [name withheld] Poison Pawn - Suzanne Larsson Likes: I read this before reading Syko's piece, and had Part 5 as the section to beat. Part 5 delivered match relevance in a way that interested me, and Part 6 delievered a back story for the future. Dislikes Parts 1 through 4 were well written, did their job adequately to introduce a few of your key characters, but overall felt very generic. Haunted - Syko Arsonist Likes: Syko IS Randy the Ram... for the first half anyway. Bonus points for Orange Tang and Traci Lords - two of my favorite things as a teenager. Dislikes. You had all the tools here - character depth, motives and an ongoing story with the drug taking.... but... this felt like two RP's crammed into one. Despite you making me work harder to read this by not using quote marks, I had you winning the match up until Syko woke up in the hospital, then it started wandering around the long way to make your point, and I lost interest. Have ambulance drivers revive him or something quick instead of the drawn out hospital scene. Summary Andy didn't win this match, Reyes lost it. Had his work been trimmed by about 25% out of the second half of this and delivered it with more focus (and quote marks!), he would have had Andy by the balls, easily. Getting straight to the point > better character depth, this time. Pick: Suzanne Larsson Dusk (16) vs Garbage Bag Johnny -- Boom Let me start off by stating that it was a pleasure to read the roleplays for this match, and a complete bitch to try and separate them to find a winner. Both pieces were well written and if I'm honest, neither man really deserves to crash out of the competition at this stage. I'll start with Garbage Bag Johnny's promo, which was, I felt, a brilliant opening piece in that it really helped the reader get an insight into his character. I am aware of the character, but have never actually read any of his stuff, so I found it to be very rewarding that his roleplay dealt with this. It took me from unfamiliar with the character, and brought me right up to date. I also liked that he didn't write it in kayfabe, maybe I'm out of touch these days but I found that to be very refreshing. It was simple, well constructed and relevant. Whilst I wouldn’t even begin to try and say that Dusk’s promo wasn’t well written, I found that it struggled a little to be as relevant as his opponent, and was perhaps just a little too complicated. Again, Dusk is a name I’ve seen around but never read, perhaps it is just a little bit unfortunate that there wasn’t really an introduction of sorts to his character so that I could feel a little more involved when reading his story. I admit that I would have liked to see where the character would evolve to (as suggested in the roleplay) despite not really getting a sense of what he was evolving from. In conclusion I have marked Garbage Bag Johnny’s card to continue in the tournament, as his promo seemed more relevant to the competition. As for Dusk I feel that against another opponent I perhaps would have been giving him the nod. WINNER: Garbage Bag Johnny Josh Marquez vs Draeden Darksky -- Pete JOSH MARQUEZ Your intro tends on the verbose side. You're flowery and it's good, but, it could have been cut down a bit in the sheer volume of language. I do like your prose however. It's not overbearing and beating the reader over the head, it's there and it works well in your story. I have a legit concern for AJ as the reader, which is wonderful -- I'm not easily moved by emotion -- because here's a guy who's just a schmoe like me, everything's fine, and then BOOM! Blindsided by chance (or fate). The issue, however, is that I feel nothing for Josh Marquez. His portion feels tacked on haphazardly, as if trying to make a stunning connection far down the road between Marquez and AJ as your shocker/twist. DRAEDEN DARKSKY I enjoy your prose as well. There's an easy reading quality to it, as if it's not meant to challenge your reader, just allow them to accept the words for what they are. I like that. I don't like the Darksky story interspersed with the wanderer's story. It reads much more meandering than it ought to, more TV than story in my mind. While each story is certainly good, and I absolutely applaud you for making the story interesting AND wrestlecentric, I can't help but think that the sum of the two parts are not equal to their whole. The story is similar to Josh Marquez' story in that I don't care about the twist -- I would have liked to have seen more, or, possibly stop the castaway/wanderer's part at Ronan letting him in. The last section adds little to the story, aside from setting up a potential second round peice. VERDICT Tough one. The loser takes a tough loss here in a story that, despite the similar distatse for the twists, I think there's a lot of potential in each writer to make the corrections and move on to play spoiler, and perhaps reach bracket finals somewhere. Draeden Darksky, welcome to round 2. Chainz vs Alias -- Pete CHAINZ I think your story developed a bit too fast. It's good, there's just quite a bit in a quick turn of time, and no real chance to get to know Dan or enjoy Dan. If his life is wrestling, and Chainz is his life, then I'd have liked to know more about how he got there. Even with a made up history of wrestlers he watched, it would still be more than the quick burn to being Chainz' biggest fan, and wanting to study his life. That said, I think you have a lot of promise in the story. It's interesting and a concept I haven't seen yet through all my years of wrestling reading. I'd love to see more out of this story. ALIAS There are a few, tiny spelling mistakes through the peice that stopped my reading pace with a stop, wondering if I read something wrong. A closer proofreading might help out a bit. As almost the antithesis of Chainz, I think you gave a bit too much in this RP, as there's a lot here to swallow in just a single round effort. That said, aside from the small proofreading errors, it flowed well, and there wasn't a time I wanted to stop. In fact, despite the largess of the information given, I want to read more. VERDICT In what could be considered an upset, if you're not familiar with Russ, Alias takes the win here. Simply a case of too much versus not enough, Too Much takes the W. Wyatt Connors (9) vs Mary-Lynn Mayweather -- Pete WYATT CONNORS Another introspective peice about a character's history. Your spin on it is unique since there are few who know a great deal about the old NWC. Your spin is fresh, and again, bravo for the wrestlecentric spin. I like the brevity of the peice, as it seems intentional -- whet the appetite, and give away the key component in your story, but, don't do too much. There is very little wrong here, and truth be told, it would be difficult to beat. MARY-LYNN MAYWEATHER I... I don't know. The format didn't click with me in the slightest. It's well done for what it is, but, I got nothing more out of it than this girl started to wrestle, looked up to Troy and really liked wrestling because it was real. I would have liked more meat, more engaging of my imagination and much more structure. VERDICT: Connors d. Mayweather. It's not that Mayweather's was bad -- by any stretch it was not. It just didn't have a whole lot of substance, and Connors wrote well. Jonathan Rhine (1) vs S.G. Martins -- Pierre Jonathan Rhine "Lost And Found: Kody" It was fine. Nothing more and nothing less. The dialogue, I didn't like very much. I didn't really feel like we got into any of the characters' heads, and it was surface-deep at best. References to wrestling are tacked on, and for me it read really weirdly for the narrative to call Rhine by his wrestling nickname in a home-life setting ("The New Life’s posture confirms this..."). A setting, by the way, that I never could gain a picture of. Rhine has lost an eye, I'm guessing from your poser/hearsay? Why don't I get told about this pretty noticeable aspect of your character's appearance (in fact, you said "his eyes still on the floor" at one point)? You're clearly a very able writer, but this is the first round of a tournament: you've got to assume the judges haven't read your work before. I can only surmise you're saving your A-game for the later rounds, because this did not read like the output of a #1 seed. S.G. Martins "Nothing to Lose...Everything to Gain" Let's take the big paragraph towards the beginning as a demonstration of how the roleplay worked as a whole. You put in a lot of effort to describe a scene, and you think of some really creative things to say. But the way that you do it isn't all that creative; it's almost a list of "I did this; I did that", often separated with an "as" which makes it read a little strangely. Let me illustrate that with your sentence "I looked down and noticed I was wearing a white tank top and pyjama pants as I started to run up the huge sand dune". The temporalities of wearing clothes and running up a sand dune are entirely different, so you shouldn't say you were wearing something as you were running up the sand dune: it's not like the clothes fell off automatically when you finished the running. Little things like this are hampering the fluency of your writing, and I'd like to see you find more expressive ways to tell the reader what's going on. And then finally, I didn't get what the two dreams at beginning and end actually added to the roleplay. I didn't understand any metaphor you might have packed into them, nor any explanatory value they might have had to flesh out the middle portion. I think this let down the piece as a whole and, although I do like to see people actually addressing the fact that they're in a wrestling tournament, there is a gap in class between your opponent and yourself and you could do worse than studying the way in which he writes. DECISION: I'm aware I gave a lot of negative commentary on Jonathan Rhine. That's because he's a number one seed and doesn't need to be told that he's a proficient writer. Jonathan Rhine wins this match, but he will have to do a lot better than this in my eyes to win this tournament. SHAKESPEARE James von Doom (15) vs Skylar Montgomery James von Doom d. Skylar Montgomery via an avant-garde asskicking. Steve Harrison vs. Hessian. -- Pete STEVE HARRISON I think your writing is done well. You don't seem to have the knack of some of the more "accomplished" writers, but, I'd attribute that more to you just getting back up onto the horse, so to speak. I think you're trapping yourself into saying more than you need to, especially in your descriptions and follow-ups. Specifically: "I am dead, don't you remember?" my dead papa said back to me, his ever creepy smile never fading. He's dead twice within the span of one sentence. A fact revealed repeatedly before that. HESSIAN I think you've improved a whole bunch since the Hessian of yore. I will say that there are times that you tend to get a bit more verbose than you need to be. Your prose is great, and you write very well. The story is part of a greater story, no doubt yet, it fits as a standalone well, too. I can offer no other suggestions to you -- I think the peice was exceptionally well written, and kudos for coming as far as you have. VERDICT: Hessian. Don't be discouraged, Harrison. You drew a hot commodity right now, and Hessian is on a tear right now. Tony Gamble vs Atreus -- [name withheld] Derrick Ramsay - Atreus Likes: I enjoyed reading this. Nice and evenly paced. Dislikes: Your hooks for making me want some future reading are a bit meh... The final section has been done, a lot. The simple phonecall and introducing Atreus like this watered down my previously piqued interest over what happened in the early 90's. A Short Story: No Pun Intended - Tony Gamble Likes: Easy to read. Enough of Adelina to make me care that something bad has happened to her by the end of it. Dislikes: I don't follow the other well known characters you've introduced here. The people who do would get some zing from their presence and the associated machinations, whereas I don't get that, so I have to take more from your own characters' presence and what you do with him. Summary: Neither of these stands out as a clear cut winner over the other. Nothing really annoyed me or distracted me with either, which is good. I would give the same advice to both writers if either advanced to Round 2 - more substance behind your words - i.e. make me care more than you did... or you will get creamed. I'm going to go with the more dramatic ending - the kidnapping. As I said, there's nothing particularly innovative about it, but it is a slight better Round 2 proposition than finding out more about Atreus's backstory. Pick: Tony Gamble. I do hope to see Atreus around in future, as you didn't go out of your way to lose this match by any stretch. Katherine Stryfe vs Buck Fledger -- Pete KATHERINE STRYFE Short. Sweet? Not so much. There's so much more that could have been explored here -- the monster within, the demons, the deaths referred to in the introduction, the masking of her own pain through her care free vibe. I can't say that it left me wanting more, simply because it left more open than it did close or even attempt to tie up. BUCK FLEDGER I liked it. I liked the length, and while I said before that another writer needed more, I think you gave the perfect balance of wanting more and leaving more for later, instead of just telling what you needed to tell. I really like that no one has a name. They're just a series of titles, even his mother is just a title. That's fantastic and I ate it up. VERDICT Stryfe, I think you needed more here. I say that because I am interested in the character, and unfortunately, I got a tiny morsel of what could be. Fledger wins this one. Jacob McKail (10) vs Lance Marshall -- Pete JACOB McKAIL A Wrasslin' Sheriff. I'm conflicted. While the prose is great, the story is... I don't know. I like it and I don't like it. The characters are okay, and I would have loved to see White's scheme foiled at least once before he finally got one over on McKail. To get over on him almost immediately is way more devious than the character should be capable of, considering he's a full on antagonist. I think I like the story, I'm just not sure. LANCE MARSHALL The story intertwines with, what I believe to be at least an SCCW story, and I think it does so exquisitely. We learn about Marshall, we learn about his life, and we learn about what's next in his life without it seeming like something different. I think you tell it well, and I think there's something intriguing here. VERDICT Lance Marshall d. Jacob McKail. While I don't think Kris did much wrong with his story, I think Dan did everything right, which went a hell of a long way toward the win. Violence Jack (7) vs The Back-Alley Brawler -- Pierre Violence Jack "Act I: The Man Who Failed Heaven" You do a fine job from the very beginning of creating a real aura for Shanahan. We then meet the man and you do a formidable job with the in-ring scene, truly bringing an initially mundane setting to life far better than most can manage - demonstrating also that your writing isn't abstracted from the wrestling setting - and then nonchalantly twisting the no longer familiar setting to one of Shanahan's own, sucking me into an utterly believable chaos that almost with relief you revealed was imaginary; this was extremely well executed. Your vocabulary is hugely impressive, though sometimes I feel you distribute it in the wrong ways; the "non-descript fellow" was actually afforded more description than many of the others! Certain facets of your game that I don't like as much as the others repeated themselves here: they are (1) insufficient characterisation of your secondary characters, perhaps relying on the reader having followed your work before; and (2) that chunks of speech sometimes aren't embedded well enough in the narrative for me to read through without wondering who is speaking (n.b. I am aware that at points this is an intended and/or irrelevant effect, but in the opening segment and at other times I began to feel lost). In closing, I am seeing a certain binary in your work between segments containing Shanahan - which are sublime - and those focused on secondaries - which are less so. Make of that what you will. The Back Alley Brawler "Hards Times In This Crazy Town" The beginning was truly excellent. I really loved it; possibly the best opening to a tournament series I can imagine, possibly some of the best wrestling-centred writing I've seen in this game. Then the second part? Not so much, I'm afraid: a little cliché. Rent-a-Hero was in some ways nice but in some ways unnecessarily self-indulgent (just as using the ~! felt dated). And as it wore on I felt more and more uncomfortably that this roleplay was horribly close to a rehash - though a good one - of something Mickey Rourke starred in not too long ago. I enjoy how you write the Brawler, his speech in particular - dialogue is something a lot of people seem to struggle with, particularly on making it distinct between different characters. But ultimately my conclusion is, if you'd kept up the quality and intensity of the beginning of the piece, you'd have just knocked out Violence Jack. As it is, you wrote well, but given how much I liked the beginning I was left feeling let down. DECISION: Bruce "Violence Jack" Shanahan takes the victory. A mouthwatering story that will only get better as our familiarity with the characters grows. More compelling secondary characters and he would edge closer to masterpiece territory. Jason Snow (2) vs Diego Foster -- Pete JASON SNOW. Your intro is not pretentious. However, I believe that it's a bit unneeded. A reader can infer through the story that Snow's not the narrator by more careful word choices. I want to tear your work apart, and take you down, but, I can't. I selfishly want you gone from this tournament because I know after the first two sections, I'm turning into a Snow fanboy again. I looked for something wrong, and aside from a teh instead of the, I'm hard pressed to find anything. DIEGO FOSTER I don't know how I feel about the footnotes. I really like that you've cited your work and sources, but, it reads weird. Maybe it's the font selection on the page, I'm just not sure. I will say, that I think this is prime example on how to make a roleplay be wrestlecentric AND interesting at the same time. There's quite a bit about the territory, the people, the places, the sights and sounds of DFW, and it's really interesting. The story itself bounds all over the place, and had it been more organized, I think it would have been my favorite of the entire tournament thus far. VERDICT Steve, had you met anyone else in this tournament, you'd likely have moved on to the second round. Unfortunately, Snow's story trumped it by both interest QUICK RESULTS: KING Suzanne Larsson d. Syko Arsonist Garbage Bag Johnny d. Dusk (16) Draeden Darksky d. Josh Marquez. Alias d. Chainz.
