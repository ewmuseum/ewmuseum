Jason Snow (2) 2-1. Karina Wolfenden (3) v. Vivica Valentine JAKE -- Ted the Roleplayer K-WOLF First, the opening paragraph. I understand the desire to wax poetic more than anyone in e-wrestling, as I am guilty of it myself. However, what I've learned is that most of the time, you're the only one who clearly visualizes what you're trying to say, and end up confusing the average reader. You could have left this out and the second paragraph would have been just as good of a beginning. That being said: shit picked up FAST from the initial bad taste in my mouth. Granted, there were a few buzzwords that probably could have been changed to make the piece clearer, but the atmosphere in this thing pushed all the right buttons as I read. The emotion in this piece just soaks the paper (I print these out to read them, because I am a fuddy-duddy) and it is just so gloomy, in a great way. Take note that the art in your writing isn't the use of complex words, but with the way you emote your characters' speech. I read this whole thing about four times and I loved it more and more after every single read. Like I said, the readability was a factor that could be improved upon, but great job. VALENTINE Decently written yet fairly stereotypical "Hey, this is me, and this is why I am such a great person" speech at the beginning. The fact that you used the term "boom box" in your writing is awesome in ways I can't begin to explain. The award presentation was 100% accurate as to what I'd expect to hear at a Make-A-Wish ceremony along that ilk, so great job there as to good solid technical writing. I also appreciate the fact that you take time to explain some instances as being off-key as to what a "normal" girl would say or do. The interactions with the shitstain of a boss were great, and the self-realization climax flowed together perfectly. Honestly, it's one of the most well-written pieces I've read in this tournament. VERDICT Two completely different tones in this matchup, which was nice (except for the part where it made it more difficult on me as a judge.) If the victory nod went solely on readability, then Valentine would have it locked up. If we were going based on which story I was more interested in, I'd have to give it to Wolfenden on that end. This was so fucking close, and after reading these pieces twice through and having a different initial winner for both times, I honestly have to go with my gut on this: I have to pick the one that left me feeling emotion, the one that illicit a "Jesus..." response. Karina Wolfenden in a very very very close decision. STEVE -- TwoLiter Karina Wolfenden The prose in this piece is just awesome. Great command of vocabulary. Valentine, 3-0 Hoyt Williams v. Drake Coleman REYES -- SYKO ARSONIST Drake Coleman: Forgive Me, My Ribs Hurt I feel like my head just exploded. I feel like I just broke into Alice's cupboard and downed some of her pills, because you know damn well that little brat had the good shit stashed away. There was a very haunting mood to this piece, and I very much liked the broken lines, very crazymanwritingadiary style. The piece reads all over the place, so much confusion, but in a good way. It's very hard to pinpoint exactly what is going on here, and I THINK (?) that's what you were going for? Like I said: very haunting, very weird. It really made me think, and I don't really have anything bad to say about it. The writing was perfect for this kind of story, I think the repetitions and broken lines were well placed. I especially liked the youarenotyou part during the bit about having no spacebar. In the end, I think this was a very haunting piece that really made me think about what was going on. Hoyt Williams: 12 Dead At Fort Hood. I think your writing is a bit hit and miss because the stream of conscious narrative can be taxing at times. It's especially true when you divulge into a community that is somewhat more used to the "something is happening" way of rping (storytelling) rather than the ramblings you had going on here. But I think for people to not look beyond that are only hurting themselves, because this piece is filled with brilliancy. I particularly liked the line about the bald eagle possibly dying of suicide. I thought it was a very powerful image - a result of America's increasing moral decay and economic recession. There were many bits of brilliant lines scattered throughout: the symbolism of the number 12, the burning bush, the idealism of heaven vs earth. I had this pegged as a solid piece based on the social commentary alone. But the actual story really had me in a vice grip. I found myself just as fooled as Hoyt was when Jesus revealed himself, and found myself anxious to await what's next. I think the both bar scenes were very well done, made me smile, and I'm waiting to see what happenes next in both terms: Jesus' whereabouts and how the GTT committee is going to catch up to Hoyt and what this means for him. Solid work here. Decision Another fucking tough decision. This time I'm trying to decide between two very out-of-the-box pieces and trying to decide which one I liked more. They both have their strengths and weaknesses. Hoyt has brilliant commentary and a really exciting story that sometimes seems derailed by the rambling style of a stream of conscious narrative. Drake has a very frightening and haunting piece that constantly kept teasing me. It made me think but it left me without closure, and I think that's where the decision here likes. Drake wrote a great piece that was haunting and eerie, but looking back at Hoyt's work I found an engrossing tale giftwrapped in brilliant commentary. Drake's piece made me think, but Hoyt's piece made me want to read more. Hoyt Williams PETE'S TIEBREAKER Drake Coleman CRAIG Hoyt Williams -- Craig owes you two feedback. Please ask for it.
