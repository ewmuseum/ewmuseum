Round 4: Mrs S. Bracket results

31 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
CHANDLER TSONDA V SALAOK V JORGE SAMUELSSON Judge 1 Salaok A time to be killed In a lot of ways, I really feel like the style of this piece really takes away from the content of it. The tense is continually shifting from present to past, which provides for an extremely disorienting read. Additionally, a few other stylistic choices, like having two characters talk in the same paragraph without delineating which is which make this piece more difficult to read than it really should be. I also note a distinct tendency to go over and over things, to sort of circle around the point for longer than is necessary. The 'match relevance' portion of the piece seems extremely forced to me, given what comes after it; the story's content makes it clear that it really wasn't all that important to have the section included. Overall, the ending was much stronger than the beginning, but by that point, it's not much holding my interest anymore. Father and Son, not the same This RP suffers from some of the same stylistic problems as the first one does, but I feel like the content is a little better written. On the one hand, I like this better, and think it was better executed, but on the other, I also feel like it had more instances of sloppy style (misspellings, incorrect word choice, and the like). It is, though, better than the first, and there's not a whole lot that I can say about it other than that- it held my attention. Jorge Samuelsson The world as a wheel! Despite the fact that this style of RP generally isn't in vogue, I don't suppose that I found anything to really dislike about the content. It's a bit clumsily written, but some of the metaphors are rather interesting, and generally, I would think that trash talk works quite well with a Viking as your character. That said, it's iffy whether I would pick this to advance- though I liked it, the writing is a bit clumsy, and I think that despite it being shorter than many of the other pieces I've seen in GTT5 thus far, it probably could have ended earlier than it did because it lost focus towards the end. There are some funny moments, but overall, it just seemed to drift, without focus. Training for destiny! I like the general idea of the prologue... the only problem being that the execution of it leaves a little bit to be desired. I think it's important to be close to a character like this, just because a modern-day Viking is a bit of a difficult concept to grasp. When I read the sudden insertion of an "I" narrator into this portion, I'm kind of like "who the hell is that?!".. which is naturally distracting from reading. It unfortunately also carries through to part two. I feel like this has so much potential, which I can see in the way that the scene is written, which I actually enjoyed very much. It's just that attempts to trash talk characters who don't really deal in trash-talking can often prove to be incredibly difficult. Plus, for some reason, there's no differentiation of punctuation- no question marks, no exclamation points... it leads to a very 'flat' read. Chandler Tsonda Goodnight, Prince I'm not at all familiar with Tsonda, but I'm extremely impressed by this piece. I feel like it was a little slow at the beginning- but that was only the beginning. The smaller segments of writing made it very easy to read all the way through without losing focus or getting bored. In addition to that, the skill with which the RP was executed made it impossible to not pay attention to it all the way through, especially by the end of it, which I really, really liked. I suppose I can't really find anything 'critical' to say about it... overall enjoyable, not so long that it was painful, and definitely very powerful in its symbolism. 1- Chandler Tsonda 2- Salaok 3- Jorge Samuelsson Judge 2 Tsonda: I really liked this, the past coming consistently back to haunt Tsonda. If there had to be one problem I had with this piece it's that there were too many different scenes going on at once. Two parallel stories was fine but once the third was introduced it kinda cluttered the head and muddled the point. Still an excellent piece. Jorge Samuelsson: I love this character, the potential there is great however I think these two RPs fell a bit flatter this round as you didn't really bring anything new to the table. There's many things that you could go into with the character but you're barely scratching the surface with him. Salaok: These two were interesting and if I had the time I'm sure the previous RPs you've had would fit in nicely into this. As I don't what I can see is a well written story which wasn't overly exciting to me (nothing really grabbed me when I read through it) but was well done nonetheless. 1st - Chandler Tsonda 2nd - Salaok 3rd - Jorge Samuelsson RESULT Smallest score = greatest value Chandler Tsonda - 2 points *match winner Salaok - 4 points Jorge Samuelsson - 6 points Chandler Tsonda and Salaok advance to the Mrs S. Bracket final. Will collects the bounty on Angelo Deville. *whistles* Brackets
