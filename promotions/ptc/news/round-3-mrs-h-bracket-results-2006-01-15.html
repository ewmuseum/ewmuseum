Round 3: Mrs H. Bracket Results

15 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
EVAN "DAMAGE" JACKSON V JAYCE BRADLEY Judge 1 Just Another Night: Stalking A New Prey: There's one thing that makes it hard for series ideas to work in tournaments and that's how to make everything be able to work on its own. You didn't totally achieve this with this piece as while it had its moments, I felt that I was missing information that I needed to go back to other RPs to glean. Because of this the RP falls a slight bit. Just Another Night: The Father/Son Talk: Again this had the same problem as the first as some of the information that was missing required the rest of the series. It's very hard to input in knowledge like that without it being obvious but it is kind of necessary for a tournament I feel. Other than these were two very well written pieces and are some damn fine work. Parallels: I'm liking this, at first I hadn't a clue where it was going but it was nicely rewarded by the end. this was well written, well thought out and a nice piece that had a lot of purpose put into it. If there could be any improvement, a little more of Jackson the character being involved would've helped this more. Favourite: Again another analogy style RP and again very good, this time with a bit more indirect mentioning of Jackson (it felt like he was the man invovled). This was again good and was well written. Overall this is a hard choice as both guys are very good at their writing and it shows. Bradley's story sounds very intriguing and one that I'm sure he's been progressing nicely along in the last couple of rounds. Jackson instead is a more live for the moment kind of style, tackling the tournament in a more traditional sense. The breaker for me is relevance. While Bradley's pieces were very good, they fell in the respect that they relied a little too much on people having read the previous installments. Jackson's on the other hand were spot on and were solely concerned with the task at hand. Pick: Evan 'Damage' Jackson Judge 2 Bradley moves on. The two stories were comparable, but, I think Bradley had the slightly better execution. Pick: Jayce Bradley Judge 3 Jayce Bradley: Throwing honesty in to begin with, this is the first JB roleplay I’ve ever read. And, I have to admit, I was a little nervy about joining events seemingly mid-series. This has tended to be a problem in tourneys before, where some expect a judge to go back and read all the other roleplays so they understand what's going on. Guess what? I don't do that =) Thus, I was pleasantly surprised that as the 1st roleplay went on, it did a good job of filling me in on that I'd missed, without doing the old trick of starting every RP with a recap of what happened beforehand. It really as a tick in the 'good' box that it kinda intrigued me a little more NOT having read in full detail everything that went before, making it both a good read as a stand-alone, as well as an important part of a series. I have to admit for being one of the people who does jump aboard the relevance train for PTC tourneys so, naturally, to begin with I was waiting for things to be pulled back to a more wrestler centred theme. No need for "i will beet u", but just some part where I'd know someone was a wrestler... and I got it. Thank you! I have no problems with someone telling a story surrounding a wrestling character that somewhat detaches, and having all the deeds done for, supposedly, Jayce's benefit helped draw all the threads of the roleplay together. The writing impressed me, also, which probably shouldn't have surprised me from a GCW'er. Both the dialogue and narrative sections were presented very well, especially the pacing of the dialogue when it needed to be focussed on. The info provided on the various characters was just about spot on for the most part, especially the reporter. That, to me, is a great example of how to bring in a supporting cast member for a short appearance, and not be either too vague, or burying readers with too much fluff. And, as rare as this may be for a GTT judge to say, I could have done without the extra wrestling/tourney related bit at the end. If I had to pick a least favourite part of the 2 pieces, it was the Joe Millar mistaken identity bit. Don't always feel the need to get a blunt tourney reference in if you think you have a strong story revolving your wrestler already on the table. Very good job. Evan "Damage" Jackson: Haha. Hahahahahaha. HAHAHAHAHAHA. I was SO getting ready to rip the 1st RP, but, yeah, you kinda won me with it. A Fischerspooner drop will do that well :-P Usually, show me an RP with no wrestler in sight and 'symbolism' stamped over it, I'll throw it right back, but it played out well. The second time... wasn't really there for me. Parallels would have acted as a great interlude RP, before firing off something meatier. But in the end, it was followed up by something of a similar, symbolism-driven theme, which was a weaker comparison than the Jenna/Sarah thing. It was still pretty well written, a few commas short as with the 1st RP, but despite the language and flow, it's premise was still going to leave it a bit flat. In summary, the 1st roleplay was a nice, refreshing it. Kinda like a little scoop of lemon sorbet between courses to cleanse the palette, but needed to be followed up by something a lot meatier and filling. Pick: Jayce Bradley. Jayce Bradley d Evan "Damage" Jackson 2 votes to 1 PADDY O'SHEA V DOREEN PEPPERPOT Judge 1 Paddy O'Shea (Don't Hand Me Over: Part 1) Paddy you sure do have a unique style. That roleplay didn't make me laugh though. On a positive I very rarely laugh at something that is written. Although I feel it lets you down a little. Lines like 'Tune in next time folks' and 'Let's go old school, let's go geocities' took a lot away from the roleplay. The tone of the roleplay seemed to me to be serious and those lines may usually have been your style (I haven't read any of work your before so I'm not familiar with it) but they just didn't seem to fit in this roleplay. However, the roleplay flowed brilliantly, the action didn't let up, the suspense was nicely done although a little spoon-fed towards the end. I found myself dying to read your next roleplay. The poem at the beginning was a nice touch, it didn't seem like an added extra with no relevence and I felt it fitted in well with the rest of the roleplay. I do believe it was a little one sided, but, I don't see why that should take anything anyway from the roleplay. It went with the character and Paddy's experiences and helped me actually feel for Paddy and actually care about him being framed for crimes he didn't commit. The questions towards the end of the roleplay were nice, but like I said before I feel you're spoon-feeding the reader a little too much. Give me a little bit more to think about next time, it's a hard balance to master, how much to actually spell out in black and white and whether to even have a narrative adding some extra details that may not have been obvious otherwise. Don't worry about that too much though, the roleplay did its job and got me wanting more and feeling for the character. Though it wasn't all sweetness, some of the points about Pepperpot seemed to be chucked in just for the hell of it to add some relevenece to the IRA story. If you were about to face the worst experience in your entire life, would you really be worried about a wrestling match? I'm not so sure. But still, only a few small negatives from a rather good roleplay. Doreen Pepperpot (Heroes and Villains) This started slow, very slow, I wondered if there was a point coming or if it was going to just be filler till the end. Alas I see a light at the end of the tunnell. About halfway through we get to the good stuff. Now, it's debatable whether or not the first half of the roleplay was needed to be as long as it was e.g. could the backstory have been told in a shorter space? Yes And it would have left some more room for a story to take place. I didn't feel that any of the backstory really gave me as big an insight into the character as I would have liked. I personally have never read a Doreen Pepperpot roleplay before. All that I took away from the first half of the roleplay was she was a seventeen year old girl with a weight problem. The history on her family was nice, but I found myself not caring. But, I'll dismiss some of that negativity as I'm not familiar with the character and it was a good attempt at letting a new reader in and trying to ease them into Pepperpot's world. Now when we got onto the actual content, 'Coach' having a rummage in the garage, I wondered where this was going. Again I'm feeling a little lost, though it's nice to see Doreen cares about him, it adds a little warmth to the relationship. owever, the style drops a little and we're led into some forced dialogue. It's cool that you've tried to just write a repsonse to O'Shea's work, but, I don't think it came off well. The entire roleplay was building up to this, yet, it seems so contrived. I don't think I've ever had a conversation like that with anybody. Even taking into account Coach has practically lost his marbles, apparently he's speaking normally at this point... I also think that it doesn't develop Doreen's character at all, it just helps Paddy along with his story. It seems to give more weight to his character than anything. Coach's last line, left me feeling 'So what?' Maybe if it'd been built up a little more then I could have empathised with him. But I felt as though, like most of this roleplay, it was just an afterthought. Overall, it's a solid effort, but for me it just didn't flow. The theme of being a winner seemed to just be an afterthought towards the end. I can't say I really enjoyed it, because for me it didn't do much at all. However, this could be saved by a good second roleplay, if certain questions in my mind are answered it could work brilliantly. But right now I'm not feeling it. Paddy O'Shea (Don't Hand Me Over: Part 2) Straight away I'm feeling immersed in the story again. Paddy, your writing style may not be the standard novelistic approach but it certainly tells a story damn well. I'm generally not a fan of writing that doesn't conform to rules but yours is exceptional. It's been a long time since I've felt this involved in somebody elses work. I was worried that after you first roleplay you'd have trouble keeping my attention but you've captivated it no problem. I notice the wrestling reference towards the beginning intertwined within the story, using comparisons from both to draw them together, a much better way of adding relevence to Paddy's wrestling career and IRA career. Ouch. Paddy, put the gun down and please don't shoot yourself in the foot again. Everything was going so well, then the fabled 'PTC Lawyer' comes in to save the day. Please, as if a PTC contract has any jurisdiction over the law. If the people interrogating Paddy were as bad as described they surely wouldn't have even let a PTC lawyer in nevermind order them around and make them honour a contract. If somebody from the WWE got arrested they may get bail, but I'm not even sure these guys you're referring to would deal with 'bails' they seemed so merciless in your last roleplay. Now they seem pathetic and weak, being beaten by a piece of paper. To me it's cost the roleplay some of it's crediability. The whole roleplay didn't flow as good as the first, however I liked the 'Did You Know' bits, they added something a little different again. It was nice to actually see a little emotion from Paddy and see how he felt to a PTC pencil pusher forcing his way in and accusing him of a being a terrorist. Him directly saying it was a tad unrealistic, he could have hinted at it through a motion maybe or a look, but still it got across the point ok. Overall, I don't see this as the well rounded work that his first was. It continued the story well and I do want to see more. But before reading Pepperpot's second this is still an open contest. If Paddy had replicated his first roleplay, I could have called this now. Doreen Pepperpot (Heroes and Villains) This roleplay led on well from the first and made me smile a little as it cut straight into a good story. I liked the way Coach was portrayed in this roleplay, cast out dejected a shell of a man. The little backstory explaining how he'd killed his son was good and this time the backstory seemed to make me care. I cared that Pepperpot felt so bad all the time. I actually cared that everything seemed to be her fault. I cared that she was hurting Coach. Towards the end though, I just wasn't sure. I started to feel detatched as the roleplay went on, it started so well with just Pepperpot and Coach but then when her mother came back everything seemed so forced again. Why would her sister know how she felt? She isn't pyschic I'm sure, it's one thing knowing how to press somebodies buttons, but to know EXACTLY what somebodies going to is suggesting a little too much. This roleplay seemed to flow a lot better than the first, it read very well and I actually enjoyed it. I got a lot more from the second than I did the first and it really has made me reconsider the pick I'd typed in pencil. Overall:: Both of you should be proud, you've both made this quite a hard decision for me, though one of you has slightly outshone the other. I felt more involved in Paddy's story and I actually felt for his character. Paddy's second roleplay let him down a little and Doreen's first let her down a little, although I can see after reading the second why it was written how it was. I really did have to have a good think about this and re-read each roleplay a couple of times. However, I didn't change the pick I'd thought of when reading the roleplays through the first time. Paddy's story really did show more depth and maybe because it was an issue that's very close to his own heart that he wrote about it so well and portrayed his character so well. Both writers impressed me as I'd read nothing by them before and I can see how they've got this far. Pick: Paddy O'Shea Judge 2 O'Shea: Don't hand me over Part one First let me say, this rp rode a very fine line. The political elements and the painted events/activities of the Brits vs the Irish is on the verge of crossing a line that could very well cause a huge debate. At the same time, the writing grabbed you by your shirt and dragged you in solidly. Despite the controversial subject it was a well executed text. The point of view being Paddy's conscience was a unique twist in the fact it wasn't the cliche split personality story. It was just a story told from an inner point of view...from outside. I could sense the outrage and fear felt throughout the telling. Bonus points when someone can make ~me~ start feeling something too..even if I know nothing about it. Couple of spelling mistakes, maybe a word or two missed, but nothing that detracted from the tale as a whole. O'Shea: Don't hand me over Part two Like the first one this one rode a fine line and, for me, it crossed it about halfway through. Writing a story based on actual events ( Yes I know of some those mentioned) can be a good thing, but taking it to the point where one feels like it is an attack on them ( ie the reader) or a forced lesson really takes away from the text all together. A strong belief is a valuable and admirable thing but when that belief begins to look like fanaticism it becomes difficult to swallow. The story was not to my liking on a personal basis, however, as a writer I have to say it was well done. There were still a few mistakes in spelling and grammar, but over all well written. I felt more like I was reading a political history lesson from a 'biased' point of view than a story about Paddy O'Shea in the end. ( Note I am not calling you biased, merely stating the general sense of feeling from the words themselves.) Pepperpot: Heroes and Villains I have to say when I read this I really liked the concept of an obeste woman wrestling. When I saw she was still a teenager in school I was rather..eh about that. Still, it certainly worked in this story. I liked the struggle she had with herself and inadverently with 'Coach'. It shed an almost picture perfect light on what pressure can do to a teenager. The psyche of 'Coach' was a rather nice touch and well executed. I fumbled over a few words and actually had to look up the meaning ( kudos to you for that). This is a catch 22. It is good, because I learned something new. It is bad because I kept losing my place. All in all the story as a whole was fluid, and did a good job captivating the audience. Pepperpot: Heroes and Villains Part 2 Wow..just...wow. We have more teenage angst/stress, pure disfunctional family, troubled thoughts, and best of all ....blackmail! I am loving it..all of it. This one was a much smoother read ( ie ..no dictionary trips) and as thus, more enjoyable than the first. When I finished reading this piece I was left thinking...how much more can she take before she just breaks? I can not recall any errors during my reading which stands well for you. I can only suggest that whatever you did here...keep it up. I am looking forward to seeing more of your stuff in the future. Pick: Pepperpot over O'Shea At first I thought O'Shea would be difficult to beat. His stories were controversial but well written and that usually equals gold. But, then I read Doreen's and upon finishing the second one was strongly in favor for this handler. O'Shea is a fantastic storyteller, but I think there was a bit too much controversy there with the sense of a forced lesson on IRA. Pepperpot just managed to make me get a better view into the character's head and heart...Pepperpot takes this, but O'Shea has nothing to be ashamed of.
