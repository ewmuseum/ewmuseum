"EMBER" V JASON SNOW Judge 1 EMBER, PROS. +interesting dynamic and different story of a jekyll and hyde manner. +seemingly thorough physical explanations for extraordinary circumstances, and not just voodoo magic. +while dragging, the instances between the Shaman and the officers were more than entertaining and carried the plot along. +loved the scene with Ember's mask removed, being face to face with his enemy; himself. +great portrayal of internal struggle, woven with a great mystery. SNOW, PROS. +highly ambitious storytelling that surprisingly does not get left in the dust. +clean and very thoughtful writing. every scene, while taken from a somewhat omniscient narrator, seems to be unique with the subjected character to the section's context. +that scene in the last RP, between Raven and Jason, god damn. that is some writing. +engrossing writing, despite how long it is. EMBER, CONS. +i felt you were over-writing a bit, and a few of the scenes reaked of melodrama. +the final doctor and the way he regarded the Shaman and how sarcastic/skeptical he was, just was unbelievable. +a few moments of key characterization moments felt cliche, such as Johnston, the young fed, who seemed so brash and quick to outburst at times. +it honestly reminded me of New X-Men and Cassandra Nova, but that's probably not your fault as comic books have probably done every evil-twin story that can ever possibly be conceived. +a lot of repetative phrases and words, while i could tell some where part of the device of the writing, it honestly got annoying at parts. +the ending between the struggle was honestly a bit lame for my tastes. sorry, but i felt very little pay-off in the end. SNOW, CONS. +like Ember's, a lot of repetitive phrases and words. +the build. like Ember's, i felt it took a lot of build and at times it seemed like a rather weak climactic point. luckily, you drew away more from the deaths of the two hits Snow was forced into, and more into the fact that he finally thinks he's out (after staining his soul, mind you), but the truth is he's apparently getting started. nice save. +some points with Alyssa still feel open, but that may be purposeful. just to leave it with Snow saying at Raven's grave "she'll never be you" is somewhat of a cop-out, no matter how torn up over Raven Jason may be. +little focus on necessary details and overt focus on minute details. i understand the mini-struggle with Emily's capture and the irony of holding out for her first kiss only to (almost) receive the most unwanted one imaginable, but this Andy character seemed kind of cheap and cliche. typical mob soldier who is too horny to listen to the boss' orders not to touch the girl? seen it before. +little focus on neccesary details, like i said before. i'll be more specific, but throughout these three pieces, characters like Hazen and Jamie who seemed so important to Jason Snoe's family aspect after you did such a well job of building it up. in the description, you do a great job of characterizing Jamie but i hardly see that in the narrative. not a huge flaw, though, as the rest of the characters were superbly written. all in all, a tough decision. but honestly, i don't have to mull it over for hours. Jason Snow's writing won me over and i can honestly admit, after reading this finale, that i wish i'd have read from the beginning. and from the beginning, i mean the Tank. congratulations to both of you, as you are no slouches on the writing front. it's a shame that only one can be the winner, but as such, i feel that Dave has really put forth the deserving pieces, and he receives my vote. Pick: Jason Snow Judge 2 Ember: Good story but it never really captivated me the way I know you, as a writer, can. There were points that I thought dragged on and moments that I felt you could have done more. I think the shorter rps, plus the influx of so many characters really watered down some of the story. The area about Ember's identity being that of Jay Phoenix, who is one of my all-time favorite characters, was well done. Overall I felt it was an rp series that was strong in some areas but weak also in others. At the level you're at, brilliant rp'er in the GTT finals, that rp may not be enough. Jason Snow: You...can write. That's all I can say. You...can fucking write. I read every word of the three finals rps, went through two thirds of Smirnoff Twisted Orange in the process (had nothing to do with you...I just felt the need, you know how it goes). You had me captivated for almost the whole series. The rape scene and the prison dialogue was not something I enjoyed but again, you completely made the moment feel genuine and in writing that's really the goal I think. Your use of words is something that I absolutely love. Loved the Emily, Nicholas and especially Jason characters. I think the only thing that hurt you is that you were backed into a 3-rp series. Had it been 12 I think you put out the greatest rp work I've seen in a long time, but with 3 it inherently made it drag on at points. I judged an earlier series of yours for this and told you that I felt that you'd reach the finals and then your style of writing was going to kill your chances. I was wrong. Phenomenal job, regardless of the actual vote...you're the GTT Champion in the mind of this judge. Pick: Jason Snow Judge 3 Just want to note before I start that I have not been following either story in GTT6, so I am basically coming from a perspective of who wrote better in this particular round. I won't be concentrating too hard on details that have probably been explained in previous pieces; so I'm basically taking into consideration that there may be a few things I read and don't understand that have been explained other places. Ember The ballad of two- part nine This first scene took me a bit to get into. I wasn't at all certain I was going to be able to do it, in fact, because it seemed like I didn't have the understanding of the story to do so- thankfully, though, you maneuvered in a lot of little details that fixed that readily. The first scene was made for me by the little details that made it so evocative. If the first scene is evocative, however, the second scene is even more so. It's *very* impressive in the psychological depth it gives the character, and it's intriguing as all hell to read and not know what's going to happen here, while at the same time, this tension is very real and tangible. As for the third part, again, I expected less than I actually got out of it- I was even expecting something of the supernatural variety, actually, and what I got was something a lot more unexpected- and a lot more brilliant as far as swerving the reader goes. As a whole, this piece packs an awful lot of punch. It doesn't seem like it would, but as you dig deeper into it, it's got a lot in its compactness. Overall, this is a good start for me, as it introduces me to the story with a powerful hook- even if I'm coming in on the end of it, it's still easy to get involved and interested in how it will play out... now, it's just seeing how the payoff itself is executed. The ballad of two- part ten The first scene picks up nicely from the first piece. I like that for its sense of thoroughness, and its ability to keep the reader absorbed in the story without making a big-time break in the line of it. Writing wise, it's on top of its game, going where it needs to go, and very skilfully cut off at points that will keep the readers interest rather than frustrating him or her. The scene with Ember in the hospital is interesting for the distance that it keeps from Ember himself. Keeping in mind that I really don't know a lot about the character, I enjoy having Sinclair talk for him, as it keeps a definite sense of mystery behind both the character, and what's going on here. The story unfolds at a pace that keeps me reading for more. I can't emphasize how skilfully I think the unraveling is done here, with the patience of someone who is *very* professional at this. The third scene, I have to admit that I found the interjection of the specialist a little heavy-handed. All the character's shouting in my opinion took a little bit away from the revelation of an intensely powerful story of Jay's brother. The cliffhanger on this one is *very* well done, as the rest of this series of three has been thus far. As a whole, I'm not sure I believe this piece is as strong as the first one, but the ending is certainly more compelling. Then again, it's perfectly logical to have the middle piece be a little bit more digressive, since the first piece's job is to hook, and the third piece's job is to powerfully conclude, it follows that the middle piece's job is to fill in the holes. Now moving on... The ballad of two- part eleven The epigraph on this piece is powerful- people always tend to use these things without really considering what they will have to live up to in using them, and I think that other writers often fall short of the mark. However, this has been an ambitious trio from the beginning, both in material and in payoff, and I think that the possibility is here for this to blow my mind- as always. This first scene is done with, I think, exceptional verisimilitude. A lot of people will throw around this kind of story and have the bystanders believe it unquestioningly. This at the same time is more realistic, and also leaves a lot more for you to work in in this last piece. The memory is very descriptive and easy to get into. I'm raising my eyebrows at the conclusion of this scene, if only because it seems almost mind-boggling to believe that the two beings existed on the same plane from a young age. If they in fact did exist on the same plane, as I presume you are about to explain. The third scene ends with a swerve that I definitely didn't expect- one that leaves me questioning a lot about what all this revelation has been for if it's not for the purpose of finding Ember/Phoenix. That, though, is why half of this roleplay is left, I imagine. I didn't comment on the rest of the scenes individually, because I actually didn't want to stop and break the rhythm of my reading in the midst of doing so. The playout of this story was beautiful, and actually had me holding my breath in places. The one thing I might have complained about is the way that everything was neatly tied up into a happy ending- people like to read that in stories, it seems, but it almost blows what you're going for as far as a real-life standpoint goes. However, with the ending of the tale there's something else to chew on- it's indeterminate, we're left to make our own conclusions as to how all these threads will tie together again later in the lives of the characters... and I like that more than I like just about anything else I've read in this trio. Jason Snow Track 9: "Cat's in the Cradle" Alrighty... coming here into my first reading of any Jason Snow piece ever, and I have to say that aside from length, I really don't know what to expect. It's all about the journey though, right? Here we go... First of all, I read the piece as a whole instead of stopping scene to scene as I did with Phoenix's, just in the interest of getting this judging to the proper authorities in a timely manner, although I would have liked the opportunity to comment more in-depth. Overall, the story here is not as tight as Phoenix's is- but it's also got more to say. What did me the most favors was separating the scenes out as they were so that I could easily associate shifts in direction without having to spend a lot of time reorienting myself. In a story this large, that's decidedly the best way to handle things. The writing style is good, the narrator's interjections humorous, and overall, at the very least, the prose isn't hard to get into. I'm not quite sure what I think of the story yet, because I just don't find it terribly engrossing- interesting, definitely, but I'm not quite wrapped up in it. That's not to say that I won't be, just to say that at the moment, I'm not really sure what the story's doing for me. I really wish that I had more to offer for this first piece, but there's just this at the moment- fleshing out the story more will, I suspect, give me more to say. Track 10: "The Lost Art of Keeping a Secret" I'm already liking this second piece better than I liked the first. Everything before the song's lyrics, for me, fits together as a seamless prelude in a way that's both stylistic and an absolutely necessary introduction to the story, I think. I'm not sure I can explain how, but the formatting makes the content a little easier to digest this time around. I'm eager to see how it will play out from here. In the second scene, I feel like I'm finally getting what I'm looking for- the conversation between Emily and Jason has this very gritty real life feel to it that makes it, in my opinion, more effective writing than the rest of it, because I feel more real emotion here than I have thus far. More of the family stuff later on in the piece- I have to say this really is the most compelling part of the story for me. On one hand, there's the hardened criminal, and on the other, this very compelling family drama as he fights to become something other than that. The lie to Emily sticks out to me- I'm very invested in how this plays out emotionally- for me, I think that's the secret to the hook, is that this isn't 'just another mob story,' because you've endeavored very hard to put a human face on your character. I'm honestly impressed with the ending of it as well- because you pulled out something that I decidedly didn't expect in a story that's already been *full* of a lot of twists and turns. A great hook to end this second piece with. Track 11: "Right Next Door to Hell" The break in time kind of throws me a little bit. Then again, it would have been pointless to see the confession played out, since the reader already knows it. However, diving right back into things with Alyssa is an interesting way to play this out; since it's the first part that continues the part of the story that's solely Jason, it's interesting that you made the choice to show a domestic relationship- which in turn shows the strength of his own choice to get away from the 'family life' of another kind. I also find it very interesting that he made the decision almost based on the things that Emily said to him- again turning inwards towards his family. The piece where Dante and crew capture him is more powerful than I would have expected it to be, because there are places where Jason's own memories play into this that make certain lines particularly poignant (like this: "Absurdly, he remembered how many times Raven had scolded him for parking there rather than in the garage. But she wasn’t here to scold him now. He tasted blood on his lips."). The scene with Raven is also very poignant and powerful, especially the last bits of dialogue about love- I think it's possibly the best insight into Snow psychologically that I've seen for the entirety of these three pieces thus far. By the mid-point or so of the piece, it's reading very fast- I especially like the way that the confession is picked up and written in dialogue only, so that there's a very real sense of necessity, and driving rhythm to the final half of this RP. One place that I actually lose the pathos is when Nicholas is talking to Jason and forcing his hand- it's very hard for me to actually believe in any sort of remorse from a man who's ordering Jason to go kill his friend. Not that it's necessarily supposed to be believable remorse, the duality just doesn't work for me. I have to say that the ending surprised me as well- it wasn't your typical action movie good guys win ending, and for that, I have to applaud you for having the guts to write it when a lot of other writers, in order to 'keep their readers happy' would have taken the easier way out. I'm not entirely certain how I feel about the ending of the piece, being that it almost seems like it doesn't fit with the dark tone of the way the rest of the story winds up. Altogether, this thing is a mammoth- it's hard to come up with something to say about this one piece as a whole, because there's just so much in there. Pick: Ember's pieces, taken as a whole, represent a very powerful story being played out with an expertise that's quite suitable for the finals of a tournament like this. There was really no room for mistake, especially being up against an opponent that is as difficult as Snow is, and Jay, you really didn't make any. The story is tight, gripping, and ends without beating you over the head with a point- all the marks of good writing in my book. And not just good writing, but a very beautifully compelling story. You took a lot of turns that I didn't expect you to take, and managed to squeeze all of that into a rollercoaster ride of three RP's. Jason Snow's pieces, as a whole, are, first of all, an incredibly voluminous piece of work, with an awful lot of stuff stuck in there to deal with- side stories, the basic dichotomy of Mafia Family vs. Real Family, action-movie type scenes, and scenes that played heavily on realism and emotion. It's pretty much an epic journey in just these three pieces, without having seen the rest of it in action. Both writers here have done an absolutely outstanding job, and are making my job as a judge very, very difficult. I knew pretty much from the outset that I should have expected that, however. There's no room to make a misstep in your GTT finals writing, and neither writer did so. That brings a choice essentially down to personal preference, and which writer I feel was more effective in carrying his story. Ember's story, as a whole, was much more concise, and felt more emotionally 'full' to me, if that makes any kind of sense. It also showed what I feel is a mastery of pacing. I was able to connect with it very easily. Meanwhile, Snow showed a lot of skill with handling a very complex story that had a lot of elements to tie together, and that was done almost seamlessly well- a sign of very intelligent writing. In the end, and with a deep breath, I'm going to choose Ember. This may come down, for me, to personal preference about which story I liked better, but that's about the only way I can make a choice when skill doesn't make that choice obvious. Both writers did an excellent job, but there were places where I just got bogged down in Snow's story, or places where the emotion derailed. Ember kept me hooked the entire time, and in addition to hooked, very engaged. Thank you to both for your efforts throughout this tournament. Pick: Ember Judge 4 Jason Snow Track 9: Cat's in the Cradle One minor typographical error at the beginning in the transcription of the lyrics. All it was was a place where a space should have been after a period, just a small note. The lyrics are nice, but I've never been a fan of a full song in the opening of an RP. Between that and a lot of excessive hard returns during the RP, kind of gave it a feel of filler. However, the RP content itself was a good read. I wasn't very fond of finding the words, "fag" and "******" in the rp more than once. I do however understand where it fit with the characters using them, but I thought it could have been more tasteful. I also felt the rape scene could have been a little more tasteful, though it was well written it was still rather pornographic. The twists and turns through out the story are interesting, and though it took me awhile to warm up to it, I kind of like the way it is broke up amongst each character and their individual story. Overall a pretty solid piece, nothing I'd want to read again, but it was a good read. Track 10: The Lost Art of Keeping a Secret The opening was interesting, but the atmosphere just didn't feel right. Anthony goes from arrogant, powerful and in control to no more the two paragraphs later a weakened person. It just didn't fit, and didn't feel right. Once again the use of the word "******" I found a little out of place an unneccessary. The dinner sequence felt a little boring, and I was hoping for something more interesting. The arrival of Johnny Silver defintely gave a break from the mundane. Only a few minor technical errors throughout the piece. One that I find often is "trey" where it should be "tray". I found the explanation of how he moved to be too redundant, you find it non stop through out all of the RPs. Not trying to sound like an ass, but most people get the point the first time it is mentioned, so mentioning it over and over again gets to be a little irritating, and boring. The flashback, though well written I didn't care for. It did help explain what was going through Jason's mind... but it took way too long to do it. The biggest save to this RP, was the turn at the end of the FBI Agent. I kind of saw it coming, but didn't expect it to come out already. Overall decent piece, but lacked in a lot of areas. Too many areas felt boring and drawn out. If an RP is going to be 30 pages, it's important to make sure it's 30 good pages. Track 11: Right Next Door to Hell I appreciated the OOC note at the beginning, but after already reading 60 pages, I wasn't going to stop short now. The intro brought you right in, and was probably the best beginning to any of his final round RPs. My only problem in the beginning is with such a high excitement opening, then going into Alyssa's part where she can't figure out what to buy for Jason, and one huge noticeable error that just throws off the entire sentence. The little vision of Raven was heart wrenching, but really made it hard to keep track of who was saying what. I could assume, but in truth I'm not one hundred percent sure. Also in the scene where Emily went to use the bathroom, a small error where it should have been Andy, Anthony was mentioned instead. I got confused and thought I was wrong about it, so read through those paragaphs a few times to be sure. Small things like that were what I found throughout the RP, nothing to major. This RP took me for a loop, the best Jason Snow RP of the finals. A few minor inconsistencies, like when Anthony tells Jeremiah it was a boy, but when the deal went down Jeremiah wanted to know the kid's name. The story played out much as I had expected, with a twist in the end. I loved the adition of the epilogue, and I think that this was an amazing conclusion to a terrific series. Quick Statistical Summary of all three RPs combined 34 Pages 14,970 Words Conclusion Jason had a beautifully mastered story right from the beginning, and each piece was well written, with a good solid ending. Ember, had a very psychologically suspenseful series of RPs. I thoroughly enjoyed the twisted ideas of the writer, and enjoyed the use of dual fetuses. Like I said previously, I loved that episode of House. In the long run however, Ember accomplished the same sensations as Jason Snow in a shorter amount of time. I feel Ember did a better job of getting his points across, and found his piece the most engaging, as after each RP I wanted to read the next. This is no discredit to Jason Snow, as his RPs were very well written as well, but I found myself enjoying Ember's RPs a lot more, and I am looking forward to see what happens to the character after GTT6.
