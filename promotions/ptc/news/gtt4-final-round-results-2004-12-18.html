GTT4: Final Round Results!

18 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Exceptional Dave Hurst vs Dr. Curiosity vs Craig Goat Third place via no-show, Craig Goat. Judging Comments Judge 1 Dr. Faustiocity. Chapter One: The Calling. We establish Doctor Curiosity post match with feelings of trepidation and fear. We address the fact that he is all by himself. The drawing of the lines on the floor was not the best described image. Even anticipating what the Doctor was drawing still made the act itself hard to follow in terms of solid visual impressions. We meet Colette’s debt collectors. We’re shown that they don’t mind stooping to murder over a game of cards, let alone a great deal of money. The scarred boss, Frank, has a nice melodramatic feel, what with the image and the laughter. A good cover with the wretched latin. Occultistically, this is classic MacGregor Mathers performance. Score a point for authenticity. The presence and appearance of the Doctor’s demon is plausible. The steps were taken to make certain that the summoning didn’t appear “hokey”, and it was pulled off well. = Dr. Faustiocity. Chapter Two: Contracts and Calls. The Doctor slowly gaining confidence and applying his schtick is a nice gradual effect. The elephant man reference was a nice touch, I appreciated that one. The switching of points of view to put both characters in similar light could’ve been handled a little more smoothly, so we knew who we were “with” when the thoughts were arriving. Pairing them up, the demon and the doctor, to see who will bamboozle the other is a nice touch. We’re introduced to more of Frank’s goons, and we’re introduced to the idea of amateur wrestling as compared to professional wrestling. We return to the Doctor visiting his brother, who when in jail, might potentially have been at least safe from the Doctor Curiosity, is instead the center of a prison riot thanks to his brother. Poooooor Eegor. = Road to Triumph. Part One: Captivity Opening addresses the streak of victory, the point of view of the person who can’t believe it most of all, EDH. A nice touch. The first person perspective has a nice organic and natural point of view for this set. It feels good so far. The set up becomes clear, and it seems like Doctor Curiosity (or someone who looks like him, a fan perhaps?) is up to no good! Uh oh! And we have a kidnapping. Thank goodness the duck is free. I think. = Doctor Faustiocity. Chapter Three: The Squared Circle We get more fun as Curiosity tries to help Eegor but instead winds up hitting him. The Doctor and the demon have a discussion and a warning, with the demon playing on the doctor’s insecurities about his own ability. However, the doctor typically has a stealthy sense of cunning which could undercut this. We’ll see. Evil Frank and friends are en route to the curious lab, though I imagine that this was just to suggest we don’t forget about them, and maybe to highlight the amateur wrestler feeling that this was pretty strange. And the demon is aware of Frank’s arrival. = The Road to Triumph. Part Two: Megalomania. We address the fact that these are some kind of deranged Curiosity fan. Which oddly enough makes sense. The Nurse’s presense isn’t all that hot. A big ugly fat woman with boils, in a nurses uniform that uses a bullwhip as an instrument of torture. And this is the fanatic’s wife? Well, a lot has yet to be revealed regarding that, but the idea of this “relationship” kind of stretches the plausibility. Having a lunatic fan of the Doctor seems plausible, but a married lunatic to an ugly behemoth? Unless of course the boils are fake and she’s supposed to resemble eegor, who often tried hard to act the part of ugly deformed assistant. = The Road to Triumph. Part Three: Compassion. Ooops. We learn that Ducky is indeed imprisoned too. Now we have the point of view of the whip bearing nurse. And my worries earlier aren’t addressed to well. I’m not thrilled about the change. I guess ealier the point of view also switched to the duck. But that seemed more incidental. Now the stretch of plausibility goes down the potty as the Nurse gets turned on by whipping Dave who takes his abuse like a man. Bullwhips are not suitable means of torture. Pliars are. Whips are used by dominatrixes, who are making a show or event, staging abuse on men who get turned on by it. Doms being paid for their loving abuse, are typically not turned on by administering their services. It’s just work. While a person can be given a great deal of discomfort through being whipped, it’s just not authentic torture, to the point where it barely qualifies as abuse. Further, base rage means you punch somebody. The steady hand required to effectively use a bullwhip doesn’t quite match up with rage. A whip shot to the face… sure. And then the break down. Which believe it or not, is actually plausible. People who are forced into the uncomfortable place of power and responsibility over others can be overwhelmed Particularly lifestyler’s of the s&m community, experiencing a bout of depression common enough to be called “top drop”. If only the character’s construction and behavior up until this point was a little better, this would be so much better. Hey, stop looking at me like I shouldn’t know these things. Uberjudges need to know everything. The issue of someone revealing their love is kind of complicated, and it’s starting to look to me like this Nurse character is bearing the responsibility of moving the story along, at the risk of the character’s choices making sense. And she produces a gun. Well, that’s a little forced I guess, with regard to her keeping Dave around, but not too much considering that a gun might be necessary to force compliance from captives. But … oh well. = Doctor Faustiocity. Chapter Four: Crescendo Crescendo is such a nice word. It’s has a kind of beauty to it all by itself, just as a word, not to mention an impressive meaning. Good choice. The fact that the Doctor is fooled by the joke attempt to get let in, is a nice touch. The fact that respectable tough guys would use a remark like that isn’t so plausible, which is worth getting into. A lot happens in the Curious Universe that is ridiculous, but for all intents and purposes, we were led to believe that these thugs were very plausible in arrangement and purpose. Sure some were ridiculously stupid, but that’s not all that unusual. So I was expecting an authentic sort of attack on the Doc, where he’d response with the silly stuff. The joke was addressed which mitigates some of the complaints, with Frank recognizing that it was somewhat absurd. And I realize that we’ve taken up the first person point of view with regard to Anthony, a transition I’m not all that thrilled about, since Doc stuff tends to exclusively be a third person trip. And it seems kind of strange for ten men to go on a trip for just one guy. Frank, Anthony and eight other guys. I hope they were taking multiple cars. Superpowerful Doctor Curiosity is a lot of fun. Using superstrength to haul an anvil around as opposed to just fighting resonates with the Doc character. The Doctor is being tempted by the power. He’s having a delightful time, and feelings strong and capable, which must be a big lure for the character, with his cowardliness. The demon’s sense of timing is good. Gauging the change in confidence in the Doctor accurately, and having events turn for the worse whenever the Doc gets uppity. This is the first time in the Doc universe that something terrible happens to someone we’re supposed to care about, which explains why the change in point of view. While the Doctor has had various victims, we’ve been encouraged to laugh at them and their fates under the abuse of Doc and Eegor. This particular guy we’re supposed to connect with, so the point of view change to first person perspective makes perfect sense. Holy fucking shit, he did it. = The Road to Triumph. Part Four: The Final Sacrifice. Suicide? Well, I guess it’s not anymore off the wall than any other of the stuff that this character has had to do. And I regret to say that the Wendy turn is out of left field for me. I imagine there’s appropriate history to match it up, but during the entire event in this round that have unfolded she hadn’t a single line of dialogue until this particular piece of work. During the entire event of her kidnapping, through Dave’s torture, she’d said nothing. It might have been intention, but it seems she was just forgotten, and then to suddenly act in this turn of events… kinda daytime drama. It’s kind of a funny moment of parity that each competitor thinks of themselves as the underdog. BANG! I think there should be just one bang. There’s something about the second time you see “BANG!” across the screen that’s anticlimactic. Ahh, the Ducky/Cigarette Case save. I like that. It’s a little contrived, but it’s a means to enter the Ducky into the story and I appreciate touches like that, even though I doubt the deflection powers of a rubber duck with regard to bullets, I’d let it go for dramatic purposes. I just wish that the set up to get the bullet in the air wasn’t so absurd. I like the finish. Good. = The Judgement. This is going to come down to one simple thing. Dave’s use of Nurse Putrid hurt his work. The most significant feature is that her choices as a character were too implausible, too inconsistent. While it was clear that the character was deranged, even deranged people need reasons to act as they do. They can be stupid reasons, or imaginary reasons, but they have to be there for the character’s actions to make sense, and it is the author’s responsibility to present those reasons in a plausible light, and that just didn’t happen. Andy’s work was good, solid, and a fine showing, bringing us several new things to Doctor Curiosity at the end of this series. He pulled some new tricks out of the hat, and they worked. In particular, the end of the series brought us to a new ground, this reader believing that at some point the Doctor would attempt to weasel his way out of the “deal” in order to win the day, was in for quite a shock when Doctor Curiosity embraced his doomed fate. Pick: Doctor Curiosity Judge 2 EDH “Road to Triumph Part 1: Captivity” This one starts out quite frankly dull. The entire prologue is just one point reiterated over and over again as if the writer doesn’t believe it himself. There are various grammatical errors in the introduction and there isn’t anything concrete in there to lead the reader onto the story. The only thing that guides the reader by the hand is that he acknowledges that something has to give. Maybe, a false sense of hope. The overall ambience of the roleplay is pretty well done. Imagery isn’t a factor here too much as the writer does go into great detail with surroundings and emotions. I’m having a bit of trouble getting used to this first person narrative as he inputs elongated opinions after every fact or asset of the story. It’s too much filler for me. Ok, to explain the sore throat sickness. Not okay to explain and compare it to everything else. The roleplay rolls along rather slowly and it seems like more effort is put into ambience and good writing than actual plot development. Oh yeah, it looks like we may have some symbolism in our hands. We’ll see how that catches up later. “Road to Triumph Part 2: Megalomania” Just two lines into this one and already it’s clearly better than the first one. There isn’t any filler introduction, or dull monologues, the writer jumps straight into the plot. Not only that, he actually develops it now! The flow of the story in this roleplay is loads better and the writing remains on par. The constant switching between actual events and opinions seems a bit forced at times, but it seems like the writer is getting better. Relevance is also a factor here, and the roleplay is heavily stressed on the Doctor Curiosity symbolism. I was a bit confused when I saw a scene break in the middle of the same scene. Then I realized it wasn’t a scene break. It was a change in point of view. Very well done, though I think unnecessary. The writer could have given the reader as much insight into the nurse’s persona without switching views. Other than that, seemed to work out fine. Rather nice roleplay and it’s ended nicely. Much better than the first, this one actually baits the reader into reading the third installment. “Road to Triumph Part 3: Compassion” Writer continues to switch between personas and for now it seems to be working fine. Writing, plot, symbolism – all of it pretty much remains the same. There are a few less grammatical errors here and the writing seems to pick up a little bit. But, much more than that is the bait factor. Now the writer isn’t just telling a story he is making us believers. Before in the first two roleplays, it was all just tell, tell, and tell. This time the emotions are rampant throughout the rp and reader feels more at ease. The second scene was the worst of the scenes. The entire metamorphosis of Nurse Putrid could have gone better either without a change in personal views or delayed change. Not to mention that this is another time I’ve seen a random women fall mysteriously in love with EDH. I should make fun of that. Hahahhaa. Moving on, the metamorphosis seems a bit forced and the ending/explanation to it wasn’t all that great. She just simply blurted out that she loved him. Yet, before that she mentioned something awkward that I thought was going to lead into bad relationships, bad life, that sort of thing. So now the roleplay went from inspiring to borderline decent. I’m looking for the ending to pick it up and it does. EDH doesn’t seem so mechanical anymore, and he might actually have a personality. Emotions run high, and he does it. He does the unthinkable. He drops a cliffhanger. “Road to Triumph Part 4: Final Sacrifice” Wow. Excellent roleplay. Clearly better than the rest by heads and shoulders. The plight of the characters was just very well written and I could actually picture the entire scenes in my head. I could watch as the Nurse died, fake Chris shoot EDH, and EDH’s heartbreak when he learns the truth. The writer clearly left the best for last and for this roleplay put out all the stops. The transition from the third to fourth roleplay was ever so smooth, and the storyline went on without a hitch. However, as good as this roleplay was there were still a few bad marks. First off, the entire switching between events and views dance gets unnecessary. I won’t hold it against him, as it is his style. But it tends to interrupt the story way too much. Secondly, the reactions and dialogue seemed mechanical. The only exception was the scene where Wendy breaks free. That was nicely done. Other dialogues were forced. Thirdly, when in the hell did Rubber Ducky make it back to EDH? In rp 3, the Doc was holding him, and unless I missed something he shouldn’t have been in there to take the bullet. Lastly, the entire hostage scene was never concluded. I assume the doctor and Wendy thought he died and left him there. The ending could have been much better to that situation. Finally, he ends it. This time the monologue isn’t a futile attempt to get respect where there is none. This time its character development and mapping the plight he has endured to get this far. Pretty good ending to an otherwise excellent roleplay. Now, time to read Doctor Curiosity DOCTOR CURIOSITY “Doctor Faustiosity Part 1: The Calling” Doctor Curiosity plays straight into the wishes of his readers in this introduction. The writing is as solid as always and one thing that goes unnoticed under his comedy is his ability to bring the scene to life. Heavy stress is laid description and the imagery is vivid. More importantly to me, is the increased stress on the isolation factor of Curiosity. He’s alone now and Andy does a great job jumping straight into that new stage of the Doc’s life explaining how his world is now different. Oh look…a little teaser. Looks like Andy wasted no time in making up a new nemesis, now that Collette is dead. The mood in this roleplay is quite different from the last series. There it was a mixture of doom and laughter. Now, it’s a lot more serious. Andy made sure that Curiosity changed after that episode and he wastes no time in making the new doc. The entire scene with the summoning is nicely written, but I was in awe at the end. The imagery of the summoned creature and the voice was so easy to picture. “Doctor Fausitiosity Part 2: Contracts and Cells” Jumping straight into where we left off, Curisioty focuses less on substance and now develops the plot. As usual, the dialogue between the would-be acquaintances is excellently done. The dialogue is easy to mimic and it was a relief to see the Doc refer back to his accent. That entire incident was nearly golden and the ending where both the demon and doc have their agendas was nicely done. Once again Andy does a great job of playing into the hands of the reader. The scene changes and we go into the developing of the new enemy. Last rp, we just merely got a glimpse, but now Andy tells us more without revealing too much info. At first it seems like just another cliché mafia beating. But with Collette in the background, and Andy at the helm I expect it to be much more. After all, what can a reader expect from a line like.. It was going to be the easiest money they ever earned. It definitely won’t be easy, we can be sure. So, now we are back to the new “friendship” and Andy plays both characters extremely well. The writing remains as great as ever but as is the case with the rest of his rps, it’s not the writing that matters, it’s the mood. In a sea of dark gloomy prospects, doctor curiosity provides a boat of comedy relief. Once again, the imagery is vivid and I could picture the entire jail scene in my hand. Especially the nonchalant demon filing his nails. Oh…look he ends it in another cliffhanger. He’s getting quite good at this. “Doctor Faustisioty Part 3: The Squared Circle” A little more of the same here. The jail riot scene is nicely written, but this time seems a bit rushed. Not too harmful, considering the isolation was made purely on curiosity and his shenanigans. Andy does a good job of baiting the reader into something that would be considered obvious yet unexpected. The part where Curisioty rushes to the aide of his brother, was golden in that you would expect the demon to lie. You know he had something up his sleeve. Yet, he makes it surprisingly, nonetheless. And. we continue on with the story. There’s a lot of variables I’m noticing here. Curiosity seems a bit unsure so far. The whole lonely transition thing is working out fine. We’re still only having a brief glimpse of the new enemy. Despite digging more into the new enemy, Andy instead decides to bring the rp back into the tournament. I really like the squared circle scene. Not much action, and the writing drops off a bit, but the relevance is as strong as any. He explains the success of his opponents in comparison to his own making his own case. Furthermore he throws in aspects of other stories in there to give both a: further realism to the tournament, and b. showcase attention. He’s been paying attention. “Doctor Faustiosity Part 4: Crescendo” well, I just read this completely in one fell swoop. Usually, I’m taking breaks to take notes to see where they went wrong and what they did right. Not this one. That will be the strongest point I’ll address. Andy’s already established himself as a great writer, but his ability to keep your attention goes unnoticed. Everything was just written nicely, easy to read and understand. Once again, I’m not too fond of switching point of views throughout the story, but in this case it works better than EDH’s attempts because of the scenery changes. Also, the changes are meant to explain thoughts, but rather help the story move along. So, it seems that the doctor gets out of another debacle. The demon wins, and we sorta expected him to. Actually, I thought he would win, but I was hoping for Curiosity to pull something out of his bag. Very nice rp overall. DRUM ROLL Wow, I’m tired. Well, I’ve reread the roleplays and reread my comments. I will say that this was a very enjoyable match to read. I haven’t read any of EDH’s stuff before this round save a few samples, so I was anxious to see what the commotion was all about. Doctor Curiosity totally wowed me last round. Before this roleplay series I didn’t think he could top what he wrote last round. I was right. But he still wins. Or at least gets my vote, lol. I thought overall the Doctor’s writing was better than EDH. It was in the very least more consistency and more substance. EDH started out very well in the first roleplay with ambience and writing. That hindered his development and as the rp and round progressed his writing took a dive to make way for the story. Andy on the otherhand, kept his writing on equal terms throughout the entire thing. When EDH started to falter with his imagery, Andy kept growing on being stronger and stronger. The transition from one roleplay to another as well from one scene to another was pretty equal among the two. The difference, however, was that with Andy’s rps, I didn’t feel myself having to take a break to take notes. I could read on and on without losing interest. EDH did admirably well in his 3rd and 4th rps, which I felt were his best rps this round. But overall, the good doctor just edged him. Dialogue seemed to be mechanical and at times it appeared like there was no distinct personalities to any of EDH’s characters. It was both hit and miss at times, and when he did hit it was totally great stuff. Andy played right into the right cards playing the “I’m alone” card, and it worked out well. He constantly changed between views and that kept him ahead. The way both went about their stories was enlightening. But, I think Doctor was just a tad bit better here. The flow of the action was better and more vivid. I also think his plight was a bit more dangerous and suspenseful. I admire the entire symbolism thing EDH did with the kidnapping and all, but at times it seemed forced. Also, the saving grace to that was rather mundane. With curiosity however, the suspense was always there and when things started to go bleak they cheered up. Vice versa. Overall, I would say I enjoyed reading Curiosity’s roleplays more than I did EDH’s. I think his story was better. It had more suspense, action, the writing was on par. The dialogue was easy to mimic. The strongest point of all I have to make is how the doctor is good at changing moods. That kept me hooked on the rps. That makes me pick him to win this one.
