Beginners: Familarize Yourself with the Workspace -White Mexican

13 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A first tour of Poser: the tools, the display styles and the controls. The reading for Task 1. Okay, you just got Poser and now your ready to use it. Only one problem, how? Well the first step is to open Poser. If your having trouble finding the program it will either be located in Metacreations or Curious Labs folders depending on what version you have. So now you have the screen below. Let's start by getting familiar with this screen shall we. If you are going to use Poser, you are going to need to be used to the layout. First off the most important part is the display window. This is where you will view everything you do. This is the box that contains the figure in the center of the screen. Now you don't have to master the layout on your first try, but try to get a general idea about what each tool does. 1. Rotate - Give this a tool a try. Click the man and start to drag and you'll see him start to rotate. Undo the last move and go to the next tool. 2. Twist - This one isn't much different then the rotate tool except it will only twist the part selected. Give it a try and then undo it and move on. 3. Translate/Pull - You'll use this one often. Click on the man's foot and pull it to the side and you'll see it affects his whole body. Undo and move on. 4. Translate In/Out - This works the exact same as the Pull except it moves the part towards or away from you. Pull down to bring the object towards and up to move it away. 5. Scale - Pretty much just how it sounds. Click and drag to increase or decrease the scale of the part. 6. Taper - Again pretty straight forward. Click a part and drag to taper one end of the object. Unfortunately Poser predetermines the end so you will only be able to taper on end of the object. 7. Chain Break - This is a handy tool that will help you later on with posing. Use the Translate/Pull tool and select the man's arm and pull it to the side. Notice how the whole upper body goes with it. Now undo and use the Chain Break and click in the center of his chest. A little Broken Chain image will pop up there. Now do the same thing as before with the Translate/Pull. Notice how the body stays put. 8. Paint Bucket - This works like any other program. Select the Paint Bucket and hover over the characters t-shirt. Click the shirt and select a blue color and let go. You now painted his shirt blue. In the future you will learn about textures but for now, use this tool to change objects colors. 9. Group - We'll leave this one for now, but this is used to in several different ways but it's not for the beginner. That's it for the Tools, next it's the display controls. They are rather simple to understand and a picture is worth a thousand words. A. Silhouette - This will display a solid color cut out of your character. B. Outline - This will display a simple outline of the major parts in your character. C. Wireframe - This will display a simple wireframe of your character. D. Hidden Line - Very similar to the wireframe but with less lines, thus making it a bit easier to distinguish. E. Lit Wireframe - Exactly the same as Hidden Line except the colors of the character show up on the lines. F. Flat Shaded - This displays a very low resolution example of your model. G. Flat Lined - A combination of Hidden Line and Flat Shaded. H. Cartoon - A 2D cartoonist rendering of your character. I. Cartoon w/Line - The same as Cartoon with a thin black line highlighting areas. J. Smooth Shaded - This is the default display setting and shows a smooth 3D looking character. K. Smooth Lined - This is a combination of Smooth Shaded and Hidden Line. L. Texture Shaded - This is the only display that will show what the figure looks like with the textures. Since no textures are on this character it looks the same as Smooth Shaded but as we move on you will find yourself using this display more and more. And that's if for the Display settings. Let's move on to some of the other ones. M. Box Tracking - This will change the Display setting so that the major parts are all boxes. It is quicker for Poser when you pose in this way but always make sure to check your pose with normal tracking to make sure a box isn't turned the wrong way. N. Fast Tracking - This is the setting you will most likely want to use. It's a happy medium between the Box and Full. O. Full Tracking - You won't notice much difference with the P4 Character but when you advance there will be a big difference in the appearance of the character in Full and Fast Tracking. Only use this if you need to see that extra detail as it will slow Poser down. P. Depth Cuing - This is a neat feature Poser has but it is only useful when more characters or objects are being used. It focuses in on the closest point and fogs the rest of the character out to add a depth image. Q. Display Shadows - This can be used to turn shadows on or off in the display window. R. Foreground Color - Click the dot and you can change the foreground color. S. Background Color - Click the dot and you can change the foreground color. T. Shadow Color - Click the dot and you can change the foreground color. U. Ground Color - Click the dot and you can change the foreground color. V. Current Figure - Click on this and you can select which figure you are working with. We only have one figure so there isn't much to chose but with the next few tutorials as you learn to add clothing you will use this. W. Current Element - Click this and another popup box will give you several options. This tool is very useful as you progress and start to use the Parameter Dials. Simply highlight the body part, camera, light or prop you want to view in the Dials and let go. X. Lighting Controls - We won't worry to much about these now but as you learn the basics we will. Lighting is arguably the most important element in art and this control will allow you to do that. Y. Camera Controls - Again we won't go into to much detail with these just yet but all the controls here will move the cameras around. For now just click the Camera Controls and select the Face Camera. Now you'll see the display has changed to a close up of the characters face. Z. Parameter Dials - Get used to these because they will make your life easier. Start off by clicking the figure's head. You'll notice that the Dials will change and they will now say "Head" above it. Let's just keep it simple for now and go to the "Twist" Control. There are two ways to use the dials, you can click in the middle and drag it right and left, or you can click the number to the top right of the dial and insert the number you want. Either way you want to do it, set the Twist setting to 22 degrees and your picture should look like the one above. I'm sure you are wondering what the little dots are in the bottom right corner. They are memory dots and can be quite useful but we won't get into that yet. Congratulations, by now you should have a general grasp on the Poser Workspace. The last thing to try now is arranging your workspace. It is completely customizable and can be moved around in whichever way suits you best. You can use the paint bucket tool to change the color of the workspace as well. Happy Posing.
