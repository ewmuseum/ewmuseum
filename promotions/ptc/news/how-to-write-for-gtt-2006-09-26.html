How to Write for GTT

26 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I'm not the foremost expert on writing or anything like that, but, I know the difference between a pile of crap, and a good peice of writing. I hope that this guide will be informative to you as you move on toward making GTT the year of you. TIP #1 - Never compromise your story. Last year, walking into my first round match with Ryan, I wanted to tell a story way different than I did. Knowing Ryan was pretty weak against a comedy writer, I wanted to tell a really funny story that would entertain more than Ryan's story would stand out on it's technical and formulaic merits. Unfortunately, I decided to tell a completely different story, and ended up getting booted early. I honestly should have never, ever done that. I allowed my opponent to dictate what my story should be, rather than dictate that on my own, and I ended up losing for it, because I was simply out of my character's element. Don't do that. Ever. If there's a story you want to tell, tell it. So what if you go out in the first round? Half the field or more is going out in the first round too. There's no shame in it. TIP #2 - Think, But, This Ain't Rocket Science, Kids. Your mind is your best friend in GTT6, but, it can also be your worst enemy. If you've come to a fork in the road, save your document, close Word, and get off the computer for an hour. Go do something. Play some video games, go throw a football at your sister's face, kick your brother down the stairs, whatever it takes to get the story out of your mind and you thinking about something other than GTT. And then, before you know it, an idea will pop into your head that you weren't even considering, and you'll be well on your way to completing your story. But, that said... TIP #3 - Don't Wait Until The Last Day You should be writing your story right now. No, not literally. But, there's absolutely with outlining what you want to accomplish and using that for reference later on. Even a simple list like "Short intro of characters, first point of conflict with antagonist, partial resolution with setup for round 2, with clear closing spot, in the event I'm 1 and done." This way, as you go, you can flesh out your ideas and add them to your outline. I often get ideas for RPs and stories when I'm not even thinking about it. I usually keep a pad of paper and a pen with me at almost all times, just to write them down so I don't forget for later. The entire Chet Worth Character was planned this way over a full year of idea gathering and outlining what I wanted to do with the character. He's quite possibly one of the most complete characters ever. You want your RP to be complete as well. So, there's absolutely nothing wrong with thinking and writing often. However... TIP #4 - Excuse Me, Girlfriend/Boyfriend. Why Are You Kissing That Other Man? Don't think/write about GTT so much that you alienate your friends, family, wives, kids, grandparents, dog, Playstation, the guy at the corner that you buy a newspaper from... and so on. In fact, if you're spending more than an hour or two on your RP a day, you're spending far, far too much time on it. Remember: You're given 7 days for a reason. It's up to you to wisely use your time in those 7 days so that you can balance your real life with your fantasy wrestler's life. If you lose GTT, it's no big deal. If you lose a friend because of GTT, you'll never forgive yourself. TIP #5 - Excuse Me, Sir... Could I Get An Opinion? Maybe you have a problem like I do, where I tend to fall into present perfect tense (have gotten, have done, have touched, etc.,) without even realizing it. Maybe you've got this passage in your RP where your main character monologues to a guy he's confronting. Maybe you're unsure if a certain part of your RP actually helps your story. Whatever the case, it absolutely, positively helps to have a friend that will read and give opinions about things to you*. Your biggest asset during GTT is your ability to IM someone and say "Hey, friend. Would you mind taking a look at this thing I'm writing? I'd like your opinion on it." And do it often. VERY often. TIP #6 - I sware to God, 2day will be so awsum, Im glad it wuz yesterday. The year is 2006. As such, every major operating system, and every major company worth it's salt has within it's cozy package of applications that ship with it a word processing suite. And inside each one is a spell checker. Use it. There is absolutely NO excuse for poor spelling, whatsoever. There are judges who will simply discard an RP because you didn't care enough to either spell check, or get a friend to read it with a fine toothed comb to make sure that you didn't make a mistake. Other things to remember in this category: - Grammar, grammar, grammar. Check your tenses, and stay true throughout. If you're explaining something that happened last week, stay in the past. If it's happening now, stay present. If it's going to happen, go into future! We're not looking for someone to perfect the english language. But, we want the story to be readable without tense slippage. - You're your own friend. Understand and use the correct terms for you're and your (You're is YOU ARE, as in You are a human being. Your denotes ownership, as in your bike, your car, your friend. - They're with their friends. Again, same as above. They're is THEY ARE, as in They are a pile of jerks. Their is to denote group possession, as in their house, their car, their friends. - Always fully spell words. Don't use leetspeak, or internet shorthand. Unless it absolutely helps the story, don't bother. It's laziness, and nothing else. - In the event you do not have a word processor with spellcheck, download Open Office. It's free, and it's just as good as Word, if not better. Use it, love it, cherish it. TIP #7 Man, What Is This Fool Talking About? I'm just some asshole that's only gotten farther than the first round of any tournament once. Fuck me, fuck what I say, and remember, above all else: HAVE FUN. The rest doesn't matter. So long as you have fun, no matter if you go out in the first round, or win the entire thing, the tournament is a success. * - Except if your friends are judges. Don't bother them, please.
