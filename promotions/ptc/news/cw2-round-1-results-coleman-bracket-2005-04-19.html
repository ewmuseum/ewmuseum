CW2 Round 1 Results - Coleman Bracket

19 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Silvio Fiore (FA) d Sebastian Moraco (FA) 2 votes to 1 Christian Darke (FA) d Peter Vetra (PRIME) 2 votes to 0 El Rayo Azul II (GCW) d "The Fallen One" Nephilim (4WW) 2 votes to 1 Josh (FA) d Jeremy Castle (FA) 2 votes to 0 Round 2 Matchups Silvio Fiore (FA) v Josh (FA) Christian Darke (FA) v El Rayo Azul II (GCW) Judging Comments Fiore v Moraco Judge 1 Moraco: The character is both introduced, and re-introduced well, managing to link in the last time he was seen in GTT4 to the present day. Also went to the length of explaining why he wasn't seen in any federations post-GTT, which a few might have been asking. One thing I liked about the RP was how it blended several times in his life and tied them together with narrative, rather than opting for separate, labelled flashbacks. It seemed to allow them to flow into one another seamlessly, not stopping and stalling through the timeline. The story of Sebastian Moraco seems well rounded and believable, tying in traits of other well-known PTC stars such as Chris Storm's amateur background and Karina Wolfenden's fierce, family defying independence, but making it totally his own character. I was also VERY pleased to see his the vicious beating he took from his father and his mother's terror at it was tied into Fiore's age. A nice little "click" moment where you realise that the history you've been given is there to solidify his current position. The University student part of the character is also a nice, subtle backdrop. He's not overly gimmicked in that he has to wrestle to pay his way through or anything like that, he's simply a guy who'll risk missing classes and getting behind to do what he loves. The reader ends the RP with a firm idea of who Sebastian Moraco is, and even better, how he relates to the tournament, his family, and the environment he exists in. Seeds of weakness are planted, possible distractions highlighted, and his strengths understated in veiled fashion. There's no trumpet being blown, no declarations of victory being made... yet somehow, you still get that he wants to win, simply because of who he is. The only trouble I found with the RP was the flow of the piece. Sometimes there were series of short, sharp sentences which carried the same theme and key words, which could have maybe been woven into one. That, along with fracturing some of the larger paragraphs would have given it a better flow to a reader like myself. I've also never been a huge fan of the "stop-start" style of novel dialogue used in the piece, and far prefer to have the dialogue and vocal narration as part of the same sentence, without a full-stop separating them. Fiore: Okay. Not sure how to come at this one from a judging point of view, so I'll do my best. This roleplay was brilliant. Maybe brilliant +1. I've always been sceptical of Sky's stuff, as his stuff usually doesn't fall within my limits of appreciation. Not a knock, since variation keeps interest. And I have to admit, at times, I wondered where he was going with it. Not giving an idea of the time elapsed since CW2 was what made this roleplay, and with "the Quick" noted as a 15 year Vet, it helped blur the link which many readers would suspect. I've also made a conscious effort to ignore the scrollbar so I don't know when an RP is getting towards the end, so the sharp and vindictive ending hit harder. Now, I do have to admit I'm a sucker for future/alternate timelines, which I can see justifying the same use as the past. This is a prime example of how they can work. Not seeing the modern day Fiore helps keep him shrouded in mystery, but the reader still gets a great snapshot of the man he will become. The use of other characters to draw comparisons was well worked, without being merely namedropping, and it was nice to see Sky manage to tie all his other creations into the story. I'll come straight out and admit that I’ve never been a huge fan of deaths and maiming in RP's, or in character's histories. If they're well written and "work" then I'll accept them as story progressors, if not, I'll usually just skip over. In this case, it worked. It needed to work. I was left thinking "exactly what transpired to justify the act of not throwing in the towel". Maybe I was a little miffed that the RP didn’t explain it, but that was outweighed by my desire to read it in future work. Conclusion: Not to be all "PTC judge cliché" on everyone... but this was a bastard to choose between. On one side, Moraco did a great job of slapping his character and motivations on the table in front of the reader, and making them take the information in. It was crisp, direct, and did the job well. The family weren't used as mere plot devices, they were part of the character himself. On the other, you have Fiore, who we only saw a future echo of, but was died into an elusive and teasing story of where the CW2 took him. I read the Moraco RP first, and thought he set a high bar for Fiore to reach. And, dropping all the judge blah stuff, I didn't think that Sky would reach it. And at times in his RP when I was waiting for things to fall into place, I still had doubts (which shows that this is really one of those RP's that can only be taken as a whole piece, and not broken down into sections). But I was wrong. With a lot of credit to Reyes, my vote goes to Sky on this one. Walking away from the RP's, I'll remember the Moraco character and his defining moments, but it will be Fiore who's etched into my brain, and demanding that I read more. Sky threw out something that I thought might sail just wide, but it curled right into the top corner and did it for me. Top work by both. Congrats to the winner. Congrats to the guy who didn't win, who by no means can be referred to as a loser here. And DAMN PTC has a way of making 1st rounds of tourneys rule. Azul II v Nephilim Judge 1 Nephilim: Roleplay title: Why? Comments: This was an intriguing roleplay, one which was centred around the question: Why? From the outset, Nephilim is presented to us as a powerful, almost God-like character, who has many subjects. He is a man who commands obedience at all times; an authoritarian figure. Nephilim has made a conscious decision to return to the ring for CW2 to regain his glory. There is a great link to the past here, and this gives Nephilim's quest for victory in CW2 all the more meaningful. He is presented as a dangerous figure, a man who has no fear whatsoever for the things ahead. Throwing caution to the wind, he only wants success in CW2 and the future. As the roleplay progresses, Nephilim becomes a darker character. The repetition of the question and title "why" is extremely effective throughout the roleplay. Nephilim demands respect from his subordinates and from everyone else. The final scene takes place in his home, where his wife rests comfortably. The most striking and probably best part of the roleplay is when she finds Nephilim's helmet or breastplate - it is unclear which - in a locked room in the house. This reminds us of the times that have passed in his life; the death of his first wife and his injuring of Johnny Blaze. This is another clear reference to his history, and shows us his accomplishments as a wrestler. Without doubt, he is eager to accomplish these feats once more. --- El Rayo Azul II: Roleplay title: Historical Perspective and Retrospective Comments: Personally, I would like to say that although I did enjoy the history of El Rayo Azul, this section of the roleplay seemed slightly too long winded. I realise it is important to give your character a good background when you are introducing him to a new audience, but I felt this section dominated the roleplay, hence taking away from it somewhat. The writing was top class, and I cannot fault the writer on this. Despite the fact that the legacy of El Rayo Azul was long, in my opinion, it was still very well-written and effective. Towards the end of the roleplay, things pick up as El Rayo Azul II is portrayed as a character completely alone. He has absolutely nowhere to go, but turns to Jesus Christ for guidance, or at least he contemplates doing so. The ending brings new life into the roleplay; it is a pity it didn't come sooner, as it would have been welcome. The character, however, has a lot of potential, and if you condensed it a little more, it would be very high quality. All in all, an impressive effort. Final verdict: Comparing the two roleplays here, it was clear that both handlers put a lot into them. They deserve congratulations for that. However, in my opinion, Nephilim appeared to have more of a purpose in the tournament as a figure who is attempting to once again reach his prime. In contrast, El Rayo Azul II didn't seem to have the aforementioned purpose. In addition, although El Rayo Azul II's writing was excellent at all times, it lacked a definite direction. Therefore, I am awarding the victory to Nephilim, but well done to El Rayo Azul II for his class effort. Pick: El Ray Azul II Judge 3 Both roleplays predicated themselves on the fact that all will be revealed in future episode(s). It's dangerous to do that in a tournament setting because, unlike in a week of fed roleplaying, in a tournament there's the possibility that you won't be able to keep writing...so you need to make sure that each effort stands alone as a good story. Both rps left me wanting more, but examining each as a stand-alone effort, I felt El Rayo Azul II produced the better read. Nephilim catered to an audience familiar with his character...I'm not part of that audience. I don't get a clear enough sense of Nephilim's role in 4WW, or of his role in the Order of the Hammer, or of the origin or purpose of the demon in his head. Nephilim mentions people that have no significance to me...Johnny Blaze, Chris Williams, the "Sean" into whose hands Nephilim put the 4WW. I just got the sense that I was jumping into the middle of an existing story...like starting a book at Chapter 10. I did enjoy the allusions to Nephilim's power without producing the actual evidence...the segment where it appeared as though Nephilim had wings and glowing eyes didn't force me to totally suspend disbelief, and I appreciated that. El Rayo Azul II wrote with the intended reader being someone entirely unfamiliar with the character. He gave the backstory without any added fanfare, telling simply the history of the name and mask before injecting himself into the story. I do feel that the gunshots were an awkward place to stop the roleplay when there's no guarantee of a follow-up, but that flaw is compensated by the rest of the roleplay's easy-going explanations. The whole roleplay just read off dialectic, like someone telling me a story, which I preferred to Nephilim's novelistic approach. If Nephilim could write his roleplay over with me telling him one thing to change, it would be to take a step or two back from 4WW details that might not be of as much value to an unfamiliar reader. Write like the intended reader has never heard of anyone or any of the events you're talking about. Good job to both competitors, as I really enjoyed the roleplays. Pick: El Rayo Azul II Moody v Castle Judge 1 Josh: Roleplay title: The Green Monster Comments: From start to finish, this roleplay was top quality. The interaction between Moody and his girlfriend was intriguing, as they went from bliss to a huge fight. The relationship was emphasised throughout the roleplay, and it was done very effectively. The sequence of events during which Moody discovers he has been accepted to CW2, whereas his girlfriend has been rejected, shows a little rivalry of sorts between the two. Despite the fight that occurs between the two, traces of humour are a refreshing addition to the roleplay as a whole. Clearly, a lot of effort was put into this roleplay, and it was well-written, with magnificent descriptions and an attractive story which kept me interested from the outset. --- Jeremy Castle: Roleplay title: What I've Got To Do Comments: This roleplay was well done, introducing us to Jeremy Castle, a man who believes CW2 to be his shot; perhaps his final one. Castle is presented as the underdog, as if he doesn't have much expectations for the tournament and his success in it. Although this roleplay was, for the most part, interesting, it lacked any dialogue. I thought that this damaged the roleplay's quality slightly, as a deeper insight into Castle's character would have been great. The finish was slightly abrupt, which also took away from the roleplay. However, I cannot fault Castle's handler for his effort; well done. Final verdict: In comparing the two roleplays here, the interaction with characters, or lack thereof, plays a part. I feel that if Castle's roleplay had included some dialogue to tell us more about Castle, the roleplay would have been better. Moody's roleplay, in contrast, introduced two characters from the outset, both of them with dimension and purpose about them. The mention of CW2 in Moody's roleplay, though not essential, placed emphasis on its importance to him. Castle's roleplay could have included slightly more about the tournament and his feelings on it. A good show by both, but I have to hand the victory to Josh. A great effort by Jeremy Castle's handler, but Moody shone in his roleplay. Pick: Josh Judge 2 Castle: Right, here's where I get torn sometimes. This was a very well presented piece. Very clean, to the point, structured, and delivered with a dryness that gave humour a scoff rather than a full laugh. The character of Jeremy Castle is one I'm now interested in. The background info, playing in well with the "seemingly not the norm" sarcasm provided a realistic base for someone to then take the military step. No overboard patriotism, just setting him up as a man who can’t recoil from what he considers duty. It's a subtle but solid tie to his tournament introduction. My main problem was that it was all delivered as a statement, which can’t really immerse the reader fully in the events. If there had been some recollections of his accident, and maybe of his Baseball days or the moment his father had to sell off a huge chunk of land, it would have really sunk in. A scene of the incidents with the dialogue and at-the-time reactions would, in my opinion, have made this a top roleplay. Which is why I'm kinda torn. Maybe I'm focussing too much on what it could have been. Anyway, regardless, it was a well written piece, with wit and pacing that I'm sure would have intrigued more than just myself. Moody: I liked the interactions here, and the dialogue was bang-on natural for the most part. In scenes like that, it's easy for some writers to keep with proper, full English, when in reality, nobody has a conversation like that in the situation unless they're... well, odd. The relationship of chirpy girlfriend to the rudely awoken, mentor/boyfriend was put over very well, showcasing personalities. Little quirks like having the alarm set for 9:27 was a very nice touch. One thing that did put me off a little was the structure. It seemed an awkward take on novel-style, with commas following full-stops and question marks, which end sentences, leaving the comma redundant. The contrast of their fortunes at the end was also a nice touch, and although you knew she didn't get in just from having seen the field, the reaction and suggestion of a rift developing made the mention meaningful. Was a little disappointed at the very matter-of-fact way that Castle was dealt with. It contrasted awkwardly with the natural dialogue displayed elsewhere in the piece, leaving it a little wooden. Conclusion: As I begin to write this, I still haven't picked between the two, so this is as much for my own aid as it is to wrap things up tidily. Castle presented a character to us and gave all the info we'd need, but didn't seem to focus in on the important parts and add an extra dimension. The dry delivery and snarky remarks added to character, but was hampered by the character never being "seen". If it was for a bio's history section, it would be a bloody good history section, but as an RP it struggled to break into 3 dimensions. Moody's was a character relationship driven vehicle to confirm his entry in the CW2 tourney. And while it gave a very natural account of the Josh/Raki dynamic, it was a little short on his motivation, and the parts of his character detached from his girl. Like Castle's, there were also nice little quirks that lifted it from the screen. And at this point, I still, still haven't picked. I'm also refusing to have grammar and RP format decide it. I'm happy to factor it into the decision, but not to decide it. So, still, still, still haven't picked yet. Both pieces went about the 1st round RP in very different ways, with Castle taking an almost stand-back reflective approach, while Moody dropped us right into the midst of his relationship. With Castle, the reader got a wide scope of his character, while with Moody's the relationship with Raki was used to build up to his CW2 entry... and also put over the tourney well by then having Raki be rejected and told to try next year. Moody's was entirely engrossed in the scene, while Castle's was a narration of his life's key moments. Still haven't picked yet. Don't worry, there won’t be a coin-toss. I'm going to read them both again. I have to say I enjoyed the way that Castle had the desires of his family's older generations channelled into him when he applied, and what happened afterwards sets him up as a character who simply deals with things as they are. The problems encountered during Re-Hab would have also made a good scene to break up the narration with. I just bloody like the RP, but not the entirely narrative approach, that would probably be the best way to sum it up. Moody's was cantered around the acceptance to the tourney, and Raki's rejection. Everything built to that moment, the actions and dialogues, and when the moment hit, the Castle bit seemed flat, but the Raki bit was well handled. Still haven't decided. Right, decided, finally. My pick for the winner is Josh. I think the part where Raki was rejected sealed the win here, as it effected the relationship shown to the reader, as well as negatively echoing the Moody acceptance. Maybe some people wouldn't have had it quite so close, or maybe some would have gone for Castle, but I think the entirely narrative approach doesn't allow the reader to feel engrossed. So, there we have it. 1 hour and 12 minutes of my life. Pick: Josh CW2 brackets
