Round 3 Results - Piglet

15 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JOHN COVEL V BRUCE "VIOLENCE JACK" SHANAHAN" Judge 1 John Covel: I like your premise, the althete who also wrestles had been done before in the past, but in my opinion you've given it a fresh spin that's restored some of its life and appeal. That said your Rp just felt a little flat to me, like you were rushing towards the end without giving the contens as much thought as you had originally intended. John's struggle to balance his wrestling life with his return to baseball will obviously be a central plot focus, but this time it felt like there wasn't enough attention payed to that struggle. Violence Jack: Maybe I'm out of the loop, but I've never read and RP where the principle character is confined to an asylum while his associate wrestles in his place by proxy, and I think it's kind of clever. The strongest point of your RP was easily the opening segment between VJ and Daniel, there was a lot that was said during there that added a lot to the depth of both your character and your piece as a whole. The center faltered a bit honestly, I understand the relevance to the overall story, but it didn't seem to carry along with the same smoothness as the rest of your piece. In the end Violence Jack's piece just had more substance to my mind, and flowed along better as an RP. Pick: Violence Jack Judge 2 This match could've been worlds different with addtional promos from either/both sides. Both sides have a great story, but VJ's writing seems to let the story flow alot better, and his story has more "Grab" to it. Covel did an excellent job, and could've sealed the deal with a second promo, but the story didn't seem to get a chance to get going. Pick: Violence Jack Judge 3 Violence Jack: I remember your stuff from the PCW days; it impressed me. This roleplay did the same. You've got a wonderful way to weave words (dig the alliteration) into mesmerizing sentences. Also, the psychology evident in the passage between Shanahan and Rourke was a lot of fun to read. It gave me this little pang of jealousy I get sometimes when I read a published author's work -- like, Jesus, how'd they think of how to put that? I really hate to seem all-praising, but there wasn't anything I could really nitpick. I wanted to attack Shanahan's dialogue, but I can't picture a guy like him speaking any differently. If he spoke more "naturally," he wouldn't come across as effectively as he does in this piece. Well done. John Covel: I'll shoot straight with you here: it wasn't as good as Violence Jack's, though it wasn't a bad showing, either. It breaks down into you falling just a bit short to your opponent; but there's not a lot of shame in that. You held my interest -- which is good, considering how laughable a game I consider baseball. What it is, though, is how you wrote the roleplay. Your story is what held my interest; your prose left something to be desired. It was almost amateur in comparison. Pick: Bruce "Violence Jack" Shanahan. RESULT Violence Jack d John Covel, 3 votes to 0 JACOB MCKAIL V CLINTON SAGE Judge 1 Jacob McKail: Roleplay One Pros - Great story and great writing. I expected that. - Fast read. Everything went by lightning fast, and I was into it all. - Liked how you segued everything with reports from a wrestling website. Thought that was cool. Cons - The technical errors hurt you here. Spelling, comma use, etc. That has to be fixed. Especially when you have a tough opponent in Clinton Sage. Roleplay 2 Pros - One of the best stories in the entire tournament. - The writing here is top notch. Really, you drag the readers in. - The ending was brillant. Really enjoying the characters here as well. Cons - Technical errors. That's it. Everything else is so good, but then the techinical errors rears it's head, and it just takes away from the brillance of the piece. Clinton Sage Roleplay 1 Pros - Great writing. You could write anything and it would sound good. That's a good trait to have. - Good technical piece. No errors that I caught and that's a good thing. Cons - The story never draws me in. It actually rings hollow. - Outside of Clinton Sage, the characters irritate me. Not a good irritate, but just seem beyond false. Roleplay 2 Pros - Story was alright. Somewhat interesting. - Good writing still. No technical errors. Cons - Story was alright. Just very lukewarm to me unfortunately. Not an easy decision because I still feel like both characters brought good pieces. However, I have to go with Jacob McKail. His story was just superb. Pick: Jacob McKail Judge 2 McKail: All around, a good story. I can get the GCW news interludes, but to be honest, they somewhat detract from the story. I would have much rather simply read the story without them being there. I have no interest in GCW, or the goings on there while GTT is going on, and I would prefer not to have the two mix. Positively, you share something in common with any great writer. You speak to your target audience, and use language as it should be. Never threateningly, or imposingly. It's simply there because it is. I like that your writing isn't pretentious, or something you assume that you're not. You write as you are, and that's great. Sage: For the relevance hounds out there, this might seem way off base. I'm not one of them, but, I can see what they mean. If Sage is wrestling each week, how is the story continuously happening? I understand that's not what you're going for, but, there was nary a mention of anything related to GTT. But, as someone that knows better, I know that this is indeed related to GTT, Spartan, and ties your tournament lives together. I really like the story, and I really like the use of language, and the structure. Pick: Clinton Sage Had we been in that area, McKail, I'd say you should indeed get a playin. Your work was very solid, and you should feel no shame in losing to Sage, who could end up as the champion of this thing. Judge 3 Man... as much as I love judging, I -HATE- Argyle for giving me this match. How dare he give me one of the best match-ups I've ever read... On a serious note. I wish one of you had some glaring mistake, or even -ANY- mistake. Both stories were amazing, and what it came down to was which story I preferred. In the end, the fact that won it out, is that McKail's story is actually alot more relevant to wrestling, and I feel that is important in an e-wrestling community. So, as much as you guys tortured me, trying to make me choose... my final decision was ridiculously hard to make. Pick: Jacob McKail Judge 4 Clinton Sage: Everything that's been said about you is true, Ryan. You're very gifted in telling stories. You know how to leave a reader wanting more. And you use these things to your advantage. Your first roleplay was a little sci-fi/comic bookish, but it worked here for me because I knew who Spartan was. The powers the meteorite gave him were actually really cool, as fanboyish as that may sound. And Hollinger's back again, too. You're almost King-like in bringing characters from other stories into a bigger one. The only thing I didn't like -- and this is something Pete, I believe, pointed out in your match against Rollins -- was the dialogue. Pete said something along the lines of -- though I could be way off with my memory -- you needing the dialogue tags, because without them the reader wouldn't be able to distinguish the speakers. I agree. Jacob McKail: God bless you, O Handler, for not making these as long as your first. >=) You know what else I really liked? The usage of the "headlines." Those were pretty clever, and added a little more to an already interesting story. Maybe I don't pay attention as well as I should, but I've never seen a "stalker" angle in efedding. Sure, the fortnight-long backstage angle to introduce some character's been done, but this looks to be different. I liked how you played out the psychiatrist bits, too. Nothing you put into the roleplays took anything away from them, and the length wasn't a detriment here. Pacing was decent, too. I'll admit I thought Sage was gonna run away with it, but McKail put up one helluva fight. However, I still think Sage wrote the better pieces, and I have to put him through. If there's a place for play-ins, I'd like to nominate McKail right here and now, though. Pick: Clinton Sage Judge 5 Jacob McKail: The story was a little bland at the start, but after reading into it a bit I was impressed. Both pieces were equally good, and there's really nothing to complain about. Keeping the wedding ring after the scene in the middle of nowhere certainly makes me want to read on for the next round. Clinton Sage: I like how 'real' this feels. It's seemingly a sci-fi story, but the way you write makes it seem entirely possible that something of this nature could happen. The only thing I have to complain about is how much I've missed out by not reading your earlier work. This match was close due to the styles of both writers. On one hand, I have a great wrestling RP from McKail, something I value in this hobby considering it's scarce. Then I've got a great story from Sage, something I enjoyed reading immensely, but wasn't terribly wrestling related. I felt the only traces of wrestling in the story were the fact that the main characters, do in fact wrestle. Is this a bad thing? No. It's a difficult decision, and one of the closest matches I've judged all tournament so far. In fact, I've spent the last 9 hours trying to decide it. Pick: Jacob McKail. I'm not sure why, but in the end it came down to two factors. The 'If's'. McKail's story seemed more complete, Sage's contained a lot of information from previous things that would've helped me follow. Also, for some reason, I'm far more hooked to read McKail in the next round at this point. RESULT Jacob McKail d Clinton Sage, 3 votes to 2 SUMMARY - Violence Jack d John Covel, 3 votes to 0 - Jacob McKail d Clinton Sage, 3 votes to 2 BRACKET JUDGES Bobino James Andrew Pete Craig Max BRACKET FINAL - Violence Jack v Jacob McKail Piglet
