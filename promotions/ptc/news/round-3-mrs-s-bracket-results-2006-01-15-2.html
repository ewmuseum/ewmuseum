Round 3: Mrs S. Bracket Results

15 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
ANGELO DEVILLE V SPARTAN Spartan "the second chance..." Establishes point of view, establishes context of comparing previous opponents behavior, with a subtext of confidence in that the comparison is about the opponents reactions to losing to Spartan. Some dancing around the issue of identity, which places an okay kind of teaser, but the state of identity isn't so significant in the truest sense. As Spartan defines his opponents by behavior, likewise behavior defines identity more so than faces behind masks. We segue from the ring to the life at home concept, which I'll mention is a difficult task. Our primary interest in this tournament is the results, and to deliberately draw the reader away from tournament related things into the extraneous is a trip a reader could be reluctant to take. Oops, actually we go to Clinton Sage expressing what the author wants his readers to be feeling, a sense of frustrated curiosity regarding the identity of Spartan. The girl next door is a loaded description in what it suggests about women who arn't the girls next door, echoes of control, misogyny, mistrust, and fear. Contrary to this, there's the aspect of the crush and the feelings for a woman that are prioritized more so than the match. It's curious and it's interesting. What use does this character have for a beautiful woman? It's suggesting some codependency based around the ideas hinted at the the relationship will define this character's future in regard to the "second chance". Knowing Ryan, the vagueness tends to suggest something shocking and violent in the hidden history of Spartan, which would fit Spartan's idea of his mask hiding his identity and potentially hiding Spartan's identity from himself so that he can anonymously attempt to reestablish his morality by overwriting his own secret history by behaving in an admirable way as opposed to filthy deeds of his past. Some more defining characterists of Clinton are provided, with some interesting and helpful events that took place in regard to the serial killer storyline. Leaving us with some names to throw about and speculate on, in the frustrating and curious manner that Clinton holds for us. There's a lot of speculation that could be made about which direction Ryan plans to take this, but that's not such a big draw to me. I fail to find the story as frustrating or curious as Clinton does, and sadly, I can't recall information in regard to Jonathan Sail enough to piece it together. Regarding what's written: it's good, it's interesting. But my socks ain't knocked off, Ryan. Angelo Deville "no third chance" A nice strong set up with good use of structure. An honestly good sense of dramatics with the image and scene presenting itself by pieces and parts, with finally the importance of said scene delivered by the cub. It's good in how it's set up, we're revealed the cub at the end of a paragraph, and we telegraph wondering if it's dead like the others or not, and our wondering is strung along like it was on a string to the revelation. The cub lives. The cub follows the pattern for death, denial, etc. It's good and interesting, the revelations about the nature of lions and hyenas and their rivalry and hatred is authentic. The cub chooses a dying fight, which while not necessarily being unexpected, is still profound. -break- What's interesting here is precisely the comparison. This kid named Abraham is trying to find out about deville the way Clinton is pondering about Spartan. And the difference in presentation has to be deliberate. On one hand, we have Clinton expressing frustration while revealing little, and even when Clinton finds something out, we don't get to know because it's cliff-hung on us. Comparatively, Abraham's search doesn't rest around his feelings, but on the information that he doesn't have, the steps of the search which has always drawn in the reader in, even when it fails. It's again a backwards revelation. Is this Angelo? Abraham things so. The unreal trip is a good one, circumstantial and unreal, but being drawn into the experience of it forces the suspension of it. The discovery of a memory? HA HAHA I'll admit freely that I was suckered. And when he's saying fuck you. He's telling me to go fuck myself. I still have to laugh. And what's worse, he's talking about why I don't judge anymore. And unfortunately, it resonates. Homogeny, mediocrity, the untouchable issue is raised: our standards are based on our ideals and our enjoyment. So is the most enjoyable writing said to be the best? Or just the most liked writing? Who has the finest palate for determining the most delicious feast for the mind? The subtext of this suggests that Jeff started writing and found himself hating what he wrote. Be based on time, exhaustion, or actual innovation. And it raised a lot of questions: why am i doing this? What is it that I'm doing? Do you know why Jeff got the play-in? Because he exhaustively spent the time necessary to carefully evaluate the would be winners and losers, and then exhaustively spent the time calculating his assessment versus that of everyone else who predicted. How long did that shit take? Is he looking at himself in that mirror and thinking, what in the shit is wrong with me that I'm spending this much energy here? Writing shit he doesn't like? Dunno. I've speculated myself into a corner. So anyway, he busted loose. ===== The Decision: What was written, Jeff wrote better than Ryan. Use of language, structure drew you into Jeff's work more than Ryan's. What was said, Jeff said more than Ryan. Ryan's puppetry with his juxtaposed characters remains fascinating, but Jeff delivered the kung fu about the standard of excellence around here. Is Jeff playing a trick on me? On us? A trick inside another trick? Why not? Should I resent the implicit threats that I've been manipulated by Jeff, and judge against him? The unfortunate thing about judging is objectivity. You don't have it. Because judging is about YOU, what YOU like, what YOU want. Jeff took a risk in talking about this community and its judges, its standards. Jeff said "Fuck You" to me. I can smile about it through. I can drink it like a shot and say, "Fuck you too, Jeff. Fuck you for insulting my intelligence, fuck you for insinuating my incompetence, my lack of discretion and discernment, and fuck you for insulting all your readers." But Jeff still wins. As long is this contest is about merit, and as far as I'm concerend it is, Jeff will lose when someone writes better than him, not when he fucks me. Ryan didn't (fuck me or write better). So Jeff advances. Result: Angelo Deville d Spartan JORGE SAMUELSSON V ALEC LANCER Judge 1 Preparing for a War: Nice, simplistic setting and one that I enjoyed, it was nice to have a break from novel writing for once. It flowed pretty well to me although it did drag somewhat once the involvement of Sun Tzu came about. This is mainly due to it being something which seemed unnecessary and bloated, you could've established Jorge a lot more by him paraphrasing it and therefore kept the importance of the piece where it should be; your intentions. Nice overall though, just watch your spelling and grammar, that slowed down my reading of it once or twice. A Lesson and Understanding: Interesting follow up but perhaps a bit too distant from the first to really mesh well. It flowed pretty well again and showed a bit of personal growth in the character. There isn't as much to say on this one since there wasn't much wrong with it, but at the same time I had trouble getting into it with the nature of the story being very basic. Dealing with Disappointment: Short and to the point. This had some very irritating grammar right at the beginning which made it chug and that didn't help. The conversation with your brother just wasn't detailed enough I think and you could've been on to something far greater with that dialogue, something to work on for future rounds but you let it slip by. Overall only one of the pieces (Preparing for a War) impressed me enough to go back and read it for pure enjoyment. It showed a greater grasp of the character that you have at your disposal and so I'm giving Jorge the nod here on the basis of the possibilities with the character and his style being more fluid and polished. Pick: Jorge Samuelsson Judge 2 Dealing With Disappointment - by Alec Lancer I don't like starting out with a complaint, but I feel it needs to be adressed. You use your characters name WAYYYY too much. The word 'He' would suffice in most of these situations, and it'd help the flow out. As for the content, he defeated Aiden Jackson. And He's now, for some reason, $200,000 richer. His Brother calls, and they have an extremely short conversation. The brother comes for a visit and explains he DIDN'T bet the 200 G's, so.. Alec has nothing. While this creates tension between the two, it could have been cut down. A lot of the dialogue is repetitive. Then, as soon as it begins, it's over. He calls someone in his debt and requests they place a bet on him for the next round. To be Honest, this RP didn't do much for me. It didn't seem to have a point, and it wasn't really 3rd Round material. Preparing For War!! - by Jorge Samuelsson The Writing is good, thats the first thing I notice. Good Flow, even though it's a little confusing to someone whose not been briefed on Norse.. stuff. It seemed to be no more than a Trash Talk piece, but not bad in any way. The character is interesting, and the only suggestion I can make is to keep the dialogue in character. Some of the phrases were a little off base from the dramatic Viking-Style speech. A Lesson and Understanding! - by Jorge Samuelsson We begin on the beach with an older Gentleman with a more youthful presentation.. This is good, it hooks me, makes me want to read more. It seems Jorge has returned to his homeland to learn about an ancient city, not a bad theme at all. The writing is pretty good, the story is pretty good, but I'm sure you can do better. The character itself is great, and you've developed it greatly this round. Pick: Jorge Samuelsson He had the 2 on 1 advantage, and I thought his RPs had a little more substance. So he gets the nod. Jorge Samuelsson d Alec Lancer, 2 votes to 0 JIMMY BONG V CHANDLER TSONDA Judge 1 Nobody Does it Worser - Episode 4: You know, not having the time usually to read half as much as I'd like to, I'll be honest in saying I'd never read any of Bong's stuff before. But damn was that funny. Sure it was cliche in places and you've probably seen it in numerous sketches before but Bong attaches his own creativity and originality to make the jokes his own. This man has the timing which is very hard to achieve (I know I don't have it) so this was a great read. Nobody Does it Worser - Episode 5: More of the same goodness and this is looking to be a very good evening for writing. This flowed just as well as the first one, liking how you intersperse the characters and their respective quirks without becoming blatantly obvious about it and disrupting the flow of the writing. The Patriarch: Reflection and Inflammation: Very nice piece here and one that did its damndest to show how far gone Tsonda really is from wanting a reconciliation with his father, and I think it worked. Personally I liked the offhand comment between the father and the party host as it said an awful lot more about life in general than you'd at first think. Top notch insight and overall nothing really bad I can say about it. The Patriarch: Staring at the Sun: Wow, that was one hell of a follow up and it just blew me away. This worked well on so many levels and the song just topped it off for the finishing touch. Just a great, great piece and there is nothing that was wrong with this. I want more. So how the hell do I choose? Two immensely talented writers who excel at different styles of writing; one comedy, one more gripping and serious. Both are excellent but I can't pick them both now can I? This is a ridiculously close decision and I'm sure that any other judge would feel the same way. Being forced to come to a winner, I'm going with Tsonda and this is down to the story element between the two guys. Bong's is great no doubt but I think Tsonda's have more oomph and coherence going through them that work much better for me anyway. If you had've put Bong against anyone else I'm sure it'd be hard to find him losing to anyone else but Tsonda barely pips this one. Chandler Tsonda in a ridiculously close battle. Pick: Chandler Tsonda. Judge 2 Jimmy Bong – first roleplay I'm new to Jimmy Bong, and “Johnny English” is one of my favourite films – I presume you’ve seen it? If not, make sure you do. I'm trying to decide whether you’re shamelessly ripping it off OR picking up where Rowan Atkinson left off OR doing the same parody without any knowledge of it already having been done in almost EXACTLY the same manner. It’s nicely enough written... it all seems a little UNAMUSING, though – with all the hype surrounding you, I’d expected a little more. I'm not laughing out loud, and I keep thinking this would look a lot better on TV, which isn’t a good sign. Your writing is coming across as stage directions for a comedy screenplay rather than comedy in its own right. “Like a stand-up comedian who isn’t funny” is also springing to mind, which can’t bode well. All your mini-jokes (names of characters and such) are something I’d have been entertained by aged nine, but mature readers aren’t so easily amused and you need something in there amongst the ocean of puns to actually hold it all together. Anthony Horowitz without the storyline. One dimension to everything. It’s skin-deep. There are also little irritations – I assume the author is American? Never would a Brit refer to “jaywalking”, and if your lead character is British it seems a little OFF to have “metre” and “sulphur” spelt the American ways in the narrative. You seem to use words like “bloody” and “scoff” just to get a kick out of it, and while this may be hilarious for an American looking in, it’s not good enough simply to use British vocabulary to make a Brit laugh. These aren’t ERRORS, as such, but just minor things that make me want to pick further holes in your work. I feel like screaming at you to leave parodying the Brits to the Brits. Things get better towards the end. We see some depth to Bong. But the word “fiend” still isn’t funny. And the piece seems to be unnecessarily long. I'm moving onto Tsonda’s first now, because I don’t feel I can take any more Bong right now. That speaks volumes. You’re a good writer, but I'm just not feeling it, my f(r)iend. Chandler Tsonda – first roleplay As with Bong, I'm not experienced with the works of Chandler Tsonda, so you’ve got a clean slate to impress me from, and early indications are good. I'm already interested in the narrator’s relationship with his father, and particularly liked the little touch of working Angelo Deville into your popular culture reference, combining our wrestling world with the real world. I like the whole “zoned out” style of writing. While we get little on what’s actually going on around the narrator, this actually allows us to empathise far more with the character himself and what he is feeling. We’ve all been there in church, but not with a dad who’s never been around. What is confusing me slightly is the age of the narrator; initially I presumed mid-teens but surely not if he’s ordering a limo? Considering the possibility of being chastised for swearing doesn’t really wash with trying to cop a feel and “Don’t you know who I am?” And overall – it’s good. Lots happens, yet nothing happens. It seems like a traipse rather than a journey through the narrator’s mind, events taking a backseat to the workings of the mind and you pull it off nicely. Jimmy Bong – second roleplay I'm enjoying Bong and Lang, but the constant tilde abuse is a mite irritating. Are you von Doom in disguise? I have half-laughed a couple of times, though – the “neck size” excuse and Bong’s “unrefinery” comment – which is an improvement. It’s a decent enough piece, as a whole... but I'm not sure what you’re trying to DO. It’s a parody, but it’s not funny. I'm getting the entire ‘stage directions’ feeling again. If I'm judging by whether I’d voluntarily read another of your roleplays, I'm afraid you’re not scoring high. Chandler Tsonda – second roleplay Quality writing once more – good exploration of the father/son relationship and a neat insight into Tsonda’s total lack of care for anyone other than himself. I liked the “January” touch and the banana kisses. The song, though, didn’t seem necessary, and apart from a few choice lyrics struck me as largely irrelevant. Conclusion I’ve got to put Tsonda through. I'm enjoying his work – I’ll be reading more of his in PRIME, if I have time – and to be honest, if I wanted Bond-parody entertainment I’d go and watch “Johnny English”. Bong’s handler... I think it’s time for a new gimmick. Pick: Chandler Tsonda Chandler Tsonda d Jimmy Bong, 2 votes to 0 ROUND 4 MATCHUP Angelo Deville v Jorge Samuelsson v Salaok v Chandler Tsonda Two highest placed advance to bracket final. Brackets
