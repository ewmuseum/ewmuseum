From The Archives -- Common Judging Misconceptions.

18 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I've seen through various threads from the time GTT6 has popped up, that people seem to think that there's some sort of magical formula that judges are looking for. I'd like to debunk those myths, if I may, and perhaps give you a bit of a glimpse into the mind of a judge. MISCONCEPTION #1 - There's no way I'm going to make it out of the first round. THE TRUTH - Truth is, if you go into anything with an attitude like that, you're absolutely going to fail. If you have any doubts about yourself, your character, your writing, or anything, talk to someone you respect about it, and see if you can find something that you can easily improve. You have a 50/50 chance in every round from the get go. Capitalize on those odds as best you can. Truth is, no matter what happens, this is just like any other sport/hobby. On any given day, at any given time, with two competitors, you could see different results every time you ran it. The fact that this is a one-off tournament is moot. If you lose, you lose. Plain as that. You win, you win. It can't get any simpler than that. MISCONCEPTION #2 - Judges are looking for a certain formula inside your RP. It's not what you want to write, it's what they want to read that matters. THE TRUTH - This is partially correct. There absolutely is a formula that certain judges look for when judging roleplays. For instance, I look for things I can identify with. It doesn't matter what it is. If I can identify with it, that's a point in it's favor. If I can't get into the story, because it's something I don't understand, or simply dislike, I'll move to judge the RP on it's technical merit. Other judges may put more into the technical side (technical side being sentence structure, grammar, word selection, etc.) than they do the identifiable side of things. However, all judges are looking at the following criteria: - Ease of reading - How easy is it to get into this story? Is this a stand alone story? Do I have to go back and read this character's history to understand just what the crap is going on here? - Spelling, Punctuation and Grammar - Does the writer use "your" and "you're" correctly? Does s/he know the difference between "they're" and "their"? I before E except after C? - Story - Is the story interesting? Moderately unique? Identifiable? Memorable? MISCONCEPTION #3 - Dude, there's no way I can write 6000 words. I'm lucky to hit 2000. There's no way I can compete in GTT THE TRUTH - Word count and length don't matter one iota. Remember, that the longer you write, the more chances you take to screw up your story, fatally. Take a look at Clyde Walkins vs. Seymour Almasy from GTT5. Both are good writers, Walkins came in absolutely unheralded, and won the PRIME Universal Championship around the same time as GTT. Walkins wrote 45 pages of a meandering, somewhat crazy RP, while Almasy did his usal thing. In the end, Almasy proved to write the better story, but, it was definately a case of Walkins shooting himself in the foot by writing so much, and having little to no point to the story. It seemed that it was long merely for the sake of being long. 6000 words are just as good as 2000 words, which are just as good as 18000 words, so long as you get your story told. MISCONCEPTION #4 - Judges hate this non-reality shit. I'm not entering because I happen to handle a werewolf/vampire/frankenstein/time traveller/CIA operative/Japanese Kung Fu Expert with a knife in his boot. The Judges will hate me. THE TRUTH - You're completely incorrect. Yes, I will admit, that those stories tend to get tired. Those stories are pretty hard to get right. But, never assume, simply because two people in one thread about it (both of whom have never judged any previous GTT, or much at PTC to begin with) said they hated them. From a personal standpoint, I don't get into the above characters/storylines related to them, simply because I'm not into that kind of writing. I've never been a fan of scary movies, or the supernatural. However, I do know enough about them to know what's a good story, and what isn't. If you don't bring your story, you've already lost. Simple as that. MISCONCEPTION #5 - I'm new here. I'm not a name. No one's going to pick me to beat someone based on that fact alone. I'm a sitting duck. THE TRUTH - Categorically untrue. Back in GTT3, aside from people in FSW, no one really knew just how good of a writer Jeff W. (Angelo Deville) really was. Sure, he and I wrote some good stuff in FSW together, but, no one knew if that was product of environment, or if it was simply Jeff being as good as he proved himself to be. Same holds true for Dr. Curiosity. I fully admit that I had no clue who he was going into GTT4, other than the fact that he handled in XUW as Ian "The Fool" Fetzer previously, and that he was a pretty good writer. GTT4 became his. And again, before Super Series, no one really gave Rich Rollins much of a shot, other than people in GCW and the UWF who'd been around him, and knew just how good of a writer Andrew really is. GTT isn't just about taking those people established within PTC and making them bigger stars. It's as much about the new people coming in, and wowing others who'd never heard of them before. MISCONCEPTION #6 - Yeah. I know a judge that doesn't like me. And I know that sometime, he's going to get one of my matches, and he's going to job the fuck out of me. So, fuck it. I'm not entering. THE TRUTH - People in this game have a loooong memory. I still remember the email address of my first fedhead back in 1996. I still have RPs I wrote as Big Poppa Pete, and I haven't handled him in 8 years. I remember people I've fedded with across the board, and I remember people that have pissed me off too. But, this is GTT. And despite my personal feelings about anyone, I am here to be an unbiased judge. I am here to simply do my task, which is to pick which RP is better than the other based on whatever I feel is better. I'm only human (partially), and I do make mistakes. Absolutely, I make mistakes. But, 95% of the time, I think I'm justified in putting Wrestler X over Wrestler Y, based on his story. And the 5% left, 4.99% it's a coin flip situation. During GTT, my personal feelings are put aside. Period. End of story. There's no place for them here in this tournament, and honestly, [name withheld] isn't about to put up with a judge with a vendetta. Most of the attacks are thinly-veiled, and the administrators have been around long enough to know the difference. And simply put, we will not stand for it. MISCONCEPTION #7 - I write comedy. Comedy doesn't do well in PTC at all. THE TRUTH - This is false. All the previous GTT winners have used comedy as an element of their stories. However, going von Doom with your story (von Doom (Vhar-guh) v - to write stories completely over the top, simply for the sake of doing so) will probably result in an early exit. MISCONCEPTION #8 - I use big words. A lot of them. Big words get shit on. THE TRUTH - Untrue. Big words are fine... within reason. Simply put (and this is personal opinion only, not the opinion of every judge) I don't want to feel like I'm reading something out of a "How To Write" book, or calculus homework on how to define Barrett's Principle of Fundamental Algebra as it Pertains to Binomial Integers Greater than 6. If you want to start your roleplay with a line such as "The crimson streaks danced through the skies wrought with clouds, as the last tendrils of the day's allocation of sunlight crept through the horizon", that's fine. But, make sure the words you do use make sense. In other words, don't be like Chuckie Sullivan[1] and use big words, simply to impress, or, think you're going to impress. Big words are fine. They're great. They're wonderful. In moderation. MISCONCEPTION #9 - I'm not entering because the judging process is broken. THE TRUTH - PTC has an open door policy. That means, that if you have a problem with anything or anyone, you have the right to come to an admin, and say something about it. In that vein, if you have a problem with judging, and think it's broken, suggest a new system. Before Ben M. started Uber Judging in UTT, most comments you would get would be "It was okay. I liked it, but, I liked the other guy better." This year, I am proposing a policy to the judges as soon as the judging forum opens to the judge volunteers that will state the following: For every comment you make that finds something wrong with an RP, you must make a comment that gives constructive criticism. This way, when you write an RP that doesn't win, you're not simply going to get a list of things wrong with said RP. You'll get comments on how (based on the opinion of the judge) you can improve your judging to do beter next year, or even in your home fed. With those comments, you can take them to heart, or, you can simply tell the judge to stuff it. It's up to you, but, keep in mind that most of the judges this year are going to be people who are pretty well respected in PTC. Their opinions are worth something, even to think about. --------------------------- Good luck to all of you in GTT6, and I hope some of you take these comments to heart, and realize what was said above. FOOTNOTE 1: Chuckie Sullivan is Ben Affleck's character in Good Will Hunting. The exchange when he goes to an interview as Will Hunting is priceless in his own stupidity as he tells the panel "--These circumstances are mitigated. Right now. They're mitigated." Sort of. But, not entirely.
