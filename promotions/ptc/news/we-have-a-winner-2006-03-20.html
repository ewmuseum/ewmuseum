We have a winner!

20 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RICH "THE RENEGADE" ROLLINS V SHANTY JONES Judge 1 Pick: Rich Rollins Comments exclusive to PTC Radio Judge 2 I could break down each RP and Pick em' apart, but both sets blended greatly so I've decided to judge them each as a whole. Rich Rollins: It starts off good. I find myself completely lost while reading, but it a positive light. RPs are so predictable now, it's good to see what I thought would happen, didn't. I fully expected Almasy to bust in and clear Rollins name, so when he was sentenced to death I was fully shocked. Couldn't wait to get reading again to see how he'd get himself out of this mess. We start off strong again, this time with a small piece of backstory showing how ruthless and unforgiving the Warden is. Good development from the start, since it counts so much to know down the road. It started off slow from there, with a lot of seemingly useless pieces such as the fight and the convo with Fenton but those proved needed elements later in the story, so I can't count it against you really. Lastly we have Rollins in solitary. Obviously something is up, he can't be killed right? NO! I keep waiting for him to escape. Use the steak knife and his celebrity with the good guard to bust out, somehow. Nope, they lead him to the chair.. and he lives? I didn't know quite how, but as it was explained later, the method was genius. Not having him escape and getting his name cleared was a suitable ending for a great tournament long story. Shanty Jones: I'm a fan of science fiction, so I can say I was a big fan of this piece. But it had a couple problems I need to address. When it comes to wrestling, the DNA experimentation and the whole 'Everything relies on this match' stuff doesn't do it for me. How did The Ripper get Rollins DNA? If Shanty has the power to mentally stop Rollins, how is this even a match? I haven't been following this entire story, so the 'Frank' character is completely lost on me, and I found it superfluous. I also must say, Shanty doesn't seem to be the main character at all. He seems to rely far too much on all your others, who seem more interesting. The story itself was good, I don't have anything bad to say about it. You got this far for a reason, and I did find it entertaining. No mistake there. Pick: I won't lie, I've judged pretty much every round after 2, and this was the closest match, as it should be. But after a half an hour of thought I've decided that the winner ought to be: Rich Rollins. Judge 3 This is the finals of the GTT5, and it shows with the quality of the works. First, there is Rich Rollins, a story that almost seems to be pulled out of a movie. And then there is Shanty Jones, which intricacies are woven with ease. I'd like to start this off by saying that both sets of work were great for different reasons. Comparing the two are hard...bery hard...but good. Starting off, Shanty Jones. A conclusion for the whole story has been given (at least in my eyes), and it's great. The whole moral issues Shanty Jones struggles with are great. At times, I think you have too many characters fucking around the whole time, but it somehow works (90% of the time). Renegade Rollins almost seems to add another chapter to this whole thing, but the thing is written brilliantly. Things are kept secret and revealed at perfect times, keeping all the RPs super readable. The whole thing focuses around Rollins, too, which is a mega-plus. While both pieces are great, I have to give the nod to Rollins. His piece...is about 3 times better. Just how well it is written and how smooth it is gives it the nudge. Jones gets wordy and confusing at times, and when the focus sometimes strays away from the main character, it gets ugly. Which Rollin's didn't. Pick: Rollins Judge 4 Shanty Jones: Rp 1: I have... Okay let's start off with the obvious....reading quality. There was a part or two where I had to reread it to make sure I understood what was going but that is not neccesarily a bad thing. Overall the story drew me in and held on like a vice. I like the way this one uses the different characters and enjoyed the ' life given' to each of them...distinct personalities for very different people. On the technical side I noted a couple of spelling mistakes likely missed due to the chaos in irl life( yes I know he is looking for a new flat), but nothing that couldn't have been fixed by a simple spell check. All in all one thing stuck out in my mind due to the lack of having read his previous work until now...who is Frank? Rp 2: Your blood.. Again...this was a fantastic read, even more so than the first. I was swept up by the tale and never once lost my focus or place. The question from one was partially answered here in number two. That fact alone has given me even more want to read number three. I enjoy the spin on the use of quantum physics here, giving the story a sci fi kind of feel while making it believable. The cliff hanger ending was perfectly executed drawing you to number three without a hitch. Kudos on that. Again, on the technical side there were a couple of errors....SPELL CHECK! Rp 3: Inside my heart Okay so this is getting rather redundant. Once again..I was riveted by the storyline. I finally understood who Frank was without knowing exactly what had happened to him..I knew what had happened to him. ( Yes..cliche..but you get the point)I especially enjoyed the twist regarding The Ripper in the end. Was he a bad man? Yes. Did he deserve what happened to him before? Yes. But in the end it showed a more human side...and though he did it for self gain. He was a hero of sorts...perhaps an anti hero. Despite the pleasure of the story as a whole...there were still some errors in grammer and such. Though this time...I don't think I noticed any spelling ones. Rich Rollins Rp 1: Welcome to Camp Cocytus: The Price of Pride As if there was a doubt...Rollins is still on a roll. The story was easily followed whether or not someone has read its predecessors. To watch as the might Rollins falls is like a dream come true for me. Personally I hate the guy. In true Rollins fashion he delivered a fantastic story with that hard edge he never seems to lack in character. On the technical side..damn you. I read it three times and couldn't find a spelling error anywhere. Rp 2: Welcome to Camp Cocytus: Prison Bitch Color me disappointed when Rollins didn't get raped anywhere in this continuance of his story. Ah well, anyway, again damn good read. He continues to astound me with the continuity of his tales taking the first couple I did have the chance to read and tying them into these last few like a nice little bow. The handler did his research...impressive. I like the warden and how he was portrayed as a corrupted man who may have, at one time, been a reasonable and law abiding individual. The way he forced Rollins to relinquish all his assets only to tell him he wouldnt get his only request AND moved up his fry date..classic. Fry the asshole....Technically speaking, well..there is nothing. Rp 3: Welcome to Camp Cocytus: The End of Anarchy Rollins...outsmarting the system? Say it ain't so! This particular piece had me turning a bit green around the edges with the description of what he had to deal with in solitary. Nasty..just plain nasty. But then again..it suited Rollins considering his personality. Showing the humanity of one prison guard sharply contrasting that of the other is nicely done. When I started this entry I only had one thought...How will Rollins get out of this one? I thought he may have written himself into a corner. Guess not. I found it rather ingenius; how he managed to orchestrate his 'legal' escape. This was a fitting end to his part in this tournament. Good job. Overall: As far as entertainment goes both of these series were superior. I have a hard time picking a favorite. Thought different as far as scenarios both men faced the same kind of foe. I like to call it '' the man" or simply put karma. Both were written in similar style, using the shift between scene constantly taking you back and forth in a way that kept you reading. In short this decision comes down to technical superiority. While Shanty did a fantastic job in keeping me entertained and even leading me to want to follow his work more closely from now on...he falls short on the technical side. Spelling errors and gramatical mistakes are ultimately the fall here. If he had proofread a bit more closely and perhaps even a couple of times...I can gaurantee this decision would have come to a coin toss.
