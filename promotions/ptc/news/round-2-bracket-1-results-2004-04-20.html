Seymour Almasy (GW) v Pietro Geist (FA) Judge 1 Very well written RPs from Pietro Geist, which is obvious from the beginning, meaning this match is obviously going to give me a headache trying to decide a winner. The theological and philosophical rantings and ravings are always entertaining, and Geist's are extremely well done. The intro's are easily my favorite part of the RPs, though, and the only parts really pertaining to the tournament at all. As enjoyable as the beginnings are, and as well written as both RPs were, the rest of them are ultimately uninteresting for me. RPs like his will always come down to the personal preference of the judges, and long, more-or-less pointless, fantasy stories with no relevance to the point-at-hand have never appealed to me, and with a flat, emotionless feel to them, Geist's RPs were a disapointment following the great intro's. Almasy doesn't have the same talent as Geist in his technical writing, he does have far more ability in being entertaining and relevant than his opponent. His approach in grounding what would normally be fantasty deeply into reality makes his RPs instantly more appealing than Geist's, which would be much better if he played the line between fantasy and reality more than simply going completely off the deep end into mythical clashes between angels and demons. Almasy's blend between theoligical and philosophical debates, idiocy, and far more realistic subjects works very well in both of his RPs, as his ability to always play his rather unique gimmick into what would otherwise be stereotypical situations comes off as well as it always has. Seymour, a character obviously drawn off of Don Quixote, commands a gusting wind to do battle just as his predecessor would challenge the windmills that loomed in the distance; that mix between idiocy and intelligence has always been what makes Seymour such a great character. Geist's RPs are well written, but they're still sadly generic and forgettable despite that. Almasy's are original, entertaining, intelligent, and progressive for the tournament and the characters, where Geist, even if he's a better technical writer, cannot claim half of that. Judge 2 This was by far one of the toughest I've ever judged. Both have great styles, but I'm giving it to Almasy because his pieces linked the tournament with his story, home life AND history. Seymour Almasy defeats Pietro Geist 2 votes to 0. (third judge MIA) Horazon (FAW) d The Fallen Fae (XUW) Horazon advances to the bracket finals. CW1 brackets
