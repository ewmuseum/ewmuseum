Round 1 Bracket 1 Results inside

5 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I'm still short some judging results for brackets 2, 3 and 4, however here's a little something to keep you going. Congratuations to the winners, and commiserations to the losers. Thanks to everyone who made the time to post roleplays, the quality of a lot of this work was a big suprise to me. Anyway, here ya go: Bracket 1 Match 1: Matt Jackson (RPW) vs Seymour Almasy (GW) vs Lorenzo Guevera (NGW) Seymour Almasy (GW) defeated Matt Jackson (RPW) 2 votes to 1 * Guevera (NGW) no show. Judge 1 Jackson displayed a very dark side of the human psyche. Stooping to the lowest of the lows. The story to me followed one event of anguish, and followed him to rock bottom. It displayed his depression very well, and although I had never read any of his work before, I managed to get a good feel for the character. It was also a more abstract piece. The problem with this was, I found it hard to follow. I LOVED the twist at the end where his ex girlfriend walked in with Matt self harming, after jacking up and injecting heroin. Almasy on the other hand showed similar signs of depression. With Almasy however, you had Aerith's optimism to balance it out. It twisted to a display to show how much he loves aerith, that he would retire after this tournament so she could follow her dreams. It was well written and flowed nicely. Both RPs had their strong points. But the fact that I found it hard to follow Jackson's really hurt him. I also found that on occassion, he over complicated things, and over described on a few parts to the extent where I wanted to skip the paragraph. Almasy's didn;t have that problem. So I give my vote to Almasy. Judge 2 Here we have a match where both guys are fighting with a lack of confidence in their abilities. Jackson is intimidated by Almasy's accolades, almost crippled by the fear that he doesn't belong on the same stage. Turning to heroin to help him cope with the stress was an interesting approach that he uncovered beautifully. I was a little surprised that he actually succumbed to the fear and injected it rather than overcoming the temptation, but I imagine it's setting up a rebound of sorts. The writing could use a proofread, but aside from some technical glitches it was very well done. The only part that put me off was the ex arriving at the end. I know cliffhangers are all the rage, but her return didn't seem to fit with the rest of the story, and there seemed to be no point to it happening this round other than to shout "Pick me to win and find out what happens next!" In a word, the ending was anticlimatic. Instead of dealing with what the whole roleplay had built up to, which is what I was on the edge of my seat for, he shamelessly plugged the next with something completely out of left field. Instead of intriguing me, it derailed the intrigue already stirring. Almasy seems to have a similar plan. I don't know if the handler is simply tired of writing as Almasy or not, but throwing out the retirement stipulation the way he did screams "If I don't get my way I'm taking my ball and going home." Aside from that, the roleplay was pretty good. The introduction was great, the suspense leading up to what was on Seymour's mind was actually suspenseful, and the writer establishes a strong feeling for Seymour's passion. The dialogue was a little loquacious, but that's not always a bad thing. Both men had a strong direction, both men can write, and both men are out to prove themselves. I wish they hadn't resorted to what I consider cheap manipulation tactics, but there's nothing that can be done about it now. I only hope that Seymour's reason for threatening retirement is a lack of drive to continue the character and not a guileful ultimatum for the judges. Either way, after long and agonizing consideration, I'm going to have to go with my gut and raise Jackson's hand. Judge 3 Matt Jackson First time I’ve read any of Jackson’s work. It took a little while for me to warm to this one and my attention kept wandering, but once it passed a certain point I couldn’t take my eyes from the screen, right up until the end. A great story of inner demons, let down by the very last line. It came across like a kid getting caught eating candy right before dinner rather than something a far more serious than that. Seymour Almasy Segments of trash talk dressed up as medieval. The underlying theme of Almasy’s story isn’t all that different to that presented by Jackson – battling with inner demons and fears. His writing is strong enough that I almost gave him the vote while reading because he was telling me that he was going to win. If both had put up two roleplays, Jackson may well have taken this one, he’s certainly got the skills to do so, and his work in parts was more concise than Almasy’s which at times dragged a bit, but as it was, his story seemed unfinished, while Almasy’s work was self contained and didn’t need a second installment. My vote goes to Almasy. Match 2: Blaise "Hazard" McClancy (FA) v Adam Syxx (FA) v Pietro Geist (FA) Pietro Geist (FA) defeated Blaise "Hazard" McClancy (FA) 2 votes to 0 * Adam Syxx (FA) no show. Judge 1 Geist's first roleplay was definitely the best of the match. The scenario with the bartender was too contrived for my liking, as was the man's flaccid will, but aside from that the roleplay was head and shoulders above the others. The introduction of the character was interesting, his contempt for humankind (aka coffin-stuffers) was tangible, and he employed effective imagery by using familiar references. I wasn't fond of how Dinah went from the fat and dumpy suicidal chick to a femme fatale of unsurpassed beauty in the same paragraph without explanation, nor was I won over by the contrived reflection about how McClancy would react to his offer of a sack of potatoes for his soul -- the point of the mockery was established through the bartender, and everything beyond seemed redundant. Overall, it was a good roleplay. I really got a good feeling for the character and his unmitigating, unrepentant depravity. McClancy responded by no-selling the entire premise of Geist's character. Blaise is obviously all business, a traditionalist in every sense of the business. I think that hurts him as much as it helps him, as I'd much rather get to know the character through anecdotes or experiences than dialogue, especially his own. Showing him working out, defining his moniker, and having him repeat that he's all about wrestling gives me exactly and only that: a hollow dime-a-dozen wrestling enthusiast with a catchy nickname. Reinforcing that monotony, the writing seemed ineffective. Everything is stated plainly and even the verb choice is mundane. Mixing it up with onomatopoeia, metaphors, similes, analogies, etc. would give the roleplay a more dramatic flare. Geist's second roleplay was a shell of his first. He bluntly refuses to accept McClancy's challenge to show how he could deal with him aside from a veiled allusion that McClancy's style is like Catholicism. The implication was effective and consistent with his approach, but I still would have rather had him deal with a man without faith instead of a man of the cloth, which McClancy most obviously is not. Maybe it was a sublime way of suggesting that he's responsible for Syxx's no-show. Regardless, without mention of some sort of brain-washing or brain-devouring power embedded in Geist's words, all of his conversations are wholly unbelievable. Conjuring the cliched ten-year-old boy was a cheap and weak attempt at humour that served the opposite purpose. McClancy's final was the second best roleplay of the round. He came out and ruthlessly tore apart Geist's gimmick, effectively arguing that his powers would be useless against a man like him, especially in the ring. I'm inclined to agree. I'm sure Geist would be, too, especially considering the fact that he stated quite plainly that he wouldn't be using his powers in the tournament, since he wanted to test himself along the same guidelines as everyone else. The primary story here is of two guys no-selling each other in their own way. Geist subtly mocks McClancy's style but avoids confronting him directly while McClancy rails against being ignored by tearing down Geist's gimmick and daring him to talk a little trash. Neither handler seems to be genuinely paying attention to the other, though. They play off one another a little bit, but key aspects are swept under the rug for no discernible reason. The styles contrast so sharply it's almost impossible to compare them, and neither man made a strong case for victory at the expense of his opponents. Almost on the merit of the first roleplay alone I'm awarding the match to Pietro Geist. McClancy gave it a good shot, but to my way of thinking, talking smack about how the other guy's gimmick doesn't faze you won't win you a match, especially when that gimmick only comes in to play outside the realm of the wrestling ring. This is about wrestling, yes, but also writing and story development. If I would have gotten a little more of a feel for who McClancy is, and he seemed to be going somewhere other than the ring, he likely would have won. Aiming the final roleplay at Geist's heart instead of his head might have won it was well. As it stands, Geist inched him out with writing, tact, and intrigue. Judge 2 A VERY tough match to call, but in this case. I'm goin to hand this one to Geist, unfotunatley a no-show on Syxx's part limited this bout to the two competitors. Both characters put in very detailed roleplays, and in reading the Geist Roleplays i read a style of writing i don't remember having come across before, and it most certainly impressed me. In all honesty that was probably the deciding factor in this matchup from my point of veiw, no discredit to McClancy who put out 2 solid roleplays but I am going to give Geist my vote to move forward to the next round. Match 3: Primetime (WMW) v The Fallen Fae (XUW) v Christian Crawford (RPW) The Fallen Fae (XUW) wins by default due to no-showing by Primetime (WMW) and Crawford (RPW) Match 4: Horazon (FAW) v Larry The Legionnaire (XUW) Horazon (FAW) d Larry the Legionnaire (XUW) 2 votes to 1. Judge 1 Horazon's roleplay, like his sentences, was unnecessarily long and ripe with superfluous information. The roleplay leaves a lot of questions without answers, as well, like what happened when he was racing for the river and everything went dark, and who Michael was. Obviously the point of all the memories was to highlight the trauma that plagues his every waking moment, but how that fits the tournament and how he plans to deal with it is left unbreached. Some of the writing is powerful, but to no evident purpose. The roleplay tells me that Horazon has had a terrible life and he understandably has trouble dealing with it, but that's where the information stops. I'm left feeling unfulfilled. The background Larry provided was helpful in determining who he is, as was the dialogue. The supporting cast left a lot to be desired, though. Al isn't funny even in a pathetic, slapstick, comical way, and Bailey has all the depth of an irrigation ditch. I was pleased that Larry was determined to focus on the match, but in light of this being a non-televised tournament, I think his time would have been better spent addressing his opponent than thinking up lines for on-air promos. Some of the "horizon" stuff was funny, but not overly so. Neither guy gave any time to addressing how they could win the match, literally or symbolically. In fact, both practically did the opposite. In crowning a winner I'm looking forward to the future. I imagine Larry would continue to engage his "sidekicks" in next-to-meaningless banter while they all marvelled about how he won. Horazon seems to have something more in mind, a direction if you will, and if he can relate his story to his opponents or at least the tournament, he has the potential to do well. It's with that in mind that I give Horazon the nod. Judge 2 Here it was a nice match, I would of loved if Horazon turned out a second one then that would of been an even better match, in the end Larry the Legionairre took the win, I found the rp's a joy to read and a few places I laughed, always a good thing. Judge 3 Horazon Excellent narrative style, and amazing description. I could imagine this being read as part of an early 20th century radio play. Part of the problem I have with it though, after a while, it got a little hard to retain focus on it, as if it was too steady, if you can have such a thing. I perked up again when Richard started using the gun, which was a good time for me to be doing that, as it was at that point I understood what you were attempting to do. Focusing on an element of your character’s history to give people here a feel for who he is came off well, but at the expense of where you actually were and what you were trying to achieve, which was to actively defeat Larry. Larry the Legionnaire Great job giving us the skinny on who Larry is, within reason. A bit all over the place. At first I thought to myself, why is this guy talking about the tag team contest in a CW tournament? Then, finding multiple subtle appearances of Rock catchphrases was like playing find the hidden Mickeys in Disney movies (not that bad, but there were at least two, if not more). After a while, I warmed to the comic writing style and got some good laughs, however found the representation of the support characters more annoying and repetitive than of value. I guess I’m getting too old for fart jokes, if that’s possible. This one is quite tough, its like comparing chalk and cheese in styles, both authors handled their weapon of choice exceptionally well, even if they did steer away from the fact that they were in a tournament outside of their usual environment. If Larry had only done one, I would have said that Horazon spanked him, but the second one from Larry evened things up. I’m going to give this one to Horazon purely on the basis of annoyance factor over the multiple fart jokes from Larry. I understand that this is bread and butter material for that character, but as this is the first time I’ve read Larry’s work, it didn’t win me over. CW1 brackets
