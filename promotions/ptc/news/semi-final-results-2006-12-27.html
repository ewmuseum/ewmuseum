Semi Final Results!

27 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
AGENTDASH V EMBER Judge 1 Good efforts by both writers here. I think what probably hurt AgentDash here is that the RP was something of a "transitional" piece in the overall storyline (at least that's how it seemed to me), dealing with the aftermath of AWC's closing and starting a new job with the FBI. Decent cliffhanger ending to lead into the second RP. Ember's piece hooked me in more, though. I particularly liked the 26-years-ago flashback scene, very descriptive and emotional. I give Ember the win here; he faces some very stiff competition in the final if he passes this round, and I don't doubt he'll pull out all the stops should that be the case. Pick: Ember Judge 2 AgentDash *From the off it seems like you're trying a little too hard to mix in long words and overly complicate sentences. It doesn't quite come off perfectly. *Good intro though, with the AWC information grounding Dash and giving him substance. *The story seems a little implausible, even as far as e-fed storylines go. CIA gent becomes wrestler, gets suspected of murder and then gets offered a job by the FBI to investigate it? Oddness abounds. *The dialogue at times seems a little stilted. Perhaps try reading it out loud to yourself sometimes, to see if it is the kind of thing that you would be likely to say in a normal conversation. *Some of the descriptive work is good, without being overbearing. It allows you to create a scene well without taking forever. *The ending is a cliffhanger, as these things often are, but it seems a little 'jerky'... a little too quick. Ember * Interesting intro. The narrator comes across as a condescending little hateful bastard, but it's good characterisation (though if he's trying to be smart, asking why a rhetorical question is called a question is not a smart thing to do). * The past story of Joey and Mike was very confusing. In action sequences be very careful not to get things mixed up (at one point you transposed the names and I had to read it several times to get what was happening). At the end I wasn't entirely sure what was going on due to the slightly jumbled nature of the sentences. *The scene in the restaurant from your previous piece is a little confusing too, and would probably be completely odd to someone who hadn't read your last round. I was struggling to remember who was who and why that guy had a gun and why he was angry with Ember *The mirror scene was a great saving grace. I can tell you've had this in your mind for a while now. The revelation, whilst not spelled out entirely, is pretty much given here, and it's a good payoff. The writing really kicked up a gear. I think you write emotions and characters better than you do action *The ending returned to confusion-ville. I got what had happened the second time around, but really, don't expect to write an action scene and expect us all to get what happens when you never mention two of the most important characters in the scene (Rick and David) anywhere else bar the final line, and where there was no real explanation of why Joey was attacking Ember. I can only imagine that other judges would struggle with who was doing what... but then again, maybe I'm just being slow. Ember confused me, but his writing in the mirror scene where Jay Phoenix and Ember are revealed as two sides of the same coin, and how it tied into the opening, win it for me. AgentDash had some good moments, but I wasn't as taken in by characterisation, dialogue or plot. Well done to Dash for getting this far. You may yet win this or other matches in this round, but if not then you've got nothing to be ashamed of. Final six in a GTT is a huge achievement. Pick: Ember Result Ember d AgentDash, 2 votes to 0 Running Tally: AgentDash: 0 Ember: 1 Violence Jack: 0.5 Sammy Brown: 1 Jason Snow: 0 AGENTDASH V VIOLENCE JACK Judge 1 AgentDash's second RP was more entertaining than his first. The ending felt sort of rushed (perhaps the writer was running short on time?), but overall a good read. Violence Jack's piece was just incredible though; well-written, well-thought out, and a complex plot executed very nicely. Rough luck for AgentDash, as his second RP may have stacked up better against some of the other semifinalists, but Violence Jack was just too good this round. Pick: Violence Jack Judge 2 Violence Jack * Simply brilliant introduction. This is Justin at the top of his game, drawing you utterly into a darkened world where there's nothing safe and nobody you can trust. The little details about leaving lights on and the seeping paranoia all add up to be so atmospheric. Totally love the intro. Some of the best stuff I've read in the tournament. * Bruce comes across a lot more forceful and in control than he had done in the previous round I read. His role as a witness and scribe combined with an almost informant, and dare I say a snake's role of temptation with Rourke is played off excellently by the doctor's steadfast belief in rationality that is beginning to reflect the world crumbling around him. Symbolism abounds! * The non-VJ segment at the end was probably the weakest of the three. I'd rather it get tied into the story in a concrete way sooner as opposed to later, but I'm guessing that you're banking on winning this to go with your free half point and then bringing everything together in the final. * The ending does leave me wanting more, but perhaps Striker should have had a cameo to remind us (or to introduce us) to what he and his organisation are about. Nevertheless, it's a minor point in comparison to the overall excellence of the piece. * One minor note which strikes me as funny is... how the hell are you going to end this? At the moment the obvious ends are unleashing this god on the world, or Bruce ascending to godhood or something suitably apocalyptic... or it all going horribly wrong and becoming an anti-climax. Either way I'm expecting something weird if you get there. AgentDash * An interesting first section to the story. * The dialogue still doesn't ring true with me. Cedric talking Mike down, as it were, doesn't seem convincing. He's exceptionally lucky that Mike doesn't want to kill him. * The return of the woman, and her appearing to have double crossed Cedric, followed by her helping him, and then returning to the other side to bolster her story (so it appears) is nicely done. * The escape and the shootout at the end though... it just seems very thin on plot. I actually feel more on the side of Mike, who's just doing a job to earn a living, than for Cedric, who craves power and enjoys killing people. * On the whole, the entire 'spy caper' thing didn't work for me so much. The CIA and FBI seem to be viewed more as branches of the police force than for their real roles in foreign intelligence, counter-terrorism and large-scale investigation. Violence Jack produces one of the best Rps I have read in the tournament thus far, to get the win. His mood-setting, characterisation and depth were the obvious keys to a good win. Well played Dash, but it was going to take something outrageously good to beat this round from VJ. Pick: Violence Jack Result Violence Jack d AgentDash, 2 votes to 0 Running Tally: AgentDash: 0 (eliminated) Ember: 1 Violence Jack: 1.5 (final score) Sammy Brown: 1 Jason Snow: 0 JASON SNOW V EMBER Judge 1 Ember: I haven't been following your work, but this was good. The intro sort of threw me -- what with the huge monologue -- but you tied it together at the end, and it didn't detract from my enjoyment. It seems that you have a pretty interesting -- or fucked-up, but that's two words, yeah? -- storyline going. There were a few grammatical errors scattered here and there, and your use of the ellipsis is a little odd, but overall it was a solid effort. Jason Snow: Everything you've written so far for GTT6 has been looooooong. Luckily for you, it's so well-written that you have to glance at the scroll bar to realize the length. This is not an exception. The multiple, multi-layered stories are each intriguing (and, for the most part, brutal), and don't take away from each other -- which is a tremendous sign of talent, I think. I liked visiting The Tank again, too. If you don't win this tournament out-right, I'm going to be surprised. This is good enough for publication. Both put out very solid efforts, but Snow's held me to the screen with a vicious grip. Pick: Snow. Judge 2 Snow: * To nit-pick, the opening paragraphs use the word 'old' far too much. To balance this out, though, in fact far more than balance it out, this is beautifully and brilliantly written. Bravo. Great conveyance of emotion. * The entire first segment is excellent. I'm not entirely sold on the idea that everything will improve for the gangsters if Snow dies, since it seems mildly tenuous, but I'll see where it goes, and the prose more than justifies continued attention. * Again, in the next segment you manage to marry my favourite parts to my least favourite. The relationship between the two characters is great, and the observations on life are well poised... but again, it's almost like Snow's obsession with Raven is reaching self-parody levels. He's literally saying or thinking about nothing else but her, and the joke is turning into something more. * This is getting very long. * In isolation I liked the segment in the Tank with Anthony getting punked out, but he's such an annoying idiot that it had less of an impact than if you had let Anthony be vaguely human, and not the uber-racist caricature. * I tend to like stories that don't rush themselves and are allowed to blossom in their own fullness. The television show 'The Wire' for example... but this is getting far too drawn out. * End segment shows potential, but that is one MASSIVE favour that J does for Anthony. I don't think a person in J's position would even consider seeing some effective n00b like Anthony, so he must have had his eye on him for some time? I dunno... it doesn't seem right, or realistic or something. The dynamic just feels wrong. Ember * Really good feel right from the outset. Dialogue feels slightly stilted though; read it out loud and there are far too few contractions for a regular conversation. * The opening draws you in, but it's taking a little long to get to a point. Maybe I'm just getting less patient over time or something. * Quote: “People assume, you know, that black is the sum of all colours and that white is the absence of any.” What? Who are these mythical people? Surely everyone knows that white is all colours and black the lack thereof? To think the opposite would be absurd! Way to flunk elementary high school physics, Ember! * I have sympathy with the bruised coccyx. I did that coming off a quad bike over a big jump and to land on it, holy crap, it hurts for MONTHS! * Bah! Saying that they found Jay's body, and then saying they got it mixed up with a geriatric... poor show. That's the plot twist of a weasel, sir! * I see the ending coming, and though it is given to me with a slight unexpected twist, by God if I don't agree with your own assessment of yourself on the boards. You have delayed on it for far, far too long. Surely everyone knows it all by now? * Egad Okay, I have been very critical on Snow and Ember in this judgment. This is for many reasons, not least that it's the last match and I'm tired! As such, please accept my apologies. It's the semi-finals of the entire tournament, and I figure you'll have everyone else loving you, which gives me a fairly large license to do what I want. With that little disclaimer out of the way, here are some thoughts. Snow and Ember – you are both excellent writers. Both of you stalled on stuff this round, and both of you made me want to skip segments. This does not stop the work you have done from being very impressive though. You are both truly deserving of a place in the final, at which point you MUST get this all tied in without writing a BAZILLION words or being too flowery and avoiding the big hit. You're both awesome though, and I have loved reading some of your work. At the end of this decision, I am giving it to Jason Snow. His excellent writing skills and style carried him over Ember here. If it was first RP vs first RP then this could have gone the other way, but despite the drag and the length, Snow had enough quality to overcome Ember's slightly faltering challenge. There's a load more of Snow moping around, and it was all I could do not to skim it at some points. It's just the whole 'woe is me' thing with Snow. It's very repetitive and could do with some editing or something. Sammy Brown * I seem to say this all the time, but it's an interesting opening. The narrator seems utterly devoid of confidence, and I think the end statement is needlessly negative, but it gives us a strong starting point. If you can build on this then I really like it. * Again the problem with tense shifts rears its ugly head. It may seem anal, but these things jar massively with people reading your work as if it were a book. Example, given that the entire thing was written in (forgive my simplification) 'past' tense: “Knuckles STEPPED (past) forward, upset at the lack of respect Sammy IS (present) offering to his employer. Mikey IS (present) quick to put a halt to his aggressive advance, he KNEW (past) that the shot he had dealt Sammy before was nothing compared to the stories he HAS (present) heard from other 'jobs' he has performed.” Reading it normally just makes so little sense you have to stop reading and go back, which is a bad sign. I can forgive it with a little bit of prejudice normally, but at GTT semi-finals? I'm afraid not... and it's not the only occasion. * It's that word again! Is it just that they have money riding on Sammy Brown to win it all? It's not made clear enough for my liking * I do like the scene, looking back, with Sammy Brown not being intimidated by the 'mob', as he's too fatalistic. That ties in well with the opening section. Nice work. Decision: Snow's story had slow patches and could have done with a talented editor. Despite this, his genuine writing skill overcame Sammy Brown here. Brown also is talented, there's no bones about that at all. He's done an excellent job to get this far, but some technical issues breaking up the flow of the story, and a lack of bringing me fully into the world he inhabits hands Snow the win.
