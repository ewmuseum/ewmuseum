CW2 Round 1 Results - Troyer Bracket

20 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thanks to the standin judges for helping get this one through (and the original judges who's input was just as important!). Archibald McGregor (FA) d Seymour Almasy (UWF) 2 votes to 1. Ethan Knight (FA) d 'The Conservative' Mildred McMann (FA) 2 votes to 1 Tessa Windsor (FA) d Joshua Taro Freedom (FA) 2 votes to 0 RAD d Dawn McFarlane 2 votes to 0 Round 2 Matchups: Archibald McGregor (FA) vs Ethan Knight (FA) Tessa Windsor (FA) vs RAD (FA) Judges Comments Judge 1 MacGregor rp “Message In A Bottle” Very nice rp, I could almost feel the pain and suffering that Archibald was going through. The writer put a ton of emotion into this rp and it showed I loved the feel, the atmosphere and the general style of the writing. It was a very pleasing to read, I saw a lot of similarities to my own character. I liked the rp and if this character is ever brought to a PTC fed I would follow the story. Grade: 8.7 Almasy rp Well I was hoping that Sean would not torture me, but alas he did and I got that huge abomination of an rp. I have to admit, Sean raises the bar when it comes to something like this. He brings his a game and it shows. I think this one was maybe the best one I have read in a while. But does he have to make them so damn long? I mean honestly I think I grew a few grey hairs after reading it and [detail withheld] and 45 minutes later and much arguing with my spell check over Final Fantasy words that it says are not words. Damn you Sean for using a name like Aerith as a character. See what that did it set it off again just cause I did it. [glares angrily at Sean] If ya weren’t so damn good I would give you such a lashing….[glares angrily at Argyle for giving me this “assignment”] oh and you’re next… Grade: 9.3 Almasy moves on and I am sure I will get that one assigned to me too. Pay attention Argyle…. Pick: Seymour Almasy Judge 2 Stand-Alone Score for Seymour Almasy "A" I've never read Almasy's work before, so it was nice to see what all of the hype was about. It was an okay read - not necessarily a work of art, but it wasn't a pain to read. A lot of things don't make sense until the end of the roleplay, however, when we finally figure out that Almasy is a bit crazed and paranoid. There are a few sentences in this that are rather awkard and a lot of superfluous sentences that don't really add much and throw people off when they read them (for example, what's the importance of the line "Table for Almasy" and why was it burned into his brain half an hour earlier?) It's interesting that you employed the use of a prologue, but it didn't really add loads to the piece overall. There were only a few pieces of important information there, and the rest was fluff and the same old "my wrestler's going to beat your wrestler" stuff that we can see from any other e-wrestler on the planet. The tone of the piece shifts from serious in the beginning to funny at the arrival of the flounder, then serious for a while more, and then to humorous again. While such a flow isn't "wrong" necessarily, I don't think the transitions were pulled off well enough just leaving awkardness as a result. Overall it was okay, but I don't think we saw "Former Global Champion" Almasy here. Verdict: An okay outing, but it takes the reader the entire roleplay to get the full effect - and this is coming from a judge. An average critical reader wouldn't read this long enough to get to that point though. * * * 3/5Stars Stand-Alone Score for Archibald MacGregor Message in a Bottle To be honest, I liked this from the onset. The language is right where it needs to be, and I can picture a small town as you intended almost right away. Not only that, but I think your prolouge added a lot more to your overall writing than Seymour's which seemed to be there for the sake of being there. Almasy suffers in that someone like myself who has never read his work doesn't do as well as he would hope judging a work in which he assumed that I knew his character already. You either realized this possibility before you started writing, or maybe it's just the free agent aspect of this, but you do a good job of setting the mood of the piece as well as providing interesting backstory. At the end of this prolouge, my thoughts are "Okay, I'm ready to meet Archie. You use a lot of metaphors. While it makes for great imagery, most of them are phrased exactly the same, and probably not on purpose. Some of the dialogue sounds a bit canned at times - especially for young people, and at times some of the lines are a bit cheesy and cliche ("Archibald had no idea his life was about to change forever.") The sudden change to first-person was a little unexpected too. At this point, the wonderful stuff you had in the previous two sections of the roleplay lose their zest. I think this would have been exponentially better if you had kept this in third-person all the way through. There are still plenty of lines and literary devices to save the few cliche's here and there. I found it funny how MacGregor is depicted as such a drunk and I think that does wonders for your characterization, and the character's self-reflection at the end also adds something to the mix. Overall, this was probably a better piece of pure fiction than Seymour's by a long shot and you still had enough content concerning the actual match to make it a roleplay. Good job. Verdict: A very professionally written piece of fiction that grabs the reader in the beginning and drags them to the end. The only downsides were the occasional cliche's. * * * * 4/5 stars Final Pick So what do these roleplays do when they're pitted against one another? Seymour's is funny and has a sense of "old" to it that probably would do well for him if I were a judge that had followed his career like most. Unfortunately, I haven't, and there isn't enough backstory to make me appreciate all of that, and I believe that, especially in a tournament setting, a roleplay should be written well enough that ANYONE can pick it up, read it, and get nearly every bit of it. MacGregor's roleplay had this, it was written better, and it seems that more care was taken with it. I'm not here to make "upsets," but I think this win was well-deserved and MacGregor is not someone I'd be all that willing to fight if I were a betting man! Pick: Archibald Mac Judge 3 Almasy v McGregor - Shadow Almasy A -Wow, when people said it was a novel, they were right. =P Honestly, though, it was really well-written. In certain scenes, it was a little too lengthily written for my taste, but the flip-side of that is that there's no possible way that you can't be in Seymour's head after reading all of that, and that's definitely not a bad thing. The strongest portion, I feel, was the confrontation with Wool-Knit- the writing here was tight and humorous, and I really felt it was the best written section of the RP. Well worth the time it took to read!- Archibald McGregor "Message in a Bottle" -I like how in depth the background to this story is. There's enough explanation of what's happened to Archibald throughout his life that I want to continue reading to see exactly how it's affecting him in the present. The scene with Heather surprisingly isn't very melodramatic, which seems to be the norm for scenes involving women, and I think I could actually believe someone acting that way without looking at a script. I think you're onto something good with the old man and the brandy. It's an interesting twist, and it introduces the questions and uncertainties that are necessary for the story to be interesting. The sudden portion relevant to the match seemed somewhat disjointed, seeming to not really be very related to the rest very much. I can see this being intentional- I don't know if I could drink that much and not have disjointed thoughts, but it takes away from the plot you're working with to interrupt it like that. The ending is well put-together, and it definitely has the tension present to keep a reader interested in the continuation of this story. As close as this is, between two great writers, it's difficult to make a choice. Both roleplays have their weaknesses, in that they both seemed to deviate from their driving tension that keeps the story moving. Almasy does this in the sense that the story, while good, had a tendency toward over-description that took away from what was going on. The Archibald post, with the exception of the match-relevant portion, seemed to flow better, and more clearly adhered to the storyline. My pick for this week is Archibald MacGregor. Pick: Knight Windsor v Freedom Judge 1 I enjoyed the slow build-up of bigotry in Taro’s piece, especially after his immediate presentation as someone worthy of pity. A lot of the garden-variety insults he lobbed rang hollow and clichéd, but the rest of it – helped in no small part by the presentation of his motivation – made me not mind so much. The transition from bitching about one woman, then all women, to Tessa, was quick and effective, and the first-person at the end tied everything together wonderfully. My main issue with the piece is that it cheapens the contest by making it a tired battle of the sexes. Taro’s stance seems to be that women are manipulative whores who are subordinate to men and only get ahead by cheating, and since Tessa’s a woman, as a man he’ll obviously decimate her. There’s a bit more to it, but only a bit. Tessa’s piece addresses the down-and-out-of-it atmosphere better, though the absolute sobbing was a little surprising, and reading about a “lake” of tears on someone’s face always irritates me. Nitpicking aside, it was a nice roleplay addressing the idea of “Once a wrestler, always a wrestler.” Aesthetically speaking, I found Tessa’s to be the more enjoyable of the two, but Joshua outshone her by addressing her specifically. Windsor concentrated on developing her character and motivation for the tournament itself, while Freedom concentrated on giving himself a reason to hate – not to mention motivation to stomp – his opponent. Unfortunately, when you go the route of character assassination, you need to sell me on it and make me agree. That doesn’t mean I have to personally subscribe to the tenets you’re preaching, but that I need to buy it as a strong case for why you should win. Taro defeating Tessa because he got dumped and hates women, and she’s a woman, just doesn’t fly. Tessa’s roleplay is little more than a vague introduction of motivation, and Taro’s is a dressed-up rant about how Tessa is a whore because she’s a woman. At the end of the day, in this battle relevance and humour fall to character-building and writing. Pick: Tessa Windsor Judge 2 Tessa had the better overall roleplay. The story was better in the sense that it was more logical, unlike how JTF didn't make too much sense at times. Though, I thought JTF's reasoning for being involved with the Cruiserweight tourny was more rational, Windsor's overall story was more entertaining with the inclusion of Chris Storm. The conversations between them allowed the roleplay to be not too terribly stale. Pick: Tessa Windsor RAD v McFarlane Judge 1 This is a tough match to judge. RAD’s story is more intriguing, Dawn’s is better presented. RAD’s writing is more succinct and effective, Dawn’s is more eloquent and subtle. The introduction of Dawn as a happy and very determined homemaker worked well, and while I thought a lot of the information was extraneous filler on the first time through, it all conspired to make me do a double-take at the scarring around her neck. I don’t want to say that scene gave something of substance to what was otherwise a very beautiful but very bland and unimaginative tournament introduction, but... It did. Without that, we have yet another retired wrestler roped into joining a wrestling tournament without their knowledge or consent, for no better surface reason than to join it and fatten the wallet. Realistic? Perhaps. Entertaining? Hardly. Without the questions raised about her past and psychological state, this is just another example of lovely, hollow writing. With that, on the other hand… The retirement makes sense while raising questions and there’s a premonition of doom. Not necessarily for Dawn herself, but for the simple life she’s been leading and enjoying. For everything she’s been burying in her garden, so to speak. I would have preferred it if that aspect was explored more, but I suppose it works well enough as a hook. RAD began providing an interesting backstory on how he’s been training to be a wrestler practically his entire life, without actually detailing any of the boring nonsense I don’t want to read about growing up training to be a wrestler. Unfortunately, he didn’t detail much of anything else, either. The section from where he was eleven seemed rushed and incomplete. Not because it was short, but because it feels like the beginning of the real story, of how RAD actually came to be where he is today. I actually like the execution of it, especially the implication I took, that “this”, what RAD does mind, isn’t just the whipping. Of course, I’m probably out to lunch, but it’s pretty good either way, since that whipping would be hell, and better, though wholly inhumane, the technique would have the intended effect. Anyhow, the presentation is good. Putting it out there simply instead of harping on in detail about it forces me to consider it for myself, and in some instances that’s better than any amount of mind-massaging imagery. My problem was that RAD never capitalized on that bait or gave any more than a veiled insight as to who he is in the here and now. That’s what I was thinking until about two minutes ago, at least, when I realized that the entire text works as a commentary on who he is now. The detachment, the resentment, the martyr mystique, the direct focus, the anger… Hmmm. That realization makes it even tougher. I was fairly resolved to Dawn winning, but now I just don’t know. Now I’m leaning toward RAD. Hopefully one final read will settle it. Alright. I’ve read again and I’m still torn. For the most part Dawn’s roleplay is a whole lot of nothing, nothing I’ve seen before countless times, written so delightfully that I’m tempted to lose sight of that core fact. The revelation of her scarring gives it a healthy dose of personality. While I would have liked more insight into that, I know the trend now is to go out on a curveball from leftfield, something to inspire a “WTF?” moment, and it certainly achieves that left un-breached. Personally, I prefer a cliffhanger resolved to one left shamelessly as round-progression bait, but that’s a rant for another time. Regardless, great writing. RAD’s isn’t written nearly so well. Stylistically, I’d say they’re a match, and on the creative front, RAD is lightyears ahead. Meaning, in essence, that I’m left with the same old thing wrapped in magnificent packaging against something unique but gritty. … RAD wins. Pick: RAD Judge 2 RAD What a fantastic introduction to the character. Very rarely have I seen this kind of in depth background in a roleplay. There were a few moments when things seemed to drivel off a bit and over elaborate on some moot points, but other than that, it generally had me hooked. McFarlane Wow. It started off hideously slowly. it's all very nice finding out what Dawn is doing since she retired and I guess it set a little scene, but a bit OTT. It got juicy when the agent turned up, and the idea about Dawn being signed up without her will. All in all a good opener. Verdict Given that both pieces were well written, my decision is based on the originality of the story and the desire for me to want to read more. RAD's did that for me. RAD d/ Dawn McFarlane Pick: RAD CW2 brackets
