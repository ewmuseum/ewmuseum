PYNCHON BRACKET FINAL RESULT

13 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well, guys, I'm sorry about the amount of feedback you're going to get. I promise that I will have mine done by Monday of next week at the latest. Between getting further feedback for the match or getting the results up, I chose the latter. Here're the picks. JUDGE ONE - Nate Desade RP1 I really like the merging of the article with the real life happenings, especially since the real life is something the interviewer would kill to have. What I’ve always loved about Joe’s stories is the amount of characters and their different opinions, voices, styles. However, right now I’m having a hard time buying Desade. She says that she is sure people will think she’s lying, and I do – maybe it’s because I’ve seen nothing but bad things from her for the past 5 years. Either way, she still rings heel to me. I like the dialogue with Amy, but it really just seems that Alex is trying too hard to be good, like she’s trying on a dress that’s a size too small and is forcing it on anyway. Again, I love supporting characters. The scene in Ashe’s van is great, and Ashe’s jokes made me smile. I also have always been a fan of the future tense in Joe’s RPs. A minute from now, Elijah Hawke will place a call to Kathryn Shaw. "She wants to see you," he will say, before Shaw even gets to greet him. "She said you would know the place." He won't give her a chance to respond, won't say another word before clapping his phone shut. "Fuck me," Kathryn Shaw will grunt when the line goes dead, and there will not be a man nearby to accommodate her. The effect is a sort of setting events in motion, and it works well with the subject matter Joe writes about. Another thing that works really well here is the showdown between Katsidy and Desade. Both personalities shine through, and it’s believable and real. (Although “Strike me down” is cheesy. But that’s okay) The situation plays out really well…until that one moment. “Make me come.” Sorry, Joe, I got put off by it. There seems to be no reason. Blame it on Kat’s sex-fiend personality, but it just seems less like an actual plot point and more like a “wouldn’t this be cool, huhuh” moment. It took me out of the entire RP, which had built up some steady momentum. She saves herself by not doing it, but it’s still ridiculous to even put in the RP. The RP recovers nicely as we go into the article again. I hadn’t seen Drusilla in awhile, and it’s good to see her. It’s even better to see her choking out a reporter. And then we end with the conversation between Alex and the rest is smooth sailing. I really like Amy and Alex’s interaction; their fights are real. The end gave me a satisfied feel, as if the RP went where it was supposed to. RP 2 I am going to cut down on comments for this RP because I made too many comments on the last RP and, not to insult Russ, but how is an outline going to beat a full RP? I will say, though, that if Russ had built on his outline, he definitely would’ve had a shot. I’ll get to that in a bit. For now, Joe’s second RP. It starts off great; the scene with Jared is an interesting mix of the new and old Desade – the ruthless one and the other who wants to be better. Poor Jared Sykes, all he ever did was be adorable and awkward, and this is what he gets for it. I hope he factors in later. For the record, though, “magical fucking night” just sounds silly. I really like the Maddox scenes, mostly because of the guard. He’s written well, and even though I had an idea of what was coming, it was done effectively. I’ve always liked Jonas, and he works well here, although the injection thing was too much like Star Trek for my tastes. I really like everything up until the end. The plans, the conversations, the fajita party, everything. It all flows really well and I forgot to comment because I was so busy reading it and getting wrapped up in it. The end, though, really doesn’t work for me. I just don’t like that the Order bends from their original sentence without anything other than an argument between Alex and Amy (although it’s worth pointing out how ridiculous it is that they have no shame and argue in front of EVERYONE, ALL THE TIME. Maybe if they fought in front of me I’d change my mind too just to get them out of my sight). But when they bend, they break. Their other option is for her to walk away. Which is exactly what she wants in the first place. So she fucks up, they have her in front of them, ready to levy punishment justly…and instead, they just let her walk away? Too neat. Too clean. It just didn’t seem like it could work that way. Especially for a woman whose entire life is built on convoluted plans. I know I’ve been harsh in this critique, and I apologize to Joe, because I didn’t hate this series of RPs at all. I just felt like there is a lot that could have been improved upon. There were mistakes that will need correcting if he is going to continue in this tournament. Alias RP 1 Unfortunately, I really can’t say much. You know what else is really unfortunate? Last time I judged something of Russ’s, it was the first round of the CWT, and he also wasn’t able to finish his story. It’s sad, because I really like Russ’s writing and I think he could’ve given Joe a run for his money. Some of these scenes would have been really cool to see. I really would’ve wanted to see the jazz club scene with Fiction, for instance. The story seems engaging, unfortunately it isn’t the entire story. RP 2 The setlist. Some really good songs in here. I love Jenny Lewis, and “Happy” is a great song. Tom Waits is obviously good too. Very cool setlist, I listened to a few of the songs I didn’t recognize and enjoyed it. I would’ve listened to these songs on youtube if I had the full RP to read. DECISION I feel bad for Russ. He had a really ambitious idea that fell to time. And I think, had he world enough and time, this would have been a great matchup. Joe may still have won, we’ll never know. But Joe’s story mixes in espionage and love (however awkward it is) very well, and despite a few mistakes delivered a very solid effort. He’s my pick. WINNER: DESADE JUDGE TWO - Trent Desade RP 1: As someone who, to be quite honest, had no idea that Alexandra and Aimz were in a relationship, I was quite happy that the writer didn't assume that I knew and instead filled me in. And, I like how you portray their chemistry. Their dialogue does justice to both characters, and also conveys the complex dynamics of their relationship. The transitions between the news article and the story are excellent. It gives me a well-rounded understanding of the main character -- not only just what she's like, but how she is perceived by others. The author manages to have it both ways: Desade is understood from different perspectives, but she's also still very mysterious and that keeps me intensely interested in the story. Some of the "article sections" are enjoyable because, to me, they read like "a beginner's guide to the SCCW universe." I know there has been some dissent about how much the wrestler's fed should factor into these roleplays -- and I would have to say that if it isn't done right, then yes, it can alienate the reader -- but this author does it perfectly. He is able to capture what is interesting about the fed and at the same time you don't have to follow SCCW to understand it. I'm sure I would get more out of it if I had a firm handle on the conflicts and characters before reading the piece, but coming out of the story I feel "in" on the drama. That is most definitely the sign of a successful writer. The author is also able to really give the reader access to different characters' minds. I'm thinking in particular of the part of the narrative that involves Kathryn -- her little exchange with the guy from accounting was just so fun to read. I also like how her casualness about the whole thing takes away from Desade's overly serious demeanour. I wish I could talk about ways to improve this roleplay, but I can't find anything seriously wrong with it. I would say that a male writer shouldn't be writing about the dramatic, erotic encounters of females but these scenes are so very well written that it would be dishonest of me to make a criticism along those lines. I think if one were to paraphrase the plot it might sound like fluff, but what makes this roleplay transcend such a definition is the nuts and bolts of the story. It is well crafted and the author avoids the danger of making it sound "mushy." Desade RP 2 The strengths of the first roleplay continue into the second. The author doesn't impose expectations on the non-SCCW reader -- for example, when Jared Sykes enters the narrative the author takes the time to briefly explain his history and his role. It's kind of a minor point, but if you ask me, it's also very important. One part I didn't like about this piece was the Sykes/Alex elevator scene -- how it was set up and how it played out. It struck me as very similar to the Kathryn/Alex scene in the last roleplay -- the reader thinks that something scandalous might happen and then the author pulls the rug out from under their feet. I prefer this method to the overdone "okay, a scandal is happening" route, but nonetheless, to use the same narrative trickery in scenes not all that far apart is a little bit reduntant. The story improves a lot from there on, though. I was absolutely absorbed by the story. Some parts were confusing, but that only lasted until later sections that filled in the blanks. I think this was partly a result of the jumbled timeline, and I think the author achieves his desired effect. It all kind of ends a bit abruptly and a little bit too neatly, but I guess that's a genre convention that the author is almost obligated to follow. When it comes to stories I think tragic endings are more powerful, but the choices that the author makes for his are ultimately logical. Across both roleplays the author does a very good job. In the second piece I think some of the characters' dialogue is a bit too insightful and refined, as if they're "intellectualizing" the stuff that's going on in the story. I think dialogue should be a bit more messy and organic, but hey, easier said than done (I myself am terrible at dialogue, so I'm not one to talk, but take it for what it's worth). Alias RP 1 & Alias RP 2 Well, this looks like it was fun to write. Not so fun to read, despite the fact that Pynchon is one of my favourite authors and the author seems to be trying to channel that style. Or, at least, uses staples in Pynchon's work to create a kind of episodic, channel-changing tour through Alias's life. The seemingly conscious imitation of the Inherent Vice plot and allusions to The Crying of Lot 49 are kinda neat for someone who has read them -- and you're lucky that I have -- but for someone who hasn't I imagine they'd be a bit lost. Despite having read the source material, I still felt a bit lost. The postmodern affectless citation thing that the autho has going on here is neat and kind of fun to read, but compared to the depth and scope of your opponent's story it's a little amateur. Whether or not I "got it" or any other reader "gets it" is not for the author to decide -- it's up to him to make the reader understand. What I am reading here is an educated, intelligent writer who is more in love with how smart he is and his writing than anything else, and the story suffers because of it. DECISION For me, this is an easy decision. Desade's story is interesting and engaging. The author has an understanding of his audience, the writing is fluid, and a lot of care is taken in crafting a coherent plot with both fantastic and realistic characters. I have a feeling that Alias's story doesn't do justice to the writer's actual talent, but unfortunately for him, I am going off of two pieces of writing against another two pieces of writing, and Desade gets my pick. Judge 3 - Aaron This isn’t going to go long, and I will write a more thorough feedback as to this decision, but I refuse to let this match hold up results because it’s not close. Desade wins the bracket literally by default due to the inability of Alias to provide anything concrete. I will provide actual feedback to Joe and also to Josh on their pieces (to Russell as well if he so desires). Judge 4 - Andy Pick - Desade, due to real life hamstringing Russ from getting up a genuine set of RPs. I will give feedback on this as soon as I can, but for the moment getting the results up are more important. THE WINNER OF THE PYNCHON BRACK IS DESADE! (4-0) Pynchon
