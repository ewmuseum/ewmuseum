The Kaikouras Bracket Champion is....

19 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dave Hurst d Angelo Deville (3 votes to 2). Dave Hurst will take on Kasper Sky in the semi finals. Judges Comments I thoroughly enjoyed reading all six of these pieces. Some of the best writing I've ever seen in the game. Though, I will say, I personally prefer a more wrestling-related slant on the game, as opposed to the creative writing, with occasional wrestling references slant taken here. That said, judging on what was there, as opposed to what wasn't: I love how both Dave and Jeff took each other's stories and characters, incorporating versions of them in their own work. I particularly enjoyed the parodies serving as symbolic trash-talk. Both were well written. At this stage of the Tournament, that's to be expected. I felt that the very end of Dave's story, with the blessing, came off a bit rushed, after a being fairly well paced for the rest. Overall, Jeff's writing tended to flow a bit better. Hat's off to both for again, some of the best writing I've ever seen online. Obviously, and unfortunately, I can only pick one, and that pick is: Pick: Angelo Deville. Dave’s first roleplay: Don’t lead the reader along. If you write it the way it should be, the reader will come to the conclusions you want them too, instead of force feeding them. Sometimes I find some of your description a little wordy but for the most part I enjoyed reading about your scenery. It seems you used the Devil’s voice to explain to the reader his/your intentions instead of showing it. The dialogue to me seemed fudged and I wish conclusions wouldn’t have been so easily orchestrated, but seemed more real if the devil actually took your mind. I would doubt with little question the Devil would just start rambling on his intentions, seems too easy. Overall, I thought the roleplay was good although the biggest downfall would have to be the dialogue of the Devil. I found it unbelievable and at times cheesy. I do like where the story is going and it was not hard at all for me to jump right into your roleplay, which is awesome! I just hope Ducky no-sells the fire and when it burns down, he’s still yellow! Or else I hope this whole Devil things is a spin on what Deville is doing, as in mocking him!!!! Angello’s first roleplay: The silence of the Devil in the begining speaks more that if you were to use words. People often forget this useful tool and I like that you used it. Good use of backstory with the mother. Gives a good sense of who she’s all about and also gives a sense of direction the roleplay is going too. Good suspense, and I figured all your detail just gave the roleplay slow start, but its coming in useful here when the Devil enters the friends house. Damn good roleplay, although Ive seen you use the style before with a roleplay against Ivan Stanislav back in WO for the round robin tournament. Don’t know if that’s how you always roleplay are not, but I dug it. Dave’s second roleplay: Again, sometimes I feel through your description that you use too much and are leading the reader instead of just getting to the point (ex: How you say you go to the computer screen, grab the keyboard, turn on word processor, etc, etc) Some of that stuff the reader can draw conclusions too if you would just say “I went to the computer to type.” Or something like that. A big no-no in most writing is to use the word “You.” Its why most prose writing is either in 1st or 3rd, I believe only one good prose writing was ever pulled off in the 2nd perspective, but enough preaching! : ) It kills the suspension of disbelief and the reader often becomes confused. Character perspective has just been confused here. I would say to this that the story is being “told” to the reader but that doesn’t make sense either because the writing is in present tense. Im not going to dock points or anything but its just something you should be aware of for future writing outside the hobby if you do that sort of thing : ) Ok roleplay, although I would have liked to see Ducky at the bottom of the barrel just refusing to burn : ) Either way, I see Ducky making like the dog Lassie in order to save Dave. Also, a little mention of a bum named Angelo, Im guessing in reference to Deville, but it came off weak and almost unneeded other than to offer slim hope that Wendy actually found Dave in the rain. Im waiting for the third roleplay to really kick things into reason. Im looking for some kind of farcical moment on why the writer decided to have Dave overtaken by the Devil much like Deville is in his roleplays. Hopefully I wont be disappointed because it could be the saving grace against Angello if he does it right. Angello’s second roleplay: A cliché moment in the beginning with the tear dam breaking. But with the turnaround that involves roleplaying, it matters little. Good foreshadow. Good use of colors in the roleplay, although I wish you wouldn’t have mentioned the name tags, seems too easy. I do like your neutral, gray stance to things : ) Can I have some of “Willy and the Chocolate Factory” crack you were smoking when you wrote this roleplay? : ) Nice Might Ducks reference, it could be the best trilogy ever made. Go Charlie! I liked this roleplay tons better than the first one. Everything made sense in the world and Im very impressed. Probably the best roleplay Ive ever read in eW to date. I just hope the last roleplay lives up to it. Dave’s Third Roleplay: The beginning part did nothing for me. Again, it was the way she was thinking/talking/feeling, it felt more like a robot/cheesy rather than a human being in my opinion. Again, in the beginning of the second part, I fee like your telling me, not showing me. Biggest rule in writing, show don’t tell. Ok, just got done reading and I feel this was your weakest roleplay of the three. You were leading me around the roleplay, telling me this and telling me that. I didn’t make any connection to anything because I was told what to feel, which shouldn’t happen when writing. I was waiting and waiting and waiting for some kind of underlying meaning, or some kind of reason for you being possessed by the Devil and I felt let down. Don’t get me wrong, the roleplay wasn’t bad but it wasn’t anything great. I expected a twist, turn, ANYTHING with this third roleplay to really bring home the whole Devil deal. It just didn’t happen. Angello’s third roleplay: Again, great start, the only thing I don’t really like was a certain line when Angello was talking to his father in the cell, care to take a stab at which piece of dialogue it was? : ) To me, it seemed like something Angello, or possibly anybody, wouldn’t say. Ok…so…f-ing money roleplay. Im really happy with this one. Its even better than the second one. It was a damn good cap on the 3 part series and although it ended soon…I think its just because Im being selfish and wanted his mom to wake up and have them talk. Good job, congrats. My pick: Well, all things aside. Throwing out dialogue, description, grammar, etc. My pick for who won all comes down to enjoyment. Which roleplay serious did I find exciting and worth my time to read. Which one entertained me more? Angello Deville gets the nod from me. I firmly believe if Hurst would have surprised me in his third roleplay with something totally out of left field it would have been a much more close match. Pick: Angello Deville Had a hard time deciding this one, but I'm going with EDH on it. Both men employed a slight parodying of each other, but while Deville's was more mocking, EDH's was more comedic, which I believe was the major focus. I never thought that someone could give a rubber duck a voice... but EDH sure did! Great work by both authors, it's a shame one has to lose and won has to win. Pick: Dave Hurst Wow. This is probably the toughest match I've ever had to judge. For a while, I never understood the hype that some people had told me about EDH. After sitting down and reading his three RPs, I get it. His use of first person is unique, and truly adds a lot to his promos. His characterization of the Devil is also stunning, as he writes the character exactly as Deville would. I had to check to make sure whose RPs I was reading at some points. The story is also top notch. The approach is simple, yet is funny, tragic, serious, and upbeat all at once. The Ducky scene is especially memorable. This is EDH's best work to date, IMO, and it comes off as intended, the struggle of EDH mirroring the struggle of the writer to overcome perhaps PTC's best. And from there, we go to The Deville. Jeff's Jeff. Enough said. His work is as always stylish, well-written, and witty. His characterization and dialogue are second to none, and he is able to get inside a character's head as well as anyone. Three RPs for both men. Six of the best RPs of the tournament. Point ratings are meaningless, as stylistically, I consider them equals. Thus, it comes down to which RPs I thought were better, based on content, which RPs pulled me in more. And when looking at it that way, I give the edge, slight as it is, to EDH. Pick: EDH What a round to judge. The interaction between these two writers was exceptionally good. They both wrote each others characters so well that I had to go back and check whose writing I was reading at least 3 times. Well done guys. Deville’s work is always consistent and tight and this round is no different. When I judged Dave Hurst’s work in earlier rounds I wrote that I thought that his work had grown with each advancement in the tournament, each round it seems to get tighter. In round one I wasn’t convinced by the presence of the duck, but this round it worked really well. I found that both writers’ work was equal in so many senses that judging it really came down to which story-line I liked the better of the two. Dave Hurst countering Deville’s Devil by ‘Finding God’ is a stroke of genius – I’m giving Dave the win. Kaikouras
