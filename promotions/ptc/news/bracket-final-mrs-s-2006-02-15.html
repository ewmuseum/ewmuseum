Bracket Final: Mrs S.!

15 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
MASQUERADE V SHANTY JONES Judge 1 Masquerade Revelations (II) - Faith Balks To A World Gone Revelations (III) - Battle Lines & Good Politics Masquerade the character is the line between black and white, there are not shades of grey in his life and Masquerade the writer has a way with words that is spell-binding, backing up the character completely and making him seem very real indeed. He/she is Tokein-esque in the depth of descrption and narrative that goes on throughout both pieces and I found that I was being sucked in, and swept along, as the writing progressed. Another couple fo authors that I was reminded off, for different reasons, throughout these pieces were John Grisham and Stehpen King. I know that they may seem like strange bed-fellows but anyone who has read works from those authors may know what I am getting at. The wealth and depth of the characters, and the convulted plotlines that snake among each other, are very reminscent of Grisham’s legal tales; I even got a flavour of old Southern gentlemen discussing their political machniations over cigar and brandy at one point. The aura of menace, from these people, and the underlying ‘tension’ was subtle but still extremely potent which was where I got a taste of King. Hopefully being compared to three authors of that stature won’t be an insult to the writer, as I mean nothing but compliments by saying so. There were so many great moments in these pieces that it is so hard to narrow down exactly why and what made it work. For example I adored the character of Father Yuri Romanov, ad was mesmerised by his demise. “"Amen." Kendall finished the blessing for the fallen priest. He'd never utter the word again.” That was a wonderful touch. The second RP continued the excellent level of writing that the first showed. The descrptive text was, again, top-notch and the narrative flowed well. The only complaint that I have with any factor of the writing is some parts of dialogue (in both RPs) seemed either over-done or too similar. It wasn’t always easy to see the personalities from the speech, whereas the narrative and description had no such problem. The character of Masquerade was again show to be a man that sees himself as a ‘hero’ in his own eyes, mocking the fact that his people would want to cheat to win the match. Shanty Jones Strands Weave Together Seeing as I started off by comparing Masquerade to some very well known authors I will do the same for Shanty, as that only seems fair. Where Masq is Tolkein I see Shanty more as an Eddings or Feist, where Masq is Grisham I see Shanty as a Brown, and where Masq is Kin Shanty is defintely a James Herbert. All of them are extremely successful and accomplished writers and masters of their own domain. They all, also, are chracter and story focussed over pure word-play. Something that Shanty (the writer) does extremely well is to progress the overall story along without the reader actually being aware of the fact that he is being pushed along where the writer wants him to be. The plot and story moves along with a seamless flow and even when surprises, or cliff-hangers, are sprung upon us theer is not a feeling of bewilderment (or worse a sigh because it is corny or cheesy) but instead a small gasp of wonder that something interesting has happened that may or may not have been expected. Another aspect that Shanty does very well is to have stories with stories, all of which blend together (eventually) into a seamless whole. There is a sense of pace that flows througout all three of the roleplays that begins to give a semblance of urgency. The reader can feel the strands (pun intended) coming together without always knowing exactly what they are. It is a knack that the writer seems to have in spades. A massive factor of the gentle appeal of these pieces was the under-staed humour in them. There are quite a few writers in these tournaments who take the ‘dark’ approach to their story-telling with conspiracies, murder, and suspense; there are writers who go all out on the fantastical approach with little basis in reality; there are even writers who go for comedy, making us laugh as we read; there are no writers (AFAIK) that attempt to do all three. There are defintely no writers that succeed at doing all three. Apart from Shanty. The comedy may be of the darker/blacker variety ("Have you taken a kidney? Please don’t take a kidney!" for example, when Artemis was trying to figure out what was happening) or even a tongue in cheek poke at our very own hobby ("No, it’s worse than that. We’re professional wrestlers.") but it is alawys there. It is never slap-stick, it is never too much, but there is always an under-current of mirth that makes it enjoyable to read. All of the RPs, also, almost seemed to have separate and distinct ‘voices’. Obviously each of the character’s dialogue was unique and identifiable to them but apart from that the disparate sections were written with similar changes in style. For example the exposition about the factories (Shanty’s inner thoughts narrated) stodd out as technically great and powerful, which was a juxtoposition from the comedy previously. Bringing together so many of the characters that the writer uses was daring simply because some people may not know them, or like them. I both know and like them and like with Ryan I applaud the convulted history that draws unique characters together ‘realistically’. Each of the RPs ended with a cliff-hanger of sorts. Now I have to be honest and say that I knew that Paul Goodman/Eogar was going to be in the room, it was telegraphed, but I still smiled at the parting comments from him. Similarly the menace at the end of RP two was great, foresahdowing the arrival to the group of the Ripper himself. RP three was slightly different as it could almost be read as an ending. Perhaps the writer was playing it safe and allowing a natural end to the series in case he is beaten. We know the backstory know, we know the players, and we know that they are all linked. That could be an ‘ending’. That is great either way that you look at it. If he loses then he has wrapped it up some-what, if he wins he can coninue with ‘chapter two’ almost. Synopsis On the surface this seems like a very similar set of RPs, both are dealing with a conspiracy type back story, somewhat fantasitical, and most definitely dark. Both of them are foccused more on telling the story rather than making it purely relevant (though both do a decent job of including the tournament at times, even if it seems a stretch in places). They, also, both seem to suffer from a similar weakness; if you didn’t read the previous RPs you would be screwed trying to understand what is going on next (though in fairness Shanty addresses this somewhat in his three RPs). The good thing for both is that I am not coming in blind at all, having previously read their work. So we have two great stories, two great writers and characters that are extremely well developed – how does one go about making a decision? Shanty wrote three RPs to Masquerade’s two, so should that mean that he wins? Most defintely not, this is about quality and not quantity and I don’t think that I would or have ever judged anything on quantity of any sort (within reason of course ;o) ). Ok then if it isn’t quantity how about quality, and hopefully this won’t come as a critisism or even an insult but Shanty is not in the same leage technically as Masquerade. In terms of pure word play Masquerade has not only the edge but the very clear advantage. So does that make him the obvious winner? Nope, not especially so because someone could have a Master’s degree in English but have no imagination (to clarify though Masq has an AMAZING imagination!!!). The fact that he has a greater technical grasp of the English language cannot be the sole factor for deciding this. At the end of the day it can only come down to a factor that is extremely hard to define: enjoyment. I want to state that I honestly enjoyed both of these pieces, as I have enjoyed their previous work throughout the tournament (and beyond that even) but both men cannot win … unfortunately. It comes down to which one I enjoyed the most and which one I want to read more of. That is the story of Shanty Jones. Pick: Shanty Jones Judge 2 Masquerade: Revelations (II) - Faith Balks To A World Gone Mad: Let me say that this is the first Masq RP I've done more than skimmed so far in the tournament. You're one of the best writers I've read in e-w as of yet, a true master of the pen. or keyboard, whatever. The only complaint I have is that I felt myself having to re-read too much, I kept getting lost. Maybe it's just me and the fact that I've got a cold, but thats my only negative word. Good work. Revelations (III) - Battle Lines & Good Politics: I feel I just stumbled into a great movie that's nearly finished. The dangers in series-writing in big tournies are obvious, and they effect your piece. Was it bad? No way, from what I understood it was great. But since I haven't read any more of your work, besides your RPs this round, I feel lost as to what you're doing, and the confusion outweighs the curiosity. As of this writing I haven't yet read Shanty's work, so no decision has been rendered.. Shanty Jones: Strands: I found this easy to get into, and the plot twist at the end works out to your advantage. You do a good job of keeping the reader hooked with the stories of the factory and etc, I look forward to your 2nd and 3rd works.. Weave: Interesting work, tying together the factories, GTT5, and Paul. The only thing that I'm lost on is the presence of The Ripper, but I'm sure that will be explained. Together: Not much I can say about this one, besides great work. Even though it seems I arrived late to the party, I'm still hooked on the story and I feel I -Need- to know what happens in the next installment.. Pick: Shanty Jones. Andy's work gripped me more than Masq's, and thats what won over my vote. Not to say it wasn't close, I just preffered Shantys piece.. Judge 3 Masquerade Last round, I felt that you needed to not make shit so God damn confusing. You have 80 trillion characters, a trillion different things happening in the plot. And now? What the fuck? You walk into this Church, and this Romanov guy gets killed by this creature with Talons. I don't know if that's symbolism or not, but me not being able to decipher it shows how utterly frustrated I was with your writing. You have such good vocabularly...but you just became too complicated! Your writing was a case of "you can't please everyone". I'm sure there are Masquerade marks out there who prepare themselves to read your writing. But, to be honest, I walked into it like I casually knew who you and Shanty were...and it wasn't pretty. Just too much. Not to say there weren't good parts. I liked the inital introduction to Brutus...but, apparently, everyone speaks like they have an IQ of trillion. Even when they're drinking. In past rounds, I didn't notice this monotony of dialogue...but it stuck out like a sore thumb. It could've been a LOT better. Shanty Jones After the confusing read of Masquerades, it was refreshing to walk into this. Sure, the vocabularly wasn't as in depth. Ok, the plot wasn't as intricate. But I could EASILY READ IT *AND* ENJOY IT! The first two RPs were very good. I liked eveything worked. On top of that, the transition between RPs were seemless. Where you were going with everything was great. Then...Jack Nobbledy and Cereal Killer. Now, I'm not too familiar with any of your other characters, but I do know from carousing around these forums that you have used these characters before. And nothing annoys me more than a handler using a trillion different characters in a storyline, almost using them to gain points from the judge. I didn't like it at first when I saw Curiosity, but you made it work. Now? C'mon. The thing is, though... Verdict The thing is, though, Masquerade's piece wasn't as good as yours, period. I've lost the urge to figure out who Masquerade was last round, so that driving force is over. THe plot just gets thicker and thicker and crazier, so that's a turn off. And, it's quite hard to read as well. So what else is there? Shanty, it would've been a lock with 2 RPs...but the annoyance with the last one almost brought it to an even point. THANKFULLY, the ending of the third was good enough for me to want more. So...Shanty Jones with the win! Pick: Shanty Jones RESULT Shanty Jones d Masquerade, 3 votes to 0. Shanty Jones is the Mrs S. Bracket Champion! Thanks to both players for such excellent writing :) Brackets
