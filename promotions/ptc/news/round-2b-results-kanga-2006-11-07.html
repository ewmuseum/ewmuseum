Round 2B Results - Kanga

7 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
ANGELICA DOMINIQUE V DANIELLE NORTHSKY Judge 1 Northsky: As I've never read any of your work before, I found myself very confused in your world. Too much went unexplained, and left me wondering what was going on. The dialogue seemed very forced and unnatural, and the interactions between all the characters seemed almost fake. Everything seemed like too much to drop on a reader at once without much explination. Fallen Angel: While your work didn't keep me enthrwaled and wanting more, I at least could understand what was going on. I was able to understand the story you were trying to get across. The other thing is that your dialogue seemed alot more natural than Northsky's. It seemed like real people talking, and that made me actually seem like something real was going on. While Northsky might have had a good story, I just couldn't get past all the unrealistic sci-fi things going on without any explination whatsoever. The dialogue also shot this horse in the leg before it could get out of the gate. Pick: "The Fallen Angel" Angelica Dominique Judge 2 Angelica Dominique: Cite your sources. There is a tendency to slip between third- and second-person perspectives. For the most part you stay consistent, using third-person past tense prose to propel the majority of the plot, but some awkward descriptive imagery manages to sneak in a little too often to ignore. Referring to the reader directly with things like ‘you,’ ‘your’ or ‘our’ (as if the narrator were speaking directly to me, the reader) throws off the pace when used incorrectly. This is a good story. It’s good in the sense that there are a lot of potential plot hooks going on at once (the kidnapping mystery, the amnesia angle and how it all ties in with Angelica/Kiara’s tumor), but I have to be honest here. The whole kidnapping/amnesia storyline has been done to absolute death, and throwing them together like this doesn’t give the genre new life. Don’t get me wrong; just because this area has been explored before does not in any way mean you are disallowed from exploring it on your own, if you want to. Be forewarned, however, that the fact I’ve seen this a dozen different times does influence the judging. Danielle Northsky Fascinating character. The one thing that gives Dani a good bit of credibility as a half-angel are the nods to texts such as Enoch and Genesis, “real” accounts of the Second Fall of Angels and how Dani fits into the greater picture. It fairly reeks of The Prophecy, but considering I absolutely love the idea of dissention amongst the winged immortals, that’s not really a bad thing. Fascinating as it may be, however, this story is also very fantastical. It’s a thin line to walk when running a character that has such a supernatural power that when put in context of e-wrestling, it begs the reader to ask… how could someone with the strength of an angel possibly lose? This is neither here nor there, though. What was written was written well, the story flowed (except for a few overbearing blocks of background history that felt a little forced), and it leaves me with a desire to read more of it. And you cited your sources! Pick: Danielle Northsky Judge 3 Angelica 1 Pros: Interesting inserts with the medical reports. I have no idea if they're plausible, as I'm no doctor, but they read well and add to the story. Good relevance without losing storyline strengths. Story draws in the reader and makes them want to read part two Cons: Trouble with technical aspects of writing (tenses, grammar, incorrect use of words) made it harder to read than it should have done, and sometimes interrupted where the story was flowing well. Angelica 2 Pros: Fills in the history of how Angelica became who she is Written well in terms of pacing and content Cons: Same technical issues Not much about Angelica as she is now. Not having read about her before I only know of her childhood and not of the person she became Danielle 1 Pros: Good contrast between hiding and showing wings as identity. Technically very proficient; no visible errors allowed it to flow well. Interesting storyline, though has potential to get confusing. Cons: Initial lines came off as 'don't you know who I am?' style trash talk. Large cast of characters made it a little confusing in places. Danielle 2 Pros: On the whole, technically well written again, with only one or two minor errors. Good use of descriptive writing for various locations. Cons: The storyline is streaming off in many directions as once, leaving someone with little/no prior knowledge of Danielle trailing in the dust. History of which god and angel is which gets somewhat tiresome. Pick: Four good pieces, and by no means a clearcut decision. I suspect a split vote will come out of this one. Danielle is the better writer in terms of technicality and description. Angelica had a story that interested me more. At the end of the day, the multitude of various characters, angels and mythology in Danielle's storyline tended to lose focus, and when the ending showed that Childress was hiding information from her, it didn't really engender anything of a reaction in me. Angelica's storyline, for all the lack of a present face attached to it, does make me want to know what will happen to her in the future. Pick – Angelica Dominique Result Angelica Dominique d Danielle Northsky, 2 votes to 1 THE PHANTOM REPUBLICAN VS JOSHUA KOSIDLO Judge 1 Phantom Republican: While you had a weak first promo, the second made up for it. The first one didn't seem to work very well for me, I didn't like you telling me, the reader how I thought and how I felt. Your story should make me feel that way without telling me that I feel anger or confusion. The first promo almost seemed like it was lacking in function. Your second promo was alot better and may have kept you in the running. Joshua Kosidlo: I have honestly never read any of your work before this set of promos left me more impressed than I thought I would be. You showed the character very well, and although there were flaws, the dialogue seemed kind of bland, even for a two year old. The story seemed slightly bland, you didn't have any blatant errors or anything that stood out and said, "I'm a mistake!" Final Thoughts: This is honestly the toughest pick I had to do, simply because it's two average promos versus a good promo and a below average promo. I also thought it was interesting that both writers happened to start their first promos with the same idea. I beleive that oddly enough, Joshua Kosidlo pulled it off slightly better, but Phantom's second promo blew everything Joshua had out of the water, evening out to a slight victory. Pick: The Phantom Republican. Judge 2 Roleplay 1: Joshua Kosidlo: He has hit the nail on the head with his first roeplay. I enjoyed the story and thought that it had a good set of substance and got me expecting to see the next rp. Phantom Republican: Politics was another good roleplay as well. Has me very interested in rp 2. Second rps: Joshua Kosidlo: Not quit as god as the first ones and semed to lack the quick fire i was expecting for a follow up. Has me asking questions. Phantom Republican: Second rp was outstanding and surprised me with the continuity and follow through in the story itself. Hard decision my choice here is : Phantom Republican. Result The Phantom Republican d Joshua Kosidlo, 2 votes to 0 SHADOW V KIMBUSA - PTC GLOBAL TITLE MATCH Judge 1 Kimbusa Promo One: Blind To Beauty I liked this promo, a lot. Not only did it show Kimbusa's motivation for continuing in the tournament after doing away with Almasy, it showed a character shift in Kimbusa being lured by the championship that he never used to value. Not a lot of weaknesses here, a strong start going into a strong finish. Promo Two: The Last Stop The first word that comes to mind when trying to describe this promo is: short. It was a lot shorter than the other, but that's not a bad thing, because this one seemed to lack where the first one shined. This seemed to be pieced together to beat the deadline, and while it's not the best work, it didn't quite turn me off, either. Shadow Promo One: Voices Although you had a story to tell, this piece suffered from a few fatal flaws. The biggest problem I had with this piece was that it was more like work than an enjoyable read. The word choice seemed almost like you were trying to impress the reader with your vocabulary, and it ended up making it very difficult to follow the story and continue through the promo. The piece itself wasn't bad, but as far as telling a story, it was just too difficult to follow. Promo Two: Echoes While this is the better of the two, it's still -VERY- difficult to get into. I couldn't get into it until about the third read-through. The sentence structure and word choices were just so far beyond normal conversation that it seemed you're trying to impress the reader into thinking this must be a good story. The problem is, it didn't need that. If the language had been cleaned up, the story would've told itself. While Shadow had a good story, her delivery was lacking in clarity and the two pieces suffered greatly from it. Kimbusa had a strong first promo, which was hurt slightly by the weak follow-up, but in the end, the difference comes down to the fact that Kimbusa's was easy to follow, and much more pleasant to the reader. Pick: Kimbusa Judge 2 Kimbusa Roleplay #1: * Judge propped the authors’ writing and ambience. Mentioned minor technical errors. Story was average other than Kimbusa’s thoughts on Almasy. Roleplay #2: * Judge felt that RP 2 complemented RP1 well, but felt that overall was lacking. The author writes well. Reflected that the storyline mimicked the actual interest level. Shadow Roleplay #1 * Judge discussed spelling errors and fragmented sentences, and wasn’t interested in the storyline, other than when Shadow was thinking about Almasy. Roleplay #2 * Judge again referred to spelling errors and fragmented sentences, and wasn’t interested in the storyline. * I’m not posting the judges comments as I received them. I have summarized the key points, but there are an awful lot of added extras that don’t help anyone and go against the GTT judging guidelines, in terms of lecturing as opposed to simply judging. I will take the pick because the essence of what is required was there. I will give the judging comments to the players if they ask for them via PM. Pick: Kimbusa Judge 3 Oftentimes I felt Shadow was unnecessarily wordy and took way too long painting a flowery picture to get to the gist of the pieces. Her first RP felt way too "inside" to an outsider who doesn't closely follow the happenings of GCW and the second piece kept building up and building up to a crescendo and then ... abruptly stopped. Where Shadow was wordy, Kimbusa's pieces just plodded along. The pacing was methodical and the spark that put him over Almasy just wasn't there for me this week. While getting into the mind of Kimbusa was accomplished, the end results fell flat. Because of this, I'll have to give it to Shadow, but I do want to give both Ben and Kat props for keeping their pieces relevant to the tournament and to their opponents. I liked that in all four RPs I read. Pick: Shadow Result Kimbusa d Shadow, 2 votes to 1. Kimbusa retains the PTC Global Title. SUMMARY - Angelica Dominique d Danielle Northsky, 2 votes to 1 - The Phantom Republican d Joshua Kosidlo, 2 votes to 0 - Kimbusa d Shadow, 2 votes to 1 (Kimbusa retains the PTC Global Title) - The Sentinel d Brian Arson (Arson didn't get his second post up in time) BRACKET JUDGES Bobino Andrew Lindz Andy Another Andy. Craig Kanga
