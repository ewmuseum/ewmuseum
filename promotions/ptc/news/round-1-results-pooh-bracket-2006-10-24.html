Round 1 Results - Pooh Bracket

24 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"AVATAR" WILLIAM GWYNN V THE PHANTOM REPUBLICAN Judge 1 "Avatar" William Gwynn One of the best technically sound pieces I've read. You obviously either have a great grasp on the english language, or you went through this with a fine-toothed comb. Whichever it it, it clearly shows, and makes it for a straight through read. I enjoyed the piece, but I found it kind of dry. In some ways, that's good as it got straight to the point, but in other ways it was very much a surface piece, and we don't get too deep into the minds of anyone, especially of "Avatar" William Gwynn. Of course, it's just an intro piece, and there is only so much you can pack into a piece, but that just struck out as me as it was very much moving from point to point, and didn't leave much time to think about everything or learn more about the characters. Good piece though. The Phantom Republican A really good piece. The story just brisked by, and I was shocked to see I was at the end of it. This one really grabbed me, and it didn't let go. I didn't know what to expect at the beginning, but after a little bit I was hooked for good. Technically, everything is there. Story-wise, it's just a really good story, and a good introduction to who the Phantom Republican is if you don't know who he is. I'm interested to see where it goes from here. Pick: The Phantom Republican. Gwynn did a good job, but his story wasn't as compelling as The Phantom Republican. They both were technically sound, but Republican's story was just better. Judge 2 To be honest, I thought both roleplays suffered a great deal. Gwynn from telling a good story and cutting into the end with blahse trash talk, and TPR telling a story that had way too much going on with little pay off at the end. Both stories were technically sound, both stories were readable, but, neither one was all that memorable. I can't identify a hook that would endear me to actually look forward to a future round. Let's hope you can figure out how to hook a reader before next round, Avatar. Pick: William Gwynn. Judge 3 William Gwynn: Your intro threw me off a little. I understand the relevance to the rest of the piece once I've fully read it, but to have the character speaking so dispassionately about herself seemed...odd. The rest of the piece was a gamble, when you write from the perspective of a character who isn't the "main" character, especially in first person, it can seem like your character is just background, a secondary character. That said, I think you managed to do it well, although the piece seemed a little stilted in places. The Phantom Republican: Heh, the opening exchange between Gordon and his third step father + mother made me chuckle aloud. Overall your piece was very well written and gave me a good look at the character itself. It really seems as though you researched your subject matter thoroughly. However, although the piece served to show his distaste for the democratic system, I didn't really find it did much more then a cursory introduction to the character. Though that may have been your point, it's not always as attention getting. Tough call in this one, I ended up going back over both pieces again. In the end though, I'm going to have to give the nod to William Gwynn. That piece felt more immersive overall, and I felt was a generally more enjoyable read. William Gwynn over The phantom Republican. Pick: Gwynn Result "Avatar" William Gwynn d The Phantom Republican, 2 votes to 1 AGENTDASH vs. JASON PAYNE Judge 1 Jason Payne Wrestling For Dummies The writer sets the scene well, allowing you to get involved with the landscape before focusing in on the dialogue and character interaction. This is a good balance between the different parts of the story. The back and forth is handled well, and the Jay & Silent Bob spoof is great. The ending, while not shocking, is still fun to read even when you know it’s coming. Great comedy writing. AgentDash Political Framework – Beginning After reading the entire story, I understood where the opening scene was tied in, which is something I liked. Not knowing about the situation and having it revealed is a good hook for this kind of story. While the writer seems to be building the opening to a balance between relaxation and tension, some of the writing elements rob from this because it has to be re-read sometimes due to length of sentences. The story is able to bring you in as a reader, trying to understand exactly what is happening. Pick: Jason Payne Payne is able to masterfully script this comedy. You enjoy the build-up as part of the tournament while laughing at his unashamed use of the Jay & Silent Bob Strike Back send up. AgentDash has a good piece. The writer obviously has a plan to drive the story, but it slips from each scene into something less enjoyable. The first scene establishes tension; the second scene attempts to but feels a little stale. The last scene is important to the story, but it falls a little flat. The writer had a good plan laid out, and the story would probably progress in a nice direction, but it loses steam and momentum in a decrescendo instead of building it Judge 2 AgentDash: Leadup pieces are risky, especially when you don't give your reader a whole lot of background into what the leapup means to your main character. Although we had a lot of look into the character of Sawyer, you didn't give us a whole lot to go on as to what the problem between himself in Cedric was, aside from the fact that it was enough to warrent an FBI investigation. Aside from that it was an interesting read, although you need to watch your use of past and present tense, there were a few places where you missed it. Not a huge deal overall, but it interrupts the flow of the piece. Jason Payne: You have a great sense for detail, I could really see the scene you laid out in my mind, like watching it on the big screen instead of reading it online. That aside however, there wasn't a whole lot opf substance to your RP aside from the spoofing of Jay and Silent Bob. I understand that that was the *point*, however even with the humour it provided it didn't give your piece much to go on. I'm going to have to call with AgentDash for this one. Payne's post was funny in places, but Dash had overall more to go with and lent me more of a feeling to the character. Pick: AgentDash over Jason Payne. Judge 3 To channel Bill Lumberg for a moment: Jason. Whaaaaaat's happenin'? Listen, we're having a little problem with your PTC RP, mmmkay? Did you get the originality memo? Yeah, it's just that, we're sort of not taking things that have already been done and retrofitting them to fit our needs. So, next time, why don't you go ahead, and have an original thought, mmmkay? Super. Bye, Jason. Pick: AgentDash. Result AgentDash d Jason Payne, 2 votes to 1 BROCK METZER V NOVA Judge 1 Nova (The Quest for a Quest Leads Nova Back Home) Way long, but gave some insight into Nova that I didn't know before. Pacing issues, that could have been alleviated by maybe cutting it down a bit, but nothing too much out of whack. Verbose is good, but it can be overdone, this came close to hitting that limit. Brock Metzer (Breaking the News) While well written, this was nothing more then a small trash talk and pump up session. I'ts honestly been done quite a few times before and was nothing new. Pick: Nova. Judge 2 Brock Metzer Breaking The News A very easy to read piece with a friendly, inviting flow for a reader to follow. The strange interactions between the characters involved are entertaining. The ending is abrupt but makes sense. Nova The Quest for a Quest Leads Nova Back Home The writer’s style is very inviting. You can easily feel the surroundings while enjoying the story playing out. There is plenty of humor mixed into the writing, and the story takes you through a little back-story, a nice modern day bar scene and wraps up back home. I think Nova does an excellent job of pacing while moving the story ahead. Trying to be cute in an awkward situation with an ex is well handled here. Pick: Nova Nova has the more developed piece that moves a story along nicely. While it can easily pick up from here to continue his homebound adventure, it wraps up perfectly. Leaving the reader satisfied with the work and wanting to read the next chapter. Result Nova d Brock Metzer, 2 votes to 0 KARINA WOLFENDEN V DIGITAL MORTALITY Judge 1 Digital Mortality Good piece all around. The writing here was really good, and while the story didn't instantly grab me, by the end I was into it, and interested in what was going on. I'm interested to see where the boss is now, and what will happen from here. Technically, all looks good. Just one or two words that slipped by. Good piece all around and you should be proud of it. If there was one problem, compared to your opponent, is that it was just a little bland. Karina Wolfenden I just loved this piece. I loved how you made the reader a character, and not just any character, but the main character. It helps bring the reader in. When I was done with this piece, I wanted more, and I was upset that there wasn't more. You have a good grasp on your vocabulary and it showed throughout the piece as you weren't slapping me with it, but entertaining me with it. Great story though there isn't an overabundance of it, but you left me wanting more. Just a few technical errors that you would need to just fix if you read it through one more time. Now, I'm interested in seeing what happens next for Karina, and what I'm assuming is the grief that will follow now that her daughter is dead. Amazing piece. This one wasn't easy as both pieces were good, but Karina Wolfenden gets the victory here. Her piece just sucked me in and I fell in love with by the end. I wanted to see more right away, and that's the deciding factor in this match. Good job to both, and Mortality, you should be proud of what you did here. Pick: Karina Wolfenden Judge 2 Use of language carries this through for Wolfenden. Pick: Wolfenden Judge 3 Karina Wolfenden (The Bride : Part Zero) Heh, why do you give me the hard ones [name withheld]? Okay, this one's imagery is pretty strong. It's not as long as the opponent's, but that's not a good or bad thing. My only issue here was that at times I felt I wanted to turn away cause I just wasn't really grasping the storyline until the very end. And it's the end that gets you and fills you in completely. Digital Mortality (The Crusade) Longer and just as well written as his opponent's. But that's where the comparison of merits really stop. The choice of story arc and path is where I loose it a bit. While this has more wrestling based input, the rest of it follows a formula that's been seen. While it's hard for this one, very hard, it's gotta come down to someone who gives me something that came across as unique for me. Both gave us a 'cliffhanger" ending. But in the end, K-Wolf's had the biggest impact to me. Pick: Wolfenden Result Karina Wolfenden d Digital Mortality, 3 votes to 0 YORI YAKAMO, JR V KILLEAN SIRRAJIN Judge 1 A basic flowing loose style with entertaining and ridiculous content made this work for Yakamo. Pick: Yakamo Judge 2 Killean Sirrajin ( The Gilded Cage) Long, very long... got really slow in the middle but then finally picked up and hits you in the gut at the end and gets really relevant. The pacing is really the only thing I don't like about this. It's steady, steady, slow, slow, and then bang. There is no rise to the climax of the story presented here. One thing that is really good is chossing to end it when he did... cliffhangers are good when used properly. Yori Yakamo, jr (Yori Yakamo, jr v. The Terminating Dildos from the Future v. Jesus Christ, Vampire Hunter) What's with the title? Heh, okay, can see how this is going to go. And yeah, long, very long again. I'ts a work of parody of the terminator series! Okay, and it's all about this man's one track mind of sex. It's parody, but it's not even funny parody. Talks all about sex, but has nothing about it really there. The big problem is, while the parodied storyline has been done, this would seem to be a storyline we'd expect from someone else and it wouldn't be as lacking in the ability to keep my interest. The pacing here is way off, and it seems to jump around too much to keep the reader's attention. In all reality, if this had been broken up into two rp's, it would have had a much better impact. Pick: Sirrajin Yes, most go for the prose style in today's world, but Killiean goes for script. And it's good script and the storyline was a bit more to chew on. He needs to work on his pacing, but in the end it was the better piece. Judge 3 Yori- Loved the roleplay. It was wild, it was entertaining, and it was fun. It's one of those kinds of rps where you just really like to sit back and have a good time. The only problem I had was that it really lacked a real character driving story. I know that isn't for everyone, and it's not the most important thing in the world, but I felt like as funny as this roleplay was and such it simply didn't do anything to make me care about Yori or even wonder what a person might have in store afterwards. It's going to be a tough call either way though as this roleplay was extrememly entertaining. Killean- Good story. Developed the character well, tied up some questions about Winters and Charity and how that came about. I liked the real human feel to Killean and how he was sitting around being a normal guy, drinking beer, eating wings, and chilling with his buddy. I really enjoyed the story and am interested in seeing what goes on from here. That said it's still going to be a very tough call as where your rp had more story based entertainment, Yori's had that zany off the wall kind of entertainment that is fun to see from time to time. This match up was honestly extremely close and extremely tough to call. It's one of those battles that I honestly am not sure that there really is a clear cut winner. I've read both pieces a couple of times now and I've enjoyed them both a lot. I think both writers brings something good to this tournament and it will be a shame for either one of them to be out after this match up. Still there has to be a winner and it's with a large amount of props that I congratulate Killean on his win and continued progress into the GTT. Yori really should hold his head up high though as I was absolutely impressed with his comedy style in his roleplay and found it highly entertaining. Pick: Sirrajin Result Killean Sirrajin d Yori Yakamo, Jr, 2 votes to 1 SUMMARY - "Avatar" William Gwynn d The Phantom Republican, 2 votes to 1 - AgentDash d Jason Payne, 2 votes to 1 - Nova d Brock Metzer, 2 votes to 0 - Karina Wolfenden d Digital Mortality, 3 votes to 0 - Killean Sirrajin d Yori Yakamo, Jr, 2 votes to 1 - Derek Barlow and Horazon battled to an epic match of the year candidate, leaving Rollins/MacGreggor for dead, that finally resulted in a double no-show. - Damien Cruz d Cadence Rain (her name is Rob, for all you sucks out there) via no-show.
