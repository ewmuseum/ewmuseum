Round 1 Bracket 4 Results inside

7 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Bracket 4 Match 13: Smokey Wead (UWF) defeated Kirk Urban (WWA) 2 votes to 1 Judge 1 Good rp's from both sides here, The win was locked in with Wead's second RP easy, all rp's kept me locked in until the end which is always a good thing and they are very talented writers, good match. Judge 2 Smokey's nonsensical roleplays are strangely entertaining, and anyone who takes cheap shots at France obviously has a good grasp of humour, but I'm going to have to go with Urban on this one. While funny at times, Smokey doesn't really put up anything for the tournament or even wrestling. He's just sort of floating through his roleplays on cloud high. Urban, on the other hand, addressed Wead on a personal level and sealed the deal with a metaphor that wrapped up the message of his roleplay. Judge 3 Give this one to Smokey. He wrote better, and had a little bit of a better story than did Urban. Match 14: Jimmy Diablo defeated Marcel Celestini and Emilio Dervante, 2 votes to 0 Judge 1 I'm going to have to do with Diablo as the winner here. Dervante told a good story, as did Celestini, but Diablo has the intangible here. It really seems through his writing that he really wanted this, possibly more than the other two. Skylar's a hell of a writer, and Simon is as well. Dervante surprised me with his writing skill as well. If Simon has posted one more RP, he very well could have taken this. Sadly, it was beyond his control, and he's the odd man out. Judge 2 There were a couple fo reasons why I thought Diablo should win. Firstly, his RP's had more relevance to the match, where the other two hardly mentioned it. Diablo's first RP was excellent, as it provided a great deal of insight and background into the character, which made it easy to realize just who the character was. The second RP was not as good compared to the first, but it still tied up his story nicely. With Dervante, at first I was a bit confused as to where his story was going and I thought there were some major loopholes with the story, until it was revealed that it was all a dream, and the blurring between reality and the dream was done very well. The first RP let him down though, I thought. Celestini's first RP was written well, but not doing a second RP didn't warrant consideration to advance. Overall I thought Diablo's RP's were more consistent and relevant to the match, and they were technically written better than Dervante's with some excellent use of description and dialogue. Match 15: Karina Wolfenden (RPW) d Limp (UPWA) 2 votes to 0 Judge 1 Limp's RPs were ... odd? His first RP was more of a stringing together of three individual ones, with no real inter-locking story. He passed between ranting at Wolfden and Chezina so smoothly that I'm not sure if the character was even supposed to realize there was any difference between the two. I'm not really sure if Limp is just deluded or not, as a few times he made references to the "many, many times" he's promised to win this tournament, which were apparently missing in his pre-first-RP-RP's? From a writing standpoint, the RPs were fairly mediocre. Run-on sentences, horrible spelling at points, obviously little proof-reading. I'm not a fan of short-stories being passed off as RPs, though, so I can look past that ... but even as a typical RP, it falls short, mostly due to the horrible structure tying all of the individual scenes/rants together. The character had a few bright-spots shining out in the RP with a handful of entertaining bits of dialogue, but outside of those, the RPs don't come close to competing at this level. For Karina ... it was the first RP I can ever remember reading from the character, and it was extremely impressive, to the point of instantly making me a fan of hers. The only criticism I can possibly think of is a certain lack of fluidity in the reading, probably because of my prejudice against overly elaborate descriptions in RPs. It just seems to sort of ruin the smooth pacing of the RP and the dialogue, but that's a fairly unimportant point. The dialogue, description, and story were all great and far better than her opponent's in every category. Karina easily beats Limp. Judge 2 Karina Wolfenson #1- We all knew we would see great stuff from Mat. Once again he delivered. I enjoyed this rp as one of the better I've read of the tournament. Pros- He really allowed you to see the Karina both the wrestler and the personality. It was beautifully wrote and the perfect balance between length and quality. You just wrote until you felt you came to a proper end without forcing length. Cons- After reading this I saw nothing that made Karina a likeable face. She's a neutral who the normal fan can not relate to. I don't like Karina the person or wrestler but that has nothing to do with the way it was wrote or anything, just the personality. I assume Mat meant to do that but I saw she is suppose to be a face and that was not clear in this rp. Limp #1- Getting Mat in the first round is the hardest thing in the tournament. Limp had a big challenge up ahead for him. Pro- You looked like you gave it a try. Con- Did you just paste different roleplays together and use "Later" as an excuse to make them seem like 1 rp? The first one you didn't even seem to know your opponent as you called Karina egoistical and him. Maybe the him was a typo. I considered it as such since you corrected it later. You mentioned too much about your own fed instead of PTC. It seemed you was more worried about that match then the one against Kariana. Winner: Karina Wolfenden. Simply put Limp got out classed in every field. Match 16: Dawn McFarlaine (WMW) defeated Tessa Windsor (RPW) and Bob the Temp (XUW) 2 votes to 0 Judge 1 Dawn McFarlaine First RP: As it is a common theme in this tournament is lack of confidence and using the tourney as a means to prove or redeem oneself, I can’t say that this is an original plot. That doesn’t take away from the warm fuzzy feeling I got from reading this story. Technical proficiency is more than acceptable, despite a few obscure typos. The one issue I have with this piece is that some of the descriptive work is done out of habit, and doesn’t actually add any value to the reader’s experience. Pick your spots a bit better. Second RP: Sticking with the self doubts… One psychological aspect of this game is that if you suck the judge right in to your work, you can actually get the judge feeling the “I can’t do it” that you are writing about, and that can work against you. Going with the “Eye of the Tiger” style closure with the sparring match was a good positive finish though. Tessa got the encouragement from a look and a smile, and Dawn got it from being goaded into making a serious effort at something. Tessa Windsor First RP: A tale of self doubt and negativity from others including complete strangers, ending with the simplest form of encouragement working to turn things around. Solid without being spectacular. Technically sound, only spotted one typo. Second RP: Tessa had a great theme in her grasp – jealousy, but wasted it by over-analysing the psychology of the tournament and the GTD match, who was doing what and who wanted it more. That filler type of material reminded me of something that someone would write in a match preview or picks thread, rather than a roleplay. Bob the Temp First RP: This piece sort of wondered around a bit, with funny comedy spots and a quite interesting why men must defeat women at all times theory, I definitely loved the spot about the mini van, but ultimately I didn’t get any real feeling of purpose to it, possibly due to there being too much Ric Austin in it. I do understand why it was done that way, but it didn’t work for me. Second RP: Talks too much about promos, making me feel odd about the dividing line between IC and OOC. Once that phase passed, I quite liked the rest of it, much better than the first one – it would have fit right at home in an episode of That 70’s Show, minus the hair styles and flaired pants. Decision: Tough one. All three are solid writers and have nothing to be ashamed about in their effort. I can’t give it to Tessa because of the second RP’s lack of depth. Bob’s are the first RP set I can remember reading in this tourney that uses script instead of novel. Both have their pros and cons which don’t need to be gone into here. How can I say this – McFarlaines novelling worked better than your scripting in terms of drawing me in and making me want to read more. Not suggesting you should change, but trying to tell you what influenced the decision. Winner: Dawn McFarlaine Judge 2 Tessa WIndsor - RPW The girl's got some skill. Her first was pretty well put together, albeit for the somewhat weak storyline. She's on a trail of doubt, thanks to Chris Storm, and unless she wins this round, she's going to prove Storm right. It's almost like the two characters of Storm and Windsor are woven together. When one has success, the other falls into utter failure. Her second effort was a bit more trash talking than I've seen from her in the past, but not done in a way where she was really trash talking them. It was more in her mind. Both were well written, and easy reads. Bob the Temp - XUW. Bob the Temp is not actually a wrestler, per se. He's the guy that does the narration in all of Ric Austin's promos. I'd imagine this is a pretty good job, despite the way that Ric treats Bob. It's an interesting dynamic actually. The guy's supposed to be the voice of the Former World Champion, and yet, the two have absolutely nothing in common. It's a good angle, but compared to Tessa and Dawn's efforts, this stands up as rather lackluster. I would have liked to see more about Bob himself, other than the fact that he's a married narrator. Dawn McFarlane - WMW This is the suprise of the tournament for me. I had no idea who Dawn was before this. She put together a fantastic first RP, and followed it up with a great second effort. She not only gave her opponents credit, but actually questioned if she should go through with it, because it seemed that Tessa wanted it more. The only downside was the sparring match at the end, but, that was still well written, but didn't seem to have much of a place in the whole scheme of things. The call. I've got to give this one to McFarlane. She wrote her tail off, and it showed. Tessa's effort was good, but proved not quite good enough. Either one of the other two would have done well against another opponent, but Dawn's got to be the dark horse after this fantastic effort. CW1 brackets
