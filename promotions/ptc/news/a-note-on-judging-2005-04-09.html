A note on judging

9 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I have provided the judges with a set of guidelines by which to do their job. The main part is that the direction for selecting a pick is this - pick which set of RP's you liked better, and explain why. That's it. There is no reference to "relevance" or any of the other things you guys love to debate over and over each tournament or event, other than to say that they should not be taking any of that into consideration when making their pick of which they liked better. That is not to say that any of that won't affect their decisions - it may well do so, and if it does, such is life. The point is that given that they took the time to volunteer and will hopefully deliver the required picks and comments, they should be allowed to make their picks based on their own values, and should not be later on vilified for doing exactly that. To make this clear, and to use a reference point that most of us will be familiar with - if they liked "Dave Hurst's work" better than "Angelo Deville's work" on a given day, then they should not live in fear having anything thrown back in their faces for making that pick. Anyone who disagrees with this, feel free to resign from the tournament if you are a player, or volunteer to judge yourself if you aren't and think you can do it "right". If you are judging, then it should be right up your alley, as it is relying on your own sense of how things should go, not mine or anyone elses. If you are none of those and choose instead to sit back and throw rocks at any of the judging decisions later on, then expect to be bundled out of here rather quickly, because I have no time for it. Constructive disagreement with the judging picks I don't have a problem with. Calling the entire judging panel something akin to a bunch of incompentent wankers, or making unprovable and thus quite safe accusations of judges having personal agendas, is what I have a real issue with. If you do that, you are seriously doing no better than what you are complaining about. Capishke?
