GROUP 2 JUDGING

27 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PYNCHON Jeb Stewart vs. Hoyt Williams -- Pete JEB STEWART Technically, your writing is sound. Emotionally, it's vacant and vapid, as if you set out to tell a great story, but forgot all the pesky emotive details that would tie a reader into your story. I will say that the sidekick getting a moment to shine and growing a spine is always a nice touch. If you advance to the next round, mix in some emotion to the story. I really don't feel like Jeb gives a damn about his mother, because while your words say so, your emotions definitely don't and there's nothing telling me much of anything about their connection in the past. HOYT WILLIAMS I like the story here. I just wish there were more of it. The non-sequitir and disjunction in every paragraph is a little much, and -- frankly -- feels somewhat formulaic. That said, the story was a hoot, and when stripped down, there's a story here that's emotional, and something that tied me in much more than I got with your opponent's peice. There's a raw power to it, an 'I lived this' quality to the writing that's probably much more Al L. than it is Hoyt Williams. VERDICT I'm going to take Hoyt Williams to advance here. Where Jeb was more technically sound, the story was also simple, and unemotional. While raw and unedited and rife with mistakes, there was something endearing about the Hoyt story. Drake Coleman vs. Spacely -- Pete DRAKE COLEMAN This reminds me of a line from Gin and Juice -- I had to back up off a bit and put my cup down. Your writing is like an acid trip, teetering from the best sentences ever to the brink of a bad trip, over and over. It's good, but, it's so back and forth, and just so... odd that it's hard to place any sort of feeling onto it. I liked it, and I didn't, but, I liked it. So weird, Sam. SPACELY It's a weird split with these first two matches. Where one is something, the other fills it's void. I liked this story because after Drake's, I didn't have to read too hard. I channelled my inner 13 year old, and I giggled. A lot. That said, I've got to think there's more than just coming up with weird places and situations to make an RP good, eh? I liked it, but, I didn't. VERDICT Drake Coleman in somewhat of a shocker. I really dug Spacely's story, and hate that he's gotta go home. But, Drake's story was amazing and horrifying at the same time, and had something that's rare within EW stories: You really had to think hard about it. Desade vs. Christian Mathias -- [name withheld] DESADE Aside from the length, I really don't have a lot of criticism for this RP. The writer is absolutely gifted at painting a picture with words. I mean, the settings were the easiest by far to visualize, and that goes a long way for trying to understand what's going on in a roleplay. There's nothing worse than trying to read someone ham-handedly describing surroundings. The story itself was very heavy, but I think it was a good entree into the writer's story. It felt both like a self-contained story and the beginning of an epic. The dialogue was good but not great, but I don't think it had to be with the stunning prose. Excellent RP. CHRISTIAN MATHIAS This was a very good read, and like with the Desade RP, I struggle to find a lot wrong with it. While I thought the beginning and ending paragraphs seemed a bit clunky, I thought that the story itself was good, with the dialogue between the players being excellent. The writer has a very good feel for how people should talk to each other. Very authentic, very engaging, very easy to read. If I was going to find fault with the piece, it would be with the prose. It felt a bit mechanical at times, not bad per se, but not really alive. There's such a contrast between the prose and the dialogue text, and I don't think it helps the RP out. Plus, I kinda cringed at the mention of 50 Cent's cologne... it felt more like a shill. Still, I thought this was a very enjoyable read. VERDICT It's a damn shame that someone is going to be going home after this round. Mathias' piece was good enough to advance against most other opponents, but Joe wrote an almost perfect RP. With the number of no-shows happening, it almost seems unfair, but someone has to win, and Desade is my pick. I would strongly suggest Mathias for any play-in opportunities in the future. His is a story that deserves to continue, but I can't put it over Desade here. Karina Wolfenden vs. "The Buzz" Cal Roberts -- Pete VERDICT Karina Wolfenden. I wish Cal would had more time to tell his story. Unfortunately, he didn't and there's a lot more in just the intro to Karina's peice than there is to Cal's entire story. I'd give more feedback, but, I can't. FAULKNER Clinton Sage vs. Jay Phoenix -- Pete CLINTON SAGE I can't get over the fact that during the moment where this monster of a man who's done quite a few evil deeds in his days, plays the dick when the doctor's trying to get a picture of the family. He's a sociopath such that even on the best day of his life, he's gotta screw with convention. I'd bet it sets up the future where he can't give it all up for his daughter and returns to being "The Seventh Sin." Just a thought. I'm not sure why you chose to bring Roman back to life and kill Ronnie again, when Ronnie was clearly the aggressor, and clearly the one who put Roman up to everything. That's a weird choice, and not the one I think any father would have made, despite the wrath clouding Sage's mind. In fact, that choice really bothers me for a reason I can't fathom. Maybe the fact that as a father, I'd have protected my child and only my child, and fuck Sage for being Sage. JAY PHOENIX This must be dichotomy day. Where Ryan's was fairly linear and easy to follow, I feel like I need a map before I even read anything here. I don't follow much of anything, linearly, and it seems to be just vestiges of good stories, tied together to make a modicum of sense. Your prose is outstanding, and I dare say far surpasses Ryan's story. I've always said Ryan was much more the Dean Koontz/John Grisham of PTC, with easily digested stories that are never too much or too little. Yours are stunningly visual with very little detail given, a sense of darkness from your word choices without being too dark, or, overtly dark. That said, I think you should have gone for the gusto. There's nothing wrong -- in the pretext of a story -- by adding the -er to the word at the end that will be censored on the forums. By using 'nigga', a word that's taken to the vernacular as meaning friend, you destroy the entire pretense of why they're there. It would have been so much more powerful with the -er ending. The -a strips it, and leaves me with the same feeling I got from Ryan's peice when he brought Roman to life, not Ronnie. VERDICT: On my first reading, I was ready to kick Jay to the curb, proclaim Ryan the winner and rub it in Jay's face in our secret, porn filled admin forum. But, I wasn't so sure, so, I read each story again, and Jay vaulted to the top on the strength of his prose, and the story -- while choppy -- has a far better payoff than Ryan's which I expected out of the gate. Using a more fosisticated eye, it comes down to characterization, and Ryan's far exceed Jays. Ryan's develop through the story, both literally and figuratively. Jay's are characitures of people that "fit the mold" of racism, when fact is, people like me -- educated, intelligent -- are far more likely to be racist than backwoods hillfolk. Clinton Sage cashes in and assures that we lose one seed from each bracket. Kaden Calix vs. Seymour Almasy vs. Jared Sykes -- Pete KADEN CALIX I think you're overstating what you're trying to say. You're using a lot of words to talk about pulling things from pockets to look at them, and it doesn't come off as necessarily better. You have a good understanding of what to write, and your structure is absolutely sound. I think what you need now is figure out how to write it, and you could, quite possibly, be a serious contender for GTT8. SEYMOUR ALMASY I am not the worlds biggest fan of Almasy. That's been apparent over the years. This story, however? A marked improvement over the characterization as well as the story. I'm not sure that I can put my finger on it, exactly. Maybe it's your age and the ability to use other characters (whereas back in the day, you were so Almasy-centric there wasn't room for anyone else) that's helped refine Almasy, but, take it to a different level. That said, the third act could have been trimmed, significantly. There wasn't a lot of need for a substantial portion of it. JARED SYKES My first gripe is that you shouldn't have given away that Amy grabbed Jared's blackberry. Would have set the story up nicer for later when he finally met with her, y'know. I also think you're a bit dry through the meeting. The story is good, and there's very little wrong, aside from very little actually happening. It's like Seinfeld, and I hated Seinfeld. The section with Amy is good, but, long. Lord, oh Lord is it long. Chop, chop, chop isn't a bad thing. VERDICT To me, it comes down to Almasy vs. Sykes. Calix, your story served a purpose, but, the pitfall of vocabulary caught you. Work on that, and this likely isn't close. So, who wins? The two stories are the antithesis of the round, being that they're strikingly similar. They're fantastic for the first half, and then drag in the second to the point of struggling, only to payoff with a great conclusion. It's going to have to come down to the story I want to see more, which is both. It's going to have to then come down to the character I want to see more, which is... both. So, who wins? I'm giving the nod to Jared Sykes. KASHIEL VS. BARON VON BLACKBERRY -- [name withheld] Kashiel - Sympathy for the Demon Putting my distaste for the manifestation of the supernatural in e-wrestling writing aside, I had a couple of problems with the piece. One, the writing felt a bit stilted to me, a bit forced and unnatural. If you're going to get cute with using the thesaurus, you have to make sure that it flows well. Several times during the RP, my internal dialogue felt like it was being interrupted despite the fact that it had my undivided attention. It felt disjointed. Secondly, it felt like the wrestling aspect was just shoved in there to make it topical to an e-wrestling tournament. I appreciate that the effort was made, but it felt like he could have inserted truck driver, actor or really any other profession and it wouldn't have made a difference. Other than that, I thought it was passable, a good opening salvo. It made me interested in how Kashiel is going to resolve the problem, and it put a fresh spin on the tired "sold your soul to Satan" story mechanism. If the writer focuses on how to make sentences flow into each other, he could improve drastically since I like his flair for the descriptive. Baron von Blackberry - Ame ("Rain") BvB is Coral Avalon? No f'n way! Reading the RP though, it's made abundantly clear that BvB isn't going to be making any appearance through the piece. It's all Coral. I don't know what to think about that, seeing that they're one in the same and that this piece is backstage in-character and not kayfabe in-character, if you catch my drift. To the piece itself, I thought that the writer did a great job setting the mood. The description of training under Joey Malone sounded authentic, which is a huge plus for writing in the first person. Once we get into the interactions with Inoue, the RP starts to unravel a bit. The energy I get from the intro, both describing the rain and the training, starts to leech out, and that authenticity in the narrative description of Coral's background begins to fade away as he moves into dialogue. Since the RP really is centered around the interactions between Coral and Inoue, I felt that it was hurt by the petering out of quality. Still, it sets up a very intriguing story that infuses schoolyard-style romantic comedy into a wrestling locker room. VERDICT While [name withheld]'s RP wasn't his best by any stretch of the imagination, it's good enough to beat Low Priest's handily. Sorry, but while the Kashiel story has a lot of promise, I can't advance it against the Baron von Blackberry RP. It's too choppy. You do have a lot of potential, so that if you work out technical issues, you'll be raring to go in your home fed or in the next big PTC tourney. Andy M. (12) vs. Tchu -- Pete ANDY M. Good wrestling-centric story. Maybe it's making a comeback with a martini glass toast to the profession before getting to the meat of the story, instead of a blatant disregard for even mentioning a wrestling background. There's good interaction here, and you do a good job of both giving Andy's story, letting the reader get entrenched a bit in his story before moving on to the next part. There isn't too much here, it's just enough to whet the appetite for the next round, if'n you make it that far. TCHU I felt more about that playground than I did for Matt, Scott or Leon. Your writing comes up great -- there's a goodly bit of prose to the peice that gives me a feeling that wherever Matt's retired to, it's downtrodden and bleek, like my neighborhood right now. Not ghetto, just depressed, aching for something -- anything to restore it to glory. Kind of like the Tchu character, considering their lives parallelled each other. VERDICT: Andy M. The stories seemed to parallell each other in some fashion. Andy's had a bit more meat to his story, and I did get much more connected to the Andy character than I did with Tchu's playground. Lane Stevens (4) v. Impulse FULL DISCLOSURE: I am friends with Pete R., handler of Impulse, and have nary said a word to Ben, the handler of Lane Stevens. If you have issue with this, or think that my bias towards a friend has clouded my judgement, please let me know. I write this without reading any of either story, just FYI. LANE STEVENS Christ, your writing is laid back, and impossibly cool. Of the Dead Man's Hand group in GTT, I think yours flows the easiest, a nonchalant brilliance to the characterizations and how easily you weave the day job in with the other bullshit. This is the second story in all of GTT that I've read from beginning to end without wanting to make a note to myself to remember for a comment for later. IMPULSE I think your story is good, too. They both have a "day in the life" kinda feel about them, and they're both well written. That said, I think some self editing could have helped out. There's a lot of superfluous information here and I think there were bits and peices that could have been cut to trim the overall length. There were a few spots that I think the story could have stopped, ready for another day, but, that's nitpicky. VERDICT In yet another close matchup, Lane Stevens defeats Impulse who wrote a roleplay that could have beaten good 90% of this field, had the draw gone differently.
