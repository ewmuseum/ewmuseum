Bracket Final Results - Eeyore

29 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
VIOLENCE JACK V JACOB MCKAIL Judge 1 Violence Jack: Like everything else I've ever read of yours, these efforts were very solid both technically and storytelling-wise. I liked the possibility of Snow's dead wife having been an affiliate of the Sect, and the inclusion of Clint Sage offered the potential of amazing storylines. Shanahan's thin smile throughout the first piece reminded me a little of Sin City -- except he's a bit cooler than Elijah Wood. And the stormy atmosphere matched well with what was happening in the second. Jacob McKail: You're not here by accident, that's for certain. What you gave me were two pretty well-crafted roleplays with a storyline you can follow pretty easily. And though I liked both the stalker and sister bits, I'm wondering if you'll have enough time in later rounds, if you get through this one, to tie up all the loose ends. You seem to be putting new strings in each roleplay. Both writers did very well, but I'm going to select VJ because the combination of storyline and finesse put him over for me. Pick: Violence Jack Judge 2 Jacob McKail Unleashing Darkness V: Apathy One thing that I think really positively about re: the structure of this piece is its simplicity. It's basically nicely divided into easy to read and digest pieces, which has the effect of making very easy reading. I don't have to go back a number of times to read and re-read, making sure I didn't miss anything. Still, though, the story doesn't lack complexity, it's just written in a way that makes the various threads much easier to deal with. There are some things about this first piece that throw me off. First, there's something about the story elements that seems a little arbitrary. I can't quite work my head around, for instance, why it's really better for the story that McKail talk to a hallucination than just to himself in introspection, nor does it make sense to me why McKail would be nice to, and even agree to help a woman described as "his stalker." He seems to have no reaction whatsoever to her showing up, and that is part and parcel with one of the other cons that I notice for this piece- emotionally, it's not quite "on" for me. I'm having a bit of trouble connecting to the story and the motives and the desires of the characters here. That having been said, though, the writing is certainly competent, and on top of that, I think that the cut in the action is artfully done so that just enough is revealed to make the reader want to keep on reading to the second part in order to see how the story plays out. Unleashing Darkness VI: Apathy (Part Two) I don't think I like this one as well as I do the first one- the story is not as tightly constructed. The first scene of this RP bogs me down a little bit in the reading, because it seems to repeat the same details over and over, which sort of has the effect, for me, at least, of dulling their importance, and making me gloss over the words when I read them. The scene with Kate was better, in the sense that it played out the story I was waiting to see, and left a little bit of a cliffhanger to work with for the future as well. There's a detail that I don't understand, though, as I'm reading- why wouldn't McKail just say that he'd done what Mary asked him to by succeeding in wrestling, and anything coming from her essentially beyond the grave that tried to change the nature of the promise was meaningless? Why does the fact that his wife wants him to move on with his life suddenly make his life meaningless? I'm just not understanding the character motivation here, which tends to have the effect of blunting the final scene to the point where I spend more time asking why it's necessary than being shocked by what McKail has asked for. That said, I like what you've done with the request, given that it's not what you would have expected on reading the first part of it. I'm interested to see where it could go from here, if only to try to work it out for myself. Violence Jack Strange Bedfellows Wow. First off, I just note how incredibly thick this piece is with details; it's very richly written, and for that reason, it takes me a long time to digest everything, though it's well worth the time that it takes. The story is, of course, incredibly intricate, not only in what's on the surface, but in the allusions to other stories that are packed in there as something of an extra treat. I enjoy the very forbidding feel of the story as well. My complaints on this piece are few: firstly, there's just so much going on that it's hard to keep track of it all. I'm trying to remember names and keep them straight as to who's doing what, and it's a bit much to grapple with, especially given that I'm coming in on the middle of the story. The other thing is that while the writing is really quite good, there are times when the sentences get so long that they get a little tangled up with one another, and I have to re-read them to pick out the main idea that I need to get. Overall, though, a highly enjoyable story, with a sinister feel that's very well played and intriguing. Justice & Vengeance, Redemption & Damnation I think this is a more than adequate payoff from the setup of the piece before it. I like the way that the structure quickens the pace of what's going on, and the writing style reflects the way everything has been tightened and sped up with shorter, terser scenes. I have to give you compliments on the way wrestling is worked into this piece so that it doesn't stick out like a sore thumb. With the sort of story that you're writing, I wouldn't have expected the ability to do that, but you do it with ease, and in a way that, moreover, doesn't seem contrived or silly. Additionally, it gets me involved- I want to know what Clinton Sage knows as much as Knight does, I start getting invested in the mad visions of VJ. That says something to me about your power as a writer, to take a character that's espousing the downfall of everything that's traditionally "good" and make him somehow engaging in his struggle. I also like the parallel you've got going with the storm, vis-a-vis what's going on with Sage and Knight; it's a subtle enough symbol that I don't feel like you're banging my face into it repeatedly, and it works all the better for that. I really can't come up with any cons for this one- I enjoyed its foreboding tone, and the way it continued the story while both leaving room for more fleshing out of all its various angles, and making sure that this RP still has a sense of ending, instead of just leaving a big 'to be continued...' hanging out the bottom end of it. I don't honestly have to deliberate too long to choose who's got this one well in hand. Both writers brought a lot to the table, and both writers are technically skilled writers. But in that, it's also clear for me that one writer has a better grip on how to unfurl a story so that it grips his reader with the vividness of the details and the tightness of the plot. VJ, with a very well-styled pair this round, takes the win. Pick: Violence Jack Judge 3 VJ: Very creative plot in both parts. Got slightly confused at the first one but the stuff I had issue with was clarified by the second. In a tournament like this be a little more willing to give background info to the reader about characters and such. Loved the dialogue and the details, but perhaps a bit more quality to the way you move from scenes....seemed you jumped around a lot in the second rp. Honestly I've seen you around PTC for years and I was never a huge fan of your work but this stuff was very good and, if nothing else, you've earned yourself a new fan. JM: The story and detail was very good. I liked the letters in the rps but the dialogue never really interested me. It seemed a bit forced at times and I felt that the length was not adequate to the story, you could have gone further in multiple scenes and really knocked a round out of the park. Instead, I felt let down by much of it. You're a great writer but I think there are areas that could be improved upon. Pick: Jacob McKail :nutkick: Violence Jack Judge 4 It's bracket finals, so I'm doing big judging. Sorry if it offends, folks. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan 1 Interesting opening. Good style of writing, though a couple of minor points could be raised, such as consistency of number/word formatting and the last line of the intro, which sounds more like a DJ than a doctor. The story drags you in though, and that overcomes any minor niggles. This is very seductive writing. It pulls you in very quickly, with great interplay between characters and gives a sense of something much larger going on between various sects, organisations and suchlike. It's almost like an pre-Apocalyptic confrontation, in the way that the forces of evil and (presumably) good are staring at one another, knowing that eventually they'll have to seriously attempt to destroy the other, but the Horsemen haven't yet arrived so they can't start just now. Couple of issues (I'm currently about halfway through): One is that I don't know enough about some of these people or about the organisations in general. Second is how is Shanahan wrestling in the tourney (I remember hearing from someone that another might be wrestling in his place, but it needs to be covered in the RP at some point)? Third is occasional (but not significant) slips in style of dialogue. The most obvious example is the 'foretell your doom' line after Marcus said it was time to cut the bullshit didn't ring true in terms of the way he was speaking; it feels like he should be shying away from the grandiose descriptive words instead of it. It's only minor though. Not sure what's going on in the Maury interlude, but it sounds interesting. It carries on, and it's written very well. Dawson's rage against Shanahan is interesting, as is the positioning of the doctor. Then the ending. I had guessed this would happen, as I recognised talk of meteors and Eyes (I read Sage's first roleplay), and the name 'Sage' had cropped up a fair few times. As such, it was more a 'meh' than a 'wow', and seemed very unnecessary to try to end with some super crazy crossover twist instead of at a more natural point (like if that last bit hadn't been there), where an excellently written tale would stand on its own instead of looking for shock value. Still, overall, a great bit of work, sir. Jacob McKail 1 Interesting introduction. It brings me into McKail's world pretty easily, which is good. A couple of notes would be that the continued repetition of the fact that Mickey was a 'hallucination' or 'figment of his imagination' was very tiresome. At the risk of being a little obsessive, you used those phrases fourteen times in a moderately short scene, and it really messed up the flow. By the third time I was pretty damn sure he was a hallucination, and you could have just said 'Mickey', or something else. Secondly... he's pretty friendly with his stalker! BUT... it is intriguing, and largely written well. The story continues, and it's entertaining and interesting. I want to know who the hollow man is, and what the rest of the letter says. I also want to know more about McKail. I'm imagining that the boyfriend is mixed up in something a little illegal, and this could go one of many ways. This leads to the ending, and I liked that. It gives you the mystery of where McKail's money has been going, and what he is saving it for, without revealing too much. You get an idea that it involves the letter, but just the right amount has been set out for the reader to follow up on next time round. Nicely done. McKail 2 To say that this one was better than the first would be something of an understatement. I actually read all the way through without taking any notes, which is always a good sign. First off, one minor negative is a recurrence of small mistakes with grammar and spelling. I won't be a Nazi about it, but please look to proof read or learn the different between your/you're, and the correct use of apostrophes (for instance, you DO NOT use them for plurals, as you did in the first paragraph). Now we move on to the positives. The resolution with the stalker was very good. I liked that at the end McKail walked away and didn't care any more. I also liked that it was implied that the girl was just hitting McKail with a sting (the boyfriend's regret at hitting her and her shock leading the reader to believe she earlier lied), but still left a tiny bit of lingering doubt with the gunshot. The whole thing could be seen as being a throw-away moment, just trying to pad out the story with something that ultimately goes nowhere... but I prefer to see it as the final straw that breaks Jacob. The letter revealed to him a betrayal from the woman he loved, and that causes him to betray her in kind with the breaking of his promise; a lovely piece of situational irony. It does lead to questions though. One is why she would have written it? She must have had a good idea that he would get pissed off, and given that he was going to live on as a wrestler, she would have achieved her goal without having to reveal her duplicity. Also, with McKail in this state (one can only assume that in the past he has flirted with depression, suicide and the like), would he still go out there and try to wrestle, fulfilling his obligations? Maybe so, to make the 'ending' a surprise to him... but it does seem unusual. The ending itself, I got a couple of lines before it was revealed, and it was a good twist, as that kind of timing means that your brain works it out just as you're reading it, though that may have just been a lucky coincidence. After the first pieces I thought that your opponent's writing prowess would be too much for you, but this has brought you right back into it. Shanahan 2 Another one I read all the way through, but this time I don't think that it was quite as good as the excellent first offering. The first section started a little melodramatically, and the shifts in tone between certain areas of the roleplay still don't seem entirely comfortable to me. I liked the ending of that section though, with the promise of Tully being raised up to the level of a messiah. The second section, with Knight and Sage, did little for me. I don't know enough about what they're talking about. I remember Sage having been around some meteor and it giving him some powers, but it's presumptuous to think I'd even know that much. The third section almost seemed to be recapping, and from then on it became a sort of crescendo, bringing together dramatic elements with the storm... which I like... but the only problem was that the Sage character had been far too briefly introduced for me to care about him in any real sense, and the proclamations by 'Violence Jack' at the end were a little, well, arrogant... pompous even. He doesn't seem to be as in control of everything as he thinks he is, and whilst furthering the cause of his 'darkness' via wrestling and a search for power is part of his scheme, I do not know what this scheme is, other than some odd plan to destroy God, Heaven and all that jazz. His methods for this? I don't know, and that niggles me somewhat. Let this not distract from the main points of the piece though. This is excellently written. All of the main characters have distinct personalities, be they the dark manipulator VJ himself, the eager acolyte Tully or the cold, calm destructiveness of Ethan Knight. Summary: This is excruciatingly close. I've changed my mind over who should win, and why, several times in the duration of this sentence. In fact, I still don't know who to give the win to. (note – whilst the next few paragraphs while I make my decision are often critical of the roleplays, it is only because with two excellent offerings, I have to find out what I didn't like more than what I did like, since I liked most things. Please be assured that I thought both efforts were amongst the best I have thus far read) VJ's strength is obvious, and stares everyone right in the face; he's a genuinely fantastic writer. The whole shadowy world that these characters inhibit is brought to life brilliantly, and I do want to see where the next round or rounds of this story could go. If he wins, does he destroy the world? McKail, with all due credit to him, is not as good a technical writer as VJ. He does well (opening repetition of words aside), but in terms of creating a mood, using vocabulary or drawing you in to a whole world, VJ is clearly ahead. What McKail did though was write a more complete story over the two pieces of writing. His roleplays grew with every scene, cranking up the tension and then throwing in a few unexpected, yet believable, turns. This contrasts with VJ, who actually reduced my interest with the second piece. The first one drew me into a world completely, and then the second one took me away into a side-plot and then recapped a lot of what was either said or intimated in the first one. As such, on the storyline alone McKail comes out the winner, and I'd say he's now ahead. But what next? With Violence Jack I'd like to see what the bother with Sage is all about, and whether he is dead or not (if he isn't you're a total cop-out merchant). I'd like to see more about how the Vatican opposes VJ's followers, and what the business with Raven was about, because that was a big part of the first lot and then not mentioned in the second at all, other than in the briefest of passing mentions. Then there's McKail... what the hell happens to him next. In one way I would like to see him go on so I can find out, but equally it's almost the perfect ending to his story, which the writer seems to indicate with his 'THE END' to finish with (unless he does that every round). Which would lead me back to VJ, were it not for the fact that one of the reasons I want to know more is that I wasn't shown enough in that second promo! Aarrgghh! I booked an e-fed for over a year, and this is amongst the toughest calls I have had. In my mind this is coming down to what VJ has done and hasn't done in his roleplays. I mean no disrespect when I say his are more complex, both on the basis of writing style, vocabulary and plot. The dual problems with this are firstly that you went too wide and introduced far too many side plots and characters that were didn't add anything to the core of the story. The second problem is that when you drop off the boil, as in the second roleplay, it is VERY noticeable. It seemed rushed and that it lacked content, as if you had the ending planned, but knew you needed to spin out the story to what you felt was an acceptable length. The second roleplay both could and should have been better. My decision now is reduced to whether in a dead heat I put VJ through on his potential greatness, or McKail through on his more complete story. This match is so close that I've actually changed my mind since I wrote that sentence. At the end of the day, I think VJ's weaker second was JUST about rescued by his stellar first and his control of language. VJ's ability to soar (and you better fucking had after this) just edges out my misgivings about his lack of focus. Ultimately, I do want to see what happens to him the most. Jacob McKail, bravo. You put in an excellent display and I had you through in my mind so many times, but the use of language, in the first piece especially, just made me doubt putting you through as bracket champion a little too much. I'm sorry, but you should be incredibly proud of your showing, and you will continue to get better as you write more. I'm not joking or exaggerating when I say you could... no, you SHOULD, be one of the favourites for GTT7 next year. VJ, you get another round to fulfill your immense potential. Pick – Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan Result Violence Jack d Jacob McKail, 4 votes to 0 Match Judges Brett Kat Andrew Andy Violence Jack advances to the Semi Final Round. Matchup TBA. Eeyore
