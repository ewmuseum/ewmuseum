Round 2: Mrs W. Bracket Results

22 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TWIZZY V RED ROCK First off I’ve got to say how much I enjoyed Twizzy’s roleplays. The little bits of trivia that he through in regarding Davis’ past such as family history and building up the readers knowledge of his relationship with Ilan. Over the two roleplays you can gage an idea of where he is at this time in his life and how one event (Ilan’s death) may be the turning point. It was well written and a nice story that plays on emotion. One little thing I very much liked was “The man in the big coat will call the police in twenty minutes and he will now feel a slight twinge of fear every time he steps onto the streets of Manhattan.” That line was my favourite from the roleplays. It shows a little insight into the consequences of his actions in a way that you don’t see too often. The first thing I noticed about Red Rock’s roleplays was how much the character really reminds me of myself. He talks the way I talk and acts the way I act, combined with our shared love of a good old lie in! What I’m trying to put across is that I found Red to be a very realistic character. The more I read the more I liked his story, when Mr. Camfield was coming onto Red in the hallway I was chuckling away, by the time we had moved onto: “Red Rock looks over at Little Spud, Boolie's 1 year old little brother who by coincidence or maybe something more sinister is eating a bar of chocolate and has smeared a little bit on his top lip just under his nose making look suspiciously like a certain German dictator. Boolie: He's not a racist! awww look at him waving at you! Red Rock: He's not waving Boolie he's saluting!” I was in hysterics. Having never previously read any of his work I have been converted to a fan. In closing, I was impressed with Twizzy’s story and thoroughly enjoyed it, however for me Red Rock shaded it. Result: Red Rock –d- Twizzy DRAKE COLEMAN V CLYDE WALKINS Drake Coleman: Another Boy Drowning I love his use of descriptive metaphors to get a point across especially concerning his pain and look or smell of the burns. The story was a fairly smooth read and able to draw you in. The transition from feeling like a winner to feeling hopeless was done beautifully. There were quite a few sentence fragments in this story, more than usual and I found it kind of distracting when I reading. A few points of awkward wording as well. I would suggest reading your story outloud after writing it, this may help with awkward wording. Good Morning Beautiful: I liked this one even more than the first. The struggle of morals vs the need to get rid of pain is strong in this. The metaphor of his otherself speaking to him like the little devil on the shoulder comes across well here..at least that is how I envision it. This was a smoother read than the first. Mad props. Clyde Walkins: Esclydepades This story was a real laugh....literally, as in I laughed my way through most of it. I personally am not a big fan of this style of dialogue in an rp, however, it was good. The interaction between Clyde and James is priceless. The cut scenes threw me off a couple of times forcing me to reread, I would suggest making the change a bit more clear to avoid this in the future. Just Like the Movies: Okay, this second rp had better scene cuts, thus it was easier to follow so kudos to Walkins. A few of the sentence fragments were kind of awkward, and there was a couple of errors in spelling but nothing that took away from the experience as a whole. The only real drawback, was the distinct feeling I was watching ( reading) a parody of Independence Day. I fully expected Will Smith to jump out at any time spouting off " Welcome to Earth." Result: Walkins d Coleman This was not easy in anyway. Both of these handlers are extremely talented and the stories were all fantastic in their own genres. The only drawbacks were the senses of feeling I was reading a DarkMan series novel, and reading( watching) an Independence Day parody movie. Coleman did a good job of getting the turmoil and emotion across. Walkins did a good job portraying the oblivious idiot/genius...kind of reminded me of Binky and Brain in some ways. In the end I have to give it to Walkins, but I am giving mad props to Coleman for a couple of great reads and look forward to more of his work in the future. "THE AVATAR" WILLIAM GWYNN V RICKY THUNDER Gwynn: The opening sequence, putting it simply, has been done to death in tournaments and feds since the dawn of time. Its a generic opening that tells me nothing interesting about the Avatar, or makes me want to read any further. If it is an attempt to appease the relevance police, best find another method should you advance to Round 3. History lesson on Lucy, continuing on from the previous round's work if I recall correctly. I say that because its important that you somehow make your work stand out enough for someone to remember it after reading 50+ RP's written by others in between. I was ready to write something vitriolic about romance writers pretending to be e-fedders, but then you nipped that in the bud with the revelation that she was drunk and wouldn't remember. Nice work. Your real strength is your story writing, providing you have a purpose behind what you are writing. From this example, where you need to improve on your delivery is the style used under both "Brooding" and "Reflection". Neither of these passages did anything for me at all, they were just "blah blah blah" with a few names and cliches thrown in. Thunder: As an anecdote, your two guys get accommodation in Japan, go to a bar, get in a brawl, get busted and get reprimanded. Oookay. Kinda like something the father in Big Fish would say, but less dramatic and self-serving. Not much you can do with 1000 words. I understand you missed the bit about there not being a word limit, the posting program had a residual label from the hype period, and that you conformed to that and posted this instead of a different piece with more content. You know the lesson has been learned that you need to pay a bit more attention, sadly the hard way :( Pick: Avatar SEYMOUR ALMASY V THE BACK ALLEY BRAWLER I read and enjoyed Back Alley Brawler's first, and I read both of Almasy's, and enjoyed them also. Almasy put forth two stories that gave much life to both his character's current psychology, and the match at hand. The effectiveness of this in both pieces is something that has been lacking in a lot of work for PTC matches by Sean that I've read over the last year - many of which had a mechanical and insubstantial quality to them. The wakeup call in the first worked well, as did the temptation then reality check in the second that steeled Almasy's resolve. The used condom draped over the picture was a particularly sticking piece of descriptive work. I totally dig the descriptive and metaphorical writing with Brawler. Having his own epiphany courtesy of the visit from Death worked well. I would have been a bit more comfortable with the sequence being sold more as a hallucination rather than having the plant still be dead afterwards, as that would offer better opportunity to explore this in conjunction with the pending drug addiction, but either way works here and now. I can't help but wish that Jamar had been that 5 minutes earlier with his second, so I could judge two a piece. I haven't read his second (yet) and I can't until after submitting this pick. With what we have now, if based on writing quality alone, I'd give it to Back Alley Brawler, as the written word is tighter than Almasy's (who displayed no slouch himself, by any means), but Almasy matched and then outmatched the character development side by getting his second RP up on time, which made up for any prose-related difference between the two writers. Props to Sean for having the balls to ask for Jamar's second to be counted. Thanks to both of you for the entertaining read. Pick: Seymour Almasy EMBER V CYRUS SKAR Cyrus Skar – Kaos Theory, Parts 1 & 2 I’m not very impressed, truth be told. The writing wasn’t bad…nothing stuck out at me, but nothing in the structure really screamed “BAD!” either. You had a few cards stacked against you from the start with the schizo angle because of its long history of being used by every handler in every fed ever, but I can overlook that, because sometimes it’s done very well (see: Trashcan Man). Unfortunately, this just doesn’t appear to be one of those times, mostly because of plot elements that felt wholly unrealistic. After the intro (kind of confusing in itself because you’re tossing “The Voice” at us on top of “Kaos,” the alter ego, and trying to explain both), Cyrus walked into his house and found a badly-beaten, spunked-on, and possibly defecated-on prostitute, realizes it’s the fault of his alter ego, and his reaction is basically “Wow, this is fucked up.” On top of that, he takes her to the hospital and then leaves. So we’re supposed to sympathize with this character (I think), but that’s impossible when he shows no signs of being human. And you’d think loading an ultra-fucked-up prostitute out of YOUR house and into an ambulance would incriminate you some. In the second piece, the focus is expanded further to an outside person who wants to get at Cyrus by burning his house down with him inside. I admit, it was cool to switch smoothly from Cyrus’s POV to Kaos’, but man, by now you’ve got my head swimming. In short, Cyrus my man, you gotta clean these up a bit. They’re pretty convoluted…and you need to follow your second rp’s theme of “cause and effect” and focus more on the “effects” of some of the “causes” in these rps…like maiming plot-device prostitutes. Ember – The Only Thing to Fear… & To Sleep Perchance To Dream I really liked the first piece. When I first started reading, I was filled with a sense of dread at the possibility of another cut-and-dry dark character, but I was quickly relieved as I discovered this wouldn’t be the case. The dreams in the first piece were good because I enjoyed the sense of confusion and vagueness about them…the indescribable quality to something as simple as being alone in the dark. I also enjoyed the interaction between Khaled and Ember…a good dynamic was set up without having to know WWA history beforehand (which I don’t), and that’s difficult to do. The first roleplay really had me sucked in. I guess you can see where this is going. The second roleplay didn’t keep me there. It opened pretty well…more light shed on Khaled and Ember’s bond, which I had enjoyed before, but the dream sequence didn’t catch me. Putting more of a concrete setting (and even adding a character) to it gave it a sense of normalcy that I didn’t enjoy as much as when it was more mysterious. There was a light at the end of the tunnel, however, as I enjoyed the next “real life” scene and then the hook at the end with the blending of reality was interesting. I admit, I’m still confused in a way, but I’m also buzzed up, and I contribute the storyline convolution to that, at least in some respect. Result: Ember d. Cyrus Skar Without a doubt. Ember wrote two solid pieces, although I thought the first was better than the second, but even second would win out over Cyrus’ efforts. Cyrus, you need to focus more on the environment around Skar…it seemed too much as though nothing really had an effect on him, rather that he just influenced everything around him. Also, there wasn’t enough to separate this from typical schizo scenarios, other than the other mysterious character involved with Skar’s fate that I just can’t get excited about. XIN XIN XIONG V KIMBUSA Xin Xin Xiong – Aretha Franklin Sing About It… I really liked the opening here…Xin ready to continue his “Seven Deadly Sins” tangent on Kimbusa (where it would definitely apply) before cutting himself off and using a different approach for a different opponent. I also liked how Xin spoke…relating the life and thoughts of an Asian ninja in perfect English with American slang. An interesting mix. The trash-talk was good…avoided the clichés, on the whole, and delivered a few lines that almost had me laughing out loud. The transition into the story was good as well…no stopping the narrative with a line break or anything. He just changes tracks and begins talking about a past experience. I liked it. The story itself held my interest throughout (I mean, ninjas against samurai? How can you go wrong?), but at times it felt like Xin rambled a bit, losing focus in the story itself to flesh out his thoughts, when it wasn’t really necessary. Also, the character of Fujita seemed too one-dimensional. There was nothing at all to show me he was anything other than a blind idiot blended with a complete asshole, and while that works for the story, it ultimately feels kind of boring and contrived (of course he gets flounced by Xin and the grandfather). He needed something else about his character to offset what a douche he was. By the end of the story, I was wondering how it tied into the work as a whole, and you gave that to me with the last line, but I wanted more than that for how much I had invested in Xin’s anecdote. Kimbusa – Prologue: A Journey into Darkness The first thing that caught me was the imagery. The dirty, neon underbelly of Tokyo (not that I’d know about the real thing) was vividly described for me. Now, oddly enough, I can rave about the imagery and conveying the seediness and all that, but it was in this first section of Kurokame wandering around that I was the most bored as well. I loved how it was described, but I knew something had happened to Kurokame, and I knew there were prostitutes everywhere. Now I needed something else to happen. Luckily, it did. The bar scene had me riveted. I really liked Kurokame’s battle not to lose his temper, and I actually felt a sense of dread when he realized he couldn’t pay for the booze. The about-face of the old man at the end was great, and Kurokame’s rebirth as Kimbusa, if a little quick, never came across to me as overly dramatic or corny. The whole piece just worked. Result: Kimbusa d. Xin Xin Xiong I liked both roleplays for different reasons. Xin had a fresh narrative style, a fine opening, and an entertaining story from the past. Kimbusa’s heavy description, both externally of the character’s surroundings, and internally of the character’s emotional state, drew me in. Ultimately, though, Kimbusa’s piece involved me with the character more. The biggest factor in Kimbusa’s victory is that now that he has undergone this transformation, I want to see what the next step is. I don’t get that with Xin. FATBADGER V DARKSTAR Fatbadger: The Road to Losing … SPECTACULARLY!! GTT5 Style Part 2 Interesting idea. A few more well-placed metaphores here and there and you'd have a work of art on your hands. As it is now, its pretty much the proverbial one trick pony. The Road to Losing … SPECTACULARLY!! GTT5 Style Part 3 Very much the same as your Round 1 effort, quips aside, it was similar in just about all aspects. I liked it then, but now its a tad flat when I'm expecting something a bit different. If you put something behind it all that is an original kicker, as you did with Part 2, then you can get away with more of the same type of banter. Darkstar The Curtain Call Saga: Part I, In Which the Past Returns I read and liked your IG work, so am pleased that you decided to continue with it. This isn't quite as engaging as the piece that won you the IG Championship, but you're still very doing well with unique interpretations of classic biblical characters and places. Pick: Darkstar over Fatbadger Fatbadger shot himself in the foot by having Parts 1 and 3 too similar. Had he used a different concept for Part 3 he may well have been able to walk away with this. MICHAEL STEVENS V DEVIL D. GRAVES Starting with Devil’s roleplay, I would have liked to see a second building on the foundations laid here. A few little spelling and grammatical errors detracted from it somewhat. I liked the scenario you created showing the friction between Deville and his family, it’s just a pity you didn’t expand on it a little more, there is definitely potential to explore that further. Again thought I would have liked to see that explored in a second roleplay. Moving onto Michael Stevens now, for me this is one of the best stories I’ve read in this year’s competition. I don’t recall having come across a guardian angel story in my time and these two roleplays left me wanting to read more and more. Again a few little mistakes in grammar and spelling but I found this to be a small price to pay for the story which kept me happily entertained. As I had said I would liked to have seen a little more from Devil perhaps that might have swayed me a little. But I will have to give this one to Stevens. Result: Michael Stevens –d- Devil D Graves SUMMARY - Red Rock d Twizzy - Clyde Walkins d Drake Coleman - William Gwynn v Ricky Thunder - Seymour Almasy d Back Alley Brawler - Ember d Cyrus Skar - Kimbusa d Xin Xin Xiong - Darkstar d Fatbadger - Michael Stevens d Devil D.
