Round 1 Results - Eeyore Bracket

17 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
AJ PALMER V SAMMY BROWN Judge 1 AJ Palmer: A decent read, but, could have been better by a second opinion. I think you concentrated a little too hard on making it four separate sections, when a few of them possibly could have been combined, especially one and three. I didn't care for the break up of the sections as it was either. It didn't seem necessary to break it up into four sections in the same peice of writing. Still, there was a good underlying story here, one that could very well build up for next rounds. Sammy Brown: Note: I want it to be known that despite coming up with this contest for John C. to use Sammy Brown, in no way do I, or did I have any involvement in the direction or use of this character. Anything written is done on John's own volition. Just want to make that clear. I'm glad to see John did something great with the story and the character. I almost hoped Sammy took the money and decided to indeed lay down in the ring. It would have given the writer and the character a completely new direction to go in, and a reason for explaining a loss, had the character been involved in any company at the time. Overall: Brown over Palmer. AJ, you told a good story, and in the end, you have absolutely nothing to be ashamed of. You were bested by someone who wrote a great story that could end up being one of the best of the round, hands down. My hat is off to you, AJ. You could very well go down in history when Sammy Brown wins this entire thing as being his first opponent, much like George English and Angelo Deville are mentioned about Doctor Curiosity and Rich Rollins' wins. Pick: Sammy Brown Judge 2 Sammy Brown: Not only did you enter GTT6 blind, Mr. Brown, but you told a good, solid story deftly. Nothing lagged; I didn't stumble across any glaring errors. Nice description ("a tall brunette with petite lips that appeared to be permanently pursed"), too. I look forward to seeing more. AJ Palmer: Good: Your character isn't the archetype pothead. He's not blazed out of his head. He's at least got substance (pun not intended); he's dealing with actual situations, like the hesitance to commit to love and the bounty on his head. Bad: Two big things here. Negligence for the piece -- a lot of simple mistakes even a brief once-over could have alleviated. The other is telling instead of showing. This is a big rule of writing. Don't tell me Palmer is depressed or ADD or whatever other symptoms he may have; show me. By the way: have you ever read Dennis Lehane? One of his main protagonists is named Angela Gennaro. Decision: Regrettably, it wasn't that difficult to choose. Sammy Brown advances. Pick: Sammy Brown Result Sammy Brown d AJ Palmer, 2 votes to 0 DOCTOR CURIOSITY V JOHN HITCHIN Judge 1 Hitchin: Overall: For what it was, it was good. Decent story, but, all in all, I really learned nothing about Hitchin. Thank you for citing your lyrics at the end of the RP. I know the citation nazis will be out in force, and would have exploded had you not done that. Good peice, John. Doctor Curiosity: Overall: This is a great story. Telling kids to cheat? That's awesome on so many levels it's not even funny. And that Doctor Curiosity isn't taking credit for his success, but, rather blaming his personal "demon", Merrick is again, something big. Almost as if Andy is saying that he didn't do this on his own, but, instead of the character having some sort of divine intervention to win GTT4, and his fWo/XUW runs, Andy himself has been touched by some divine spirit. Result: Unfortunately, Hitchin, your first round opponent is very determined to win the entire tournament, and pulled out all of the stops to do so. Had you faced someone else, the result may have been very, very different. But, on this day... Pick: DOCTOR CURIOSITY. Judge 2 Dr. Curiosity: A well-written set-up to future roleplays, I'm certain. The only thing I can pick on is the inconsistencies in the good doctor's dialect. You'd sometimes have him pronouncing w as a German, and then as an American/Briton. Keep an eye open for that. John Hitchin: This may have won against a lesser opponent, but drawing Dr. Curiosity for the first round is sort of God's way of saying you need to transcend even your best. Technically, it was all right. But I felt that a lot of the humor was forced (like farting, then seguing that into "No, facing Christopher Lloyd is disgusting"). Fortunately, a story began to form late in the piece. Unfortunately, it was too late and not as good as your opponent's. Lo siento. Pick: Dr. Curiosity and his drastically shortened life. Result Doctor Curiosity d John Hitchin, 2 votes to 0 THE ILLUSTRIOUS FACE EATER V HUNTER JONES Judge 1 This is the closest competition that I've reviewed so far. With use of language and dialogue being almost equal, it's decided by storyline. Clearly neither story is weak, both are quite strong, but the storyline that we're getting from the IFE simply possesses more "hook" to it, helped by the facts that the wacky antics of ife are such a departure from the typical . Pick: IFE Judge 2 Hunter Jones: Good story, good turn on the injury angled turned miraculous recovery. There wasn't any sort of divine intervention. Jones was just lucky. Aside from minor spelling and consistency errors, it's technically sound. 8. The Face Eater: It was memorable, and the story was very technically sound, aside from the spelling mistakes. Overall: This could quite possibly be the match of the round, to be honest. Granted, this is the first two RPs that I've read in their entirety, but, they were both fairly solid pieces of work with only very minor mistakes once or twice throughout. Both had a good, solid story behind them. But, the Face-Eater had a story that has just a tinge more of the desire factor. Hunter Jones seemed to simply end his story without much more to be added later that will fit the entire story line. Face-Eater has a lot more invested in it, and I think that fact alone carries him to the next round. Pick: The Illustrious Face-Eater by a very, very slim margin. Result The Illustrious Face-Eater d Hunter Jones, 2 votes to 0 KERRY OWENS V JIM AXTELL Judge 1 In favor of: Jim Axtell. Solid use of both description and dialogue, setting up a very believable set of circumstances. Caveat: the kidnapping plot device has been used often in gtt, so this particular story will have to work harder than normal to feel interesting to readers. With that in mind, the execution of this story has been flawless so far. Pick: Jim Axtell Judge 2 Kerry Owens: Short. Very short. Somewhat ineffective. I really don't know more about the character than I did going in. I would have passed this on to a buddy to maybe read for you to check off some of the spelling and grammatical errors. For advice: It's okay to use contractions. People do it all the time, and it doesn't make the writing better not to. Jim Axtell: This is probably a play on Rob's biggest nightmare: He's a new(er) father, and losing his child is, and would be a huge nightmare. As such, he's translating what his feeling would be onto the character, as his biggest nightmare, if it indeed happened. The story is nothing new, and yet, seems different. This isn't just some guy that never saw his kid, and decided that to be honorable, he should get the kid back. This kid is his life, and he's got to do whatever it takes to get him back. Pick: Jim Axtell. Kerry, thank you for coming over from NFW to compete here in GTT. I do believe that with a little work, and a little more care taken toward your stories, you could go far in GTT. Unfortunately, you simply met someone in the first round with a better establishing story, and one that reaches out to this judge right now, because I can identify with it. HIGH FLYER V STEVE WATSON - CPA Judge 1 Steve Watson - CPA: This surprised me. This is a great story from a writer that is obviously better than your average bear. Minor technical and grammatical glitches aside, I enjoyed the story, and I enjoyed the take on the mystery wrestler angle. Kudos to you, Mr. [name withheld]. High Flyer: I'm not familiar with High Flyer in the least. However, this story made me want to go back and read all I can about the character. While I didn't learn a crap load about the character, I didn't need to; the story can stand alone, and give you a glimpse into the character, without having to go back and read everything, like a good TV show does. You don't have to go back and watch all the episodes you missed, you just want to. Pick: High Flyer. So far, to all those who said that the Piglet bracket was going to be the bracket of doom, I have to say that High Flyer, Doc C., Brown and Facey are going to make stiff competition for each other. Watson told a great story, and if it were possible, I would absolutely give him a wild-card shot for the double no-show from Jamester and Smiley. His story deserves to live on to at least another round. He was simply bested by a great writer with a great flair for this. Judge 2 High Flyer (Go Team VIAGRA : "The Pilot" or "How I Spent my Summer Incarceration."): It seemed to have issues with the timing and flow. I wanted to turn away and maybe read something else and come back, but I forced it. Now I've heard of this person, and can see where his reputation comes from, but this appeared to have quite the bit of 'ring rust' in it. Impressive all the same. For the flow issues, I'd only be able to suggest having someone review it for you before you post and get their feedback. Steve Watson CPA (Numbers): I liked it, very well written, flowed quite well. One thing at the end with the 'fax' that came across (easy to pick out on the misspelling.) This kept me reading to the end though. One thing that I didn't get was how the pictures were of a masked wrestler, when he mentioned that he wrestled under his own name. My Choice: Both high quality pieces with one going for a classic script style and the other for prose. For me though, this has to come down to who kept my attention the best and gave a compelling story. That would be Mr. Watson. Pick: Steve Watson - CPA Judge 3 Flyer: I'm not a fan of the style used by Flyer, I prefer something to having a definite purpose rather than playing for laughs, but I have no problem conceding that this is a good piece. The dialogue does the job of introducing me to the characters without annoying me with unanswered questions very nicely. CPA: I marked out hard at the start of CPA's piece, because I really like anal retentive type characters. The issue I have here is that there was one too many "oddly worded" sentences that could have been fixed with one more re-read or get someone else to read it before posting, and the ending didn't satisfy or titilate me. Take heart in that there are some others in the field who you would have taken out had you been matched up with them. Pick: High Flyer Result High Flyer d Steve Watson - CPA, 2 votes to 1 SUMMARY - Sammy Brown d AJ Palmer, 2 votes to 0 - Doctor Curiosity d John Hitchin, 2 votes to 0 - The Illustrious Face-Eater d Hunter Jones, 2 votes to 0 - Jim Axtell d Kerry Owens, 2 votes to 0 - High Flyer d Steve Watson - CPA, 2 votes to 1 - Easton Hall d Josh via Moody no-show. - Vangelus Olsig d Vangellus Oxious via Oxious no-show Bracket Judges: - Pete - Andrew - Ben - RJ - [name withheld] Notes: - I have edited out some judging comments that focussed on grammatical errors and the like. I would like for the judges not to not focus on that sort of thing in detail, but stick to passing references if there are issues. Eeyore
