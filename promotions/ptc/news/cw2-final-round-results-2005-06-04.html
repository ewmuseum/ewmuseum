CW2 Final Round Results!

4 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Judging comments un-edited and in their entirity: Judge 1 I decided to be succinct rather than uber, giving a brief synopsis of what I liked and didn’t instead of performing an autopsy on each piece. I’m not trying to demean the effort put forth, it’s just my stance that the work stands well enough without my dissection, and at this point it goes without saying that the writing is sublime. Cereal Killer The first three roleplays were awesome. You have an uncanny ability for stringing the reader along and it was encapsulated in these pieces brilliantly. No sooner would I go down the track of suspicion than my questions would be resolved, or at least confronted by Frank, and the ending to each was both satisfying and intriguing, leaving me smiling, nodding, and wanting more. I loved the line “The darkness can often work as oil in the machinery of power.” The ending to the fourth roleplay was disappointing by contrast to the first three. Teasing Cereal Killer as an anti-hero was wonderful, and I liked the overall message, which caught me by pleasant surprise, but the execution of it fell flat. The epiphany and resolution simply could have been so much more. Thankfully, the fifth roleplay provided some of the closure that the fourth lacked, and I’m absolutely enthralled that you foreshadowed The Ripper’s ownership of all that transpired – not to mention mocked yourself for it – in the roleplay before. The malicious tone and ending went over huge. Overall I’m very pleased with this performance. The first three pieces set a remarkable standard for the rest (your roleplays and those of your opponents) to follow, and the latter two only suffered by direct comparison and the expectations that you’d set. Superb job, Andy. Archibald MacGregor The first roleplay didn’t really do anything for me on its own, but in the grand scheme of things it was the perfect calm before the storm. And with the second roleplay, the storm came directly from Typherion himself, figuratively and literally. The sense of dread and foreboding established was paradigm, climaxing with Archie being addressed directly by the underworld through his iPod. That segment of the roleplay was pure gold, and this roleplay as a whole was the pinnacle of the round, barely edging out CK’s second and third. Unfortunately, the final roleplay was a step down. I was agreeably charmed with being given the power, so to speak, of determining what the ultimate destiny would be, but something seemed off. Aside from one shallow mention, the tournament relevance was great, using it as a tool of the devil to bring about the evil undercurrents that accompany success. I also liked Archibald’s knowledge of it, and his resolve to fight against it. The thing that made it quirky for me was the presentation of God and his stance on the matter. Instead of ending things on a powerful and inspiring note, he wussed out pretty hard. Thinking it over a little bit more, I believe the last roleplay would have been more inspiring had you switched around the order so that we were left with Archibald’s stance on the situation instead of the Almighty’s. The movement from Man’s conviction to God’s uncertainty seemed backwards and anti-climactic. Overall this is another strong showing, highlighted supremely by the second piece. Everything was brought full circle, yet left open enough to leave the reader wondering. Great story, Ryan. Tessa Windsor The first roleplay began very strongly, drawing me in to sympathize with and root for Tessa, but from there it got convoluted and just . . . annoying. A major selling point of the piece seemed to be that Dawn McFarlane and Christian Zenith were in Tessa’s corner, as if that’s some huge swerve of a boon to end all boons. I was happy that the women crushed the idea that the sex tape somehow got Tessa where she is, but their volatility over the matter seemed undersold and the whole thing just fell kind of flat. The second roleplay, like the first, began radiantly, giving me exactly what I wanted: Tessa’s take on the doubts of those around her, and her thoughts on where she was. Then it got convoluted again, blowing up in my face what was glossed over slightly in the first one, but still hung like a phantom: the idea that the sex tape HAD somehow helped her achieve success. Instead of leaving it stomped out in the dust, it’s revisited, and some of the success is attributed to it, not only through fanbase, but in a general sense. Without that descent into absurdity, this would have been a phenomenal introspective piece. It still was, to an extent, but it’s cheapened by the continued lip service to those who unnecessarily exploited her. Overall there was a lot of powerful stuff here, but it didn’t hit home the way it should have because of the infusion of unnecessary nonsense like the unveiling of the mystery~ figures and the contradictory and confusing stance on what brought Tessa to the big show. That said, it was written beautifully for the most part, and again, some of the self-exploration was top-notch. Well done, Sammie. The Decision With all due respect to Tessa Windsor, this ultimately came down to a choice between Cereal Killer and Archibald MacGregor, who I feel outclassed her this round. Between those two it was a tough decision, but I’m honestly not very torn up about it. Had MacGregor delivered on the third as he did on the second, I believe he might have edged The Ripper’s Little Killer, but in the end the latter was too consistently pwnerful to be overtaken by the competition he received. Pick: Cereal Killer Judge 2 Tessa Windsor RP 1 Straight away this RP feels more grounded in reality that either of her opponents. Using a topical reference about ‘American Idol’ makes you place Tessa in the same subconscious part of your mind as the show itself … the real world. I think that Sammie’s strength, and main difference from Andy and Ryan, is keeping Tessa and her world real and believable at all times … and more than that she keeps the mundane interesting, which is not an easy thing. However the problems, for me, started at the argument in the RP. I felt that the three characters … Tessa, Dee and Champ … merged together and didn’t stand out as individuals. The dialogue wasn’t different or character specific enough to make it clear who was saying what to whom. Confusion then set in with the abrupt change of names … from Dee and Champ to Dawn McFarlane and Christian Zenith … which to a causal reader would have made no sense and to someone that knew the names felt jarring. End of the day the RP said a lot without really saying anything at all. RP 2 While I liked the concept and tone of this RP … in fact I liked the premise more than the rest of the series … it was too abrupt. This felt like it should have been the introduction to a RP and not the actual RP itself. When I got to the end I was left feeling disappointed and wanting more … and as a final RP in a RP tournament that isn’t a good thing. Cereal Killer RP 1 A look into what made Frank into the Cereal Killer with a heads up on his neuroses. I liked the nod towards the other aspects of the chaos theory with the divergent paths leading to another outcome … if Frank hadn’t stopped to talk to Emily, if he hadn’t turned right … etc. This was a good story, a nice set-up for the final round and was quite interesting … but nothing that really made it stand out, nothing that was exciting or different. … until the last line that is, and the revelation which left a great cliff-hanger in a very typical Andy way! RP 2 “Five toenails ripped of their respective toes” I was eating some lunch when I read that and even though I am a horror fan and not squeamish in the slightest the line gave me a small shudder. J I have also read the Ripper character before this … and of course therefore Andy’s work as well … and have always loved his innate grasp of characterisation. The line, “Even the Ripper smiled” was a great example of this in my opinion. “Demonic melodrama 101” was a great way of bringing a touch of reality into the RP that wasn’t forced but still poked fun at the genre … and the over-the-top scenarios that Andy uses. Double twist! I thought that the Ripper was fictional too, another one of CK’s delusions so either I have been reading Andy’s work for far too long o he KNOWS how to lead a reader along the path right where he wants them … so a nice swerve with the Ripper being real. RP 3 I am liking the Walter Mittey-esque nature of the Cereal Killer … and it isn’t often that you compare a fictional efed character to Death of a Salesman! Technically this was a well written piece with an interesting look into three disparate characters … nice look into the Ripper’s methodology too … but the final scene of CK being alone in his own mind was a little bit downplayed in my opinion. It would have had more impact if there had been more flashbacks in this RP showing his history and the voices prevalent in his head in advance to emphasise the change. RP 4 I have read this one twice now and am still not sure what I think about it. It was either the worst of the series of the best. The dialogue between the two … Ripper and CK … was very strong and believable as was the pervading feeling of temptation. Ripper made a very insidious ‘serpent’ in this one … the dealer promising something good and all CK had to do was reach out and try it … the first one is free so to speak … In other ways though it felt too forced, too predictable; almost as if I was watching a bad movie and knowing exactly what was going to happen next. I do like the fact that, as with Ryan, Andy has written an ‘ending’ for the character that works both ways. CK has a reason to win and lose now. RP 5 OK, I take back what I said about RP 4 … Andy was leading us all along just as the Ripper was leading CK! The link to Ryan’s own RPs and the nod to the shared Hell was an extremely nice touch and very done. It made me actually grin as I was reading it. The parting touch about CK realising how addictive the drug would be was intriguing too. … at the end of the day this was definitely more about the Ripper and his ‘control’ of situations and people than it was about CK himself, but then again I think that that was a nice point. Archibald McGreggor RP 1 We see here how Macgregor is dealing with the news and meet his grandmother … a small plot device to perhaps accentuate the ‘normalcy’ of Macgregor before all of this started. The only small niggle I had with this was the initial setup that Macgregor had to wait a week to see his grandmother but then when there he said he would see her the next day (perhaps he was lying, or perhaps he had booked a second visit previously? No-prize waiting! LoL) The introduction of the Devil, Typherion, was nicely handled without cliché while the tone of the writing seemed slightly drier and more precise ... perhaps to accentuate the character. Typherion was portrayed more like a board-room chairman than he was a diabolical fiend, which again was refreshing. Envy, hate and greed … in my opinion … make a better foundation for Hell instead of the typical fire and brimstone, and are much more interesting. RP 2 Another small niggle right at the start of this was why did Macgregor look around the house instead of going to the attic where his grandmother said the information would be? Not a biggie though. Nice personality touches with showing the different tastes in music for Macgregor and Heather. As I was reading the RP … and making notes at the same time … I remembered watching the new/remake of the Amytiville Horror movie and realised that this RP was much better in terms of suspense/tension building that the movie itself was. The cut scenes between Macgregor and Heather added to this and were well handled. The translocation of the doll was … while expected … well done and a classic touch of horror noire. The reveal and double blow at the end … loss of Heather and the death of the Grandmother … leading to the (possible?) fall of the wagon was perfectly timed. RP 3 A lovely twist … actually two … that I wasn’t expecting. The fact that the Devil made Macgregor one of the best wrestlers in the World is a touch that, I presume, Ryan added upon getting to the finals and adds another facet to the tale believably. The second twist was that I expected Macgregor to resist the alcohol, but he DID fall off the wagon … not the archetypal thing for a hero to do, and I underestimated Ryan with that point. … I prefer my heroes and characters to be ‘flawed’ and so that really worked for me … God = Santa?!?!?!??! BIZARRE LOL The ending of this tale was special on two fronts. Archibald himself came full circle and while on the surface he may not have visibly changed too much he now KNOWS that he is a pawn in a much larger game … but puts that to one side and focuses on the child that will son be born. A nice and subtle touch that grounds his humanity in the landscape of a celestial battle looming. The second point was very clever in that you could almost say that if Macgregor wins then Hell had it’s way and if he loses then God ‘saved’ him … nice fence sitting from Ryan and sets up the final result no matter which way t goes as part of the predefined character. … I wonder if Andy and Ryan were sharing notes? LoL Synopsis In my opinion there is a clear third place person here while first and second are much … MUCH ... closer. While I was impressed to see Sammie get Tessa this far her heart didn’t seem in it as the two RPs felt a little flat, unfortunately, and had no chance at all of competing with Andy and Ryan’s inspired output. Technically there was not much difference between the writing of Andy and Ryan … overlooking the attempt of the Italian judges to cloud the issue by screwing up Andy’s formatting of course … and to be honest the fact that Andy did more RPs isn’t a factor here either as it was the intrinsic story elements that mattered. Andy gave us a look into two existing characters, and used the neuroses and temptation aspects very nicely indeed … but I think that he could have played more on both. Ryan gave us a lovely look into a human who finds himself in the middle of a battle beyond his control, with glimpses at his take on Heaven and Hell … but again I would have liked to see more, especially Typherion and Hell as I only whet my appetite on it. Andy put some lovely, yet subtle, touches of relevance in his … both towards the tournament and towards his opponents. The inclusion of elements of Ryan’s own story was wonderful. Ryan didn’t use anything particularly relevant to the tournament or the opponents but he did give a believable (in the context of the situation) reason for a wrestler being part of the story … and for that wrestler doing so well in the tournament. Both of them told entertaining and intriguing stories, and I enjoyed them both. This is probably one of the closest matches that I have ever had to judge and there is no major reason for one to win over the other (and unlike judges I have read in the past I am NOT going to toss a coin! LOL) It was Andy himself that said, in one of his RPs, “Sometimes the tiny, immeasurable differences can be all important.” Well it is one of those almost immeasurable differences that is important here, simply put which one I enjoyed more overall … and that was Ryan’s concept and story. Third place: Tessa Windsor Second Place: Cereal Killer First Place: Archibald Macgragor Judge 3 Not a problem. Cereal Killer Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Six: I haven't really been following Cereal Killer throughout CW2; this is the first piece of his I've read. I have to say that I'm thoroughly impressed by it, on that note. The only place I could honestly say needs a little bit of clarification is the flashback- I feel like it's very hard to tell his age. Usually, when I think of kids roaming the mall, I think of teenagers, but when Frank talks to Emily, and she asks him to play, that's sort of disconcerting; it's not that big of a deal, but it makes the flashback a little confusing, and hard to place, at least in terms of time. That having been said, however, this is a wonderful piece, especially in the present- at first, I was worried that this was your typical guy-sacrifices-wrestling-for-his-significant-other thing, but I have to say I'm very impressed with the way it's been handled. It's obvious that the handler came into this with a good idea of what he wanted to do, and it's been pulled off really well in this first piece for the RPing period.. I'm eager to see how it will wrap up. Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Seven: "Your number has been called" This I think is a nice semi-pause in the storyline. So much went on in the first piece for this round that it's almost like you need a moment to catch your breath, and this background information comes in at just the right time to allow you to do that. The explanation for why this all happened was logical, well thought-out- again, evidence of how much preparation went into this. The tension in the last scene was amazingly well played out; I was hanging on every word waiting to find out if The Ripper was real or not... and now that I know he is, I'm really interested to see what this journey involves. Butterlfies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Eight: "Don't let yourself down, don't let yourself go" I like the way this was handled- I think it's a very good re-introduction of The Ripper as a very important figure here, but he's not so overbearing as to make him look more important than CK. I don't have much to comment on- this, as the two before it, is well written, and goes on a good, even keel throughout, setting up nicely for the end of the story, which I'm very eager to see. Butterflies and Hurricanes - The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Final: "Your time is now" Wow... talk about a nail biter. I hung on practically every word of this RP from the beginning to the end, and I was actually QUITE surprised to see the way that it turned out. I never expected to see Cereal Killer doing what he did by the end of the piece. It was a great tie-up to the series, with a nice ending that, I admit, I didn't see coming... and now, I'm off to read the Epilogue... EPILOGUE Wow again... I don't even know what to say here, as, once again, I'm totally surprised by something I was not at all expecting. This was absolutely beautiful, a wonderful quasi-ending for the story. I like the way that it also ties in Archibald MacGregor, and not only that, but the way it takes your typical movie ending of "good guy wins" and twists it into the typically rare "bad guy wins." I'm VERY impressed with this series, to say the least. Tessa Windsor What It Comes Down To (Part One) I feel like this first part is an interesting follow-up to the tapes; after all, after reading the first time they were mentioned, I was definitely wondering where the story was going to go as regarded them. I also think it's incredibly interesting the way it came out in the end- that it's the supposed leader of Tessa's stable in Global that's involved, and for such seedy reasons... I'm definitely wondering exactly how you're going to deal with this in your next piece. I think that thus far, it's been written well, and left-off at a good point to finish off the series. What It Comes Down To (Part Two) This is definitely an interesting follow-up, because here, I think that we see another side of Tessa Windsor- not one that's been weakened by the 'shame' of this whole issue, but one that has accepted what went on and has decided that she's hell bent to win, no matter what it takes. I feel like, in places, the story of the whole series dragged a little bit, considering the issue of the tapes was always put off so much by Tessa, and now, in the end, she seems so unaffected by it. That puts me off of believing that it was a big deal in the first place, but in any case, I still like the way it turned out; though I think that the drama of finding out who it was could have been played up a little better, it was still an enjoyable series. Archibald MacGregor "Futures & Pasts" I've been following most of this story, and I think this is a really great continuation of it, from the last warning of Archibald Matthews, to where MacGregor stands right now. I really enjoy the way that you've set up MacGregor finding out the truth about his family, but more than anything else, I like what you've done with hell and Typherion. The descriptions of Hell are very creative, and I think much better done than what most people would attempt- without fire, brimstone, and the usual trappings of 'evil' you've created an extremely intriguing devil, one who I'd have no trouble believing would want to break MacGregor. I think you've set yourself up extremely well for the next couple of pieces, and I can't wait to see how you close it up. "Dolls & Bouquets" Wow... I don't think there's any piece that I could point to in this round thus far and say that I was disappointed by it, and that is definitely true of this one as well. This is one amazing piece of work. The vignettes all fit together in such a fashion that I couldn't stop reading- some of the tightest and most gripping story-telling that I've seen all round. It's so well written, and there's so much going on that I feel like I'd have to read it several times to digest it all, but now I want to find out what happens! So... I really feel like this is an incredibly strong piece that puts the MacGregor story right on the map again... up against such strong writing, though, a lot depends on what you did with the end of the story. "Given and Taken" This, I think, is a very interesting way that you've chosen to finish up your series. I really like the way that it ties everything in- both Typherion, his actions, the effect of those actions on Archibald, Archibald's own resolve, and God's response to all the goings-on. I almost feel like, in a way, a lot of material was put into this piece hastily, like it was pushed together in order to finish out the storyline- that may not necessarily be true, but for some reason, the ending has a little bit of an unnatural feel to me, since it doesn't really resolve everything that could have been resolved. At the same time, I appreciate that, since even though everything isn't neatly tied up, it leaves a lot for the reader to think about. Pick: Picking between these competitors is probably one of the hardest things I've ever had to do. All three put out very well-written pieces that tied up their storylines nicely, and in a manner that didn't really leave anything wanting for the reader. It's a very hard choice, as I gathered that it would be once I saw who was left in the finals. To win, I would pick Cereal Killer; it was him who left the reader with the most complete story, and, in my opinion, the best written. It was obviously well planned out over the course of the tournament, but more importantly, well executed. I felt like all the loose ends were tied up, and not to mention that, the reader was left with one wicked plot twist to end the story. Archibald MacGregor is a close second for me- it would have been even closer, but I felt that Archibald's last piece lacked the punch that Cereal killer delivered with his pair of closers. Though I really enjoyed MacGregor's work throughout the tournament, I feel like the story could have been capped off a little bit better- as it is, it sort of seemed to peter to a halt for me, instead of ending with one final impact, which is what I think it would have needed in order to beat across the board solid writing from Cereal Killer. Although, I do have to mention, Archibald's second piece really blew me away- which is perhaps why I was expecting such an ending in the third. Tessa comes in third for me- as I said, it's not that I think any of the writing was any worse than the others this week, it's just that for me, Tessa's storyline lacked the punch of her opponents. It was a very interesting storyline by the close of it, but it just didn't grab my attention, and up against MacGregor and CK, it needed a little bit more energy to pull through. So... the recap, then... CK for the whole thing, MacGregor second, and Windsor third. Pick: Cereal Killer WINNER OF PRIMETIME CENTRAL'S CW2 TOURNAMENT Cereal Killer (2 votes to 1 to 0) Congratulations Andy :) Thanks to everyone who judged all the way through. Thanks to everyone who emergency judged last minute. Thanks to the bell boys, flower girls and taxi drivers, and everyone else who contributed! CW2 finals
