Round 3 Results - Pooh

23 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
DAMIEN CRUZ V YORI YAKAMO JR Judge 1 Aside from a few delightful lines, Yori's roleplays didn't do anything for me. Different sense of humour, I guess. Cruz lacked a unifying theme that I could discern, and only posted one roleplay, but parts of the character and the story were intriguing and I wouldn't mind reading more from him, so he gets my pick. Pick: Damien Cruz Judge 2 Damien Cruz vs. Yakamo Jr. Damien Cruz Roleplay 1 Pros - Good story. I wasn't immersed into it, but it was still a good story. - Technically sound piece. Cons - Extremely blocky writing. Huge chunks of writing is rather hard to read through. Break it up more. Yori Yakamo Jr.: Roleplay 1 Pros - It's just pure crazy. I like pure crazy. I enjoyed the story though I had a headache afterwards. - Good writing. Good technical piece overall, I think. It's a little tough when you're writing with accents and fake things ;-). Some issues though, so watch out for that. Cons - It's crazy. Some people are going to want the PCP you're using. Watch out not to go too far... wait, too late. Roleplay 2 Pros - Good continuation of the last piece. Just more crazy stuff. Cons - Just felt like one long piece so there really aren't any cons. I guess. Pick: Yori Yakamo Jr. Judge 3 I believe the winner lies in the story being told here. The way you can see the drive and determination of Damien Cruz allows you to get a lot closer to the character. I think that gives his writing a big edge. The writing for Yori is alternatively very funny. Often times, confusing, but amusing none-the-less. Spelling and Grammar are spot on, both were really good pieces. Damien, gets the win though. Pick: Damien Cruz Result - Damien Cruz d Yori Yakamo Jr., 2 votes to 1 KILLEAN SIRRAJIN VS AGENTDASH Judge 1 Killean Sirrajin Both roleplays are excellent technical pieces of work. The story moves along a little slowly for me, but the framing does help set how much Killean seems to be isolated. I never saw the heart attack coming, and the rush to a friend is good. AgentDash The story here is very layered, with lots of blanks filled in as the writing moves forward. Even though the reader may not be as familiar, you can easily get into the story thanks to well crafted explanations of the scenario. Overall, I simply found AgentDash had the more memorable story that sticks out in my mind. So the winner is AgentDash Pick: AgentDash Judge 2 Dash 1 Interesting choice to have the main character not appear for a few segments. Good intro to the others, but something about the writing style is a bit off. There are some good lines followed by ones that are just somehow awkward to read. Ditto with the dialogue. Most of it sounds realistic and comes off well, but it still sometimes just feels a little wrong. Hard to put my finger on why; probably word choice or turns of phrase. On the whole good so far though. Finishing the piece, I have some things I like, but a lot of misgivings. Now, I'm not well up on US police procedures, but if this Holmes fellow is a private investigator, which it sounds like he is, then I doubt he would be interrogating people on behalf of the police. If he is a police detective, then he wouldn't be in a detective firm, would he? So, really, I'm not sure what his role is in all of this. I could be wrong, but it seems a little muddled here, as if the relationships aren't quite made clear. Also, the whole 'mystery' seems slightly mechanical in nature. It goes forwards two steps, brings in new info or a twist, moves forwards, adds info... lather, rinse, repeat. It seemed like pretty much every single paragraph was trying to bring something new into the story, and whilst it's an admirable sentiment, it was done in a way that seemed to be rushing the story and taking it faster than its natural state. That said, it was an enjoyable read. Sirrajin 1 Okay, I found the opening a bit boring. In a polar opposite to AgentDash, Sirrajin isn't advancing anything. There were too many paragraphs essentially saying the same thing; Killean is bored. But now we're moving. So... who is Mary? Most of the way through and I am very much in the dark as to who most of these people are, what they are doing, their motives and so on and so forth. Without knowing how Charity left, Sirrajin looks like a fool blaming her for leaving the kid, when a girlfriend staying with someone she doesn't want to be with because a kid grew attached to her would be a horrible mistake to make. The only thing I know about Matt and Mary is that Killean was trying to destroy Matt... and now he's looking after the baby? I need details about these people. And who is this Vegas guy? I am currently feeling very disconnected from the piece. The journal piece, whilst very good, had tenses flying all over the place, which made it harder to read than I would have liked. Gave an insight into Killean though. By the end of the piece, aside from the tenses within the journal getting very confused, I was left with a roleplay that was very well written but that seemed hollow. I wasn't given any kind of introduction to the characters, and as such I have struggled to identify with them. Hopefully the second piece will be better. Sirrajin 2 Picks up where the previous one left off. We're progressing, but we've got bits of various stories with no cohesion to them. I know nothing about Killean's alleged crimes, nothing about Charity, nothing about Vegas, little to nothing about the Wards... It gets a little interesting that Matt would help Sirrajin out. And so Killean returns the favour at a PRIME show. I don't know anything about PRIME other than passing stuff posted on the PTC site, so all the relationships are a mystery to me... but I get the idea of him changing things to help the man who just gave him a lead on Charity. Overall though... am I supposed to know anything about Matt and Killean? All I know is that they had a feud a while back, but no details, and nothing to tie me in at all emotionally. I don't mean to sound overly critical when writing this. You're evidently a skilled writer, but for people unfamiliar with your work in the past, it's got too little background or information that would make the story emotionally connect. Dash 2 Opening segment a little too melodramatic, and I still don't get exactly what kind of detective this fella is. He seems to be some sort of contracted out detective, that can be hired in to do a job in the police force with all the privileges of rank as if he was a genuine cop... does that actually happen? Massive apologies if that is standard practice in America, but it seems crazy. On the whole, it's an unspectacular, but entertaining piece. The writing isn't as fluid as that of your opponent, and the subject matter is all round very implausible, but it's not bad. There were definitely points in it that I enjoyed, though a little more characterisation would help, in place of a constant stream of action. Maybe reveal things about people via their actions as opposed to their 'files'. Summary: For me this a quite straightforwards decision. Killean Sirrajin presented better writing to me in terms of style and flow, but both pieces were entirely inappropriate for tournament writing. They did nothing to involve me in the lives of any of the protagonists, and it needed a ton of background knowledge of the characters involved, as very little was given throughout the roleplays. AgentDash was a little formulaic, a little implausible, and a little worse in terms of writing aesthetics, but his story was accessible and didn't rely on outside knowledge, which allowed me to get at least a little involved in it. As such... Pick: AgentDash And I would say that Dash (or Sirrajin if he wins) needs to step up next round if they want to take the bracket. Result - AgentDash d Killean Sirrajin, 2 votes to 0 Summary - Damien Cruz d Yori Yakamo Jr. 2 votes to 1 - AgentDash d Killean Sirrajin, 2 votes to 0 Bracket Final Damien Cruz vs AgentDash Bracket Judges Pitt Andy Jorden Jeff Craig Pooh
