Round 2 Results - Eeyore

1 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
THE ILLUSTRIOUS FACE EATER V JACK MURPHY Judge 1 First roleplays. Murphy, your roleplay was . . . all right. Technically, I don't have much at which to line the crosshairs. But on the side of storytelling, I thought it a little flat. No real peak -- or, at least, nothing that I thought was a peak. You suffered the piece with a little too much description (and especially for a first-person piece); tone it down a little and drop a lot of your adverbs and I think it'll flow better. Facey, you're insane. At first, I thought it'd be a whole bunch of stoner humor I wouldn't really get (though I did love the Kevin Bacon line), but it evolved into something really impressive. I liked the suspense you built throughout the hostage situation. The inclusion of Ellis Nash, who I'm a big fan of, made it better. Both were solid pieces and I dug the same-ending bit a lot. For the first roleplays, the Illustrious Face-Eater takes it. Second roleplays. Murphy: I can see why you're a mainstay in AWC. You're good. A lot of the unneeded description from the first piece wasn't here, and that only benefits your writing. I enjoyed the extra oomph of emotion in here, concerning the couple's baby-making situation and their opposing stances about his career. Facey: This was a step down from your first piece, but I'm glad you got up a second piece. Well -- I'm not too familiar with the Adam Dick character, so this is probably my misunderstanding, but it didn't even seem like the same character. In a way, I liked that. That duality of a person. Just wish you could have finished it. After the first set, I was sure Facey would have taken this without too close a margin. Then Murphy posted a strong piece against Dick's unfinished one, and that evened things out. I hate you both for making it difficult. In the end, though, I think Eater had the better overall pieces with more potential than that of Murphy's. It's a tough choice, though, and neither of you should be ashamed. Pick: The Illustrious Face Eater Judge 2 A victory for Murphy due to having two solid peices to Facey's one and a quarter. Pick: Jack Murphy Judge 3 IFE: He could of been a contender if he had time to finish his second piece. Although the rules are toss what you have up there, it wasn't enough to get by two rp's from Mr. Murphy. Murphy; This one came down to time management. Both are good writers, and both have their own style. So I can't fault them on anything. However if i was grading them on a scale, they'd come down to a very close grade of each other. But for this one, Murphy scores with two full rp's, and IFE with 1 1/2. I don't know if it would have gone this way if Facey had posted his second in full. Overall: Murphy with the pick. Result Jack Murphy d The Illustrious Face Eater, 2 votes to 1 VANGELUS OLSIG V DOCTOR CURIOSITY Judge 1 Vangelus Olsig The two rps he did told a very good story but seemed to connect poorly with the GTT6 until the end of the 2nd rp. It was well written and obviously alot of thought was put into but I just didnt see the connection to the event you're rp'ing for. I think it was good but you may want to make the tie-ins to the event a little more noticeable, espcially in the first rp. Dr. Curiosity The dialogue was good, I felt the way you seperated the characters verbally made it very easy to track the story without getting confused as to who said what. I wasn't able to follow parts, perhaps from not having a base knowledge of your previous work but I think there were points where new readers would be completely lost as to the character and in an event where you are being read and judged by new people you need to be able to keep them in the story whether they jump in halfway through your series or go back to the beginning. Pick: Vangelus Olsig Judge 2 Doctor Curiosity Roleplay #1 - I thought this was a real good roleplay through and through. The opening started off real good I thought between Chris/Curiosity and his sister, got the tempo of the piece started, and the ending was really good as well with Merrick revealing his plan. This piece also had me laughing which is also a good thing. The writing is good throughout and you get a real good sense of who Doc C is, and this is the first time I've ever read a Doc C piece. Technically, the piece is sound outside of one or two errors that I caught. One was spelling and another was a fragment. There may have been others that I just didn't catch, but if I didn't catch them then that's a good thing for you. Storywise, real strong. You get enough story here to whet your appetite, and make you want to read the next piece. The downside is that the middle felt rather clunky. Everything around it was rather good, but it felt like everything just came to a halt in the middle, and I zoned off a few times. That's my only major complaint. I liked the story, I liked the characters, and I thought the piece was well written. Good use of words and all that. Good job here. Roleplay #2 - Another good piece, and I think better then your other one for this round. Once again, you tell a great story here, and this time you don't run into the same problems in the middle that you did last time which is a huge plus. This piece had me laughing, had me interested, and had me actually thinking that Doc C may actually die. That's amazing. Technically speaking, this piece was sound all around, and I saw no errors. Storywise, great. I don't have much to say about this piece other then that it was good all around. Congrats man for writing two great pieces. Vangelus Olsig Roleplay #1 - Let me open this up by saying that this is the best roleplay I've read thus far. Simply amazing. You weave a tale here that is incredibly strong and interesting. When I finished this piece, I was eager to get right into the next piece, but decided to write down my comments first. Your way with words is extraordinary, and the way you write it's like your painting with them. Simply amazing. Technically speaking, this really could've used another read through as there were errors scattered throughout this piece, and that does hurt it in the long run. If those errors hadn't been there, this would've been a near perfect piece. Even with them though, this is a pretty good piece, and like I said, one of the best I read. The conversation between Vangelus and his sister was just phenomenal I thought and it shows that you have a good grasp on dialouge and the characters you're using. The ending was phenomenal as well. If anything, the opening was slow, but I felt it was a good start. The ending more than made up for it with the death of Esposiot, and you've added another twist in your story. You have talent here, and I hope that roleplay #2 continues with that. Roleplay #2 Wow... just wow. Another great piece, and most of the technical issues that hounded the first piece are nearly nonexistant here. There are still some errors with comma use, spelling, things like that, and that's going to have to be improved upon in the future I would think, if you make it out of the round. However, this piece just shoots out of the cannon, and the discussion between the two lawyers was amazingly good, as was the conversation between Vangelus and Carlos Boss. I think I said everything I needed to say in the first roleplay, but this is just another great piece. Technical issues will trip you up though if you don't take care of those problems. I know you had a tough week with PRIME and this, but in the future, just take the extra time to make sure that things like comma use, semi-colons, spelling, grammar, etc. are covered so that they're not pointed out and cause you to lose. Pick: Vangelus Olsig Although, Doc C brought good pieces as well. This was not an easy decision. Congrats to both men. However, Olsig had the stronger story and the stronger writing. Doc C, you brought both, but not on the same level as Olsig. Judge 3 First roleplays. Olsig, first of all: Colombia. That said, you're a pretty good writer. But I didn't care much about the storyline or the character. He isn't the most endearing fella, and it was difficult for me to connect with "royalty" on any level, physically, mentally, or emotionally. I liked the quick-changes of scenes at the end, though. That worked well. Curiosity: After I read the line where Curiosity's digging through his sister's crotch-huggers, I knew it'd be one helluva ride. You proved me right. I loved the dialogue ("I get eaten by a tiger. Oh fuck off"), the slips in dialect that I now know are meant to be there, the situations, the twist you-get-to-live clause, and then the other twist. After the first roleplays, Curiosity has it. Second roleplays. Olsig: I feel sort of shitty saying this, but there's really nothing else I can say after the first piece. You're really not a bad writer. It's just the storyline that's throwing my interest another way. Maybe this should be more of an in-fed thing; for competitions, writing a storyline that makes your character appealing, even if he's not that great a guy, would work better. I also think that you should kill off the prologues and epilogues. Weave them into the roleplay itself and make sure to avoid the dreaded info-dump. Curiosity: A good follow-up piece. I laughed out loud at the Spice Girls thing. Like I said to Olsig, there's not much I can add here. You know how to translate comedy onto the page very well, and I've become something of a fan. I'm picking Curiosity here for the win. His storyline was more engaging than was Olsig's, and his writing edged out his opponent's as well. Pick: Doctor Curiosity Judge 4 Vangelus Olsig: Title: Trial Series: The Great Scopes Scandal I was taken in by the roleplay from the beginning. Olsig combines great descriptive efforts with a superb vocabulary, something often overlooked. The introduction helps set the scene for the reader, and then you are brought right into the isolation of Olsig in his prison. The politics and background bring you into the story easily. The ending is superb, with a simple “Shift” helping you switch back and fortht hrough the scenes occuring at the same moment quite easily. You are left wondering who helped Olsig and why since he seems dead set on a trial. Title #: Trial Series: The Great Scopes Scandal (2) You feel the air of bravado and certainty sucked out of the city by Olsig’s escape with just a few paragraphs. Masterfully established by the writer before moving into the release. The hidden hand is revealed as the crime boss with an even more desperate decision. The summary of which is simply: A chance at winning GTT. Or the grave. Nice tie-in to the tournament on the second roleplaya nd showing the desperation of where Olsig has to go. Doctor Curiosity Title: The Infernal Ways of Death Avoidance (pt 1) The style is very easy to follow and get into, which I appreciate as the reader. The story moves along at a nice pace and introduces characters I myself am unfamiliar with, but the Doctor makes sure I feel a sense of connection to each as a first time reader. The blending of GTT, XUW and his own personal issues all blend together nicely. I think the image of the Doctor being eaten by a tiger is quite funny, and I want to see what anti-tiger devices are. A re-read might have caught a couple of small spelling errors. Title: The Infernal Ways of Death Avoidance (pt 2) The Eegors are just fun. A group of want-to-be wrestlers willing to do all sorts of crazy things is never bad. The interaction between them and the Doctor is great. The “Rabies” sign made me laugh, and then it is kept a little real when Sporty Eegor walks away. The tiger scene seems a little rushed, but still fun. The image of playing with the white tiger is great, something you could only hope for, until the attacking tiger interrupts. The death of the tiger actually ruined it for me a little because it seemed too convenient and implausible. Pick: Vagelus Olsig defeats Doctor Curiosity Olsig’s pieces are so well rounded and thought out they take the day. They draw you into a very complicated, very real world and allow you to experience the richness of the environment. The story is a pleasure to read. Doctor Curiosity is fun, and makes you laugh. The one issue that creeps into my mind as a reader is a bad balance between the real life aspects and the improbable. Demons I was okay with, the Doc6tor character is fun, the sister is nice, and the Eegors are fantastic because wrestling groupies are believable. Unfortunately, the death of the tiger, the ease of making it into the tiger pen and then the ending with the cross fall a little hallow for me. Great piece, but these seemed out of place. Judge 5 Doctor Curiosity by a hair. Literally. Two great stories, two great writers. Sucks I have to choose one. Result Vangelus Olsig d Doctor Curiosity, 3 votes to 2 SAMMY BROWN V EASTON HALL Judge 1 Sammy Brown I really enjoyed your work. I could tell right off the bat that this is a character who was not supposed to win and still managed too. You portrayed him as almost an everyman and it worked. Your writing was superb and I always felt like I understood the character of Brown. I got slightly confused at the scene changes in the first one and felt the second rp could have been a bit longer, but it wasnt anything to bring the overall stature of your work down. Easton Hall I'm not a writer but the point of view you use, whatever its called is not one I like very often. You made it work though, it seemed like I was reading both from his point of view and from outside of it at the same time if that makes sense. You kept the GTT as a main focus throughout despite where your story went and that's good. The endings seemed stressed, like you were trying too hard to finish them and the song lyrics through the rp were not something I liked though at least you made the lyrics somewhat match what you were doing in the rp. Pick: Sammy Brown Judge 2 Sammy Brown: Title: How Much are Your Dreams Worth? It took me a little while to understand what was happening between the two scenes of the roleplay. Once I understood, I had an even deeper appreciation for the story. I enjoy the casting of Brown as a down on his luck wrestler who never achieved his dream, because that is a great story. The gambling angle is a twist I would not have expected considering his losing history in the story, but it is interesting to read about the consequences his actions have on others. However, do people bet serious money on wrestling? Title: Greed… It's Overwhelming. There seems to be a lack of fear from Sammy in this roleplay. If he just betrayed people who could hurt everyone, I would expect more fear. The handling of the scene with his partners and the crime boss is handled well. The story moves along nicely with an ending that makes the reader want to know what the next play will be for the tournament. Easton Hall: Title: The Singing Woman The chase might be a great name for this roleplay as well, as the images of the chase through the streets will be what sticks with me. The eerie, singing voice chasing Easton Hall and spurring him on as he chases the thief sticks out as an interesting pattern. The ending confuses me as the reader, maybe it is connected to another part of his story. However, rather than a negative, it spurs you to read the next roleplay to understand what is happening. And you ask if Easton is not going to buy Raisin Bran. Title: The Singing Man The switch between vision and reality was definitely a trip that took some getting used to. I understand the two worlds intersecting, but as a reader it made it quite confusing. I’m not sure how to suggest making this a little easier, but it felt bewildering to me. Overall, the end product is very intriguing if a little confusing. I found myself re-reading to ensure I hadn’t lost myself in the flow of the story. Pick: Easton Hall defeats Sammy Brown Easton has an intriguing story and a slightly perplexing style, but when you read the story it draws you in rather than pushing you away. It takes a little more, but it is rewarding. I would work on the second roleplay to make it slightly more palatable, but it is great. Sammy brown has a good, classical story in the making. The underdog has his day, but several of the holes in the story bother me. I don’t see how paying Sammy Brown that much money helps make the crime boss even more money. That makes it somewhat difficult for me to buy into the entire story. Is Sammy is a perennial loser, why would the odds against someone else favor making money here? Judge 3 Sammy Brown's elegant simplicity puts him over Easton Hall. Pick: Sammy Brown Judge 4 Sammy Brown: The handler here has taken a character that shouldn't matter in anything, and is actually making an intriguing storyline here. i'm thoroughly enjoying it. Simple where it needs to be but enough storyline hooks to keep you wanting to read more, meaning it's not too simple. Easton Hall: Well written but not as simple for those of us who may not know the character and his full storyline. Both are great writers and could go far, but today, Brown had something that Hall didn't. So Sammy Brown is my pick if I had to choose one to pass on, which i do. Result - Sammy Brown d Easton Hall, 3 votes to 1 SUMMARY - Jack Murphy d The Illustrious Face Eater, 2 votes to 1 - Vangelus Olsig d Doctor Curiosity, 3 votes to 2 - Sammy Brown d Easton Hall, 3 votes to 1 - High Flyer d Jim Axtell via forfeit BRACKET JUDGES Andrew RJ Pete Pitt Brett Craig Eeyore
