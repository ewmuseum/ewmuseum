Round 2: Mrs S. Bracket Results

22 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SALAOK V SAM WOLACK Judge 1 And we have my first amnesia angle of the tournament. Thank you, Salaok. Unfortnately, I have no idea who JD is, but, the small summation of his harassment over the last few weeks gives me a small glimpse. I'm not exactly sure why someone would hold someone's entire memory bank away from them, simply for the sport of it. If Salaok is merely trying to ignore that part of his past, okay. But, I'm not sure what purpose it serves, other than to lend a protagonist to a somewhat weak story. Grammatically, it's a bit of a mess with misplaced commas and apostrophes. "Something's are best not to be remembered" should be "Somethings are..." I found it difficult to get into the first peice, and the second wasn't much better. I find it hard to have any sympathy for Salaok, especially since the narrative voice, who seemingly knows it all doesn't give any sort of glimpse into anything, other than pain. There's no shots of joy, or anything redeeming about it. It almost seems as if the writer decided to show as much painful imagery as possible, and didn't much care about anything else. On to Wolack, and the somewhat popular theme this year of the CIA, FBI or other governmental agency. The tense switching from paragraph to paragraph is an absolute pain in the ass, and it makes reading this somewhat unbearable. It was short, and honestly was simply a dream sequence followed by nothing else. Also, please, please, please, learn to love and accept pronounce. "Wolack" doesn't need to lead off every paragraph and sentence about Sam. "He" will suffice nicely, in the future. In the second, again, tense issues pick Sam up, slam him to the ground, and make him his unwilling boyfriend, only this time, we're switching every sentence. Again, it makes it a tough read. The content behind it is fine. I would suggest asking someone to tense proof it for you in the future. The dreams are weird. Really weird. I don't know if they're uniquely cool weird or just really weird weird. I really didn't learn much about Wolack, other than he has some strange dreams. In the end, despite the tenses, and the other stuff, I think Wolack takes it. Salaok's effort was good, but, there's a certain je ne sais quoi in Wolack's writing that I get into, aside from his tense issues. This is obviously not the best effort from Wolack, which seems to be a theme for this kind of year. Maybe Argyle shouldn't have tournaments around holiday times, when people have other things on their minds. Pick: Sam Wolack. Judge 2 Both writers, I wasn’t overly impressed with either of your first role plays. They both seemed weak and lacking to me. However, you both pulled it together for the 2nd RP. Salaok, I found your second RP powerful. Especially your use of descriptive detail, but suggest you consider giving more emotional detail also. Wolack, the introduction was fantastic, however I was a bit disappointed by the FBI/nightmare/drowning your sorrows combo, it seemed a bit over done IMHO. Both of you could do with a proofreader. Salaok the win. Judge 3 Wolack: For the Love of Moloko My first response upon reading this was that it is nothing extroadinary. Though I read the piece easily it did not jump out and grab me. The concept was good, but poorly executed. Though quality is always favored over quanity, in this instance, I would have to say more quanity may have given this piece more flavor. Try not to be so repetitive with certain words as they will make a piece grow monotonous to read. Journeys: I can say I enjoyed this second one more than the first one. I was with the character on thinking " What the fuck?" Each time something changed on him. The dream sequence here was well done, though still weak at times. Again I am going to suggest taking more time for description in order to draw your reader in. Towards the end it kind of lost me I didn't understand the whole outfit thing there. Salaok: Sometimes it is better not to remember It took me a moment or two to really get into the story. I think the letter is what finally truly brought my mind into the scene. The rest of this was nicely executed, the subtle descriptions were well done and made the object/action evident without having to go into deep detail. I have to applaud that. The only thing that really bugged me was the mad amount of paragraph/sentence breaks. Hope is for those who don't know: I like how this second installment was a direct continuance of the first. The emotion drawn in this story is hard to avoid while reading. Like the first the paragraph breaks did take away from the experience a little but not enough to lose my interest. Pick: Salaok d Wolack Though I understand Wolack's intention with his stories, the execution just wasn't enough to make me go wow. Saloak's stories were a bit more hard hitting though a tad bit cliche with the whole memory lost and slowly being regained bit. Niether opponent truly made it pop for me but Salaok got close..he takes the match. Salaok d Wolack 2 votes to 1 FRED GREST V JORGE SAMUELSSON Fred Grest Rp: First thing I noticed is that you must work on your use of the comma. When you are decribing things and you say something like sweet nice lovely voice...then you need to use a comma between those. I believe it should read more like sweet, nice, lovely voice. The story itself is...well...frankly its not much of anything. Now when I say that I don't mean that you're some hack of a writer. What I mean is that there simply isn't anything to it. Grest talks to woman. She talks bad about his wrestling career. He defends it. Blah,blah,blah. Simply isn't much to that. It is a roleplay we've seen a million times and frankly doesn't really lend you much in terms of trying to win a big tournament like this, even when just trying to get through the second round. Simply doesn't do much for me and left me feeling like maybe you just threw something together really quick in order to get a roleplay up. Jorge Samuelsson Rp#1: First off the setting is put together very nicely. You worded it just enough to give me the mental picture I need to see where your at without making it too wordy and causing me to become bored and want to quit reading. Very nice way to start this piece of work off. Then we take a little trip down memory lane so to speak. The description of his pride for his family and how he wants to live up to the name is well done. Over all this piece is very well written and had me reading it from start to finish. A very nice job by Mr. Samuelsson. Jorge Samuelsson Rp#2: Once more you set the scene perfectly for me. You kept it to the perfect amount without ruining the flow and starting out flat. Once again a very nice job. We get into the story and find him extremely angry over the loss of his family heir loom. I liked seeing his anger. I liked seeing him talk a little smack to the cops and such. I thought it got a little off track with him bullying the bouncers and such in his search but it fits the character and his anger I gather. I like how you continued to show his determination to prove himself and to prove how stupid people are for over looking him and such. You might have went a tad to far with it but not so much that it really hurts the over all feel of this piece or the two pieces of writing. Final Thoughts/Winner: Samuelsson wins this one in fairly easy fashion. Grest simply didn't seem like he brought his A game (As I know he can do better having seen him do so) and I think the first round bye might have actually made him a tad rusty and hurt him. Samuelsson established his character pretty well and gave me reason to like him and get involved in the character. Looking foward to seeing what he puts forth in the third round. Pick: Samuelsson ALEC LANCER V AIDEN JACKSON For both writers, there where times that your work just didn’t read ‘right’. Examples of this (and these are only two of many) are (for Aiden) “The lovely pane glass windows reflected the light inside in a seemingly beautiful manner.” And, (for Alec) “His teeth were covered with the rests of food and the air escaping his mouth was breath-taking.” Aiden, I didn’t find your story line to be strong, in fact, I found it very hard to follow. Overall, I was expecting a higher quality of work this round. Pick: Alec Lancer ETHAN FROST V ANGELO DEVILLE Darkness by Ethan Frost Good introduction, it gave me an idea of what you were going to talk about and an understanding of the characters thought process, the whole devil/evil thing was pretty good. Then introducing us to the past of what's occurred was fairly well done. From there it went into a dream that seemed a bit confusing, but I did manage to understand it. And from there I saw the usual fear of having to face Angelo. The story of the roleplay was decent, and had me interested to read to the end. Light by Ethan Frost It starts off with an introduction to string you along, but not really bringing it to action. The discussion about the match continues and how Frost should be nothing more then a small bump in the road for Deville. Not too often do you see the guy saying he has littl to no chance of wining...but it does happen...it is Deville. Ethan woke up in an abandon warehouse, where the owner of UWF has decided to keep him from the competition. Overall the story was half decent and a good way to motivate a wrestler to go after his opponent, because it showed a good personality change in Ethan that he needed to win. Sounds of Silence by Angelo Deville The piece started having me a bit confused but the story was flowing very well. I wasn't sure exactly what it meant but decided to stop asking questions and follow along with the story, that I found pretty interesting. By the end of it all you know you still want more, with the roleplay ending in a sequence of surprise that shall be revealed in the next. A pretty good piece of writing, hopefully the next one ties it all together well. Born of Dreams by Angelo Deville He has some good description in the opening paragraph and I see he's decided to continue on with the characters of the first. The suspense of the car being there had me thinking of what was about to happen, and the man hanging from the tree just made everything click together well. And the aftermath of it was good, and had me thinking that's a hell of a predicament Deville had went through. Overall it was a hell of a twisted story but I enjoyed it. He had a slight clinch over Frost I felt with this crazy story, and gets the thumbs up for the victory from me. Pick: Angelo Deville. CHANDLER TSONDA V ARMAAN Judge 1 Armaan, I can’t tell you how many ‘dream sequence’s’ I’ve read in my roll as judge for both GTT4 and GTT5. In fact, this is my second dream sequence of the night. I’m starting to think it’d be a good idea to start a thread on over used story lines that judges never want to read again, mine would include dream sequence’s, tortured souls and flashbacks of abusive parents. Your RP’s did contain some good dialogue, and as a whole flowed well.. Tsonda., I started reading your first RP thinking to myself, ‘What the….?” You very nearly lost me, but thankfully you warmed up into your RP and choose not to use a horrible cliché. I expect you to step it up in round three! Pick: Tsonda Judge 2 Armaan Rp #1: Hmmm...kind of a slow and blah roleplay. The writing just seems to really lend itself to a very bland read. The story itself isn't a bad one. The sick mother. Wrestling becoming unimportant at this point. A decent story but one that is used just about every time you turn around with either mother/girlfriend/friend/wife or whatever else. Not a terrible piece but not what I'd have expected of a guy who pulled off such a major upset in the first round. Looking to see a lot more in the second piece. Armaan RP#2: Pretty much what I got out of the first one. A big problem was that you didn't space your paragraphs.I'm sure you did when writing it but when you posted it you didn't notice or something. Made it a bit harder to read. Some of the same problems that were in the first. Parts of the descriptions didn't make sense and the writing was done in a very bland manner. Plus the fact that his mother in fact did die was predictable and followed the usual trend of other rps written in this manner. Over all a very average piece, much like the first one of yours this round. Tsonda Rp#1: Ok so this roleplay starts off with a history lesson. It is done in a decent fashion. Not exactly anything that inspires me to a great degree but it works alright. So we move to the story and the real meat of the roleplay and things pick up a bit. The over all flow of the roleplay is adequate but nothing special. Nothing stands out as excellent in the roleplay but nothing stands out as just horrible. Just a very middle of the road roleplay. Tsonda Rp#2: The second roleplay starts with the description of a scene were going to be experiencing. I like the beginning of this better than the history one simply cause it seemed to flow better. Still it doesn't jump out at me. One piece of advice is to some how start the work off with a hook line. Something that gets a reader involved with it right from the start. Much the same way you'd want to write a speech. Now we move to the dialogue part of the roleplay.The amount of description between speaking parts is good. There isn't too much to where it becomes a problem or takes away. The problem with the rest of the roleplay is that the story itself isn't anything grand. Pretty standard fare and not what I'd expect from someone in GTT trying to make the third round. Again this isn't cause I think you suck or anything but the story and the dialogue for it are just very, very, boring. Again please don't take offense to that but the idea and the writing just doesn't lend itself to being exciting or keeping the reader wanting more. The good things here are that you haven't got any horrible spelling or grammer issues in your work. It flows from sentence to sentence in terms of not being hard to read. I didn't find myself having to go back and re-read parts of it to get the gist of the sentence. So take some props in the fact that you don't make major mistakes within the writing itself but you must come out with better and more entertaining/intriguing work in order to get further and further into the tournament. Final Thoughts and Winner: Picking the winner on this one was tough. Neither guy jumped out as me and had me just dying to push then ahead. The fact is that if this is the writing that goes on in round three it will be a exit in round three for the winner. In this round, however, I had to go with the roleplay that flowed better in terms of the writing. For that reason alone I'm going with Tsonda to move on. I'll be expecting a far more exciting piece of work for him in the next round though if he expects to go any further. Pick: Tsonda Chandler Tsonda d Armaan, 2 votes to 0 SUMMARY - Salaok d Sam Wolack 2 votes to 1 - Jorge Samuelsson d Fred Grest - Alec Lancer d Aiden Jackson - Angelo Deville d Ethan Frost - Chandler Tsonda d Armaan 2 votes to 0 Brackets
