Lucas Black is not a compassionate man. He revels in the pain and suffering of others, drawing strength and inspiration from their tears. As strange as it may seem to objective observers, the suffering of others is ambrosia to Lucas Black. In the ring, Lucas enjoys nothing more than hearing his opponents – or his prey, as he refers to them – cry out in pain. Often, he prolongs the agony for as long as he can, eking out every last ounce of sustenance that he can drain from his prey. Even better if he can hear them beg for the mercy that Lucas will never give. Lucas Black is not a compassionate man. Early Life In childhood, these tendencies led to numerous encounters with police and psychiatric professionals, but his sharp intellect and disarming charm left authorities powerless. Manipulative and perhaps even a little megalomaniacal, Lucas often employs others to do his dirty work with promises of grand rewards or terrible vengeance. As he reached adulthood, Lucas Black discovered professional wrestling. His intrigue and excitement knew no bounds. Soon, he had started the long, grueling struggle to become a professional wrestler -- a career that would allow him to grow wealthy and gain status by inflicting torment. He immersed himself not only in wrestling, but also numerous forms of martial arts and physical conditioning. Today, Lucas has transformed himself, physically, mentally, and tactically, into a phenomenal specimen. Career World Wrestling Alliance Lucas Black signed a contract in July 2008, and made his WWA debut at the beginning of that esteemed organization’s 2008-2009 season and seized the WWA Television Championship weeks later, from Intrepid in just his third WWA match. Black has remained undefeated in singles competition, ever since his debut in September at the first ever edition of WWA Glory. Birthday Bash X was a defining moment in the short career of Lucas Black. In one night, he lost the WWA Television title to newcomer Elvis Hunt, only to become the WWA World Heavyweight Championship later in the night. When he climbed the ladder over seventy feet about the arena floor atop the WWAtron, to capture the gold with the "Mile High" victory. Black would eventually lose his World Heavyweight Championship to Nick Adams inside Ultimate Glory II at Revolution 2009.
