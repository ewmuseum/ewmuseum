Cyberspace Championship Wrestling Open Since: March 1998 Member of PTC Since: Fed Style: HYBRID(Roleplay and Angle) Roster Size: 46 Weekly Shows: SHOWS HERE PPV Names: January: CCW(Insert Year Number Here) February: CCW Dead or Alive March: CCW Irish Spring April: CCW April Showers May: CCW Memorial Day Bash June: CCW Shriekfest July: CCW Heatstroke August: CCW Gold and Glory September: CCW Cyberslam October: CCW Freakshow November: CCW Group Therapy December: CCW Countdown 2 Destruction Current Champions: World Heavyweight: Neurosis Women's: Devyl Trans-Atlantic: The Valiant One Pacific: John Jupiter Independent: Kensuke Suzahara Hardcore: Scorpion Tag Team: The Blood Brothers(Benny Blades & Neurosis) Recent Signings: Ricky Coyote, Grimm Reaper History CCW began back in September of 1998 as a small, unorganized, and little known e-fed. A small roster and computer problems forced the shutdown one month later prior to the scheduled first-ever PPV. Months later, the computer problems were fixed and CCW began reconstructing in January of 1999. CCW President Jonathan Stevens, who founded the original CCW, began advertising the little e-fed prior to the official grand opening. He set that date for February 26, 1999. By this day, several wrestlers had joined up in hopes of finding fame and fortune in the new but independent CCW. A tournament unfolded to find who would be CCW's first real champions and this tournament concluded on the first PPV in CCW history, Irish Spring in March of 1999. There CCW's first champions were born and business simply skyrocketed! After a couple months of operation, CCW's roster grew to 20, then 30, and now sits at 40. A Women's Division soon opened followed by a Hardcore and Lightweight Division. CCW enjoyed a couple successful PPVs in the early Spring of 1999 before debuting the real competition... The Champions of the Ring Tournament. This 32-Man tournament, which is known today for making legends in CCW, would sketch one man's name in stone for the rest of eternity. It was proven in 1999 as The Supreme Machine won the inaugural tournament, and proven a second time in 2000 when Phil Burner won the tournament and the CCW Heavyweight Title in one night. Concluding the first COTR tournament was the Summer PPV, Heatstroke. It went down as the greatest PPV in CCW history to date when Sid Powers took on Samson in a Closed Cage Match where the loser had to leave CCW. In the end, Sid Powers won the match but due to the politics that comes with just about everything these days, Samson was reinstated. For winning the first COTR, The Supreme Machine got his reward at his own PPV, 'SuMa's Destruction' in September of 1999 where he reclaimed the CCW Heavyweight Title in what would be his last Heavyweight Title run. Showdown I, in November of 1999, saw the return of Phil Burner as he won his first ever CCW Heavyweight title by defeating long time nemesis, Tim Inman. He went on to hold the title a record 104 days. The year 2000 ushered in some great talent for CCW such as Andrew Hawk, Bobby Blades, Jason McAllister, John Jupiter, Neurosis, and Malcolm Next. Veterans of CCW came around such as Brad Lamen, Scotty Showtime, and Jim "Klown" Bowe. CCW shut down for the month of July in 2000 for rennovations. Upon the reopening, it was announced that Jonathan Stevens had died and a new leader would take over in October. That leader turned out to be Phil Burner. Burner's reign over CCW turned a lot of focus on CCW from outside organizations, but many scandals ran rampant near the end of his control of CCW. Several stars left CCW because of their suspicion that Phil Burner was choosing unfair winners and not abiding by the regulations set forth by the late Jonathan Stevens. In March 2001, miracuously, Jonathan Stevens returned to take back control of CCW. It had turned out he was not dead, but hiding out in Europe while investigators in the USA probed his many friends and relatives in an attempt to find the the person behind the death threats and exploding packages he had been receiving back in October of 2000. However, Jon Stevens soon again resigned, and this time for good. He left Bryan Grosnick in Charge, who, under his guidance, kept CCW afloat, and stable. This was the first in numerous Presidential Changes. Soon after, President Grosnick left the scene again, leaving now Nick Ware in Charge of the ship, who also kept the ship afloat, but soon the ship began to take on water. President Ware kept the Ship afloat for another few months, but he had to leave as well, leaving Jeff Awtrey in charge, now the ship began to sink. President Awtrey ran a tight ship here. CCW was running good. However, President Awtrey Dissapeared. Leaving only, what seemed to be a logical choice to leave at the helm of the ship. Doug Ellington, by the hand of President Awtrey took control of CCW in October of 2001, and hasn't relinquished it since. Except for an involuntary leave for the majority of the month of January. CCW is stable again.
