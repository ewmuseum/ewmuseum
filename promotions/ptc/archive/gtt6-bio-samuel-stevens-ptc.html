Samuel Stevens Biography Biography Samuel Tobias Stevens was born in April of 1983 in the city of St. Paul, Minnesota as the third of four children to Jonathon and Melissa Stevens. Jonathon was a priest of a local church and Melissa was an elementary school teacher. The two had been high school sweethearts and following Jonathon's tour in Vietnam, a short-lived one, he found God for the first time and he also found that his love for Melissa had strengthened in the time he had been away. The two were married in July of 1971 and shortly thereafter they had a son Keith in 1972, a daughter Melanie in 1978, Samuel in 1983 and finally Jon Jr. in 1985. The family was lower-middle class, the income of a priest and schoolteacher never bringing in much money but their home was always warm, inviting and a safety net for the children to fall back on. Jonathon and Melissa were both loving parents and as Samuel grew up he was always surrounded by that love and safety that fewer and fewer children were able to experience. In early 1999 that love was challenged as vandals set the church that Jonathon had been a part of for the past 27 years, including the last 11 where he acted as the head priest, on fire. The flames ate away at the house of God until there was nothing but charred wood remaining. The next eighteen months would see the hardest times come over the family as Jonathon spent hours on end every day searching for a position in another church, trying to make ends meet with various jobs including construction, teaching, small roles at various churches and even acting as the night manager for a local clothing store in an effort to keep food on the table of his family. Despite this, much of the savings that Jonathon and Melissa had ended up having to be used to get them through the trying times. Samuel's older brother Keith had graduated from the University of Wisconsin in 1995 and Melanie was in the midst of her junior year at the University of Michigan where she had earned a full-ride scholarship. However, Samuel did not have the luck of Keith to have been out of school by the time his father lost his church, nor did he have the skills in school of his sister, who was able to get through college without having to pay a cent. Samuel found himself as an average-at-best student in his second year of high school and his parents had no way of assisting him in getting to college. Samuel was at a crossroads in his life and chose to leave school in 2000, at the end of his junior year, no longer seeing the benefit of the education he was getting now that the goal of college had seemingly gone up in smoke as quickly as his father's church. Samuel had been an average student, never working to hard at it and seemingly being satisfied with C's and the occasional B. His only extracurricular activity had been as a part of his school's wrestling and baseball teams. Sports had always come fairly naturally to him, his older brother Keith being a local legend in high school football and baseball and Melanie a swimmer and national-level softball player. As such Samuel got an immediate job as an assistant coach for a small high school wrestling team a few miles outside of St. Paul and he would spend the next two years going between that job during the school year and a job unloading trucks for a local shipping company in St. Paul that he was able to do year-round. It is important to note that by early 2001 Jonathon had received a position as the priest of a newly refurnished church in St. Paul. Almost immediately upon receiving the guaranteed paycheck Jonathon went to his second oldest son and offered to help pay for his college tuition but Samuel declined, prideful of making his own way in the world. At the end of the 2002-2003 wrestling season Jonathon began feeling the urge to get into the circle himself and compete. He thought it over for a few weeks and finally made the choice to give a childhood dream a shot and see if wrestling could be a realistic goal. He went to a training camp in Minneapolis run by Greg Gagne, son of famed wrestling promoter Verne Gagne, and for a down payment of $2000 he began his training. Training did not come easily for Samuel but the talent was obviously there and the love for wrestling was undeniable. Samuel had been fascinated with the old AWA and NWA territories as a child, his interest with the personalities of the sport ranged from the babyface Steamboat to the heelish Flair, Lawler, and Von Erich Family to the unique characters of the Junkyard Dog and Andre the Giant. As an adult he found himself more and more intrigued by the heels of the top promotions; PCW's Tyler Nelson, OSW's Ivan Stanislav and NAW's Jason Hughes among the most famous. He would continue training under various stars including then-Legacy Champion Jason Hughes in the summer of 2004, developing his in-ring skills to what he hoped would bring a profitable and enjoyable career path. Samuel had begun a tour of various promotions throughout the Midwest in late 2004 as the babyface "Stylin' and Profilin'" Sammy Stevens, a total comedic rip-off of legendary NWA Champion Ric Flair. He would spend a full sixteen months as that character, never once gaining a title shot of any kind or even a shot to truly display his ability as a technical wrestler -- always being told that he had not ‘paid his dues'. However, he continued to do as he was told with the hope that his time would eventually come. At the same time he was in no way making big money with the five-minute squash bouts he was in, always coming up on the losing end, and he was forced to work second and third jobs in order to get by. The career of ‘Sammy' would end in late March 2006 when, following another loss to another big man, Samuel finally had enough and did the unthinkable -- he broke character and let his anger show through. His contract with the local promotion was up in a week and Samuel felt he had nothing left to lose. He began ripping the cheesy Flair-like robe he was forced to wear to shreds, grabbing a microphone and telling everyone in the audience how much he hated the character he portrayed. He screamed about wanting to fight, wanting to win gold and wanting to make a name for himself. He said he would never be able to achieve that by riding the coattails of Ric Flair, and even that he was not allowed to do, having been told to act stupid and ‘like a clown' in his gimmick. ‘Wrestling isn't about gimmicks, it's about winning!' The next week, his last show, he promised the promoter in charge that he would behave but instead he ignored the props and costumes, went out to the ring and wrestled. He fought his opponent, who had been completely unprepared for this showing of aggression, and his punches were so stiff that they drew blood. He ended the match with a sleeper hold and as his opponent dropped to the canvas unconscious Samuel left the ring and grabbed a house microphone to utter the phrase ‘…and your winner, by submission…Samuel Tobias Stevens…fuck you!' The crowd erupted, the rest of the event being overshadowed by the constant chants of ‘We want Stevens!' As a result of his complete ignoring of the promotions rules, the promoter of the event told Samuel that he was going to be run out of the area and that his career would be as good as dead. Samuel accepted his fate and left the arena, angered that the promoter ignored the obvious ability he had on the microphone and in the ring. But over the next few weeks word about this unknown wrestler from St. Paul began to get around and Samuel was given offers to appear at various independent promotions across the United States including California, Arizona, Texas and even a few Canadian promotions. The most intriguing offer came from a newly-founded promotion called Axis, where Samuel was given the shot at starting out new with a clean slate, new ring name and on his terms as far as how he wanted to be portrayed. The money would be little and the glory would be perhaps even less, but at least he would have the shot he had craved for so long. During the summer of 2006 Tobias Stevens would find himself working two promotions at the same time; the first being in a one-night, forty man match called Gold Rush for a national promotion called CSWA that took place in August. The second was for the short-lived Axis Wrestling promotion which lasted only a couple months before folding due to monetary issues. Prior to its folding however Stevens was showcased as a brilliant technician in tag team matches with veteran Jacob Cross and in singles matches, along the way finding that dislike goes both ways as he was constantly in verbal disputes with another skilled technician, Anthony Draco D'Ambriosi. When Axis folded in September Stevens made the choice to move to a promotion familiar to his mentor Jason Hughes, Global Wrestling located in Texas where Hughes had reigned with various belts and had founded a faction of The Upper Echelon. Stevens looked at Global and found two reasons why he had to be a part of the national promotion. First, with a very lacking level of skilled technicians he felt that he would be able to stand out in such a promotion, and secondly he scanned the roster and found the name of a man he simply had to be on the same roster as -- Anthony Draco D'Ambriosi.
