PTC Cruiserweight Tournament

captured 7 Mar 2008

PTC Cruiserweight Tournament is the name for two tournaments that took place in Primetime Central. Open to cruiserweights only, the tournaments are identified by number (i.e. CW1 and CW2). CW1, held in 2004, was won by Seymour Almasy, handled by Sean Williams. CW2, held in 2005, was won by Cereal Killer, handled by [name withheld]. CW1 Fresh off of the success of the Primetime Invitational Tournament, Primetime Central decided to hold another tournament. While many options were considered (such as a female characters only tournament), it was finally decided that the tournament would be for cruiserweight wrestlers. The brackets for the tournament were officially posted on March 22, 2004, by Tournament Director Argyle. The tournament consisted of four brackets, known by number. Forty two entrants were accepted to CW1. Unlike the PIT, free agents were permitted in the Cruiserweight Tournament, with the caveat that they would be placed in triple threat matches, while PTC competitors had a chance to have a one on one match as their first round contest. The Fallen Fae (XUW) v Christian Crawford (RPW) * Horazon (FAW) v Larry The Legionnaire (XUW) Bracket 1 saw a series of strong performances in the first round. While Lorenzo Guevera no-showed, Matt Jackson took Seymour Almasy to his limit, the PIT champion prevailing by the slimmest of margins. In perhaps the bracket's biggest upset, the free agent Pietro Geist defeated the well established 4WW star Blaise McClancy. The Fallen Fae won his contest by no-show, while Horazon earned a tough victory over Larry the Legionnaire. The second round saw Almasy and Geist go at one another hard, with Seymour ultimately pulling out a victory over the demonic priest. Horazon, too, triumphed in his second round match over Fae, setting the stage for the Almasy/Horazon final. Unfortunately, Horazon no showed, giving Seymour the victory by default. Bracket 2 * Carys (RPW) v Fury (WMW) v Armaan (WWA) * Jay Phoenix (4WW) v Max Roswell (GW) * Syko Arsonist (FA) v Angel (FA) v Alex Caine (FA) * Robb Hardy (NGW) v Justin Walker (WWA) v The Fool Ian Fetzer (XUW) Bracket 3 * Jason Starr (NGW) v The Cockjockey (XUW) * Coricidon (4WW) v James Edwards (RPW) v Raijin (FA) * Gordon "Denrol" Korth (WWA) v The Blue Coral (4WW) v Thai Trang (UWF) * Ethan Knight (RPW) v Rocky Adams (WWA) Bracket 4 * Smokey Wead (UWF) v Kirk Urban (WWA) * Emilio Dervante (LOW) v Marcel Celestini (FA) v Jimmy Diablo (FA) * Karina Wolfenden (RPW) v Limp (UPWA) * Tessa Windsor (RPW) v Dawn McFarlaine (WMW) v Robert Theodore Temporary aka Bob The Temp (XUW) Finals The final four of the tournament thus saw matchups of Seymour Almasy vs. Syko Arsonist, and Ethan Knight vs. Karina Wolfenden. Almasy v. Arsonist was a battle of the last two men to win big PTC events; Almasy having won the PIT, and Arsonist having been victorious in the No Limits rumble that earned him a shot against then Universal Champion Angelo Deville. After another hard-fought battle, Almasy prevailed over the dangerous Arsonist, boosting Seymour to a tenth straight tournament victory. Karina Wolfenden triumphed over a game Ethan Knight in an RPW vs. RPW clash, sending the controversial face of RPW's Eclipse Division to a finals battle against Seymour Almasy. In a battle of PTC's present versus its future, Seymour triumphed over the high-flying K-Wolf. Almasy's victory would not deter Wolfenden, however, as the Negasonic Lupine went on to become one of the focal members of PRIME, capturing multiple titles in the process, as well as winning Seven of the Best and becoming (ironically, along with Almasy) one of only two competitors to have held both the PTC Global Title and the PTC Tag Team Titles. As for Almasy, his tournament winning streak would run to fourteen matches over the course of PIT, CW1, and GTT4, before falling in the fourth round to Craig Goat. Unlike Karina, he would return for the second rendition of the tournament, CW2. CW2 Cruiserweight Tournament 2 is the smallest tournament in PTC's history, consisting of only thirty-two competitors.
