9 February 2004 :mVw: Explanation For Abrupt Hiatus Many where left wondering what the hell was going on Monday, January 19th. Millions of viewers tuned in live on pay per view to find a message from In Demand that "for unknown reasons" mVw's much anticipated Rumble Rage had been postponed. Until today's press conference, held in the lobby of the mVe headquarters in Manhattan, New York. After a short introduction and apologies from mVw Public Relations Director, Scott Siz, the Chairman of the company took the stage and made the shocking announcement that he was under federal investigation for tax evasion. Josh Escobar (better known to the wrestling world as Taj Escobar) informed the hoards of wrestling and news media alike that he was advised by his team of attorneys to pull the plug on the Rumble Rage program, and take mVw off of TV until the situation was settled. Escobar was very stern when stating that the claims against him where false, referring to the government as "crabs who see a gangster from Brooklyn going legit and making some serious money, and they wanna pull him back down into the ghetto." Escobar's attorney's, who where on hand, seemed to be furious at their client for some of the statements the flashy Chairman made. Even trying to pull him away from the podium at a point. As always, Escobar persisted to comment on the situation, finishing the conference stating that, "Much to the dismay of my team of worthless lawyers, mVw will return to television on Sunday, February 15th. Luckily, Rumble Rage was prerecorded and will air, one hundred percent free, on Spike TV at 8:00 PM. Malice will resume the following week. Monday, February 23rd. I apologize again for the abrupt halt in mVw programing." Escobar then left the stage with the rest of his staff to an outpouring of questions, which where not answered. Before exiting the stage, in usually Taj Escobar form, he stepped back up to the mic, raising his fist in the air and proclaiming "Fight the power!" Before laughing and exiting the stage. We will keep you updated as information comes out. Escobars hearing is set to take place March 10th in Manhattan. In a press conference today Christina was asked about the rumors of a lawsuit fired against GW by WMW Owner Brad Johnson. As all know the Global Arena is ran by a master computer. We have learned that the core of the Arena where this master computer is kept; was breached and several files tampered with. Hopefully we can reclaim the footage and show both WMW and GW fans... To those who were anticipating this match to be aired, I am truly sorry about this.” When asked about the car accident Kenji had and was reported to be before the match, Christina had this to say. “The car accident happened as Kenji was heading home. After his match thus he was not able to compete on Evolution. The news story on the GW website was altered again by who we have no idea but have launched a full investigation into this situation.” === 13 February 2004 [NGW] Steel Thunder Spotlight John Hitchin – “Where did it all begin? Well, that’s not really the easiest answer to question. There was a fed or two before we actually started calling ourselves Steel Thunder. I mean…TCWA…AWE…we never really had any plans for any sort of an alliance really.” Scott King – “Well definitely, I would have to agree with John here, one-hundred percent. There was never any intention whatsoever…we were just two guys, working for the same companies. What we thought was just by fluke…perhaps ended up being fate. Who knows…all we really do know, is that Steel Thunder is stronger than ever now.” They are Steel Thunder. One of the most prolific tag teams on the independent wrestling circuit. From their humble roots in DWA, to their triumphant 226-day tag team title reign in NGW…they’ve done it all, side by side. This is…Behind the Thunder. It’s 2000…the new millennium… When he was still a newcomer to the sport, John Hitchin signed onto a contract with the AWE, a wrestling federation where the balance of power was in constant change. “Mad Dawg” Munny McMahon, and Scott “Jimmy” King were fighting back and forth for ownership. Then the night of AWE’s very last pay-per-view, a friendship would form, and a legacy would be started. John Hitchin – “It was a phenomenal event without question. I mean…any time that John Hitchin is on the card is going to be a phenomenal event…especially when he wins two titles in that one night.” Scott King – “He only won those two titles because I allowed it to happen. Nah…we had favors for each other that night…who would’ve guessed it would’ve all ended up the way it did.” During the course of the pay-per-view, John Hitchin had won the tag team titles with Byron Maddox, and ran in during a Hardcore Title Ladder Match, knocking out the two competitors, and taking the title. Two titles in one night, not bad for a debut. John Hitchin – “I’d say it was a good night, I mean…I didn’t exactly need anyone’s help. I mean…I AM John Hitchin.” Scott King – “Well at least I’m man enough to admit that I got a little help when I needed it…” The main event of the pay-per-view saw “Jimmy” King take on Mad Dawg Munn **Munny McMahon** for the ownership of AWE. Munn’s “Outlaw Nation” ran in and tried to give him the advantage…but John was to have none of it as he and Matt Douglass stopped the Nation, and King won ownership of AWE. It wasn’t too long after…that AWE closed its doors. Scott King – “Well…I’m not exactly too sure why I closed the doors to the AWE…it was just something I felt needed to be done. McMahon and myself had spoken about getting back together in another fed, and continuing our rivalry. I ended up bringing Hitchin with me, and it was probably one of the best choices personally and professionally that I’ve made.” John Hitchin – “I always love leaving federations with titles around my waist. But, as King said, us sticking together post-DWA, was a great decision…and one that comes with no regrets.” With the AWE behind them, and a friendship brewing…this was only the beginning of things to come. Steel Thunder was well on its way… The year is 2001… One of the top syndicated cable network shows of all time debuts…Steel Thunder TV (STTV). John Hitchin and Scott “Jimmy” King used some of their wrestling salaries to fund this, Jerry Springer-like, show. Hiring actors to portray wrestlers in their federation, usually people that they were going to face that week. The crowd loved it; their opponents hated it. There was one thing that was for certain however…it got ratings…and with ratings, came money. And with money…came success. Scott King – “I remember when Johnny first phoned me up. My exact words…‘you’re crazy mate!’ I mean, I had worked with the man almost two years before, so I knew there had to be some method to his madness.” The show first aired while Hitchin and King were tag team champions in Dark World Alliance wrestling (DWA). Combined together as “Steel Thunder,” the two men were DWA’s first tag team champions. Balancing their television success with their in-ring success, they seemed unstoppable. Munny McMahon – “I’ve had a long history with King, and I knew that being a mentor for John would pay off big time for both of them. When they won the tag titles in DWA, I knew that they were destined for much greater things.” Steel Thunder was on top of the federation, until a tag team, by the name of the Strike Force, took the tag titles, and almost took something much dearer to Hitchin. Hitchin – “Monday Night Manic…there’s an event I’ll never forget, the event that almost ended my career…” During the duration of the match, when Jeffery “Jman” DeJohn knocked John Hitchin onto his head it seemed that Hitchin’s wrestling career…was over. John Hitchin – “All I remember was that I was standing the ring apron, and Jeff dropkicked me. I was supposed to turn my body as I was flying through the air, and land through a table. Well…let’s just say I flew too far, and I ended up hitting my legs on the table, and my head and neck on the floor below.” Jeff DeJohn – “When I saw him flying off of the ring apron, all I really thought to myself is ‘Wow, he’s really selling this one well.’ Well…I saw his head hit the floor mats…and my heart just stopped. He wasn’t moving. I was almost ready to call his fiancée…” “Big Bad” Brad Felker, a long-time friend of Hitchin and King, had seen what happened…and opted for the match to end quickly. King agreed as Brad and Jman became the tag team champions, and King attended to his friend. The emergency medical technicians arrived soon to the scene. Scott King – “When Hitch went down, there was almost a panic running through me. I mean, here we were…leading the way in the fed, nothing holding us back…and then…a brickwall.” Hitchin suffered from a broken neck, and would be sidelined for seven months. The legacy of Steel Thunder would be put on hold…for now… Moving on, we come to the year of 2002. John Hitchin – “2002 was a great year. I sat at home and recovered from my neck injury, all the while watching King rise to the challenge of the Outlaw Nation, and vie for that World Title.” Scott King – “Munny just didn’t want to give it up. Well…not until Hitchy came back.” Hitchin returned to action in DWA during the early months of the year. Scott King was starting to struggle, and Hitchin’s return…wouldn’t help him out any. [I] Archive Footage Mad Dawg Munn and Scott King are standing in the ring across from each other; Munn has the DWA World title on his shoulder. King looks poised ready to strike. But as the Outlaw Nation music starts up, he knew he was in for a long night. He just didn’t know how long. Announcer – “Introducing…the 3rd man in this triple threat match for the DWA World Title…he is part of the Outlaw Nation…John…HITTTTTTTTTTTCHIN!” King looks to the ramp and his jaw drops as his best friend comes walking down the aisle wearing an Outlaw Nation T-shirt. Munn just sits back and laughs. [/I] Scott King – “What a crazy night that was…I mean, I was so glad to see the return of John, but on the other hand…I wasn’t too impressed when he handed me my own ass.” John Hitchin – “There’s really nothing better, that I like doing, than winning a world title on your first night back after a career threatening injury. I could see the look on King’s face from the ramp…he wasn’t too happy. I didn’t just kick his ass though, Munn got a whoppin’ too.” John’s return would spark something between the Steel Thunder members, and it would almost look as if they were going to break up. The DWA feud for the world title continued to roll on, Hitchin and King put themselves through Hell. In May of 2002…DWA closed its doors for good. It would not be a long break for King and Hitchin however…NGW welcomed them with open arms. Within 2 months of being there, the two men had patched over their differences and won tag team gold. July 7th, 2002, was the start of the longest title reign in NGW history, as Steel Thunder held the tag team titles for an unprecedented 226 days. A record in NGW in both tag teams titles and singles titles. At the end of the title reign, something came over Hitchin…and he defeated Scott King for the other half of the tag titles…and then leaving NGW. Hitchin was not gone too long however…but Steel Thunder would be put on hold for the rest of the year… In 2003, we would see Steel Thunder’s legacy as a tag team, come to a grinding halt. Both of the men went separate ways, and had their own agendas. For Scott King…his agenda was clear…a World Title needed to be around his waist. It didn’t take long for King to get to his goal, as Scott King defeated long time friend Matt Douglass for the World Title at XtremeMania IV. A match in which the referee, Munny McMahon himself, tried to keep the title out of King’s hands. Scott King – “The World title win was sure something else. I mean, there I was in the ring with two men that I pretty much started the business with…both against me. To come out on top like that…it was just amazing.” King held the title until December, when Prometheus defeated him in an unsanctioned match, and put him on the shelf. John Hitchin’s agenda…wasn’t as clear as Scott King’s was. Early in the year, Hitchin defeated Big Daveylicious for the American Title, and then went on to go against Scottywood, NGW’s owner, in the Perfect Match. A match in which Hitchin said would be his last. It’s a match that Scott Woodson himself designed, so he had the unfair advantage…or did he. Coming into this match, Woodson was 0 and 2 against Hitchin…John had his number. Things didn’t change this match either. Hitchin went on to defeat Scottywood, and go out gracefully…or so we thought. John Hitchin – “It was an awesome match…I can’t really say I give props for Woodson’s athletic ability…but I do give props for the ass-whoppings that he keeps coming back to me for. But I’d have to say the spot of the night, was when I announced I was stay, as 50% owner of NGW…that was the bombshell right there.” So with Hitchin taking a backstage role in NGW, the ring was left without its other Steel Thunder member. Hitchin took a huge role upon himself, to getting NGW fully back on its feet and functional once again. His constant firings of superstars seemed to Scottywood, that it would kill NGW once and for all…but it did just the opposite, as NGW continues to thrive to this day. Scott Woodson – “All hatred for the man aside…he’s brilliant, and he knows what needs to be done…and how to do it. I give props to the man.” At the end of 2003, Scottywood became the NGW World Champion…and John Hitchin assumed full control of NGW. He only stayed in control a few short weeks…that’s all the time he needed, to ensure that his place in NGW history would be solidified… Bringing us into 2004… It’s been a year to remember already for the Steel Thunder brethren. The one of the first events of the year, John Hitchin returned to the ring, and took on Reggie Rivid in a huge match for the Xtreme title…Hitchin came out on top. With a four win and no loss record, Hitchin is picking up right where he left off. John Hitchin – “What can I say…I love to come back in big ways…and what better way to come back, than to face NGW’s hardest working man…and kick his ass!” Scott King would not be outdone however…as the first pay-per-view of the year, CounterStrike, would become a memorable one for him. King would regain his World Title in a match against Scottywood. An event that would be 2 out of 3 falls to win it. This night saw the return of Time Bomb and the return of Dallas Reeves…but more importantly, it should the return of the triumph…of Steel Thunder. Scott King – “You could definitely call it a return of sorts for all kinds of things. I mean…the belt around my waist sure does feel good again. But Steel Thunder back together…on top of things…nothing feels better than that. It’s only up from here…well…not really…it’s only domination from here on in.” So what is next for Steel Thunder? John Hitchin – “Well…by the time this show airs…I think we’ll be set up for a match against 2 new guys into the fed. When I say we…I mean Scott and myself…the return of the Steel Thunder tag team you know? We’re going to use our legacy to get some of the new guys in the fed a chance to show us what they got.” Scott King – “What’s next for us? How about a good retirement fund? Nah, I think we’ll be putting a beat down onto some of the disrespectfuls in the NGW. So look out you guys…ha ha! This is the rebirth of Steel Thunder…no one is safe…and everyone is watching.” Source: NGW === 24 February 2004 GW EVOLUTION EVOLUTION HAS HIT THE AIRWAVES! The last evolution before HIGH OCTANE.
