Professional wrestling has come very far over the last several years. It has gone from a carnival side show to mainstream media. Pop culture has created a special place in its heart for pro wrestling that doesn't see going away anytime soon. We sat down with a national wrestling star to see what he thinks the sudden increase in draw to professional wrestling is. Luke Justice was the man we have chosen. In his career, he has seen it all. From barbed wire ropes to three stacked cages and scaffolding, this man has been around the block. When asked why he thinks professional wrestling has become so mainstream, he responded, "Well, Jimmy, the most basic thing is a lack of excitement in regular television. Everyone is looking to the more extreme for entertainment. What we do is the epitome of extreme. I love my job and there is nothing that would ever keep me from it. What people see when they look at me and the guys I work with in the ring is larger than life. We go out and take punishment that they only wish that they could endure. We are superhuman to most people. They watch us for something to look up to and something to strive for. Physical perfection and intense strength." He's right too. Ever since wrestling has become mainstream there has been an increased amount of people heading to the gym and increasing their size and stamina. So-called backyard feds have been popping up like crazy and the independent wrestling scene has been on fire. All of this spreading of independent and backyard federations brings us to the title of this article. How far is too far? Wrestling as an olympic sport is dangerous, broken bones and torn muscles, but is professional wrestling much more dangerous? "I don't know if I would say more dangerous, Jimmy. We train for hours on end every day of the week to keep ourselves loose and on the mark. We are trained on how to take falls and not get hurt, but it still happens. I don't really think that professional wrestling is more dangerous, but the impact of the moves makes the injuries more serious." Luke Justice should know about serious injuries. He has taken falls from scaffolding that is 60 feet in the air, and multiple shots to the head and other body parts with various items. "Well Jimmy, you don't get into this business for the money. If you get into this business for the money, you don't belong in this business. The world of professional wrestling is about being the best you can be. It's physical competition and it's mental competition, but most of all it's about entertaining the fans. If you aren't in the business to entertain, you don't belong. And as for the question of how far is too far? There is no such thing as too far when it comes to entertaining people. That's all there is to it." There you have it. There is no such thing as too far, according to Luke Justice. Shortly after my interview with the big man we received word that he was sidelined from his work in LOW after crashing his vehicle into a guard rail on the Jersey turnpike. Doctors say that Justice was not injured in the crash due to the airbag, but several old concussions caused him to pass out at the wheel. After looking at his recent matches, doctors have revoked Justice's medical clearance to give him time to heal up old wounds. Week after week of extremly intense matches have finally taken their toll on one superstar. Our thoughts and prayers go out to Luke Justice on a speedy recovery.
