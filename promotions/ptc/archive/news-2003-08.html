7 August 2003 [gcw] GCW added to PTC Public Access In breaking news, GCW has been added the public access of Primetime Central. When reached for comment Mike Mosall, GCW Commissioner, was thrilled at the news. “It’s absolutely fantastic for the GCW to be associated, in any fashion, with PTC” said Mosall. “We have always looked at PTC as something to aspire to. Now we are going to get that opportunity. Public Access is only the first step for GCW. We aspire to reach the full network after proving GCW’s value to the network as a whole. We have been doing this since 1998 and, as we like to say, WE got your history RIGHT HERE.” Welcome to the GCW! GCW can be found at: http://gcw.e-wrestling.org/ === 9 August 2003 [gcw] THE SPOTLIGHT ON WRESTLE BOWL V THE SPOTLIGHT ON WRESTLE BOWL V In a brief press conference early this morning Global Championship Wrestling’s President, Eric Grimm, made the final announcement on the final date for Wrestle Bowl V considered “The Granddaddy Of All Wrestling”. The President had this to say about the GCW’s most coveted event… Eric Grimm: “As many of you have known we have been keeping the actual set date of Wrestle Bowl a pretty close secret as we were going through some changes, and more so wanting to give the fans a taste of something new. Never has GCW kept from the fans this long the official date of Wrestle Bowl, and I am happy to say while the anxiety may have built into something awful for many, the pay off will be great, because Wrestle Bowl V is going to be something VERY special.” Grimm went on to explain how it (Wrestle Bowl V) could very well be the greatest show ever produced by GCW which would of course be a more than adequate challenge for the amazing Wrestle Bowls of the previous four years. He had nothing but good things to say as he went down the list of the final card being set until he came to the World Championship Match itself. He was asked a question regarding the strange turn about in the Taintking’s attitude towards things and he smiled and had only this to add in reply… Eric Grimm: “Taintking has reason to be worried, he holds a title everyone is gunning for and I wouldn’t be so surprised about the recent turn of events. Taintking is many things but stupid is not one of them, he may very well have gotten wind of something the rest of you have not. The World Title match is sure to blow everyone away.” The vague statement a heartbreaker to many reporters but comes as no surprise to many of Grimm’s long time fans who know it as a centerpiece to the way he does things. Once he had taken a few more questions he decided it was time an gave the announcement everyone had come for… Eric Grimm: “ Wrestle Bowl V will be in Atlanta, Georgia on the twenty-fourth of August 2003.” And with that the press conference ended, and the build up for WBV had begun. === 13 August 2003 UPWA: Old School with a New Twist Wrestling has a very different history from most sports. This sport has been around since man has fought man for that sweet piece of trout in the days of cavemen and homo-erectus. Yet, along the way, it went away from the mainstream, and became the theatre of the bizarre, or more simply put by my old man 'A bunch on a$$-hole Americans trying to make a sport out of this sh*t!' Throughout the 20th Centuary, wrestling was at it's most purest in the eighties, as hardcore wrestling (hardcore as in the purest of it's form) was one of the big forms of entertainment in the Alabama/Carolinas/Georgia/Tennessee region. Matches then would be based on ring psychology, a story which the match is built on, genuine emotions displayed by the wrestlers, and holds and moves that looked like they actually hurt. In the northen part of the United States, wrestling was more gimmicky, with characters like Millionaires, Macho people, Hulking Heroes, and people who liked Iran or Iraq. This went out through the regions of New York/Philadelphia/Boston. Wrestling in the Pacific region was more violent, with bloody battles of violence and brutality, while in the Chicago/Milwaukee/Minneapolis, family values were practised, as the top stars of the Region would lose to the World Champion, who happened to be married to the bosses daughter (This is still done today). But, back onto the main topic, wrestling only became popular because of the big star of the North East region, all the while taking out the challenge, competition and sportsmanship that the south had provided. Many true wrestling fans followed the southern product, while the Northern Product seemed to be the in thing, until it became an out thing. Today, wrestling is a big thing, on a more undergroundish scene, but a small amount of it is in the mainstream world. So, to have a successful promotion, which entertains and brings an element of competition, what are the main ingredients? Which aspect of wrestling? Psychology is a must, as a match without it is just a bunch of high spots with no direction, which leads to no crowd interest. Involving the crowd is a better tactic to gain crowds (duh, obviously), as a really intense, psychotic match turn fans away, as they aren't there to watch an artform; they are there to be entertained. And finally, how long a match. The shorter a match, the quicker you get onto the next one. These days, 3 minute matches rule the tv screens, but that really doesn't help the movement that the show needs. Big matches should deserve more time, while progressive matches need as much time availible and convinient for the storyline it's following. So, what does the UPWA encourage and produce on it's show? Well, Pay Per View shows tend to have old fashioned brawls, with less high spots and more technique, which is the same as out television product, although it does tend to have a lot of high spots, to justify a short match time. Backstage segments are generally just words between feuding wrestlers, and interviews are just short opinions of wrestlers about to fight, not long winded emotional blow ups, which deserve to have stayed on the psychologist's reclining chair. This generally tends to differ from the rest of the PTC's shows, like WWA's sports entertainment brand, and Plague's risky, hardhitting fighting federation. Instead, the UPWA goes for 'Old School...with a new twist'. Thank you, and goodnight. ~PJ London === 19 August 2003 UPWA Circus Of Pain: Live from Las Vegas! Every week, the show seems to get better, and things can only get better, now the UPWA is onto it's forth show. After last week's show, the were new champions all round, and a whole lot of confusion. This will all be settled, when the UPWA takes a trip to the west of the United States, going live from Las Vegas via PTC Public Access. All Matches have been announced, and they all carry their own value. For instance, take the opening bout, a 7 man battle royal, with pinfalls and over the top rope throw outs serving as means for elimination. The matches special stipulation is that the last two men in the ring will be the number one contenders to the UPWA Tag Team Gold, currently owned by Louis Royal and his latino lackey, Sanchoz. The Battle Royal contenders are Al Magz, LoboXcalibur, Chuck Douglas, Blacknight, Black Magic, Neo Dragon & Superdos, all who (With the exception of 2 to be left nameless) have a legitimate chance of winning the contest. The most legit however is Chuck Douglas, who last week defeated Kip Atlas in an all out brawl. This man from Bolton, England has recently found his calling as a hillbilly, and is now looking for some hardware. UPWA Newcomer from the Shootfighting world Gary Cohen will get former Champ Dr.Love in a steel cage match, where the only stipulations are submission and knock out. this is certainly going to be a brutal contest, and I think Dr.Love should certainly get prepared. Finally the Main Event Contest: Kip Atlas versus Storm J, the Heavyweight Champion. Storm is a willing competitor, and is likely to cause damage, but is also quite new to the workings of the UPWA. Kip Atlas on the other hand is a technical mastermind, and Vegas odds are on him taking the title on the 24th.
