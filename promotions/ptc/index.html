An interfed alliance of separate e-feds — UWF, GIWF, XUW and roughly two dozen others — framed as a fictional TV network, "PTC TV," with member feds as its channels. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

PTC wasn't a promotion in its own right so much as the hub every member fed plugged into — a shared universe layered on top of separate, independently-run e-feds, with its own shared titles pitched explicitly as side attractions beneath any member fed's own championship scene. Some of PTC's own member feds are documented promotions in their own right, connected as affiliations rather than lineage (a shared alliance is a connection, not a family history) — see Wings for the full member list.

What survives is the alliance's own public shape: its top title's partial lineage, its marquee tournaments, and a served run of its own documents — thirty months of its own news, from the January 2002 relaunch through July 2005, its standing FAQ and rulebook (two generations of each), its editorial columns, its Shopzone merchandise directory, dedicated profile pages for sixteen of its own network feds, and, in full, its own flagship supercard.

PTCpedia is the network's own encyclopedia — a MediaWiki the membership wrote about itself, at ptcpedia.primetimecentral.net and later under community.primetimecentral.net/ptcpedia/. 322 of its articles are held: member federations, their titles and tournaments, and the characters who worked them, captured between January 2008 and August 2010. It is a secondary source, written in-community and years into the run, and it reads best against the network's own contemporary pages rather than over them.

PTC Intervention (16 Apr 2002) is the alliance's own fullest surviving broadcast — a card fought across roughly two dozen member feds at once, called by Nate Dumas, Jeff Marx, and Mr. Batee. Its LA Takedown tag gauntlet is where the Phenomenons, mid-UWF tenure, make their one confirmed PTC-stage cameo, eliminated early alongside Suburban Uprising and Civil Disobedience.

The PTC Global Championship was the alliance's top prize, defended in an open 128-competitor tournament (eighty signed in the first seventy-two hours). Its partial lineage runs through Vampir Nosferatu (PCW), a DWO superstar, Chris Storm (RPW), and Ignatius Lisieux (PRIME), who “defeated the dreaded Global Title Curse” just to earn his shot. Seymour Almasy won his own bracket in the Golden Turnbuckle Tourney outright, and it is in this same tournament tree that Jay Phoenix reached the Global title final and lost the judged decision by three points — the closest the tournament's own bracket ever put him to the biggest prize the whole alliance offered.

Affiliations

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004)

Operates PTC (2002) (as of the Feb 2002 relaunch) — User attestation, 2026: true, but only initially — PTC later went independent of PCW's operation. Consistent with (but not fixed to a date by) the corpus's own PTC Global Title lineage, which names no PCW-affiliated champion after the Feb 2002 relaunch (DWO by mid-2003, RPW/Chris Storm Jan 2004, PRIME Apr 2004) — no exact split date survives in ptc-site.md or elsewhere in the corpus. (user-attested)
PCW is PTC’s founding host — PTC’s own site calls PCW “host federation” outright, PCW’s forums were hosted directly on PTC’s Ikonboard, and PTC’s recurring cast (Batee, Duke Williams, The Phantom Booker, Tyler Nelson) all have their day jobs at PCW. In tension with this: PTC’s own FAQ explicitly excludes PCW from its official “Channels”/rankings/network tier even while hosting PCW’s boards — two different senses of “member” coexisted in the alliance’s own writing.

Old School Wrestling (OSW, Easton, 2002)

Affiliate of PTC (2002)
OSW’s own site nav links Primetime Central/PTC on every page, and in-fiction dialogue groups OSW roster alongside PCW and DRWF wrestlers as co-equal “PTC members” (“fellow PTC members Jack Hendrix of DRWF and Miyagawa of PCW”) — but PTC’s own FAQ explicitly excludes OSW (alongside PCW, NTICW, RUSH, XWA, PWA) from its formal “Channels”/rankings/network tier even while hosting OSW’s own board on its Ikonboard. Two senses of “PTC member” coexisted: an administrative tier that formally excluded OSW, and a looser narrative one OSW’s own writers used freely. Off camera, OSW and PCW (along with several sister promotions) were also run by the same real-world operator, who isn’t named here.

New Frontier Wrestling (NFW)

Affiliate of PTC (2002) (as of 7 February 2013)
Declared from both sides: NFW’s own affiliates page corroborates a banner found on PTC’s own forum pages that first surfaced the connection.

Chris Williams Memorial Cup

Crossover booking with PTC (2002) (2003; 2007)
PTC hosted the tournament's only fully-held staging, 2007, run in Flagstaff, Arizona as "the first ever PTC Indy Event" across six of its own show rows, decided for The Illustrious Face-Eater over Digital Mortality. PTC's own news separately reports the Cup's one other dated winner, Saij, in 2003.

Shared ownership

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004), Old School Wrestling (OSW, Easton, 2002), Puroresu Ultimate Ring Entertainment (PURE), Canuck Heat Zone (CHZ), Primetime Wrestling Academy (PWA, Primetime, 2000–2003), Pro Wrestling Circuit (PWC, Pro Wrestling Circuit, 2003) (2003)
A genuine out-of-fiction copyright notice — separate from any in-fiction alliance storyline — names one real-world operator behind a dozen promotions, including PCW and PTC, plus PCW2, PWA, Neutral Zone Wrestling, OSW, NTICW, WrestlingTattler, RUSH, Canuck Heat Zone, PURE, and the Primetime Indy Circuit. These were genuine sister sites under one owner, not just an in-fiction alliance; the operator isn’t named here.

Members

Every member promotion this alliance's own record names.

Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling (XUW)

Member
Confirmed repeatedly from XUW’s own side: DJ’s interview states outright “We’re the only PTC fed to have a developmental fed at this stage,” treating “PTC fed” as a category XUW belongs to; the PTC Global Championship was defended at an XUW PPV (Reborn); Sidhe Nimh’s interview references entering a PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. Independently corroborated from PTC’s own side: a 2008 PTCPedia wiki carries live XUW articles.

Global Wrestling Federation (GWF)

Member (as of Apr 2002)
One of six named channels on PTC’s own April 2002 “PTC TV” channel map — the clearest single snapshot of PTC’s formal member-fed structure to survive.

The Wrestling Alliance (WWA)

Member (as of Apr 2002)
Channel 6, “West Coast Network,” on PTC’s April 2002 channel map. Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed.

Cyberspace Championship Wrestling (CCW, 1998–2005)

Member (as of Apr 2002; New Era 20 Apr 2003)
Channel 8, “International Sports Network,” on PTC’s April 2002 channel map. Opened 1998; ran through at least five named presidential successions. PTC’s own profile page for CCW confirms its founding (“Open Since: March 1998”) but leaves its “Member of PTC Since” field blank — no precise join date survives. CCW also sent a delegation, including Ace Hawk, to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, at ccw.wrestleonline.org — a site that consistently calls itself Cyberspace Championship Wrestling, though the New Era supercard’s own program billed the delegation as “Classic Championship Wrestling.” CCW’s own writing settles the expansion its own way: “Cyberspace Championship Wrestling” appears throughout it, “Classic Championship Wrestling” nowhere, and PTC’s own member-fed profile page for CCW opens with the words “Cyberspace Championship Wrestling.” Both readings are recorded; the fed’s own usage is the one adopted.

Big Money Wrestling (BMW)

Member (as of Apr 2002)
Channel 9, "ACCESS Network," on PTC's April 2002 channel map. Opened 2001.

LCW Mercury

Member (as of Apr 2002)
Channel 10, "Great Lakes Television," on PTC's April 2002 channel map. Roleplay-based half of a split LCW (the other half, LCW Evo, is angle-based) — both trace back through EHW → GHW and the defunct MMWF.

Furiously Addictive Wrestling (FAW, 2001)

Member (Member of PTC Since: February 2002 (PTC's own profile page for FAW))
Channel 11, “EEL Network,” on PTC’s April 2002 channel map. Opened Jan 2001; spun off a training promotion, FAWA. Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed. PTC’s own president had rescinded an earlier offer to admit FAW on 8 October 2001, over an EWC front-page incident during the PTC's own president affair (“we are rescinding our offer to allow FAW into PTC”). FAW’s eventual February 2002 membership postdates this rescission — the sequence runs offered, rescinded, then joined.

LCW Evo

Member (Member of PTC Since: February 2002 (PTC's own profile page for LCW Evo))
Channel 12, "The Wrestling Channel," on PTC's April 2002 channel map. Angle-based half of the LCW split (co-owners Edward Olmstead and Gravity, née "LCW neo").

PRIME

Member
Ignatius Lisieux’s fed, already known from the PTC Global Championship’s own lineage as the promotion that “defeated the dreaded Global Title Curse.” Co-owner Toshiaki Motoki, shareholder Ian English, and “Gods Champion” Hoyt Williams are also named in PTC’s own record.

Universal Wrestling Federation (UWF, 2001–07)

Member (as of 31 Mar 2002) — The UWF's own news archive (uwf.org.uk) dates the alliance precisely: "This morning, the UWF's negotiations with Primetime Central... finally came to a close... we happily joined the PTC family" (31 Mar 2002), with Commissioner Lew Spectre quoted on the terms.
UWF’s own affiliates/link-exchange pages banner-link Primetime Central (“The World’s #1 Interfed” / “Spiffy Interfed of Horror!”) — direct confirmation of UWF’s PTC membership, previously known only through storyline (Almasy’s Golden Turnbuckle Tournament run, Jay Phoenix’s PTC Global Championship reigns). UWF sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed. The event’s own site was even uploaded to and served from UWF’s own domain, ftp.uwfonline.com/httpdocs/i-ppv — a member fed hosting the alliance’s own event site.

Lights Out Wrestling (LOW)

Member (as of 20 Apr 2003)
Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed — the single largest delegation on the card. The fed’s own site ran across two sequential domains, lightsoutwrestling.net and lowonline.net, confirmed as the same promotion’s two hosting eras.

Xtreme Wrestling Federation (XWF, 2003)

Member (as of 20 Apr 2003)
Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed. Two other, unrelated promotions have also used the initials XWF.

Dominant Wrestling On-Line (DWO, 2001)

The DWO’s own front page of 7 August 2003 announces it has “decided to break ties with Primetime Central.” PTC’s own owner list names the fed’s owner as Callisto Bane (“true owner”), with SurReal as a returning owner-by-proxy, and PTC’s own championship history carries an unresolved “a DWO superstar” PTC Global Championship reign around mid-2003. DWO also sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed.

Underground Brawler's Club (UBC, Underground Brawler's Club, 2002–2003)

Member (2002–2003)
Its own Rules page lets a fighter "represent the UBC in any type of PTC tournament" after two matches, its Payouts page pays an executive bonus for "Peforming in other matches, such as in PTC, representing the UBC", and its news of 10 December 2002 calls its roster "the Brawler's of PTC"; Primetime Central's own wiki lists Underground Brawlers Club among the network's past federations, in the navigation box of its article on another federation, captured 19 August 2010.

RUSH Pro Wrestling (RPW, RUSH Pro Wrestling, 2003–2004)

Member (2003–2004)
Primetime Central's own news archive carries its news under its own byline ('[RPW]', 'Source: RPW') from 18 December 2003, and its write-up of 21 March 2004 has its Mid South Champion Chris Storm carrying 'the PTC Global title on his right' shoulder; Primetime Central's own Global title lineage names him 'Chris Storm (RPW)', and its own wiki calls the federation one of 'a larger group of companies linked for a singular purpose'.

Atlantic Wrestling Club (AWC, Atlantic Wrestling Club, 2005–2006)

Member (2005–2006)
Its own shows place it inside Primetime Central: "You can either re-sign with AWC, or try your luck elsewhere in PTC" (Fresh!, 19 September 2006), and "GBJ now has the first spot in GTT6", the network's own tournament (Fresh!, 12 September 2006). The Primetime Central wiki's entries for it, captured 4 December 2008 and 19 August 2010, read "Atlantic Wrestling Club was an e-wrestling promotion with membership in the Primetime Central network".

RUSH Wrestling

Member (2002)
Primetime Central's own wiki article on RUSH opens “RUSH Wrestling was a federation that was part of the former PTC”, and its navigation box lists RUSH among Primetime Central's past federations (captures of 7 May 2008 and 19 August 2010); RUSH's own Rules page states the same family in its own words, “RUSH Wrestling is a member of the WrestlingTattler/Primetime Central family”. Against that, PTC's own FAQ of 3 June 2002 answers whether PCW, OSW, NTICW, RUSH, XWA or PWA will be part of PTC with “for the most part the answer is NO”, keeping them out of its Channels and rankings, so the membership is the looser one the wiki and RUSH's own page describe, not a place in the network's formal tier.

Animosity Wrestling (AW, Animosity, 2002)

Member (2002)
Primetime Central's own news carried its bulletins and profiles under the -AW- banner from 22 September 2002, reporting that its owner would do everything it takes to bring Animosity Wrestling to the top of PTC, that the owners of DWO, WIW and EPW were already in close relations with it, and that its Director of News would promote it through PTC News; PTC's channel line-up in a later capture lists it as Channel 10, Featuring AW.

Excite Pro Wrestling (EPW, Excite, 2002)

Member (2002–2003)
Primetime Central's own front page of 27 January 2002 lists a wrestler of EPW among the entrants to PTC's Global Title Contenders Tourney and its ratings for 28 January to 3 February 2002 list EPW; from April 2002 the fed's cards and bulletins are posted to PTC's news engine under "Source: Excite Pro Wrestling", and its profiles of January and February 2003 are posted there under the ~EPW~ banner. PTC's channel line-up in a later capture lists it as Channel 7, Featuring EPW.
