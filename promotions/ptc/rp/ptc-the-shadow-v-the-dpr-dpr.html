The Shadow (XUW) v. The DPR (RPW)

22 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
22 March 2004 Julie had never before met a man so handsome and beguiling. Seated in her receptionist's chair in the small Absolute Fitness gym, she scolded herself for even thinking about him. She knew she was beneath his notice. He was so suave and powerful, so charismatic, caring, and thoughtful. So talented. So famous. Whenever he walked into a room even the lights seemed to swoon and sigh before brightening in his presence. He was like a king, and she just a lowly serf forever denied his true attentions through the difference in their stations. Still she couldn't help imagining herself in his loving embrace, wrapped up in the delightful poetry she was sure he spouted as easily as other men grunted and swore. Anything more than the professional greeting he gave her whenever he showed up for his workouts would be rapture. She ached for his touch, for his soft breath against her ear. She knew all women must. Therein lay the problem; with all of the women he met undoubtedly throwing themselves at his feet, how could he notice a pathetic wretch such as herself? She sighed and steeled herself against the tears threatening to erupt. He would be there soon and she didn't want him to see her cry. What would he think of her then, blubbering and carrying on like a schoolgirl? She didn't even want to think of the disappointment that would well up in his beautiful eyes. But then . . . What if it made him concerned for her? What if he swept her up in his powerful arms and asked her what was wrong, told her not to worry? A shudder ran through her body at the image of it massaging her mind. Taking a deep breath to calm herself, she looked around to make sure no one had noticed. No one had. No one ever noticed anything about her. Very few people even bothered saying hello to her when they arrived. Except him. He was such a gentleman. What would he do if she just threw herself at his feet? She let her imagination run wild as a silly grin stole onto her face. She knew it was just flightless fancy and that a man so important would never ravage her in such a way, but hey, if she couldn't have it in truth at least she still had her daydreams. And her other dreams, at night, alone in her large bed. Yes, he was undoubtedly the man of her dreams. And who could blame her? Any woman would kill for such a man. Her heart nearly leapt up out of her throat when she heard his voice outside, good-naturedly rebuking some careless passerby who wasn't watching where he were going. "Get out of the way, wretch!" She could hear his contained ire and it infected her for a moment. How dare some mindless oaf get in his way? She thought of running outside to help him belittle the thoughtless ruffian, but before she could bring her legs to move he was sweeping through the doorway. She sat ramrod straight behind her desk, smiling up at him, her mind going in a thousand directions at once, all jumbled into a hopeless mass of things she wished she could say but would never have the courage to. "Can you believe the nerve of some people?" he asked as he spotted her and began striding purposefully over, the dashing smirk on his face sending her heart aflutter. He looked as handsome as ever. His hair was neatly combed, and he was already wearing the form-fitting shorts and shirt that would in no time be drenched with his blessed sweat. It took all her effort to keep her eyes schooled on his. She felt helpless in the crosshairs of that intelligent gaze. Gesturing toward the door, he continued, "A man can't go five feet without some dirty little urchin demanding some of his hard-earned money." She nodded frantically before he could possibly think she'd take the little devil child's side. The man screwed up his face and made his voice ripe with mocking scorn. "But mister, please, I'm hungry." He shook his head ruefully before returning to his regular voice. "Bah! Of course the little brat is hungry! I'd be hungry too if I was a lazy little bastard who sat around on my arse all day refusing to work! Same as you, right, Judie?" "J-Julie," she stammered, crushed that he had forgotten her name. "Huh?" Brow furrowed, lips drawn down in a frown, he looked to both sides before returning his gaze to her, obviously incredulous that someone as low as her would think to correct him. She wanted to die of shame. "Uh, right . . . Julie." He shrugged in apology and leaned over the counter. "Is my friend here yet?" "Yes sir, Mr. Storm," she said, nervously rummaging through papers on her desk as if the answer was written somewhere among them. "That silly pirate is waiting for you in the aerobics room." Storm rapped his knuckles on the desk and gave her the double-gun salute before turning around and walking away. "Thanks," he called over his shoulder without looking back, "you've been a doll, Judie." Julie sighed. "Roberts! Roberts!" Storm glared around the empty expanse of the room with his hands planted firmly on his hips. Thin mats covered every inch of the floor and some were even piled against the wall to one side. Floor-to-ceiling mirrors on every wall had Storm glowering back at himself from all different angles. A chin-up bar hung down from the twelve foot ceiling, as did a pair of ropes with circlets dangling from their ends. Well, the circlets would have been dangling, at least, if one of the mats hadn't been folded and tied between them as a makeshift hammock. Within this resourceful setup The Dread Pirate Roberts stirred at the sound of his name being called. Stretching, yawning, and somehow even sighing all at the same time, he poked his head up over the side and arched an eyebrow at the raging Storm. The mat teetered precariously with his movement, threatening to spill him to the floor. "There you are!" Storm fumed. "What the hell are you doing up there -- sleeping?!" The black mask disappeared down between the sides of the mat once again, then the whole hammock swung around, dumping The DPR to the floor. He landed on his feet and immediately dipped into an elaborate bow, spreading his arms wide. Sheathed in his suit of black silk, his scabbard swept behind him, he flashed Storm a mischievous grin. "Well I was," he answered smoothly, "until you started yelling." The Global Champion stormed across the room waving an angry finger. "There's gonna be a lot more yelling if you don't smarten up! You do remember that you're wrestling your first match this week, don't you?" Straightening, Roberts waved a dismissive gloved hand. "That's not until Sunday." "Sunday's only a few days away," Storm growled. "More like a week." Now directly in front of Roberts, Storm struggled to contain his frustration. "And you think that's enough time to whip you into shape to face the XUW World Champion? Yesterday you couldn't even master a headlock takedown! A freakin' headlock takedown!" Storm called up a mental image of him suplexing God in order to calm himself, but it did no good. "Today you're sleeping!" The Man in Black narrowed his eyes and cocked his head. "What was that about the XUW World Champion?" "The Shadow. Your opponent." Biting off each word, Storm began trembling with ire as The DPR continued to stare at him blankly. "The Shadow is one half of the XUW World Champion!" "He's only half a man?" Roberts asked, suddenly grinning again. "This will be easier than I thought." "No, you idiot. He and another wrestler, some fat guy, are joint Champions. They each hold half the Championship!" "So?" "So they're both World Champion!" "No, they're both half of a World Champion." Seeing that Storm was about to erupt again, The DPR placed a comforting hand on his shoulder. "Don't you see, Chris? A champion would be something to cause worry in a wrestling match, just as a man with wit would be something to cause worry in an argument . . . but a half-champion? Think about it. Would you be worried by a half-wit?" Remembering who he was speaking to, The DPR amended, "Do you think I would be worried by a half-wit?" Shaking the hand off of him, Storm threw both of his up and began pacing. "You're freakin' doomed! Doomed!" "Come now," Roberts scoffed, "it can't be so bad as that." "It is!" Storm cried, still pacing. "You're up against The Shadow, a World Champion, and you've never even wrestled a match! You're doomed!" "It may be true that I've never wrestled a match," The DPR allowed, hustling over to pace alongside Storm, which earned him a glare, "but I'm far from doomed. I'm being trained by you, the PTC Global Champion, RPW Mid-South Champion, and dare I say, The Last Great Technical Wrestler. Do you think this Shadow fellow had such an excellent tutor? With you in my corner, how can I lose? I'd say he's the one who's doomed." Storm paused, considering. Rubbing his chin, he donned a half-smile. "Well, yeah, I suppose there's that. There's no way The Shadow had a teacher like me." He was coming around, but still didn't sound convinced. "See, there you go." Roberts' grin finally took hold on Storm and they both laughed. "Aren't you the one always saying you could train a monkey to become RPW World Champion?" Smiling, Storm nodded and shrugged. "And isn't RPW better than XUW?" More nodding. "So then this match is already won . . . unless you think a monkey has a better shot than I do." Roberts laughed again. Storm didn't join in. He blinked, then adopted an angry, hopeless scowl. Throwing his arms up and pacing again, he roared, "You're doomed!" Dim candlelight washed the small room in a flickering yellow glow. Bare-chested yet still wearing his mask, The Dread Pirate Roberts lay at the focus of the four small candles arranged in a semi-circle behind him. Face-down on the cold cement, his panting breath condensed to form a wet round spot in front of his mouth. Sweat drenched every inch of his body and made a considerable puddle beneath him. Steam rose from his back. Storm had practiced with him deep into the night, intent on molding him into a wrestler of consequence. He lacked confidence that it would ever work, but had agreed to meet Roberts again the next day to continue the training. Roberts couldn't help but think that was extremely generous of the Global Champion, even if he was only doing it because of a bet they had made. Storm said he didn't hold out much hope for The DPR ever becoming a champion like him, but Roberts didn't take the discouraging words seriously. That was just Storm's way. Still, Roberts wanted to win. He loved the fight, loved the battle, but he loved victory even more. He didn't know if he had what it would take to defeat The Shadow, a decorated wrestler of the highest esteem, but it was for that exact reason he had challenged him. "We triumph without glory when we conquer without danger," Roberts quoted under his breath, then forced out another agonizing push-up. He knew that winning wasn't everything. The desire to put up a good fight, the need to give it all you had, the want of victory was. When he finally straightened his arms he wanted to just crash to the ground, but he lowered himself slowly, counting backwards from five, ignoring the flames searing his already numb and burning muscles. A mumbled cry breached his lips as he settled on the ground once more, clamping his eyes tight and turning his head to the side. He would not lose this fight for lack of want to win. He would not lose this fight for lack of preparation. He would win this fight and all others set before him. Behind him, from the darkness, a shadow began to emerge. It stepped carefully into the light, testing the air with its many tentacles, slipping up the wall and growing ever bigger and more dangerous. It could hear Roberts panting; it could smell his exhaustion; it was drawn to his fear like a hound to blood. A monstrous thing of many legs and arms, it rose up on the wall in front of him and began ghosting over to where he lay. It promised destruction and doom. It vowed to lay waste to the unfortunate pirate, to make him suffer like he had never suffered before. Inching along the wall, sucking away the hope and light, it threatened to devour his entire being. It had no mercy. It felt no remorse. It wanted only to punish and consume. It was the same cold day, in the same rundown gym, with Storm and Roberts running through the same tired motions. They had been at it since nine that morning, 14 hours before, breaking only when Storm saw fit to rant about something (which happened more than you'd think . . . even if you know Storm) and once during the afternoon for a hasty five-minute lunch. Storm had scowled at Roberts the entire time as he scarfed down his two tuna sandwiches, angrily muttering about how eating was for pussies. The Man in Black couldn't fathom how the man lived with himself, but said nothing. He was still swallowing the last bite when Storm suddenly seized him around the chest and without a word suplexed him on his head. After Roberts had coughed up the half-chewed food, he asked what Storm's problem was, only to be told that the Global Champion was tired of his "dilly-dallying". Before he could ask what "dilly-dallying" was, Storm's foot was smushing his face to the mat as his arms mercilessly twisted one of Roberts' into a pretzel. Roberts didn't cry out, and Storm didn't relent. The excruciating hold was maintained until Roberts devised a way to get out of it, and as soon as he did, The Last Great Technical Wrestler cinched in another, over and over again, each more devious than the last. Between lectures, that was how they had spent their long day. "What the hell are you doing?" Storm repeated, apparently on the brink of another lecture. His wet clothes clung to him like a second skin, drenched with sweat, and drops of it ran down from his matted hair to fall from his chin with the steady consistency of a leaky faucet. He was panting, but only barely. His fists seemed to be rooted to his hips. At his feet, curled into a fetal position, was The Dread Pirate Roberts. He was in the same sweaty state as Storm, but his panting came in heavier, choking rasps. Through the holes of his mask his eyes stared blankly, almost lifelessly, as if searching the world beyond for a place to rest since he would find none here. The mirrors encircling them were covered with the fog of their exertion and the air itself was rank with the stench of their sweat. When Roberts had gone to open the door to allow some fresh air into the room, Storm only laughed and suplexed him from behind. Even if he hadn't exhausted himself to failure, Roberts knew he would have trouble breathing the thick, stagnant air. Rolling his head weakly to the side to regard Storm, he answered with a single anguished word: "Resting." "Resting?" Storm spat, scrunching up his face as if tasting the concept and finding it most disagreeable. "Resting?" He made it sound as if Roberts had said "raping your daughter." Angered beyond words, Storm bent down, twisted one of Roberts' arms up behind his back, pulled the other across his throat, then scissor-clipped his head and rolled backwards. Roberts grunted at the sudden pain scouring his body. "Is that going to be your excuse after The Shadow pins you in our home, on Adrenaline?" Storm demanded, exerting unconscionable pressure. "That you were resting?" The Man in Black wanted to answer, to tell him of course not, that he was going to win, but Storm's thick legs were crushing his own arm against his Adam's apple, making it impossible to speak. Helpless to do anything of substance, Roberts futilely tried to wriggle his way free. He could feel his pulse thundering at the base of his skull, feeding torment to the already painful bruise swelled there by the treacherous wrench of Jason O'Neil. "Resting is the same as giving up," Storm roared as he reared back to increase the pressure. "Is that what you're doing? Giving up?!" Roberts could no longer draw breath. He could feel consciousness slowly slipping away from him, deserting him and the pain that staying awake meant. In a very real way the coming darkness was welcome to him. All he had to do was lay back and let it wash over him and the torture would be over. The aches would be gone, the taste of blood in his mouth would be gone, the anguish would be gone, and most importantly, the grating, taunting voice of his teacher would be gone. "Are you giving up?" The words, despite their tone, were tantalizing. Roberts wanted to do nothing more than surrender and have it over with. He couldn't take the pain. Blackness washed over him. He was finished. "Are you givYAH--" Storm hadn't been expecting The Man in Black's teeth to clamp down on the tender flesh behind his knee. Distracted by the sudden crippling hurt, Storm relaxed his hold, giving Roberts the momentary leverage he needed to flex his arms and roll onto his stomach, curling his knees up under him as he did. Staggering under the weight and his tenuous hold on his wakeful state, Roberts managed to get to his feet with Storm still attached to his head and shoulders. Simply standing was almost more than he could bear. With Storm's weight added to the mix it was only through superhuman will that his knees didn't buckle. He didn't have to abide the pressure long; kicking his feet out to the side, he let Storm's head crash to the mat first, followed by the full weight of his body and the force of The Man in Black's. Letting out another cry, Storm released him, clamping both of his hands to the back of his knee. The Dread Pirate Roberts was on his feet in a flash, crouched toward Storm in case the man tried to kill him again. Chris slowly rose to his feet, smiling and nodding genuinely for the first time all day. "I was wondering when you'd think to use your teeth," Storm chuckled, wiggling his bitten leg as if to shake off the pain. "You were strangling me," Roberts panted, still on guard, "you could have killed me." Storm snorted. "I only would have knocked you out." His face suddenly grew gravely serious. "The Shadow might not be so generous when you face him. Remember that. Remember to use every weapon at your disposal." The air rang with the distinct sound of steel grating on steel as Roberts drew his sword. Before Storm had time to gape, Roberts deftly sliced the outline a circle in the shirt around Storm's heart, then stabbed through the middle of it. The wet circle of cotton stuck to the end of the blade as he withdrew it and held it between them. The whole display had taken less than two seconds. Storm's exposed pink skin boasted not a single scratch. "I doubt it will come to that," Roberts declared casually, regarding the point of his blade. "As you can see, I've already mastered other weapons." Storm frowned at the sword and folded his arms over his chest. "What are you going to do with that?" "It's an extreme match." The Man in Black shrugged nonchalantly. "I figured I'd get a little extreme." Storm waved an agitated hand at the sword as a look of disappointment marred his face. "You think your sword is extreme?" "It can be." "Let me tell you something," Chris said, moving past the sword to stand directly in front of Roberts. "The entire time I was WIW Hardcore Champion I never once used a weapon. These fools you hear spouting off about how they're rough and tough because they can wield a pipe or a wrench are just that: fools. Being hardcore has nothing to do with weapons. Your sword isn't important." Holding The Man in Black's gaze, he patted the sword. "Extreme isn't here." He swung his hand around to Roberts' chest and jabbed a finger into the heavy muscle on the left side. "Extreme is here. It isn't about using weapons, or liking pain, it's about conquering both. It's about never giving up. Using weapons and refusing to admit you're afraid of pain doesn't make you tough. It doesn't make you extreme. It makes you weak and delusional." He tapped Roberts' chest again. "This is where true strength lies." Another punctuating stab with his finger. "This is what makes you extreme." You are a cold, bitter man who holds the wrong things dear and expects to counter his inadequacy with primal boasting and self-defeating denial. Long ago, you were the unsoiled child of potential. It seemed you had but to flex the muscles of your arm and your fingers would curl deservedly around the world and everything in it. There was no goal beyond your reach, no obstacle you couldn't overcome, no pursuit outside the realm of your ability. Any path you would have chosen and committed to fully would have delivered you to a happy life free from turmoil and anguish. You could have had as much as any man could ever hope for, more than most men could dream of obtaining, but you squandered the opportunity. You tried to seize it all at once, to follow every dream with a singular mind, and in the end denied yourself the completion of each and every one. Stretching yourself in a hundred different directions, wearing yourself perilously thin, you lacked the dedicated singularity to conquer any of your conquests. You were so close to attaining them all yet at the same time a misspent lifetime away. Each new failure plunged you deeper into your despair, eventually forcing you to project the fault out at whatever was near or convenient. You could never come to terms with the truth of the battle; you could never accept the limitations of your power. Every insignificant failure became a mountain crushing your shoulders and your pride, and the more you struggled to shift the weight the more it bore down upon you. You became an outcast. With shades of your being in every possible pursuit but your roots settled in none, you had no one to identify or sympathize with. People appeared and professed to want to help you, to guide you, but you saw them for what they truly were and scorned their veiled attempts to stifle your greatness. As a release for your pent-up frustration you embraced violence. Your life had been a rollercoaster ride of pain and agony, scary yet somehow tantalizing, and you wanted to deliver your unique thrill to anyone close at hand. You wanted people to empathize with your internal torture, but since they could never come to terms with your unique genius, you benevolently gave them the next best thing. You tried to bring people closer to you through the only endeavour that has ever granted you even marginal fulfillment: pain. Eventually you found others like you, misfits without a home or a cause. You banded together and lashed out at the rest of the world for their cold refusal to embrace and understand the internal workings of your nature. Being part of that allegiance was the first and only time you ever felt that you had belonged somewhere. Through your excursions you amassed a bit of wealth and a sense of purpose, a feeling of accomplishment. The community frowned at the shades of your trade, but you persevered. In your own way you had become a success. But then, when it seemed you were on top of the world, it all came crashing down. All of your ill-begotten goods were unjustly stolen away from you, and though you again found yourself surrounded by others like you, in a very forlorn way you were once again horribly alone. Society once again locked you away from them, this time vowing to throw away the key. Over time you came to understand that you are where you belong: with nothing to call your own, with nobody to love or be loved by, irrevocably and terminally alone. You could probably still save yourself if you'd dedicate your efforts to the cause, but again you branch out in all directions, assigning yourself a fatalistic ultimatum of everything or nothing. You lacked the integrity to stay the course. You retreated into an eternal cocoon of fear and loathing. The more things change the more things stay the same. You are a bitter man. Not only have you isolated yourself from all others, but now you're a vile creature even in your own eyes. You cannot stand the sight of your reflection. You want nothing more than to explode our of your own skin. You need a release. You need to wash away your failures in the blood of an innocent. You need to make someone share your pain so you can feel your empty smile once again. You need to lose yourself in the pain and torment of another. You are a criminal. You are a mere shadow of a man. The Shadow towered above the fallen form of The Dread Pirate Roberts, lying on his stomach in the cold basement of his home. His arms were splayed helplessly to his sides, as if in surrender -- or to pay homage -- to the beast looming over him. Discerning blue eyes were walled away behind clenched eyelids, preventing him from discovering the imminent danger he was in. Sprawled on the floor, Roberts' greatest concern of the moment was gathering his breath. The dark form inched inexorably toward him along the wall, feeling every which way with its numerous arms, as if tasting the air for the scent of fear it craved, the fear that would sustain its power. The threat was as ominous as it was hidden, sneaking along with the stealth of thought. It had no conscience of which it or its victims were aware, no governing body of morals or ideals that made it accountable beyond its own whims; it lived simply to conquer and consume, to feed on the ignorant and unwary. Without sound, without warning, it stalked its next victim with an assured calm. It had only to touch him for a brief instant and it could seize his heart in its paralysing grip. It had only to sow the seed and the battle was over. If the victim didn't recognize what was happening before it was too late, all the better. The Man in Black opened his eyes and saw it just as it was about to descend on him, its intangible tentacles stretching to infiltrate his mind. The immediate shock of it overwhelmed him and he barely stifled the fearful gasp that welled up in his throat. Every muscle in his body tensed. Panic threatened to take him. Then it was past. He sighed and let his body relax, then a silly grin broke over his face. He rebuked himself for a fool, then laughed at the absurdity of what he had almost given in to. The Shadow recoiled, curling into a defensive ball, at the sound of his vibrant laughter echoing through the small room. The Shadow had lost its edge. Without fear to exaggerate its menace, the pirate was at small risk of succumbing to its proposed dominance. Through the goggles of fear The Shadow was truly a nightmare to behold; through sober, confident eyes it became so much less. In the brilliant light of truth it was not so much a threat as a curiosity. Rising to his knees, Roberts conjured a dizzying kaleidoscope of shadows onto all the walls of the room, each blending with the others in varying severity, cast from the multiple candles spread across the floor. The Shadow, alone so dark and foreboding, was lost and indistinguishable in the humbling grandeur of their scope. Roberts hadn't forgotten about it, though. He chuckled at the heart-attack its creator had almost given him, then turned and scooped the minuscule spider up from the lip of one of the candles. It struggled furiously in his open palm, indignant at being scooped up so easily, and The Man in Black couldn't help but marvel at the voracity of the would-be monster. "You cast a mean shadow, my little friend," Roberts whispered conspiratorially as he raised it up to his face, turning his hand so he could continue regarding it as it hurriedly scurried in search of an escape, "but you need to stay away from the light. We wouldn't want you to get burned." Smiling at the courageous trooper indulging his fancy, Roberts set his hand to the ground until it retreated back to the stone floor, then watched with palpable amusement as it seemed to turn and shake an impotent fist at him before skittering away under the door. Strange that something so small and inconsequential could manifest itself so dangerously if you only let delusion guide you. With a smile, Roberts conceded that the opposite rang just as true. He glanced at the sheathed sword leaning unobtrusively against the wall and permitted himself a wry smile. Storm had no idea how right he was when he scorned the weapon and dismissed it as having little importance. Most people saw the blade before they fully took in the man wearing it, and when their gaze finally did appraise him it was skewed by what they knew to be a very deadly and dangerous weapon. People feared the sword as if it might spring to life of its own volition and cut them down. Roberts mused that their fear was misplaced. The sword was but a means to an end; a symbol of risk. The sword was just a warning. The Man in Black was the weapon. As The Dread Pirate Roberts looked at the myriad titles decorating his wall, some from the ancient depths of PTC history, he smiled at the prospect of unveiling to the world that he knew how to wrestle like few others they had ever seen before. Countless gimmick titles -- Internet, Television, Intercontinental, and Primetime, to name but a few -- adorned the outer rim of the celebrated circle. Inside them ranged the Hardcore, the Extreme, and the World titles he had held with reverence. The crowning jewel, the belt on which he had truly made his name, the title that transcended them all, sparkled from the center with the clandestine majesty of the elite few who were able to brave the ordeal of capturing it. He reached out with a trembling hand and felt the gold plate embellishing its centre, the frigidity of the element paradoxically warming him to his core. Memories of battles fought long ago, of challenges taken up and defeated, surged through him, transporting his mind to the hallowed glory of the conquest. Each belt represented a journey of countless trials and tribulations, of foes feared, fought, and vanquished. He smiled in the grim light as he let his hand drop, knowing that he was embarking on another storied adventure. A calm pandemonium torrented through him. He let himself be carried away by the stormy prospect of adding another trophy to his collection, another ballad to his epic history. It was true that The Dread Pirate Roberts had never wrestled a match . . . . . . but the man behind the mask knew the dance quite well.
