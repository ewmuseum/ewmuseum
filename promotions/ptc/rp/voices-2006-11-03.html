Date: 2006-11-03 .:: ::: ::. Backstage Post-Dangerous Games "To victory!" "Victory!" came the answer. The cheery clink of amber-brown bottles was an aphrodisiac for the incredible tumult of excitement inside the newly-combined Dies Irae/Knightmare Brigade locker room. Surely, enough of them had reason to be annoyed right now- Bryan Mayhem had seemingly betrayed them. Dawn had not come away from her match with Lestatt Knight holding a gleaming 40-pound World Title. Eva VanLorne had reappeared, making Vivica J. Valentine's deepest-seated anxieties into a reality. But it was water under the bridge right now, because no matter what else had happened, they had still achieved their main goal. This party was, at its core, for her. The glory of victorious battle still hung close about her- she could still feel the sheen of sweat that came underneath those bright lights, that came as she fought tooth and nail for this one thing that was supposed to erase all the ills of the past. She smiled down into her wine glass, seeing her own face reflected in its burgundy depths. It only made her smile a little wider, because she hadn't yet gotten used to associating her face with that of the Dangerous Games winner. They'd put her picture up, captioned just so, on the GCW website in the morning. The fans would talk about her amazing performance, and they'd herald her as an amazing competitor in all the dirt sheets. This was balm. Balm for a wound she had been carrying around since WMW. There was no way they could tell her she didn't deserve it. Once that contract was signed, as it would be on Monday, it would be iron clad. Only a handful of weeks stood between her and that summit she had so forcefully plummeted from after the Rampage tournament. She turned to Vigil, her rock, and the secret smile he gave to her said that he knew- he knew more than anyone else just how much this one thing meant to Lia Vlaszy. He would have sacrificed whatever he had to give it to her, but he hadn't needed to; she had only lacked the motivation to find whatever she needed inside herself. "So what's next for the 2006 Dangerous Games winner?" quipped Alexis, holding a recently uncapped beer bottle towards Lia as a microphone. Lia had to smile- not to mention, she had to briefly consider asking Alexis if she really needed to get completely tanked before going back to the lab with Gray tomorrow. But, as she noticed that most of the eyes in the room were fixed on her, she relented. "No rest for the weary…" "BOOOO!" came Josh Brand's voice from the back of the room. "You need to learn to lighten up, Lia," Gray said, vocalizing his younger brother's sentiment with a look that was surprisingly impish for him. She raised her eyebrows. "Coming from you, I'll take that as an order," she said, causing Gray to shrug and chuckle. "Another toast!" The voice was Blaise McClancy's. Nine pairs of eyes turned to him, as he gestured around the room. "To friendship!" It was heartily returned, at least as far as Lia could tell. The room was filled with spontaneous, raucous yelling, the exuberant noise of ten people who had affirmed something much larger than their individual selves over the course of the evening. She barely heard the knock at the door over all the noise, but she squeezed through the crush of bodies to open it. Behind it, a frazzled looking older man, forty years slowly creeping across his face, stood with his headset still on, his GCW polo shirt tucked crisply into slim-fit black jeans. "Yes?" she asked, with a sense of noblesse oblige that would have made anyone in his right mind a little nauseous. "I don't mean to disturb your… party," he said, with a more than evident hint of annoyance, "but you're going to have to pack it in. Mr. Caldera wants everyone out of here by midnight." She glanced at the clock- 11:44. "Packing it in" in 15 minutes would be a task. "Tell you what… I'll let you tell them," she said, stepping aside to beckon him into the room. Shooting her a look full of daggers, he pulled his headset off and let it hang around his neck. "Excuse me…" Flatline. Nobody had even registered the intrusion. He adjusted his stance and cleared his throat. "Hey! Excuse me!" The shout was finally loud enough to pierce the veil. Surprised faces slowly turned in its direction. "Hey you guys ah… you're gonna have to clear out. You've gotta be out of here in fifteen so we can start cleaning up." "Hey buddy, have a beer?" Joshua Brand grinned, holding up an unopened bottle- which would naturally draw the man's attention to the massive mess that had already accumulated on the "kill count" table. The crewman paused, and reddened slowly. "No thanks… Look, just get out of here, alright?" he rejoined, an unattractive hint of whine in his voice. "No skin off our backs, man. We'll clear out." Blaise's voice was calm enough to at least defuse the situation. "Well… soon," he mumbled gruffly. "Yeah, yeah, yeah." But Josh's comment bounced off the crewman's back as he retreated. "Well, gang…" Nobody needed to finish Blaise's sentence. The room erupted into frenzied, frenetic motion. Hands that had been battle tested a scant few hours before now gathered trash and ring gear, each to its proper receptacle. It was only a couple of minutes after midnight when they spilled out into the hallway, prowling lazily towards the parking lot like a pack of sated predators. The silence had become rather comfortable, but Lia broke it with a mischievous smile. "So… are we picking up this party back in the suite?" Blaise grinned, and slapped her on the back. "Will you listen to yourself? You lived in Las Vegas most of last year, Lia, what kind of question is that? You know the party doesn't stop until everybody passes out!" Raucous cheers and laughter accompanied the statement, a sign that this euphoria wasn't wearing off any time soon. The first hot breath of the outside puffed through the air-conditioner chilled arena as Vivica, at the group's vanguard, pushed the door open. Lia closed her eyes for a moment, savoring the difference in sensation. But an irritated growl from behind called her attention back into the present. A rush of wind wrapped around her, tearing at her shoulders with force. A few moments later, he came into view- and even the ten of them strong were held at bay for a moment witnessing the source of such fury. Brandon Youngblood. Raw hatred seethed around him- no matter how nicely he was dressed right now, no matter how he kept his chin raised defiantly, there was no pretense to conceal the violence of his emotion. His hard eyes met hers, but between the two of them there was no contest of intimidation, not this time. There was no respect. There was only that anger- you took something from me that I deserved. I put my heart and soul into it, I put my all into it… and you took it. Something in the pit of her stomach twisted violently. Just like that, he was gone, but she stared after him, seeming to still feel him ethereally burned into that place opposite her. Blaise chuckled. "You want another shot at him so soon, Lia? I'm sure it'll be coming… won't be long before he's crying about getting his due." But it wasn't that. It wasn't that at all. The simple fact was, for the first time since she had met him, Lia knew exactly where Brandon Youngblood stood, and exactly how he felt. For a few long moments, the two of them had stared into the eyes of a monster, and only seen themselves reflected. .:: ::: ::. Las Vegas, Nevada The seasons seemed not to touch Las Vegas. Late October still held more than seventy degrees of desert heat even now as the sun was slowly beginning to slide behind the sanded mesas. It was noticeable right now, but something she might not have paid attention to had she not been outside, because the past few days had been days of constant preoccupation. The paper in her pocket was the bane of her existence right now. It had come in an innocuous gray envelope just a few days ago, with an innocuous green and white logo printed on the upper left-hand corner. She'd scanned the contents to avoid the curious questions of her stablemates, who would have wondered why she was treating this tournament update as something that might contain an airborn pathogen once opened that would rampage through the suite, killing all of them in Red Death fashion. Now, she took it out, unfolding it as though taking secret pleasure in tracing a meat-carving knife over a cut that had finally drawn itself shut. Flip past the form letter. Flip past the travel itinerary. There- brackets. And there, there it was again. Kimbusa (GCW) vs Shadow (GCW) - PTC Global Title Match It was the same reaction, all over again. The clenched teeth, the fingers that desperately wanted to claw, the slightly sick feeling that something about this wasn't turning out how it was supposed to. Because everyone knew how it was supposed to turn out- she was supposed to get her rematch against Almasy. She was supposed to be able to rub his sickening little face in the mud for daring to act the way he had, daring to cut and run when he'd had his fill of pretending to be a team player. Wasn't that right? Wasn't it? Then how, exactly, had this come about? It was an obvious answer, one that any ten year old casual wrestling fan could have given her- Kimbusa smushed Almasy. Turned him into roadkill, and after making him an insignificant smear, had walked away with his newly won Global Title. It was a fitting end for Almasy's Global Title reign, if she looked at it a certain way. It was nice to see him go out as ignominiously as she had, if not more so. There was still a part of her, though, that insisted that it wasn't fair. There was still a part of her that, right now, could let go a deep, wistful sigh as though the world had caved in on her. Vengeance had always been a staple of her life. It had been an unspoken motivator, something that kept her getting up and coming back for more no matter how many times someone knocked her down. Each defeat added fuel to the fire, and each subsequent victory was all the sweeter for it. In the past four days, since the letter had come in, she had been forced to face herself, forced to confront her own sins of wrath. So much would be made right by a match for the Global Title, because it was a second chance- and at that, a second chance that was rarely granted. Once the brackets had been made public, the KB: DI camp had been in a constant state of preparation, making sure she and Danielle were prepared to represent proudly the force that was now holding ever-increasing dominion over GCW. But all the hope, even all the confidence in the world would not change the fact that what Shadow really wanted was to be facing off against the weedy Judas that had set her off down this path in the first place. The wrath wouldn't die. It was a haunting specter, reminding her of the injustice. Perhaps it would not have been so bad, had she not read all of the tournament brackets. In a way, she wished she hadn't. Ignorance would be better than this. Killean Sirrajin (PRIME) vs Seymour Almasy (FA) "God dammit!" The guttural pronouncement was like unto a warlock's curse. The paper crumpled in her hands. It put a bilious, bitter taste in her mouth to see him crop back up, and in a different bracket, no less. By whose joke had it happened? Who in the name of god, any god, could have found pity for someone so pathetically selfish? These were all pointless questions, of course, because it didn't matter who was looking out for Seymour- somebody was. And that benevolent hand had taken away from her something that she needed. The best hope she had was for Killean Sirrajin to slaughter him. The sensible side of her knew that this obsession was a dangerous one, one that could drag her into rash action, and put her out of the tournament before she had time to think twice. An obsession that could easily lose the Global Title for her. She had to put it out of her head, that was the only way to handle the situation. Sirrajin was a shark- he'd smell fresh blood on a weakened and desperate Almasy and take him to task, putting him out of GTT6 for a second time. She had to look at it that way, no matter how much it took her to convince herself of that fact. "Hold yourself together, Lia," she muttered fiercely to herself. She stood from the bench that she had sequestered herself on for the past two hours. As the sun-warmed heat of the black iron radiated away from her, it was as though the anger went with it. Each breath pushed it farther away. Her fingers flung the bracket listing headlong into the wastebin at her left shoulder. Fuck Seymour Almasy. This was her week. It was about reaffirming Lia Vlaszy. Though she had to admit- and did so with a sly smile to herself as she turned from the park back towards the hotel- he'd be watching. He'd have been stupid not to keep an eye on somebody who knew him so well, inside and out. Perhaps in this case, the best revenge was fear. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate The library door protested as it was opened, and then shut with a politely quiet click. Lia's eyes did not change course, but continued skimming across the pages of De Bello Gallico, written in Latin. The wine-leather chair directly across from her squeaked as someone sat down in it. "A little strategy reading?" came a female voice, accompanied by a nervous chuckle. Lia looked up from the page to see her protege, and sporadic tag team partner Vivica J. Valentine. Her lips creased in a slight smile as she pulled off her reading glasses and wet her lips with her tongue. "I suppose you could say that." Vivica nodded thoughtfully, not quite able to raise her teal-colored eyes to her partner. She seemed to be tracing every fiber in the thick rug. "Hey, could I… Could I talk to you about something?" Lia pursed her lips and shut the book, nodding thoughtfully. "Of course." She paused to let Vivica speak- it wouldn't have been the first time that Vivica had come seeking council or advice, and after all, Shadow considered herself to be there for just such a purpose as far as Vivica was concerned. Especially now that Vivica was anchored far more to the McFarlane Estate than she was to such places bearing the name of Zenith. No questions came immediately. The Black Mariah appeared to be struggling, wallowing around in uncomfortable thoughts and feelings that would not form themselves into audible words. Finally, the fire burst forth, machine-gun rapid. "Are you sure this whole merger thing is cool?" It caught her off guard, because it had been more than a week since the decision had been announced. True enough, Shadow had not consulted most of Dies Irae about the idea, but it was the leader's job to know when to act, and Dangerous Games had called for action. Deadly and strategic action. She looked at Vivica for a long moment. "Didn't it work out well enough at Dangerous Games?" Vivica grunted aggravatedly. "Well… yeah, it did. I'm not saying that, it's just that…" Vivica sighed and laced her fingers together, stubbing the toe of her black and green Puma sneakers into the carpet. "I don't know if I necessarily, you know… fit in the way things are." It took Shadow slightly by surprise to hear those words. Of all people they could have come from, the brash, cocky, headstrong woman sitting across from her would have been about her last suspect. "Why do you think you don't?" Vivica made a face, and gestured towards her tag team partner. "Are you kidding? The rest of you have that supernatural… stuff that you do together. And it's just like… when you start talking about it, I'm in a completely different world, totally lost. I mean, I've known about it… I've been around you guys since back in Global, but it doesn't… I just don't fit in with… that crowd." Vivica was dancing delicately around the topic, trying not to say something that Lia would take offensively- but the most offensive part of it all was that Vivica felt like she had to walk on eggshells in the first place. "Vivica…" she started, and an annoyed sigh punctuated the sentence, "That's not what the whole group is about. We all had the same aims in the first place in the ring, it's just that the stuff outside it gradually came with it. I don't want you to feel like… these people are so far removed from you. They're friends, nothing more and nothing less." The slight growl under Vivica's breath was nearly inaudible. "No, that's not it at all, Lia. I just…" And suddenly, she exploded. "How can I be friends with these people? It's not exactly like they want to hang out with me for my company over coffee! I don't have anything in common with them, I don't have anything to talk about, and anyways… I mean, there are some days when Dawn and Virgil act like they don't want to talk to me! And now we're bringing all these other people into the equation?" Whack! The leather-bound book found its way to the end table with an unceremonious war-like gesture. Lia's glasses followed more slowly, more methodically. "Why even start anything up about Dawn and Virgil? Haven't we just gotten things settled in the house without Seymour around? Maybe… Vivica, maybe this is just how things are. It's not like when you're not around, Dawn and Virgil and I do Maypole dances and sit around the fire singing lovey-dovey camp songs." "Jesus, I know that, I'm just saying! What about all the rest of them? What about-" "The rest of them," Lia butted in, her eyes flashing with jade-colored fire, "would be just as willing to give you a chance, if you'd accept them as they are and not expect to have a slumber party with them after the shows. They're not bad people. Maybe you just need to go into it with an open mind, instead of bringing all your preconceived notions to the table." The silence spoke volumes. Vivica held Lia's gaze for only a moment, before returning to her absorbed contemplation of the carpet. Lia eyed her like a lioness watching a gazelle. Her fingers curled around her book, and the spine of it cracked open like a gunshot in the heavy wordlessness. It was the punctuation point- whatever more Lia had to say on the topic would not be pleasant words, and it was something that the both of them knew. .:: ::: ::. October 25, 2006 Dallas, Texas "I think," Dawn put in quietly, shifting her weight on the sofa, "That Blaise is definitely the smartest choice we can make. He's the one who's got all the experience in these big matches, and if we don't put him in there, we'd be making a mistake." Several heads nodded in agreement, before silence descended on the room. Blaise and Lia stood in front of them all- a surprisingly formal setup for all that they were only meeting in a Dallas hotel suite. They were all present- except for Vivica, who had had press engagements to keep. The sting of the conversation Lia had with her, too, was still fresh, having happened only a few days before, so it was perhaps best that she hadn't come. Lia cleared her throat. "What about me?" Gray arched a brow. "You have experience with this kind of thing?" Lia bit her lip thoughtfully, and shook her head. "No, not really, unless you count the first Crossfire event. But if we're talking about smart moves, I think that would be a smart move- we put our leadership out there, we show them that we mean business, and in any case, it's smarter to get both of us in there so that we have a better sense of organization inside the ring. If one of us is incapacitated, the other can take over." Virgil nodded to that. "That's true… it would let our team operate a little more seamlessly." Blaise shrugged. "I'm down… and that's half of our team, too… now who else?" It was the question of the hour. Ever since Ian Childress had announced a KB: DI vs. Ascension match at Devil's Night, they had all been on edge, trying to figure out how to get the best advantage over their opponents. She swallowed, with some difficulty. "What about Virgil?" Gray nodded at that. "That makes sense… We need a big man in there. Tip the scales our end for power." Silence again. Someone cleared their throat nervously. "Vivica?" Dawn spoke up. Lia whipped a glare in her direction, before remembering that Dawn didn't know what she herself did about Vivica. Blaise shook his head. "No… Vivica's getting her match with Lestatt Knight." A cold anger gripped Lia's frame. Virgil looked at Blaise, plainly non-plussed, but Blaise only shrugged. "It's not that big of a deal… Knight needs to realize what he's dealing with here, and this is a good way to show him." "A few weeks ago, we would have kicked someone out of the group for that," Lia said under her breath, but loud enough for everyone in the room to hear. "Mhmm…" Dawn noted, nodding almost imperceptibly. "It's a different situation," Blaise went on, eyeing his co-leader warily, "We've got to take care of business, and this is the best way to do it." Lia looked pointedly away from all of them, the annoyance seething roughly inside her. Doggedly, Dawn spoke up again. "Put me in, then." Blaise shook his head. "Uh-uh, no dice. You're special reffing the Knight/Vivica match, and I don't think we need to spread ourselves thin." Dawn tried not to let her frustration show through, sticking a fingernail into her mouth and gnawing it to keep from biting off a response from the sting of getting shot down in such rapid succession. Virgil ran his fingernails through his goatee, his brow wrinkled thoughtfully. "What about Danielle? Someone with such recent success would be the next best choice." "My line of thought exactly," Blaise said, nodding. Gray smiled almost grimly- he too, noted that the tension in the room was getting a little thick, and all emanating from one point. "If being on the same page is any indication," he said, "then we seem to be off to a great start." "We can't get lazy with this," Lia cut in, her eyes zeroing in on Gray. Blaise glanced back and forth between them for a moment, tapping his fingers on top of the television. "We're not going to be lazy. That's the next order of business, to set up a time when we can all get together to train and work on strategy." "Yeah, God knows what'd happen if we lost… Almasy and Youngblood could finally quit using their voodoo dolls." That was it- a harp-string snapped, and she threw all her limbs into motion. Ebon hair flying behind her, Lia stalked out of the room without so much as a word to anyone. The door thudded shut behind her, a dread pronouncement. Joshua Brand found himself on the end of a couple of pointed stares. "What? I was joking!" "I'm going after her," Dawn said resolutely, standing up from the couch with marked disdain for anybody who could sit in inaction at this moment. But a dark-maned lion caught up with her in only two steps, and grabbed her shoulder. As Dawn spun slowly around, she found herself under the disconcerting blue gaze of Virgil Ambrosi. "Let her go," he bit off, "If you go out there trying to pet her and soothe her, you're only going to make it worse." Dawn let her hands drop to her sides, defeated- and defeated in more ways than just the immediate situation warranted. It was as though, once again, Lia Vlaszy was slipping out of everyone's hands, into a deep abyss. It didn't make sense, but it might have just been Dawn grasping- there was no reason for Lia to be unhappy again, they had just gotten rid of the reasons… Lia, however, carried a deep sense of tragedy within her, like a curse- her spirit forever easily disturbed by the right combination of blows. No matter how much Dawn, or even Virgil, had wanted to shield her, there was no shielding. There wasn't even a solution- save letting Lia, besieged as she was, fight it out for herself.
