Why? Because I NEED it More

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 “The doctor will see you now Mr. Nakazura,” the receptionist’s soft voice came from behind the small, thin sheet of glass that separated her office from the rest of the waiting room. Ichiro stood and forced a smile as he walked by the receptionist. She was young and quite attractive. Her blonde hair fell to her shoulders but it was pulled back into a small ponytail. Her eyes were blue and seemed to shine brightly even in the monotonous white walls and cheap lighting of the doctor’s office. He received a smile back from the young receptionist, whose name tag read Mary, as he walked into the room. He had been waiting for almost an hour, and his legs were stiff from sitting. Ichiro grumbled to himself slightly about the wait, but managed to hide his dissatisfaction as he walked through the door into the corridor filled with offices. He walked down the long, white corridor. The light hurt his eyes as he walked down the hall, searching for the appropriate door. Finally, he came up to the door he was looking for. The golden name plate hung up on the door read Dr. Jacobson. Ichiro took a deep breath, from what he read of the doctor’s first diagnosis this wasn’t going to be easy. He knew that this might be emotional and painful, but he had to do it. He knocked softly on the plain, white, metal door. “Come in, come in,” the voice called from inside the room. The voice clearly of Dr. Jacobson, Ichiro remembered it from his last visit, to this lovely little clinic. Ichiro pressed down on the small metal handle and heard the click of the door latch opening. He pushed the door open and stepped inside. Once he was inside he took a few seconds to analyze the surroundings around him. Although this was his second time to the clinic, this was the first time in the doctor’s office. The last visit, had him connected to wires and scanning machines the entire time. The room was very different from the outer hallways. This new room was filled with color. The walls were a solid red, while the floor seemed to be a burgundy. On top of the carpet sat a large desk made out of cedar. Dr. Jacobson sat behind in a large business chair. On his desk was a computer, and a vast array of papers. On the walls behind him, several plaques and certificates indicating his PhD’s and other forms of recognition for his accomplishments. Ichiro was brought back into reality as he heard the door shut and click in behind him. He turned to see the door now fully closed, but quickly turned back towards the doctor. The doctor seemed to be just finishing up with his wife on the phone, and there was an irritated look on his face as he motioned for Ichiro to sit down. Ichiro kindly accepted and took a seat in a large leather chair, which was actually quite comfortable. He sat and waited, mindlessly looking around the room as the doctor finished his phone conversation. “Yes Hun. Uh huh. Of course, bring home milk and bread on my way home. I got it, okay goodbye dear…I love you too,” the doctor’s voice finally ceased and Ichiro turned back as the doctor hung up the phone. “Wives, can’t live with ‘em, can’t live without ‘em,” the doctor said, with a rather large grin on his face. “I wouldn’t know,” Ichiro said quite flatly. “Oh,” the doctor searched for a reply to Ichiro’s cold response. “Well, I guess there’s no matter with that. We should be getting started however, we only have an hour for our session, and I have a lot that I would like to assess. But, before I get started do you have any questions about the diagnostic note that you received?” Ichiro scanned his brain, he recalled the letter and its contents. “No, I don’t believe I do.” The doctor nodded, he reached into his desk and pulled out a small pamphlet. He reached across the table, and handed it to Ichiro. Ichiro took it from his hands, and curiously thumbed through it. The pamphlet was a small informational packet for people who were curious about Multiple Personality Disorder; the disease that Ichiro had been diagnosed with. After a few minutes, Ichiro looked up at the doctor and nodded. The doctor returned the nod and began to shuffle a few papers. “Alright then, let’s get started shall we?” asked the doctor. Ichiro made no reply. He simply placed the pamphlet on the floor underneath the chair. He sat back up straight and looked at the doctor. The doctor looked at his watch and began to speak. “Well, by my watch we have approximately fifty-four minutes left in our session, so let us not waste another moment. Just to let you know, I’m going to be recording this conversation. I will also be making notes. These notes are strictly confidential, however, by law specific results which will aid in the research of the disease. Are you okay with all of this?” “Yes, sounds good to me,” Ichiro’s reply came. “Not a man of many words are you Mr. Nakazura?” the doctor asked as he placed the small voice recorder in front of him, on his desk and set it to record. Ichiro did not even so much as glance back in response, he merely continued his blank stare towards the doctor. The truth was Ichiro hated being here. He hated having to face the facts. He hated being in a place when he could not justify his true reasons for being there. He hated being anywhere, so open and public. He hated being placed where he would be judged, without the ability to prove himself in competition. But mostly, he hated being anywhere that threatened him being able to take part in the one enjoyable thing he had left. Wrestling. He loved it. He loved the smell of the sweat, blood, tears and spit in the atmosphere. He loved the chance to inflict pain on others, as a crowd of others cheered him on. He loved the thrill of competition. He loved the agony of defeat, he loved the thrill of victory. He loved everything about the sport. And he shuddered to think that he might be robbed of the one thing he loved. It would not happen. “Alright, first question Mr. Nakazura. Oh and by the way, I am glad you finally gave up your name,” the doctor began. “Don’t mention it,” came Ichiro’s quick reply. “Right. Well anyways, you said to me last time I saw you, that you felt empty. Like you had nothing left, do you still feel this way?” Ichiro slouched down in his chair slightly. He didn’t like being in a psychiatrist’s office already, and these questions made him feel even worse about it. He leaned his head back against the rest and closed his eyes before responding to the doctor’s inquiry. “Yes, I do feel empty,” said Ichiro. Of course I feel empty, what human being wouldn’t feel empty. He had undergone massive amounts of mental and physical trauma in the past months. The road of rehabilitation from his injuries was not an easy one. But, he made it through. And he made it through on only two thoughts alone. Ichiro knew that the only reason he was doing it was to get back to the thing he loved, the sport he loved, wrestling. But, he also knew, that getting back to wrestling was the only way he could get revenge. Revenge that he had sworn when his father was killed. Revenge that he had sworn when his name was disgraced and his uncle paid that price with his life. Revenge that he had sworn when his brother was stolen from him. Revenge that he would soon fulfill. GTT was important to him, but to Ichiro it was a merely a stepping stone. He wanted to compete in GTT4. The idea, brightened his hopes. It had pushed him to complete his rehab quicker than expected. He wanted to get into GTT4 for the great competition that he was sure to get. But, he also wanted to get back into true in-ring form, for when the time came for his revenge. “I feel empty, but I think that any human being in my position would feel empty. You said in your diagnosis that I was depressed. Of course I am depressed, I thought that depression was a common method of humans coping with struggle,” said Ichiro defending his statement. “Yes, that is true,” the doctor started. “However, being extremely depressed is quite harmful. It can cause chemical imbalances as well as throw off one’s judgment as well as several other psychological effects. That’s why many drugs are prescribed to manic depressives. Depression is normal, and it is normal for a human being to deal with problems by depression, but manic depression can only lead to more problems.” Ichiro nodded, the doctor was right. Too much depression had affected his way of thinking. He seemed much more easily agitated. He also, felt the thrill of competition and the ability to inflict pain not so much as a gift, but as a right. He saw that ability to inflict hurt and suffering as his right of redemption to the world. However, since receiving the drugs prescribed by the wonderful doctor who now sat across from him, Ichiro had realized what wrestling truly meant to him. “Now, can you admit that you have become over depressed? It is the first step to your recovery,” the doctor continued with his examination. “Yes,” Ichiro started. “However, those drugs have helped the depression, and the blackouts have also been gone.” “I’m glad to hear that. So with your blackouts gone, have you felt these battles with the demons that you explained to me last time?” the doctor asked. “Yes and no,” Ichiro could tell the doctor was confused by that comment, so he continued. “The blackouts are gone. But the demon isn’t gone completely. I still see him in my sleep some nights. He’s like a nightmare that won’t go away. But, I find that I am not struggling with him when I am awake anymore.” “Now, before you struggled with him often?” asked the doctor. “Yes, frequently. I could always hear him talking to me. At first, it was just a voice inside my head, I thought it was my conscience at first. Then I realized that it was putting me down, and battling with me. It was not trying to help me, the voice was contradicting everything about me, and everything I stand for. Then, he began to try to take over…” Ichiro stopped talking and looked down at the ground. “Go on, this is good,” the doctor encouraged Ichiro to go on with his story. “It’s hard to explain. He seemed to want to take over my body. Like he was inside me the whole time, just trying to emerge. Trying to break free from my subconscious,” came Ichiro’s further explanation. “That makes perfectly logical sense. The demon that you are seeing, it must be one of your multiple personalities,” the doctor began to reason with Ichiro. “It would be completely natural for you to feel a struggle with him, because in essence that is what is going on. The two of you are struggling for control of the same body.” “Yeah, well you told me I had MPD, but is that for sure. Are the test results conclusive? And how harmful is the disease?” Ichiro asked inquisitively. This was what would make or break him right here, this is where he could find out how quiet he would have to keep this from the wrestling world. “Disorder, not disease. But, yes, the results are quite conclusive. And as for effects, you can look at anywhere from none if you treat it properly with great drug treatment, all the way up to extremely dangerous and turning you clinically insane. Unable to perform even the simplest of tasks if left untreated,” came the doctor’s chilling response. Ichiro shuddered. Those were the exact words that he did not want to hear. Unable to complete the simplest tasks? Unable to have full utilization of even basic motor skills. No, that would end his career for good, and that was not something he could let happen. He knew now that he would never get clearance to wrestle, he needed to keep this quiet. “Now, you also said to me last time, that you had one thing that kept you going. One thing that was fuelling you through the rough times that you were experiencing, it is of the utmost importance that I know this hobby or pass-time. It may just help in solving your personality problem, it is often from wants or fantasies that these personalities evolve from, so anything you can tell me may help,” the doctor requested. Ichiro swallowed, and when he did he felt like he was swallowing an entire pumpkin at once. This was the moment of truth. If he revealed that he was a professional wrestler, he might not ever step foot in that ring again. However, if he did tell him, the doctor might be able to help cure him. To eliminate these blackouts and hallucinations. If the doctor could help him, then maybe the risk would be worth it. Life is more important than wrestling. Or is it? Thought Ichiro as he contemplated what he should do. On one hand he had the one thing he loved. The one thing that would lead him to further self-fulfillment and more importantly vengeance. Vengeance for himself and for his family. Wrestling, a great sport. But still, only a sport. On the other hand, he had his life. He might end up living in a mental institution for the rest of his life. And if this happened, there sure as hell would be no way that he would ever step foot inside that ring again. He would never hear the roar of his fans, or the sounds of the arena. He would never feel the thrill of competition, the rush of adrenaline. He would never feel the thrill of victory, never again suffer the agony of defeat. But, such is life. And filled with harsh decisions it is. This was just one of many Ichiro had faced, and one of many more surely to come in his life. Ichiro looked blankly at the doctor. He had to make up his mind, and he could tell by the look on the doctor’s face that Dr. Jacobson was becoming more than a little irritated with the amount of time Ichiro was taking with the question. “Mr. Nakazura? Please if you could just give me anything?” Dr. Jacobson asked. “Alright,” Ichiro started. He took a deep breath, this would be difficult, and a risk to his career. But he felt that he had to make it. “I am a professional wrestler. That is my hobby that is my pass-time. That is what has been keeping me going.” “Wrestling?” the doctor asked curiously. “What kind of wrestling?” “Mainstream wrestling like you see on television. I am under contract by a company called Global Wrestling, however I’m currently on injury leave. I am however, competing in a tournament called Golden Turnbuckle Tournament 4,” Ichiro explained. “That’s what I do. I have felt empty, I have felt like I have nothing to live for. But, to be truthful, that’s what I live for. I live to wrestle.” “That’s quite interesting. Tell me more if you can,” the doctor said, encouraging Ichiro to continue his explanation. “You wouldn’t know unless you do it. Just stepping inside that ring, inside the arena. The buss of the fans, the anticipation hangs in the air. Then, you compete. You do battle against a foe as the crowd cheers you to victory or defeat. Either way, win or lose. You feel better about yourself afterwards, you feel like you’ve earned a pat on the back and a job well done. Then, the next week, it starts all over again. That’s how I’ve been living my life lately, just passing the time until I get that chance to do it all over again,” Ichiro finished. “You know this disorder, it may seriously affect your ability to be in the ring, competing like that. The adrenaline could spark a blackout, or a struggle for control at any time. And I am by law, obligated to make a report about that,” said the doctor. Ichiro’s heart sank. It was going to happen, he was going to lose the one thing he wanted to do. He was not going to be able to compete. “Doctor, please, there has to be some other way, you can’t just…” The doctor raised his hand, silencing Ichiro, before he continued. “Now, I have no reason to believe that at this time you are a danger to yourself, or to anyone else. At least, not because of this disorder, depending on how well you are at your job.” Ichiro let out a slight laugh. Before silencing once again as the doctor continued, “So at this time I will not make a recommendation to have you removed. On two conditions.” “Yes, name them. Anything to still be able to wrestle,” Ichiro leaned forward in his chair listening. “Alright, number one, you meet with me once again. We go step by step, trying to improve your progress and trying to help you overcome your disorder. You see there will come a time, when your two personalities will confront one another, and it will be quite painful for you, but I will do everything I can to aid you in this process,” the doctor started. Ichiro nodded, “Okay, that sounds reasonable.” “Secondly, you stay on a strict prescription dosage of drugs. Both for your MPD and for your depression,” the doctor said and put his pen down. Looking for acknowledgment from Ichiro. Ichiro nodded once again, “Absolutely.” “Alright then,” the doctor said as he checked his watch. “Our time is almost up, so here’s what we’ll do. Set up a time with Mary out front, we’ll meet every week at that date and time.” Ichiro nodded and began to stand up. The doctor grabbed his pen and pulled a small pad of paper from his drawer. He scribbled two drug names and dosages on the sheet of paper, and then signed the bottom before handing Ichiro the prescription. Ichiro smiled and bid the doctor farewell before turning to leave. He left the doctor shutting off his voice recorder and scribbling down a few notes in his pad. Then, once again, Ichiro heard the soft clicking of the door shutting behind him. As he walked down the hallway to make an appointment with Mary, Ichiro had a slight bounce in his step. He was happy, he was excited. He had only one thought on his mind as he made his way down that hallway; the thrill of the hunt, the great adrenaline rush of the competition, the anxiety and excitement in the air, the cheering of the fans, and the roars of their approval, wrestling and most importantly, the glory of victory. My next match in GTT4 is this weekend. My opponent is tough, but not tough enough. As tough as Karina Wolfenden is, I want it that little bit more. NO! I need it that little bit more. I need it because, I can’t let it end. Not now, not ever. I need wrestling, maybe more than it needs me. But, that won’t stop me, nothing can stop me. Because this weekend, when I step into that ring, I am stepping into that ring to do the thing that I love. And nothing, that happens this weekend is going to stop me from walking into that same ring, the weekend after!
