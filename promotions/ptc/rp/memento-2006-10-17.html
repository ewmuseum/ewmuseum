Date: 2006-10-17 Prologue: A Glimpse of the Future I can’t change the past. I can’t change what time has already etched into history nor can I free myself of regret. I kneel down on the freshly cut grass, swallowing my apologies as has become routine here over the past couple of years. I hear a slight breeze whistle past my ear and think that it’s him forgiving me, but it’s not. Two white roses lay side by side in front of the unmarked gravestone – a tribute to his duality in life. Always, there are two white roses laid side by side on his gravestone. The St. Mary’s Cemetery in Albany, New York smelled faintly of potpourri, mostly because of the myriad flowers that mourners had left on top of the graves of their loved ones and the seasonal rain that falls between May and October. Albany has a Mediterranean-type climate with warm summers and mild winters. During the summer and fall months, the rain continually washes away the top soil leaving the cemetery with a sort of musk scent. The faint potpourri smell lingers in the air. In the distance, I see the dim orange and red hues given off by the falling leaves in this October month. Soon, the trees will become as barren and lifeless as the cemetery they call home. Soon, winter will cover this land in unforgiving snow. The snow will be relentless. But the memories will linger. I know this because I was here last winter, and the winter before, and the winter before that. I finish my prayers and selfishly bless myself. I wonder if he ever hears my prayers. I wonder if I retold our story enough times, would it be enough to change history. I’ve wanted to change our story countless times. If I’m to retell our story, I suppose that it is always best to start at the beginning. "Wait," Angela tried to scream but her voice was still meek. She was never so glad to see the police. Ironically, the moment she needed them was the very same moment where she was unable to get their attention. She continued to hug the side of the buildings nearing the blurring lights she was seeing. And then she passed out. # It was now nearing 5:00 am and Wolfgang was not getting any closer to locating his daughter. His hands trembled at the wheel, a combination of a lack of sleep and desperation. He had been drinking all night and now cold sweat slid down his forehead. This is my fault he thought. If he had just been honest with her, if he had just been a good father, if he had just…no it’s too late for that. Wolfgang cursed at his own weakness and more so at his own charade. In the ring, he was the Sentinel. He was the protector and defender of the weak and helpless. But in real life, he was helpless. He couldn’t even protect and defend his own daughter. Wolfgang often wondered how different things might be if his relationship with Abigail Jackson had not ended the way it did. He loved her the moment he laid eyes on her and spent the majority of his high school and college life vying for her affection. When he had finally won her over, they were as happy as a couple could be. They were together for six years and tried to have a child for four of them. Abigail’s doctors warned her that her reproductive system had not fully matured. It was a diagnosis that not only sent Abigail into a state of despair, but Wolfgang also. Their relationship became cold and barren while they tried to cope with the idea of never having a child of their own. But then a few months after her twenty-fourth birthday, she gave birth to Angela Moriarty. It was a miracle by all accounts. Unfortunately, that miracle was tainted. Wolfgang James Moriarty cheated on his wife. During her depressed moments, Wolfgang felt helpless and found solace in another woman who had long lusted for him. Her name was Karla Segur and where Abigail was beautiful, she was voluptuous; where Abigail was timid, she was vibrant. She was a self-proclaimed bohemian who loved the color green and all things about nature. Her relationship with Wolfgang was strictly amatory. When Wolfgang finally realized the wrong that he did, it was too late. Abigail had left him and their child. Wolfgang peered out the windows of the Land Rover, he was now back in Main Street. At least the sun was coming up, he thought as he struggled to keep the car aligned. He was have a hard time trying to keep the car centered, swaying the Land Rover to and fro on the streets. There was no sign of Angela anywhere. He came upon the same location he was at earlier that night and shook his head in defiance. He wondered if the lady had misled him again. There was no way Angela was at Sistinas or Pipeline. Wolfgang flipped opened his Motorola cell phone. He was about to give Chuck a call and tell him of his resignation from the copious GTT6 tournament. He was going to swallow his pride and let Snow advance without so much of a fight. Fourteen years of wanting to fight the best was going to be swept under a rug. At least, it would have been that way if Wolfgang hadn’t spied a slumped over body in the middle of the alley. His spirits quickly rose as he found a place to park. But they were quickly dimmed as a dozen scenarios ran through his mind. Wolfgang shut the cell phone as he stepped out of the car and headed towards the body. Please let it be her, he thought. And it was. Wolfgang gaped. A rush of emotions ran through his body as he looked down at his daughter, Angela, passed out in the middle of streets. He wanted to wake her up and scold at her with everything he had and warn her never to do it again. On the other hand, he wanted to wake her up and tell her how much he loved her and how sorry he was. In the end, he decided that was best to let her sleep. As he picked her up, Wolfgang took a long look at her sleeping face. Her smooth skin, her wavy locks, she was the spitting image of her mother. How he wished that she was still here! He wiped his eyes on his shoulder and carried Angela back to the Land Rover. He adjusted her in his arms and managed to pull the back door open. Carefully, he laid Angela down in the back seat of the Land Rover. He made sure she was as comfortable as could be before closing the door behind her. Wolfgang walked around to the driver’s side of the car. Sunrise began to break across the sky. Wolfgang looked up and small flakes began to drop on his nose. This is the big snowstorm? He thought. The weather reports had announced the storm to be unrelenting and fierce – something the likes of which they hadn’t seen in years. But now a full a day had passed and the storm was nothing more than merely over-hyped. With his daughter back with him, Wolfgang knew that everything was going to be all right. He was going to face Jason Snow – the 4-time PCW world champion and defeat him. Wolfgang prepared to get back inside the car. And then his phone rang. "Hello." "Hi," the woman answered. "Thank you for your help tonight." "Do you remember me?" Wolfgang had been thinking about it. The voice was a bit raspy, obviously being hidden with some sort of audio device. Still, there wasn’t anything in their conversation that jumped at him. "I’m sorry." "You’re breaking my heart, Wolfie." "What did you say?" Wolfgang asked, shocked at the woman calling him "Wolfie." No one had ever called him that in over fifteen years. "Where are you?" "Here." Wolfgang looked around him but couldn’t see anything at first. Then he turned to his right and nearly dropped his phone. Wolfgang stood aghast. Standing before him was a stunning woman dressed in white, sporting an appliquéd skirt and wearing a wisteria on her head. The lady broke the silence. "You look like you’ve seen a ghost, Wolfgang." "This isn’t possible."
