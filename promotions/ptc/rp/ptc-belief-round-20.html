Belief - Round 20

3 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fleet Centre Boston, Massachusetts As 4CW Storm Front came to a close Joshua Taro Freedom was still arguing with management over the phone about the outcome of his match. Jessica as his perennial on and off and on again girl friend handed him news of a match he had been looking forward to. The Infinite Gauntlet round 20 would pit two of the men from round 18 against the champion from round 19. Joshua hadn’t been expecting the call, hadn’t even given himself an outside chance of getting the nod for round 20 after his showing in round 18. Yet as he switches off his phone annoyed at the 4CW official on the other end Jessica brightened up his day. You’re in, Jessica told him with cheeriness in her voice. She understood the disappointment Joshua had felt after round 18 and this was a chance to improve that standing. I'm in? Joshua replied with a since of shock and ecstasy. But I was awful, he continued knowing how unimpressive he had been. Maybe but you’re getting a second chance. A chance at the IG title, not even the world’s greatest music could have sounded better to either of them. The nights disappointment could wait as for Joshua, the title chance he was seeking had come from outside his chosen company. Joshua’s Apartment, New York City Inside the Freedom trophy room Joshua Taro Freedom stands staring at the accomplishments he had so far gain during his life. Primetime Central wouldn’t know the junior championship he won aged seven. Or have seen the amateur tournament for the under twelve’s he won just two weeks before he would be turning nine. They would however recognizes the gleaming gold he won in Prime age twenty-one. The replica Alias, Five Star and Tag Team titles took pride of place in the centre of room expect for one empty holding. The gap that Joshua wasn’t even looking at and seemed to never look at. Just to the side sat the WWA World Television Title, which also gleam. Each clearly getting the treatment and due attention they earned. Joshua: The disappointment I felt at not being victorious was heart breaking. My entire life had been about getting close. Getting within touching distance of that glory level and getting rejected. Again and again I’ve scraped and crawled the almost endless trail to the summit of this industry and been thrown down. My friends, the few I have left in the world and the few I allow to truly see my soul all say one single thing. Its not getting to the top that makes you great. But getting close and failing only to start on the journey once more. For the first few years of my career I had believed that to be the case. I’d sit at home failing once more and believe their words. I mean surely I wasn’t terrible. My career is three years old and I have been within touching distance five times. I’ve gained many wonderful memories and championship. I sat and told myself surely I was worthy enough to one-day make the leap into greatness. But at IG18 I wasn’t first. I don’t believe I was even the best runner up. If I had to place myself it would have been last. Seeing that, and speaking it aloud I can feel the aching growing within me. Normally I stand boosting about my coming victory. Bragging about my pure natural talents that surpass all others. But how can I? How can I stand given my failure at IG18? I merely aim to restoring my self-respect and open the eyes of those watching to the potential I contain. My whole life has been built around the wrestling world. I began training at four and didn’t stop. The only break I took was in the months following my mothers’ death and even then my father pushed me to get into a ring. Everything I have ever been gotten from my father has been work towards this sport. This art form that so many take up, but so few master. I believe so much that I have become one of the few. I believe I have the mentality to make it in the far-reaching wrestling world and not just the limited routes of my chosen company. I believe I have the tools to one day gain the world title at my company 4cw, and in the interfed community. To have my name ranked among the greats from all the other companies. I want that so much. I live for that moment where no matter what else becomes of my career. That day, that moment I was the best of anyone. Forever remembered among the elite. I’ve worked so hard, so long for that moment. It has to happen for me it has to. People say life’s unfair but I’ve cheating my way through countless ranks and yet still remain short of that main glory goal. Life is very much fair as it has dealt me enough set backs to make sure I’ve yet to peak. And the reasons I gave heading into the IG18 where true. I opened myself up to all of you and I for the first time spoke the absolute truth. I want you to understand that. Understand that this competition means so much to me. I'm ready to die wrestling for this title. I'm ready to put my body and soul out there for that moment. I'm that committed to this. I know the opponents will seek the same thing as me. I know that, but something about this chance. Something about this time tells me it’s my turn. This is my moment to taste victory on the inter-promotional stage. Its in the air, its surrounding the match hype and I can see the moment in front of me. All I have to do is reach out and grab a hold. Take this chance and pull myself over that final barrier. Joshua places his hand of the glass that surrounded the belts and other trophies. His warm hand smeared the clear glass blocking the view of that one empty title belt holding. Joshua: I got that fitted whist I wrestled in Prime. So expecting the world title victory, which would allow me to fill this gap. Then again in WWA, I picture the day I could walk home carrying the real and replica titles that would fill that space. Now I long for the day I place the 4CW world title into this void. The gab is torture for me. I can’t enter this room without wanting to scream out in frustrations at missed chances and un-given opportunities. Can Jake Hix and Jacob McKail understand where I am coming from? Is there a similar void in their lives? I honestly do not know. I have yet to seek out their career stats and maybe I will never. All I have to say is that this drives me on more then hatred, passion and love every will. So understand I come motivated, eager and with mentality that this time is my time. Joshua takes one and only one finally look at the empty place within his trophy room and then walks away. 4CW’s Offices, New York City. I'm glad you came, as we here are so proud of your achievements within the inter-promotional community. Said Dr Anthony Peterson who was the owner of 4CW. Joshua how was on increasingly growing bad terms with his employers after two defeats under blunt and unpunished outside interference had blemished his record. I'm sure you enjoy the attention I bring your company. I’ll go all out to bring the championship home. Joshua’s reply came with the attitude of a man not entirely happy with his current working environment. Dr Peterson was a master of reading people and quickly shook Joshua’s hand and left. It was merely a meeting between the two for the official website and getting involved in any current political issues was not on Peterson’s mind. The reporter their to cover the story had simply one question he needed answering and asked with an almost smarminess. Joshua, can you really be expected to win this title given your average at best performance the last time you had a shot? Joshua didn’t answer and just walked out. Infinite Gauntlet round 20 was coming up and he had nothing but victory in his mind.
