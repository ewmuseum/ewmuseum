Worth Telling - Chapter 1

19 October 2006

Date: 2006-10-19 Worth Telling Chapter 1 – Setting the Stage Tuesday, 9:23 p.m. Los Angeles, CA "My name is Bryan, and I’m an addict." He hated the meetings. Every single god damned one of them. Why’d he need them, anyway? He’d been clean almost a year, hadn’t gone near drugs or alcohol since he left Arizona. He was fine, and all this meeting did, all every meeting did, was bring up things he never wanted to talk about again. But, every week, no matter where he was, he’d find a meeting and he’d go. He’d say his piece, he’d listen, and he’d go on his way. At this point, it wasn’t so much what he said that mattered, but that he’d gone and said it. The meetings weren’t therapy any more, they were routine. It was routine that kept him from falling off the edge again. Keeping himself on point, on schedule, that was what kept him sane, and more importantly, clean. With what he had done to himself before, keeping that everyday routine was paramount. Type A personalities plus disposable cash and too much free time were a dangerous brew. He hadn’t gone completely Spartan, nor had he gone all Zen on everyone, but he was strict on himself, demanding, making sure he didn’t wander too far. With the path he walked, one step astray and all of a sudden your dangling from the edge of a cliff with none-to-friendly jagged rocks awaiting you at the bottom. Precipitous? That’s a understatement. He was living a life without a safety net, one step away from back where he’d already pulled himself up from. But that’s another story for another day. This particular meeting, like all the others, was nothing special. A high school gym in Los Angeles like so many others where he’d attended so many meetings. Tell your story, listen to the others, maybe make a little bit of "progress", have a cup of coffee and head out the door. Go back to the apartment or the hotel. Sometimes he wouldn’t have the time, and it’d be straight from the meeting to the arena, the airport or the road. Thankfully, he had a little downtime and could go home and sleep before his next trip. It was a simple thing, going home for a night, but over the last nine months the moments he’d gotten to spend at home were fewer and farther between. This night was nothing overly special. Nothing too distinctive about a regular night at home. Just catch the late SportsCenter and hit the sack. Of course, when Bryan opened his e-mail that night, he’d get a message that would change his life forever. He’d just made an innocent inquiry. Who’d know it would eventually throw his world upside down? "Damn internet connection," he said. It was too slow, as always. One of these days, when he was home for more than a week, he’d get his provider on the phone and give him a piece of his mind. Finally, Internet Explorer opened. He got to his e-mail inbox quickly enough. Cluttered with SPAM as usual. "Nothing…nothing…nothing again." Click and delete, click and delete, over and over. Man, he needed a better SPAM blocker. Finally, way down at the bottom of the page, was the message. From: [email removed] To: [email removed] RE: A simple request… Bryan- Got your message, and I think you’re an idiot. Why the hell would you want to try this? But, being your agent and personal savior, I made a few calls, got you an appointment. Call E. Naughton at [detail withheld] to confirm. By the way, give me a call about an opportunity in the business you ACTUALLY WORK IN. This is a big one. -Brendan P.S. Ever been to Singapore? Well, he’d gotten his answer. He had some calls to make. "Yawwwwwwwwwwwnnnn." Well, it could wait until morning. The next day… Brendan wasn’t in, and his cell was off. Seriously, the guy calls himself an agent? He’d get to him later. The news couldn’t be that big. At least, not big enough in relation to the crazy idea he’d saddled himself with. Why had he thought anyone would care about this? Well, it was worth trying. "Better give this Naughton guy a call," Bryan said, picking up his cell phone. He dialed, and after a moment, a female voice entered on the other end. "Devlin/O’Neill," the voice said. "Yes, this is Bryan Storms, I’m calling to confirm an appointment with Mr. Naughton," he said. The voice on the other end seemed to be breaking up. And, did he hear giggling? Nevermind. "This is the office of an E. Naughton, correct?" "Yes. I have you as a 12:15 appointment. Can you make that?" "Yeah. And tell this guy that he needs to have a more courteous secretary." 12:13 p.m. Office of Devlin/O’Neill They kept him in the damn waiting room far too long. He was early, and apparently this Naughton guy was way too high up to leave his midday shiatsu appointment to meet with a potential client. Even one that everyone thought had just a few screws loose, trying to venture into this arena. "Well, better just take matters into my own hands," he muttered. He pulled himself out of the leather armchair and walked straight over to the door clearly marked "E. Naughton". And he didn’t knock. Like an idiot, Bryan Storms just paraded straight into the office. He stumbled for a moment coming onto the shag carpet and spun around. He spun around so he didn’t have the chance to match up a face with the voice that said… "Bryan, thanks for coming in. I was about to go get you when you came-," said a female voice. A very familiar female voice. A voice that, if his sense of direction was spot on, was coming from right behind the desk. "-wait. You? You’re E. Naughton?" "Ellie Naughton. VP for External Relations. Brendan Garrity’s an old friend of mine. He didn’t have faith in you. I do." Bryan turned around, finally, red in the face from his stupidity. His eyes were met by the smiling face of Ellie Naughton, late 20s, brunette, very attractive in an "I’m-smarter-than-you-and-you-damn-well-know-it" kind of way. "I am so sorry for the mistake, I’m generally not this stupid, Miss Naughton," he said. "Please, call me Ellie," she responded. "And I know your not this stupid, Bryan. I’ve read your resumè. Graduated with honors from Columbia with two degrees, accepted into Yale Law but turned it down to go into professional wrestling. Odd career choice." "Well, that, and I was generally coked out of my mind through everything after the LSATs. But, yeah." "But, you’re clean now, right?" She was probing, seeing just how deep she could get. "Almost a year now," he said, used to the question. "Greatest achievement of my life." "And that’s what brings you here, if I’m right." "Apparently, you always are." "You don’t strike me as someone who wants to write a book, Bryan," she said. "Brendan sent me some tapes, and-" "-don’t trust anything that comes from that man’s desk. He sent you the OLD reel. Can’t believe I haven’t fired that jackass yet." "Trust me. Don’t. He’s the best thing that’ll ever happen to you. Brendan Garrity may not be Mr. Moral Support, but he does good business. Case in point, he sent you here. But, as I was saying, you always came off so cocky, even a little stupid." "I thought it would sell. It didn’t. I also thought that the drugs would take me away to a magical fairyland where I wasn’t slogging away in curtain jerker hell. That was a mistake, too." "But why? Why write about it now?" It was a fair question. "It’s cathartic, I guess. It’ll help the process." "That’s bullshit and you know it." Funny how people you’ve never met are always the most observant. "Yeah, you’re right. Honestly? I think it’s a story worth telling. Plain and simple. I want to show the world something about me, and maybe through that they can learn something about themselves. Fuck, I just want to write it. It’s sitting at the tips of my fingers ready to bust out. I just need a publisher." She responded quickly to this one. "Well, Bryan, I can’t make guarantees, but with any luck, I’ll have a deal on the table from Devlin/O’Neill Publishing within days." They thanked each other, but Bryan made clear he had to leave quickly. He had another call to make. Two days later… Back in his apartment, Bryan had a lot to think about. The call with Brendan was monumental, something he’d never expected. GTT6. It wasn’t like he hadn’t thought about it. He’d watched the tournament, followed the competitors for years like any wrestling junkie. But to compete? Out of the realm of possibility. Apparently, until just now. It wouldn’t be easy, but he’d taken the easy route too often and that wasn’t the right way to go any more. Worst thing that could come of it was a quick trip home and more time to work on the book. Even still, the trip would give him plenty of time to write. Clear the mind, and, if you’re lucky, take home the big pot at the end of the rainbow. Ticket to Singapore in hand, he went through his mental checklist. Ring gear? Check. Laptop? Charged up and ready for the plane. Cell phone? Armed with anti-Garrity calling feature. Nerves? Beating a rather syncopated samba in his stomach. But, there’s a first time for everything. At the very least, it’d make for a damn good story. "All my bag’s are packed, I’m ready to go…" TO BE CONTINUED
