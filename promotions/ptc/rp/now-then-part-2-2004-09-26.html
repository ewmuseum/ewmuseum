Now&Then Part 2

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 It’s interesting to see how much a person changes over a long period of time. How somebody used to react and how he reacts now. I mean, how much can a person change? For Nick Persia, making the right choice was always a very hard thing to do. His instincts always failed him when they were really required while the voice inside him told him everything but an answer. But as emotions always take the upper hand in deciding moments, we mustn’t forget that your emotions have the power to tell you your biggest desire, whether you were conscious about them or not! That was exactly what Nick needed to hear. Because it gave him the confidence he needed. It gave him courage for the future again. After his father died, Nick Persia began to wonder how people would react if he died. He started to wonder about the possible consequences. His own reaction turned out to be quite cold, putting his job before his father’s funeral. But is it really that cold and heartless? Let’s not forget that everything has its end. Not just his father, not only himself but also all the other people. Even the earth will disappear, all in due time. It sounded very scary to Nick. When you die, your time is up. The sentence looks so peaceful yet so frightening. He didn’t really want to admit this to himself, but from within Nick felt really scary about dying. Especially after recent events. Everybody hears the stories. Ray Taylor, known around the world as Big Bossman, died totally unexpectedly. People say he was in the midst of his life. But if you listen to what the priest says, you know he was just at the end of his life. It’s quite a strange thing to know that everybody thinks in a different way about your death. Some would say that he deserved death. Some would say his life was taken too early. But as Gandalf the White told us: “Many that live deserve to die and many that died deserve to live. But it’s not for us to decide. All we have to do is deciding what to do with the time that is given to us.” From behind the clouds the sunlight blinded Nick. He held his hand before his eyes to prevent the light reaching them. He blinked a few times to get used to the overwhelming amount of light. Everything was still wet from the storm that had raged last night. The water reflected the light and made everything glitter. The grass sparkled and the water showed us many small lights, like stars in the sky at night. The evening breeze brought some fresh air into his nose. He smelled fresh lavender in the air which was a sign that the flowers would start to flourish soon. After allowing himself to take some rest he ordered himself to get moving again, as he knew what lay ahead of him…a very long night full of sleep. Crossing the street he made no intention to look right or left to see if any traffic was approaching. He went straight to the café. The door resisted against being opened since Nick had to push hard. Nick entered the room which was full of smoke. People were all caught up in their discussion, except for a few loners. Nick scanned the room quickly, to see if he saw any familiars, but as he expected there was no one he knew. “A Heineken, please,” Nick ordered to the waiter. The waiter just nodded in agreement. He turned around and picked up a green bottle. “Two dollars, sir,” the waiter said while he removed the cap. Nick grabbed his wallet out of his pocket and paid the man. The bottle was green. Now what’s so strange about that? Heineken is from Holland, as is Nick. And in Holland you don’t get Heineken in a green bottle! He still can’t get used to the fact they give you Heineken here in a green bottle. It was just more evidence for him that foreigners just didn’t know how to appreciate a good Dutch beer. What a waste Nick thought to himself. He sat down on the foot stool and took a big swig of his beer. It was more bitter than the original Heineken, but the beer was still better than most of the beer that was sold outside of Europe. People here are what Nick would call beer-barbarians! You know, people who don’t use beer as it is supposed to be used. “Hey you,” a female voice said. Nick turned around to see who she was talking to. He looked straight into her sparkling eyes. “M-me,” was all he stumbled out. “Yes, you,” she rolled her eyes “who else should I be talking to?” This nice looking lady didn’t have any problems with saying what she was thinking. Nick found it to be very rude to say something to someone you don’t know. Where he grew up they wouldn’t say thing like that. People aren’t as close with each other as they are here. But above all, much more polite. “Sorry,” Nick apologised “I must have been deep in thought.” “No need to,” she waved the apology away “I guess you didn’t expect that someone would talk with you.” “No not really,” Nick smirked “but America seems to be full of surprises.” She just smiled as a reply. “Can I offer you a drink,” Nick asked polite. “Yeah, sure,” she answered “a Heineken will do just fine, thanks.” Nick put two fingers in his mouth and produced a sharp whistle. A few moments later a waiter appeared in front of them. Nick just ordered two bottles of Heineken as his was empty. “I’m sorry,” Nick said “but I am afraid I didn’t catch your name.” She laughed “I forgot to tell you, but I am Maria.” “Like the bible?” Nick joked. It wasn’t even really funny, just a poor joke to look humorous. Nick was a serious guy who didn’t make many jokes and when he did they weren’t funny. He was just poor at making them. “Like the bible,” she replied “but how is your name?” “Nick Persia,” he said firmly. “Like the wrestler,” she said but you could hear by the way she asked she wasn’t sure. Nick started to laugh out loud. “Yeah, like the wrestler.” Her eyes grew wider and her mouth fell open. She looked astonished by this answer as she obviously didn’t expect the answer. All Nick did was just continue to laugh. “You must be kidding me,” she stated “because that would mean that you are the first famous man I ever met in real life!” “Congratulations,” Nick said “not everybody has that honour.” “Arrogant bastard!” she exclaimed. “But what did you want to ask me?” Nick asked on a more serious tone. “Oh right,” she muttered “you were sitting for quite some time there. So I figured something could be wrong and decided to distract your thought from your problems for a while.” “What makes s you think that I have problems?” Nick played the fool. “Whose leg are you trying to pull?” she commentated “even a blind man can see you are having problems.” “Is it that worse?” Nick asked as he focussed his eyes on her instead of the bottle for once. “It’s that worse,” Maria said, but quickly proposed something “and drinking isn’t going to help this problem. I know this doesn’t concern me, but I am a good ear.” Nick knew she was right, but admitting she was right and he was wrong is something else. He nipped from his beer before answering. “My bottle is empty. Do you want another one as well?” and he added sarcastically “on my health!” “On your health then,” was all she said. Nick whistled again and the waiter looked at him. Nick put a bottle of Heineken in the air and with his other hand he put two fingers in the air. The waiter appreciated this extraordinary way of ordering two new Heineken. Nick however was more surprised on the fact she accepted it. How dare she! Do you know what a beer costs these days? Two whole dollars. Now he paid for it, a total of four dollars for her with nothing in return. A good ear, alright, but you don’t buy anything for that. It needs to be said that when money is involved Dutch people are very greedy. More than any other culture. When they ‘offer’ you a beer they expect to get one in return. It’s to make a gesture, not to really give you one. Because that would mean she was having a good night on his expense! That is out of the question. Anyone who is from Holland likes his money the most. Close followed by his drugs. Thereafter his beer. Remember that when you visit Holland. The same waiter delivered two beers, but didn’t charge them. He walked away before Nick could even react. “Why aren’t we charged for the beer?” Nick wondered. “Because I am the daughter of the owner,” she smiled “so you can take as much as you like.” That was some good news. She repaid him, unlike some other cheap whores. But let’s not get into that too much. Instead he remained polite “I didn’t know about that.” “I am sure you didn’t know about that,” she commented “because I don’t know much about you either.” “What do you want to know then?” Nick asked. “I want to know why you are looking so sad,” Maria said. “Because I am in a bit of a situation,” he answered, not giving her too much. “What kind of situation?” she informed, insisting him to answer her. “I made a promise,” Nick whispered “but I am not sure if I can keep it. My father died just a few days ago and I promised him I would beat Jerms. However, as the date comes close I am not too sure if I can. He’s really good, and well, I am not sure I am the better man.” “Well, I know this,” she replied “that love is one of the purest and finest emotions. It’s so powerful that you can fall in love with someone by just looking at him. But the way you loved your father was different, but the love is equally strong. And that love will give you the love you need,” she assured him. “I don’t know,” he expressed his doubts “it can also make your nervous and cause you to make an unnecessary mistake. I don’t really like being nervous. Especially not that nervous.” “I can’t help you with beating Jerms,” she told him “or telling you how to deal with your nerves. I can only tell you I have all the faith in the world that you will win.” “And why is that?” Nick asked her. “Because love is stronger than anything,” she said with a wink. “Maybe it is,” Nick repeated “maybe it is.” Nick stood up, and as he did, the girl did also. “When do I see you again?” “Some time soon,” he said with a smile “and that’s a promise.” As he walked away the girl sat down again leaving her all alone to her doubts. She waved him goodbye, wondering if he really would come back and if I really kept his word. One way to find out, and that is to watch the tournament. But for now Nick Persia had another date. A date with destiny.
