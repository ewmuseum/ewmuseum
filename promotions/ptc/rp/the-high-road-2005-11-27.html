The High Road

27 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-27 He was used to this. The hype, the spotlight, the unwavering attention of every mind and eye in the business, the undulating of every tongue, they had attached to him like a late-afternoon shadow. It was less than a year since he’d first set foot in the Academy, initially ignored by all but those begrudged to tutor him, yet already he’d become accustomed to the number one ranking he’d popularly been voted, the ultimate stature he’d long since secured. He was used to facing the best beneath a microscope. Every movement he made was measured behind narrowed eyes, every word he uttered was weighed against those that came before and those that someday might. When he conquered, they said it was to be expected; when he faltered, a matter of time. He expected it when he became their champion; lordship was a road he’d travelled before. This foray was the same, yet different, exacerbated exponentially because he was the first of his kind. In the past the legacies had already been established, and though he set out to exceed those whose footsteps he followed, the parameters of grandeur were predefined. This time, as the inaugural Universal Champion, the potential was his alone to distinguish and demarcate. It was with that in mind that he’d begun the Universal tour. When challengers were selected he met them on their terms, in front of their fans, in the ring they called their home. If Universality was to become more than a fancy moniker, its champion had to steward every territory of the realm, every arena was equally his kingdom, every ring his throne. After bringing Primetime Championship Wrestling and the Dominant Wrestling Organization under his sovereignty, the committee overseeing the title finally succumbed to his demands, granting him autonomy over future direction. In another first for the world and Universe at large, the champion would handpick his challenger. For him it was a simple choice. He was well aware of his dominion and the inhabitants therein. In strength, in cunning, in skill, in will, he knew he exceeded them all save one. There was one man, an oft-overlooked and almost forgotten veteran, who would prove a challenger in more than name alone. So it was that less than a month later, in March of 2003, Angelo Deville stood heaving in an Old School Wrestling ring, before a capacity crowd of screaming fans and innumerable eyes at home. Across the storied canvas waited the evil supergenius Alan Kriegman, as wearied from the epic battle as The Deville himself. There had almost been an unjust end before the match could really get going, when Kriegman landed wrong on the outside and fell unconscious. The referee had reached a damning nine before The Deville intervened, rousing the challenger and raising him to his namesake. It would have been an easy win, another foe fallen, but it would have damaged more than Angelo’s pride: it would have tarnished the opulence of the legend he was creating. Instead The Deville let Kriegman recover his wits; he made the match continue. They’d been struggling and striving, lunging and parrying, outwitting and countering for slightly more than half an hour. Logic and physics dictated that they should be immobile from exhaustion, but with every set of hands stammering applause, every vocal cord quivering with shouted excitement, their vibrancy made a mockery of human limitation, subverting mortal bodies with interminable spirits. Angelo was used to that, but his opponent was another story. Kriegman refused to be baited as no other had, sticking to his strategy no matter what Deville did. It was as if Kriegman was fighting someone else altogether, his game-plan consisting of frustrating his opponent through neglect. Deville had never encountered the like before, had never fathomed the execution, but experiencing it, rebelling against it, he couldn’t deny its effectiveness. Hiding his frustration beneath a veneer of nonchalance, Deville circled inward and met Kriegman in the centre of the ring. He transitioned from the grapple to a rear-waistlock, heaving Kriegman overhead and planting him on his shoulder blades, then twisting neatly into the pin. The count was broken by a shoulder at two, and as Deville rose, pulling Kriegman with him, knife-like fingers stabbed his throat. Vertigo overwhelmed his remaining senses as Kriegman rolled him over, cradling him for the pin. It’s hard to define darkness blurring, but as Deville reversed the cradle it was like the world itself was a living tribute to Doppler, every sensation phasing in and out. Twice he heard the mat being slapped, then again reality flipped upside down. Another two claps, another turning over, then again, and again, around and around the ring like sparring partners. With the deafening roaring of the crowd, the perpetual pounding of the canvas, the exploits of the night catching up to him, his mind and muscles numb, Deville lost track of which roll was his, of which way was up or down. A nanosecond too late he turned, but that immeasurable margin was all it took. The familiar sounds of the announcers screaming penetrated the claustrophobic din. The piercing chimes of the ring-bell, the suddenly suffocating volume of the crowd, the match ending and his consciousness of it, all of these things were to Deville like darkness to a robber. He lunged to his feet with a surge of victory as Sympathy for the Devil struck up its tune, then paused and paled, uncomprehending, at finding Kriegman upright beside him. It was the hand of the evil supergenius the referee grabbed and thrust into the air, not his deserving own. As crippling realization began sinking in, it was hammered home like a vice around his heart by the announcer’s call, his voice near orgasmic in timbre. "The winner of this match and NEW UNIVERSAL CHAMPION! . . . ALAAAAAAAAN KRIIIIIIIIIIIIIEEEEEEEEEEEEEGMAN!" He’d lost. The Deville had fallen. He wasn’t used to this at all.
