Glimpses of the past

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 They say that fire is beautiful. You can watch it spread across a room, or run along a wall, little tongues licking, sensing where to go like a living being. There’s something primeval about it, and something dangerous too. It’s a genetic memory, back to a time when fire was so important to surviving, the difference between life and death being the mastery of a substance that can kill you if you don’t respect it enough. When you’re close up to it though, the beauty is somewhat diminished. Watching it on television is not the same as being caught up in the middle of an inferno. When the fire is all around you, and fills every inch of your vision, you’re too busy burning to try and appreciate any level of aesthetic value. Sometimes it can come at you too quickly, and you can’t even understand what’s happening before it’s all over. It was like that with the explosion. We were on a routine patrol in Basra, though you can never really call them that. Every hour will provide something out of the ordinary, something that could terminate your life or lift your soul with an act of astonishing humanity. The people of Iraq have, to me, provided the two ends of the spectrum as far as it goes for what human beings are capable of. There are numerous stories of loss and of murder, of suicide bombs and mortar attack, but for the men and women that live or work there, you can outnumber these with the selflessness of those who have laid down their lives, or risked them to save others. It’s like a crucible, burning down everything to the concentrated constituent parts. I don’t know where it came from or how it happened, but the bomb went off as we were turning around to go back to the base. We’d had to go to the police station as an escort, and as we went to leave, my vision turning to searing white. Despite being blinded, now I can look back and almost see it in slow motion, with a play-by-play announcer describing the action. "Well Bobby, you can see that the bag containing the bomb has unleashed its contents, and the soldiers don’t look like they’re happy. They’ve only managed to half turn and the nails strapped to the side of the incendiary device are already hitting them, perforating lungs and tearing through flesh. Steven’s looking right at the blast, and here comes a nail! Wow, it’s gone straight through his eye socket and out of the back of his head, remnants of brain trailing behind it! That’s gonna leave a mark!" That’s how I died. There were eight of us inside that room, and two just outside, along with a few local policemen and a cleaner. All of us died, apart from one man, Shanty Jones, who was left on a life support machine. The building burned down. Apparently it was beautiful. *** Shanty Jones was born to proud parents on the first of May, 1976. The family home in New Jersey reverberated with joy, as the many friends of the Jones family came around with fruit baskets and little blue baby clothes to join in with the celebrations. Gareth and Cynthia were happier than they’d ever been before; two previous miscarriages had made them fear the worst for the future. Yet time has a funny way of changing things, and a healthy baby boy was the perfect tonic to their lives. Sadly, time’s funny ways are often followed up by those of fate, or karma, and the news that soon followed of Gareth’s mother falling seriously ill took some of the wind out of their sails. Soon it became clear that she was in her final days, and her son had to take the journey back home to Wales. A week passed, and then two. Mrs Jones was a strong character, and she would not let go of life very easily. In the end it took her over two months to separate body and soul, and by that time both Cynthia and Shanty had come over to be with their family. One thing lead to another, and before long they were settled in, back in the small town, enjoying being away from the commutes and the cities. It was almost like a vacation, and with Gareth’s occupation as a freelance photographer there was just as much call for the scenic landscapes of the valleys as there was for the portraits and high rise buildings that the city companies used for their brochures. So there they were, back in the homeland of Gareth Jones, just down the road from where he himself had grown up. It had been a nice childhood for him, and deep down he had missed the rolling countryside of the Welsh hills and valleys. Marrying an American woman had been a surprise to him at the time, but what can you do when love strikes you like that? Just as it made a good place to grow up for Gareth, so it worked well for Shanty. A long childhood seemed to keep his innocence and naivety intact into his teenage years, whereupon he started to grow a little sullen. He was prone to lashing out on occasion, and disliked his remote home, longing instead for the brighter lights of a city like Cardiff, or better still… London. From the age of fourteen there were three tempestuous years of gradually deteriorating grades, with Shanty’s effort reducing further and further until he was only passing two subjects, and one of those was just because he was the captain of the rugby team. Just before his seventeenth birthday he became the first person in the school ever to get a grade of an A 5 in Maths. The highest attainment grade with the lowest effort grade possible. Shanty was more proud of that, perhaps, than anything he’d done in school up to that point. But it wasn’t going to stay that way for long. He changed, and… and I can’t see why. Not every scene opens up for me, but it was soon after that. Something happened, and he turned his life completely on its head. Apathy made way for effort, and hatred turned to respect. He excelled in all his classes and even stopped shortening his name to ‘Shan’, something he’d done to get away from a name that he had said was a curse. His rise continued, excelling at University in Cardiff and then upon his graduation, again captain of the rugby team, he made his way at last out of Wales to London, and his destiny. *** So I know all of this, and yet I’m not really sure how I know it. Some things remain hidden to me, and some are as clear as daylight, as if memories have been committed to film and are being shown in glorious technicolour in the cinema of my mind. Beyond the history of my former comrade, I know that I’m dead and I know what caused it; a bomb packed with nails that ended up destroying the vast majority of a decent sized building. Yet, beyond this I’m pretty much in the dark. I’ve never been a religious man, so I wasn’t expecting there to be an afterlife. I’d considered it, but I’d preferred to think of life as a cosmic coincidence, something to be appreciated while it was there, but that I’d not be ‘waking up’ on the day after I died. Then again, I was hoping to die differently from this. Personally I was hoping to be shot by a jealous lover when I was sleeping with his supermodel wife on my hundredth birthday. Alas, sometimes things don’t turn out so well. Is this Purgatory then? I really don’t know. I’ve not seen anyone or heard anything in the conventional sense, but then again, I don’t have eyes, or ears, or even a body. It’s hard to describe. Time isn’t the same up here, or down here. I can see things, or perhaps a more accurate description is that sometimes I get shown things. I’m not even sure who’s showing them to me. If it’s God then they were certainly right about him working in mysterious ways. No matter what else I hope to see, it’s mostly the life of Shanty Jones. I’ve seen how he was brought up, and his life prior to meeting me in the Territorial Army. I’ve seen some of the things he did when he was in Iraq, some inconsequential things, and others that I’d rather have passed on, but I don’t get a choice here. It’s like a direct feed into my brain, or at least it would be if I had a brain to feed in to. It’s not just from back when we were in the army though. It’s the times since then. All the times up to what you’d call the present, and everything in between. *** Shanty Jones arrived home from the Middle East a changed man. For starters he was missing a decent amount of body mass, and had gained a good deal of scar tissue. One such scar punctured one cheek, following the path of a six inch nail, and came out of the other side, pausing at the time only to shatter the corner of his jaw, sending fragments through the skin that needed several operations under the knife of a plastic surgeon. Returning to the calm and serenity of Pontllanfraith after the endless weeks in Basra and the hospital were not easy times for Shan. He grew surly again, a fully grown bitter parody of his teenage self. This time there seemed to be no epiphany that would change him, and no way out of his downward spiral. He found himself drinking every night in the local pubs and bars, despite the strong medical advice that it could destroy the liver that had been damaged in the explosion. Pain medication and hard liquor were Shanty’s way of dealing with whatever plagued him, but they also lead to occasional fights and the police cells, where he spent many a night. They knew his father and they knew what he had been through, so he was cut more slack than a stranger would have been. It didn’t work though, as Shanty resented the special treatment he was given, seeing it as a way for people to ruin his attempt at self destruction. After another particularly hard night, Shanty decided to cut loose from what was becoming a recurring nightmare. He bought a ticket at the last minute, and went to visit the place where he had been born, taking a flight out to the East coast of America. *** I’m getting better at seeing things now. There are still those moments that are hidden to me, from Shanty’s youth and whatever he did to end up with us in Iraq, but I’ve concentrated hard, and some scenes have opened up to me. Almost everything he did since he arrived in New York has been revealed to me. I’ve seen him do the strangest things to lead him to where he stands now, poised on the brink of something that I think is perhaps what he was born for. I don’t understand where these premonitions come from, it doesn’t make any sense. Something’s happening though. Shanty Jones is important. *** If Shan thought that moving to America would change his situation then he was sorely mistaken. After a few drunken bar fights he started to realise that he was on shaky ground. He could stay indefinitely and there were no visa issues, but he needed something to occupy his time, or even better, a way to legitimise his desire to inflict violence upon people. So he started working the door at some of the lousiest bars and nightclubs he could find. He moved across the country, not wanting to stay for too long in any one job, not wanting for people to find a pattern. The money was fairly poor, but Shanty didn’t care about that. What he now had was an endless stream of drunks that he could provoke into swinging the first punch. After that it was simply a case of finishing them off and then if they complained, hey, the security videos show who instigated the whole thing, right? Oh, if only it were so easy. After working like this for several months, Shanty got careless and just plain attacked a rich kid who was trying to slum it by hanging out in dives. Shan could smell the odour of the fake on him, and for some reason it drove him to break the kid’s nose. He didn’t seem to feel any remorse, not at the time, nor when he was sentenced to spend a year in prison for persistent violent conduct and assault. Even there he wouldn’t let it drop. When two men came to him and said that he had better do their bidding, he smashed his forehead into the bigger man’s nose and then broke his accomplice’s arm. It was a statement that allowed him to be left alone in his cell, but it also cost him any hope he had harboured of making it out on parole before the end of his twelve month sentence. It all changed when this recalcitrant Welsh thug came to the attention of someone important within the prison. Not a governor or a guard, but one of the prisoners. A very well respected prisoner. A prisoner inside the jail for the crime of murder, and who had served just one year of his life sentence. Shanty got the note: "I want to see you – Paul". He was not a man you refused. *** "You came." "I had nothing better to do," said Shanty, looking at the cell wall just behind the head of the man addressing him. "Of course you didn’t," said Paul, with a laugh. "There’s not exactly a hundred different things to do in this place, unless you know the right man." "I’m fairly adept at killing time." Shanty’s voice was quiet, but it conveyed a certain degree of confidence; the feeling that he didn’t need to speak loudly to be heard and understood. Paul looked at his shaved head and admired the scars that littered it. Added to the ones from the bomb in Iraq were new arrivals from pool cues, broken bottles and old fashioned brass knuckles. "I’m fairly adept at killing…" said Paul, his voice making as if he were about to copy his guest, but then changing on a tone of low menace. He was actually surprised to hear himself say such a thing, but appearances had to be kept up. He had only ever killed one person by his own hand, but it wouldn’t do to have everyone know that. "Well, I hope that works out for you." Shanty stood there, his muscles relaxed but dangerous beneath his dirty green vest. Paul smiled again. "Mister Jones. I’d hate to see us get off on the wrong foot, because just looking at you I can see a few ways in which we could be of use to one another. Now I know, I know, you’re not really fond of authority figures, but I don’t want you to see me as one." "I don’t see you as one." "I guess I walked into that one, so I’ll let you keep it." Paul’s tone remained jovial and even tempered. He had prospered in the prison for a variety of reasons, and one of them was that his life before it had prepared him for just about anything life could throw at him. Amongst the other reasons were a series of coincidences and strange happenings that seemed almost supernatural in origin. "Either way, my friend, I think you’ll find that you won’t have any trouble working with me here. I’ll let you keep yourself to yourself, and in return perhaps I’ll just get to know you a bit better." Shanty raised a worried eyebrow, his first show of emotion since he had arrived at the prison. "I didn’t mean like that," said Paul. "So, where’s the catch?" asked Shanty. "Oh, I’ll think of one in the end, Mister Jones. In the meantime, I’m sure you’ll find that working with Paul Goodman is the easiest thing in the world." *** Shanty was very surprised to find that Paul was almost impossible to dislike. No matter what mood Shan was in, he somehow found it reflected back to him by this most unusual man. As such, they grew closer together, and towards the end of the twelve months of his incarceration, the Welshman had become a far more mellow and amiable fellow. He was still guarded around everyone whose name wasn’t Paul Goodman, but he was less likely to try to break limbs, unless there had been what Paul would call an ‘incident’. Most of the time the mere threat of Shanty Jones was enough to quell anything untoward that was happening. Everyone could sense it, that there was something odd about him. He moved with a strange graceful movement that belied his ruined visage, and men far bigger than Jones’s six feet and two inches gave him a wide berth in which to move. As the days of November ticked away, it was only a matter of weeks until Shanty Jones was going to have to rejoin society. He had been assigned officers to look after him in so many ways, and he was already dreading the paperwork, and in a strange sense, was dreading leaving Paul behind. Of course, nobody sentenced to life imprisonment would be getting out after a single year, but after being in the man’s company for so long, Shanty wondered how exactly he had become essentially a kingpin in the prison crime hierarchy. Paul just really didn’t seem the type. Nevertheless, any time a suggestion was given (for they were never orders), Shanty would obey without hesitation. Some seemed a little strange, but they must have a higher plan behind them. Until one day, the suggestion was a little stranger than usual. "I want you to deliver a message to my brother. He’ll be visiting you today in about an hour." *** Sure enough, one hour later, Shanty Jones was sitting on a stool, with a message that he had committed to memory. He had to deliver it to the man visiting him, who would apparently understand it all and respond. Quite why Paul couldn’t do it himself was beyond Shanty at the time, but maybe there was some sort of sibling rivalry going on. Ten minutes late, a man sat down opposite Shanty Jones, and the Welshman delivered his message. "Paul says that he’s sorry that he can’t see you today, but that I was to come instead. He sent me with this message. You’re not going to be able to defend it, you know, and I bet you’ve found something else frivolous to take up your time instead. Don’t worry about it though, as a replacement will present itself very obviously to you. It’s all going to work out very well." Shanty delivered his message and sat back, only then going over the words in his head and wondering about the ‘obvious replacement’ part. "Jah! I have decided zat defending ze GTT4 title is not vorth my time! I have grander plans, better designs and all ze vorld at my feet!" Shanty blinked once, and then again. Why the hell was this man speaking with a fake German accent. "Excuse me. I’m sorry but I think I might have given this message to the wrong man. You’re not related to Paul Goodman are you?" "Jah! I am related to Eegor, zat good for nothing little scoundrel. I swear he only comes here to hide from his proper duties as my assistant!" "Woah, he was your assistant?" "Of course! I am ze very famous Doctor Curiosity! He served me for years!" Shanty sat back and tried to think things over. This was very strange. Very strange indeed. "A replacement though, eh? Who did he mean? Surely not Logic, zat useless lump, or Rana Venenosa… amateurs, ze lot of zem!" Shanty was shaking his head, knowing exactly where this was going. He’d do a lot for Paul Goodman, but this one might be asking too much. "Who else?" asked the doctor. "Vhere vould I find someone who… oh!" A smile cracked the insane face wide open. "I do believe ze guard told me zat you vill be released soon…" *** I don’t know what Shanty Jones imagined his release from prison would entail, but I doubt he was expecting to be entered into a wrestling tournament at the behest of the previous winner’s brother. I also doubt that Paul would have expected Shanty to show much patience in dealing with the good doctor. In a funny way I feel it was perhaps payback for something, though for what I don’t know. I’ve not been shown anything that could shed light on the matter. Their story seems to have already been told. The future though, that belongs to Shanty Jones. *** "You’re wrestling a giant Mongoloid called Belial. You vill definitely need my assistance in ze form of some Curious Device!" insisted the doctor. "I think he’s Mongolian, not Mongoloid." "There’s a difference? Nein! It matters not. I have been creating a distraction based upon a holographic representation of a giant horde of Genghis Khan! It vill get ze Mongol’s attention, and you can strike!" "I don’t need your help, thank you. I’m just doing this for Paul." "Nein!" said Curiosity, pacing around the hotel room. "Look, why haven’t you entered yourself if you’re so concerned? Why have I taken your spot?" "Vell, I have other projects going on." "Other projects?" "Jah! I am running ze vorld’s first wrestling orphanage." "A wrestling orphanage?" asked Shanty, incredulous. "Jah! I have seen so many wrestlers in zese tournaments, and a shocking amount of them have had their parents die at a young age. In fact, I did an analysis via my Curious Correlation Machine, and it said zat if a child’s parents die then it is a HUNDRED times more likely zat zey vill become a famous professional wrestler! It’s sometzing innate! The trauma scrambles a part of the brain guarding emotional responses, but opens up the part I have named ze ‘wrestling cortex’. So I have decided to get ze poor orphans early and train them until I have an army of devoted wrestler-slaves! Muwahahahahahaha!" "You’re insane." The doctor looked around shiftily. "Nein! It is true!" "I don’t need your help." Shanty grabbed Curiosity by the scruff of the neck and dragged him towards the door. "You need my devices!" "There is no one person or machine that I need. Especially not you." The doctor was deposited outside the door. "Nein! You must use my skills! I von ze GTT4!" "Don’t need it," said Shanty, slamming the door into Curiosity’s face, and wondering just what the hell he had let himself in for. *** But that’s not all. I’ve seen glimpses of the past, and they were strange. I’ve been able to answer so many questions about Shanty Jones, but new ones just spring up in to my mind. I see things again, and I don’t know if they’re the past or the future now. I don’t even know if I exist within time or outside of it completely. All I know is that Shanty Jones holds a key. And there is more to all this than meets the eye.
