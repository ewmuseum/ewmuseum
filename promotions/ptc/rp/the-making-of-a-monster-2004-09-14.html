"The Making of a Monster"

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 To Whom It May Concern: Please read this. Please listen. Please understand. I’ve done something terribly, terribly wrong. Something that will probably haunt me – and the rest of civilization – for many years to come. Even thinking about it now brings me a chill and feeling that I may not be able to return to normalcy for the rest of my pathetic, sinning life. Now, to explain what I’ve done. I’ve created a monster. And now…now I can’t control him. *** [The room we enter is one of assumed wealth and providence. Which is to say it’s pretty fucking big. As soon as we enter, so does a tall, slender man with a lithe and speedy gait. He has a phone in his hand, chatting in an airy manner.] “No, not one hundred, one thousand! Do you think I’m going to make any money off of a measly hundred? I didn’t get where I am today by betting pennies and nickels. You have a lot to learn, James.” [Soon after a young child strides in after him, much in the same manner. An excited look is on his face.] “Dad, dad, I have something to show you!” [With barely a flicker of recognition, meaning the eyes as they roll into his head, the tall man sighs.] “Can you hold on for one minute? I’ve got a pest to deal with.” [He puts his hand on the receiver and whirls around at the little boy.] “First, don’t call me dad. I’m not your dad. Call me Mr. Jetters, and do it respectfully, for Christ’s sakes. Second, I don’t need to be shown anything. I’m busy. Never talk when I’m on the phone. When I’m done, I better not find you or you’re going to get a whacking like you never felt before.” [Without any more words, he whirls back and begins to speak again.] “Yes, James…no, just the brat. You’d think he’d be happy that I feed him and shelter him, but he’s always got to butt in somehow.” [And with the same lithe manner, the man leaves the shot, leaving our focus – the child – with something of a tremble on his lips. We expect him to begin the waterworks in seconds…] [But he doesn’t. The sadness in his face is washed away immediately, so quickly that it could have been imagined by the viewer. Then he walks out of the shot in the opposite direction, and the camera follows him.] [He walks down the narrow hallway quickly, as if eager to see something. As he reaches the end of the hallway we see that the door leading to the backyard is wide open (he’d be beaten for that if Mr. Jetters ever saw), and the child walks through and kneels down right outside, picking up a stick and staring down in something of awe.] [As the shot pans, we see what the child wanted so desperately for Mr. Jetters to see.] [A dead cat. And not just a dead cat.] [A stabbed cat.] [The wound is long and deep, and it doesn’t take long to search for the implement responsible for the mark, as the small pocketknife lies, still extended, on the side of the dead animal.] [The child smiles widely as he pokes the cat with his stick a couple of times, checking to see if it really has shuffled off the coil. Then – and until this time all of this could be stricken off as coincidence – he lets out a laugh. A horrible, cold laugh.] [Meet Reginald Atkins, age 8.] *** I know it sounds trite, overdone, cliché. But that doesn’t change anything. That doesn’t make what he does any less horrible. Words don’t cheapen the effect. I could call him a monster just like countless people have done in the past; it doesn’t mean that he won’t cut out your heart at a moment’s notice. It’s all my fault. I adopted him when he was 4, mostly for publicity reasons. You see, when I was a young man I was often in the media as I was a representative for a local broadcasting firm. I made speeches and presentations and whatnot. And my advisors told me that I’d appear more personable and down-to-earth if I had a child. No woman will have me, and now I know it is for good reason, so I decided to adopt a small child whose parents had died months earlier. It was very charitable, and everyone said they were so proud of me for taking on that responsibility. It was nothing to me; anything for your name in the papers, right? I was a terrible father. For the first few years, I actually tried to love him. God, how I tried. But I couldn’t. I didn’t care about him the slightest fig. He was my tool to the people, we were planning a career in government before everything started happening. As he got older, I actually began to despise him. Before he was around I would spend my free time sitting, sipping tea, just being a single and free man. But… “Mr. Jetters, play a game with me!” “Mr. Jetters, look at this thing I found!” “Mr. Jetters, spend time with me!” Never a free moment. Never. After a few years, though, he finally realised that I wasn’t there for his entertainment. I was there to keep him alive, and to my credit I accomplished that. I gave him good food, a good home, and the best education money could buy. I thought I was doing him a favour by ignoring him; I wouldn’t want my privacy being interrupted by someone who isn’t even my real father. I saw it that I was raising him like I would a dog. Give him the essentials and hope he raises himself. I guess I’m not apt at raising dogs or children. *** [Mr. Jetters is the first thing we see this time, older and more gaunt. He does not carry the shine he once did, as his face seems irrevocably molded into an expression of distaste. There is not much time to dwell on what has caused this condition, as a knock on the door diverts both our and his attention.] [Jetters looks at the door with anxiety, knowing and dreading what is coming. He hates this, hates it all, just wishes life was as simple as it used to be. It had gotten complex over the years, but now it would be a conundrum that possibly had no solution. His political life in shambles, his life ruined, and still here outside forces were giving him one last kick while he was down.] [With a heavy sigh, he opens the door.] “Hello?” [And he backs up as a young teenager rushes into the house, his eyes covered by black sunglasses. For being such a young age, he seems almost as tall as Jetters and much more massive. He snorts at the owner of the house and walks through, no words spoken. Jetters turns to the man who has just entered, a small and timid sort of chap, and smiles weakly.] “So this is…” [After a slight pause in which he tries to vocalize his confirmation, a word finally escapes his lips.] “Y-yes, sir.” [Stealing one last gaze at the teenager, the timid man zooms out of the door. Jetters closes it slowly and walks over to the young man, staring awkwardly.] “So, you’re the new arrival, are you?” [Unlike with the first child, Jetters actually tries his hand at formalities.] “Oh, what is that stench?” [And fails.] [Jetters turns up his nose further while going to inspect the boy for anything that would cause such a horrible smell.] [Maybe it’s the blood that’s now on his hands.] “B-blood? Young man, is this…is this your blood?” [The young man stares at Jetters and snarls.] “Nein. Meinen Schwestern.” [And with that, he leaves. Jetters stares at him, perplexed.] “Miney sweaters? What does that mean?” [Then comes a cold, slightly high-pitched voice.] “It’s German, pops.” [The camera quickly pans to the direction of the voice, where we see Reginald Atkins at age fifteen. It is clear by his mannerisms that he knows he owns this house. He lies back on the steps lazily, a cigarette in his mouth. Jetters looks incensed.] “Wha…? Hey, Reggie, you can’t smoke in here.” “Don’t you wanna know what he said?” [Reginald exhales, smiling. He takes another deliberate puff and smiles.] “Because I can tell you.” “You speak German?” [Atkins rubs his cigarette out on the stairs, which gets a muted reaction of rage from Jetters.] “I dabbled here and there. ‘Meine Schwestern’ means ‘my sister’s.’” [Suddenly and quite conveniently there is a crash from the other room. Jetters looks to the direction of the noise and cringes.] “What have I gotten myself into?” [Reginald Atkins merely smiles and rests his boots up on the stair post.] “I like the kid. He’s got spirit.” *** With the two of them together, there was no use fighting. They say that karma comes along to bite you in the ass if you’re not careful, and I guess I wasn’t. What goes around comes around, the good-humoured person would say. But I’m far from fucking good-humoured. The key element in this reaction gone horribly wrong is trust. I trusted in myself and my abilities to balance two lives, or rather ignore one life while living another. My trust was misplaced, obviously. And now they aren’t kids. They aren’t teenagers who break car windows and steal purses from ladies on the street. They’re adults, they’re independent, they’re criminals. And they’re on the loose. God help me, what have I done? *** “Trust me, Shaman: you’re gonna have a right old time getting past this first round.” [Present day, finally. And we see something that is vaguely reminiscent of our previous picture. Reginald Atkins, now an adult, is leaning back in a chair, feet up, hands behind head, wide smile on his scarred face.] “Not guaranteeing victory, mind you. Not pumping my fist in the air, promising my emergence into the second round. I’m just going to promise you one thing. “You’re not going to find a harder challenge in the first round.” [He shakes his head as if to confirm this. Or negate. Whatever.] “You see, not only do I have trust in my abilities, but I have trust in my friend here. Now I’m going to do a Tony Montana and introduce you to my little friend.” “Ich bin nicht kleine!” [And there is our boy. Grown, bigger, but still wearing the shades, meet Lyell Jett. He growls into the camera.] “I know, Lyell. I was just quoting a…forget it. “Yeah, I know what you’re going to say. He’s not fighting you, I am. You’re right. Only one of us will be in that ring with you. But that’s not exactly right. You see, I’ve spent over ten years with this man here, and I know his nuances, his subtleties, everything about him. I have trust in this man to save me out of any situation I get in if it happens to be too big for me to handle. It usually isn’t, by the way. “But let’s say that you, Shaman, give me a run for my money. You push me to the limit. It doesn’t seem like I can beat you. I have an advantage. I have Lyell. I trust him to help me in any way possible. Even if he doesn’t hit you with a chair or something equally asinine, his encouragement, presence, and trust will help me find the strength I need.” [He slaps Lyell on the back and smiles.] “So you might be good at giving pain, Shaman, but just stop and consider. I am not too shabby at hurting things either, and I certainly lack the remorse to care if my friend decides to show his prowess at pain inflicting either. It might not be enough to topple the might WWA star, but just know, and I’m going to say it again: “You are going to have a right time trying to get the W against me.” [He smiles widely and opens his palms to the camera.] “Trust me.”
