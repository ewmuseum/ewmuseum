TTD: Invincibleness~! -v- The Corporation

21 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
La Envidia Mata Look at the world, my friends. Watch in apathetic wonder as evil and corruption cover the Earth and swallow it whole, devouring it with a ravenous hunger; an insatiable appetite for that which is pure and innocent and good. Watch as the world of decent, hardworking men and women is destroyed, replaced by the black hearts of scum walking the land of the Lord. Drug dealers. Mafia. Rapists. Murderers. I see them strolling through crowded and empty streets alike, at all times of the day and night without a care, justifying their vile actions by calling what they do “human nature” and “business as usual”. They only try to survive, after all. Who am I to judge them? Who indeed. I am the punishment laid down upon the world of the living; the flood to purge the Earth sop that it may become fertile and vibrant once again. I am the rain of death, the burning sulphur at the hearts of evil. I am the righteous infliction of hate and vengeance. I am hell with a halo. I am he whom God hates, yet cannot exist without. I am his servant, yet he hates me with more spite than the evil which I cleanse. I am a thorn in his divine side. Am I… gasp… the “devil”? No friends. Far from it. I am no demon. I am just a man with vision, passion, and conviction. I am that which the blind lack. I am La Envidia Mata. Welcome to my cold reality, dreamers. Pathetic. A disgusting waste of God’s divine power. This man beat his wife to death when she discovered his mistress. He caught her throat in both hands and strangled the poor woman when she screamed out. She was dead in minutes. His mistress, a filthy whore who answered to Rose, fainted as the other woman’s eyes rolled back in her head and faded to nothingness. Rose, too, had been guilty of infidelity. The man died by my righteous hand with the sins of everlasting torment dragging him to hell, kicking and screaming. Rose will be serving twenty years for murder when she is found, out cold, clutching the pistol unnaturally in her fists. Twenty years, if she doesn’t find a decent lawyer. I was there, hidden in the shadows… watching it all unfold. And, like a wind of death, I tore through. There was not a trace of my existence left. The woman was innocent, yet… her sacrifice was necessary. If she did not die, two of God’s fallen children would still blemish his land. So many have perished at my bidding. My hands are stained black from the blood of the evil and my eyes red to strike fear into the hearts of men. My soul is filled with God’s burning hatred. And thus, I walked away from the bodies of the fallen, to the Spirit of the City. It was my time of the night. The moonless, cloudy sky howled as bitter winds circled; its jaws snapping at the exposed flesh of my face. Cold. So cold to the warm hearted fools crowded inside that cesspool of humanity in front of me. Hundreds of them packed into a run-down hold; sardines line up, side by side, waiting to be devoured by a hunger more ravenous than their pitiful lusting for wealth and pleasure. He’s been waiting eons to swallow those he hates whole. God is starving, and he’s clawing at me along with his duped oysters, reaching to bring me into the fold. But I won’t go. In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. I don’t ask for forgiveness. I don’t ask for respect. Just watch, Holy Father, as your world becomes lighter by another few souls thanks to me. Watch as the new angel of death does what you cannot. I had one goal that night, in that disgusting hovel of vermin; to wade through the filth, to saturate myself with the diseased and plagued social lepers, and become invisible. He told me to wait until it was over. Wait for one man. The room was stiflingly damp, lit dimly along the walls by small pot-lights in a shallow trench surrounding the room. The dirty concrete floor broke onto a light wood paneled area, where a sea of bodies rose and fell to the droning, hypnotic beat of music. All of them belonged in this cesspool. I should burn it to the ground. Flickering lasers and purple beams flared and flashed, seeping through the cracks in the honeycomb of diligent, brainless drones as they danced to the monotonous music, dragging their fingers through the thick air. Dance tonight, you disgusting rats; if I had my way, you would all be dead. Running my hand over the polished oak bar, I strode to the far end of the room, wanting to keep as far away from them all as I could. I needed the shadows. I was invisible among them until the wind of death sent me along my way. “Hey,” the bartender exclaimed as he laid already glazed, simple eyes on me. “Weren’t you here last night?” For a slob serving the lowest forms of life on this planet, he was reasonably well dressed. It didn’t fool me; a rat is a rat no matter how expensive or fashionable his coat. “No.” I didn’t have time to waste on this one. He could already be here. “Really? I thought I-“ “Shut up.” Taken aback, the alcohol pimp stared at me with those dim, glossy eyes. He was already drunk, just like half of the people there. “Pour me a drink. Scotch on the rocks.” Without saying a word, the bartender quickly poured the drink, sliding it into my hand with a scowl. “Be careful. Your face might stay that way.” “Yeah, right buddy.” It will if I kill you the next time you stare at me. He must have been watching as I walked to the outskirts of the club, slipping into the shadows. Let him watch. When I choose, I’ll be the last thing he sees. Standing out among the drunks and cocaine junkies was one man. He was young; vibrant jade eyes, thick chestnut hair and a certain air about him. He seemed to fit in with the faces and body language, as if he’d known to live alongside these types his entire life, but his eyes were so much more alive than the rest. He was panning around the club, searching the environment for someone. Someone… and then his eyes locked with mine. I don’t know what it was, but a sinking feeling grew right then in my stomach. I wasn’t afraid, but… Invisible. I backed away from the bar, breaking the stare as I waded through the flowing mass of bodies. Managing to conceal myself among them, I continued through to the other side and returned to my search. Two booming voices wafted over the music close to the section in which I found myself. They were different. They were… familiar. I knew these two voices. Pressing through a line of people, my eyes met two black suited men, one larger than the other by a substantial amount. Yes, I knew them. A young couple brushed past me. I thought about grabbing the man’s arm, but I caught a glimpse of jade eyes and chestnut hair, felt the aura of the same man from a short while ago, and decided against stopping him. A woman with sunset hair draping past her shoulders had her arm linked with his as they stumbled through the crowd, pushing past with ease. I turned my attention back to the two suits. The smaller suit’s mouth dropped when I pushed past the last person. He gaped, tugging on the larger suit’s sleeve. As the larger one turned, his long greasy black curls left a trail of hair oil along his broad shoulders. Like the smaller suit, his jaw hit the floor. “Me cago en la madre,” he exclaimed breathlessly. “Envidia, what the hell are you doing here?” His name was Duo. At forty years of age, his Italian accent was as heavy as ever. The little one was much younger. I couldn’t remember his name, though; he was only with us for a short time and mainly served as Duo’s protégé. “I’m waiting… for the moment.” I took a second to look Dup up and down. “You’ve let yourself go.” He laughed heartily, clapping the smaller suit’s shoulder. “Not all of us can stay so perfect in two years, but you! You haven’t changed at all.” “I am the same person I was then.” His joking expression faded to a wry smile. “Is that so?” Staring at me with plain brown eyes, Duo shook his head. He commented on something else, but I couldn’t hear his mumbling. Two years since I last saw him. It’s been two years since it collapsed entirely. Now, everyone was alone, surviving with the skills we learned together. God, I wish it could have continued. It’s a shame we taught ourselves so well; it’s been tough tracking them all down. “What are you really doing here?” “I told you.” “I see. La Envidia Mata, for some reason I don’t believe you. Let’s just get one thing straight; I own this place. This is my business. Don’t go kill someone and f*ck this up for me.” “Kill, Duo? Perish the thought, old friend.” He knew me too well. “I know it must be hard for you with all these people, given what they all mean to you.” The scum of the Earth. “I have exceptional self control.” Nodding, Duo tilted his head to the smaller suit, whispering a comment. He pointed out into the crowd of people, answering with a whisper of his own. Duo looked back at me. “Envidia, if you’ll excuse me for a moment, I have to conduct some business.” “I thought owning this place was your job.” “Well, that’s part of it.” Straightening out his suit, Duo brushed a speck of dust off of his shoulder that wasn’t there. “I have to accommodate my guests. Just being a good host.” The small suit subtly pressed a small, clear plastic badf into his palm. There was a white substance in it, packing the bag tightly. Duo caught my eye focused on his hand and quickly tucked it back. “It’s a sample of the house’s finest for that redhead who was just here. You know,” Duo wagged a finger at me, “I think she was working with Quatro.” I didn’t care about the woman. “A sample of what, Duo?” He smiled thinly. I returned it, only mine was not pleasant. It better not be what I think it is. Not you, Duo. You and the rest were my brothers; you knew what I wanted to do with the power we had. The world was corrupted and diseased with the pursuit of pleasure and greed, with raping, selling sex, drugs, and the degradation of good people. I wanted to cut evil away like a cancer, so that the good may flourish. “Duo…” “What,” the thick Italian burst, “you’re still on about your f*cking quest? Envidia, that was the only problem I had with you; that stupid dream. If it wasn’t for that, we would have stayed together. It wouldn’t have fallen apart! The eight of us could have done so much, made so much money, held so much power…. We could have ruled the world!” He’s not the same man I knew. Duo gave into temptation and became corrupt. ‘So much money,’ ‘So much power,’ ‘rule the world,’ just the way the fallen children of the Lord think. Greed is his master now and what’s worse, Duo is leading others away from him. He is my enemy; he who was my brother, who fought alongside me… must be cut away with the other cancers. Perhaps my wish of burning this disgusting haven will be realized. “I am the same man you knew, Duo. Through and through. You know what I must do.” “We should have killed you a long time ago. I would have saved some time.” Threats never bothered me at all. Talk is cheap. That’s rule number three. “Come on, Envidia; let’s go for a walk. You can bring your drink if you’d like.” “I’d rather do it here. Since I want to end this thing quickly before Canadian Perfect and myself fight in the next round of the Tag Team Title Tournament against Invicibleness where they will see that in this ring I am God and I have no equal. Why? Because La Envidia Mata.” Why not end it with an audience? Once I’m done with you, all these people would be fish in a barrel. Terrified fish. “Have it your way, Envidi-“ The smaller suit drew his weapon before Duo had a chance to finish. It’s a shame they didn’t think I had my finger on the trigger the whole time. He was dead before he hit the ground. Rule two; patience is the greatest virtue of all. The fish scattered at the explosion, panicking as they flooded towards the exits. There were screams of terror and fear filled gazes; none of which were directed at either myself or the man with a gun to his head. “Envidia,” Duo whispered once the club was empty. A haze remained at eye level from the smoke and lights; still flashing. His lips trembled. My hand was as steady as it always was. People say that just before someone dies, you can see God in their eyes and they no longer fear death. The windows to their souls open and they pass on to another place with God’s love cleansing their wounds. I think not. The same greed, fear, and self-serving ambition remained pulsing through Duo’s veins until the very moment my bullet ledged itself in his head. “For Thee, m’ Lord, for Thee,” were the very last words Duo heard. I used the payphone at the back of the club, dropping a quarter in from the bartenders’ brandy snifter. “Quatro,” a deep, commanding voice answered. Just the man I wanted. “Duo is dead.” “Excellent,” he purred. Things must have gone exactly as he predicted. “Did you meet him?” “Yes, of course I did. He’s dead, isn’t he?” “I wasn’t talking about Duo. “ Then, who…? “His name is La Envidia Mata. I’m sure you know that name.” Impossile. Fate would not have been so cruel to let a man like him… that monster… to let him walk under my nose and slip away so easily. Impossible. God may hate me for what I do, but He is not without a sense of justice. “He wasn’t there.” “Oh, but he was. He was there along with another person you and I know all too well.” “Who?” “Kristine.” No. Impossible. I would have known it was her; there was no way I would mistake my own… The couple leaving the club. It hit me like a slap in the face. Her hair was a kaleidoscope of sunset and shadows, accenting the soft curves of her body which I hadn’t seen in over a decade. She was the spitting image of her mother when she had her youth and sanity. Absolutely beautiful. The other… chestnut hair, jade eyes, a thin jaw line goatee, and a calm, profession air about him. That could not have been him. Impossible. “He would have been with Kristine. He was her bodyguard.” Impossible. I was looking directly at him. I did it twice. I did it twice. “How do you know all this?” “I assigned him.” ‘What?” He must be joking. Impossible. I don’t believe it. “I did it so that the two of you would meet. I know what you would have done to La Envidia Mata. You and Kristine would have been together again, and La Envidia Mata would be dead. I could have killed him… it’s not my place. He has done nothing but serve me right from the start. I would have been guilty of the same crime as he.” “This is impossible.” “Hurry up and get down here. I ran into some trouble.” …La Envidia Mata and Kristine… right there in front of me. Impossible. “What kind of trouble?” “A woman… and now she’s in a coma. Her brother is after me and money doesn’t seem to work on him.” “Then run. Run or fight.” Survive. That’s rule number one; live to fight another day. Survive and do what you must tomorrow. “Good idea. Either way, get down here. You and I have quite a bit to talk about.” “Indeed, Quatro.” “I’ve really missed that name, Otto.” “I haven’t missed that one at all. Goodbye.” I stepped away, letting the receiver hand near the floor by its cord and made sure to avoid the steadily growing pool of blood. Canadian Perfect Chris Wrestling [I'm not a gambling man.] [Well...] [I'm not a gambling man in the traditional sense of the term.] [It's very rare I'll ever squander my money on anything other than essentials, or what I deem to be essentials. Food. Water. Clothing. Housing. I don't like to leave my money up to games of chance and luck, because if I do, I might lose everything I have. You would never find me in Vegas betting on black jack, or watching a Roulette wheel spin. I wouldn't say it's because I have bad luck, or that I just don't have enough money to give away. The truth is, for the most part I do have money, and for the most part, lady luck is usually right there by my side. There's just something about leaving sh*t up to chance that bugs me...because cards, dice and wheels with numbers? There is no skill to that, it's pure dumb luck. Sure, you'll hear someone brag occasionally about their skill…but it’s bullsht.] [I’m sure you’re all snickering about the “liking my luck remark”] [Stop] [Sure, there have been instances lately, where....I haven't had the best of luck in 4WW. I guess most recently I could point out the appearance of 4WW Management disqualifying me in my match with Jerms. I'm not even going to pretend you people don't know how that little ordeal turned out. Also, just as recently, is the appearance of a team in PTC known only as Invicibleness. The team has a certain mystique around them, and most of the PTC teams have been trying to figure out how to beat them.] [So my luck? While most often I'd consider it good, has recently shifted into the gutter. Lucky people don't have their first match in a tournament as a loss. Lucky people don't have their XWF Corporate Title stripped away from them. Lucky people don't wind up in the bottom rung of a tournament for their Tag Team Championship title. Which I guess, is why I'm not the gambler in the traditional sense. Because, quite frankly? I'm to busy gambling with life to waste time with money.] [Not to sound like a junkie] [But it just seems more fun, to gamble on sh*t that matters] [Yeah, if I could back...You know, had some giant rewind button on life, I'd go back and try and change what has happened. I'd go back and prevent Illuminati from ever beating The Corporation, that way when we beat Invicibleness we could go on to the Tag Team Title Match and it would never have taken what we worked so hard to gain.] [Aside from the Illuminati sh*t...] [I'm constantly gambling on life.] [First of all, we've got the whole move thing. Not just walking around, but the sheer fact that I pick up a guy and slam him on the back of his head, that's a big chance right there. Everyone's different, and there's no guarantee that I don't kill the guy and go to jail for murder. Then again, there is that little thing, where sometimes I get dropped on my head, and assaulted with steel objects. No one has ever told me that I won't get hurt, and chances are one day, I might not be able to go on any more. Like gambling in any casino, we take big risks....we get even bigger pay offs....And, that's only the stuff that happens in the ring. How we approach a crowd with our voices, how we try and market ourselves...face it, we're big old slot machines spinning around just waiting for little gold bars to match up.] [Placing myself into a situation where we had to fight Illuminati was a gamble, because we were unprepared for the tournament. Going into a pay per view with Sean Summers, a man who I had never beaten in a singles match, was a gamble. Teaming with La Envidia Mata after a long time, was a huge gamble. I make a lot of choices daily, that could result in a possible career ending situation. I don't mean to sound like my life is more important that yours, but choice is a real gamble that defines life...and I guess you could say, that right now?] [I owe the house] [But I have my chance to beat the odds, and beat the house.] [That chance is the Tag Team Title Tournament. An eight team tournament that concludes next week. Started off with matches between competitors in brackets. My bracket has four teams in it. Illuminati. Lunchroom Patrol. Invicibleness. But I'm not seeing names, oh no...I'm seeing chances, opportunities to advance in this division since we are too late to advance to the title match.] [I'm putting a lot on the line here too. If you look at past challengers, not a lot of men and women have been able to capture the belts. What's to make me any different from them. For all I know, this was my ride, and now it's over. But let's forget all about what I'm putting up on this. Because I'm not worried, the only way you'll find me sweating, is if it's hot.] [Illuminati. Lunchroom Patrol. Invicibleness.] [Tell me, do any of you know what probability is?] [Let me break it down for you. Probability IS chance, just with mathematical equations to back it up. Silly little theorems that make it true. For instance, if you flip a coin a hundred times, at least one of those times will be head. If you roll a dice six hundred times, at least one of those times will have a one on it. Gamblers use it sometimes to figure out their odds. Because there is no better way to clean up, than to beat the house when the odds pay off the most. With probability backing you up, you only increase your odds. If your the roulette wheel spins everything but a 3 for a hundred turns, chances are, sooner or later it's going to be a three. So that's what you bet.] [But probability doesn't just work with who we'll be facing. Sure it might help, but Las Vegas fight promoters use probability to make bets, to set up the odds on who will win a fight. Tyson and Holyfield. Ali and Foreman. All broken down into tiny little statistics, the closer the numbers, the better the odds, the less the pay offs...but the further the numbers apart, the better the pay off. I have a feeling that if wrestling was a sport that people bet on, that the Tag Team Title Tournament would be a hot topic on the minds of the gamblers. Heh. Kind of funny, gamblers using their money, to bet on those that gamble regularly with their lives.] [Wrestlers are gamblers without cards and dice.] [Wrestlers are just gamblers, who deal with a different currency] [Wanna know how the house ranks you?] [That's something like how the rules of probability would go.] [Something.] [Because we all know one thing. That probability just factors in numbers. JUST numbers.] [One important thing is left out. What is that one important thing? Skill.] [Push away all the numbers. Forget the theorems. Forget the laws of the universe. Forget all about luck, chance and anything that goes against the grains of reality. Because wrestling is no more a game of probability then it is a game of chance. Because one thing rises above all of it, and decides what happens in that blue squared circle. One little factor must be added to each equation, and quite frankly, you can't put that "thing" into any numbered form. And what is that one thing? It's not name value. It's not how many belts you've held....it's not even how long you've been in the federation. It's not about your heart, or your loyalty to the company. It's one simple little thing; ability. Quite frankly you could have all the luck in the world, and it won't mean a thing against the man who is just more talented than you. Hope and pray as much as you want, this is a game of talent and ability, and there is no way you can compare to us. Invicibleness will know that I am the greatest technical wrestler in wrestling, that I'm Wrestling and that above all I am absolutely Perfect.] [Would you expect anything less?]
