Search for the Hidden Meaning

31 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Could you at least let me wipe the blood off the handle?” The questioning voice belonged to Brook, overweight butler extraordinaire to PTC’s angriest Englishwoman, Lacuna Firenze Debris. The pair stood in a hallway of Lacuna’s Washington home; Lacuna cradled her slim and toned arms around her favourite possession. Her grip on the object was tight and she bent her head down towards the floor like a defiant child. “Nuh-uh” She didn’t even meet Brook’s eyes as she swayed the item gently. This had been going on for some time now and both were beginning to degenerate. Lacuna did what she always did and took a step back towards childhood. It seemed the logical step to her, but then again logic was never her strong point. Brook was beginning to lose his temper. “For God’s sake Lacuna, it’s a shotgun not a teddy bear” This caught the attention of Lacuna and she swung her deep brown eyes up towards Brook, who crossed his arms defiantly across his chest. A sudden anger glowed in her eyes. “Steve is more of a person than you could ever be, Mr. Brook. He’s always around to help me out” she spat. “And he’s probably better looking than you too” That one hurt, you could see it slowly dawn across his double-chinned face. He didn’t understand her love for Steve the shotgun, and it was quite possible that neither did she. It was just one of those things that seemed to happen, and they had become very attached as of late. There was an obvious difference in their relationship, Lacuna and Steve that is, than that of Lacuna and her fleeting ownership of Quoth the Raven. At times she would spend hours with Quoth, her fascination would last for about a month before she would get bored and look elsewhere. But Steve, however much Brook hated it, was here to stay. “That was uncalled for” he muttered quietly, turning to leave the large corridor. The room seemed longer than he had remembered earlier, with Lacuna’s deep brown eyes glaring a hole through his back. He admitted defeat to himself and let his mind wander to the other things he should probably be doing. There was much cleaning required of a head butler, and he had to arrange the visit of a builder, because Lacuna had decided to build a new ‘shed’. A shed to Lacuna consisted of a large building upon the outskirts of her estate of one large room which would be used to store things she was bored of, but not yet wishing to be rid of. It wasn’t technically a shed, but that rarely bothered her over active conscience. Lacuna eyed the figure of Brook as he waddled across the red carpet down the centre of the hallway. He had tried to avoid the tiled flooring to the sides of the carpet as usual, to save his cleaning staff a job. This was part of her problem with Brook, as he had probably never been spontaneous in his life. Spontaneity was a trick Lacuna had learnt from Jade once upon a time, and she held in high in her regards ever since. She turned her eyes down towards Steve the shotgun, which glistened in the light provided by the huge arched window above the door, at the end of the hallway. The handle was a large, ornately carved lump of solid oak which she generally used to beat the living daylights out of people who she didn’t like. The barrel was smooth and silver, reflecting the light with a clean shine. It was splattered with dried blood. The handle itself was coated in it. This was her quest for the meaning of extreme. It was never a word she had really understood, and rarely an act that she had participated in without driving herself insane with rage first. The idea of being violent and malicious came naturally to her, the temperament of Lacuna allowed her to play pretty much any role she wished. Her mood swings stopped her from owning a definable personality. It didn’t, however, allow her to become an extreme person. Indeed, she was extreme of wealth, political views and even friends, but Lacuna wasn’t too sure if this made her an extreme wrestler. What could a mass of bloodstains prove? Bloodshed was as natural as birth, and that Lacuna had brought it about seemed only natural to her ever-changing mind. But this bloodstain was different. This was the bloodstain of righteousness. To anyone who didn’t happen to be Lacuna, it was a deep red smudge on a very expensive looking shotgun. But Lacuna didn’t value conventional thought. Conventional thought acted as a propaganda machine and doctrines the views and opinions of the individual to a specific ideal of a world leader. Lacuna also failed to have any trust in those with power. Some may call this paranoia, and some would be correct, but to her it was rigid individualism and she was very proud of herself for using an intelligent word and applying it to modern day circumstances. But anyway, I digress; this was indeed no average bloodstain for indeed, the bloodstain of righteousness was present and correct on Steve’s ornately carved handle. ‘Why?’ I hear you ask. Why is a very good question. Why is a good way of delving deep and inferring the truth from any situation. It was a question Lacuna pinned her values deeply onto. The Bloodstain of Righteousness way her search for the truth from the world, the search for the meaning of extreme and the reasons why anyone would even bother with such a stupid concept. For gods sake, she often chided herself, why in gods name would anyone want to get seven shades of shit beaten from themselves just so they could reserve the right to call themselves extreme? Confused? It’s not a difficult concept. Lacuna wanted the truth. She looked for her owns truths and trusted the word of nobody but herself and the occasional individual who followed a similar line of thought. The Bloodstain of Righteousness represented her search and her findings, her delving and her triumphs, her shortcomings and her agony. Nobody would truly understand why, but the human mind is unique and so impossible to interpret. But to Lacuna, extreme was more than a word and a wrestling cliché. Extreme had a meaning, a psychological relevance that made people give themselves up. She wanted to find this: She needed to know why. She ran a finger over the matt red of the Bloodstain of Righteousness, its smooth dry finish contrasted the glossed varnish of the handle and the touch excited her slightly. The questions raged within her mind, their answers separated by the void she had to delve so deeply into. Lacuna wasn’t sure is she was feeling philosophical or just confused, but the two seemed to have gone hand in hand recently. Her eyes clouded over in thought as they unfocused on the hallway allowing the ornate decorations, oak furnishings and general expensive things she liked to keep in the hallway, to fade into a blur. Her tanned hands fastened tighter around the smooth metal and varnished wood which she cradled still. Brook had gone, and the sounds of the evening meal were loud from the kitchens. If she were to find a proper time to think, then it would have to wait for a while. The head chef, Brown, was bumbling again, and raised voices echoes down the hallway. Apparently Brook had decided to stick his nose where it had not been requested and the shouting snapped Lacuna back to the world with a sudden shock to the system. Now she had a domestic to sort out as well as some major thought to be undertaken. She had let her quest for extreme slip her mind again as she softly placed Steve back into the glass case reserved on an oak table. Her black combat boots stamped loudly on the cold grey tiles beside the red strip of carpet as she barged her way down the long hall, ignoring the expensive paintings which usually delighted her attention at her time of passing by. With a swift kick from her right boot, she belted the heavy oak door to the kitchens open and readied herself for a major kicking of employee shins. Extreme could wait, she had herself a domestic.
