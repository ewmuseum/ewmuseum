IG29 - The Scientist (FA)

11 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Deep within the dense jungle of Santa Fe la Vieja lies a heavy steel hatch, beneath which a million years of secrets live on. Below the hatch over two thousand people once lived, thriving from the natural resources of their habitat. But as the years progressed the resources have become scarce and the population of this undiscovered civilization has fallen drastically. Fearing for their lives the small group of survivors invite a select few Argentineans to help save their dwindling society. * * * The dark laboratory was lit only by the dim blue glow of a fly zapper on the rear wall. Beneath the glowing light a long stretch of machinery beeped and buzzed as it output the results of ongoing experiments. Printers rolled off long reams of paper that dropped in to baskets deliberately placed on the ground, monitors displayed detailed graphs and charts. Around the expansive room several test tubes rested in racks and Bunsen burners warmed boiling tubes filled with a spectrum of colors. In the center of the room three large glass cylinders stretched from the sterile white floor up to the high sterile white ceiling. Within the cylinders three motionless humans floated, their feet just a few inches from the ground, black masks screwed in to their scarred and disfigured faces. One of the humans, the only female of the three subjects, had two long tubes firmly attached to her nipples. The tubing reached down to the bottom of the glass cylinder and in to a metal slot which exited the cylinder and in to the ground. Across the room, sitting at a microscope, sat The Scientist. One hand scribbled away at a notepad that rested alongside the microscope whilst his left hand twisted a dial on the side of the microscope, carefully adjusting the focus. The Scientist sat back in his seat and used the thumb and forefinger of his left hand to rub his eyes. He was tired. Having been wandering around the same dark laboratory for almost two full days without any sleep he had began to see things. Things that simply could not be there. Facts that simply could not be facts. He got to his feet and wondered across the cold laboratory floor towards the printer at the back of the room. The whirring of the mechanism within the printer had ceased only moments ago. He tore the sheet away and looked at the most recent results, shaking his head. He dropped the paper and stood still for a moment, staring at the wall in front of him. The Scientist was not an old man, but the skin on his face and the hair on his head would suggest otherwise. His black hair had begun to recede a few years ago and was now a good two inches further up his head than in his days as a student at Universidad Maimónides. And he felt old, too. Maybe it was the five years spent underground with only his subjects as company. Or perhaps it was the things that his subjects had shown him in the five years of experiments. But one thing was for certain – If he stayed here for much longer, he would almost certainly die. Set at the opposite side of the room was a large metal structure. It was several feet higher than The Scientist and black in color. Set in the center of the heavy structure was a door, locked with a revolving padlock. Only The Scientist knew the combination. This was his own personal experiment. The Scientist sauntered towards the structure fastening his laboratory coat as he went. As he reached the door he pulled the round glasses from his forehead and dropped them on to his nose. He spun the lock around, entering the five numbers to release the bolts that sealed the door. Click. The Scientist heaved the door open and entered the dark chamber. By the side of the door a small table rested. On top of the table was a thin torch, which The Scientist picked up and clicked on. The thin stream of light bobbed around the chambers floor eventually resting on the naked body of a large black gentleman. The subject was tall and powerful, his body toned to perfection. In place of his eyeballs were two black spheres which bulged out of the sockets. Wires securely bound with electrical tape were attached to the spheres and cascaded down the man’s chest and down to an extension cable which rested several feet in front from him. He was strapped in to his seat heavily by thick metal straps, padlocked to the steel chair, which in turn was bolted to the ground. He had no fingernails and seemingly never had. The same applied to his toenails, nipples, and bellybutton. In his mouth, sealing it shut, was a transparent mouth guard. A clear pipe stretched from the front of the guard and behind the subject, up in to a drip bag that stood on a tall metal stand. Two more drip bags stood at each side of this one, each attached to one of the subjects arms, one contained a clear solution, the other a luminous blue liquid. The Scientist carefully examined the needles in each of the subjects’ arms and checked the connections of the eyeball sockets and the mouth guard. He then checked the levels of the drip bags and gave a quick nod. He unclipped a chart from the back of the subjects chair and made a few notes before placing it back on the chair. Finally he placed his hand on the back of the subjects’ head and pushed his face forwards. Set in to the back of the subjects’ head a cap covered a two inch opening that stretched through the skull and directly in to the subjects’ brain. The Scientist unclipped the cap and pulled a long corrugated pipe from a hook on one of the drip bags and pressed it in to the subjects’ skull, twisting it until it clicked. He then turned around and pressed a green button on a small knee-high machine that sat between the bags and the subjects’ chair. The Scientist smirked as he meandered around the subject’s chair. He shone the torch on the face of the subject and watched as he screamed in anguish. Through the mouth guard the muffled screams of the subject could barely be heard. His fingers stretched and contorted in pain, his toes curled in anguish. The skin around his eye sockets peeled back in agony, beads of sweat began to form across his entire body and his entire body shook. The Scientist stood on a button set in to the ground alongside the extension cable and a whirring sound filled the room, shaking the steel walls of the structure. The eye sockets of the subject hissed and the smell of seared flesh wafted up The Scientist’s nostrils as the two spheres began to glow a fiery red color. After precisely sixty seconds the room fell to an eerie silence. The whirring slowly wound down, the buzzing of the small machine behind the subject grinded to a halt and the blood curdling screams of the subject came to an abrupt end, his head dropping to one side and his entire body slouched as much as the secure fastenings would allow. The Scientist looked pleased, a wide smile crossing his pale face. “Muy Bueno…” Beamed The Scientist. ” Muy Bueno.”
