IG 41 || Jacob McKail || OBSESSION

THE MORNING AFTER THE NIGHT BEFORE Some cheap motel room || New Orleans, LA || Now… Ugh, McKail’s mind belched into rudimentary consciousness, then: what the fuck happened to me…? His brain pounded against the inside of his skull violently and without mercy, his gut churned and lurched with an entire lack of stability and he gagged a little, his throat dry and frustratingly uncooperative. Being a borderline alcoholic himself, McKail was understandably well-versed in the potential consequences of a heavy night of self-induced liver poisoning, but this was extreme even for him. What the hell did I drink last night…? He sat up in his concrete-hard motel room bed and winced as every muscle in his body ached and his brain smashing against the inside of his skull intensified it’s attack. Or more to the point, what in the hell didn’t I drink last night? With movement, his stomach reacted quickly, the liquid contents of which bubbled uncontrollably and threatened release, but did not merely threaten for long. Ignoring all other pain, he removed himself from the bed with urgency and headed toward the bathroom within the blink of an eye, finding the toilet bowl without a microsecond to spare. However, with retching and inevitable expulsion, came a certain clarity of thought and brief, yet uncontrolled, flashes of memory from presumably the previous night. The dive bar…The numerous drinks…The illogical reasoning…The mixed up girl… The fucked up conversation…The threatening of suicide…? “Fuck…” McKail muttered to himself, reaching for a towel to wipe his mouth with. It was true, the details of the previous night had yet to fully return to him, but his subconscious screamed at him inside his head to do something, anything. Go find the bar! It demanded. Go find the girl! Go stop her from doing something stupid! Of course, McKail’s natural reaction was to resist his subconscious preaching’s. Without the necessary proof or the comprehensive memories to back his instincts up, he questioned the validity of it all, but his subconscious was more than willing to persuade him. This is big! This is important! It told him. This is a matter of life and death! And it’s all your fault… He rushed out of the bathroom and searched the place desperately for the previous nights clothes, locating them without trouble and slipping them on without effort. If this was the real deal, he was sure as hell going to find out. If not, well, he didn’t have a hell of lot else to do today anyway and he his conscience would be clear - or at least a little bit clearer. He darted out of his motel room without really much of clue where in the hell to go. COLD FEET Some local dive bar || New Orleans, LA || Last Night… “I really don’t see what your problem is, pal…” McKail ripped his eyes away from his tenth whiskey of the night and shot the bartender a silent, but questioning look. “Seems to me, the lady really cares you,” the bartender continued, drying off a glass with his bar towel, “isn’t that reason enough to be happy?” “Guess so,” It was clear the bartender spoke sense, but these days McKail’s life didn’t much involve sense; it mostly involved alcohol, “but that don’t mean I gotta like it!” The bartender shook his head disapprovingly. “Whatever, man. It’s your life…” McKail nodded and finished his whiskey. “Damn right it is! Get me another…” The bartender shook his head pensively. “I don’t think that’s much of a good idea…” “Strange,” McKail began, pushing his emptied glass forward a few inches, “I do…” The bartender sighed, but took the glass nevertheless and McKail smiled. Last thing he needed right about now was the liquor to run out… “I think the bartender has a point,” a voice to his right controversially announced. The voice was female and perhaps unsurprisingly McKail didn’t recognise it. McKail turned to find the woman sat on the barstool right next to him, which came something as a surprise to him. Strange, he silently considered, I didn’t notice her take the seat…The bartender pushed the fresh glass of whiskey in front of him and then it all became clear. He knew he was drunk, but he didn’t realise he was quite that drunk. Despite McKail’s natural ignorance to women and everything in general, even he had to admit she was stunningly attractive. Shoulder length curly brown hair, flanking a sharp and delicately freckled face; her smile was wide and suitably advertised her Hollywood white teeth and intoxicating laugh. Regardless of her beauty, McKail had no great desire to succumb to her charms. It’d been a year since he lost his wife, Mary, to cancer and ever since then he’d effectively closed himself off from the world; he didn’t much need anyone else and for a time, nobody else needed him. At least, not until Caroline walked into his life at any rate. She was Mary’s sister and after she was buried, Caroline saw that McKail was on a downward spiral, took pity on him and had helping him get his life together ever since. During that time, they got close despite McKail’s stubborn resistance. Even now, he wasn’t sure his relationship with her was an entirely good idea and felt that every moment in Caroline’s company was a betrayal to his late wife’s memory. Caroline tried to understand, but it was understandably difficult and truth be told McKail had no idea why she still put up with him. Now Caroline wanted to take the next step of their relationship to another level, she wanted him to spend Christmas with her and her son and McKail wasn’t entirely sure he was comfortable with that. But obviously both the bartender and the pretty girl sat next him didn’t see what his problem was and not for the first time in his life, he was beginning to regret sharing his troubles with complete strangers. “Yeah?” He asked, taking a sip of his whiskey, “About what exactly? The liquor or my alleged love life?” The woman produced a wry smile, seemingly happy that he as simply conversing with her, despite the slight, but nonetheless distinguishable, venom edge to his words. “Both,” she told him, taking a sip of her own drink. It was a murky-coloured cocktail of some kind, McKail couldn’t tell what. “You’re plainly drinking too much and you’re getting cold feet, simply because your girl wants to spend more time with you. Typical man…” McKail sighed. All of his troubles he thought were so big, summed up in just one castaway sentence. “Yeah, well, nobody ever tell you that everybody loves a bastard?” “We both know that’s not true, Jacob,” she began, “because only a handful of people, if that, still love you…” McKail’s eyes widened and alarms rang so loud it deafened him. She knew his name and yet, he never introduced himself, to her or the bartender. Of course, his face was splashed around on the Showtime network on a weekly basis, but the only people who bothered him out in public were the wrestling-obsessed lunatics who were convinced he fucked them over on that fateful day when he aligned himself with Clinton Sage and the Affliction. McKail glanced around the bar, sifting through the faces for a possible angry boyfriend just waiting to cause havoc, but he saw nothing of concern, which for some reason began to worry him more. “Oh sorry,” the woman giggled, hand over her mouth, “I didn’t introduce myself! My name’s Claire and I’m your biggest fan!” Oh fuck. CONFIRMATION The very same local dive bar. || New Orleans, LA || Now… When he arrived back at the bar, he realised it was actually much later than he thought it was. It’d hadn’t taken that long to retrace his steps and find the bar again, but he just hadn’t once checked the time and he’d wrongfully assumed it was still morning. By now, it was early afternoon and that brought a deeper sense of urgency out the pit of McKail’s stomach. Even without any kind of verifiable evidence, he was beginning to succumb to his instincts and he already feared the worst. On the bright side, however, this diverted his attention away from his galactic hangover the likes of which only Oliver Reed and a few others had ever experienced. He entered the bar, not really sure as to what exactly he was looking for to find the place as sparsely populated as it was the night before. McKail’s eyes investigated the interior of the place intently, looking for something, anything that might help him, but nothing stood out. It’s too late! He silently despaired. I’m too late! “I figured you’d show up,” the voice announced, somehow funnelled directly at McKail and striking a somewhat familiar chord. It was the bartender and once McKail had visually established as much, the meaning behind his words sunk in, not that they made sense. He scrunched up his face, confused. “Why?” The bartender shook his head and grinned. “Man, you left with that sweet-ass girl last night, but the state you were in, I didn’t expect you’d seal the deal, but obviously I was wrong.” What the fuck? “What the hell are you talking about?” This time it was the bartender’s turn to look confused. “Well, she came in earlier looking for you, I figured you’d left without saying goodbye or without getting her number or something…you sly bastard!” McKail rolled his eyes. “And? What did she say when she came by earlier?” “Not much,” he replied, reaching under the bar momentarily. He re-emerged with a piece of paper, which he held out between two fingers for McKail to take. “She just left this note.” McKail took the note and muttered, “Thanks…” He opened the beautifully scented and engraved letter up and read it immediately, although afterwards, he wished he hadn’t: Dear Jacob, I love you. I know that’s not what you want to hear right now, but I love you and I KNOW that you love me too. I know this must sound crazy to you, but you DO love me too, it’s just you don’t KNOW it yet. But I can prove it, I can prove that you love me. You see, by the time you’re reading this, if you ever read this, I’ll be DYING. I will have taken some pills, I’m not sure what yet or how many, but they WILL get the job done. I’m assuming you’ll have a hangover (you were pretty drunk last night) but eventually, you’ll get yourself together and you’ll remember last night. You’ll remember me and our conversation. You’ll make it back to the bar and you’ll get this letter. You will read it and you’ll come save me. You’re a good man, Jacob and I’ll PROVE it, not only to you, but to EVERYONE! I’ll love you always, Claire. XXX “Fuck…” McKail muttered, inaudibly to himself. Before reading the note, the danger didn’t seem real, it just felt like some bad soap opera with so many twists and turns in it, he didn’t know what the hell was going on. But the letter made it real and the full impact of its content hit him like a sledgehammer to the chest. He went pale instantly and nausea swelled within him, this time having nothing whatsoever to do with the hangover. “You okay, man?” The bartender questioned. “You look a little pale…” “I’m fine,” McKail lied, heading out of the bar. Pushing his selfish feelings of guilt and culpability to one side momentarily, he instinctively flipped over the letter to find an address; presumably her address. There was no question in his mind what he had to do next. Fuck the bad publicity. Fuck the police interrogation. Fuck the possibility of being the victim a possible and elaborate joke, crafted by the mind of a very sick woman. None of that mattered, not now. DELUSION Some local dive bar || New Orleans, LA || Last Night… “I’ve seen all your matches, you know…” Claire declared, staring at him in such a way it would make even Ron Jeremy blush. McKail rolled his eyes and took a sizeable mouthful of his drink. He wasn’t quite drunk enough to dismiss this potential lawsuit as danger entirely, but he was drunk enough to resist the urge to run for his life. Perhaps, it was because the whole thing managed to catch him off guard. After all, not many FUSE fans were actually nice to him. At least, not these days. “A lot of people I spoke to on the chat rooms and the forums really turned against you when you joined the Affliction,” she continued, seemingly unable to rip her eyes away from him, “but I told them all to grow up! I told them it was just a plan to catch Sage unawares and then take him out for good!” “That’s nice of you…” McKail muttered, as he finished his whiskey and nodded at the bartender for another. “That is what you’re doing isn’t it, Jacob?” She asked, doe-eyed and innocent. “You’re tricking them aren’t you? You’re tricking everyone?” McKail received his fresh whiskey just in time and took a satisfying mouthful. “I’m ‘fraid not…” She visibly deflated before his eyes. “But--but--you’re a good man! I just know you are!” McKail shook his head dismissively. “You don’t even know me…” Claire stood from her barstool defiantly, her angry posture less than subtle. “Of course I know you, Jacob! I love you!” McKail’s eyes widened. Danger, Will Robinson!!! “What was that?” “I, um, love you,” she sheepishly replied. “But please don’t think I’m crazy or anything! I’m not deluded enough to believe you love me yet!” She takes a step closer and her posture becomes sultry, caressing his arm gently. “That’s probably going to take a little time…” McKail finished his whiskey and stood. “Oh Jesus…” “Um, where are you going?” She questioned, eyes innocent. “Home,” he simply replied, already heading for the door. “Great!” She declared. “I’ll get my coat!” “Alone,” he added. “But--but were meant to be together!” She declared. “Not in this lifetime,” McKail responded, stumbling a little. Claire rushed forward and put herself between McKail and the exit. “Don’t say that, Jacob! Don’t say that! I don’t know what I’ll do!” “Get over it, I would think.” He tried to sidestep and get around her, but she countered it nimbly. “I don’t know if I can! I don’t know how I’ll be able to carry on!” “To be honest,” he began, shoving her to one side, “I don’t much care.” Tears began to well up in her eyes. “But I thought you were a good man! I thought that we would be happy together!” He reached the exit. “You were wrong.” “I’m going to kill myself, Jacob!” She yelled after him. “You hear me?” “Yeah, whatever…” He left. OUT OF TIME Claire‘s place || New Orleans, LA || Now… McKail found her on the bathroom floor. It’d taken him an hour just to find the place, but when he had, he’d discovered the door had been left ajar for him and didn’t even hesitate to burst in. He’d expect to see her waiting for him with a smile and the letter and threats of suicide just an outright lie in order to coax him to exactly where she wanted him to be. Or at least, that’s what he hoped it would be. He stood there in silence for the longest time, eying up her still lifeless body, noting the emptied bottle pills centimetres away from her open palm, a number of small, while circular tablets scattered around haphazardly. After fully taking the scene in, he shook himself free from the shock and bent down to cradle her head and take her pulse; he was no medic, but he’d seen enough episodes of ER to get by a little, or so he hoped. Her pulse was existent, but it was very faint. He reached over for a towel, rolled up and placed under her head and he was just about to go call for the ambulance, when she opened her eyes and spluttered into life. At first, it took her a few moments to get herself together, but once she had, fear spread across her face like an infection and she began to panic and struggle. “It’s okay,” McKail told her. “You’re going to be okay…” Her eyes fixed on to his own and she smiled. “I--*cough*--I knew you’d come.” McKail shook his head. “Don’t try to talk…” She ignored him. “I told you that you were a good man…” He was about respond when her eyes closed again, he feared for the last time, but he didn’t have the heart to check again. Instead, he rushed for the phone and dialled nine-one-one. McKail knew that she was wrong. He wasn’t a good man, he couldn’t be. A good man would never allowed this to happened; a good man wouldn’t be responsible for a innocent woman’s death. He searched her apartment after making the call and found himself a bottle of bourbon. A good man wouldn’t drink himself stupid, in order to avoid reality. He opened the bottle and drank as much as his body would allow; he didn’t want to be in this reality right about now… END
