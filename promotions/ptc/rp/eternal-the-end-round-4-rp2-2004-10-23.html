Eternal: The End (Round 4 RP2)

Date: 2004-10-23 Eternal: The End <><><><><><><><><><><><><><> A dark figure loomed over the light creeping in through the old wooden door, the darkness of the shadow raping the bright of its purity and mystique. The silence that enveloped the run-down house was disturbing, with not even the sound of the creaking of the wooden floorboards that were layering the ground. An appalling stench of filth seeped through the surrounding air, contaminating it in a deliberate but slow manner. After a momentary pause he stepped in, his steps creating two soft thuds in quick succession. The black boots grazed against the wooden floorboards with distaste, a sharp contrast with the glimmer and dull of the two surfaces. His lips were pursed with apparent disdain as he stood, his cold crystal blue eyes darting from corner to corner, recording every single bit of detail to memory. The unfamiliar environment caused his muscles to tense, sensing for the slightest bit of movement. A sudden sway of his right hand, and the door was slammed shut, the loud thump reverberating against every wall of the desolated house. The furniture was generations old, he observed. With a quick shuffle of his feet, he sauntered across what used to be a majestic living room, careful not to rub himself against any of the cobwebs stretched from the ceiling to the walls. “You’re finally here.” The voice came from the corner of the room, buried deep within the eternal darkness of the shadows. It was clear and concise, an aura of confidence that he couldn’t quite lay a finger on. Annoyingly, it disturbed him. “We are finally able to meet, Kasper Sky.” The voice continued its pace, with its owner drawing nearer, his footsteps threading upon the ground with symmetry. Kasper grinned to himself as he heard his name spoken, his fists clenching in reflex instincts. He had been waiting for this moment ever since he knew of his father’s existence from his recently found sister, Krystal. The father that he once thought was dead, was suddenly the main suspect of his mother’s death in his imaginative mind. With his hands clasped behind his back, the man appeared from within the darkness, flicking the switch of a table lamp that miraculously lit. Kasper winced, his eyes taking its time getting used to the newly discovered dim light which now gave a face to the man. He took his steps carefully, his blue eyes directly gazing at Kasper, a smile playing softly on his lips. His blonde hair was combed back in a slick manner, which caused it to gleam in the yellow light. Kasper’s silence amused him slightly, stopping his movement in front of the blue-eyed immortal, a distance of about five meters separating the two bonded by blood. “I’ve been waiting generations for this day to arrive,” he spoke with a chuckle, shaking his head trying sarcastically to rid the laughter. “And now that it’s here, I wish that it hadn’t approached this soon.” His smiled transformed into a cold stare. His amusement hastened to disappear, and Kasper wryly noted the similarities between the two. The same icy composure, revolting stares; they were almost identical. “I’ve come to pay your dues for you,” he spoke abruptly. “My dues?” The man replied, raising a brow as a sign of surprise. He slowly smiled as he raised his finger at Kasper, “I see that the bullet that I put through your chest ten years ago are still having its effects.” He grinned wickedly, his words cutting through Kasper’s mind like a jagged knife tainted with rust. Kasper grinded his teeth, his head clouded by his rising tensions of anger, just waiting to be unleashed. “I wouldn’t be so joyous if I was you,” he spoke warningly, squeezing the words out through his gritted teeth. The man gave a short laugh that echoed through Kasper’s ears and against the walls. He issued two piercing claps in a deliberate manner, causing two crystal blue eyes to arrow sharply towards his pale hands. “I should have killed you when I had the chance.” The voice spoke those words slowly, stressing upon each word that entered Kasper’s head like silver bullets through a werewolf. And subconsciously, a shiver ran up his spine. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> Vengeance. Infliction of punishment in return for a wrong committed. Before a month ago, I knew of not such a word. My world had always been stagnant and cold, revolved around the search of my sister. That search came and gone like the winter winds even though it spanned years on end. With the finding of hope in the form of a blood sister, along came other details which have enslaved me with self-doubt ever since. Trapping me in my cage of disjointed pieces of memories breezing through my mind with an air of ignorance, it pulled everything together in a sharp respite. I should have known that it would come to this. Sins that I never remembered making, had finally caught up with me like a whirlwind flooding through the intensity of my soul. Some say I should repent, to start the process of purifying my soul. Others say that I should be killed for the manner of my sins. I say that this is simply retribution. But not for me. This is retribution for all those sins that you had caused, propelling me forward as the dealer of death. Maybe you can’t understand the logic behind all of this. You say that I should be killed because I have sinned. But maybe I should kill you because you have sinned. And I’m just the medium for that to happen. This is just like the pebble and ripple effect. You are just there to bear the consequences. Like Token Weed, you stand no chance of putting one up against me. Like him, you will be defeated. I know you still don’t believe me. And I don’t blame you, especially since your life will be on the line. Give me a few days, and you will see Token Weed’s name plastered with the shame of the conquered. He will be overthrown, as will you. The only difference will be he being a drug lord spreading his own false propaganda, and you being the manipulative deceitful killer. Then again, there isn’t exactly that much of a difference. So you will be thinking right now in your fragile frame of mind, how can I be stopped? When will I be killed? By whom will I be overthrown? One word answers all the questions above. No. And then you will ask yourself – Who exactly am I? Again, there’s a simple answer. Deep inside, you already know. Yes, you are right. To you, I am Death. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> “We’ve led our lives through different roads, and fate has it that today our two destinies will intertwine.” The man shot Kasper a look that would have burned a hole through steel, as he slowly paced around the immortal, as if observing his prey. Kasper stood still with his head down, his eyes in thin slits recording every movement that the man made. “And fate has it that you will not live to see the day, that you will now suffer for your sins.” Kasper shot back in a mere whisper, his hands almost ready to snatch at the Beretta residing in his jacket. The same Beretta that he had used to kill Brock, the guy from whom he “bought” it from, was now sitting comfortably waiting to send a bullet through its barrel. “You really think you know me, don’t you?” The man sighed as he quizzed Kasper with a questioning look. “Well, you don’t.” “You talk of sins as if they were light, but you are right now carrying most of the burden of the load.” The man bellowed, his shouts flooding through the silence of the air. “You’re the one who had sinned,” he snarled, baring his teeth. Kasper smirked. “I admit, I had sinned.” The man shrugged his shoulders in agreement, “But that doesn’t mean that you will not die today.” He stopped circling, his eyes focusing on the back of Kasper Sky. “A father will not kill his own son. Especially after killing his wife.” Kasper turned his head slightly, glancing at him from the corner of his eye. The man’s eyes widened as he heard Kasper spoke those words, and for a split second there was clear sadness in his eyes, but that was soon replaced with burning anger. “Kill his own wife? What are you talking about?” The man asked in apparent confusion, a slight hesitation enveloping his mind. Kasper kept silent, not wanting to play along with his father’s games. The truth was already clear in his mind. This man had killed his mother when Kasper was just a young boy. “I have to say you’re wrong on both,” his father said, heaving his shoulders in sarcastic disappointment. “I will kill you.” “Don’t bet on it.” Even though Kasper couldn’t see his father all that clearly with only his head turned slightly, he was still able to tell his hand slipping to his back pocket and bringing something out. And in a matter of seconds, a gun was pointed directly at his head from behind him. Beyond his own emotional control, Kasper shuddered. “Maybe I will regret this one day,” the man spoke in a low tone, a confident sense of sureness in his voice. “I guess it’s time.” With a swift movement of his body that propelled him to a position facing his father, he grabbed the Beretta from under his jacket and pointed the cold barrel straight at his head. Their cold crystal blue eyes gazed at each other, unflinching. All it takes now was one quick squeeze of the trigger and one of them will fall. “You killed your own mother, now I will kill you.” Those words shocked Kasper to the roots of his soul, sending his unstable mind into imaginative overdrive. “You lie!” Kasper spat out, his body trembling and his fingers shaking. Yet those words lingered on his mind, as he desperately tried to push it out, fighting to keep control of his own thoughts. “You know I don’t lie,” the man replied coolly, tightening his grip on the trigger. “God will see that tonight justice will be done.” Kasper smirked, regaining his composure after taking his father’s words as lies. “I think God will sit this one out,” he replied curtly, giving a wry smile to his father. “Then I will do this deed for him!” His father screamed, taking a step closer in Kasper’s direction while further tightening his finger on the trigger. “You don’t have the right to play God.” Kasper whispered menacingly, his crystal blue eyes gleaming like icy bolts from the winter cold. A moment later, a thunderous crack of a bullet piercing through a barrel rang out. It echoed through the house, sending shudders against the worn out walls. Another second later, a body slumped to the floor. His blood slowly seeping through, staining the wooden floorboards a crimson red. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> Death. They say that death is just another journey. Another road that runs parallel to life. Another dimension where the dead try to repent for the sins they committed in the earthly world. Don’t believe them. I say dying means defeat. That there won’t be a single minute after death. Maybe I’m wrong. I’ll never know. At least until I die. Wait… Now that I come to think of it… me dying means defeat? Nah, that’ll never happen. I simply don’t lose. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> TO BE CONTINUED
