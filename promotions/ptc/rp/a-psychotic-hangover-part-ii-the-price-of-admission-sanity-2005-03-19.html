A Psychotic Hangover, Part II: The Price of Admission... Sanity

19 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-19 The muffled sounds of arguing coming from outside the van where I was currently seated came to an abrupt halt. I continued to just sit there as the silence continued without thinking about it too much at all. I was being abducted, or how I put it anyway, by these two crazy muscle heads, one of which I had yet to hear say anything at all. I had assumed, from the markings on the side of the van, and the way they were dressed, that they were working for some kind of insane asylum. Anaheim Hills Mental Institution, or something along those lines anyway. Not only was it bad enough that I let them catch me, but the one who had tried helping me get away was also someone I should remember, but for the life of me couldn't think of anymore than a name. To make matters worse, I had no idea why I was being taken away, well, maybe I did, but it was still confusing me as to who may have turned me into this place. Not only would being carried away like this look bad, but it may also prevent me from getting to the big event. That big event being Primetime Central's Seven of the Best; the biggest event in my career to date. I had already been through enough in this, the month of March, already. Losing my IWL World Title to Drake Coleman, losing part of myself, and now losing my dignity by being carried off like this. I had to get out of it, I just had to. Something as big as Seven of the Best was not an option for me to miss. So I took my first initiative, before the van even began moving. I started pounding on the doors on the back of the van to get some attention. But instead of anyone yelling at me, the doors had popped open with the final shots of my fist against the cold metal of the van doors. As I peeked outside, it was a strange sight. "Old Man" Spencer was sitting on the bench in front of his store shaking his head, and giving me the stare of death. The two muscles heads were being talked to by Lia herself, as if she had talked some kind of sense into them. I hopped down from out of the van and walked over to where Lia was at. She gave me a quick smile, and then nodded to the two asylum workers who simply turned around and walked off. The teenage girl scream that followed was accompanied by a hug to me, by Lia herself, and the arsenal was finished with a kiss to the cheek. Had I gotten out of it without a big fight at all? 'You okay back there?!' Were the words blurted out by Blaine "Old Man" Spencer. I began to wonder what the hell he was talking about. Lia's attention had also been turned to her father, but with a concerned look on her face. We then witnessed him pounding on the empty space on the bench next to him. 'Are you okay back there?!' He blurted out again, much louder this time than the first. It wasn't long before the questions going through my mind were answered. The sounds of two vehicle doors closing. I snapped out of it. 'What the hell is wrong with you? You did nothing but stare at that side of the van for the whole two and a half hours we were driving.' He said. He, as in, the big bald muscle head who's name I had yet to figure out. His sidekick and twin brother with hair was right next time him, as usual. I just stared at the both of them, is a daze, like I had just woken up or something. 'Well if you aren't going to answer, at least get out of there so we can get this over with. Oh, and make it easy on yourself this time.' I wasn't in the mood to put up a fight at the moment, so I did as I was told, an act very rarely done by myself... Doing as I am told. The two then grabbed one of my arms apiece, and proceeded to carry me through security, and all that good stuff, on our way into the inside of what will be known as my personal Hell for the next however long it is I'll end up being stuck here. I tried not to pay too much attention to the place as I was being checked in, and hopefully if I manage well, like I did in the back of the van, just clearing myself of all thoughts and allowing myself to fall deep into some kind of trance will make my stay here go as quickly as possible. Then again, if I wanted to get out of here quick enough, I would need to act as normal as possible. 'This is the guy. He fits the description perfectly.' Said a man, also dressed in white, but a different kind of white outfit this time. A doctor maybe? 'If he's as crazy as that guy who called him in says he is, I don't understand why they let him out of Southwind to begin with. Take him to his room. The doctors will be there shortly to examine this one.' He finished as he made some mark on a piece of paper on the clipboard he was carrying around. I was angry, and surprised, all in one that they had someone managed to find out information about me and Southwind. The mental institute I had willingly admitted myself to for a month nearly a year ago. It had helped me some, but not enough. I also had wished to just forget about that point in my life as well, so the mere mention of it had sent anger through me. I stared at him as he walked off, as if to accomplish something, but with no luck. My attention was brought back to the two muscle heads carrying me around as a needle was stuck deep into my left arm. 'Jesus... What the fuck?!' I blurted out as I stared at a mildly attractive blonde nurse who had just been the one to bury the needle in. 'That was an awfully mean glare you gave Dr. Hornung. This should calm you down some.' The twins laughed their robotic laughs softly as I began to feel a little relaxed. 'That should keep him quiet for now. You know, just in case.' Said the nurse to the twins as she walked off. The two then took me, as directed, to "my" room. The door was already open for me, and when we arrived, I was greeted with warm hearts, and cold concrete. The two threw me onto the floor, and whatever was in the shot they had given me had caused my reflexes to slow down tremendously, and I never had time to get my hands up before hitting the floor. My body, mostly my head, had bounced off the concrete upon impact. The door was closed behind me fairly quickly too. At this point, though, I began to feel almost weightless, a floating in a pool of water feeling. I felt no pain from the impact of my head on the ground, nor did I feel the trickle of blood running down the side of my face. I didn't care too much at all either. The high I was beginning to feel had caused any anger to be washed away inside of me as well, but hadn't taken the ability to think away from me. But how I was thinking didn't seem to be my normal state of mind. I crawled over into a corner, sat up, and brought my knees up to my chest. I began to scheme on how to get out of this, but for some odd reason my thoughts weren't staying in my head, they were being transferred into real words. Not a good thing when you are being monitored. But the way I was feeling, at this moment, didn't seem to bother me too much. The thoughts that were being said, at will, out loud weren't very clever at all either. Something a child, maybe of six or seven years of age may think of. What had those meany heads given me?! 'Maybe I can just wait until recess, and then climb over the fence and run as fast as I can home. That might work.' It would never work, but I sure seemed to think so at this point. 'Gosh, I hope this time out doesn't last for much longer. I have to pee really bad.' I was laughing at myself on the inside for the way I was talking, thinking too for that matter, but I just couldn't stop myself. I had turned into a child again. 'Where is that stupid-head doctor? I need to tell on those two bullies that weren't playing nice. They should be in time out instead of me!' More internal laughing at myself, and I'm sure anyone watching surveillance right now would be loving every minute of this as well. The way I was acting, I seemed as if I belonged here. I placed my head in my hands for a quick moment, as if to rest for a couple of seconds. When I pulled them back and saw the blood on them, and realized I was bleeding fairly good, I began to act like, you guessed it, any other child would at the sight of their own blood. But with the drugs they had given me, I didn't do much moving, nor much screaming at all, but I sure felt like I should have been doing both. The excitement, however, had wore me out a little, and put me back into a daze similar to the one I was in, in the back of the can on the way here. No daydreams this time, I just sat there, in the corner of the small room, staring at the door. At this point, time began to fly. The natural light from outside quickly went away, and the inside lights were forced to be turned on due to the night fall. I was certain we had arrived at about one this afternoon, and by a quarter to two, I was worn out, and began staring at the door. Four and a half hours had passed, at least, in what seemed like a matter of moments. It was then, at the moment the drugs felt to be wearing off, that the door to my room opened. The grey haired doctor from earlier walked in, clipboard in hand, and smile upon his face. I continued to sit there, in my corner, as he sat down onto the bed. 'Steven? Can I ask you some questions?' The way he had asked this question, was as if he was speaking to a mentally retarded child. I nodded my head at him, but with a glare on my face. 'Ok. But do you promise not to hurt me?' He continued to talk in a slow manner, as if I wasn't going to understand him. 'I'm not promising anything here doc. But for your sake, I'll tell you this. Don't go overboard with what you ask. You'll be safer that way.' I then wrapped my arms around my knees, which were still up at my chest. I buried my face into the pocket formed by my legs and arms, with only my eyes remaining to be seen. You know, so I could keep an eye on this guy. He kind of situated himself, maybe as if he started to feel uncomfortable with me now. He then started to ask me another question, but interrupted him before he had the chance. 'Oh, and yeah. I'm not retarded, so you can talk to me like a real man now. Like I said, you wouldn't want to do anything to piss me off any more than I already am, would you?' A question of my own in his direction. He shook his head at me rapidly as if to say "Hell no!" before marking some things down onto whatever piece of paper he had on his clipboard. He then began to ask more questions, with an uncomfortable tone to his voice. 'Well... Steven... Are you crazy?' Straight to the point, just what I was looking for. A smile came across my face and I lifted my head up as I began to answer this one. 'Who isn't crazy? There's always a part of someone that secretly drives them to do weird things, talk to themselves, or pretend to see things that aren't there. If you are basing stuff, such as that, as means to mark me a lunatic, then yes... I am crazy!' My expression went back to what it was before, as well as the placement of my head. Dr. Hornung continued to write stuff down. A few moments passed before he finally stopped. He then pulled a piece of paper out of his white coat pocket. He unfolded it and looked over it for a moment or two before asking me yet another question. 'Is it true you admitted yourself to a mental institution called Southwind in the state of Kansas about one year ago? If so, it says here you offered up the information about being psychotic, delusional, and suffered from multiple personalities. It was all true. I had admitted myself, worst decision of my life. Trying to turn myself into something I wasn't... How foolish of me. 'Yes, it's true. But I'm not as psychotic as some of the nut jobs you got running around here, I'm sure. I'm still intelligent and all that. I sometimes just can't control myself. As for being delusional... Yes, from time to time I see things that aren't there. And my split personality hasn't really been around in a good month or two. So as far as I'm concerned, he's gone.' I said, with all the truth in the world behind it. He nodded his head, and like previously, began writing some more on his clipboard. He then looks up at me, through those thick bifocals, and with complete seriousness in his eyes, asks me a question I never want to have to relive while trying to explain. 'You know why you are like this Steven?' All I did was bury my head into that little pocket formed by my arms and legs even further than it already was. I wasn't going to say anything. 'Steven?' He asked again, without really asking. Tears began forming in my eyes, but anger started to run through my body. I wasn't going to allow this man in white get to me, so I tried to ignore him. A few moments passed, and I could tell my ignorance was starting to annoy him just a tad. Although he's got to put up with this kind of behavior everyday, it still got to him. 'Are you going to tell me or what?!' His tone of voice was raised this time, and my head remained buried. I was not going to give him the satisfaction of prying it out of me. Then it happened. He wasted no more time. He blurted out something that set me off the deep end. He told me what he wanted me to tell him. 'It's because you witnessed your parents brains get blown out! Isn't it?!' What little of his face I seen after he said that was one full of shock. I don't think he realized what he had just done. Just like I didn't realize what I was doing as soon as he said it. As soon as I heard those words, the words of a story from my past that IS the reason I am like this today, I snapped. I was on top of him on the bed, my hands wrapped around his neck. It wouldn't be but another split second or two later, however, before the twin muscle heads were in there prying me off of the doctor. 'You stupid fuck! What kind of person are you, saying something like that? Bringing back the past like that! If you knew already, why the hell did you even bother asking! Remember this you punk, what happened here today was all your fault. ALL your fault!' The anger I was feeling was more than represented in my tone of voice. The doctor, stunned from what had just happened, scurried out of the room as fast as he could, but not before hearing my words. I was then thrown down onto the bed, and a nurse with a syringe in hand came in, and stuck me. This time it wasn't just to calm me down, this stuff knocked me clear out. I wasn't sure how long I was knocked out to be exact, but when I came to it was light outside, and I was on the floor. I looked around the room, still in the daze of waking up after a long period of slumber. My vision was blurry for a few moments, but as soon as I regained my sight, and cleared the sleep from my eyes, I noticed something, someone. A boy, young man if you will, standing in the corner of my room. Strangely enough, he looked familiar. He looked like... Me. Me at the age of eighteen. I quickly crawled onto the bed and back myself into the corner that the bed was positioned in. I was certain it was just another illusion, but why was my mind playing trick on me like this? What was it thinking about that I wasn't able to realize? The boy, me, started walking towards the bed. I tensed up; even though I knew exactly of the tricks my mind was playing on me. 'Steven, don't be scared.' Said the eighteen year old version of myself. 'I've only got a couple things to say to you. A couple of things you seem to have forgotten over the years.' I wasn't sure I wanted to even listen, but I didn't have a choice really. As the little version of me got closer to the bed, I sprung up, and moved over to a new corner, making sure he didn't get too close. Though he's only part of my mind, I've been known to hurt myself when these illusions start messing with me really bad. 'Go away; you've got nothing to say to me!' He didn't go anywhere except in whatever direction I was going. I finally situated myself in one corner, and just closed my eyes, hoping I would go away. Despite not being able to see him, my mind still allowed me to hear him. 'Steven, what you need to hear is that our parents are gone, and there's nothing we can do. We've been the way we are now since it happened. Must we both remember what we told ourselves the day we boarded the place to Tokyo? We made the pact to keep our parents in our thoughts, but not to let it bother us, nor let this new way of life we had to live bring us down. I look at me now, you, a little over 8 years removed from that unfortunate night, and I realize, despite what we've been through, we've got a good thing going for us. A profession we enjoy, and don't care what others think about us. We've been in the spotlight for a little over five months with IWL. We've got an opportunity of a lifetime right around the corner. Now is not the time to let our way of life get in the way of what we've been doing without being bothered for almost nine years!' And with that, the voice went away, my eyes remained clenched tight. I remained in the fetal position for another five minutes or so, afraid to open my eyes for fear of my mind continuing to play tricks on me. But I was brought to by the sound of an unfamiliar voice. 'Who the Hell were you talking to?' I opened my eyes, the eighteen year old version of me was gone, and the twins were standing there. 'Robert said you were talking to someone, so we came in to check it out.' He speaks! The one I've known as Franklin actually speaks. And the bald one actually has a name too. 'Yeah, who were you talking to?' Robert said. I just shook my head. I stood up and moved back over to my bed. 'Nobody.' I said with a smile on my face as the words I had just listened to, from myself, had begun to sink in. They both shook their heads. Robert spoke up again. 'Well, it's "exercise" time. It would probably be best for you to attend. Shake some things loose before your meeting with the head doctor later this afternoon. You have been asleep for about a week after all.' I was a bit shocked about how long I had been out when the two walked off, but I wasn't worrying about anything right now except on how I was going to convince this "head doctor" that I'm perfectly fine to be let go. Oh, and the fact that I was going to make it to Seven of the Best, and prove myself worthy to be apart of this profession of wrestling. Outcast or not, I belong. The scene fades to black.
