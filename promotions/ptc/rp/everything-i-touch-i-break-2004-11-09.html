Everything I Touch, I Break

9 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-09 This is a continuation of the previous story from Shadow's other GTT4 appearances .:: ::: ::. She would have called herself numb, but there was a tingling sensation creeping through her aching, tired limbs. In one sense, there was disbelief—disbelief that she had pushed herself so hard, so fast lately. In just the span of a few weeks, she had defeated Jonathan Sail in a match that left her feeling half-dead, no pun intended, been handed the Heartlands Championship by Garvin, laid out an open challenge for her own title, and defended it against Syberus at the last moment at El Dia de los Muertos. And yet again, she had come away with success. It would have been easy for her to start believing that she was untouchable. But even that much success was not enough to convince her. All the wins in the world would never stop the criticism that she could heap on herself daily, for hours upon hours. She’d made it to bracket finals. She was WMW’s best and brightest hope for making it anywhere in this tournament, and the pressure on her shoulders was heavy. She knew that whether or not they liked her, they all wanted her to succeed. They all wanted her to put WMW’s name out there—not out of a wish for her personal success, but out of some innate need to make WMW seem “more important.” Of course, she’d never won any popularity contests, so she could have guessed they wouldn’t offer any personal well-wishes. But them hanging their hopes on her for WMW success was a bit much—she didn’t claim to be anyone’s savior, and didn’t want to be. With knowledge weighing heavy in her mind, she stepped out onto the veranda. Despite the fact that she was dealing with a Ravnos, and that always meant ostentation to no end, she was impressed with Gideon’s estate. Napa Valley stretched out before her in the dim of the early evening, dusk hugging the rows upon rows of the vineyard below the house. The stone steps were cool under her bare feet, despite the fact that it had been fairly warm here all day, she supposed. She didn’t know too much about daytime temperatures anymore. She always saw the dark, cold side of the earth. Whether that was symbolic or not, she didn’t really care. She lived her life like a worm on the earth’s surface, only coming out into the garden when the moon was up. She sighed deeply. It had been several days since her last match in WMW. She was booked again against Scott Vyper, but right now, she wasn’t concerned about that. Another GTT4 match was looming on the horizon, and this time against the immovable mountain of Kasper Sky. She was again doubting herself, doubting her efforts, though she tried to will herself not to. Sky was sitting around having a pity party for himself over whether or not people had started to flock to her side as the tournament went on… who was he kidding? The vast majority of the dirt sheets were still shitting all over her and worshipping the ground that Kasper Sky walked on. It would be another uphill battle and she knew it, but that was part of the challenge, the part she could taste in her mouth. It tasted coppery, like blood, but bitter as well. Bitter because she knew that she’d always be the underdog, no matter who she was up against. It seemed to simply be the nature of proceedings: she’d always be the ‘dark horse,’ or the surprise of the night. But then again, why did she want anything more? Why would she even want cameras constantly shoved in her face? Why would she want someone scrutinizing her every move? Right now, her focus was vacillating. Last week, Syberus had told her that if she didn’t focus on her wrestling life, he’d beat her. She hadn’t listened to him. It was rare that she actually took what her opponents said to heart, although she often did find herself changing her own tactics based on the insults that they slung at her. With Syberus, it hadn’t been necessary. From the beginning, he’d let his ego get in the way of what was really going on around him, and that had been his biggest weakness. That had been what enabled Shadow to take him out and retain her title. But this week was different. This week, she knew that Kasper Sky was going to be doing the same thing she’d been doing for the majority of this tournament: taking care of his business away from the public eye. Making sure that the left hand had no clue what the right hand was doing, so to speak. Two could play that game. She wasn’t like Syberus; she didn’t need a crutch, didn’t need to stand around and browbeat her opponent about every little thing in order to make herself feel better. Besides that fact, there was a lot on her mind about her own life. In just the past week, she’d not only advanced herself in WMW yet again, she’d left her temporary home with Riven in Cleveland, jumped on a bike to Chicago with Gideon in order to flee from Geffroi LaBatte and his band of Assamite-hunters, and from there, come all the way out here on the back of a Ducati to Napa Valley, California. Since she’d gotten here, she’d been sleeping. Perhaps it was the exhaustion of so much work finally catching up to her, or perhaps she needed to sleep for a few days in order to get her head ‘back in the game.’ The reasons weren’t important. But this was the first time she’d been awake from a sleep that bordered on torpor for days. This was the first chance she’d had in days to look around at her lush surroundings and think about what had gone on. She still didn’t know much of it, since Gideon had refused to tell her until they were safe in Napa Valley. She wondered if he’d tell her tonight, or if he’d forever withhold the information. She sighed, and in response, the vineyard stretched out endlessly in front of her, mockingly enclosing her field of vision. There was no way out. No way to get out of this shitty situation, and it was all because, once again, she’d listened to people who were telling her what to do. She really needed to stop doing it, too, and she didn’t know how many times she’d told herself that, but… she wondered if she’d ever listen. That was odd, wasn’t it? Questioning your own head about whether or not you were listening to yourself ramble? But she’d never pictured herself as normal… or even particularly stable. Chalk it up to normalcy for a very fragmented head, and that covered things nicely. It was at that moment that the facts hit her, cold and hard in the face: at this moment, she was on a sinking ship. That wasn’t anything new. More often than not, she felt like she hardly had a grip on her own head, but the shocking thing was, in spite of all that, she was still managing to forge ahead with her life. Despite the fact that she seemed to have absolutely no clue where she was going, or why she had to go there, she always ended up there in one piece, and doing better than she had been before the journey. She supposed it was luck, but for once, she was tired of it. For once, she wanted to be the one in control, but it was all scrambling into a big mess: Cleveland, Chicago, Napa Valley, Riven, Julian Falcon, Geffroi LaBatte, Corvus, Gideon de Medici, WMW, GTT4, Assassins, memories, hallucinations, matches, duties, faults, fictions, facts… nothing was making sense anymore. She wondered how it had ever made sense in the first place. Again, she sighed deeply. She didn’t really have control over her own actions at this point, she was so lost in her own head. Or at least, one would hope that explained the reason why a three hundred year old vampire suddenly found the need to expel breath. “Beautiful women never look quite so beautiful when they sigh in that manner.” She started, and would have jumped out of her skin, if that were at all plausible. Trying to play it off, she turned around to him, attempting to wipe the surprise off of her face, but only succeeding in replacing that look with one that looked like she’d just swallowed a handful of lemons. Gideon stood behind her, forever imperturbable, or perhaps that was just something that he’d ingrained into himself. She didn’t particularly care. What she did care about was that it aggravated her to no end that he never lost his cool. Gideon’s eyes looked like burnished cobalt in the low light, focused entirely on her. She could feel them piercing through her, and in some strange way, it seemed like he was trying to read into her thoughts, but even he didn’t have that ability, no matter how much he liked to pretend that he knew everything about her. Of course, he did seem to know an awful lot, and she didn’t recall ever meeting him before. Either he’d done his homework, which was a little obsessive, not to mention unlikely; but the only other option was that she’d actually known him some time in the past. She almost preferred to think of him as some sort of creepy stalker, because it was easier than trying to come up with some time in the past when she could have met him, making memories out of the black void that encompassed her mind whenever she tried to recall him. He seemed unusually petulant this evening; usually, he at least retained his ‘smooth’ outer exterior. As he loped towards her from the other end of the veranda, the expression on his face became mercurial, shifting back into neutrality from peevishness. Perhaps she’d imagined that he looked to be in an ill temper. She could hardly tell, out in this darkness. Gideon leaned on the banister and watched her for a few moments longer. Without a word, he walked over to a nearby wrought-iron table that she hadn’t seen before. He distributed the red liquid from its bottle equally between two glasses, and carried one over to her. It was vitae, of course, and she lifted it to take a sip, suddenly feeling a great need for its strength. A strange scent caught her—not just blood, but something extra. She smelled it again, but couldn’t discern what it was; lowering the glass, she regarded Gideon suspiciously. He smirked. “Gypsy secret. It will only kill you if you ask what it is.” As if to prove it, he took a sip of the vitae in his own cup. Seeing that he didn’t turn into a toad or implode into himself, Shadow took a sip. The mixture was thick and heady, slightly warm from sitting out in the humidity—but it was tasty. She could almost feel the life beginning to flow back into her, after lying chilled and still in the bed of Gideon’s guest bedroom for nearly three days and nights. She closed her eyes, letting it run down her throat. Gideon smiled, not at all in a smarmy way. “See? I wouldn’t hurt you, amor, just trying to restore your health a bit, the best way I know how.” She blanched slightly. “Please don’t call me amor.” That was the last thing she wanted right now, people she hardly knew tossing around terms of affection. Or maybe she did know him well enough for that… she couldn’t truly say. In any case, she didn’t want anyone close to her right now. Right now, she wanted to make it on her own. As all this was running through her head, Gideon simply watched. He worried about her sometimes, although he couldn’t exactly put a finger on why he gave a damn. He was just doing what he was instructed to do, but here he found himself having sympathy for her. He started to shrug her comment off with something abrasive, but then paused. No, that really wasn’t how he wanted to handle this situation. “Lia… you worry me.” She was tempted to make the same face again. Worried? Why would he be worried? All he cared about was schmoozing her into the bedroom. Or was it? She turned to him, confusion writing lines through the jade of her eyes. “Tell me what happened back in Cleveland. Details. I don’t want to be spared anything.” He nodded. He could have figured that eventually, she’d be questioning him about what went on. That was just the type of being that she was, inquisitive about everything. Always seeking answers that she knew she didn’t want to hear, and getting wounded when the truth came out. He chewed on his lip for a moment, trying to think of some way to explain it that was concise, while still telling her the details that she wanted to hear. His hand instantly pinched the bridge of his nose, and he stared down into the russet liquid in his wine glass, thinking about how to begin—or rather, what to talk about, and what to leave out. “I don’t know how you managed to get yourself in so deep. It’s only natural to get a little put off when you realize that there ARE Kine out there who are slayers, but cutting and running from his mansion was your first mistake. Call it bad timing if you like, but he was very aware of your presence, and called a hunt… the equivalent of a Blood Hunt, I suppose. You’re lucky in that you cover your tracks well, but not lucky in that you flinch a bit too much for an Assassin.” She sat up a little straighter. “Come on! Don’t tell me that you would have stayed calm after seeing that!” He held up his hands in a conciliatory gesture that was probably largely futile. “Pax, pax… I am not passing judgment on you. In any case, when I found you, they were closing in on you. They would have killed you that night, came very close indeed to doing so, had Monsieur LaBatte not been such a terrible shot in his great excitement at seeing you.” She bit her lip, her eyes widening. And that had been yet another night when she’d spent more time than she wanted to admit entrenched in one of her visions… hallucinations… whatever you wanted to call it. She dropped her head. If he hadn’t found her… He nodded. “Right. If I hadn’t found you when I did, you’d be dead.” And if she were dead, he would have been up to his ass in alligators, in more ways than he could have imagined. Good thing he’d come along when he did. Finally, she screwed up her courage. “You were going to tell me something about Riven?” He slowly took the last sip of his vitae and set the glass down, each motion making its own crisp noise, as though the sharpness was enhanced by the silence of the vineyard and countryside. He stepped closer to her, the alternating soft light of the room and darkness of nature around them making his eyes swim in both navy and cerulean at the same time. He rested his hands on her shoulders, magnetically drawing her gaze to his. “There isn’t much to say about Riven. And to be blunt, there isn’t much left of him either.” Her stomach muscles tensed, threatening to throw her into a ball on the ground. But she doubted that she could have cried for Riven. He just didn’t understand. Gideon was waiting for an inevitable storm from her, but it did not come. She simply started to get a broken look in her eyes, which almost caused him to feel sorry for her again, She looked away from him. His gaze dropped to the ground as he continued. “There wasn’t anything you could have done. They knew that he was associated with you, and they made quick work of him. He was just a Sewer Rat anyway. I don’t know what good he would have served you, other than providing a haven.” “Just a Sewer Rat! Gideon! He was my friend!” He paused, looking up at her, a sharpness in his eyes. For a moment, he thought about snapping at her, but it wasn’t what she needed to hear. He bit his tongue, holding in the urge to get irritated. Shadow looked out across the vineyard again, feeling the cold, encompassing, whispering vines reach out, trying to speak to her on the wind. Her voice was dull and flat. “I guess I should go back and get my things when events have died down a bit. I don’t want to stay there with Riven gone.” The look in his eyes caused cold fear to crop up inside Shadow. “I’m afraid there’s not much to go back and get, Lia. They gutted it, and destroyed everything that was inside.. Well, except for your journals.” Her throat closed up before she could spout off her alarm about it. They had her journals? And they’d destroyed everything else? Gideon could almost feel the shock waves reverberating off of her. She was getting to the frantic point, and to that note, he wasn’t quite sure why she wasn’t scrabbling to get back to Cleveland. Not that he would have let her go. He’d already gotten her this far, and there was no way in hell he was letting her go running back into the lion’s den. His iron grip encircled her shoulders, holding her at bay. He forced her to look at him. “Listen to me.” His voice was low, urgent tones, swimming towards her ears in the murky deep of the night. She felt like she was shrouded in an inconceivable mist. “You’re not going back to Cleveland. The second you try to go back to that estate and get your journals, you’ll be dead. They’re waiting for you. In fact, they’re banking on the fact that holding your journals will bring you back.” “So I can’t go.” Her voice sounded distant and far away. But she sounded as though the idea registered, as though she understood. Vaguely, Gideon began to feel some sort of pity for her. He fumbled for words. He wasn’t used to actually being kind to the people around him, as opposed to using them for what he wanted. “Can I do anything for you?” She shook her head, and turned away. Her voice continued in a timbre as though it was echoing from a far away cave. “Just… please… I want to be alone for a little while.” He was almost offended. She was just tossing him away? But he supposed he could understand, at least if she’d actually felt some connection with Riven and her life back in Cleveland, though he didn’t see how that was possible. Shaking his head, but acquiescing to her silently, he slipped back inside the estate, heading to his bedroom. The veranda seemed to get cold when he left, perhaps just because of the absence of his physical presence. Was Gideon really that much to her that the whole world dimmed when he was gone? No, she didn’t think so. It wasn’t so much that she liked Gideon but that she hated herself. But was there any reason for that? She had to consider whether she was being rational or reactionary, but at this moment, she couldn’t put a name to it. Her head swam, the vineyards blurring in front of her eyes. Shadow……… She could have sworn she heard someone call her name. Was it a familiar voice? It didn’t sound like any voice she’d ever known, but it echoed, resonating deep within her soul. Lia……… Her balance slipped, and she just barely caught herself on the ledge of the veranda before smashing her face into the stones. A sort of shuddering sob broke forth, but she didn’t understand why there were tears springing to her eyes. Hadn’t Gideon been right? She hadn’t cared THAT much about Riven. In fact, she’d been happy to be rid of his influence during the time she’d been scrounging about on her own. She’d felt like Riven had been holding her down. But now, she was realizing that she’d been a bitch, and her guilty conscience was weighing on her for treating him so callously, not even in her actions, but in her mind. No, she realized, it wasn’t that at all… It wasn’t that she’d had that much of a rapport with Riven because, well, Gideon was right. It was difficult to have any kind of rapport with a Sewer Rat unless you were a Sewer Rat yourself. But it was something else entirely… she felt guilty because she hadn’t liked him as much as he’d seemed to like her. Because she hadn’t felt that attachment that he’d seemed to feel for her, and in fact, she’d never felt that attachment with other people. She’d never felt it with Corvus, and she’d never felt it with Amir. She’d held on to all three of them as though she was sinking at sea, but it hadn’t meant a damn thing, and she knew that. Where had that sort of thing gotten her? It was as though the moment she got ‘involved’ with someone, the entire situation fell to shit. No, it wasn’t even that. It was that she didn’t feel any sort of strong connection with anyone around her, no matter how strongly they said they felt towards her. It didn’t matter how hard she tried, she knew, on the most minute level that she was forcing it. She would force herself, without even realizing, to wrench herself away from the situation… And often, in the wake of such spiritual violence, there would be destruction. After all, look at what had happened in the past: a relationship with centuries behind it ruined beyond repair because she couldn’t give Corvus ‘normal’; Amir dead in the Parisian streets because she couldn’t give him the ‘perfect Assasin’; Riven dead in a sewer full of shit because she couldn’t give him the companionship he reached out for. It was her. It was all on her, and if she’d ever believed in a God, she would have believed that he’d send her straight to the seventh layer of hell for such conscious devilry. She would have sent herself there if she could have. She was tired of fighting this, tired of always coming out on top at the expense of someone else. She might have said that she had a fit of conscience, but it wasn’t even that. She wasn’t a good person, and she made no bones about it. It was the simple fact that she couldn’t handle the emotional backlash of destroying relationships and lives anymore. It was selfish, but it was her own impulse, perhaps the first real impulse or emotion she’d allowed herself to feel in some time. She’d succeeded in her wrestling career, but at the expense of what? She had no one around her now to care, save one jackass Ravnos that would sooner get rid of her than give a damn about her innermost thoughts and feelings. She really didn’t know why he kept her around. Hope soared within her as she realized that she didn’t care. Perhaps he’d be the one to redeem her, to show her how to make a saint out of her sinner’s soul. But no… that was too much to ask, and every Ravnos certainly had a vice himself. That wasn’t the point; the point was that she needed to get away from him before she destroyed him too. She liked him too much to wreak that on him, and she was tired of destroying everything in her path just because she always put herself first. She was going to get away. Para mia…… That voice shattered her thoughts again. So deep, sorrowful, plaintive… He needs me, she thought, but she didn’t know who he was. In his bedroom up the stairs, Gideon stared at the wall. He was in a rather ill humor, mostly because she’d just spurned him. A quiet fugue rang out from his cell phone, and he turned over, irritatedly leaving the world of his contemplations. “Si?” “Tell me there’s good news.” He paused for a moment. No, he couldn’t bullshit this call. “I don’t think I can say anything of the sort. I won’t lie to you… she’s fairly bad off.” “How so?” The question cut urgently into his sentence, and Gideon was tempted to get annoyed, but he held it back, and wisely so. This one could have snapped him in half with the flick of a wrist, and Gideon de Medici was simply not ready to leave his nightly wanderings just yet. “I got her out of Cleveland before they were able to lay a filthy finger on her, true enough, but her mental state isn’t quite so well off. I…” He paused. How to say this without alarming his caller to the point that he would come all the way down to Napa Valley to disrupt the whole process? “I feel that perhaps she is not stable, or at least that she will not remain at this plateau for long.” There was an irritated growl from the other end of the phone, up in Seattle. “This cannot go on forever! She is putting herself in grave danger, and you as well.” Gideon’s Iberian temper began to flair, dramatically cresting inside him like a lavaburst. “What would you propose I do?” Gideon snapped. Irritation rose in the man on the other end of the phone. “Sometimes, the vase must be broken in order to put it back together in its correct order! That’s what I propose you do. You’ve done a good enough job of convincing her to trust you, so why can you not finish the task and make her see the truth?” Gideon rubbed his temple with his free hand, lying on his side in a natural, slinky lounging posture that seemed to come with the infusion of Ravnos blood. There were times when his brother greatly aggravated him. “Look, I will do what I can, si?” He knew that wouldn’t be good enough. “I know how much you miss her. I’m not going to let anything happen to her.” There was a pause on the other end of the phone. “Gracias.” Gideon arched an eyebrow, shaking his head. There were times when his brother sounded like such a broken man, and all over this one little woman. “Anything for my flesh and blood.” With that, Gideon snapped his phone shut. He knew that his brother would be calling him back within the next few days, to poke and prod about any progress that might have been made. But that was okay. He slid out of his bed, preparing to go back downstairs and conquer Lia’s temper when he interrupted her reverie. It wasn’t anything that he’d never seen before, but nevertheless, it wasn’t going to be pretty. He didn’t care. What had to be done had to be done. As he slipped fluidly down the stairs, he had to admit that he wasn’t just doing it for his brother, either. No, he was quite fond of Lia himself. He’d just try to make her feel better, in whatever way he could, and use a bit of that famous Ravnos conniving to make her see the truth. He smirked. There was, of course, the prospect that it could turn out pretty nicely for both of them. Given both of their temperaments, he couldn’t put it completely out of his head that something might happen between the two of them that would make his brother very angry. Rightly so? No, he figured that it wasn’t. After all, he could just say that he needed a few extra days… maybe weeks… to get her back to the point that she needed to be at in order to return to him. And if his brother ever found out? Gideon wondered if ‘collateral damage’ would be a good enough excuse. The veranda was not peaceful. Shadow was in pieces herself, almost hanging over the railing as those intoxicating and seductive vines whispered to her insistently. Lia……… Vuélvame……… Her hands clawed at her ears, willing the voice to go away, but it would not abate in its relentless pursuit. The vines whispered back and forth, threatening to strangle her. Terror was struggling forth, but the sorrow overwhelmed it, and tears rushed up to her eyes. She went limp, letting it take over, too weak to stop it. It was then that Gideon stepped out onto the veranda again. His brow furrowed, genuine concern stirring forth dustily for the first time in aeons. Slipping over to her, he pulled her out of the fetal position; blood tears were running down her face, and he pulled her close. “No grite.” She didn’t stop. He realized that he wasn’t really sure what to say, so he ran his fingers through her hair, a perturbed look coming over his face. “Parada para mia por favor.” She didn’t know why she understood his Spanish, but she did. Maybe it was that close to French. Or maybe there was something sort of reassuring about the tone of his voice. No… it was just similar to the one that had been calling to her out of oblivion moments ago. Almost exactly the same, but still different. She bit her lip, trying hard to ebb the flow from her jade eyes, but it just wouldn’t stop; in fact, it came harder. Gideon didn’t know what to say. What had he done wrong? He held her closer, not even knowing if he was soothing her. She pulled away, looking into his eyes. “God, I miss him…..” Gideon’s thoughts froze at that exact moment, crystallized. What had she said? Time seemed to stall, and confusion racked her brain. Missed who? .:: ::: ::.
