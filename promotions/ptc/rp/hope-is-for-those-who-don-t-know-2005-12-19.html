Hope is for those who don't know.

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 Sitting, he stares at the bed. The images never leaving his site. The images that happened over a year ago now fresh in his mind as he is forced to relive the moment all over again. It is one thing to not be able to remember you past. But to have to relive the most horrid parts of it again without any warning is enough to not only stop you dead in your tracks, but to derail you completely. And here he was. Derailed again. Rocking back and forth, the image never leaves his inner eyes while thoughts of what his former life held were running through his mind like a car with no lights driving in the pitch black of night. There was now some direction but there was nothing to cling to. Salaok now remembered he had a son who he lost through murder but who was this son with and why had that memory not come back yet? Had she too suffered so much from their son’s death that she’d gone into hiding? Had Salaok forced her away? Was she even alive? If so, why had she not tried to find him this last year? So many a question but not a single answer in site. From out of the silence, which had become all consuming, footsteps rang out over the cold wooden floor and then heading up the staircase. While the footsteps registered in Salaok’s head, he did nothing about seeing who it was as so far this life he was finding out about had more curve balls than a season of baseball. Growing louder, Salaok listens as they move up the stairs and down the hallway, finally stopping inside the doorway. Without even turning his head to look at this intruder, Salaok asks the question that he’s been asking himself since entering the room. "Why was I brought here first?" There is a slight silence that Salaok found irritating and was about to ask the question again when the voice of the man sounded out. "Because you need to understand again." "Understand what?" Salaok bites back, still gazing at the empty bed of his late son in front of him. "Understand what it’s like to be who you are. Understand what the price was for being who you are and understanding what it was that drove you. Once upon a time Salaok, you were untouchable and yet with the next generation you became complacent and let your guard down." The man spoke with no emotion and only offered what those who wanted Salaok to remember had instructed him to. Stepping forth, the man offers his hand out to help Salaok to his feet but gets no hand in reply. "Salaok, it’s time to go." His voice still void of emotion but this time gaining a visual response from Salaok who turns his head up to look at the stranger for the first time. A look of knowing comes over Salaok’s face as he makes eye contact with the man who’s offered his head. Salaok once again stared into the eyes of the man who had begun him on this path of memory last week when he turned up at the night club. "You! Who are you?" Salaok’s question was demanding an answer but it got none as the man simply repeated himself. "Salaok, it’s time to go." "Go where?" The bite in Salaok’s voice becoming more and more dominate, this time gaining a response from the man. "There is one more place I have to take you before you leave for round two of your tournament against Sam Wolack" As the sun begins to set on what had been a day of pain for Salaok, two cars pull up in front of a cemetery, each with a driver and no one else. From the first car steps out the man who had seemingly been given the task of making Salaok remember who he was. His skin well tanned and his hair slicked back with a strand falling over his face, swinging like a windscreen wiper. The second car carried the dejected and hurting Salaok. It had been a few days since he had walked into his son’s room and remembered what had happened there. His face told true of the days gone by as his normally well shaven face had half a beard beginning to engulf it. Salaok walks up beside the man who waits at the gates and the two begin to walk into the grounds of the dead. With each step, Salaok fears what it is he is going to be shown this time. And as he walks closer to a group of headstones, he sees that all but one of them is covered by black cloth. "What’s with the covers?" Salaok’s voice not that of a man with great happiness in his life, and for good reason too. "You aren’t ready to see those yet. In time you will have them revealed to you but know that it is all because of you that these people have died." There could not have been a colder explanation to Salaok’s question if the man had tried. But little did Salaok know that it was all true. The life that Salaok had lead, and the group in which he had been apart of ever since a young male was the lone reason for the death count in this cemetery. In the group of nearly twenty headstones, the one that was uncovered was the one that he was expecting and yet didn’t want to see. Darrell Davis 1998-2005 Your time on this earth was too short my child You will be missed and mourned forever. Your spirit is what drove us. One day I will hold you again There it was, as clear as day, the headstone of his son. This was the confirmation that Salaok wished he didn’t have to see as until the hard proof, there was a small glimmer of hope that his memory was playing just another game on him. However there was no game being played here, only the cold hard truth. Standing in front of the headstone, Salaok had never felt so alone. Before he remembered his past, all he had ever known was life on his own. But now he knows that he wasn’t alone at one point in time and yet now alone more than before. It is better to have loved and lost, than to have never loved at all? Before he knew he had loved there was hope, now that he knows, there is only pain.
