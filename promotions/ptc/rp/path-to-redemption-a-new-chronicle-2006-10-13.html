Path to Redemption: A new Chronicle

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-13 Disguise your shape and silence your noise. Be amorphous and without urgency. -- excerpt from Sun Tzu's Life Strategies from the Art of War An Introduction Every life has a story and every story has a life. As with stories, lives have a climax, that pinnacle point where everything comes to a plateau and the story begins the journey to conclusion. This is the way of things. But what if this were not the case? What if a climax was merely the point of journey to a new climax? What if the story told was merely a chapter in a much larger book? Perhaps stories are not like lives after all, instead, they are a far cry from the tales told in the existence of living. A life is filled with a great many chapters and books, a compilation of short tales that are a mere fragment of a greater journey. For instance, take Mya Yamomuko. She has a great many chapters to her life, each filled with a plot, a climax and a conclusion. The first few chronicles of her life are of no consequence unless one is interested in the typical tale of an ordinary childhood. It is the third installment to her series where one might become interested in the story that is hers. It began at the age of fifteen, with the glint of a knife seen too late to stop what she was doing. It, inevitably, led her on a journey of deception and betrayal. There has been no true peace for the girl now known to many as Tigera. Only a long battle filled with unexpected truths and a web of lies that have left her feeling cold and empty. It is this book that is coming to an end but it is not the end of her story. It is merely the beginning of a new chapter in a new journal yet to be written. Perhaps this time the conclusion will have a happier ending… Path to Redemption: Live and Let Live or Die -BANG- The heavy fog was split by the ferocity of a single bullet. The game was over. The king had fallen. Mya stood frozen with her gun still carefully aimed at the figure she thought she saw in the distance. Time was suspended for that single definitive moment. Then it all came crashing back down again. An invisible poker ripped through her chest, sending searing pain spiderwebbing throughout the scorched cavity as glazed, disbelieving eyes lowered to gape at the cause. Her shirt bubbled and collapsed to the crimson flow beneath absorbing it like a dried up sponge. At that moment everything went numb. Her world melted into a grey existence as the gun slipped from senseless fingers. She turned, those eyes searching for her assailant and there he stood. Eijo Yamomuko, her father, her enemy, her killer. It wasn't supposed to happen this way. She was supposed to win. She always won. " But I saw you." " I saw you run in here. You were supposed to be over there." " You were…supposed to be… over there…" The words died away as the mist parted revealing the true image she previously assumed to be her father. There, on the weathered brick wall was a mural of a man looking to the heavens. Stoned amber eyes zoned in on his face taking in the expression on his face. She studied that face. The tight, drawn back posture of his lips despite his mouth sagging open. He seemed young, yet something in his face aged him beyond his years. It was those eyes. His eyes were filled with agony, confusion, perhaps even rage. Somehow, someone captured the very essence of her inner most being without even knowing who she was. She continued to stare at the graffiti focusing on his eyes. The question that was foremost in her mind was reflected in his gaze. Why? Why me? A chilled tingle traveled up her spine. The world had gone cold or perhaps it was merely Mya, whom had grown cold. At this point she could no longer tell. The sound of a low triumphant chuckle brought her attention back to the man whom had brought her here. The man who had molded her life up to this point. The man who had destroyed everything she ever believed in. Why? The wet sticky feeling spread within in her ruined shirt, warming chilled skin as a hand lifted and pressed against the wound. It was an odd feeling, the sense of her own warm blood flowing freely outside her body. She has dealt with wounds before but never has she been shot. Those eyes squeezed shut briefly as she tried to keep the world from spinning. It was to no avail. When her eyes lifted the fog filled alley spun out of control and brought her crashing to her knees. There was no pain as her knees collided with unforgiving asphalt. All that was left was a frozen sense of numbness and confusion as he spoke to her at last. " Silly child. I made you, remember?" " NO!" She raged. " Yes." " …No…" It was more of a plea than a conviction. She wanted to rant and rave. She wanted to get up and tear his eyes out for the things he had done to her -- to his family. So much was running through that clouded mind. Why? How could he do this to those he was supposed to hold dear? Why? The scream of a wounded animal erupted from her lips as she doubled over and gave into the breaking dam of tears. " YOU! WHY?" " Why would you do this?" Rage then suffering, it was like a roller coaster out of control. One moment she was screaming at him. The next she was nothing more than a confused child begging for truth that simply could not be understood. Her head touched the cold stone as she was overcome wretched sobs. She was choking on the pain and deception. He had broken her at last; shattered any hope she had left.The stony fortress that stood around her psyche came crumbling down leaving her vulnerable. Bare. He wasted no time capitalizing on her weakness. The sensation of warm fingers softly caressing her chin caused her to flinch away. Again he grabbed her chin, this time his grip was vice clamped painfully into her flesh. With a jerk, he forced her head up so that he could look at her disillusioned face. " Revenge." It was a single word, but it had the impact of a jackhammer to the stomach.That word drove into her core savagely ripping apart her insides until there was nothing left but an emptiness where hope once thrived. Revenge? For what? What could any of them have done that was so terrible he would need to seek revenge on his own flesh and blood? Slender brows drew together as confusion flooded her features as she looked up gazing into his eyes. They were the eyes of a stranger. No longer was the man she knew standing before her. None of this made any sense. Up until the age of 15 she could only remember a peaceful and happy existence. The Yamomukos had never suffered for anything. Eijo had a high paying career as an ambassador. Both she and Tsuko had gone to the best schools. Eijo loved his Irish wife and doted on her often. So what was it he felt he needed revenge for? What was so important that he had gone so far as to set up this elaborate plan to destroy his family all to forge a weapon out of his daughter? " I can see it in your eyes, Mya. You are wondering why. Why would I allow my own son to be murdered. How could I have my loyal wife of 23 years killed. Why all this trouble to make you into what you are today-- a killer." Mya gagged as her stomach twisted painfully. He sounded so cold. Distant. Again that tender organ would churn in warning of volcanic eruption. She struggled to maintain control, but the turmoil won as her stomach ejected it contents in body contorting fashion. " Have you figured it out yet? Perhaps, even now, you do not have all the pieces of the puzzle. The answer is right there Mya. It is right in front of you and, yet, you refuse to see it-- just as I knew you would. It was why I chose you over Tsuko." A shaky hand wiped across her mouth smearing blood and bile over smooth skin as she looked up to him once more. She had missed something. Something small, something obvious. His voice faded into the distance as if it had been submerged beneath a pool of water as she searched her mind for that final piece. The slow movie picture began to spin as she flipped backwards through the pages of her life. Two weeks ago. " You…it can't be…" She was floored. Trembling hands lowered as her offensive faded away to one of horror. The mask had been stripped away at last. Now she knew who the White Tiger was and with this knowledge everything changed. " It can, and it is." " But you.." She choked back a small sob of dismay and shook her head in denial. " It can't be. You wouldn't…" Her voice died away as every event which led to this one flooded her mind. " Tsuko…Mother? That was all you?" The horror was already beginning to ebb away, replaced by rage. " IT WAS YOU! MY OWN FATHER!?" Three weeks ago. Everything had been so easy, just as her father had predicted. The Oyuban of the Yoshio clan spotted her in the club and had one of his men approach her to join them in the VIP section. Despite the terrible need to crush the man's nose she pasted a shy smile on her face and agreed. Within an hour she was in his hotel suite bedroom. She put up with his clumsy pawing at her smooth skin, playing her part perfectly. When he went to remove her dress she stopped him with a hand shyly pressed against his chest and smiled coyly. " Why don't we play a game?" He agreed, oh so easily. Within moments she had him tied to the bed and straddled his hips as she blindfolded him. Once it was in place she sat up and stared absent absentmindedly at the scroll on the wall above the bed. How predictable. Even here he had the audacity to fly his clan's symbol. Strange, it felt familiar. Shaking off the uneasy sensation she looked back at him removing the bladed hair sticks from her mane and shaking the strands loose before she plunged the weapon deep into the cavity of his chest again and again… One month ago. " I want you to quit, Mya." The silence was deafening as she clamped her teeth into her inner cheek to keep from screaming. Quit wrestling? Disgrace herself for the sake of his paternal demand? Never. " No." One word was all it took for this powder keg to explode. The fight that followed was nothing short of a world war between two individuals. In the end she left vowing never to speak to him again. It was a vow she knew she couldn't keep… Five weeks ago. The moans of those left alive were lost to her as she stepped over the broken forms of men she and her father just decimated in order to get to the leader of the Pride, the White Tiger himself. She nearly stumbled as one reached out grabbing her ankle. Looking down with disdain she merely kicked his hand off and stomped on his face ending his misery. It was far more than he deserved. " Now tell me what I want to know!" Her father bellowed as he shook the masked figure vehemently. Mya looked on with quiet fury, her hands curling into vicious fists as she fought the urge to merely turn that masked face into a pile of pulp. " Tell me now!" He shook him again. " Tell me or I will kill you." " Kill me then," came the snide reply. Mya had heard enough. Snarling she stepped forward and snatched away the mask, needing to see the face of the man whom had tortured her family. It was a face she knew well. It was a face she had trusted. Mask still in hand she stumbled back a step in shock. " Piya?" Her trust betrayed once again and by the very man who had pretended to help her. The man whom she confided in about everything. This was the man she went to for aid he was her bestfriend. He was her enemy. Treachery was beginning to know no bounds. Nearly three months ago. " Papa?" The house was devoid of life. The sound of her questioning voice echoed in the empty chambers of her home as she pressed the front door open further to peer inside. It was a ravaged scene. Everything was destroyed. Furniture flipped, glass cabinets shattered. Panic lanced her heart as she quickly rushed down the hall to his bedroom. It was the same. Everything in disarray. Even the family portrait above the dresser had been sliced beyond repair, but that wasn't what stuck out in her mind amidst the chaos. It was the glimpse of bright color behind the frame. She approached it cautiously, carefully taking the ruined image down to reveal what was beneath. There, on the wall was a scroll with a strange symbol painted on it. Why had it been hidden? Suddenly, that head snapped up as it all came together.The memories fled as the truth came forward. The scroll! The jigsaw puzzle was finally complete. She sucked in her breath and slowly turned her boiling gaze to meet his intense stare. He smiled. He knew that she knew. " I see you finally remember." " You…" She couldn't speak. The rage was too much as she planted both hands to the damp asphalt desperate to calm the storm unfurling within. " You…" She spat on the ground, trying to rid herself of the bitter taste of truth revealed at last. " The scroll, " she seethed through clenched teeth," I didn't know at the time. That symbol. It was the symbol of the Yoshio clan." Eyes of infernal hate rolled up to stare at him as she continued to speak as he backed away chuckling. He wanted a good view of this moment of revelation. He wanted to drink it all in. " You were in that clan. You were a Yoshio. YOUUUUU!" She swiped at his legs like a wounded animal fighting its way out of a corner. Eijo easily avoided her grasp as he laughed outright at her fury. " That's right. I was the son of the Oyuban. I was next in line for head of the family. But I was cast out. Marked as a traitor to tradition when I fell in love with a gajin. They tried to kill me. When they failed my own father gave me one last moment of mercy and allowed me to leave. But I could never return. Your mother ruined my life. I could have held more power in Japan than the Emperor himself but my heritage was given to another! MY POWER! MINE!" She could barely hear his rant through her heated breathing. She glanced up as he turned away, a slow cruel smile touching her lips for a mere glimpse. With the fog's thick blanket once again on the ground, she carefully moved her hand, fingers seeking her fallen pistol. The rage replaced the pain of her wound. Those slender fingers acted as a radar while her anger fueled the motivation to see this through… to see this to the very end. " …The only way to regain my position was to kill the new father. I couldn't go myself and a hired hit man would not get to him easily enough. But you.." Her fingers collided with the cold steel as he spun around to look at her with a malicious grin. She froze and stared at him as if she had been looking at him the whole time. " You could get close easily as a female. The new father has a penchant for beautiful young women. So you see, I needed you to do the dirty work. And once it was done… I could reclaim my birth right. You were perfect, Mya. I placed you on my strings and you danced like the marionette for its master's hands." She snarled rising up to sit on her haunches with gun safely in hand, hidden from view. " You disgust me." He laughed as he looked to the pistol still snug within his grasp then looked to her. " You made me proud. You proved more useful than I could have imagined. But---" He raised his pistol aiming it at her. " Your use has expired." -BANG!- -BANG!- Two pistols fired off in unison. Mya's body hurled sideways and back as another bullet ripped through that same shoulder. A cry of agony poured past her lips as she lay sprawled, half on her back, on that cold damp ground. With the last amount of energy she had left she pulled her head forward to peer at him as his bewildered gaze dropped to the red fountain sprouting in the center of his chest. His gaze turned back to her as he teetered then fell forward like a great oak. The war was over now. Despite the journey and the pain, she had won. Smiling, she allowed her world to fade to black. ~*~ " Mya. Mya? Mya!" Mya jerked as her eyes flew to the face of the person who spoke her name. Confusion was wrought in her amber eyes as she blinked several times trying to clear the fog heavy in her mind. Had she been daydreaming? Where was she? " Are you alright?" " I…." She frowned as she straightened up in her chair focusing on her surroundings. She sat at an oak table. Ahead of her stood a rather imposing raised podium with two oak 'boxes' on either side. To the left was another oak box containing twelve chairs within. She was in a courtroom. The moment she realized where she was, all confusion was swept away as if by some unknown force. Again she turned to the woman beside her. She knew her name. Cassandra Grace. Cassie to her. Mya put on a tentative smile and nodded. Even though she knew where she was and whom she was with, she still couldn't remember why she was here. " Yes, Cassie, I am fine. Just..tired." " I can understand that after all you have been through." Mya frowned. What on earth was Cassie talking about? She tried to remember but she was drawing a blank. She couldn't remember anything past the moment Cassandra had said her name. Odd. She turned to Cassandra to speak but found herself interrupted by the voice of another booming across the expanse of the room. " All rise for the honorable Judge Geoffrey O'Donnell." Cassie urged Mya to rise as the judge entered the room from a door just behind the podium. As Mya came to her feet she moved her eyes forward to catch a glimpse of the man but found herself unable to lift that gaze. Fear had rooted itself into her bones. This man- this judge would determine what would happen with her future. Too bad she couldn't remember her past. " You may be seated." Mya fell into her chair like a lead weight. Was this the beginning of the end? Or would this be a new lease on life?
