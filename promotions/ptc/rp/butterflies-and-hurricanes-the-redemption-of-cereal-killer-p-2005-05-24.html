Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Seven: "Your number has been called"

24 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-24 Imagine that you’ve never really tried your hardest throughout your life; not because you’ve been pampered or been looked after to such an extent that effort is an anathema to you, but because you were scared of doing so. Imagine living your whole life accepting and even embracing mediocrity, living within yourself, a mockery of what you could have achieved if you had made the effort and given it your all. Imagine being satisfied with second best; never reaching within yourself to try and place first, just so you can never experience the true feeling of failure, of having done everything you could and still come up short. Imagine living like that for your entire life, and then trying to make a change, trying to exceed your expectations just once. Imagine that the one time you stake your fragile ego on the line, it got broken, and your body along with it; slammed through a hail of wood, metal and fire, another victim of a group of Ultra Violents. -------------------------------------------------------- Frank woke up in the hospital back in January 2003, and immediately regretted it. His body ached in ways he didn’t think was possible. It was as if his body was rejecting itself, each organ and every square inch of tissue complaining about the events of the previous night. He thought that the painkillers were only fogging his mind up and not doing their job, but in reality they were the only thing keeping him from screaming out loud. The injuries inflicted upon him as he had made his last stand were many and varied. He had five toenails ripped off their respective toes, a shard (splinter being too small a word) of wood driven through the sole, three broken toes and a tendon that was perilously close to snapping. That was just his left foot. The litany of injuries moved up the body, taking in irrevocably damaged cartilage, torn muscles and herniated discs. Ribs had been broken, his kidneys had been bruised, his neck been badly sprained and one arm had two fractures near the wrist. Added to these were a host of smaller cuts and bruises, a concussion, and a few patches of second and first degree burns. Whilst his life was never really in great peril, his grasp on reality, tenuous at the best of times, was wrenched away from him suddenly, and though most of his mind returned to him, there was less remaining than he thought. -------------------------------------------------------- “What have you done with her?” shouted CK, looking up to see Valerie no longer there. “She never existed. By now, surely by now you know this.” “I loved her… I mean, I still love her! What have you done?” pleaded Frank, his eyes red-rimmed. “You’ve spent a long time in this deluded state, but now you have to move on. There are greater, more important things than keeping your charade alive.” “But she read to me, she told me about the letters from XUW, about my contract expiring if I don’t win the tournament!” The Ripper lowered both his head and his voice. It was always going to be hard, destroying a fake relationship that he had been involved in creating and perpetuating, but he had forgotten exactly how intractable Cereal Killer could be. “You read them yourself. You would open the letters with a knife, and find out what they said. Only later would you finally tell yourself of it, letting the bad news come from lips other than your own, or so you thought. The envelopes, they were always next to you. Everything you thought she had done, it was done by you. By you… or by The Ripper.” “You?” asked CK, his mind a confused blur. “The Ripper has been here before, without your knowledge. He has seen how you live your life; half of it reclined, watching television, and the other half sitting on your own, talking to yourself in different voices. It seems that it has become no longer enough to hear them, but since your injuries, you have started to become them; to live in this state of yours, acting out a normal life, but with a cast of just one solitary person.” “That can’t be true. It just… it can’t be. The tournament, how did I get entered? I didn’t want to wrestle again… I knew this would happen and I’d be alone again!” “You did want to enter, though. Despite yourself, you wanted to go back and put right some of the things that haunted you, and haunt you still. You wanted the thrill it gave you… and the voices, they wanted more freedom.” CK looked around himself, as if in a room completely unfamiliar to him. The walls, they seemed to be drabber than before, as if the life had been sucked out of them. The floor seemed dirtier, the entire room less tidy than before, less ordered, less homely. “I did all this?” “You had some help,” explained The Ripper. “The federation would never have put you forwards as their only guaranteed spot, not with the depth of talent available to them.” “Who did this?” “You even need to ask? The darkness can often work as oil in the machinery of power. The merest guidance, the smallest obfuscations, that is all that was required for The Ripper to control them, just as he has controlled you. Some smokescreens were applied, and before they knew it, nobody there even cared who was being entered into the tournament.” “So, why me? Why did you choose me? Not just now, but back when I was starting? Why did you pick me to be your partner?” “All things will be revealed in the fullness of time. You do not need to know this right now, and because of that your eyes will remain shielded from the very simple truth.” “Hang on, I’ve seen this movie before. You’re not actually my father are you?” Even The Ripper smiled. “No.” Silence descended on the room as Frank looked around. It just didn’t seem right, or fair, to have the rug pulled from under his feet so starkly. He was just getting back on track, and now this man, this paragon of evil was twisting him, and showing him things he never wanted to see. “You couldn’t have just left me alone?” -------------------------------------------------------- With his mind detached from his environment, Frank lay in his hospital bed, keeping company with intravenous drips, catheters and sterile bandages. Nobody came to visit him from his place of work, from the wrestling federation that he had been with for several years. Not a single friend turned up, because the number of friends he had was the same as the number of toenails he had on his left foot; zero. So he lay there, wondering what would become of him. He’d finally summoned up the courage to take a chance, to use all of his strength and ability to take down someone higher up the pecking order than him. He’d looked like he was going to do it, too, but at the crucial moment his target, Blake Wolfson, moved slightly to one side, and he missed. From there he was jumped by the men he was attacking, and the rest was history. Alone, in a room all to himself, he thought back over his life. He recalled all the character he had built up, all the personalities that he had taken from his bedroom and transplanted to the wrestling ring. He’d been a Teen Heartthrob, a Sadistic Sage and an Emerald City Assassin. But no longer was he the King of the Dark Match, or the Jungle Soldier of Borneo. The memories of being the Next Big T’ing were fading. It was all passing him by, crumbling away like dust. Ashes to ashes, Cereal Killer; may your career rest in peace. So he lay there, his mind wandering around, trying to construct some sense out of the anarchy, some order out of the chaos. Days passed without him speaking a word, watching impassively as the large male nurse, Sam, checked his fluid levels, and the female nurse, Veronica, came to take blood tests and check that he was getting better. Thought came to Frank, thoughts of the past. He remembered being a child; a passable athlete and a fairly smart kid too. He could have been the same as the other children, he could have slotted into a hole in a clique and been accepted, but his mind wouldn’t let him. Few were interested in talking to him when he’d only respond in the most monosyllabic way, or at times he would not reply at all. He remembered Emily, and the time she talked to him, before he called the police. Thoughts of her floated in and out of his mind, her butterfly existence in his mind getting mixed with the nurse who attended him. Veronica healed him, as Emily’s memory tore him apart… …and Valerie was born. -------------------------------------------------------- “The Ripper could not have left you alone. You would never have made it this far.” “What makes you think that I wanted to make it this far?” asked CK. “The Ripper did not mean your wrestling career. You have not been alone here these past months. When is the last time that you remember going out to purchase food?” “I think I went to… I mean… I can’t remember.” “Exactly; you should know now that The Ripper’s interest in you has not just been a fleeting one. He was there when you started to believe in your creation of Valerie Rose Dubonnet.” Cereal Killer looked away, and dropped back down to his couch. The wind had been taken from his sails with the mention of the woman he thought he had loved. He was becalmed, free from emotions, released from reason. “You fused her together from your past and your present, the better to carve yourself out a future that might be worth living in. The doctors would have noticed were they not influenced to fail in their duties.” “You were there?” “The Ripper saw how you changed, how your mind created something so complex that it ended up fooling itself. You made a woman who would be a perfect fit for you. She was damaged, as you yourself were. One bad eye, a name she would never have chosen as her own and a singular lack of family. She was untraceable for you, someone who could have fallen for you and not been dazzled by the bright lights elsewhere. Had she been too good then you’d have noticed it, and in the end it was the imperfections that made her perfect for you.” Frank lay back on the couch and listened, as a man he had never truly known exposed his every weakness and intricacy. “You drew strength from her. The thought that there was someone for you, that you weren’t alone gave you the will to carry on. You could leave wrestling behind, and maybe you could make something else out of your life. Of course, you really never were alone. All that time The Ripper watched you, and judged to see when the time might be right.” “To break me? Don’t you think I was broken enough?” “If The Ripper wished you broken, then you’d not be here now. The Ripper had his own reasons for pursuing your development, and those are not reasons you need to know. All you need to do is pick yourself up and prepare for a fight. Living with Valerie built you back up, but if it were to continue unchecked, then you would have been destroyed. You are now capable, and if you are willing then you can win the tournament.” “Why are you so excited about a damn wrestling tournament? If you’re some freaking evil genius, then what does what I do for a living matter? You want to see me win the title? Why? It’s just a freaking sport, and when it’s all over and done with, I’ll still be alive, and everyone who watched the match will stay the same. This is wrestling, not Demonic Melodrama 101…” Frank’s voice trailed off, and The Ripper moved in seamlessly, taking a step towards the smaller man and changing the pace of the conversation. “Valerie taught you to try and be strong, and to not back down from people. She taught you control over some of the voices in your head, to hear only one of the multitude that forever try to control you, that yearn to be listened to like she was. She could only take you so far, and after that you need to make a decision. You can wallow here, or chance that you could come out victorious without the help of The Ripper. The other option is to come with The Ripper, to take a trip into the darkness.” “To the domicile?” asked CK. “To the domicile,” confirmed The Ripper, with an incline of his head. Cereal Killer thought the offer over, sitting up, and then standing. A broad smile crept across his face, like a rising sun. “Valerie did teach me a lot,” he said, still trying to understand that she was just one facet of his own personality. “She taught me that not everything is what it seems. She taught me that I can stand up for myself and succeed when nobody thinks that I can.” “You had assistance.” “So you claim, but Valerie taught me not to believe everything that strangers say to me. She taught me that my mind can play a lot of tricks, and that sometimes I can create personalities that seem to have a mind of their own.” “She taught you a lot, little killer,” said The Ripper, the mocking tone evident. “That she did, Jack, and she taught me that sometimes I can’t tell the difference between reality and fantasy so well… but she also showed me that she wasn’t so good at telling them apart either. She never did anything, did she? Everything she said she did was my work, was the things that I had done for myself. She never knew she was a figment of my imagination. They don’t, the voices, they think they’re an entity of their own. They don’t even know that they only live through me.” Cereal Killer was laughing now, enjoying every moment as he turned the tables. “I’ve never seen you touch anything in this room. I’ve never heard you come or heard you go. I’ve made you in my mind, for some reason I can’t fathom, and so here I stand, a crazy man taking advice from an imaginary version of my worst enemy. You told me I was fucked up, jack, but Jesus… I never knew it was so bad!” “You fail to conceal the glee in your voice. You seem excited about this,” replied The Ripper, measuring every single syllable. “It means I can go away. I can go back to whatever I wanted to, because you don’t exist!” “Your beliefs are once more short of reality.” “No; this time I am right.” CK shook his head briefly, a little note to tell himself that he could do this, and then he swung a punch at the place that would have been occupied by The Ripper’s head, had he been there at all. The punch never landed on the chin of The Ripper. It was caught easily in his vice-like grip. With one enormous hand he started to squeeze, and Cereal Killer’s eyes bulged, the veins popping and bursting as lights flashed in front of his eyes. The pain was intense, but the shock was worse, so much worse. “Your ignorance fools you again, little killer. Now your arguments stand for nothing. Let us journey to the domicile.” Frank heard the words as if from afar, the sound like tectonic plates crashing on a different continent. He had never felt so lost, so alone in all his life. Wordlessly, hopelessly, he stood and followed The Ripper from the room.
