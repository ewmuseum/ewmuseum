IG 32 Jay Phoenix Five Prologue The hotel suite was luxuriously outfitted, the leather chair in which he sat hand-made and costing a year’s salary at minimum wage; it may have well have been a crate in a back alley for all the comfort that it afforded him. He didn’t actually know how long he had been sitting there – staring at the same spot on the wall - but it didn’t matter to him; at that point in time, at that moment in his life, nothing really did. All he wanted was for everyone to leave him alone, for life to forget that he existed, which was why he had booked himself into the hotel in the first place. No-one knew he was there and that meant that he was left on his own, just as he wanted, cut off from everyone and everything that felt a right to a piece of him; at least he would have been if it wasn’t for the small silver Nokia that sat on the arm-rest beside him, silently mocking the weakness that wouldn’t allow him to sever the last ties to his life ... a life that Jay Phoenix no longer recognized as his own. Chapter One Denial It took him a few seconds to realize that his phone was ringing, and even then it wasn’t actually the familiar refrain of classical music that got his attention instead it was the movement along the arm of the chair of the small cell-phone as it vibrated in counterpoint to the tune. Glancing down at the blue back-lit screen Phoenix’s brow creased as he recognized the number. For the last few weeks they had been calling him and for the last few weeks he had gone from confusion, to patience, to exasperation; today, though, he was leaning more towards frustration. Picking up the phone his thumb hovered above the end button but as he considered ignoring the call he changed his mind and, with a flick of his wrist, flicked the clam shell like lid of the phone open. “I’m not interested,” he said, without even pausing to listen to the pleasantries – or lack of them – on the other side, “I’ve told you more than once that I am not coming back, I’m not fighting anymore.” Running his free hand over his face Phoenix was a little shocked as he heard the shake in his own voice and hoped that it wouldn’t be picked up on. Stay strong he thought to himself, don’t let them think that they have you on the run here.. The irony of that thought wasn’t lost to him, as he sat in a hotel miles from everyone and anyone that he knew, and he barely choked back the small laugh that could – he knew, he knew all too well - turn so easily into a sob; once the tears started he wasn’t sure that they would ever stop. “I don’t give a damn about no-showingthe Unification Title match,” he said, taking a deep breath to calm himself, “I don’t care about any other matches that you guys at PRIME scheduled me for, even this Infinite Gauntlet match – after the crap you pulled with your fake ‘outing’ of me on your news site I don’t know what you expect but as I don’t even work for you …” His grip tightened on the cell-phone, his knuckles whitening, as the voice on the other end of the phone obviously interrupted him. “That wasn’t me,” he almost snarled down the phone, cutting off whatever was being said on the other side, “I have told you that before, as well, your contract is meaningless. Ember wasn’t me!” Chapter Two Anger Trying hard to open his eyes Phoenix groaned as the loud buzzing tried its best to break through his skull and explode out of the front of his head. He reached up and tried to prise his skin from the sticky substance that coated his face, gagging on the stale stench that pervaded the room. As his eyes opened he gagged again and then, leaning hurriedly over the side of the rumpled bed on which he lay, fully clothed, he retched as he realized that he was lying in a pool of his own vomit. Barely more than a trickle of vicious fluid escaped from his mouth - enough to burn his throat and clog his nose – as Phoenix fought to regain control of his stomach. Taking a deep breath he wiped the back of his hand across his mouth, grimacing as a tendril of residue formed a spider-web type link between the gap. Leaning back against the bed, his head swimming, he rubbed at his face wishing that the ringing in his ears would stop as he glanced – blearily – at the state of the room; only one word sprang to mind. Carnage. Fighting back another wave of nausea Phoenix groaned as recollection – flashing like a broken silent movie across the inside of his memory – flooded back. The call from the PRIME head office. The news that he was entered – against his wishes – in yet another match, in yet another tournament. The threat of litigation if he didn’t fulfill a contract that he maintained was nothing to do with him. The one name that kept coming back to haunt him – Ember. Seeing the phone lying on the bed beside him Phoenix remember shutting it – and the call – off before wrenching open the mini-bar and hurling its contents onto the bed. Never a drinker previously he didn’t even look at the labels to see what he was consuming instead – and simply – opened each bottle, one after the other, and downed them. As the heat burned the back of his throat all he hoped was that they would – finally – also drown out the memories that plagued him. He knew that no-one would ever understand it but even though he hadn’t been Ember he remembered everything that that man had done as if he had been. … and those memories were slowly driving him insane. Shaking his head, trying to rid himself – albeit briefly – of the memories as well as the ringing noise that echoed inside his head, Phoenix suddenly caught sight of the phone on the cabinet beside his bed and realized that the noise was not internal. Reaching gingerly across the sticky pool of bile on the bed he picked up the receiver. “… yes?” he whispered, wincing at the echoes inside his own head as he listened to the unseen speaker. “Noise, I am sorry, what noise are you talking about …” Phoenix’s eyes widened as he sat up and with his higher vantage point glanced around the room. Broken glass littered the floor everywhere, the television unit that had rested in the corner now sat on the floor on its side, one of the curtains that had formerly shrouded the window lay in a crumpled heap – its torn remnants barely hanging onto the pole above it. Colour rose to Phoenix’s cheeks as he remembered the last thing that had happened before darkness had descended on him the night before; at least he assumed it was the night before, it could have been longer. When the alcohol had run dry, and the thoughts and memories in his head had been too much to take, he had done the one thing that he had been bottling up inside himself. He lost it. The empty bottles had been the first to go, flung across the room to shatter into a million pieces against the wall. A table and chair that had been placed before the television had been his next ‘victim’ with the table being left in two pieces and the chair ending up God knows where. As he caught sight of his reflection in the TV screen he gave an inarticulate scream of rage before kicking out at it as hard as he could; the explosion of glass had knocked him backwards and he grabbed the curtain on his way down. “Listen,” he sighed, quietly, “I think that you had better get your manager to come up here and see me to discuss the ‘noise’.” Standing up, unsteadily, he reached into his back pocket and pulled out his wallet and slid a handful of notes out. Stretching his neck backwards he suddenly froze as he discovered where the missing chair had gone too; hanging upside down, its legs were impaled into the ceiling above. “Actually,” Phoenix asked as he fell back onto the bed, pulling a platinum American Express card out of the wallet, “just tell him that I am not going to fight whatever he wants to settle this.” Chapter Three Bargaining For the fifth time in as many minutes Phoenix closed his cell-phone before it could fully dial the pre-set number. It wasn’t so much that he didn’t actually want to make the call rather it was more that he was afraid of doing so – or, more to the point, afraid of what he would say. If he even picked it up, of course. With a long sigh Phoenix rubbed at his eyes, wincing as his hand scraped across the rough stubble along his cheek. Auburn haired and fresh faced he wasn’t used to having facial hair, in fact it took at least a week of not shaving at all before anything even became visible. This time, though, he knew that there was nearly three weeks worth of growth there. Three weeks since he had last shaved; three weeks since he had last spoken to … “Rick?” he asked, his mouth working faster than his brain as he answered the ringing cell-phone automatically without checking the number. It was better this way, he knew, taking the call from Rick rather than putting of making the call himself; it was easier this way. With another sigh he realized that just like his life always seemed to be lately nothing was ever easy. “…oh, Sinclair, it is you.” Hardly listening to the voice of his lawyer Phoenix sat back in the chair and let his eyes wander around the room. You would hardly realize that only twenty four hours later it had been trashed but then again it was amazing what a American Express card and a hefty tip could do to get things smoothed over. If only everything, Phoenix thought to himself, was that easy. “I am sorry,” Phoenix said, suddenly picking up on what Sinclair had said, “what?” Getting to his feet Phoenix started pacing around the room as he animatedly talked into the cell-phone. “You are meant to be my lawyer, one of the best as you keep telling me, so tell me why you can’t fix this!” “I don’t care,” Phoenix nearly shouted into the cell-phone, “they can’t get away with this John, they can’t make me do something that I don’t want to do, I won’t take part, I won’t fight – and they can’t make me be something that I don’t want to be, no matter what their perverted marketing guys put out as so-called ‘news’!” Suddenly stopping in his tracks Phoenix stood still as if pole-axed. “…Rick?” he breathed, his eyes widening, “I didn’t know that you were there; I didn’t mean …” His voice stopped as he listened to the sound of silence on the other end of the phone. “I didn’t know John had me on speaker phone, I didn’t mean … perverted was the wrong word … I didn’t know … Rick, are you still there, Rick?” “… Rick?” Leaning against the wall, on the other side of the room, Phoenix slowly slid down it until he was sitting on the floor, resting both elbows on his knees as his head fell forwards and his flowing hair formed a curtain of darkness around his face. The cell-phone fell, silent now, as his shoulders began to shake. Chapter Four Depression Sometime during the night he stopped crying for the first time. It wasn’t a conscious choice, he didn’t suddenly decide that enough was enough or anything quite so prosaic, the truth was simple; he simply couldn’t cry anymore. Falling asleep where he sat, crumpled against the wall with his arms on his knees, his tears stopped as his body fell into the regular rhythm of sleep. The respite, though, didn’t last as something – he wasn’t quite sure what – woke him up. Bleary eyed from tiredness and from crying, Phoenix glanced around the night shrouded room, taking it all in and ignoring it in the same breath as his gaze was drawn to the single point of illumination in the room. The small en-suite bathroom light had been left on and from his vantage point Phoenix could see the shelf where he had left all of his toiletries; everything that he traveled with. His toothbrush rested beside his hairbrush while his favorite shower-gel was strangely juxtaposed with a container of vitamins. None of this, however, was what had caught his attention. Haloed in the reflected illumination from the overhead light, resting against the large mirror behind the shelf, sat the medication that his doctor had prescribed after his ‘episode’. Phoenix laughed to himself, harsh and humorless, as he repeated the word in his mind. Episode. Two years of his life lost; a bout of multiple personality disorder or a psychic possession from a never born brother – depending on who you asked, of course; a career that was being forced on him rather than enjoyed; and, of course, the news and gossip throughout the industry rumor-mills and smark forums about his sexuality. All summed up in the small, insignificant word. Episode. Something to help him sleep. Something to help with the pain. Something to help him forget. Something to help him … hey, at least one of them worked as he found that he couldn’t remember what the fourth one was for. With a small smile to himself he realized that it didn’t matter what they were originally for, because he knew what they were for now. His grandfather always said that one day he would go into the Light and there it was; calling to him. No point fighting, he knew, when everything was already lost. For the second time that night, as he got to his feet and slowly walked to the bathroom – to the waiting Light - he stopped crying. This time, though, it was his choice. Reaching out for the bottles he brought them back into the room and, one by one, emptied them onto the bed as he sat down and slowly picked one up. Bach’s Toccat and Fuge in D minor seemed an apt choice to fill his mind at that particular moment, always being one of his favourite pieces of music but it took him a few seconds to figure out that he wasn’t imagining it. Holding the small, innocuous blue pill in one hand Phoenix reached down and – almost dream-like – picked up the cell-phone. “Rick,” he said with a small smile, seeing the familiar number on his caller I.D. before dropping it back, unanswered, onto the bed. “I can’t fight anymore …” Chapter Five Acceptance “… then don’t.” As if in a daze Phoenix stared at the unanswered cell-phone and then up at the open door, where his addled brain realized that the voice had come from. Through the rainbow haze of tears he saw his childhood friend, his best friend, Rick James standing there and for an instant he wasn’t sure if he was awake or not; if he was dreaming. If he wasn’t already dead. The slap that knocked his head viciously to one side made him realize, quickly, that he was both alive and very much awake. Seeing Rick’s hand raised again Phoenix quickly reached up and stopped the blow before it could fall against his face. Rick tried, in vain, to push against Phoenix but wasn’t able to move the much stronger man. “You walked away,” Rick said through his own tears as they began to fall freely, “If you hadn’t used your credit card I wouldn’t even have found you!” Wrenching his hand free from Phoenix’s grasp Rick scattered the pills from the bed and screamed into his friend’s face, “you holed up in this place and were going to leave … you were going to leave me!” Stuttering to himself Phoenix stood up in front of Rick and tried to come up with something that would calm his friend, some lie that he could state that would belay the obvious – something that he could say to convince his friend that he had the wrong impression, that the pills were for something else, something other than … “Suicide?!” Rick exclaimed, spittle flying from his mouth, “after everything that you have been through, after everything that WE have been through, you were going to do that?!” “I don’t know why I bothered saving you,” Rick said, his shoulders slumping, “I should have just let you die!” Remembering waking with Rick’s lips on his own, with Rick’s breath filling his own lungs and breathing life back into his lifeless form Phoenix felt a pain worse than the slap from moments before as he gazed at the utter dejection – complete loss - on his friends face. “I can’t do this anymore Rick,” Phoenix said softly, reaching out one hand to his friend but then stopping before he touched him, “I can’t be what they all want me to be anymore, I can’t fight this anymore …” Slowly Rick looked up and caught Phoenix’s gaze with his own, tears still streaming down his face as he stepped forward, slowly – so slowly. Reaching up he placed his palm flat against Phoenix’s face, a gentle mirroring of the slap, and repeated his words from only moments ago. “Then don’t,” he almost whispered, his mouth close enough for Phoenix to feel the warmth on his face. “Don’t fight it, Jay, be what you want to be … what I want you to be.” Tears fell again from Phoenix’s eyes as he closed them, but this time he didn’t try to stop them, this time he let them fall unchecked as he realized that he didn’t need to fight – that out of everything that he had been fighting against there was only one that mattered, only one that he cared about at all. Remembering Rick’s lips on his own, so many weeks ago, when he had breathed life back into him Phoenix found that – once more – his friend, his best friend … his love, the wonderful words leapt finally to his mind … was bringing him back from death again. This time, though, as he opened his eyes and saw the depth of love mirrored there – scared as he was – awed as he was – he realized that he didn’t need to fight anymore. He realized that he didn’t want to fight. With their tears mingling, their bodies fighting to become one, he returned the kiss. Epilogue The shutter clicked away to itself as the telescopic lens closed in on the two men framed in the hotel doorway, their arms wrapped tightly around each other as their faces pressed together. Allowing the camera to fall against his chest the unseen photographer leant back into the shadows and flipped his phone open, hitting the speed dial and barely having to wait two seconds before he heard the call connect. “You were right to get me to follow him,” he said with a small, vicious laugh. Staring down the hotel hallway he grinned as he watched Phoenix take Rick by the hand before slowly closing the door. “Oh yeah, I got it” he said triumphantly, “I got it all!”
