Forward and Back

1 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-01 Okay, bad idea--maybe. Jimmy goes outside and hails a taxi, quietly slipping over an explanation for the bag of luggage he's dragging along and just going to the airport. Seemingly learning from his mistakes, Jimmy takes a fistful of cash from his dad and buys an expensive seat on a private jet to the Bahamas, already planning his new life as a high priest of love. At least, he figures, he could enjoy it for a few days. The flight takes off without a hitch, until eventually a stewardess finds it out that there's this twelve year old kid just sitting by himself on an expensive plane. His dad owns Wisconsin, or something. Fill in the blanks however you want. Jimmy tells the story, playing up on his innocence enough that she just thinks he's joking. After it's over, she takes him by the wrist to the cockpit and knocks three quick times before going in and pressuring him to tell it to the captain. There's another run-through, and Jimmy again tries to feign out of actually giving the payoff, the understanding, the purple box. The captain, not being an idiot, pries Jimmy until he gives, with an eventual "I can't just kick you off the plane." "Okay, okay. It was 'purple box.'" A shudder courses the pilot's spine. "Get off my plane, creep." "What's the worst that could happen?" Trey laughed, not really understanding. "Shit, just a hike." "Just play stupid. Nothing's 'just a' around here, you know it. Constant psychoses--the name of the whole damn game." "I dunno, you get tired of crazy shit after awhile." "Maybe you. I'm still running great." "You ain't had a real job in a year, man." "You ever have one?" "I count wrestlin'." He laughs. "And I'm the crazy one?" "Nah, the retarded one." "Retards don't run multimillion dollar companies." "Neither do you." "Touché." "French now?" "Asshole, actually." "Close." "Yeah. You up for this?" "The hike, or the half-stupid metaphorical shit you're gonna pull?" "Look at the goddamned sky." Lighting, red against the hellish sky, skirt through the dense black clouds. The winds kick up. A man singing "The Sound of Silence" is crushed by an avalanche. Virgins, children, and mothers weep. "Shyeah, looks pretty good." Before that was after the bar was wandering in and out of warfare, from high to low in less than two hours and working on getting the back to the peak. We smoked and slipped under high arcs of blood, stumbled blindly past disemboweled saints. "Good guys win." "Don't buy it, J. Good guys never win." "They won those Star Wars, didn't they?" "S'a movie. Said it yourself, bein' an asshole made your career." "No, I said being an asshole didn't hurt it." "Ain't really helpin' mine." "You're not a good asshole." "Then teach me, wise one." "Asshole." "Cool." "No, you faked it. Good lesson is, well, think back to that chick you never called back that was still always bugging your ass. How you finally managed to rip the bitch off your back." "I was nice to her, man." "Bullshit. You can't be nice to those." "Nah, let her down gently." "'Sorry, but you're not hot enough to blow me'? There's no nice way to do that break." "Shyeah, just find someone new an' don't let the psycho come any closer." "That's the cheap way, you pussy-ass nice guy." "Ain't…" "Yeah, I know. You're still one of the nice guys when it comes down to it--whether you're the good guy or not." "What, I'm the bad guy?" "Never know--it'd be pretty smooth if done right." "Life's a joke?" "There is a man at a computer!" "C'mon. You know that shit was stupid." "Yeah, whatever. You didn't meet the creator." "I did. Wasn't that cool." "Were you there?" I paused and spent a second hiding under a street lamp as a car screamed past, roof being torn open by some indescribable abomination, a rough heap of limbs, eyes, and twitching mouths. "Shit. Forgot about that." "Drugs'll do that to you." "Yeah, fuck you. Least I got out if wrestling before I went too far." "What, not bein' able to beat a chick 'cuz you felt bad 'bout it? Some bullshit, man." "Maybe I just got my ass beat. But you're not that much like me, are you? No, never. Fuck, I've got it!" "What?" "How to turn you into an asshole. How'd you ditch Eva?" "Wasn't much to ditch." "Seen her almost every week since." "But we weren't really an item, y'know? Ain't my type." "Mine either. Too domineering--and tall." "Hah, shyeah." "How'd you do it though?" "Dude, wasn't anythin' to end. Just didn't hit it anymore." "Got what she wanted, I guess. Pretty good ending for that story, but what about yours?" "What you mean?" "We've gotta write the ending to your story sometime; might as well get it over with." "World don't always work like that." "Yeah?" I laughed and started moving again. The stone was crushing in, that uncertain and sometimes dreadful night time high where the whole world starts caving in until nothing's more than an inch away and there's no room left to breathe. I tried passing the joint, but Trey waved it on. Shrug. "So--go out a proud winner, an uncontrollable fuck, or a wiser loser?" "None of the above, J. Who said anythin' about goin' out? Doin' pretty good." "Were. Would be now, if you could control yourself." "Ain't got a match about control lined up." "Yeah--just two with Eva, one of those with Borealis dancing around too. You're not that great at making friends." "See, I'm an asshole.' "No, you're just stupid--but incredibly talented at backing yourself into ugly little corners. Big difference, there." "You never fucked yourself over? Bullshit." "Seriously--are you trying to be me or something? Just because I'm a fuck-up doesn't mean you get to be one too." "Free will, man." "Fucking, look." Stopped, ignored the rising tide of violence cresting over the hill. Wiped a bit of blood from my shirt. "I'd be a bankrupt jackoff right now if it weren't for some random lucky shit that happened thanks to some unseen universal magic. Someone likes me, in other words, and I get the odd feeling that that same bastard that likes me decided to pick you up and fuck you around until you got smart enough to deal for yourself--so he doesn't seem to dig you quite as much." "Well, if shit's like that, fate an' destiny an' all, what's the point of tryin'? Everything's gonna happen, can't do shit about it." "Since when? You just haven't had the balls to pick up your own life and do something that took effort. Christ, you barely have the balls to win a match now, when we both know damned well that you could kick the shit out of these people. I fuck myself over? Yeah, maybe. Might've thought myself out of a shitload of sure bets, but I'm not half as bad about it as you. You fucking beat yourself, Trey. May be time to give it up." Jimmy pulls the parachute too early and gets to coast down for the better part of an hour. His suitcase, strapped to the pack with the pilot's belt as a quick solution, doesn't make the ride much quicker at all, but the lunchbag at least keeps him full on the way down. Breaking through the clouds, he notices a small island and aims himself to it. Thirty minutes, right here. He crashes hard into the sand and gets bumrushed by a man more bent than the hobo, but not quite as raving. He helps Jimmy to his feet and gives him food, quickly asking about his story. Jimmy buys time, plays stupid, and ol' beardy spills on him in the name of human contact. Ten years, stuck. Not even a goddamned volleyball. It ends up pretty boring, well worth skimming over before the attention goes back to Jimmy. "An' youu?" "Well, I was walking to school this morning 'cuz I missed the bus, and this hobo came out of the alley and started screaming at me." Pauses. Thinks about it. "Saying 'purple box, purple bo-'" The man grabs Jimmy's hand, dragging him along the beaches and through the brush to a small clearing. He points to a rough-hewn raft, bobbing in the crisp blue tide. "I've been workin' on 'at raft for eight years. I wan' you off m' island." I don't remember how long ago I met Trey. It was in my dive run, stringing by on other people's money in a fed that time forgot. He wasn't very good back then either, but he was a lot better off. The money wasn't great, but he seemed like a happy kid caught in a bad rut--circumstance or his own fault, I couldn't tell. At the least, I liked him alright. He was a good guy, the clear cut nice one in any situation. No one really took notice, not even me. The type that slipped to the back of my head and don't tend to rise again unless in the midst of a week-long binge or a chance based on sheer bad luck later on. He was Casey Torpid's friend, so he showed up in 3WA a little after Casey did. At the end, I'll blame Torpid for everything that ever went wrong. Shit, the end. We were coming up on that pretty quickly with no real explanation. Well, shit happens. If the whole world starts to crash down, suddenly making sense isn't going to be the top priority. No one really needs understanding when end game's already been played, and there's not gonna be a rematch. So, we walked on from the gore of the city, to the woods, and he said "What's the worst that could happen?" Now, I get to laugh at that. To the summit, smoking and staring down of many of death's eyes as either of us could take at a time. The cigarette takes focus away from my eyes, half-closed and twitching nervously. I'm wishing it was high fantasy, or cheap sci-fi, or even a caveman movie that allowed the use of bows and arrows. No, we're fucked. We've always been fucked. "We gonna take this bitch, J?" "What?" "Well, I mean. S'two choices. We sit around like chumps and let ugly outshine us, or we stomp him. Simple, y'know?" He's laughing, letting his teeth pass for armor. "There ever gonna be a new Weezer album?" "Prolly." "Then I'm ready to die. Should I quote The Light Brigade?" "Nah. S'cool." So, game over. Do you put another quarter in, or just give up and move on to the next machine? Jimmy drifts aimlessly for a few days, surviving off the sandwich-based meals stuffed in his backpack. Even the dolphins start to look menacing after long enough, and the sounds at night are more than enough to push him past his adolescent limits of sanity. The whales singing in the distance, sirens. The barge making its bargey noises, Hades. The Statue of Liberty patina-ing--well, holy fuck, the State of Liberty. He dips his hands into the ocean for the first time, paddling with as much peanut-butter fueled energy as he's got left in him, and tries his best to hide some pussy mixture of tears and laughter. Almost to the bay, he's spotted by a Coastguard boat and pulled onboard, fed, watered, and questioned. "What's a boy like you doing out in the ocean?" "Well, I…and then I got kicked off the island." He takes a deep breath, not fortunate enough to get to gloss over his entire story with the freedom of ellipses. "And you got into all of this over what?" "You're gonna kick me out of the country, I know it." "I'm not going to kick you out of America, kid." "Purple box." For just a split second, Jimmy almost gets kicked out of America. It's on the man's face, eating at his brain like cancer, until a shade of understanding hits him. "You don't have any clue what that means, do you?" Jimmy shakes his head, no. "Well, alright. If you go in two blocks, take a right, and head another two blocks, you'll see a big brickk building. It's just past the Italian restaurant, can't miss it. If you go to the top floor, you'll find out." "Really?" I've been smoking too heavily for years. Hell, most people that smoke have, but that doesn't mean I've got nothing positive to say about it. Addiction's a wonderful thing to have around--it's the asskicking that everyone occasionally needs, but always there to laugh at you. When something bad happens, when you end up soaking wet and still covered in shit for trying to clean yourself, that cigarette, that needle, that pill, that line all exist as reminders that it could be worse. You could be dry, fiending, empty and hopeless. It keeps you in your place, and in the end, it's never the friendly drugs that'll destroy you. It's always yourself. Trey doesn't really have an addiction. He burns a pack of smokes in three or four days. He's kicked through his days of coke, dangerously close to hitting that line where "it's not a real physical addiction" is a blurry statement that doesn't hold any merit, and he's come out of it a little smarter and a little skinnier. He smokes pot, but not enough to push him into lung cancer. He's keeping his life in his own hands for the most part. But, of course, those hands aren't exactly the most reliable in the world. There's the problem, he always falls back to them instead of just using his head for something other than idling. It never gets past first gear when there's blood on the line. "See anythin' sharp layin' around?" I look, quickly. The plateau's barren, harsh, cracked ground and dying bushes. The closest I've got to what he wants are a few piles of bark, stripped from a tree that doesn't seem to be hanging around for the show. At the far side, closest to that lovely little hell towering over us, is a massive maple--or something--with dead branches that twist in a bramble of hard spikes thirty feet from the ground. So: "No." He rolls his jacket's sleeves up. I flick my cigarette to the ground and light another. The creature, barking like a herd of rhinoceroses, still blocks out the crimson moon with twenty tons of bloated corpulence. Its scaly flesh hangs, pallid green and decorated with ornate turquoise scales that glimmer in the haze, from its body and forms awkward pouches of fat that droop and sway as it moves, forming and killing shadows like a skyscraping Orson Welles. There's no way out. Our heroes, namely me and a friend, are backed into a corner and, well, outshined. Thanks, Trey. It's a good word. Hopeless for having any hope, we advance in heavy strides that could move mountains if we wanted them to. The smell, growing, just keeps getting worse as I notice the subtleties--a hint of scat here, a whiff of fetid rot there. Peace in the valley, yeah. "Can't back down now, J. We're all in." "You were all in the moment you woke it up. I'm all in because, hell, we're both fucking idiots." "Hah. Shyeah." Let that be the battle cry that future generations dine in the memory of. Let it stand as our divine wisdom in the face of certain death. Let it be the slogan for all fatalists to come after us. I don't care; just bring them on. We're laughing now, trying to drown out the beast ahead even as it leans in to gnash its tentacled-maw feet away from us, trying to think of any escape in as little time as possible. It's not for me to regret Trey getting fucked up enough a month ago that he brought about the fall of mankind, so I just smile at the opportunity it opened up. The next thing I know, I'm fifteen feet in the air and adjusting for a landing out of reflex. Understanding doesn't hang around these parts well, and it's after I fall into the brush that I see the beast's hand lifting from the dirt. Rocks, pebbles, dust, wood still drift down and spiral out from the crater it left. And Trey, Trey's latched onto its middle finger and trying to climb up. I watch from my little sanctuary, his hands clenching on rough scales as he slowly progresses against the, well, big ass fucking monster's will. No, there's no accounting for man's stupidity when faced with low chances of survival. Behind me, a crackling becomes a roar and succeeds in drowning out the bleating of our good friend, the doombringer. Trey, a black shape against the sky, grasps with one hand on a shoulder as the bronco bucks. His boots find a nook to slip into, and he climbs again. Believe it or not, we all know when we're unwanted. We just wish there were another way around it. Jimmy kicks through the city streets on his last leg, ignored by the pimps and pushers and the rest of Travis Bickle's enemies. Hollywood lied to him. When he gets to the tall brick building he'd been pointed to, he finds it unlocked and mostly empty. There's the obvious archaeological find of some sort of primitive meeting place--fluorescent light bulbs, potted plants, a large wooden desk beneath a golden monogram of JR, the shining doors of elevators that shouldn't work but do. At the top floor, he passes through a forest of cubicles, each outfitted with a computer, a chair, and exactly three pieces of worker-specific flair. The "Hang in There" poster, a few cut out Ziggy strips, and Monday Monkey become his best friends, his motivation to walk to the edge and be relieved of all suffering. The ticket was expensive, but it was his and there was no reason left to not just finish the ride and figure out what it was all about. At the wall of windows, he stands and looks out over a sleeping city--but mainly at a sign with a massive arrow pointing down, beneath the words "Purple Box?" He runs--not walks--to the elevator and forces it to drop quicker for his almost-catharsis. Apollo as his heels, he's out of the bottom floor in three-point-seven seconds flat and out into the street, dashing blindly. Slam. Fuckin' bus. Jimmy crumples. Death. Bam. Trey, legs wrapped around a tentacle, rears back and aims for a punch that goes straight through one of the eyes. A shriek follows it, and the body comes crashing down for a second. It's long enough for Trey to slip off and dart from towards me, at the edge of a drop off, to watch the thrashing show. Flames lick and twist in the gails of spasming monstrosity; lap closer and closer and all we can do is keep laughing. No, it doesn't make sense. No, it's not how it should've happened. No, there's nothing wrong in winning by sheer chance. It's the best some people ever get. It whips its body to the side, catching on that massive tree, now burning, and tears open its stomach in the mass of thorns. Fire, twitching at its dripping entrails, chars and torches and tears through its not-quite-so impenetrable hide and the roar, Jesus. No words. The audio equivalent of rape, genocide, and torture all wrapped up in one long shriek that slowly drops into the sea and still rises from that great abyss like the haunting voice of a dead clown. Remember kids--poorly discarded cigarettes can cause forest fires. Christ, that all made perfect sense. Right? So, maybe not everyone's lucky enough to figure out the punch line right before they die. Maybe I was wrong, and maybe Trey's in that group of poor shmucks that get fucked out of the whole deal. Or maybe I was wrong, and I'm the jackass for thinking it's worth knowing. The moral might ruin the whole deal for some, seem related tangentially if at all. Because there are people that care about the ending lining up with what they wanted to happen. They want things to be rational, make sense, add up in the end. Fuck fun, fuck surprise; life's got to be more than something to ask questions about. They want direct answers. Fuck 'em. If Trey's in that lot, fuck him. This is the best anyone's getting, and if they want to argue, it's their own fault for not paying enough attention to fill in the blanks themselves. The moral of the story? Look both ways before you cross the street. I warned you at the beginning, okay.
