Something to Prove: Part 1

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 The silhouette of a man stands before a trio of wall length video screens. Inspirational music swells for a long moment as he begins to speak. "Having a parent who aspired to greatness can be both a blessing and a curse for any child who chooses to follow in their footsteps. For some, their parent’s success becomes a shining example, a light to guide them through the hardest parts of their chosen course. For some, it becomes the impossible dream, trying to live up to the expectation that you will be as skilled or as wonderful as the man or woman who came before you…if not more so." The video screens change to show shots of Dawn McFarlaner from different angles, dressed in her ring attire from WMW, RPW and Global. "Primetime Central’s GTT5 is finally underway, with more then a hundred athletes from all manner of feds competing to prove they have what it takes to be the best of the best. Among the throngs of hopeful wrestlers is a first time participant for GTT, the ‘Steel Rose’ Dawn McFarlane. Since her debut at CW1 while under contract to WMW, Dawn has garnered a modest amount of acclaim and a decent fan following. What many, even her fans, are unaware of is that Dawn is in fact the second generation of McFarlane’s to set foot inside the squared circle. The silhouette slowly lightens until a young man in a black suit and tie can be seen. "Hello. My name is Marcus Gioavanti, and tonight I’ll be your host of ‘Something to Prove’, a look at the life of wrestling great James McFarlane, and the trials of his daughter Dawn." "The world of pro wrestling is a difficult one to break into, often taking years of long road trips and painful bumps before you can finally find a place among wrestling’s "elite". But everything seems a hundred times worse, when you’re living in the shadow of someone who came before you." "James McFarlane has been acknowledged as one of wrestling’s all time greats, a name that only the very newest to the sport would be unfamiliar with. His biggest victories have been recorded so as not to be lost over the ages, some of them practically carved in stone as a testament to his greatness inside the ring." The screens change again to three different shots of a very large man holding a title belt over his head. Each screen shows a different belt and a different location. "While these victories have been etched into the hearts and minds of wrestling fans everywhere, it was James’ biggest loss that touched the hearts of co-workers, fans and opponents alike…when he lost the battle with cancer. James McFarlane passed away on March 16th, 1998 after struggling for months with the cancerous tumor that finally claimed his left. He left behind an ex-wife, two daughters and a legacy that still touches pro wrestlers and their fans alike to this day." The scene fades out to a picture of a younger Asian American man seated in an arm chair. He has shaggy dark and piercing blue eyes that are looking to a point offscreen. A small floating tag at the bottom of the screen says "David Hayter: Formerly wrestled under the name "Ronin". "James was a hell of a man, both inside and out of the ring. He was one of the established guys when I first trying to break into the scene, and he always had a word of advice or a friendly suggestion or a pat on the back to keep you going through tough times. He was one of those men that always had time for the younger guys, no matter how big in the company he was at the time." The scene changes again, this time to an older looking man with a potbelly and a graying mustache. The tag under his face is "Neil Sullivan: Former owner and promoter of Pacfic Midwest Wrestling" "James was a lion in the ring, a fuckin’ lion I tell you. I could put him in there with guys that had several inches and a few hundred pounds on him, which was hard to do lemme tell you he was a big guy, and he’d go after them like he was fighting some kind of scrawny lightweight that didn’t know his ass from a whole in the ground. It didn’t matter where we were, or how many fans were packed into the stands, James gave a hundred percent in the ring and a hundred percent for his fans." Once again the scene shifts, this time to an attractive young man in a "PTC RULES!!11!" t-shirt and glasses. The tag says "Sean W., Rabid Wrestling fan." "Mr. McFarlane was an AMAZING guy! After one of his shows, he stood outside with us in twenty below weather just to make sure he signed all the autographs people wanted and posed for all the pictures people wanted! He even took like half an hour afterwards to sign my whole collection of PMW action figures! The whole thing! He signed James McFarlane, Daemon Knight, Bloodline, Shadowbind…" The scene finally fades back to Marcus, standing again in front of the three video monitors. This time the screens show home photos of James McFarlane. The first shows him swimming with two very small girls, helping them learn to swim with water wings on. The second shows him embracing a tall, blonde woman with striking green eyes, grinning like an idiot. And the last shows him flexing for the camera, with the same two girls (now slightly grown up) also flexing. "Stay with us through the break, when we come back we’ll look at the more family oriented side of James McFarlane. Stay tuned."
