Date: 2006-11-10 THE FALL OF BOGOTA By Richard Slain Ten long years of journalism, and not once have I witnessed a rebellious arc as bitter as that which has plagued the city of Bogotá. As an outsider looking in, I've bore witness to tales of tragedy, betrayal, and conflict, all of which revolve around the unfortunate crisis surrounding one man, Vangelus Olsig. This time last month, The former Arch Bishop and son of the great Czar Olsig was heralded as a sovereignty amongst men. Faithfully, he led a city of Christian faith through all threats of violence, establishing within them newfound confidence of which they'd never known. But in the blink of an eye, his sunny path turned dark. One faithful evening, he proudly rejected the teachings of the bible, labeling them as inaccurate and misleading. Needless to say, it gave birth to an uproar…the likes of which Bogotá had never witnessed before. Not only had Vangelus completely betrayed the trust of his followers, but he'd broken the bias land-law of Bogotá…the one which stated that no man, under any circumstances, held the right to crucify the teachings of the gospel. Enforcements came and Vangelus was put on trial. With even the jury morally against Vangelus, his fate seemed all but sealed. The greatest lawyer known to Bogotá had been hired as the prosecuting attorney, adding an even greater burden against Vangelus. The greatest blow to Czar's son came, however, when his sister confessed his unknown secret to a man known as Corbin Esposito. From there on out, Esposito held the case in his hands. His informing of that very secret to the court served as the basis for the trial. Without it, the trial could not happen. With it, Olsig's fate was sealed. But Esposito was shot. The town trembled with fear. The fact that Vangelus had been acquitted became overshadowed by the question of just who would kill Esposito? Only 2 percent of Bogotá citizens were non-Christians, and all of them had been jailed because of so…so who killed Esposito? No one knew. But now a greater mystery has arisen within Bogotá…one in which now circles around Olsig's famed uncle, Tony "Sharker" Olsig. Sharker was killed yesterday evening. I've become amused by the sudden panic which has taken over this city. A manhunt for this 'serial killer', so to speak, has been issued. These people are in a state of terror, but I saw that coming a mile away. My only question is who killed Sharker and Esposito…and why? Being the top reporter I am, however, I'm pretty sure I'll come across the answer soon enough…if I haven't already. This is Richard Slain, signing off. --- "Can…can we talk?" Fearfully, she stepped in front of Vangelus, preventing him from progressing out of the dimly lit office room. Plaided in a black trench coat accompanied by an equally black hat, Vangelus found his path towards the exit of his home delayed by the presence of his fiancée and soon-to-be wife, Elisa. The glimmer in her emerald shaded eyes portrayed a woman, broken by the tragedies surrounding her. The tremble in her voice confirmed that theory. "I'm on my way out the door." he replied, stepping forward in an effort to brush past his companion. They had kept her distance from each other for the past two days, and prior motions made it seem as if the lack of communication would extend even further. In realizing this, a sense of irony fell over Vangelus as he failed once more to brush past Elisa. "Ye-…I know but…we ne-…" "Look. Give me thirty minutes and I pro-…" "I know you killed him." Serenity. The glimmer in her eye became glossed with a tear as she pondered into the bewildered eyes of her opposer. His lips parted open, clearly displaying his sense of shock at the statement in which he'd just been issued. He shook his head as to regather his thoughts before re-addressing the query. "Excuse me?" "Please, Vangelus…just tell me the truth. Did you, or did you not kill your uncle?" she asked, desperately attempting to mask her tears with an expression of seriousness. "What!? I can't believe we're even having this discussion!" "Just tell m-…" "What makes you think I killed him? Huh!?" Screaming. Never in the course of their one year relationship had he raised his voice at her. To witness that seemingly non-existent side of him brew right in front of her broke down the mask in which she'd been attempting to hide behind. She couldn't bare it anymore. She broke down in tears. Vangelus looked with a hybrid of emotions. A side of him wanted to comfort her as she engaged in an act of emotion, but the other side of him wanted to slap her for even questioning him in such a way. Undecided on which side to follow, he watched her reach into her pocket, retrieving a familiar .22 magnum in which she presented to Vangelus. "I…I found this gun in your room. It's the same one that was described in the papers as being let at the scene of Tony's death." Vangelus grabbed the gun and glanced at it for a moment before forcefully slamming it unto the ground, subsequently placing his finger into the face of Elisa which caused her to lean in a backwards motion with tremble. "What the hell were you doing in my room!?" He shouted. Her lips quivered as she struggled to retrieve an answer that would not advance the situation to a greater debacle than that in which it'd already ascended to. "I was looking for…something." "You have no business whatsoever being in my r-…" "JUST ANSWER THE DAMN QUE-…" She caught herself before saying anything else. Just as she had never witnessed him raise his voice at her, she'd never been pushed to the brink where she had to raise her voice at him. But now…now she'd entered a totally different realm than any in which she'd experienced prior. It was almost as if she was having a total out-of-body experience. "Just answer the question, Vangelus. Why do you have the gun?" "Elisa…there are thousands of .22 magnums in this country! That doesn't mean the one I have is the one in which my uncle was shot with!" "LOOK AT IT! It matches every specific detail described in the papers. The taped handle, the serial number that's scratched off, even the red dash of paint slightly above the trigger!" "…your point is?" The boiling point had been reached. She clinched her fist tightly, desperately trying to restrain herself from unleashing a path of rage upon the man in which she'd been led to believe she loved. But love was absent now…all she wanted was the answer. He was not giving her that…and because of such, she exploded. "Are you fucking kidding me!? How naive do you think I am to believe that YOUR .22 magnum just so happened to identically match the one that killed your uncle!? I'm your soon to be wife, Vangelus, and you wont even tell me the truth? How am I supposed to marry a man whom I can't even share trust with!?" As if a sense of realization had replaced the previous sense of irony, Vangelus found himself in complete silence, unable to mutter a word even if he wanted to. He took a seat in his office chair, burying his face into the palms of his hands, seeking an escape from the mess in which he'd made. "Why do you have the gun, Vangelus…" In a total display of her fury, she grabbed a chunk full of Olsig's hair and yanked his head backwards out of his palms so that his baggy eyes met hers directly. "…and tell me the truth." For the first time, he trembled. It felt like the trial was occurring all over again. His soon to be wife stood in the place of the angry masses, all demanding answers while he stood in the familiar place of himself…the one who struggled to give them that. Knowing good and well the burden of trying to get a lie across her, he settled on the truth. "It's evidence." She released her grasp, glaring at him as if he'd just called her a fool. "Evidence? You're not a lawyer…you're not an attorney…what kind of evidence do YOU need?" "In case you forgot, I was a private detective once, and he is my uncle. I can assist in bringing down his murderer, can't I?" His argument seemed quite valid, but something still sat uneasy with her. "What was so hard about telling me that?" He brushed his palms across his face, preparing to pour out his emotions. "I didn't want to drag you into this mess any more than I already have. This city is ran by lunatics right now, all of who want MY blood. The biggest tournament in my wrestling career is in less than two weeks, and I want you to be there for me. If something were to happen to you…I-…" During this entire strain, she'd gathered a deep hatred for him and his lying ways…but as she watched two tears shake hands at the end of his chin for the first time since meeting him, she couldn't help but feel compassion. With a knot of sorrow building up in her own belly, she grabbed his head, pressing it against her bosom in an attempt to comfort him. "I-…I just want this whole mess to end. If I could've foretold what has transpired, I would've never opened my mouth. Now I have the internet moguls labeling me as a murder. My fans have turned against me. For all I know, I could be on the brink of being fired from PRIME for all the negative publicity I've gained them. Isn't like they need me, or anything. They're doing me a favor by constantly chartering my back and forth trips here, so I doubt they'd have any problem cutting me and my expenses loose. Then where do I go from there? Right to the poor house…again." She kissed him on the forehead, positioning his head so that she could look him directly in the eye once more. "Baby, don't think like that. We've got to take this thing one day at a time. Even if you loose it all, remember that I'll always be there for you. Always." With assurance in his heart, he pushed himself out of his seat and tugged his coat as he prepared once more to make his exit. Slowly yet surely, he ascended up the steps before coming to a complete stop, turning around to address his fiancée once more. "Elisa…just trust me, okay?" With that, he progressed up the stairway, out of the office room leaving behind a emotionally battered companion. She took a step forward, inadvertently stepping on the gun. She glanced down and peered at it as Olsig's parting words continued to linger throughout the atmosphere. Softly, she whispered to herself… "I wish I could, baby…I wish I could." --- Stepping into the district attorney's office, Vangelus swiftly flicked on the lights. Much to his surprise, there sat Morris Essex…the famed prosecuting attorney who was hell bent on prosecuting Vangelus. He was not the same, proud Morris Essex in which Vangelus had become accustomed to, however. No…he was a wreck. His hair went every way except the right way…his suit was as sloppy and slouchy as could be…and his face looked as if he'd just endured an hour long fight with a bear. Add that to the fact that he was sitting alone in the dark, and you could see why Vangelus was a bit worried. "Why are you doing this to me?" Essex stated, barely lifting his eyes to glance at Olsig. "I beg your pardon?" Olsig asked, still bewildered by his awkward appearance. Shift It was Tuesday. Nothing was on television, the streets were empty…it was a typical, boring Tuesday night. The calmness of it all allowed Elisa Lykes-Olsig to gain a measure of much needed rest. Outstretched on the leather couch, she engaged in a deep slumber.
