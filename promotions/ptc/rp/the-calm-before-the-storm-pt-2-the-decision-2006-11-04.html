The Calm Before the Storm (Pt. 2 The Decision)

4 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-04 Decision >noun 1 a conclusion or resolution reached after consideration. 2 the action of process of deciding. 3 the quality of being decisive. - Oxford University Press ******************** Joshua finds himself now on the brink of an important decision. What he chooses to do will shape his future and likely change his life. As William Shakespeare once wrote, ‘to be or not to be, that is the question.’ Joshua can choose to ‘bare arms’ against the troubles awaiting him, but in doing so could give the victory to those opposing him. With his concerns shed in the loving arms of the holy father, Joshua finds himself now on a bright lit afternoon in the city of Rome, New York. The sky is clear, and the sun is shining casting reflections on the crystal clear water of the river. Joshua lay back upon a rock formation at the side of the river just before a ten-foot waterfall. The grass is a bright green usually found on a spring or summer afternoon. The birds have flown south for the winter, the small animals have gone into hibernation but the deer are plentiful. Across from Joshua at the opposing river bank a muscular brown white tail button buck drinks from the cool water. Joshua’s eyes focus on the beautiful creature, and as he watches the animal he finds himself at one with nature. Traditionally a Garou (or Werewolf) has a very close connection with mother earth, only now has Joshua really began to feel it. Quietly he lifts his right hand from the rocks to brush a portion of his long white hair behind his ears. He feels completely relaxed and comfortable. The comfort is partly due to his attire. A simple pair of loose fitting hunter green corduroy pants, and a baggy button down olive shirt with short sleeves. Joshua watches as the deer raises his head from the cool drink and stretch his legs out in front of him, laying down in the cool grass. Smirking Joshua thinks to himself that the young deer has chosen to do the same thing that he has. However Joshua is quite certain the young deer’s reasons are different then his own. Joshua received a phone call last night from his Vampire friend Damian. Damian had been searching for leads to aid Joshua in his quest to find and destroy Harold. Damian found something. Joshua’s plan was to attack when the sun set. Joshua was aware of the dangers of going into battle with Harold and that he might not make it out alive. If he chose to encounter Harold without taking the serum that contained vampires blood it might kill him but then again if he did take it there were no guarantees that he would survive either. For all intended purposes this could be Joshua’s last day alive. If this were to be so, he wanted to make sure he could enjoy it in peace. Joshua watched the deer rise from the grass with his ears perked. Noticing the alarm in the deer’s eyes, Joshua listens intently. By aid of his supernatural senses he can hear the snapping of twigs and the rustling of leaves. He can smell sweat from a human body. Joshua sits up to try and startle the deer, but it is to no avail. *Crack* The deer drops to the grass instantly. Joshua knows he is dead. From the wood emerges a short plump man. He kneels beside the deer to confirm what Joshua already knew. The plump hunter grins as he rises to his feet. Without so much as acknowledging the deer again he turns and walks back into the wood leaving the deer dead on the river bank. Joshua’s heart breaks as he stares at the lifeless body of the young buck. With a single tear crawling down his face, Joshua stands and turns to leave. Immediately he places as much distance between himself and the tragedy of nature. This had been the message God had intended for him. But what exactly did it mean? Whatever it had meant Joshua did know one thing for sure. It didn’t help him at all, as either way Joshua could still die however he took the message. Joshua brings his right hand up, wiping the tear away with the back of his hand. If today was his last day alive, he had some things he had to do. Joshua walks on for a mile until he reaches a long stretch of houses. In the middle rests a brown house with white posts. He ascends the front steps of the porch following them to the front door. Inside of the front door is another pair of stairs, following them up to a landing he finds one more door. The door is white painted wood obviously a half-assed job by the looks of it. From his pocket Joshua extracts a key which he uses to unlock the door, and then he enters. Once the door opens a small blonde girl, no more than two-years-old charges her father and wraps her arms around his knees. "Daddy!" From behind the child steps her mother. She has long wavy dirty blond hair with a tint of red. He breasts are held firmly to her chest in her maroon sweater. The baggy magenta pants she wears tries to cover up her pregnancy but by the sixth month it is becoming impossible. Joshua kneels down and kisses his daughter Bonita on the forehead, giving her a huge hug. As he stands, he turns his attention to his wife Angel, somberness fills her eyes. "Hello dear." "Hello" she responds, holding back tears. She has not been the same since she watched their friend Scott and Joshua’s son Andy killed before her eyes. According to O’Malley she may never be the same again. "Have you found anything of interest?" Joshua shakes his head from side to side before lowering his gaze. He cannot bare to look upon his wife in so much pain. He knows she is beating herself up because it was his child, and she was convinced he held her responsible. Joshua held only two people responsible, himself and (if you could call him as much) the man who killed them. "I did however see a short fat man shoot an adolescent deer." Comments Joshua, "he didn’t even take the meet, he left it there to rot and spoil." "That’s stupid. There is no point in shooting it if you aren’t going to use it." Responds Angel. "Just senseless slaughtering." Joshua nods in agreement as the room falls silent. Both parties are aware what is going on. They are trying to make small talk because they are afraid. Afraid because this may be their last day together. Afraid that if they recognize it for what it is, that Joshua is going to die. Superstition. Blind, stupid - superstition. "Gonna watch it Dora, dad?" Bonita asks as she stares intently at her father. This whole child’s world revolves around ‘daddy, Dora and Frodo.’ She knows nothing of the consequences of death. She just can’t comprehend it, or maybe she is already doing what most of us tend to do. Pretend like it never happened. After all, no one can pretend quite like an imaginative child. "No, we don’t have any Dora here Bonita, I’m sorry." Responds Angel, consoling her daughter. "Maybe later it will be on tv, okay?" "Daddy wants to watch Dora…" Rebuttals Bonita. "Daddy want to watch it too." Joshua can only chuckle at his daughter. She will try anything to watch Dora, but like his wife had said, they had no Dora DVDs here. Bonita would have to wait. "I’m sorry kiddo, but daddy didn’t think to grab your DVDs." Bonita only looks up to her father before accepting defeat. If her mother had told her this she would not have quieted, but daddy is the law. There is no changing that. "Thank you, she won’t listen to me." Angel tells her husband. "She always wants to know where Daddy is, and I can’t blame here. I cannot keep the kid safe anymore. I realized that last week." These words sting Joshua, he was still upset but watching his wife lash out against herself tore him apart even more. "Don’t say these things babe, because after tonight it is all going to be over, one way or another." "Yeah, I can see how great that is gonna be, we have a fifty to fifty percent chance that you’ll live long enough for us to see you again." States Angel, with fear in her eyes. "I have to do it." Retaliates Joshua. "I have to beat Harold or we will never be safe. Whatever happens tonight between us he will never come for you again. I promise that." "How can you be so sure?" Protests Angel. "There is no way for you to prove that, no one can tell you anything for sure. Joshua nods glumly. Things do not look good for him, but he has to do what he has to do. "I know, but sometimes I just can’t change anything. I can’t help what’s going to happen babe." Finally she merely gives up on it, there is no point in trying to argue with him. Joshua approaches her and wraps his arms around her, giving her a big hug. "I love you babe, and if something does happen I want you to remember me just as I am." Angel nods holding herself to him tightly. "I love you too… but you have to come back to me, or I can’‘t let you go anywhere." "I will come back to you I promise." Reiterates Joshua. Joshua releases his hold and slips away from his wife to face his daughter. As he bends down to give her a kiss he notices she is holding something. It is in a glass and metal canister with two metal handles and a piece of white plastic to push the liquid out with. "Gotta take your shot dad, time to take your shot." The adorable little two year old stammers. Joshua’s eyes merely widen. It is as though God were telling him that if the deer where not obvious enough then he would have to be more deliberate in showing him his sign. Joshua takes the serum from his daughter and gives her a kiss. "I love you kiddo." "Love you too dad." Responds Bonita. Joshua rises from his feet and backs out of the door smiling at his family one more time. Maybe the last time. "I love you guys." "Love you too." The girls respond, as the door shuts in front of them. With a heavy hearted sigh Joshua turns and heads down the stairs as the thought of what just happened strikes him. God made things clear to him, and now he would have to decide when. He just hoped to live through it. As soon as the sun goes down Joshua will attempt it. Besides in the end he’s got nothing to lose whichever way he decides to fight this. If he is going to die either way he might as well try his only chance at living. Until the sun sets then, for then Joshua will risk his life for the biggest chance anyone has ever had. But if it works, it will all be worth it. If it doesn’t, at least he got to tell his family he loved them. Until the time comes.
