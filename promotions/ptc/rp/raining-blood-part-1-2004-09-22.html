Raining Blood-Part 1

22 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-22 What does a man do to show his worth? To make his mark? A man will try to do his best at whatever he does and show his peers that he is someone that should be looked up to and respected. In our business we don’t need to like our opponents, but a little bit of respect is always a good thing. Those who reach the top though, forget that they were once at the bottom of the barrel, they forget all they had to go through to reach the position they are in now. They forget how they respected everyone, and most importantly, they forget the business. That is what happened to a man everyone knows as Digital Mortality. He arrived to the GIWF as a nobody. He wrestled nobodies and sometimes lost to nobodies. Until he could prove himself, until he could prove to everyone that he could hang in there with the big boys. He wasn’t always the big name he is now, and he came from humble beginnings, but like all powerful men, he lost sight of what was really important, of what really mattered. It’s funny, when he looks at his career and sees all the mistakes he has made, all the errors in his way, the days when he would become an egotistical prick, and all the friends that ended up becoming enemies because of his selfishness, Digital Mortality pities himself and what he has become. It took the death of Justine, the woman he loved, to put things into perspective. He had stopped drinking, and had been sober for three months. I know that is not such a long time, but to an alcoholic like Digital, it was a long time. He felt good physically -he was healthy and strong- but mentally he was an emotional wreck. He knew that he would never be able to see Justine, and it tore him up inside. To know that someone loved you and thought of you as the most important and influential person in her life and not be able to be with that person in life is torture to the mind. Digital could say that he would go back to his UWF ways. Be the big shot, the main event… become arrogant and selfish as a way of hiding what was really in his head. But he knew that it was futile to attempt and quench his thirst for love. It was the only thing he had never fully attained, and when he did, he lost it. Fame, fortune, respect- he had all of these things in the bag already, but these would never fill his life. They were mere trivialities that took his mind off the bigger picture for a while. The only person that had really been with him through the tough times was Burton, but he had pushed him aside so he could brood by himself. It was not a wide choice, and right now when he needed his help, his never ending patience, and his guidance, he wasn’t there. The thoughts going through Digital’s mind at that moment were not very favorable for his health. He had thought of killing himself many times. He had thought about giving up on life, giving up on himself because he didn’t want to feel the pain. This was worse than anytime he was in the ring. The pain was excruciating. He needed it to stop if he wanted to continue his life. Should he kill himself? Should he continue on, even with this agonizing pain, this unbearable reality? It was impossible for him to see his future, to plan things ahead because there was such a blur over his life. He was unsure of what he would become, he feared that he would snap and turn into a monster. There was a side of Digital Mortality that he himself was always afraid of. He would suddenly shut every emotion out and become hollow version of himself. We saw that when he joined the Iron Curtain for the first time, and now that he joined the Judao Communist Revolution. The JCR would be like the Curtain, and they expected Digital to be that person who had his name on a Hall of Fame trophy. They wanted him to be that person who was the main attraction in the good old days of the UWF, the guy who everyone looked up to. That person was gone though, and would probably never be seen again. That optimistic, arrogant version of Digital was gone forever. That guy had been replaced by the grim, cynical, and hopeless Digital Mortality. ‘It’s over’, he said as he looked at himself in the mirror on his bathroom wall. He looked exactly how he always did. His long red hair all messed up and not tied up into his usual ponytail. He looked physically healthy, but emotionally he looked like a train wreck. He opened the faucet and passed his hands under it, wetting his palms and bringing them to his face. He kept his eyes closed and kept cleaning his hands. When he finally opened his eyes you could see the deep sadness that was troubling him, the sense of hopelessness that had taken over him. He had had the chance to take his mind of his troubles for sometime with the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, but thanks to hurricane Jeanne, he wasn’t able to make it to the venue in time. So he now had a lot of time for himself, time which he didn’t want. He didn’t want to be alone; he didn’t want to think anymore. What he didn’t know was that he had been given a second chance, and that his Round 2 opponent was J.T. Freedom from PRIDE. He looked closely at his wrists; he saw his veins where his blood, what kept him alive, flowed. He could not help but think that they were empty, that there was nothing inside. He could not help his need to see if he was really alive; he needed to see his own blood to be assured that his life was still worth it, but would it help? Would the pain of losing Justine go away? He didn’t know. He took his eyes from the wrists to the mirror, and he looked at himself. ‘Look at what you have turned into… wishing you were dead. Is it worth it to keep living like this? Should I just end it right here, right now?’ he asked himself. He looked deep inside himself looking for answers that he couldn’t find. He turned away from his own reflection, the faint scar from the time Tsunami Oni burned his face was now reflected in the mirror. He could not look at himself anymore. He felt like he had lost control of himself, like he had been possessed- and in some ways he had been. He went to his room and sat down on a black leather chair. His face was in his hands and his hair over his face. Trying to put together the pieces of a life completely broken, and at the same time trying to keep from hurting himself in a way that he promised himself he would never do. He looked around his room searching for something, anything to keep his mind of wandering into the same territory every time. Out of the corner of his eye he saw a box full of papers. Trying to find something useful to do, he sat on the floor next to the cardboard box and started to go through its contents. Newspaper clippings, wrestling magazines, and old letters from the booker of the UWF, and stuff that wasn’t worth putting up in his wall of achievements were all inside the box. Photographs of him with wrestlers that had disappeared and some of those that were still around. He saw a picture of himself with Gold E. Fish and White Wolf when they were members of the GIWA. Next was a picture of the Elements of Destruction. Suddenly his heart took a jolt; it started to pump faster and faster as he realized what he held in his hand. It was the letter that Justine had written to him, but had never delivered until she died, when her mother gave it to him. He hadn’t read it in a long time because it just depressed him more and more. He wasn’t in the mood to read it now, but he just felt drawn to it. He had it in the original envelope with his name, his real name, written on the front. He held it in his hand but wasn’t sure what to do. He threw it on the bed and stood up. He walked to the kitchen to collect his thoughts. He paced around the kitchen; the house seemed so empty. He opened the refrigerator and started to look for a beer, but there were none. ‘Damn,’ he screamed to himself. He had thrown them all in the trash to help him with his transition, but times like this asked for something to clear the mind. That elixir he didn’t use anymore could help him to forget for a while, but it would come back again anyways. There was no escaping it. He took out a carton of orange juice. He poured it on a glass and drank from it while on the way back to the bedroom. He wished it was vodka he was drinking, but he knew that he had to stop drinking. He got to the bed and grabbed the letter. ‘Screw it, I’m going to read it again…’ he said to himself as he opened the envelope and took the letter out. Dear Antonio, How are you doing? Is everything ok? I couldn’t help but watch the UWF show last week and saw that you had been hurt badly. I didn’t want to call you because of the way our relationship ended…you know. I miss you. I watch every show, just to know that you are ok, just to see your face. I’m really sorry about what happened between me and Joshua Dunn. It was a mistake, something that I will regret my whole life, because thanks to that mistake I lost you. I haven’t been able to continue fully with my life because it nibbles at my conscience all the time. The thought of how much you went through for me and how much suffered because of me is unbearable. It was not fair to you or even to me to have to live with this. Frankly, I thought you would have come back already. I don’t know if it is because you didn’t love me, or because you had already found another girl to fill that void in your life. It’s hard for me to write this letter, not knowing how you might react. I know I wasn’t the best of girlfriends, and you know you weren’t the best boyfriend either, but every couple has its ups and downs, its highs and lows, its bad times and its good times. I know this is a hard time for you, as it is for me, but I thought you might want to hear this, because if I didn’t tell you, or write it down I was going to explode. I love you Digi, I always did love to call you that. I love you with all my heart. There is no question in my mind or in my heart that you are the one that I want to be with. You are the only one that knows who I am, and I like to believe that I am the only one that knows the real you. I understand if you say no though, I understand if you feel like I don’t deserve another chance, but think it through. Sober up one night and put your mind into it. I love you, and if the question you asked me is still there, my answer is yes. Love, Justine ‘She wanted to marry me… all those times where I would just pass through her house just to get a glimpse of her. All the time I spent on this very same bed thinking about her. Thinking about us together. Now all of that is gone. Taken away by some fool who couldn’t handle his alcohol,’ he closed his eyes and silently cried. No tears were falling down his cheeks, but he was crying. He put the paper down and when he looked down at it, he was surprised to see that there was something else written behind. He took the paper once again and read what he had missed before. Umm… I didn’t know how to tell you this, but I’m pregnant. I’m 100% sure that it’s yours too. I want us to be together, I want our child to grow up loving his daddy like I love him. It took a few seconds, but when it hit him, it hit him hard. He staggered to his feet and felt very dizzy after what he had read. Not only had he lost the love of his life in the car accident, but his unborn baby too. This was too much to handle in his emotional state. He staggered to the bathroom where he started to rummage around searching for something. His eyes filled with anger, suffering and unfathomable sadness, anger at the man who had killed them, suffering and sadness for the lost ones he would never see again. He found what he was looking for, and he looked at it the way you looked at an ally, the way you looked at someone who was about to save your life. He brought the metallic object and put it in his wrist. The smell of blood already flowing through his nostrils. He couldn’t look down, as he cut the flesh in his skin and excavated for that vein that gave him life. He soon found it. The blood was already running through his forearm, a small pool of blood was already forming in the floor. He already felt the dizziness as he went for his other arm. He worked with the same determination on his other arm, and with the same result. He fell to the floor unconscious, the pool of red blood staining his already red hair, and his black clothes. Could he recover from this? Only time would tell…
