Welcome To The Obsession

9 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-09 My eyes find the house for the first time and I’m awestruck. Despite my general knowledge of the previous inhabitant’s wealth, it still shocks me to see that some people actually live like this. This isn’t coming from a bottom-dweller either. The house is gorgeous and then some, a rich cream color with oil-black shutters and status oozing from all sides. Of course, there’s a reason why it’s still vacant too: you don’t want to live in Angelo Deville’s old house. Waving in the movers feels righteous, like a good swing with a hammer. Countless men in drab, blue uniforms move in swiftly, resembling some low-intelligence furniture-carrying SWAT team. Rarely, one of them acknowledges my presence with a nod, but they mostly keep to themselves, scurrying around the house. The locksmith was here an hour ago to open it all up; now is the time to shake skeletons out of these closets. * * * * * "The door opened to my office that day nearly two weeks ago and a crazed Asian guy stormed in. He had a fire in his eyes like I’d never seen before. He looked like he’d seen a ghost, but at the same time, he looked a little relieved. He introduced himself as Chandler…oh, what was that last name…well, it was something real Asian. Now, we don’t get many folks of his type down at my office, so I decided to hear him out. He looked mighty queer, dressed in all those fancy clothes, but I’m legally obligated to hear arguments like his, so I settled in. He started off by bragging about some win in a boxing match or something against a guy with an Italian name. I recognized the other guy’s name: Angelo Deville. I’m no redneck, but everybody knows who Angelo Deville is. He’s as big as The Rock at this point. So after he name-dropped Deville, I was pretty rapt with attention. Pretending to run up some figures for what he was proposing, I Googled his name and got some hits about wrestling. Apparently, he’d beaten Deville in some sort of tournament when Angelo had gone missing. I was slightly less impressed, but I continued to listen to his plan. It was ballsy, I have to admit." * * * * * Stepping through the doors of Angelo Deville’s house is a rush like none other; sure, I took over his spot as top dog last week, but there’s something to be said for the visceral nature of trespassing in a man’s home. The threshold is marble, a sure sign of things to come within the hallowed halls of invincibility. I half-expect to find the house empty, with a note, smug and unrepentant, reading "I’ll be back when I’m back." There is no note, though; the furniture is eerily impeccable and the main hall is in pristine condition. A small part of me wants me to find Angelo Deville, wrists slit and brimming with dried blood. There is no hope for that after a couple more minutes of moving around. Realistically, I can’t hope to find much of any real evidential value here; a man with multiple homes around the country and in Canada doesn’t simply pick the most visible one to hide out in. Then again, the purpose of this mission is not to find Angelo Deville. Take out the supports and even the highest tower will fall. I’ve got a while until that insurance claims officer gets here. I’m not sure if I trust him, but that’s probably a good sign, since anyone truly trustworthy wouldn’t have signed on in the first place. Even among the splendor of the house, I can feel the tarnish we bring with us. Sure, there are dead bodies in the walls, but what I plan is far grander than mere murder. It’s social assassination, to be sure, but not criminally so (or so you think). Murder is impersonal though, grasping with a vice from just out of reach. I have already found the chink in the armor of the great Angelo Deville and now the crime seeps in like a leech. Am I obsessed with this titan? Yes. But if you can tell me that you’ve gotten even a technical victory over Angelo Deville, then we can talk. Should I be more concerned with Salaok, creeping up on me? Yes. I’m fascinated by the heroic fall, hubris running rampant. Perhaps that will be my fate, too, since I’m so concerned with it. "You know when Ramis is gettin’ here?" one of the workers mumbles. I look at him blankly, shrug, and lean against a wall in the house that Deville built, narrowly missing my own demise as a result of the blinders on the sides of my head. * * * * * "He was making a stink about how Deville had disappeared. He gave me a whole back-story between the two, back to some place called PWC that I barely listened to. He basically made himself out to be some god on earth for beating Deville in a match that Deville didn’t even show up for. I was totally confused, but then he told me what he actually wanted to do. He started, ‘I haven’t checked all the codes and such, but I’m pretty sure that a claims investor with a good knowledge of the law would be able to help me procure (at least temporarily) one of Deville’s very expensive, very luxurious houses.’ I looked at him blankly for a second, while he just sort of wore a smile cocked to the side. There was something really…bad about the smile. I didn’t trust the guy as far as I could throw him. Tsonda went on to outline this rather hare-brained scheme. I was going to fudge some papers and find a legal loophole; he was going to pay me twenty-five grand just for my hand in it and I was going to get more if we could actually wrest legal control of the house from Deville, in absentia. Supposedly, he was taking advantage of PRIME’s power struggle and Tyler Nelson had given him temporary power enough to basically claim the house as PRIME property. It made no logical sense, but the law isn’t always logical. I was able to work out some things and in front of a judge I play golf with every other weekend, we were able to convince him that Deville’s flight, in the face of…his past alleged criminal behavior, we were able to convince the judge that his house could be searched and then seized if anything incriminating was found. Tsonda chuckled that they’d find more than their fair share of reasonable doubt." * * * * * After urging out a mover, I commence tearing into Angelo Deville’s personal records. For a mobster, he has an admirable system of documentation. His computer is mostly encrypted, except for a large cache of files labeled "Targets." The first search through the house found all the legal evidence needed for this seizure, but it certainly couldn’t hurt to ensure that he stays in whatever Latin American shithole he’s crawled into. I open the folder and scroll around, finding over a hundred files. It takes a moment for the number of files, listed at the bottom of the page, to register. 127. Well, I am Asian and my intrinsic math skills tell me that this number is one less than the number of entrants within the GTT. "Of course," I offer to no one but myself. He’s got a file on every single competitor he could ever happen to meet. Many are blank, other than a name and vitals. Others have scribbled notes about the first or second round, but after that, the notes seem to come to a stop. There is nothing written about any action after the second round, which is a pretty good sign that Deville has certainly not been hanging out in this fine abode of his. Feeling confident, I look at my own profile. There’s a picture of some fantastic looking Asian guy on my pr…oh wait, that’s just me. Zing. My vitals are listed, along with some typical Deville comments: "Possible fag. Thinks he’s the absolute pinnacle of all things since he tied me in a Bar Brawl. Force him to tap out the Soprano. Priority: Medium-High." I’m offended that my priority is lingering near medium. Then again, at least if no one else thought I’d go anywhere in this tournament, I could always count on my buddy Angelo have some faith. A thought hits me that seems counterintuitive to everything that I’ve just been doing. I scroll through more useless files of men who have fallen off the brackets and this train that’s bound for glory. My eyes spy Salaok’s file and I instinctively click on the profile. Unsurprisingly, there is very little written down; having re-emerged into society recently, Salaok is a bit of a wild card, no matter whether I’ve beaten him once before. Apparently, he’s one of those wrestlers who has also conveniently contracted amnesia and is also a vampire and a sea captain and conquistador. It’s everything that’s wrong with wrestling, which is something that Deville and I can strangely agree on. "Little known. Little cared about. Priority: Medium." Talk about typical Deville. * * * * * "He was obsessed with your…with Angelo Deville. I tried to explain to him after the initial team had gone in and grabbed all pertinent evidence that he could go ahead and focus on his match with Salaok (at this point, I’d looked up a lot of stuff on Tsonda to make sure he wasn’t some nutjob). He insisted, though, that he go in to help move out and freeze the assets in the house. That’s the last I saw of him, this morning when he left my office. I swear, I don’t know anything else. Please, just don’t hurt me." * * * * * As I watch the first of the stuff being loaded out of the house, I feel victorious; trumpets blare throughout the grandest halls of my mind. This Valhalla will be raped of its grandeur now and whether Deville knows it or not, I’ll do whatever is necessary to lure him back out into the open. And, in all likelihood, that means making myself public enemy number one. What better way than to win the GTT? Turning my back on the house, I can’t help but feel I’m being watched. Perhaps it’s the dark, heavy feeling that Salaok is bearing down on me. Perhaps it’s life in a paranoid world where Angelo Deville has not been defeated, but merely cast into a dark corner from which he can spring at a moment’s notice. It must just be my mind playing tricks on me. * * * * * "I can help you," Jordan Ramis pleaded to the dark figure who had been interrogating him. The corrupted darkness was creeping in all around him, as the lightbulb overhead slowly and silently swung. "Please, just don’t hurt me. I know that you work for Mister Deville or…it doesn’t matter who you work for. I won’t say a word!" The claims adjustor’s voice was more nasal now, signaling a forthcoming stream of hot, guilty tears. But the other figure, covered by the dark, merely patted Ramis on the head. "You have served your purpose. And you’re right: it doesn’t matter who I work for. Do not try to contact Mr. Tsonda about this meeting and you will be left alive. Do…and you will meet the unfortunate end that many of Angelo Deville’s friends and enemies have also met." The harrowing silence bit into his skin like a rapier. He waited for a cold muzzle to meet his temple, but there was no more sound. The stranger was gone.
