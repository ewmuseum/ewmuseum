The Fall of the House of Almasy A Tragedy in Two Acts Act Two .:: ::: ::. Disclaimer: The following story is not necessarily the same one that Seymour Almasy OR his publicist will tell you. .:: ::: ::. Several Weeks after GCW’s Warpath Early Summer 2006 Morning on the McFarlane Estate was always a singularly pleasant experience—the sun rose in the distance, spilling orange and pink dawn all over the grounds inch by inch as the birds awakened to sing along with its coming. It was unfortunate, then, that most mornings, every single inhabitant of the sprawling house was asleep when it occurred. It was not so today. Shadow’s keys jingled in the lock as she opened up the door from the garage into the kitchen. She had gone early this morning to pick Vivica Valentine up from the airport, as she was flying out to stay for a few days. Naturally, the task had been left up to her, being the only early-riser in the house. Vivica was still rummaging the last of her bags out of Dawn’s Corvette, and was a few steps behind—lucky thing, because as Shadow stepped into the kitchen, she paused. She never expected to find anyone up at 6:45, but there he sat. Seymour Almasy was at the breakfast table, shirtless and wearing only red plaid flannel pajama pants otherwise. His feet were curled underneath him, indian-style, and he leaned over the table with a spoon, digging into a large bowl of Froot Loops. The box sat in front of him, as though this were perhaps round two or three of breakfast. She raised an eyebrow. “Good morning, Seymour... You’re up awfully early this morning.” Seymour chewed, swallowed, and smiled slightly. “No, actually, I’m up late. Been playing X-2 all night, and I stopped for a refuel.” Shadow’s brow furrowed, but she nodded in any case. She hadn’t figured out the appeal of these games yet, but they kept Seymour happy, so she supposed she didn’t have any right to judge him for the inordinate amount of time he spent playing them. “Just got back from the airport,” she said, by way of excusing herself—she was rather tired at the moment, and was looking forward to crawling back into bed to snag a few more hours of sleep with Virgil. “Airport?” he asked, tilting his head slightly, “Why were you there?” “Picking up Vivica?” she returned, a bit irritated to be sure, since she had told everyone in the Estate that she was going to do it, multiple times. “Oh,” Seymour said, completely unaffected by her annoyance, “I’m surprised Virgil didn’t go along with you, as much as he hates to be apart. You two should get a leash for each other or something.” The joke was lame, and it fell on the ears of one very unamused Shadow, who folded her arms over her chest and said nothing. She leaned in the doorway, watching Seymour finish his meal. Moments later, he sprang to his feet and started towards the sink to wash his bowl out. Over the din of the water, he spoke up again. “Hey listen... I’ve really been meaning to ask you about something.” “Oh? What’s that?” She tried to muster some interest, but the truth was, at the moment, Seymour was annoying her greatly. The noise of the water ceased suddenly, and he turned to lean against the counter, eyeing her with a slightly suspicious glance that she didn’t really feel she deserved. Perhaps that was just the tiredness talking. She took a deep breath and pushed it out silently. “What’s up with Mayhem?” “Mayhem?” she repeated, “I don’t know, what’s up with him?” “Come on, Lia... you know what I mean,” he said, and the smirk and familiar tone he gave her prepared her for a salvo, “Mayhem and... well, us. Mostly Mayhem being a part of us now. What’s up with that?” She shrugged. “Well, think about it: he’s loyal. Doesn’t ask questions, as long as you don’t try to restrict him from being as violent as he wants to be. And he’s an excellent shock troop. Now that we have someone his size in our camp, we’re as well-rounded a force as we can possibly be. The question’s not why we let him in, but why shouldn’t we have?” He looked at her, gape-mouthed for a moment. “Are you kidding? We shouldn’t let him in because he’s gonna be a handicap to us!” “Seymour,” she said, sighing, “everyone can be trained to be better than they are.” “Whatever,” he said, plainly not interested in the explanation, “I just thought we were about dominance, and... well, he’s not exactly the person I would have thought of to bring in, but I’m not the leader.” “No,” she said tiredly, “you’re not.” “Speaking of dominance,” he went on, and as he did, Shadow began to wonder what sort of cocaine was in the makeup of Froot Loops that he could be able to just stampede through everything she said in order to get to some indiscernible distant point, “I think we gotta do something about Warpath. I mean, I don’t know what happened, but we looked bad out there.” “Yes, I know,” she responded, her teeth clenched as he brought it up, “and I’m already reorganizing our plans. We’ve got to think about this instead of blindly striking out in whatever direction we please.” “I was thinking... somebody’s got to go get that World Title off of Knight. He got lucky winning it off of Dawn, but... okay, here’s my idea: Caldera owes me a shot at the World, right? Next WorldWide, I’ll just go out, drop a challenge, and Yevon knows, Knight is so overconfident, he won’t be able to turn it down... boom, we’ve got our World Title back, no contest.” “Look Seymour,” she started, “Dawn’s supposed to be getting a rematch for it anyway, so why don’t we hold off?” He didn’t seem put off of the idea. “Yeah, but wouldn’t it be better for her to face an ally than an enemy?” “Not necessarily, just—” And then her train of thought was cut off by the cursing behind her. She turned to see Vivica, bending down in the garage to fix the strap of her bag, which had just come undone, unbalancing the Black Mariah with its heavy weight and nearly sending her tumbling to the ground. Seymour, of course, had gone on talking, irregardless of whether he had her attention. “...just mind-blowing, you know? We go in there ready to show them what it’s all about, and when we walk away, Vivica’s the only one who’s still got her title,” he was saying, and then he smirked, “Which, you know... I gotta hand it to her. I didn’t expect her to be able to hang on to the damn thing for so long, especially without Zenith here to help her do it.” Shadow winced, as Vivica came up behind her, just long enough to hear that. She was already aggravated and tired, and as the words caught her ears, her face flushed with anger. Like a pint-sized freight train, Vivica Valentine barreled into the kitchen. “Yeah? Well, I’ve found out something amazing too—you’re still able to have an ego as big as yours is without any proof whatsoever to back it up!” Before he could retaliate (as though he cared to), she stormed out through the side door, towards the stairs that led to the guest quarters, still fuming to herself. Shadow stepped forward into the kitchen, all illusion of patience with him wiped firmly away. Much to her surprise, though, Seymour took the blow... or ignored it, rather, shrugging his shoulders. “Well... looks like I’ve been up here long enough to rest my eyes,” he said, nodding at the clock, “I gotta finish stat-maxing before I hit the sack for the... well, day.” Unbelievably, he headed for the basement stairs. “Seymour!” she snapped, her voice much more harshly commanding than perhaps the situation called for. He turned to her. “Yeah?” “I promised you friends... I can’t make them like you.” “What?” he said, the statement seeming to give him genuine pause for a moment. Then, he waved it off, “Oh that... whatever, it’s just a friendly rivalry between Viv and I. Maybe she’s PMSing or something,” he quipped, starting back down the stairs. It wasn’t a very funny joke. .:: ::: ::. GCW Offices One Week Before Dangerous Games September 2006 Steven Caldera didn’t like Shadow. He didn’t like her at all—which was why she found it exceedingly curious that he had called her cell phone and left a request for a meeting to discuss ‘an urgent matter.’ They let her into the inner office like they were expecting her, and she wondered if, somehow, she’d done something to break her contract, and was getting called in to receive her pink slip in person, to add insult to injury. President Caldera, as he liked to be called, sat across from her, behind a large oak desk, the sort of thing that you would verily expect the president of a wrestling company to sit behind. His hands were laced together and folded primly on top of his desktop calendar, and the expression on his face was one of complete and utter exasperation. “Shadow,” he said, because they two had never gotten very far past formalities, “There’s something that we need to talk about.” She nodded, and tried to avoid making a smartass quip about not expecting him to have invited her for tea. “Yes, that’s why I took time off to fly in, Mr. Caldera. You don’t look terribly pleased, though—have I done something?” He shook his head, pursing his lips together for a moment to show his displeasure. “No. No matter what enmity we have between us, I have no complaints about your performance. One of your stablemates, however... is a different story.” She didn’t have to wince, she knew exactly what was coming. Caldera sighed exasperatedly. “Shadow... I called you in here and not him because frankly, I didn’t know if he’d show up. Since you’re the ostensible leader of all of them, and seem to have a lot of influence... you’re the next best shot I’ve got to discuss this issue seriously.” Her arms were already folded over her chest. Her eyebrows raised slowly. “I hope you’re not thinking that I’ve got any kind of sway over him, because frankly-” “Look,” Caldera said, holding up his hands, “I’m not putting the blame for his actions on you, but you’ve got to take some sort of responsibility for him, if he’s as much your charge as the others are.” There was a brief standoff between them, lightning sparks from her eyes to his and back again. Plainly, she wasn’t impressed with his reasoning. Caldera looked away, irritated, and continued. “I’m going to put this the best way that I know how. Almasy’s been... well, just look at the past few weeks in the ring, Shadow. He’s lost match after match after match, and he hasn’t just lost them... he’s gone out there and submitted. He’s taken pinfalls after moves that shouldn’t have finished him off. He’s gone out there and worked sloppy. And he’s worked sloppy because he’s blown off training the week before. But he’s done this for people who, bad guy or not, paid a lot of money to come see him wrestle.” From the business end of things, he was thus far solid. She found herself nodding along. “It’s not just that, though. Are you aware of the promotional dates that he was scheduled to do recently?” She shook her head to indicate the negative. “I really have no clue about those sorts of commitments. He didn’t make a habit of telling us about them.” Caldera’s hand thudded down to the desk in a closed fist. He sat forward in his chair, his eyes bearing down on her. “You need to understand this a little bit better from my perspective. Seymour Almasy is a big name. I considered it good fortune to sign him to GCW when I did, because people love to watch him. Even after he sold his soul to you guys, people still wanted to tune in just so they could hate on him a little more every week. I consider that good for business. But do you know what’s not good for my business?” He only left a split second of space, not long enough for her to say well, no, of course she didn’t. WHAM! His fist pounded down on the desk and the wood groaned under the blow. “Screwups aren’t good for my business! People who sign their name on promotional dates, and then welch on them like crazy! Two weeks ago,” he said, his face beginning to flush crimson, “two weeks ago, I have a Seymour Almasy GCW signing... for which Seymour Almasy doesn’t show up! What am I supposed to tell those people who have waited in line to see him, and... interviews! The last three interviews I had him scheduled to give, he’s blown off completely!” Caldera’s fists clenched tightly in rage—impotent rage at the moment because, of course, he wasn’t even raging at the person he wanted to rage at. She eyed him, carefully picking and choosing her words. “I’m afraid I don’t quite understand what you want me to do about it.” “Do about it?” he practically howled, “You need to first of all tell him to get his shit together, because I am sick and tired of having to make excuses to cover for him! People are going to start making up rumors when they can’t figure out what’s going on. As hard as I’ve worked to get GCW back where it needs to be...” He trailed off without finishing the thought. He didn’t need to finish it anyway, because Shadow knew the exact direction it was heading. She sighed. “Mr. Caldera, it may surprise you to learn that what I’m about to say, I’m saying with the utmost respect for everything you’ve done for the company, and even what you’ve done for us since we started here... I just don’t have anything that I can tell you. I’ve tried, believe me I’ve tried—you saw WorldWide, you know what happened when I tried to tell Seymour that he was out of line. He reacted like any good teenager—told me I was had no right to ask him to do anything he didn’t want to do, because... well, basically because he’ll do what he wants to do, no matter who tells him otherwise.” Caldera shook his head immediately. “No, no, no, no,” he said, ire still hammering through every word, “No, that is not what I mean, I’m not talking about sending him after Youngblood, or reprimanding him for not doing it right, I’m talking about letting him know that everybody’s noticed his lack of effort. That he can’t just act in a vacuum, and—you just need to tell him!” Caldera’s words exploded in her ears. “You need to tell him that he’s got to start trying!” Shadow eyed him coldly. “I’ve already told him that too. And whatever else you want me to tell him... I’m not sure how I’m going to do that. Because he’s moved out of the Estate.” Caldera’s face slowly drained of all color. “Moved out of the...” Shadow nodded. “Moved out. Don’t know where he’s gone.” “Christ,” Caldera said, running his hands through his hair with a classic ‘CEO migraine’ gesture. Silence reigned supreme for a few moments that seemed like eternities, before he looked up from his desk, and spoke quietly, each word measured and furious. “This is all I’m going to tell you right now, and after I tell you this, I’m going to ask you to leave my office, because frankly, I’m a little sick at this situation. I’ve scheduled a promotional appearance for Dies Irae and the Knightmare Brigade. I want all of you there. All of you. I don’t care how you get him there, but if you call him a friend... you better get him there. You don’t want to know what I’m going to do him if I don’t see him there with the rest of you.” There was a driven hardness in President Caldera’s eyes. She quietly affirmed his statement and stood up from the seat she had taken in front of his desk. As she left the office, she felt as though a heavy weight had descended from on high to hang around her neck. But this albatross was not hers to bear, and she did not intend to bear it any longer than she had to. .:: ::: ::. The Present The Wynn Las Vegas, Nevada Shadow’s boots made no noise as she paced the thick-piled deep purple carpet of the Dies Irae suite in the Wynn Hotel. It was very rare that they went this upscale, but this was a special occasion. That was the last thing on her mind, however. Her cell phone was pressed to her ear so tightly that it felt like it was beginning to sear through the skin. One ring... two... three... Three pairs of eyes were watching her pace. The tension in the room was palpable, and when she stopped cold in her tracks, it was as though everyone in the room was holding their breath. “Hey this is Seymour... if you’re getting this message, it looks like I’ve blocked your number. *chuckling* Better luck next time—oh, and if you happen to be Lia, Dawn, Virgil, or Vivica? Maybe you guys should try calling Youngblood first.” Bitterly, Shadow depressed the end key and tossed the phone onto the sofa, right next to where Virgil was sitting. He made an abortive gesture to recover it, and then paused, zeroing in on her. “What happened?” She ran her fingers through her long, soft hair and smiled wryly. Her look first fixed on Virgil, then on Vivica, who sat cradling her US Title in her lap. Finally, her eyes caught Dawn’s, sitting behind the two of them, whose expression was so bitter it looked like she had heard the message herself. Or perhaps Dawn just knew exactly what to expect out of Seymour. “He’s blocked our numbers... said that if we really wanted to talk to someone, we should try calling Youngblood.” Virgil’s expression darkened. “When we catch up with him, Youngblood will be the least of his worries. Runs out on us and then...” "That little BASTARD!" Dawn exploded, thrusting herself up from the chair, "Christ on a stick, you'd think you'd have offered Youngblood a spot in DI or something the way he's behaving. We'd have been better off with Rich Rollins over that whiney little girl." Vivica rolled her eyes. “Then they’d just be arguing about who nailed Laura best.” “Forget about the dig... we’ve got to figure out what the hell to do,” Lia said, her eyes scanning across her stablemates. News traveled fast within Dies Irae, naturally. They all knew about Caldera’s ultimatum, and imagining what was going to happen to one of their own if it got broken wasn’t pretty... or perhaps it was. “I say let him twist in the wind,” Dawn growled through her teeth, “And I’ll even provide Caldera with the rope.” “Not many other options,” Virgil chimed in. Shadow wouldn’t have really suggested defending him in any case, but the question had been aimed to make certain they were all of one mind. Once more, she scanned over them—her stablemates, but more than that, her family. They were all people she could depend on to defend her no matter the cause. But Seymour had never been a part of that inner sanctum. He’d gone so far as to come to her privately and complain that no one was accepting him, but with the way he’d treated them all, she couldn’t blame them. “Well,” she said, her face painted in resolute battle lines, “that’s that, then. Let’s get ready for our appearance, shall we?” A collected sigh was released, all the tension seeping out through the cracks underneath the door. They’d been waiting for this—waiting for the moment when something would break. Shadow had always hoped, deep down, that Seymour would one day realize the sort of intensely loyal people he was surrounded with, and he would change his tune. She couldn’t speak for the rest of them, though she could wager a bet that wasn’t the first thought in their minds. This had been a long, long time in coming. “Look,” Virgil said, breaking her out of her reverie with a jerk of his head towards the television. They looked—and again that pall fell over the room. Someone unmuted what turned out to be a short teaser for GTT6. The details of it didn’t matter, only that his face had cropped up again—in the place where everyone had seen it lately. Seymour Almasy’s mug plastered all over PTC banners. Seymour Almasy’s mug plastered all over GTT6 posters. Seymour Almasy’s mug plastered all over Infinite Gauntlet ads. In those few short moments, Shadow began to wonder who it was he thought he was kidding. But she knew who he thought he was kidding, and all of them were sitting in a hotel suite getting ready to go to a promotional appearance for GCW right now. The simple, bare fact was that Seymour Almasy thought he’d pulled a fast one. It started with a pity story, and the pity story had gotten him an invitation to join Dies Irae. From there, he’d felt secure, like as long as he signed his name to the rising faction in GCW, that he could force Caldera’s hand over the World Title. And when Caldera had balked at the idea, then Seymour had gone on a complete bender—blowing off promotional events, blowing off training, blowing off losses like they were nothing, like he didn’t give a rat’s ass how he performed in Caldera’s ring. ‘See me? I’m your golden boy... mistreat your golden boy, and this is what happens.’ Only that, too, had not had the desired effect. GCW had been a disaster, and he stood no better chance of getting a World Title shot there right now than anyone else entering the Dangerous Games battle royal. In fact, a lot of people would have said he stood much less of one. That was why all this pomp and circumstance was necessary. Everyone had already heard the rumors from the dirt sheets: ‘Seymour Almasy to retire soon,’ ‘Almasy says GTT6 is his last run.’ And if it was his last hurrah, then by Yevon, he planned on going out as the conquering hero. He planned on going out with the clarion trumpets and the golden calf that GCW would never give him! It was unfortunate, then, that it was smoke and mirrors. You didn’t have to look too far to see that Seymour Almasy was a very, very desperate man. Ever since GTT5, he’d been running from the specters of his past, running from Laura, who was chasing him down with the reminder that fate had already called, and he was long overdue for a meeting with her. Underneath it all was the rancid taste of desperation, the sort of taste that came with still sitting alone at the 3AM last call of the skeaziest dive in the city. The pity party had ended, unquenched, and so Seymour Almasy had to put on a new mask for GTT6. She wondered if reality had ever occurred to Seymour as a viable option, but it was a stupid question aimed at a man who called himself the Final Fantasy. For all anyone might have said about her, there was also plenty to be said about a PTC legend who was scared of his own shadow. .:: ::: ::. "True friends stab you in the front." Oscar Wilde
