(UNFINISHED)Obsession or Madness Part One, A Documentary on "The Buzz" Cal Roberts"

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Wednesday, October 21sth 9:30 P.M. Voiceover: In my eyes, it is the king of all sports. I don’t know what it is about it that is so exciting, but nothing is able to light a fire in my soul quite like it does. It is who I am and what I do. It is my one true love in life. The one thing that I can always count on to never let me down. It’s my teddy bear or security blanket, my crutch when I’m feeling weak. It is the only addiction that I’ve ever succumbed to. It is known quite simply as, Professional Wrestling. “Ten!” “Eleven!” “Twelve” “Thirteen!” “Four. . .teen!” The weight was pushing back at Cal Roberts, like that of the world trying to force him into submission. Roberts refused to give up, not without the last repetition. With all he had left in his legs, he squatted the weight back up into the standing position. He was just able to rack the weight back onto the power rack before his right leg buckled and he collapsed to the floor. His lungs were burning from the intensity of the leg workout. While his leg muscles were numb from their exhaustion. He slowly sat up in the middle of the rack, and attempted to catch his breath. A drop of sweat fell from his forehead, splashing onto his traitorous right leg. His spotters quickly moved in to help him up, but he waved them off quickly. Voiceover: My passion for this business has never faltered, but all the drive in the world can only take you so far. Eventually time will catch up and slap you right in the face. It’s what you do when you’re hit with adversity that determines just how legendary you truly are. Roberts pulled himself back up using the rack; he ignored the sharp pain in his leg that would never completely dissipate. Roberts nodded toward his spotters; they each added a plate to their respective side of the bar. “My strength is legendary for a reason. I push through mental and physical failure by digging deep into my soul to find that special something that only the true greats possess.” With his leg acting up, the process was a difficult one. Roberts never backed down, and stood strong battling his iron foe. Each repetition was harder than the last. The pain growing as he grunted to push the weight up. “This is how legends are born!” Roberts gave everything left in his body to force out one final rep, racking the weight as soon as he was upright. His breath was labored and his face red. “Sparring, weight lifting, cardio are just pieces to the puzzle. It’s all about passion and the desire to become the greatest of all-time. Some are just born with the innate talent to succeed at whatever they set their mind to. But you must dedicate yourself regardless of your natural gifts. Without the effort even I would fall to levels of mediocrity.” Roberts gave a wave toward his spotters and they disappeared from the camera. He walked over to his wrestling ring in the center of the gym and sat on the edge. He found himself playing the ring ropes and one could imagine the flood of memories filling his head. A smirk spread slowly across his face as he sat in thought, now alone in the large training facility that sat attached to his estate. Voiceover: The life of a professional wrestler has become more documented now than ever before. But you can never truly understand why. Why we put ourselves through the intense pain in the squared circle. This is all I’ve ever wanted out of life. The respect of my peers and the fans, and all the glory that comes along with being the greatest to ever lace up a pair of boots. It’s what fills my soul every morning before I wake until I lay down to bed at night. My dreams are not even safe from this “dream” of mine. The only downfall being that it has cost me any semblance of companionship. I don’t set out to purposely hurt the feelings of others, but they serve me very little use in achieving my goal. Therefore they are quickly discarded. I don’t have time for a family or to sit around and bullshit with the boys. I’m afraid glory comes with a price. Roberts grimaced in pain as he stood back on his feet; sitting had left his leg stiff and sore. He limped toward the door leading into his home. Once there he turned back and motioned for the camera to cut. Voiceover: I injured my leg in a major car accident back in early 2005. I was driving to the airport after a short, and was t-boned in the driver’s side door. The impact shattered my right fibula, and left me a broken man. I never lost hope that I would one day return to the ring, and because of that mindset I was able to return ten months later to the national stage with nBW. After an intense rivalry with Blake Gray I was forced to tap out for the first time in my career during a submission match. It sent me spiraling into depression, and nearly cost me my career. I retired directly after that match, and for a time had no intention of ever returning. Truth be told, it wasn’t until I began to watch some of my own matches that I realized just how good I was. I owed it to myself to get back up on the horse and ride to the top of the mountain once more. The injury is something that stays with me to this day, I know it’ll eventually be what takes me out of this game. However, coming to terms with that might not be possible. Thursday October 22nd 3:30 A.M. The trophy room of Cal Robert’s estate was dark, filled only by the light of the small television set that sat in front of Roberts. On screen was Karina Wolfenden with her hand being raised in victory. Roberts chuckled to himself and took a note on a scrap piece of paper that sat in front of him. “I find a weakness in each opponent that I might encounter. With Karina I found that weakness years ago. It’s something I fully intend to exploit in our match. Her weakness is quite simple the fact that she is not a man. She might be able to keep up with your average wrestler, but not an elite athlete like myself.
