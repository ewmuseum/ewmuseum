Part One: Genesis

13 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-13 So the road has brought me here. That statement has a double meaning, actually. Metaphorically speaking, the ‘road’ has brought me to Primetime Central’s GTT4. After years of hard work, I finally made it out of the indies and into UWF. I knew when I entered about the PTC inter-fed network, although I didn’t want much part of it. It seemed to me, at the time at least, that there were nothing more than muscle-headed big shots running around over there. Nothing but snub-nosed wrestlers anxious to protect their spots within their company and corporate suits who wouldn’t give me the time of day unless they thought they could get something from it. Nearly three years amongst the UWF ranks and I’ve avoided PTC until now. Despite some past unpleasantness that seemed to confirm my suspicions, here I stand at a fork in the road of my career. Do I take the path towards glory and fame, the path that leads me to GTT4? Or do I turn the other way and continue about my way of success in the UWF? I’ve come a long way since the days of wrestling amongst the garbage feds. A long way from jumping off of houses to plummet down through tables where my opponent laid covered in tacks. A long way since emulating my wrestling idols before I developed into the person who stands here, at this fork, today. It’s taken me many, many years and many, many injuries to get where I am. And I know that I would be nowhere without the fans. I know that if it wasn’t for the people who stood behind me through all of the highs and lows, I’d still be jumping off of houses. Or maybe not… They believed in me when I was told that I didn’t have what it takes. They cheered me when I was told that my style would never work. They’re the ones who sent me get well cards when I was laid up in the hospital. Most recently, they’re the ones that showed me respect when I stood against impossible odds against Kenny Mercury in a One Way Streetfight. I think I’ll continue about my path as usual. That doesn’t mean I’m turning right or left. I’ve never been a man to conform and go one way or the other. I’ve always been that grey area in wrestling. My path isn’t right or left. It’s straight down the fucking middle. This is where the road has brought me. Literally speaking, that road has brought me to the last place I thought I’d ever be. A place I’ve loathed for so long for so many reasons. Much like the wrestling world, this place was ripe with hypocrisy and people just out to make a quick buck. People who lie and steal, backstab and betray. This place that had shone like a beacon for others but seemed evil and twisted to me. A house of iniquity, sorrow, and lies. A house built on the very blasphemy they considered a sin. The road has brought me to the house of God. Misfit steps into the cathedral, looking around to take in its beautiful imagery. For being such misled screwballs, the Catholics did believe in artistry to an overzealous extent. He looked like a rose amongst a bed of thorns standing in the holy halls of God; not that he was beautiful or anything, just that he presented a huge contrast to the ornate decorations. The stained glass windows about the church displayed beautiful depictions of the saints and various notable scenes from the Bible in an array of glorious colors. The woodworking of the pews is marvelous, each bench made from a dark-stained oak with ornate shaving and design work. Towards the front of the congregation, just before the pulpit, stands an altar of the same wood and similar design as the pews. An elaborate candelabra rests on the altar with a cup of wine at either side and a silver plate with bits of sacramental bread scattered about it. A beautiful, three-foot column stands at either side of the altar, each one holding a large ceramic bowl of holy water. Behind the altar stands a fantastic pulpit, made of more oak with very intricate woodcarving done and all sides. Behind the pulpit sit the choir benches, each one draped in beautiful red velvet. A massive carving that depicts the crucifixion of Jesus looms ominously at the front of the cathedral, the arms spreading out to the width of the entire inside. Several lit candles litter the altar, pulpit, and floor at the front of the congregation. Some have flowers or rosaries near them, signifying the great importance whatever patron placed them there has in their power. Misfit draws in a deep breath as he surveys the Catholic sanctuary, smirking as he looks down at his own attire. He wears dirty, scuffed work boots and a pair of black jeans, the knees heavily worn almost all the way through. His black t-shirt stands out the most inside this holy house of God, an inverted cross splashed across it in red paint. A large quantity of scars spread across his arms, many of the wounds self-inflicted and the rest suffered while participating in the ring. He reaches up to brush the blue tips of his black hair away from his eyes, revealing his somewhat unshaven face from behind the veil of hair. Drawing in another breath and lifting his head, he glances around to find the confessional booths. Spotting them off in a rear corner, he turns to head towards them, revealing the number ‘666’ scrawled onto the back of his shirt in more red paint. Grinning at the few people on their knees praying, he reaches for the handle of the door. He stops to observe them for a moment, some of them marking the points of a crucifix on their head, chest, and shoulders while others clutch rosaries. “Fools,” he spits under his breath, laughing slightly. He continues to observe as one patron stands and slowly walks towards the front of the church to light a candle on the altar. Shaking his head, he turns back to the booth and enters, taking a seat on the small wooden bench inside. He clears his throat, trying to make some small amount of noise inside the booth so the priest will notice him. The panel between the thick veils slides up, Misfit looking to it for a shape or outline of a man of the cloth. “Father?” he questions. “Yes, my son,” echoes the voice from the other side. Silence. Misfit sits inside the booth pondering why he came to this place he loathed so much. The Catholic faith had always been a joke to him; faith in general, actually, had been a joke after all he had seen. “Are you there?” questions the priest. “Y-yes,” Misfit stammers. “How long since your last confession?” Silence again. Misfit rolls the question over in his mind for a moment before a devilish grin crosses his lips. He sits back in the booth and relaxes. “I’ve never confessed,” he says, lifting one leg up onto his other knee and slinking back against the booth. “I’ve never felt the need.” “You feel that there is no need to clear your soul of taint and evil?” the priest asks, perplexed if not offended by Misfit’s statement. “I wouldn’t put it that way. It’s more like… I always thought this whole Catholic thing was a joke. Really, what can _you_ do to cleanse my soul? Isn’t that for your God to do?” “That is most presumptuous and rude of you to say,” the priest says. “Why would you come here if you’re not religious?” “Who said I wasn’t religious?” Misfit spits back, uncrossing his legs and leaning forward. “I just said that Catholicism was a joke. You’re the most accepted and respected cult in the world.” “We are no cult,” the priest stats. He starts to continue, but is cut off by Misfit. “Oh, come now,” he says. “Isn’t all of this practice a little frivolous? If you’re God is so omnipotent and so caring… what’s with all of this confession and penance bullshit? Shouldn’t I just be able to pray to God instead of talking to some monkey in a robe?” “My child, this… verbal assault of yours is most uncalled for. Were I a man of lesser faith, I’d have expelled from this church immediately. However, my bonds with Him are very strong, and I can see that He’s sent you here to me.” “Has He now?” Misfit questions, mocking the priest’s tone as he emphasizes his reference to God. “Yes,” the priest answers. “Why else would you have come to confession? Don’t you think that it’s possible that He lead you here because you’re in need of healing?” “Well, I am in need of healing,” Misfit says as he rolls up the left sleeve of his t-shirt and holds his right hand over a large cut he received during his match with Kenny Mercury at the UWF’s last pay-per-view, Terror in Tokyo. “But I don’t think that your God will cover my hospital bills. I don’t think that even someone with their hands in the pocketbooks of millions of people could afford what I pay our every year for that.” “I mean spiritual healing.” “Oh… what the fuck would I need that for?” Misfit questions as he leans back again, wincing as he stretches out his legs. “Everyone is in need of spiritual healing, my son. It keeps us all safe from the evils of the world.” “Oh, I see. Keeps us safe from the evils of the world; but what if I relish in those evils?” “Then you are a sinner, in need of much more healing than some,” the priest says, his voice stern. “Or what?” “You mean to tell me that you have no idea what awaits you if you do not turn to God?” “Oh, you mean Hell? Fiery pits, eternal damnation, lake of fire or some such nonsense, right?” “It is not nonsense, my son. It is the Lord’s truth.” “And I’m supposed to believe the word of someone I’ve never seen?” “But you see his work every day, my son. The trees, the birds…” Misfit interrupts the priest and continues his list. “The pollution, the industries… real beautiful shit there. If He lets that kind of stuff happen to the world He created, what does that tell me?” “What do you think it tells you?” “I think it tells me that this motherfucker can’t be trusted. He gives us his word through some ancient book about how we’re supposed to be good and share his word with everyone so he’ll love us. But inside the pages of that same book, he tortures one of his most devout followers to win some stupid bet with Satan. But, isn’t gambling wrong?” “Yes, it is. But I fail to see what that has to do with Job.” “It has everything to do with Job. It sounds to me like God had Satan over for a game of poker and some beers. Satan says to God ‘That guy will turn on you in a second’ and God replies,” Misfit pauses for a moment, switching his tone to that of an irritated child. “’Nuh-uh!’ So they go down and kill this guy’s family, curse him with diseases, ruin his business… and the guy fucking praises him? What a moron!” “God did this to show Job’s loyalty to Satan; to show him that Job would not falter in his faith.” “Or, in modern terms, he did it to win a bet. You know what I think?” “What?” “I think that your God is evil. I think he is an ingrate and a bastard. What kind of righteous being would do that to somehow so wholly devoted to Him?” The priest begins praying as he Misfit unleashes the wave of blasphemy against God, asking for forgiveness of his transgressions and guidance in helping him find his way. “Save it, Father,” Misfit seethes. “He’s an ingrate, just like Korey Drock.” The priest takes a deep breath, calming himself as he ends his prayer. “Korey Drock?” “Yes,” Misfit says as he stands up inside the confessional booth. “Korey Drock, my opponent in the first round of GTT4.” Misfit takes a deep breath, twitching slightly at the mention of his opponent’s name. “Korey Drock is an evil… evil man.” “And why do you believe him to be evil?” the priest asks, a bit confused as to what exactly Misfit is talking about. “Because,” Misfit begins. “He’s in this business, the business I love… for money. Just like your God, all he’s after… is money.” ”I hardly see how you can compare God to a competitor in some kind of tournament,” the priest says, a bit offended for the first time. “They’re both evil,” Misfit says, nodding his eyes as if he’s speaking to himself. “I’ve shed my blood, sweat, and tears for this business. I’ve given my time, work, and dedication to wrestling.” “You’re a wrestler?” the priest questions. “Yes,” Misfit answers, looking back at the veil separating him from the priest. “The UWF Intercontential Champion. Two time UWF Television Champion. Two time UWF Tag Team Champion. A UWF Shoot Wrestling Champion. The fucking King of Pain.” He looks away from the veil again, his eyes going blank. “I’ve done everything for this business… for my love of wrestling. I’ve broken bones,” he says as he sits back down. “I’ve been busted open,” she says, looking over some of the many scars on his exposed flesh. He twitches slightly, an angry look coming over his face. “I’ve taken concussions... brain damaging concussions for this business!” Misfit explodes into full-throated yelling, a near-psychotic episode taking place inside the confessional booth as he stands and thrashes around. “AND THIS FUCKING ASSHOLE WANTS TO STEP IN JUST TO MAKE MONEY!?!?” Misfit slams his hands against the panel that separates him and the priest, nearly rocking the entire booth. “Korey Drock is an evil man,” he hisses, panting heavily as he comes down slightly. “He’s a cancer on this business; a cancer on my life. I’m going to take it upon myself to rid the world of his filth. I don’t need any God to help in this cleansing. No confessions or communion. Pure, unadulterated hate… is all this will take. Fuck your God… _I_ am the only savior this business needs. I’m the only Christ the fans can turn to; I understand. I care. I’ve given to these people more than what you dogmatic assholes think your God has given to you. I won’t have this business infested with leeches like Korey Drock.” Misfit pushes away from the panel and falls back into the wooden seat of his booth again. The priest sits silently in his side of the confessional, speechless after Misfit’s outburst. Misfit sits and composes himself, twitching lightly every so often as he breathes heavily and digs his fingernails into his arms. “You certainly are passionate, my child,” the priest finally says. “God has given you a gif…” “God has given me shit,” Misfit interrupts. “I’ve worked for everything I have. I wasn’t tainted by money or material possessions. I saw what I wanted, and I worked for it. Where was God when I was living in an alley, drinking rain water? Where was God when I broke my neck in a ladder match? And where was your precious God when a chair shot cracked my skull?” ”Perhaps He was trying to give you a wake-up call,” the priest answers. “A notice to stop living your life in sin.” Misfit scoffs at the notion. “Is it so hard to believe that He loves you, son?” “After living my life, yeah; it’s pretty un-fucking-believable. You know what, actually,” Misfit says as he stands up. “Why don’t you just save God’s love for the altar boys? I think you’ve helped me enough.” “Have I?” the priest asks, a bit taken back by the ‘altar boys’ comment. “Yes,” Misfit says as he stands. “You’ve helped me realize what has to be done. It’s time for a cleansing in this wrestling world… and I’m _exactly_ the right person to do it.” Misfit stands and exits the confessional booth, the priest following to try and stop him. He enters the cathedral again, the priest bolting out of his chamber within the booth. “Please, my child, you must understand,” he pleases with him. “I understand, Father,” Misfit says, stopping and turning to him. He places a hand on the priests shoulder and points to the massive depiction of Jesus’ crucifixion. “That,” he says, “is what I have to do.” A patron of the church, who had been quietly paying attention to Misfit’s conversation with the priest ever since his outburst inside the confessional booth, drops her rosary to the ground as she gasps at his blasphemous statement. “I have to pick up my mantle as martyr once again,” Misfit says, referring back to his feud in the UWF with Lew Spectre, Sam Gordon, and the other members of management who tried to stop the drowning POOL from running over the UWF. “But this time,” he says, “I pick up the mantle for my entire business; for the very center of my being. I have lived, breathed, slept, and ate this business for years now. No little rich kid is going to throw me down so he can put a few more bucks in his pocket. Even if I have to die to prove my point.” He pats the priest roughly on the shoulder and walks towards the exit of the church again. “It’s my time now,” he says. “I will cleanse the wrestling world of all its evils; and I start with Korey Drock.” He leaves the church, the large wooden doors slamming behind him and interrupting the prayers of those who had lost themselves in concentration. “May God have mercy on his soul,” the priest says as he recites the holy trinity and motions the crucifix across himself.
