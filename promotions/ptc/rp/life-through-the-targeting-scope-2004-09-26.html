"Life through the targeting scope"

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 * * * * * I often wonder what my life would be like, had I not taken the manila envelope from Victor. Would I be free of my murderous career, or still living off of the blood-money I earned by ending someone’s life? Victor was aware of my passion for wrestling. Educated in the fact that during the downtime between the contracts he offered, I would venture across the country and wrestle for various “Indy” promotions. It kept my skill sharp, and my need for the competition in check. I often found that suppressing my desire to wrestle was equally as productive as attempting to empty the oceans with a teaspoon. The more I recollect, the happier I am that I took the envelope – it ultimately became my final contract for Victor Arlington. The contents of the envelope set me free, and for that, I owe the boy an appreciation without end. * * * * * While traveling from place to place, usually intent on sending someone to the afterlife, Legion found the best mode of transportation was the public tour buses that made their way from city to city. It was cheap, and didn’t require the intense security checks that September 11th brought about. He could easily conceal his weapons, the selection vast and varied, in a small suitcase or duffel back that wouldn’t be given a second glance by the bag checker. While the contract was time-sensitive, with only 48 hours available to him before other killers would be dispatched, Legion didn’t worry about taking the slow coast-to-coast bus route. However, it’s nearly impossible to make it from California to Pennsylvania during such a quick timeframe, so he caught a small charter flight to Chicago. He would pick up some weapons from his loft, one of many scattered around the country, and then hop on a bus to Philadelphia. After leaving Chicago, Legion finally opened the manila envelope, the contents of which held the details for his ‘mark’ in Philadelphia. Atop a few papers was an 8x10 glossy photograph of the targeted ‘mark’, and Legion quickly glanced away while he turned it over, placing it at the bottom of the pile. Legion had developed the ‘Three Rules’ so he would know next to nothing about the person he would be assassinating, only vital information, so he didn’t gain any sort of attachment to the person before ultimately killing them. He would only glance at the photo when the time called for it, which would be only a few minutes prior to the slaying. As fellow patrons aboard the bus passed Legion, he made no attempts at making eye-contact or keeping the contents of the envelope readily visible. The sun was beginning to set as the bus left the city limits, and the sky was starting to darken not only with nightfall, but the threat of an oncoming storm as well. Rather than studying the information, Legion returned everything to the envelope, and stared out the window at the passing landscape, attempting to lull himself to sleep. High above the ground in the nighttime sky, nearly ready to be gobbled up by the ominous thunderheads, a small sliver of the moon pierced into the night like a lunar sickle. The rain was just beginning to lightly drum against the roof of the bus as Legion fell into a slumber. Unbeknownst to Legion, it would be the last night of relaxing sleep he would have for nearly a month, and he would soon return to Chicago a marked man. * * * * * I have no doubts that my match against Iron Lung will be a tedious battle. The only information I know about him, other than what I’ve seen during first round of the tournament, is that Mr. Lung is often involved in bar fights. For some reason or another, he likes to take out his aggression with the combination of alcohol and his fists, rather than channeling the rage into a fuel for his wrestling. Channeling is key, especially in this sport, in which success is hinged on desire and motivation. Having tunnel-vision when you’re inside the ring can be a key to success. Visualize the goal, and the goal only. Don’t let other things distract you, or overtake your concentration. My tunnel-vision comes in the form of targeting scope, which generally sat atop the rifle I once used as an assassin. Earth, thousands of miles in circumference, is reduced to only a few meters in width when you’re looking through a rifle’s targeting scope. And the only thing you see is what you’re aiming at, quite literally. When looking outside of the scope’s range, you lose a sense of what you’re focusing on, and you could miss the target quite easily. However, if you keep your eyes trained on the crosshairs…it’s only a matter of time before the trigger is pulled, and you’re victorious. It’s pathetic that the analogies I continually use to boost my self-confidence inside the squared-circle stem directly from the life I’m trying to forget. But at least I know I’m done with that life. I may once again fall within the intimidating gaze of Victor Arlington, but if I ever do, it will be as a professional wrestler and not a hired assassin. My life changed forever when I opened that envelope, and matched the black and white photograph against the face I saw behind the crosshairs in my targeting scope. * * * * * The information inside the manila envelope led Legion to West Chester University, a small college-town about 30 miles southwest of Philadelphia. After waking up on the bus from Chicago, only an hour outside of the City of Brotherly Love, Legion finally decided to review the information about the intended target. At 11am, the ‘mark’ would be heading towards the campus football field for a daily jog around the quarter mile track, and while classes weren’t in session for the summer, the boy obviously lived within the borough, and was able to use the collegiate facilities for personal training. Legion found these conditions to be suitable, the campus could probably be best compared to a ghost town during the summer, so the chances of being spotted after the murder were slim. His disguise of choice wasn’t too extravagant – a standard issue UPS outfit, a box containing his rifle, and a brown haired wig with Elvis sideburns, which seemed to be popular these days around the college campuses. Also noted in the folder was the age of the mark, aged 19 years -- going on 20 in November. The mark was also an athlete in high school, thus the reason for his predictable training schedule. Legion thought the irony of the situation was splendid: his ritualistic self-betterment would be the thing that ended up killing him. At 10:57am, set up in the parking lot adjacent to the football field/track facility, Legion finally removed the glossy photo from the manila envelope, and glanced at the intended mark. While the photo was black and white, Legion knew the mark’s hair color was sandy blonde. He also knew his eyes were a sea-foam green in color, and the smile he often wore across his face was more honest than his fathers, and more charming than his mothers. Legion’s pulse quickly started to race, and he could feel his heart beating against his sternum. It would be safe to say that Legion knew who the target was, and he immediately realized exactly why Victor Arlington hadn’t used his butler for the usual reconnaissance. Legion rifled through his memory, wondering exactly why he was sent to assassinate the young man who had taken the wrestling world by storm nearly a year ago. Joseph Aidan O’Brien, better known to the wrestling world as Aidan, was strolling over the crest of the hill, making his way towards the track for his daily jog. His face swayed from left to right within Legion’s targeting scope, the crosshairs gently dancing from one side of his head to the other. The horrors Aidan went through during the competition and eventual victory of the Universal Title Tournament would pale in comparison to the journey he was about to embark on. For the first time in his career as a professional assassin, Legion hesitated. As Aidan came closer, his finger unconsciously moved away from the trigger. * * * * * Never would I have guessed a boy 12 years my minor would have such an influential impact on the direction of my life. * * * * *
