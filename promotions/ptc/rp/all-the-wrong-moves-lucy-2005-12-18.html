All the Wrong Moves: Lucy

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 Brooding Phoenix, Arizona December, 2005 "A favorite," he mutters, that's what he is called now. A look of disgust crosses the face of "The Avatar" William Gwynn. A year ago he was dismissed as nothing, a rookie. That was GTT4, and this is the difference a year can make. In previous years, William was dismayed at how small a difference that a year makes, but in the year since GTT4, everything has changed. Events seem so miniscule as time goes by, often we're bored. Looking back, we realize just how busy, and just how terrible or great these times are. It's called nostalgia. Hindsight, they say, is twenty-twenty, but it's also skewed and deceptive. "The Avatar" has not been having the greatest of times lately. He's battled with great wrestlers, with some he's come out on top, with others, he hasn't. He's not as impressive as an Angelo Deville, Rich Rollins or Seymour Almasy. "The Avatar" William Gwynn is a workhorse of a company. He wins just slightly more than he loses. He fights the tough guys, and he comes out bearing bruises and working hard for the next week. People like Deville, Rollins and Almasy don't have to face such misfortunes. They're the pretty boys of the industry. Maybe they had to once, but the pain has all but disappeared. Now it's men like "The Avatar" William Gwynn and Ricky Thunder who have to bear the bruises to keep their companies on the high road to success. Core Wrestling is not steady because of Veronica Paige and Hiroshi Ryusaki. It's not steady because of Jason Hughes or [name withheld]. It's steady because of "The Avatar" William Gwynn. And there was a time when he held the biggest slab of gold the company had to offer. But since it has been taken from him, there has been a hole in the soul of the Avatar. He has a craving, a lust, for glory. This tournament is offering him just that, a chance to make a name for himself. "The Avatar" wants to prove that he's not simply a workhorse, but a fantastic wrestler, at that. At the very least, the Avatar wants to come out of this tournament with a winning record. He wants to hold his own. William Gwynn has overcome the hump of 64. At the moment, should he lose, he'd be tied for 62. But 62 is not good enough for William Gwynn. 32 might not be good enough for William Gwynn. Gwynn will take his matches one at a time, though. He is aware of what happens to those that look too far ahead. Some of the bigger names have already written themselves in to the finals. The Avatar is not so obtuse. Gwynn knows that there are competitors just as dangerous as he is out there, just waiting to be overlooked, to knock off that big name, to be declared 'Dark Horse'. William himself has that ambition on his mind. And yet he would much rather make his way through the bracket as quietly as possible, stirring up only minimal attention until he is one of the final ones left. He's watched people do it before, and he's watched people win tournaments that way. Gwynn will not lie. His ultimate ambition is not just to do well in the tournament or make a name for himself. It's to win the tournament. That's everyone's ambition. They see themselves as six or seven year olds do. "This is the putt to win the Masters, or the buzzer-beater at the end of the fourth quarter to win the game. This is two outs, full count, bases loaded, seventh game of the World Series against the New York Yankees, this is that fateful stroke, eyes closed, all on the line. This is that for wrestling. This is GTT5. It's all on the line, each and every week. To get to the end you must first get through the beginning. I have that drive. Ricky Thunder, I pray that you do. I pray that you'll give me a challenge in this second round. But don't be surprised when I pin you in that ring like I did to Bomber not so long ago. Time has dwindled, the end of your little run in this tournament is coming to an end. What a difference a year can make." William's eyes close and he sighs. "And what a difference three years can make." The First Year of Lucy Phoenix, Arizona December 31, 2002 She was dancing. For the most part, William Gwynn has found dancing an entirely futile exercise. Very few people do it well, and the people that don't do it well make it absolutely horrifying. She did it well. She did it very well. William at this point was an established businessman, having plenty of money and a membership to the local country club. There was a New Years party at the Country Club, but he attended a different one, finding that group entirely unsatisfactory. This group was very different. And it had very pretty, young, successful women, which William Gwynn needed a "steady, solid dose" of, as his friend Igloo made sure he knew. It was actually in a hotel ballroom. It had all the traditional ingredients, champagne and "Auld Lang Syne", but it had a bit of flare to it. It's the flare that the young Avatar saw in this woman's eye. She wasn't alone, she was amongst plenty of her own friends. It wasn't just her fiery eyes or complete lack of humility that drew William's attention, it was her long, slightly curled dark hair. It was the slight make-up that accented her facial features. It was the bright dress and the sparkly necklace. It was everything about her. William always tried to look away, but often his eyes wandered, and when they did, they tended to wander back to her. She was the only thing on the floor that mattered. "The Avatar" was sitting with Igloo and dozens of others who are actually watching the coverage of the New Year. As always, Jay Leno is counting it down on NBC, but that's still quite a ways away. They've been showing countries all over the world ushering in the new year. William remembers two years before, when everyone expected the world to explode when that ball fell. But that thought only lingered for a moment, the girl was on the move. "Hey," she said, as she walked toward the group. She was looking directly at William, and her friends hadn't followed her. "Hey," William says, looking directly back at her. "Can I - " "I saw you looking," she interrupted, smiling, "and yes, you can buy me a drink." William found himself dumbfounded for a moment, but he bought himself and the lady a drink. William took something particularly strong. He actually wasn't going to ask her if he could buy her a drink, rather he meant to say 'Can I help you?'. William was not going to admit that, seeing how well her assumption had worked for him. "Thank you," she said, as the bartender handed her her drink. William got his. "A toast?" she requested. "To what?" William asked. "The New Year," she said, "of course." "To 2003," William Gwynn said, with a grin to match hers. "To 2003," she echoed, "now, why don't you finish that drink and - ? Big swig, now." William tossed it down the hatch. "There you go," she smiled. "And why don't you come with me?" "And where would we be going?" William asked. "What?" she protested. "Don't you trust me?" William looked up at her, stunned. "Should I? I've never been approached like that bef - " he began. "There's a first time for everything, darling," she said, "but there's nothing wrong with meeting new friends, is there?" And that smile of hers could have tempted Hoyt Williams. "Of course not," William said, and placed the glass back on the bar, paying with a tip that he certainly would never have given were he sober. He stood from his stool and took the hand that was offered to him. Low and behold, she led him to the dance floor. "Oh, nuance," he muttered, "I never would have guessed we'd make our way here." She only smiled, and moved in closer. "We have to see what you've got, don't we?" she asked. "You're not married, are you?" "No," William answered. "Then don't protest," she said, "you know that you've dreamed of being in a place like this with a girl like me, the New Year only minutes away. No one wants to be lonely when that clock tolls twelve." She moved awfully close, so close that William could feel her breath on his shoulder and neck. William had improved a great deal over the past six years, but not enough to make him "good", just "decent". William can't keep up with the fast songs, and if the music gets loud he tends to get a headache. William appreciated this slow song, and her head on his shoulder. She swayed with him, as content as William could hope her to be. "What's your name, darling?" he asked. "Mm," she purred. "I was wondering when you'd ask that. It's not 'darling', though." "I'm sorry," William whispered. "Don't be," she said. "Lucy," she answered. "And yours, hotshot?" "William," he responded. "William?" she asked. "Good, strong name," she said, "not feminine like Ricky or Louis. I like it. And not Bill. Very good. Do you like my name? Because it's important for friends to like one anothers names, you know." "Critical," William said with a smile, "yes, Lucy, I like your name." The conversation stopped for a moment, a very comfortable silence. She looks up at him. "You know, William," she says, readjusting herself against him, her hand creeping along his shoulder, "if I didn't know better," and she taunts him a bit more, "I'd say that you came tonight against your will, and you think you've finally found that girl." And he smiled. "Unfortunately, you do," William muttered, and she looked a bit shocked. "I came here with my will. The other part - " he let his voice trail off. An unintelligent girl might be confused, but this one smiled and looked away. "I think it's about time," she said. "How about we get a couple of drinks and sit down for a while?" William nodded, and she stepped back, and grabbed his hand, leading him away once more. "I've got them this time. Do you want the same?" "Oh, no," William answered. "Just a Jack and Coke would be fine," he said, being polite and inexpensive. The two found chairs slightly skewed from the party, with a nice sized television in front of them. They were away from the drunkards and the noise. They had their own little corner of the world, and they were about to usher in 2003 together. The countdown was nearing its end. The ball was about to fall. William was looking into Lucy's eyes, a smile on his face. She smiled back at him. William draped his arm around her, and she leaned on his shoulder. They watched as the countdown neared one minute. The excitement was building in the ballroom. Some of the couples were standing nervously around, wondering what was about to happen. William had a stroke of brilliance. As the countdown began, he pulled her even closer. 10. "Ten!" William ran his hand through her hair and down on to her shoulder. 09. "Nine!" Lucy took William's other hand. 08. "Eight!" Lucy turned to face William. 07. "Seven!" They both began to watch the clock together. 06. "Six!" Lucy began counting down, and tapped her foot with each count. 05. "Five!" William joined in. 04. "Four!" William bit his lip, and she caught it and grins. 03. "Three!" The grip tightened. 02. "Two!" William locked eyes with her, and she smiled and closed her eyes. 01. "One!" And William leaned in, and they kissed for several moments as those around him cheered. "Auld Lang Syne" began, but neither of them cared. They were there for one another, and nothing else mattered. The surroundings didn't matter. Nothing else. William had found her. After years of searching, it was all over. She was his. He was hers. The search was over. And now the love begins. As their lips disengaged, she breathed out … … and that's when William realized that she was drunk. She wasn't going to remember this the next morning. If she did, William would be lucky. He gave her his number, putting it in her purse. Eventually she'd find it and wonder what it was about. That was his first kiss. His mouth was still swimming. But the passion was gone. Again, the empty feeling set in. She wondered what had happened to his passion. He drove her home, but he knew that his resolution had already been destroyed. This year would be just as unfortunate as the last had been. William was in no better shape. His greatest hope had been dashed. The years make no difference. Reflection Phoenix, Arizona December, 2005 "Lucy," he mutters, straining. "That was a long time ago. The same theme recurs, of course. Failure. A man just can't be perfect, he can't have it all. I had skill, let's not just throw that away. I still do. My skills in the art of wrestling came at the cost of my skills in romance, I suppose. It's like one of those sports video games, where you create your player. You can only be skilled in so many things. You have to lack something. The unfortunate part of all of it is that I would rather have Lucy than that golden belt I held for several months in Core Wrestling. She was the most beautiful, the most personable person I have ever met. She was wonderful, and she could have stopped it all. What is it about women that fail to see in me what I see in them? Can't they see that I am more than willing to support them? To help them? To ease their pain?" He sighs. "It doesn't seem to matter anymore," William whispers. "I've given up on her now, three years later. It took me a year to realize that there was nothing there. That sparkle in her eye was just familiarity. It was just … friendliness. How could I be so mistaken, so misguided and so astray? How? How could I have allowed myself to be so tangled in the deceit of my own mind? Was this how Hitler felt? Lying to yourself so often that you believe it? Is that what I did? I have so many questions for myself, and they're all just as skewed as my hindsight. What I did was right by my own mind, but wrong in circumstance. All these years it took me to figure that out, so much brooding and reflection. All I've come to figure out is that I truly have nothing to lose. My back is not against any wall. I'm just out there, each and every day, battling the same old enemies, week after week, straining myself. Is she watching? Do I need the money? Probably not. She's probably married to some hotshot. Ironically, I'm a hotshot now." William is far too grieved to grin. "This week it's something different," William says, "it's a completely new face. He's a name in GCW, but GCW is foreign to me. His name is Ricky Thunder, about as stereotypical as Lonnie Lightning or Buddy Storm. He must have some talent, though. Some people think he might win. Some people think that I'm not driven enough to go further in this tournament. They think that you are, Ricky. I think it's favoritism. I think they're just taking a guess in the dark. But then again, maybe you are impressive. Maybe you've got what it takes. Maybe you're a … " William pauses, stroking his beard with his fingers, eyes cast down. " … hotshot," he finishes, "but whatever you are, you are fighting a man with a purpose. You're fighting a man who has forsaken lesser talents, forsaken earthly, human feelings for this industry. I am a man who has given up my own life for this sport. I am wholly devoted. I am passionate for only one thing, this. This round, this match, this tournament, it's mine. There is no question as to my drives or my motives. I will run you through. I'll make you cry. I'll give you that empty feeling inside, when you know that there's no longer a chance. It's that interception on the Hail Mary. It's the pop-up, slowly descending into the first baseman's mit. It's the end. And it's the end for you, Ricky Thunder. You've reached it. One round was all you could hope for. You ran in to the wrong man at the wrong time. You got the tough draw this round. You've got the Pride of Core Wrestling in round two, you've got "The Avatar" William Gwynn. You won't pass me, Ricky. I'm in my own league now." To Be Continued, and Fade.
