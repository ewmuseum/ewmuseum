I always thought he was kind of funny.” “You don’t know him like I do.” “But what about that time he had his rogue try and craft armor that had a plus-one bonus to ‘pantsing’?” Mervin tried to stifle a laugh. It got stuck in his throat, escaping as something of a snort. “That was pretty dumb,” he said, hoping that Ollie wouldn’t catch on. “No way!” his chubby friend persisted. “Half the party was naked by the time Pete put a stop to it.” The rain had picked up by the time they made it to the crosswalk. Across the street and nestled between a 7-11 and the Cambridge Public Library was their destination. They could see through the window that a small crowd had gathered, but not nearly as many as they’d expected. George O’Donnell had also come to Cambridge for this game, though he had no intention of playing in it. The first man he knew from television, from his days as a teenager in Minnesota and more recently on Cinemax’s “Temptation” program. The second man was a stranger, but he was of little consequence. George watched from inside the 7-11 as the two men stepped into the crosswalk. He pulled the hood of his own sweatshirt up and tilted the brim of his cap to conceal himself as much as possible. When he stepped out into the cool autumn air he let his hand find its way to the cold steel concealed in the pocket of his oversized shirt. He felt a surge of power course through his arm when his digits tightened around the molded rubber grip. The crowd was large enough, but that didn’t matter. This was it. There was no other moment. Everything else had fallen into place. He had taken the apartment. He had even managed to slyly introduce himself and make him aware, even on a small level, of his existence. But to sit at his right hand he must first remove the one there. “Oh, and what about when he tried to enchant that lute?” Ollie was still recounting tales of Jared’s exploits when the three men briefly shared the same space. An accidental elbow caught his arm. “Oh, sorry man. I didn’t see you there.” Ollie heard what sounded like thunder, though it was closer than anything he’d ever experienced. Around him people had scattered, some screaming. He turned to see the man in the green sweatshirt, the one who’d bumped into him, disappear around the corner of a small Indian restaurant. It was only when he turned back that he understood fully what had happened. The life bled from Mervin Humperdink.
