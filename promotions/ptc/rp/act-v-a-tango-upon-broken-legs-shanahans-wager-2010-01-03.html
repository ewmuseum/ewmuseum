Act V: A Tango Upon Broken Legs; Shanahan’s Wager

3 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2010-01-03 Pascal’s Wager (1) It is possible that the Christian God exists and it is possible that the Christian God does not exist. (2) If one believes in the Christian God then if he exists then one receives an infinitely great reward and if he does not exist then one loses little or nothing. (3) If one does not believe in the Christian God then if he exists then one receives an infinitely great punishment and if he does not exist then one gains little or nothing. (4) It is better to either receive an infinitely great reward or lose little or nothing than it is to either receive an infinitely great punishment or gain little or nothing. Therefore: (5) It is better to believe in the Christian God than it is not to believe in the Christian God. (6) If one course of action is better than another then it is rational to follow that course of action and irrational to follow the other. Therefore: (7) It is rational to believe in the Christian God and irrational not to believe in the Christian God. ---Blaise Pascal, French philosopher & mathematician ???: This is Doctor Virgil Stiner of the Greater Newfellow Lane Mental Institution, London, England. The time is zero-nine-thirty-five hours on December 23rd of 2009. I am preparing to conduct my interview with Ambrose Whitaker, patient designation 912730, a former English professor of some distinction before he came to us. This interview is being carried out as a follow-up to the inmate riots of December the 20th that left three dead and five hospitalized. The entirety of the interview will be recorded for further documentation and study, as needed, on this audio tape. (A shuffling, the metallic clank of heavy chains. Labored breathing as a heavy door slams shut in the background, sealed with the thunderous, mechanical shift of thick deadbolts.) Stiner: Hello, Ambrose. A pleasure to see you again. Whitaker: No need to patronize me, Doctor Stiner. I killed one of your guards with my bare hands. It is no pleasure of yours to see me. Stiner: Very well, straight to the business at hand. Tell me the reason behind the violence. Who initiated this uprising and for what purpose? Whitaker: It was not our will. We couldn’t resist. Their power is blossoming, as that of a flower drenched in the rains of spring. The stars are aligning, and our minds were ripe for His sendings. From atop that hallowed throne in the great deep came His divine wishes, and we carried them out. To do otherwise would be damnation. Incidentally, you shouldn’t be treating this as a witch hunt. You should be thanking us. Stiner: And why would that be? Whitaker: We saved the souls of two of your employees. Heretics will find no escape when the Old Ones return, for even the dominion of death will fall under their sway once more. Had they continued down the path of a calloused heart, their souls would never have reached what you refer to as ‘Heaven’. They would suffer far more than their mortal forms ever could for their lack of belief. Stiner: (shuffling of papers) Was there a purpose to this uprising? Whitaker: They do not waste time with random acts, doctor. Of course it had a purpose. That purpose was to bring about a current of great energy, energy They would use to empower. Stiner: Empower who? Whitaker: The prophet. Stiner: And who is the prophet, Ambrose? Whitaker: Shanahan. Bruce Shanahan. Much of the world knows him simply as a professional wrestler, a common ruffian known as Violence Jack. But we know of his true ambitions, and we know the stark reality of his gospel. It’s a gospel you pig-headed know-it-alls think is deranged hogwash, or scripted sermon from a room full of writers. But you’re wrong. And we’ve proved it. Stiner: How have you proven his words, Ambrose? Whitaker: Is it safe for me to assume that you know of Shanahan‘s private exploits, even without your open admission? Of his past incarceration, the studies on his psyche, his declarations of worldly demise? Stiner: (pause) I don’t know of the man, or what he believes. Whitaker: You lie. It’s in your eyes, doctor. Don’t dawdle with us, and don‘t think us blind… Stiner: How have you and your kindred proven Shanahan’s gospel to be the honest revelations of a prophet, Ambrose? Whitaker: (gentle laugh) Surely you watch the news, doctor. Stiner: Yes. Yes, I do. Whitaker: Then don’t play naïve. This isn’t isolated. Asylum mayhem, epidemics of madness and paranoia, prisons everywhere on full lockdown? The violence is worldwide, is it not?(A long pause) Whitaker: I SAID IS IT NOT???!! Stiner: You know that you’re a level 4 inmate, Ambrose. With your background you don’t have authorization to know what goes on outside these walls. I cannot divulge… Whitaker: I am not some fucking monkey to be caged and blindfolded, you insolent little prick. They tried to imprison the prophet and parade him around like some trophy for their twisted science. But they paid for it. The worm who presumed he could make him into an experiment, a colleague of yours, was too weak. The prophet destroyed him. What makes a piece of shit like you believe that you stand a chance in the face of the champions of the cosmos? What the fuck affords you that luxury? Stiner: Because I’m not a superstitious man, Ambrose. I’m a man of science, and logic, and reason. What you claim cannot be real. The delusions of an unstable mind. It cannot scare me. Whitaker: Then humor me. What is the sweat on your brow? Why do you tremble so?(Another lengthy pause.) Whitaker: Because it’s ingrained in you. Somewhere deep down, you know. I am not so great a man as the prophet. I am not imbued with Their magnificence and favor. I cannot break you as easily as he could. But I will make you see revelation. You will either seek Their favor, or pray for death. * * * * “Do we need a translator?” “No. He’s speaks fluent English. Tutored almost since birth. May as well be a native, he knows the language far better than many domestics.” With the enigma that was Gabriel at his side, Maury Dawson stared coldly through the two-way mirror at the Yakuza boss they‘d pulled from Shanahan‘s clutches. Normally he’d offer no quarter to a beast like Mizakawa, a vicious man who had killed multitudes to get where he was. But things were different now; he not only needed to extract information from the Japanese, but he needed to do so quickly in light of the catastrophe. Bruce had seemingly become desperate, and exercised what they all felt had to be one of his aces in the hole. He had bombed the Vatican itself, killing dozens. Reprisal at its boldest for his near-capture, and the attempt on his life prior. It was as if he’d lost the control that he was so accustomed to, and in a panic, launched an attack unprecedented even for the madmen of the Sect of Black Wisdom. Innocents had lost their lives as their covert war raged, and though Dawson had shed tears for the loss, he could ill afford to offer much more. “We need to know everything,” he stated matter-of-factly to his counterpart. “We can’t let this happen again.” They left the observation room and walked into the interrogation room where several more of Blackhelm’s best had already begun the questioning. The crime boss offered them nary a glance as the door slammed ominously in their wake. Mizakawa’s eyes resonated forcefully, a man impassioned as he delivered his answers. “Shanahan cannot reclaim the relics. To do so would advance his schemes beyond the point of hope. You may hate us, you may think us ruthless and even barbaric at times, but in the end we’re all human beings. It’s what binds us all. We chose to keep the stones for designs greater than our own. More than just because we feel they could be of use to us. We…” He trailed off, eyes lowering to the tabletop as his fingertips massaged his temples. Tatsuya Mizakawa, a gangster of most ill and brutal renown in his homeland, trembled with despair as he pictured what occurred that fateful night, pieced together by the mountains of grisly evidence… …the scar it left would never be as horrific as the terrified, tortured screams of his associates, his mind anguishing with the images of their mangled remains cast about in tatters in that warehouse. Everything had gone horribly wrong. Why they'd been betrayed he didn't know. Something had struck at them from seemingly every corner of the room, pulling Lo-Bone and Kojiro into the rafters almost simultaneously, and blood and gore had fallen like a scarlet summer shower. Nothing should have been able to survive the amount of firepower they poured into the rapidly shifting shadows of that warehouse, but it had. It had taken them at will as bullets clattered to the floor in a metallic, brass hail. And he'd left his brothers behind, trying to drown out the gut-churning ripping of flesh as a final, desperate act of self-preservation. Tully. He'd been the man they met. Surely he'd perished in the attack, that bastard with his pompous demeanor and that smug little half-grin that just dared Yancy Hersch and his consorts to say fuck the deal and drop the cocksucker in a volley of death. He'd vanished the moment the first trigger was pulled and the slaughter started. He turned a blind corner, crashing to the ground in a rain of old newspaper and tumbling boxes. He felt more blood run down his leg but felt no pain, ignored his brain's efforts to warn him of an injury that would otherwise bring alarm. Death was at his heels, and to be caught was to know suffering. To die would be an escape he wasn't sure he'd have until long after he'd be unable to beg for it. He still saw things as he made his way in a panicked stupor through the dirty ghetto backalleys. Shadows moving unnaturally to block his path, tendrils of animated blackness reaching for him, baleful voices echoing on the breeze, cackling and whispering to him both of carnal promises and the most painful of ends. He had no doubt his mind was on the verge of breaking down but he refused to believe what he'd seen descend on them in the storehouse was delusion. Was it just one man? Was there more than one? Jesus in Heaven, was it a man at all?? What the fuck was...... He nearly ran headlong into a wall, sliding to a stop only inches from knocking himself unconscious. A wall that shouldn't have been there. He knew these streets and every adjoining alley between them. He hadn't missed a turn. He knew he hadn't. A trash can crashing behind him. Turning on his heel, Yancy Hersch let out the most savage cry he could summon and lifted the hand cannon to bear on his unseen pursuer, squeezing the trigger and sending every round in the chamber hurtling into the shadows. He heard bullets ricochet, and several others punch holes in the metal canisters littering the alley. He hoped for a moan or the collapse of a body, prayed that the ordeal was over. But nothing. Just as he'd feared. He took in the acrid aroma of gunpowder and backed up against a chain-link fence, dropping the empty weapon, offering a petition to the heavens that he hadn't in years. God didn't answer him. The only response came from the spectral hand that came to rest on his shoulder. Tears mingled with the blood coursing from the laceration in Yancy's cheek as he felt sobs begin to rack his body. "You can't help us with that which we seek," the other hissed, a voice inhuman and hungry for something Yancy didn't want to think about. "You're just a petty follower, a pawn serving the whims of masters pulling the strings. No. You're good for just one thing." He turned slowly, staring into two burning embers that radiated the fires of perdition. It was the abyss, and it stared back into him, rending at his soul with unearthly hunger in his last terror-stricken moments of life. For a split-second, before being lost in the fiery orbs that burned into his own eyes, he thought he saw remnants of that same smug, callous half-grin.... "Sacrifice. Their favor demands blood sacrifice." The thing in man's clothing rendered a wicked parody of a smile, extending a hand to him. In the fading light, he swore the gnarled fingertips were crowned with serrated talons. The devil was set to embrace him. He caught a glimmer in the corner of his eye, another just like what reached out to him. His cries split the tranquil night of downtown Los Angeles. Never had death seemed so inviting a mistress… (1) He snapped from his reverie, peering sadly at the Blackhelm operatives. His armed inquisitors, watching him like some animal with its teeth bared, and prepared to take him down at a moment’s notice. “…We didn’t trust you, or your countrymen, to keep them safe. You have no honor, no rapport with those who share this world with you. You are driven by avarice, greed, gluttony. For a price, any man may sell out to Shanahan and sentence us to death. What we do pales in comparison to what we fear from you Americans. Even you, the so-called ‘white knights’ of Blackhelm, the blood-encrusted blade of the Vatican. A Vatican that now lies much in ruins. Especially now.” His eyes narrowed, glaring vehemently at each of his captors in turn. “What might you do now in retaliation? What measures will you take to exact vengeance?” Dawson didn’t like the direction that Mizakawa was taking in his accusations, but he understood it. And knowing what he did of the higher seats of Blackhelm, of that spectral hive-mind beneath its many guises and gray robes, even he had to collect his thoughts and ponder what was to come in the wake of the brazen attack on the holy seat. “When my men marched into that warehouse over a year ago, they did it to stop the spread of a plague. Horace Tully was a carrier of that plague, and if he was left to grow in his ways then that plague would only mature, mutate, and become even more lethal. I wept when my men were killed by what the cult sent into this world. Not just because they were Japanese, and not just because their deaths were a horror no man deserved to suffer. I wept because I realized the man who had forever tainted Tully, and unleashed that shadow from the other side, had forgotten what it was to be human. “Shin,” Dawson uttered, remembering the insane ravings of the survivor a year ago. “The devil.” “Is there a more chilling prospect than to know the fates of men are determined by those without humanity?” If you only knew, Maury thought silently. He allowed himself a glimpse at his associate, unsurprised at the stoic countenance of the other. Dawson suspected that Gabriel’s waning humanity was one of the final links the upper echelon held to the world, and dreaded the hour when it flowed dry. “My men are marshaled, Maury Dawson,” the Yakuza replied. “They will aid in the destruction of the tyrant as easily as they did in my escape. Provided you choose wisely in your tactics. Provided you‘re willing to sacrifice in bringing down the monster and his campaign of wanton death. And free me to lead them against whatever he may send.” Dawson swiveled on his heel, marching away to ponder the Yakuza’s words. He didn’t know what to believe, who to trust. In this strange world he’d chosen to tread, enemies appeared as friends and allies like antagonists. Few options presented themselves as to know who to follow into war. “The menagerie,” he said softly. “We have to return to the asylum, and use them. Or use their talents, in any case.” Gabriel took his side with a smirk. “Only your so-called ‘damned’ can save us right now. Ironic huh?” “Tragic is more what I was reaching for. Poor Kerry, poor Milo…” “And poor humanity if we refuse to use their gifts for our benefit. I’ve stood at the side of our masters only briefly, Maury, and to you their methods are strange and distant. They speak for an intelligence reaching beyond the speck of existence that our kind takes up in this vast, cold void called a universe. But they’re here for a reason, as guardians to us all. So are Kerry, and Milo, and the others you call cursed. Like I said once before, as bleak as it may be, even the light of Heaven is sometimes painted like the darkness of Hell. The bridge doesn’t always offer a scenic voyage. It can be dark, gruesome, dismaying. But it takes faith to know it isn’t always that way.” He set a hand on Dawson’s shoulder. Even his touch was rapturous; Gabriel was an uncompromising sentinel, and Maury Dawson was a paladin to the forces of good as much as Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan was a champion for the infernal. “I’ve come to consider you as my friend. And I’m imploring you to do what’s right. Unleash the force of the Blackhelm Coalition. It may be the only way to withstand the Sect of Black Wisdom, and all of its tendrils that have already snaked across the world.” Dawson had to put aside his loathing and agree. It was ugly, and it was inconvenient, but it had to be done. God works in mysterious ways. Still, as shameful as he was to acknowledge it, he’d even begun to doubt the ambitions of any God. * * * * Stiner: You realize there is no way I can believe any of this. It’s all the machinations of a sick mind. Whitaker: (A growl, hideously animalistic) Fucking heretic scum. You are issuing a clear invitation to the dance. Stiner: Your meaning is lost on me, Ambrose. Whitaker: I refer to the masquerade. The plan that was set in motion eons ago, and now enters its final stage. A dance between visionaries, madmen, martyrs, and all those in between. Stiner: Is the Vatican attack linked to this masquerade? Whitaker: I’m not privy to the designs of the prophet. I’m a simple worshipper who has peered into the desolate realities behind the veil that blinds most others. But even the prophet has weaknesses. And, just as every other great seer before him, he’ll need to overcome those weaknesses to step foot on the promised land. Or, in this case, to burn your promised land. Stiner: No man is invulnerable. Whitaker: He is powerful, but with that boon comes pride. Then arrogance. For so long his schemes have met little unforeseen opposition. But now with an underworld empire at his beck and call, challengers will seek his crown. And the crown of such a man is a heavy burden. He will face betrayal and an ever increasing cadre of foes who fear a macabre kingdom shaped by his hands. If he cannot overcome these failings, his crusade will end. But to overcome those flaws is to know true strength. I’d wager the assault on the temple was his doing. But I would also bet it was a last resort, set to break the backs of his enemies. Stiner: How so? Whitaker: By attacking their faith, to prove nothing is sacred in his war. If that’s true, the masquerade is quickening. The final dance can’t be far off now. And even greater challenges will await him, and all who stand against him… * * * * Shanahan: Yes? ???: I won’t allow you to shape the future of this world. Shanahan: Who the hell is this?? ???: I am your enemy. My name is not important. Neither is whether I’m your closest cohort or a justice seeker far across the globe. Who I am, quite simply, can be described in a couple simple words. I am your end, the heel to your Achilles. Shanahan: Impudent jackal. It was you. You gave away our position to their lapdogs and the Yakuza’s guns. And it was you beneath the mask in Germany, pouring bullets into Krauser’s mansion in hopes of killing me. ???: You desire a world plunged in darkness. Engulfed in the nightmare tides of a terrible celestial flood. But that’s not a decision for you to make, Bruce. And I’m one step ahead of you yet again. Shanahan: Wretched filth. When I pull you out of your hole, you’ll suffer a fate far worse than any death… ???: Save your threats. You’ll need the energy for the rebuilding. Shanahan: What rebuilding? ???: Let’s just say I maneuvered the horse into Troy quite easily. Your masks have failed you. * * * * “…I propose to you a hypothesis. A wager if you will, as derived loosely from that of a philosopher of an age past. What is the most rational of avenues to take in our faith? In theory, to believe in the God of common myth is to follow that of the greatest risk vs. reward finality. To deny the existence of a supreme founder is to, at best, be right and incur nothing. But to be wrong in that assumption is to know the eternal savagery of fire, brimstone, and the lashes of whips wielded by Hell‘s taskmasters. To be right is to know glory that mortals can’t fathom, and to be wrong has no true consequence. As such, would a logical man not therefore lean toward belief, if only because it’s the side with most to gain? I hereby refute this wager on the basis of the truth we all have come to know. We know His power to be a lie, as fabricated as the illusion he constructed to screen us from reality. Take your choice. Faith in your soon-to-be toppled king? Or loyalty to the creators of a glorious new kingdom, built on the ashes of the conquered old? Which offers greater promise, and a worse fate? Only the enlightened can ever know that answer. Science has proven inefficient, and we have all seen into those forbidden vistas beyond time and normal mortal musing. Our standard shines brighter, is raised higher than that of any fallen immortal hiding behind a curtain.” We are the new age. Let us consecrate ourselves in the fires of doom, and march forth to burn the heretics and the doubters. If this world is to be charred to a cinder amidst our revolution, so be it. Humanity deserves no better end than a final gaze into blackened skies, and a charred grave that was their civilization. Their God is nothing. That will be proven when we strike His head from His shoulders, the death-rattle of a dying race. ---Bruce Shanahan; sermon dated 3/18/2009 * * * * Dawson was awe-struck as he lowered his cellphone. Their approach to Blackhelm’s secret place was already a swift one, but he feared it may not be swift enough given the repercussions set to come. “I don’t know whether to be ecstatic or terrified.” Gabriel looked on, unsteady, as if he expected the horror that awaited. “What’s happened, Maury?” “Someone has just managed to do what we haven’t. They hit his stronghold. Wyatt Manor has just been leveled.” (1) -- Italicized text borrowed from roleplay titled ‘Shin’, written for PRIME Jewel in the Crown tournament in November 2008.
