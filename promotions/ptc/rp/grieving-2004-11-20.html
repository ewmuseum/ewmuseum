Grieving

Date: 2004-11-20 Craig stood in the doorway and carelessly tossed his bag on the filthy floor. He wanted to scream and curse and wail to any gods listening at the unfairness of it all, but knew it would be useless. He was tired and worn out from his anger, and although it was still very much simmering inside him, it had been almost burnt out until only embers of it remained… much like his home. “This isn’t so bad” Nurse pronounced as she jostled past him with her sparse luggage, “I think it’s kind of homely.” Her words fanned the embers. “Homely? It’s a goddamn locker room no bigger then a prison cell!” Nurse Esme Matilda Wretched smiled at him, “You know what they say, and ‘home is where the heart is”! “No” Craig spat, “Home is the house I was born in, grew up in and lived in all my life. Not this crappy place.” Nurse pointedly ignored him and set to unpacking. Tired, and knowing how useless it would be to continue the argument with her, Craig sat down on the bench and slowly looked around him. He knew this locker room well and had been here many times, but he never before was expected to spend the night. It was typical of all the locker rooms in the XUDF Arena: it was cheap, had bare stone walls and stank of old sweat socks and jockstraps. Four battered lockers stood in a semblance of a row along one wall. They were of the old fashioned grey metal variety, twisted in places with faded pictures of topless models tacked on. Only one appeared to have a working lock, and while Nurse dug through her luggage for a pine air-freshener, Craig took the opportunity to claim it by tossing his bag in and pocketing the key. Craig tried desperately to look on the bright side, but came up almost blank. The only benefit of living in the locker room would be access to the XUDF ring for training. But given that there had been a ring and gym in his old house, that was a tentative positive at best. He never thought he was so attached to Goat Mansion, but once it was gone, he missed it so much it tore at his heart and made his eyes well up. Never again would he be able to cook in his own kitchen, sleep in his own bed, even his Playstation and X-Box were lost to him now. As tears started to slowly run down his face, his silent melancholy was disturbed by an unusual sound… Nurse Wretched… singing! He turned to stare at her. She was shaking out her few meager possessions and hanging them up to air out the smell of smoke. Her head nodded in time to the tune she sang and she shook her hips at the chorus. How dare she be so happy, thought Craig. “Esme, stop that racket.” Nurse paused and turned to him, a confused look crossing her face. “What racket? I’m not doing anything.” “You’re singing. You’re practically dancing too. I never knew you were a fan of S-Club 7.” Nurse picked up the closest thing she could find (a sledgehammer unfortunately) and threw it at Craig. “Don’t be such a freaking asshole! I don’t sing. You’re hallucinating or something. You did manage to get a new prescription filled didn’t you?” “Firstly, that bloody hurt! Secondly, I have taken my medication. And thirdly, I fucking hate you and you were bloody singing!” Craig stormed out of the locker room and blindly ran down the corridors that twisted like a maze under the Arena, once more sobbing. Nurse was a little surprised by Craig’s outburst, but not much. He always had been a bit too highly strung. Had she been a better person, Nurse would have felt ashamed of herself and felt some modicum of guilt over her recent actions, but she wasn’t and she didn’t. Being evil had the advantage of never having to justify oneself. She did however feel hurt by Craig’s accusations and the anger he directed at her. Nurse thought of this as a side effect of the even stranger emotion she had been experiencing recently; love. As Craig had progressed through the rounds of GTT4, he had changed so much from the sniveling sycophant he had been into a strong (except for the occasional cathartic tears) man with a purpose to his life. Nurse had found herself drawn to this new Craig, a man finally worthy of her, and had accidentally fallen in love with him. The realization that she loved him had been hard on Nurse; she screamed, she protested, she even vomited, but now she had finally become resigned to the fact. Throughout the GTT4 tournament, Nurse had been restless. On a sabbatical from her own career, she had lacked direction and so had poured her energies into training her young protégée. But that hadn’t been enough. Craig had soon surpassed the need for her guidance and took his first faltering steps towards independence from her. He had beaten Jonathan Borealis, Jenna Ryder and Flying’ Brian Calvin with her her help, but had faced Seymour Alamsy totally alone. Even though he claimed she had been invaluable against Jimmy Bong, Nurse knew deep down that it was only lip service, Craig hadn’t needed her help except as a sparring partner. She sighed wistfully, “He’ll need me now though. Without his mother and his mansion, I am the only person left in his life. I am the only one who loves him and a man like Craig can’t win without love.” Nurse was in many ways a perfectionist. When she tortured someone, they were thoroughly tortured, when she wrestled an opponent, they were thoroughly beaten, when she nursed a patient, they were thoroughly nursed (usually to death though; too much medication is a very bad thing). So now that she knew she loved Craig and it wasn’t something that could be purged or cured easily, she loved him thoroughly. Craig wandered the bleak corridors of XUDF. Being a mere developmental federation, it lacked the funding and style of the bigger wrestling federations, such as the champion producing XUW. He found himself upstairs in the Arena. Empty, it seemed cold and haggard. Once, he used to come here weekly to watch Nurse Wretched wrestle and hope and yearn for the day when he too could step into that ring and fulfill his dream. But today, the ring looked less inviting, its mat stained and its ropes hanging loose and battered. He wondered if he’d have even wanted to become a wrestler if he’d never met Nurse. Where would he be now? Would he still be in the Sanatorium being treated for agoraphobia and anxiety? Would he be at home in his mansion sitting at his computer producing role play after killer role play for his beloved efed? Would his mother be baking him her famously bland wheat-free cookies and checking that he’d washed behind his ears properly? But the biggest question of all was; would he have been happier? “Probably,” he muttered bitterly into the empty space, “surely it would be better than the nothing I have now.” He sighed as he took one last look around at the Arena, covered in the filth and chewing gum of the fans and smelling of the sweat of its wrestlers “So this is what a dream looks like in the daylight.” Having no-where else to go, Craig returned finally to the locker room. He braced himself as he opened the door. After more than a year of living with Nurse and being her whipping boy, it had become habit to prepare to duck or run or scream in pain whenever he entered her presence. Today however, there were no nasty surprises awaiting him. Nurse did have a blowtorch in hand, but instead of being aimed at him, its blue flame was focused on one of the old metal lockers. Startled by his return, Nurse wheeled around, almost singeing his eyebrows. “Umm, I’m just fixing the place up a bit for you dear. Did you have a nice walk?” Craig growled wordlessly and lay himself down on the bare bench in the middle of the small room. “Are you hungry dear? I’m going to cook you some dinner. You need to keep your strength up for your next match.” Curious at the idea of Nurse actually cooking food for the first time ever, Craig opened his eyes and looked over at her. She still held the torch in one hand, but in the other was a can of beans and loaf of bread. “I used to be a girl guide you know. Beans on toast a la blowtorch is pretty tasty.” “So this is what we’re reduced to, eh Nurse? Living in this hell hole and eating like tramps. Tell me though, why do you seem so bloody happy about it? Don’t you miss the mansion? Don’t you miss the wide screen TV and walk in wardrobes. How about the Jacuzzi, you used to spend half the goddamn day in it.” Nurse considered the question carefully before answering, “Yeah, it was great. But where was the challenge? It was too easy, and we became complacent. Can’t you see that? Now we have something to work towards. We start at the bottom again and work our way up. I’ve done it before and no doubt I’ll have to do it again. One day you’ll thank me Craig. Until you’ve had to fight tooth and nail and scheme and coerce and beg and steal to get what you want, you’ll never fully appreciate it.” “Don’t patronize me Nurse. I knew you when you were nothing. I saw how desperate you were for fame and glory and money, and it wasn’t as pretty or noble as you make out it was. If you think I’m going to be by your side while you do it all again, you’re mistaken. I’m going to win this tournament and make a hell of a lot of money from it. But the moment I’m out, I’ll be gone, without you.” Hurt, Nurse tries to hold Craig’s hand, but he pulls away from her. “Don’t be like this. Don’t fight with me. I love you. I must have told you a thousand times in the last few days.” “Yeah, but it still doesn’t make it any less of a lie Esme. You’re incapable of love. I loved you for so long, but now all I feel is … empty. You’ve robbed me of everything I ever held dear and for that I’ll never forgive you.” Unable to keep up her caring side for long, Nurse sneered and whispered to Craig. “What you lost wasn’t worth very much to begin with; a rattley old house and a mother who could barely tolerate you. I’m not sorry for her death. And deep down I think you’re not either.” A rumbling in Craig’s throat increased its force and pressure until it was let out as a primal scream, loaded with anger. But an even louder sound eclipses it. The tin of beans that Nurse had been heating with the torch exploded, covering the walls, Craig and Nurse in a sticky orange splatter. Even more furious now, Craig’s anger found its voice and he slammed Nurse into the wall. There had been an explosion, two explosions. Both Goat Mansion and the guest house had blown up simultaneously and Craig’s mother had disappeared, presumed dead beneath the rubble. Craig had tried to live in denial, tried to live as though it weren’t true, that she was only away on holiday or perhaps had lost her memory in the blast but would return soon. But the hardness in Nurse’s eyes shattered his illusions. When she wanted someone hurt, they stayed hurt, and he knew that if she ever wanted someone dead, then that’s what they’d be. “My mother loved me! And you killed her. Never… I’ll never forgive you for that! Yet Craig stayed there in the bowels of the Arena with her. Lacking any other offers and disorientated as to what direction his life should take, Craig didn’t think he had any other choice. The semi-finals of GTT4 were almost upon him and the desire to win burned stronger in him then ever. It wouldn’t bring his mother back, but it might distract him from the crippling pain of his bereaved soul. He needed focus, and so he vowed he would pour all his hatred and grief into his match against Chris Tarrant.
