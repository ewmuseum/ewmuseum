The But for the Grace of God Part One

15 December 2005

Date: 2005-12-15 :|: Disconnect - Two Weeks Ago :|: It was Mary Connell’s first day on the job and she didn’t know all of the correct protocols and procedures yet. When applying for the job, she expected that her first day would be solely spent learning the fundamentals of the job, but instead they just threw her in at the deep end, expecting her learn all that she needed to know haphazardly. Throughout the morning everything when without a hitch, but then in the early afternoon, when Mr Stakiri had an incoming call, she hit the wrong button and instead of putting the call through to her employer, she somehow patched herself into the call he was currently taking. "Winters killed her?" Stakiri questioned. Mary’s eyes widened at the word ‘killed’ and wondered just what in the hell they were talking about. Perhaps they’re talking about some kind of new computer game, she thought, deluding herself. She frantically searched for disconnect button. "Yeah," the other voice confirmed. "Looks like he just put the sword right through her. We found this ‘Legend’ character unconscious at the other end of the warehouse. He’s been like that for at least an hour and she’s only been dead for half that time." "Interesting," Stakiri responded. "I’ll say," The voice replied. "Winters must be developing a lot better than we expected…" She finally found the disconnect button and sat for the longest staring into nothingness. They were just talking about some new video game, she told herself desperately. It was just a new video game, in fact, she was pretty certain that she’d heard her son talk about the same one this morning, over breakfast. :|: Approach - Now :|: I sat on the edge of the rooftop of my apartment compelx, my legs dangled over the building’s edge recklessly, watching the busy mid-morning traffic descent into anarchy below. It was winter now, much too cold to be sat out here wasting time feeling sorrow for myself, but I really couldn’t care about anything at the moment, much less the weather. I was still trying to deal with the whole GTT5 thing. I lost. For the first time in six months I lost a match. My undefeated streak within the WWA and my ego has been built up so much within the last couple of months that I now found myself unable to deal with the whole concept. Granted, the match was something of a close call. It could’ve gone either way; it just didn’t go mine. It continue to tell myself that it’s kind of understandable really. I tell myself, Seymour Almasy is a veritable legend in these kind of tournaments and due to the closeness of the match, his winning instincts and experience took control, and he managed to get the upper hand. Now, I can just about allow myself to tentatively accept that I lost the match fairly, but it doesn’t mean that I have to like it. Then I got the call. It came over the weekend whilst I was pigging out on Papa Johns pizza and watching nothing in particular on the tube. It was the representatives from the primetime committee telling me that I have a second chance at this thing. Great, huh? They told me that a number of their confirmed bookings have had to pull out and they needed me to be a play-in. They need me to fill in the numbers. After my loss to Almasy, I guess that’s all I am to them. I told them I’d think about it and ever since taking the call, I’ve been asking myself the same questions over and over again, perpetually torturing myself into making some sort of decision either way. One the one hand, I have the opportunity to go back into the mix, the honour competing against some of the best wrestlers working the circuit today. On the other, I already know that I won’t win. How can I? I’ve already been beaten by one of the combatants and despite being a legend in the field, he wasn’t even one of the favourites to win the tournament. What would be the point of taking my second chance only to lose, if not in this round, then the next or the one after that? I took another sip of my beer and then stopped mid-gulp, the bottle still centimetres away from my lips. My mind quickly refocused, completely wiped of my wrestling troubles, and concentrating upon footsteps lightly tapping upon the stairway leading to the roof. It was definitely a woman; she wore high heels and by the sound of her heels pounding against the concrete steps, she weighed about one hundred and ten pounds max. My mind raced at the thought, but then quietened down when I remembered I wasn’t doing anything illegal or suspicious. The rooftop can be accessed by anyone who lived in the apartment block; it’s just that nobody ever bothered with coming up here. Perhaps I scared the others away? For some reason I find the thought pleasing in some small way and I allow myself another sip of beer. With great difficulty, the woman barged the heavy door leading onto the rooftop open and stumbled onto it’s cold, concrete surface. I’m tempted to look around to see what see looks like, but I realise that I probably won’t be interested even if I like what I see. It’s been that kind of month. I hear her footsteps approach me, stopping within about two foot of the buildings edge. The rooftop isn’t a pleasure enjoyed by everyone, it seems. "Aren’t you worried?" The woman asked, without even bothering to introduce herself or reading the rather large fuck off I have metaphorically painted across my face. I didn’t reply to her at all. I simply glared at her, taking another large mouthful of me beer. She seemed pretty enough to me; brunette, brown eyes, slim edging around the mid-twenties, stylishly dressed. But not pretty enough for me to stop ignoring her. After a minute or two she seems uncomfortable with the strained silence - and me blatantly ignoring her - and grew restless. I kinda figured that she’d just fuck off at that point, but instead she folded her arms - almost in defiance - and stood her ground stubbornly. That told me one thing; she was determined to get some kind of response from me and specifically came up here to find me. She wanted something from me. "Didn’t you hear me?" She pushed. "I said: Aren’t you worried?" This time I do not glare at her at all, I simply look in the opposite direction, to the busy road below. However, I sense her presence still lingering over me. A real stubborn one this. "You’re supposed to say: about what?" She told me. "Then I am supposed to reply: about falling off the edge of the building." I shake my head and grin to myself, still doing my best to ignore the woman. "Look," She began. "I don’t have time to go through all of this bullshit. I need you’re help and I’m going to get it one way or the other." I continued to ignore her. I didn’t even know the woman and she’d come to me for help. I really didn’t have time for this. I stood from my seated position and made my way over to the doorway, leading back into my apartment building. She took a side step and got directly into my path. I felt like hurling myself over to the next building along, just to get away from the woman, but then she’d truly know what I‘m capable of and that just isn‘t an option. More to the pity. Instead, I continued, refusing to alter my pace even slightly and barged past the woman, almost bowling her over like a skittle. She stumbled backward, obviously amazed at how somebody could be so rude. As I reached the doorway, she sparked up again. "Don’t help me then, Mr Winters…" I stopped at the doorway. She knew my name. My neighbours didn’t even know my name - not that I tried to hide it from them. Then, of course, I realised that my showing in the GTT5 had brought me more attention than I usually received from my usual WWA exposure. I smiled to myself, secure for a moment with the knowledge that the woman didn’t truly know anything about me, and took a step inside. She added more. "Mr Stakiri sends his regards…" I stopped mid-step, hardly believing my ears at first, but when the message sank in, I rushed out, onto the roof once again, eying the woman up. I felt the anger well up inside me; this wasn’t good. How did she know about my working relationship with Stakiri? What the hell is going on here? More importantly what did the woman want? I decided that I didn’t much care. "I thought that’d get your…" She trailed off, as I darted toward her, grabbing her roughly by the throat. Despite her stubbornness and guile, she hadn’t expected that; hell, I hadn’t even expected it. "…gahk…attention." The more the anger developed within me, the tighter my grip around the woman’s neck became. Soon my grip would become to strong for her brittle bones to withstand and she would inevitably die. I couldn’t seem to care, my anger took care of all that. I wouldn’t even begin to feel guilty about it until much later, when all this was over and the woman had been drained of all life. I’d imagine that my reaction would seem a little drastic to the outside viewer, but I’ve had so many of these people just turn up on my doorstep - or rooftop - wanting something from me before and then when I refuse to help whichever madman it is that week they threatened me with exposure or killing someone close to me. The last time it happened, Aeryn - the woman I loved - had died by my hand. I’d had enough of it. "What makes you think you want my attention?" I asked her, walking over to the edge of the building, her neck stick firmly clasped within my hand. Outstretched in front of me, I dangled her over the edge of the building. The woman struggled slightly, glaring downward at her own doom, eyes wild, tears forming at the corners of her eyes. "…gahk…I…I need your help…" She managed, now looking directly into my eyes. I could see that she didn’t much care what happened to her at this point, whatever she needed me to do meant more to her than her own life. I could tell then and there, that she was different from the other lunatics. She had no hidden agenda, she was just a woman who needed help from someone who could do things most men can’t. And for some reason, this realisation cause all the anger I had welled up inside to simply dissipate into nothingness. Still didn’t mean I would help her though. Whatever story she had to follow this plea for help up. I whirled around one hundred and eighty degrees on the spot and released my grip on the woman’s neck. She slumped down to the floor with a thud, still coughing and spluttering. I may have already caused permanent damage. "You’d better tell me what you need from me," I told her. "Before I reconsider the dangling you over the edge of the building thing." I helped her to her feet. "The names Mary," she told me. "Mary Connell. Nice to meet you, Mr Winters." I grunted something in response and led her toward my apartment; it was the least I could do. :|: Event - Three Days Ago :|: It happened as she was leaving Stakiri Towers one night, just after work had finished. Her son, Michael, had come straight there from school; they’d planned to go clothes shopping and, on the plus side, it also kept Michael from hanging around with pot-heads he called his friends. Michael didn’t bother anybody, from what she could tell he enjoyed the working environment and Mr Stakiri didn‘t mind him being there either. He‘d once told her it‘d be kind of hypocritical on the account of his daughter showing up in his office all the time an all. Her receptionist work was dull and it wasn’t the work she’d imagine she’d be doing after leaving college, but nevertheless it did pay well. Upon leaving the building, Mr Stakiri caught up with her and gave her a bonus on the spot for some. Two hundred dollars. He was good like that and often did it to a number of others in his employment. He’d heard some her colleagues talking about buying Michael some clothes and decided lend a helping hand. It was just the kind of generous employer he was. She didn’t precisely know what made the thought pass through their brains. Perhaps it was the thank you kiss she’d given him in thanks for the money? Perhaps it was the way he looked at her longingly? At this point it didn’t matter. In any case, they’d jumped to the wrong conclusion and assumed that she and Mr Stakiri were an ‘item’. It happened so fast she didn’t even have time to react. The masked men came out of nowhere, shoved her aside and took Michael away. She could still hear his screams as they hauled him away at gunpoint. One of the kidnappers told Stakiri something that she couldn’t understand and kicked him savagely in the ribs. She got to her feet again, quickly, and made after her son, but the man who’d been working Stakiri over, grabbed her by the hair and dragged her back. She fell to the floor with a thud and saw the man looming over her menacingly. She saw his lips move, but couldn’t quite make out the sounds as everything back black and she slipped quietly into unconsciousness. To Be Continued…
