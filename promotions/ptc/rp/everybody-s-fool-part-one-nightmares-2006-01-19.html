Everybody's Fool Part One: Nightmares

19 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-19 Late Afternoon. Monday 16th January 2006. Alamodrome. San Antonio, Texas. I yawned and gently massaged my eyes. Didn’t get much sleep last night thanks to that damned nightmare. I’d been having the same one for weeks now and they always left me feeling oddly disturbed the following morning despite remembering absolutely nothing about it. Like always I awoke more exhausted than I was before going to sleep. Weird. Trying my best to ignore the disturbing sensation firmly occupying my mind, I heaved my unreasonably heavy bag up, off the locker room bench, and grunted at the twinge of pain that shot through my nervous system as a resultant. With my free hand, I fished inside my left jacket pocket and withdrew my pain pills. The stand-in backstage doctor gave me them for the pain a couple of weeks back, after taking a career threatening tumble from the WWATron at Birthday Bash. "Fuckin’ Mal Somers!" I cursed for the millionth tine that week, my anger taking the edge off the pain. I’m quite happy with the decision to go onto pain medication at the time; the regular doctor always seemed reluctant to give me pain pills backstage. He was one of the countless WWA staff that bought into the whole ‘Winters is a traitor to the WWA’ sentiment that’d been floating around for the past couple months and therefore, didn’t want to waste any sort of medication on me. But regular doctor hadn’t shown up for work that morning and thankfully, the new doctor hadn’t bought into the backstage anti-Winters gossip; at least not yet, he hadn’t. The stand-in doctor had told me that my ribs would hurt for about a month - two at the most - and advised me to take the time off from the wrestling ring. After I informed him that wasn’t going to happen - keeping my sarcastic comments to a bare minimum - he handed the pills over. "You should only take them a couple of times a day," he’d told me, "anymore than that, and we could have serious problems getting you off the medication when your rib injury subsides." At the time I simply nodded at him, silently, and he probably thought that I was just going to disregard his advice as if it were Lex Luger’s booking information, but before he’d even brought the subject up, I’d made a promise to myself not to be too reliant on the medication. It’s a weakness that I am unwilling to even contemplate. After a couple of minutes of struggling around desperately attempting to wrestle the child-locked top off, I extracted two of the small, circular, white pills and ingested them immediately. Then, pulling a face at the bitter taste the pills left in my mouth, I made one final sweep of the locker room, searching for any possible item that I could‘ve left behind; the last thing I needed was to turn up at the GTT5 with one damn elbow pad missing. Satisfied that everything of mine was safely enclosed within my bag, I hit the ‘play’ button upon my black, four gigabyte, Apple Ipod Nano and took a step out into the backstage corridors, absently flicking through my play list. In the end I decided upon "Everybody’s Fool" by Evanescence; perhaps a depressing song to some, but you couldn’t argue with the elegance of the lyrics. Besides, I like depressing music. Perfect by Nature Icons of Self Indulgence Just what we all need… I crank the volume up high, put the delicately small device in my right jacket pocket and headed for the exit. More lies about a world that Never was and never will be Have you no shame don’t you see me You know you’ve got everybody fooled… "Mr. Winters," the voice called, faintly hovering above the lyrics. "Before you go…" I continued along, pretending that I hadn’t heard the man calling my name. Another reason I loved Ipods. Wearing them provides an air-tight excuse to ignore people - a device that I’d been trying to locate for a considerably long time now. Look here she comes now Bow down and stare in wonder Oh how we love you Now flaws when you’re pretending… "Mr. Winters!" The voice perseveres, closer now. I doubt I will get out of here cleanly now, but the exit door was in sight so there was still a small chance. Just a couple more steps. That’s all I needed. Then I’d be free. But now I know she Never was and never will be You don’t know how you’ve betrayed me And somehow you’ve got everybody foo… The hand reached out and grabbed my right shoulder firmly, just centimetres away from the exit. Well as the old cliche goes; so close, yet so far… I turned around and sighed, glaring at the perpetrator angrily. At least I had the satisfaction of watching the little greasy-haired bastard cower a little, maybe realising that his mistake might be fatal. I pulled out the left earpiece of my headphones. "What?" "I’m, uh, sorry didn’t know you were listening to music…" He trailed off. I sighed again. This was just getting pathetic now. "It’s okay. What the hell do you want?" "Well, I realise that you were just about to leave," he began, "but Mr Ventressa would like to see you in his office." "In his office? Now? Are you serious?" I questioned, relishing watching the guy squirm uncomfortably on the spot. I really had to try to contain my smirk. "Yes, sir." He responded. "It’s about the GTT5." I sighed and gestured for him to lead the way. The moron took a couple of seconds to run this through his seemingly tiny brain and you could almost see the light bulb materialise majestically above his head when the meaning of the gesture finally hit him. A couple of minutes later, after getting lost on at least three separate occasions within the maze-like corridors of the Alamodrome, we finally came to the door of Ventressa’s office. I shook my head, disapprovingly, as he opened the door and waved me right in. As I entered the office, I was immediately propositioned by the vision of WWA owner Vik Ventressa sat behind his make-shift desk, talking on his mobile phone to someone. He noticed me enter the office and then held up one finger, signalling for me to wait for a minute. I rolled my eyes around the back of my head; the nerve of this guy. Without waiting for instruction to do so, I took the comfortable black, leather seat opposite Ventressa and rebelliously rested my feet up on his desk. I figured it’d piss him off, like he’d already managed to piss me off. My suspicious were indeed vindicated when he glared, firstly at my feet, and then at me disapprovingly. But before I could shoot him a ‘I couldn’t give a flying fuck even if you’re my employer’ look, a vibrant image I somehow recognised from the nightmare I’d been having for the last couple of weeks, shot through my head, forcing me to focus in on the image of a woman’s bloodied face. I couldn’t be sure, but she appeared to be dead. I jumped in my seat and lowered my legs from Ventressa’s desk, now motionless in shock. Ventressa shot me an angry/confused expression then grunted something under his breath that I couldn’t actually decipher and added, "No, sorry…I was speaking to someone else. I gotta deal with this, I’ll call you back." Ventressa hit the disconnect button, folded up his phone and then laid it out before him on his desk. He glanced at his watched. "It’s about time you showed up!" I turned back toward the door and glared at the greasy-haired moron who’d feebly attempt to lead me here. He developed a sheepish expression and then promptly left, closing the door behind him. Ventressa shook his head. "Damn, I gotta get some better staff around here." I nodded absently, then remembered that I was pissed off. "Why am I here, Ventressa?" Ventressa grinned. "Right to the point…why did I expect anything else?" I ignored his feeble attempt at humour and glared out of the window of the office, indicating to Ventressa just how uninterested in all of this I truly am. "Anyway," Ventressa began, "I’m here to talk to you about the GTT5…did you know that one quarter of all competitors remaining in the tournament are from the WWA?" I glared at my employer, unsure of what to say beyond ‘I don’t care’. "Fantastic." "Well, huh, I just want you to understand," He started, wondering whether there was a slight sprinkle of sarcasm over my comment. Of course there was and I’d laid it on pretty thick. "How proud I, and upon extension the entire WWA, are of your efforts within this career defining competition." Then he paused for reasons beyond my comprehension. Am I supposed to thank him for his comments or something? Well, that ain’t gonna happen anytime soon. It was perfectly transparent to me that Ventressa being ‘proud’ of us for our efforts in the GTT5 was just a poorly shrouded euphemism for ‘thanks for making us a helluva lot more money than usual this month’. I remained silent and stared at him blankly. "Well done," He continued after a few seconds, realising that my thanks wasn’t forthcoming. "And as a thank you I’m giving you next Monday Meltdown off for you to fully prepare for your match," then muttered under his breath, "because you’re gonna need it." I nodded and made sure the sarcasm in my voice was so thick you’d have difficulty cutting it with a chainsaw, "Thanks." I stood from my seat and headed toward the door. "And," he began, rather too chirpily for my liking. "Good luck." I half-heartily nodded a ‘thank you’ and opened the door. The greasy-haired moron had been waiting for me exit. "Can I show you out, sir?" "No," I told him, "I have a match next week in a very important wrestling tournament…I could do with getting there on time." He smiled, moronically. "Okay, sir…" His face then promptly morphed from a glow of happiness to a scrunched up expression of confusion as his feeble brain finally clunked to the conclusion he’d received an insult. But, by that time of course, I was gone, finding my way back to the exit door in seconds, with minimal difficulty. I hit the ‘play’ on my Ipod and stepped out into the parking lot taking a grateful deep breath, inhaling as much of the fresh air as my lungs could hold. The sun had set and with it the air outside seemed clearer, tasted fresher; the sky awash with yellow and purple colours, bizarrely reminding me of a bruise, it’d even begun to turn black around the edges. Early Tuesday Morning. 17th January 2006. A Motel. San Antonio, Texas. The glow of the moon seemed particularly strong that night; its light filtered into the room, via the large back window, with a strange hint of red. I stood at the centre of the massive room, inspecting its interior carefully, looking for clues as to where the hell I was. It certainly wasn’t my apartment. I didn’t recognise any of the furniture or the composition slightly. But then, I felt a small section of my brain at the back of my head pulsate, wanting - and receiving - my immediate attention. I tried to access its contents, but suddenly that pulsating section seemed a lot farther away than it had been before. That’s because it’s moving away from you!, another, more rebellious, section of my brain informed me. I quickly realised that it was speaking the truth and I could no longer even see the pulsating memory any longer. Then a door to my immediate left sprung open, taking me quite by surprise and a woman stepped through the door. She wore an elegant, white dress, he long, soft, blonde hair draping over it innocently and I couldn’t help but notice her striking resemblance to Sarah Michelle Gellar. I know what’s going to happen before I react, I fight to stop myself from going through with it, but my resistance seemed futile. I was unable to prevent my right hand from rapidly reaching out to her neck and snapping it as though it was a bread stick. I could feel my face contort in horror as her lifeless body slumped to the floor in a heap. In control of my body again, I back away the dead woman, glaring at my now bloodied hands. I glanced down at the dead woman and although there were no visible cuts anywhere about her, the whole floor was already stained with blood that seemed to just flow, perpetually. I screamed, for arguably the first time in my life, terrified of what I‘d just done and what I was beginning to transmogrify into. I bolt up right in my bed, wide awake now, soaking wet through from the sweat. This was the very first time I remembered the gory details of the nightmare, but I knew deep down, it was the very same nightmare. I instinctively held out my hands in front of me and checked them for blood. There was nothing there of course, but the compulsion to make sure was too strong to resist. Once adequately content that there was no blood anywhere near me, I sighed and lay back down, knowing full well that I wouldn’t bet getting any more sleep for the rest of the night. It was strange; I just couldn’t shake the feeling that there was something more to these dreams, as though my subconscious was keeping things from me. The undeniable sensation that the images obtained from the nightmare actually meant something to me was too strong to simply ignore. I should’ve spent the night figuring out some kind pliable strategy to take on Rollins, O’Shea and Bradley in the GTT5, but instead I spent the rest of the night on something totally unproductive instead; absently wondering whether I could have actually killed the woman from my nightmares. Late Wednesday Evening. 18th January 2006. Rooftops. Somewhere In New York City. Aeryn was already waiting for me at the designated rooftop when I turned up. She didn’t sense my arrival until I actually landed on the rooftop. Sloppy, even for her. I shook my head, disapprovingly, as she finally turned around to greet me. "You’re getting sloppy…" Aeryn sighed and folded her arms, her knee long leather coat flapping wildly in the wind. "Oh hello, Aeryn. Good to see you. Like the hair, had it cut?" I grinned. Her usual sarcastic self. The uncomfortable tension between us had almost completely evaporated now, but I could still sense minute traces here and there every time we had a conversation. I had no time to take note of such things today. This nightmare business had been playing on my mind throughout my entire journey back to New York and it was becoming increasingly distracting. I need closure - and soon. "Aeryn," I began, desperately searching for the right words to adequately explain everything to her. "I had a nightmare and…" Aeryn rolled her eyes around the back of her head. "A nightmare? You called me out here to discuss a nightmare?" "Yes," I replied, a little sterner than I’d meant to be. "I’ve been having the same nightmare for a couple of weeks now and they’re disturbing me." Aeryn could see the distress painted across my face and immediately dropped the sarcasm. "Well, what was the nightmare about?" "I killed a woman," I told her. No reason to cushion the blow. Not from her. "With my bare hands. Snapped her neck. Blood everywhere." "You killed a woman?" Aeryn asked. I noted the odd concoction of terror and disappointment in her eyes. "In the dream, I did," I responded. "Ring any bells with you?" Aeryn appeared to be taken aback. "What?" "Does the whole scenario seem familiar to you at all?" I questioned. "What, exactly, are you asking me?" Aeryn replied. Angry and confused now. "I want to know whether the nightmare actually is a nightmare…or whether it actually happened in a time before I lost my memory…" I said, head down, ashamed that it’s occurrence was even a possibility. "If this is another one of your attempts to mine information out of me, then you’re barking up the wrong tree…" Aeryn answered. Still angry, but no longer confused. "So by that you mean it didn’t happen?" I asked her, a clear undertone of relief interlace within my voice. "I didn’t kill the woman who bore a striking resemblance to Sarah Michelle Gellar?" "Jonathon, you realise that a lot of normal people have vivid nightmares all the time, don’t you?" Aeryn asked, rhetorically. "I’m afraid you’re no exception to that rule…" I nodded in response. "You’re right. It was just a nightmare. That’s all." Aeryn smirked, lovingly, and hugged me. What’s more, I let her. *** I was half way home from my meeting with Aeryn when I heard the call for help. Normally, I would ignore the plea. Not my problem. However, this time it was my problem; for reasons beyond my comprehension I felt a compulsion to follow the woman‘s so strong I couldn’t ignore it. I recognised the voice from somewhere. I traced the voice to an alleyway two buildings over. From my vantage point from the building top, I could tell that he was definitely mugging her; didn’t know whether he intended to rape her not yet. I held my position for a couple of seconds, desperately trying to get a clear view of woman’s face to see if I would recognise it as I did her voice. She kicked the mugger in balls and quickly headed for safety into the busy streets looking for help, however, the mugger recuperated quickly and hauled her back toward him by her collar. I tuned out to the kicking, screaming and the muggers threats and finally caught a glimpse of the woman’s face. Upon realising the impossible that was unfolding before me, the shock took all power away from my legs and I tumbled backward, my ass scraping against the gravelled roof surface. I just sat there staring at nothing in particular, running whole scenario through my head over and over again, desperately attempting make some sense of it all. But I couldn’t. It was her. The woman from my nightmares. The Sarah Michelle Gellar look-a-like. I couldn’t believe it. I even had to take another glance over the edge of the building just to make sure that mind wasn’t playing tricks with me. Upon my second sighting, there could be no doubt. It was her alright. I had to know what the connection between the two of us was. Perhaps I’d only tried to kill her before my memory was wiped and I became the man I am now? Or perhaps I’d even prevented her from being killed back then? I picked myself up from the floor and launched myself over the side of the building, landing upon dank, squalid alleyway floor with the grace and agility of a particularly virile feline. I winced in pain little upon impact, the injury to my ribs not reacting well with the drop. The mugger heard me land immediately, but I wasn’t much concentrating on him. I never took my eyes off the woman, as the mugger released his grip on her and she slumped to the ground painfully. He charged at me, eyes wild with anger and hatred for reasons that I wasn‘t remotely interested in. I swatted him away with minimal effort, sending him sprawling into a number of trans cans. I continued on toward my goal with unwavering determination. I held out my hand and pulled her to her feet. She sobbed quietly, seemingly finding it difficult to accept what had happened to her. I placed my hand under her chin and delicately made her look me in the eye. "Do you know me?" She developed a confused expression. "Y…y…you saved me…" "I know," I told her, "but have you ever seen me before tonight? Do we know each other?" Since waking up after the amnesia I learned that someone, probably my former employer Mr Stakiri, had changed my appearance, presumably via plastic surgery. Even if we did know each other from that time, she wouldn’t recognise me now. My questions were pointless. "W…wh…what are you talking about?" She spluttered, confused. "I’ve been having nightmares about you…there has to be some kind of connection!" I realised long before the words left my lips that it was a crazy thing to say, but in my bewilderment I couldn’t seem to care, hoping beyond hope that she had some sort of answer for me. She didn’t. "Get the hell off me…HELP! HELP SOMEBODY! HELP!" I reeled back in horror when I saw the same terror in her eyes as I remembered from my nightmares. I released my grip, allowing the woman to break free and dart toward the busy streets. I was tempted to stop her, but realised it would do me no good. But, nevertheless, there had to be some kind of connection between the two of us…didn’t there? I didn’t even know anymore. I was exhausted. The combination of my injury, my exertion on Monday Night Meltdown in the three way match up and lack of sleep had me too tired to even care anymore. I decided to get some sleep. Tomorrow I will find this woman again and enlisted Aeryn’s help in determining the connection between us. Perhaps then I will get my answers. To Be Continued…
