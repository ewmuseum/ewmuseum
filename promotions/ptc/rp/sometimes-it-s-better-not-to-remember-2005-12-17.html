Sometimes it's better not to remember

17 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-17 "You can doubt me all you like, but I am still the man I used to be and I will prove that to you all" Salaok Davis – GTT4, Round One He had done it. Salaok had taken the first steps to finding out who he truly was again and yet was still feeling no closer to remembering the full story or exactly who he was. His invitation to this wrestling tournament was to be the key that opened the windows to his soul again and yet he was greeted with people who didn’t know him. Surely there was going to be someone who could tell him something about his past out of the other 125 people entered and yet no one said a word. While it bothered him, he knew that there was going to be an easier way to get the information and that was back at the house, which he had so far been hesitant to enter. Today that would change. Shutting the door of his hearse, which he was quickly, falling in love with again, he stood at the foot of the 42 stairs, which lead up to the two massive red wood doors that hold the past behind them. After over a year with no recollection of his past and who he is, finally he believed that once he opened those doors, the memories would begin to flood back. Gripping the key in his hand, the walk up the stairs begins and just like the last time he was here, Salaok steps over the 18th and 27th stairs without knowing or realising what he was doing until he was past them and didn’t want to have to stop to go back. Stopping and going back wasn’t what his life was about now, it was going forward and finding out the truth. A truth which had been stolen from him. A truth which he longed for more than anything in the world. A truth that he hoped would surround him with people he knew and loved. With the key extended in his hand, it slides straight into the keyhole of the door and then nothing. It wasn’t that the key didn’t work, it was the mere fact that Salaok knew that once that key was turned, there would be no going back to his carefree life that he had lead over the past 12 months. From this moment on, he would have a past more than just that of a professional fighter. What lied on the other side of those doors? Family? He could only wish for such a situation as he knew that there was indeed something missing from his arms and that was someone to hold. Slowing his breath and attempting to get control of the nerves, which were running rampant through his entire being, he pauses and then turns the key. The sound of the latch clicking was possibly the finest sound he had heard in the past year as that was the sound not only of a door unlocking, but the sound of his past becoming unshackled. From this point on there was no one anything that would hold him back from finding out the truth which had been unknowingly kept from him. Pushing against the door and turning the handle, the door begins to open with ease and no moaning from the joints that one would expect to hear from a door that hasn’t been opened in the last year. Or had it? Even though Salaok hadn’t been here for at least a year now, it was beginning to reveal that other people had been and judging by the piece of paper that sits on the floor of the entrance way, someone had been here this week. At first Salaok didn’t see the note on the floor, but with his first hesitant step into the dark building, it crumpled under foot and the sound caused him to look down with confusion written in big bold letters all over his face. It wasn’t that there was a piece of paper under his foot that was someone confusing, but the fact that it had his name printed on it with yesterday’s date in the top concern. Someone had been inside his house in the last twenty-four hours and he had a feeling that it was going to be this JD persona which had lead him here in the first place. Ever since receiving the primary letter from JD a week ago, Salaok life had developed worth again and evidently this JD held the keys not only to Salaok’s past, but also to his house. Reaching down from his grand height of 6’6", his large hand grasps the piece of paper from the well polished floor boards and holds it up to the light to read. Salaok, Congratulations on you victory in Round One, however do not think that the rest of the trip will be as easy as that. But right now I understand that GTT5 is the last thing on your mind and the first thing is knowing more and learning about this house in which you own. Yes Salaok, you do still own the house and much, much more than just this. I have no need right now to tell you all of the details because this journey is about you finding out who you are and regaining those memories no matter how much they effect you. You are allowed to turn the lights on after you’ve finished reading this but you are only allowed to look in certain areas as some hold memories which you are not yet ready for. After you’ve turned on the lights which you will find on the wall to your left if you are still standing in the doorway like I am expecting you to be, I want you to head up the stairs, turn left and go into the third door. The door mind you is locked and you threw away the key in order to block out that room from your life. It’s time you reopened that door Salaok as it’s a memory I need you to remember. I’m sorry Salaok, JD. If ever there was a situation that wasn’t to be trusted, this would be the one as not only had this JD gotten into his house, knew exactly where Salaok would stop to read the letter, but he is leading him to a room that will bring back memory or so he hopes. Reaching behind him on the wall, Salaok hits the lights and sees the money that he once flaunted displayed in front of him as a gigantic slap in the face. For the past year Salaok had worked hard to get enough money to put himself up in crumby hostels and yet this was waiting for him all along? The artwork, which hangs from the walls, is priceless. The extravagant plants, which rest in every corner that, is visible from where he stands. The floorboards, which are so well cared for that they act like mirrors, reflecting everything perfectly. And then there is the staircase that JD had mentioned in the letter, also he didn’t say how beautiful it was. Made from a deep red wood which half sucked all the light that hits it into the deepness of it’s grain and yet bounces the other half off into the room, making it look like it radiantly shines. The two foot wide red carpet, which extends perfectly down the middle of the arrangement oozes with class and didn’t look to have a single mark on it. But the eye catcher seemed too good to be real but something in the back of his mind said that it was one hundred percent true. The railings were gold. Not only were they gold but were decorated with tiny phases, etched into the gold with the most controlled of hands. He let his fingers run over the etchings as he slowly moved up the stairs and looked to the left at the door, which hopefully held the keys. Each step bringing him closer to knowing what he longed to know and what he wanted to know what exactly who he was and what he had done to deserve a home such as this? As he feet touch base with the top of the stairs and he begins to move to the door, he doesn’t look anywhere but at his desired target as too much time had been wasted over the last year and searching the rest of the house for clues was just about wasted effort at this point in time. Salaok had no doubt that the other rooms also would hold secrets he needed to relearn, but this was the one that the mysterious JD had instructed him towards. His feet finally come to a rest in front of the third door and this was it. Between Salaok and knowing something about his past now stood a locked door which had no key. It wasn’t so much nerves, which now riffled through his body, but excitement, which could explain way it was Salaok’s foot shot forth and smashed the lock, swinging the door open with great force. He never should have. Something’s are best not to be remembered. This room was the pure definition of that. Instantly Salaok’s knees give way from under his large frame and he plummets to them with his eyes wide open and his mouth distorted in pain. A pain that remembers. Why had JD directed him towards this room first? Salaok’s eyes pierced the small child’s bed that sits in front of him with the linen still perfectly made and a small teddy bear resting up against the pillow. While the room looked innocent, the visions that Salaok was seeing were anything but. It was one thing to be reliving a memory, it was another to still be able to smell it. If only the memories that were flashing back into his mind where those when he held his son in his arms for the first time, or played with him in the back yard then the situation would have been anything but this. For the memory that stares Salaok in the face, fuelled by the smell of the room was that of his son’s death bed. It’s said that the smell of death is something that once has stained it’s surroundings, can never be lifted from the gain in which it embeds itself and that was the case in this room. Every inhale drove Salaok one step closer to insanity until it all came up. Everything Salaok had eaten coursed out of his mouth like a multicoloured water feature at a children’s amusement park. It was too strong no matter how hard he tries to control himself, the pain inside his body was no in control of everything he did and felt. And memory of that day had returned. The innocent child laying, seemingly asleep on his bed but his skin pale. No breathing, no heart beat. The tears of pain rolling down Salaok’s face now as they did then over a year ago. The memories of Salaok peeling the skin colour tape from around his son’s neck revealing the ear to ear cut which had drained his son of his life and Salaok of all hope. But it was the insult that was added by the maggots pushing out a small chunk of little Darrell’s flesh which rolled off the bed onto the floor where the blood stained grubs continued to eat at it. Why had JD brought him here? Why was it so important for Salaok to remember this horrid event so soon? Walking backwards, he bumps into the wall and slides down it, sitting on the floor like he did when he found his son that fateful day. Finally his eyes steer away from the empty bed and look down at the stain on the floor next to Salaok which he had caused himself as for days he had sat there, unable to move, not even to go to the toilet. That feeling had returned. Salaok had been defeated in his own memory again.
