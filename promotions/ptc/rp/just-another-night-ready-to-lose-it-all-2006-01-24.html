Just Another Night: Ready To Lose it All

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 "You asked to see me," Jayce asked as he knocked on the door of the office. "Jayce, good to see you," Steven Caldera was grinning at him; something that immediately put him on edge. Caldera was being friendly, that was strange on its own. But seeing a sincere grin on the face of the man that rules over GCW with an iron fist was uncanny. "Jayce, I just wanted to congratulate you on the excellent progress you've been making here in GCW, as well as in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament," Caldera practically gushed all over his seat, "We have all been impressed with everything that you have been doing." "Thank you," was all Jayce could manage to say in response. He wanted to get everything off his chest, to let Caldera know that he had hired a madman, a murderer, and that he needed to do something about it, but it was pointless. "There’s something else too," Caldera continued as he opened a drawer on his desk. "Considering you are the Cruiserweight champion, I figured you could use a little extra money in your pockets." Caldera passed a bone white envelope across the desk toward Jayce. "Excuse me," Jayce thought to himself. "I wonder what he’d give me if he knew that I had a hand in killing over thirty people." None of it came out though, and Jayce just smiled in Caldera's face as he grabbed the envelope from his hand. "Come on now," His grin tightening with each passing second. "Open it up." As he tore one of the ends of the envelope, his brow wrinkled in confusion. "Is this some sort of joke," he asked as an uncertain frown creased his lips. "Not at all," Caldera replied, "It’s all yours." Jayce tapped the open end of the envelope against his palm as he said, "But there’s nothing in here." He stuck his fingers into the open end, searching for any sort of sign that maybe he was wrong. There was nothing there, and as he pulled his fingers from within, he saw that there was a dab of blood on his fingertip. He figured he had just got a paper cut, but the drops on his palm led him to believe otherwise. "What the…" Jayce gasped as he noticed that blood was just pouring out of the envelope, his hand now fully coated in the thick, and rich crimson. Jayce leaped from his chair with a repressed wail, dropping the envelope and watching in horror as it landed on the floor with a damp thump -- scarlet droplets spraying out in a wide fan-like pattern and soiling the off-white carpet. His heart began pounding harder and faster in his chest, as he looked up and over at Caldera, "What the hell is this?" "You killed them," Caldera said, the once warm and welcoming smile fading away to the accusatory gaze of the man in charge. "Murderer!" he screamed, extending his finger to point in the direction of Jayce. Just as the word bellowed from his throat, the door behind Jayce busted open. Jayce didn’t have any time to react before he had his face slammed into the unforgiving oak of Caldera’s desk. "Don’t you move!" said a voice behind him as a pair of handcuffs were locked around his wrists. "What?" Jayce questioned vehemently. "Wait a minute, he’s lying… I haven't done anything!" "Quiet down you bastard!" the voice said in the gruff, uncaring tones of your stereotypical police officer on some cheap late-night flick, "We know about everything you have done, and I know I’m going to personally enjoy watching them stick that needle into your arm." "You can’t do this to me!" Jayce cried out as he was turned around and marched out of the office. A crowd had gathered outside of the office, and Jayce stared out at them, his voice unsteady. "Somebody help me, I didn’t do anything!" A few rows down, Jayce could see Russell Braun standing there with a slick grin on his face. "Russ, Russ! Tell them! Tell them it wasn’t me!" "Sorry, son," Braun began, his tone conversational as he chewed on a piece of gum, "I wish I could help you on this, but you have to pay for what you’ve done." "Wait a minute, what!" Jayce cried out, only to receive a stunning blow on the back of his head. "Keep moving, shithead!" the cop demanded. "We don’t have all day." He shook his head from side to side, struggling to remain conscious; he lifted his head just in time to find himself about to stumble over a small doll stroller. "How could you do it, Jayce?" a young girl screamed up at him, tears coursing down her cheeks. "How could you let this happen to me?" "Why doesn’t anyone listen to me? I didn’t do anything! It was Russ… Russ I tell you!" "Don’t you fucking lie!" cried out a voice from the growing crowd. "Take responsibility for your crimes!" "Feck it," chimed in another. "Jus’ shoot the bastard now!" "Kill the racist prick!" agreed several other voices, and before long it became a chant. "End it now!" "End it now!" "End it now!"" Soon, Jayce and the officer were surrounded by once docile office workers, their eyes now filled with an animalistic rage as their voices cried out in harsh barks. "Shoot him now!" they all chanted. Braun, Caldera, Winters, and O’ Shea; all of them joined in the malicious exhortation. "Well, I guess I know what I have to do," said the officer's voice behind him, his tone filled with remorse. "I know they are not going to let me take you out of here alive, and I’ll be damned if I let them take me with you." Jayce could feel his bladder release as a hand clamped down on his arm, forcing him to his knees. "You can't do this! You can’t let this happen!" "I can do whatever I feel like," said the cop, walking around to stand in front of him. Jayce watched as the man reached down to undo the clasp on his holster, to the cheers of the crowd. "I’m a cop." Jayce craned his neck up to look in the eyes of the officer. Where he found a red bandana pulled tightly around the officer’s head, brown hair draped over his shoulders. It was Rich Rollins. "This is where it all ends, kid," Rollins grinned as he pressed the cold, nickel-plated steel pistol against Jayce’s forehead. "Your free ride is over." The shot echoed out as the gun went off. "NOOO," Jayce screamed as his body stiffened from the shock, taking in a harsh, rasping breath as his eyes flittered open. The musty aroma of sweat that clung to his body tickled his senses, as he bounded off the edge of the bed. "My God," Jayce mumbled incoherently, the memory of the nightmare still fresh in his mind, the deafening echo of the shot still ringing in his ears. Out of the corner of his eye, Jayce noticed the laptop he had been using to watch previous matches of his opponents had tilted over when he had stumbled around on the bed. He shook his head as he righted it, clicking on the little house icon on his browser before making his way toward the bathroom. With a twist of the knob, the cool clear liquid began to flow from its spout. He cupped his hands beneath its steady stream, filling his hand enough to allow it to dribble off the sides before splashing the refreshing fluid across his face. "Let it go, Jayce," he said to the smeared reflection that stared back at him, "snap out of it." He stood there until the horrible image of that pointing finger, that cry of "Murderer!" started to fade. He had been deluding himself. Walking around like this was all part of some grand adventure, and it was all a bunch of fun. But it was real. There would be no escaping the past, but he could do something to secure his future. He turned back to the laptop, thinking back to an image of a little girl with tawny locks twisted together into braids. The daughter of a police detective the story had said, missing for a few days now. Jayce tried to dismiss the thought; Braun had never resorted to cleansing children before. Surely, he thought to himself, he would not begin now. Slamming the laptop closed, as he snatched a set of keys off the dresser with a jingle, he made his way toward the door. "There’s only one way to know for sure," he muttered as he twisted the knob of the door. =¤=¤=¤=¤= =¤=¤=¤=¤= A sudden wave of light splashed into the room, drowning her senses and temporarily blinding her before her eyes could adjust. The opening of the door suffocated the darkness that had once encompassed her lithe frame, the darkness being lifted away as if a wedding veil had just been lifted and a kiss was about to give way to a new dawning of her life. Her heart fluttered with anticipation as the warmth of the light kissed her dried and chapped lips, before once again growing cold as a shadow was cast upon her. Her eyes adjusted to the light, Audrey Anne Pride suddenly felt the warmth and brightness of the light whisk itself away. There, standing in the wide open door frame, stood a menacing silhouette of the man who took a sick pleasure in tormenting her. Russell Braun. As hard as she tried to fight it, the tears had overpowered her weary lids and rolled down her flustered cheeks. "Why are you doing this," she cried out in a whisper, the sobs doing nothing to ease the incoherency as she clutched her knees to her chest and lowered her head uneasily. Her father would come for her soon, and then he would make the mean man pay. Braun casually walked over to where the child was rolled up into a fetal position, his aging joints relentless in their pursuit of reminding him of his age as he dropped down to a knee beside her. He gently ran his decrepit fingers through the ginger strands that draped messily over her face. Whisking a few strands away to stare into her bloodshot eyes, he could see the fear that hid behind her bemused gaze. The child shifted uncomfortably under his contemptuous glare, before closing her eyes and turning her head away. "If you trust your father, my child," his tone was composed yet foreboding, etching itself into her mind as he spoke in a whisper, "then you should have no fear of me or my threats." She was frightened, and he knew it. Maybe she didn’t have the same confidence in her father that he felt for him. He kneeled there for a moment in silence, comparing her doubts to the ones Jayce had felt for him. He had to stop thinking about it. Jayce let him know how he truly felt, and he had to do what he could to protect him from all of this… even if it meant taking responsibility for it all. "I brought you a sandwich," he said with a fatherly tone, almost as if he actually cared whether she ate or not. He placed a small Styrofoam plate at her feet. "You should eat something before you get sick. It won’t be much longer now." Braun strained as he stood up to his full size, the soreness in his limbs forcing a slight groan to emanate through tightened lips. He struggled toward the door, forcing himself to leave her in peace for at least a moment. He stood in the center of the door’s rectangular frame, glancing back as she unwound herself from her ball and seemingly swallowed the sandwich whole. Jayce use to love peanut butter and jelly too… strawberry was his favorite. "Are you out of your damned mind?" The voice startled him at first, he figured that the detective had been better than he had expected. But it wasn’t the detective, and it was something that he did not expect. He turned toward the source of the voice, shutting the door in the process. "What are you doing here," Braun asked as he massaged his temples gently, an unforeseen wrench in his plan. "Trying to figure out what the hell is going through that thick skull of yours." Jayce Bradley stood there with his arms folded across his chest, anger coursing through his veins like a cancer. He could not believe what the old man was doing; it was as if he wanted to get caught. "I told you that you were getting careless, and then you go and kidnap the lead detective’s daughter!" "You wouldn’t understand," came the relaxed response, as Braun made his way over to one of the chairs that flanked the wooden table in the center of the kitchen area. "Then how about you try and help me understand," Jayce was annoyed with the situation, he should be in a gym somewhere getting into better shape, working on a few new moves that no one would expect, and just getting himself into the right mindset to deal with a match of this magnitude. Instead, he was about to take a seat across from a man that had made his life a living hell, in hope of understanding why the old man would do something that could destroy both of their lives. "Because right now all I see you doing is risking everything that we have worked toward these past few years." "What we’ve worked for," Braun scoffed at the comment, actually insulted by the fact that Jayce had pretty much made it seem as if he was forced into this lifestyle. "S-" he hesitated, stopping himself from saying the word son after Jayce had nearly severed his head from his neck the last time he had said it. "Jayce, you don’t need to worry about this anymore. Just go back to your little wrestling ring and do what you have always wanted." "What I have always wanted…" Jayce trailed off, shaking his head as he chuckled softly to himself. "Since when has this ever been about what I’ve wanted? The whole time I have known you, it has always been about what he wanted. Everything we have ever done has been for his word, and now you want to make this about me. You have to be fucking kidding me." Braun sat there quietly, staring down at the floor as the thoughts ran through his mind. There were so many things he had wanted to tell Jayce, but none of them could make up for the last few years of murder, deceit, and pain. "I’ve already dug my grave, Jayce." Jayce’s eyes narrow, his face contorted with confusion at Braun’s last few words. "What’s that supposed to mean?" "Exactly what I said, Jayce. You know I’m more careful about the things we have done, and this time would be no different. I’m fully aware about the consequences for my actions." Jayce lifted his head to the ceiling, closing his eyes and sighing aloud. "Then why do this…why take the chance?" "For you." The words stung him like a cobra’s venomous strike, and for a second he almost allowed a tear to escape its imprisonment. He opened his eyes and turned his attention to Braun, who sat there staring at his feet. For the first time, he did not see a brave man arming himself for the war that lie ahead. He saw what most others saw, a feeble old man with nothing left for him but the arms of death themselves. "It has always been about you, Jayce. Everything I ever did, I did for you. From that first dot head that we suffocated with his own turban to that pot bellied beaner that we carved up on the sixteenth of September. This has all been about making this world better for you, Jayce. I expected you to take over after I was gone, cleansing the Earth of the abominations that have laid claim to our World. He stopped, sighing as he shook his head from side to side; flustered. "But now I know that you don’t hear his words, that he feels your resentment and has decided not to bless you with his wisdom. So now I need to end it all." "End what, Russ?" Jayce looked around, waving his arms. "We travel all the damn time, living in piece of shit houses like this to have somewhere private to conduct our business. We’re not even making a damn dent, Russ!" "You’ll never understand it the way I do, Jayce." Jayce could see it in his eyes; Braun believed everything he was saying. For some odd reason, Jayce was starting to believe it too. Jayce threw his arms up in frustration, before pushing himself away from the table so he could stand up. He slowly shook his head from side to side, placing his hands on his hips as he bit on his bottom lip. "It’s always the same thing with you, isn’t it?" The question was enough to draw Braun’s interest, his ears practically shooting up into the air like a Doberman’s. "No matter what’s happening with you, I’ll never understand. How about you try talking to me, instead of always pushing me away… Have you ever thought about that?" Braun did everything he could to keep from making eye contact with Jayce, twiddling his thumbs on the edge of the table as if he was thinking about the right words to say. "No." As soon as the words escaped his lips, he knew he should have thought about it a bit more. ‘No’ just wasn’t going to cut it this time around. "No…" Jayce said in a high-pitched voice, allowing a slight chuckle to escape alongside a sigh. "That’s all you have to say after everything that has gone on in our lives? Why can’t you treat me like an adult for once, because in case you haven’t noticed I’m not a child anymore!" "Don’t you think I know that," Braun’s response was filled with vile intent, striking out like the tail of a scorpion. "I’m the one who’s been here doing what I could to protect you, Jayce… Like a son." Regret struck Jayce like a husband scorn, making him feel as insignificant as a fly on the wall. "And I’m doing the same thing now." Jayce turned to face him, holding back emotions he should not feel… Emotions were weakness, Braun had told him at an early age. "You need to leave now, because it won’t be long before they find out where I am." That is when Jayce realized what exactly Braun was about to do. "You want to get caught?" Braun nodded. "But you’ll be…" Braun held up his hand, silencing Jayce before he could finish his sentence. "Don’t worry about what’s going to happen to me. I’m old, and there is nothing left for them to take away from me…" He looks up at Jayce, into the eyes of his son. "I’ve already lost it all."
