Skeletons in the Closet (Act III): Sturm...

29 November 2009

Date: 2009-11-29 Prologue: The Future, Imperfect The most important thing to remember is that this story is true. The wheels are already in motion; the die has been cast. In just three short days, these people will be in this position. This is the end of the story. * * * * * Jack London Square in Oakland's waterfront district was another of a long line of cookie-cutter, high-end shopping centers; the kind that sprung up whenever a city sought to "rebrand" one of the seedier areas of town. The mall had all the old standbys – a Barnes & Noble Booksellers, a Starbuck's, three more trendy clothing stores than it could possibly need, and a state-of-the-art, nine-screen multiplex with far too many daily showings of The Twilight Saga: New Moon. Teenaged girls traveled places like these in packs, their chatter and the repetitive beeping of their cellphones announcing their presence, like the winds that presage a hurricane. The flock that filed out of the four o'clock showing of the movie hid a pair of wolves whose lies started with the fact that they'd only been in the theater for twenty minutes. The two women hung towards the back of the pack, the blonde flipping open her cellphone and then shutting it again, while her raven-haired companion nodded mutely as a pair of teens gushed out their love for Robert Pattinson. The two had given their names as Meredith and Jessica Gage, insisting the rest call them "Merry" and "Jessie". They were not exactly new to this particular circle, though none of their friends was quite sure when they'd joined – one day, they were just there. Certainly, none of the others knew that not only were they not the Gage sisters, but they were actually mother and daughter, enjoying perhaps the most unusual bonding experience in history. It was no secret Quinn Gregory led a hard life; being the daughter of a professional wrestler was difficult enough, but trying to balance her mother's other extracurricular activities weighed heavily on her mind. There were times where Quinn just wanted to be a normal girl, and over the last few weeks, her quest for normality had led to time spent with Quinn and Alexandra playing at being other people. It was just as crazy as it sounded. "Seriously, I am sick of that movie." Quinn's teeth were clenched in Jessie's smile; unlike her mother, she just played a puffed-up version of herself, a slightly gangly fifteen-going-on-forty-year-old girl with dark hair, sea-green eyes shielded behind wide, cat's-eye glasses. "How many times have we seen it?" Her mother hid her smile in a turn of her head. "You didn't even make it through the first time," she explained. "Meredith" was a few years older than her sister, with blue-gray eyes that were the color of a cloudy sky and a spray of wheat-blonde hair. Usually, Meredith did the talking for the pair – Alex Pierce's abilities as a social chameleon were without peer – and she'd secured the Gage sisters' place in the clique with a purchase of wine coolers the very first night. "Hey, I had a stomach ache." They hung back further as the other girls bustled ahead. "Which may or may not have been caused by Kristen Stewart." She slid her eyes to the side, lips turning downward as her mother glanced down to the phone in her palm. "She'll call, don't worry." "I know she will." Pierce's voice was always quiet, but it lacked its telltale iron. She lifted limpid blue eyes that weren't hers to her daughter. "Are you okay with this?" "I'm pretending to be some random girl while my mom shaves fifteen years off to be my sister." Quinn grinned. "How do you think I feel?" She slid a hand down the other woman's arm, taking her mother's hand in her own. Alex drew her lower lip into her smile. "Bet Amy's mad." "Not mad – worried." Quinn's own smile was softer than her mother's, proud and strong. "We all are." Pierce touched the heel of her hand to her cheek. "I hate these new contacts." It was a lie – the tears came from another source entirely – but it was one her daughter would allow. Quinn turned her saddest smile towards the pack of teenagers. "Uhm, guys?" she called. "Merry's not feeling so well... is it okay if we catch up?" The leader of the girls – a tall, willowy blonde named Rebecca – scrunched her brows together in concern. "Is she okay?" "Yeah." Consciously or not, Quinn mimicked one of her mother's nervous habits, tucking a lock of raven-black hair behind one ear. She looked over her shoulder. "I think she ate some bad clams, you know? Like, she's been way sick and stuff." "Ah." Rebecca shifted from foot to foot. "We'll be at Starbuck's if she's feeling better." "Great, thanks." Behind Quinn, Lady GaGa's "Bad Romance" issued in shrill tones from the pink holster at Alex's waist, and the girl took a deep breath as she turned back to her mom, who hooked the simple flip-phone to her ear. "Hi," Alex/Meredith trilled. Her fake, plastic smile wavered at the voice on the other side of the line. "No, I don't think she's too young for this. I mean, she'll be sixteen next month, you know?" "Is that Amy?" Quinn asked, stepping to her mother's side. Pierce nodded, putting up a "one sec" finger. "The show's already started," she said. "And I told you, I am not going to do that." A long, exasperated sigh. "Fine, you can talk with her." Alexandra lowered the phone, her affected tone (one part Valley Girl, one part bored teenager) never wavering. "Talk some sense into her, will you? I have to use the little girl's room." Quinn pasted on a smile as she lifted the phone to her ear. "Hello?" she asked. "Hi." She crossed to the concessions, watching her mother duck into the women's room. "No, I'm sure I want to do this. I trust her." The girl's smile was small. "I will, trust me." She looked over her shoulder, shaking her head in mute amazement – "Meredith" even walked differently than her mother. "She's back, so I'm going to let you go. See you in a few." "Everything cool?" Alexandra asked, accepting the phone back. "As cool as it's going to get." Quinn shrugged, linking arms with the other woman. They were back to being "sisters," and she knew it should feel weird (and it did), but it somehow also felt... right. The two pushed out through the double doors of the theater, those ridiculous, oversized shades slipping back over the cloudy blue contacts that hid Alex's real eyes. A trio of black and white squad cars blocked the gates, red-and-blue lights twirling. "On the ground now!" It was a woman's voice – strong, powerful, and proud. Helena Maddox's tone brooked no disagreement, especially with the power of a bullhorn behind it. Quinn looked to the woman at her side, her eyes wide. "Mom...?" She couldn't help the question. "It's okay, Quinny," she whispered. Meredith's lilt slipped behind her words again. "Uhh, officer? I think you've, like, way made a mistake here..." Broad men in dark blue uniforms rushed the scene. "Get on your knees on the ground, now!" Maddox shouted. A policeman scooped an arm around Quinn Gregory's waist, lifting the girl off the ground. Quinn reached for her mother, legs kicking. Her voice just added to the tumult. "Mom! Mom, no!" "Don't hurt her!" The fake teenager was gone, but Alex couldn't recapture the steel, at least not all the way. Pierce dropped to her knees slowly, both hands in the air. "I surrender! Do you hear me? I surrender!" Burly officers came to either side of the woman, her arms wrenched behind her back. The cuffs bit into the flesh at her wrists. There was no fight, no resistance, no excuse given for Helena Maddox to use the firearm at her side, its holster unsnapped. "Alexandra Pierce," Maddox intoned. "You are under arrest for the murder of Lauren Fox. You have the right to remain silent; anything you say can and will be used against you in a court of law..." "Don't make her watch." Alex Pierce's gaze was pointed at the ground. "Don't make her watch this." "You have the right to speak with an attorney and have them present while you are being questioned." Helena wouldn't mess this up by not reading the woman her rights. She did nod to Bernie Dixon, and he and the man who'd moved her escorted Quinn out of sight. They dragged Alexandra Pierce to her feet, escorting her to a patrol car as Maddox read from the card all officers carried with them. "If you cannot afford to hire an attorney, one will be appointed to represent you before any questioning if you so wish. You can decide at any time to exercise these rights and not answer any further questions or make any additional statements." The strong hand of Perry Mathis pushed Pierce into the back of the squad car. "Do you understand these rights as I have explained them to you?" Maddox asked. "Having these rights in mind, do you wish to talk to us now?" "I didn't kill Lauren." Pierce stared straight ahead. "I did not do this. Please. Make sure that—" Maddox cut her off with the slamming of the car door. Scene 10: The Diner Sixty-five hours earlier. Let's call this "Day One". 12:03 AM. The tiny bell above the door didn't ring when Detective Helena Maddox pushed into the diner, its clapper long since missing. Maddox huddled even further into her jacket, ducking her head as she looked over the sparse midnight crowd. The diner (it didn't have a name beyond "Diner") was the very definition of a greasy spoon, its green vinyl benches cracked and peeling. One of the fluorescent lights flickered and died even as a bored-looking woman of indeterminate age gestured towards an empty booth. Maddox grimaced as the bench crackled when she sat down; she wouldn't risk putting her hand down to move. The faux-marble tabletop was pockmarked with war wounds, and the employees shuffled around, their shoulders bowed as if they carried some unseen weight. The menu hadn't changed since the late '70's; still, Helena didn't feel comfortable ordering more than a two-eggs-and-bacon platter, and even that was sketchy. The woman was a trained police detective, a whiz when it came to putting together cases against some of the worst crimes the Bay Area had ever seen, but she still felt small and out of place in a truck stop diner. More than once, she wondered why she'd come. More than once, she questioned her decision to place the call that had led her here of all places. More than once, she considered just sliding out of the bench, jamming on her well-worn Oakland A's cap, and getting the hell out of there. "Coffee?" the waitress mumbled when she spoke – which was rare enough. The pot in her hand was filled with a liquid the consistency of mud. "No, thanks." Helena hoped she'd managed to avoid wrinkling her nose. "I'll be fine." The waitress shrugged disconsolately, shuffling off to the next occupied table to repeat the same inflectionless mutter. Maddox pulled the business card out from the inside pocket of her coat, flicking her thumb across its edge nervously. Minutes went by – Helena didn't know how many – while she waited for... she wasn't sure what she waited for. In the end, she was waiting for him. "You don't look like you want to be here." The voice was just as quiet as the serving girl's, but there was a tinge of humor in the words. Maddox's eyes snapped up to the man – she hadn't heard him approach, hadn't heard the discordant jangle of the clapper-less bell, but the man she knew only as Jonas stood at the head of the table. "May I sit?" His voice was neutral, quiet. He might have been asking about the weather. "Of course." Detective Maddox had only met him once – the night they found the body of Lauren Fox floating in a hot tub – but the same thing struck her now that had then: he was plain. Jonas' dirty blond hair was neatly combed, his smile a flat, unimpressive line, and his cornflower blue eyes were entirely... ordinary. "I didn't think you were actually going to come," Maddox said. "I didn't think you would ever call." Jonas shook off the waitress with a negligible shake of his head. "You seemed like you were making excellent progress." "Really? Because I'm right back where I was when I started – a whole lot of nothing." Maddox's left hand tightened into a ball on the table. "I feel like I'm trying to solve a jigsaw puzzle with half the pieces facedown." "Which is what led to your call to me." There was no judgment in his words – in fact, Maddox couldn't read him at all. Most people had a tell, something that she could pick up on, a hint of what they were thinking. Jonas' face was neutral, a blank canvas. Helena might as well be looking at a statue. Helena tossed the mangled business card onto the table between them. "I didn't want to. I've tried to throw that thing away a hundred times." "But you didn't." Just the tiniest raise of his eyebrow. "You know something. Hopefully, at least." Her index finger idly spun the card on the table. "I have a professional wrestler who was on television eight hours earlier in Arizona who somehow gets from Tucson to Oakland, dresses up like a schoolgirl, and ends up at some model home just in time to die in the hot tub." She blew out a sigh. "The only person with cause to want her dead was also in Tucson for the same event, and she has an alibi from her girlfriend, who happens to be my neighbor." The detective squinted one eye shut, peering at the man across the table with the other. It was a surprisingly girlish expression. "Somehow that's supposed to make sense. I mean... I don't know what I mean. What am I missing? There's a piece to this puzzle that is just... not there." "And you think I have that piece." "I'm pretty sure of it. I did some checking on you." Helena sat back in the bench, much more relaxed now that she's been called upon to prove her thesis. "The feds don't like to talk about it, but I found a half-dozen reports with a reference to a source named 'Jonas', albeit also with a half-dozen different surnames. Everyone I've interviewed describes you as kind of boring, but knowledgeable, and I have no doubt that you do know the answer to this puzzle." "Then why are we not at the station, where you can ask me questions under threat of prosecution?" A scrawny busboy came by, filling the man's glass with ice water without asking. Jonas did not touch the glass. "Because I don't think that would work with you, so I let you pick." She half-shrugged, the conversation dying for the moment it took for the waitress to set down her plate of runny eggs and limp bacon. "Ugh, why did I bother?" she muttered. "Forgive the choice of meeting places, Detective. Truth is, I wanted to see if you would come this far out of your way." "Is this a game to you, Mr. Jonas?" Maddox's brows beetled, her eyes lifting from the nauseating meal to the man across from her. "A young woman lost her life." Something passed through the man's gaze for a moment – anger, perhaps. It was the first glimmer of emotion he'd shown. "A girl I knew far better than you did, Detective, so spare me the guilt trip." "If you knew her, why aren't you helping me?" Helena leaned across the table, her voice a desperate hiss. "If you have information that can help me here..." "If I did have this information, it would change everything for you, Detective." "I need to change everything if I'm going to solve this." "Yes, but it may not be a change you want. The people you are dealing with... the people involved in this are not the type of people you can un-learn about." He folded his hands on the table. "If I give you this information, there will be some of them who will be unhappy, and who will not be swayed from venting that frustration just because you have a badge." Maddox dared to cut into her eggs, but the watery run-off of the yolk forced her to turn up her nose again. "You're saying they might try to kill me." Her smile was small, wry. "Just so I'm clear." "You would not be the first." He didn't move or flinch, a small smile tugging at the corners of his lips. Helena wondered what it would take to ruffle the man. Suddenly, the rundown, seedy diner was the last thing on her mind. Her fork dangled from her fingers, forgotten. "Will the information you have help me find out that did this?" she asked quietly. "In all likelihood, yes." Helena forced herself to take a forkful of the eggs. They tasted flat, boring – not rancid, but not good either. "Then what are you waiting for?" She wished she could say that brashly, like they do in the movies. She imagined herself staring at him steely-eyed, swallowing down the entirely unimpressive meal. Instead, it came out with a squeak, the pitch rising at the end. Still, it drew a smile from the man across from her. "You remind me of a girl I used to know," he said, off-handedly. The manila envelope he pulled from his inside pocket had seen better days; it was the type that was held shut with a winding string. A large "X" had been marked through one side of it in thick black ink. Jonas set the envelope down on the tabletop, but didn't move his hand away. "Think very carefully about what you are doing here, Helena." His words were kindly, and she didn't quite know why. "I know you think I am being too ominous, that I'm doing this because I like to be mysterious – and I do, believe me. But I am also being serious. Is your life worth the contents of this envelope?" "The truth is worth any consequence," she said, reaching for it. It took a second for the man to lift his hand, and she tugged it across to her side, flipping the thing over to unwind the thread. He slid up out of the booth, turning away as Maddox paged through the contents. She looked up sharply, both brows lifting. "You're not staying?" "I have other appointments to keep." "After midnight on a Tuesday?" Jonas' smile was just as banal as the rest of him. "Not everyone keeps to banker hours," he said smoothly. "You of all people should know that." "What if I have questions about what's in here?" "I have no doubt that you will." The man turned, his long leather duster sweeping about his ankles. "We will speak again. Try not to die in the interim." The bell above the door didn't make a sound as the man left, and suddenly, Helena felt the weight of eyes upon her. She dropped a twenty-dollar bill onto the table – more than enough for the cheap food she hadn't finished – and hugged the envelope against her chest as she slid to her feet. Maddox didn't make eye contact with anyone else in the diner as she hurried after the man. "Wait," she called. "Just... wait a damn minute." Jonas was nearly to his silver Lexus, cellphone in hand. He turned over his shoulder, the yellow-orange streetlight casting long shadows across his face. "Yes, Detective? Do you have a question already?" "Why are you doing this?" she asked, crossing the distance. "What's in it for you? If this is as dangerous for me to have as you seem to think, why would you even give it to me? If they come after me, they'll come after you, too, won't they?" "They might." He pulled open the driver's side door, a motion that conveniently hid his face from the woman. "Then why take that risk? And why me? I'm just a local detective." "You are far more than you think you are, Miss Maddox." He turned back, and his smile was as sudden as it was charming. "I'm giving you this chance because Lauren would have wanted someone to try to solve it. And because I remember the day that I met her, and I remember another girl that I helped break." Jonas didn't say anything else – no further explanation, nor words of encouragement. He just slid behind the wheel of his car, pressing the "dial" button on his phone as he slammed the door shut. The person on the other end of the line didn't say, "Hello" when they answered, but Jonas knew it connected. "It's done," he said. "Good," was the only reply he would be given. As a light rain began to fall, Helena Maddox was left with beaten envelope clutched to her chest and a hundred questions. She just hoped that there were actually answers held within. As he drove away, Jonas Stryker just hoped he hadn't gotten Gwen's sister killed. Scene 11: The Doctor Day One, 9:11 AM. It was the relentless buzzing of her cellphone that stirred Alexandra Pierce back to consciousness. She blinked hard, jerking the steering wheel to force the car straight, but the pickup she'd absconded with less than twenty-four hours earlier wasn't even running. Pierce rubbed the heels of her hands against her eyes, trying to get her bearings. She leaned out the window, ducking her head just enough to catch a glimpse of a sign – the McDonald's golden arches – and her brows bunched together in concern and confusion. It was the third time in recent memory that Alex awoke in a strange place with no idea how she'd gotten there, but this was somehow worse than the first two. She came to in a fog, just as she had in the past, but this time, it came with a crippling headache, forcing her to squint from one eye. Dimly, Alex recalled her phone vibrating – which meant it had been ringing. She fumbled for the door handle as she picked the phone up from the seat beside her. "Amy," the face of the phone read. "Missed call (3). Voicemail (3)" Pierce cursed softly under her breath, retrieving a knit shoulder bag from the foot well as she half-climbed and half-fell from the truck. She didn't figure she had much time – even if the Good Samaritan she'd assaulted at roadside to obtain the vehicle hadn't regained consciousness, it was a well-traveled thoroughfare, and someone was sure to find him before long. His stolen truck was too easy to identify, with a pair of steel testicles hanging from the trailer hitch and a "BOOMDOGY" vanity plate, and if she'd been in this parking lot for any length of time, she was ripe for discovery. That was assuming there was no little birdie to point them in the right direction – an idea proving more and more likely, given the timing of these episodes. Even in her uncertain state, Pierce still had the wherewithal to wipe down the steering wheel and the console – she wasn't so concerned about hair fibers, since she was wearing a dishwater blond wig, but removing traces of her presence was almost second nature for her. Sure enough, as Alexandra meandered towards the Wal-Mart the fast food restaurant stood in front of like a sentinel, a black and white squad car rolled into the parking lot. Alex ducked her head as she stepped through the automatic door, wadding up her pilfered ball cap and burying it in the trashcan. The Spider snagged a blue carrying basket, quick strides carrying her deeper into the store. Alex made for the ladies' section, plucking a flowered sundress off the clearance rack. The dress was a size too big for her, but that suited her purposes perfectly. She coupled the dress with a sky blue, button-up sweater, a padded bra a cup size off, a set of plastic barrettes, and a pair of reading glasses just a smidge too wide for her face. The entire trip cost her less than forty dollars, and a stopover in the restroom left the blonde who'd come in behind, like a snake shedding its skin. A mousy, bespectacled redhead (Pierce decided her name was "Annie") shuffled past the uniformed officers meandering into the store, her phone pressed to her ear. "Billy," she implored. "Bill, don't do this to me..." Alex stepped sideways through the cops with a murmured apology, lingering just long enough near the doorway to verify the police were not looking for her by name. Then she hurried to the bus stop, just in time to catch the route 55 bus down Davis Street. A quick check of Google Maps (and the assistance of a kind stranger aboard the bus) led Alex to the offices of Dr. Scott J. Petersen. She tossed her Wal-Mart bag (containing all that remained of the other woman she'd been) into a dumpster and strode up to the glass door. "Walk-ins welcome," the sign proclaimed proudly. Alexandra Pierce managed to restrain her smile as she shuffled through. * * * * * "Annie Moskowitz?" Stacey Hudson was bone-tired, barely able to keep her eyes open. She hated the early morning shift with a passion she couldn't properly express, but her car payment was so high she couldn't afford to be picky, and she needed the extra shifts. That didn't mean she could stifle her yawn as she waited for the next patient. The girl stood timidly, gathering purse and sweater as she stepped up to meet Stacey. "That's... ah, that's me," Annie stammered, pushing her eyeglasses up her nose – probably a nervous habit. Stacey thought the girl might be kind of pretty with a bit of self-confidence and better fashion sense. Annie glanced uncertainly over her shoulder, wetting dry lips as she followed Stacey into the office and to the scale. "And how are we today?" Stacey asked with a small, put-on smile. It was a meaningless question, designed wholly to make a patient feel more comfortable – she had to ask it, but most of the time, she wasn't even listening to the answer. Anything that was truly wrong would be on the chart, anyway. Something about the girl's hesitation drew Stacey's eyes up, however. Annie's brows were bunched, lower lip quivering just a little. She seemed paralyzed by the question, ashamed to even attempt a response. "Uhh... not really, ah... not really g-good," was what she settled on, punctuating her answer with a meek smile. Stacey patted the frightened woman on the forearm comfortingly. "Let's get you over here into room number three, and Dr. Petersen will fix you right up, okay?" This was the part of her job that Stacey hated the most: babying sick people. It was like a simple case of the sniffles was debilitating, and all they wanted was their mommy. In short, she wanted to give them House, but they wanted Scrubs. "Just have a seat over there on the table, and the doctor will be in to see you shortly, okay?" Stacey was already turning – she didn't see the other woman nod, fingers touching her hair. She didn't hear the phone vibrate in the girl's bag, either. * * * * * Amy – Missed Call (4) Voicemail (4) * * * * * If this were the 1980s, Dr. Scott Petersen would almost certainly be called a yuppie. The doctor was young – just over thirty – and he still dressed like a businessman more than a physician, in pleated slacks and a bronze, button-down dress shirt. He eschewed even the lab coat, breezing into the exam room with an easy smile. "Well, hello there," Dr. Petersen said, taking a moment to glance at the chart in his hand. "I'm Dr. Petersen, and you must be Annie?" The girl managed a small nod, slender fingers combing through her hair. "Yeah," she breathed, swallowing hard. "What seems to be the problem today, Annie?" "I..." The girl started and stopped, her eyes dropping down to the floor. "I think... I think I might have been, ah..." Annie took a deep, bracing breath. "I think I may have been drugged." She kept her gaze low, hands knotted in her lap. Dr. Petersen's brows lifted. "That's pretty serious," he said. "Have you contacted the police?" "No." The question drew her eyes up. "I didn't... didn't want to bother them unless I was sure." The man crossed his legs, left ankle on right knee. "Well, what makes you think you might have been drugged?" "I was... I like to drink, uh... you know, on the weekends and stuff." Annie's cheeks colored with shame. "Nothing... uh, nothing hard, but I like to cut loose a little, y'know... sometimes. Not all the time, but..." "I think we all do." His smile was the very picture of good bedside manner, just the way he'd learned it in medical school. "Uhm, gosh this is embarrassing," she muttered. "It's okay," Petersen assured her. "Take your time." "This morning, I woke up in a bed I don't remember falling asleep in, and... and I think I—" Tears shined in her eyes. "Uh... can I... is there just some test you can run or something?" He let that calm, comforting grin slip across his lips again. "Tell you what I'm going to do. I'm going to go get Stacey. She'll get a urine sample from you, and we'll take some blood, too, so we can run some tests." "Oh, thank you, Doctor..." Annie said breathlessly, but Petersen raised a palm. "Now, if we do find anything, you know we have an obligation to tell the police, right?" Her head bobbed in a nod. "I understand." "Great." Dr. Petersen stood smoothly, again favoring her with that smile. "She'll be right in." "Thank you again." Annie lifted her chin, smiling softly as the doctor slipped out of the room. "We are going to solve the police problem, once and for all," she said quietly, rust-red hair hanging down in her face. "I'll be upstairs." The Spider did not wait for an answer – not that her team would give her one – as she crept slowly up the steps and disappeared into the master bedroom. "She gonna be okay, y'think?" Ashe didn't look away from the game this time. "She has not been 'okay' for some time, Roderick." Hawke watched the staircase for some time, until his partner's words drew his eyes away. "Y'said somethin' about spaghetti, right?" Scene 14: The Rock Day One, 7:19 PM. "How many times do I have to tell you not to touch the fucking radio?" Amy Campbell asked as they pulled into the driveway. Amy leaned forward, peering up at the plain, sky-blue house. "You're sure this is the place?" "As sure as I have been all day." Drusilla Devonshire shrugged as she unfastened her seatbelt. "Which's been pretty damn uncertain." Donnie Bell fidgeted in backseat of the Cadillac, frown etched onto his features. It was the third such place the trio had visited this afternoon. The first two – in the back office of a pawn shop and a motel room near the San Francisco Airport – had proven empty, and Campbell was beginning to sour on even turning up Alex Pierce's lackeys, nevermind finding the woman herself. "They're not called safe houses because they're easy to find, you know." Devonshire unzipped the front of her jacket, meandering towards the door. "Just let me do the talking." "Because that worked so well at the pawn shop?" Bell hung back away from the two redheads, as he tended to do, one hand jammed fitfully into his pocket. "That was not my fault." Dru grinned for Bell, even tossing in a wink. "If you guys had kept me up to date on the passphrases, we wouldn't be in this situation." "God, will you two just fuck and get it over with?" Amy muttered bitterly; three hours in a car with these two was taking its toll on her. She gestured up the path. "Well, go on." Devonshire adjusted the collar of her jacket, summoning up a smile as she knocked briskly on the door. She cleared her throat gently, beginning her spiel the moment the door opened. "Hello, sir," she said. "Have you ever—" "Druey?" Roderick Ashe squinted down at the slight redhead. Maybe Devonshire could have talked her way past the big man, but the other small redhead plowed past her to level an accusatory finger in Roderick's face. "She can fuck off now, it's you I need." She sneered. "We're going to find Alex, and if we don't—" "She's up—" "Roderick." The sharp, curt interruption came from the thin man with the cane stepping from the kitchen. "Miss Campbell, good evening." He flicked his hooded gaze past Amy to the other two. "I see you brought the riffraff with you." "Hey!" Donnie Bell might have been in the back, but his objection rang the loudest. "I ain't bloody riffraff, buddy!" "You were tossed into the dumpster the second you told me you don't mind her." Amy rolled her eyes at Bell, then turned to Hawke. "What was Roderick saying? I haven't been able to get in touch with Alex..." Ashe didn't respond immediately; he'd long since learned it was usually best let Elijah do the talking. "She..." Hawke said carefully. "I am given to understand Director Pierce is investigating the murder of Miss Fox. I will certainly—" But sometimes, Ashe couldn't help himself. "She's upstairs," he said, half-turning away. "Roderick." The second interruption was even more forceful. "What?" Ashe turned something of a glare on Hawke. The expression softened as he met Campbell's mismatched gaze. "She's up there, 'less she snuck out the fuggin' window. She ain't in real good shape inna head, but—" "Where upstairs? And Eli, you lie like that again and..." Amy was shaking, trying to control wild nerves. "Why the fuck wouldn't you just tell me? I'm going up." She started off, but paused to level a finger at the others. "You stay right there, or I'll bribe Ashe to start breaking legs." The big man folded his arms, his cigar sticking out of his grin. "I come pretty cheap, too." Hawke pursed his lips, but he did not move to block the smaller woman. "She asked for solace..." He raised his head, meeting the eyes of his partner, and in that moment, he relented. "Go," he whispered hoarsely. "The master bedroom. End of the hall." Amy didn't have to be told twice, taking the steps two at a time and racing down the hallway. The door to the bedroom wasn't locked, but it didn't matter – Campbell hit it with such force, she threatened to take it off the hinges. "Al?" Within, Alexandra Pierce sat on a chair looking out the window, her lithe form covered in a thin, wispy robe. A half-empty bottle of whiskey sat on the small table beside her; beside it was her pearl-handled pistol, alongside a fresh, unopened box of bullets. She didn't look up from her faraway stare, the well-worn black hair-tie she nearly always wore – the first thing Amy Campbell gave her – stretched out in her left hand thoughtfully, as if she was preparing to play cat's in the cradle Very briefly, Campbell came up short – this wasn't what she expected, not that she could put into words what she was. Relief washed away her doubt as she rushed across the room to wrap her arms around Alex, face buried in the other woman's neck. "I thought... I thought..." "I know," Alex said quietly. One hand slipped across Amy's back; Pierce closed her eyes at this touch, a tear slipping out of her eye. "I know." It took a moment of searching for the words, and delivering tiny, worried kisses along Pierce's shoulder before Amy spoke again. "What happened?" "It happened again." Alex looked sad, but Amy couldn't quite figure out why. "Twice more since the last time I saw you." "What happened? Don't talk in riddles." Amy could have pulled over another chair, she could have moved to the bed, or could have squatted beside Alex, but any of those things would have meant letting go, and that wasn't happening. She settled for the arm of the chair. "Tell me what happened." "I woke up on the side of the road in... in some rental car in a ditch. I was... I was wearing a blond wig and... I couldn't remember how I got there. I can't remember how I got there. I-I think I was left there to be found by the police. If not for the man who found me..." Alex looked down, but her right hand snakes around Amy, thumb hooking in a belt loop. "There was a birthday card in the trunk. Addressed to me. It was a threat. I took the man's car to the... well, I was going to go home. I woke up the second time several hours later in a McDonald's parking lot, still in the car – but with no memory of the interim. Someone is taunting me, Amy. I hate... I feel so goddamn helpless." She set her jaw, eyes out the window again. "Is this what I do to other people?" "Not anymore." Campbell pressed lips to the side of her head. "These days, you just worry people. Did you... are you okay? I mean, physically and... and why are you drinking? Can you remember hurting anybody?" "I can't remember anything from when I was out." She didn't mention how she got the car in question, and Amy didn't ask. "I don't know what I was doing in that car, I don't know what I was doing in that parking lot, but... they called the police. I feel like it was all staged so the cops could find me. I... I need to solve the police problem if I'm going to find out who did this." "Not right now, you don't," Amy ordered. "What you're doing now is coming home and getting to bed, and staying there for however long you need to be safe. No whiskey, either. I've got my car." "I can't go." The three words were painfully sad; they came out in a single breathless whisper, choked by a sob she wouldn't release. "Of course you can go. It's home. C'mere." "I can't." She shook her head again. "I want to. I just want to lie in bed and forget everything, to say fuck all of it, but... what if it happens again? What if I close my eyes and I wake up somewhere else, as someone else again? Or what if I wake up and I've... I've..." She couldn't finish the thought, she wouldn't say the words. "Not gonna happen." Campbell's grin was sad, she tipped her head and tried to perk it up for Alex. "I'll stay there with you. I won't leave the room. For days or weeks or however long you need, and you do need it. It's a little unconventional, maybe a bit inconvenient, but I could probably tie you to my back and take walks to get a little fresh air for us. You can black out all you want, you'll be safe and my legs will thank you for the extra workout." "I don't want to expose you to this." She reached up, touching the tips of her fingers to Amy's cheek. "You're supposed to be my escape." "Wrong. I'm the soft place to land when escape isn't a solid option." The little redhead still grinned, and it was still a sad thing. "I'll never run away from it with you, because that's not fair to you, but we can walk through it. You can run to me when it starts to hurt, even if it means having to hear me consider strapping you to my back and strolling through Oakland." "You'll stay no matter what?" Alex finally met Amy's eyes, gray on blue-and-brown. "Because I would... I'd really like that. Roderick and Elijah would stand with me, but neither of them is happy about all of this. Kathryn, Drusilla, the rest... I need someone who is here for me. Not because it's their job or because they find it funny or because they feel targeted. But for me." "I told you, I'm here. All I ask is that we don't hide in strange houses – you deserve your own bed, your things, Quinn's ridiculously loud... okay, so I'm the one with the loud videogames, but you see where I'm going. You have a hard enough time being yourself, stupid safe houses and wigs don't make it easier – and you know who I'm with." "Thank you." For a long moment, it was all Alex could manage to say. "I don't know why you put up with me, really." She rose unsteadily, using the table for support. "I will go home with you. I'll rest. Then I'm... I have to face what I have done. I can't hide from it anymore. I have some... whoa." Pierce nearly toppled, but for the quick reflexes of the smaller woman. "I have some ideas. Will you..." She paused, breathed deeply, and sighed out the words desperately. "I need your help, Amy." "Let's not worry about that yet." Amy hoped her easy smile masked her uneasy heart. "We'll go home and get you some rest." Campbell swung around, facing the other woman, who leaned heavily on her. "Promise me you'll get your – fuck, how much have you had to drink?" She turned her face away, nose wrinkling. "Just a... just a li'l bit." Amy had a lecture lined up, it was on the tip of her tongue – she wanted to tell Alex that she hated all of this, that she hated most of them downstairs, that she wanted to hide Alex away, to cut her off from all of it. It was a reprimand she held inside, however. She'd promised to be there for Alex, to be that soft place to come home to, and she wasn't prepared to break her word, no matter how worried she was. "All right." She slid back to Alex's side, holding the woman up. "They're all downstairs, and we're all worried about you. You need to take some time to—" She couldn't hold all of the lecture it in. "Can't." Alex shook her head again. "My fault. Lauren's dead, Donnie's all alone, Drusilla is..." Pierce couldn't see the sudden snarl on Amy's lips. "You're not taking the blame for her. Not for her." "I made her that way. I broke... I broke her. I break everything." "She was broken in the first place, and you did nothing to her." Amy growled, "She's below you – nothing but a stalker, a disgruntled employee, whatever you might call her. Don't even consider her. Donnie's not alone, Drusilla shouldn't be on your mind, and Lauren is dead because... we don't know yet, but I will stand by you while we find out, just don't... don't do this. This self-loathing thing. And drinking? That's not you." Despite herself, Alex laughed, a quiet puff of disgusted noise. "How do you know? I don't even know what's me." Pierce forewent Campbell's grip, leaning against the doorway while the smaller woman wrenched it open. "A couple of hours ago, I attacked a man I respect more than anything else in the world for saying I should go home. I said I didn't want to be treated like a wilting flower. And then you know what I did?" The grin wasn't pleasant, but its fire was directed inward. "I came up here and I got drunk and I cried. And... that's pretty much it. I'm trying to be everything for everyone. Everyone for everyone. They need me to be strong, you need me to be there for you, Quinn needs her mother... all of my balls are up in the air, but I am tired of juggling." Laughter drifted up from downstairs, the distinctly British "BOOM!" from Donnie Bell over it all. Alex squinched her eyes shut. "And now I have to be Desade just to leave the fucking house." "Getting drunk and crying... you're taking pages from my book now." Amy tried to chuckle, wanted that to be a joke, but the realization that things she did everyday could be so painful to watch others doing quickly killed the humor. "Just... don't talk. I'll tell them we're going home. I'll take you. Fuck the rest of them, fuck their expectations. Just try to stay relatively upright while we get to the car, and try to remember that I... well, I love you. Might not be the best time to mention that, I know, but sometimes I think you need to be reminded that you know two people who love you for who you are, not what you can be for them."  The next smile was genuine, and Amy continued. "And the other one is probably still in her bedroom back at the condo, so let's go make sure she knows you're okay." "Okay." When Alexandra slid into Amy's embrace this time, it was more grateful, some of the metaphorical weight coming off her shoulders. Amy showed an impressive grasp of subtlety as she guided the other down the steps. The rest of them had set up shop in the living room, enthralled in the Modern Warfare 2 battle between Bell and Ashe. Even Drusilla – seated on the couch beside Pierce's renegade protégé – failed to notice them leaving, and Amy wasn't about to announce it. Only Elijah Hawke saw them go, tipping an invisible cap to the smaller redhead as the pair slipped away into the night. And so it went, Amy Campbell again the rock that Alexandra Pierce had to lean on, the only one of them without preconceived notions or hopes of gaining something from their relationship. Alex left that night with the roller coaster pulling up to the top of its highest high, a dreamy smile feathering onto her lips as Campbell shut the door and darted to the other side of the car. Pity it wouldn't last. The clock was already ticking, the sand slipping through the hourglass. The car was already on the rails. In less than forty-eight hours, Alexandra Pierce will be in a police car, her dreams all gone to nightmares. Soon, the countdown will hit zero, and it will not pause for anything, not even these two star-crossed lovebirds. That's why this is called a "tragedy". So consider this an intermission.
