A closed mind

22 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-22 As the sun rose to greet another warm day in the desert, Doctor Curiosity awoke from his slumbers. He stretched as he rolled sideways, and one pyjama leg poked out from underneath the sheets. Lazily, he swung his legs from the soft mattress, and dropped down from the brass bed to the floor. "Aaaaiiii!" The doctor yelled; the same as he did every day when stepping on to the cold stone floor of his bedroom. He hopped from one foot to the other quickly, before locating his pair of fluffy bunny slippers, and sliding his feet in to their lush fur. Thus began the morning ritual of Doctor Curiosity. He absent-mindedly pressed a red button on the side of his bed, and walked towards the door that led from the room. As he reached it, a mechanical hand ran along a railing installed in the ceiling, and a recently steamed bathrobe was deposited quickly on to the shoulders of the doctor. As happened every morning, the robe was scalding hot, forcing Curiosity to throw it to the floor, and stamp on it whilst cursing loudly. The other activity caused by the pressing of the red button was about to be seen, as he opened the door from the bedroom, and a toasted bagel hurtled at him at incredible pace. He had to shimmy to the right to catch it one-handed, but he held on to it, and took a bite. The curious doctor was now awake via his unusual morning routine. Unconventional, and maybe foolish… but effective. Words that could be applied to the man himself. ------------------------------------- From an early age, Chris Goodman had shown himself to be an imaginative soul. Never the most popular kid in the class, Chris was one of those that spoke to an invisible friend. It wasn’t any great psychosis or anything special really, just an alien that only ever told things to him. It made him feel special, and feel a part of something more than his own existence. Each day his mind would create new embellishments to the mythos of this imaginary alien, to the point where he had an entire alternate universe created for himself. If he’d been a contestant on Jeopardy, then he’d have wanted “my invisible alien friend” to come up as a category. Fast forward a couple of years, and the alien would be consigned to history. A few people now knew Chris Goodman, but still he’d always have the most fun within the confines of his own imagination. He’d find escapism in the same ways as every other child of twelve, in video games and cartoons. He could convince himself that they were real, but no matter how many times he blamed the spills on the carpets as being done by Mickey Mouse, his family never believed him. Another couple of years on, and Chris had entered his sullen phase. Almost every teenager gets there at some point, when the world is out to get them, and nothing anyone does to them could ever be considered fair. Chris used to throw tantrums when he didn’t get his way, the same as nearly every kid out there. He used to get so worked up in his lies and fantasies that he’d believe them to be the truth. If he shifted the blame for his mistakes on to his sister, Leona, he would swear blind that it was the truth, and within a short time there would be no difference in his mind as to what had happened. When he said the words to himself often enough, they folded his mind in on itself, and the genuine events were locked away. The lie became his truth, and his sullen moods increased. Again, this is not far removed from any other child of that age. The difference is that most children grow out of it. ------------------------------------------------- The doctor had woken up, and his first stop, as usual, was the Curious Laboratory. Despite his feelings of love for Collette, or what his mind believed to be love, he knew that there was also a match to be won. This was not done by sleeping in until late, and so he had to get to work on something slightly more substantial than a Curious Electromagnet. “Where the hell have you been?” queried Eegor’s voice. “It’s almost midday; you must have been sleeping in until late again!” The doctor allowed himself a smile as he tightened the sash around the waist of his bathrobe. So, he’d had a relaxing morning; was that really going to cost him dear against the Muppet Man? The smile stayed on his face as he walked over to his brother’s side. He had motivation to win the match now; he was doing it for his wife, for the woman he loved. Surely something as powerful as love could overcome all the curious gadgets in the world? Doctor Curiosity was shaken from his daydreams by the ringing of the phone. He answered it, with a smile that widened when he heard the voice on the other end. “My darling,” exclaimed the good doctor. “How may I help you?” “You could let me in,” replied Collette. “Vhy must I do zis? You have keys.” “You’ve changed the locks.” “I’ve done vhat?” “You changed the locks. You also put up a sign next to the door saying ‘Go away Collette, we don’t want you here, you slut’… which I don’t think is very nice at all.” The doctor’s smile vanished as his eyes moved over towards his assistant. Eegor cringed and started to edge towards the door the led away from the laboratory. “Eegor, you come here right zis instant!” yelled the doctor, throwing the nearest glass jar at him. The jar exploded upon contact with the stone walls, narrowly missing the agile assistant’s head as he ducked through the portal and down towards the staircase. “Vell, at least let her in vhile you’re down there,” muttered Curiosity, as he sniffed the air. There was a very unpleasant smell coming from the vicinity of the doorway, where the glass jar had smashed. He looked over and saw that it contained some sort of organic matter, be it the eyeballs of a sheep or the legs of some other mammal. The doctor was a squeamish sort, and preferred not to see what kind of strange scientific ephemera that Eegor sometimes ordered. The doctor was all for keeping up appearances in the mad scientist stakes, but some things were best left to the biologists. If only Eegor could understand that… Curiosity’s train of thought was rudely derailed by the return of Eegor, who ushered a clearly distressed Collette in to the room. The doctor grabbed the nearest thing to him, a scientific text book, and hefted it threateningly. Eegor bowed his head and scurried out of the room at pace. “I must apologise for his behaviour, my darling. Vell, you know what zey say, zat it’s so hard to find good help zese days…” “He’s your brother, just ask him nicely,” muttered Collette. “Vhat did you say?” “Nothing, dear.” Curiosity smiled, but his face was rapidly losing its good humour. “So, vhat can I help you vith, oh light of my life?” asked the doctor. Collette was looking unimpressed as she stood there in another business suit. This one was jet black, to match the new colouring in her hair. It was cut short, but managed to look alluring rather than severe. The cut of the suit indicated that she was clearly not buying her clothes at a discount store. This was tailor made for her, and she wore it very well… so well that Doctor Curiosity had to make an effort not to drool as he asked her the question. “I’m here because you told me I should come over today.” “Jah! Zat vas it! I thought ve should spend some qvality time together!” replied the doctor, rubbing his hands together in an excited fashion. “So… I’m here,” deadpanned Collette, acting for all the world like a bored teenager. Doctor Curiosity was back in heaven. His beautiful wife was by his side, looking even better than ever, and he was confident about his match against Gonzo the Great, or whoever he was going to be facing. Now to marry the two subjects together… “Dear wife of mine, I have had an idea as to vhat could bring us closer together and complete our reconciliation!” Collette took a step back. She had feared that Curiosity would make carnal advances, but she didn’t think he would be so forward so quickly! “I vant you to help me create a device so I can defeat Muppet Man very easily.” Collette breathed a sigh of relief. Her husband was just up to his usual tricks. She wasn’t sure exactly what the doctor had in mind, but it would doubtless be stupid and ineffective. “So, what ideas have you got?” she asked. “Vell, I have so many ideas zat I could not only make von device for ze slaying of ze Muppet Man. I have made two curious inventions, but need help combining them to create a contraption of such awesome power zat ze Man of Muppet vill be powerless to stop it!” The excitement burned in the eyes of the doctor. Whilst managing to look unfocused and lackadaisical, he had still found the time for some new creations. His public face, his façade, his mask… they were not everything that Doctor Curiosity had. Hidden depths lurked under the skin of the man who had deposited George English, Jade and Alex Grey in to the bin marked ‘losers’. Curiosity almost skipped towards the two bulky looking shapes in the corner of the room. Both were, in accordance with tradition, covered with large white sheets. The doctor bowed deeply as he whisked the sheet of the first of the shapes, not losing his sense of drama even at a time like that. “Behold, ze Curious Disney Device!” The latest invention of Doctor Curiosity was unveiled to rather less impressive gasps than he had been hoping for. It looked very much like the Curious Electromagnet, and that was mostly because it was, in fact, the Curious Electromagnet. “What the hell is that?” asked Collette. The doctor added the finishing touches, by replacing the rubber chickens with various stuffed toys of characters featured in Disney movies. He stood back and looked at Collette, desperate to see if she liked it. Her words were not to his liking. “Vhat do you mean? It is a Curious Disney Device! I have meticulously researched ze fortunes of Ze Muppet Show and ze movies associated vith it, and have come to ze conclusion zat Disney has generally beaten zem in every aspect of cute storytelling.” “So what?” replied Collette. “So vhat? So vhen ze Muppet Man sees his most vicious competitors, ze company zat has usurped ze popular position of his beloved Muppets, he vill fear me, as vell as breaking down in tears, heartbroken beyond the comprehension of mortal man. You like ze idea, jah?” “Well…” said Collette, hesitating. She didn’t have a clue what her husband was ranting on about, but if she was too scathing then he might not be so cooperative. “It’s certainly interesting… but I’m not sure that you opponent is actually a man who worships The Muppet Show.” “Vhat? Oh, you mean he might actually be a Muppet? Some strange furry puppet brought to life by some arcane ritual, probably involving candles, darkness and a lot of strange chanting in Latin?” “No, I just think it’s a name. Your device is… well… fantastic, but…” “A name? Vhy vould someone call themselves by a name if it is not true?” queried the doctor. Collette shrugged. “You’re called Doctor Curiosity, but you’re not curious about everything, are you?” “Jah! Curiosity is vhat drives me, voman!” Collette rolled her eyes. To her, the good doctor was not named for curiosity as a desire to learn, but for curiosity as a word applied to the strange. No matter what he said, Collette was sure that the doctor was not driven by curiosity. She wasn’t sure quite what drove him at all, but she was hoping that misplaced love would be enough to take him to the glorious end of the wrestling tournament. “Some people aren’t like you, Chris,” she said, her use of the doctor’s real name jarring him, as it always did. “Muppet Man is just named as he is because… well, he’s… he wears… actually I don’t know.” “You see, he is a devoted fan of ze Muppets!” “No, Chris! I don’t know why he’s named that, but your theory is stu… is implausible.” “Vell, maybe he’s a Muppet taken on human form, like you said earlier!” replied the doctor, his enthusiasm for his inventions and theories refusing to be dampened by Collette’s skepticism. He scooted over to the next white sheet, which loomed over a slightly smaller object. He removed the sheet with another flourish, and revealed something that looked extremely mean. “Behold, ze Curious Flamethrower!” Collette actually smiled. “You see, my meticulous research also reminded me zat Ze Muppet Show was full of creatures made of cloth. So, if Muppet Man really is a man transfigured from a Muppet, zen I vill fight him vith ze von thing furry, cloth-based life forms fear ze most… fire!” Collette chuckled despite herself as she looked closer at the flamethrower. For once, it did indeed appear that Doctor Curiosity had created something that might live up to its name, and be useful. Collette wasn’t sure how he intended to get away with using a huge flamethrower on wheels in a wrestling match, but that was a problem for Curiosity to deal with later on. “Vhat I need to do, my dear, sweet Collette, is combine zese two machines. Ze Curious Disney Device can attract ze Muppet Man using ze power of magnetism. If vill zen frighten him vith images of Disney characters, such as ze Little Mermaid, Snow Vhite and Shrek…” “Shrek wasn’t Disney.” “Vhatever… it looks cute and vill probably scare him anyway,” replied the doctor. “Zen if ve can combine it vith ze flamethrower, it vill set him ablaze, and I vill win! It is very hard to wrestle a match vhen you are on fire.” Collette was unsure about it all. She was staring at a huge lump of metal, with cuddly toys attached to it, and at a large flamethrower… and this was supposed to make sense? “Now, darling, if you could move ze flamethrower over here tovards ze Curious Disney Device, zat vould be great. Be careful not to press ze button, my dear.” “Which button?” asked Collette. “Zis von,” replied the doctor, pressing a red button set on the side of the Curious Flamethrower. As he did so, there was a rushing sound of air becoming very hot very quickly, and of a flame becoming lit. Curiosity looked round to see the flamethrower spew forth a gout of fiery liquid, which for the most part sailed over Collette’s shoulder and set fire to the laboratory workbench. Sadly, not all of it missed Collette, some hitting her on the head; or more accurately, on her hair. Collette screamed, as the doctor turned off the flamethrower and dashed over to help her. Curiosity’s looked panicked, but also as if he was on the verge of bursting in to laughter, as he took off his lab coat and used it to extinguish the flames on Collette’s head. She slumped to the ground, wailing still, and gingerly moving her hands to see how much of her hair had escaped damage; the answer being ‘not a lot’. The noise alerted Eegor, who rushed in with a box of matches and a cigarette lighter. “Put zose avay, Eegor! Fetch some vater!” The assistant reluctantly placed the matches and lighter into his pocket, upset that the doctor had not come to his senses and tried to burn his wife in the manner of a witch in Medieval England. He scurried off, as the doctor tried to sooth Collette. “I am so sorry, my sveet Collette. Zat vas never meant to happen… though it does prove ze effectiveness of ze Curious Disney Flamethrowing Device!” Collette had to restrain herself from reaching over and trying to kill her husband, but he was no use to her dead… at least not with Eegor returning to the room to act as a witness. The assistant, today not looking particularly deformed (but he was wearing briefs made of sack-cloth to make sure he was feeling suitably remorseful) was carrying with him a small test tube containing water. He presented it to the doctor. “Vhat ze hell is zis?” yelled Curiosity. “Water,” replied Eegor, with a stoic look on his face. “I meant in a bucket! How am I going to save Collette vith only a test tube full of vater?” “Then don’t save her! Let her hair continue to burn. Let her smoulder, and walk around looking ridiculous. Maybe it’s her turn to be laughed at instead of me?” “Is zat it, Eegor? Is it just jealousy? Do you tzink zat I vill ignore you now ze voman I love is back in my life? Do you tzink zat you vill be left at the side, an onlooker as ve ride off happily into ze sunset? Are you zat jealous?” ranted Curiosity. “No! It’s not jealousy at all. I’m trying to protect you, brother. Listen to me; she does not want good things for you at all!” “I am actually still here,” remarked Collette, from the floor of the laboratory. “Quiet!” exclaimed both of the Goodman brothers at once. Doctor Curiosity stood still, and looked in to the eyes of his elder brother. He thought long and hard about his next course of action. Eegor hoped and prayed that his words had sunk in. To him it seemed as if the doctor was fighting against his own mind. Maybe he believed deep down that Eegor was telling the truth and that Collette was manipulating him? Maybe he believed it, but the allure of the fantasy, the allure of the lie, the allure of the imaginary friend was too much for him to resist. The delusions that he’d never grown out of, could he break their shackles now? Eegor looked at his brother’s eyes and prayed that he was thinking those thoughts, and choosing to live in the real world. Whatever Doctor Curiosity was thinking, his eyes betrayed nothing. The mask he chose to wear, the persona he chose to adopt, it gave away nothing. The doctor sighed and moved over to his brother, placing one hand on each of his shoulders. “Eegor, I’m sorry. I have been a fool.” Elation danced through Eegor’s stomach as Curiosity put an arm round him and walked him towards the still blazing workbench. Shortly before reaching the bench, the doctor let go of his brother, but continued to walk to the desk. Eegor was smiling as Doctor Curiosity’s sleeve brushed against part of the bench that was still unharmed by the conflagration. The smile turned to horror as he realised where he was standing. The doctor slammed his hand down on one of the buttons set in to the side of the bench, and the floor gave way beneath Eegor. The Curious Trapdoor had claimed another victim. As Eegor flew downwards, cursing, he could just about hear Curiosity speak softly. “I truly am sorry, brother.” -------------------------------------------- A figure shot out of the wall of the Curious Laboratory, and flew in to the moat that surrounded the gothic residence of Doctor Curiosity. Amidst muttered curses, the figure, better known as Eegor, climbed out of the water and stood on the wrong side of the moat. In the windows above him, he saw the reflected flickering light of the blazing workbench, snuffed out as the doctor found a proper fire extinguisher. Somewhere up there, Doctor Curiosity was turning his back on the real world, surely making a conscious decision to disbelieve the truth, wrapping himself up in a security blanket of lies. Eegor looked up at the window, four stories above him, and at the stone walls and moat that separated him from his home, and from his duty. He was going to have to get back in to the laboratory one way or another. Collette could not be allowed to win.
