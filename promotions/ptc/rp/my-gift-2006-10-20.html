My Gift

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-20 The thing you hear most about in these circles is that wrestling takes away from the family unit. Being on the road constantly, it makes you miss the important moments in the lives of your kids growing up. It makes your wife get on edge. Remember your parents…well…let's say you had (or for that matter have) folks that are overprotective. Intentions good and all, but at the end of the day their constant nagging starts to grate you hardcore. It is the necessity of our sport, sadly. We go on the road and we kill ourselves for the entertainment of others. It doesn't even matter exactly the reason or the cause, because at the end of the day you're still shortening your lifespan and destroying your body with the sheer amount of punishment you take. Wrestling is a contact sport. Every single time you hit the canvas it is akin to a 35 mile an hour car crash. And that is not even getting into the toll your knees and back take from the constant movement and the variety of offenses thrown around. You're a high flyer? You take tremendous punishment on the knees just jumping up and down like a damn jumping bean. Technician? Your knees and back will surely take some punishment, especially since people like to elbow you right in the face to get out a submission hold. There's the suplexes that drop you right on the top of your head that cause your neck to make a clicking noise every time you try and turn. The submission holds that rip apart at your joints that you are simply too prideful to tap out too. And there is the striking. The mixed martial arts stuff is really becoming big, so of course everyone is now taking elements of it and using it. It has gone beyond just chopping. Kicks to the face, elbows to the side of the head. And it isn't like wrestling has the safety measures in place that MMA does. Makes it kind of funny with how the sport has started to transition to more high impact styles that John McCain hasn't pitched a fit about it. But I ain't complaining. I've been doing this since I was about twenty, and I'm going to be twenty eight in January. I knew what I was getting myself into when I dropped out on my wrestling scholarship at Iowa. Hell, if you want to draw upon that than I've been wrestling ever since I could damn well remember. Or care too. Or maybe that's my memory getting hazy. But the point is, if you come into this sport, you damn well know what you're getting into because people are sure to be upfront about it the second you walk into a training session. You get the spiel about how 'Only a small percentage of people who try out actually make it, and then an even fewer portion actually can do this and make a living with it.' How, 'It's gonna be hard, probably the most arduous thing you've ever done.' And then they are sure to tell you 'You're gonna have to toil through and pay your dues.' That essentially means that if you do not already have another solid source of income, you're eating canned tuna fish and rice in bulk at a shitty hole in the dump apartment complex with milk crates as furnature. You survive that and make it, you start making some decent cash. And if you are really good, you start making A LOT of cash. It was four years before I got my first decent payday in the sport of professional wrestling. I used to work an overnight job at a department store, getting down shifted in hours on a basis of which holiday season it was. It's why I enjoyed a lot of what Kanye West rapped about when he first came out, because I identified with it. Guy makes beats on the side as he toils in minimum wage Hell, and he doesn't stop trying to get his music out there. And eventually, through hard work and luck, he's standing on top of the Sears Tower grabbing his balls and giving the finger to the world for so much as questioning his drive. But what separates me from Kanye West is that while he sweats on stage and busts his vocal cords, I'm breaking my back and bleeding my guts out on a canvas that might not even have been sanitized. I take falls onto concrete and get people snapping chairs at me. I get to have doctors who stitch me up and pat me on the ass, saying 'You're alright to work the house shows coming up.' Getting back to the point that I opened up with, about the family unit coexisting with wrestling. So often it gets through the pipeline that Joe Blow Main Eventer is having a wife that is threatening to break up with him over the fact that he's busting his hump in the world of professional wrestling. They are so scared of seeing the man they love (or latched onto like a damn leech) end up in a wheel chair. So they get whiny and overprotective and you see these guys just rolling their eyes trying to deal with it. I've seen it time and time again, and it is something at is start to wear thin on me. Hearing about how Tchu has to deal with this shit? Pisses me off. And you want to know what? Tchu didn't need pain killers to pick himself up during the day. Tchu doesn't have his right arm go numb for long stretches at a time. Tchu doesn't have holes drilled into his knees to alleviate pressure. And I know damn sure that he does not have problems with his neck that make it hard for him to sleep at night. As he is knighted by a promotion I MADE, I get to hear that through the PTC grapevine. I get to hear about primadonnas like Angelo Deville coming back to elation after he went into every person who ever bought a ticket to go see a wrestling show's bedroom and take a giant shit on their bed. I get to see and hear in my own locker room the constant wondering about Rich Rollins and Seymour Almasy. And I get to hear about their problems. Their issues. I get to see Jason Snow, who just came out of fucking nowhere make a return and almost instantly jump into this huge spotlight. You see these kinds of people, and they take time off. Months and years at a time, and they heal their injuries through surgery or they simply refuse to work the arduous schedule. And because they are deemed the 'Money Makers' they get away with it without any damn recourse. I had a wife too. I had a damn life. But wrestling became me. Everything I am and stand for is the integrity of a sport that is a mockery in the United States. And even though I put on a brave face and become the embodiment of unrelenting rage in a wrestling ring, I am beginning to feel as if it is forced. I am starting to develop a different sort of relationship with the sport of wrestling. Maybe it is the stuff that closed out last year acting as a mortar. Having to deal with being forced out of PTC for months because I was on the invisible blacklist that Steven Caldera finally rescued me from, it took a lot out of me. But having to watch and see a promotion I helped build turn me into their own personal canvas dwelling pin cushion, getting my ass run down by having them try to take their stock up a couple of points by downsizing mine? After all I did for them? After all I fucking accomplished there and after how many people who get fucking Hall Of Fame nods that didn't even graze my cape? It's something I've had to deal with every single day of my life since King Of Kings 2005, when Duke Williams chair shot to my head caused a ripple effect that flushed my life through the goddamn drain. A once proud man, I've been relegated to simply being a lapdog for the masses. Something to prop their feet upon. Fuckers like Nova who only beat me when he had a tag team partner. Fuckers like Karina Wolfenden who have been humiliated by my hand. Fuckers like Angelo Deville and Hoyt Williams who needed opportunities to surpass me in the ring yet talk as if I was owned bell to bell. Fuckers like Seymour Almasy who just got destroyed by me in under five minutes yet somehow still comes off as more prestigious and capable of winning this damn tournament than me. But I let that stuff slide for a while. After all, I mean, what can I do? PRIME firing me pissed me off, but it probably saved me from a grave after a OxyContin OD. And for a long time, I sat back idly as they threw darts at my name and took the high road. I dunno, maybe it is the injuries. Maybe it's the fact that in GCW I've just suffered the indignity of losing to Shadow, screwing up YET another shot at an elusive World Championship that has alluded me my entire career. Maybe it is the fact that Chet goddamn Worth decided he was going to throw YET ANOTHER shot my goddamn way, critiquing my abilities when that fat bowling fuck was pushing up curtains while I was selling out arenas as 5-Star Champion. All I know is that it is making me finally get off my chest a lot of my emotions that have been bottled up for almost a year. But for whatever reason, even through my gritted disdain for the politics of the sport and the fact that after the adrenaline rush I experience in a match I basically go into shock, I find myself on my way to Singapore about to face some midget named the Low Priest in the sixth annual GTT tournament. I give and I give. I've overcome my pain killer addiction, I've stopped drinking alcohol, and I'm training the next generation of kids who want to get into this sport. So why the hell does this all feel so damn empty? Why does it feel like no matter what I try to do, it always blows the hell up in my face? They say the pain is temporary but the pride is forever. What do you do when you're the proud man trying to somehow redeem himself in the eyes of everyone? When you've literally seen your entire life crumble before your eyes? I'd be remiss if I did not include Melissa into the whole conversation. Not a day goes by when I do not put up a faux callous demeanor when the questions about my ex comes up. Ain't a day goes by when I don't think that I should've given her something for putting up with me. We weren't getting along, but it wasn't up to me to 'put her in her place'. Yet her jaw got wired shut and all I had to do was pay a fine, do some community service, and sign some paperwork. Love and everything like it…I'm starting to miss it. We got married on the beaches of the Gulf Coast, and I promised her the entire fucking world. But at the end of the day, I'm not the right man to give that. My gift isn't to bring joy into the hearts of people. I go into a ring and I beat the hell out of others who have a sick desperation in their lives. I take piledrivers onto my head, bleed buckets, and have my limbs ripped apart. I travel the world over yet do not see anything but the same arenas and hear the same breathless adulation from blood lusting fans who want to see the good guy prevail and the bad guy pay for their misdeeds. I'm the baddest motherfucker walking the planet and I snarl and never stop picking myself up. Until the lights go off. And then I'm back to being Superman on kryptonite falling through a skylight window, craving the next burst of adrenaline to carry myself through the day. I've beaten heroes, humbled legends, and stricken fear in the hearts of heartless. Yet at the end of the day, it doesn't matter. My gift to the world is to die for entertainment. And no matter how much I try to fight it, I'm in too deep now to stop it.
