Dr Faustiosity - Chapter Four: Crescendo

13 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-13 Just like that, they were back in the Curious Laboratory. The main lab room was looking just as it had before they had left it. There was still a hole in the far wall, and the roof was still scattered over a three mile radius following the blast that had destroyed it. Despite all this, the doctor felt glad to be back in the place he had been calling home for the past few years. “Merrick, vhat vere you…” began Doctor Curiosity, before he was interrupted by the doorbell. He looked around, not seeing where the demon had vanished to. It perturbed the doctor, and he answered his intercom in something of a daze. “Hello?” asked the doctor, confused as to who it could possibly be. “Hello. This is… ummm… the piano tuner. I’ve been called out to tune your piano.” “I never called out any piano tuner!” declared Curiosity. “Yeah, I know… your neighbours did, mate!” replied the voice at the other end, as it tried to stifle a laugh. “Oh, very well,” replied the doctor, absent-mindedly. “You can come in.” He pressed the button and turned, looking to try and find where his demon had hidden itself. He walked over to the scorched oak workbench, and leaned over it to see if Merrick might be on the other side. All of a sudden, he stopped himself. “Vait a minute… I don’t have any neighbours!” He looked around in alarm, searching for either a weapon, or the demon he had called up to protect him. “I don’t even have a damn piano!” ----------------------------------------------------------- …and we’re in, just like that. I don’t think Frank expected his little joke to be taken seriously. He’s even got the decency to look a little embarrassed as we walk across the lowering drawbridge. I’m looking upwards now and all I can think is that this feels very strange. I’m glad I’ve got the balaclava to hide behind, because this place is creepy as all hell. It’s like some kind of ruined gothic castle. I’m sure it was impressive once upon a time, but at the moment there doesn’t appear to be a roof; and one of the walls is looking distinctly absent. “Hey Anthony, watch where you’re going,” says one of the other guys. I don’t even know their names, to be honest. I’ve met Frank a couple of times before, and I used to play pool with his driver, John, but this whole ‘hoodlum’ gig is pretty new to me. It’s not like I went out of my way to end up like this; I was just never really that good with temptation. It’s times like these when I clutch at Shamrock. I know it’s a stupid thing to do, but he’s my lucky mascot; a little green plastic shamrock than my girlfriend brought back to me from Ireland. Perhaps it’s not the most inventive name in the world, but I’ve not had much go wrong since I got him, and so he stays for my peace of mind as much as anything. The door is open now, and we walk through in pairs. Frank threw down the metallic battering ram that was to open the doors as soon as they opened, and now it’s just us and him. Ten of us, and one Doctor Curiosity. Why don’t I feel safe? Why do I feel like someone else is pulling my strings here? Why did I just count eleven men instead of ten? ----------------------------------------------------------------------- “Merrick? Vhere in ze blue blazes are you?” said Doctor Curiosity, worry creasing his brow. The demon was nowhere to be seen, and Curiosity had to act fast. He was on the third floor, which would give him a few minutes, but who was he preparing for? What kind of person had arrived at the Curious Laboratory? “They’ve come to take your money,” said a voice from behind him. The doctor spun, coming face to face with the demon, as if he’d always been there. “I have no money! I tzought ve established zat!” “Yes, you did indeed, but these men do not give up debts easily.” “I have no debts! Zey vere all Collette’s, and since she died, I shouln’t… vait… did you say men?” “Yes I did.” “How many?” asked the doctor. “I believe there are ten of them, and they’re armed.” The doctor’s face dropped, and inside his heart did likewise. Merrick put one arm around his shoulder and spoke to him with fake, cloying sincerity. “Hey, don’t be down about it. You said you were the favourite to win that wrestling tournament of yours, so you must be adept at handling yourself in a fight.” “Jah, but…” “Don’t be so negative,” the demon interrupted. “Sure, it’s a few more than you’re used to, but I bet none of them are as good as Dave Hurst and Craig Goat, are they? You seemed to be so sure you could defeat those two on your own that I’m sure ten heavily armed thugs will be a walk in the park…” The words were left hanging, and Doctor Curiosity tried to speak, but it was agony. “I’ll just be leaving,” continued the demon. “You made it quite clear that you could win without me, and that you wouldn’t need my powers, especially against ten experienced fighters carrying guns and knives.” “Guns?” asked Curiosity, his top lip beginning to quiver. “Yes, big guns. I’m sure you can handle them though, so I’ll be going now. It’s such a shame you didn’t want to make a deal though…” The doctor closed his eyes and another layer of control was penetrated. “Merrick, help me.” The demon turned, sneering down at the closed eyes of the man who had called him from Hell. “Help you? Why would you need my help, oh mighty doctor?” he asked, the tone of his voice suddenly turning sharper, like a knife used to butcher the self-respect of his victim. “I… I can’t take on ten men. They’d kill me.” “You truly desire my help?” “Yes.” “As an enemy of mine once said, ‘Ask, and ye shall receive’,” replied the demon, with a laugh. *SNAP* ------------------------------------------------------------------ This place is like some kind of labyrinth. We’ve split into two groups of five, looking for where this guy might be hiding out. After all this effort it’d be funny if he wasn’t here at all, but Frank assured us that nobody has driven back down the road in the last few days. We’ve been waiting for him, he said. Everything here seems a little more familiar to me than it does to the rest of the guys. I don’t think any of them have been in a castle before, so they’re expecting Sir Lancelot to jump out with a broadsword, or Harry Potter to come around the corner and cast a spell on them. I can’t say I’ve lived in a place like this, but some of my friends used to like getting married in style, back before I came to America. Another door creaks open, as if it’s pointedly been left to rust a certain amount. The noise goes right through me like fingers down a blackboard, and it’s painful to walk any further for a second. The man at the front kicks the door so hard that the frame buckles, and we all get showered in a mixture of dust and splinters. “For fuck’s sake John, quit doing that,” says someone, but I’m not even looking to see who. We go through the portal and we’re in another small room. None of these rooms seem to have any purpose. They’re just taking up space. It’s unnerving to think what kind of person would live here and ignore all of the possibilities of owning this kind of real estate. I don’t even mean that he should sell it off, but owning a castle and leaving it empty… it just seems a little strange. We’re at another door, and someone restrains John, stopping him from kicking it off its hinges. We all allow ourselves a wry little smile, but then it’s gone in an instant. Something hits the lead man on the head, and he goes down. It takes a matter of seconds for confusion to descend upon us. I don’t know which way I’m going now, but I’m running away. I don’t know why, but something is wrong here, and I don’t want to be here to see it. I’ll lose the money, but I’m fine with that. The shouts that come from behind me fade into the distance as I dart round a corner, and run straight in to a red blur. The floor seems very cold, and I think I’m passing out… ----------------------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity giggled with glee as the Curious Mallet collided with Anthony’s face and knocked him to the floor. The men in the castle were now in disarray. He had taken out four of them in different parts of castle, within seconds of one another. The first had taken a hit from an anvil that he had hauled over a doorway, and the second to a well-concealed trap-door. “Tie him up, Merrick,” said the doctor. Curiosity looked down at his hands, which showed red underneath the grime. He had no idea how he had managed to find the strength to lift the anvil and balance it so precariously on the edge of the door. He looked over to the demon, only to see a rope fly through the air and wrap itself around the man in the balaclava. Ah yes, that would be where the strength came from. “No time to waste, doctor.” Curiosity nodded, and with a snap of the fingers they both vanished. ------------------------------------------------------ Ouch. I feel like I’ve recently drunk six gallons of vodka on an empty stomach and then head-butted a wall. Ugh, the wall was probably covered in spikes too. The room I’m in now is different from the others. There’s furniture, evidence of this place being inhabited… oh, and there’s no freaking roof. Hell, there’s barely even walls. Over on the far side of the room are five of the other men. They all seem to be bound in much the same way that I am. I’ve no idea how we all ended up in this kind of predicament, but I’m guessing that he must have had some help. Our only hope now lies in him either making a mistake, or the other men finding us. I can’t see Frank, and he’s a wily old fox, so I’ve got to think the latter is the more likely. The doctor guy is walking around and talking to himself. I can’t really make out any of the words, as he’s speaking softly, not to mention my ears are still ringing from whatever the hell hit me in the head. The bonds holding my arms seem to be tied extremely tightly. They’ve got almost no ‘give’ in them, and… Holy shit, where the hell did he go? Man, I’m feeling woozy again. I must have been hit harder than I thought. I’m starting to see things… -------------------------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity appeared right behind one of the remaining intruders, and a swift demon-assisted frying pan to the head saw the large man fall to the floor. “Jah! Ve are destroying zem!” declared the doctor. Merrick merely smiled, and held his fingers up into the air. “No… I think I can do zis now.” The demon raised one perfect eyebrow over his blood red eye, and gestured for the doctor to try it out for himself. *SNAP* They vanished. ---------------------------------------------------------------------- So I’m being held captive by some demented German doctor who talks to himself and can somehow overpower… nine men it is now. Frank is the only one not here, tied up. I think I’ve been passing in and out of consciousness because it’s all I can think of to explain what’s been going on. Maybe the shot I took earlier dislodged something in my brain. I’m starting to worry about it really, because I don’t feel too bad, but I keep on blacking out. It’s as if I’m losing huge chunks of time as I lie here, trying to move my hands, trying to touch Shamrock, my lucky charm, trying to do anything other than just pass out on the cold stone floor. He really is trying to talk to himself though. It’s almost like he’s having an argument with himself, or with thin air. Maybe I’m not the only one here missing a few brain cells. Hang on, something just happened. I moved my arm, and it came out from behind my back, clutching Shamrock! I knew that damn thing was a lucky charm! Now I’ve got to get my other hand free, and my legs, and I’ve got to do it without him noticing me. I’ve got outwit him before I can outwrestle him. -------------------------------------------------------------------- Merrick looked over Curiosity’s shoulder and saw that the English wrestler had pulled one hand free of the restraints. It had taken long enough, as they should have been loose enough for the past few minutes. Sometimes humans were shockingly slow to catch on to things. Take the doctor, for example. He thought he was controlling everything now. He thought that he could use the power on his own, that he had no further need for the demon. The doctor stood in defiance of Merrick, his arms waving around to encapsulate the men he had taken captive. “Nine men, Merrick. Look at vhat I have achieved! Maybe I don’t need your help after all. You can move me around, but ze hitting is done by my own fair hands.” Merrick shrugged his shoulders, the immaculately tailored suit moving with a faint rustle, just enough to cover up the sound of Anthony freeing his other hand. His legs followed quickly, and he covered the ground to the doctor in a flat sprint, almost reaching him before Curiosity turned around. *SNAP* Nothing. Anthony slammed in to the doctor, and the pair hit the floor as one. ------------------------------------------------------------------- I’d done it. I’d broken free of his straps and I’d jumped him from behind. He seemed to snap his fingers, and for a second I expected magic to come and strike me down, but it was not to be. We went down together, and though all of my instincts told me to be careful, I couldn’t help myself, and I tried to attack him. I’ve got his head now, and I can turn him with ease. He’s got some strength, and some skills, but I think he’s just so surprised that I escaped that he’s not prepared. I get to his neck now, and I’m choking him out. He’ll be gone in a matter of seconds. His body tries to fight me off, but I have him. The fighting subsides, and he goes limp. I keep the hold locked in for longer. I’m not sure if I’m killing him now, or just keeping him out for longer, but I’m scared and I don’t want to get trussed up like a turkey again. I pull even tighter on his neck and he’s a dead weight. One final paroxysm from the body, and I’m thinking for a second that it was some nervous reaction, some death rattle. Maybe he’s… Maybe… He’s moving. How can he… He’s moving, and he’s turning and oh my God his eyes. They’re red, and inside the pupils I can see fires. I can see fire and torture and pain and Hell. I pull tighter on his neck, but it’s like trying to throttle a giant redwood with a foam finger. My grip is slipping, and I don’t know why. He turns, and his face silently mocks me. Shamrock falls from my pocket, and I try to move and lunge for it. I don’t know why, but it seems important to me now. None of this makes sense, and I need something to tie me to reality. He’s moving slowly, but it’s like a tectonic plate shifting. His arms grab me with a strength I could never resist, and now it’s my time to be turned, and his arms come across my windpipe. I try to stop him applying pressure to my arteries, but it’s impossible to oppose his will. I see Shamrock out of the corner of my eye and I try one final time to grab it. This gets his attention and he reaches out ponderously, snatching up the plastic toy and examining it. His arm returns around my neck and he’s holding Shamrock right in front of my eyes. Without his fingers moving, it starts to melt. He didn’t need a machine or a flame to do it; he just melted the plastic without effort. I’m realising now that this isn’t just some parody of a doctor, or just some clapped out professional wrestler. This isn’t some comedy figure plucked from a television show. I thought I was on the road that would lead me to a triumph, but it the end it seems that I was wrong. Those eyes… Those eyes burn in my memory, even after my sight is lost; and I tumble into blackness. ------------------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity got up from the floor, his eyes still burning red and his breath coming in ragged bursts. “Are you convinced yet, human?” asked Merrick. “I could have… I could have… ugh,” panted Curiosity. “You were gone. You would have died, doctor, but for me. You would have been outwrestled by this nobody, this man you have left in your wake now, with the power… my power.” Curiosity bowed his head. “It can be your power, doctor, but you have to believe me… but if you won’t believe me, then ask him.” Merrick stepped aside, and behind him was Frank, his arm outstretched, pointing at the doctor. For the second time in his life, Doctor Curiosity was staring down the barrel of a gun. For the second time in recent weeks, the gun was fired. For the first time, it was loaded. Doctor Curiosity hit the floor, and rolled over. His arm hurt, but he was still alive. He rolled to one side, and then got to his feet, ready to dash for any cover that might present itself. Merrick cleared his throat. The bullet was hovering in mid-air, the entire tableau frozen in place, in time. Curiosity checked his arm, and found he had just banged it on the hard floor. Cursing quietly in German, he got back to his feet. “The power is mine, but it could be yours. Your options are now clear, human. You must face either the bullet or… the other option.” Curiosity nodded. *SNAP* ---------------------------------------------------------------------- They were in a fairly small room where the only light came from a circle of candles on a table set against the wall. Curiosity looked around, confused. “Where are we?” “How sad that you do not know…” The doctor looked more closely at the walls, and the unmade bed. Pinned to most of the vertical surfaces were posters and news clippings from various sources. All had one thing in common. Doctor Curiosity. “Eegor…” whispered the doctor. “Correct,” observed Merrick. “Your brother was quite the fan, it would seem. He supported you for all that time, as his bedroom testifies, and now you follow his sacrifice to achieve your fleeting moment of glory.” Merrick’s body started to shift, and his face lost some of its handsome lustre. “It is time to sign the contract.” Curiosity nodded his head. It almost seemed funny how everyone seemed to have lost out over the past few months. His wife was dead and his brother was going to spend the rest of his life in prison as a result. That just left him, and here he was, about to sell his eternal soul to the Devil for a modicum of power, for the ability to finally control his own destiny. “What happens to those men?” “They go back, they die, they do whatever you want them to do.” “I’d not like them to die. Enough have lost out already,” said Curiosity. Was it all really worth it? Was this about destiny, or was it really just about the wrestling tournament? He’d never won anything in his life, ousted just before the end all throughout his life. The tournament meant more to Chris Goodman, the man inside, because it was his chance to finally achieve something he could be proud of. Yet, the price would be high. Merrick drew a knife and slashed across the proffered forearm of the doctor, not hard enough to wound, but just enough to draw out some blood. At times the doctor had felt that he had nothing more to lose, and that his soul had been tormented enough that Hell seemed to hold no fear. When Merrick held out the quill, dipped in the doctor’s own blood, the fear hardened. “There can be no second thoughts, human.” Underneath it all, at the very core of his being, Chris Goodman was making a decision. Perhaps he could throw himself on the mercy of God and ask for forgiveness? Perhaps he could seek salvation after the tournament ended, and escape with both his soul and the victory. Merrick nudged the doctor, and as he did so the power flowed through his veins. He felt drunk on it, lusting after the feeling he had achieved when breaking Anthony earlier, and defying the ten men who had dared to come and try to collect a debt he would never pay. In that moment he knew that Merrick had used his powers to this end all along. He had brought together an assortment of men who would never have acted that way, and had sent them to the Curious Laboratory as bait. He had loosened Anthony’s restraints, and made Frank squeeze the trigger that could have ended his life. He knew it all, but he didn’t care. The power surged in his breast, and he felt like he could overcome the entire world if he so desired. Deep inside, Curiosity knew that there would be no going back. Signing the contract would be final. There would be no appeal, not when the power could still flow through him, a drug he wouldn’t be able to give up. The decision was to be made now. If he planned on repenting at the eleventh hour, he was sure to die at half past ten. The candles cast a macabre glow across the room, and Doctor Curiosity bent over, towards the contract. The light flickered in his faces, and in his eyes tiny flames dances, the fires of Hell that gave him the power he would need; the power that he craved. Chris Goodman looked up and laughed, knowing his fate and not caring as the power coruscated across his soul. He knew what he would do, and he knew it would bring him victory in the tournament. Grinning, his face so much like the demon’s that it was hard to tell them apart, he sold his eternal soul without a backward glance, and without a single regret.
