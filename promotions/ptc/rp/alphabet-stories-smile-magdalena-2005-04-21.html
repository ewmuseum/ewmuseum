Alphabet Stories: Smile Magdalena

21 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-21 Sometimes I wake up and I think I’m in a book. And then I realize that I am. Except this isn’t the type of book one would read, it’s the type of book you hear about but can never find. This book never ends, because it never stops growing. With every breath and every second a new story is formed and the book grows. I’m a part of this book and as is everybody else. The only things that will ever join us all together are the stories we tell. Unfortunately, the Alphabet has it’s own chapter. A chapter closed off to the rest of the world, alone in it’s sadness and alone in it’s eternity. I would hear these stories as a kid, some of them made up and some of them real. I never really knew what to believe but I knew that they made me feel unique, as if I was a part of a larger family. Alphabet City is made up of all types of people with no direct correlation what so ever, but the stories…the stories are ours. I was only twelve when I sat outside the corner of 15th and listened to Left Eye Leroy tell me and my friends all the old stories about the Alphabet. The stories were exciting and all had a lesson. Every few days we’d gather together and bug Left Eye Leroy to tell us a new story. We called him Left Eye because his left eye was red. Nobody knows why it was red. That was the only story he wouldn’t tell. So one day after school my friend Benny and I went to Leroy for a story. Benny was hyper and excited because a girl he had liked gave him a kiss using her tongue. Benny figured the girl was in love with him and that soon they’d have sex and he could become a real man like his brother Hector. Leroy told Benny that just for him he’d tell us a story about the most beautiful girl to ever walk the Alphabet. ___________________________________________________________________________________ Her name was Magdalena. Icy smooth hair with a golden light sparkle, skin as pure and exotic as fine leather, lips like a cherry Popsicle, and eyes as clear as a glass vase. She was one of a kind. The girl that the other girls envied and the boys couldn’t get enough of. Magdalena was a star, a balloon of love with a tint of heaven. Magdalena was all that one could ever want, except Magdalena wishes she would have never been at all. Beauty that obvious is dangerous people would say, and they were right. Magdalena broke hearts, but she also made them race. She had everything needed to make it far, but all those things kept her back. Her father was a short broad man with a needlelike beard. His voice was like a derailed train and it made you want to get out of his way. Red hands and long dirt fingernails. To think something as beautiful as her was due to him seems unrealistic. Her mother was a smiler. Always had a smile and never took it off. She hid everything she had well enough to make us not wonder anymore. Your eyes never smile, because a smile is a lie. Magdalena loved attention. She knew she was wanted and she put up a fine display for her product. Gitty and gorgeous with a smile that explodes. Most guys were intimidated to even touch a figure so precious in fear that she would break like porcelain. But she was stronger than porcelain, much stronger. Certain days she’d disappear and rush home. She wouldn’t be seen for a few days, until she came back with the same heart stopping beauty she always came back with. Nobody knew why she was gone, but nobody questioned it. Every guy flirted with her and every girl complimented her, but nobody ever talked to her. She was popular on the outside but lonely on the in. Her mind always wandered and her attention was short. She was always preoccupied upstairs and you couldn’t get in as hard as you might have tried. She hid her emotions well the same way her mother hid her own. The mystery only made her more wanted. Magdalena, why didn’t you let anyone in? Why were you scared to breathe deep and let the wind take you where you wanted to go? We’ll never know though, because when Magdalena fell from grace she fell hard enough to shake the Alphabet asphalt hard enough for all of us to notice. Her beauty became an accessory, Magdalena no longer rushed home after school or at awkward times in the day. She just stood put and every day when the kids came out she was they’re waiting. Every night when the kids went in she remained out. Nobody knew what she did or where she went, but her tiny round feet didn’t run home, they stayed. The streets were never safe, but when the doors were locked and the whisper of the sky could be heard, it wasn’t a good sign. Magdalena changed, a little bit more everyday. Her beautiful smile became a disgruntled frown. Her silky smooth hair became raggedy with visible dirt and deterioration. And those eyes, the eyes that couldn’t lie were engulfed in deep red cracks. A beauty gone sour and a love turned hate. One day I decided to stay out and see what Magdalena did at night. I figured it was nothing too enjoyable, but what I saw was an injustice to humanity itself. I put on my favorite black sweatshirt to shelter me from the numbing fall wind and a sock hat to try and fit in with the outer element. I stood ten steps behind and tried to be as silent as can be. She never turned around, never even a glance…. The girl who was just playing with the rest of us and sitting next to me throwing spitballs was now somebody completely different. Like a wolf at the break of midnight she transformed right in front of my eyes. She took off her polka dot cherry pants and revealed the fishnet stocking with noticeable tears and wear. She put on these high heels that made her look like she could touch the sky that she would never get to enjoy. Then she began her journey, like running up an escalator she knew it would never get her anywhere. She walked the streets like they were hers she showed no fear. It’s said that all emotion is based on fear. It’s a good thing that the emotion of Magdalena had already been sucked dry. She walked in a twenty-four hour convenience store. She went to the clerk and gave him five dollars, grabbed a drink and then clinched the two tiny bags he handed her in her soft palm. She walked out unsuspicious and was on her way. As she left a junky ran up aside her and screamed the same words over and over. Want to go halves, please Magdalena let’s go halves. Magdalena brushed him off and made her way toward three large dark men on the stoop of the Chinese Mai Tsu Eatery. They handed her a white piece of a paper with a location on it. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] She just kept on walking until she got to the location. She stomped her boot twice against the wall until a dark window opened. She placed a few dollar bills in it and just second later a needle and plastic container sat gently against the same window cell. She tucked the items gently in her purse and walked the darker side of the street to an echo of whistles and hollers. A car pulled up aside her and handed her a paper bag and a kiss on the cheek. It sped off and she sat there in the dark corner of a brownstone, mixing and rolling… Mixing and rolling… She took out a spoon and put a lighter underneath. Just mere seconds later she looked like she was going into a daze with smoke forming all around her face. She then put away her spoon and took out the needle she just got. She tapped it against the sidewalk and ran her finger up the needle. She lifted her sleeve to a reveal a string of bruises that were noticeable from even the other side of the block. She injected the needle into her arm and kept it in there until her head began to slowly drop downward. A few moments later she had awaken from her daze. She stumbled to her feet and ran her hand across her nose. She walked in slopes and slants to a nearby parking lot where she waited. The first car that came by was an old Ford Bronco with worn paint and a taped up left mirror. Magdalena approached the car and the man grabbed her by the hair and threw her inside. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] Alone. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] Finally she mustard up enough strength to leave and she bought some food at a nearby store. She took it down as quickly as she could and then just threw it back up over and over. The horror had ended…but just for one night. It was still only four in the morning and she gathered herself together and moved toward a set of tables with two older men setup behind them. The tables were filled with all types of books that were probably stolen and sold there for a fix. Magdalena didn’t buy a fix like I assumed though, instead she grabbed two books and paid for them. She then fixed her tiny body under a streetlight and began to read. She read for the rest of the night. As she read her face glowed, she was no longer a victim, but a victor. She just kept on reading until a smile came across her face. A smile that explodes… a smile that brings light. She read until I fell asleep and when I woke up she was still reading. It was just an hour before school was about to begin so I rushed home and got ready. As I made my way outside to go to school I saw Magdalena waiting there, with her nice smile and clean pants. A big smile on her face, but not the smile I witnessed, not the smile that I knew she had. The other kids gathered around her and she played with them as if everything was just a big dream. It wasn’t a dream though… It was a nightmare… one that I’ll never forget. Magdalena. The beautiful angel by day and junky turned prostitute by night. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] The girl who sells herself to make enough money to read a book and escape the reality that she is a part of. The girl who’d rather live the street than go home to her father. Magdalena was one of a kind. It wasn’t until about a year later that she disappeared forever. All the kids told their stories of how she probably went off to Hollywood or made it big in the City. A few kids even talked about how she was going to be in Miss America. I knew the truth the smile never fooled me. They were right in one sense though. She was in a much better place now, far, far away from the Alphabet that destroyed her. _________________________________________________________________________________________ As Leroy finished the story a tear jolted down his red eye, followed by another and another and another. As he gained tears I lost them. Benny shrugged off the story without giving it much attention but I took it to heart. Not many things ever affected me, but that story has always remained. Leroy told me the story he’d never tell and I was happy he did. At that point I realized my life would never be the same. I was no longer living in a child’s world. I wasn’t even sure if I was a part of any world. All I knew was that it just isn’t right. It’s all wrong. Every time I see a young girl with a bright glow I think of Magdalena, and I think of her in heaven smiling down, with the smile that only Leroy could see through.
