Distracted: Misplaced Aggression

23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-23 He hit him too hard. Train knew it the second the second he threw the punch. The buckling of flesh under his knuckles, only confirmed the obvious for him. There was a loud thump that rung through the air like the toll of a bell as his fist made contact. The warmth of the blood splattering onto his knuckles just let him know part of that loud ring was the sound bone and cartilage of the kid’s nose breaking down at the point of impact. His head snapped to the side and whipped back, nearly instantaneously, with the same gruesome ferocity with which it had turned. The kid’s skull wobbled on top of his neck like some sort of grotesque bobble head. The was no chance for him to defend himself now. Hell, he hadn’t really had a chance to defend himself before. Wasn’t more than a second ago the kid and gingerly inched out to the center of the ring in which they stood, his arms raised, half tensed as he wiggled his fingers in anticipation of heading into the tie up. If the kid had been everything he’d been hyped to be he would have known there was something bad coming his way as Train had just stood sneering at him, arms down at his side...waiting on him. He’d lunged forward like he wanted something. Train let him have it. It was a damn shame really. The kid didn’t deserve it. Train knew that full well. This was just supposed to be some light sparing. The half speed prelude to a bigger event. That shot wasn’t meant for him. The son of a bitch that deserved it was a long way from here... without a scratch on him. The bastard had gotten into Train’s head. That pissed him off. Not only had he done it, but he’d gotten away with it. That pissed him off worse. And both of them had seen it... Humiliation on top of personal insult.... The straw that broke the camel’s back. But again, none of that was this the kid’s fault. Beneath the swirling ocean of rage that was boiling inside him Train’s conscious did what it could to turn down the heat... to tell him the right thing. He needed to stop. Just because watching someone bleed would make him feel a bit better was no reason to make this kid suffer any more. The damage was more than done, the first him good enough to be the last. Let the kid down easy the tiny voice yelled at the top of it’s lungs. Unfortunately the swirling water drowned it to a whisper. And then there was another voice that growled from an even darker recess, it’s questions fueling the fire... Let him down? You mean the motherfucker is still standing? Why is it some folks don’t know when to fall the fuck down? The ocean bubbled over. The kid hit the canvas as dead weight, his skull bouncing violently off the mat after bouncing even more violently off the end of Train’s fist one more time. Train just stood flat footed for a moment, staring down at the damage and the small pool of blood that gathered in the center as the stunned on lookers that flooded into ring to attend to him. Their frantic yelling was muted in Train’s ears, hardly making an impression as they ran together and overlapped each other. Not that Train didn’t get the jist of what was being said. What are you doing? Do you see what you’ve done? What the fuck is wrong with you? Blah, blah, blah..... He understood it all, but none of it meant much at the moment. As he stood in the ring, a frenzy of bodies buzzing around a decimated young man whom he didn’t even give enough of a shit about to remember his name, Train could only think about a man who’s name he knew all too well. He could only think about how much he wanted this to be him. ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== Three Hours Ago... He’d never been this nervous. He knew how ugly the view was looking down the barrel of a loaded gun. Knew the distinctive sound bullets made when they sliced through the air right next to your ear as they whizzed past your head. He’d Been blinded by flashing red and blue lights as faceless men and women demanded the surrender of freedom. He’d sat in a courtroom in the defendant’s chair as 12 people, supposedly his peers, decided his fate. They were all storms brimming with lightning, rumbling with thunder, spilling a deluge capable of drowning most. He’d weathered them. Walked through them and come out dry as a bone. Now he sat in the leather seats of the rented Navigator half a step away from catching the shakes. The Autumn leaves, already turning their bold shades of red and orange, blew along the side walk in the gentle breeze and made for an idealistic feel to the street lined with brick brownstones, all some subtle shade of red. The setting was soothing, but didn’t help his nervousness very much. This was never any place he thought he would wind up. Then again he never expected to find the picture of a little girl that became family the second he laid eyes on her. He never expected to spend half an hour sitting across from a preschool playground like he had just a couple of hours ago. But, just like he’d been there, now he was here. It was nice to see that Gabrielle was doing well for herself. Back in the day Gabby spent a lot of time depending on a man to provide for her, but the PI that got Train the address had made a point to note that he hadn’t seen any sign of any man here in all the time that he spent watching her and the little girl. He knew better than anyone that she had an independent streak beneath the surface, dying to get out. It was obvious it had been discovered and released from captivity. Maybe for her own sake. Perhaps for the little girl’s. Either way it had obviously provided for both of them. She’d carved out a life for them. He hadn’t seen or spoken to her for more than a couple of hours in the last five years, but he didn’t need to be her confidant to know she wouldn’t be happy with him just showing up in the middle of that life. What was he doing there? How did he find her? Where did he get the picture? What was she going to tell the little girl? Was there anything to tell the little girl? Questions swirling out of control with no time or space to dodge them as they flew at your head. They could both wind up with concussions from the impact. This was a risk. This was probably the wrong way to go about this. This was something he could avoid forever. Fuck it. Here goes nothing. As he stood at the door Train realized he had no clue what the hell he was doing. It was probably a bad sign that he’d forgotten the few steps out of the truck, across the street and up the front steps and just suddenly appeared on her doorstep... like he’d dropped in out of some episode of Star Trek where Scotty had beamed him down to an alien planet. Yeoman Anthony James reporting for duty....in his brand spankin’ new, shiny, red uniform... and no fucking phaser... Train could just feel the hostile, unknown alien just creepin’ up on his heels, waiting to turn him into a plot point to get in an “He’s dead Jim.” This isn’t a good idea. Shut the fuck up and be a man. Train took in a deep breath, remembering who the hell he was. Knowing if he didn’t gut it out he’d regret it. He crunched his fingers into a fist, ready to throw the first blow in what could turn into an ugly battle if it went the wrong way. He was in mid swing when the alien pounced. Before he could even knock the door popped open and there she was. Even with her red mane pulled back in a slightly frizzed ponytail and wearing the standard “I’m not going anywhere today” girl uniform of a pair of sweats and little wife beater Gabrielle glowed the way she always had. She was startled for a moment as she opened the door. He was startled when she opened the door. No doubt she hadn’t been expecting anyone to be standing on her front porch. Let alone him. Now doubt he hadn’t expected to be standing on anyone’s front porch. Let alone her’s. Train glanced over and saw the letters protruding from the small, metal box attached to the brick wall next to the front door. A mail truck had pulled off just as Train had pulled up. No doubt this was that time of the day and that was the reason she’d emerged. It was a serious relief. Last thing he wanted to think about was her waiting on him. He gingerly reached over and retrieved the swath of white envelopes, quickly offering them up to her. For a moment the shock only allowed her gently claim them. For moment all either of them could do was stare. Silence is golden. Nothing lasts forever. The haze of confusion cleared enough for Gabrielle to figure out her first move. She stepped forward, forcing Train back a step. Emerging on the small porch she pulled the door close to shut behind her. Train could only think one thing as the woman stood, pressing her palm to her temple and her lips searching for words... Here it comes. “Ummmmm... Tony,” she began to ask, her dumbfounded state finally dissipating. “What are you doing here?” Ding. “I mean how...,” she continued, taking the initiative to press the issue farther. “How did you even find me?” Ding. Now he was the one struck dumb. He knew the answers full well, but found himself without the capacity to even open his mouth to utter them. She stared back at him for a moment, but his silence wasn’t a good enough answer. “Tony...” she said, prodding him with as gentle a command as she was capable. “Look,” he finally responded, pushing his way through the haze that held him silent, but not past the haze that blinded him, keeping him unsure of his direction. “I... I know you got a lot of questions right now. But...before I answer any of yours... you got to answer one of mine.” A puzzled look, even more tense than before, washes over her visage as he reaches into his pocket and produces a small wallet sized picture that carried the weight of the world. She held out her hand and he slid it into place. She cradled it for a moment. She shuddered for a moment. “Who’s that?” Train asked as she peered down at the picture of a little girl with a bright, yet gapped smile. He saw in her faces that she was prepared and caught completely off guard at the same time. She was standing strong, the chances being she’d rehearsed a million different times a million different things she could say in a moment just like this. But the slight waver despite herself gave her away. Rehearsals were one thing. The show was always different out under the lights. “Where.... Where did you get this picture?” she demanded, no longer making any attempt to be gentle as she cupped her hand over her mouth, fighting to hold back the flood of emotions that wanted so badly to overflow the dam. Ding. “Fell out of your purse. Back at the motel. So.... who is she?” “My daughter. Brianne. Why did you...” “At first I didn’t even know to tell you the truth. She just reminded me of somebody so bad... I couldn’t let her go. For a second I just couldn’t remember who it was and then it hit me...” Train reached into his pocket and produced another picture. It was the same size and shape... only this one was different. Worn. Tattered. Old. He slid it into her hand right next to the first. The pictures were different. The girls were damn near the same. Gabby grew steadily more puzzled. “That’s my sister. Back when she was about the same age as Brianne.” Train stated plainly, allowing a long pause as Gabby took it in. “You know what my question is... don’t you?” She shuddered again and shook her head...the swinging more vigorous and time passed. “No.” she said, refusing to accept the situation. “I don’t have time for this right now.” “Make time.” he said, refusing to take no for an answer. “Tony... My little girl is here right now. She needs help with her homework, I have sketches I need to finish and I.... I have... company coming over. I’m not going through this with you right now.” “Look... I ain’t trying to make your life hard. But... if that little girl is...is... is ours... I got a right to know. And I already know that you know that.” “Oh God.....” Gabrielle exhaled in exasperation. For a moment Train thought it was him that broke her down like that. And it was...to an extent. But it wasn’t really the present that was weighing on her now. It was the future. And not the distant one with visits and custody issues. It was the future that was just a few seconds away. She saw something he didn’t. Didn’t see coming up behind him. Didn’t ever want to see again. “Son of a bitch.” a deep voice behind him stated angrily. It was a voice that was unfamiliar, but instantly recognizable. One he hadn’t heard in forever. Whoever it was needed to learn some manners as Train knew damn well he was talking about him. Train turned to greet the intruder on such a private conversation. They say muscle has a memory. Even when it has laid docile and unexercised for a long time... Even when it’s been robbed of the vitamins and protein it needs to reach it’s maximum potential... When the elements the muscle needs to return to it’s former state of peak condition return, the muscle quickly regains it’s shape, reborn into it’s former role. Hatred has a memory too. The two men locked eyes. The new man might normally look ordinary. The khaki pants. The black Polo shirt. The cell phone hanging off his hip. The perfectly groomed, 50 dollar a pop salon hair cut. His uniform might normally set him up to look like any other yuppie. But then again most Yuppie’s aren’t 7'0" tall. He’d changed some..aged a bit...lood a lot different without all that fucking stupid silver and black make up, but it was definitely him. For a moment in the last round of the tournament Train had thought this was the man he was going to be facing. Unfortunately it was just wishful thinking. Train knew the man who he’d hard had long since retired from the wrestling scene had a real name. But, no matter how much had changed, he only cared to call him by one... Arch Angel. The nemeses locked eyes for the first time in years. Judging by the bouquet of roses he was holding in front of him as he stood half frozen in shock it was obvious he’d come for Gabby. This must be her... “company.” Train strode down the steps. Christopher, as Arch Angel had become more commonly known since his retirement, turned and tossed the flowers back into his drop top convertible that had pulled up silently behind when Train wasn’t looking. He turned back quickly and the two locked eyes again. There didn’t need to be a reason for them to fight. Eachother’s mere existence was enough provocation. Gabby knew that full well as she charged down to the sidewalk in front of her home and held out her hands, pushing them both back to a slightly more manageable distance. “No!” she screamed at both of them. “You are not going to do this! Not here, not now!” “What the fuck is he doing here Gabrielle?” Christopher asked coldly, never taking his eyes off him. “I thought you told me this bullshit between him and you was over a long time ago? So how the hell is it he’s here now Gabby? Huh?” “Damn it Chris, I don’t know. He just.... he just...” “You don’t need to make no excuses for me.” Train stated just as coldly as his perpetual opponent. “I’m here cuz we got business that you ain’t got shit to do with. Which is why you’re so fucking mad, right? I mean you was her husband...and the only child she ever had was mine.” Chris stepped forward, his huge frame towering over both Train and Gabrielle. Neither gave up ground, Gabby in fact pressing her position. She turned completely to Chris, planting both her hands into his chest to hold him and push him back. “No!” she yelled. “God damn it Chris, stop it!” “So now you hide behind her?” Arch Angel asked. “Yeah... The big, bad, gangster. You never were anything but hype.” It was Train’s turn to press forward. “That’s right,” Arch Angel yelled. “Come on!” It was Gabrielle’s responsibility to stop him. She turned and this time pressed Train back. But Train had, had enough. He was calculating the best way to remove her from his path without hurting her when they all had to pause. “Mommy?” a tiny little voices jingled from the now open front door of the brownstone. The little girl with olive skin and bountiful curls looked confused and concerned as the adults scuffled in front of her. “What’s going on? Why are you fighting?” Train could only stare at her. He was dumbfounded again. This was the first time he’d gotten to see her up close. He’d never seen a more beautiful face. That’s why it was so disturbing to see it on the verge of tears. Gabrielle turned to her, trying as casually as she could to smile, dropping her barricading arms to her side slowly as not to alarm her any more than she already was. “Hi baby.... No... we’re... we’re not fighting. Chris and my friend...they were... they were just playing around sweetie. Baby go back in the house, ok? Mommy will be in to help you with your homework in just a minute. Go on, baby go back inside.” The little girl heard the instructions, but she was a little too smart to be contented by such a lame answer. She knew two of these people. It was the one she didn’t that interested her. “Who are you?” she asked in an innocent manner that still managed to hit Train like a punch to the gut, knocking the wind out of him. “Brianne!” rumbled her mother, her tone now letting her know this wasn’t a request. “Go back inside... Now!!!” The little girl relented, turning quickly and heading back through the door. Gabrielle put her back to the door and turned to both men. She no longer cared to officiate. “Both of you leave. Now.” They both left. Train left needing to hurt someone. ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== ~ ===== The Present (five minutes forward).... Now Train felt bad about not feeling bad. He still hadn’t learned the kid’s name. He was sure the two men Jerry had tracked down to train him for his match with Deville had said it, but when they screamed their French Canadian accents were a little too thick to comprehend fully. And frankly he wasn’t sure he really wanted to know what they had to say sense, even if it was in french, he could recognize cursing in about any language. but now, all the yelling had stopped and the place had long sense cleared out. The other cats that had been brought in to spar weren’t about to step into the ring after what they’d seen happen to the other kid. After they’d witnessed Train’s need to hurt someone. Train sat alone, in the silence of the gym he’d spent about a grand to rent out just for the purpose of this training session. He’d hurt someone...but it still wasn’t bad enough. The rage.... it was still there. Sure....the aggression was misplaced...hitting an undeserving recipient... But fuck it. It had taken a little of the edge off. But he didn’t know how long he could make it until he could place it where it belonged. Suddenly the front door of the gym swung open and Jerry walked in. The expression of his face said it all. He’d thought of himself as a man who could sell ice to Eskimos, but apparently he hadn’t been able to talk anyone into staying and continuing the session. His hands were locked behind his back and his head shaking as he looked down at the ground even as he moved closer. “Well, “ Jerry began in an exasperated tone that even Train had never heard before. “Good job. I had it all set up. I mean you were going to be set up like nobody’s business. Deville’s old trainers to teach you his style. A spot out of the way where you concentrate. You were headed towards a huge win. as it stands now? You’re headed towards a lawsuit. What the fuck were you thinking?” “I wasn’t thinking nothing,” Train said with all honesty. “It wasn’t about thinking. I needed to hit somebody. A little misplaced aggression.” “Misplaced?!” Jerry railed, not a fan of his understatement. “You broke the guy’s nose in 2 places?! He’s probably gonna have to have surgery just to make it look halfway straight again and you say misplaced?! Jesus... un-damned-believable.. Alright now Mr. Misplaced? What are we going to do now? Huh? What now?” “Me?” Train responded coldly. “I’m gonna do some more misplacing. You? You send that muthafucka a little message for me. You tell him, fuck hype.... I’m everything I say I am.” Train stands and strolls for the door, Jerry standing up behind him more confused than before. “Send him a message? Send who a message?” Train pushed the door open and stepped into the cold New York night. He left Jerry behind, but uttered a name just before the door closed behind him, cutting off all communication. He intended the message for a man that didn’t didn’t exist enough to receive any messages but enough to suffer.... “Christoper Deville.”
