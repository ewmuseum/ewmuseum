A Peace of Mind; A Piece of Redemption

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-14 The Honolulu Prince Hotel The windows were slammed shut and the curtains were opened just enough to let the hot Hawaiian sun seep in into the room. On the bed, still wrapped up in the silk white sheets, was a young man in his early twenties. He had the built of a strengthened swimmer: tall, slim, but with a cut physique. There was a reason Sebastian Moraco had to be in such great shape. For three years he was part of his college’s wrestling team. For three years he trained to be stronger, swifter, and higher than any of his teammates. That amount of intense training led him to be one of the more revered amateur wrestlers in the state of New York. So much so that the decision to announce the team captain was left without any nominations but a local named Ryan Howell, and Sebastian. Ryan won that proverbial coin-toss, but it wouldn’t be the first thing he edged Sebastian out of. Sebastian’s prowess in the collegiate amateur wrestling league gave him a chance to be selected for the United State’s Olympic team. A feat he long strived hard for. Every extra sit up he did, extra push up, extra lap around the track, was to get selected for the team. There were many who believed that he should have been the Captain of the team. Nothing to take away from a very capable Ryan, but Sebastian’s charisma left the fans wondering why he wasn’t the team’s leader. Their disappointment and his wouldn’t end there. When selection time rolled around, it was Ryan Howell once again who edged Sebastian. The last spot on the team was given to Ryan. Sebastian was offered a backup role, but in his state of despair refused. It felt like three years went down the drain. Yet, there is plenty more to that story. Sebastian turned over in his bed. He was still a mess from the drunken stupor he was in the night before. His left arm dangled lifelessly at the edge of the bed. On the carpeted floor, lay riddled a mess of empty Miller Lite bottles and a bottle of Jack Daniels. Sebastian’s clothes also made themselves useful in decorating the floor. Aside from Sebastian’s snores, there was no other noise in the room save the occasional wind. It was so calm and peaceful. Quiet enough to hear a pin drop. Or in more realistic cases – a phone ring. RING! Sebastian’s obsidian-colored Cingular cell phone rang and vibrated on the nightstand besides his bed. It continued to ring profusely not giving up until Sebastian would awake and answer it. The sound of the cell coupled with the midday sun shining in through the curtains proved to be formidable opponents for Sebastian’s sleep. Sebastian tossed again in his bed, but this time his serene siesta would not go on as planned. He turned over to his nightstand and lazily answered the phone with a muffled ‘hello’. “You awake, man?” came the reply from the other side of the line. Sebastian let out a disgruntled sigh as he sat up on his bed and slipped his sandals on. “Yeah, I’m up awake man. I’m awake.” He answered back automatically as he walked over to the window and pulled apart the curtains to let the full sun shine in. He squinted for a moment looking away from the window. “What time is it?” “Noon, man.” Answered the other line. It was Aaron, his best friend growing up in New York City. It was Aaron who first inspired Sebastian to give a shot at the Olympics team. It was also Aaron who first sent his condolences to Sebastian when he learned that his best friend wasn’t selected. In an attempt to cheer him up for a bad night a few months ago, Aaron and his girlfriend Stacy offered to take Sebastian, on a two-month trip to Hawaii. “Noon already?” asked Sebastian bewildered. “Damn man, I probably would have slept all day.” “Yeah probably.” Aaron retorted. “Hey we’re in the lobby and we’re coming up.” “Alright.” Sebastian answered and hung up the phone. He tossed his cell on the bed with ruffled sheets and walked over to his dresser. He opened up the top drawer and stared down at a gold medal stored away in the corner. No it wasn’t an Olympic Medal. It was the gold medal given to him for the State Championship his team won earlier that spring. Sebastian picked up the medal, not letting its dull luster from his grasp. I can still remember that day. The memory of selection day still rampages in my mind like a wild boar. Its tusks rip through my mind in brutal manner. I can still feel the nerves piling up inside me; the anxiety of waiting so long for that day. I remember the softness of the seat I was in, and how my hands grew cold as the coach started to announce who was going to make the team. Sebastian gripped the medal in his hands firmly. His veins appeared as if they were going to pop if he continued to put more strain on his grip. I can still remember the sound of Ryan Howell’s name coming out of Coach’s mouth. It replays over and over in my mind like cruel medieval torture. Each time his name passes through my mind in a slow eerie manner. I can still remember so much about the day my dreams went down the porcelain tank. I can still remember so much about a day I want to forget. Knock! Knock! A hard knock on the door broke Sebastian’s thoughts. Sebastian threw his medal back into the drawer and proceeded to put on a pair of cream-colored slacks, and an Aloha t-shirt. He walked to the door and opened it for his expected company. Aaron walked in first followed by his girlfriend Stacy. Aaron was a year younger than Sebastian and on their Division II basketball team. He was good, but like Sebastian was overshadowed by others on his team. Stacy was a brunette who grew up in Massachusetts before attending the same college. She had a unique sense of humor, especially when it involved innuendos. There they stood, Aaron in shorts and Aloha attire, and Stacy in a short pants with sandals. “You leave too much work for the maids here don’t you, Sebastian?” Stacy asked rhetorically kicking aside an empty pizza box. “Yeah whatever.” Aaron interrupted. “Come on, we’ll deal with this later. We’re going over to Pool and Pizza.” “Alright, just let me get my cell phone.” Sebastian answered before picking up his cell phone off his bed. Aaron picked up the empty bottle of Jack Daniels. “Jesus Christ man, you can’t keep drinking like this. At least not by yourself, share the wealth a bit.” Sebastian snatched the bottle and threw it in the empty garbage can. “I’ll be fine Aaron.” Sebastian retorted. “Hey come on, Seb.” Stacy said in a sweet voice. “I know you’re still disappointed about that, but you have to get over it. That’s why we brought you here with us. This is Hawaii, Seb. You’re supposed to be having fun here, not drowning your sorrows in alcohol.” Sebastian stormed past the both of them towards the doorway. He turned back in an exasperated manner. “Well, are we going to pool and pizza, or not?” He asked forcefully. “Yeah we are…” Aaron said, almost sounding unsure about it. “Drink all you want. Just promise me one thing, Seb.” “What?” “Promise me you won’t lose your mind over this.” Aaron asked of Sebastian. Sebastian let out a small chuckle and smirked. “Lose my mind? I haven’t, nor will I, lose my mind over this. I’m simply misplacing it.” Pool and Pizza Aaron, Stacy, and Sebastian sat in the outside seating area of the Pool & Pizza restaurant. Pool & Pizza was a local pub that they had all grown to having a certain likeness for. Just like the name applied, the restaurant was famous for its Pizza decisions and many pool tables scattered across the floor. One pool by every large plasma screen TV where dozens would gather around to watch the nightly game. But pizza and billiards aren’t the only attraction. It isn’t a family restaurant. Liquor is served here, and liquor is served in a large clear pitcher on Sebastian’s table. Sebastian poured himself another full glass of Sam Adams. He gulped it down as if the alcohol were sweet nectar, or purified water. Ironically, Sebastian never used to drink abruptly before. “I hate moments like this.” Sebastian blurted out flicking at the plastic cup in front of him. “What are you talking about, man?” Aaron retorted back bewildered. “You aren’t even giving this place a chance.” Sebastian playfully crumpled up the plastic cup in his hand and looked up. “I’m talking about these unusual moments of silence; those moments where seconds seem to last a lifetime. And throughout all those times, everyone realizes what is really going on. I hate this. I hate this unusual silence when we know it isn’t going to make things any better.” Aaron stood up from his seat and walked back inside the restaurant. Stacy moved over to take a seat next to Sebastian. “Hey come on, cheer up.” She cooed at him. “The coach made a bad decision and things turned out that way. I bet if you continue to work hard like you were before, you can make the team in Beijing.” Sebastian looked up at her. “It isn’t that simple.” “Why not?” she asked. “You didn’t get selected, so you have to try harder, right?” Sebastian nodded in forced agreement. He didn’t want to tell her that there was more than not being selected to the story. Hell, he wouldn’t even call it a story anymore. A plot, a conspiracy, an enigma, anything better than a story. He didn’t want to expose to her to the corruption he had seen transpire before his very eyes the night before selection. “Yeah, I knew you’d agree.” Stacy answered feeling confident that Sebastian would put away the sorrow-faced mask and don his usual cheerful face. “Get back into training and before you know it: This year you will be the captain and in four years you’ll be on the Team.” Sebastian smiled and nodded. This moment was too quiet for him. Not peace of mind kind of quiet, but just too eerie. Sebastian’s cell phone rang on cue. He answered the phone, but the person on the other line was someone he had not heard from since the last day of the spring semester. It was [name withheld], the team’s assistant coach. Jeff was one those who believed Sebastian was robbed of his deserved spot. He tried to make appeals and give Sebastian one more chance. But the committee rules, coupled with the Coach’s arrogance and Sebastian’s refusal to play the substitute resulted in his attempts being futile. “Jeff?” Sebastian asked standing up from his seat. “What is this about?” “We need to talk about something that could turn things around 180.” “What can do that?” Sebastian asked. “That’s the thing. We need time to talk. The short answer is quick, it’s the explanation that’s going to take a while.” “Excuse me then.” Sebastian answered. He muffled his cell phone and turned to Stacy. “Hey, when Aaron comes out tell him I had to go.” “Why? Who is it?” “It’s the assistance coach, [name withheld].” Sebastian answered. “Apparently he has some kind of answer.” Sebastian excused himself from the table and walked across the parking lot to a field a couple of yards away from any intolerable noise. “Alright, what’s going on this time?” Sebastian demanded. “I know not being selected for the Olympic team was a huge scar on your dream. You must have felt lower than the dirt on the bottom of my worn out boots. The fact that Ryan Howell was later disqualified for illegal drugs must have made you feel worse.” “Jesus Christ, stop it.” Sebastian interrupted. “You’re starting to sound long-winded and drawn out. Your words are like nails scratching against a blackboard to my ear. Every sentence you spew forth is riddled with nothingness and it goes on, and on, and on. Until the sheer calamity of it is that I am forced to find something more useful: Like shooting myself in the kneecap.” “I…” “You were just getting to the point, I know.” Sebastian answered for him. “Now tell me how you think you can rectify this situation?” “You can still earn yourself a chance to be one of the world’s revered amateur wrestlers. The world championships are coming up. Committees, coaches, and trainers from all around will be watching you. Events like this help decide who goes to Beijing.” “Aren’t they a while a way?” Sebastian asked. “Don’t I have to be selected for the team as well? You’re putting me in the same Goddamn situation here Jeff!” “You’re right. The events are a while away. However, entering them won’t be a problem once you do what I’m really looking forward.” Jeff cleared his voice in anticipation. “There’s this off-set wrestling tournament coming up real soon that I think will greatly enhance your chances of getting in.” “Wait a minute.” Sebastian paused. “I don’t understand this. How is this Tournament going to help?” “Everyone will be watching. I know this isn’t like any of the other wrestling matches you’ve been in, but if you do well here then you will prevail. You’ll be asked to be in the World Championships, and then you’ll be asked to be on the Olympics team. It’s a just thought really.” Sebastian sighed. “You think this kind of exposure will work? When do you need an answer?” “I’ll be flying in to Hawaii tomorrow night.” Sebastian hung up the phone and looked back at the restaurant. Maybe there was going to be redemption for this despicable atrocity. The Oceanside View Sebastian stood outside in his balcony leaning over on the rail. He sighed and just stared straight ahead into the peace Hawaiian horizon. Night befell the island of Oahu, and the nightly breeze began to wisp by being ever so cool and gentle. Sebastian stared at the white-sanded beach his hotel room overlooked. Diamondhead beach was a very popular beach resort for tourists, especially for scuba diving along their reefs. The waves repeatedly pounded upon the shoreline, but in the oddest sense they seemed peaceful. Everything was peaceful. Everything was quiet. The night breeze felt cool, the moon shun in full, and the sounds of the water crushing down on the rocks mixed to form a natural melody. Sebastian sighed, all the while playfully wrapping his state championship gold medal around his hand. Training, sacrifice, and strenuous work had all been wasted and overshadowed by one act of greed. Now it was time to rectify that situation. Why can’t everyday feel like this? Why can’t everyday go on peacefully where no one worries about how much they need to get done? A day where no one wakes up the morning dreading the long day ahead of him or her. No worries, no regrets, no omens. A day where everyone is just content with themselves and with their lives is what we need. One of those days where people forget about such words as betrayal, deceit, redemption, and even competition. Yes, even the most dynamic words made to give us inspiration are riddled in negativity. Competition begets selfishness. Selfishness begets alienation. Alienation begets Hatred. That’s all the collegiate team was – a glorified competition among the ranks. Ryan Howell edged me out in being the team captain. He edged me out again in being selected for the Team. His nauseating acceptance was just a mere stain. He wasted away his chance to represent his country, and in that effect spat on my dreams. Oh retribution will come indeed. With that said and done…no wait. With that, he gripped the medal and walked back inside his room. Honolulu Prince Hotel: The Next Day Sebastian sat across [name withheld] in the lobby of the hotel room. “You must have been very confident I would say yes for you to fly all the way out here.” Sebastian began. “You know me all too well.” Jeff laughed. “Don’t be ridiculous. There’s no way I would fly 15 hours for an answer I could have gotten over the phone. This is Hawaii, and I’m here on my own little escapade as well.” “Then don’t let me waste your time. The answer is yes.” Sebastian retorted. “I still don’t see how this is going to work. You’re putting me into an offset event that will most likely feature the lesser docile kind of man. Half of them people aren’t wrestlers. They’re fighters.” “Sometimes to get a little farther in life, you have to take the wrong turn. Take the road less traveled so to speak. Trust me Sebastian. I’ve never let you down before, and I’m not about to start now. This tournament gives you exposure, and that’s what you need.” Sebastian shook his head. “I don’t need exposure. I need retribution. I need redemption. I need peace. I want something that can give me peace of mind.” “You can get that here. When you finish this tournament and you see all the coaches in America, no the world, coming after you there will be nothing short of peace of mind. Serenity will be a thought away. Anyway, let me tell you who your first opponent is going to be.” Sebastian sat up in his chair, wide-eyed and enthusiastic about whom it was going to be. “Who is it?” “Marq…” Jeff began to read. “No wait, I’m sorry. That’s my other list. It’s a guy named Inphino Blitz. Supposedly he is supposed to be very good.” “How good?” “He’s too good for you kind of good. He was the runner up in last year’s tournament kind of good.” Jeff answered. “You know that kind of good. The kind of good where you stand no chance.” Sebastian laughed. “This is some kind of exposure I’m gonna receive…” “Don’t worry about it. You were the best in the state. Everyone knows how good you are. This event is going to slightly different, but I think you are good enough to adapt to it.” Sebastian leaned over the table extending his hand. “Let me see that picture you have of him.” “Sure.” Sebastian took the tournament documents, newspapers, and everything Jeff had bought with him. Sebastian looked over all the articles and was in awe at how important this tournament had been, and still is to others. Then he took notice of the fighter he will face first. “Inphino Blitz…” Sebastian murmured. “Quick, agile, merciless. Came within a second from winning the tournament last year. He sounds almost impossible to beat. Sounds like a lot of other men I’ve beaten. Yet…they matter nothing considering the circumstances in our sport.” Jeff excused himself from the table and stood up. “Well, he will indeed be a handful. But beat him, and everything else will fall in place. Now if you’ll excuse me, I’m going to enjoy my summer trip.” Sebastian looked up at him. “Will I see in the tournament?” “Yes you will.” “That’s good to hear, Jeff.” Sebastian replied. “You’re a good guy Jeff. Is Ryan going to be here as well?” Jeff shook his head. “I doubt it.” Sebastian laughed. “I would love to see him here as well. See him reach the finals with me, and then destroy him. You will be there, won’t you Jeff?” Jeff nodded. “I’ll be there to see you in the finals.” With that, Jeff took his leave and left Sebastian alone with the clippings of the fighter known as Inphino Blitz. Sebastian looked at the clippings once more. “Defeating you is supposed to make the past right, isn’t it?” He said to himself. “Get into this tournament, receive the exposure that was denied to me. Do all that, and I’m supposed to be at peace with myself? Be at peace with my mind? Who says that is going to work? It sounds too conditional and flawed to work. How is one night going to change a lifetime of suppression? No, it can’t. Sometimes things just have to be. Redemption will come, but peace of mind is more insatiable. Defeating you will put me at peace. This tournament will be my serenity.” The End? Outside the Honolulu Prince Hotel, [name withheld] opened up the trunk of his convertible and tossed his briefcase inside. He slammed the trunk shut and walked over the driver’s side. Before getting into his rent-a-car, he took out his cell phone and dialed a number left to the imagination. “Hello sir?” Jeff said. “Yes sir, he’s in.” With that he hung up, and smiled. It’s here once again. The event that makes winners out of losers, and men out of boys is here once again. Many will kill to go far, and others will be murdered because they are too weak. Competition isn’t a word of sportsmanship anymore. It’s a word of hatred and blood. Boys will become men. Girls will become women. Men will become kings. Kings will become Gods. The GTT is here once again and everything in the world must evolve their orbits to circle it. The GTT has become one with the universe. It has become it’s own sun. The GTT is here and everything that was once unfathomable is now clear. The GTT has become a sun in its own heliocentric model. And we are all now obliged to follow it. The GTT is here once again.
