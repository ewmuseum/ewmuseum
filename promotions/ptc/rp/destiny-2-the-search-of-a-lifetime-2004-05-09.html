Destiny 2: The Search of a Lifetime

9 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-05-09 “Because you are who?” Syko asked in shock “You’re mother…” Syko rolled his eyes and shook his head, a smile almost forming on his face. “My mother? You show up here at my front door and just out of nowhere claim to be my mother?” “I know it’s very awkward and hard to believe me, but I…” “But nothing…” Syko retorted raising his finger in a silencing manner. “This is quite frankly the lowest thing I have ever seen anyone do. What is it you want? You want an autograph?” Marissa started digging into her purse. As she continued to search for whatever it was she was searching for, she replied back. “Syko, I’m not here for an autograph. I know you don’t believe me. I know for sure I sure as hell wouldn’t believe me either if I was in your shoes.” “Then that means you’re here for charity. I am worth a substantial amount after my successes, maybe you want some charity?” Marissa finally dug out some rolled up stapled papers. She looked up at Syko and handed them over to him. Syko looked at her and saw that she was shaking a bit. Syko didn’t believe this liar, but still couldn’t help notice how upset she was. “Here, take this Syko.” Marissa stated. “It’s an adoption decree for St. Patrick’s Orphanage. You were once part of St. Patrick’s Orphanage were you not?” Syko snatched the papers away and crumbled them up. “Yes, I once was in St. Patrick’s Orphanage. But any fool could have looked that up on the Internet. If you want a damn autograph, or charity donation, all you had to do was ask. You are by far one of the lowest people I’ve ever met. And I’ve met a lot of them…” Marissa couldn’t take it anymore. She knew Syko wouldn’t believe her. She knew this would happen, but she had tried to search for him for 26 years. Marissa couldn’t take it anymore, and just began to cry. “That’s not going to get you anywhere. You expect me to accept some stranger, who claims to be my mother, with open arms? You are either very ignorant or confident. Get out of my sight, right now!” “Excuse me, sir!” A voice called out from down the street. Syko looked up to see a man in a business suit walk out of a Lincoln. He was over six feet tall, had short black hair, tanned skin, and appeared to be in great shape. Apparently whoever it was, was traveling with Marissa. The man, a detective, walked up the driveway towards Syko. He walked past Marissa and comforted her a bit, telling her to calm down. “Who are you?” Syko asked. “My name is Detective James Krueger. How are you?” he answered extending his hand in a greeting manner. Syko accepted the handshake and retorted back, “I’m doing alright. Now what the hell is this about?” “Do you mind if we both talk inside?” James asked. “What of her?” “She’s a bit shaken up. I think it’s best I explain the situation to you and then let you work it out with her.” “Alright.” Syko answered while opening the door. “You can come in.” James watched Syko walk inside his house and then turned around. “Ms. Santos, I’m going to try to talk to him. Just wait out here, or better yet, in the car and I’ll be done as soon as I can.” “Please, you have to help me. I know he won’t believe me, and I know we’ve been through dozens of families, but I know this time for sure he’s the one.” Marissa pleaded trying her eyes up. James gave her a hug. “Yes, I know it’s hard for you. I’ll try to explain it to him. Now get back in the car, and wait for us.” Marissa walked back to the car and James walked inside Syko’s home. <<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<0>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>> Syko was seated on his black leather couch when Detective James Krueger walked in. Syko pointed to the sofa across from him, and James took the hint and took a seat. “Get to the point.” Syko demanded. James took out the crumbled pieces of paper Syko had thrown to the floor, and smoothed them out. “These papers are adoption decrees from the Orphanage where you once lived at. I know you haven’t looked at them, but these are real.” Syko scoffed at the notion. “They may be real, but what name is on them?” “That’s completely irrelevant, and we both know why.” James answered. “Because Syko Arsonist isn’t my real name, right?” “Yeah, and neither is Joshua del Fuego.” James retorted. “How did you know that?” Syko asked sitting up straight. “I’m a search detective.” He explained. “I’ve been hunting people down for the past 10 years. I’ve solved kidnappings, run-aways, everything. Ms. Marissa Santos, the lady outside, has hired me for the last 4 years trying to find her son; whom she thinks is you now. So, to put it bluntly, I’ve come across several amounts of information.” “What name is on the adoption papers?” Syko asked. James looked down at the papers in his hand. “The baby that Marissa Santos left for adoption was named Christian Santos. I know that name means nothing to you, because you don’t know your real name, do you?” Syko looked down at the ground. He knew that was a fact. “You’re right, but there’s still something else, though. Something that proves that me being in that orphanage is merely a coincidence.” James nodded. “I think I know what you are talking about.” With that said, he took out another set of papers from inside his black jacket pocket. “What’s that? More ’real’ documents?” Syko asked sarcastically. “Not quite.” James answered. “In December of 1981, that kid, Christian Santos, at four years of age was transferred from St. Patrick’s Orphanage to St. Joseph’s. Now, you were in St. Joseph’s weren’t you?” “I….” “It’s really quite a coincidence is it not?” James continued. “You have been in the same places as the child in question. You have no recollection of your own name, your past, or your family. You even bear the same scar on your face that the child had. I know it’s a lot to bear, but take your time. I’ve been through many cases like this…” “That was very good.” Syko retorted, having not believed anything he heard. “You are a smart detective. Digging all the information on me, thinking I would be easily swayed. Get out of my house, right now. Go out, leave, and take that woman with you. This is the lowest shit anyone has ever done to me.” Syko and James both stood up from their seats. James tried to plead his case, but Syko viciously ushered him to the front door. Standing at the front door, James began to hold his ground. “All the families I’ve helped, none of them started out smoothly. It’s hard to accept a stranger is your mother, or son, or father, or even sister. So, I know what you’re going through. But this lady has been searching for her son for twenty-six years. I know it’s hard for you to accept anyone with open arms, and we’re not asking you to do that. But, think for one second. Think of how she feels…” “Think of how I feel!” Syko growled opening the door. “You show up to my house and tell me my mother is here? My parents are dead, you fucking asshole. Now get the fuck out of there!” With that, Syko pushed James out the door and slammed the door shut. Outside the door, James gathered himself and knocked on the door repeatedly. Syko was still standing by the door on the other side, but he didn’t answer it. “Alright!” James yelled from outside the house. “You have every right to feel like that, and we all understand. But I just want to ask you one favor! I want one favor from you. You can prove this all wrong with just one task!” Syko was definitely interested in knowing how, but he still did not answer the door. “I’m going to leave my business card here on your front porch.” James yelled out putting a small index card on the ground. “It has my name, work phone, and cell phone. You can prove all of this without the shadow of a doubt, by taking a DNA test. When you’re ready to do that, call me, and we can get this all straightened out.” Syko opened the door hastily. “DNA testing?” he asked. “That’s fairly accurate, isn’t it?” “Very much so.” James replied. “It will take a few days, but the result will last a lifetime. The lady in my car has been searching basically her entirely life for her son. The least you can do is take the test and prove once and for you either are her son, or are not.” Syko picked up the card and walked back inside. James sighed and returned back to his car. He stepped inside his car in the driver’s side and locked the door. “It went as expected…” he solemnly told Marissa. Marissa sighed and buried her face in her hands. “Why did this happen to me?!” “Ms. Santos, be calm.” James comforted her. “I know it must be painful for you. He didn’t believe it, as was expected, but he did seemed interested in taking the DNA testing. I think he’ll call us in a few days.” Marissa looked out her window and sighed. Her face was filled with depression. “What good will that do? We’ve tested many men before, and none of them were my son…” “I don’t know what to tell you, other than to just pray and put your faith in God.” “I put my faith in him all my life, and what happens? I lose my son…” <<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<0>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>> Later that day, night time Night fell a long while ago, and Syko was dressed in his robe getting ready to go to sleep. The semi-finals of the Cruiserweight Tournament were just around the corner, and Syko needed all the rest he could get. However, the amount of rest he could get was limited. He had escaped from Karla once again, only to find that she had ordered his gym to be burnt down to the ground. Syko wasn’t physically tired, but mentally he was devastated. His gym, his sole purpose in life, was destroyed. Once again, he had crossed paths with Karla and one of them nearly died because of that. But there was still so much more. Out of nowhere, a woman shows up with a detective at his house claiming to be his mother. That moment wandered around in Syko’s head the entire day. He couldn’t go a moment without remembering what had happened that morning. Syko walked inside his large bedroom and dimmed the lights out. He sat on his bed, leaning against the wall and turned on the TV to watch anything that might be showing. He flipped through the channels until he landed on ESPN, and then just left it there. He accommodated his pillow to support his back and slouched down, feeling a bit drowsy. So many things on his mind, so little time to take care of them. But for now, he had to worry about facing Seymour Almasy. That was the big task right now: to defeat the former Global Champion. Just two more fights. All I have to win is two more fights and I can say I am done fighting. I’ve come too far over the past couple of years to just quit like that. I know I want to, but I can’t do that. If I quit right now, I might get what I want, but I would have just wasted away five years of my life. I’ve gotten my just rewards. I’ve gotten my world titles, and trophies and fan’s adulation. I’ve earned my millions of dollar, my cars, and my homes. I’ve gotten everything that I was promised I would get when I was hired my Chris Davis, the owner of the first wrestling organization I ever worked for. It seemed like a hoax at the time. But, I took it because I had nothing else. I was young and naïve, and needless to say, very greedy. It all seemed like a grand opportune, a dream come true. I took it, and while it took a while for me to become what people may call ‘famous’, the rewards came in by the truckloads. I was surrounded my wealth, luxury, and women. I got everything I wanted. Later on, I found out that what I got, wasn’t really what I wanted. Time passed, and I began to realize the true evils of fighting. Sure, I was getting a lot of money and attention. But I was losing far more important things. I’ve lost my friends, whatever family I had. I lost my time, my education, my home, I even lost the woman I loved. I’ve basically lost everything, and no amount of money I have right now could ever bring any of them back. I want out right now. But, going out right now will just waste away what I have accomplished. Furthermore, it will just leave everyone with a chance to ridicule me and say they were better than me. I won’t go out a loser, I’ll go out a winner. In order to do that, I’ll have to win the Cruiserweight Tournament. I’ll have to advance and beat the former Global Champion Seymour Almasy, and then find a way to conquer either Ethan Knight, or the apathetic Karina Wolfenden. Either one of the latter two will be a huge challenge. Though, first I must beat Seymour Almasy. Out of all the challenges I have faced and beaten, Seymour is most enigmatic. He’s very talented in the ring, and I am afraid that I may not be able to beat him with one Asylum Grounds, or make him submit with my Nightmare Deathlock. I do hope, that no matter what happens, that I can fight him to the last second and give him a run for his money. It’s been a while since I’ve had a set of challenges as grueling as I have had these past couple of weeks, but deep down I find they were worth it. It will be a task to beat Seymour Almasy, considering that my back isn’t at 100 percent, nor will it ever be. Ring! Ring! The ringing of the telephone by Syko’s side broke his focus on the upcoming match with Seymour Almasy. Syko turned the volume on the TV down and leaned over to his side to answer the phone. Syko answered the phone, and was greeted on the other side by Jacob, his much older friend, and former administrative counselor for the Del Fuego gym, which was now burnt to the ground. “What’s going on?” Syko asked. “I was just calling to let you know that the detective wants us back tomorrow morning for some more questioning.” Syko sighed. “More questioning?! We already told him everything we know. Hell, I’ve told him things he didn’t have to know. What more could he want?!” “Well, I don’t know what is it he wants. All I know is that he called me today, told me we were still needed for questioning, and said he couldn’t contact you. Come to think of it, I tried calling you all day…” “I was busy.” Syko replied with nonchalantly with that cliché remark. “What time do we have to be over there again?” “Nine in the morning. Oh, and there was one other thing. A detective and a woman, a brunette, showed up in the police station. They stopped me to ask a few questions about you.” Syko sat up straight on his bed, fully awake. “What kind of questions. Who were they?” “They were both about 40 years old or so. They both looked young for their ages. But, the man was a detective and they were searching for someone. They didn’t ask anything really, just a where you lived, or where they can find you. He seemed to know you pretty well, so I didn’t think anything of him being up to no good. Besides, his badge was real, so if he did have anything wrong, he was going to jail.” “I know…I met the duo today…” “Oh you did? What happened? What did they want? Are you okay?” “Sheesh!” Syko replied mockingly. “Calm down, dad!” “That’s not funny…” “Well, neither is the subject we all talked about.” Syko explained. “That woman, Marissa Santos, came to me with that detective, James Krueger, claiming to be my mother.” “Oh my God.” “My sentiments exactly. Needless to say, I crumbled up the proof they had and sent them on their way.” “That’s some fucked up shit. What kind of proof did they have?” Syko scoffed at the notion of evidence they had. “Just some adoption papers, and a tale about how I got my scar. She seemed very serious that I was her, but they had no proof.” “That’s incredible. Someone shows up at your door, and claims to be your mother? I would have sent them packing too!” “That’s exactly what I did,” Syko explained. “She seemed upset, but how do you think it made me feel? My parents died a long while ago, and she expects me to accept her? I do feel sorry for her though. Apparently, if her story is true, she’s been searching for her son for over twenty years. If that’s true, I feel sorry for the woman, and I hope she finds her son, one day.” “Wow…Syko, I don’t know what to say on that. That’s all pretty deep. Have you though about what could happen if she is your mother?” “Yeah..” Syko retorted. “The detective gave me his number to call him about taking a DNA test. I thought about it, and even if the tests prove that I am her son, I don’t think I can accept her as my mother. I’ve grown up my entire fucking life without her, how the hell can I just forget and forgive like that?!” “Well…” “You’re right, I can’t. Anyway, I do not want to talk about that anymore. I got other things on my mind to worry about. First there’s the question we have to all over again for that retarded detective, and after that there’s my match with Seymour Almasy.” “Good luck with that…” “Thanks, but the tone in your voice indicates that something’s up…” Syko asked. “Well, the rumor going around is that Seymour is changing. He’s becoming more focused, and more in tuned with the world. In other words, he isn’t going to be the same loose cannon with mental afflictions anymore. He’ll be at one hundred percent, and totally mentally free of any problems. To put it bluntly, he’s Superman right now.” Syko smirked. “Are you afraid he might be too much for me to handle?” “I’m not sure.” Jacob answered on the other line. “He’s a strong and talented fighter as it is. But now that he’s seemingly beyond his wild beliefs, and his mentally stable, I think his power and prowess just doubled. You’re a great fighter Syko. I wouldn’t have forced the burden of my debt on you if I didn’t think otherwise. But you, as a fighter, can attest with me that being mentally ready is a big preparation process.” “I am mentally ready.” “Are you sure? You just had your Gym burnt to the ground. Karla tried to kill you again. And, on top of all that, some woman shows up claiming to be your mother. You can’t tell me you are ready after all of that?” “Look…” Syko began. “I’ve been a wreck for the majority of my career. I’ve endured far worst things than what are going on right now. Each single time, I’ve managed to overcome them and defeat my opponents. You’re right. I am not a full strength physically. My back is still injured. I am not totally mentally ready. But, I wasn’t ready last match and I was still victorious. I know I can beat him, and that’s what I damn well am going to do.” Syko said his goodbyes and hung up the phone. He turned the TV off, turned his light off, and went to sleep. <<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<0>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>> 8:25 AM -GASP!- Syko shot up in his bed in a cold sweat. His long crimson hair was like a web of tangled lines, and he was breathing profusely. Obviously, he had just suffered from another nightmare. “Mother…” he whispered. Then he shook his head. “No, she’s not my mother.” He told himself. Syko gathered himself and looked over at the nightstand. There, next to the phone, was a small business card. Syko picked it up and read it: Detective James Krueger. Syko sighed and stared at the phone for a minute. He then picked it up and dialed Detective Krueger’s cell phone. After a few rings, Detective Krueger finally answered. “James Krueger here, what can I do for you.” “This is Syko Arsonist, I’m sure you remember me…” Syko answered. “I do indeed, sir. How are you this morning?” “I’m fine, but let me get to the point…” Syko stopped for a moment and took a deep breath.” “When can we do the DNA testing?”
