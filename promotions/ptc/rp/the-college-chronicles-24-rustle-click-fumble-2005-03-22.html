The College Chronicles #24: 'Rustle, Click, Fumble'

22 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-22 continued 14th March 2005 21:07 The phone rang again. And again. And again. A rustle. A click. A fumble. “Hello?” It was Kate’s voice on the other end. I breathed a sigh of relief. Tony collapsed, sinking into the soft mattress with exhaustion, content at last at having ascertained the safety of his girlfriend. Ooh... It definitely felt strange referring to Kate as Tony’s girlfriend. “Hello? Who is this?” Kate breathed, sounding fearful. My eyes gleamed as I hurried to respond; an anonymous phone call would only serve to intensify her worry. “Er – hi, Kate, it’s Tim,” I said, feeling extremely silly. “Tim?” She sounded surprised. “Where are you?” “I’m calling from the Biltmore Greensboro Hotel.” I paused, unsure of just how much to say as to why I was here a day early. “For your next show?” I nodded, then realised telephones couldn’t see. “Yes, I – er – came down early,” I said lamely. There was a pause. “Is Tony there?” she asked tenderly. I swallowed before responding; looking down at his prone form I could well have been observing the corpse that had been lingering in the back of my mind, and, doubtless, Kate’s too. “Yes, he’s – he’s here –” “How is he?” she cut in eagerly. “He’s...” I paused. I wanted to say ‘fine’, but I just couldn’t. Not with him in his current state. There was something seriously wrong. Something that was greatly affecting Tony’s mind. He’d been doing his best to keep it to himself, but it had undoubtedly been bubbling under for a while, and with the revelation of my knowledge that the word ‘Knucks’ was a clue, everything had boiled over at once for him. It was too much for a pressured young athlete to handle. I could forgive his short temper; I could forgive his menacing attitude. But in order to do both those things, I really had to know what had been going on. “What’s wrong?” Kate interjected, a note of urgency sending the pitch of her voice up an octave. “Well – I don’t know yet,” I admitted. “Have you asked him?” I sighed. “He sort of... freaked out on me, Kate. But –” I was desperate to reassure her. “– he really wanted to make sure you were okay. That was his first priority. I suppose he realises how stupid it was to worry you like that –” “It’s a good thing he did! At least we know, Tim. At least we can help him. At least we can try to protect him.” “But we don’t know, that’s the thing –” “We know something is up. He’s not gonna hide it from the two of us.” Usually, I would have been inclined to agree with her, but on tonight’s evidence, I wasn’t sure just how much Tony had been hiding. My tired brain couldn’t process all this information together. “Yeah,” I replied belatedly. “Er –” “Let me talk to him,” Kate said impatiently. “I’m – I’m not sure that’s such a good idea –” “What?” I glanced down at Tony’s form, which was now rising and falling rhythmically; his eyelids were firmly shut. “He’s asleep.” “Tim, you’d better not be lying to me –” “I’m not!” “Oh come on... does he really not want to speak to me?” Sorrow flecked her thin voice. I felt a sudden pang of pity for this poor girl. It was bad enough having a long-distance relationship, rarely seeing her boyfriend, but at a time like this they badly needed each other. That much was evident. But at least they had a relationship. “Tim?” Bitterly, I turned my mind back to my birthday party a month and a half previously. The night Adam Brent had assaulted Tony, smashing a bottle over his head. The night Kate had once again shown her weakness for the fallen hero. The night Tony had spent with her in my parents’ bed. “No, he doesn’t,” I spat cruelly at the receiver, and shoved it roughly into its dock. I felt instant regret – I was Tim Shipley, I was nice, I didn’t do things like that. Then I put it out of my mind and joined Tony in an early bout of slumber. 15th March 2005 02:47 A tavern in the Wild West, circa 1890. A country band hitting up some funky music. The waistcoated bartender letting the whiskey flow freely as he tops up along the bar, all on the house. “Y’all be diggin’ on down, huh?” a busty woman cries enthusiastically as she descends the stained wooden staircase. Understanding fully what she means (something of a miracle), I nod vigorously and yell something that sounds suspiciously like “Hell, yeah!” “Yee-ha!” a cowboy yells, his white horse rearing up (yes, inside the inn, don’t ask me how) and neighing. The cowboy – complete with dark waistcoat, dual tan leather holsters and brown hat with red feather – fires off three pistol rounds into the roof, and the attendance cheers him enthusiastically. I join in, being a regular of this establishment. “High noon!” someone yells, and everyone repeats the hugely meaningful phrase in unison, showing their agreement. People power. Mob rule. “Not so fast,” drawls another cowboy who has magically appeared upstairs on a black steed of his own. He is the bad guy – of course, I know this already. I hurl insults at him; others hurl bar stools. He takes no notice and leaps the balcony, his horse landing perfectly on all four legs on the remarkably strong wooden planks making up the floor. “What say we make this a duel, Bill?” the bad guy snarls. Bill nods slowly, and instantly we are transported to a deserted part of – er – the desert. The horses align alongside each other, facing opposite directions, and on the first signal begin to trot apart. Another signal indicates that they must turn round – the two duellers are now approximately twenty yards apart. “Draw!” is the prolonged yell simultaneously issued from all four of their cumulative lungs. Everything moves into slow motion as a recognisable sequence comes into action. A rustle: Guns come out of holsters. A click: Hammers flick back; shots ring out. A fumble: Both hit, both in the chest, they collapse off their horses, snatching desperately at the empty air for handholds, to land in two equivalent heaps and send up two equivalent dust-clouds. It is interesting, I notice as my dream comes to a close, how pathetically inaccurate and stereotyped my subconscious perception of the Wild West era really is. I make a mental note to learn more about it; naturally I will forget this note by the time I wake up. 15th March 2005 03:40 “Why hello there,” sneers the little moustachioed Italian man as I make my way across the immaculate lawn. “And what the freakin’ hell do you think you’re doin’ on my lawn?” I have no answer to that. I shrug, expecting a violent outburst from the white-suited Giampiero Pazzini, for I now know that to be his name. But instead, he regards me measuredly, and slowly chuckles. “Go figure. Some dumb kid turns up on my lawn... somebody’s onto us. Paolo!” He clicks his fingers high above his balding head. Almost instantly, a thin, reedy servant appears, running across to him while doing his best to avoid any damage to the lawn Mr Pazzini so treasures. “Mr Pazzini?” “Paolo, go fetch me Tullio and Emile. We’re about to teach this dumb kid the true meaning of pain.” My eyes widen. I start to back off, slowly manoeuvring my way backwards across the lawn. Pazzini just laughs, knowing there is no escape for me, and a second later I know it too, remembering that the only way out is the back gate, which is now manned by a big bodyguard with a sub-machine-gun. Desperately, I start to sprint to the front entrance – stupidly enough; it’s guarded – but a quick burst of machine-gun fire throwing up soil around my frantic feet soon puts paid to that attempt. Pazzini, however, is no longer smiling. “You! Dumb eeediot! What the hell you do that for? Huh?” He strides angrily over to the guard, who has now lowered his gun and is looking worried. “He was trying to escape, Mr Pazzini,” the bodyguard explains hoarsely. Pazzini clicks his tongue and shakes his head, turning around to witness the advent of Tullio and Emile, his favoured personal bodyguards. “Tullio, Emile! I got a leeetle job for you. Two now, in fact,” Pazzini adds, turning around to throw a look of scorn at the guard whose machine gun pellets have mowed up his lawn. “You take care of Gianfranco later.” Gianfranco gulps, knowing his fate. “But for now –” Pazzini nods in my direction. “– we give this dumb kid a leeetle guided tour of the place. Hahaha! Better make it good, guys – the inside of my house will be the last thing this dumb kid ever sees! Hahaha!” Laughing hysterically, Pazzini, his arms folded behind his back, walks slowly up to the veranda overlooking his immense swimming pool. I hear the unholstering of a handgun. Rustle; the now-familiar mantra begins. A moment later, I feel the cold steel of the gun’s barrel against the back of my neck. “No fast moves, kid,” Tullio grunts. “You’re comin’ with us.” I gulp as he flicks the safety catch off. Click. “Who are you? What is this?” I shout, desperate to at least know why I’m being killed. Tullio and Emile appear to find this funny, judging from the belly laughs issued in response. “We’re nothing, kid,” Tullio tells me. “Nothing at all. Just... Knucks.” He takes me by surprise with a shove to the back, and I stumble clumsily to the ground, my jacket catching on Tullio’s holster and then ripping as I brush against his leg in my descent; Tullio shifts his weight to avoid my falling body; the handgun spins out of his hand. Fumble. I dive desperately for it but he recovers to catch it in the same hand before it hits the ground. Now angry, he lines up the barrel pointing right through my skull and into the earth beneath it. It’s time to die. 15th March 2005 08:23 It was the telephone that woke me; its shrill peal pierced my dreams right to their very hearts. Earth split through its core and dissipated in a split-second as the insistent ring assaulted my eardrums. With a groan, I reached above my sleepy head, patting along the wall in my search for the receiver. “H’llo,” I grunted sullenly. “Tim,” came the impatient voice of Dr Kasidy Drake, “I have an important message for you.” Groaning, I sat up in bed. Tony, I noticed with a quick glance, was still sleeping like a baby alongside me. “Go on, Kasidy,” I instructed him resignedly. “It’s from your parents,” he told me. That came as something of a shock. “My parents?” I repeated in disbelief. “Are you sure?” “Well, your mom is Maggie Shipley, right?” “Yes... yes... what did she want?” “She wouldn’t say, but she said you had to call her back right away. I’ve got the number –” “I know the number, Kas,” I interrupted with another groan. “Okay, thanks for the message, I’ll – I’ll get right on it. Bye.” “See you at the show.” I forced myself out of bed, away from the simple comforts of duvet and pillow. Although I had no intention of calling my mother ‘right away’, as I had been instructed, I would phone back pretty soon. My parents barely ever called me, and in the event that they did and I happened to be out, they would choose to call back another time rather than bother to leave a message. In this case, then, there was evidently something quite important my mother needed to speak to me about, but I couldn’t for the life of me imagine what it might be. How memories fail... It is incredible that such a highly-developed brain as mine (oh, for God’s sake, I’m past modesty) could have failed to make the most basic of connections after the sombre Christmas Day I’d spent at my parents’ apartment in Albany. At the time, I’d known there was clearly something wrong with my father, but for some reason or other I hadn’t taken enquiries further, and though I didn’t know it yet, I was about to find out the horrible truth about what had been ailing Clemence Shipley these last few months. I stepped into the shower, blissfully unaware of what was to come, the piping hot jet of water eliciting a gasp from my parched lips. I slurped from the cold tap, blissfully unaware of what was to come, recoiling at the metallic taste of the piped water not technically meant for drinking. Finally I picked up the phone, blissfully unaware of what was to come, and dialled my parents’ number. The phone rang. And again. And again. A rustle. A click. A fumble. “Shipley residence,” came my mother’s voice, devoid of its usual jovial rhythm. Already, I felt a touch of alarm. “Mum?” “Tim,” Maggie sighed with relief. “Tim, I’m afraid it’s – it’s –” She broke off, and I heard a sob from the other end of the invisible, intangible and nonexistent line that connected us via two distant telephone receivers. “What is it?” I probed as my heart raced. “What’s happened?” “I’m afraid it’s your father,” she continued as her voice cracked. “I should have told you this before – oh, I’m sorry –” “What’s wrong with him? Mum?” My hold on the receiver began to slip as my sweaty palm gave up on the task it was just not up to. Desperately, I swapped hands and clenched the receiver tight, pushing it up against my ear, anxious to absorb every detail of what was ailing my father. “Your father has – heart disease, Tim.” That was it. The killer – no pun intended, trust me. That was the moment my world froze. Priorities? I had none, bar this. Worries? They all paled in comparison. Core, Harbrook, Sammie, Kate – nothing and no-one mattered anymore. My future? It could wait. It was my father’s that was in danger. “I’m coming home right now, mum,” I told her decisively, but she surprised me by interrupting. “No, Tim, you stay where you are. There’s no – no urgency to the situation –” “What? Then why did you call me?” There was silence. “I thought it was best you know,” she said, confused, as if her answer was the most obvious thing in the world. “No,” I interjected, “I mean – you’ve been keeping it from me this long. Why today of all days?” “He – er – he had a scare. Yesterday. He had to go into hospital –” “And you didn’t think to let me know yesterday? When I was still in Albany?” I said threateningly, my blazing eyes conveying their anger through my stone-cold voice. “Tim! You have to understand; I didn’t want to worry you –” Ignoring her pathetic attempt to justify her reticence, I went on. “There’s no immediate danger?” “None,” she confirmed after a pause. “And you’re not lying to me?” “Of course not!” she replied, affronted. “I would never –” “Okay, okay. I – I guess I’ll stay out here – I’m in North Carolina until Wednesday night –” “Yes, yes, you carry on with your job, we can’t risk you losing it over this; you’ll see your father on Thursday, then,” my mother told me matter-of-factly. But I wasn’t really listening. I’d just made a catastrophic error. Perhaps, in retrospect, my silence at this point was ill-advised; it gave Maggie Shipley time to digest the information I had just given, and allowed her to wonder... “Tim?” “Yes,” I replied, shaking. “What are you in North Carolina for?” My parents didn’t know I was a wrestler. As far as they were concerned, the new job I’d taken up alongside my studies in September was just another part-time money-earner to support my college life – and, of course, their well-being, with the small package of money (it would have been bigger if I had my way) that I transferred to their joint bank account every month. It was time to think on my feet: something I was notoriously bad at. “I’m – I’m working for FedEx,” was the best I could come up with. As soon as the words had left my mouth, I realised a whole host of reasons why this cover had been a terrible, terrible choice. My mother, with the attention span of a hawk, didn’t miss a single one. “FedEx?” I was silent. “But you can’t drive!” Ah. Thanks, mum. “They’re – er – they’re teaching me.” “Hmm. So how did you get to North Carolina?” “I – my senior drove me down; I accompanied him on a delivery so I could – er – learn about stuff.” “Stuff?” “Procedure and all that. And he talked me through some of the basics of driving a big truck, too.” “Hmm. And how long have you been working with them?” “Since – er – since September.” “And they’ve been training you all this time?” “Er – pretty much.” “And you still can’t drive?” “Well, some of the time was spent in the depot, sorting and loading...” “I see. But Tim... are you sure FedEx is the best choice?” “Er...” Damn, I thought, has she got onto the ‘long hours’ line? “How are you going to fit in time for your study with eighteen-hour shifts?” Bingo. “And – and what are you doing out there now? It’s a Tuesday! Don’t you have lectures?” “I – er – I manage...” “I hope you’re not getting behind, Timothy...” “Mum! It’s fine. I’m fine. Really.” “Hmm...” “Er,” I stated defiantly, and that ended the argument. Leaving us in the supremely awkward position of having to return, as every phone call must, to the original subject. “Thanks for letting me know about dad,” I said slowly. “That’s okay,” she responded in a similar monotone. There was a short silence. “I’ll – I’ll come and see you on Thursday... you and dad...” “You don’t have to,” she protested half-heartedly, but we both knew I would go. 15th March 2005 10:11 Tony rolled over in his bed; I cracked open a can of Pepsi from the minibar – normally I wouldn’t allow myself such an extravagance even though Core absorbed the expenses, but today I felt I deserved it; I felt around blindly with my free hand and found my jeans on the chair behind me: a rustle, a click, a fumble. A couple of minutes later, having finished dressing myself, I was impatient to hear the details of what had been worrying Tony. Never mind the ‘it’s always best to let someone under stress sleep it off’ – I was probably more worried than he was by now, and I needed to know whether he – we, perhaps – was in any immediate danger. “Tony.” I spoke in a hushed tone, clearly not sufficient to penetrate a singularly deep sleep. I didn’t recall him having taken any pills to help this, but so much had gone on the night before, it could well have been the case. “Tony,” I said again, this time at normal volume, as I gently touched his shoulder and rocked it back and forth, softly but insistently. With an involuntary moan, he shuddered and awoke, his eyelids slowly parting to greet the harsh daylight streaming through the net curtains. “Tim,” he grunted, shielding his eyes from the daytime glare with his left hand. “What time is it?” Just about able to make out his question through the heavy muffling created by the fact that his face was still deep in his pillow, I let him know that it was twenty-two minutes past ten. His eyebrows rose. “I – I overslept,” he informed me. I raised my own eyebrows and smiled in response. “You do realise this is my room?” he continued with a frown, noting that the other side of his double bed had been slept in. I winked. “Imagine what they’ll think,” I joked, nudging him sharply. He laughed and shied quickly away from my elbow. “You need to get up,” I said amicably. “Why?” he grinned. “I can lie here all day if – if –” He yawned widely. “– if I want to.” The smile disappeared off my face. It was time to get down to business. “No, you can’t,” I corrected him, “because you’re going to tell me exactly what Knucks is, and what we can do about it. Or them. Or him.” His face slumped, remembering the reality of the shadow of an existence his life must have recently become with what was seeming more and more like a death threat hanging over him. “I – Tim, I can’t talk,” he said unhappily, rousing himself from his peaceful rest and sitting up against the bed-head. Despite the gravity of the situation, I couldn’t help but admire the glaringly muscled nature of his bare upper body. Although he chose – as I did nowadays – to wrestle topless, in the ring I had never really absorbed quite how toned my tag partner was. There was always too much to concentrate on to make such trivial observations. Like now. I tugged my mind away from triviality and returned to reality. “Tony –” “Okay, okay,” he nodded slowly. “I’ll tell you. But – later. Outside. Somewhere safe...” “Isn’t here safer?” I questioned, worried. “No, no,” he mumbled, waving me off with his hand. “I – later. Okay?” “Okay,” I muttered neutrally. I felt a sudden queasiness and knew I wouldn’t press him any further. I wouldn’t have felt up to telling him about my father, so it was wrong of me to make him feel so bad about keeping this to himself. “Now I need something to eat,” Tony declared, pulling himself out of bed with admirable vigour considering that he had been in a deep sleep just a minute earlier. “Then we can talk about our strategy for tomorrow!” “Kusaka Masato,” I blurted out jokingly, and he laughed. Kusaka Masato was the man we would both be facing the next night on PRIDE, in a mini-gauntlet initiated by him after his disappointment at our inability to find him a tag team partner. Masato was quickly becoming a running joke backstage – although I felt a touch of pity for him and his misguided ego, I was somewhat relieved that in the last few weeks I’d managed, in no small part due to his arrival on the scene, to move away from my image as the ‘ultimate loser’ among the wrestlers. No longer was I the butt of every joke, and although there had been rumblings of discontent (I’d been shocked myself) when I had been announced as Core’s representative in the Seven Of The Best tournament – the winner of which would receive a Primetime Central Global title shot; not something to be sneezed at – I had now been generally accepted as a man of at least some talent. I heard a cellphone beep. “It’s mine,” Tony announced through a mouthful of toast and jam, and strode over to his jacket (slung over the back of the room’s solitary chair) from which he retrieved his phone. His eyes widened. “What is it?” I asked with concern. He swallowed all his toast quickly – some struggle between peristalsis and an oversized food bolus. “It’s from Core,” Tony declared. “And... and they’ve changed our match!” “What!” I exclaimed, horrified. I rushed over to see the message for myself. “Yeah,” he went on, “they didn’t want to go with the Masato gauntlet. We’re facing Dynamite instead...” I looked at Tony. Tony looked at me. Knowing what was to come, I smiled inwardly, and our synchronised shrug with a non-committal “Meh!” sent us both into fits of laughter. When the hilarity had finally subsided, I, regaining my composure, just needed to check one thing... “Tony... we can beat them, right?” He stared at me. “Dynamite? Wells and Cormac? Those Australian degenerates who turned on us last time around? Er, Tim... of course we’ll beat them.” I smiled, reassured. “Okay, okay.” “And what about Best Of The Seven, huh?” he asked with a grin. I shrugged. “I don’t know. What is it?” Tony frowned. “The tournament you entered...” I smirked. “Nope. Unless – unless you’re talking about Seven Of The Best...” Tony pushed me playfully into the wall. Laughing, I sat down heavily on the bed, and he joined me. “Best of the seven, though?” I mused wistfully. “Wouldn’t it be nice...” “You can do it, Tim,” Tony encouraged me, but I shook my head. “Not me. Not now. I’m not the right person for this...” “You damn well are!” he broke in with hostility. “You’re a great wrestler, Tim! Why else would Core have nominated you? Why else would you have got those Classic and PRIDE title shots? You just don’t realise how good you are...” “I’m not in the mood, Tony,” I said shortly. “Deluding me won’t help –” “I’m not deluding you!” he insisted. “You’re damn good! Hell, you’re the one carrying this team, buddy!” I looked at him, stunned. I was the one carrying The Pioneers? All this time I’d felt like I was holding Tony back. Surely it wasn’t the other way round...? Too late, I realised Tony had simply been fishing for an exchange compliment. I smiled graciously. “We’re even, and that’s that,” I told him, pursing my lips, and he nodded, happy with that assessment. “So for this Seven Of The Best... what happens? It’s a seven-man tournament?” “Not quite,” I told him. “There are fourteen wrestlers in it from the start, in seven singles matches. The seven winners go on to an elimination match, of which the eventual winner gets the Global title shot.” “Right,” Tony nodded. “Who do you face first round?” “woof woof.” “Woof Woof?” “No. woof woof.” He frowned. “Yeah, Woof Woof, that’s what I –” “That’s not his name, Tony. It’s woof woof.” Tony wrinkled his nose. “woof woof?” “Yes.” “You’re sure?” “Yes.” “Oh. What kind of a name is that?” “A silly one.” There was a pause. “Yeah,” Tony agreed. “Silly. Where does he wrestle, buddy?” “Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling,” I replied in a hushed tone, emphasising (or perhaps mocking) the pretentiously over-the-top name. “Exreme Ultimo Wrestling... EUW... got it,” Tony nodded, but he hadn’t quite got it. “Xtreme,” I corrected him. ”No ‘e’?” he asked in surprise. “No.” “XUW, then?” “Yes.” There was a pause. “Yeah, okay. So what do you know about this guy?” I raised my eyebrows. “What do you know about him?” Tony shrugged his shoulders. “Nothing.” “Hmm, same here,” I sighed. “Oh – well, I do know one thing...” “What’s that?” “Ooh, no, two, in fact.” “What are they?” “Oh, don’t worry, you know them too,” I teased him. “What?” he said, confused. “I don’t know anything about a ‘Woof Woof’!” “No, nor do I,” I said, shaking my head, “but I do know two things about a ‘woof woof’.” Tony growled and gave me a quick cuff round the ear. I took the stinging blow willingly, still smiling. “So, we know two things –” “I don’t!” he protested. “Yes, yes you do.” “Alright, well, remind me,” he ordered, rolling his eyes. “You’re going to kick yourself...” “Try me,” Tony said shortly. Smiling, I pursed my lips. “Well, the first thing I know... is that he has a silly name.” Tony looked up at the ceiling. “Yeah, okay,” he conceded grudgingly through gritted teeth. “One thing. What’s the other?” “That his name has no capital letters in,” I revealed triumphantly. Tony turned his head slowly towards mine. “Does it really matter whether his name has capitol letters in?” he yelled. Unable to control myself, I was now laughing openly, and although he was keeping up his pretensions of anger, I knew very well that Tony was desperate to burst into hysterics. His shining eyes sold him out. It was time now to utterly confuse the poor man. “No, Tony, it doesn’t matter whether his name his ‘capitol’ letters in,” I replied softly. “It was rhetorical!” he broke in. I brought a patronising finger up to my lips. “Wait, wait – I didn’t finish. It doesn’t matter whether his name has ‘capitol’ letters in, but it sure as hell does matter whether it has capital letters in.” “I’m lost,” Tony admitted, bemused. I rolled my eyes. “How do you spell ‘capital’, Tony?” “C – A – P – I – T – O – L,” he recited, not missing a beat. “Exactly,” I said haughtily. “Moving right on, the third thing I know about woof woof is –” “Hey! Hold up! You only said there were two things!” I sniffed. “I didn’t call for a Spanish Inquisition!” Collapsing into giggles, I rolled around helplessly on the bed as Tony watched me with all the sense of humour of a prawn sandwich. “You do worry me sometimes, buddy,” Tony told me as he reached for the TV control. “Ditto,” I retorted, pulling open a bag of roasted peanuts. Rustle. Here we go again. Tony stared at me. “Tim, it’s mid-morning.” “I’m aware of that,” I replied, tipping a handful of peanuts into my open mouth. Tony shrugged and hit a button on the remote. The television snapped into life. Click. Uninterested in pop culture’s daytime offerings, I lay back on Tony’s bed, resting my head on my arm. In just over a week’s time, I would be given the opportunity of a lifetime. Seven Of The Best could act as a springboard to a career of gold and glory; it was already acting as a set-up for Core’s own Gold & Glory on March 30th, where the law of common sense stated that Tony and I would receive our rematch with Josh and Hiroshi Ryusaki for the Tag Team titles. I would be doing my utmost to take both these opportunities with arms wide open. Realistically, I was kidding myself to think that in the space of a week I could win out over thirteen of PTC’s finest and then capture the first ever gold of my wrestling career by defeating a legend-in-the-making and the hardest-kicking man in existence, but there was no harm in trying. Nor was there any room for error if I wanted to realise but one of these goals. There would be no falling at the final hurdle. No falter, no failure – that was how I had to approach the upcoming week. If somebody tosses you a bone, you catch it. That was exactly what I intended to do with my PTC and Core Wrestling bones. Hands cupped; body steady; muscles primed. A clean catch. No fumble. Not this time. A rustle and a click... but no fumble.
