Darkness and light

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 It cannot be seen, cannot be felt, Cannot be heard, cannot be smelt. It lies behind stars and under hills, And empty holes it fills. It comes first and follows after, Ends life, kills laughter. - - - - - Have you ever stopped what you were doing simply so that you could appreciate the beauty of a perfect sunset? Staring in awe as the life giving Sun, a giant ball of flame that over arches the planet that we stand on, starts to majestically descend out of sight in a final blaze of glory. The horizon blazes with color, vibrant reds and oranges that paint the sky with a thousand shades of burnished gold. The few clouds that grace the sky are silhouetted from behind and stand out, like sentries guarding something precious, stately and grand. Finally, in its own time, the Sun slips from view until all that is left is the awesome memory of its passing, as the night sky begins to darken, fading from azure to black. Or does it? Even at the midpoint of night, in the heart of winter, is the sky ever truly black - truly dark? No matter how cloud-filled the night sky, no matter how much the Moon has waned; it is never black, never dark. A multitude of stars fill the Universe with a cosmic lightshow that never dims, the night sky itself is a myriad tapestry of dark velvety shades, purples, blues and greys; never truly black, never truly dark. Close your eyes. Tighter. That’s it. Now think back to a memory of a sunset, or a sunrise. Try to recall what your saw and how you felt at that moment. Do the colors came back to you as if you were actually standing there, does joy fill your soul when the memory comes to life within your mind? You see, even with your eyes closed it is never truly black, truly dark. Even with your eyes closed you have the memory of color, the perfect sunset is yours forever. What if, though, your eyes had never been open? What if, however, you had never been graced with knowing what the word ‘color’ truly meant simply because you had nothing to refer it too? If you had never seen the sky would you truly understand the concept of ‘blue’? If you had never seen the light how, then, how would you know the darkness? - - - - - The trees below me reached up, their branches hand-like, and tried to capture me; pull me down and make me Earthbound. With a cry of challenge, and triumph, I soared out of reach and laughed as I saw the ground recede further below. It couldn’t hold me, it couldn’t master me. I was above it. Always. I think that I smelled the flames before I saw them; I definitely didn’t feel them until I swooped down closer, rapturous joy filling my soul as I watched the Earth burn. Plumes of red and orange danced together, tearing through everything in their path. I joined them and the inferno raged on. Exuberantly I opened my wings and let the updrafts from the heat carry me home, a scream of sated desire rising up in my chest and … … I woke up. I didn’t recall actually going to sleep and to be honest there was no difference that I could actually tell. The darkness was so complete as to make sleep and waking virtually the same to me. There was never any difference and, so, I had stopped contemplating if I slept or not. Then the dream came back to me; the memory of soaring high above the ground, free from any shackles, returned to haunt me. I felt loss, complete and utter, tear my soul in half as the colors, so majestic, returned to blaze once more in my mind. For an instant, a solitary moment, I remembered. I remembered everything – who I was, what I had been - everything. Then, like the dream itself, it began to slip away until all that was left was the memory of the memory. I knew that I had had it but I couldn’t recall what it had been. I couldn’t recall whom – what – I had been. I wiped away moisture from my face, surprised by the salty taste and wondered what it could be, sure that I should know. With a sigh I dug my nails into the palm of my hands, beginning my timeless ritual, and breathed in the sensation of pain, ignoring the darkness as best I could, not wishing for anything else; after all when darkness is all you have seen, how can you know the light? - - - - - "So, did you find the place ok?" The voice that belonged to the words was gentle and warm at the same time, confident without being overbearing, and I allowed the tones to wash through me for a few seconds as I felt myself begin to relax. Even though I had told Khaled that there would be nothing to worry about, that I had everything under control, it wasn’t until I saw the white cane that I knew that my words were true. The WWA, in their infinite wisdom, may have decided that I had to see a psychologist in order to say in active competition, but they couldn’t make sure that she saw me. I chuckled softly at my own attempt at humor. "Something amusing, Mr Ember?" the psychologist in question, a Doctor Carole Gordon, asked me, cocking her head to one side. "Just recalling the fact that I must have found the only cab driver in the city who could get lost" Ember lied, smoothly, ruefully realising that the myths about people who had lost one sense making up for it by having highly developed other senses was a reality and filed that knowledge away. It wouldn’t be a mistake that he would make again. "Well I am glad that you made it," the doctor said with a smile that lit up her face. Ember stared at her intently, completely unselfconsciously, as he knew that she wouldn’t see him doing it, and closely examined her eyes. The size and shape was as with any regular eyes but the iris was completely devoid of color, as was the pupil. With a smile, silent this time, Ember realised that her eyes were exactly the same color as his own. "Anyway, would you like to follow me into the office?" she asked, indicating behind her to a door that was half open. "We can be more comfortable there." Without waiting for his answer she turned and walked to the door, reaching out to push it open, before going through into another dimly lit room beyond. Ember stared in grudging respect as he realised that she had not made use of the cane. If he hadn’t known that she was blind he wouldn’t have been able to tell from her gait, she walked with a confidence that he hadn’t seen in many sighted people. Following her into the office he looked around himself, taking in the sparsely furnished room in a glance. "What, no couch?" he quipped. The room was decorated in a pale cream with a hint, he thought, of green, which he found somewhat soothing. Two bay windows overlooked the street below but drapes had been pulled allowing only faint light from outside to filter in. A desk took up on section of the wall to his left, with a large, well stocked, bookcase behind it. To his right, and dominating the room, was a collection of chairs. "No, I found too many of my patients were falling asleep during the session" she laughed, lightly and easily. "Please, though, have a seat." Watching as the doctor sat down in an oversized armchair, a small table to one side of it, Ember walked over and began to sit in a similar chair opposite her. As he did so he heard her make a small noise. "Ah ha" was all she said, nodding slightly to herself. Ember stopped his downward momentum, hovering absurdly between a seated and standing position, and stared at her, wondering what he had done. He looked around him at the other changes, noting the difference in size and color between them and wondered if it was some form of test; the psychologist version of a rat in a maze perhaps? Did his choice of seat give something away before he even spoke. "What?" he asked suspiciously, slowly rising back to a standing position, waiting for Doctor Gordon to explain herself. Her reaction, when it came, wasn’t what he was expecting. She burst out laughing, the noise ringing clear and true. "Sorry, I couldn’t resist, it always works you know" she said when she had caught her breath, "all I have to do is make an approving noise when people first go to sit down and they assume that I am testing them." "No, I didn’t assu …" Ember started to contradict belligerently, a little annoyed with himself that he had seemingly fallen for a practical joke. He was just about to go into what could prove to be the biggest match of his career, taking on a former PCW champion and admitted legend, when he faced Darkstar for the privilege in progressing in the GTT tournament and instead of training, or even simply relaxing and getting his body back to one hundred percent, he was wasting his time with a shrink with a sense of humor. With a level of self-control that amazed him he bit his lip and remained silent. "Of course you did, no need to be embarrassed about it" she interrupted, "but something that you should have realised is that I didn’t even know which seat you were going for. I can’t see you, remember." Tapping the white cane that she rested against her left thigh she smiled self-deprecatingly. "Right, yeah" was all that Ember could say as he fell into the original seat. Normally full of self-controlled, too much so according to some people, he wasn’t used to being off-balance like this but for some reason he was. Everything is building up, too much has happened in the past few weeks he thought to himself as he forced his body to relax even for me. Khaled’s words, the words that had nearly provoked him too far against his only true friend, came back to him. "… sleeping in the street in the middle of the day, having nightmares every time you sleep, seeing strange women who no-one else can see and now zoning off into your own little world … that can’t be normal can it?" Ember sighed, softly, to himself making sure that the pyschologist in front of him didn’t hear. Normal. That is what Khaled had said and Ember admitted, in the quiet recesses of his mind where no-one else could hear him, that even by his standards it wasn’t normal, no. At least he mused I only passed out once and the nightmares stopped after a couple of weeks too. He knew that they were the least of his problems though, but at least he hadn’t seen the ‘strange woman’ as Khaled called her, since she had attacked him in the kitchen. Without conscious thought Ember rubbed at his masked face, above the section of his cheek that still bore the fading scratch from her nails. He hadn’t known how to explain that to Khaled so had said the best thing possible in that situation – nothing at all. "Shall we make a start then?" the doctor asked with a smile, interrupting his reverie, as she reached out to the table and picked up a small, metal object. "Do you mind if I record our conversation, it is obviously easier than writing it all down." "No, not at all, that is fine" he replied with a forced neautrality. He would have preferred to have not had to start at all, let alone have his words recorded, but he knew that he didn’t really have a choice in being here – he just had a choice in what he said. A small smile played across his lips as he thought of the bill that the doctor would send to the WWA, a bill that he fully intended to ensure was a complete waste of money; it would be a fine line, though, to keep the doctor satisfied while telling her nothing of worth whatsoever. "Excellent," she said as she flicked a small switch and turned the recorder on, "so then Mr Ember, where yould you like to begin?" she relaxed back into the chair and waited patiently for Ember to reply, obviously willing to let him take a lead in what was said. Ember himself raised an eyebrow, hidden behind the mask, and wondered how long the gentle nature of the session would last before she started digging into his true thoughts. He was about to reply to her question when he was interrupted. "I always find the beginning an excellent place, don’t you?" Whatever level of self control Ember had thought he had had was lost as the dulcet female tones floated across the room. He spun around in the chair and stared at Doctor Gordon’s desk. Sitting on the edge of it, one leg crossed demurely over the other – but with a look of fierce, sadistic, humor that contradicted the pose – was the black clothed woman from his dreams. At least they were dreams as far as Khaled was concerned; Ember, though, knew better. It didn’t matter that Khaled hadn’t seen her when she was right in front of him, it didn’t matter that – Ember knew – no-one else would either, he knew that she was real. As he continued to stare at the woman in shock he caught a movemnt out of the corner of his eye and turned his attention to the blind pyschologist, staring at the pose of her body; her neck craned to one side, listening. Amazment rushed through him as he relised what that meant. She had heard the voice too! - - - - - Riddle by JRR Tolkein - The Hobbit
