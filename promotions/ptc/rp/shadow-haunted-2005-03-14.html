Shadow Haunted

14 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-14 Trailing his slow anchor-black shadow behind him, he walked down the street feeling the breeze rustle his being as though it was an irate phantom, trying to push and pull him every which way else. Light from street lamps and car headlights made the pavement dance with night-time nightmare dementia. He dared not look, but he knew that the shadows were closing in around him, closer and closer with every step that he took. He finds that shadows are always too near for comfort, these days, and comfort cannot be found here in this place. Perhaps that is the reason that he walks here as often as he does. Maybe it’s to see those with real problems, to remind himself that there are always people far worse off. He stalked close enough to the sad and pathetic cardboard refuges of the damned, the addicts, the disaffected and the destitute to be able to feel their misery in the ether. The city’s societal detritus that it brazenly discards and tries so very hard to forget about live there. They sprawl in the shadows of the streets like cockroaches. This is where they seem to congregate, like dying flowers still trying desperately to grasp the light that they have been reaching toward for all of their existence. Still striving, no matter of the futility, to touch the sun just once. He often looks upon them trying to feel sorry for them. But he cannot. Their problems are tragic, yet they are palpable, the conditions of which are easily grasped and abhorred. His, though… no one feels pain like poets do. And what is he, if not a poet of pain? The sceptical stares and unfriendly faces haunt him from those shadows, piercing him. Yet he barely notices such a thing anymore. So many stares from so many people, all filled with hate… he has become used to that kind of treatment. He thinks only of how the world should be. And the way the world is. His thought process is halted, though, by a squalid canine rubbing against his leg on its way past him. The busy solitude of the creature strikes a chord with him. Searching for a companion, company of any kind, and in its quest exasperatedly the animal sometimes chattered at its vague reflection in a shiny surface. A likeness is drawn to himself, as he wanders the haunted halls of his mind searching for something. Something that even he has lost sight of. It is at the point where he feels nothing. And all he can do now is succeed or fail. He will feel no sorrow of loss, nor will he feel the exultation of victory. No sadness. No joy. Just the certainty of himself. Then, suddenly, his thoughts were like paper. Folding, unfolding, ripping, falling in pieces to the floor like peaceful snow. In the ephemeral moment before the pain, he vaguely thought that he should be used to the sensation now. Then that thought was shattered as images like hot shrapnel drilled into his mind. Sharp pains stabbed though his brain as he stopped dead in his tracks. No longer was he staring at his feet upon a dingy, squalid pavement. The ground beneath him was now as white as his mask, the carpet new and never before trodden on. Before him, a door. Blood trickled from underneath it. Whispers echoed in his mind, bidding him onward. Gingerly he reached out for the door handle, the lifeblood on the floor running around the soles of his boots, and the metal handle protesting as he pushed it down. As the door creaked, sliding open, his gorge rises within him, and he prevented himself from vomiting only through a preternatural effort of will. It’s the stench that hit him first, so unable is he to take in the imagery of the scene. The stink roved like a living thing, polluting and debasing the room. And blood everywhere… streaking across the floor, smeared and splattered all over the walls, spurted upon the ceiling. The giant leathery engine of his lungs began to work overtime, rumbling as he takes in the sight. Corpses littering the room, spilled viscera slopped about the floor, intestines hanging from the walls and roof. The bodies, myriad and defiled stared at him blankly, or would, if they still had eyes. Those that don’t still seemed to be seeking him. Men. Women. …Children… His masked scowl is corrupt with an acrid sense of disgust as he surveys the putrid scene. His gaze wandered to the farthest corner, where he sits with his back to himself, rocking, singing a lullaby long forgotten in his mind. The counterfeit version of him turned its head for scant few seconds before turning it back. ‘Thought you’d never get here…’ it rasped, a voice like nails on a blackboard. ‘But I knew you’d come. After all, how can you argue with results like these?’ Casually and deliberately, it began to rise from the ground, leather gloves scratching for purchase on the cold, wet walls. ‘It’s not the ends that I’m worried about,’ he replied to himself. ‘It’s the means.’ The daemon laughed with a sickening, throaty gargle and turned around to face its alter ego. It stares holes through him, the mask’s wider yet emptier eyes like submarine windows, leading to an ambiguously life-threatening darkness. It squeezed the remnants of whatever entrails it was playing with, making the juice squirt and run out between his fingers. The mask’s mock smile sends out waves of disease and disorder, as it always does, as if it was some kind of radio for the transmission of chaos. ‘Poor Deszaras,’ it spat with rancour, ‘always feeling so sorry for yourself, hmm? Always fighting what you are and are destined to be.’ ‘I would rather fight to be what I want than submit to what I think I should be, Dhar.’ replied Dez with ample spite. ‘The day that I simply let you take over us is the day that the Devil shits snow.’ The monster and the man stood glaring at each other, trying once again to force looks to kill. ‘We are a gun, Dez. When we’re pointed at someone, they go away. Now we’ve been aimed at some very big, very bad people. Much worse and a lot more talented than we are.’ Suddenly Dhar appeared mere inches away from Deszaras, standing at his side and reaching out to him. As the beast’s hand touched his shoulder, Deszaras dropped to his knees, holding his abdomen. Dhar stood over him watching the spasms as he fell to the floor, his insides roiling. ‘You are a blank, Dez. I, on the other hand, am a killer round. Step aside, before you make us both look stupid.’ it said, giggling as it watched his doomed struggles to retain the form of his organs as they shape-shifted and broke and tore and exploded within him. As his guts dissolved to protoplasmic jelly, blood ran from the holes of his mask and he gargled pathetically in an attempt to speak. ‘Don’t be so pitiful,’ goaded the daemon. ‘The only one to blame for this… this mess is yourself. The mess of your mind is down to the weakness of needing someone, of letting them in, and then being buggered when they are taken from you one way or an other.’ Dhar swiftly and harshly kicked Deszaras in the ribs, which crack, and are subsequently added to the chaotic soup of his internal struggle. ‘The mess of your mind, by letting such unimportant bullshit get to you so often.’ Another kick, more crunches. ‘You are the mess, little man, and I am the cleansing. Now, stand aside and let me deal with these new threats. Show some brains for once in your miserable life.’ Blood sprinkled out of his mask-holes as Deszaras shook his head as furiously as he could manage until Dhar grabbed him viciously by the throat. ‘You should be scourged from our being, weakling filth.’ it says, voice raised from it’s normal malign whisper to an irritated pitch of animosity. ‘Scourged for harbouring personal passion. Such a thing should be forbidden in the life we chose. Yet you seem almost determined to fuck everything up! Determined to fall on your useless face and drag me into the dirt with you! Well make no mistake – I will not go quietly into your night. I will create my own and force everything there to bend to my will.’ Dhar snarls as Dez is thrown back to the floor. ‘And I’ll begin with your useless carcass.’ He forced his eyes to open from their painfully tight squint, only to see the very uninteresting street upon which he had begun his walk. The same street that he walks to when he needs somewhere to think, and the street that he had all but forgotten about for the past while. No longer was his existence hounded by baying, depraved shadows, there was no hissing traffic passing and there was light in the air. And then he was forced to think of how long he had been standing there. He looked upward, only to see that the dawn sky was being choked by black-bottomed clouds. He checked himself, feeling his unbroken ribs, touching his mask that is not drenched in his own blood, and with every passing second it seemed that the stranglehold on the sky became tighter and more vicious, the clouds becoming more foreboding all the while. Then, assassin-soft, she approached him. He can taste her in the air, now. Kiri’s velvet whisper soothed his pseudo pain as she spoke. ‘Why do you still come here, Deszaras?’ she asked softly. ‘Is it not time to put the past behind us?’ ‘And lose the images in my mind? Lose my desire? Desire that can bring frenzy, especially when frustrated? Surely I must court frenzy in order to pass beyond delirium into incandescent purity?’ he said in reply. She walked closer to him. ‘Desire is cousin to lust. Do you really lust after this destruction? Self-destruction? Are you really so determined to destroy yourself in search of things that are out of reach?’ She sighed, deep and heavy with emotion. ‘It pains me to see you this way…’ ‘What way?’ ‘A mental anorexic. Wanting forever less and less inside your head, even though what you have is perfectly fine if only a little scarred. So you starve and deprive yourself of those things that make you happy or inspired or interested… only so that you can punish yourself more.’ Kiri began to back away, looking deep into his eyes. ‘And so it is a pain in me. For I don’t know what to say or do to force you to see clearly.’ As Kiri left Deszaras standing there, he felt like she was leaving his funeral.
