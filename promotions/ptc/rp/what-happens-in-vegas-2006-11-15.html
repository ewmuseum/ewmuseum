What happens in Vegas…

15 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-15 Bored would be the only word to describe Killean’s state of mind. He had made his way to the second round of GTT and Seymour Almasy decided not to show for the match. He also had no pressing engagements in regards to PRIME and ReVolution 110 so he was immediately sent home again. He didn’t get to wrestle at all. When he arrived back at home, he was hoping beyond all hope that he would receive a response from Mary Ward. As it turned out, Mary called back once but didn’t leave a message. Or maybe it was Matt, calling to tell Killean off for even the thought of asking for such a favor. Regardless, Killean attempted to return the call anyways but had no luck in reaching anyone. Even their voice mail had been disabled. He kept trying to call for a couple days and eventually gave up. It might have been overkill on the amount of phone calls he placed but he really had no one else to turn to. To keep his mind occupied since, Killean had done some spring cleaning in the fall. He really shouldn’t have neglected it for so long but he was so busy with his return to PRIME in the spring that he couldn’t get around to it. In a few short days, he had gone through every box in storage. He even had two gentlemen come to his loft from the Salvation Army come by to pick up some giveaways. The mundane tasks continued. The main goal was to do something… anything to take his mind off Charity and his own legal standing. But it seemed no matter how much he did, it just didn’t seem to do the trick. But he tried and tried again regardless of his success rate. He had also completed work on cleaning out the fridge. It’s funny what you can find by just a simple rummage through the refrigerator. Killean managed to toss away a bunch of old leftovers that had been over two months old. He swore some of the science experiments that were brewing in there could have walked to the garbage bags on their own. After holding back his gagging, he removed all the good food from the fridge and wiped down and disinfected all the shelves and the walls. Killean was never good at determining which food was bad and which food was still good. He found himself throwing out things that would still be good just in case. These actions made his fridge rather barren however and after cleaning up all the empty containers from the fridge, he needed some groceries. Sure he had a few things left in the freezer and a few choice things from the fridge, but he always liked to keep his food stock full just in case company happened to drop by. By the time Wednesday the 8th of November rolled around, he had done every bit of chores that he could do in the loft. He even did some of the twice like the dusting, vacuuming and mopping the floor. What else was there to do? He had watched all the TV he could stomach. Just the sight of Opera or any other day time talk show would make him lose his lunch. Vegas had dropped by earlier in the day but left rather quickly because he said he wasn’t feeling well. He promised though to bring back some groceries from a list that Killean had given him before he left. Nine o’clock rolled around and Killean was seated on the couch in front of the television waiting for CSI:NY to come on. He had loved watching all the CSI shows whenever he could but New York was the only one that he constantly missed. Normally, he would be at ReVolution on Wednesday nights. On this night, he was taping the show to study it a little closer later. He had made a promise to himself to be at ReVolution 110 but when the authorities were advised that Killean had no active part in the show, his appearance was cancelled and he was forced to go home. Just as the opening credits rolled on the screen, his phone rang. Sliding across the couch he leaned over to take a look at the call display on the side table. He fully expected Vegas to be calling to give some sort of an excuse as to why he was late. Instead, what he saw was the phone number belonging to Matt and Mary Ward. He sat looking at the call display without blinking for two rings. Really, what would happen if he had answered? Mary could say no and it would do nothing to remedy the situation he had now. Oh who was he kidding, he had to answer it anyways. Reaching over for the cordless phone, he pressed the button to answer the line. Killean: Hello? Mary: Hi Killean. How’ve you been keeping? Killean: As good as I can be I guess… how about you? Mary: I’ve been good thanks. A long pause followed all of the kind greetings and Killean was left in somewhat of an awkward state. Was she calling to advise him of a decision to help him or was she just calling to find out how he was doing amidst all of the stress. Mary broke the silence however. Mary: So, about the favor you asked me last week… Killean: Yeah, I thought that you and Matt had fallen off the planet or something. I was calling during the weekend and I… Damn, he shouldn’t have opened his big mouth. He didn’t even intend to sound snarky about it but for some reason that’s how it came out. Perhaps it was all the dealings she had in the past with Killean, but it didn’t even seem to faze her as she interrupted him cursing himself. Mary: Yeah, sorry about that. Matt and I went away for the weekend. We took Georgie on a road trip with us. I also wanted to wait to call you until Matt was not here. I heard you were not going to be at the show this week so I thought tonight was the perfect time to call. How did she find out he wouldn’t be at ReVolution 110? Perhaps Matt told her? Either way, it actually worked out for her to call when he wasn’t with her. Killean didn’t want to start anything with Matt as their animosity was already way too high. It wasn’t worth the headaches right now… not at this moment. Killean: Hey that’s great. I hope you guys had a good time while you were gone. Mary: We did thanks. So as for the favor… Matt and I talked it over and we aren’t sure we want to get involved just yet. He is skeptical about everything and anything involving you still. Can’t really blame him I guess. I’m over most everything that happened mind you. Killean’s heart sank as she spoke. Her soft voice normally comforted him and he enjoyed hearing it. She usually had that effect on people. But at that moment, her voice was not making him happy, instead he was disappointed and he actually didn’t hear her finish her sentence. Instead he had been thinking of what else he could really do. There was no other option now. He was silent as she continued. Mary: Killean, I know you need our help. I know you really have no where else to turn. The only thing I can say is I can try to talk to Matt tonight when he calls home. That’s the best I could possibly do. Killean: I would appreciate that Mary. I know it’s hard to see past everything that has happened. Really, I can’t believe you could be over it all yet. It was only a short time ago. Mary: I can try to talk to him, but I don’t know what the results will be… Silence again. It was turning out to be a very awkward conversation and both of them were stuttering their words and pausing quite often. Mary knew that they were Killean’s only hope and she actually wanted to help him. But she couldn’t go against what Matt had said. He was her husband after all. Killean: Don’t push too hard. I can understand that Matt may be touchy on this so I don’t want to get under his skin anymore than I already have. Ask him again and if not, I understand. I’ll have to pull the impossible out of my ass if this doesn’t go well, but I’ve done it before. Mary: I’m sorry Killean. Georgie is crying, I need to go get her. Killean: Give the cutie a kiss on the forehead for me. I miss her very much. Maybe someday, things can be washed under the bridge and return to what they once were. Mary: Maybe… take care Killean and I’ll call once I hear from Matt. Goodbye. Killean: Bye Mary… take care. With that, he turned off the cordless phone and placed it on the counter. Leaning back a little he let out a very deep sigh and looked at the roof. He was essentially out of ideas. His mind was racing and his heart rate had definitely increased. His friends from San Diego would do almost anything for him, but they were not exactly close to the Ohio and Indiana border. He just couldn’t ask them because it made absolutely no sense. He asked Vegas because he was still closer than they were. He ran his fingers through his hair and actually pulled at it a little. He then let his fists fall to the counter. His glass of water jumped a little as he stared out the window. "What am I doing?" He said aloud to himself. He had a look of disbelief in his eyes. These thoughts had been popping up a lot in the last day or so. What was he doing? Why was he getting all upset about this? Charity had made the decision. She left him and did something that she wanted to do. The pamphlet he found meant that she had this on her mind for quite some time. So why was he so down about it all? Girlfriends had left him before and he had left other women before. It wasn’t like Charity was the first. But as he thought about it more, he remembered the one innocent person involved. It surely wasn’t him because Charity even said that he was most of the problem when he was intent on taking Matt W. out of PRIME. She wasn’t innocent because at first, Charity egged him on. She was part of the motivation to send Matt to exile back home in Ohio. Her attitude changed however when she noticed how personal things had become between the two titans in wrestling. But the one person who was bound to suffer from everything was his daughter Desiree. She was staying with Charity’s parents and they didn’t mind watching her while Killean worked to find out more information. He had called them a couple times and had shared what little he found out. But they offered to watch Desiree as long as it took. Desiree was attached to Charity. Killean saw it from the beginning. The way Desiree smiled when Charity was with her… The way she laughed when Charity played with her… Not to mention the twinkle in her eye when Charity showed her any kind of attention… Desiree loved Charity. That was why Killean was intent on having Charity as Dizzy’s new mother. But now, Charity had other plans. The only one bound to suffer the most from her selfish actions was Dizzy. Killean was old enough to understand and really was cursing himself for taking her departure, temporary or permanent, so hard. But Dizzy was young and she wouldn’t understand. She could try to find Charity and regardless of how much she cried and begged with her actions, Charity would not be there for her. The thought of Dizzy reduced to tears looking for the woman she thought was her mother just made Killean’s heart break. It also made his blood pressure rise and his anger grow. Why should he be so upset because HE lost her? He should be most upset for the selfish way that she abandoned the one person who loved her and had no understanding of why she left? How could she be so heartless as to leave Desiree behind for her own personal satisfaction? He sat in silence, an occasional deep breath or sigh escaped his lungs and let out a few moans from stretching his over worked body. For almost half an hour, he sipped his water and tried to take a different stance on Charity than the worried, upset and whiney one that he had from the beginning of this long episode. He wanted to feel mad and he wanted to forget about it. If she came back fine… if not then it was her decision. But no matter how much he tried to pry his heart from overtaking the more sensible thoughts from his mind, he was having less and less luck with it. Sure he loved Charity, but he just couldn’t figure out why she was any different than the last women he separated with in the past. He loved some of them too but he never spent his days moping around his home over it. So why this time? Charity wasn’t all the special… was she? He had held his head in his hands for quite sometime when he was startled by the ring of his phone. He swept his hand down to the counter but accidentally knocked the cordless phone to the floor. The battery cover popped off but the phone continued ringing. He stumbled from the chair type stool he had around the island counter and picked up the phone without checking the caller ID. He rubbed his eyes and half expected it to be Mary again. Maybe she talked to Matt by now. He was trying to get his bearing about him and fired off a question instead of a standard greeting. Killean: Mary… is that you? All he heard was dead air for the first three seconds or so. Killean: Hello? Someone there? Female Voice: Is this Killean Sirrajin? Well, it wasn’t Mary. Wasn’t Officer Lynn Jacobs either and it definitely wasn’t Charity. He didn’t recognize the voice on the other end at all. His curiosity peaked. Killean: Yes this is. Who’s calling? Female Voice: Mr. Sirrajin, my name is Barbara and I’m calling from the Misericordia hospital here in Winnipeg. Do you know a Mr. David Vegas? Killean: Vegas? Sure I do. What’s this regarding? The pauses between the questions and answers really started to annoy him. But his annoyance soon turned to fear and worry when he listened to her next statement. Barbara: We have you listed as an emergency contact Mr. Sirrajin. Mr. Vegas arrived here by ambulance. We think he had a heart attack… ~o~ Journal Entry It had been a long time since someone I knew had suffered from a heart attack. The last person I knew who was unfortunate enough to have one was a friend back in San Diego. There was a nice old man who worked in the corner market that I frequented. He was the one person in the neighborhood that everyone knew. He was always a happy man and he seemed to enjoy his life and his job of running his own corner store. I have quickly become a regular at his establishment. It wasn’t the largest place in my neighborhood but it carried most everything that I liked to purchase. It was also a store that had the small town feel to it. The old man had done well in establishing it as a place that everyone loved to go. It even had that older generation aura about it. This man was back from the days when smaller stores could thrive in a city. Now with all the large retail outlets opening and all the super centers and wholesale stores that were popping up, the smaller hard working businessman was getting swallowed up in the waters of business. However, Jorge kept his store going and he was still turning a profit and the warm sight of him and his smile brought happiness to many customers who were loyal to him and his establishment. Jorge was coming of age fast and sometimes I wondered how old he really was. I never asked though because he had the outgoing soul of a teenager and it was a welcoming experience to talk to him a few times every week. But one morning on my walk to his store for a few household items, I noticed a small crowd of people standing outside his store which was lifeless for the first day in quite a few years. Instead of Jorge being inside to lighten the lives of many people who shopped there, the cold dark space sent chills up many a spine. When I had gotten closer, I noticed a sign on the door that a few men and one woman were reading. After stepping through the crowd to get to the front of the store, the sign came into view. ‘Store closed until further notice.’ Not much else was known at the time but the nightly news answered most everyone’s questions including my own. Turned out that Jorge had experienced a heart attack and was being kept in ICU. He was scheduled to undergo a bypass operation. The news broadcast surely had broken the hearts of the many people who experienced Jorge’s kindness and outgoing nature. I knew that my own heart ached for the kind old man who ran the corner store that I had come to love in my years in San Diego. Month’s later, Jorge had recovered from his ordeal but he did the most difficult thing he could ever do. He sold his store. On one of my walks, I came across Jorge in front of his store removing some posters and signs from his window. He was still the same man I remembered but he looked exponentially older than he had prior to his tragedy. The years were great to him previously but this one event seemed to make him age years passed what he really was. Jorge told me that he sold his store. It was sold to some developers from Los Angeles and they didn’t have many plans for the land. Last I saw, the store stood vacant still. The hustle and bustle of the store’s glory days were now gone. When I last walked by it, the old wooden sign was still hanging over the window and the shelves still stood inside collecting dust. I just had to take one last look inside because the next day, the building might not be standing anymore. Plenty of things were still in place. The old style cash register stood on the counter with the "No Sale" tag sticking up. The old wooden shelves reminded me of Jorge immediately. He had built all of those shelves himself with the help of his brother who died only a few short years earlier. Some flyers were scattered on the hard wood old style floor. The building still had that aura about it though. It had brought a warm feeling to my soul even though it was a shell of it’s former self. The store still had the love and hard work of Jorge all over it. After moving, I heard that the store was scheduled for demolition. As it turned out, the store still stands to this day. The new owners of the land decided to preserve the old store as it was and actually opened up a business of their own. But the best part of it all was that Jorge was behind the counter again bringing smiles to everyones faces once again without the stresses of running his own business. He was the weekday attendant and his job did not require even half of what it used to. I was happy when I heard the news and hoped that one day I could visit Jorge once again. Vegas had been my friend for many years. He was not out of shape in the least and he always ate relatively well. He would have been the last person I expected to suffer from this serious of a problem. Once again my heart broke when I received the call. I half expected Mary to be calling me back and now I wish it was her instead. Now, my best friend is in the ICU and was not in the best condition. Really… what else could happen now? My best friend is nearly comatose and my girlfriend has left me for a god damn cult and eternal happiness or some other bullshit. I almost felt like ripping the electronic bracelet off my leg and running for my life until I just couldn’t run anymore. As I sit here writing this while listening to the incessant beeping from the monitor machines that my best friend was hooked up to, I just wanted it to all end. Surely death could not be much more painful that constant heartbreak and repeated fears of loneliness. ~o~ "I’ll be there as soon as I can!" Killean turned the phone off and then on again in a state of panic. He had to redial a couple times before getting the right number. "Lynn? It’s Killean… look I don’t have time to explain but my best friend had a heart attack. I need to go to Misericordia Hospital right away!" On the other end, Lynn agreed to send a car right over to pick him up and accompany him to the ICU at the hospital. He grabbed his coat and made his way for the door to wait downstairs in the lobby for the squad car. Before opening it he ran over to his large table behind the couch and withdrew his journal. He then opened the door but stopped, looking to the roof like he was expecting something to emerge from it. "I have to fight this… everything… all of it. Dave has to fight too…" he said in a whisper as he continued to look at the roof while he slowly pulled the door closed. He needed to battle the demons that had possessed his life. Never being a religious man, he tended to shy away from anything that it involved. But he even thought of a short prayer for Vegas and Charity in his mind. Why he thought that way, he didn’t know. But one thing was for sure. He needed to stop crying and start over again… this time with a new attitude.
