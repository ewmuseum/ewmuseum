Deux - Love Lost and Found

2 December 2009

Date: 2009-12-02 ''Ah, Mister Markson.'' Darcy Markson had tried so hard not to look nervous when he entered the back office, but the doctor noticed that when he continued, assuring the man with his smile. ''Gerard James. Just call me Gerry. Sit down,'' Malcolm Girard gestured to the couch and chair combination on the right side of the room. ''Sit down. Miranda there is new, I trust she didn’t keep you waiting long just because I was in here with my notes?'' ''Just a few minutes. She’s nice enough.'' The other man was hesitant to sit, but he made his way to the chair and sat, leaving the couch empty. ‘Gerry’ smiled at Markson, holding his gaze as he took to the couch. It was a cute trick, one he’d seen a criminal psychologist pull on him at one point. Back then, Malcolm had chosen the chair. He liked that about Darcy. ''Isn’t she, though?'' He breathed in a deep, centering breath. ''Funny that we start by discussing a woman, hm?'' ''Is it?'' ''Well…'' Malcolm replied. ''From what you mentioned after our fenders met, you were quite upset with one. Likely nothing like Miranda, of course.'' ''That nice girl out there?'' Darcy’s initial laugh was more of a howl. It fit his full eyes, the dark stubble and scruffy hair that gave him a wolf-like appearance. ''God, no. Nothing like her.'' ''Ah. Let’s start at the tip of the iceberg, then.'' ''And how do you suggest we do that?'' Malcolm Girard put on his best ‘Gerard James’ when he leaned toward the taller, seated man. He never broke eye contact. ''Tell me about her.'' Now. ''Even when I hate her, I love her.'' – Amy Campbell to Jared Sykes. For a year after she betrayed her best friend, Amy Campbell’s every nightmare involved Katie Malick. The woman haunted her dreams, driving home that she was a failure, a loser, a liar and would never be free of that, never truly be happy – not if she was the sort of person who’d cut their closest friend just to get to Malick’s boyfriend, Jon Rhine, over something as pathetic as wrestling. A few days ago, Katie Malick died. Amy attended the funeral aside a newer closest friend, Jared Sykes. Alexandra Pierce was nowhere in sight, and that fact had coaxed a few extra tears from Campbell that day – but that wasn’t the time nor the place to miss her girlfriend’s calming touch. What she truly missed, more than anything that day, was the voicemail Katie had left her a few days after Amy had finally and truthfully apologized for all she’d done. It was a simple thing, a plan to go for a drink after the next Sin City event. It had meant the world to the little redhead, but the message itself was insignificant enough to clear out with the rest of her voicemail inbox. She had no way of knowing that it was the last time she’d hear Malick’s voice, not even a week after it seemed they’d finally reconciled. The days that followed the news and the funeral were filled with liquor-induced blackouts and the kind of upset Amy hadn’t felt in years. Being caught cheating was one thing – that was her fault. There was guilt there, but little reason to feel truly hurt. Katie Malick was completely innocent, she only ever wanted the best for the both of them. Amy felt all of the hurt that Malick couldn’t, as did Jon Rhine, because it was plainly unfair for the girl to have been taken so early. The service had been beautiful, had brought out people she hadn’t seen in ages, for better or for worse. They all behaved – even Lane Stevens, in his own way, sent by Alexandra Pierce. That was what bothered her when she sat down to drink that afternoon, hearing Pierce’s key in the lock. Lane had been there. Not Alex. Amy held Jared Sykes’ hand blindly during the eulogy, forgetting that Alex wasn’t the one beside her – she’d just expected it to be that way. Alexandra accompanied her for everything, helped shield her from the awful things in the world. Alexandra stepped through the door, her phone against her ear and her voice a livid hiss. ''I really don't care what you have to do find—'' She came up short, stormy gray gaze flicking across the living room. She used to relish living alone. The quiet solace of even places like this condominium served as a place for her to decompress after a long day, whether that day had been spent in a wrestling ring or in her... other... pastimes. But Alex now shared the two-bedroom flat with two other women, and she'd gotten used to the little things that came with sharing her space, too. The soft ''Hey'' from her lover or her daughter, the sound of videogames blaring in the living rooms, the joy of seeing the two people in her life who mattered to her every day... it was almost overwhelming, but in a good sort of way. That afternoon, the living room was silent – no Call of Duty or Borderlands on the flat-screen plasma TV, no quiet greeting. She found only Amy Campbell on the leather couch, curled in a small ball. ''I'll call you back, Elijah,'' Alex said quietly; she didn't wait for a response as she disconnected the call and pocketed her iPhone. ''Amy?'' Both of Alex's brows lifted as she drifted into the room. Campbell's only answer was a sniffle, so Pierce pressed on. ''Amy, what... what happened?'' ''Lauren hated me.'' ''What?'' ''Your ‘friend’. Lauren. She HATED me. She tried to kill me.'' Amy hiccupped. ''Everybody around you hates me, and I can’t stand any of them.'' ''I don’t under—'' ''I went with you to her wake. I didn’t want to – that was the last place on the planet I should have been – but I went because you needed me.'' Alexandra narrowed her eyes, searching the tiny redhead’s expression for anything – any little hint that had her on the subject – when Amy lashed out fully and suddenly. ''What makes you so fucking special?'' ''Special? I am... I'm not special, Amy.'' ''You weren't there. I needed you, and you weren't there.'' ''Ah.'' Alex's gaze dropped to the hardwood floor almost immediately, her hands coming around to knit at her waist. ''I'm sorry.'' ''You’re sorry?'' Amy growled. ''You’re sorry that my friend died? Or that you were the one who helped and pushed me to hurt her more than anybody else could? Or are you sorry that I needed you, and you – of ALL fucking people, you - bailed because it might be a tough day. You sent Lane Stevens. I hugged Jon Rhine that day. I hugged Jon. He didn’t lash out, didn’t tell me to leave. We were allowed there, welcomed. I had a friend who I loved dearly and hurt so fucking badly, and I’d finally started to make up for it… and when she dies, you bolt. When one of your underlings gets taken out, I cry with you at her wake. I hugged her sister, I drank with her friends. I made it work.'' She stifled a sob with another drink. ''And the great Alexandra can’t afford me the same thing. This relationship I’ve given my everything for, this thing I’ve defended and risked my life over… you left me alone when I needed you the most, and you showed them how hollow it really is to you.'' ''You're right.'' The cobblestones of her eyes were quickly spattered with rain. ''I should have been there, but... it wasn't fair to him.'' ''Since when has that ever mattered to you?'' ''I don't know, but it does now. Lauren Fox might have hated you – she might have tried to kill you, but you know that in her twisted way, she was doing it for me.'' Alexandra's voice was quiet, even contemplative. ''When I asked Shweta to poison Jonathan, when I briefly ended his career, when I orchestrated the events that broke up his relationship with the very woman you were there to mourn, I did it for me.'' She didn't step forward, didn't really move – if this was another time, a time when Amy was capable of understanding it, the fact that Alexandra Pierce actually regretted something would be noteworthy. Now was not that time, and all Pierce's expression of guilt earned her was a soft snort from the woman on the couch. ''Time and again, I have gone out of my way to be cruel to Jonathan Rhine – and many others like him. To show up at her funeral... I would have been a distraction, and not a welcome one. I wouldn't do that to him.'' ''He’s a tool, but he’d have understood you being there for my sake. He said that.'' For a moment, Amy wouldn’t look at her. She took another drink and rolled over on the couch, fetal again. Because of that, her voice was muffled. ''Jared told me you’re afraid his kindness would rub off on me. Like it’d make me… make me a better person, make me realize how fucking awful you are even when you’re trying not to be. Maybe it is. I certainly felt it that day. He barely knows me, he held me and let me cry. I think Lane would have even done the same… but it was too fucking hard for you. You’re making excuses because you avoided it. You hurt me me, pretending you were trying not to hurt Rhine.'' ''Don't make this about something it's not.'' Alex wouldn't lift her head. ''I... you're right, I should have gone, but I was afraid. Afraid to see all of them like that. It's... I like to say I don't regret things, but the truth is that I don't face things I've done. It's weak, it's cowardly, but it's true. I didn't want to see her in that coffin, knowing I helped rob her of those last few moments of happiness.'' Pierce sneered, but the hate was directed only at herself. ''So I stayed here like a coward and it killed me.'' ''Oh, don’t give me that. I fall for that wounded bird routine every fucking day with you. You’re so brave… so fuckin’ brave, you can put on a wig and wave a goddamn gun, but you can’t hold my hand when I need you more than anyone.'' Campbell sniffled. ''I left somebody who’d do that – who vowed to do it forever – because you were there, looking like the right option. I guess I got tricked like they’ve all been saying. Standing there, I was miserable. I loved Katie to death, and I hated you in that moment.'' ''That's not fair!'' The shout was desperation. ''Dammit, please don't do this, don't say that. I'm trying... to be different, but this isn't easy for me, and you being hurtful doesn't make this easier.'' Amy’s raised voice was again muffled by the sofa. ''Yeah, ‘cause I’m the one who abandoned this for convenience. Maybe you should go play dress-up, put on one of your wigs, turn into somebody else and poison my tequila. I’ll make sure to drink it slow so whoever you become can enjoy my last few moments.'' ''Is that all you think of me?'' Pierce's voice had gone quiet. ''It didn't used to be.'' ''I... I'm going out.'' She spun on heel and toe, back towards the door. ''Abandoning me again? Will some harsh truths scare you away, too?'' It took everything the proud Spider had to stifle her sob as she yanked that door open. The problem with Alexandra Pierce was that she'd hidden her emotions behind a wall for so long that she didn't know how to deal with them. The door slamming was the only coherent response she could generate. Alex only made it around the corner before she broke down in tears, grateful for tinted windows and inattentive neighbors to hide her. Amy wasn’t about to stop drinking, but she needed to if she was to realize what she’d done. Then – ‘Tell Me About Her’ ''Gah! Not from there!'' Amy Campbell bit into her own hand, albeit gently, when her husband scaled the ladder. ''You’re gonna break your fool neck!'' She added. ''Relax, Ames.'' Darcy had done what resembled a small, jiggling dance on the top wrung for the sake of a safety demonstration. ''I’m fine. I’ve fallen off taller ladders than this.'' ''Onto people, not the patio.'' Amy winced when she saw that the string of Christmas lights had made its way around one of his ankles. ''And I just drank half the red wine for the stirfry, so I am not taking you to the hospital if your dumb ass comes off that thing!'' ''I’ll remember that when I get these up, then come down perfectly fine to find you’ve tripped over your own foot and broken something.'' Campbell could only wince, still biting into her fist. ''Shut up, this is seriously making me nervous.'' ''Then don’t watch!'' ''… But then who’s gonna tell you when the lights are crooked?'' ''Christmas is the only time I regret marrying a Canadian.'' ''Not my fault your stupid state is against snow and happiness.'' ''You’ve lived here for six years!'' ''And every winter, I try to spend more time back home than I do in California.'' ''And every winter, you call me bitching that your mom’s trying to get you to stop drinking, laser off the tattoos, grow your hair out to be its more natural red…'' ''And I thank you for listening, now put that shit up. Are the nails still there from last year?'' ''No, Amy. I secretly came out last February and took each one out by hand.'' He shook his head, ''Because they were so easy to put up in the first place.'' ''Not my fault it took eight hours. I’m certainly not the one who pretended you were a handyman.'' ''Tell me again why I don’t beat you?'' ''Boobies.'' ''Ahh,'' He smirked and made a show of sliding down the ladder as haphazardly as he could, causing a squeak from the pint-sized Canuck nearby. ''That’s right. For your sanity, I’m gonna do this after I take you to the airport tomorrow, okay? I promise I’ll have Johnny spot for me.'' ''But…'' ''And I’ll text you some photos so you can bitch about how they’re not quite right and re-do it yourself when you’re back home.'' Amy could only smile. She was supposed to be annoyed, and tried to play at that, but couldn’t quite manage. ''Wow. I don’t know why you like me.'' ''In your own words, ‘Boobies’.'' Darcy answered, untangling the Christmas lights to walk over and slip arms around his wife’s waist. ''And because sane girls are overrated. I like my crazy cranked to eleven.'' She swatted his arm, and it was genuine but came alongside a gasp and laugh. ''Fuck you!'' ''You mean it? We’re up to two times a week now?'' ''Okay, you’re evil today.'' ''No, evil is your new buddy.'' He noted. ''Alex isn’t that bad.'' ''So she’s not Desade anymore?'' Markson held her at a distance for a moment to allow the question to be answered. ''We’ve moved onto ‘Alex’ now?'' ''She’s weird, but I’m pretty sure half of it’s a character. Half the universe thinks I’m an awful bitch too, if you remember.'' ''And you are,'' He winked. ''But seriously… she actually is, Ames.'' ''Oh, c’mon. She’s just a little frigid.'' ''And the North Pole is seasonably cool.'' It went on like that for days, weeks, almost two years in total. They were the picture (im)perfect love, a man and a woman who adored and accepted one another. They weren’t without their faults, but they made it work with little effort, but day by day, the resentment grew. Markson loathed the woman Amy had befriended in SCCW, some new ringmaster named Alexandra Pierce. The arguments would go on, get worse every day as his distaste and her defense grew. Somewhere along the line – and Darcy wasn’t quite sure where – Amy had stopped adoring and accepting. Their seemingly infinite ability to make it work dried out. One half of the pair had been trying the entire time, holding it down for the both of them. That half hadn’t been her and, when he found that out, the world stopped. Their world died. Neither would admit it – not just yet – but their fall was not for lack of love. Resolve - Now. The most important fixture in Alexandra Pierce’s home was the bed, at least if you asked the woman who spent the most time in it. Amy was inherently lazy in the afternoons, most often found wrapped in sheets, catnapping after the small slice of morning she always tried to capture. It was night time when Pierce walked back in and found the little redhead in her favorite spot. The setting, the tone… they were entirely different. Wearing torn jeans and a red t-shirt (Amy hated wearing clothes to bed), she was curled up in a ball, puffy-eyed and red-nosed. The pillow, strewn away from her head, was stained either with whiskey or tears. Alex lingered there a long moment, a small smile – the one she could never help – finding its way through the clouds. It wasn't that she didn't want to say anything, but rather that she didn't know what to say. Her silhouette gave her away first, painted by the fluorescent lights in the kitchen onto the wall. The little bundle shifted, moving; it couldn't make out any details, but in the end, it didn't need to. ''I'm sorry,'' Alex finally said. ''I'm sorry I'm a coward. I'm sorry I left you alone. I'm... I'm so fucking sorry.'' When the ball of Campbell unfurled, Pierce could see her right hand and the smear of blood on the comforter. Amy knew that’s what she’d see first, it seemed, because she ignored everything in hopes of offering an explanation. ''I hit the wall. A lot. I’m sorry…'' She hiccupped again – that always happened when she mixed wine and hard liquor in one session. ''I’ll pay for the repairs.'' ''You don’t need to do that.'' Alex crossed the distance quickly, crouching at the bedside. ''You know you don't need to do that. Is your hand okay? Your match with Wyatt...'' ''Bruised, cut. Maybe broke a knuckle. Nothing I can’t fight with. I… I hurt you, though. I can’t do that. I don’t… I’m not supposed to get that mean.'' ''It’s okay.'' Amy shook her head – almost rolling over on the bed from the dizziness it caused. ''It’s not. Neither was leaving me alone at Katie’s funeral, but you didn’t deserve that. It’s just been hurting so fucking much, all of it… it’s building up and making me hate you sometimes.'' Alex swallowed hard, nodding her head again. The tears threatened again, but she'd cried herself out on that day. ''I know,'' was really all she could manage to say, however. ''But that’s not okay.'' Amy repeated. ''I’m good at love, I’m great at hate… it’s in between where I freeze. I don’t know how to make this live and last and not fail like everything else has. Just being isn’t… it’s not good enough. It won’t keep you. It won’t keep me happy.'' ''What can I do? I want you to be happy, almost as much as I want you to be here with me.'' Her eyes dipped, but her hand slid across the bed, touching just Campbell's fingertips. ''And I want that more than anything.'' ''I don’t know.'' Campbell sniffled. ''Make me a better person. Find out the way to fix me, and then tell me, because I’ve been looking for it for so fucking long.'' She didn’t give Alex the opportunity to respond before sliding closer, curling around and kissing the other redhead’s arm. Pierce could smell the liquor and see in Amy’s shaking that she’d continued to drink long after Alex walked away that afternoon. ''I’m so sorry for the things I said. I wanted you to feel half as badly as I did, to hurt and bleed as much as me… and that was so stupid, so wrong. And… and… and I know I’m drunk, but I mean it.'' She winced. ''I mean it.'' ''Did you... did you mean it then?'' It was the last question Alex wanted the answer to, but perhaps the most important one she'd ask. Amy had to choke a sob when she nodded. ''But I was angry. I… it looked worse then. I didn’t think.'' ''I am sorry. You know that, too, right? I make no excuses. It was unforgiveable.'' ''I hope that’s true.'' Was all her partner said before trying to pull Pierce close. ''I can’t get up. I need… can you just come to bed?'' When nodded and came to hold her, Amy could only cry. She could feel the end. She could feel… everything. She felt the year of agony all at once, too much loathing and loving to fit into only a few hundred days, into one small, disgraced woman. Alexandra cried, too. She saw that Amy needed him – or, more specifically, needed something more than just Desade. Another ‘Him’ – A few hours ago… He knew the Director was coming, he’d even told his company to leave the door unlocked when they left. The two young women he’d played host (and more) to had taken the edge off the stress of having Alexandra Pierce want your head, though it was only a temporary relief. His experience with the Spider dealt with the rest of that anxiety – knowing she couldn’t touch him without breaking the guidelines of their organization. Still, it was Desade. He wasn’t sure what this new version would do, she’d been acting like she had a cub to protect at that restaurant. The truth, if she’d ever know it, was delightfully ironic. It was absolutely no surprise to wake up and find himself confronted with a knife in the dark. ''Alexandra,'' His voice was hoarse from the slumber, nervously low because of the blade’s tip at his jugular, but still smooth. ''Didn’t make much of your usual informants, I take it. Amy never came back, so I imagine you told her all about me, hm?'' ''I told her no such thing.'' He was certain he was dealing with Desade -- the cold, ruthless leader of the Order cell known to the wrestling business as the ''Dead Man's Hand.'' There was iron in her eyes and death rode with her. The hesitation there wasn't like her, though. The hesitation was interesting. Malcolm knew he could be dead -- should never have awakened at all. But he still breathed, which meant she didn't want to do the deed. Not really. ''I should kill you. Spare her the stress. Spare her whatever you have planned. The Order's fight is with me, not her.'' When she lifted her chin, Malcolm could have excused the gleam in her eyes as the light from the parking lot -- except he knew better. ''I’ve been talking to her husband under the guise of a therapist. He still loves her, you know. Dearly.'' The knife’s tip had started to cut. That spurned Malcolm to add ''If you're going to do it, then just get it over with.'' ''Tell me why I shouldn't. By now, you know I don't want to. I made a promise -- one I do not intend to break. But her safety is my concern, and I will not allow my weakness to endanger her. Tell me why I should spare you.'' ''You should know by now that no harm will come to her by the hand of the Order.'' He said, ''Haven’t you wondered why she’s been able to get away with so much and still be alive? Even you know your affection isn’t that powerful among us.'' ''Not my affection.'' Pierce sneered. ''My reputation. If you even think of cleaning her, I will make your death--'' ''You’ll have to forgive me for belittling you while there’s a knife to my throat, but it’s hardly your reputation that keeps her from harm. I was given this job to watch you, because I chose not to retire as your superior.'' He hissed. ''This is only about you on the surface, Alexandra – you flatter yourself too much.'' Before Pierce could say anything, Malcolm locked eyes with her. The slight bit of light peering through the window caught the brown fleck in one of his blue eyes – a curious, genetic trait he always had to cover with contacts. ''Amy Campbell has and always will be safe from any real danger, with or without you, because of me.'' ''Because of you, Malcolm? Why on Earth would--'' Malcolm interrupted her with the simplest answer he could give. ''Because she may be your lover, but she’s my daughter.''
