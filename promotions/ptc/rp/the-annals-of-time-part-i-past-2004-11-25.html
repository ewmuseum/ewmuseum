The Annals of Time – Part I: Past

25 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-25 The Annals of Time – Part I: Past It was the most excruciating, fearsome and formidable moment in my entire life. A man who some had said went through hell and returned to life to fight; a man who some said was possessed by The Devil Himself; a man who some people said was unstoppable, invincible, impregnable. He was twice the size, twice the strength and with limitless ability and skill. I was a simple, humble wrestler wanting to achieve something in my career to look back on when I’m retired and to tell my grandchildren about. I’m not the sharpest tool in the box, I’m not particularly big, I’m not particularly strong and as much as I used to parade myself in such a way, I’m not the greatest wrestler in the Universe either. It was never meant to happen… Some people still don’t believe it. Some people will never believe it. Some people feel that the match was a farce and that the referee was incompetent, biased or even corrupt. But somehow, some way, I reached down into the very pit of my soul and produced something. And through all the punishment, all the torment that I received throughout the match from a never-ending, limitless attack, I never gave up. I never faltered. And then when many people wanted it all to end, that I couldn’t take any more of a beating, something magical happened. You could call it divine intervention, you could call it a miracle, but through the hundreds of blows that I had received I managed to counter an attack and turn it into a pinning predicament, a complicated predicament that took more than three seconds to escape from. Three seconds was all that was needed. The referee slapped the canvas three times and all was over. The crowning achievement of my career, the crowning achievement of my life and the sweet jubilating sound of the timekeeper’s bell, the incomprehensible had actually happened. The unstoppable was stopped; the invincible was defeated; the impregnable was vanquished. Exceptional Dave Hurst was in the semi finals. -----=====|=====----- “Aaaagh!” For fuck’s sake, you would have thought I could at least move a little bit in my bed. Every time I move any part of my body my chest feels like it’s caving in. I just want a sip of water, but every time I reach for the glass on the bedside table it feels like a pneumatic drill is hammering into my ribs, I feel each rib crumbling under the strain. They’ve put me in one of those large rooms, sharing it with a bunch of equally pained people groaning from their own wounds. I love the way the hospital try and subdue the misery caused by their patients’ injuries by painting the walls in bright colours. As if looking at a bright yellow wall is going to make me feel better. When I’m feeling the way I am the last thing I want to see is happy imagery. It’s like feeling down and somebody coming up to you with a huge smile on their face going “Wow! I’m such a happy and gleeful person today! Isn’t life great?” Of course I’d tell them “No, life is not great,” only probably with stronger words than that, and slap them across the face. The last thing you want to see when you’re miserable is happy people. I shouldn’t be so selfish though, my injuries are nothing compared to the old frail guy lying next to me. I have no idea what’s wrong with this old man, but a drip and a life support machine shows that it’s not exactly a fractured wrist. I lie back in my bed utterly bored and frustrated at being stuck in this place, utter silence only accompanied by repetitive rhythmical beeps from the heart monitor of the old man beside me, the occasional groan from one of the other patients in the room and the clicking of the clock above me, every second, over and over again. The endless tapping and beeping is cut off as the door at the end of the room opens and the doctor, in his white coat and holding a clipboard, enters the room. As the doctor slowly makes his way across the room passing each of the hospital beds on his way to my one, I can’t help but worry about my injuries. I know if there were any serious problems I’d know about it, but you have to worry. “Hello Mister Hurst,” the doctor says as he wanders over to me turning to my bed and placing the clipboard on the front of it. “What’s the proglamatosis then, Doc?” I say to the good doctor, he looks back at me blankly obviously not knowing the reason for such an intellectual term, so I reassure him by saying “I mean, ‘how am I?’ Innit.” “Oh!” the doctor says now understanding what I mean, “well you’re fine. Only some bruised ribs and a strained calf muscle. Nothing major, you should be able to walk. Well… haha limp, out of the hospital tomorrow without much problem.” Ah some good news. That’s good to hear. I knew I would probably be okay, but I just needed to hear it from the horse’s mouth. “Excellent Doc,” I said letting him know that I was pleased with the result. In response he looked back at me, shook his head and chuckled. “I would say you shouldn’t involve yourself in any physical exertion for the next few days, but I know there’s no way I’m going to stop you from that, I know what you wrestler types are like.” “No doc it’s okay I understand.” “You do?” “Yeah, I don’t even like hiking.” The doctor’s face was as confused and puzzled as ever, his face always tilted to the side looking to the ceiling deep in thought as he slowly back-peddled out of the room, not saying a word. The door slowly closes shut leaving the room once again in a shroud of deft silence, the rhythmical clicking and beeping continuing once more. I start to get used to the noises, finding them almost soothing and relaxing, I rest back in the bed my head burying into the pillow. Pulling my arm across my chest to my breast pocket, I reach in and pull the contents out. “Allo Ducky!” I say enthusiastically to the little plastic duck clutched in my right hand, “how’s it going little mate…? Good?” The duck stares back at me with a content expression as I respond with a gleeful smile of my own. “You know, I’ve only got bruised ribs the doc says, it looks like I’ll be able to fight Kasper after all…” As if the words were a catalyst to bad things the door suddenly swings open slamming into the wall with a deafening crack. The groaning from some of the people in the room suddenly changes into a symphony of gasps, the only sounds now the ticking and beeping of the clock and the heart monitor. Slowly turning through the doorway into the room is a short plump man, with a thick sheepskin coat covering his shoulders, some dirty 80s-fashioned sunglasses and a cigar hanging from his mouth. He slowly began walking down the room towards me, each step making a distinct click as he moved ever closer. He stopped at the bed next to me, turned and looked at him with a devious grin forming on his face. “Sorry father,” he said bowing his head, his smile never waning, “but you could never keep your end of the bargain. We had a bet, and you lost, now you must pay the price in the only way you can.” Casually the sheep-coated man reaches forward to the life support machine, and before I could do anything to protest, or even realise what it is that the man is doing, he yanks the plug clean from it’s socket causing the heart monitor to turn to a constant screech indicating a malfunctioning machine. That suck fuck! He just pulled the plug on his own father! How the fuck can he kill his own dad like that without an ounce of remorse on his face, instead having a disturbingly pleased expression? He looked like he enjoyed every moment of it! With the smile slowly leaving his face, the short plump man turns away from the bed, noticing me lying there looking incredibly shocked at the man for killing his own father and he suddenly seems pleasantly surprised to see me lying there. “Wait a second… Exceptional Dave Hurst!” the man says in an incredibly fake tone as if he’s pretending to be some kind of huge fan. “I’m Jasper Achilles… wow, it’s great to see you here. I have been looking for you, you weren’t at your house or even at the gym you visit so often…” I’m not even going to credit this person with a reply. Instead I stand my ground with what he’s just done. Well… lie my ground, or bed, or whatever. I don’t move. “Well that isn’t the kind of greeting I expect from you Hurst,” Achilles says now shooting a much more serious look and in an intimidating gravelly voice. “Especially coming from the man that just cost me half a million dollars.” And then as if it had been planned all along, the plump man clicks his fingers and two incredibly burly men enter the room, storming swiftly over to the bedside and the side of Jasper Achilles. The men stand there not saying a word. Instead they shoot their own glare in my direction with hate and eagerness looking like they were ready to just rip into me at any moment. “I don’t think you realise the seriousness of what you had done when you had defeated Angelo Deville in the last round of the tournament. Deville was supposed to win. It was all set in stone. It was a foregone conclusion that you were going to lose. It was obvious that I was set to earn myself millions from the result. I don’t build my empire from miniscule flutters on the horses. I don’t build my empire by taking huge gambles at the expense of huge amounts of cash. I build my empire through calculated risk, through safe bets, and I always ensure that I win.” Achilles starts to pace back and forth across the end of my bed before stopping, taking a puff on his cigar and then turning to talk to me once more. “But you had to ruin that, didn’t you Mister Hurst? You had to defy the fucking odds, you had to pull some kind of fucking miracle out of your ass and actually beat Deville. I even paid the fucking referee to influence the fucking match and he was either too much of a dumb-ass to realise which way, or he felt like being a rebellious little bastard to my own crooked ways and sway the contest in the opposite direction just to piss me off. But oh, I dealt with that son of a bitch. You don’t have to worry about your beloved ref being around anymore to help you.” The gambler says grinning wickedly, the same face shown as he was pulling the plug on his own flesh and blood, the sweat dripping from his forehead, the aftermath of his verbal tirade. “Now there is one thing that you can do for me, Mister Hurst. One very simple thing, and it won’t require any effort at all. Do so, and I promise I will not lay a finger on you.” Yeah, and you look the type that would honour his promises… arse. Jasper Achilles walks over to the side of the bed, the putrid smell of cheap aftershave mixed with the scotch on his breath made me feel nauseous and made my stomach turn. He quickly snatched from my hand, before I could offer any resistance, my duck and held it up to his face. “But if you decide to be as rebellious as that pathetic referee last round…” Achilles drops Ducky from that height and lets it fall to the ground… “Do not expect to set foot out of a hospital bed ever again…” With those last words, the stout gambler lifts his knee upward and abruptly stamps his clunky suede shoes downward over the plastic duck toy, squashing it into the floor with an audible squeak. I give an angry glare back at the murderous fuck that just attacked my Ducky. He stares back with an equally furious look before circling and heading to the door, the two heavies following him and straightening their suits as they go. That piece of crap Japser Achilles thinking he can force me into losing the semi-final match in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, the biggest achievement I’ve ever accomplished. Unfortunately for him, it’s not that easy. Exceptional Dave Hurst will never take a dive, EDH will never lie down and I’m sure as hell not going to knock myself out of this tournament.
