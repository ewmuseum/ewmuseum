Favourite

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-09 It’d been a while since he had been to this place, not through choice it has to be said, but other interests and concerns had intruded on his life, consigning this place to his past, with the cobwebs being blown off for the occasional visit, such as this one. As usual, the visit was solely for pleasure, no ulterior motives existed. With his schedule recently, he deserved a break. Back when he had time to come here frequently, he was a well-known figure. Attendants would same his parking spot, bar staff would see to his needs first and the best spot would be saved for him. Overkill, some may say, but when you consider how much of income this man disposed with here, it’s understandable. Now, he was unsure of the welcome he’d receive. Obviously it wouldn’t be as over the top as it had been but a little respect and a few small courtesies wouldn’t go amiss. As he pulled his Lamborghini, origin unknown as always, into the complex, he turned off the sound system, no need to announce his arrival, they’d know soon enough. At this time the place was packed, parking was going to be a problem. Back in the day this wouldn’t have mattered but now it became a serious problem. Walking didn’t really appeal to him but it looked like…there’s a space beside that Range Rover. Someone likes him today and this divine favour couldn’t have come at a better time considering where he was. Getting out of the car, being careful not to get any dirt on his suit of course, the first thing that hits him is the noise of the crowd, thousands of voices shouting in unison, despite their varying preferences. This was what got him every time, the sound. It was this aspect more than any other that got his heart racing, the adrenaline pumping. It was a feeling unmatched by few (legal) things. Time to move into the heart of it… ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Back in the old routine already. Any worries about being at the bottom of the totem pole as far as special privileges were concerned had been quickly dispelled. Vicky had recognised him the minute he entered the bar, had set out his usual - double vodka and ice in case you’re wondering, and had flashed him one of those smiles that comes from genuine happiness. Like he’d never left. That had been forty-five minutes and three drinks ago and now he was ready to move on to stage two of his visit, the main event so to speak. He’d already confirmed that Harry was still here, though there had been a slight moment of disappointment when Vicky had to check if he was on today. When she brought back the good news, his spirits rose another notch, everything mattered when you were here, the slightest details magnified their importance, becoming major concerns. After saying his goodbyes to Vicky, he made his way through the throngs of people in the stand, making his way past men, women, even a few children on his way to ground level, to his destination. All human life is, indeed, here, from the affluent with their top hats and trophy wives, to the middle classes, their attire slightly less ostentatious, their marriages based on emotion rather than appearance. Then at the bottom of the food chain, the working class men, those who come here to relieve the monotony of their everyday lives and nagging wives. It’s halfway through the day’s festivities and the atmosphere is getting better by the minute. The perfect time to join in, it would seem. There he is now, good old Harry. Hair slightly more grey, shoulders a little more hunched but still undoubtedly Harry. The flicker of recognition in his eyes is an encouraging sign, maybe he will decide to share his knowledge of what’s hot and what’s not today. He was feeling generous today, giving our subject tips for each of the next three races, horses that could only lose, so he said, as a result of an act of God. As for the last race of the day however, he refused to give up his picks. No amount of prying or prodding would unlock that box, leaving our man with a decision to make. Yes it’s true that he didn’t have to bet on the last race but it was his way, he always put a lot on the show race, didn’t seem right not to. Without the wisdom of Harry, he’d have to go with his gut, which didn’t always agree with popular odds. Race 5, the first one he’s betting on. The favourite is a very fair 6-1, our man‘s horse comes in at 13-2. Harry’s advice better be good…and it is, the favourite burns himself out and Rock-A-Roller takes it in the closing stages, netting him a tidy sum. One for one. The next race is a similar story, different odds. Harry said back the favourite and sure enough, it lived up to it’s billing, cruising home with a lot to spare. With a name like Pocket Rocket you’d expect nothing less. This streak can‘t keep going though, can it? Looks like it can. Albion Star just sneaks race 7, winning by a nose after coming from behind. Harry may be getting older but his knowledge of this sport, of these beasts, is unmatched in our subject’s experience. Shame that his tips have run out now, our man will just have to wing it for race 8, the big one… ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* What the hell is going on? The place was bedlam, total and unmitigated chaos, for no apparent reason. The half-hour intermission between the seventh and final races of the day had given him the opportunity to collect his winnings and pay a visit to his car, where he picked up an overcoat to fend off the worsening chill. When he returned to the rail, the place was in pandemonium. Now here he was, left with no idea what on earth was going on, a roll of bills lying heavy in his pocket, waiting to be wagered on whatever horse takes his fancy. The commotion seemed to be concentrated around the tote booths, a betting discrepancy perhaps? Only one way to find out and that is to find Harry. This seems familiar, pushing his way through the hordes towards the booths. Eventually reaching Harry’s spot, where the man is studiously ignoring the shouts of the punters, seemingly impervious to the uproar around him. Luckily our subject is a special case, eye contact is enough to start a conversation. As Harry talks our man’s confusion grows, why should that cause this much tumult? Then, in a glorious instant it becomes crystal clear. Turns out that there has been a pile-up on the road leading up to the track, about ten miles away. Alright, so what does that have to do with this? Turns out a lot of the trainers, five to be exact, were bringing their horses up late to the feature race and as a result have been caught in the carnage. By all reports the horses are fine but that doesn’t change the fact that they are ten miles away and unable to race. So there’s a problem, people want their money back, there’s no huge problem there. On the surface no, but when you consider that two of the other runners are ill and unable to race and that the original field was nine, with a little preschool maths it quickly becomes obvious that there are only two runners remaining. And that, my friends, is a problem. You see, in a situation like this it doesn’t matter if you’ve put $1 or $1000 on a horse, the race is now a dead rubber. The race will be run but as the odds are now meaningless, no money will change hands. Disappointing for any race but especially for one of this magnitude and importance. Still, there is a valuable lesson to be learned, one that applies directly to our man’s life at the current time. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Many view GTT as a race with a large field. 128 men, 1 winner, how many times have we heard those words uttered these past few weeks? True of course, but to suggest that to win you must defeat 126 men and a chimp is ludicrous. The magic number as far as GTT is concerned is seven. Seven victories in matches set up randomly is what it takes to win this thing and that is a fact that keeps Evan Jackson going. In the grand scheme of things, he may not be in the top 32 wrestlers entered in GTT this year, hell, he may not even be in the top 64. But in his narrowed field, he’s proven his worth and now he’s down to the last eight in his bracket, the one named after the delightful Mrs H. His opposition now, the proverbial dark horse (if you’ll excuse the pun), Jayce Bradley. A threat to anyone in this tournament, in any bracket, on any day. A man not to be taken lightly. He’s got the tools, and he’s got the support, some people would stake their house on him winning and advancing. By all rights Jackson should have no chance. Thing is, people seem to have forgotten the golden rule - in a two horse race, all bets are off.
