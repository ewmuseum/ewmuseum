Target Part Two of Two: Best Served Cold

30 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-30 What Now? Now I had her name. Sounds like nothing, l admit, but it does mean something to me. Correction: it means everything to me. It’s a subject that I’ve been mulling over ever since I first laid eyes on the radiant beauty back in that diner on the outskirts of the state of Kansas. My memories had only just returned to me and I was in a decidedly vulnerable state. I had nowhere to go; no idea who the hell I was and I’d had a somewhat of a bad experience back in that little ol’ town. I don’t particularly want to talk about it at the moment. That situation may change over time – but I wouldn’t count on it. Aeryn found me. Gave me direction. Protected me. It was almost as if she was my guardian angel - a naïve thought I’ll admit, but like I said I was in a decidedly vulnerable frame of mind. Now I have here name and that’s almost as good as knowing the ins and outs of my past. Almost. Perhaps this fluttering in my gut means something after all? Maybe. She doesn’t say anything, but I know she follows my wrestling career. I get the image in my head of her huddling up to the TV every Monday Night for WWA’s flagship show Monday Meltdown and waiting for me to appear. An Idealistic thought, I know. On the other hand she’d probably forced to watch it to track me for her employer. Not for the first time since knowing her, I wonder why she works for our mysterious employer. I can tell by looking her in the eye that she clearly doesn’t approve of some of the things he has me do and if that’s the case, why doesn’t she just walk away? I see her wanting to tell me everything, wanting to get off her chest the real reason she is working with him, wanting me to get her out of it. All she needs do is ask. I would gladly sacrifice knowing about my past if it was to help her, just the way she helped me back then. It’s been almost a year now since waking up in that damned cornfield and my mysterious employer still hasn’t provided me with any information regarding my past. Not even my name. I wonder, does he even know about my past or was he just the one who found me first and manipulated me into helping him pursue his interests? Perhaps the governmental force chasing me is actually the ones who know me? Perhaps it’s actually them who created me, providing me with my extra abilities? Perhaps. It’s possible. I force the thoughts from my head with considerable ease. I’d had plenty of practice in that department recently. Dwelling on such thoughts will do me no good until all the pieces are in place, besides I needed to concentrate on my preparation. The match at the Super Series draws closer and I still need to prepare. I still need to heal from the Suicidal Six Way match at the WWA’s recent pay per view Labour Day Brawl. I needed to take the momentum for the match and turn it toward the super series. This is the big time now, something that I’ve been working toward for the better part of a year. This – wrestling – is more important to me than anything else. It was the only memory that accompanied me through the amnesia, knowing how to work, the ins and outs of the business. When I first realised this, it was like a lifeline to my past. I’ve been in the business ever since. I think the knowledge was implanted too deep into my mind for whatever – or whoever – erased my memory to even touch. Most of the knowledge retained is deeply embedded with the instinct section of my brain and since I’ve retained my instincts to some degree (I don’t actually know this, but it’s a well educated guess. For instances, if my instincts were not intact how would I even know that I knew Aeryn in my previous life?) it’s only logical that this is the case. I just hope and pray that my instincts are enough when the Super Series comes around because on some levels, my instinct is the only thing I can trust in this world. Interrogation Johnny Marconi squinted, as the black velvet bag they’d had over his head was removed. That’d had the damned thing on him for the better part of two hours now, so it took a couple of seconds for his eyes to adjust to the new light levels. He tried to rub them in order to ease the transition, but he couldn’t actually move his hands – they were bound behind him with ropes. The same two thugs that had helped to ambush him by his car, near the Adders Coil night club stood over him, menacingly, with a glint in their eyes that told Johnny they wanted nothing more than to beat the hell out of him. They parted, allowing Johnny to see the familiar face of Dennis Kujo sat on a simple wooden chair at the other end of the blank room. The room had no furnishings and was coloured in a rather sterile white. Johnny couldn’t put his finger on it, but there seemed to be something more to the room. However, upon seeing Dennis Kujo smiling viciously at him, the thought fell from his mind. “Wh-what do you want with me?” Johnny asked. “I’m just Barrone’s pick up guy, I don’t know anything important.” Kujo laughed and gestured at the walls with his hands. “See these walls?” Johnny glanced at the walls again and nodded in response to Kujo’s question. “They’re sound proof,” We’re in one of my safe houses. It’s a place on the east wing of a local mental institute, which I help to fund. There are about one hundred people in this complex and not one of them will be able to hear you scream.” Kujo smile widened as Johnny face twisted in terror. He was loving this. “I don’t know anything, I swear!” “Excellent,” Kujo said, as he stood from his chair and slowly approached Johnny, brandishing something shiny and metallic. “You’re not one of those sissy boys who squeals before a fellow has a chance to torture you. I applaud that, I really do!” “T-t-torture?” Johnny murmured, feeling a slight nauseating at the pit of his stomach. “But I told you, I don’t know anything!” Kujo chuckled. “We haven’t asked you any questions yet, Mr Marconi. How do you know whether you have any information to give me or not?” Johnny became quiet, hoping that somebody – anybody – would come to his aid, but he knew that just wasn’t going to happen and this was going to be a long day. “Now,” Kujo began, only a few metres away from Johnny now. “Tell me about the ‘extra’ help Barrone used to retrieve his daughter.” “But I don’t know anything about that!” Johnny exclaimed, hoping Kujo would simply believe him and let him go. Kujo shook his head in disproval, tutting for effect. “This appeared to be a highly professional affair, so I doubt any names were used, but I do know there was some kind of designated meeting place. I also know that you were involved and you passed on Barrone’s instructions to the right person.” Johnny had already begun to shake his head before Kujo had even finished speaking. “I swear I don’t know…” Kujo sighed. “I was hoping you’d say that. My boys here,” Kujo nodded at the two thugs stationed at either side of him, “didn’t believe me when I told them that I’d once spent the better part of forty-eight hours torturing some poor fool back in the eighties.” “Forty-eight hours?” Johnny questioned in disbelief. Kujo nodded. “I’m glad I have a chance to prove them wrong.” “But I don’t know anything…” Johnny persisted. Kujo dismissed him with a hand gesture. “Oh don’t whinge Johnny. Be a man. Accept your punishment with a little dignity. You know, a very popular torture technique in Asia is to fill your victim up to the brim with dry rice, then when you’re sure he’s full, make him drink a lot of water. The rice expands and the stomach explodes. Can you imagine that? It’d be some sight all right. Personally, I’ve never felt any compelling desire to actually try that one. Not enough pain in it for me. Sure, it’d be mighty painful to have your stomach just explode like that, but where’s the fun in watching you stuff your face for a couple of hours? I’d prefer a more hands on approach. Boys, soften him up first. I like ‘em nice and bloody.” “What meeting place? What are you talking about?” Johnny pleaded, wearily eying up the two thugs either side of him who had begun to roll up their sleeves. “I’m sure with a little persuasion,” Kujo began, “your memory will return soon enough.” It did not take Kujo long to get the answers he was looking for. Contact with the right people had to be made and Barrone needed to be destroyed. Crippling Aeryn and I reached the rather dank and rough section of the city within the hour. We perched ourselves on a building top across the way from a shady looking warehouse. It appeared as though a rather large section of the roof hand caved in and the place was abandoned, but the cars parked around the place told me differently. Such an obvious give away, I figured, but I’m betting that Barrone’s men own this neighbourhood so it doesn’t really matter who realises that the building isn’t as abandoned as it looks. As for the police, well, they don’t tend to venture around these parts very often. A rather sensible option in my honest opinion. “So what is this place?” I asked Aeryn, warily eying up the rather well pair of armed guards circling the perimeter. “Kujo gave us the address,” Aeryn replied, also noticing the armed guards. “Apparently hitting this place will ensure a level playing ground between the two sides.” “Not that I don’t trust Kujo,” I told her, “but why will taking this place down level the playing field?” “It’s his drug purifying station, Barrone’s largest source of income,” Aeryn explained. “Hitting it will essentially cripple his finances. That means he’ll have to join Kujo and start over again. Hence levelling the playing field.” I nodded “Sounds like a good plan.” Aeryn grinned. “Thanks.” “So any idea how in the hell we’re supposed to go about this?” I asked her. “I went over the plans of the warehouse just before I came to meet you on the lower-east side,” Aeryn answered. “The warehouse is essentially one giant open space, any kind of cover will be minimal.” “Oh, fabulous,” I responded. “But, there is a high level walkway running throughout the inside of the warehouse,” Aeryn added, “and there isn’t much lighting in the place. Should be enough shadows to mask our presence.” That sounded better. Before hand this sounded like some kind of suicide mission. I noticed the guards around the outside come back around for another run. “And that leads me to my next query,” I said. “What do we do about the guards covering the outer perimeter?” “Good point,” Aeryn conceded. “I guess we could slip by them and enter the warehouse, but…” “…they will still be on the outside when things start getting blown up inside the warehouse,” I interceded. “It’d be difficult to get out if at all. We could take them out before entering the warehouse.” Aeryn nodded. “But if we make too much noise and the others realise the two on the outer perimeter are missing, then our cover is blown.” “Still,” I said. “If that’s the case, we lob the explosives in and hope for the best. Once the dust has settled we could go back and make sure everything is destroyed.” Aeryn shook her head. “We really need to make ourselves scarce as soon as the explosion goes off. We don’t have the time to go back and check.” I developed a confused expression. “But why? The police probably don’t even dare to enter this part of the city anymore.” Aeryn nodded in agreement. “Yes you’re, my intelligence says that they don’t, but most of the locals are either buyers or working for Barrone. If they hear the explosion they’ll surely come looking for blood.” “Well,” I said, “I guess we need to take the outer guards first then.” Aeryn nodded. “I figure that’s the only way around the problem. We just have to make sure nobody hears or sees us do it.” “And,” I added, “we have to make sure that they don’t get word off via radio. I noticed one of them speaking into a two-way during their last pass.” “Aerial attack?” Aeryn ventured. “Yeah,” I agreed. “We need to be on the warehouse roof though.” Aeryn stood from her crouched position and took a good number of steps backward; I followed in suit. She then charged and the edge of the building and as her last step touched the edge of the building hurled herself forward, toward the warehouse. She landed upon the warehouse roof silently, but with considerable grace. I could only hope I would be able to echo such grace, but I very much doubted it. I ran and propelled myself off the building also, but I managed to over shoot Aeryn by a foot or so and to my surprise my landing was flawless. We waited about a minute or so for the outer guards to reach us again and as they were about to pass us I lowered Aeryn downward by her feet. As soon as they reached her, she grabbed each of their necks and cracked their heads together. Then I hauled her back up to the roof, she dragged each of the guards up by their belt buckles. We left them in a pile on top of the roof; they’d be out for about fifteen minutes or so and we’d be long gone by then. I held one of the guns up. “Should we take these with us, just in case?” Aeryn shook her head. “What would be the point? We have to be silent.” “But when we make ourselves known to the guards, we’ll be known anyhow.” I argued. “The guards aren’t even going to know we’re here!” Aeryn declared. “But we’ll need to get down there in order to plant the explosives,” I replied. Aeryn shook her head. “I think the better option would be to bring the building down. I put the idea forward to one of our employers trusted structure experts earlier, he said it’d work just fine.” I tossed the idea around in my head. “That’s a bit drastic isn’t it?” “That’s the only way we can both destroy their gear and neutralise any of their personnel at the same time,” Aeryn explained. “We’ll be killing a good twelve people in the process though.” “Great,” I said. “Let’s do it.” It was clear to me that Aeryn didn’t like my reaction to her after thought, as she turned and opened a rather large window that led to high level. I couldn’t help wonder whether such a reaction from me reminded her of what I was before the amnesia, in my previous life, it was obviously something she detested; was I turning into that man again? I followed Aeryn into the warehouse and prepared the explosives. Best Served Cold “Sir,” Kujo’s bodyguard entered his temporary makeshift office. “You have guests.” Kujo glanced up from her desk and grinned. “By all means, show them in.” The bodyguard at the door nodded to Kujo’s other bodyguard who stood with Aeryn and I. The bodyguard closest to us, needless said, “Follow me.” Aeryn went first and I took up the rear. We entered the room and for the first time in a good number of weeks I was face to face with Kujo again. I could actually see the evil painted across his face as though he was proud of the fact. Knowing him, he probably was. The bodyguards shut the office door behind them and took up flanking positions at either side of us. This had ambush written all over it. I’m sure Aeryn was thinking the same thing; at least, I hoped she was. “A mental institute, Kujo?” Aeryn asked. “Quite fitting I’d say…” I muttered, but in a volume that everybody could hear. Kujo chuckled. “Coming from two people who like to walk around in S&M gear, that insult doesn’t seem to wound me as you’d have hoped. I own this mental institute and now it serves as one of my many safe houses. Satisfied?” “Hardly,” I responded. Aeryn rolled her eyes. “Can you just stop flirting, Kujo and get on with this.” Kujo turned to me. “Has quite the temper, doesn’t she?” “You haven’t seen anything yet,” I told him and meant it. “I take it by all of the dust and debris you’ve managed to cover yourself in, the mission was a success?” Kujo asked, placing an expensive looking cigar in his mouth and lighting it. Aeryn nodded. “Yes. We brought the building down. Their operation has now been crippled.” “Any survivors?” Kujo asked, a maniacal glint in his eye. “No,” I told him. “Nobody managed to get out in time.” “Excellent,” Kujo praised. “Indeed,” Aeryn replied. “Our deal is done.” Kujo held up his hand. “Not quite…boys?” With that, each of his bodyguards pulled their handguns out, with surprising pace and pointed them at each of our heads. Aeryn and I glanced at each other and smiled. “Well that was a shock,” Aeryn declared. “Was it really?” Kujo asked. “I’d of thought two ‘special beings’ as yourselves would have saw this coming a mile away.” “We did dumbass,” I told him. “She was being sarcastic.” “Sarcasm has always been lost on me,” Kujo admitted. “After all, they do say it’s the lowest form of humour.” “That’s not the only thing that’s been lost on you either Kujo,” Aeryn declared. “Really?” He asked, taking a drag of his cigar. “Yeah, your also lack a sense of reality also,” I said, quickly knocking the handgun that’d been pressed up against my head away. The bodyguard fired the shot too late and bullet shot through a rather stylish lamp that had been the focal point of Kujo’s desk. Then I hit the thug with a good right hook, sending him flying across the room. I heard another gunshot – this time the bullet hit the far wall – and realised that Aeryn had done the same as I. She hurled her victim directly through Kujo’s office door. “If you thought that those guys were actually going to stop us.” Kujo shook his head. “No, I didn’t. But you can’t blame a guy for trying, right?” Aeryn sighed. “Our deal is done. Never make contact with our employer again. The next time he will be more than a little unresponsive to you queries.” We left Kujo sitting behind his desk, smiling at something neither of us could even begin to guess. His thirst for revenge had been quenched one would hope.
