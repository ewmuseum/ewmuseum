Christian Darke: Volume Two (Silence)

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 You shouldn’t love me. You should know that it’s all my fault. I should have stopped it. I should have been there. I should have protected you. I failed you. I failed our son. I don’t expect you to forgive me. I’ve spent so long trying to be someone else that I forgot who I really was. I want you to know that I love you. Always have. The soft pitter patter of rain begins to work its way through the rotten wood of the ceiling. The short staccato melody of the downpour causes a momentary break in my writing. I stare down at the letter scratched in front of me. A small flickering lantern on the end-table provides me with only a dim view of my words. The walls of the room are barren and cold, the off-white concrete paint cracking away. These safe-houses were never meant to be luxurious or welcoming. They served as reminders to all of the officers who stayed in them that this was only temporary, that there was always something better back at home. That was no longer the case for me; there was no home, nothing better, so sitting in the confines of the safe-house was fitting. Turning my attention back to the scribbled lines of the letter in front of me, an overwhelming sense of guilt rushes over me. It’s during these silent moments that the darkness takes hold of me. You let them die. I shake my head, determined to banish the voice. It’s been like this since I woke up. Been like this since that night that was the night that my life changed. “Fuck you, you motherfuckers!” I didn’t know how many of them were left. I didn’t even know if any of them were left at all, or if they had simply chosen to leave me here to rot. My entire body was in pain. My face was swollen and stinging. The pain was amplified as the cold breeze caressed my bloodied face. My bones and muscles ached; my entire body felt tattered and torn. Every inch of my body felt like it was broken. But still there was one thing that hurt more than everything else. It was the thing that was causing the nauseating emptiness in the pit of my stomach. My hands were bound behind my back; I was defenceless. My legs were wrapped by several layers of duct tape; the sticky grey adhesive bound me to the cold wooden chair. I stared out into the darkness. My vision was blurry, my eyes burning. I couldn’t see anything but the blackness of the shadows, but I knew that someone was out there. “Fuck you!” I screamed again, my words once again echoed off of the walls of the empty warehouse complex. “You fucking coward, show yourself.” “Tsk... tsk...tsk...” the sound of a tongue clicking rang out from somewhere in the shadows. I looked around, trying to find the source of the noise. Between the tears and the searing pain in my eyes from the beating I’d taken it was nearly impossible to see anything. I blinked and squinted, doing my best to clear the tears from eyes and make out the scene playing out around me. “Do not make threats that you know you cannot possibly deliver upon Christian,” the voice began. The voice was cold. His words sent shivers up my spine. “Or should I call you Aaron? That is who you are right? Officer Aaron Cobaine... Las Vegas Police?” I didn’t say anything. I didn’t know what I could possibly say. He was the one in charge. I was simply a pawn in his game. A pawn chasing a king that I would never reach. I would die long before I ever got close enough. “It’s your fault that they had to die,” he said. “Fuck you! Don’t blame me you sick fuck! I wasn’t the one who pulled that fucking trigger! Show yourself! Show yourself and I swear to God...” “What?!” the voice boomed, angrier than before. “You’ll do what? Kill me? Hurt me? Torture me? What?! Nothing! You’ll do nothing. I’m sorry you pathetic worm, but you did your best to hurt me and you failed. Just like you failed your fiancée. Just like you failed your son.” I opened my mouth, ready to scream. I was ready to unleash all of my rage at the man who had stolen my family from me. But instead of a blood-curdling scream of vengeance, no words came out. Silence. I was unable to speak. Trapped in my own silence, my emotions began to boil over once again. The only thing that was there to keep me company in my state of near insanity was the shadows and the soft beating of my broken heart. “You see Aaron, this was nothing personal. This was only fair.” “Fair?! Fair!? You murdered my family you bastard! You stole everything from me!” “And you tried to do the same to me!” the voice screamed back. “You tried to attack my family Office Cobaine. You tried to disguise yourself as one of our own to attack my family! It may not have been with the threat of death with a bullet or a blade, but it would have been death just the same.” “Family?” I tried to scream, but it came out as merely a whisper. “What do you know of family? You’re a monster. You prey on the weak, on the innocent. Families are about compassion and love, something that you are clearly incapable of. What the hell do you know of family Davis?” It was the first time that I had addressed him by name; the leader of the Nevermore. I knew it had been him all along. He was arrogant. Proud. It would only be him that would deal my deathblow. Beyond that, his voice was unmistakable. It was a voice filled with natural charisma and a dark savageness. A voice that had been scarred by years of tobacco smoke and cheap bourbon. “Do not preach to me about family boy!” His voice began as an angry roar, but quivered to a faint murmur by the end. It was the first hint of diffidence or vulnerability that I had ever gotten from him. “I had a family once and they were robbed from me. Do not speak to me about loss or about love or about family. But that was long in the past Officer Cobaine... Long in the past.” He went silent then; stoic and still. I wondered what this meant. Trapped in this hell I tried to decipher the monster’s words. He was strong and intelligent. In all of my dealings with him while undercover for the past thirteen months I had never seen him sway or stutter in the slightest. A man of strong conviction; Salaok Davis never showed doubt in himself. I chose my next words carefully, knowing that the man lurking in the shadows was somewhere beside his usual calm and calculating self. Knowing that these words would have a large effect on the amount of time remaining in my life. “Davis. I don’t give a fuck about your family. I don’t give a fuck about the Nevermore and I certainly don’t give a fuck about you. I feel sorry for any family you once had. Living everyday with a monster like you... Living everyday with an unloving, thoughtless, careless bastard like you must have been hell.” I could almost feel his hatred. I could almost feel the rage boiling in his blood. He stepped slightly forward then and I saw his silhouette in the shadows for the first time that night. Outfitted from head to toe in black he was merely a spectre moving through the darkness. A silver ray of moonlight battled its way through the waves of black and I caught the slightest reflection of his face. Any doubt about him that I had was gone in that moment. His face wore a Cheshire smile; an evil grin brandishing sadistic and evil intentions. The darkness washed away the moonlight and returned the monster to the shadows. Even in the dim light, I could still barely make out the sight of his lips moving. “You think that living with me would be hell? Hell is nothing compared to what I’ll do to you. You will beg for hell after I’m done with you! You say that I am a monster, that I am evil? I will show you just how evil I can be.” If only there was a way to make him remove my bindings. A way to make his pride take over. There was nothing that I wanted more in that moment than to get my hands on him. I wanted nothing more than to wrap my hands around his throat and suffocate every last breath from his body. He was the one responsible for all of the pain. Salaok Davis may not have been the man that raped my fiancée. He may not have been the man that put a bullet into her skull when he was done. He may not have been the man that tortured and murdered my son. He may not have ended those lives himself, but I was certain that he was the man who caused their demise. There was no question in my mind that Salaok Davis was the conductor of this symphony of death. He took another step forward and another flash of moonlight hit his silhouette. Something bright and metallic shimmered and gleamed in the silver radiance. Something silver. Something tucked into the waistband of his pants. His pistol. He reached down; his hand caressing the cold silver steel as he pulled the pistol from his beltline. He continued his march forward, bringing the firearm to meet my gaze as the monster stepped into the light for the first time. After wrestling my stare away from the barrel of the gun the first thing that I noticed was his hair. Usually kept neat and tied back today his hair was wild and loose. A soft silver streak of hair traced the border of his face. The rest of his mane was raven black and it blustered lightly in the soft wind. His eyes were bloodshot and constantly darting side to side, those were not the eyes of the Salaok Davis that I knew. Those were the eyes of a madman. For all of the uncertainty in his eyes, his hands told a different story altogether. His hands told a tale of confidence; he held his aim steady and true. The focus of the pistol’s barrel never left my eyes. I still wanted nothing more than to get my hands on him, to make him pay. I wanted to make him pay for all of the suffering he had caused me. I wanted to kill him. When my eyes met his however, I saw something that I had never seen in him before. It was a pained look. A look of sadness, almost a look of remorse. And then in an instant, it was gone. Now replaced by the snarling, vicious eyes of the monster once again. It was in that moment when our gaze met that I realized something. That single split-second of compassion in his eyes, the milliseconds of anguish when something hit me. As much as I wanted to hurt him, as much as I wanted his blood on my hands, as much as I wanted to avenge the deaths of my family, there was something that I wanted more. I wanted to be with them. I wanted to be with my family again. Call it crazy, insane, suicidal. Call it whatever the fuck you want to call it. I’d to anything to see their smiling faces again. I’d do anything to run my hands through my fiancée’s soft blonde hair, to hold my son in my arms once again. Anything. I put my head back and closed my eyes. I prepared myself to embrace the bullet. I prepared myself to embrace everything that it would bring to me. Its skull-shattering violence was no longer something that I feared; instead it was something that I welcomed. Something that I longed for. “Do whatever you have to do...” my words sounded like his now. They were cold and emotionless. I kept my eyes closed and kept my head back. I awaited the threnody of gunshots that would carry me back to them. Back to my fiancée. Back to my son. I ignored the soft laughter he emitted, it wasn’t unlike him to enjoy the last few moments before a kill. But his next words were more confusing than anything he’d said all night. “Christian...?” It was the first time that he had called me that all night. The first time since he found out my true identity. Why now? I opened my eyes slowly the monster’s cold stare there to greet me. Finally, he gave me the subtlest of winks before uttering his final words. “See you soon.” The gunshot erupted from the gun and roared like thunder in the empty sky. After that everything was silent. Deathly silent. Staring back at my words I feel surrounded by that same deathly silence. I don’t know why I lived through that night; don’t know how I did it either. The doctors said that if the bullet had penetrated another millimetre it would have been the end of the line. One single millimetre and I would be six feet under right now. But I’m not. Maybe I was wrong on that night. Maybe I had it all wrong. Maybe I wasn’t supposed to join them yet, maybe it wasn’t time for that. Perhaps, I was supposed to avenge them first. That’s what the darkness is here for. I know it. As much as I may not want to admit it, I know. Looking down and reading my words over again I feel a nauseating emptiness deep inside me. Inside me the darkness is growing. It’s time. They will all pay. It starts with one, but they will all have their time. It starts tonight. There’s a small picture of all of us on the night stand. It’s one of the few that I have left. My fiancée, my son and I; all of us smiling, happy, innocent. It shows a better time. A time before I let the evils of this world into our life. Before I brought the monsters home. I allow myself a few moments to slip back into my memories of them. I give myself a brief allowance of happiness, because it’s something that I didn’t feel very often. I bring the photograph towards my face still studying their faces the entire time. I bring the image to my lips and kiss the memories of my family. The outline of my lips glistens on the photo paper, reflecting in the soft lantern light. I place the photo into the letter and fold it into a small white envelope, sealing away the memories once again. I grab a pack of cigarettes from the desk beside my cot and using my teeth pull a single cigarette from the pack. I grab my lighter and bring it towards the smoke, bringing it up to light the smoke. All the while my eyes never leave the white envelope in my other hand. My mind wanders from the cigarette and back to the letter; back to their memory. I snap the lid of the Zippo open and the butane ignites a glowing blue flame. Bringing the flame to the corner of the envelope, it takes only a few seconds before the envelope is engulfed in flames. My eyes never leave the flame. I watch as it dances, swallowing the memories of my family as it does so. Watching the fire consume the envelope, I let my mind rest. The flame will erase the words, erase the photo, and erase all of the memories. I raise the envelope towards my face and use the small paper fire to ignite my cigarette before tossing it into the corner of the room. Even the rain can’t stop the flame from consuming everything now. The envelope smoulders as it finishes burning, another bit of my happiness removed from my life forever. I close my eyes and listen to the rain again, the soft staccato of the rainfall is the only thing that keeps the silence away. The only thing that keeps the darkness at bay. I take a long drag from the cigarette, inhaling the tobacco smoke as I do and allow a few seconds for the tobacco to pollute my lungs before exhaling. The envelope finally burns out completely; the fire has consumed everything now. Its greed has finished everything, the memories, the words, the images, all of them are gone now. With nothing left to feed itself the flame flickers, grasping for its last breaths of life before being extinguished. I take a final drag from the cigarette before flicking it into the corner and standing up. Heading to the door, I realize that the darkness was right. It is time. And I remember that I made him pay for everything that he did. I allow a smirk to creep over my face as I look at the last few crime scene photographs. I know that the detective will notice it. I know that it will give him hope in his crusade to get a confession from me. Although it will merely be false hope. I look up at the detective and our stares meet for the first time in what seems like ages. In his face I can see pain, pain that I once felt. Pain for the loss of a loved one. It’s then that I realize that I pity him. I wish that I could offer him something. Anything. Instead, I say nothing. Silence.
