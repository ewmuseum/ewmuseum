The SPAM Of Roleplays

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 It is said at the end of days, Heaven and Hell will engage in one final battle between Good and Evil, and the souls of those who have come before us will be the seraphic warriors for this celestial battleground known as Earth. Fire will fall from the sky like rain, and its own weight will crush the ground below. Pain and torment like no other shall be cast upon the Earth, but when the Right Hand of God has dealt the final blow, Evil will exist no more. Good will triumph. So it has been written, and so it shall come to pass. There are exceptions to all things, however. Even the greatest of plans can be unraveled by one tiny hiccup, and the greatest plan of all is no different. So when all the i’s had been dotted, and all the t’s crossed, God went to work on Divine Plan B. An angel, in the form of a human, sent to Earth in order to keep Divine Plan A on track. To ensure Satan didn’t throw a monkey wrench into God’s Divine Plan-Making Machine, the angel would have no memory of his real identity, so not even the Dark Lord himself could know he existed. And when Lucifer hatched Malevolent Plot W-416, the angel’s true form would be revealed, and he would cast the demons back to Hell until the end of time as we know it. However, because Fate’s a bitch that got pissed at God for not calling her back, the angel got AIDS from having a gay thought at twelve-years-old, and died. Turns out, Hoyt was right all along. For lack of a better term, God crapped his pants. And now we know what causes solar eclipses. But what no one knows is how God will stop Beelzebub from overtaking the world, when his holy guardian is six feet under. Enter Clyde Walkins. …Not literally. A seeming halfwit, with the intellectual capacity of an old light bulb and perhaps some gravel. Far from Heaven’s Perfect Warrior, but he’ll have to do. Besides, who else is there? You? Don’t make me laugh. …Because that’s my job, you see. To make you laugh…so you don’t have to do it for me…ahem… … -_- I’m just gonna go ahead and start the story then. Walkins: Why do we have to stay here again? Farwell: Because it’s closest to the arena. Walkins: But I thought staying at the hotel shaved three hours off our driving time? Besides, it’s creepy here. People died in that house. Farwell: Last I checked, my name wasn’t Rockefeller. We’re not staying at a damn hotel, Clyde. Anyway, the house isn’t creepy…and people have died everywhere in the world by now, so it’s not like it makes it any different from where you’re standing. Upon hearing this, Clyde drops his suitcases on the driveway to the old house that James inherited from his uncle a few months back, turns around swiftly, and gets right back into their rental car. Exhausted with Clyde’s idiocy already, James grips the handles on his suitcases ever tighter, finally walking up the driveway with the intent to leave Clyde out there all night. Walkins: Hey! Where are you going? Farwell: I’m going inside. By the way, the heater’s broken in the car, and it’s supposed to snow tonight. Sweet dreams. And with that, James opens the front door and steps inside. A moment later, the door shuts and locks, and Clyde is left on his own inside some shitty automobile. Like a Micra or something. Walkins: Ok, Clyde, you can handle this. No need to be scared…no one’s died in this car…that you know of. Jumping as a flash of lightning cuts through the formerly bright sky, Clyde peers out the window at the rapidly approaching grey clouds. Walkins: It’s just a little storm…nothing strange about a black sky at ten in the morning… The booming thunder does it. Within seconds, James rouses from reading the newspaper in an exceptionally comfortable chair, and responds to the knocking at the front door by opening it partway. Farwell: Yeeeees? Sopping wet, Clyde holds his suitcases in his arms, a forlorn look on his face as rain drenches everything not under a roof. Walkins: Outside…so…c-cold…wet…pneumonia… Farwell: Suddenly a haunted house doesn’t seem so bad, does it? Walkins: N-no, sir… Having given Clyde a hard enough time, James pulls the door open and lets the dripping retard into his house. Farwell: Next time, I’m leaving you out there to fend for yourself. A quick one-over of his wet companion shows James the damage. Farwell: Go upstairs, take a hot bath, then find some dry clothes. I’ll take your things out of those wet suitcases for you. Walkins: Th-thank you… Shivering, Clyde slowly walks past James and up the stairs to the bathroom. The running water indicates Clyde is following orders, and so James gets to his end of the bargain, thusly cracking open both suitcases. Farwell: AHH! Walkins: AHH! Clyde pops his head out of the bathroom. Walkins: What are you screaming about? Farwell: There’s a dead midget in one of these bags! And what are you screaming about? Walkins: The bathtub is peeing blood. I told you this place is haunted. Farwell: Nonsense, it’s probably just a venereal disease. Now have your bath while I dispose of this…thing. Shaking his head in disgust, James carefully picks up the lifeless body of the little man with his index finger and thumb, pinching a piece of fabric from the midget’s pants with which to carry him. The door opens and the cadaver streaks across the front lawn after being tossed from the house, before Farwell promptly locks out the world once more. Farwell: Damn Clyde and the shit he brings home… Meanwhile, Clyde rests upstairs in the bath he’s drawn for himself. Chest-deep in some sort of red liquid, he splashes his feet and makes the bar of soap race around the tub like a speedboat, singing the entire time. Walkins: Splish-splash, I’m in my red bath…something just touched my thigh! Downstairs, James goes back to his reading after leaving Clyde’s stuff strewn across the front hall. Newspaper in hand, he kicks off his shoes and slides on a fuzzy pair of slippers, relaxing once more in the unusually comfortable chair left to him by his uncle. Farwell: Five…four…three…two…one… Walkins: Jaaaames! I need a towel! Still staring at the paper, but not actually reading anything anymore, James calls back up to Clyde. Farwell: You already have a towel! And then a pause. But not a long pause. Walkins: I need another one. Throwing his paper to the floor, Farwell lets out an exasperated sigh and heads straight for the stairs. He suddenly feels more and more like finishing Clyde off once and for all, so that he’ll never have to put up with another stupid antic as long as he lives, and begins taking the stairs two at a time. Almost three, but he tripped when he tried it, so it went back down to one for a little while there. Farwell: I swear…if you’ve done something already… The rage inside James disappears the moment he pushes the bathroom door open and sees Clyde’s naked ass staring at him from under a cover of red. Farwell: My eyes! Falling back, James shields his vision to the best of his ability, as Clyde starts to turn around. Farwell: No! Don’t! Forty-five minutes later, and Clyde is finally cleansed of the red liquid that had completely covered his body. James tosses a stained towel to the side, and pulls a cigarette out from his shirt pocket. Walkins: I thought you quit? Farwell: With you, it’s either this or suicide. Lighting and taking a drag in the same instant, James coughs slightly as he inhales the smoke from one of a few cigarettes he’s had at all in the past year. He looks up at Clyde, who brushes his hair off with the second, cleaner towel, and takes another puff. Farwell: So…what, did you decide to bathe in wine or something? Walkins: I told you, it was the bathtub! It has a urinary tract infection! Farwell: …Maybe it was rusted. Walkins: Rusted with blood! James takes another drag. Farwell: It wasn’t blood. There’s a reasonable explanation for this, and it probably involves you doing something stupid. Walkins: Why is it always my fault? This is more of a witch-hunt than that Kobe Bryant rape trial. Cut to a courtroom, with a lawyer standing in front of the female accuser. The rest of the court is packed with family members, friends, jurors, Kobe, the judge, and the remaining legal counsel menagerie. Lawyer: Now is it true that Mr. Bryant raped you on the night of June 30th, 2003? Accuser: Sniff…it…it is… Lawyer: And how is that possible? After all, he’s a multimillionaire, and I think you’re some kind of topless dancer. Why would he possibly want you…unless you put a spell on him! Witch! Jury: She’s a witch! She’s a witch! Judge: In light of this new evidence, I find in favor of Kobe Bryant. The witch is to be burned at sundown. Back to The Forsaken. Farwell: Mock all you want, but the United States government spends billions of our tax dollars each year protecting us from those very witches. It’s why we took Iraq, you know. Walkins: It’s just not fair. No one ever believes me when I tell the truth. Farwell: Oh, I’d believe the truth. What I wouldn’t believe, however, is that the spirit of an old man with bladder problems seemingly possessed our bathtub. Walkins: Fine! Don’t believe me…but we’ll see what happens when the house eats you and I have the last laugh. Pointing a finger at James, Clyde narrows his eyes, then promptly stands and marches out of the bathroom. Five seconds later, he returns, the angry look still on his face. Walkins: Which room is mine? Farwell: The one on the left. Walkins: Thank you. The angry stare never leaving his face, Clyde marches in reverse out of the bathroom, turns, and continues marching backwards into his room. James just sits there for a second, amidst red stains, the cigarette in his mouth burning down, and wondering what kind of retarded vengeance he can expect tonight. Deciding to call it a night at three in the afternoon, he gets up and flicks the cigarette into the toilet, takes a quick piss, decides against rubbing one out, and turns the light off as he leaves the bathroom. Sometime during the night, the voices speak to Clyde. Softly at first, but they slowly grow in volume, until he can hear them clearer than his own thoughts. He doesn’t believe you, Clyde. He never believes you. He doesn’t love you. No one loves you. You’re all alone. Alone. But we love you. We’ll never leave you. You don’t need him anymore, Clyde. You don’t need anyone anymore. James hears the door to Clyde’s room open and close, and what sounds like Clyde talking to someone as he walks downstairs. Deciding against checking it out, James simply rolls over and goes back to bed, while Clyde starts work on his revenge plan. Walkins: How do I do it? How do I beat James? You must go into the past. Yes, Clyde. The past. Bring him back. The only one that can beat James. Bring back the one who will carry humanity’s destruction on the back of his arrival! Walkins: I don’t know…I just want to beat James, not usher in the whatever of whoever. Then he’ll just beat James. Yes. Just beat James. No destruction of humanity here. Clyde scratches his head as he sits at the computer in the study. Looking through websites for plans to build a time machine, he finally stumbles across one entitled "How To Make WMDs & Time Machines With Nuclear Power In 3 Easy Steps". Walkins: Hmm…this could be the ticket. But it says this thing takes over an hour to make, and I think that’s a little much to ask for a measly time machine, don’t you? Sigh. Fine. We’ll build the damn thing ourselves. Happy now? Walkins: Quite. And just because I don’t feel like filling in all the blanks, we’re gonna skip forward an hour and fifteen minutes. The time machine is built to satisfaction, and Clyde is but a moment away from stepping inside. Now remember. Yes, remember. You must bring back a sample of his DNA so we can clone him. We will bring him back to life. And then humanity’s destru- Walkins: Yeah, yeah. Humanity’s destruction will be swift and sure. Gotcha. Jumping headfirst into the portal, Clyde only succeeds in banging his head and falling back to the floor. Sorry, I didn’t turn it on yet. A look of slight anger on his face, Clyde rubs the back of his neck and then gingerly steps into the portal. Back in the house, an eerie laughter awakens James from his slumber. Farwell: Poltergeist or not, no one wakes me from a dream involving Aimz and Karina Wolfenden! At least in that world, I can’t catch something from 1-800-GOT-AIDS and Rent-A-Whore. Ripping the sheets from his body, James turns and steps off the side of the bed, his feet landing squarely in his slippers. A robe covers his body less than ten seconds later, and he’s out of the room before the voice has a chance to stop laughing. Coming down the stairs, Farwell clenches his fist and slams it against the wall as the laughter is cut short. Ow! That hurt! Farwell: I’ll teach you for being a cock-blocker! Whack. Hey, quit it! Yeah, it was only a dream. Not even a wet one. You were only at half-mast. Farwell: That’s the best I’ve gotten in yea- James stops himself as the house listens intently. Farwell: Hours. It’s the best I’ve gotten in hours. Uh…huh… Farwell: And what did you do with Clyde? You haven’t killed him yet, have you? Clyde’s…uh… Clyde’s not available at the moment, but if you’d like to come back another time… Time! How droll. Thank you. I thought so. Oh, it really was. You’re not just saying that, are you? Of course not, silly. Farwell: When you ladies are done chatting, I’d like a real answer. James now stands at the bottom of the stairs, surveying the area for anything of use. Farwell: Or else I’ll have to…put a few new holes in you, if you catch my drift. I’m not sure I do… And we’re guys, you jackass! Farwell: Whatever you have to tell yourself to make the rubber and plastic seem more real. Now where is Clyde? Spotting what he was looking for, James reaches beside one of the suitcases he left downstairs, lifting a sledgehammer from off the floor. Farwell: I knew bringing this thing here would come in handy. Ok, ok…we’ll tell you. Just put the hammer down. Suspicious, but cooperative, James slowly drops the hammer onto the ground. Hey! We feel that too, you know. His face doesn’t show it, but his mind is smiling. Farwell: Thought as much. What was that? Farwell: Nothing. Now…about Clyde? Some eighty years ago, Clyde is in the process of working his way up the ranks of the Nazi military. Trying his best to blend in without having to speak, he sneaks past armed guards and other elitist bastards, finally coming to the place he’d been looking for. Walkins: Now just to sit and wait for my time to shine. Pushing open the door in front of him, with a sign marked Fuhrers on it, Clyde finds himself in a Nazi washroom, and quickly takes to hiding in the furthest stall from the entrance. It isn’t long before he finds that time travel isn’t all it’s cracked up to be, though. As the door to the bathroom opens, a large lumbering Nazi general shuffles into the stall next to Clyde, takes down his pants, and squats. The ensuing stench is foul enough to take Roseanne down a peg or two. Walkins: Oh, Jesus…this is so horrible…even worse than the time OSW closed. Cut to James and Clyde with boxes and bags packed, moving out of a fancy house to a small apartment, while the tune of The Jeffersons plays in the background. "Oh, we’re moving on down…to the West side…" Still photos of Clyde and James unpacking as much as they can into their tiny new home, and then of Clyde taking out the trash in a nearby alley. "Gangs, guns, and violence…I might die…" More images of Clyde being harangued by street toughs, followed up with a shot of him stabbed in the gut, his eyes shut and a pained expression on his face as his arms come out in front of him. "Oh, we’re moving on down…to the West side…" Photos of police cars and sirens in the background. "Someone finally stabbed Cly-y-y-yde!" And a final image of Clyde lying face down in a pool of his own blood, his left leg bent slightly, and both arms in a position of attempted crawling. And now back to The Forsaken. Minus James. But in the early 20th century. After the general finally leaves - without flushing, I might add - the door opens again and the one Clyde’s been waiting for makes his appearance. Standing in front of a urinal, Hitler unzips and prepares to urinate, but something’s amiss. No, he isn’t urinating at all… Waiting to see what’s taking so long, Clyde holds a specimen cup in his hands and peers over the top of the stall. And there he sees a sight no man was ever meant to see. A seductive picture of Ava Braun in his left hand, and a bulging member in his right, Hitler goes to town in plain view, leaving even Clyde mildly disgusted. But disgust only lasts so long, and just as Mount Hitler is about to have an eruption, Clyde leaps from the stall and holds the cup right in front of him. Hitler: Ach! Mein sploogen! Walkins: Now it’s mine sploogen! Capping the lid, Clyde takes off at full speed, running out of the bathroom with Hitler in close pursuit. Pulling his trousers up from around his ankles, Hitler calls for his army to attack Clyde, and soon every Nazi available is chasing Walkins through their base. Just as he’s gasping for air, Clyde finally comes to the time portal, and with enough time to spare, decides to showboat a little for the crowd before going home. As he swings around and starts doing the Robot through the portal, the moron somehow manages to trip on the ledge as he steps back into the present. The cup of sperm flies into the air, loses its lid, and lands on the ground, spilling its contents across the rug. His arms still flailing, Clyde turns his body and tries to stop himself from falling, but ends up landing directly in the sploogen, his face streaking along the befouled carpeting. Peeking his head through the portal, Hitler switches focus from Clyde to the computer screen, and quickly takes note of what he sees, before ducking back into the past as the door to the study opens. In the background, holes and pieces of house missing are evident, but Farwell’s figure soon blocks out the wreckage as he appears with a tray of freshly baked cookies in his hands. Farwell: I know I was a little hard on you, so I wanted to say I’m so- Stopping in mid-sentence, James’ jaw slowly drops as he sees Clyde writhing in what can only be another man’s baby batter. Without saying another word, Farwell backs out of the room, shuts the door, and pretends he didn’t see a thing. The next day, James and Clyde both meet at the bottom of the stairs, awakened with renewed vigor. Farwell: How did you sleep last night, Clyde? Walkins: Like a dead midget. You? Farwell: After I got a few images out of my head, fine. But I think there’s something you should see. Opening the door, James steps aside and lets Clyde peer out. Amidst fire falling from the sky, and the world crumbling into itself, Satan and Hitler walk together arm-in-arm as the Nazi regime overruns the rest of the world. Walkins: I don’t get it. How did you clone Hitler without his DNA? We didn’t need to clone him. You gave him the Bomb. So we didn’t have to. Now he rules the world. More evil laughter ensues, followed by James cracking another hole in the wall. Farwell: Shut up, dammit! Walkins: Don’t worry, James. I know how to handle this. Without warning, Clyde makes a mad dash for the time portal as the world around him collapses into nothingness. Arriving in the past just ten seconds before he did originally, Clyde stands patient for a second or two, then checks his watch. Finally, a flash of light brings another Clyde into the picture. Walkins: Hey, who are you-agh! Responding the same way as in The Jeffersons montage rip off stabbing, Clyde drops into a pool of his own blood, as Clyde From Ten Seconds Ago stands proud with his hands on his hips, before returning to his own time. Walkins: There, I did it. Farwell: Did what? Unbeknownst to James and the rest of the world, Clyde saved the day. An unlikely hero, caught in the battle between Good and Evil…in the Twilight Zone. … What do you mean, I’ve still got five minutes left? Uhh…well…let’s see here… After they left the house…James decided to make it up to Clyde. Yes, that’ll do. He decided to make it up to Clyde…by…taking him…hunting! Perfect. I get to throw in some filler, and no one’s the wiser. On the drive to the hunting grounds, James decides to pry at Clyde for a bit of information. Farwell: So, have you actually been preparing at all for your GTT5 match? Or is this just one of those things you intend on half-assing all the way through? Walkins: PRIME and all the wrestlers in it are counting on me to win. Of course, I’ve been preparing for it! Cut to another video montage, this one playing Clyde’s theme of "Now You’re A Man" by DVDA. First off, we see Clyde walking slowly on the treadmill, then lifting five or six pounds on the bench press, and then he moves on to the heavy workout. This time Clyde briskly walks on the treadmill, and even maxes out the weights at ten pounds per arm. Finally, he enters the Terry Fox Run and starts off with a head of steam. Less than a mile later, Clyde collapses from exhaustion as little old ladies pass him. And then it starts to rain. Back to The Forsaken. Farwell: You know, for some reason I don’t think your shenanigans are going to help against LeStatt Knight. Walkins: Who? Farwell: …Your opponent. Walkins: Vampir Nosferatu? Farwell: You know what? Sure. It’s Vampir Nosferatu. Have you at least trained for any kind of match? Clyde just hangs his head. James knows the answer without having to be told. Farwell: Well, after we’re through hunting, I’m getting you ready for that match. It’s gonna take a lot of hard work and determination to beat the greatest GCW champion there’s ever been. Apparently. But you’ll do it. Walkins: And if I don’t? Farwell: Then we’ll kill enough people until they let one of us back in! Finally arriving at the hunting lodge a few moments later, Clyde and James take out their rifles and gear, and head to the cabin. Inside, James starts putting on brown pants with a matching coat, hat, and gloves. Walkins: Why are you trying to look like an animal when everyone’s shooting at them? In no mood to explain the intricacies of hunting to his retarded companion, James rolls his eyes and motions for Clyde to leave. Farwell: It’s complicated hunting stuff. You wouldn’t understand. Now let me get changed. Shrugging, Clyde decides to entertain himself outside for a while, remembering to take his rifle with him for protection. After a few minutes pass with no excitement, the front door of the cabin opens and out steps James in full hunting gear. Farwell: How do I look, Clyde? Walkins: AHH! IT’S A MOOSE! Then Clyde shoots him in the kneecap.
