A Christmas Present, So To Speak “Dude, c'mon, it's Christmas!” “No,” I say. “Look, you guys have a good time, get pissed and leer over women. I'm in training; I can't afford to coast now of all times.” There's more whining, but they've accepted that I'm going and really all they're doing is killing time before Derek finishes his drink and they can move on to the next bar. They're my work colleagues, by which I mean my day job in the postroom. I've taken a good deal of stick from them all for my other job, that being to clothe myself in lyrca and grapple with oiled-up men. If there's a new way for them to insult me about that, I've yet to hear it. “Hey man,” slurs Pete, stumbling towards me and putting one hand on my shoulder in a gesture of manly compassion. “You be careful out there. Don't get shot or nuthin'.” I laugh at tell him that I'll be fine. There have been a couple of murders recently, but I can handle myself. Besides, I'm probably the only person out tonight who's not drinking. I've told the guys that it's because of the big match I have lined up, and to an extent that's true, but really it's because of Karen. Beautiful, radiant, capricious Karen. I was lucky enough to be caught in her orbit for three whole months; the best time of my entire life. I fell head over heels in love with her and she became my everything. I tried to tell myself that it wouldn't last, but she does strange things to you. My world began and ended on her name, and I became blind to any failings she possessed. I could only see perfection, right up to the point where she casually tore out my heart and spread it across the walls of 'our' bar. She never even apologised. I try not to dwell on it, but it still affects me to this day. I walk home, like I'm doing now, fresh off the bus, and it reminds me of her. I can recall every little joke she made as we walked down this road, turning left down the secluded lane towards the road I live on. We used to hold hands and walk slowly down here, lingering for longer than necessary as we kissed and dared each other to go further and further, always chickening out, the fear that someone would see us as we got down to business. Nobody ever did, as hardly anyone ever comes this way. I can look around here now, and imagine what it would have been like had she not left me, and as ever it leaves me angry, so I move on, almost flinching as a man blocks the end of the pathway. I walk towards him, wondering who he might be as he blots out the light coming from the other end of the lane. There are fences on either side, with bushes and trees and other assorted greenery creating a canopy. We're alone, the two of us, on rails that will ultimately bring us together. I wonder if he's the murderer. If this will be my final night on this planet. If he could bring me release from the pain I have suffered since Karen went away. Not just away but to another man's arms. She didn't even say sorry when I caught her at it. She just smiled in a way I think she meant to be disarming or charming, but which I found simply infuriating. Remembering that smile makes it easier for me to do what I do, both within the ring and outside it. The man walks closer, and I almost find myself worrying. If I was killed here tonight, if he was the murderer then what would my legacy be? Certainly not what people would expect it to be, that's for sure. Apart from it all though, do I feel hope for my chance in the big Infinite Gauntlet match? I'm not sure, you know? I have a great chance; up against a nobody like me and a woman so feared that even to throw her name about is to be in dangerous company. Does she intimidate me? Not at all. Am I impressed by who she hangs around with? Not one bit. My life began and ended with Karen. Alexandra Pierce is a means to an end, nothing more. The man walking towards me is close. I can almost smell him. The butterflies are in my stomach. We both hesitate... In a second the gun's in my hand and pointed right at his head. I push him back with it and he stumbles, landing on the floor. They all do this, and I have no trouble pushing the barrel right back into his forehead. He's almost kneeling as I press him back, lifting his head up so I can see his eyes in the pale sodium street light. This is why I do it, the brief seconds where he's wondering if I could do it, if I could pull the trigger. For the brief moment, the split second when he realises that I can, and that I will. When his mind releases the primal knowledge that I'm the one with the power here, and that all his hopes and all his dreams are going to count for nothing, that's the moment I live for. It's the look in their eyes as they realise that they are mortal. The brief instant when they feel nothing but pure despair. That's when I pull the trigger. It's not as loud as you'd think, as the skull fountains out at the back and the body drops (again, you'd be surprised at how quickly it does this). There's a dull bang, a dull thud and the end of what was probably a dull life. Even a third time, it doesn't feel anything other than justinteresting, maybe even intriguing. I put the gun back in my pocket, take a couple of quick photographs and then walk back on my way. I know that I've been careless, committing a murder on near enough my own doorstep, but I couldn't help myself. She was in my mind again and this is the only way I can chase her away. Three times now, and more in the future. I don't care that my career could take off; all I care about is making things right in the here and now. Yes, people will find me strange, freakish even, but I don't care. And if I go on to win the Infinite Gauntlet then all the better. I've not hidden, I've not tried to destroy the forensics at every crime scene. They will find me eventually, probably even pretty soon. That's the plan. They'll find me a suspect, maybe not right now, but soon, and they'll kick down my door. I might be there or I might not be. They'll prowl throughout the flat, and they'll look into the spare room and see all the evidence. The photos of the men I have killed will be there, right next to the pictures of her. I've done all I can to make sure they use the word 'shrine'. I've done everything in my power so that they will see what I have assembled and be repulsed. They will call me sick, they will call me a pervert and they will call me a murderer. And they will say that it's all down to Karen. That my obsession with her caused those men to die; that their corpses can be laid down at her doorstep, so it becomes her turn to be unable to sleep, unable to move on with her life. And maybe then she'll be fucking sorry.
