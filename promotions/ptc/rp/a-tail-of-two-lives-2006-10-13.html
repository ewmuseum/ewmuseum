"A Tail of Two Lives"

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-13 * * * * * September 1974 The Chevrolet sped around the turn, leaving a gust of wind in its wake that sent the fallen leaves blowing towards the side of the road. Quickly glancing in the rearview mirror, Daniel Keene couldn’t see the headlights of the car that was chasing him, at least not right at this minute. They were maybe a mile or two behind him, but the road was narrow and winding, and their car was more than likely around a bend, or hidden below the low rolling hills. Next to him, his son continued to cry out in pain. But with the sun quickly fading, and the need to use the headlights to navigate, he only had a few minutes to spare. There wasn’t any time to tend to the infant, to see if the fresh wounds on his back were making him cry, or if the high speed car chase was scaring him. The Sage farm was only a quarter mile away, and he had to hurry. Once again glancing up at the rear view mirror, Daniel saw a pair of headlights come roaring over a small hill, shining for a moment as they reached the apex, and then disappearing back into another valley. He had only five minutes at most, before they would be atop the Chevrolet with their guns pointed and trigger fingers primed and ready. He had betrayed them, the entire cult, along with his wife Valerie. The Eyes of Fear considered betrayal to be the final signature on your own death certificate. Valerie’s still cooling corpse, back at the hospital, only proved that they weren’t as ignorant and incompetent as Daniel had planned. Jerking the wheel to the right, the Chevrolet turned towards the Sage farm with a screaming of rubber as the tires tried to bite into the asphalt. Daniel prayed that his pursuers hadn’t crested the next hill, and noticed the change in direction. There wasn’t much time. Screeching the car to a halt directly in front of the Sage farmhouse, Daniel quickly unlatched his infant son and gingerly pulled the wounded child from the car seat. The bandage across the child’s back was mildly blotted with blood, but it appeared as if the bleeding had stopped. With the speed and grace of an Olympian, Daniel Keene raced up the steps to the front door of the Sage farmhouse, ready to pound on the front door with vigor, when he was surprised to find the door already open with the couple standing in the doorway, their faces perfectly matched with a mix of confusion and horror. "Sir…can we help you?" asked Maxwell Sage, one hand opening the screen door while the other was holding back his wife out of protection. "My name is Daniel, and I am going to ask you the biggest favor that anyone could possibly ask you," taking a deep breath, about to make a request that a father should never have to make, "I need you to look after my son, Clinton." Horror and confusion melted away from Lauren Sage’s face, replaced by disbelief, "Wait…why? For how long?" Off in the distance, a car engine could be heard racing through the small valley. It sounded like it was being pushed to its limits, as if they were outrunning death itself, rather than guiding it’s hand unto others. "For the rest of his life," Daniel quickly stepped between the befuddled Sage family, holding Clinton close, "I know this may sound ridiculous, but my wife and I have done research and we know that you two have been trying to have children, and have failed many times. We know that you two have thought about adoption, and have put the paperwork in for it." Lauren Sage winced at the mentioning of failed conception. Daniel continued, "I know this is perhaps the strangest thing anyone will ever ask of you, but I need you to take my son in, and raise him as your own." "What about you and your wife?" Maxwell asked, his mouth still agape. "My wife is dead." Lauren’s eyes widened, her voice a whisper, "Dead…h-h-how?" "There are men after me. My wife and I were part of something that isn’t exactly noble, and now they’re coming to finish me off as well, and take our son. Right now the only thing that is important to me is getting him in your possession, and getting the hell out of here to keep them away from you…" Maxwell put an arm around Lauren, and both stood there looking down at the infant, who continued to scream and cry. "That car you hear racing somewhere off in the distance? It's them. I’ve only got minutes here, so I’m going to ask you to never tell anyone of this night. Simply say that your paperwork went through, and the adoption agency brought you a black haired baby boy. Please, please, keep him from the public eye for quite some time. Especially with the wounds he has." Daniel slowly peeled away the bandage across young Clinton’s back, revealing three large reddish welts, one on each shoulder blade and another at the small of the back. At each welts center, a small tattoo in the shape of an ellipse with a circle in the center – a hieroglyphic eye. "Who in God’s name would do this to a child?!?" Lauren exclaimed, more rhetorically than actually expecting a response. "Bad people. People I no longer want to be a part of, and people I never wanted my son to bear the mark of. My wife gave her life to give us enough time to escape – to give Clinton a chance at a normal life – and I’m praying you can grant me this final wish." Mouth quivering and eyes shimmering with tears, Daniel Keene kissed Clinton on the forehead and handed him over to Lauren Sage. She took hold of the child, careful not to disturb his wounded back. "These men have been under the impression that his name is Jason. We’ve called him Jason only while in their presence…but Valerie and I settled on the name Clinton. I hope you can keep that part of him the same, even with both of his parents gone and dead. Clinton Sage." Daniel smiled at the name, as it had a nice ring to it. "What’s your real last name?" Maxwell asked. "I’m sure you’ll see it in the papers soon enough. Just look for a husband and a wife in the obituaries." He said, a voice heavily laden with truth. Daniel once again kissed Clinton on the forehead, and raced down the porch steps two at a time, and hopped back into the Chevrolet. With the headlights turned off, he sped off into the darkness, leaving his only son in the hands of the Sage family. Moments later, a car roared past the Sage farm, undoubtedly chasing after Daniel. The sun had finally set, and the stars finally covered the night. Bouncing little Clinton in her arms in hope of quieting the crying baby, Lauren Sage looked down at the newest addition to their family. Shooting across the sky, leaving a sparkling and smoking tail, was a meteor that burned itself up in the atmosphere in the most brilliant emerald green Lauren and Maxwell had ever seen. For a moment, the child stopped crying, perhaps perplexed by the sudden change in color of the sky. For the Sage’s, the night was surreal. From the mysterious arrival of a long-wanted child, to the meteor streaking across the sky, the gravity of the situation left them floating in thought for a moment or two, until the distant sound of semi-automatic gunfire pulled everything back down to earth with life-altering gravity. * * * * * August 1998 The first thing the SWAT team noticed to indicate that anything suspicious had occurred, other than the 777 landing itself on autopilot, was the 10 inch kitchen knife stuck into the partition between first class and coach. The orange handle was smeared with blood, while the sharpened steel blade was coated with it. A few drips traced their way down the partition, and spotted the floor. Every passenger was buckled into their seat, silent and looking forward. The engines were still humming with life, even though the plane had landed 5 minutes prior, the airliner was just sitting on the runway, unresponsive. Nobody tried to get up and rush for the exits because they all were dead, their throats cut with such precision the wounds appeared almost identical. All 156 of them, passengers and crew. Row by row, the SWAT team had made its way from the pilots cabin back to the tail section, there appeared to be no survivors. Gradually, as the minutes passed by, the attempts and finding a survivor with a pulse diminished, so did the SWAT members attempt to reach for one. Everyone in sight bore a bib of blood from their neck to their waist. The expressions on the passenger’s faces varied from pure terror, eyes wide and mouth open in a scream that only God could now hear, to the calm and accepting, a smile curling from one side of the mouth. But they all faced forward, and remained seated. No one apparently put up too much of a fight. The expression on Nathan Hollinger’s face, sitting in row 48, seat 1, was catatonic. He sat slumped to the side, but still upright, with his head tilted back slightly and resting against the back of the seat. His eyes weren’t wide open in terror, but his pupils were dilated into the largest circles of black that any of the SWAT members had ever seen. There was no wound on his neck, nor any blood on his shirt. His hands, however, were covered in a sticky crimson. It was congealed under his fingertips in darkened clumps, coated his fingers all the way to his knuckles, and was dried on his forearms all the way up to the elbow. "We have our man!" one of the SWAT members radioed to the other members, and soon the trumpeting sound of dozens of footsteps converged on the sole survivor of Flight 1020. "Sir, remain seated and put your hands on your head!" someone shouted, with a dozen muzzles pointed in the direction of Nathan Hollinger. Their laser sights illuminated his face in the same color of his hands. He sat, slack and slumped, unresponsive. "Cuff him! Radio down to the Fire and Rescue, tell them they need to send in the rolling morgue." Another SWAT member barked at his fellow officers, "Better make it a few." Cuffs were slapped on the red wrists of Nathan Hollinger, and when the SWAT members stood him up, he surprisingly moved in unison with their directions. His eyes never regained focus, nor did he ever respond directly to any questions, save one, "Hey asshole, what the fuck possessed you to do this?" "Do what?" Nathan whispered. The engines of the 777 had been silenced, and soon the far off wail of sirens closed in on the plane. The SWAT team led Nathan Hollinger down through the scores of bodies aboard Flight 1020, down the steps, and onto the tarmac. With news helicopters circling overhead and hundreds of flashbulbs erupting all around, before the day was through Nathan Hollinger had become Pennsylvania’s most notorious mass murderer. * * * * * July 2005 Serving time in jail is nothing more than simple routine. For the last seven years, Nathan Hollinger became accustomed to waking up, reading for hours, eating, working out and training, and returning to his cell for sleep. The state of Pennsylvania had found him guilty of the massacre aboard Flight 1020, even though he has no recollection of the crime. Or any point of his life prior to the moment they asked him why he’d done such a crime. When a knock came on the door of his cell, he tore himself away from the latest book was reading, the graphic novel depiction of the 300 Spartans final battle, he saw the warden of Pennsylvania State Penitentiary, Leo Levandowski, waving a piece of paper at him. "What’s that?" Hollinger asked his old friend, sitting up and folding the page of the graphic novel with a slight dog-ear so he could resume it later. "You’re being released." Leo said with a broad smile, motioning towards the guard to open Nathan’s cell. Nathan Hollinger sat up immediately and didn’t know what to say, and wasn’t sure if he even believed what was going on. What had happened seven years ago put him behind bars, and he couldn’t remember any of it. In fact, he had grown so accustomed to prison, and since he couldn’t remember the life he had lived prior, that he actually knew of nothing else in life. "Some little wrestling asshole, Clinton Sage, found proof that you had nothing to do with Flight 1020. It seems that Harlan Raines led him on some crazy wild goose chase that resulted in him finding not only the bodies of all those Harlan had killed and hidden away, but also proof that several of the other crimes committed throughout Pennsylvania, New York, and Delaware were the planned precision of this group…The Eyes of Fear." The cell door slid open, and Levandowski walked in. His gray hair was slicked back away from his wrinkled face, looking every bit of his 69 years in age. He sat beside Nathan and put an arm around his shoulder, "I knew you didn’t do it!" "The Eyes of Fear?" Nathan wondered. In all his years in prison, he had read about Harlan Raines and all the terrible murders he had committed during his years that led to his incarceration, but he had never heard of this cultish name. "Some group that now only has two surviving members – Raines, and apparently that prick Clinton Sage was born into the cult, but never actually was a member. His parents managed to get him out of the cult’s clutches and into some farm family’s hands before they were murdered years ago." Leo paused, a smile across his face. He truly was happy that Nathan was being released, but also sad to see a longtime friend leave his presence, "And in two weeks, when Harlan Raines is executed on August 1st, Clinton Sage will become the sole surviving member of this horrible group of people. But you’ll be free, completely exonerated of the crimes held against you." "How many others are being released, are there similar cases like mine?" "Around 15. It seems this cult was very precise in what they did and who they put the blame on." Nathan and Leo sat in silence for a moment, before the warden stood up and made his way towards the exit, "Gather your stuff, you’re going home." Looking around his cell, which had been his only home that he could remember as a result of this amnesia that the cult no doubt instilled upon him, Nathan Hollinger simply grabbed the graphic novel "300" then slowly and cautiously took the first free steps of his life. * * * * * December 2005, GTT5 The curtains swept themselves closed behind him, but he could still hear the arena rattling with disapproval. Inside the squared circle, Angelo Deville still stood defiant, flicking off the thousands of fans in attendance. Hidden under the mask of Spartan, Nathan Hollinger felt cheated. Raped. He had dominated Deville inside the squared-circle and had the match won. When setting out as Spartan, Nathan tossed away his identity and grew a beard to disguise his face for when the mask wasn’t on, but inside the ring, he had defined everything that was right about the sport with honor and precision. A simple schoolboy rollup had put the nail in the coffin of Angelo Deville, and he was only a half second away from being the second man to beat Deville during GTT within three rounds. But then Deville poked him in the eyes, he actually managed to stick his fingers into the two slits of the mask, and it temporarily blinding him. Deville reversed the schoolboy pin, and as the ref was making the three count, Nathan could feel the bobbing and shifting pressure from Deville – a result of Angelo Deville hopping up onto the ropes for leverage. And with the final slap of the referee’s hand against the mat, Spartan’s quest through GTT had come to a sudden and resounding stop. After gaining so much momentum, and inspiring so many people along the way, it all came to a halt as a result of the very thing he was trying to avoid – cheating inside of the squared circle. After making his way towards the back, passing by wrestlers who consoled him with pats on the back and words of encouragement, even though they didn’t know who was hidden behind the mask of Spartan, he returned to his locker room and tore off the mask. Nathan Hollinger stared at himself in the mirror, and ran a throbbing hand through his shaggy beard. It was time to return to normalcy, time to try and make something out of his life now that his dream at changing the wrestling world as Spartan came to a nightmarish end. He picked up a pair of scissors, trimmed the beard close, and then lathered up. A few minutes later, the beard was gone. Stepping out into the hallway, he immediately came across several wrestlers once more who instantly recognized him as Nathan Hollinger – the supposed mass murderer – even though he had been proven innocent of the crime months earlier. A feeling of hopelessness began to press on him, and he was sure enough that if stupid wrestlers could recognize him and who he had been, then the sole person on this planet that he cared about would certainly recognize him as well, and turn away when he tried to comfort her. Leaving the arena, Nathan Hollinger returned to Denver and hoped that his favorite waitress, the woman he had fallen in love with over the last three months, would still look at him the same way. During that time, he e felt ashamed enough about avoiding telling her who he really was, and now he was about to live up to it. The night melted away into morning, and the blazing sun lit the snowcapped mountains in a brilliant white and gold glow. Nathan Hollinger hopped into his Nissan Pathfinder, and made his way towards the Rehabilitation Clinic where he would tell her everything. Everything. And in a few weeks, upon her completion of the rehabilitation program, he would accept Isis Maguire into his life as a reformed alcoholic, but would she accept him as a proven-innocent mass murderer? * * * * * Yesterday "Don’t you just love the cold? Screw that 100 degree weather, give me freezing winds and snowflakes any day!" Nathan Hollinger sat and smiled, staring up at the nighttime sky, "Well, sure. Unless you’re a dinosaur." Isis Maguire had her arms wrapped around his torso, her head on his shoulder. Her light brown hair was pulled away from her face, held there by a thick bandana appropriate for the cold rural Denver nights. Both of them sat wrapped in a giant wool blanket, huddled close. Upon his smart ass remark, she squeezed him as tightly as she could, which wasn’t enough to even make him wheeze, although he played it up with an over exerted "Bleh! Such strength!" "Asshole," she said, smiling up at Nathan. "Hey, you were the one who said it. I just stated a fact. You love the cold, and it’s a great thing we’re not dinosaurs, because then we’d be pretty much screwed. Haven’t you seen the movies or read the books?" "Well anyway, I just love it because being cold means that you get to cuddle with someone and get warm. But when you’re hot and sweaty from the summer’s heat, you pretty much just want to stay away and get cool, all by yourself. Where is the fun in that? I absolutely love sitting here with you, with nothing but love and happiness to keep me warm. And a wool blanket." Nathan looked down at her and matched her gaze, "You’ve been watching the soaps again, haven’t you?" Her brow furrowed, "Yeah, and yesterday there was an episode where this crazy guy with amnesia killed a ton of people aboard an airplane. Then he found this alcoholic girl and fell in love with her, but since he couldn’t carry on a conversation without giving some sort of smartass remark, she left him for a chocolate cake and a purple vibrator." He grinned and shook his head from side to side. With little effort, Nathan picked up Isis and put him on his lap so her legs straddled his midsection, and her arms were wrapped around his shoulders and neck, "Now that’s more like it, returning the sarcasm and smartass comments. How did I ever fall in love with someone like you?" "You killed people, you retard." She said in a high-pitched cutesy voice, rubbing her nose against his like an Eskimo would. "Did not." "Did too, that whole Eyes of Fear thing is just a sham. It’s much more fun dating a real mass murderer, rather than one who was proven innocent." Nathan tried a cutesy voice all to his own, but failed miserably, "I just might start with you, if you keep this up." Instead of rubbing his nose against hers, he ran his index finger across her throat and winked. Looking into her blue eyes and matching her perfect smile, Nathan Hollinger cupped Isis Maguire’s head in his hands, kissing her. Their passion to start new lives with one another came out in affection, love, and admiration, and even though Nathan couldn’t remember his life prior to 1998, he was still sure that he had never kissed anyone so passionately in his life. A few loud crashes of thunder shook them from their liplock, and rattled the freshly fallen snow from the branches of nearby pines. When they both opened their eyes, the night sky was awash in an emerald glow. Streaking across the sky was a meteor, which quickly broke up into four or five pieces as it burned in the atmosphere. The tail that followed it sparkled and dazzled in the starry Denver sky. Isis’ eyes were wide and amazed, "Wow, that’s beautiful." A few more sonic booms shook the landscape before the meteor crossed their little slice of sky as Nathan Hollinger stared up at the sky, while the emerald glow slowly faded away. Darkness retook its place high above them. "Did you make a wish?" Isis asked, "I know I did. But don’t tell me if you did, that supposedly ruins the luck and the wish, and honestly…tomorrow we can use all the luck we can get." Still smiling at the sky and her wish, she looked back down at Nathan, who was staring blankly at the stars. "I’ve seen this before." He finally said, dryly. "That’s impossible, hun. Maybe you’re mistaking it for the Aurora Borealis, or one of those other fancy light shows we saw at the planetarium a few weeks ago." "No, that green meteor before, I’m almost certain of it." "What do you mean? I don’t get it…you couldn’t have, you were in prison for all those years, and it’s not like a meteor of that size passes over every night, you know." The alarm on Nathan’s watch started to beep, and he quickly silenced it and sighed – 11:57pm, "Alright, I’ve got to go." "Stay another minute. Please? You’ve still got three more to spare!" she asked, giving those beautiful puppy dog eyes he loved so much. "You know I can’t. We’ll talk about this later, but I really can’t stay. You know how much I really love watching those old reruns of ‘The Renegade’ on Channel 481." Nathan said with a sarcastic sneer as he got up from beneath the blanket. "Your love affair with Lorenzo Lamas is starting to make me jealous. That show is so ridiculous and unrealistic!" she sighed, rolling her eyes at the thought of the longhaired bounty hunter and his motorcycle. Playfully, he replied, "Is not." "He goes through more crap and crazy adventures in one season than 10 people would do in a lifetime. What…prison? A bounty hunter? You might as well put him on death row and watch him escape, sort of like you did." They both laughed at the childishness of their conversation, then he tapped his watch and shrugged his shoulders, "Babe, I’ve really got to go. It’s bad luck…" "Oh alright, I guess you have a perfect excuse." Isis looked up at Nathan and winked at him, "You traditional jackass, you." He bent down quickly and gave her another quick kiss, and quickly hurried off into the darkness. Isis Maguire took a deep breath of the fresh Denver air and glanced back up towards the starry sky. She looked over and saw Nathan jogging down the snowy path from which they had come, never looking back, and began to wonder exactly where the emerald meteor possibly could have landed. Either way, it didn’t matter. Tomorrow was her wedding day, there was much more to worry about than silly kryptonite. * * * * * Clinton Sage was in his study when the meteorite hit the mansion, in the opposing wing. The house shook from the rafters holding the roof in place down to the concrete foundation it rested upon, and the sudden compression of the air in the home blasted out windows all over the place. Jumping to his feet, Clinton prayed that it got the garage. Whatever it was, it didn’t matter as long as it hit the garage. He didn’t care about any of the fucking cars he had, but he wished and prayed that the impact was anywhere in the house, save for one room: The shrine. Racing across the study, out into the hallway and down towards the other end of his lavish estate, the acrid and sickening scent of smoke started to fill the air. With each frantic step, his silent prayer became stronger and stronger, trying to will away the fear that he undoubtedly knew was true: something had hit the east wing of the mansion, and it was aflame. Sweat started to bead on his brow without the help of heat. All the memories and the newspaper clippings. The articles, interviews with other wrestlers, personal memorabilia…and the map. Dear God, please not the map. He needed it. Before he turned the corner, Clinton could see the orange glow of fire reflecting on the glassy fronts of his various pictures and prints that adorned the walls. As he finally rounded the corner, he saw the doorway to his shrine had been blown out – resting in two pieces and thousands of splinters on the hallway floor – and flames licking at the Maple doorframe, staining the roof and surrounding walls black wherever they touched. "Son of a bitch!" Clinton Sage yelled in a stunned disbelief as he raced towards the flames, completely disregarding the possibility of personal injury. Leaping through the fire that blocked the doorway into the shrine, he landed and rolled on the burning carpet just as the sprinkler system engaged. Soot and water washed over him, and he could feel the heat of the fire quickly diminishing, but it wasn’t enough. Too little, too late. Clinton hopped to his feet and dashed between the flames, quickly locating the fire extinguisher and began to discharge it across all the glossy photos, the glass cases filled with the clippings, and most notably, the map he had created over the last year. But it was no use…the red pushpins had melted, and the yarn connecting a few of them was either completely burnt away or singed, dangling aimlessly. By the time the flames had been drowned by water, nearly everything was lost. A year’s worth of his time, money, and patience – burned to the ground and left for nothing. Screaming at the top of his lungs, he threw the empty extinguisher across the room and fell to his knees. Trembling, fists and eyes clenched out of complete rage, Clinton Sage knelt for a minute to try and calm his own inner fire before inspecting the room for what had caused the damage. He knew something had exploded, but wasn’t sure of what exactly was the culprit. Soaking wet, even with the roof shattered and the cool autumn air circulating through the cauterized shrine, he didn’t shiver. His blood was boiling hotter than the fire, and he needed to know exactly what caused all this damage. A quick scan of the room showed turned over tables, shattered glass, burnt memories and a large crater in the far corner. Nestled a foot beneath the surface of the floor, atop blacked and tattered carpeting and floorboards, was a meteorite dark green in color and about the size of a basketball. As Clinton knelt beside it, he was surprised to find that there was no residual heat coming from the space rock. It appeared sage in color, ironically, and the surface was marbleized and appeared glossy, more than likely from the heat of re-entry. In places, there were small pockmarks and cracks, but all in all, the rock appeared like a gem more so than a meteorite. So this is what destroyed my shrine, Clinton thought to himself. He hovered his hand only an inch away from the rock, testing once again for heat, but found that he couldn’t feel anything. The chilly winter air, mixed with the cold water from the sprinkler system, was actually starting to give Clinton a numbing feeling. He knelt beside the meteorite for another minute, before reaching down with both hands and jerking it out of the crater. The pain didn’t set in for more than a second, but when it did, it was worse than anything he had ever felt. The meteorite started to sing and pop and crack with the sound of burning flesh, and Clinton immediately tossed it to the ground out of pure instinct. His hands no longer trembled with the cold shivers, but now they trembled because of a searing pain that was shooting through them. Looking down at his hands, the skin of his fingers and palms was seared away. Chunks of muscle were showing through, along with some tendons and ligaments. The nauseating scent of burnt flesh mottled the air, overpowering the burnt wood and carpet’s own aroma. The wounds on his hands oozed blood, but it didn’t run as freely as one would have anticipated. Clinton Sage found himself stumbling back towards the hallway from which he came, delirious with a mix of rage and agony. Clutching his ravaged and quaking hands close to his chest, he made it out of the shrine only a few steps before the overwhelming pain caused his body to shut down, sending him to the floor with an unconscious thud. * * * * * Today The morning sun set the stained glass windows of the church afire with brilliant shades of red, orange, blue, and yellow. The ceremony was small, smaller than most because of their trouble pasts, and moved rather quickly through the steps. There were no groomsman or bridesmaids, and the only two witnesses in attendance were the priest and Warden Leo Levandowski, who wore a tuxedo and a cheap flower even though it wasn’t required of him. With Nathan Hollinger waiting, Isis Maguire escorted herself down to meet her future husband. With both of her parent’s long dead, and alcoholism straining every friendship she ever had, there was simply nobody else to ask of such an honor. She had come alone on the long journey to sobriety, so the thought of walking herself down the aisle alone didn’t upset her. When Nathan first saw Isis walking down the aisle, clad in an off-white dress (hey, who could keep his hands off of THAT body?) his eyes started to fill with tears. He tried to blink them back, keeping his composure all the while, but it was an impossible task, and by the time she was standing a mere foot from him, looking through a lace vale, there were two streaks slowly marching themselves away from his eyes and down towards his chin. She noticed, and rolled her eyes. He laughed, and held her hands as the priest went through the motions. When the time came for the rings to be exchanged, and the vows to be recited, both Isis and Nathan had written their own to show how much they had meant to each other. Isis cited her favorite singer, Chantal Kreviazuk, in saying that her life with Nathan "feels like home" to her, and that when everyone turned their backs and accepted her alcohol abuse and just let her destroy herself slowly and surely, he was the only person on the planet that simply would not stand for her to drink her life into oblivion. Nathan, still fighting back tears of joy, looked into her eyes and thanked her for believing in him when it came to his adamant stance on the drinking, and forgiving any past he might have had. Understanding that one day, he might remember the memories that preceded 1998 and it might cause problems, but thanked her for being strong enough and brave enough to go on that journey with him. The priest smiled, putting a hand on each of their shoulders, and said those magical words, "By the state of Colorado, I now pronounce you husband and wife. You may kiss the bride." Nathan Hollinger took a step forward, lifted the vale over Isis’ brow, and cupped her head in his hands just like he had done the previous night on the snowy trail, and gave her the most passionate kiss that God would allow them. * * * * * Clinton Sage woke up atop a gurney, still in his smoldering shrine. His hands had been bandaged with countless layers of gauze, and an IV-drip was inserted into his left forearm. The pain in his hands overwhelmed the pain of the needle. "Mr. Sage, it seems you had a meteor strike your home. But, since you live in a mansion, I’m sure you can afford to repair the damage." A voice said to his left, and when Clinton turned, he saw a short and fat police officer filling out paperwork. They were the only two left in the room, and Clinton was already annoyed with the fat bastard for mocking his lifestyle, "It’ll probably cost more than what you make in a year." The officer nodded and introduced himself, "I’m Officer Hanes. Normally I introduce myself with a handshake, but right now I don’t think that’d be appropriate. Unless you’ve got ample morphine in this place, that is." Clinton sat up on the gurney, wincing as pain shot through his arms and hands. The tips of his fingers felt white-hot, and although the room smelled of flame and smoke, there was no more fire. "Like I was saying, the meteor struck the house and incinerated your little…shrine…here. Once I saw your size, I figured you were a bodybuilder or a wrestler, but as soon as I stepped into this room I had no doubt it was the latter. Are all of these pictures, or what’s left of them, various points in your career?" Clinton didn’t like how the cop’s eyes narrowed and continually watched him, "No. They’re of someone that I…admire." "Admiration is one thing, this looks like borderline stalking." "You can’t stalk what you can’t find, Officer Hanes. I’ve been trying to find out who this man is for the better part of the last year." "Just ask your boss who he is. They keep paperwork on that stuff, you know." Officer Hanes replied, chuckling at his sarcasm. Clenching his jaw shut, trying not to explode towards the cop in a fit of rage, Clinton replied as best as he could, "Don’t you think I’ve tried that? You’d marvel at the amount of money I’ve spent trying to find out who this Spartan is, so don’t try to play nice with me and assume you think it’s as easy as it sounds, because it’s not." The cop continued to write on the notepad, even as he watched Clinton, "So why him?" "Because he defeated me, and mocked me. I’ve never been beaten so easily in my life, save for this one guy. But then, about a year ago I had a sort of…awakening…and I realized that this one man, Spartan, had given me so much more." Clinton almost licked his lips with delight as he finished the sentence, but refrained, "He gave me purpose." "What kind of purpose?" the officer asked, and the pen had finally stopped scribbling. Clinton hopped off of the gurney and yanked the IV drip out of his arm. He walked over to one of the various glass cases and plucked a half burnt photograph of Spartan, as gingerly as his tattered fingers would allow, and stared down at it with a delight that the officer hadn’t seen in anyone but serial killers and rapists. "I need to know who he is. Everything that I’ve done since I realized how he changed my life has been so pure, so efficient. My wrestling career has started to rise once again, and the matches I’ve wrestled in have been mostly one-sided because of him. I now see the world from different angles, so to speak, and it’s all because of what he did to me, and what he has become to me." Clinton’s lips quivered with sadistic ecstasy. He would leave the intended violence out of the description, but everything else was true. "The man remains an enigma, but he has given me so much. So much! And this shrine, as you called it, was my research as to whom he was, and where he possibly might be. But now…it’s all gone, and my time has been wasted." Clinton said, his voice trailing off into a whisper and hiding behind a frown. Officer Hanes was amazed at the display of psychotic affection, "You sound absolutely crazy, Mr. Sage. I’m going to recommend…" Clinton screamed out in anger at the officer’s accusation, dropping the burnt photograph and turned towards stout policeman. Within two quick paces, he was face to face with the short and chubby officer, clenching his fists and pointing a bandaged finger in his face, ready to unleash a verbal raping that the cop wound undoubtedly throw him into jail for, but then the pain quickly set in and Clinton was overwhelmed. His clenched hands ached and burned and made him light-headed once again. He continued to point the finger at the officer, but only because he feared the wounds would sting with even more pain as he returned them to a slack position. Officer Hanes looked up with his cherub face and grinned from ear to ear, understanding what was happening to the bastard standing before him. Instead of taking out his frustration on the officer, Clinton Sage stormed over to the glassy emerald meteor and once again yanked it off of the ground, and with all his might, tossed it through the window, wishing that all the pain it had caused him would befall the man under Spartan’s mask. All his time, effort, rage, and pain were thrown with the space rock out of the window and into the front lawn, tumbling alongside the shattered glass. And, just like last time, the pain set in a second later, and Clinton Sage tumbled to the ground unconscious. The last thing he remembered were his hands burning again. * * * * * They kissed for what seemed like an eternity, and Nathan could feel the warmth of her cheeks in his hands and lips. He held her close, and when they finally parted, and opened their eyes to stare at one another for the first time as husband and wife, it was apparent something was very wrong. Her beautiful cheeks were covered in blood, and Leo had dropped his flowers to the floor. The priest was taking a cautious step away from them both, his eyes wide with disbelief. Immediately sensing something was wrong as well, Isis Hollinger raised a cautious finger to her cheeks, and was greeted with a coat of warm blood across her fingertips. She quickly looked down at Nathan’s hands and screamed loud enough to startle the few birds that had been nesting in the steeple above them. Looking down at his hands, Nathan’s palms and fingers were tattered, burnt, and torn, with blood oozing out of the wounds. He could see ligaments and tendons, and within a second or two, the pain set in and brought him down to his knees in an unmatched agony with equal parts physical pain, and emotional pain on what was to be such a beautiful day. The priest backed away, mumbling something about stigmata. Leo Levandowski, long trained to immediately react to a situation, pulled his cell phone and called 911. * * * * * Clinton Sage awoke once again, atop a gurney, except this time there were a dozen people surrounding him alongside Officer Hanes. There was no IV drip this time, and the bandages on his fingers and palms were missing, and for a minute, he could feel this tingling sensation in his hands that reminded him of a limb reawakening from sleep. Looking down at his wounds, he found none. The skin was normal, not even slightly pink. His fingernails weren’t melted and mangled like they had been moments earlier. "How long have I been out?" he wondered aloud, still staring down at where blood and missing chunks of skin should be. "10 minutes, maybe 15 at most." Officer Hanes replied. The barrage of questions came towards Clinton in a dizzying fashion, from every direction, with him unable to discern one from the other. The medics and officers were asking him what exactly happened, and how the hands they had treated earlier in the day had already healed. Watching the wrestler clench and unclench his fists in delight, Officer Hanes was suddenly reminded of the psychotic glee that filled the eyes of Clinton Sage as he described the passion and drive towards man he was hunting. He saw the same look again when Clinton realized his debilitating wounds were no more. What disturbed Officer Hanes the most, however, was the fact that as workers buzzed around him with question after question, Clinton Sage sat atop the gurney and smiled. * * * * *
