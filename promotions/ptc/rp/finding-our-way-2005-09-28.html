Finding Our Way

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-28 Home Sweet Home He was first up, which was likely the greatest role reversal in the history of the family after him being selected over Pitt to fight against Sid. Jake was trudging through the house, trying to collect himself after a long slumber. The home was erected behind a high brick wall topped with wrought iron including razor sharp spike tips. Jake’s feet slapped against the tiled kitchen floor loudly as he made his way to the coffee maker. Jake added a couple of heaping spoonfuls of the coffee grounds in and hit the start button. Remembering he had forgotten, he grabbed the coffee pot and filled it up. He dropped the water into the reservoir and then leaned back against the counter. Jake rubbed his face, trying to bring himself to life. There was plenty of baggage, and even with the family safe at the moment there was still plenty of baggage weighing him down. He was past the moment of elation, and now he needed to worry about the future. Sid would not give up this easily, and so Jake could not go on vacation from protecting his family. Jake walked back to his bedroom on the ground floor, and pulled a fresh pair of jeans out of the dresser. He pulled them on, and then went with socks and the cowboy boots. Still shirtless, he returned to the kitchen. He verified the security system was still on with no incursions. He released the alarm on the back door and walked out onto the deck. The morning air was cooler, making his five a.m. visit nice. He crossed his arms, holding onto himself as the coolness brought some goose bumps on his arms. For this moment, briefly before the day began, he was happy. He didn’t have a care right now, and wondered if his father used to feel this way. He remembered watching his father in the early morning, just as the sun would peak over the horizon as he leaned his foot against the rail fence around the ranch house. The winter had blanketed the ground in fresh snow, and the steam from his coffee made him smile as he inhaled the aroma. Jake had always wondered why his father was so happy in those moments. For a long time he thought his father enjoyed the only alone time he could have, but as they got older the children would join him. You never spoke, and he was still just as happy. Jake imagined it was contentment that had made him so happy. The knowledge his family was safe, well cared for and he had done his job. This morning, he had the same feeling, even if it would only be fleeting. The backdoor opened, and he looked over his shoulder. Xavier was coming out with two cups of coffee in hand. Xavier passed one over, “Sugar and cream, right?” “Yeah, when I drink it I do.” Jake took the cup and lifted his leg up onto the low railing. He took a large sip of the coffee, enjoying the flavor. He needed a little something to help him get going today, and this was perfect. “You know what this reminds me of?” Xavier asked before he sipped his own coffee. Jake laughed, “Dad in the mornings?” “Yeah.” Xavier smiled, “I always wondered why he was so damn happy in the morning.” With a knowing smile he answered, “Me too. Guess we’ll never know.” They sat there, sipping their coffee and relaxing in the cool morning. Once they had finished, they went back inside the house. Jake reset the security alarm on the back door, not wanting to take any chances. “I’m grabbing a shirt. Should we make breakfast for everyone?” Jake looked over his shoulder as he walked away. Xavier had a pained expression, “No, I’ve had your cooking.” “Funny man.” Jake went to his room. He pulled on a gray Corona tee shirt and returned to the kitchen. Alex was up, and already rummaging through the refrigerator. “What are you doing?” “Xavier’s right, you’re cooking sucks.” Alex pulled out bacon, eggs, milk and butter. “I’ll make breakfast. I haven’t gotten the chance in a while, and I like cooking for a big group.” Xavier slapped Jake’s shoulder, Let her do her thing.” Jake followed Xavier into the dining room. Jake took a seat at the head of the table, and Xavier sat down to his right. They were staring at each other, and then Alex brought two new cups of coffee to them. She set them down in front of her brothers and then left again. “We need to talk about what we do now.” Jake started the conversation. “I know, I just wish we didn’t have to.” Xavier looked around the dining room “How long have you had this place Jake?” “I bought it when Donna bought the property in Louisiana. I figured if anyone was checking in my name it might throw them off your trail, and then I also thought if we ever got back together it would be better to be here than the ranch. There are too many bad memories of the ranch. I couldn’t…I couldn’t take Alex back to the ranch after what happened to her and her family.” Jake turned his coffee cup around on the table. “Good plan. I don’t know what we should do. I’ve got all these ideas running through my head, but I don’t know which ones are right and which ones are wrong.” Xavier leaned back in his chair. He folded his arms over his wide chest. Jake took another sip. He let the coffee warm him a bit, “I’m feeling the same way. Nothing is perfect, I think we know that now.” “Look, we tried fighting and we won. I sent him to Hell, but then he came back.” Xavier rubbed his temples as he spoke, “We shouldn’t be hiding anymore.” “Now wait a second, I’m not saying you shouldn’t be hidden away. I just think we need to be even more careful about it now. We’ll find a safer spot, and then we’ll make sure to distract Sid while we smuggle you out of town.” Jake countered. “No.” “What?” Jake asked incredulously. Xavier put his arms down on the table and leaned forward. He was never a man to be intimidated by anyone, not even his older brother. “We’re not running anymore.” “Xavier, come on.” Jake shook his head, not believing he was being so stubborn. Moving his hair back behind his ears, Xavier’s eyes were hard and set. “Look, we’ve been running and staying away from the world for too long. We’ve been facing this for a long time, and I’m not running scared from that son of a bitch anymore.” “Don’t you know what he’ll do if he gets you?” Jake shook his head at his brother. “How can we risk that?” “Think of who you’re asking. I have all of Sid’s old memories before the joining, and even though I blocked them out I keep remembering more and more. What are we risking? I don’t want to be a prisoner to Sid’s whims anymore. If we do what he wants us to do, then we’re just more pawns for him to play with. I won’t throw us into the corners of the world anymore. I’m coming back into the world, and I want to fight against everyone who’s sided with Sid. We won’t run anymore. We won’t be afraid.” Jake didn’t know how to respond. He wanted to be with his family once again. Nothing other need inside him burned as intensely as the need to be surrounded by his loving family once more, but he couldn’t risk the family just for his own selfish desires. Xavier had fought through so much, and he just wanted to stand up and be counted. Jake thought he was too proud for this situation, and sometimes being a man was about making the most difficult choice. Pride was clouding Xavier’s judgment in Jake’s opinion. “Xavier, we can’t let him hurt the family anymore.” Jake pleaded with his eyes. Xavier wasn’t swayed, “If we run he’s hurting our family by keeping us apart.” “Xavier, what is the problem? Are you too prideful to do what your family needs you to do? This isn’t the time to worry about being a real man, this is the time to save the family. We need to protect what we have left.” Jake’s eyes were a little watery as he realized all the losses were coming back to him. He fought them off, keeping himself under control. A long sip of the cup of coffee settled the mood a little bit. Xavier set the cup back down on the table before responding, “Jake, I need to do what’s best for my family. Running and hiding is not the best way to protect our family. If we run again, we could be isolated just like we were in Louisiana. If we get cornered, we won’t have anywhere to run because we’ll be alone. Hiding means we are running in fear. Sid’s supporters can only do so much, and if we don’t hide we deny them waging a war on us in secret.” “That’s a hippy pipe dream.” Jake was getting upset. “They don’t care if it’s in public or in private. They’re fanatics who will not stop until they hunt down and kill each and every one of us. We can’t give them the choice.” Xavier gave a little laugh, and he saw that it infuriated his brother. Xavier held up his hands, “Wait, I’m sorry, just wait. They are going to come after us no matter what. So should we live on our terms or their terms Jake? Together, we’re a stronger family than we are apart. Yesterday you thought you had stand against that group alone. You forgot we were all there to support you. They didn’t back away from you, they backed away from US.” Jake grabbed his cup roughly, spilling some of the scalding hit liquid onto his hand. He ignored the pain, wiping the liquid away. He took two large sips of the morning coffee, almost finishing the refreshing brew. Jake put the cup back down a little too hard, “What would you have us do?” Looking around with a big smile, Jake knew before he spoke. “You’ve set up a good home here, with state of the art security and security guards we can rely on. We have a new home, and all we have to do is live in the palace you built Jake.” Jake tensed his jaw, thinking about his options. “What about what I think we should do?” A voice came from the doorway. Jake and Xavier turned to see who had come into the dining room. There he stood, tall and strong with a proud look over his face. For a long time, Pitt Hix had been the patriarch of the family. He was used to being the leader of the family, dictating what would happen and keeping everyone tightly together. He had lost that role in an instant when Sid had broken his back, but now he sought to reinstate himself as the leader of the family. “Of course we care Pitt, but Jake and I are uniquely selected for this task. You know that, and so we have to look carefully at the options.” Xavier offered a soft rebuttal, not wanting to offend his brother. “Oh sure, Jake is uniquely suited all right. So far we’ve seen him send us away, then watch as his great plan unfolded in front of his face. We were thrown out of the house he chose thanks to the flood! He didn’t listen to me, and look at what happened! He threw us aside, and now we’re out in the open!” Pitt yelled, keeping his hungry eyes locked on his brother Jake’s darkening features. “Now guys, let’s wait a sec…” Xavier tried to keep them calm. Jake pushed his chair away and stood up. He stared across the table at his brother, “You’ll keep your tongue when it comes to that Pitt.” “Will I? What’s the matter Jake, you don’t like it when people call your choices into question?” Pitt began moving around the side of the table opposite Xavier. “You may have been chosen, but your choices so far suck. You always were jealous, and now that jealousy is allowing you to be reckless with these stupid decisions.” Jake stepped toward his brother, and dropped him to the floor with a hard right first that landed across his chin. “Jake!” Xavier yelled as he stood. Pitt had come back to almost one hundred percent after facing the ravages of his injury, but that one hundred percent just made him a normal man. Pitt hadn’t been in serious competition training for a long time. Physically fit though he was, he hadn’t been ready for what had hit him. Jake had been in constant, daily training for over ten years. The punch had more power than Pitt had felt in a very long time. The Big Dawg stood over his brother with an angry glare, “Stay down there if you know what’s good for you.” “I don’t think so!” Pitt yelled as he tried to scramble back up from the floor. Jake caught him with a knee to the mouth as he was getting up. The blow broke open both his lips, and blood streamed down his face onto his chest before he fell back onto the floor again. With his finger pointing threateningly, Jake spoke with an icy edge Pitt had never heard. “If you try and get up again, I’ll put you back in that damned hospital bed.” “Jake, what are you doing?” Xavier started to come around the table. “Stay there, this is between me and him.” Jake kneeled down as Pitt wiped the blood from his face. “I figured out that mom and dad never loved me as much as you. I learned that all the anger and hatred I hurled at you was because I was mad at them. Now that we both know, I won’t apologize but I won’t make the same mistake ever again.” Pitt didn’t say anything. “Donna picked the location to conceal the family in, and she made all of the arrangements through a special little slush fund we set-up.” Jake stood back up. “She loved this family enough to put herself on the line for us. She never called when the hurricanes arrived, and I found out that was because she was being followed. She ran away, hoping not to be captured and reveal anything damaging about our family.” “So if you ever insult the plan that was instituted to save your ass I’ll put you on your ass again. You always forgot about the hard work the so-called little people were doing on your behalf. You’ll never be allowed to forget it again while I’m still taking breath. As for what to do with our family, Xavier makes a fair point. If we keep running, then we’ll always end up apart and working against each other like this in the end.” Jake walked over Pitt’s body and into the kitchen. He checked in on Alex as she made breakfast. He knew Pitt would need a little time, and there was no reason to rub salt in the wound. Pitt took Xavier’s offered hand, and used his brother’s assistance to get back up to his feet. Pitt pulled his tee shirt off and wiped away the blood, tasting it as he swallowed a little bit. He spit into the cotton of his shirt, “Guess I should have rethought that comment.” “You were out of line.” Xavier returned to his chair, “Jake has done everything he could, and no one knew this was coming. You’re just feeling a bit…insecure.” “Insecure?” Pitt scoffed at the notion. “Yeah, insecure. He’s leading us right now, and you owe him some respect. He admits he was wrong is the past, so quit holding that against him.” Xavier finished his coffee. Pitt walked away. He would get cleaned up and change into a fresh shirt, but then it would all be over and done with. They would come back together as a family. There were plans to be made. Jake walked back into the dining room and retook his seat. He looked over at his brother Xavier, “Alex is cooking up a great breakfast. No ones going to be hungry here.” “That was never a huge concern when we were at home.” Xavier laughed. Jake nodded, “Some things change, and some never change. So, where do we go from here?” “I’ve got an idea about that too.” Xavier broke into a big grin, “And I bet you never saw this one coming.” “What is it?” Jake was curious, and he never saw the answer coming. Midnight Wonderings The coolness of the evening was surprising, yet not unwelcome. He sat on the stone railing of his balcony, looking down into the evening garden as it sprang to life in the moonlight. The fragrant blossoms wafted up to relax him as he tried to work through the complex issues in his mind. Here, all alone he was able to face the stunning truth. He knew that he would have to face Sid again, and the death he had experience in the bayou had reawakened the struggle within his soul. Fragments of them remained, like the last strands of silk refusing to leave the corncob. He could feel his part in the play was not finished, and that simple knowledge allowed him to face what was coming. Xavier jumped off the second floor, and landed on his feet on the grass. He felt fine, nothing interfering with his drop from such a high level. He was feeling the new energy as it ran through his body. He wouldn’t tell anyone, not even Bella who he trusted and loved more than anyone else in the world. Some secrets had to remain hidden if he was to play his role in Sid’s downfall. Xavier began moving around the yard, finding he felt like running for hours just to relieve the new energy coursing through his veins. Jake and Sid would not feel like this anymore. They had long since learned to simply accept the feeling much like they had accepted breathing air in and out of their lungs. Only the first few days of the experience offered the magic sensations of being filled with power greater than you. As the moonlight came down on him, he thought about the choices he faced. They had eliminated the idea of going into hiding again. The family deserved better than living on the run. The two choices before him basically came down to recognition. He could live a quiet life behind the walls of the estate, and never know fear for himself unless they tread into his own territory. On the other hand, he could walk out into the harsh glare of the spotlight once again. Both choices crippled part of him. One crippled his security, but the other crippled his freedom. The wall had a framework where the ivy and roses were growing. Xavier ran forward, jumped off the stone near the wall and climbed up the wall to his balcony quickly. He returned to his position sitting with his legs hanging over the side. He kicked his feet back and forth, knowing he had made his decision without consciously realizing it. The Ascension began anew in many, many ways,
