Zero Hour (Cometh)

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 He observed his fellow guests with a wandering, nonchalant regard, wine lapping at the brim of the chalice he held by its crystalline stem. It was a hotel conference room, not an isolated mansion in some God-forsaken backwoods hamlet he was accustomed to for these summits. Someone had done their proper homework; there would be no betrayals this evening, at least none ending with an actual knife in anyone's back or a bullet in the heart. It was standard practice in a cutthroat society that fed on cloak-and-dagger tactics and dared not involve law enforcement, lest face one's own conviction, and there wasn't a scrupulous man (or woman) in this suite who felt aversion to the practice. All present had either ordered murders or executed them personally at one time or another. There had been a time when he wasn't among them in that mentality, but just as the times changed, so did people. He relaxed idly in the corner, gently rolling the glass about in his fingers as he noted the hushed words and mannerisms of his 'brothers' in this secretive fraternity. Ramsey Meier, social-climbing head of the feared Hawk Valley Cult in West Virginia, discoursed with this meeting's host, Carlton Drake, an affluent patron of the arts and notorious sociopath out of Maryland. Both were difficult to bargain with, but he was sure he could sway them into following his plan. His empty hand nervelessly fingered the briefcase to the side of his chair, sipping slowly at his wine as his eyes diverted from that duo and fell on the cool-headed Jacob Deidrich. That was perhaps the only one in this room that he may hesitate to nail to the wall if given the proper implements. The clock tolled ten o'clock and the others moved to their seats, swaggering mechanically to the round table like robots programmed to do so in unison with the hollow chime. Things were finally set to commence. Beneath a rolling cloud of cigar smoke, Cubans no less from this congregation, chairs shifted and the heads of the Old Ones' northeast faithful took their seats around the table. He was the only one who remained where he was, not feeling the urge yet to leave the comfort of his plush highbacked chair and the shadows he had come to know so well. Being but a mouthpiece for one of said leaders and not actually one of their brethren, his exception from the festivities drew no immediate attention. "I wish to welcome all of you here this evening," boomed Drake, raising a highball glass of bourbon in toast. The man had a tendency to speak rather loudly. "May the Old Ones grant Their boon unto you all, and let the gates unto Their kingdom soon be opened for all time. Ia Shub-Niggurath!" The others eagerly repeated the closing remark, echoing that foul chant as they raised their glasses, trying not to spill liqour or fine wine on their ties or stain any of their new Versace wardrobe. From the sanctuary of the darkness, he could only grin at the sight. Not all those who pledged their lives to gods of immorality and malevolence lived hellish lives under black robes, waving torches and dancing around naked, bound sacrifices as they chanted guttural mumbo-jumbo. Let Resident Evil 4 exploit that demographic. Cults like that would never persist if not for the more 'conventional' of leadership present here. "I only wish this meeting were called under different conditions," resumed Drake, voice tapering off to an semi-saddened tone as he looked down into his still whiskey. "Bruce Shanahan was invited to attend tonight and explain his recent actions to this council, but declined personal attendance and instead sent his representative, Brother Ethan Knight." The delegation shifted their eyes to the shadows, observing Shanahan's blond young retainer as he gazed into his wine, swirling it around in its glass as he contemplated what he'd say this night, presumably on behalf of his mentor. Though the young man was present at a number of recent council meetings, few could get over seeing him with his hair now shortly cropped as opposed to the shoulder-length style he once exhibited many months back. And though it had been a gradual evolution, by now everyone was aware of what unholy sentiments were harbored in the boy's soul. No more needed said on behalf of the host. Drake found his chair and lowered himself, resting his elbow on the armrest as he casually smoothed his solid silver tie. Surely Carlton perused his 'guests' with as much subdued interest as Knight did from the shadows over the lip of his quickly-draining chalice. The time was now to execute his plan, and no matter what reservations he had about what had been plotted, something told him the end would justify the means. And that was what was important in the long run. His days of fearing the consequences were long buried. Rising from the chair he'd found comfort in ever since his arrival at the hotel, he took several long, sluggish strides toward an empty chair at the table. One may have initially thought it was the wine he had consumed en masse since he'd made his appearance an hour or so ago, but it had nothing remotely to do with that. Ethan Knight knew what he was doing at all times, and as he stood poised above his chair, he pursed his lips and took a long moment to deliberate his forthcoming words. He made sure to carefully avoid eye contact with any of the cigar-chomping members of the council who would be intent on his every word; he didn't like any of them, outside Jacobs, and he'd hate for his plans to be ruined because they could see the hatred flaring in his eyes before his first word. They could be a fickle lot. "Before coming here, I was given painstaking instructions on what Father Shanahan wanted me to tell this council, and what he wanted me to say nothing of. I don't fancy myself an orator the way he does, so I beg your pardon on that front in regards to what I have to say. What I have to say here tonight, I say after taking careful consideration of all I was told and learned of." Ethan Knight nestled into the chair provided him and leaned back, his fingers forming a steeple as he reclined and carefully followed the reactions of all at the table. "I've opted to forego my instructions, and address something far more dire. I've come to elicit aid from this council in getting rid of Bruce Shanahan. He must be killed as soon as possible, lest his madness spread further and decimate the ranks of the Old Ones' prophets and servitors." His delivery was consummate, playing off of the ill feelings he knew every man in this room held for Bruce Shanahan. And as expected, the accompanying silence after the bombshell was marred with disbelief and doubt from the others of the council. It took several long moments before any of the stunned 'delegates' could stammer out a response. "Preposterous!" sneered Daniel Colby, openly incredulous as to the other's intentions. A wiry man with a sour look perpetually etched on his bony face, Colby ran the Niles Mountain Cult in north Tennessee, and there likely wasn't a man present who hadn't had notions about slashing his throat at one time or another. Looking at him now, Knight could only smile slightly as he envisioned gutting the little bastard like a fish. "What sort of fools do you take the members of this council for, Brother Knight?! Not only are you here representing that overzealous maniac, but you've evolved into the spitting image of the man! I, for one, am greatly insulted." "You're one to talk of 'overzealous maniacs', Brother Colby," sighed Knight. "So trying to overwhelm and terrorize an entire community in Tennessee with a fledgling group of twenty-five lesser initiates wasn't the least bit brash or 'overzealous'?" The infuriated expression that contorted Colby's face would have been perfect fodder for one of those Mastercard commercials. The goblet in his hand slammed down on the table, spilling wine onto the lacquered surface. "Your 'mentor' has enslaved an entire village in northern Vermont, around Wyatt Manor," he said with an embittered glower. "So don't you try to instruct me about being overly fanatical.....!" "Father Shanahan also didn't fail like you so miserably did," interrupted Ethan, not even attempting to mask his amusement at such a fool dispute. He felt no need to beguile this lot that way, least of all Daniel Colby. "He knew the situation, knew how to do it, when to do it...." He leaned forward, peering at Colby who grew more furious with every passing syllable. Through the beet-red color flushing on the elder councilman's face, Knight estimated another thirty seconds would have caused that big, pulsating vein on his forehead to explode. "....and he wasn't a miserable, inept fuck-up who overappraised his capacities. Father Shanahan is a genius, albeit a lunatic who has lost his way. You're an ingrate who would have nothing if not for your predecessors' work, and I'm sure it will take you a fraction of the time to destroy it all." It would have taken a turbo-charged chainsaw to cleave the tension between the two men and their dueling tongues. But Deidrich Drake, host of this meeting, was quick to stifle any possibility of bloodshed between the warring parties. Again, though this wasn't taking place inside any grand midtown hotels, it was a public domain all the same and open attacks would be intolerable. "Enough!" thundered their host, scowling at the pair through a trail of cigar smoke. Colby threw up his hands in frustration and leaned back, lifting his glass and choking down a mouthful of his own bourbon. Whereas the other was openly aggravated, muttering curses as he clenched his emptied glass, Knight was notably unperturbed. Daniel Colby wasn't a man to easily get under his skin; he was far too much of a repulsive weasel to bother the hardened youth anymore. Knight shrugged as eyes fell back on him, relating his logic simply and candidly as if telling a story about what happened on the way to work that morning. "Besides, I've grown tired of walking under that man's shadow the last couple years. For once, I'd like to be able to walk in the light personally, rather than hear sermons explaining what the light feels like." It wasn't the most noble of ideals, but in the minds of this brotherhood, it didn't matter much. Ethan Knight had already proven himself a saint compared to the madman called Shanahan, merely by expressing interest in silencing him. And as they murmured amongst themselves, considering the fidelity of his statement, he drew from his coat a dagger. None paid any notice, remarkably, but he hoped the underlying message wouldn't be lost on those who may indeed make implicit note of his fidgeting. This weapon had been his key into the world he now stalked like a great jungle cat, the thing that had sent Jago Sensaki to his grave and instilled a new sense of divine duty in the young man. "What exactly did you want from this house?" asked Ramsey Meier, gazing over a document in hand. His preference was always to refer to these gatherings as a house summit and not a loose-knit alliance, thought it was much more in line with the latter. "Simple. Use your contacts to aid me getting into round two of this tournament, and I'll deliver what you want. I'll give you Shanahan on a silver platter." "Of course there'd be strings attached...." muttered a gravelly voice behind a veil of rich cigar smoke. Knight's eyes were as hard as the blood-encrusted dagger rolling in his hands, settling on the somber source of the grievance. And his tone was more venomous than the cobra that Ricardo Silas had found in his bed the morning before, when his own plans to usurp the mastermind Shanahan were discovered. Silas was not present tonight, for obvious reasons. "I'm a businessman, not your pawn, Marquis Boller. Don't make that mistake." He gingerly stroked the edge of the weapon like one would soothe a crying baby, eyes never shifting from his tensed cult brother. This was certainly not the yet-unmolded Ethan Knight this council had wantonly manipulated a year ago, with his still-insecure approach and demands that echoed more like shaky requests. This was Ethan Knight, Sect of Black Wisdom negotiator and purported assassin with a list of kills that would rattle even the most hardened San Quentin inmate. Shanahan and Sterling had taught the boy well, too well in the eyes of who still harbored thoughts of betrayal. He adjusted his tie, keeping his expression blank and unreadable as he elaborated; he'd learned that doing otherwise at this juncture may upset the process and he wasn't about to allow that to happen. Too much thought had gone into this. "Mildred McMann may be an unknown, but that makes him all the more dangerous. Potential tie-ins to influential political figures, a network of conservatives to be specific. Father seem to have invested a great deal of time and money in the whelp's potential career in politics, and is using notoriety in this thing for momentum with the voters. Or so I assume. I'm not privvy to what goes on in those offices, it's outside my territory." Black-and-white photos of the individual in question found their way onto the table by way of the double-locked-and-latched briefcase he carried on him. The council perused the photos and notes he passed about, deliberating the scheme and its intricately-outlined supporting tiers. "It appears to be a fair deal to me, ladies and gentlemen," he proffered as he stood, launching into full bargainer mode. "Incentives across the table, monetary and otherwise. I progress through this little competition as I want, and each of you receive gifts of my, or should I say our gratitude. Not only does my domination further establish the strength of the Old Ones to this world, but all of you will receive generous donations to your own organizations' accounts. Utilize your web of agents to aid my advancement and you'll see a ten-fold return in what expenditures you make." "Aiding you in this whole 'pro wrestling' endeavor seems a small price to pay to see Shanahan's mad ambitions quelled," commented Meier, a cigar jutting from the corner of his mouth. He openly scoffed as he spit out the 'pro wrestling' part, never having been one to understand the draw of the low-brow pastime to ones like Knight, Shanahan and Horace Tully. "And your propositions are well-specified. A terrible pity that Shanahan won't have you as his lapdog anymore, he really could forge an empire with this sort of help. Brother Knight, I should have no problems offering you my fool assistance in the matter." Ethan was pleased with how his deal was ultimately received. None wanting to be the odd-man-out, everyone was quick to fall in line behind Ramsey's venture into Ethan's well laid-out strategy. If Mildred McMann was to surpass the ambitious Sect representative, he'd also need to escape the deceitful agents of this wicked alliance in the process. Provided all went according to plan, their involvement would remain concurrent with Knight's success from there on. Though he had very little preliminary idea of how the members of this united faction would act as his benefactors, he could only grin as ideas were passed about and argued over. Treachery was in their blood it seemed, and even he couldn't help but grin at some of the propositions put forth by Deidrich Jacobs' unholy mind; for a man appearing so mild-mannered on the exterior, he exuded wickedness from every pore. Pandering over how to best deceive and conquer an insect like McMann was this demonic group's idea of merriment. "Now, there's the matter of dealing with that wily guru in upstate Vermont." The transition was flawless, reverberating that the affair was simply a matter of professional business. "Unfortunately, at this juncture, Shanahan has already garnered more power than this entire council collectively possesses by assimilating those groups he has in recent months. His network of informants is abundantly thick and thus any outright assassination scheme would, in all likelihood, fail miserably, essentially trapping any and everyone involved in the plot between his crosshairs. Involvement of law enforcement can't be risked, as it's obvious that lengthy investigation into any organization or single member of this alliance would result in our own prosecution." Ethan Knight stepped around Marquis Boller and plucked a handful of grapes from the bowl at the center of the table. If the look on that particular man's face was any indication of the collective, he already held them in the palm of his hand. "I agree that both avenues have too many possible drawbacks to attempt," iterated Deidrich, reticent as always. "Unfortunately, I can offer no other tangible solutions. What did you have in mind, Brother Knight?" He popped a morsel of the fruit in his mouth, silently deliberating the scenario a moment longer. What he had decided on could send the entire summit meeting into a small-scale riot if it were rejected. It would boil down to the lesser of two evils in the eyes of this group. "A truce. With the Blackhelm Coalition." The group reaction easily rivaled that of his disclosure over covertly planning Bruce Shanahan's death. Ironically, the Blackhelm society had come closer than any to killing Bruce in recent months than any, and it was one he once held membership in before his turn to the side of darkness. "Forge an alliance with the Blackhelm dogs?!" scoffed Drake, slamming a mailed fist on the table and rattling the empty saucers around the circle. "Utterly preposterous. No matter how drastically Brother Shanahan's motives have been perverted, in the end his objectives are still for the good of the Old Ones. One of our kind will never become a more imposing adversary than the agents of the Church. Besides, those pompous, Bible-wielding bastards would never agree to such an accord." "They, as well as us, know of the existence of the Sathlatta that Brother Shanahan is said to have finally discovered," retorted Deidrich through a casual sip of his tea. His demeanor was somber yet decidedly relaxed as he peered in turn at his council brothers, a man always weighing the advantages and disadvantages of any possible scheme. "And their agents view such a thing as even greater heresy than what we have all perpetrated. Even God's self-appointed hitmen would momentarily forgive a handful of past deaths at our hands as opposed to the possible murder of all humanity. They would agree, however temporarily. Trust me when I say that the Coalition and their allies view an immortal Bruce Shanahan as an adversary infinitely more dire than a handful of moderately-powerful cults, divided or not." "Shanahan's become a liability," agreed Colby with wholehearted vigor. "He's no longer a brother. He's a traitor. He's already killed our own to attain his maniacal goal and there's no telling who amongst us he sees as the greatest threat. Mark my words when I say he'll make no hesitation in disposing of any who challenge his bid for vengeance against God. For all he makes of his public stance against politics amongst our kin, he's just as guilty as any among this body, if not moreso. Hatred has clouded his vision, however pure it once may have been." "He killed Brother Thorne and Brother Ignalls in hopes of furthering his goal to gaining immortality and rising to become one of the Old Ones' generals in the End Times," clarified Ethan, his tone so matter-of-factly that he may have been explaining how to solve a basic algebra equation as opposed to detailing the irreverent plan of a homicidal madman. "Unfortunately, I fear Brother Colby is accurate. Though his designs may be for the good of the cause, they've become too personally driven to be pure. Hate and rage have defiled his plans so that I've begun to wonder if his innermost loyalties now lie with the edict of Nyarlathotep, or within the maelstrom of his own tortured soul." Carlton Drake drummed his fingers on the table surface, casting uneasy glances at the others, predominantly Diedrich and the still-silent Virginian, Harvey Parrish. Parrish never spoke much at these meetings, but his actions in the aftermath always spoke volumes more than he ever could during the discussion. And if there was ever a time to be silent and think about what was to come, this was it. Shanahan had but become an uncrowned lord above them in recent times, and to defy his whims and even plot his assassination was mutiny in a sense. Ethan Knight had gone to painstaking lengths to protect his interests however, and it was difficult to reject his plan when it was so well-considered. That boldness was deserving of respect amongst this body. The personal courier of the Sect of Black Wisdom suppressed a meager smile as the voting took place. With every hand that went up, a proverbial death warrant was signed. And when all was said and done, it was a unanimous decision; his mentor would be seeing to the slaughter of this entire alliance if word reached him of their malign treachery. Of course, with the deep resentment toward the man that this alliance held, it wouldn't have taken an epic scheme anyhow. Sliding the dagger back into his coat, Ethan pulled himself to his feet and turned to go; his business was done with this council, having no need to partake in other business deliberation as long as they had agreed to his pact. He paused momentarily as Drake snapped his fingers and a striking woman entered the room. An immediate jovial air encompassed the whole of the conference body as she made her presence known, and Carlton swiveled his chair to face the taciturn blond deceiver. A broad, mischievous smile induced him into removing the stubby cigar from his mouth. "Brother Knight, please....take this one. She will do things you did not know were possible of a woman. And she will do so with a smile and a delighted scream. Enjoy yourself the rest of this evening, and may your return trip to Vermont be secure." The traitor known as Ethan Knight casually glanced over the offering before him as she was paraded into the room amidst reserved laughs and not-so-subtle innuendos. With innocent eyes that belied her true 'loose' nature, the raven-haired beauty had been handpicked by Carlton Drake himself as an appeasement to Bruce Shanahan's premier advisor and intermediary. Her ample bosom, accentuated by a tight dress with a scandalously low neckline, would have been the first thing to catch the eye of the typical gawker, and did so in turn with the other 'guests' of Drake. Knight, however, had progressed beyond such a petty factor in a potential lover, and her cup size took a backseat to the grace and elegance with which she traversed the room, fluttering her eyelashes at every other potential suitor at the table. Her body language spoke far greater volumes than any of her other 'assets' could, in pertinence to personality and otherwise. The woman reached his side, her eyes drowning out the laughs and muffled catcalls emanating from those seated around the table. The boy stood firm, turning his head to follow her movements but making no effort to do otherwise as she maneuvered behind him. Her voice was silken and vibrant, like the song of a bird. "I am called Carmen," she replied, wrapping her arms around his shoulders and resting her head between his shoulder-blades. "A pleasure to meet you, Ethan Knight." She whispered into his ear as they trotted off without a further word, leaving the others to debate other 'minor' business in their wake. His intentions were resolved for tonight, and he had no need to listen in on their talks of expansion and spreading the gospel of Great Cthulhu and his ilk. It was time for relaxation. * * * * He flung the women effortlessly onto the bed, spinning to face her. The time for foreplay was long over, and so was the time for their final act of passion. It had concluded the moment he pried that serrated dagger from her hand as they embraced, only moments after entering the suite. She was sloppy, probably new to the trade. Sterling would have waited for the deed to be done, and slit his throat while he exhausted under the sheets. Ethan cast the weapon across the room to careen off an empty turquoise vase, toppling the antique ornament so it landed on the carpeted floor beside the glimmering blade. He seethed, completely having shed the skin of a restrained negotiator as he bore down on the snarling woman. She sat up, supporting her weight on her arms, as she angrily glowered at her targeted kill. She was a seasoned one for sure, probably answering to a fake name from the moment she swept into the conference room minutes ago. There was no Carmen. Knight had been targeted by hired killers on two past occasions, but neither time by a woman; he had escaped the efforts of two previous men under similar circumstances, on one occasion even being forced to use a gun to finish his attacker. He'd come to hate firearms, preferring the intimate feel of a finely-crafted blade over the assembly-line-styled execution offered by a gun; he had his own methods did The Infinite. On this occasion, as a fiery rage raced through his veins, he would have had no qualms about putting a bullet in her head. She didn't deserve the honorable end he gave most of his own appointed quarry. "You're about to die, you amateurish whore," he pronounced balefully. "And in time, for his treachery, so will that snake you serve. I'll be sending Drake to punish you for your failure soon enough, but it'll be in the afterworld. "On the contrary, you doddering fool." hissed The One Called Carmen. "Carlton Drake was not the only one who spoke to me before I was sent to share your bed. You need me alive to know who sent me with instructions to kill you. I know enough of you, Ethaniel Knight, to know you're not a complete fool." Her voice, still instilled with that seductive purr from the moment they were introduced, exuded sly confidence. He could not afford to slay her now, lest he find himself forever ignorant of the identity of her would-be contractors. Ethan, however, was mirthful as he grinned from ear-to-ear. His grip loosened around the harlot's jaw, fingertips gliding down to rest just atop her heaving chest. That golden dragon pendant resting between her breasts held his fancy a moment before his eyes abruptly re-fixed on her own. Her smile mirrored the notion that nothing had even happened, expecant of his sparing her life out of necessity. "I can appreciate your self-assurance. But as an assassin, you've neglected one clear and present fact." His eyes bored into those of The One Called Carmen, devoid of passion or feeling. An inquisitive twinkle flashed remotely in hers before the reply came. "And what would that be, Ethan Knight?" His face was strangely reassuring, almost rueful. "That we share the same occupation," the Sect vassal calmly whispered into her ear. "And I'm one of the best." The brief twinkle in her eye nearly matched the speed of the knife that found its way into Ethan's hand, and subsequently penetrated her heart. Not even the talents of The One Called Carmen could match those of Ethan Knight; she had never even thought of him stowing a blade of his own beneath the mattress, not while seemingly offguard and so trusting as she caressed him with gently pursed lips. Her eyes went wide as saucers, breath catching forever in her throat as he extracted the silvery blade in a swift, smooth motion. The sharp-minded negotiator didn't even offer a glance at her motionless form as he nonchalantly wiped the blood from the weapon. Killing had become an effortless mechanism that no longer warranted a second thought, and the death of The One Called Carmen would bring no lament from Ethan Knight. It had been a quick exchange, but then he wasn't feeling up for a drawn-out dialogue after what he had endured with those treacherous wolves downstairs. He needed to gather his belongings, send for Drake, and depart before the man reached the suite or the body was discovered by hotel staff. At least if Drake arrived before any hotel personnel, and with Knight long gone, he'd do all in his power to hide the body and avoid the police, including having the others help him in ridding the room of The One Once Called Carmen. Why do it himself when that villainous son of a bitch and his brethren would do so quicker and just as adamantly? Besides, after the agreement they'd made, they had no choice but to follow through on their promises anyhow. Knight offered but a grunt as he glanced down at the pendant again, thinking over what he knew of many female assassins before flipping it open. He actually hoped his instinct would be proven inaccurate as he hurriedly opened the pendant and read its contents, rudimentary instructions with the identity of her contractor. He scanned the crinkled slip of paper, grimacing at the name. A gamut of possibilities and potential solutions cycled through his mind, none more enticing than the last as he ripped off the jewelry and jammed it into his pocket. This was something he suspected was inevitable, were it true, but not one that he knew would be easily dealt with in any case. He had miscalculated the daring of those enemies who wished to see him dead, and that was an unacceptable fault. Had he been swept off his feet by The One Called Carmen as his would-be killers hoped, he'd have never detected that knife until it was planted firmly in his spine. His instinct at smelling a set-up wasn't as developed as he'd thought, and hoped. But that train of thought was for another time. He was too preoccupied with his exit to worry about his flaws for the time being. Retribution would be swift; of that he had no doubt. His opening statement had actually been more truthful than he had hinted at, and ripe with more falsehoods than had been guessed at..... * * * * ......Culling our numbers may seem counterproductive to the cause, but at this stage, it's still better to have a smaller and convicted force of the pure than a motley army of inept goons who favor power over their own faith. With Thorne and Ignalls eliminated, this is the next step in the Sect of Black Wisdom's march to domination. The cycle of betrayal and manipulation has continued long enough, and the day has come for it to all be phased out. No longer will politics override the true cause; the resurrection of the Old Ones must be the primary objective. Last year, I was an infant groping my way through a world of treachery and deviance. Emotional, untempered, hesitant in regards to what needed to be done. Now, that same seedy underworld trembles at the mention of my name. Many of those who once endeavored to kill me have since found themselves upon the edge of my blade, the very weapon that severed the life of one Jago Sensaki a short year ago. The road ahead is long, and paved with those hoping to halt my ambition. I will not be denied like I was a year ago by Karina Wolfenden, back when taking life was still the most loathsome means to an end. Besides, my accomplishments since are much more infernal in nature. It all starts now. There's no going back, not after what has already been perpetrated. The landscape of this sordid little society, a dark and secretive world that's always existing under the doctrines of blasphemy and conventional sin, has been altered forever. And very soon, even the outside world may feel repercussions of the wrath erupting therein. We couldn't lurk along the outer fringe of this Earth forever, and what will happen will potentially be felt the world over. It's been like a power keg from the moment Father Shanahan murdered Maurice Thorne, and soon the one essential spark will fall and set the world afire. All hell will break loose, and perhaps those prophecies will come to pass. Then we shall see exactly who the world kneels before when the name Ethan Knight becomes synonymous with power. Zero hour approaches. Their darkest hour, my finest......
