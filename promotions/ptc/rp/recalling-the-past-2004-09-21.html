Recalling The Past

21 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-21 My past is a near mystery to me at this point of my life. It drifts further away each and every day, carrying with it every single thing that I had to endure and fight for. Its ship sailed a long time ago, leaving me with a new sense of freedom, a freedom that I had never experienced before. I, as any other man would have done, embraced this freedom with open arms. I tried to forget it all - the gang wars, the nightly beatings, the constant anxiety and the pent-up rage. Sometimes it is impossible to defeat, though. Memories are impossible to defeat. When they exist in your mind and they are just as vivid and just as real as they always were, there seems to be no way out. Somewhere in all of us, there is a side that we'd rather not acknowledge. Whether we admit it or not, it is ever present. Like a monster inside of us that is just waiting for the chance to pounce and escape, grabbing its prey in its knife-like claws, it exists and it is very real. Most of us have the ability to control this ‘monster’ and while that may be all well and good, I didn't have such luck. On the night of 26th September, 1995, the monster inside of me escaped. Running rampant, I had no clue what kind of damage I would eventually cause. To make it worse, I did this damage within the space of a few minutes. There is no excuse for what I did and even today, it creeps into my mind like an unwelcome guest. To say I wish I could go back and relive it, change it, would be a severe understatement. The memory sticks in my head like a sore thumb... ++++ Flashback ++++ It had been a painful two hours since my last cigarette and I could tell that, day by day, my craving was only getting worse. I yanked the last packet I had brought along out of my pocket and readied my lighter. A flash of light from the street interrupted me, causing me to freeze in my tracks, but it was soon gone. It wasn't a copper's car. They only came out when the real loons started parading the streets. The gang and I, we were nothing but punks. A bunch of teens out to cause trouble just for the sheer buzz of it - that was our M.O. I lit the cigarette and took a deep drag to satisfy my craving. After two hours, it felt good in a sickly sort of way. “Yo Tony! We gonna get this show on the road or what?! C'mon man, you're taking way too long!” The voice of my friend and fellow gang member, Louis Brett, called from afar. I stood in an alleyway which had been plunged into darkness hours before when the sun went down. Since then, Louis and I had remained there, waiting. The rest of the gang were around the city. We figured, at least at the time, that working in pairs would suit us fine. For what we were waiting for, we weren't exactly sure, but we had a strange feeling that that night was going to mean something. I heard the tone of urgency in Louis' voice that was heard so frequently on these heists. “Hang on, Louis. Can't you see I'm beatin' up a copper right here? The blood's gettin' on my jacket... Oh damn!” I cried sarcastically, milking the idea that I had accomplished the mission before any of them. The plan was simple - catch a copper and do whatever you want to the unfortunate soul. As farfetched as it seemed at that time, it wasn't impossible. Just a few weeks beforehand, the L.A. Times had reported a sixteen year old kid breaking a cop's nose, as well as teaching his partner a thing or two about wearing protection. The kid was a rebel, nothing more. When he did the job on the copper, he had no back-up and no sense of what he was doing. It was an act of a teen in desperate need of attention. Despite his crude methods, he got what he sought - attention. In Los Angeles, the only gang in need of attention was us. Breaking into shops and scaring the living daylights out of the shopkeeper as well as every single shopper wasn't enough, even if it was done on a daily basis. The other gangs kept coming back with something bigger and better. It was a tradition in L.A. to have gang wars; in fact, it was necessary. If you wanted to be cool, you joined a gang and you made a name for yourself. No games, just getting the job done, no matter what price you had to pay. Louis and I strolled out casually onto the sidewalk, trying to act normal. Perhaps we overdid it just a little bit, both of us mocking a rich man's walk. I quit the charade quickly, though, heading into the shadow of a dumpster. Louis approached me then with a scowl on his tanned face. He obviously wasn't entertained by my fake assault on the imaginary copper I had pulled a few minutes before. I suppose I wasn't either, come to think of it. We had bigger fish to fry and we both knew that. Problem was, we were taken off guard. It was instantaneous, like a gunshot to the head. No time to think, no leeway. The look of horror took over Louis' face and I knew what was going to happen next. “Tony!!!,” he yelped in shock, almost falling down onto the wet stone beneath him. I jumped into action straight away as I saw the cop car approaching. The car was at least ten metres away, heading towards Louis and I at high speed. This was unusual. For the coppers to come out at 10pm was out of the ordinary. Not only that, but it threw a wrench into our plan. Instantly, I grabbed Louis out of instinct and we took refuge in a nearby alleyway, crossing our proverbial fingers that they'd pass us by. They did. We both let out a sigh of relief. The traditional L.A.P.D. cop car, with its metallic blue stripes across the side and the large L.A.P.D. insignia emblazoned on the top, sped past us down West Sunshine Boulevard. Obviously, they had something else locked in their crosshairs. Then again, I had always thought - at least at that time - that the L.A.P.D. were a bunch of half-wits, and so they never caught ‘the baddies.’ “Screwed it up again, man!,” Louis shouted at me in celebration of our lucky break, with enthusiasm in his breathless voice. “I told you,” I began, “these coppers don't know the first thing about their job!” Back then, I was Mr. Confidence. The epitome of cockiness. Almost the antithesis of all I stand for today. Still, this cockiness was a springboard for me to bigger things. Louis stopped smiling suddenly and look deep into my dark green eyes, a pastime he was all too fond of. He enjoyed being intense and almost acted the part of ‘the aspiring gang member’. I stared back at him blankly, not giving any emotion away. “Where'd the rest of the guys go, Louis?,” I asked with a noticeable tone of annoyance in my voice. Louis scratched his neck nervously and broke that stare for just a few seconds while he thought about how to put it to me. I could predict what was coming next. I knew the bad news was about to escape from his mouth, as if to prove my doubts. He had failed in his part of the deal. I just went for a quick smoke - Louis promised he would keep tabs on where the rest of them were. “Man, I-I think I might've lost 'em...,” he tried. But that wasn't good enough. Not for me. I felt myself perspire out of frustration. I grabbed him by the lapels of his brown leather jacket and lifted him up against the wall of the alley with a force I had never felt in my bones before. Perturbed and distraught that our perfect plan could have just been ruined, I gathered my thoughts. Attempting to think logically, I calmed myself down slightly. Louis slipped back down to his feet and caught his breath. There was no time to waste, though. I shot him a glance and he knew I was deadly serious. “You are seriously telling me that we have lost the rest of the guys? Are you telling me that, Louis?!,” I yelled at him like a father would at his son. Louis held his hands up as if trying to show me that he had nothing in them. I wasn't convinced, though. Either Louis intentionally got us away from them or else we were just unfortunate, pulling the short straw. “W-well... maybe we could just trek around the streets a bit, try and find 'em...,” he responded timidly. The build-up of this plan was perfect. It had no flaws, it was destined to be successful. The gang was ready to attain some stardom, some recognition. No more did any of us want to stand in the shadows of the other city gangs. We wanted to be the top guys. But as I stood there with Louis Brett staring into my eyes with a look of stupidity and guilt written all over his face, I realised that this kid had screwed it up for us all. Just like I craved cigarettes, I craved fame. He had just ripped the sweet victory away from me... and I couldn't stand that. I made an axe with my hands and smacked Louis in the stomach with such force that I could have broken one of his ribs, if not several of them. The monster inside of me had come out. At that moment, the idea of control had disappeared from my mind. I was a wild animal with minimal understanding of why I was doing this... or when I would stop. Louis fell backwards, tripping up on a piece of stone jutting out of the concrete behind him. I approached his prone form on the wet concrete and knew I had to finish him. This man had cost me what I thought was my only chance at recognition around L.A. I got ready to finish the job. Suddenly, I was grabbed from behind. It was a strong and tight grip that I couldn't escape from even if I tried. I turned around but by then, they had already cuffed me. The L.A.P.D. coppers had finally got their hands on Tony Aliso. I knew that even if I attempted to release myself from the cuffs, it just wouldn't be worth it. That night was already in shambles; what would I have been gaining if I had found a way out? Emotion overtook me as the copper dragged me away forcefully to the trademark L.A.P.D. vehicle on the street. I wondered how the coppers were so silent in their approach that neither Louis nor I ever heard them. Darkness had swept across the sky and in what seemed like an eternity, I was pushed into the car head-first. I sat down in an almost dream-like state, unaware of where I was or why I was there. So many thoughts flooded into my head at that moment. Anger at myself, regret over what I had just done to, in truth, one of my best friends. Most of all, though, I was ashamed of myself for letting go of my control. The monster that exists inside me struck... and it never should have been that way. I stared out the car window and spotted Louis. One middle-aged cop was helping him to his feet. Louis could barely stand and it was clear that even with just that one strike to the sternum, I had caused serious harm to him, albeit unintentionally. Louis turned his head to the right and we locked eyes for a moment. It was time to turn things around, I thought to myself. I had to break the cycle. ++++ End Flashback ++++ It still lives on. The things I have done don't go away in an instant. As a matter of fact, what doesn't kill you only makes you stronger. 26th September, 1995, I may have made a huge mistake in doing what I did to Louis, but I don't think I would be where I am today were it not for that monster coming out of its cage. It sounds absurd, doesn't it? To thank yourself for doing a bad deed? Going against everything your father taught you - all your ideals thrown to the wolves. Sometimes it has to be done. After that incident, my life took a U-turn for the better. I wasn't a gang member any longer, I was Tony Aliso. I had my own identity for once in my life. My father, though he wasn't around to appreciate what I had become, would have been proud of me. I rejected all my past when I took those first steps out of the L.A.P.D.'s Parker Centre on that chilly December morning. It was like stepping into a new skin, a new skin of freedom. That was the day I regained control... ++++ Flashback +++ The guard's bulky figure moved out of my way as I slowly stepped towards the gates of heaven, at least, for me. Parker Centre's glass doors had never looked so good to me, for now I knew that this was the time to switch it all around and make it right. Officer Hanson held a hand up to my chest just as I was about to push the door open. Hanson sure knew how to pick his times. “Ummm... Mr. Aliso,” he began in that croaky Southern accent, “you'd be best fixed if you stayed outta trouble, you heard?” Spoken like a true Southerner, I thought. I cracked a smile at Hanson and then, I played a trick that until now, I wouldn't have been able to do. I had to do it - it was my last chance. “Hey Hanson, what's that thing on your foot?,” I announced while pointing down at Hanson's uniform shoes, black as coal and obviously shined this morning. Hanson, like the bumbling idiot he was, looked down. I seized his cap with the L.A.P.D. insignia knitted on and ran outside. Freedom. It flowed through my veins now. Sure, a few passers-by gave me that signature L.A. look, as if to say ‘Who's this lunatic?’, but I wasn't about to let some overweight so-and-so's ruin this. I only then realised that Hanson was shouting at the top of his voice after me. I turned around to see Hanson standing approximately six metres away. He resembled some sort of drugged ape in his outrage. I still held his precious hat in my right hand. Then, it hit me. I cocked my swinging arm back and threw the feather-light cap into the famous fountain of the Parker Centre. Hanson screamed like a woman in labour - that is how bad it was. Renewed, revitalised and having just thrown a uniformed officer's hat into the fountain a.k.a. the place where hats go to rest, I fixed the lapels on my jacket and walked down the steps. I was back in the real world now. No excuses, no ducking out of the truth. It was my time to shine. ++++ End Flashback ++++ Now it's my time to face the music. The music I speak of isn't some kiddies melody about The Chipmunk Family. No, it's the prime tournament of today's wrestling world. The tournament I speak of is, of course, the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. And it's reached its fourth instalment, it seems. Imagine... hundreds of hopefuls have gone through the tournament before. Superstar after superstar, elimination after elimination, it has all gone down before. I took a few long strides across my smooth bedroom carpet and reached out for it. The GTT4 invitation envelope which, at this stage, had been torn open. It lay on my bedside table, not as a burden, but as a reminder of how far I had come in my life. From petty theft to superstardom as a professional wrestler, things had certainly changed for yours truly. The envelope lay to the side of the letter itself, beside it a pen where I had signed a while back. I stared down at the letter as I would stare into an abyss - with a look of pure emptiness. So many thoughts flowed through my mind. I stopped myself in the tracks of my mind. Deep within, I knew at that moment that wrestling was my calling. It's not like I hadn't already figured that was the case; it's just that I felt it just then, as I absentmindedly clicked my fingers in one of my nervous habits. All of us have a calling in life. Mine was wrestling. The stage for my rise to greatness - the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. Come hell or high water, I was going to win. My mark hadn't yet been etched on the scripture of wrestling history. It was time I changed that. It was time everything changed.
