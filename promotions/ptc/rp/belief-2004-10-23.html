Belief

23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-23 Isn’t it great? When all the chips are down, when everything seems to be going against you, you reach down into the bottom of your heart and find one small moment in your life that changes you for the better. It pushes you. It psyches you into pulling out that extra special something that you need to come out on top. So some people remember a piece of racial hatred and take that as their motivation to do bad things to people, turning them into a bitter, twisted person ready to tear limb from limb on the next person who stands before them. I have no need to dwell on the past. What’s happened has happened, as much pain and suffering in my life that I’ve had to endure, looking back on it will only turn me into somebody that I’m not. I don’t need to look back on my life, at the many different moments that I failed. At all the times I fell short of the mark, because this time around things are going to be different. I have something different. This time I have a little bit of something that will take me further than I’ve ever managed before. A little something called belief. I believe I can do this; I believe I can win this tournament and I believe I can beat Odium along the way. “What are you watching, little mate?” A stupid question really, as always, Ducky is sitting watching a documentary on the documentary channel. He never watches anything else. The channel is showing an hour long special on the Klu Klux Klan and the crazy stuff they got up to, each to their own I suppose. It’s not a topic that interests me, but I’ll take a seat anyway. I carefully lower my mug down onto the new glass coffee table that was received by Primetime Central as a replacement. They were a little annoyed at me breaking the other one, but because I did so well in my match they sent me another. Leaning back I take in the full comfort of the nice padded leather armchair. At first it was slightly cold to the touch, but once relaxed in it adjusted to my body temperature and was nice and warm. With the dim lights of the room and the fire in the corner sending a blanket of soothing heat over my body my mind started to wander, my eyelids slowly closing shut. -----=====|=====----- “I have a flush, sorry mate. I think I win this one.” I said turning over my cards and looking up at my friend Craig with a smug look on my face. “Oh really…? Well I have a full house!” he exclaimed turning his own cards over and showing that I had in fact lost that round. He was a good lad, Craig was. Way too clever, too clever for his own good sometimes. He used to quite often get beaten up by kids at school for making a wise remark. I seem to remember one time when a rather large kid trying to pick a fight went up to Craig and said “What are you looking at?” and he replied with the quip “I don’t know. I haven’t figured it out yet!” That got him a bloody nose and two black eyes. For someone so smart, he can do something really flaming stupid. It was my 15th birthday, and we were playing poker for a laugh, just a bunch of the lads around my house for a quiet night in. A video to watch later, some buffet food mum had lain out and a game of poker (betting with sweets, of course) for now to keep us occupied until we watched the film. To leave us be, my parents had gone out for a meal, so we had the house to ourselves. They didn’t mind, I’d had friends over before and we were always well-behaved. “So, what film have we got to watch tonight then, Dave?” Mark asked. Mark was a pretty small kid, used to get picked on a lot for his height, was always polite to people though. It was pretty sad to see someone that was so nice to everyone get beaten up for no apparent reason at all, but that happened a lot to our circle of friends. We were the outcasts, the geeks, the dweebs, whatever you wanted to call it. We were the people that all the cool kids used to look good in front of their own circle of friends. They’d gain popularity by beating us up, by calling us names, by pulling pranks on us. But there was nothing we could do about it. There’s no way they’d ever want us to hang with them, and there’s no way we could stop them from doing what they were doing, because there were so many of them. It wasn’t that we couldn’t handle ourselves. For a bunch of geeks we were pretty tough. A few of us played rugby for the local team and were built like brick shithouses. “Terminator 2,” I informed Mark, and with that being said a large grin beamed across his face. “Cooool!” he said enthusiastically, “are we putting it on now?” I reached over the table to the video box from the local video rental store and popped it open, pulling out the video. As I walked across the room I turned to the lads at the table and said “Why not, eh?” They all got off their chairs and made their way towards the television set, sitting down on the carpet cross-legged waiting eagerly for me to put the video in the recorder and get things going. As I went to push the video through the slot in the recorder, there was a ring at the door. That’s strange… I my parents weren’t supposed to be back for another hour at least, it’s too late to be getting any callers… I wonder who it could be… “Who is it?” asked Mark as I leaned towards the front door looking through the stained glass trying to see who it was. “I have no idea…” I said, my voice trailing off as I wandered closer to the front door, my curiosity getting greater by the second. I walked over to the door, pushed the handle down and slowly pulled the door open to see who it was. “Heeey! Davey Boy!” said the happy, grinning kid from outside the door. It was Lee Englefield. God I hated that kid. He was in the big gang of kids that always beat me up at school, and his face wasn’t genuine that’s for sure, that grin wasn’t a grin for a happy greeting. That grin was devious. The grin was showing me that he was about to do something to me, and my friends, at my house. He quickly whipped his foot into the gap in the door, stopping me from being able to close it and forced the door open with a big shove. He sent me backwards and almost falling onto the floor as he stormed into my house. Following him was a group of people, all dressed up, girls and boys with bottles of booze, cigarettes hanging from their mouths and evil grins on all their faces. It infuriated me that they would do such a thing on my birthday, but I felt completely helpless. There were about 20 of these people here, but only 5 of us, and I’d only be the one standing up to them anyway. There wasn’t any way I’d be able to stop them from tearing this house apart. My parents would be so disappointed in me, I failed to keep the house safe, I failed to keep the house clean, and that doesn’t even count any damage that these kids might do. I’d never be able to make them believe that these people weren’t invited by me and that they crashed my quiet night in. Ever since I started primary school, these people have hounded me constantly for being brighter than them. Segregated me and my friends from the rest of the school, and then did the same through secondary school too. Every day I’d get verbal abuse from them, every day they’d either beat me up, do some sick prank like steal my rucksack, only to say later they’d left it by the boys toilets and it being filled with piss, spraying graffiti on the walls of the school saying “Dave Hurst is a faggot” and a whole host of other terrible things to me. They’d taken my life from me. I wasn’t a smiley happy kid, I wasn’t the happy-go-lucky EDH you see today, I was just Dave Hurst, the kid whose whole purpose in life was to take abuse from this gang of kids. But I’d had enough of it. I wasn’t going to let Lee Englefield and his gang of thugs get away with it any longer. So I flipped. But this wasn’t any normal fit, because I wasn’t ever the type of person to ever harm anyone. I never lashed out, never showed my emotions, just always kept them locked up inside where nobody would see how I really felt. I was breathing heavier than I’d ever breathed in my life. My eyes were glazed over, the veins in my eyes bulging, my face going bright red. These people weren’t going to get away with this anymore. It was like I’d lost control of my body, I wanted to calm myself down, but I couldn’t. A few of the kids noticed, one of them being Lee, he gave a cocky grin and looked back at me. “What’s wrong Dave?” he said with his smug look never receding, “There something you want to say?” He knew what I wanted to say. He knew exactly what I wanted to say, but he also thought that I was never going to say it. What he didn’t know though, was that however many times he would have gotten away with that before, this time was going to be different. But it was a really strange experience. I never felt like I had any control over myself. I felt like a spectator to the entire event, merely watching myself doing what I was doing. And what I was doing was reaching down, standing by the fireplace, and reaching down to the stand for a long, metal poker. This was going too far and I knew it, but I couldn’t stop myself. It was as if I’d been possessed. I reached down, clasping the metal poker and lifting it up. The kid’s eyes widened with concern as I took the poker and proceeded to walk towards him. “You’re not going to hit me with that, are you Dave?” he asked, his grin slowly diminishing. No answer. Not just because I wasn’t going to give him one, but because I couldn’t. I fought with myself to take the poker down and stop messing about, but I just couldn’t. Lee Englefield saw the glazed over look in my eyes, he saw the fury building inside of me and he backed away from me, as much as he could to avoid anything that I might do. But I quickened my pace, now almost marching towards him, as he moved faster too, not looking at where he was going he back-peddled as fast as his legs could move him, but I just matched it, walking faster and faster. I pulled the poker back behind my shoulders like a baseball bat… He tripped on the rug, falling back and smacking his head on the wall… All he could do is sit against the wall and watch… The poker flew forward, every ounce of effort in my arms from all the torment that he and his friends had given me for the last 10 years of my life, all put into one single swing… …All connecting with his temple. I don’t know if it was that blow that killed him, or the dozens of blows sent to his head afterwards, but that was the moment that Dave Hurst stopped being just the kid that got picked on. But I never want to go back to what I had become. I was a monster, I had no control over my life, had no control over what was going to happen next, over what was going to happen to me. That was how I got to be where I am now. But it’s not something I look back on for motivation. It is not something that I use to keep me focussed, it’s not something that I use to give me the inspiration for my next match. It is something that has haunted me, for my entire life this dream has circled me, over and over. It took me another 10 years to turn myself around into a normal human being, and I hope to God that I never have to go through all that again. -----=====|=====----- “…and that concludes our scheduled program for tonight, on next: Wildlife in the Sahara.” I slowly open my eyes, escaping from the horror of the nightmare plaguing me once again. I wipe my eyes and get out of my comfy chair, Ducky is still there watching the television show he seems pretty content with that for now. I walk over to the hallway, reach down and pick up a watering can. I may have just had a pretty upsetting dream, but this is something that really cheers me up. As I tilt the can, pouring a spray of water onto the soil. The soil soaks up the water like a sponge, as above it stands a lush green stem holding lush green leaves. After going to the brink of death, this plant has rejuvenated into a beautiful green plant, as healthy as can be. And all it needed was a lot of patience, to trim one of the branches, water it regularly, but not enough to drown it and the most important ingredient of them all, belief. I believed in myself that I could bring this plant back, I believed in the plant to being able to hold on and survive, and with that will-power grew a lovely healthy green plant. You see, me and the plant are a lot alike, in many different ways. At first, nobody took any notice of me, I was just EDH. In the same way the plant was just a plant. At first, nobody gave me the light of day. Nobody took any notice of me. I didn’t take any notice of the plant. But then when things were down, when things were at their worst in my life and the plants, we bounced back. We climbed back from the brink of disaster and became something more than we’d ever thought to have been. We became exceptional. And all it needed was one simple concept, one single little thing from the people around us… …Belief.
