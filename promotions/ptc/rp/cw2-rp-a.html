A (CW2 Round 1: Seymour Almasy vs Archibald MacGregor)

Final Round Matches | Rules | Player Login | Roleplay Board | Discussion | PTC Website Author: Seymour Almasy Fed: UWF Match vs:: Archibald MacGregor Title: A Date: 15-04-2005 Location: Varying Round: 1 Roleplay I. In years gone by, he would have been playing Final Fantasy. Coming up with some fanciful quest to aid him in his ultimate goal of tournament dominance. But time, success, and reality had changed Seymour Almasy. He saw professional wrestling as what it truly was: something that he enjoyed, something that he was good at, and perhaps most importantly, a business. The Universal Wrestling Federation could no longer contain the two-time PTC Global Champion. Neither could Primetime Central. Two deals, signed with All-Star Championship Wrestling and New Frontier Wrestling (the latter for a cool $1,000,000 a year, plus incentives) gave Seymour the notoriety and competition that he felt no federation in PTC could give him. The competition that before, only Tournament could provide. To Seymour, it was spelled with a capital T. It meant more than any title representing a country, continent, or style of wrestling. More than a World Title. Beating one man to become a champion was good, but beating six or seven to become a tournament champion was even better. Cruiserweight Tournament 2 was a chance at redemption after the unforeseen catastrophe that was GTT4. An opportunity to prove what Seymour Almasy knew in his heart. That he was the best wrestler in the world. Of course, here he’d have to settle for proving that he was the best CRUISERWEIGHT in the world. Of course, we all know it’s not going to go smoothly… II. “Table for Almasy…” The words were still reverberating in Seymour’s ears a half hour after he heard them. He and his lovely ex-wife Aerith had been escorted to their table. The bottle of wine that Aerith had brought sat on the solid wood tabletop, expertly uncorked and poured into two fine crystal glasses. He held the glass in his hand, raising it to his lips for a small sip. Wine wasn’t really so bad, he’d come to realize after hating it at first. It was an acquired taste, one that he had gained after sampling dozens of bottles. This was the Manhattan Ocean Club, one of the finest seafood restaurants in New York City. A place that, mere months ago, he would have been laughed out of. But now, his sharply dyed hair was overlooked due to the fine suit he wore. Considering the briefcase that lay at his side, most would have assumed him a businessman rather than a wrestler. In a sense, they would have been right. “Seymour…” Aerith’s voice called to him from across the table, but Seymour’s attention was elsewhere. Opening his briefcase, he pulled out a series of dossiers, scattering them over his side of the table. The names on them were familiar from past performances in PTC sponsored events. “Ethan Knight,” he mumbled to himself, pulling information from one of the manila folders. Written on the sheets of paper were information about Knight’s fighting style, height, weight, and personal life. The amount of research done was impressive, and truth be told, probably a little frightening as well. He placed the data carefully back in its folder, reaching for another. This one read “Dawn McFarlane”. Seymour paused for a few moments, looking at the picture on the cover, before Aerith’s voice finally snapped his attention back. “You’re not eating dinner with Dawn McFarlane, you know,” she said, agitation in her voice. “Yet,” was Seymour’s flippant one word reply, “although continued distraction while I am attempting to review possible opponents may result in me exploring the possibility.” Her jaw dropped in astonished shock, before she recovered. She knew that he wasn’t serious, but she also knew that she had broken one of Seymour’s sacred rules. No bothering him when he was preparing for Tournament. Frowning, he placed the McFarlane and Knight folders back in the briefcase. “I’m sorry, Aerith,” he mumbled, taking another sip of his wine. “I’m just on edge. Tonight, they’re announcing the tournament brackets for CW2.” He had ordered Primetime Central offices to call with the brackets the moment they were made official. Among the dossiers remaining on the table was a white bracket sheet, ready to be filled out by a joyous Seymour. This WAS his March Madness, his Bracketology. Almasy could have cared less about Bucknell vs. Kansas, Syracuse vs. Vermont, or even UNC vs. Illinois. What he found appealing were matchups such as Windsor vs. McFarlane, Carys vs. Phoenix, and Knight vs. Wolfenden. The matches that would shape a tournament, and make it unique. And in his own opinion? It was super scintillating sensational, thank you very much. “This is so exciting,” he squealed, resembling a child on Christmas morning. “I wonder who I’ll get this time. Jackson put up a hell of a fight last year.” It had been a narrow win for Seymour over the former RPW star in the first round of Cruiserweight Tournament 1. The sort of contest that gave Seymour chills to think about, for he had come so close to being eliminated in the first round. To lose was bad enough, yet he could eventually come to grips with losing. To lose in the first round, however, would be humiliating. Degrading. Embarrassing beyond belief. He had vowed to himself that it would never, ever happen. Their meals had arrived, but the Final Fantasy didn’t notice. Aerith, on the other hand, was contentedly diving into her salmon. While she certainly supported Seymour, she didn’t quite entirely understand the fervor with which he launched himself into his tournaments. “Ooh, flounder!” Finally, it seemed, Seymour had noticed that his stuffed flounder had arrived. Grabbing a folder marked “Tsunami Oni”, Seymour jabbed a fork into his food, raising a heavenly mouthful of the crabmeat filled flounder to his mouth. A look of unabashed joy came over his face, as he washed down the succulent seafood with a large swallow of merlot. “Eating well, it seems, truly is the best revenge,” he said, Aerith not sure how he knew the proverb. Such things had been happening more and more often lately, as Seymour grew more…refined. She looked up at him for a moment. While in the past, suits had always looked out of place on him, the black two piece number that he wore now fit, somehow. He was a far cry from the rabbit-decapitating nut job he had started his career as. Of course, the introduction of reality had something to do with that…but at the same time, much of his change was self-engineered. Seymour Almasy was nothing if not driven, both inside and outside the ring. A few more forkfuls of seafood entered Seymour’s mouth, but as time passed, his heart really wasn’t in eating anymore. His stomach growled, not out of hunger, but out of nervousness. It took all of his self-control to not stand up, leave the restaurant entirely, and pace like a psych ward patient outside until he got the call he was waiting for. His fork was halfway to his mouth when it came, prompting Seymour to immediately drop the eating utensil. It hit the plate with an audible clang, the vibrations against Seymour’s hip serving notice that his fate was about to be laid bare for him to know. Coolly, he dipped into the pocket of his suit pants, retrieving his cellular phone. With a quick flick and a push of a button, he was on the phone with a PTC official. “I hope you’ve got good news for me,” he said, beaming at his wife across the table. This was it. The moment of truth. Seymour would find out whom he would have to go through to receive a chance at tournament redemption. Names came at him like machine gun bullets, but Seymour didn’t flinch, writing them all into the brackets as each smaller eight man bracket was filled. He wrote the names in the so-called Devito Bracket calmly, eyebrow rising in suspicion at the words “Cereal Killer”, before the official explained that he was a CEREAL Killer, not a serial killer. To each their own, Seymour figured. Poor guy must have had a bad run-in with Captain Crunch or something. The Coleman Bracket was next, as the familiar names spilled into Seymour’s ear and onto the page. Nephilim, a man Seymour considered an infernal coward. Sebastian Moraco, a strong competitor on GTT4. He nodded along as the names were rattled off, shaking his head ‘no’ to Aerith’s nonverbal question. As the Baker Bracket was announced to him, he was slightly surprised not to hear his name yet. That meant that he was to be in the final bracket. Tsunami Oni’s name gave the Final Fantasy pause. If anyone in wrestling had Seymour’s number, it was the Devil from the Deep Blue Sea, as much as he hated to admit it. As it stood, if Seymour made it out of his bracket, the odds of him meeting Oni in a tournament semi-final were quite good indeed. “So, guess that leaves my bracket,” Seymour said, pleasantly. He was dismayed to note the employee taking a deep breath. Generally, that meant bad news. Wonderful. “No use in hesitating,” he told the obviously nervous man on the other end of the line. “Just tell me who’s in the bracket. If it helps, leave my match until last.” Even as he said the words, however, he knew that something wasn’t right. His mind flashed back over the few dossiers that he had glanced at over the course of his meal. Ethan Knight. Dawn McFarlane. Neither name had been mentioned. This…was not good. At all. “I’ll start from the bottom then, Mr. Almasy,” said the voice in Seymour’s ear. “Dawn McFarlane versus RAD.” Seymour couldn’t help but let out a sigh of relief. That was one potential bullet dodged. McFarlane had made an immense name for herself in CW1, defeating Tessa Windsor and giving Karina Wolfenden all that the Negasonic Lupine could handle, and more. “Tessa Windsor versus Joshua Taro Freedom.” “What?!” Almasy was flabbergasted. Another strong pair of competitors in this, the Troyer Bracket. Windsor had defeated him before, and Freedom was, in the past, one of PRIME’s most decorated competitors. The winner of this match would face the winner of McFarlane/RAD, and unless RAD was as rad as his name, that would likely be McFarlane. A most interesting match, no matter who won between Tessa and JTF. Of the confirmed names he knew in the tournament, only Ethan Knight remained. The next match announcement would be important, indeed. Could Primetime Central have managed to give Seymour a HARDER match than he had received in round one last year? “Ethan Knight vs. Mildred McMann” was what Seymour heard. Finally, he could exhale. No Knight in round one…although, this meant that he had the ridiculous proposition of facing the member of the Sect of Black Wisdom in round two. Recently, he had taken the Sect’s leader, Violence Jack, to a time limit draw. PTC tournaments had no such provisions, however, and Knight would likely be seeking vengeance on the Pariah Saint’s behalf. Now he understood why the man on the phone with him was so reluctant to announce Almasy’s bracket. It was absolutely loaded. Even if he could somehow beat Ethan Knight, a semi-finalist in CW1, he would have to contend with Windsor, who had already beaten him, the impressively credentialed Freedom, the unknown RAD, or the rising superstar McFarlane. Any of the four of them could defeat him. “Seymour Almasy vs. Archibald MacGregor.” Ah, yes. In Seymour’s moment of panic, he had forgotten about his first round contest. He had never heard the name Archibald MacGregor before, but it sounded noble. Far more impressive than first round opponents he had easily beaten, such as Ash and Ethan Evans. Realistically, he had little reason to be concerned, but the back of his mind would not stop sounding a tiny alarm bell. “Mind giving me some information on him, or am I going to need to go into this blind,” he asked, clearly wanting to commence his preparations for Mr. MacGregor. He jotted down the sparse information as it came. Submission specialist, check. Bad home life, check. Alcoholic, check. After a few formalities, such as travel arrangements, Seymour clicked the phone off. The smile on his face from earlier was gone, replaced by a look of quiet contemplation. “What’s wrong, sweetie,” asked Aerith, clearly confused at hearing only one end of the conversation. “Is everything okay?” “If you call being in the bracket of death okay, then yes, I’m fine.” “Oh no!” was Aerith’s reply. “Who’s in it?” “Ethan Knight, Tessa Windsor, Joshua Taro Freedom, and your new favorite wrestler, Dawn McFarlane, among others,” he said. “I’ve never heard of my opponent in round one, Archibald MacGregor, before, but this information I just got on him is giving me a bad feeling.” “A bad feeling,” Aerith asked. “As in, you’ll lose to him?” “It’s more than that,” Seymour responded, grimly. “His information seems similar to that of two other tournament entrants in events that I’ve been in over the past year or so. A broken home life, with solid wrestling credentials yet no record of any past promotions. All PTC knows about his wrestling skills is that he’s apparently an expert submissionist. There’s no videotape, no nothing. It reminds me of Hollinger and Sail.” “Who?” For a moment, Seymour had forgotten that Wrestle God 4 and GTT4, tournaments which the aforementioned two men had competed in. “Nathan Hollinger and Jonathan Sail,” he said, slowly, a touch of contempt in his voice. “Both of them came from questionable backgrounds. Hollinger was accused of a horrific mass murder on board an airplane. Sail’s past was far murkier, but rumors of him being an assassin persisted. Neither has appeared since the end of their tournament, in Hollinger’s case, or their loss, in Sail’s.” Not unexpectedly, a look of horror formed on Aerith’s pale face. “And you think that MacGregor is also a killer,” she asked, in astonished disbelief. “Yes,” was Almasy’s blunt response. “Of course, I can’t prove it. But if he IS…I’ve got a bad feeling that there’s a conspiracy out there. Hollinger beat Violence Jack in the first round of Wrestle God 4. As much as I hate V-Jack, he’s one of the top wrestlers in the world. Nathan was set to face Angelo Deville when the tournament was mysteriously called off. Jonathan Sail met Kasper Sky in the second round of their contest…and I don’t think anyone’s heard from Sky since that encounter.” The implications of Seymour’s statements began to sink in for both husband and wife. After a few moments, and several attempts the Final Fantasy is able to verbalize the thought that had scared him so. “Someone’s after the top wrestlers in the world,” he whispered, not wanting to believe it. “And I’m next.” III. Two Hours Later. The former golden couple of the UWF was now on the Long Island Expressway. A couple hundred miles away, on a table in the Manhattan Ocean Club sat $250 and two half-finished plates of fine seafood. As often happened, fear had turned to anger, and Seymour Almasy had gathered his wife, loaded her into the car, and began a frenetically-paced nighttime drive. “Where the hell are we going,” she asked, after the shock of the past few hours had worn off. It took a bit for Seymour to remember, himself. He had placed an angry call to PTC Headquarters the second he stepped outside the restaurant, undoubtedly annoying the secretary who had expected a quiet night. Still, she had proven helpful, and an arrangement had been made for Almasy to meet with the PTC Tournament Director, Wool-Knit. … Yeah, I know, imagine the teasing that poor guy must have undergone in middle school. “Coram, New York,” he finally managed, the name of the small Long Island town having escaped his mind. “It’s where PTC’s U.S. headquarters is for some ungodly reason. 4861 KAKIWAKAS~! Drive.” Aerith could only shake her head at the somewhat ludicrously named road, before Seymour explained. “Apparently, second place in GTT4 got to name the street. We’re lucky Hurst didn’t decide on Ducky Place, or Angelo Deville’d be on a mass murder spree right now.” She blinked twice, before finally deciding that she didn’t know what Seymour was talking about, and further, had no desire to know. Her fingers drummed in boredom against her thigh, suppressing a yawn with her other hand. In short, she really didn’t want to be here right now. “Can I stay in the car when we arrive,” she asked. Okay, so tact wasn’t necessarily her strong suit, but she was tired. Another source of motivation for her was the rage that she saw in her husband’s eyes. It was quiet and dormant for now, but she fully expected it to erupt the second Seymour and the Tournament Director were behind closed doors. That was a drama she didn’t particularly want a front row seat for. To her surprise (and delight), Seymour agreed. “Certainly. I probably don’t want you to hear some of the things I will say to the Director if things get heated. This is about more than just my life being on the line. This is ALSO about getting screwed in an unfairly loaded bracket!” Yes, he meant what he said. Forget the fact that Seymour was facing death at the hands of a man who was quite-possibly a well trained killing machine; Seymour was still pissed off about his bracket being filled with the likes of Dawn McFarlane and Ethan Knight. PRIORITIES, people! PRIORITIES! So what if Seymour’s life was on the line? That gave him all the more reason to win the tournament and beat Archibald. After a few more minutes of driving, Almasy brought the car into a left turn, announcing in a solemn proclamation: “We’re here.” The massive wrought-iron gate before them was flanked on either side by two statues. These were not, however, the gothic gargoyles that one might have expected. They were, instead, sheep. Aerith was speechless, although really, that was fast becoming a theme of the evening. Seymour continued through the gate, looking up at the monolithic skyscraper looming over suburban Long Island. The parking lot contained roughly two other cars, allowing the Final Fantasy to easily park his vehicle. “I’ll be back as soon as I can,” he said, turning to Aerith. “Wish me luck.” She embraced him tightly, flinging both of her arms around his neck. Really, she knew that there was likely nothing that could be done. The Tournament Director’s decisions had always been final in the past, but she would still give her ex-husband all the support in the world. She quite liked him alive, after all. With their goodbyes said, Seymour opened the door, stepping out into the crisp New York night. Running a hand through his hair, he sighed deeply. It was now or never. IV. “You can’t honestly expect me to believe that, can you?” In his years of running tournaments and other inter-promotional events for the PTC, the Tournament Director had heard many ridiculous allegations. Accusations of bias were rife in seemingly every event PTC put on, made by angered fans and those who felt that their favorites were slighted. But in all his years, the Director had never been told that one of his tournament members was a contract killer. He stood in his office, facing down a clearly upset Seymour Almasy. The office was sparsely furnished, but cluttered, as papers of all sorts were strewn practically everywhere. Obviously, he hadn’t been expecting company. Even more obviously, Seymour didn’t give a damn about the cleanliness of the office. “Not only do I expect you to believe it,” Almasy said, firmly, “but I expect you to kick MacGregor out of this tournament and replace him with one of the play-ins.” The Director shook his head, and sighed. There was absolutely no proof, beyond the most circumstantial evidence, of Seymour’s claim. If Almasy was right, however, the potential consequences and negative publicity could be disastrous for PTC. But there was no way that a witch hunt could be permitted. Archibald had to be accepted at face value, for his story seemed to check out. “I can’t do that, Seymour,” the Director stated, “and you know that I can’t. You have no solid evidence for your story. It certainly is an interesting theory, and I will keep it in mind, but your scheduled first round match will go on as planned.” He winced, as Seymour’s eyes narrowed. Obviously, the Tournament Director didn’t know what it was like to stare down the barrel of a gun. Seymour’s run-in with a crazed fan before his Global Title loss to the Latch Key Kid assured that he never wanted to experience such a thing ever again. “I’m sick of this,” he thundered, voice raising for the first time in the conversation. “I’m tired of being mistreated. Even IF you’re right and Archibald’s not a murderer, what’s with putting me in the hardest bracket of the tournament? If I didn’t know better, I’d say you’re TRYING to make me lose!” Anger was feeding anger. Almost seamlessly, Seymour had slipped to the other object of his ire. Once again, the Tournament Director looked at the co-Wrestler of the Year in disbelief. “Seymour, you KNOW that all matches are selected at random by the randomizer!” This conversation was going downhill, and fast. “Oh yeah,” Seymour asked, sarcastically, “then where IS this randomizer I hear so much about. No one but you has ever seen it!” “It’s over there,” the Director responded. “Under the sheet.” Almasy’s eyes followed the director’s pointing finger, until he spotted the sheet, hiding what appeared to be a truly enormous object. Even in anger, Seymour understood the value of this moment. Finally, he would know what had determined his destiny for so many tournaments, the God-like device that the Director swore by. Yanking off the sheet, Seymour couldn’t help but feel sort of…underwhelmed. “THIS is the all-powerful Randomizer?!” he asked, in complete and utter disbelief. “It looks like a gigantic bingo cage!” “It IS a gigantic bingo cage,” the Tournament Director said, smiling. “Isn’t it great?” Almasy frowned, as he tested the device, practically able to dent the cage by merely touching it. “What is this thing made out of, tinfoil? I bet you ordered this from an infomercial!” “Actually, you’re right. This is the Ronco Electric Randomizer O’ Doom! Perfect for bingo, lotteries, and wrestling tournaments, just like the commercial said. And it only cost me $19.99, well, plus $100 shipping and handling, but they threw in a free Flavor Injector! Let me tell you, with that, I can make the best leg of lamb you’ve ever had in your li—“ “YOU CAN SHOVE YOUR FUCKING LEG OF LAMB UP YOUR ASS FOR ALL I CARE!” The Director looked at Seymour, stunned into silence by the volume and ferocity of the Final Fantasy’s voice. He could only look on in astonishment as Seymour continued his violent diatribe. “I’ve done EVERYTHING you’ve ever asked me to, Wool-Knit! I’ve entered all the tournaments, done all the radio shows and autograph signings, AND THIS IS HOW YOU REPAY ME?! You put me in a bracket with Tessa, Dawn, and Ethan, and then decide for kicks ‘Why gee, let’s give him a POSSIBLE ASSASSIN’ in the first round! THAT’LL BE FUN! IT’LL GET RATINGS! You’re such an ungrateful bastard!” Wool-Knit grimaced, hoping against hope that the onslaught was over. But it wasn’t. Not by a long shot. “Let’s cut the bullshit….I AM PRIMETIME CENTRAL! NO ONE gives a damn about Dave Harley, Jesse Jamester, Chris Storm, Tristan Belmont, or any of the other idiots who have claimed to be PTC’s best over the past two years! I’ve let you use me for everything, for the GTT4 advertising campaign, for the campaign for this tournament, and in exchange YOU FUCK ME UP THE ASS?! Is that any way to treat the man who allows you to put food on your table?” The Director tried to regain control, with a firm “Listen for one sec—“, but he was immediately and unceremoniously cut off again. “Don’t you get it? Unless you’re going to kick Archibald out of this tournament, or at least shuffle the brackets in a more equitable fashion, I don’t want to hear you speak. You’ve betrayed me, and I hope that you can look at yourself in the mirror when you get home and live with that fact.” Enough was enough. MORE than enough, really. Wool-Knit had faced many bad situations, but this had shaped up to be the worst, by far. Unlike Seymour, the Tournament Director kept his cool, looking at the unhappy superstar. He spoke softly, yet firmly, trying to get his message across into the stubborn Seymour’s head. “Look, I don’t know what’s happened to you over the past year,” he said, still trying to process Seymour’s tirade, “but you have changed, greatly. You’ve become paranoid. I’m not trying to screw you, or anyone else for that matter over. PTC events are conducted in the fairest manner that I can possibly achieve. I’m sorry that you got a bad draw, but there’s nothing I can do about it. If I were to let you influence my job, we may as well not even HAVE tournaments. No one is bigger than PTC, Seymour. Not Storm, not Hughes, not Chryste, and not you. No one is out to get you. It’s all in your head.” Seymour was too far gone to recognize Wool-Knit’s diagnosis as what it was; a pretty damned good one. His mouth twisted into a mocking sneer, as he responded with quiet aggression. “That’s where you’re wrong,” he insisted. “I AM bigger than Primetime Central. I am, beyond a shadow of a doubt, the biggest star PTC has ever, or will ever know. That’s why I’ve achieved success beyond this hell hole. I’m holding two titles in All-Star Championship Wrestling right now. Defending them against greats in this industry that guys like the Latch Key Kid and Emilio Rage could only DREAM of being. I signed a one million dollar contract with New Frontier Wrestling. Seymour Almasy doesn’t need PTC, Wool-Knit. PTC needs Seymour Almasy.” Unwilling to allow the Director a chance at a rebuttal, the Final Fantasy stomped angrily to the door of Wool-Knit’s office, preparing to fire his final salvo. “If you’re not going to help me, then I guess I’m going to have to help myself. Fuck PTC. I don’t need you guys anymore. So, you know that UWF by name on the tournament brackets? Cross that off and put the big FA in its place. The UWF may be my home, but I’m not representing a federation in this hell hole. Not in THIS tournament. Remember GTT4? A free agent won that tournament. And THIS free agent standing before you is going to win THIS tournament. When I do, I will prove that I am better than PTC, once and for all. Have fun advertising THAT!” With a mighty slam, Seymour Almasy closed the door on both the conversation and the Tournament Director’s office. He stomped angrily down the hall, wondering exactly what he was going to tell his wife when he got back down to the car. In his mind, he’d made a righteous stand. Seymour Almasy was representing Seymour Almasy in this tournament. Archibald MacGregor, assassin or not, was but the first speed bump in Seymour’s path. For the sake of his ego and reputation, he could not afford to lose. Whether in the ring, or with a gun to his head, Almasy would yield to no one. THAT was what he would tell Aerith. ---------------------------------------------- (Credit to Dick Vitale for use of “super scintillating sensational”.
