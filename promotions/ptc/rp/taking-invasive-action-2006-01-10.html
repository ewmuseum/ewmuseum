Taking Invasive Action

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 ~<>~<>~<>~ Outside… They gathered in the grip of expectation, wiping runny noses on shirt sleeves and shuffling foot to foot as the minutes continued to tick away. Voices rose and fell, swirling together like the dozen different brands of cologne that hung in the air to create one giant bastardized mess of adrenaline and Axe body spray. Small tents had been erected against the wall of the Great Arena, sheltering some of the more dedicated few who had been waiting on this day for months. A veritable village of nylon homes that slammed shut with zippers separated the zealots from the mom and pop crowd; one side of the fence chanted for blood while the other covered their children’s ears with gloved hands, throwing scowls at the older crowd that did little to quell their drunken enthusiasm. Such was normally the scene at your average PTC meet-and-greet, but today was something special. Everyone had one thing in common: Jeremy Hicks held half a dozen carefully hand written posters close to his chest, waiting to catch a glimpse of his boyhood idol. Allison Driscole had been in line for fifteen hours just to get her tits autographed. Gwen Kingsley waited on tipped toes for the chance to flash her favorite superstar. Benjamin and Tracey Lowell cuddled together against the chill, hovering over the tickets they had won in a contest for a local alternative radio station. Dan Henderson hoisted his seven-year-old son Danny Jr. on one shoulder so he could see over the crowd. John Doe felt his wallet to make sure the photo money was still there, quietly sulking over the fact that his parents had actually named him John Doe. Frank Jacobs fondled the pistol tucked away in his coat pocket, sweating profusely despite the cold. They all had one thing in common: They were here to see Rich "The Renegade" Rollins. ~<>~<>~<>~ Inside… "If you think I’m jumpin’ in the ring with that thing, you’re out of your fuckin’ mind." Folding his arms with a distinct finality, Rich Rollins shot the stiff sitting opposite him a rigid scowl as he leaned back into a cushion of expensive leather, driving his point home with each click of the chair’s reclining mechanism. Rapping his fingers across his desktop in annoyance, Tom Bellmont tried to mask his growing frustration but was failing miserably. As the local Primetime Central Events Coordinator (PTCEC for those who prefer flashy acronyms) it was Tom Bellmont’s duty to make sure that the talent showed up and that the talent stayed sober long enough to put on a good show. The tournament had been progressing more fluidly than he had originally anticipated, running with fewer no-shows than he and his colleagues had initially estimated. Tom’s job had, up to this point, been easy. Trying to get the PTC Global Champion in the ring with a chimpanzee, however, was another matter altogether. Tom sighed. "The chimp’s a hit with multiple demographics, Rich. Every man, woman and child from fifteen to fifty can’t get enough of the idea of a wrestling monkey, and the whole testicle thing? Pure gold." "He’s a chimpanzee, not a monkey." Rollins shook his head and frowned. "Whatever," Tom replied with an over-exaggerated roll of his eyes. "The point is-…" "The point is I don’t want my nut sack chewed up like a pack of bubblegum by some ape with an agenda," Rollins interjected coldly. "That’s how AIDS got started. For all I know the little fucker’s dirtier than that monkey from Outbreak, and then where would I be? I’m VD free bucko, and I intend to keep it that way." Tom absently began to massage the bridge of his nose as the Renegade smiled in defiant victory. "Would it help if I told you I hired a trained animal specialist to coach you through the finer intricacies of animal wrestling?" Tom finally looked Rollins straight in the eye. "She’s the top of her field." Rollins merely snorted. "Is she now?" He was far from impressed. "She’s wonderful," the desperate Events Coordinator cooed. "I can have her meet with you after the autograph session this afternoon." "Autograph session?" There was a lot of fine print in Rich Rollins’ many Primetime Central contractual obligations, but he had made sure that his time wouldn’t be wasted sitting behind a signing table. He hated meet-and-greets. "Do I look like a shmuck to you? Look pal, if you want somebody to dance like a damned monkey, why don’t you get him to do it?" The Renegade indicated a large Pete the PG Tips Chimp poster hanging on the far wall behind him, his expression grim. Trying not to gnash his teeth, Tom Bellmont put on a painfully fake smile as he leaned over the desk as if to tell some great secret. "So you won’t wrestle the monkey and you won’t sign a few autographs for the kiddies," he said. "Let me put it to you this way, Richard… you’ll do exactly what we ask you to do, because you are our little dancing monkey. That fancy Global title of yours would look just as nice around the waist of, say… The Uber Judge. You follow?" Rollins did follow, but he didn’t like where he was being led. "Was that an official threat from the office, or should I be holding out for a real temper tantrum?" The Renegade let a slow sneer slip across his lips. "I’m not wrestling a fucking gorilla." "Chimpanzee," Tom corrected. "Whatever." Rollins could already tell it was going to be a long day. "I’ll let you mull it over while you attend to your adoring public," the Events Coordinator said as he pulled a black magic marker from a desk drawer, sliding it over to Rollins. "Hope you brought your tap shoes." The desk jockey was trying to act like the big man. Rollins found it amusing. Pulling himself out of the chair, the Renegade swiped the marker off of Tom’s desk as he shook his head sympathetically. "Keep it up, kid. The chimp ain’t the only expert at removin’ a man’s testicles," he warned, then paused with a smile. "You follow?" It was slight, but Rollins could see the man sinking deeper into his own chair, trying to act like a big shot while whimpering down his nose behind an expensive desk and nameplate. Offering the Events Coordinator a mock salute Rich Rollins made his way to the door and the waiting throng of mindless well-wishers on the other side. ~<>~<>~<>~ Outside… "He looks bigger on TV," said the teen in the black hat. "Totally," replied his friend in the red bandanna. "I thought he’d be a lot taller." "It’s like Mel Gibson and shit," Black Hat mused. "Or Sylvester Stallone even. They say that all these big-time celebrities are really fuckin’ short in real life." "Really?" Red Bandanna frowned. "It’s kinda disappointing, if you ask me." "Tell me about it. I thought Seymour Almasy would be bigger in person, too." Frank Jacobs began to breathe heavily in an attempt to drone out the two teen’s senseless banality. The kid in the black hat and his friend in the red bandanna stood in line immediately ahead of Jacobs, running through lists of their favorite matches of all time (Rich Rollins versus Angelo Deville was high on the charts) and reasons why Eva VanLorne was more attractive than Karina Wolfenden (her variety of "passion red" Victoria’s Secret lingerie). The only thing Frank really heard was Rich Rollins’ name. His finger tightened on the trigger of his hidden handgun out of reflex. One month ago Frank Jacobs had been called by Chicago authorities who informed him that his only daughter Jessica had been involved in a serious car accident along the Bishop Ford Expressway. Another vehicle had jumped the median and careened straight into head-on traffic. The drunk driver behind the offending vehicle’s wheel had survived while his daughter had gone into a coma, only to die in his arms one week later. Rich Rollins had not only survived, but had come away without so much as a scratch. It’s always the drunk who walks away, Frank thought bitterly. Just not today. "Dude, there is no way Rollins is getting his nuts bitten off by a fucking monkey," Red Bandanna stated with conviction. "Pete’s a chimp, not a monkey," Black Hat corrected. "Whatever," Red Bandanna shook his head emphatically. "The Renegade is Global Champion, man. Global fucking Champion! Ten bucks says Rollins rapes the ape tomorrow." "You’re such a fuckin’ Rollins mark." Black Hat rolled his eyes. "Another ten bucks says he wins it, but still loses a testicle." Red Bandanna grinned sheepishly. "Deal." Frank Jacobs nearly shot them both in the head. He figured he’d be doing the world a favor. An overwhelming cheer tore Frank out of his sinister musings, forcing him to crane his neck over the assembly. A large commotion had started underneath the autograph awning; fans waved handmade signs, throwing devil horns into the air as Gwen Kingsley propped herself up on her boyfriend’s shoulders and proceeded to flash a grateful crowd. The chant rose up like a Sri Lanka tsunami: "Rollins! Rollins! Rollins! Rollins!" Frank Jacobs suppressed an involuntary shudder. His daughter’s killer had finally arrived. ~<>~<>~<>~ "So really… how did you get inside that Harvard building to win the Global title?" The kid couldn’t have been old enough to drive, but he was still driving Rollins crazy. The Renegade didn’t even look up as he finished signing Allison Driscole’s breasts. "I bribed a security guard," he said, "then beat the shit out of him and took my money back. Next." The crowd swirled and swelled around Rollins’ table like a single solid entity broken into a thousand eager faces, each one of them impatient to break off a piece of the Renegade’s personality to take home and frame on their mantle. It wasn’t so much a job to Rollins as he sat alone behind his table (Michael Stevens and Kimbusa had been relegated to another tent at their own request), at least not in the general sense of the word. It was more in the pulling-out-toenails-and-hair-by-the-roots territory. Over the years Rollins had learned that a fake smile could last only as long as your sense of humor, and when ten thousand rabid wrestling fans kept sticking markers and posters and magazines and collectable cards and replica belts and every other piece of memorabilia that had your own likeness slapped on the front of it and shoved in your face, your humor was all you had. "Hey, Renegade! I’m your biggest fan!" He heard it every day of his life. He didn’t have the energy to tell the kid he didn’t care. "Rollins! I love you!" Ironic considering he’d love to be anywhere else. "You’re gonna get owned by a chimp! Chomp chomp!" His humor was all he had. Too bad the well had run dry about an hour ago. "Okay assholes," he said aloud through teeth clinched tighter than Steven Caldera’s pocketbook, "I’ve got to take a piss. I’ll be back in ten." He had no intention of doing so, but his faithful flock didn’t know the difference anyway. He was still rising from his chair when a sweet alto voice chimed in from behind: "So this is the great Deville slayer, huh? You looked bigger on television." Rollins had a clever innuendo about the size of his member being larger than it appears ready to unleash on this new arrival, but any and all witticism died the moment he turned around. The woman standing before him was only a woman by definition; she was the sort of beautiful that made a man feel invisible by comparison. Long coils of blonde hair fell across her face and shoulders in tightly-woven dreadlocks streaked with honey brown highlights, surrounding a set of forest-green eyes that smiled independently of the perfectly round mouth that smirked just two inches lower. The Renegade regained his bearings quickly, extending a hand to the curvy beauty who he assumed was with the media. "You seem to know me pretty well," he took her hand gently, winking. "You’re not a press whore, are you?" Her laughter quite nearly twinkled in the air between them. "Not quite," she said. "I’m the animal expert that Tom Bellmont hired for you. You got the message, right?" "I might have heard something along those lines," he said with a smile. The woman nodded once as a coy grin tickled the corners of her plush lips. "Good. If you’ve got a few minutes I can run you through the basics of-…" "Animal nature?" Rollins finished. Her giggle was pure gold. "Hey Renegade," called the next fan waiting for his autograph, "can you sign my tee-shirt?" Rollins didn’t even look in the kid’s direction. "Shut the fuck up, I’m busy." He gestured back into the VIP section, catching the woman’s eyes with his own. "I’m sure I can spare a few minutes. What was your name again?" She brushed a few strands of honey hair away from her eyes, offering a look that was completely disarming. "Janet H.," she replied. "But you can call me Jane." Rollins thought about it for a second then shook his head, offering Jane H. his left arm. "I don’t know, Mrs. H. kind of has a ring to it." Frank Jacobs watched them both like a hawk as they sauntered off arm-in-arm. Much like his patience, Frank’s trigger finger was getting tight. ~<>~<>~<>~ Inside… (Out of respect for Ryan-o - or his mother, anyway - I’ll leave this scene out.) … (But they still fucked like rabbits.) ~<>~<>~<>~ Outside (a good time later) … "Okay." Turning to his partner in crime, Black Hat took a sip from his ten dollar Pepsi as he fiddled with his nappy black hat. "Who has the bigger overbite, Pete the PG Tips Chimp or King Kong?" Red Bandanna pursed his lips in serious contemplation. "Well, Pete only bites people. King Kong could rip the testicles off of Paul Bunyan. And he doesn’t have a severe tea addiction. I’d have to go with Kong." "Are you kidding? Pete could bite the balls off of King Kong, no problem." Red Bandanna shook his head. "Yeah, and they’d be the size of fucking watermelons." "So?" Black Hat offered a toothy grin as he took another sip of his overpriced beverage. "Pete could spit out the seeds like a machine gun and take his big hairy ass to town." Frank Jacobs still stood behind the dim-witted duo, and he still wanted to do something horrible. The only thing that kept him from blowing Black Hat’s brain matter into Red Bandanna’s bandanna was the fact that the police probably wouldn’t let him stick around long enough put a hole in Rollins’ head, too. He’d already waited for nine hours against the bitter breath of Mother Nature and he’d be damned if it would have been for nothing more than a one-way trip to San Quentin. He had no intention of going to prison. His daughter was waiting for him elsewhere. ~<>~<>~<>~ For the second time today Rich Rollins sat behind an autograph table, scribbling names and personalized messages on any piece of cloth or cardboard the crowd could scavenge around the Arena. Convincing the Renegade to sign autographs even once was rare. Twice in one day was a God-given miracle. Fortunately for Rollins, God had given him a hot little number known as Mrs. H. She had charged his batteries right back up, and viola, his good humor had returned. He had smiled and winked and woven his way into the hearts and minds of millions of people around the world. It never ceased to amaze him how even as a cocky dickhead they lined up for miles just to be within his aura; what that aura was exactly he could never quite understand. He wrote the sappy little notes and laughed at their sappy jokes, but behind his fake smile and glassy stare he was merely laughing at their sappy little lives. For ten bucks a pop anyone with a piece of paper could take home a memory, signed and sealed by the one and only Rich Rollins. Then, sorry, but next. Kids barely old enough to ride a tricycle clung to their star-struck father’s legs as daddy spilled his guts, gushing forth an endless string of praises that usually came out as one unintelligible word. Rollins had privately come to call this mysterious language "freak speak," although by now he’d have figured himself to be a fluent translator. He wasn’t. But they still kept gushing. Then, sorry, but next. Geeks were a different lot altogether. The Renegade could spot a self-proclaimed wrestling aficionado from four rows back. Their pasty white skin might as well be a neon sign screaming "I live in my parent’s basement and jerk off to bootleg celebrity porn," and the bugged-out look in their beady little eyes always indicated too much time staring at a computer screen. They would come up to his table and have the nerve to tell him what his greatest match was, or who his best opponent had been. They knew more about his own life than he did, apparently. Then, sorry, but next. Profiling his fans had over the years become a hobby of sorts for Rollins. A lanky man with a receding hairline was an old school type who probably had every 80’s era NWA DVD ever released. Kids who dragged their parents along were his bread and butter. Parents who dragged their kids along were just plain creepy. A woman with one of his Era of Anarchy tee-shirts could be either single or attached, but he could still have his way with them with little more effort than a whispered name to the nearest security guard. They cried for his attention, sometimes literally. Then, sorry, but next. He would go so far as to consider himself a professional profiler when it came to the ins and outs of the wrestling public. Even so, when Frank Jacobs stepped up to his table there was something odd about the man that Rich Rollins couldn’t quite place. ~<>~<>~<>~ His palms were clammy. His mouth tasted like sandpaper ought to taste. The alkaline tang of exhilarating fear stuck to the back of his throat. His heart beat hard, his head dizzy from the sudden flow of blood to his brain. Rollins said, "You got somethin’ for me to sign?" but the words were mute to Frank as he reached casually into his coat pocket. He pulled out a GTT5 promotional poster. Rich Rollins’ smug sneer graced the center, marred with a hasty fold-up crease through his PTC Global title. Rollins took the poster and flattened it out in front of him, wielding the magic marker like a magic wand. "Who do you want me to make it out to?" Frank swallowed a mouthful of acid. "To your biggest fan," he almost choked. "My daughter, Jessica." "To Jessica, the best fan a Renegade could ever hope for," Rollins dictated, trying to keep his insincerity in check. "Yours truly, Rich ‘The Renegade’ Rollins." Lifting the poster to hand it back to Frank, Rollins winked. "I wish she could have been here." Despite his best efforts, Frank couldn’t keep his eyes from welling up. "So do I," he whispered. The tiny pistol’s barrel cast a dull glint as Frank Jacobs tore it from its hiding place, trying to be calm and precise while moving faster than Rollins’ could react. The Renegade caught only a glimpse of the weapon before he was staring at it nose-to-barrel. It wasn’t the first time a man had pulled a gun on the Renegade. It was however the first time that time itself didn’t slow to a crawl as he waited for the inevitable bang; everything kicked into hyper drive as the gunshot exploded across a packed courtyard, and before he knew what had happened Rollins found himself lying underneath his upturned signing table, listening to the panic that had suddenly engulfed the crowd. "Stop that fucker!" somebody roared. A burley security guard pushed the table off of Rollins’ legs. "… he all right? Somebody call an ambulance!" cried a concerned onlooker. His ears wouldn’t stop ringing. A dull pain began to throb across Rollins’ right shoulder. "Where did he go? What was he wearing?" Police were trying to calm and question the nearest witnesses, accomplishing neither. The guard flashed a penlight in Rollins’ eyes. "Rich Rollins has been shot, repeat, Rich Rollins has been shot," the guard bellowed into a shoulder-mounted walkie. He tried to move but a new face with a nurse’s nametag told him to lay still. "Suspect is armed and at large," a second police officer informed Dispatch. He couldn’t help it. Rich Rollins tried to stop himself, but his laughter couldn’t be contained. "He’s in shock," the nurse said as she motioned for the crowd to back off. "Everybody stay back, we need to get him out of here." "No, you don’t." Shrugging off the nurse’s attempt to keep him horizontal, the Renegade winced as he stood and raised a defiant fist in the air. Confusion washed across the face of every onlooker, officer, guard and nurse as Rollins reached for the collar of his Era of Anarchy tee-shirt. Overwhelming applause erupted throughout the courtyard as Rollins tore his shirt away, leaving him clad in nothing more than a tattered pair of blue jeans and a form-fitting Kevlar vest. The nurse’s face was priceless. "My agent thought it’d be a cool gimmick, seeing as how I’m such a marked man in this tournament," Rollins said to her. "I’m definitely sending him a fruit basket." ~<>~<>~<>~ Never get too comfortable. Just when you think you have it made, you’ll always end up on wrong side of Fate’s sense of humor. I knew he was waiting for something the minute he walked up to my table. He had that look - the one that says he hadn’t slept in days and didn’t care - that immediately raised my red flag. Another couple of inches to the left and I might have been raising daises right there in front of ten thousand people. Still I can’t lie; it did give me at least a little charge when he pulled that pea shooter on me. It’s almost a compliment, having someone try and assassinate you. If I ever find the asshole, I’ll make sure and return the favor. Until then? I have a chimp to contend with. Luckily for me, that Kevlar vest wasn’t the only bit of protection I’ve been given in the last week. I got a package in the mail two days ago that held just edge I’ve been looking for against that tea-touting ape. It was a steel-plated groin guard. I’ll be sure to thank Jamie Shock the next time I see him. ~Fin~
