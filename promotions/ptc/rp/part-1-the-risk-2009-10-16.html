Part 1: The Risk

Date: 2009-10-16 In this world, there are rules. In this world, there are also people like Wyatt Connors. People who believe that the rules do not apply to them. For these people, there are still rules. # # # Chicago was a lonely place at night. Far away from the dance clubs and open-all-nite taco stands, a cold wind rushed through the streets. Flakes of snow whipped around in the gusts before coming to land. The only sounds to be heard were the rustling of dried leaves and the steady tap, tap, tap of a man's shoes against the pavement. A solitary figure traversed the sidewalks, his steps even and deliberate. He hunched over in an attempt to protect himself from the inclement weather, and jammed his hands deep into his pockets. A streetlight flickered overhead as he turned sharply to the right, towards a series of storage units. The man continued on until he arrived at a unit whose number had long ago been covered with graffiti. He removed a leather glove from his right hand, then pulled a key out of his pocket. The cold air stung his bare skin, but made no effort to shield his hand as he unlocked the door. Even here, far out of the sight of his enemies, he still could not afford to show weakness. As he lifted the door, it gave out a metallic rumble before settling into place. The man flicked a switch, and the light bulb cast its pallid glow over the space within. In the center of the unit, there were two medium-sized boxes; one labeled "Garbage," the other not labeled at all. Two boxes. All that was required to hold the last ten years in the life of Wyatt Connors. Wyatt stood in the open doorway, as snowflakes melted on the shoulders of his wool overcoat. He took a halting step forward, and found himself unable to take another. Immediately, his self-preservation instincts kicked in. This is stupid, he thought. You know this is stupid. Someone could have followed you--someone is following you. You're going to ruin everything! He laughed bitterly. "Bit late for that," he said aloud. The words tasted like ashes. His better judgment ignored, he approached the boxes. Connors leaned down to open the unmarked one, his thin frame casting a shadow over the contents. Inside the box was a collection of odds and ends that had little, if any, relation to each other. A few of them in particular caught Wyatt's attention. A DVD case labeled "National Wrestling Council Presents: The Best of Across Enemy Lines." A playing card--the ace of diamonds, spefically. A blue necktie. A business card with a phone number scrawled on it. An unmarked CD in a thin jewel case. God only knew if it still worked; they said these units were climate-controlled, but that didn't mean anything. Not that it mattered; just having it in his possession was bad enough. Down at the bottom of the box, there was a folder with a few pieces of paper poking out. The first item that Wyatt reached for was none of these, actually. Instead, it was an unmarked black binder, held closed with a snap. He unfastened and opened the binder, taking a good look at the first page within. It was a picture of three men: Wyatt Connors, standing between a pair of fresh-faced young wrestlers with awful hair and ridiculous smiles. Written underneath were the words, "Crash & Burn, Future World Champions," and dated September of 1998. He casually flipped through the rest of the album, which didn't take long--there was only one picture on each page, and there weren't that many pages. Each shot featured Connors with people who fell into one of four categories. He had pictures of Clients (other wrestlers whom he represented as an agent,) Lackeys (people like Dale and Ratigan, on those occasions when he employed protection,) Victims (people he targeted with underhanded tactics, whether they knew it or not,) and Associates (Desade, Katsidy, Hunter Sabuani, and other members of the group that shut down the NWC.) He did not have any pictures of Friends. Wyatt set the album aside, and turned toward the other box. He glanced quickly at the box's label before opening it, and his lips curled into a tiny smirk. Even in the feeble light of the storage unit, the contents glistened back at him. When a wrestler loses a championship, it is common practice for a federation to give him (or her) a replica title belt for their own personal use. Some people hang theirs on a wall; others put theirs in a trophy case. Wyatt Connors keeps his...here. Connors rummages around in the box, shifting the replica belts to one side. His latest championships were not represented; he has not yet received a replica for the Elite Title, and the SCCW Livewire belt was left behind at the Decency Compound. For all he knew, someone had taken and sold it. That was fine by Connors; the fewer reminders of his time in Ibrahim Seck's thrall, the better. That still left five title belts, representing eight years. Most of them belonged to companies forgotten to the mists of time; all of them were won under questionable circumstances. One championship--the heavyweight title of Phoenix Underground Wrestling--he held twice without ever actually winning, and categorically refused to even touch. The belts themselves really didn't mean much to him; he wasn't always sure why he hadn't discarded them already. There was one that was different, though, and it was that belt that Wyatt Connors lifted out of the box. The World Heavyweight Title of the National Wrestling Council. Fifteen pounds of gold and leather. At the time, the most valuable and sought-after prize in the industry. And for one month, it belonged to one of the worst wrestlers that had ever set foot in the ring. Wyatt held the title belt in his hands, remembering all that had come with it. Not his reign as champion; that was short and mostly forgettable. His victory opened up a whole world of opportunity for him. It caught the attention of the real power structure within the NWC. He met the right people, and made connections. From there, the formation of C-S-P Limited and the death of the NWC. Money. Power. The chance to pull off one of the greatest cons ever seen. And it was all thanks to this championship belt. He wished he'd never won the damn thing. # # # December 3, 2001. It had been three months since Wyatt Connors had been forcibly moved from New York to Phoenix, thanks to the people in charge of Phoenix Underground--previously Hell's Kitchen--Wrestling. Fortunately, his acquisition of the World Heavyweight Title had come with another perk: a free trip back to the Big Apple, which was to be the site of his first defense. As soon as the match had been announced, Wyatt Connors made his usual arrangements whenever visiting another territory. The first thing he did was to rent out the largest available office space at the venue, so that he had a place to conduct his business. It wasn't the most impressive working area, and nothing was especially expensive...but everything was nice. And besides, it beat the hell out of working from a supply room in the rattrap arena where PUW held its shows. Wyatt arrived to the arena early on that particular morning, successfully fending off the weather and the heavy traffic. As he walked down the hall, he shifted his briefaces from right hand to left, giving him easier access to his office key. He made a direct line for his office door, which bore the temporary nameplate of Wyatt X. Connors, but pulled up short. The door was open. His first instinct was to make a hasty retreat, but he knew that there was no point. Instead, he slowly approached the office entrance. His nostrils caught a trace of perfume--Chanel...not No. 5...Coco Mademoiselle?--leading him to believe that either his client Luke Robinson had finally come out of the closet, or a woman had broken into his office. Wyatt slowly inched toward the door, briefcase close to his chest, in case he needed to block a sudden attack. Instead of violence, however, his approach was greeted by a soft, sultry voice. "Why don't you come in, handsome? It's your office, after all." The jig, as they say, was up. Connors pushed the door open, and was greeted by the most amazing pair of legs he'd ever seen, resting in his office chair. His eyes traced her form, from smooth thigh to curvy hip, to heaving breasts, to pouting lips, to hazel eyes. Kathryn Shaw was impressed. Most men never made it that far. "Good morning, Mister Connors," she purred. "I don't believe we've met." "Not directly, Miss Shaw," he answered. "But I'm familiar with your...body of work." She smiled broadly, and Wyatt could quite easily tell how she got one of her nicknames. 'The Kat That Ate the Canary.' "Why, thank you," she said, uncrossing her legs and taking her damn sweet time in crossing them again. Wyatt's heart raced, but it had nothing to do with her legs. Well...maybe a little. Mostly, it was because he knew better than to think that anyone would approach him without an ulterior motive, let alone Kathryn Shaw. She had a reputation, aside from the one most people expected. "I just came to congratulate you on becoming the new champion. Hunter's not an easy man to keep down...at least, not in the ring." The flattery sent up red flags in Wyatt's head. "We fell fifteen feet, and I landed on him. Not much to congratulate." He was sure she'd see through the modesty--he didn't even believe it himself--but he needed a better feel of what she was after. "Oh, don't sell yourself short, honey," she cooed, leaning forward over Wyatt's desk to better display her cleavage. "Hunter is one of the best to ever set foot in the ring, and Heaven & Hell was his match. He'd never lost one before. But you got in his head, and you beat him...you made him beat himself, actually. We're all very impressed." There it was. "We?" "Right to business, aren't you? Too bad." She pouted a little, before straightening up in the chair. Connors was slightly grateful for that; any more and he would have had to loosen his tie. "I'm here as part of an...'organization.' We're looking to fill out the ranks. After your performance last week, we thought of you. The truth is, we thought about sending an even bigger fish than me to talk to you, but the boss can be a little...intimidating." Connors nodded slightly. He was certainly intrigued by the idea, and Shaw had been known to gravitate toward the power players. He had little doubt of what she could legitimately offer. "This 'organization' of yours. Does it have a purpose?" "We've decided that the Council is not best served by those currently in charge. We need someone new at the helm; someone with a bold vision." "Someone like you?" Connors asked, although he expected the answer to be more complicated than that. He was right. "Not quite like me, although I will be involved. We have a power structure in place; all we'll need to do is gain control." Wyatt thought for a moment. Certain things didn't add up. Shaw had once been attached to the man he just defeated for the World Title. Could this be some kind of elaborate revenge plot?" "Our mutual friend Mr. Sabuani wouldn't be involved, would he?" "No...I'm afraid my dear Hunter and I haven't spoken much as of late. I dearly hope that the bridge will be mended someday, but for now, we must move forward without him." She shook her head, sadly. He was not convinced. "So who does call the shots, then?" Kathryn smiled, and put a finger up against her ruby lips. "That's a secret, handsome. Priviledged information. First, we need to know if you're in. You'll have to prove something to us." "I expect the same in return." Kathryn seemingly took no notice of his own demand, but launched into her pitch. "You're familiar with Ben Van I., correct?" She did not wait for his answer, because that answer was obvious. Everyone knew that name. "Rumor has it that he's planning something big. Bigger than anyone has ever tried. Do you think you can find out what that is for us?" Wyatt mulled over the prospects. Ben Van I. was probably the biggest name in the business--multiple World Championship reigns, member of the Uppermost Echelon, and rumored to have his fingers in a whole lot of dirty little pies. Wyatt had been meaning to ingratiate himself to the man for some time, but getting notice was a little difficult when you worked for the laughingstock of the Council. "Do you expect me to walk up and ask?" Shaw laughed. "No, silly. I expect you to do what you do best. You're an intelligent, resourceful man. I'm sure you'll think of something." "Very well. Suppose--I'm not accepting just yet, mind you--suppose I accept your offer. Be your mole, join your organization. What's in it for me?" Instead of answering, Kathryn Shaw stood up from the chair. Her heels clicked against the tile floor as he approached him. Part of him wanted to back away, but something told him that would be a mistake. Stand your ground. Never show weakness. When she arrived at the door, she pressed her body tightly against his, and with a whisper like a serpent's hiss, she said... "Anything you want." # # # She was right. He'd gotten all that he wanted, and more. True, it came with a heavy price...but he'd been willing to pay, at the time. Now, eight years later, he fully learned just how much his choice had cost. He threw the NWC World Championship--once the most prestigious title in the sport--back in the box marked "garbage." Before Wyatt Connors left the storage unit, he grabbed a few items out of the other box. He knew it was a catastrophic mistake, but he felt it didn't matter. Not after he'd broken one of his most important rules. "Never Look Back."
