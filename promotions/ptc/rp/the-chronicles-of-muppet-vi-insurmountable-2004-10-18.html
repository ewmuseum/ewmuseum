The Chronicles of Muppet VI - Insurmountable

18 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-18 The Muppet Man awoke, a little surprised and confused at first, to a sharp pain in his neck. He instinctively raised a hand to rub it, soothing the pain a little, and looked around. Everything was as he remembered it, before he had drifted off. The carriage was void of any activity, despite the considerable number of people, and would have been completely silent were it not for the soft rattle of the vehicle moving. It was aggravatingly dark, with the occasional beam of light streaking past, letting him catch a slight glimpse of the rolling countryside. Trains. The Muppet Man couldn't stand them. Maybe it was the jerky, unsmooth motion, not allowing you to sleep when you wanted to, but waiting for you to get so tired that you had no choice. Maybe it was the total lack of privacy. Maybe it was the agonisingly long journeys, with little or nothing to do, and no one to keep you company apart from your own mind. It didn't really matter. This was the first time, and not the last, that he would drift off that night. Once again, he was shattered. It had certainly been a long week. He really disliked living like this... working so hard that he fell asleep at his desk, while travelling, et cetera, and then doing the same the very next day, but he really had no choice in the matter. This was the life he had chosen, and there was no way he was going to give it up, whatever he had to do. But, things were definitely not getting any easier. In fact, they were just getting started. In the past week, he had successfully defended his United States title against the WWA's longest running wrestler, Shaman, and advanced through round 3 of the GTT with an impressive victory over Horazon. Both matches had taken an unprecedented amount out of him, but they had done another thing, too. They had convinced him. If there was any doubt in his mind that this was how he wanted things, then they had dispelled it. Just as he was rebuilding his shattered confidence, though... the news was broken to him. Dr. Curiosity. Suddenly, the task ahead was no longer a mild challenge. It was insurmountable. The Muppet Man had never faced anyone like Dr. Curiosity before. This was the man on everyone's lips, the one that every wrestling fan was talking about. He was famed for previous wrestling tournament experience, where he had impressed greatly, most notably in the UTT. Victories over George English, Jade and Alex Grey only helped cement his place as one of the solid tournament favourites, alongside such names as Seymour Almasy and Angelo Deville. This was, by far and without a doubt, the toughest test the Muppet Man had ever had to face. And what have I done? Muppet attempted to let himself sink into the seat with little success, posing the question to himself. Sure, he was the WWA US champion, and he had advanced to round 4 of possibly the most prestigious tournament in the history of wrestling at a very taxing point in his life. Well, that's what he kept telling himself. When had it not been like this? When had he not had to fight, tooth and nail, for everything in his life? When would it change, when would he have a 'normal' life? Never. As soon as the word was spoken inside his mind, he knew it was true. The truth was, this happened to everyone. He wasn't special. He didn't have any sort of deep emotional scarring, like so many people do in today's society. He wasn't any different to anyone else, yet he kept trying to convince himself that he was. How could he have thought so? He was a wrestler. He was just one of millions, each trying to find some sort of purpose to their lives. And yet, he kept this false hope, this blind belief that everything would turn out ok. I'm pathetic. The unspoken words echoed through his brain, dragging him down, deeper down into that pit he had worked so hard at escaping, so many times in his life. Looking at his opponent, the 'Good Doctor', showed the Muppet Man above all else that he was truly nothing. This man had a past like no other, that had haunted him for his whole life. And now, now that it was coming back to haunt him in the flesh, he continued to fight through all of the hardships, and prevail. However loose his morals were, this was a strong man. This was a man that Muppet had to go through. This was the next obstacle on the mountain. I. Am. So. Fucked. --- "What do you mean it's gone?" The Muppet Man was livid, almost shouting at a seemingly normal passer by on the street. The man himself didn't look too pleased, and kept pacing up and down, his brow furrowed. "It was stolen, there was nothing I could do," he replied, still angry at himself. He spoke the truth, though. There was nothing he could have done. "I leave my championship with you for two minutes and you lose it?!" "It wasn't my fault, ok!" "Ok, ok..." Muppet trailed off. He flung out his fist in frustration, driving it hard into a lampost, which didn't move an inch. He let out a small yelp, beginning to blow on his fist. Normally, the man would have laughed, but this was a little too serious. "Who did it?" Muppet continued. "I... I don't know. It was a bunch of guys in suits, they looked pretty rich, and they were driving a gold car. Not gold plated, or anything, just painted gold, but Jesus did that car look flashy." "A gold car, eh?" Muppet said, a touch of a smile appearing at the corner of his mouth. "This shouldn't be too hard. There must be about ten gold cars in the damn country..." He trailed off again, following the gold car that was just passing by with his eyes. His expression dropped, slowly but surely, and his hand slapped against his forehead. The so-far-unnamed man couldn't help but chuckle a little. "This... may be a little harder than I thought. Damn it, I've got a match tomorrow... I should be training!" Muppet half-shouted. "Well, good luck then," the man said, quickly turning and beginning to walk away. It was a few seconds before he realised he wasn't actually going anywhere, and turned to see Muppet holding his collar from behind. "Don't think you can escape that easily. It wasn't really your fault, but you deserve more blame than anyone else, so you're coming with me," Muppet said, to a groan from the man. "Aw... do I have to?" "Yes!" "Fine, fine..." the man muttered, feeling dejected, and a little scared, to be honest. "By the way... I'm Mike." "Nice to meet you, Mike," Muppet said, shaking his hand lightly. He looked ahead, up the road, staring up at the endless stream of cars. "And so, my tricky enemies, we enter... end game!" "What the hell are you talking about?" Mike asked, confused. Muppet was silent for a couple of seconds. "I have no idea, my dear Mike, I have no idea." "You've only known me for about four minutes! What is with you?" The Muppet Man ignored him, sizing up the task ahead. From where he stood, and with what he knew, it looked insurmountable. It was going to be tough, no doubt, but he had to do it. And I will, he thought with a grin. I will. ---- I WILL! With a start, the Muppet Man woke up again, almost leaping directly up to his feet, before steadily calming himself. Flashbacks of his time in Washington DC were not the ideal thing to soothe his mind in anticipation for his big return to Muppet Manor, and his even bigger match with Dr. Curiosity in the fourth round of the GTT. His time there had not been well spent. He had left his home, Muppet Manor, and his best friend and manager, King Muppet, in order to find 'answers'. Now that he looked back on it, he wasn't even quite sure what 'answers' he had been looking for. If it was simply whether he wanted to dedicate the rest of his young life to wrestling, then the answer was undoubtedly 'yes'. He was sure, though, that there was something else, some deep personal question on another level. If that really was the case, then he was still unsure. But he didn't mind. He now had enough to carry on. The Muppet Man could continue on his journey for glory in the wrestling world, a journey so many embark on, but so few ever finish. Dr. Curiosity himself had not yet finished this very same journey, but he was looking to. This time, it might just well be his for the taking. This time, he might just fulfil the dream of every other wrestler on the face of the planet. But not if the Muppet Man had anything to do with it. Again, trying to vainly sink back into his seat, Muppet closed his eyes and let the soft rattle of the train almost soothe him. He had no more complaints. He was going to make the most of his time on the way back. He had a lot of preparation to do. Maybe this belief would help him overcome the toughest test he had ever faced. Maybe this belief would be the final, ultimate, deciding factor. Maybe he could finally accomplish something... after waiting for so long. Heh. Not likely. ...But I can always give it a shot. ---- The Muppet Man walked down the street, his head held high, and the WWA United States title draped over his shoulder, glowing with pride. Beside him was Mike, the poor guy who had been dragged into this whole thing when the Muppet Man had asked him to hold his title for a minute or two. Muppet was walking with a slight limp, but he didn't seem to notice. Either that, or he was a very good actor. "That was incredible!" Mike said. "Yeah, I am pretty good, aren't I..." Muppet replied, acting uncharacteristically full of himself. "No, no! I mean, the way you took a beating for so long, and kept coming back for more! It was... Foley-like!" "Oh, yeah, great, thanks..." Muppet said, a little quieter than before, thoroughly disgruntled. "And then the way you paid them off... real classy," said Mike at the top of his voice, really rubbing it in. "I'm surprised you saw that much from behind that crate in the corner!" Muppet shouted, rounding on his new found friend. His hard expression quickly melted into a grin, though, and they both started laughing. "I'm glad that's over, anyway. It's been a long day." "You can say that again." It had been a long day, and it was getting dark. Muppet scratched the back of his head, realising how tired he was. He had a big match the following night, which of course he would end up winning, but he wasn't to know that at the time, and he was incredibly worried. Well, he always was. That was one of his main failings... among many things. It was time to get to his hotel, and get some sleep. No time to 'find the answers' today. "Well, Mike, it's been nice meeting you, but I must be going." "Yeah, me too. Catch you later." "I hope so. Won't be here too long." With a handshake and a nod, they parted ways, on the same high street where they had met. Muppet made his way back down the street, through the thinning crowd. The US title hung loosely over his shoulder. He was still flaunting it openly, glad to get it back, even if he had dealt with the wrong crowd in the process. He would proceed to wipe those events from his memory, and he didn't particularly feel like recalling them. Suddenly, he felt faint. Putting his hand to his head, he felt himself burning up, and couldn't help but close his eyes as he staggered blindly down the road. He didn't see what was directly in front of him, and accidently barged into an excessively tall man. Luckily, the man ignored it and carried on, and so did the Muppet Man... until he realised something. He felt around on his right shoulder. The belt was gone. Again. "Fuck," he muttered, spinning around. He just about caught a glimpse of the title, racing away on the top of a taxi, where it had fallen from his shoulder. He immediately began to sprint, back down the road, through the crowd. There was enough traffic to slow the taxi significantly, but Muppet was having a good deal of trouble darting to and fro between the mass of people, all rushing to get back to their homes. Realising that this wasn't going to work, the Muppet Man sped off the pavement, running as fast as his weary legs would carry him down the narrow strip between the pavement and the cars. He gained rapidly on the taxi, and a hint of a grin appeared on his face, but just as quickly as that, it began to accelerate. He was two cars away. Thinking fast, he leaped up, running across the top of the car next to him (much to the dismay of the driver) and using it to launch himself for the taxi. It worked. Well, sort of. Muppet grabbed the title on the way down, elated, but that only lasted for a fraction of a second, before his back connected with a horrible crack against the large, rectangular 'taxi' sign on the top. There was instant pain, and he shouted out loud. Most people had already stopped, and were staring at him, mouths agape. Rolling off the taxi, he landed hard on the road, the US title flung out of his hands. He didn't notice. Getting groggily to his feet, the Muppet Man glanced around. He couldn't see the belt anywhere. As he darted between the cars, to much honking and screaming from the occupants, he searched up and down for his precious belt. Finally, he caught a glimpse of gold in the fading sunlight. A glimpse of gold being kicked on the pavement on the other side of the road. A glimpse of gold falling down into a manhole. "Shit!" Sprinting, he dived into a sort of roll across the bonnet of one car, leaping onto the other pavement and staring down into the open manhole. Below, a train was speeding past. So, in a moment of stupidity that would come back to haunt him many times in his dreams, he did what his body told him, willed him to do. He did what any person with more than a split second to think about it would NOT do. He jumped in after it. Smashing down onto the top of the train, the Muppet Man had no chance of ever regaining his footing. The pure impact and shock, coupled with the sheer speed of the vehicle, launched him backwards. He bounced sickeningly off the roof, almost directly on his neck, and proceeded to roll back down the train. In a last ditch effort, Muppet flung out his hand, just as he was launched off the back of the train, grabbing hold of a pipe. He brought his other hand forwards, grabbing hold and keeping it. The G-force was incredible. With no time to contemplate how close he had come to ending his own life, the Muppet Man clawed his way back onto the roof of the train. Finding something to hold onto each step of the way, he began to slowly crawl up the train, hand by hand, inch by inch, carriage by carriage. Finally, the train showed signs of slowing, and he began to relax, stopping to crawl and resting his aching muscles for a few brief seconds. Eventually, the train came to a halt at the stop, and he got to his feet, sprinting down the top of the train. He saw the belt... saw it fall into the crowd below. His eyes widening, Muppet leaped from the top of the trian, landing in the midst of the shocked crowd, and beginning to search for his lost belt. He couldn't find it anywhere, so he decided to make his way to the entrance. He would head off whoever had it there. Reaching the entrance, the Muppet Man waited. It was only a few seconds until the familiar gold came into sight, in the hands of a small boy, no more than 8 or 9 years of age. The boy ran up to Muppet, smiling and laughing, and handed the belt to him. Muppet was so overcome with relief that he lifted the boy up and hugged him. "I can't thank you enough, but, well... thank you." "C... can I have your autograph, s... sir?" the boy asked timidly. "Please, call me Andy," the Muppet Man said, pulling a pen from his jacket and signing the boy's shirt. "You'll beat Horazon, right?" "Well, I don't know..." "You'll beat him. I know you will." As the Muppet Man looked down into that boy's face, his eyes gleaming with hope and expectation, he realised that he could do anything. He was going to beat Horazon. He was going to beat Shaman. Hell... he was going to beat everyone who came in his path. "Yeah," he said with a smile. "Yeah... I will." After they had said goodbye, the Muppet Man left the station, taking a detour into a backroad. He had no idea where he was going, but it was ok. He'd find his way eventually. As he walked along, brimming with pride and expectation of himself, he stopped to look up at the sunset. "Beautiful..." he remarked to himself, before setting off again. At least, he tried to. Alarmed, he found himself unable to move. Looking down, he was stuck fast in rapidly drying cement, slowly sinking deeper. His title draped solemnly over his shoulder, in the glare of the sunset, with no one around, the Muppet Man was helpless. This just wasn't his day. "Uh... somebody... please... help..." ---- The Muppet Man woke up just as the train pulled into the station. Wiping the sleep from his eyes, and pulling his back onto his shoulder, he moved out of the vehicle, a part of the great sea of people. As he made the final leg of his journey home, Muppet considered what lay ahead of him. Not just the return, not just the massive weight that he would be able to drop when he arrived, but what lay further on in the path. That boy had faith in him, knew that he would come out on top, and Muppet was sure he wasn't the only one. If all these people believed in him, then why shouldn't he believe in himself? What did everyone else have that he didn't? Nothing. He wasn't special, but he sure as hell wasn't inferior, and he was going to prove this to Dr. Curiosity... and the whoel world. This time, he would give it everything he had. Really, there was nothing stopping him, other than his own negative thoughts, so powerful had they become over the years. But now that he was past that... He had nothing to lose. Insurmountable? Heh. I don't think so. Rounding the corner, he looked up towards his beloved home... and his face fell. "What the hell..."
