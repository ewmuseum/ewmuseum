[IG 29. Jacob McKail]: Bitter

10 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hanker’s Bar Minneapolis, Minnesota Yesterday Six whiskey’s in, the bartender’s interest was visibly piqued. Jacob McKail eyed him up warily as he shuffled over toward him, unable to wrench his gaze away from the man’s diabolical comb-over. The bartender tried to appear inconspicuous as he walked, over-drying a glass pitcher and then wiping the chipped oak surface in front of him. “So,” the bartender ventured, with a questioning look, “wanna talk about it?” McKail’s fervent scrutinising intensified. “What?” The bartender grinned and shook his head. “Something’s wrong.” He shrugged. “I can tell. It’s part of the job.” McKail half-nodded a response and took a lengthy sip of his whiskey The bartender grinned, quickly coming to the realisation that McKail wasn’t exactly the talkative type. However, it didn’t seem to prevent his unflappable perseverance. “Do you want to talk about it?” McKail rested his whiskey on the bar and simply glared at him for a minute, before supplying a typical abrupt answer. “No.” He directed his attention away from the barman the best he could, only now taking the time to fully absorb the bar’s aesthetical delights, or more accurately, the lack thereof. On the outside looking in, McKail figured the place to be some kind of typical American nineteen-eighties themed watering hole, but upon further inspection he realised that wasn’t the case. By the state of the place, it was quite clear the bar actually was from the nineteen-eighties, it just hadn’t been renovated. McKail raised an impressed eyebrow. It wasn’t the greatest, or the cleanest, bar in the world, but such distinct negligence for moving on with the times seemed to amuse him somewhat. It brought back nostalgic memories that McKail hadn’t thought about for a very long time. It brought the first time he’d ever laid eyes upon Mary, the woman who much later became his wife; it brought back memories of the good times. He finished the last remnants of his whiskey in one gratifying gulp and then simply glared at the emptied glass. He’d been drinking like this for so long now for precisely opposite reason. Now, he wanted to forget such memories; they only served to hold him back and to anger him beyond all comprehension. And the young couple in corner booth didn’t help matters. They were in McKail’s eye-line and he couldn’t help but witness the joy upon their lips, the love in their eyes and reckless abandon in their laughs. He thought about moving, to the far corner of the place, somewhere out of the way. But it was too late for that now. He very much doubted he still had the capacity to walk anymore. He just stared at them bitterly, hoping they were savouring what time they had. Mary… His mind screamed. “Man,” the bartender began anew. McKail adjusted his gaze accordingly, glad of the distraction. “If I were you, I’d be pissed off.” McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. “What the hell are you talking about?” “Clinton Sage,” the bartender told him. McKail remained silent, analysing the man carefully. Is this guy fucking with me, or… “I’m sorry,” he began, absently chuckling to himself, “Name’s Jerry. I’m a big FUSE fan. Watch Killzone every week. I recognised you as soon you came in, man.” A wave of realisation washed over McKail’s face and he nodded, signalling with his hand for Jerry to fill him up for the seventh time. “That Sage, man,” Jerry began, reaching for the half-emptied whiskey bottle, right by his head and poured it. “He’s the biggest prick I ever did see…” McKail took a sip of the fiery liquid and raised both eyebrows in agreement. “…and you live in Minnesota.” “Ha ha,” the bartender sighed, “very funny.” McKail allowed drunken grin. “I thought it was funny.” “But then you’re a drunk,” Jerry responded, “and you’ve been through half a bottle of whiskey already.” Again, McKail couldn’t help but nod in agreement; the man was right and he knew it. “But seriously,” Jerry persevered, “after Revolution, you gotta be pissed!” McKail finished his whiskey and then nudged the empty glass closer toward the bartender. “Now, what cause do I have to be angry…” *** FUSE Revolution The United Centre Chicago, Illinois Saturday 10th February 2007 Nearing the top of the steel cage, Jacob McKail allowed one last gratifying glance at Clinton Sage’s bloodied and battered body, as it lay prone on the mat. The crowd’s cheers were deafening and for the first time in an age McKail smiled. It’d been a month of anger. A month of pain. A month of waiting for this very moment. And then boom, it’s snatched away from him all in the blink of an eye. The culprits? Who else? The Affliction, a newly formed clique of FUSE’s resident asshole’s, banding together to snatch away more air-time in order to boost their already over-inflated ego’s. Of course, the king asshole himself, Clinton Sage, was the figurehead for this little circle of ass clown’s and so what happened next was only to be expected. “McKail!” It was Lance Stevens. One of Sage’s over-eager puppets, vying for his master’s precious attention. McKail recognised the voice as soon as he heard it and when he did turn his attention to its source, he already knew that, even unconscious, Sage was about to fuck him over. Again. “McKail, stop right fucking there!” Looking back, McKail wished he just ignored the lunatics ramblings and concentrated on simply winning the match. He wished he hadn’t allowed that crucial second of distraction and had just pretended he didn‘t hear Stevens sickening Southern drawl. But there was nothing he could do about that now. “If you climb down that cage, McKail,” Stevens began, pointing toward the helpless form of Ethan Frost, “I’ll break his fucking arm! You’d better decide right now whether winning this match is worth this man having his arm broke!” What the fuck? McKail’s mind screamed. They wouldn’t… But, as soon as the thought shot through his head, he noted the sickening intent in each of their eyes and the grins plastered across each of their faces. McKail knew then that these fuckers were not only serious, but they were also enjoying it. “I’m not fucking around, McKail!” Stevens yelled, sharply tugging Frost’s arm upward, causing a ripple of pain to shoot across the blue-haired face. “Get back in the cage or I’ll snap his arm in two!” McKail glanced at the scene unfolding ahead, at Sage’s unconscious form and then back again. What the fuck am I supposed to do now? It was a tough situation to be in. Either give up glory or allow them to break Frost’s arm. He cursed under his breath. What the hell are you doing out here, Frost? Why did you have to stick your damned nose in my business? Perhaps it was a slightly selfish and inappropriate thought to process, given the fact that Frost only came down to the ring to save McKail’s ass, but what else could he think? He was selfish and appropriate most of the time and now wasn’t any different. Was it? Of course it is, McKail reluctantly accepted. Since arriving in FUSE, he’d changed, not considerably, but he had changed nonetheless. Some would say for the better, but he wasn’t entirely sure of that. He did know, however, that letting those bastards break Frost’s arm just didn‘t sit right with him and in the end, there really was no choice to make. Slowly, McKail began to descend the cage. I’ve demolished Sage in the ring tonight, he consoled himself, and I‘ve proved that I'm the better man. The thousands and thousands of people around the world had also seen it and so the evidence was undeniable. McKail allowed the bastards one final glance and noted the evil smirk prominently displayed on each of their faces. McKail wanted wretch. No. McKail wanted to jump over the damn cage and go and snap each of their necks, but perhaps only the former option was possible at this juncture. “Okay,” Stevens began, relinquishing his grip on Ethan Frost's arm and backing away. “I won’t break his arm.” But then Generation Gap remained in place, both of their arms wrapped around their prey like a fat man desperately clutching onto his KFC. Stevens evil grin widened. “But they will!” “What?” McKail questioned outraged. They can’t do this, not now, not after… Then, breaking McKail’s thought process, Travis Reed hauled Frost up into the air by his throat and slammed him to the ground with a sickening thud. “Fuck no!” Then, whilst Jake Reed scaled the guardrail, Travis entangled Frost’s arm deep within the steel chair. “No…” McKail muttered under his breath, his words rendered completely inaudible by the deafening jeers of the audience. Frozen with shock, McKail was rooted to the spot, the thought of scaling the cage and attempting to stop this horror far out of his mind. And then it happened. The moment that will be forever carved into McKail’s memory until the day he died. Jake Reed hurled himself off of the guardrail, planting both feet firmly down upon the cold steel of the chair and, despite the volume of the crowd, the sickening sound of the bone in Frost’s harm being mercilessly snapped in two echoed throughout the arena. The crowd became quiet. This was no longer a show for them. This was no longer entertainment. This was simply a couple of thugs with nothing better to do. Then... CRACK!!!!! Before McKail knew what in the hell happened he found himself falling to the mat, a sharp agony of metal against bone shooting from the back of his head all the way down throughout the rest of his body. As he hit the ground, he was able to glare upward just to see Clinton Sage stood over him, a wide grin etched into his face and a steel chair in hand. Then, everything went dark. *** Hanker’s Bar Minneapolis, Minnesota Yesterday “You didn’t!” The female voice exclaimed, a little too loudly. “I did,” her male companion chimed in, “I put a rock through her bedroom window and her father chased me around the block naked!” McKail sighed, audibly, then took the first sip of his eighth whiskey. It must be the good stuff, he silently considered, noting the distasteful expression spread across Jerry the bartender’s face, because I can’t feel my toes… “What’s the matter with you?” Jerry questioned, probably wondering whether it was a wise decision to give McKail another after all. “They’re young and in love.” “I know,” McKail admitted, bitterly, “don’t it just make you sick?” Jerry shook his head. “It ain’t them making you sick!” McKail ignored the jibe. “I just wish they’d do it elsewhere, is all.” “That so?” Jerry asked, not waiting for an answer. “Sound a little bitter there, chief. Trouble with the missus or somethin’?” “Somethin’ like that,” McKail lied. He figured, a careless lie was easier than the truth. Easier on both them. He just didn’t need the hassle of explaining precisely how his wife died, only for the guy to feel bad for enquiring. Now you’re lying to yourself, McKail! He told himself, almost ashamed. Truth be told, he couldn’t give two shits whether he made the air tense and the conversation awkward. He just didn’t like thinking about that part of his life, much less openly discuss it. Each explanation he was forced to churn out, whenever the inescapable occasion arose, always seemed to tear open the wound fresh and it was getting the point where he just didn’t have it in him to do it anymore. And those two fuckers in the corner where making the ordeal a hell of a lot worse. He’d come here to drink himself into oblivion, but leach loving glance reminded him of Mary and it was grinding him down to the bone. Jerry chuckled to himself like a simpleton who’d just caught two dogs humping right in the street. “Don’t wanna talk about it?” McKail allowed a half-smile and nodded. “Got it in one.” “Man,” Jerry began, “for a drunk you‘re not that talkative, ya know that?” “I guess not.” Then, like a gift from the gods, the jukebox suddenly cranked into life and something Johnny Cash bellowed out of the speakers. McKail allowed a thankful smile. “So,” the bartender prompted, “when will Clinton Sage get the ass whoopin’ of a lifetime, huh?” You don’t know when to shut the fuck up, do you brother? McKail thought to himself, keeping his eyes fixed on his whiskey. Usually, such thoughts quickly transgressed into words shortly after conception, but on this occasion, McKail restrained himself. After all, Jerry was the one providing the alcohol. “Good question,” McKail responded inhaling more whiskey. Jerry shook his head. “And that’s bad answer.” “Don’t I know it!” McKail responded, “That fucker only ever gets in the damned ring when he thinks he can‘t lose.” “Don’t think you’ll be gettin’ a third shot then, huh?” McKail shook his head. “Fucking pussy…” Jerry raised a lone eyebrow. “But didn’t he just win Primetime Central’s Global title? Come on, you gotta admit that’s a pretty prestigious belt.” McKail nodded, reluctantly, in acceptance. “Perhaps.” “But I gotta admit, the level of competition wasn’t up to much,” the bartender declared, suddenly an expert on the subject. “Those other guys were so goddamned useless, my ma could’ve taken ‘em!” What the fuck would you know, dick brain? McKail shot him a false smile and pushed his freshly-emptied glass forward for the ninth time. Jerry chuckled at his own joke and absently poured another finger of fire water for McKail’s reckless consumption. “Might work out for ya, though,” Jerry offered. McKail raised the glass to his mouth. “How?” “Well, he’s a wanted man now,” Jerry explained, “and not just in FUSE. That belt is gonna bring him a whole mess o’ trouble and then when all the smoke has cleared, you could just swoop right on in there for the kill.” “Not my style.” “I didn’t reckon so,” Jerry admitted. But then a wave of realisation washed over the bartender’s face and McKail sighed. “Didn’t I see somewhere there was some kind of uni, uhm, un, er, undi--” “Unification match?” McKail offered. “Yeah,” Jerry responded, pointing at him, “that. Probably would at least get you in the ring with Sage…” “But it ain’t a free for all,” McKail told him, “Participants need to earn the right to enter it.” “So, earn the right,” Jerry replied, “there must be ways.” Of course, McKail’s mind raced, the Infinite Gauntlet. It won’t guarantee me a spot, but at least I’ll be one step closer to getting my hands around that bastards throat! McKail finished his whiskey and desperately tried to make it to is feet with at least an ounce of dignity. Managing to do so, he promptly reached into his pockets for cash, throwing a bunch of scrunched up ten dollar bills on the bar and turning to leave. “Thanks.” The bartender scooped up the cash and promptly processed them. “No problem. Glad I could help. Can’t wait to see that bastard Sage get what’s coming to him!” McKail continued towards the exit, trying to walk as straight a line as possible and failing miserably. “You and me both, brother.” “Oh and,” Jerry began, safely tucking the notes into the cash machine, “and I hope you smooth things over with the missus.” McKail stopped for a second, allowing time for the painful memories of Mary to subside a little. “I’m afraid some things can’t ever be resolved.” He left.
