Act I: The Man Who Failed Heaven

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-15 “Mankind’s lone salvation lies within its cognitive limits. Men may curse and lament their limitations, but if they knew the things we, the faithful, know, they would instead both praise and embrace every one of their mortal restraints. They are a failsafe, and were that removed, humanity would destroy itself upon learning of its hollow destiny: placeholders on a desolate rock, keeping it warm for the return of its true masters. The Cleansing would be unnecessary; we’d see civilization engulfed in purifying flames of its own panic-stricken design, destroyed in an orgy of self-immolation and despair.” -- Bruce Shanahan, sermon dated 9/18/2009 “…The man is an arcane lightning rod, dragging the forces of the abyss with him wherever he goes. Of that I have no doubt anymore. How could I, a man of high learning and so much experience, have not seen him working to bring ruin to this asylum from between the walls of that paltry cell?! Whether he is director for their machinations or simply a modest harbinger I have yet to distinguish, yet he was brought here as our destroyer. These Old Ones…could they be real, made flesh out amidst the stars and constellations? No, no, no…forgive me…even my own mind has been affected by that man and the influences he‘s brought down on my house of fiends and neurotics. But he won‘t win! By God or Satan or whatever may be watching, I will reclaim my institution!… Maury Dawson grunted irritably, switching off the recording as the rant became increasingly tinged with rabid laughter. The others present in the room remained silent as they awaited his further briefing, elaborating on the raving man immortalized forever on the cassette. “Doctor Rourke was chief psychiatrist at Weirsburg Institution for the Criminally Insane, and acted personally as Shanahan’s overseer during the time of his incarceration. He thought he successfully held the maniac in check, delving into his mind and helping to unfurl the tainted fabric within it. He wanted to make a career out of him, the very epitome of a brilliant yet psychotic patient; the man admitted as much to me personally in conversation. Unfortunately for the good doctor, the individual he’d hoped to use as a foundation for decades of research ending up breaking him.” Perusing the specialists assigned to him around the room, Dawson gestured toward the recorder. “What you just heard was the final audio report offered by Rourke on the evening the institution was swallowed by the mouth of Hell, figuratively speaking. The building still stands, but as a burnt-out husk. The power failed, the inmates were freed, and in the ensuing chaos they proceeded to burn the structure and murder the on-duty staff.” “And Bruce was the key to the grid failure, and the epidemic of heightened insanity?” Dawson paused, nodding in the direction of the speaker. He retracted his stern gaze long enough to flash a meager smile, the most a man of authority could allow in their situation. “True. And congratulations on the promotion, major.” Tapping the glittering golden leaf on his lapel, Major Marcus Stryker stood at the forefront of his team on the western side of the briefing room. A humble soldier, he was proud nonetheless of his accolade, as was his squad. They were among the finest field operatives the Blackhelm Coalition counted as loyalists. “We don’t know at what point Shanahan’s medication failed to affect him. Perhaps he had immunity all along, and simply toyed with Rourke and his staff throughout his stay. But everyone in this room has either had sufficient contact with him in the past to know of his capabilities, or has been entrusted with enough biographical information to be nearly equivalent. He has a charisma, a presence…dare I say a tangible power to influence. We, of the Blackhelm Coalition, don’t sway from truth, and as I stand before you here and now, I assure you that Bruce Shanahan singlehandedly caused the strife, turmoil, and paranoia that ripened that asylum for its fate. You are open-minded and capable of believing that fact, and that is why you are part of this organization.” The first to proffer query to the head of the Coalition’s latest task force was a non-descript fellow in the corner, a clipboard in one waving hand. Sporting thick spectacles and arms not much thicker than the rims encompassing the lens’, Dawson guessed that he wouldn’t be of great use in a combat situation. He’d need to get to studying in-depth info on his new people. “What happened to Doctor Rourke, Agent Dawson?” “He vanished. No sightings of the man, nor remains found, in the three years since the incident. He’s assumed dead, probably by the very maniac he sought to study. We don‘t know exactly what happened there that night. There are conflicting stories from the inmates, and most are so deranged we can‘t make heads nor tails of their accounts.” His gaze floated to the far wall and the clock seated above the white eraser board. It was 3 pm. In his head, he issued a curse. “Major Stryker, kindly take over the briefing. I have business that needs attending.” The other merely nodded. There was a small echelon that knew of Maury Dawson’s situation, and the latter trusted the dedicated Stryker with knowledge of his quandary. As he hustled down the hallway, Dawson mulled over the events of the past few years, and all the men and women that had been swept up in the turbulent designs of their cloak-and-dagger war. He knew it would never be fully quelled until every dark follower of every fringe cult was gone. It was a fight that would never end. But at least if Bruce Shanahan and his malefic intentions were finally stopped, there could be a modicum of peace. It would not be peace. But it would spell the beginning for a peace. * * * * He collapsed to one knee, each breath an agony, every second an eternity. In the ring he had long ago ordained as his hallowed church, a final sermon of suffering has been bestowed on the shattered body of his adversary. Rest could finally come soon for the man the wrestling world had christened as their Lucifer. The irony of the familiar comparison often brought a chuckle to his lips; Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan knew there was no Lucifer. He knew what was to come as the microphone lowered from the rafters and into the quivering paws of that jackal of a ring announcer, the insect who had rooted for the Bringer of the Black Gospel to fall as his holy war culminated in this final battle. But could you blame him? With the victory of the man who championed the devils outside the cosmos, his doctrines became reality. There were no boos; the initial silence is what defined that crowning moment. The stillness of the moment was disrupted by the first sobs, a growing wave of despair that engulfed an audience that exploded into desperation. Some attempted to flee, as if to escape this coliseum were to evade the icy throes of death. Shanahan knew it would only delay the inevitable. Eyes closed, Shanahan basked in his triumph, ears strained with warped glee as the announcer fought to hold back his terror and weeping, stammering out the words he‘d prayed the evening prior that he‘d never have to utter. Obviously, someone hadn’t been listening. The first screams would rend the air as the despondent announcer sobbed the final syllable of Shanahan’s name. A crack split the Earth, and from its fiery recesses sprung a series of rattling chains that latched onto a row of panicked onlookers, dragging them screaming into the gaping maw of oblivion. Something took the ring announcer as he moaned his impotent prayers only inches away from the victorious Shanahan, only a splash of blood left to impart the motion he had ever existed. Violence Jack would have been inclined to feel a morsel of pity had the worm not had the gall to attack him at ringside during the contest, his battered fists an insulting attempt by a hundred pound weakling to sway the result. He didn’t watch as chaos reigned. He noticed the television positions hastily abandoned by the retreating network commentators, stationary cameras left without operators and handhelds trampled in the stampede of violence. No more than that. There was only the heat of the spotlight, staring into the glaring luminance as the Earth began the process of returning to its ancient inhabitants. He smiled broadly, imagining the spotlight as the stereotypical lone light bulb in an interrogation room. Before him, mighty and omnipotent and brandishing the badge of universal law, the God he despised glowered down on him, accusing him of haphazardly destroying His Creation. He smiled in retort, confessing. Yes, I orchestrated the demise of all you molded and shaped, you miserly bastard. And you’re next. Beneath his feet the canvas quaked as a surge of people ran for the ring, clutching onto the flapping apron with white-knuckled fists. Standing alone in the center of the light, splattered with the blood of the announcer, he saw their horror as they ran from their faceless pursuers. On the fringe of the darkness, clawed hands emerged and fastened onto their prey, the hopeless heretics that implored the head of the Sect of Black Wisdom to somehow undo what he had wrought. He scoffed at the notion; he had tried to be their savior once before, why should he revert to that thankless role now? Like the announcer, countless of their number had tried to subvert his attempt at the tournament crown. Why spare the meaningless lives of these despicable vermin? One by one, they were pulled away back into the darkness, their frenzied cries drowned in a maddening chorus of hellish cackling and tearing flesh. One held firm to the apron, fingernails digging into the mat as his muscles quivered with the effort. The cult mastermind stayed steadfast, unblinking. Not a muscle in his body shifted as the fingernails, forcefully lodged in the canvas, were ripped from the fingers as the shrieking woman was devoured by whatever lurked in the darkness beyond the cultist‘s vision. The bloody fragments wavered for a moment, like pikes impaled in the ring, the light glistening off of their ruby-red polish. The roof was sheared off, cast into the city like a lid from a tin can; he could discern no visible source, but he had long ago come to know that trusting his sight in Their presence was a mortal flaw. The glow of countless fires reflected against the passing clouds, the mark of a doomed civilization and the burning necropolis to be left in its wake. Sirens rose above the clamor and insanity, the constant clatter of distant gunfire echoing across the valley and into the stadium. Mankind was preparing its final stand, as futile a demonstration as it might be. A great, gaunt shadow loomed skyward, and Shanahan knew only of its presence as everything became meaningless in comparison. Tentacles lashed outward against the night as two great orbs burned into sight to either side. The eyes, His eyes, fell onto His disciple. Shanahan neither wanted nor expected any throne or semblance of domination for his part, for to serve Them was his greatest reward. The Old One was pleased. Wings spreading against the very heavens they would soon gather to topple, He crouched and leaned into the exposed stadium, tentacles squirming as they brushed the canvas…. “Father Shanahan. Father Shanahan?” He awoke from his reverie, a gentle voice prodding him back into consciousness. Were it not Brother Ethan Knight that roused him, his ire might have been a terrible thing. His voice was calm and collected as his eyes fluttered open, pupils readjusting to the faint light of his chamber. One hand rested on his great crystalline shrine he maintained to Azathoth, the great bubbling Chaos Himself. “The faithful await your presence. And our…guest…has arrived.” Shanahan couldn’t suppress a smile at the venomous growl his apostle voiced. Many of the other faithful would be brandishing their blades as he traversed the great halls of Wyatt Manor right this moment. “Then let us go. Let it never be said I‘m not a proper host.” * * * * “Shanahan moves again.” Men cloaked in shadow, the council surrounded the table as they schemed in candlelight. Their intentions were known to very few, and in many cases, not completely to each other. The world of man requires a constant equilibrium, and such a construct is rarely so black-and-white. “We all must suffer for his ascension, for his vengeance to become incarnate. He seeks to destroy the very throne of Heaven, and the creator that sits atop it. His rage expands, it‘s become uncontrollable.” “My sources indicate he seeks to have demons is at his control. Already he commands a host of madmen, a veritable legion of assassins. He’s mustering all of his forces and turning to the most forbidden of formulae to reinforce them. How far do we dare tread in this conflict? What shall be our expenditure, the tax on our resources?” “The balance is most important. It must be maintained, at any cost.” “And what of our alliances? Which shall we exercise?” “Everything. He cannot succeed.” Murmuring. Sighs. A rare collective agreement. “Then it is law, and is shall be conducted. We act on this occasion without restraint. Stop at nothing, short of his death.” A moan, sorrowful. “May God have mercy on us, brothers.” “Amen.” * * * * Maury knew how unwelcome he was. It was only by the bidding of their wicked mastermind that the mindless mob of the Sect of Black Wisdom didn’t bleed him dry as he stood in their mansion, an honored guest. “It’s a gift, my friend. Please. It’ll be essential in your ‘transactions‘. He shed the tissue paper, drawing the item from its box. The paper gently sailed to the floor as Dawson cringed, looking away from the awful relic he’d been seeing in his nightmares for the last three years. “Do you like it, Maury? Surely you remember it?” “How can I forget such a thing?” “So you do? I‘m pleased.” Dawson paused, wishing he wasn’t shaking as he clutched the horrid thing. That wretched mask that veiled Shanahan under the guise of Masquerade. It was a symbol of so many memories, none he cared to revisit. In that featureless face he saw so much death, so much manipulation, so many lies. “I won’t kill for you, you know.” “There will be no need. I’ll have more than enough followers willing to extinguish life in my name. Your tasks will be far more….esoteric, shall we say? Please, humor me for a moment. See how it fits.” A follower of the Sect knelt before them, offering a silver tray. It held two chalices, each filled with wine that lapped at the brim. As his host retrieved two goblets of wine, Dawson did as he was requested. He could almost feel the thing burning into his flesh, cursed after being entombed in this mansion beside other hellish relics taken from long-dead worlds. Shanahan, however, appeared pleased. Maury was sure it was simply an effect of his diabolical personality. “Just charming. Do you know what I see when I look at you, Maury? Do you know what I observe as I take in that rubber mask, and those hate-filled eyes beneath it?” “A man who fell into a pact with the devil?” A gentle shrug, eyes gliding over the featureless mask. “I see a man who has failed his kingdom above. Tell me, my fallen angel Gabriel, what does it feel like to have God cast you from his throne, from his very grace?” Dawson’s lips never parted. He only lifted the goblet before his lips, smirking silently beneath his pallid veil. It mirrored the emotionless demeanor of his oppressor and ‘business partner‘. “I know you will betray me. Maury. Of that I have no doubt. But when? To the Vatican and its myriad lapdogs? That is the game, old friend, and one that intrigues me. Partake in the contest with me, a duel of minds and politics. A new masquerade begins tonight, and it is an evening to celebrate.” He raised his goblet, and in turn, his congregation of madmen lifted their glasses high. Dawson shuddered as he beheld their ilk, the cult of brainwashed maniacs that would seek to destroy their own species. Not even a murmur among their ranks as they awaited the poisonous words forming on his forked tongue. “To the death of this world. And to the new dance, may it please you all, my children. Bless us all, the damned.” A hideous collection of masks appeared among the deranged revelers, veiling the madman’s congregation as they danced into the night. From his balcony above, their master watched the token ceremony as the games began anew. * * * * Father Randal Craven turned from his work, groaning from the tedium. He’d often voiced his displeasure with his assignment, feeling consigned to preach to the converted as opposed to making a difference in a neighborhood in need of the word of the Lord. Comfortable suburbanites that viewed the church as a headquarters for gossip and bake sales were a fickle lot, unlikely to waver in their lifestyles or faith. He turned away, stirring his mind by thumbing through the mail. Beneath the standard array of junk, bill notices, and pleas for divine aid was a solitary, thin package. Unmarked, the outside gave no return address. Before even tearing off the perforated flap, Craven felt a familiar notion of dismay wash over him. It was a featureless rubber mask, pearl white, its solidness laced with yellow slashes. With it came a note, penned in the distinguishable, longhand scrawl of an old friend. A friend he now mourned for on a nearly nightly basis. Randal, The dance begins again. You know the steps well. Just follow the music. And don’t worry. I haven’t forgotten our other old friends. Our reunion will be coming along very, very soon. Sincerely, Bruce Randal Craven reclined in his chair, drawing the blinds shut. Cradled in the darkness, like a babe nestled in the womb, he wept for the days to come.
