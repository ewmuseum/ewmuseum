LIVING IN THE SHADOWS: THE HOYT WILLIAMS STORY (part 1)

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-28 [A slide show of pictures depicting Hoyt Williams growing up are flashed on the screen as the narrator talks. The pictures cover everything from a young skinny kid to the PRIME Universal Champion.] Narrator: I’ve always been fascinated by the spectacle that is wrestling. I grew up an only child living deep with in the fantasy’s my childhood mind produced. Over imaginative kids are drawn to the over the top sport of modern day gladiators dressed in tights and reading soap opera style scripts. The two go hand and hand like Visine and weed. My father is a great documentary maker having created some of the best documentaries in the world. His camera has captured the struggle of the snapping turtle, the fanatics of fantasy football, and his Oscar winning documentary on the rise and fall of Jonathan Brandis the kid from Seaquest and Ladybug who took his own life at 27. Living in the shadows of a legend is tough. [The pictures flashing across the screen are now of Duke Williams, father of Hoyt] Narrator: I know, I’m living proof of a shadow dweller. I guess that’s why for my documentary I decided to cover somebody I can relate too. The son of a legend who is on the brink of world wide exposure and success as he competes for the first time for the PTC Global title arguably the most important title in the wrestling world. Something his father never was invited to compete for. Who is Hoyt Williams? Can he step out of the shadows of his famous father? Will he become a legend at the tender age of 23? MAIN TITLE SCREEN: LIVING IN THE SHADOWS: THE HOYT WILLIAMS STORY by Al L. -->Fade to:TITLE SCREEN 1: HOSS DADDY Narrator: He is a living legend who has appeared on more hours of wrestling shows then anybody in the history of the sport. He is in the ICWA and PCW Hall of Fame and has held world titles in both federations. He spent his ring career as the blue collar hero of the drinking man. He chased Tyler Nelson out of PCW, went toe to toe with Boda and won, and helped spring board Xavior Kannon into superstardom. Despite all his in ring success he may be best known as one of the most popular color commentators in the history of the sport. Working on the mic for both the PCW and OSW called some legendary matches as the voice of the wrestling world. Now he is semi-retired and living in a trailer just outside of Missoula, Montana. To truly understand Hoyt we must first look at where he came from. We must explore the chap they call “Malice Man” [The camera opens up on the lawn of a pee green trailer with a proud American flag flapping in the unseasonably warm September wind. The grass has felt the effects of the dry summer as its lackluster green blades almost seem to beg for a drink like a survivor in the Super Dome after a hurricane. A small plaque under the mail box identifies this as the home of PCW Hall of Fame member Duke Williams who apparently will shot all trespassers. Our narrator walks up to the door with camera in hand and knocks three times on the loud tin screen door. A moment later a large bearded man with an angry disposition opens the door.] Big Al: Hey Duke, I’m Al L. we spoke on the phone last week. I’m here to interview you for your son’s documentary. Duke: Ahhh hell Hoss I thought you said three o’clock, it’s one-thirty you woke me up. Big Al: I’m sure I said one-thirty. Can we do it now? [The Malice Man’s aged broken body is shown to us as the crusty eyed legend is in nothing but boxer shorts. The trailer has a strong smell of alcohol mixed with filth and a hint of cat turds. The culprit of the turds is soon discovered as Duke’s calico cat Guthrie (named after Woody NOT Arlo) jumps up to explore the strange intruder. He lets out a cry of approval as he rubs against the film maker before going back to his cuddle of uninterested.] Duke: Mind if I get dressed I prefer to have a semblance of my character when I talk to you. [The Malice man disappears for a moment as the cat seems to watch the film maker with a look of distrust. Not really an unusual look for a cat, as cats really don’t trust anybody except maybe the can opener and the dryer. Trust is something Duke has always prided himself on. He is a man of his word and when he makes a promise he keeps it. At least a verbal promise. The Malice man returns from the back dressed still in his boxer shorts and shirtless. This time however he is wearing his hat, shade, and smoking a big cigar. He walks over to his well used min-bar and pours himself some WILD Turkey liquor over the rocks. He raises the bottle offering the young film maker a taste of Turkey but Al shakes his head with a “no thanks”. Duke walks over and sits down in his lazy chair raising the recliner.] Duke: Lets get down to brass tacks. Big Al: Your son is on the verge of reaching the top echelon of pro-wrestling. Despite your unarguable success you never peaked that high. You were a consistent star but never a superstar. Why? [A plum of smoke billows upward as Duke scratches his nuts. Perhaps these interviews were a bad idea he had no idea they were going to be this tough. He looks over at the cat for help but Guthrie is too busy licking his asshole almost mockingly as cats tend to do.] Duke: Yea, ahhh I was a cult wrestler and will always be known such. If I was a writer I wouldn’t be in the top ten list of the New York Times bestsellers but I would sell a lot of books. A cult wrestler just like a cult writer finds his audience and they stick with him through thick and thin. They are never the best out there but what they say or write hits dead on with their audience. So no I never made it big and that’s ok with me because I never set out to tell story’s, saga’s, epics, soap opera, or any of that other horseshit. I set out to do one thing and one thing only and that was to make hard working people laugh. Nothing more nothing less and if people didn’t get it FUCK’EM!! If they did then they are in the Hairy Assed Hillbilly club and good Americans. [Duke makes an unapologetic smile as he sits even further back in his chair taking three drags off his Cuban cigar. He sips his whiskey with the same respect a millionaire would sip his thousand dollar wine. Our filmmaker sits on the edge of the sofa ready to drill Duke further.] Big Al: What else held you back? Duke: Drugs, alcohol, and women. This country boy lived the rock and roll life style. I used to live on a house boat on a lake in Arizona. It was a drug boat anything and everything you could want. I passed out after riding the cocaine train and I choked on my puke. Which was better then my third wife’s cooking I have to admit. But anyways, I some how rolled over or somebody rolled me over not sure which. That saved my life but I still slipped into a coma for three days before coming out of it. First thing I did was get high again. All the while I was contending for the ICWA world title. Big Al: Your son’s first memory of you was you snorting coke through a century note. [Duke takes a large drink of alcohol as that question was even less flattering then the first few. He finishes the glass and promptly gets up and pours himself another one. He sits back down this time sitting on the edge of the chair and looking directly at the film maker.] Duke: What do you want me to say? I was a terrible father? I was no God damn good? I fucked up? Is that what you are here to hear? Because fuck it I was all of the above and then some. You got your God damn footage. You heard what you wanted to hear I was a bad dad. But you know what, it was that neglect that made my son what he is today. It was me who straightened him out and took him to wrestling camp where he excelled. Father of the year? Fuck you. Did my son turn out alright? You bet your ass. [The young film maker raises an eyebrow as he leans even closer changing his tone to that of tough journalist. He is not done with Duke yet as he isn’t here for a confession but rather a story in a documentary.] Big Al: Your son believes he is the son of God and the second coming of Christ, is that normal? Duke: I don’t know what the fuck normal is do you? People with rings in their nose and rings in their belly buttons hell is that normal? Fuck if I know! Who I am to judge? Is it normal that most kids in their late twenty’s are still living at home, is that normal? Because my kid has a job and profession he’s fucked up? Fuck you. Big Al: Do you believe he is the son of God? Duke: Listen if I was Joseph, a nice Jewish carpenter and a hard working man and my hot as hell Jewish wife Mary shows up knocked up. You think I would believe her? Fuck no, that bitch would be out in the streets. The angel of God knocked her up, you know how many times I heard that excuse? Bullshit! But it turns out that baby Jesus wasn’t just a bastard but also the son of God. Many didn’t believe it but in the end the dude turned into a hell of a bartender making water into wine and dancing on top of water. That’s soul jive Hoss. Big Al: You still haven’t answered the question do you think Hoyt Williams is the son of God? [Duke uses his hand to comb back his flowing long black hair as he thinks the question over. He takes another tall sip of booze as he lets out a loud sigh. He then takes a puff on the cigar as he watches the smoke rise as it seems to dance as it dissipates into the heavens.] Duke: Like I said who am I to judge. I think he is my son and I will love him no matter what. Big Al: Fair enough. Your son has openly hated you how does that make you feel? Duke: Like a shit in the shit stool. Look Hoss I wasn’t there when he needed me I can’t expect him to just forget about our past and create a loving future. Right now we are working things out and talking. Things are good and I think they will get better. Blood is thicker then water that’s for God damn sure kid. Big Al: I can understand that. Tell me about Hoyt’s mother. Duke: She was a church women with raven black hair and a sexy body she over dressed. A delicate flower waiting to be deflowered by a tall dark stranger man. Big Al: You? [Duke smiles a smile recalling the love and beauty he let get away or took advantage of those 24 years ago. In hindsight all love is magic but in reality it often was far from that.] Duke: Yes me. She had never had alcohol before and I talked her into it and then we made love all night long. I tried to have a relationship with her but she wanted me to change and buy into all that Jesus jive. I’m a man of God I believe in God but I couldn’t give up my ways for him or her. I didn’t know about Hoyt until a few years later and by then I was full blown gone. An addict and an asshole living in El Paso. Big Al: Would it have been different if you had known about him? [Duke ponders the question as he again reclines in his chair and takes a sip of Turkey. He shakes his head as if he doesn’t know the answer to the tough hypothetical question.] Duke: I’d like to say it would have been but I don’t know. I just don’t know. To be honest……probably not. I enjoyed my life back then and I’m not sure anybody or anything could have changed it. There was a drive in me that took me higher then I’ve ever been before both in my career and life. I was a free living man and I didn’t need nor want the responsibility of a child. Big Al: What was it like when you found out? Duke: I didn’t feel very fatherly. When Hoyt would visit I saw it more as a pain in the ass then as a moment to cherish. Look I worked on the road most of the year so when he visited me it was while I was in his hometown of Chicago in a hotel room or a motel room in a distant town he flew to. Getting laid was the top thing on my mind not taking care of a kid. Most of the time I had a hotel room and so did he. He was nine and I was next door being 69ed. [The grin on Duke’s face is clearly satisfaction of the sexual variety. Even in memory the good times still roll. A young man holds on to his wallet and dreams while an old man holds on to his memories. ] Duke: I was a deviant and his mother thought I was Satan. She taught him the bible back and forth while I took him on drug runs with me. I was stupid and I bad dad. I’m lucky his mother was as good of a women as she was. A real women not a whore or a gold digger. Big Al: Did you support them with money? Duke: Most of my wealth was going to my addictions and what I had left kept me traveling. Fucking inflation and you think gas prices are bad. Big Al: You still live that way? Duke: No, I drink but I’m clean outside of that. My son was the alleged leader and when the Chicago Police department finally caught him he called me. I knew the chief of police and I used my star power to get him out. I knew I had to get him out of that environment so I sent him to live and train with wrestling legend Ted DiBiase when he was sixteen years old. You have to know when to let your body rest and when to push it and those two guys helped him learn. Big Al: It didn’t hurt he was raised by Ted in a wrestling environment. Jesus answered, "I tell you the truth, no one can enter the kingdom of God unless he is born of water and the Spirit. Flesh gives birth to flesh, but the Spirit gives birth to spirit. You should not be surprised at my saying, 'You must be born again.' The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound, but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit."** [The filmmaker closes the book and begins walking toward the church. The camera follows a butterfly dancing in the wind in front of the church] Narrator: A butterfly starts out ugly and doesn’t have much hope. He struggles for food crawling on his belly like a serpent symbolic of Satan himself. But once the struggling creature reaches a stage in his life he builds a cocoon a place of rebirth and change. Much like a butterfly Hoyt entered this building in Normal, Mississippi an ugly person struggling to survive. He like the butterfly left flying high and completely changed. [The camera cuts from the butterfly to our filmmaker and a large framed man sitting on a pew outside the back of the church. In the distance a ring is set up with two young wrestlers sparing.] Big Al: Ted DiBiase is under an exclusivity deal with the WWE and could not speak with us. In his place is one of Hoyt’s main trainers and teachers the Reverend Maxwell Masters. Tell us about Hoyt when he arrived here. Maxwell: He did not want to be here and he let us know it. He was a punk kid with a bad attitude and on a road straight to hell. Big Al: Make us understand who he was? Maxwell: Ok he would steal from our wallets in the dressing room. He would swear and disrespect Mrs. DiBiase while she cared for him. He once tried to steal a car to get out of here. I caught him and he took a beating that night like one has never taken before. He was a tough kid but at 180 pounds he wasn’t the monster he is today. I beat him from Genesis to Revelations. That’s when he started listening. Big Al: Is that how you guys do things here? Maxwell: Ted didn’t know and he was a special case. He needed an ass whooping to feel pain. He needed to feel something besides rage and anger. I’m not proud of it but he started to change and when he did look out. Big Al: What do you mean? Maxwell: Most guys take a year before they can step into a ring and put on a good match. Hoyt was ready to go in three months. I mean look, with me as his trainer and Ted it’s hard not to be that good but he had something else. It was like when he put his mind to it he was geared to go. No holding back. Full steam ahead. Big Al: Do you remember when besides the beatings he started to change? [A massive slam is heard as an overweight guy takes a slam in the background. Reverend Masters takes a moment to think about the question before snapping his fingers with excitement.] Maxwell: YES! We did our first show and he came with to help set up the ring. He watched a great fight that night as I put on a hell of a show. Ted did his sermon and we raised a good chunk of money that night to help keep us a float and spreading God’s word. Big Al: Raised money? Maxwell: Yes we spread the word of Jesus but that doesn’t come free. We break even but Hoyt did seem a bit interested in the dollar that night. Remember this is before he was reborn. After that night he took a serious interest in wrestling. He went from not listening to being a sponge wanting to absorb all the info Ted and myself have. And we have a lot to give and I taught him everything he knows. If it wasn’t for a neck injury it might be me you’re doing this documentary on. [Our film maker stops short of rolling his eyes.] Big Al: You are a modest guy. Maxwell: Thank you, it’s a tough gig but God shows me the light. Hoyt is the best the wrestling world has to offer. He learned to fight when he was 180 pounds as a technical wrestler. He then put on the muscle and become a hell of a power fighter. Plus you factor in the street skills he had coming into this thing and you have the total package. At 23 years of age I think he is the most dominate and well trained fighter in the business. It’s really amazing. Big Al: Do you think he is the son of God? Maxwell: Are you kidding me there is only one Savior and that is my lord and savior Jesus Christ. Hoyt is a guy with God given skills. I’ll even go as far and say I think Devine intervention played a role in where Hoyt is today and how he got there. But to say he is the son of God is just silly and blasphemy. Big Al: Well he sure thinks he is. Maxwell: No he’s just spreading the word of God. HE is a good kid and I hope he wins hell I know he will win the Global title. This man is on a mission from God and he isn’t going to stop until his mission is finished and the lord calls him home. Big Al: Are you sure it’s God who’s giving him his mission orders? Maxwell: Oh no doubt. Big Al: Tell me about the day Hoyt was reborn. Maxwell: It was a Sunday as we do all our baptisms on a Sunday. We took him down to Porter lake right down the street. It was a cloudy day full of overcast with a threat of rain. I still remember that because it was just so darn gloomy. Anyways Ted was there and administered the words and dunked Hoyt’s head in the water baptizing him for a second time. The wind got real, real, real cold and went everywhere. The wind is the spirits and they were dancing. The sky turned black and it rained hard for about two minutes you know one of those summer downpours. All of a sudden the sky opened up and the sun shinned down almost like it was shining just on Hoyt. It opened up some more and a rainbow stood in front of us. It was picture perfect. Big Al: Was God telling you guys something? Maxwell: God is always sending messages you just need to make sure your phone is charged. Big Al: Thanks for your time. [The credits begin to roll as the narrator speaks.] Narrator: We have now reached the rebirth of Hoyt join me Friday for the ending of this tale leading up to Super Series. We will talk to Hoyt’s fiancée, his friends, and most of all we will have the first ever one on one interview with Hoyt Williams not given by a member of his church. We will investigate his organization the RISP and we will uncover Hoyt Williams and find out what he is doing in the shadows of his famous dad and if he is indeed the son of God.
