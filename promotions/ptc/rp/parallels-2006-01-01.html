Parallels

1 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-01 The Good A typical classroom scene in modern day America. The classroom was quite bright at this hour, the mid-morning sunshine streaming in through the windows overlooking the football pitch. Jocks sat at the back, their iPods cunningly concealed, their minds concentrated on the latest music rather than the history lesson being given by Miss Jenkins. Maybe if she was younger and hotter they would care more. In front of them, their groupies. The girls whose only concerns were how to snare that all important football starter, their minds already turned towards the prom, notes about dresses and purses being passed round even at this early stage of the year. Then we hit the good kids. Those who dress modestly, work hard and get good grades without being overly smart. These are the ones who will go with each other to prom, ‘just as friends’, the plain looking girls happy to be going at all, the guys anticipating a night of watching those they can never have flirt with and make out with the jocks. And finally at the front, we have what are best described as nerds. Yes it’s not PC anymore but how else to describe them. Button down shirts, leather satchels, milk-bottle lenses in their glasses. The ones who keep the pocket protector companies in business. They work hard, they play with themselves and prom isn’t even a concern for them. This room contains all facets of American youth in it, with the possible exception of the emo kids and the stoners. At this time the emos are probably discussing the merits of razorblades over kitchen knives and sewing patches onto their standard issue bags. The stoners, well, they are probably in a haze somewhere. A decidedly average class…or is it. To pigeonhole people like this is always a dangerous exercise. Perhaps Curtis, the hulking linebacker, is in fact a closet homosexual who likes long walks in the country and plays the oboe. Or Janine, the ‘it’ girl, maybe she helps out at an old people’s home every Saturday morning and gives her allowance to charity. Maybe Peter, the geeky guy sitting front centre is in fact a demon soccer player who takes part in amateur porn web casts. These things are all possible…but in this case they aren’t true. In this group, everyone is who they appear to be, their personalities and characteristics matching perfectly with their outward appearance. Except for one person. Katie, one of the good kids. She has more to her than meets the eye. Outwardly she is a good, wholesome, all-American girl. Not stunning but definitely above average in the looks department. No crazy hairstyle, no huge fashion statements, just a nice, normal girl. Personality wise, there don’t seem to be any shocks either. She isn’t rude, answers the odd question and gets on well with most people. This is her by day, in school and when she is at home. But when night falls, she is no longer plain old Katie… The Bad Tuesday at midnight. Most people, from the elderly to the school kids, are asleep, resting up for another big day, one that will happily mark the turn of the week. The outside the Green household, the only sounds are those of the cicadas and the occasional owl. That and the sound of a window squeaking open. As the girl makes her way onto the garage roof, she gives herself one last look in the window. Everything seems in order. Her low rider jeans and stiletto boots aren’t the most sensible attire for clambering about in but we get the sense that this is a minor point, especially considering the off the shoulder top that completes the ensemble. As she moves under the porch light and we get a closer look, more things become apparent. The belly-piercing, the lavish amount of eyeliner, the pouty lips and the straight blonde hair all add up to one gorgeous looking girl…far from the Katie we saw earlier. Talk about double lives. Now the cellphone comes out, nothing flashy, just one that gets the job done. Looks like the number was on speed dial. "Come get me." That’s it, no pleasantries, no small talk, straight to the point. The tone of that though, doesn’t seem like she was talking to a girl. Flirty, with a bit of a tease in it. Must be a guy. Right again. The car that pulls up outside her house is a classic guy car, a black Mustang with tinted windows and non-standard rims. The driver’s window rolls down to reveal the stereotypical jock, right down to his football jacket. Not the kind of person we would expect Katie to socialise with, at least not the Katie we saw this afternoon. "Hey baby, come on, we’re already late." "Are you not going to tell me how I look?" "You already know, now come on." A flicker of annoyance crosses her face but quickly disappears as she gets into the car. The rest of the conversation is lost as the sound system is cranked up to ‘Wake The Neighbours’ level and the car takes off, leaving a scent of burning rubber in the air. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* "Hey give me some of that shit!" The children’s play area had been transformed, the small kids long gone and replaced by slightly older, bigger kids…sorry, young adults. There must be about 20 of them, a pretty even mix of guys and girls, the guys are the jocks we expected, the girls the usual mix of girlfriends and future girlfriends that are always there, with the exception of Katie of course. It is her who made the charming request, grabbing the popper bottle from the guy next to her, the same one who picked her up. He looks pretty out of it at the minute but even in this state, concern is clear on his face. "Baby, just calm down a bit. This is some serious stuff, take it easy." "Jared stop being such a loser." She takes a big hit of it now, leaning back on the grass and enjoying the feeling. Leaning over Jared, she starts getting…involved, so much so that she doesn’t see the approaching figures, their shadows falling over the happy couple, blocking out the faint shafts of light creeping through the trees. "What the hell do you think you’re doing you little whore?!" That quickly breaks the clinch up. Katie turns round with a look of disgust in her eyes, brushing stray hair from her face. Jared’s face conveys shock, nervousness and something else, possibly frustrated libido. "God Jenna, what is it now? In case you forgot, he broke up with you, he’s with me now." "More like you stole him you bitch and now you’re flaunting it in front of me." "Whatever, you’re just jealous you ugly skank. Now, if you don’t mind, we were in the middle of something." With this, Jenna grabs Katie by the hair and pulls her to her feet. Her friend, still nameless, holds her the woman scorned slaps her hard across the face. As Katie falls to the floor, Jared leans over her in a protective motion. By contrast, Jenna gives her a disparaging look before turning on her heel and walking off. "Are you alright?" Pushing Jared away from her, she sits up straight, adjusting her clothes and fixing her hair. She touches her face, the side that was slapped, feeling the heat coming off it, the redness spreading as she does so. A smile spreads across her face, not a happy smile, one of those that speaks of future revenge, of scores being settled, and emphatically so. "I’m fine, just fine." The Ugly The next day, back in school, Katie back to the plain, unassuming girl she was yesterday afternoon, before dark. She sits in class in her usual seat, Jenna sitting near the back of the class, happy that she was able to put the rival in her place. Jared is in another class, thankfully, so the animosity is toned down somewhat. A knock on the classroom door causes the teacher to pause in her account of the life and times of Tennessee Williams, much to the happiness of the class. "Come in." The chatter that had broken out stops instantly with the entrance of the new arrival, his mere presence enough to send a ripple of fear through the class. Surprising in this day and age that there is still respect for his position but there you have it. The Principal, the most intimidating figure in school, steps to the front of the class, steely grey eyes roving over the assembly of humanity in front of him. "Is there a Jenna Hughes here?" He doesn’t need to wait for a verbal response, the swivelling of a sea of eyes toward the back of the room giving him his answer. She is the proverbial rabbit caught in the headlights, a look of shock on her face. What could she have done that would cause this intrusion? Sure her grades had been average but there were countless worse than her. "She’s just there at the back Principal Willis." "Miss Hughes, could you come to my office immediately please?" ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* The interior of the Principal’s office. Austere in every sense of the word, perfect for the events that take place here. Jenna is sitting nervously in a dull brown chair, the Principal towering over her from across he desk. "This is a very serious matter Miss Hughes. I hope you appreciate the gravity of this situation." "I do sir, but I honestly don’t know how that stuff got there. I wouldn’t do anything like that, I’ve never seen that stuff before in my life." The ‘stuff’ in question is sitting in a plastic bag on the desk, almost looking like a bag of sugar at first glance. Obviously it isn’t though, after all, why would that be cause for a summons? "Perhaps you could explain then how this bag came to be inside your locker then? Did some fairies place it there?" She breaks down now, probably quite legitimately. Huge, loud sobs wracking her body, breaking the relative calm in the room. The Principal seems disinterested, he’s seen this routine before. "Very well Miss Hughes, as you seem unwilling to explain to me, perhaps you will be more forthcoming to the police, or your parents?" Hardball. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* As luck would have it, the police car stopped right outside the English block, the teacher stopping of her own accord to have a peek through the blinds, her class following suit. It was rare to see such an occurrence at this school, so naturally it garnered a lot of interest. What was even more shocking, however, was the sight of a Miss Jenna Hughes, the odds-on favourite to win the title of homecoming queen, being unceremoniously bundled into the back of it, make-up a mess, tears streaming down her face. "Uh…that’s enough people, let’s get back to work here." The blinds are snapped shut now, the pupils having no choice but to focus on the lesson, an exercise in futility for most. For one though, it is easy to concentrate now, her goal has been accomplished. It wasn’t easy and it was a pretty big risk but, she had to admit, it was well worth it. As she turned back to her notes, Katie couldn’t help but smile. The Moral An important tale, this one. One that should be taken to heart by everyone, but especially one Jayce Bradley. He is the Jenna, the popular one, who everyone knows and everyone believes in. The masses know what he is capable of, nothing can spoil his perfect world. Or can it? In every walk of life, there is a Katie. A person who seems meek and timid, a hyper mediocrity (thank you Fischerspooner) who poses no threat to the popular favourite. Like Katie, however, these people aren’t as helpless or as submissive as you would first think. Evan Jackson, there’s a Katie. A man who by all accounts was in GTT to make up the numbers, a competitor destined to go out in the first round, his elimination unlikely to cause a ripple on the surface of the tournament. That was wrong though, he managed to stumble through that into certain eradication by Dallas Reeves in round 2. Again, however, the script was torn up, thrown out the window. Which brings us to the present. Now he stands on the precipice of something big, a chance to become someone in this world, a world he was a stranger to up until a few short weeks ago. That nobody knows enough about to him to discuss him, that they focus on Jayce Bradley, serves as a slap in the face, a slight to his position and ability at this time. And we all know what happens when a Katie gets slapped.
