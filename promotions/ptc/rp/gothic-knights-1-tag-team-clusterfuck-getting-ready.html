(Gothic Knights #1) Tag Team Clusterfuck: Getting ready

8 April 2003

Guest Posted: Tue Apr 08, 2003 8:39 am Post subject: (Gothic Knights #1) Tag Team Clusterfuck: Getting ready A dark haired man stands in a cemetery looking at a tombstone as the wind whips his brown locks around. His icey eyes stare hard at the cold granite as the camera pans across, showing the stone. On it is written the name Fitzgerald Bane. The camera zooms back and shows the figure from another angle. The figure is holding a red rose in his hand. He kneels on one knee in front of the tombstone and places the rose at the base of it. The figure is the brother of the deceased, Edward. They had been inseparable in their day, Fritz and Eddie. They had run the indy circuits as the tag team simply known as Bane. That was before the plane crash, before everything fell apart. Eddie reached down and grabbed some of the grass, yanking it out and running it through his hand. He remembered that fateful day... the day his life was forever changed. Bane had been a successful tag team, and they had just successfully defended their titles against a tough team. They were on top of the world. If only he knew then what he knows now. The findings of the FAA were that the plane had been way overdue for maintenance. It shouldn't have been flying, much less flying in a snowstorm. They were on their way to their next card, which was the very next day. They never made it. The twin props both gave out at the same time, a rarity even the most experienced pilots had only heard of. The plane took a nosedive 2 minutes after liftoff, killing Fritz, the Pilot, and another wrestler that had been with them. Eddie's cousin, Tommy Noble was also in the plane. The two of them were the only survivors. They had come out of rehab only mere months ago. It wasn't something that you plan for, but it happened. When they were cleared for action, he and Tommy were quick to try to jump back into the action. However, they never got off the ground. Instead, they went to the Cirque de Soliel, offering their services as acrobats. Eddie didn't like it, but it was work. What he wanted to do was wrestle. However, Tommy had held them back. Tommy had re-injured himself practicing in the gym, putting a damper on their career. Eddie had decided when Fritz died that his career would be linked to Tommy's. If Tommy decided to strike out on his own at some point, Eddie would retire, but for now, he wanted action. However, Tommy didn't. Tommy had whimped out. Fritz would've never done that. Fritz would've worked through the pain. Fritz loved entertaining people. He was a Bane... a member of the flying Banes, who had been the mainstay of a small backwards circus that toured most of the continent of America at one point. Tommy had been thrown in with them when they were young. His father and mother had died in a car accident, and he was left an orphan, with Eddie's father as his only relative willing to take him in. Tommy and Eddie were like brother, but Tommy lacked the drive. Somehow, Eddie would have to instill some of the drive Fritz had into his cousin. But kneeling there brooding wouldn't be the way. Fritz was gone. Eddie had finally accepted that. Now he had to do the hard thing... return the ring without him. He looked out over the cemetery with a hard look. "F*** this," he said. He stood up and walked over to his Mustang GT, climbing in and starting the engine with a growl. His first stop would be to the agent. He had heard that the PTC was holding a Pay-Per View, and they had a tag match open to all comers. He wasn't going to give Tommy a choice in the matter this time. They were going to fight, and they were going to re-establish themselves in the wrestling industry. As he drove down the street of his hometown, he thought about what it would take to get back in the ring. He thought about the pain that they would undoubtedly suffer when push came to shove. Eddie knew he could bare it. What he wasn't so sure of was whether Tommy could. He had to find out. He turned into the business park, rolling to a stop in a parking place and getting out. He walked into the office of his agent, Steve Vicker, who had lined them up with the Cirque de Soliel after Tommy's re-injury. Steve Vicker, a studious looking man with wire-rimmed glasses, looked up as the door opened and smiled. He stood up and walked over, offering his hand to Eddie. "Eddie Bane," he said. "What gives me the pleasure of your company today?" Eddie smiled. He was about to blow this man's mind. "I'm here to ask you to enter us in the PTC Tag Team Clusterfuck." Steve studied Eddie for a moment, to make sure he was not joking, then went back to his desk. "Have a seat, Eddie," he said. Eddie walked over and sat down in the chair, leaning back and watching his agent closely. "I had you a deal lined up with the DWO, and you blew it," said Steve. "Tommy got in the gym and hurt himself again. How do you think you're going to compete in a tag team match? I don't think Tommy's in any shape to do this." "That's for Tommy to decide," said Eddie. "Besides, if Tommy can't do it, then I'll find someone else. I gotta get in there." "Well, they have an open invitation singles clusterfuck too, you want me to enter you in that," asked Steve. Eddie shook his head. Eddie was many things, but he wasn't a singles wrestler. He liked working as a team. He was used to tag team tactics. "No," he said. "It has to be the tag team clusterfuck, and it has to be Tommy and me." Steve sighed and leaned back. "Okay," he said. "I'll do it. You better show up, though. I don't want the same thing to happen again. If you can't do it this time, I suggest you remain in the Cirque de Soliel and forget about wrestling." "Don't worry," said Eddie. "We'll be there. You just sign us up for the match." With that, Eddie stood up, giving a lingering glare at his agent before turning and walking out of the building. That was the easy part. Now things would get much more difficult. He climbed back into his 5.0 and roared down the road. He turned into Tommy’s favorite hangout, the Dark Talon Bar. He pulls into the parking lot and finds Tommy’s black Firebird, parking right beside it. He gets out and walks to the door, nodding at the bouncer, who opens the door and lets him. Tommy and Eddie were well known regulars at this bar, and as a result, were let in without a search. As he walked in, he looked around, nodding to Art, the owner as he passed him by. He checked the bar first, then the dance floor. Not finding Tommy, he went to the back corner booth that was always reserved for them. There was Tommy laying face down on the table with a bottle of beer in his hand. Eddie shook his head. This was not the Tommy he knew. Since the re-injury, Tommy had taken to drinking heavily. Something in him had snapped when they couldn’t perform in a wrestling ring. Tommy had been excited about the prospect of joining the DWO. He was the one that had a big grin when they were offered a spot on the roster. But, it was also he, the very next day who had injured himself while working out in the ring. Eddie didn’t blame him, really, he was just sick of all the drinking. He was going to do something about it in spite of Tommy, and he knew just the thing to get him riled. He walked over and knocked the bottle out of Tommy’s hand, while dragging him out of his chair and slamming him up against the wall. Tommy came to and looked up, an angry look crossing his face. “What’s the big idea, Eddie,” he asked, with a slurred voice. “You want me to kick you’re a$$?” “You’re too drunk to kick anyone’s a$$, Tommy,” said Eddie. “Look at you, you’re pathetic. Get you’re a$$ up, I’m gonna beat you down myself!” He walks over and lifts Tommy up by the scruff of the neck, launching him into the air, where he flew and landed back first into the bar table, breaking it. “Damnit,” said Art, over in the corner of the bar. He reached out and put his hand in front of the bouncers as they rushed to break it up. “Let ‘em fight,” he said. “I think this is one that’s a long time coming.” The bouncers looked from Art to the two fighters, then smiled and returned to their places. Art shook his head and began chuckling. A guy at the bar, who was a regular customer, walked up beside Art. “Ten bucks says Tommy’ll take him,” said the guy. Art’s smile gets bigger. “I’ll take that bet. I think Eddie’s gonna knock some since into his dear ole cousin.” Over in the corner, Tommy is trying to mount an offensive, grabbing a bottle and throwing it at Eddie. Eddie moves aside, letting the bottle smash against the wall. He moves in on Tommy and yanks him up again by the scruff of the neck. “I’m going to enjoy this,” he said. “You’ve been wallowing in your beer for too long. It ends today.” “Why don’t you leave me the hell alone,” said Tommy. “I’m not Fritz. He’s dead! Face it you idiot!” Eddie stopped and glared at Tommy, then smashed his fist into Tommy’s jaw. “Leave him out of this,” he said. “This is between you and me.” He continued to pummel Tommy, until Tommy lay on the floor, exhausted. He then grabbed him by the shirt and dragged him to the door, throwing a wad of Ben Franklins on the bar in front of Art. “If you need more, let me know,” he said, as he dragged Tommy out of the bar. He took Tommy to his Mustang and opened the passenger door, throwing him in. He buckled him in and walked around, getting in and firing up the Detroit V8. As they pulled off, Tommy rolled his head over and looked at Eddie. “Why are you doing this,” asked Tommy. “Why can’t you just leave me to my misery?” “Because we’re going to make an impact, Tommy,” said Eddie. “We’re going to make our dreams come true. We’re going to wrestle at the PTC Pay-Per View.” Tommy sat up, grabbing his jaw. “I’m not in shape to wrestle, Eddie,” he said. “You know that.” “What I know is that you were cleared for action last week, which means we can go for it,” said Eddie. “That’s why we’re going to the gym. We have to get you back in shape.” “I don’t want to,” said Tommy. “Just leave me alone.” “Oh, I’ll leave you alone alright,” said Eddie. “I’ll leave you alone just long enough to sleep this off, then we’re going to start training. I’m not letting you quit, Tommy. You’ve never been a quitter, and I’m not going to let you start now.” “F*** you, you’re not my daddy,” said Tommy. “No, cause if I were, you would’ve gotten a worse beating than the one I just laid on you,” said Eddie. He turned into their apartment complex and rolled into his parking place. “Come on, you’re getting some sleep, then we’re hitting the gym. And Tommy, if I find you at Art’s any time in the next week, your back will be the least of your worries.” “Whatever,” says Tommy, as he gets out and staggers to the door. Eddie smiles to himself. Tommy was talking tough, but deep down, Eddie had hit a chord. Starting tomorrow, Tommy would get a new lease on life, and so would Eddie. The camera fades to black as Eddie gets out of the car and walks into the apartment, closing the door behind him.
