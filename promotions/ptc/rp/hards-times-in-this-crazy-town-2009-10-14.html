Hards Times In This Crazy Town

14 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-14 “I said I’d git it dun. I’m good for it ain’t I? Den stop bustin mah balls, a-ready! You’ll git da money…just got dis thing I gotta do, naw-wha-I-mean?” Repeating. Echoing. Joe Balboa slowly woke from a world of darkness; the alarm in the form of his phone conversation from earlier in broken record form. Bright fluorescent lights roasted him from above. His eyes winced as they grew alert, his arm coming up to cover them from their intensity. Sight was followed by sound, the volume growing increasingly louder as a smattering of voices chanted in bloodthirsty unison. “BRAWLER!! BRAWLER!! BRAWLER!! BRAWLER!!!!” His face scowled as he tried to shake off the cobwebs, his body in a drunken stupor that only sleep could afford. “Sumbody tell those guys to shut da fuck up…,” he lightly groaned as he rolled to his stomach, planting his meaty hands into what appeared to be a gray canvas. As his vision cleared, he noticed unmistakable stains on the canvas. Crimson. Blood. His blood. He knew it was his because droplets of it freshly stained the mat. Joe palmed his face, sliding it down and giving his palm a hard look. His hand was drenched in his life force, the punctuation to his hypothesis. Joe knew this situation now; adding up all the factors around him and realizing rest was nowhere in sight. Instinct kicked in. Nitrous coursed through his veins as he rose from all fours to his feet, being met with the thunderous roar of a crowd. He was in Floyd Carter Gymnasium, still the rundown hotbed of pro wrestling after 6 decades; its familiar stench of dried sweat still in the air. His stagger rivaled the decrepit walk of Terry Funk as he reached out behind him. His fingers comforted as they wrapped around another familiarity; steel cords wrapped in cheap electric tape. Joe was home. Joe was in a wrestling ring. “BRAWLER!!! BRAWLER!!! BRAWLER!!!! BRAWLER!!!” The crowd was frenzied, on their feet as they roared with ferocious might. The ropes struggled to hold his 6’6, 327 pound frame as he leaned deeply into them for support. It was not until then that he noticed the carnage before him. Blood spatters dotting the ring. A table broken into jagged fragments in the corner. A bent steel chair on the edge of the ring. The seat bent in with the unmistakable dent of a human skull. And most noticeably, the laid out body of Japanese deathmatch legend, Gunner “Beast” Gojoh. The monstrous Asian was laid face down in a mattress of thumbtacks, lifeless. The referee (who probably couldn’t spell “puberty” let alone hit it) was checking Gojoh carefully, noticeably shocked by the visual. He took notice of the risen Joe Balboa and cautiously moved to him, taking both hands to raise his arm for what seemed to be an apparent victory as the crowd came alive once more. “Wha…wha happened hea,” Joe asked in disbelief, wiping blood from his eyes with his free hand. “Y-y-y-y-ou did, sir,” sheepishly replied the referee as he went back to check on Gojoh. If he would be returning to Yokohama in a body bag was anyone’s guess at this point. Joe never claimed to be a smart man, but it didn’t take CSI to add this equation up. His dumbfounded, almost innocent stake in the situation started to melt away. Confusion was replaced by a rugged smirk dripping with more swagger than he dripped blood. He knew good and well what happened even if he couldn’t remember HOW it happened. By the numbers…. One, the amount of times Gojoh and The Back Alley Brawler had squared off. Two-thousand, the amount estimated in the building to see that clash in the gullies of Yonkers, New York. Nine, the number of concussions Brawler had in his career counting this night. It always felt the same. A Rip Van Winkle slumber, followed by child-like confusion, followed by a realization that even with no recollection or control of his actions….he could destroy anyone in his path. “YOU’RE THE FUCKIN BEST BRAWLER! YOU FUCKED HIM UP GOOD!” “NO FUCKIN WITH BRAWLER!” “BRAWLER IS GOD!!” Random voices in a flood of people, reminding him that the Joe Balboa capable of feeling and conscious thought was a brief illusion. He was a barbarian of the Bronx, built for one purpose and at 46 years of age; a worn relic still closed-fist punching his way through life. Embrace it. A look at his blood soaked fists provided closure, using the ropes to assist him to turn around so he could acknowledge the side of the crowd behind him. They know who you are. So come to grips with it…. You are the Back Alley Brawler. He pointed to himself with his thumb, smugly asking, “I did didn’t I? I FAWKED ‘EM UP GOOD RIGHT?!” The response was academic. “BRAWLER!! BRAWLER!!! BRAWLER!!” Their adoration of his barbarism made the chaos he left easier to cope with, a number of referees coming out to aid in carrying away Gojoh from the battlefield. If a part of him was able to feel remorse for the situation anymore, a single sentence banished it away. Would he have spared the Brawler? Hundreds of rhetorical answers were being pulled from the skin of Gojoh. His opponent would hear no chorus of adoration and would not have the distinction of being able to leave under his own power. Chances are, he wouldn’t wake up to the victory that Brawler now enjoyed. And Brawler was perfectly okay with that. .-.-.-.- “Fuck. Dey wasn’t dis much blood when Carmen was born. Got damn.” He had scrubbed and wiped for going on 20 minutes now. Most wrestlers were afforded the luxury of showers and antiseptic. At Brawler’s level, he was thankful for a sink bath and Wild Turkey as makeshift peroxide. Stripped down to his boxers, he looked in the mirror and took notice of his hulking frame, battered from over 20 years of ring wars. Each scar a story with new tales told in some armory, flea market, or gym every weekend. The toilet tissue was no real substitute for a wash cloth, using warm water to wash away tissue debris and blood remnants. The sink’s porcelain was now tattooed with his bloodstains. “Guess I fuck up whateva I come in contact wit. Geez….,” he quietly expelled, reaching for another sliver of toilet paper. All he felt was the roll. Brawler threw out a fiery right that ripped through the air in annoyed fury. “FUCKIN PLACE! LEAST DEY COULD KEEP SUM GOT DAMN TOILET PAYPA!! UGH!” Brawler threw his hands up in defeat, sliding up his tattered Dickies and fastening them. Bare-chested, he exited the bathroom and back into the hallway leading to the ring. Now that he “cleaned up” it was time to get paid and get home. Ass-whippings don’t come cheap is what he’d say on the subject. Brawler did his best to stay upright, the pain trying to overcome him and stop him from any forward progress. Eventually, he made it back to the makeshift locker room. Bottles of free water from earlier and chairs cluttered the area. Only a young talent by the name of Livewire was left, lacing his Chuck Taylor’s up in prep to go home. He matched the stereotype of the scrawny, spot monkey trying to be the next Jeff Hardy; but was a genuinely nice kid with a raging passion for the ring. “Nice shoes youngsta,” Brawler bellowed, lifting up his pants leg to reveal a pair himself. Livewire smiled and replied, “Heh, why the black and blue old-timer?” “To match da bruises I leave on muhfuckas.” “Haha, makes sense. Awesome match by the way man. Fucking SICK.” “Wish I could say I rememba’d it. They all kinda just run ta’getha afta a while. I liked ya match too, even tho’ I was neva one for all dat flippy shit.” “Thanks! Rent-A-Hero was my favorite back in the day. I kind of pattern my stuff after him. Used to watch him all the time when Primetime Championship was airing.” Brawler shook his head before having a seat next to him on the bench, patting his back like he had pity on a cancer patient. Livewire brushed a long strand of bleach-blonde hair from his eye, giving an inquisitive look. “Rent-A-Hero?! Fa real? Guy was a fuckin’ joke in spandex. His matches was only good about half da’ time and he was only good for a catchphrase or two. Bidness is betta without the fuckin’ halfwit. What da fuck is he doin deez days anyway?” “Rehab. Huge crack addict. Heard he used to wrestle indies for 100 bucks and a gram at his worst.” “Heh, figures. Anyway, you seen da promota? Trying to get mah enevelope, bro.” “Dude, he’s paid everyone before the main event. Said he’d get you guys after ya’ll came back and got cleaned up.” A sinking feeling came over the Brawler. One he was all too familiar with, but it stung a hornet’s sting every time. Not a feeling you get used to. Livewire look dumbfounded as he asked, ”Something wrong? He did get at you right?” “I’m sure I jus’ missed him, bro. Thanksfor da’ heads up. Listen….ya got poten….poten…pa-ten….” “Potential?” Brawler was never the strongest at vocabulary. “I WUZ BOUT TA’ SAY IT! FA FUCK’S SAKE.” Livewire sat back, scolded like a young child interrupting an elder as Brawler continued, rising up from the bench. “As I was sayin’…ya got skills kid. But skills don’t count for a fuckin’ thing if ya eva stepped across from me. Winnin’ is winnin’, flip or no flip. Lata days.” The youngster had no real idea how to take that, lacing his shoes back up at the cryptic advice Brawler gave. Never one for a straight-forward compliment or advice, that was Brawler’s way of saying there’s ways of winning without high-spots. Of course, that sounded too nice…and nice wasn’t the Bronx native’s style. Besides, a little veteran flak keeps the youth of the business respectful. However, his mind was preoccupied with a bigger issue. Brawler, a veteran of being fucked out of pay before, knew all the signs related to the act he was experiencing. Promotor gets scarce. He has to run to “buy cups” after intermission. He’ll mail you a check. Excuse after excuse, but none of them leading to pay promised for the pain endured. Veins bulged and fire coursed through him as he exited the locker room, Brawler trying to think clearly as to what his next move would be and how much time he had before he’d begin a city-wide manhunt for his money. “Dat fat fuck. He thinks I ain’t bout to collect, but I’ma get dat money…:” Years of loan sharking caused a light bulb to flicker in his head. Brawler knew his next step, walking intently towards the double doors marked “EXIT” at the end of the hall. Halfway down, he backtracked after noticing his lack of shirt, poking his head into the locker room and yelling. “AY, WIRE! RUN YA FUCKIN’ SHIRT! I GOTTA LOOK DECENT FA’ MAH PUBLIC.” .-.-.-.-.-.- “Come on, you need to hurry up with daddy. I’ll take you to McDonalds when we get out of here, okay?” Chester Johns. Fat. Unsavory. Welch. Johns had a notorious reputation for underpaying talent and skimping off the top for “benefit” events were nobody should have seen a profit. He drove an Escalade while wrestlers had to bum rides or even walk to shows if that says anything. He’d been accused of many a cardinal sin as a promoter and booker, but even if he underpaid, he never skipped on paying wrestlers. Until today. Gojoh was rushed to the hospital, so he’d have no forum to argue about pay. As for the Brawler, he’d simply dodge him by paying all the lower-end talent before his match. It was a masterstroke. Only one hitch kept him from riding off into the sunset…a 6 year old son that had to go potty. Johns may have been an eyesore to the wrestling business in the New York area, but he tried his best to be a worthy father to his little boy, David. Granted, the little one enjoyed some nice Christmas and birthday presents on behalf of the Unpaid Wrestlers of New York fund. This year, his Christmas looked even better with a $6000 gate after expenses and screw jobs, tucked away in his father’s trench coat pocket. “Dad, why are we moving so fast,” he asked, being almost dragged along towards the Escalade in the ghost town of the parking lot. The chilly breeze combed his red mop-top, the father and son finally at the passenger side door. “Stop asking so many questions, PLEASE. We just gotta go, okay? I’m sorry we’re rushing.” “Okay dad. The show was awesome! Your best yet.” The door was opened, Chester helping to fasten David in the seat with a smile. David didn’t know anything about the backstage politics, underhanded tactics, and general grime that came with the business. At least in his eyes, he was a superhero. The seatbelt clicked into place, Chester planting a kiss on David’s forehead. “Thanks son. I can promise you, Christmas is going to be the best this year. We’ll get you a costume tomorrow, okay? Spider-man right? “YEA! You’re the best!” Chester’s heart was warmed. David’s smile made all the knives he firmly planted in people’s back worth it. He closed the door, preparing to round his way to his side… I say preparing, because at that moment a meaty right hand that dropped legends like Hammer Harrison and El Demios III barreled into Chester’s face. The force knocked him back into his car window, making a rough thud and cracking under the pressure. Disoriented. Writhing in pain. He lifted his head and found the owner of the fist, a raging Back Alley Brawler. Chest could hear his child screaming from inside the Escalade. “Da…Dav…” WHAMP~! A left hook drew blood and loosened a tooth, Chester falling back into the Escalade and slumping down slowly. Brawler snatched him up by the collar to keep him from falling. “Nah muhfcuak, you’s standin’ up fa dis. Look at me when I’m talkin’ to ya, bitch!” A short headbutt followed, the ugly sound of skull on skull contact echoing in the cold air. “STOP IT! STOP HURTING MY DAD,” exclaimed David after opening the door, frantically trying to take his seatbelt off. Brawler pulled Chester’s ear to him, menacingly whispering, “Tell him to stay where he’s at. I only wanna hurt you…but if I gots to…” “DAVID, STAY IN THE CAR. Don’t come out! Please…stay put.” Brawler smiled. David stop with trying to unfasten the seatbelt, merely looking back at the sight of his father being held hostage in a helpless state. This monster of a man, that he just witnessed destroy another man for sport, now had a hold of his father. Fear understandably covered the young boy. “Kid…what’s ya name?” Silence. The sound of cars rushing by the building and the cold wind were the soundtrack of the evening. “Answer him David…please,” pleaded Chester.” “David.” “Well David…I can tell you love ya dad. As ya should. I’m sure he takes good care of ya and life is good, eh?” Silence. “Kid, answerin’ me would be in ya best interests.” He punctuated with a solid gut punch to Chester. His yelp was pained and pitiful. David’s eyes watered up. “YES. YES, MISTER, YES.” “I’d imagine. Well ya fuckhead of a fatha’ keeps you and ya family nice and cushy by skippin’ out on payin’ savory cats like mahself…it ain’t til now dat you seein what me and every one of us wrestla’s wanna do to him.” Disbelief overtook David’s face. How dare this unkept, surly thug try to say that his father was evil. Lies, they had to be. Brawler locked eyes with him, the love and admiration obvious. He looked back to Chester. ‘You’re lucky ya son loves you so much. He thinks I’m da biggest liar in da world right now. And he’s entitled to dat. But right now, I’m entitled bout this paper. So let’s see what means more…ya pride or ya health. I’ma ask ya dis just once before I bring da pain. Where’s mah fuckin scratch?!” “I don’t have it. We barely broke even… I promise I’ll pay you double next sho…” WHAMP~! Brawler’s rage took over, slamming Chester’s head into the back passenger door. The dent rivaled the one on the steel chair from earlier. Chester’s eyes weighed heavy, Brawler holding his heavy frame up. He smiled, looking to David. ‘We’s gonna play a game kid…see…everytime ya dad lies to me bout this money, I’ma try and break a body part. The trick is, Ima’ make YOU pick. If ya dad cares, you’ll neva’ have to make a choice…” “Don’t do this…please…he’s my so..” “SHUTDAFUCKUP~! You had ya chance! I got shit back home that’ll eat me alive if I ain’t got dis bread and you tryin’ to fuckin’ play me. It’s be my head before yours. Now it’s come to dis and you gonna accept what happens next!” David had entered a new circle of hell, tears streaming down his face as he looked on. Brawler’s remorse had left for the evening, a smirk as he nudged Chester’s chin up with his index finger. “Where’s da money?” “I…d-don’t ha..have it.” “David….his left or right arm?” David held his head in his hands, his tears water falling as he shook his head violently. He refused to make a choice. The Bronx scowl came over Brawler’s face as he locked eyes with David, the whites now red with frustration and sadness. “Pick or I’ll break em’ both. Don’t try me youngsta….now choose.” A long hush came over everyone again. Brawler wanted David to choose. David wanted to not have to. Chester just wanted this to be over. The only promise was somebody was getting what they wanted and soon. “-sniffle- Left….” “Say it again…like a FUCKIN MAN. Let dis prick hear you pick an arm! David looked to his father, a silent beg of forgiveness towards him. Chester nodded, having no choice but to understand his predicament. ‘LEFT.” “And so it is…:” The Brawler opened the back passenger door and placed his flabby arm in the open space. Chester squirmed, but it was useless in the grip of a powerhouse like the Brawler. He locked eyes with David, deadly serious in tone and facial expression as he commanded, “ You watch this. WATCH IT.” He did. He watched his father’s arm let out a sickening snap as the door slammed full blast against it. Chester’s scream was banshee-like, emasculated. If his son wasn’t already a crying mess, he would have followed suit as well. Brawler lifted Chester up by the collar, digging his hand into his inside pocket and unearthing a thick wad of hundreds; the $6000 in gate money. The expression on David’s face was a look of horror, surprise, and dejection. “Ain’t got it huh? Sorry fuck. Now if you’ll excuse me, I got life lessons to teach…” He dropped Chest as he landed in a heap on the concrete, nursing his broken arm as Brawler took slow steps to David. Sadness and rage could be seen on the youth’s face. The Bronx Bomber eclipsed him, looking down to him with the wad brandished. “Yous gonna look back at this moment and call me a monsta’. Yous gonna see me in ya nightmares and associate me wit every bad moment in ya life for years to come. I’m okay wit dat. If you EVA turn out to be da shit ya fatha’ is, I’d do da same fuckin thing to you kid. And please believe…if you got issue when ya get olda. Come find me. I’ll be in da Bronx and old fuck-up of a man, but I’d take ya life in a hartbeat if ya fuckin try me. You seem like a smart kid too. So ask ya’self, why is dis money in mah fuckin’ hands if ya dad is such an honest man? Betta yet…why would he have you choose which arm to break instead of comin’ clean? Shit to think on youngsta…” Brawler undid the rubberband, taking his agreed upon $1500 for himself and tossing the rest on the broken heap that was Chester Johns. Money rained down the likes of which strippers would have flocked too. All David could do was sit there, his mind trying to compute issues and images that no child his age should ever have to his eyes resting on Brawler one moment, his father the next. Brawler nodded his head to David, turning his back to the situation as he walked off into the night with what he came for. Brawler was a firm believer that not all lessons in life are kind, they just happen. David Johns would have to take that situation for what it was. David unhooked his seatbelt, joining his broken and bloodied father on the concrete. Before this he would have been quick to hug his father, bask in the security and honesty he provided. All he could do was stand there over him, the pain in Chester’s eyes suggesting an ache beyond his physical. David still loved his dad, but now he wasn’t even sure who he was. He wasn’t sure about a lot of things anymore. Tears finally overcame Chester, karma coming full circle. For all the pain he dealt, he would suffer this humiliation for life. Even worse, his child now knew that all that glitters was not always gold. A chill gripped the Johns family. Colder than any wind they now stood in. .-.-.-.-.-.-. “Ya. I got it handled. Nah, not too much trouble ---- see ya in da mornin’ with the money. ----- When do I NOT get it handled? ---- Heh, yea. Bye.” Brawler hung up the payphone, money in hand as got back inside his rustbucket of a Cutlass Supreme. He knew the drive back to the Bronx would be a cold one, the heat giving way in the car just last week. The walk back to the car brought up second, even third thoughts of what he just did. What he has done. Even if he could feel bad, he knew he’d do worse if it came down to it. Only the hard survive the hard times and sensitivity was a weakness that could be exploited by any. That’s the business and that’s life. If he showed any mercy tonight, be it Gojoh or Johns; the goal would not have been met. Excuses and sympathy didn’t keep the rent paid and lights on. He slammed his car door shut, taking a deep breath and thanking a higher power that he was holding $1500 in his hand. It didn’t matter how he got it or who he went through; bottom line was, he got it. Popping open the glove compartment, he took out an envelope and placed the full amount in it. He licked the inside fold and sealed it shut before laying it on the empty passenger seat next to him. “I’m comin’ home Carmen.” The sun visor came down, a studio picture of a beautiful young girl taped crudely inside. In magic marker at the bottom it read “Carmen Balboa – 7 Years Old – MY LIFE!!”. Everyone has a motivation that makes the risks worth the reward. Every deathmatch, long distance match, and “violent” collection was well worth it if it meant food in her mouth and a smile on her face. She was his only link to humanity and the only thing he felt he ever created that was worth something in the world. There’s nothing he wouldn’t do to protect that. To put it simply, his daughter was the only thing that made him feel like Joe Balboa. Proof that a monster can make a masterpiece when they try. He cranked the car up, lurching to start but finally doing just that as he let it warm up. “Hard Times” by Baby Huey blared soulfully over the radio as he nodded, full agreement with the chorus as he looked to the envelope. “Carmen’s Child Support Money” was written on it. The fact that it was filled meant that he could rest easy….at least until the next time it needed filling. With that, he drove away from Floyd Carter. A promise to keep. ”Havin’ hard times in this crazy town Havin’ hard times, there’s no love to be found Havin’ hard times, in this crazy town Havin’ hard times, there’s no love to be found…” –Baby Huey “Hard Times”
