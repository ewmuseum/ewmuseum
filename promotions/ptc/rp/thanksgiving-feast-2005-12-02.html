Thanksgiving Feast.

2 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-02 He was surrounded, everywhere he looked there were more of them. The feeling wasn’t one he’d experienced before really, at least not that he could ever remember. His chest was tight making it difficult to breathe, and he could feel the cold sweat starting to form on his palms. He shouldn’t be here, something was most definitely wrong, he had to find a way to escape, and soon. Glancing around the room he saw no clearly marked exits, he wasn’t exactly sure where to turn. That didn’t make any sense, he should know this room like the back of his hand, it’s not like he hadn’t been here a hundred times before. "Glad you could make it son." The voice from behind was accompanied by a strong grip on his shoulder. For all the world he wanted to leap out of his skin and scream, but he kept it under control. Couldn’t make a scene, not here in front of all these people. Besides while he was definitely startled by the sound there was something in the grip on his shoulder, something in his father’s voice that calmed him, eased his jittering nerves. If it were anyone else he would’ve called it warmth, but that couldn’t be the case, not from Dad. Slowly he turned, making as much of an effort as he could muster to wipe the look of confusion from his face. Try as he might he couldn’t remember how he’d even gotten here, the last few days were nothing but a blur. The last thing he truly remembered was throwing himself in front of a car. And then he was here, with all these people… It was the smile on his father’s face that shocked him most of all. So full of kindness, that kind of love you only see between family members. He couldn’t recall the last time his father had looked at him like that, if it’d ever happened at all. William Stevens had never been overly fond of his son, a fact that Michael had always been painfully aware of, however right now, in this moment it all seemed different. Like something had changed, again he searched his memory for what it possibly could have been, and came up empty. "Hi, Dad." As father threw his arms around son, he felt his eyes almost begin to well up with tears. This couldn’t be real, his fondest wish come true, his father actually accepted him. For the first time in his life he felt full, filled up with the familial love. Desperately he attempted to regain his composure, focusing on the feel of the wineglass pressing into his back rather than the embrace itself. After a moment the wave of tears past, but the feeling would remain, he was home, he was happy. William turned his son slightly shifting the fully hug into a simple arm around the shoulder. Together the two men looked across the sea of faces in the room, and Michael started to recognize the assembled. Everyone he’d ever known, everyone he’d ever really cared about, they were all here. Alex, his cousin stood in a corner, speaking with Michael’s sister Cindy, and her new husband Jeff. Flirting shamelessly with one of the servants was Curiously Bruce, the minuscule man laying on as much charm as he possibly could to get the guys number. Standing near Bruce, poor cousin Jimmie, who’d been known by many names "The Retarded Sailor", "The Chair Swingin Retard" etc. He was probably the reason Michael had donated so much money to the special Olympics over the years, a good kid who’d never fail to bring a smile to your face. Yeah, they were all here, at least almost. There was one face he missed, she obviously wasn’t in attendance. Although it’s not like he could really blame her, if he’d been in Sheena’s position he wouldn’t have come to an event like this either. Still even without her it felt amazing to be in the company of these people again He’d felt so alone, for so damn long it was like a giant weight was lifted off his chest just to be seeing them all again. And the best part was, no one looked at him and quickly looked away. He wasn’t being shunned, they all knew his dirtiest little secrets and accepted him. For the first time in a long time, Stevens felt safe. "Go on son mingle. We’re all so happy to have you here. We’re going to be eating in about ten minutes, so you’ve got a little time. I’ve to check on a few last minute details. See you at the table." Again Michael couldn’t get past the real emotion in his father’s voice. Besides the simple dislike he’d always shown Michael he’d never been a particularly warm person to begin with. He just had never been the type to really show his emotions, it just wasn’t something he could do. Yet, on today of all days he was more friendly and had more love in his voice than Michael had ever heard before. It was thrilling and just a bit frightening all at the same time. As father moved away, the sense of peace began to fade just a little, and the sweat started to reappear on his palms. He couldn’t do this, he couldn’t talk to them, it just wouldn’t work. He was an evil man and they all knew it. He’d treated all of them poorly, used them and abused them, in fact the only person who might still accept him was Jimmie, and that’s only because the poor kid didn’t know any better. He’d never quite been able to recognize that Stevens had never laughed with him, only at him. He knew in that instant he couldn’t face them. He’d done too much damage to too many here. He didn’t deserve to share their company, didn’t deserve to be around them. He had to go and quickly. He realized that the exit must be directly behind him, more and more he was starting to realize exactly where he was. The sitting room and his parents mansion, and if he just turned around he could get to a hallway and out the front door. It’d be like he’d never been here at all, and he was certain despite what his father had said no one would miss him. And just like that there she was, standing right behind him, waiting for him to turn. Sheena looked stunning dressed in a simple black dress, like nothing he’d ever actually seen her wear before. She’d let her hair go back to it’s natural mousey brown, he’d never realized just how beautiful such a plain color could be on her. She looked so poised, so elegant, for a second he couldn’t tear his eyes away from her. "Hello Mikey." It stabbed through him like a knife to the heart. Her voice was so carefree so loving. He’d heard her voice like this before, in the good times, when he knew she loved him. There was no anger, no resentment. Her eyes showed no traces of sorrow of her loss. She wasn’t here to blame him for what he’d done, she wasn’t here to yell at him, just to make him feel safe. She was here because this is where she belonged, with him, surrounded by family and friends. He started to speak, stumbling over his words. The song of his soaring heart rising up to his lips. More than anything he wanted to tell her he loved her, drop to his knees and beg her forgiveness. Public humiliation in front of all these people be damned, he was going to do it. Except he never got the chance. Putting her arms around his shoulders she stood to her fullest height raising up to the tips of her toes. Quickly she brushed her lips against his own. The sensation was like lightning coursing through his body, sending every muscle twitching. His heart beat faster, he thought for a moment it might burst. In this one exquisite moment he didn’t care, he could die a happy man, surrounded by family, in the arms of the woman he loved more than anything in the world. "Don’t talk, lets just see our friends." The words were whispered coming only seconds after she removed her lips from his. Her cool breath on his skin felt like water to a man dying of thirst baking under a desert sun. She was the fire that burned him, and the ice that cooled his immolating flesh. Wrapping her arm around his, she began walking, silently he followed. And he knew he was home. This was right, and he could face anything. He didn’t need a drink, didn’t even want one. He wanted to capture this moment forever in his mind, so perfect, so golden, like the first rays of sunshine on the first morning of spring. He did belong here after all, in this house, on this day, all was forgiven, the family was together. Life was good. Soon they arrived before Alex, Cindy and Jeff. She pulled away from him only long enough to hug the woman he believed would one day, possibly soon become her sister in law. In the down time Alex embraced him as well, he shook hands with Jeff. "Mike, I can’t believe you made it, we’ve all been so worried about you." It was Cindy who spoke, and he could tell she meant what she said. It’s what family did, they worried about the ones who’d lost their way. He felt like the prodigal son, finally returned home from his long journey of arrogance and pride. "It’s good to see you too sis, I can’t really believe I’m here either." Yet, he’d stopped worrying about that. It didn’t matter how he’d come to be here, only that he was here, the place he belonged. Home. God, it felt good, better than any drunk he’d felt, better than any night with the most gorgeous woman in the world, better than winning any title. He finally understood all those things his father had told him about the importance of the Stevens name for once he felt pride in himself, pride in the family. If they could welcome him with open arms after all he’d done, then certainly that was something to be proud of. The sound of a door opening arrested their attention and all eyes turned to the wide double doors of the dining room as they glided open guided by the hand of Niles the families trusted Lead Butler. His ancient face so lined with love was broken wide by the huge smile on his face. Clearly it was these moments he lived for as well. "Dinner, is served." Slowly all assembled made their way into the dining room. The sights and smells assaulted him immediately upon entering. His stomach rumbled loudly, and his face reddened. Sheena laughed and slapped his arm playful, he tired to remember the last time he’d eaten anything, but had no idea. "Well, certainly sounds like someone’s hungry." It was his mother, and he’d never seen her look so lovely. Too long she’d buried herself under an avalanche of pills finding in them the love her husband never seemed willing to give her. Today, though, her eyes were bright and clear, her face smoothed of the hard worry lines. She smiled sweetly, hugged him around the neck and kissed his cheek. She’d never done that before, it was nice. A gesture of maternal affection that he’d never before experienced. This whole situation was quickly nearing a sensory overloaded. It was almost as if everything was too perfect. The turkey smelled too good, the stuffing just a bit too fluffy looking, the mashed potatoes too smooth and creamy. Leaving his mother they were all seated around the stout old table. Many generations, and many thanksgivings had this table seen, but none as glorious as today’s. Immediately he dismissed any negative thoughts. He couldn’t ruin the situation by worry, he’d simply allow himself to enjoy the moment. He watched as father went to the head of the table still standing before he seated. The old man looked so happy looking out over his assembled extended family. Taking the hands of both people next to him they did the same and soon the whole family held hands heads bowed. For the first time he could remember Michael lowered his head as well, and was truly thankful as grace was spoken over the meal. He was happy. "MICHAEL!" The voice was harsh, abrasive. It came from no where shattering the beautiful peace of the moment. Frantically he looked around searching the room, none appeared to have spoken, and he couldn’t place the voice. Neither did the family have appeared to hear it either. Closing his eyes again he prayed, fast and furiously. He must’ve just been hearing things, a reaction from everything falling into place so well, just nerves, that had to be it. "MICHAEL!" It wasn’t just a shout, it had become an accusation. In that darkly thunderous voice he heard the echo of all his sins. The moment was ruined, and he felt a sick knot twisting his stomach, this wasn’t his home. These people were not his family, they were good, and pure. He was a vile loathsome wretch. This was the message the voice spoke in his name. "No, no no." He whispered the words to himself, but Sheena must have heard him. Clutching his hand she leaned in close to his ear. "What’s wrong Michael?" Her voice had twisted, changed grown ugly. Now a sarcastic question, her tone mocking. Pulling away from her he saw her as she once was, covered in shadow, bubbling horrible laughter on her lips. Around the table they all laughed now, as he looked down and saw himself, naked and cold. Shivering, like some nightmare before the first day of a school. Startled he jumped up from the table as he heard the sound of the doors slamming behind him. He was truly trapped, there could be no escape from their mocking laughter. No where to run, nowhere to hide became desperate sprinted to the door. It wouldn’t open. Frantically he beat against the wood, it stood firm against his assault, not even shuddering with his blows. It was like thrusting your fist against iron. One note of laughter rose up above the rest, as he turned. Father now stood wreathed in flames, his hair wild, eyes lost into a cruel insanity. "Why son, don’t you want to eat?" Gesturing towards the table Michael saw it was all rotten. The decaying bird a center piece for things more gruesome than he could possibly imagine. He felt the muscles in his stomach churn, knew that soon he’d become violently ill. The once heavenly aroma replaced, by the sickeningly sweet smell of rotting flesh. Around the table his family died before his eyes, some of them burning, others simply wilting away. Yet death brought them no peace, they continued to move about, continued to laugh. Sheena tore clumps of hair from her skull, laughing as blood dripped from the small bits of scalp that came with them. "Sit down Mikey, yoooooou belong here." Her voice had taken on a sing songy tone. Like a siren calling him to his ultimate doom. He tried to look away, yet the images would not leave his sight. Again he beat against the door, screamed. "HELP ME OH GOD SOMEONE PLEASE HELP ME!" Feeling himself lifted from his feet, he saw what once must have been Alex behind him. A huge grin on his nearly skeletal face. The huge man tossed Michael like a rag doll. He landed in the center of the table with a crash, rotted food flying all around him. He felt their hands being to tear at him, and always that mocking laughter. The fire that had wreathed his father had now spread, the entire room was ablaze. Their charred sent mingling with the already rich stink of the place. The hair on his head was slowly burning away, each individual hair a torturous pin point of heat against his skull. He knew this was the end, he was going to die, either burned alive or ripped apart by these things that were once his family. A sharp pain pulled his attention down to his feet were his mother had begun to gnaw on his toes. She looked up at him, eye sockets empty a sneering grin filled with rotting teeth splitting her face open far larger than any human could manage. "Tell us what you’re thankful for son, tell us what makes you glad today?" As she spoke her shrill laughter filled the room, dancing above her voice that had become something like nails on a chalk board. The booming voice of his father quickly followed. "Yes Michael, tell us what’s in your heart, we’re all so glad you could join us." He screamed then, begging for help. Begging for someone, anyway to hear his pleas, save him from this orgy of fire, and madness and death. Everything hurt, all the small little pains merging into some great lumbering giant of pure agony that consumed his entire body. His father stood over him, plunged his hand deep into his chest. "I want to see your black heart." "MICHAEL!" The voice again, the one that had started all this. He reached out towards it, his eyes growing dim, he knew the heat from the fire was melting them. "Yes, god, save me, please save me!" *********************************************************** When he awoke, he felt the awkward pain of his twisted arms. They were pulled above his head, handcuffs used to support his weight from something above. He was shivering, it was too cold here, he wasn’t wearing enough. Hunger forced his stomach inward to leaching off itself for sustenance, it would soon begin eating away at his muscle tissue. He’d die if he didn’t get something to eat soon. "Not quite God Michael, I told you, I am merely your guardian angel." This voice spoke quietly now. The same as in his dream, but different. No longer did it come from inside his own skull, but some somewhere beyond the cold light of the single naked bulb that hung before him. In an instant it all came flooding back, the days on in spend going from bar to bar. Throwing himself in front of a car, this man saving him. His guardian angel with the Devil’s wings. He’d escaped once, found his way to another bar, desperate to drown his sorrows. There’d been a cop, then darkness again. And now he was here, looking into the darkness and finding only a pair of eyes that nearly glowed with an ice blue chill. "Wha, why? Where?" "Because you need it Michael, like I said, I’m your guardian Angel. This is your punishment, it’s only what you deserve after all. Your sins are great and varied. By all accounts you’re a monster." He felt the hot tears come now. He just wanted a drink, wanted to crawl up into a cozy little bottle and die. He knew he was wretched, he knew he was evil itself. But no one deserved this, nothing he’d done was bad enough to bring him here. He knew what this creature was, certainly no angel. A demon, his own personal demon. Which would have to make this… "Welcome to hell."
