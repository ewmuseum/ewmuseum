"The second chance…"

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 * * * * * Life works in funny ways, if you ask me. When I entered the ring in the GTT5 qualifier against Clinton Sage, I entered it with the mentality of becoming the new essence of a hero. A good guy, someone that the wrestling fans have been lacking over the last few years. Where there were shades of gray, I would be the brilliant beam of white light. There would be no doubt behind my actions, no dishonest intentions. Rules were rules, and I was going to follow them. So I found it ironic that after defeating the asshole of the wrestling world, Clinton Sage, that I would be forced to face off against two of the more beloved men in the world of wrestling. The first round gave me The Legend of Pete, a man far beyond his prime in the ring and far beyond the last notch in his belt buckle…but he is a man the fans love to see. Unlike the wars of the world, sometimes our sport forces us to square off against someone of the same moral foundation, the same fibers of decency. When I applied the Triangle Choke, Pete tapped. I promptly released it, and helped him to his feet. He knows there is still honor in a loss, and he didn’t berate me like Clinton had. Then came Emilio Rage, a man who more recently has ‘seen the light’ so to speak. Once again, a Triangle Choke cut off the flow of blood to his brain, and cut off his path to the finals. He tapped, and I gave him a helping hand. He stared at me, much like Clinton Sage did, but he never insulted me by asking me the question Clinton did. Then he smiled, and nodded. He understood. A silent acknowledgement of my quest through this tournament, and the intentions behind it. I’ll forever respect Emilio Rage for being the sportsman I only hope we get more of. But the road has suddenly turned darker, more violent. Now I get to face off against one of the most hated men in the business, Angelo Deville. Someone who is on the downward slope of his career, and realizes it. Being eliminated in the first round by his protégé, he’s undoubtedly going to be trying to break and bend every rule known, just so he can get his revenge on Rich Rollins. I smile at the chance to face such men, because much like Clinton Sage, Angelo Deville is the reason why I wear the mask. Why I shun my face from public eye, and let my actions define my image. To be the hero of the day, and not because of what I look like, but because of what I do. Perhaps Angelo Deville is the pair of shimmering green eyes that have haunted my nightmares for the last few months. I knew this day would come, where I would step into the ring with the vilest of opponents. Conquering Deville will only amplify my quest, and as I’ve silently made my way from round to round, the silence will be ripped away in one fail swoop. Angelo Deville will be choked out, and sent packing. And much like Rich Rollins, the weight of defeating such a man will suddenly be cast unto my shoulders. Soon, there will be more questions as to why I wear the mask, and I’ll keep answering them with resounding victories inside the squared circle. They will want to know my identity, and I’ll just keep showing them that it doesn’t matter who I am, but what I do. Now, if only I could gain the confidence I have in the squared circle, and apply it to my life at home. Atop the canvas, where I am always analyzing and trying to be one step ahead of my opponents…at home, I tend to stumble and over-analyze. Especially when it deals with the company of one waitress in particular, Isis Maguire. * * * * * Clinton Sage sat in his leather recliner, the night swallowing the living room in darkness. The moon cast unearthly and lurking shadows all around him, sparse beams of light here and there. A soft orange glow came from the far side, where the fireplace sat. The embers were slowly burning out, giving the room a faint, smoke-laden scent. The night relaxed Clinton, because he knew most of the world was asleep and no longer questioning him. Prying at him, because of what he discovered. The book lay open across his thigh, Clinton staring down at the leathery spine. Even in the darkness, he could see the gold-emblazoned title: Merlin. For Clinton Sage, his pool of calmness never went undisturbed for long. Soon his mind wandered to the book, its potential meaning, and then ultimately to the man who had given the gift, Spartan. Soon the surface of calm was rippled with anger, and it didn’t take long for the ripples to turn into crushing waves. Clinton threw the book across the room in anger, the shadows swallowing it from sight. Running his fingers back through his black hair, he sat seething. Thinking. How could a man hide his identity from the world? What purpose would it serve, especially someone who is supposedly fighting for good, to hide his face? The questions kept tumbling in Clinton’s head, stoking the fires of his anger. He stood from the chair, and walked to the window overlooking his property. Outside, sharp winter gusts whistled through the branches of trees. The American flag flying in his yard was tattering at the edges, the red and white fabric fraying. High in the nighttime sky, Clinton felt like each of the stars were eyes, watching him. The moon also cast a mocking smile, in the form of a crescent. "I’m going to find you," Clinton said aloud. He turned from the window and flipped the switch on the wall, the shadows sent back to their realm by lamplight. Across the room, half open against the far wall, was the book he had tossed. Catching the attention of Clinton, however, was the piece of paper that was resting next to it, at the base of the fireplace. From his distance, it looked small, like a newspaper clipping. As he made his way across the room, Clinton’s superior eyesight picked up the first half of the boldfaced headline printed across folded clipping, and his heart sank a little deeper in his chest: "A killer link: Pennsylvania’s two serial killers…" * * * * * When two people start to like each other, and develop that high-school crush, they both know. With Isis, I knew immediately that I was going to fall for her. It was undeniable, because she had this confidence and charm while she was waitressing at the diner. Her smile, her brown hair and matching brown eyes, or her olive complexion. She isn’t overly gorgeous by any means, but she is cute enough to catch every mans eye. The girl that passes you by and you think she’s cute, but not beautiful, and won’t cause you nerve-wracking misery every time she leaves the house, for fear of other men hitting on her. The girl next door. Inside the squared circle, I can see a move coming from a mile away. But when Isis started to give me her phone number, it took me 3 days to realize what was happening. Then, it took me another 7 days to actually get her phone number. After flirting over breakfast for about a month, she started to put single digits onto my checks. I noticed them immediately, but didn’t make the connection until the third day. When I confronted her about the numbers, she simply smiled and cocked her head to the side, saying "Well I guess you’ll have to keep on coming back to get the rest, now won’t you?" Her brown eyes were always shining, and because of my bad luck with women, I was reluctant to call. It took me a full week after I had collected all the digits to actually call her. Even then, I would chat with Isis on the phone for an hour each night, never asking her out on a date. It was only last week, when we both entered the diner because of separate, stressful nights, that I asked her out. We planned the date for next week, after I return to Denver from the match with Angelo Deville. Whether or not I’m victorious doesn’t matter, because I look at it this way: Angelo Deville isn’t a beautiful woman, nor is he half intimidating as one. This weekend should be easy, whether I win or lose, compared to the date next week. After all, I haven’t been on a date in years. My nerves are already wound tighter than any wrestling hold Deville could possibly put me in. My previous profession sort of shunned relationships, I guess you could say. For years there was a void in my life, and I’m looking to take the step forward and fill that void. Times are changing, indeed. I just hope that once Isis finally knows the shadows of my past, if she accepts them, then I can be truly happy. I guess you could say that she is my ray of hope, because her acceptance of me will ultimately set the tone for the final chapters of my life. It’s the second chance I’ve been looking for. * * * * * The sun sent the dark night running westward, giving way to a brilliant day. The winter might seem like a bleak season to some, but Clinton preferred it. Everything was cold, crisp, and always starkly more vivid than during the heated summer. Winter mornings always looked bluer than a summer’s morning. Perhaps it was the lack of humidity in the air, but it didn’t matter to Clinton. Today would be a joyous day, he thought. With the book in hand, he made his way to his silver Corvette and hopped in. It would be only a 2 hour drive down the highway to Washington, DC – perhaps 90 minutes if he pushed it – and then he would have the fingerprints lifted from the book and the identity of the masked man revealed. A mask might cover his face for now, but technology will rip it away. Clinton had barely made it out of Pennsylvania before his mind started to wander back to the newspaper clipping. He remembered the original printing of the article, because it was published only a day after Harlan Raines had been executed, when Clinton Sage was known across the state of Pennsylvania not for his notorious actions inside the ring, but for being the one man to discover the bodies that Raines had hidden for years. The bodies that the FBI couldn’t find, nor the local detectives or police. The clues Harlan had given him seemed to lead him directly to their final resting place, and ultimately, gave Clinton the fame he had never asked for. The public wanted to know more about Clinton Sage, they wanted to make him into a hero. He gave them no such pleasure. It sickened him to think of such a life. The article centered on the fact that when Clinton had found the bodies Harlan stashed years ago, the link between them all was blatantly obvious: they were all members of a cult that Harlan Raines – and an unknowing Clinton himself – had been members of: The Eyes of Fear. As more details emerged in the hours before Raines was put to death, the pieces of the puzzle began to fall into place. Suddenly, unsolved tragedies over the last 15 years in the states of Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, and New York became linked. Mass murders. Bombings. Bankruptcy claims. You name it, The Eyes of Fear had their fingerprints all over it. The article centered on the fact that two of Pennsylvania’s most notorious serial killers, Harlan Raines and Nathan Hollinger, were linked. As the evidence mounted, it was proven that Nathan Hollinger was innocent of his crimes aboard Flight 1020, where over 140 people lost their lives in 1998. The Eyes of Fear were proven to be behind it, and Hollinger was only a puppet selected to take the fall, much like others in various crimes throughout the surrounding states. When the dust finally settled, it was proven that 10 men sitting on death row needed to be exonerated. Various others serving prison sentences were released within weeks. Thousands hailed Clinton Sage for bringing the truth to the surface, and he hated every minute of it. There are some people in this world that would relish the attention in the public eye, like Clinton had received. It only made him want to put a gun in his mouth, and pull the trigger. Any extra attention on his shoulders set his life into a tailspin. He liked to control everything, he relished it. As the Maryland countryside sped by in a blur of farms and trees, Clinton smiled to himself. He quickly glanced down at the book, and the newspaper clipping that sat atop it. Had Spartan placed it there, knowing Clinton would confront [name withheld] R. Guyle? Spartan seemed so intelligent inside the squared circle, so cunning. Always one step ahead of Clinton, for every move Clinton tried, Spartan seemed to counter it almost immediately. His intelligence was off the charts, and it baffled Clinton. Finally, a worthy adversary. The loss was Clinton’s worst and most embarrassing. Even driving down the highway, anger once again started to boil his insides, and he clenched the steering wheel tight enough to make a knuckle pop. Glancing down at the clipping again, he noticed something as the sunlight passed over it. He could see a double image, the text of the article, but beneath it -- through it -- he could see the face of a man. A ghosted image from the other side of the article. Quickly and carefully, watching the road all the while, Clinton Sage grabbed the newspaper clipping and flipped it over. Much like the triangle choke that Spartan had applied to attain victory, the air was quickly cut off from Clinton’s lungs. It was a face. A man Clinton didn’t know by name nor face, but the eyes he would remember forever. Clinton remembered them staring down at him, as Spartan stood above him in the squared circle on that fateful night, offering him assistance to his feet. They were the eyes of the man who defeated him, choking him out, sending him on this rampant journey of revenge and manipulation. Beneath the photo, there was a name: Jonathan Sail It was an obituary. * * * * *
