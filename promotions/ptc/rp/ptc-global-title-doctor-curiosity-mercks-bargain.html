10 February 2007 Doctor Curiosity vs some other people, Global Title Match part the first Doctor Curiosity was in need of relaxation. He was of the belief that the best way to prepare for a titanic physical contest was to do as little as possible in the week preceding it. That way he would be rested and ready for anything he might be called upon to do in the ring. To this end, the doctor had devised himself a Curious Relaxation Plan. His life had been getting a little too cluttered with work and personal tension, and he was keen to escape all of that. The not-so-good doctor wasn't sure what other devilishly evil heel wrestlers did to relax, but he was personally quite a fan of laying back in a hot tub, and soaking himself in steaming water as his nostrils drew in the scents of lavender and sweet orange essential oils. Yes. Doctor Curiosity likes aromatherapy. He lay there, letting the stresses and strains float away from him. His loss of the XUW World Title was becoming a distant memory. His recent trouble with his girlfriend, Gemma, evaporated in a moment of olfactory bliss. His upcoming match against two of the biggest names in PTC (and Clinton Sage) ceased to exist, replaced instead by happy thoughts. Curiosity's mind ran pleasant daydreams as he lat there. The XUW Intercontinental Title was the first image; the symbol of success for him in that he'd been holding it for nigh on ten months straight. Then the GTT win floated past as he reigned victorious over Dave Hurst and Craig Goat. Ah, but they were small fry compared to his ultimate goal, the one he could visualize so clearly... the PTC Global Title. He could see himself pinning any and all of the three men to become the new champion in the Match of the Decade. It was all so... “What the hell are you doing?” The doctor's eyes flicked open in an instant, and his mood of pleasant relaxation was shattered into a million pieces, carried through the air on a cloud of water vapour that scurried out of the now open door. In front of him stood his girlfriend, Gemma, and she didn't seem either particularly happy or relaxed. “Are you just going to lie in here all day? Don't you have better things to do?” “I'm preparing myself for ze match, my dear.” “Sure thing. I bet the other guys will be quaking in fear if they knew you were preparing by smelling the lovely flowers. What's next? Are you going to get your aura cleansed?” “I have to do vhat vorks for me, darling.” “You think your opponents will be doing that? Can you see Angelo Deville relaxing like this?” she said, spitting the word out as if it were a curse. “No. He's probably off... I don't know... sacrificing a goat somevhere.” “Or Kimbusa?” “He's probably just eating a goat.” “Sage?” “He'd be more likely to be fuc...” “Chris!” snapped Gemma, her temper getting the better of her. “Aren't there other things you should be doing?” “Can't I just bathe in peace?” “My brother, Chris. You said you'd try.” The doctor closed his eyes, held his breath and slipped below the water. It stung his face, and in a way it was invigorating, but he knew that he would have to surface, take a breath, and brave what he'd been trying his best to ignore in the hope that it would go away. He'd promised to try to find Gemma's brother. Curiosity's head burst the surface and he gasped for air. His reddened eyes looked up at his girlfriend, who still had a stern look on her face. Even so, his heart couldn't help melting a little at the way the dark ringlets framed the soft porcelain of her skin, at the way he knew her eyes would turn if she was to crack a smile. She was, after all, the girl, the young woman who had given him what he felt was a second chance. It was just that she didn't fully grasp the enormity of what she was asking... but he had promised, and he'd have to keep it, or he couldn't keep her. He'd promised to call Merrick. *** How a young, pretty girl had become Doctor Curiosity's girlfriend had been the subject of much debate for those who followed the wrestling promotion known as XUW. One day he had been alone, and the next she seemed to have popped right out of thin air. Had anyone cared to actually ask the doctor then he would have gladly told them the prosaic truth of the matter; that she had met him after seeing him wrestle, had instantly struck up a rapport with him and that they had become an item in a matter of days, but it would not have impressed the gossip-mongers who would have preferred her to be the mind slave of some Curious Device, or the trappings of a deal to sell his soul. The couple had progressed their relationship in a surprisingly conventional manner, with the doctor even proving to be quite romantic when in private. The only problem was that whilst Gemma's feelings towards Chris Goodman, the man behind the metaphorical mask, were genuine, she had been harbouring a nasty little secret about why she had met him in the first place. When Gemma had first gone to see that fateful wrestling show she had been intent on discovering the whereabouts of her elder brother, Adrian. She had not heard from him in well over a year, and when their father passed away, Gemma set out to try and find Adrian, to give him the news. It didn't prove to be as easy as she had hoped. Every time a phone number or an address seemed to lead her closer to her sibling, it just ended in failure. It took a long time and a sizable chunk of money for her to find out that Adrian had fallen on hard times and started working as muscle for an illicit small-time Vegas moneylender. From there the trail brought her to a recently deceased lady who had borrowed an amount of money that she had not paid back. Collette Goodman. Adrian had been part of the 'crew' sent to retrieve her debts from the poor, bereaved next of kin; her former husband, Doctor Curiosity. It was bad timing on behalf of Adrian and the other men with him. Caught up in a torrent of emotion at losing his wife to the grave and his brother to prison, Chris Goodman had overstepped the mark and actually succeeded in raising a demon, named Merrick, to whom the doctor wished to sell his soul. The men arrived to collect a debt and were caught in the crossfire. None had been seen since. What Doctor Curiosity thought was the worst part was that he had no idea what had happened to the men. He didn't think that he had killed them, or that Merrick was allowed to kill them by the fastidious and ineffably complex rules that governed the behaviour of demons when materialized on Earth... but that left the question of what exactly had become of them? A question that he prayed would have a happy answer, but suspected would not. Only one entity truly knew, and it was the one that Curiosity wanted to see, and call, the least. *** The uneasy couple sat in the front room of Doctor Curiosity's rented apartment. A stony silence between them was broken only occasionally by the louder moments of whatever dross they weren't watching on television. “So, will you do it now?” asked Gemma. “I'm tired now.” “Then tomorrow?” Curiosity sighed. He knew that she wasn't normally like this, but the dam had broken between them and trying to put a finger over it to stop the torrents of water would have been laughable. They were at the point of crisis, and how the eventual meeting with Merrick turned out would shape their paths forever. It would either be one path they trod, or two. “Gemma, can we not just leave this until after my next match?” asked the doctor, shedding his fake accent and persona for a moment. “No! It's always 'after the next match'. Chris, you've always got something important going on, and every match means something to you. I need to know what happened, and I don't want your excuses about being busy.” “But this time is different...” “No it isn't!” said Gemma, her voice once more beginning to raise above her normal, delicate tones. “This is one of the biggest matches of my career. I want to have time to focus. Can't I just have an easy life for this one week?” Gemma looked like she was about to snap back at him, to end his whining and procrastinating with a withering glare and barbed put-down. Instead, her pretty face broke into a smile and she looked directly at her lover, who seemed to suspect that some new gambit was about to be brought to the table. “Why would you want a quiet life?” “To prepare myself. It seems like every week I've got some new crisis lurching towards me, and it...” “It what?” cut in Gemma. “It allows you to work to the best of your abilities. It's allowed you to become famous.” “I'd just like to have one match where the week before it I could lie down, get some rest, be bored even!” “It doesn't work like that, Chris, and you know it,” said Gemma, willing the doctor to believe her words. “People like you just aren't cut out for the humdrum, boring, nine-to-five drags that everyone else suffers. Look at the men you're wrestling against. They're the same as you.” “Nein!” declared Curiosity, slipping effortlessly back into his persona, like a porcupine curling into a ball when under attack. “Kimbusa is about eight times ze size of...” “That's not what I mean. You all live for the adrenaline. You all live for the challenge, to be teetering on the brink of collapse, only to overcome the odds, or to make the odds your own, and finish as the champion, as the winner. Nobody boring becomes the champion, because the ones with the skill and flair to achieve something like that are all out there, living their lives to full. It takes more than just being a wrestler to be a champion.” She looked him dead in the eyes. “You of all people should know that.” The doctor wanted to disagree, but found his head strangely nodding in assent as she continued. “You sit back and do nothing all week, and you can kiss goodbye to your chances. Just look at what you've achieved. You won GTT4 after your brother killed your wife and you sold your soul to a demon! You won the Intercontinental Title for the first time when you got a short-lived reality television show. You got the World Title in a week when a demented drug addict was trying to kill you. Don't you see the link?” “I need better security?” proffered the doctor. “No! You need the drama. It gives you the drive to drag yourself to whatever heights you choose to ascend to. All wrestlers are the same; they need the adrenaline not just in the ring, but in their entire lives. They just can't live boring lives. Name me a single boring wrestler who's been a success.” The doctor furrowed his brow for a moment. “Vell, Seymour Almasy von hundreds of titles and I always thought he vas pretty boring...” “He's been one of the most popular and controversial stars of his time, and anything but boring.” “Kimbusa?” “You don't know a thing about the man.” “Jah, but vhat's ze most exciting thing zat could happen to him? Maybe one of ze ten cows he eats a day turns out to be mad?” “Look, you're just throwing out any names now. Accept my point. If you won't call Merrick for my benefit then do it for your own, you selfish bastard! I need this and you need this. We can put it all behind us and then you can become as boring as you want right up until the moment the bell rings...” Gemma paused, “and why are you smiling?” Curiosity sat looking at his girlfriend, grinning from ear to ear like a demented Cheshire cat. “Brandon Youngblood.” “What?” “Less interesting than watching non-drying paint.” Gemma thought for a moment, her features betraying her annoyance. “There's an exception to every rule, Chris.” From the face of the doctor, he didn't seem to care. “This isn't points scoring,” said Gemma. “Are you going to call Merrick or not.” “Not right now.” “Why not?” “Vell, firstly because I have been sitting here for ages and I really need ze bathroom.” “Oh.” “And secondly, because I need to get some materials for ze summoning. It's not like I can get out my cellphone and dial straight into Hell. I need candles for lighting, chalk to draw out ze pentagram, incense to cover up ze really horrible smell you get vhen calling forth someone from the Infernal pits, a rosary to protect my resolve, and a bottle of Scotch.” “What's that for?” “I'm sure as shit not doing zis sober, my dear.” The doctor turned, blew his love a kiss, and left the room. *** Despite how confidently he had walked through the door and left Gemma behind, Doctor Curiosity only lasted two paces on the other side before it felt like someone had poured molten lava into his stomach. It was all he could do to avoid soiling himself as he staggered the last few steps to the bathroom, locking the door behind him and dropping to the seat in a flurry of buttons and belt loops. He shivered as his body told him exactly what it thought of the idea to call Merrick. His blood felt like it had been replaced with freon and every hair on his body seemed to buzz with an electricity that was just plain wrong. These were not entities with which you could toy. This wasn't a game where you could make a mistake and live to tell the tale. His very soul was at stake here, in a more perilous position than ever, and literally nobody knew quite how close it had come to falling apart at the seams. Curiosity ran his fingers through the mop of white and silver hair that stuck out at all angles from his head, and tried to think of some way that this could all end happily. Nothing was forthcoming. Gemma, for all she knew, could never understand exactly how this made him feel, and how the very thought of the meeting was making his bowels evacuate themselves in a series of moderately painful spasms. But how could she know, how could anyone know the terror that the doctor was feeling? After all, he was going into a position which nobody else could really claim to have experienced before, because the last time that Curiosity had met Merrick... the demon had killed him. ---- 11 February 2007 Doctor Curiosity vs three unfortunate losers, Global Title Match part the second Lazarus, then Jesus Christ, and now Doctor Curiosity. Coming back from being truly dead is not an everyday occurrence; we're not talking about being out of it for a minute or two on an operating table. Doctor Curiosity, like those storied men before him, ceased to live on Earth, and his soul passed from this life on to the next. It was not a pleasant experience. In fact, it was worse than anything that can be imagined, for Doctor Curiosity's soul went to Hell, went directly to Hell, did not pass Heaven and collect 200 really, really nice things. That is where the story would have ended, or indeed kept on going for eternity, were it not for his soul's pained entreaties to someone, to anyone, that the rules had not been followed. The Court of Purgatory is nothing if not a stickler for the rules. It was found that Merrick had murdered the doctor, and since it is against the rules of the contract for a demon to kill anyone who has sold him their soul, something had to be done about it. Doctor Curiosity was perhaps not smug within the courtroom, recovering as he was from quite the ordeal, but there was a great deal of satisfaction in his demeanour as the Judge reinstated his life; but it was not without a few extra rules being inserted into the contract. It would have been wrong in the eyes of the Court for the doctor to get away from the bargain he had made without some form of punishment or atonement. As such, he was allowed to return to life, unharmed and as mis-shapen as ever, but with the threat of the contract still hanging over his head. Upon the death of Doctor Curiosity, his soul would still reside in Hell. The only way to prevent this would be genuine repentance for what he had done, and where it had got him. Merrick, for his troubles, was given the demonological equivalent of being busted down to Private. He could only reside in the lowest, backwater areas of Hell, with elementals and demons who were too insignificant for anyone to care about. The kind of indecisive spiritual beings that were not full of malice, but had just hung around with the wrong people. The kind that were caught in the wake of Lucifer's expulsion from Heaven before they even knew what was happening. This kind of demon was powerless to appear in the physical plane on Earth and tempt a human being. Merrick still owned the soul of the doctor, but until his death gave the demon power, he would be forced to inhabit the metaphysical equivalent of a particularly sadistic torturer's basement. Not to mention the restraining order they put on Merrick, forbidding him from coming within a mile of the curious one. What the Court had not counted on (or perhaps they had, for who can know the intentions of the Divine?) was that Merrick was almost as smart as he was angry. Corralling a few weaker spirits, he channeled their demonic energy to appear and tempt one more lost soul to his cause. A man named Anthony Douglas, a former middle manager who had fallen on unemployment, homelessness and drug use after a foolish tryst with the boss's wife. He had been promised his life back if he could kill the doctor, and he was desperate enough to make the deal. Thankfully for Chris Goodman, and the wrestling community at large, Anthony failed to commit the murder and Merrick was forced to go back to Hell to recuperate. The doctor and the demon had not had contact since. *** For the second time in his life, Doctor Curiosity was on his knees, drawing chalk lines on the cold stone floor of the Curious Laboratory. He looked around him as he did so, to the tall candles that were barely sheltered from the light breeze blowing through the holes in the walls. The doctor had not rebuilt the top floor of the Curious Laboratory since it was destroyed by Merrick's arrival the first time around. It was the very stones that saw the doctor lose his soul that would be witnesses to whatever happened this time around. “Will the chalk hold him?” asked Gemma. “I hope so,” said the doctor, though he didn't know if the circle would even snare the demon in the first place any more. “So, are you ready?” Curiosity raised his eyes to the heavens, remembering that the lack of a roof was down to what had happened here the last time. “As ready as I'll ever be.” With that the doctor wandered around the candles and the pentagram, murmuring in bastardized Latin. The actual words were not as important as the mind and the will behind them. It would be Curiosity's soul that would call out to Merrick, not him fixing up the grammar of a dead language. Gemma looked on as Curiosity continued to pace, and the chalk started to glow red hot. Soon smoke was rising from the grey stones and swirling in a loose column around the centre of the pentagram. The doctor's girlfriend drew in a deep breath, as quietly as she could, and began to bite on her perfectly formed lower lip. Curiosity stopped walking as the column of smoke grew darker and darker, and he stood facing it, scant inches away from where the demon was scheduled to appear. Amidst a buzzing noise that had grown over the past few seconds, the doctor's voice grew and grew until he screamed out two syllables. “MERRICK!” There was a small thunderclap and the smoke cleared to reveal a figure very familiar to the nefarious one. ... Angelo Deville. “Vhat? I vas trying to call Merrick back. I'm terribly sorry.” Deville raised an eyebrow and tapped his foot, as the doctor scurried over to a book and re-read some of his notes. “Ah yes, jah, I am sorry. I've had a lot on my mind, you know?” “An honest mistake. Could happen to anyone,” replied the Devil's Don. “I'll have you back home in just a moment.” “In your own time...” The doctor pushed his cracked glasses back up to the bridge of his nose, sighed, and then began his mumbling again. The smoke soon rose up from the floor and engulfed the doctor's opponent, swirling and twisting until he was entirely obscured. With a snap of his fingers, the doctor yelled out the demon's name once more, and another figure appeared. “What's with all the smoke?” coughed the engulfed silhouette, who immediately tried to step forwards, only to hit an invisible barrier. Behind the wisps of grey and black he looked like an ordinary man, perhaps the kind you'd see on a forecourt trying to sell you used cars. His suit was sharp, but did not fit in the precise way that a specifically tailored one would. Here was someone trying to look more important than they were. “Merrick. We meet again.” “Yes, we do,” said the demon, clearly unhappy at the current state of affairs. “Is there any way that you can get rid of the smoke? It's a devil on my lungs, if you pardon the expression.” “I like the smoke. It stays.” Gemma watched the doctor and the demon sizing one another up. She had never seen Chris as intense as he looked, with his life and soul potentially hanging in the balance. “Well, if I get lung cancer from secondary smoke, you're looking at a lawsuit, buddy. You do, after all, so love the Court.” Curiosity ignored the barb, and continued. “I know it pains you to be here Merrick, so leave the pleasantries aside. I just want to know one thing, and that is what happened to the men who came here to collect on Collette's debts?” A twitch of a smile appeared in the corner of the demon's mouth. “Why would you want to know that?” “Just tell me.” “Why should I? After all, you're not exactly my favourite person in the world right now.” “You're still under contract to assist me. The ruling of the Court means you can't help me physically, but you can give me answers. Now tell me, what happened to those men.” Merrick spoke through gritted teeth, “I don't know.” “W... what?” stuttered the doctor. “How can you not know? There were only two of us there.” “I know. I made a trade with another demon though.” “What? A trade?” “Yes,” said Merrick, with a laugh. “Someone wanted some... shall we say, 'subjects for experimentation'. Now, if that is all then I'll be on my way, unless you want anything more.” “Who? Which demon was it?” Merrick laughed, and it was not a pretty sound. “You still think in such limited dimensions, my little human nemesis. Demons do not have names. We exist on a metaphysical plane that you cannot comprehend. We have as much need for names as a cat needs a set of wrenches. Now, I can help you no more on this matter. I don't have to ask this demon anything for you, and even if I did, he would be under no obligation to tell the truth.” The doctor glanced across at his girlfriend, feeling dejected and defeated. The demon saw a chance. “Of course, I could try harder, if you released me from the, shall we say, 'extra' terms of the contract.” Curiosity remained silent. “And I could help you with so many other things. We could be a team again. Remember winning GTT4, didn't it feel good? I could help you. Let's face it, you're terrified of Deville, Sage and Kimbusa, and with good reason. Unaided they'll destroy you. Turn to my help, and you could be the champion... not to mention I could maybe get your little girlfriend's brother back for her...” At that, Gemma and the doctor turned to face one another, startled looks on their faces. “Yes. Of course I knew why you wanted to find them. Oh come on, give me some credit!” The doctor found himself nodding. Gemma was unable to speak, torn between her love for her boyfriend and her desire to see her brother again. “I don't need any help with the wrestling, but I'll take the deal if you can bring us back her brother.” “Of course you need help with the wrestling. You're a joke compared to them. Face it, the thought of fighting Angelo Deville gives you nightmares.” “Leave my job out of this. I'm giving you what you want.” “Do not presume to know what I want, mortal,” purred Merrick. “I want you to win the Global Title, and to know forever that you only did it because of me. Can your pride stand that?” The doctor's face screwed up, his hands repeatedly uncurling and then tightening themselves into fists that he knew wouldn't damage the apparition before him. “You'll know that you needed my help to win the title because you're simply not good enough on your own. Everything you won was because of me. I shaped you into what you are today, and without my influence, you'd just be a failure. Another forgotten man. I made you what you are!” Something broke inside Chris Goodman, not for the first time, and the veil slipped back into place. “Nein! I am ze greatest wrestler, and you are nothing but a charlatan and a liar!” “Deville would tear you apart.” “Yes he vould... if ve found a time machine and fought in 2003! Vhat has he done lately? I'm twice as awesome... no... I'm pi times as awesome as him!” the doctor declared proudly. “Kimbusa?” “He only vent for ze Global Title because he tzought your body shape had to resemble a globe to enter. Ze bigger zey are, ze harder zey fall... and ve'd better hope zat ze match isn't held near any major tectonic fault lines, because it's going to be one hell of a sound vhen I finish him for good.” The demon laughed, clearly delighted with the way things were going. “So, you reject my offer?” he said. “Jah!” “Great! Then Adrian will die.” The doctor's arms, raised into the air in triumph, suddenly fell as he stood, crestfallen, coming back out of himself, or back into himself, depending on your reading of his psychological state. “No.” The voice was leaden, all trace of accent gone. “Then you accept the offer, and all it entails?” With regret and hatred in his voice, the doctor nodded, “Yes.” *** Two days later, and they were still waiting for a response. The demon had vanished into the ether, promising a response as soon as he could find out precisely what had happened to Adrian, and work out a way to bring him back for them. Doctor Curiosity had no reason to disbelieve his Infernal business associate, as the binding terms of the Contract would not be lifted unless he was successful. Even so, it made sitting around in the basement of the Curious Laboratory and watching television a slightly strained affair. Gemma had been monosyllabic since the meeting had taken place, unable to reconcile trading her lover's safety for the possibility of seeing her brother alive again. She'd not even spoken to Adrian in over a year before her father died, but the search for her last remaining family member had taken on the burden of a quest for her. The chance to finally reach her Holy Grail, whether it be a golden chalice or a broken wooden cup, was too irresistible for her to be truly remorseful at what Chris Goodman stood to lose. As for the doctor himself, he was nervous, but more disappointed. He had spent the entire last two years trying to distance himself from his achievements under Merrick and building up accolades that were undeniably his. The record breaking runs in fWo and XUW were testament to that, and ow his quest, his Holy Grail was to be tainted by the demon he had been trying to escape the entire time. What were his options though? The only thing he could do to get out from under the thrall of the demon would be to repent, and to truly believe what he was saying. Sadly, that was not an option. Whilst the doctor regretted having won GTT4 by such Infernal means, it had brought him fame, fortune and the ability to shape his own destiny. Before he had these successes he had always felt second best, had always been at the behest of others. His failed literary and scientific careers had crushed him inside, and finally proving his doubters wrong had freed something deep inside. Besides, the sequence of events had led him to Gemma, only the second woman he had ever loved, and how could he sorry for that? How could he ask to be forgiven and that he regretted everything when he was, for the most part, happier than he'd ever been? Well, holy shit, you're right! Now just leave me alone and let me get along with what I've got to do.” “I'm sorry Chris, I just...” “Don't sweat it. Just let me do what I'm good at. You'll see me again soon.” Swinging the canvas sack over his shoulder, Doctor Curiosity went off to war. *** Driving along the highway towards his destination, Doctor Curiosity slightly regretted that he could not take public transport to his destination. If nothing else, he was becoming more environmentally aware as he grew older, and his failed inventions were more often than not made of recyclable materials. That said, given the current political climate, going on board a bus or train with a bag full of potentially explosive implements would perhaps not have been the smartest move ever made. As such, the doctor drove his car forwards, never engaging cruise control, always keeping his mind on the job at hand, and the job that would soon be presenting itself. He'd always wanted to be in control of his own destiny, and the doctor's hard work over the past two years to actually make himself a genuinely good wrestler had been impressive to the very few who saw the surprisingly large amount of hours he put in to training. With the amount of time he still put into creating obscure devices with no obvious use, it was almost a shock that the doctor ever got to sleep. Yet there he was, driving along with a knapsack full of enough gadgets to make James Bond die of envy. They represented the scientific side of his work, the kind of things he had been saving up for a special occasion. What could be more special than this? A few hours later and the doctor was checking off street names as he drove through the leafy suburbia of Fullerton in Orange County. He felt surprisingly calm as he mentally ticked off names, eventually taking a right on to a street that was as nondescript as it was lovely. He drove up to the house number that he had been told about and stopped the car in the driveway. Without hesitation the doctor grabbed his bag and slung it over his shoulder, moving purposefully out of the car and towards the front door. As he rang the doorbell the first flutters of uncertainty hits his stomach, but he figured that it couldn't be much worse than facing a demon down. Soon the door was answered by a shortish man wearing a white suit, and Doctor Curiosity greeted him with a punch to the face. The short man stumbled backwards as the doctor pushed the door back and walked into the room beyond it. He saw several men arrayed around a table, looking over something that looked like a cross between a map and a schematic diagram. They looked up as one as the doctor kicked the door opener in the stomach and threw a small, round, chrome-plated device across the floor. It slid until it came to rest underneath the table, cuasing several of the men to jump like a skittish woman regarding a small mouse. “Before you even start to ask, zat's a Curious Neuronegator. No, you don't know vhat zat means, but rest assured that it can fry the brains of anyone I choose to let it.” Doctor Curiosity let this sink in for a second. “What the fuck is going on?” complained one of the men around the table. “Is this the German Jack Bauer? Dude, get out of my house before I kill you!” Curiosity flipped open what looked like a cellphone and pressed a button. Immediately a bolt of what looked like green lightning flew out of the Curious Neuronegator and slapped the speaker in the temple. He slumped back into his seat and started to drool. “I apologize for anything he stains, but that's his own stupid fault for getting in my way. Now, is anyone going to disagree with me any more?” The remaining men stared at Doctor Curiosity with something approaching awe in their eyes. This was the stuff of television shows, not eh everyday life of... whatever it was that they did for a living. “Good,” said the doctor, breathing deeply and trying not to let them see through his facade to the lack of confidence underneath. “Now, which one of you is Keith Holloway?” Each man around the table tried to make it look as if it was not them, but under the glare of the others, one was eventually forced to half raise a hand and mouth that he was indeed the man the doctor was looking for. “Good. Now tell me how to find Adrian Howard.” “Who?” “He was one of several men you should have met just over two years ago. I don't expect you to remember them all, in fact you might not even know their names, so here's a photograph to go along with it.” The doctor expertly flicked a Polaroid from Gemma's personal collection through the air. It landed in the lap of the startled man. “He was part of a group. I believe that the word 'shipment' was used in reference to them.” Holloway looked around at the other men in the room. Curiosity got a feeling that this was a legitimate business meeting, and that he would get more out of the man on his own. Still, he was enjoying this empowerment, and the chance to test out his devices was one not to be missed. Keith shook his head. The doctor smiled. In an instant the pistol was in his hand and the trigger was pulled. A small dart flew into the neck of Keith Holloway, and he grabbed it as quickly as he could pulling it out and looking at Curiosity in an accusatory fashion. “Poison is it? You're going to kill me?” “Of course not. Well, not yet. Just a little Curious Truth Drug to help our discussions along.” The rest of the men sat around the table were shuffling slowly to the far wall. As if noticing them for the first time, Doctor Curiosity motioned towards a utility room and nodded. “Get in there.” Nobody disobeyed the order, and once they were all safely locked away, Curiosity pressed a couple of buttons on his cellphone and lightning flew through the wall. There was the sound of several bodies slumping to the floor at once, and the doctor smiled. He grabbed a wooden chair and spun it round so that the back faced Keith. The doctor straddled it and smiled at the only other conscious man in the room. “So, Keith, that is your name, right?” “Yes,” replied Holloway, before blinking in surprise. “Yes, you have to answer me now. It's a truth serum, like spies use. It blocks the brainwaves associated with lying, and enables the conversational receptors. You'll find it very hard to hold anything back from me now.” Mentally, Curiosity ticked another box relating to a working device. That was two out of two... he never had that kind of success rate! “I'm cheating on my wife,” blurted Keith. “Okay, I'm sure you are, but that's not what I need to know.” “I do love her, but it's just that the secretary at work is so hot, and she wants to make me happy, and...” “Enough! I don't want to hear about that. I want to hear about the shipment two years ago. Tell me what happened to Adrian.” “Sometimes at night I can't sleep because I think that there's going to be a nuclear bomb, and I can't run away from it fast enough.” Doctor Curiosity made a mental note to reduce the dosage of the next dart he fired. “Look Keith, I'm just interested in finding out what happened to Adrian.” “He was brought in at the same time as the others. They went on to Mike's place.” “Who is Mike?” asked the doctor, feeling ever more like he was in an episode of some kind of thrilling television series. “The next link in the chain. Look, please let me go. I'm going to wet myself if you don't let me go soon.” “Really?” The man shrugged, saying without words that yes, he was telling the truth, and that a man pumped full of truth serum tends to do that most of the freaking time. Before more words could be uttered, there was a knock at the door. “I vonder who zat could be,” said Curiosity, with things slowly falling to place inside his mind. He went to the door and opened it. “Who are you?” exclaimed the man standing there. “Come on in, Mike,” growled the doctor as he grabbed the man by the scruff of his neck and threw him at the feet of Keith. “And I suppose you were here for the meteorite, correct?” “I don't know what you're talking about,” said Mike. “Yes! Yes he was,” said Keith, before looking embarrassed at what the truth serum had forced him to do. Curiosity eyed the pair of them up, before grabbing his bag. He took out of it two small devices the size and shape of a pair of metallic slippers, and placed them over his feet. At the click of his heels the slippers started to hum, and lifted the doctor into the air. “Anti-gravity slippers, do you like zem?” asked the doctor, smiling towards his captives. “What the fuck are you doing?” asked Mike. “Yes. I love them! I also masturbate to the dog show,” confessed Keith. “Shut up Keith,” said both of the other men, in unison. “Look, I don't know what you want, man, but I've got nothing to do with anything,” pleaded Mike. Curiosity looked towards Keith. “Yes he does! He has the meteorite! And I actually sometimes fantasize about him and me and the meteorite and we could be so happy together if we...” “Shut up, Keith!” said the men again, in stereo. “I'm sorry about him,” apologized the doctor. “Ze truth serum was a little too potent.” “No shit.” “Anyvays, vhere vere ve? AH, jah! You vere going to give me ze meteorite.” “No I wasn't! I keep it hidden away in my... ohhhh, you little trickster!” said Mike, trying to hide his disgust at giving things up so easily. “Before you reveal ze location of ze meteorite to me, I need just one more tzing.” “What? Anything... just don't kill me.” “Just vatch zis.” Ze nefarious one walked over one last time to his bag of tricks and withdrew from it a complicated device that seemed to be entirely made of levers and buttons. Strange chrome pipes twisted and turned within the odd monstrosity of an invention. It seemed to serve no purpose other than to have more options for pressing and pulling things than anything that came before it. “What does that do?” asked Mike. “I've never known,” said Curiosity. “I made it out of boredom one time when I was waiting to wrestle. You know how shows can get delayed these days.” “Oh tell me about it! I was waiting a week for my favourite one to come on television the other week!” replied Mike. “My favourite television programme is The View!” “Shut up, Keith!” Curiosity smiled, and regarded his invention. “I've never known what it was for, but I bet you anything that if I pull this lever...” He pulled the nearest lever. A wall drew back, revealing a glowing meteorite the size of a bowling ball. “Well, who'd have thought it? All along I had the right device with me!” declared the doc, sarcasm dripping from every intoned syllable. “You discovered my secret!” said Mike, sounding more and more like a bad soap opera actor. “I keep my secret locked away in a motel wearing a gimp mask,” said Keith, sounding more and more like a man who you really wouldn't ever want to meet. “And I'd be willing to be that I have to kill you to get the combination behind that secret forcefield that I can't see, right?” “How did you know that?” replied Mike, full of disbelief. “Okay, that's it.” Doctor Curiosity steadied himself and made sure he was on the ground, Curious Anti-Gravity Slippers well and truly discarded. He looked at nobody or nothing in particular and called out. “Merrick, this isn't funny any more. Show yourself.” Nothing happened. “Merrick, I'm not fucking around here,” said Doctor Curiosity, though he had shed the persona once more. Nothing happened for a long second, and then everything seemed to coalesce. Time slowed down to a crawl, Curiosity able to turn, but only in slow motion. One wall blurred out of existence and the doctor knew somehow that he was seeing not just one world but two. Through the wall walked Merrick, looking radiant in his suit, this time one tailored to fit his perfectly pernicious self. “So, you worked it out. What did I get wrong?” asked the demon. “Well, for a start it was too easy. I should never be able to take on this many people. Sure, I'm a trained wrestler, but there's plausible and then there's fantasy.” “Okay... anything else? Keep in mind that those were real people... they were just, shall we say 'modified' a little.” “The other giveaway,” said the doctor, able to speak but not move in this strange fugue state between moments in time. “The other giveaway... look, could you make it so I can move? You caught me at an awkward angle.” The demon nodded, and Curiosity was released from the normal constraints of space and time. “The other giveaway was that all of my devices worked. I stole the truth serum from the CIA, so I wasn't too surprised by that, but the others were mostly junk. One of them working was possible, but all of them? The last one was just junk randomly welded together!” “I see,” said the demon, smiling. “Did you enjoy my little game for you?” “In a way I did,” said the doctor, seemingly unbowed by the machinations of his foe. “How is that? I moved you around like a piece on a chess board? I wasted your time and effort, pushing you further until you would have snapped. Without those minor details you would have killed those men. You would have become the lowest of the low, just to please your girlfriend... how does that make you feel?” the demon rasped, his voice becoming more sibilant as he got excited. Curiosity looked at the demon, and actually smiled. This unnerved Merrick, for he was supposed to be the one in possession of all of the cards. “You want to know why I'm smiling, don't you Merrick?” The demon nodded, suddenly less than one hundred percent sure of itself, for the first time. “I spotted something else that was wrong.” “What?” “You're not this powerful.” Merrick laughed. “The constraints of the Contract were lifted, foolish human. I have all the power that I ever did!” Curiosity shook his head. “And that was never this much. Stopping time? Conducting little charades with human beings, for kicks? Making everything work for me, just so? You never had that.” “I did...” spluttered the demon. “I always had control.” Curiosity shook his head again, the smile remaining on his face. “In fact, Merrick, I'll go out on a limb here and bet you that that there's someone else pulling your strings this time.” The demon's eyes drew narrow with the prospect of a wager. “Bet? What would you stake?” “I'd wager my soul, Merrick... but for some reason I don't think it is yours to bet any more.” “What?” cried the demon, his eyes going blank for a second as he conducted a kind of Infernal inventory. “Nooooooooo! That's not possible!” Curiosity grabbed the nearest chair and sat down upon it. He ran his fingers through the mess of hair stop his head, and addressed the room at large. “Come on, above or below, show yourself. The game is up.” It's only with experience that you can be so blasé in the face of the supernatural. “No!” Merrick continued to scream. “No! He can't have done! That's not faaaaaaiiiiiiirrrrrrrr...” His voice dropped off, the scream becoming more and more distant, his body fading from existence entirely, becoming translucent, and then becoming barely there at all. A few seconds later and Curiosity was alone in the room. One thing the doctor had learned was that in certain situations you are powerless. Sometimes there is nothing that you can do to help yourself. If he found himself battling Deville whilst Sage pinned Kimbusa, there wouldn't be a thing he could do. If he found himself squashed under the current champion's girth, there'd be nothing he could do to get it off him. True power, and the true application of power, rests with knowing what you can change, and what you are powerless to affect. Doctor Curiosity sat, and waited for his fate to become apparent. He felt it first in his feet, in the feeling of the floorboards vibrating. He knew that he was not in some simple room in Fullerton, but instead that he existed between here and there, a place without time or space, but with something 'other', something that kept everything from happening at once. Soon the whole 'room' was shaking, and with it the doctor's body. He tried to remain calm, but no matter what you know you are powerless to stop, it's hard not to be nervous in the presence of one so alien to you, and one so clearly superior. The body of the giant seemed to form together from the dust, the floorboards and the very essence of the strange room. It stuck together in pieces that looked comical at first, like a Power Ranger sponsored by Home Depot, but gradually, piece by piece, it amalgamated to form a shape that was not human, but looked like how you might draw one if you had heard of them, but never seen one. “So you're the one behind this?” asked the doctor, desperately trying to keep his voice from wavering. “YES,” boomed the giant humanoid. “Is there any way that you can be less tall?” asked the doctor. “Twenty feet is quite impressive, and the voice has got me. Rest assured, I'm both in awe and terrified of you. Any chance of having this conversation at my level?” The giant broke into an impression of a smile, and then, seconds later, there was a smartly clad man stood in front of the doctor. “SORRY, I JUST LIKE TO USE THE OTHER FORM TO INTIMIDATE PEOPLE!” boomed the regular sized man. “The voice... please?” “Sorry, I often forget that too.” “So, what's the deal?” asked Curiosity, feeling that things couldn't possibly get any stranger. “Well, you've been dealing with Xtyhw... with Merrick,” said the figure, correcting his attempt to pronounce a name that would have meant nothing to the doctor. “He's been a little, shall we say, out of control.” “Tell me about it.” “No,” said the man, with an air of finality that did not warrant questioning. “His behaviour has been unacceptable to us.” “Us?” “The... let's call us the upper management circle,” he said, in clipped, English tones. “Of Hell?” “Where else?” “Aren't you all fiery and have cloven hooves and all that?” “I can if you want, it's just that I thought you wanted...” “Ah yes,” said the doctor, understanding. “So, we have disapproved of his conduct. These days we need hundreds of souls harvested, and he has spent literally years chasing after yours. We tried to convey to him that your soul was taken care of, and that we had it under control, but he would not listen. So, we had to give him a little power back and, well, give him enough rope with which to hang himself. Rest assured, he is now most well hung.” The doctor tried not to smirk. “So what happens to me?” “In a word, nothing.” “Nothing?” “No,” said the manager. “We have your soul, and we've run a set of predictive calculations that indicate you won't be repenting any time soon. Certainly not any time soon after the wedding...” “Wedding?” asked the doctor. “Surely you don't think that lovely girl will wait around forever on you? Oh no, that just wouldn't do.” “So, you'll return me to my life?” “Yes.” “And Merrick's terms are gone?” “Yes.” “Including the ones about the match?” “Oh yes, you can win or lose it to your heart's desire,” said the manager. “And my soul?” “It is ours.” “And Adrian?” “Find him yourself.” The doctor sighed. The manager laid a hand on his shoulder and tried to commiserate. “I'm afraid that we do run Hell here. We can't just be giving away information for free, you know?” The doctor nodded. So it was that he would go back to his life and back to his wrestling, without the one question he really wanted answering. His quest would not finish in that week, and he would be able to spend the last few hours before the fight being as boring as his heart and soul would let him be. “Oh, and one last thing before you go,” said the manager. “What?” “This isn't over.” And with a wink, he was gone.
