The past seems longer than the future

12 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-12 It was supposed to have been his finest hour. It was supposed to have been his crowning glory; one year after winning GTT4 he would pluck someone from the ether who had never ever wrestled before, and train them up to become the GTT5 champion. He'd come so close, making it right up to the final, when he had become complacent, and one person had ruined it for him. Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins. The stories had said that Rollins was in prison, on Death Row even… but something had gone wrong with the execution and he had walked free via a crafty loophole. They hadn't even prepared for him properly, relaxing in the knowledge that the doctor's protege, Shanty Jones, would be crowned the new champion, and that Curiosity's legacy in the sport would be assured. Sipping Cristal in his skybox as the final went ahead, the Curious One watched as, at the end of a hard fought contest, Rich Rollins took apart Shanty Jones piece by piece and ended the dreams of being involved in back to back victories. As his champagne turned to vinegar in his mouth, he was reminded of one few nuggets of wisdom passed down by his father. *** "If you want something doing properly, you've got to do it yourself." Doctor Curiosity looked up at the classroom full of schoolchildren in front of him. Being the XUW Intercontinental Champion was something that had given the doctor a much needed boost of cash, but with the annoying side effect of having to perform many promotional duties, including teaching kids about morals and sportsmanship. An ironic set of events, given the doctors usual methods of victory, but such things must be done in the name of sponsorship and money. "Jah, you cannot give your burdens to others and expect zem to do it for you. In zis life you have to vork for vhat you vant, and not expect it to just be given to you." The doctor continued with the platitudes, as a small voice in the back of his head told him that he was slowly becoming a cliche. Come on, teaching kids morality? What was next, a midweek in-ring promo saying how lame his GTT opponent was? He wearily glanced over to the teacher, who nodded her head and exited the room, leaving him along with the children for a few minutes. "Right," said Curiosity, with a little more assertiveness. "You don't get anything for free, as I said, but vhat you can get can be made a whole lot easier by taking shortcuts." The twelve year old boys in the audience were suddenly more interested. "I'm sure I am not adding to your vocabularies by telling you to fuck ze rules." Giggles from the kids. Given the state of youth today, they had heard this word before and more, but to see it coming from an adult who was supposed to be teaching them was something of a thrill. "I am ze Intercontinental Champion of a premier wrestling federation, and I did not get zere by following rules and being von of ze good guys! Nor vas my victory in Prime Time Central's GTT4 achieved by not cutting a corner or two. Jah, of course it still involves hard vork, and you have to be on your toes, noticing things that others vould miss, but you can gain as much by cheating as you can by good, honest graft." A hand went up in the classroom. "Jah?" "My family say that cheating is wrong and immoral and you shouldn't do it and if you do you will go to Hell and have to eat broccoli forever and ever." "Are your family fundamentalists?" "Are they… what? I love my family." "Vell there's you first mistake, child," said the doctor, warming to his subject. "Never trust your family. My brother is in jail, for killing my wife, who was trying to extort me for money and zen do avay vith me!" "What does extort mean?" "It doesn't matter. Vhat you have to remember is zat ze people who you think are supporting you and nurturing you might not actually have your best interests at heart." The children looked unconvinced, especially the vocal one. "Vhat I mean to say… is zat your mother is poisoning you. Have you ever noticed zat your food tastes slightly different from usual?" The doctor paused, his smile not wavering at all as the boy nodded his head slightly. "Jah, zat is your mother adding chemicals to you, making sure she can mess vith your brain." "But mommy wouldn't do that!" "I can prove that she would!" "How?" "Vell, ze next time zat you have food zat tastes a little different, ask her about ze mind altering drugs she has been feeding you. If she is devious and lying to you, she vill deny all knowledge of mind altering drugs. She vill claim to have not even heard of vhat zey are, whereas everyone knows vhat mind altering drugs are, right children?" A chorus of approval registered throughout the room. After all, if there is one thing a child does not want to appear in front of a group of his peers (and, indeed, someone famous) it is ignorant. "So, if she claims not to know vhat zey are, zen she is lying and poisoning you. Alvays remember zis." Curiosity chuckled to himself, but the voice in the back of his head reprimanded him for it. Sinking to such depths as to try to break the bond between a mother and her child? That's pretty bad. Oh, you think it's harmless mischief, but you can never know the kind of lesson it'll teach a boy. The doctor ignored what appeared to be his conscience and addressed the class again. "Children, it's never easy to take a stand against the oppressors. It's just as hard in its own vay to be one of the reprobates, ze cheaters, ze men living outside the law… but it is vorth the effort, and a hell of a lot more fun than earning things in legitimate vays." The doctor's keen ears detected footsteps, and then a hand on the door to the classroom. "And zat is vhy you should stay in school, eat your greens, and just say no to drugs, jah!" He ended with a flourish as the teacher came back into the room. She was a little surprised that he was done so quickly, and that the class hadn't caused him more trouble, but was happy with his platitudes, and she let him go off to do whatever it is he would do. In the corridor outside the doctor tried to compose himself. It was going to be a big couple of weeks between the various competitions he was entered into. It couldn't be too hard though. He'd been a champion in XUW for months and months now, and the last time he'd entered a GTT he had strolled all the way to the final and picked up the trophy. Oh yeah, said the voice inside his head. You did that all on your own. No help at all, right? It was then that Curiosity realised that he had not been talking to himself at all. "Merrick, vhat are you doing here again… I thought ve had an agreement?" *** It was September of 2004 when Doctor Curiosity began his journey from also-ran to major player. He had come up with one surprising tournament showing before entering GTT4, and had been designated a 'dark horse', whatever that was supposed to mean. His first match was against a man known as George English, and it was a match that he won. Further victories followed, mostly due to his various nefarious deeds and incomprehensible (yet effective) devices that tilted the balance of power in his favour. After a further four victories, he faced off against the Latch Key Kid and somehow won that to make it six straight wins and a place in the tournament final. The doctor was a shambling wreck by then. He had run out of energy, out of devices, out of friends and out of luck. His brother, the man known to the wrestling world as Eegor, had killed his wife. It was an entirely understandable act, and he had been pushed to it, but in the eyes of the law it was going to be a murder charge, probably of the second degree, and a lengthy prison term. Sadly for the doctor, his brother had been more than just a sibling to him. He had been a trainer, a chef, a cleaner, a confident, a mechanic and a distraction around the ring. In short, Curiosity became convinced that there was no way he could win the final against Exceptional Dave Hurst and Craig Goat without some extra impetus, some additional illicit edge that could see him through. After all, EDH had just beaten the Deville himself. So it was probably not a coincidence that Curiosity found himself looking in the direction of the Infernal for a chance; a chance for him to win the tournament and for the Underworld to gain revenge on EDH for defeating a man believed to be their champion. With rituals, incense and a heart burning full of rage and desire, Doctor Curiosity called upon a demon to grant him the power to defeat his opponents and become the GTT4 Champion. The cost would be his immortal soul, but once he saw the kind of strength he would wield, he signed it over gladly. The rest, as they say, is history. Unprecedented success followed the doctor around in the coming years. The GTT4 Championship, and then a lengthy fWo Cruiserweight championship reign was followed with an entry to XUW and the longest ever Intercontinental Title reign… and yet very little of this bore any hallmarks of the supernatural. In each and every case, Doctor Curiosity seemed fired by something, but could it not have been his drive, his determination to improve himself and achieve things the old fashioned, hard way? For all his cheating and underhanded designs, that level of success was not bred purely from turning a blind eye to the rules. 'Merrick' was the name he had given to his personal demon, but was it a true one, or a metaphor for something; the way alcoholics and drug addicts talk about the ones they have to battle constantly? Would it even be possible for him to tell the difference, or had the doctor taken one step too far, a little too close to the abyss to pull back entirely? Whatever the scenario or situation, Doctor Curiosity could hear the voice as clear as day, as vibrant as the sun. It spoke to him as he left the classroom, for the first time since he had finished with GTT4, back in late December 2004. It seemed more eager than before, more excited. This worried the doctor, and he refused to listen to it until he got back to the only place he would be able to deal with it. His sometime home, and where all of it had begun. The Curious Laboratory. *** "If you're going to talk to me then at least show yourself." "A pleasure," said a voice from the air. It sounded like a thousand things at once, conjuring up images of honey, fresh water and caramel… and yet tempered with fire, brimstone and decay. It was enough to set anyone's nerves on edge, and the doctor's were fizzing, the synapses in his brain firing chemicals hither and thither in an orgiastic frenzy. Before him, a form took shape out of the air. It was a simple form, that of a man in the kind of suit beloved of salesman. Nothing too flashy, but enough to indicate that they were making some good money, and it was because they sold the good stuff. Curiosity had seen it before, and knew better than to take anything Merrick would say at face value. "So, vhy are you here? We had a deal." "Yes we did," purred Merrick. "And the deal is nearing completion." "No it isn't! You didn't set any time limit on how long I had the power for! You said that I was given the power to do as I pleased for as long as I lived, and I'm in fine shape. I've got no illnesses to speak of, and I get checked up for all kinds of crap on a weekly basis." "That's very thorough," observed the demon. "Yeah, well, hypochondria has its benefits…" muttered the doc. "I'm sure it'll come as a great relief to you to know that you're the perfect picture of health then. Your heart is almost scarily healthy. You've got no brain damage at all, your lungs are in perfect working condition, and your cells seem to have resisted taking up cancer thus far." "That's… that's good to know. So, why are you here." "Ah yes. I'm here because of the accident." "The accident?" "Yes. The accident." "You sound like a scene from Back to the Future." "Oh, how often life imitates art, my dear doctor. You see, I am here to take your soul. You promised it to me and sold it wholesale. It is mine now, and once that weak body of yours expires to its inevitable mortality, I will contain that which resides therein. I shall possess you, Herr Doctor." "Then leave me alone until such a time occurs. I'm in the peak of physical health, and I'm not doing anything stupid in the coming months." "It doesn't matter." "Why?" asked Curiosity. "You are going to die before this tournament ends. It is your destiny." *** So, how had the doctor achieved such success in wrestling matches in the past couple of years? Had it been something otherworldly, or just his application to the task at hand? Had Doctor Curiosity just been naturally gifted in the squared circle, or was he drawing upon powers unavailable to his peers? Certainly, after looking past the veil, there has been far more to the not-so-good doctor than has always met the eye. Upon realising that he was not a match, long-term, for any of the upper echelons of athletes in either fWo or XUW (or indeed the tournaments of Prime Time Central), it had fallen to Curiosity to take a drastic plan of action. This time it had not involved any demons, unless you counted the ones that lay in the kind of bottles that Craig Miles drank. Instead, to the surprise of the few people who found out about it, Doctor Curiosity had started a very punishing regime of wrestling training. He learned from the great and good of many respected wrestling federations, including 'Cocky' Craig Miles, The Uber Judge, 'Superstar' Vince Jacobs and Rana Venenosa. The specific names were unimportant, even as illustrious as they were. The important aspect was that Curiosity was becoming a more rounded athlete, and a more proficient wrestler. Whether it was learning roughneck tactics, how to fly with grace and style, how to ground and pound or how to counter the toughest locks and vices, he approached all aspects with the same, scientific mindset. He proved to be an adept student, and soon beneath the comical exterior and the feigned lack of wrestling ability, at crunch times his new knowledge would shine through. Over time he won titles and held them, and everyone knew that there was something a little different about the doctor. There was an edge to him that they had not seen throughout GTT4. The rumours of him selling his soul came out, though nobody would admit to knowing where they had started, but it all slipped past the notice of the man himself. In fact, soon he started to believe that his past obsession with demons and Latin and invocations were just a passing phase; were just a momentary madness that would pass with time. Of course he hadn't sold his soul to a demon; that would be ridiculous. And yet, there was one stood with him, promising him death. *** After around half an hour of being very puzzled, alternately sitting down and pacing the sold stone floors of the Curious Laboratory, the doctor decided that he needed to know more. He looked up, and was not surprised in the least to see Merrick stood directly in front of him. "So, vhat are you here for, to gloat?" "No, I'm here to collect your soul." "You mean… I'm going to die tonight?" said the doctor, his face and voice full of fear. "Oh no, you don't die for at least another week. In fact, you die on the… no… I've said too much." "You know vhen it is? Do you know what happens?" "Well, it shouldn't even have been in the arena in the first place, but…" "But what?" demanded the exasperated wrestler. "No, I cannot say. If you were to know the method of your own demise then you might be able to prevent it, and that would cause all sorts of paradoxes and ripples in the space-time continuum." "Really?" "I'm not sure… but if you survive then I don't get your soul, and that's a good enough reason for me." "So you did come here to gloat?" Merrick thought about it for a minute, and then nodded. "Yeah, I guess so." Curiosity kicked out at a chair in frustration, and then paced away, trying to think of something he could do to get out of the situation. All of a sudden he had a thought. "Hang on, if I die in over a veek, and I am in ze arena… does zat mean zat I vin my first round match?" Merrick thought this over for a moment. "Maybe you're just in the crowd?" "Dammit!" exclaimed the doctor. "I don't vant to die and spend eternity in Hell! If I die zen I can't vin zis tournament!" "You can always come back as one of the undead. Like a vampire or something…" "Don't be stupid," snapped Curiosity. "Vampires don't exist!" "And demons do?" Merrick left the question hanging in the air, and Doctor Curiosity's eager face turned somewhat solemn, but only for a couple of seconds. "So, you say that I'm going to die in just over a veek, jah?" "Jah, mein doctor." "And zat zere is nothing I can do to prevent it." "Nothing at all, unless you find out how to avoid the certain thing that will kill you." "So you know what this means?" asked Curiosity. "You want to beg for your life?" "No! It means zat until zat time… I am immortal!" "I think you're missing out on the entire definition of the word there, doc." "Oh, jah. Zen… I am invincible!" "You… you certainly could say that. You should enjoy the week while you can. That incident during your second ma… no! I've said too much again!" "Hah!" exclaimed the doctor, as the demon's face turned red. "You are a foolish demon!" "I know, I know…" Merrick's voice trailed off and he walked to the far corner of the room, leaving the doctor alone. It was a strange mix of elation and despair running through the thoughts of the GTT4 Champion. He believed that he would triumph against John Hitchin and progress to the second round, but he also believed that it could end up causing his own death, and what's more his soul being taken to Hell for all eternity. Yet, did he really believe all that? Merrick was notoriously untrustworthy, but he had never come out with any bold faced lies such as this. There must be a reason for him to be told all of this, but simple gloating did not seem to be enough of a reason. Nevetheless, he would have to be careful. This match would be one thing, but the subsequent one would be another entirely. He had just over a week to work out what Merrick was up to, avoid being killed and enslaved for eternity, and to work out a winning strategy for GTT6. It was almost too easy… … … … and across the room, speaking in a hushed voice, Merrick was talking to someone on his cellphone (Hell has surprisingly good signal reception. After all, every last one of those phone salesmen and customer service operatives go there when they die). "Yeah… yeah he did. He bought it, the whole thing. Hook, line and sinker. Just wait until next week, it'll be hilarious… yeah… absolutely. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy." The demon clicked the phone shut, and smiled the smile of the damned.
