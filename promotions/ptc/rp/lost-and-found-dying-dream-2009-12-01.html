Lost and Found: Dying Dream

1 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-01 Self-Aware You’ve always been too self-aware for your own good. Even before you were a wrestler, it was a problem. You’d notice how your arm was resting on the desk, or how your foot sometimes hit the chair in front of you. You’d spend hours reciting poems and song lyrics in the mirror just so you could see what you looked like when you spoke. There wasn’t a day that passed in your adolescent life that you didn’t analyze and overanalyze every little thing you did. Obviously being the star hero in a successful wrestling promotion has done nothing to ease your awareness. Now you have tape of both your matches and your backstage exploits to see exactly how you’re acting, and it’s merely lent itself to you being more cognizant of everything you do and say, and naturally that had effects on your relationship as well. It sounds terrible to say, but this may have made it so that your feelings weren’t natural. When you fought with Katie, or talked with Foster, or even pined for Sarah Riley, you weren’t sure if you actually felt that way. You just thought it was logical to feel that way, so you tried to. You went through the motions. Even then, you were watching yourself as you talked, trying to make sure you were acting the proper way. You haven’t been that way for the past three days. The cliche is, of course, that people going through tragedy put up mental blocks to prevent dealing with the pain, and that’s why a widower can look back years after the loss of his wife and not remember anything that happened in that small period. You always heard it and figured that you were too aware of everything to ever be like that, but sometimes there’s a reason the cliche is so cliche. Now your actions have been natural. They have been true. They haven’t been run under your own social microscope to make sure everything is right. Now you are real. And all it took was for somebody to die. Taking It Hard "Hey, Jon. Are you getting ready?" Foster Nackedy knocks a few times on the hotel room door before abandoning pretense and putting his ear to it. "Not much time left." A few seconds pass without an answer, and Foster nods, turning around and leaning against the door. He lets out a big sigh, which becomes a cough as soon as he sees Shweta Kallemullah standing in the hallway. "Hey, Shway," he says when the coughing fit passes. "Taking it hard?" She says simply, but judging by her baggy eyes and weary smile, this may not have been a question, although Jon Rhine’s trainer takes it as one. "I guess," he says, standing up straight. "Nothing like the boy in there, though. He hasn’t answered all morning, and it’s boring being in a hotel by myself." "And you think he’d be up to entertaining you?" Foster shrugs. "I know, he’s got this thing. But…I dunno. I hate seeing this. Jon has never shut himself off like this." "Never?" Shweta takes a few steps closer, leaning against the wall. "Not after a bad loss, or after the breakup…or, or after I…um…" She looks down, rosiness creeping into her cheeks. She doesn’t want to say it. Foster doesn’t want her to either. "Yeah, that stuff got him upset. And he shut himself away after those things, sure. But this is different. After those things, even after what you did, he was still functional. He was still whole. You could look him in the eyes and know that no matter how much time it took, eventually he would be all right." Foster looks at the door and shakes his head. "Now? I don’t think he will be. He’s shut himself off, and I’m not sure if he’ll ever be on again." Shweta nods, then looks past Foster down the hallway. Foster looks at her and chuckles. "What about you?" he asks. "What about me?" "I think most people know you’ve never been a big fan of Katie, even after you came into our group. Jon said you tried to break them up the day she died." "Wait a min—" she starts, but he doesn’t let her finish. "And it’s not like death is something that you’re not used to, seeing as you used to be such a high profile member of the Dead Man’s Hand. Death to you is like getting mail, happens 6 days a week." Shweta opens her mouth suddenly, then closes it. After shaking her head, she opens it again. "I’m not sure whether to be offended at your attack on my character or your terrible analogy. Both were pretty bad." Foster shrugs. "Dodging the question much?" "I’m not dodging anything," she says, stepping off of the wall and walking up to Foster’s chest, looking up at him with the sort of fire she stopped using when she quit the Dead Man’s Hand. "I didn’t think Jon and Katie were right for each other. That doesn’t mean I wanted the woman to die, Foster. Jesus, what kind of person do you think I am?" Foster’s response is automatic. "The kind of person who fakes a relationship to ruin someone’s career." Once again Shweta’s mouth opens and doesn’t emit a sound. She takes a step backward and looks down, causing her to miss Foster’s equally shocked face. When she does look up, she is too upset to notice it. "You…you’re not over it?" Foster closes his eyes, then heaves another sigh. "How do you expect me to be, Shweta? How do you expect me to think anything different when you hurt the two people you’re working with without a second thought? How do you expect any of us to trust you?" "And you bring this up now? Not before, when I came to you about coming back to SCCW with Jon? Not before we became so invested in each other?" Foster opens his eyes, but he looks the other way, determined not to look into her eyes. "I bring it up now because I see who you’re focused on, and I don’t want my friend getting hurt like I did. More hurt than he already is." Shweta’s eyes widen. "You think I…Jon?" She starts to shake her head, but the glare from Foster stops her. She decides to look down instead. The silence carries on for far too long. Foster finally turns and knocks on the door. "Hey, Jon, let me in, okay? Let’s have a chat." "Leave him alone," Shweta says, turning around, shielding her face from her former boyfriend. "He needs some time to himself. So do I." Shaking her head, she walks away. Foster watches her and once again leans against Jon’s door, sighing. For Now, Forever, For Always "Hey, Jon, let me in, okay? Let’s have a chat." It can’t really be called ignoring if he doesn’t hear it at all. Jon is sitting on his hotel bed, earbuds in, screwdriver in hand. "What are you hiding?" he asks lowly. He slowly works on the bottom of the laptop, placing the screws on the table as he goes. Removing the hard drive from the burnt laptop, he holds it up and examines it. "Please let this work," he mutters. After a few minutes, he successfully screws the hard drive into his own laptop and plugs it in to the power cable. "Work, work, work, work." Jon looks down at his hand and blushes when he sees his fingers are crossed. Uncrossing them quickly, he rests his hands on the palmrest of the computer and waits for it to recognize the hard drive. As he waits, he repeats the question that has been nagging him since he found out about this laptop. "What are you hiding?" he asks again, and this time a sudden voice answers. "Why are you so interested in finding it?" Jon looks up suddenly to see Katie standing in front of him, wearing a black dress. He blinks at her for a few seconds, then puts the laptop to the side. "I’m trying to find out more. There’s a reason you saved this laptop." She nods, then walks towards him and sits on the bed. He can smell flowers, and the smell brings a smile to his face. She matches it and says, "Yes, there was. So?" His smile drops. "So? So I need to find out what that reason was." "Why, Jon? Why is it so important to break into my laptop and disturb my privacy like that? You’ve never shown any interest in looking at it before. Why now?" Jon looks up at Katie, dumbfounded. "Are you serious? Why now? Now because you—" He stops as a beep from the computer stops him. "This folder’s named Katie. It’s password protected." Katie offers him a smug smile. "Yes, it is." He stares at her for a few seconds, waiting for her to elaborate. When she doesn’t, he sighs. "And you aren’t going to tell me what the password is." "I don’t have to. You already know." He looks down at the keyboard for a second, then furrows his eyebrows. "Is it…?" His fingers stroke the keys slowly, as if he doesn’t want to type. He presses enter and hears a satisfying click, but has to fight back tears. "katierhine…" "Cheesy, isn’t it?" she says, grinning. She reaches out to pat Jon’s arm, and he shivers at her touch. "Please don’t cry." Nodding, he runs a hand over his face, then clicks and begins looking at the folder. "Dear Jon?" He asks, looking at the document. "This is dated November 23rd, the day…" "Yes." "Why did you write me a letter that day?" She shrugs. "I’m sure the letter explains it. You’re already there. Just read it." Clearing his throat, Jon begins to read the letter aloud. "‘Dear Jon, hopefully when you read this I’ll be watching you read it with a smile on my face.’" He looks up at Katie, whose smile grows. "Well, I was right, although I had other circumstances in mind." Shaking his head, Jon continues. "‘Hopefully that means we’ll have gotten over all of this mess and started working on being a family again. And that’s why I’m really writing this, why I want to try, why I hope you come home tonight. Family.’" Jon looks up at Katie and raises his eyebrows. "Family?" Katie chuckles. "Have you always been this impatient? Read the rest." He looks back down at the laptop. "‘I am sitting here as I type this, and all I have to do is look to my right and see little Connor, working on a cake for you, to see what is important. Family. Love. Having children, Jon. I want to take that next step. I want you to use that ring you’ve been hiding from me from the past few months.’" Jon looks up, his eyes widening. "You knew?" Katie nods. "Of course I knew. I just didn’t know why you never pulled the trigger." Reaching into his coat pocket, Jon pulls out the ring box and looks at it, then looks up at Katie. "I guess I shouldn’t have let you do all the laundry." "Or, you know, take things out of your pockets before putting them in the hamper," Katie scolds, though her smile shines brighter than ever. "‘I want to be with you, Jon. For now, forever, for always. Please make me your wife. Please make me a mother. Please make us a family.’" Jon finishes the letter, then looks up with tears in his eyes. "I…I wanted to, Katie. I wanted to." "I know." After a moment, Jon’s mouth becomes a snarl, and he closes the laptop quickly, shoving it to the side. "You killed yourself for this? You went back into the house to save a love letter? Do you have any idea how stupid that was?" Katie closes her eyes and says nothing, and this angers Jon further. "All you needed to do was talk to me, Katie! Your life was more important than a stupid laptop! Why did you do that to yourself? Why did you do that to me? Why…?" He loses energy and bows his head, his chest heaving as he struggles to breathe. "That wasn’t the only reason," Katie says lowly. He looks up. "What else?" "Open the laptop." Jon considers saying no. His lips tremble, his fingers are shaking, and he starts to shake his head. But then, sighing, he grabs the laptop and opens it, then clicks the folder. "‘Dying Dreams’? What’s this?" He opens the document and stares. "You…when did you write this?" "That one?" Katie shrugs, looking away. "Probably when I was 22 or something. We had just met. It’s only so-so." Jon’s eyes begin scanning the monitor quickly. "It’s more than so-so. It’s good. I had no idea…" He clicks again and shakes his head. "There are so many. Are these all yours?" "Yep. I think there’s about four hundred in there. Maybe more, I stopped counting this year." He looks up again, the tears gone but a new look in its place. It’s hard to ascertain exactly what it is, but there’s a good chance that if you pointed at a random face on one of those emotion charts, it would be at part of his expression. "Why…why didn’t you tell me?" She stands up and smiles again. "Would it have changed how you felt about me, Jon?" Jon stares at the computer screen for a few minutes, thinking of his answer. When he looks up again, he is alone in the hotel room. "No," he says with a smile. "Not at all." What To Say There’s something that English professors like to call coincidental realism as a method in older, allegorical texts. The writers would use symbols to convey certain emotions. One of the biggest examples of this was the weather. When the author wanted to show that a character was in dark times, the sky would be cloudy, tumultuous. It’s been used so much that it is almost expected that the climate is going to reflect the mood of a particularly upset or happy person. Coincidental realism doesn’t exist in the world of Jonathan Rhine; it is a beautiful day for a funeral. The sun makes the arriving party forget that it’s Indianapolis in November, and Foster Nackedy takes sunglasses out of his coat pocket. Putting them on, he frowns as he looks out into the parking lot, watching the mourners mill about. Shweta Kallemullah comes from behind him and slips an arm in his. His grimace deepens, but he doesn’t move his arm. "Big turnout." He nods. "She was loved." Shweta shakes her head, looking at the people, many of whom are wrestlers. "I don’t think that’s the reason everyone’s here." Foster looks at Shweta, then moves his gaze to the lone man standing by the funeral home, only his blonde hair showing as he bows his head. "Tell that to him." As the former couple watch, the funeral director, a large man swathed in an overcoat, gingerly approaches Jonathan Rhine. They see him say something, and they see Jon’s gaze remain at his feet. Shweta sighs. "This is too much. I’m going over there, we need to do some—" "No," he says, holding her arm. "Like you said, this is time he needs for himself. You’ve been giving him advice about Katie for the past few weeks. It’s probably best for you not to remind him of that by talking to him right now." She nods, but her concern is still showing. "But what do we do?" "Nothing, Shweta. There’s nothing we can do right now except get through this day, and be there when he eventually needs us." He is looking at her as he speaks, but she is looking past him to Jon, who begins to walk in their direction. Foster shakes his head, but Shweta can’t help himself as Jon gets closer. "Jon, again, I’m so sorry," she says. Jon looks at her for a moment, then nods and keeps walking. By the time Jon reaches the gravesite, the congregation in the parking lot have started to make their way across the grass. Jon gets a few seconds of solitude before he is tapped on the shoulder. "Hey, umm, Jon?" Today even the normally boisterous Jared Sykes is solemn and reserved. When Jon slowly turns Jared doesn’t look up, instead keeping his eyes on the etched granite marker that bears her name. "I just wanted to say that I’m sorry. I’m so sorry. I can’t even imagine what this must be like, and… God, I never know what to say. It’s like I should say or do something, but I don’t know what would make it better." Jon matches Jared’s gaze, but after a few seconds of staring at the headstone, he looks up again. "I think this is my new home. He looks around, then looks back at Jared. "I really like it here." "That’s... Okay. Kind of an interesting, I guess." Jared quirks an eyebrow, and though his head remains bowed his eyes dart quickly to the left. "Not quite what I expected, but I guess I can understand where you’re coming from." "I don’t know, it just feels right." He shrugs his shoulders, then bows his head. "I guess I don’t really know what to say either." Jared’s laugh is quick. "Oh, thank God," he says, finally pulling his eyes away from the headstone. "I thought I was the only one. I’m normally so bad at this, Jon, like you don't even know." He puts a hand on Jon’s shoulder. "At least I haven’t said anything stupid. Well, not yet, anyway." "Yeah, and you don’t have to give a speech in front of everybody. I don’t get it, man. I talk in front of thousands of people in nearly every town in the country. Why can’t I think of a single thing to say in front of some of my closest friends...?" Jon stops, looking up and seeing Lane Stevens walking across the grass, a bag of popcorn in hand. "...and Lane Stevens." "It’s not the same thing." Jared sighs, trying not to be distracted by the overenthusiastic Stevens. "Being out in front of everybody, in front of a couple thousand people, it’s the easiest thing in the world. Doesn'’ really matter what you say or what you do. Odds are good that somebody's going to love you for it. Hell, look at that guy." He gives a quick nod in the direction of the River Rat and his bag of popcorn. "There’s probably even a few people who dig on what he does. But this?" Jared takes a step forward, turning towards his friend. He shrugs his shoulders, slides his hands into the pockets of his pants, and continues. "This is hard because these are the people who actually care about ya’. Like, the ones who really give a shit, you know? It’s hard because they’re the ones you’re worried about letting down, because you care what they think. I’ll tell you a little secret, though." Jared’s smile is bright, if not a little sheepish. "You could read the damn phone book and they’d still love ya’ for it." Another laugh follows, this one at his own expense. "You know what? I take it back," Jared continues. "I’m actually really good at this." "You are," Jon says, finally smiling. "I hope I’m half as good as you are when the time comes." Jared nods, then looks over his shoulder at the approaching Lane Stevens. "I think this is the part where I walk away for a bit, since I have to be honest with ya’. I don’t know what it’d do for my ego to get tooled on at a funeral. Seriously though, if you need anything - anything at all - just let me know. Whatever else there is between us, I’ve still got your back here. Now, if you’ll excuse me, I need to find a nice mausoleum to hide in." With that, Jared walks off hurriedly, leaving Jon with a few more precious moments to himself. This is, of course, ruined by the smacking sound of Lane Stevens eating popcorn. Loudly. "What’s wrong, Jon? You look like somebody died." Lane smiles, smacking popcorn. Jon sighs, shaking his head. "I don’t want to sound ungrateful for well-wishers, but why are you here?" Lane shrugs. "Desade wanted to send her condolences." He gestures to his body. "I’m one of the condolences." "And what are the rest?" Jon asks, fearing the answer to the question. He’s right to fear. Lane lifts the bag. "Popcorn?" Jon closes his eyes, praying for the patience to deal with this. "No thanks. Maybe you can offer it to some of the other guests." "I tried. See Clinton Sage, leaning against the tree? I offered the bag and he just glowered at me, so I walked away. Figured he’d tattoo something on my legs like he did to that one chick…I forget who…" He looks at the headstone and chuckles. "Oh yeah." "I don’t have the energy to fight you right now, Lane. I can’t do it. Why are you really here? Why is Clinton Sage here?" He looks up at sees a sea of black suits and dresses. "Why are all of these people here?" Lane shrugs. "I think that’s sort of a dumb question. They’re here because for some reason they like you. People don’t come to these things for the dead, they come here to pat the living guy on the shoulder and offer shallow words. At least I’m offering something tangible." He pops another kernel in his mouth. Jon looks at Lane for a second before holding his hand out. "That a boy!" Lane says, pouring out some popcorn. "Just look at all these people, Jon. All here to console you. It’s all a little bit silly, isn’t it? I mean, I guess it’s nice." Despite who it’s coming from, Jon nods. "Exactly, it seems all so ridiculous. Do these people care about Katie? Do they care about me? I mean, I know you don’t." He looks for confirmation to Lane, who shrugs. "But I mean, look." He points at a man walking with a pregnant woman. "That’s my childhood friend Jack. We went to school together. We went to wrestling academy together. Until I got called up to FUSE, we were inseparable. And I didn’t…" He sighs. "I didn’t even know he had a girlfriend." "Or that she was pregnant," Lane says. "But look at her. She’s sure got a bun in the oven." He pauses for a second before looking at Jon with a grin. "Get it?" Jon just stares at him for a second, unsure of what to do. Luckily, Foster Nackedy had been hovering near the pair, and he makes this his time to interfere. "Jon might not have the energy to fight you, but I sure as hell do, and I don’t care who sees. Go eat your popcorn in the corner and don’t bother him again." Jon doesn’t hear any of this. Lane is watching Jon too, ignoring Foster. After a moment, Jon whispers, "Bun in the oven…" then begins to laugh. It starts off as a small chuckle, but then quickly grows. Snicker, chortle, guffaw. Soon Jon is bending over, gasping for breath as he laughs. Foster and Lane look at each other. Lane smiles. "I love this guy. See you later, Jon. Let me know if you need any more condolences." He walks away, leaving Foster watching Jon with an odd look. After a moment, Jon recovers. He stands up, looks at Foster, and tries to smile. "Thanks, Foster. But that was a good one, you’ve got to admit." Foster shakes his head. "Okay, Jon. Whatever you say. Hey, hope you don’t mind, but someone wanted to see you before we got things started." "Kody!" Jon laughs as the American Eskimo dog gives a loud bark, nearly jumping into Jon’s arms. "I haven’t seen you in days, boy." He looks up to Foster. "Has he been okay?" "Don’t ask me, Shweta was watching him. I’m terrible with dogs." Jon wraps Kody in a big hug. "Hey, little buddy. Was Shweta nice? Was she good to you?" Foster shifts from one foot to the other, looking away. Finally, he puts a hand on his former student’s shoulder. "Are you ready? Everyone’s here, and the director said we could start at any time." After giving Kody one last big pet, Jon stands, nodding. "Yeah. I think I’m ready. Let’s go." A Dying Dream "Thank you for gathering here at Flanner and Buchanan, everyone." The funeral director speaks in a slow, labored tone. He has made similar speeches, but he can never be accused of sounding bored or predictable. The pain is evident in his voice as he looks at all the eyes turned downward. "Today is a solemn day, but your mere presence has certainly made it easier on the grieving parties. We are here to celebrate the life of Katie Malick. We do not mourn her early departure from us, but we focus on the great times. The happiness she delivered to everyone around us. The selflessness she showed. And the love she provided her friends and family every day." He makes a gesture, and Jonathan Rhine walks towards the front of the burial site, head lowered. "To celebrate her is the man closest to her. Jonathan Rhine." Usually with his name comes cheering, clapping, music. Today, there is only silence. And, after a moment, yelling. A few people look up at the noise coming from the other end of the cemetery, where a man is shaking and yelling at the top of his lungs. Kody begins to bark loudly, and the yelling suddenly stops. Jon looks at the man in the distance longer than anyone else, and the director has to tap him on the shoulder to regain his focus. "Sorry," he says, clearing his throat. "Katie was…she was a great woman. She had a lot of friends who cared about her deeply. She…" he stammers, looking around at the attendees nervously. "She always cared about making everyone happy, and she had a…she had a very good heart." A tear forms in the corner of his eye, and he bows his head. "I’m sorry, guys, I’m really bad at this." He looks up and scans the sea of faces. The first face he sees is Lane, who has a large grin on his face. Apart from Lane, most of the reactions are the same: sympathy. The reaction makes his face redden, and he is ready to stop. Then, he sees Jared Sykes. Jared nods, then taps his heart with his forefinger. Jon stares at him, then after a moment nods back. Then, he opens his coat pocket. "I’m sorry. I have a really hard time finding my words in a situation like this. That’s why…that’s why I’m going to let someone else’s words speak for me." He pulls out a piece of paper littered with wet spots, then looks at it for a second. Then, he looks down and begins to read aloud. A Dying Dream You reached for me once. You held your hands out, offering everything And asking nothing, just wanting to grasp my fingers And pull me closer to you. I kept my hands in my pockets. I told myself it wasn’t right You weren’t right We weren’t right And I kept my eyes ahead, on the future, On a looming dream. You didn’t have my hands or my eyes. You hugged me once. Brought me to you, squeezed gently, And lent your warmth without wanting any in return You pulled me closer to you. I kept my hands in my pockets. I waited for the clutch to end, Imagining cell walls, anaconda coils, An endless traffic jam And I kept my eyes ahead, on the present, On a passing dream. You didn’t have my arms or my eyes. You loved me once. Overpowered me, drowned in sentimentality And finally asking something of me. You wanted me closer to you. I reached out. It was right You were right We are right And I keep my eyes on you, Away from that dying dream. You have my hands, my arms, my eyes, And my heart. Jon looks up, his eyes full of tears. As he looks out in the crowd, he sees his reaction is not uncommon. Jared Sykes puts his hand over his eyes, and, standing next to him, Amy Campbell follows suit, looking down with her hand covering her entire face. Shweta does not hide her tears; she wails as Foster puts his arm around her, bringing her closer. Jon looks down again. "Katie wrote this." He waits a moment for that to sink in, and then nods. "It’s surprising, because to most of you, she wasn’t a poet. To most of you, it’s hard to tell what she really was. I guess she was just a valet, or your friend’s girlfriend. To me, she wasn’t a poet either. I had no idea. I found all of this after she…after everything." He looks up, and the tears have been conquered. Like an opponent, like anything in his way for the past few years, he defeats them. "To me, she was not a poet. To me...to me, she was so much more. She was everything. And she always will be." He looks down again at the poem, then tucks it into his pocket. "Thank you for your time." As he walks away, noise begins to clamor up again. People begin talking, murmuring, and Jon walks towards Foster and Shweta. Before he can get there, Amy "Aimz" Campbell – one of his biggest rivals in wrestling, the reason he was forced from SCCW – walks up and wraps him in a huge hug. He looks at her, taken aback. "I hope it’s okay that I came," she says lowly, then turns and walks back to Jared Sykes before Jon can say anything. As he watches her go, he simply nods, then completes his walk to his friends. "Good job," Shweta says, reaching out and rubbing his arm. Foster looks from Shweta’s hand to Jon, then nods. "Very strong, buddy. I think you’re gonna be all right." Jon tries to smile, but realizes it isn’t working. "I think so too. I just had to get through today, right?" He gets two smiles as his answer. Bending down to pet Kody, Jon forgets everything else. Foster, though, still has a lingering question. "True, but what about tomorrow?" "What do you mean?" Jon asks, looking up. Foster shrugs. "What do you do from here?" After a second, Jon stands up, tucking his hands in his pockets. "I…don’t know." Shweta nods, then pulls him into a big hug as Kody puts his forepaws on the back of Jon’s legs. Foster watches this scene for a moment before wrapping his arm around the group. What Matters The day was a blur to you. You don’t remember why you even had the poem in your pocket, except you felt it was like carrying a piece of Katie with you. When you got home, you remember putting it in your jeans pockets and not removing it until laundry day. That’s about as much as you remember. For the next few days you thought a lot about the laptop, and about what Foster said, and you realized that they were related. You went back and counted, and Katie had written four hundred and sixty-four complete poems. There was a folder labeled incomplete that had another thirty-three. Almost five hundred pieces of writing that you had no idea existed. Katie never said anything, and you never felt the need to pry. When you first found them, you had an initial shock, and you were a little bit hurt that she never told you about them. But when that subsided, you realized it didn’t matter whether or not she told you about her poetry. What mattered was that it was a part of the woman that you loved, and whether you knew about it or not, it didn’t effect the way you felt about her at all. Similarly, when Foster asked you about the future, you were initially disturbed by your ignorance. With all of your self-awareness, you always have had an idea of what was going to happen next, even if something unpredictable happened. But after this, you literally had no idea what to do next. And, just like the poetry, you realized that didn’t matter either. Facing the future isn’t going to make a difference whether you know what’s coming or not. You thought you had it all figured out, and then something happened that made you realize you didn’t have anything figured out. But maybe having it figured out doesn’t matter. What matters is that you aren’t going to give up. What matters is that you’re going to live. And however unsure you are of the future isn’t going to change that. Just like Katie, not knowing about what you’re doing doesn’t effect the way you feel going forward. And how do you feel? Right now, you don’t feel anything. But you’ll feel okay soon. And that’s what matters.
