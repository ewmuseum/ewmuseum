All the World's a Stage

23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-23 .:: ::: ::. .:: Snow fell from the sky. It was dirty snow, ugly snow. But all of Russia was ugly right now, or at least the parts of Russia that were under Hitler’s boot. But that was why she was here. Nobody was going to take this territory without a fight, but it seemed like several million German troops had the funny idea that it would be a piece of cake. ::. .:: The Fiends had awakened when they had heard the tramp of boots coming across the Carpathians. The land had awakened them to defend it. And here they were now, Fiends amassing inside the stolid walls of dreary Stalingrad, digging in for a last battle. They would either defend their homes, or die trying to do so. It was the only way. ::. .:: A gunshot rang out in the night. She ducked behind the building, listening intently. The Germans had broached the last line of defense during the day, and now they crept into the city by legion, fighting for inches. That was where the Fiends came in. Even a Cossack soldier could only go so far before his exhaustion overcame him. The night time was the province of every Fiend that Russia could muster, slinking through the streets snapping necks and consuming the blood of Russia’s would-be usurpers. ::. .:: She couldn’t say she would have complained about the circumstances. War meant fresh kills every night. War meant the chance to experiment without being held back by the meddlesome need to make sure that too many people didn’t disappear. This was war. If too many people disappeared, no one cared, save to try and replace the sheer numbers they had lost. ::. .:: It would have been more convenient, had it not been so dire. Fiends were rarely roused from their own business save when the homeland was in danger, and this was certainly one of those cases. Russia was giving in, bit by bit. Somehow, the Germans had managed to find a way into the heart of Tzimisce country, a loophole, a convenient passage that was by chance unguarded. ::. .:: Truth be told, they’d taken the Fiends by surprise. The Germans had crept in, and once they had gotten past the first line of defense, they’d leapt out of hiding with their panzer and their blitzkrieg, and stormed across the Russian plateau undisturbed. Their main fault was that they got a little too cocky, a little too quickly. Now, they were in deep shit. The Fiends had circled around behind the army, closely following their advance into the motherland. And then, once the Germans had bogged down outside Stalingrad, the Tzimisce had slipped in unopposed. ::. .:: They slipped into the Russian camps in waves, strange figures clad in black robes, ghastly pale. But around the fires, gentle Slavic accents rolled off their tongues, and they spoke of freedom and comradeship. They were allies, strange and powerful creatures that had prospered lush in the forest holds of the Carpathians and beyond. It mattered not where they came from; what mattered was they loved this land as fervently as their Russian and Siberian comrades, and would defend it until they were dust. They weren’t bears like the Siberians, or wily like the Russians… but they were strange, unbreakable creatures, stoic, mysterious, and intelligent. They were accepted with nervous glances, but open arms. ::. .:: Now, the Tzimisce lay in wait with the Russians, waiting for the Germans to get cocky again, waiting for them to make another foolish advance on the troops, Kindred and Kine alike, that were massed within the walls of Stalingrad. It was a cunningly deceptive trap; to the Germans, it looked like the city had given up. It was nearly rubble anyway. But inside, thousands upon thousands lurked, scores of regiments of brutal, grim, and dedicated troops. They who defended this land were legion. Compared to the cold efficiency of the German troops, the Russians could not measure up. But to the Germans, Russia meant nothing. For the Russians, she was mother, not just a hunk of land. In Stalingrad, she held them close, nursing them until they were ready to strike back and drive out these strange invaders with their harsh-sounding language. It was not so much for them as it was for the sanctity of mother. The Germans had attempted to fracture something inviolable, and for it, they would pay, to the man if possible. ::. .:: Lia held her ground in the alley. Tonight, she was supposed to be up in the Tractor Works, sniping any German stupid enough to venture close. But they had a deadeye too. He was somewhere nearby, perched in the top of some ruined building. The moment she’d peeped her head over the top of the Tractor Works, he’d taken a hard potshot at her, and hadn’t stopped until she’d slipped back in the stairwell and gotten back to ground level. ::. .:: Obviously, the Sixth Army was planning on moving in on the Tractor Works tonight, and that one little sniper was providing cover. And he knew about her. That was perhaps the most important part. For the moment, Lia was struck with a huge amount of indecision. She could go up on top of the Tractor Works again, and risk her own hide to try and find him, or she could choose another position and take care of whatever the Sixth Army was doing tonight. ::. .:: It was at this moment that she was caught in such indecision. Her rifle was dead weight on her back. It should have been heavier, but preternatural muscles were not concerned with weight. She was behind this building, waiting for the sniper to quit shooting long enough for her to slip back up. He must think she was still up there if he was still firing off rounds. ::. .:: … Or he might just be providing cover for something else. ::. .:: Lia whirled around at the sound of footsteps scruffing in the gravel behind her. So, they thought they’d sneak up on her, hm? She whipped out her Skorpion. Thank God for preternatural sight. Her finger crushed down on the trigger, and a spray of bullets split the silence. They rained through the air like a hail from heaven, and by the time she finally let up, three Germans lay dead in the alleyway. ::. “Running around the Tractor Works at night is bad form, boys.” .:: She smirked down at them. A moment later, however, a pair of arms assailed her from behind, trying to tie her up. Obviously, he wasn’t quite prepared for her strength. She threw him to the ground easily enough, soiling his pretty little uniform. She pounced on him voraciously, tearing out his throat with her incisors, nearly gorging herself on the spray of blood that bubbled forth from his neck. ::. .:: She loosened her grip on the machine pistol, and that was just enough time for Mond to grab her from behind. She might have been strong enough to match a footsoldier, but he had the aided brute strength of Brujah breeding behind him. He easily pinioned her arms, but he wasn’t prepared for her fighting like a tiger. ::. “Let me go bastard!” .:: He couldn’t help but laugh at her. But Lia’s rage was nearing frenzy point, as she became a squalling tempest of legs and fists. It was all Mond could do to keep her in check. He did the last thing he could. Snarling, he sunk his fangs into her neck, beginning to draw out the blood that was sustaining her. ::. “You fucking fascist pig!” .:: The blood drain was beginning to weaken her now. Her voice split the night, rife with hatred for him, for everyone who was trying to take this land away from its people, the Fiends included. ::. “Feiger hund! Mach es dir selber!” .:: She went down after that. She hadn’t gone down without a fight, though. It was for that reason that Mond decided not to kill her. It wasn’t so much that she’d be useful on their side. She was a Fiend, after all, and Fiends were all half-crazy about their damn Carpathian wasteland. She’d never fight for them. But she would scream for them. Herr Mengele would see to that. ::. .:: :: ::. .:: Her eyes flew open. Her hands were still bound. They had been bound for days upon days upon days. She didn’t know how long she’d been here, but she knew that she hated it. She expected that was what they wanted her to feel about right now, and she hated them for playing her like a fiddle. ::. .:: She struggled against her bonds, but as usual, there was no change in their unblinking strength. The cloth cage that wrapped around her was strong, strong enough to hold a brood of tigers at bay. That’s what she feared the most—that they knew enough about her to keep her tightly bound at all hours of the day and night. ::. .:: She lay on the floor all night, unable to move. She had not the will to move. Her place was outside, defending Stalingrad, defending her home. And yet she lay on the floor of this dank prison, day in and day out. They never gave her any blood, not until the day was about to dawn, and she was reaching the point of frenzy from the Hunger. Then they would come, shining a light in her eyes that made her scream every time. Three vials of blood were injected into her every morning. They contained some sort of drug that made her sleep, faster than she ever slept on her own. ::. .:: Sometimes, during the day, she would wake up strapped to a table, bright lights shining into her face. She would hiss and curse frantically, but always, always, there was an orderly waiting nearby for the words. ::. “Darfen sie schlafen.” .:: Herr Mengele’s voice slithered through the blinding light, winding about her like Eve’s coiling serpent. And then she plummeted into darkness again. ::. .:: She didn’t know how long she’d been here. That was part of the ploy, part of the game. They were trying to make her go crazy, pushing her mind to the point where delusion took over. Apparently, they had no clue what type of mind a Fiend would already have. Then again, she wouldn’t have expected them to discover anything about who or what she really was, unless… ::. .:: Yes, her mind filtered back to that night. The man who fed off her and turned her in. He had told them what she was, given them free reign to ply their trade. He’d practically given Herr Doktor the scalpel and told him to have at it. If she ever found that bastard… but she knew she’d never find him, and empty threats were pointless. She didn’t even know if she’d get out of here alive (or unalive, as the case was, certainly). The only thing she knew for sure was that she was tenuously hanging on to being because Mengele wouldn’t want to throw away a golden opportunity like this. ::. .:: All night long, she lay unmoving on the floor, images of Stalingrad seething in her head. She didn’t know what had happened there, didn’t know if the Fiends and the Russians had successfully repelled the bastard Germans, or if the Germans were sitting in the middle of Stalingrad like fucking Kaiser Wilhelm at this moment. They were already occupying Prague like they owned it. ::. .:: God, how the thought of them sitting in the middle of Vaclav’s Vyserhad like fatted toads made her ill. It seized her with a violent urge to kill every German she could get her hands on. That was why she’d gone to Stalingrad to fight. They’d gotten their hands on Prague, and Bucharest, and Budapest, but damnit, it had to stop somewhere. So she’d packed up her Skorpion and walked into a Russian army camp just inside Stalingrad. They’d looked at her like she was manna from heaven once she’d shown them what sort of shot she was. And then they’d handed her a Mauser, fitted with a scope. It was vindicating, so vindicating to sit in the rooftops eviscerating Germans, one bullet per head; she imagined that every Jerry she killed put her one step closer to recovering Prague. ::. .:: A sharp noise out in the hallway snapped her to her senses. Bullets. Bullets mercifully spraying through the walls of this place. She wondered what was going on, but at the same time, she prayed for a release, prayed that someone would step through those doors and obliterate her, anything to leave this wretched existence. ::. .:: The door of her cell burst open. A feeble, shaking electric light poured in. It blinked on and off, as though the power was not supplied in a steady stream. In fact, the Americans had cut the supply lines late in the afternoon, and the alternate generator had been blinking on and off all night long. Standing in the door was a huge, hulking form who looked down on her. ::. .:: Her darkness-bleared eyes took a few long moments to adjust to the way the light came bright for a few seconds and then was smothered again. A familiar face, at which she croaked weakly. ::. “Sascha!” .:: The big man stepped into the room, looking down on her. She’d become thin, gaunt, and hadn’t had use of her gifts in a long time, he could tell. Nor had she been fed proper blood. Sascha Medvedev could not stand seeing a former comrade in such a state. He pulled her to her feet, ripping free the buckling that held her fast. Her arms almost stayed behind her back by habit, even though they were free. ::. “Lia Vlaszy, you are a sight for my eyes… I thought they had killed you, for quite some time.” .:: She wasn’t happy to see him. She was bewildered. ::. “What happened… out there?” .:: He paused for a moment, realizing that she had been in the dark for years. ::. “It was a bloody battle, but we pushed them out of Stalingrad eventually… that has been over for years. It’s 1945 now, Lia. The Americans are occupying, pushing through this territory. After the war in Russia ended, I joined up with a volunteer force. I’m not yet done making these bastards pay for raping Stalingrad.” .:: He paused. There was no reason to keep the secret from her. ::. “Vykos ran up on me one night… wounded badly in the street. I was mumbling, saying my goodbyes to everyone, wondering if you’d died in the street the same way. He heard me say the name Vlaszy, pressed the story out of me. When I told him, he Embraced me on the spot, and told me not to rest until I’d found you. It looks like I got here just in time. The Germans are under orders to burn all of Mengele’s facilities. This is the last one left.” .:: She weakly shook her head, as though trying to take in everything he said all at once, and assimilate that this place had stolen years from her life. She felt insulted that no one had informed her. ::. “I’m not done here. I have a few words I’d like to say to Herr Doktor.” .:: Sascha’s eyes narrowed. ::. “Mengele is here?” .:: She nodded. Sascha felt wrathful ire swelling in his chest. This was good fortune, the best fortune he could have hoped for. Just about every Kindred in the world was out to kill Mengele right now. ::. “He’s the one who’s been keeping me in such a pathetic state this whole time.” .:: Without another word, she stumbled out of the cell and into the hallway. There were dead men in Nazi uniforms scattered everywhere. It appeared that it had been a real slaughter across the facility—nobody had expected a sneak attack in the middle of the night. In fact, nobody even knew this place was here. Except the people who had a vested interest in it, such as Kindred. ::. .:: Lia’s feet guided her as though on autopilot, as though she had gone this route a million times. And she realized, suddenly, that she had. As the fog lifted from her eyes, she began to remember that she’d passed this way hundreds upon hundreds of times, strapped half-lucid to a gurney, on her way to be stared at under a spotlight by Mengele. ::. .:: Moments passed that seemed like eternities in and of themselves, but Sascha and Lia soon reached the place they were looking for—Mengele’s personal operating room, at the end of the facility, well hidden from everything else. Lia burst through the door with a feral growl, fangs bared. ::. .:: She found Herr Doktor scrambling frantically around, trying to save his records. They’d always been important to him, more important than anything else. Unfortunately for him, she thought, that would end up his undoing. She stood in the shadows, watching every ant-like little move. ::. “Herr Mengele… finally we meet, and under quite different circumstances than normal.” .:: He betrayed no sign of fear, and did not turn from his work. ::. “Lia Vlaszy. Was wollen sie?” .:: She shook her head, clucking her tongue. ::. “You are an inhospitable wretch, my friend. Fiends do not take kindly to being mistreated, especially when in someone else’s home.” .:: Mengele’s shoulders tensed. She figured he knew he was in deep shit. But him being who he was, he was probably also prepared. ::. .:: It all happened in slow motion. Mengele whirled around, slinging a grenade in her general direction, but she dodged. Within seconds, she had darted towards the good doctor and speared him viciously to the ground. Papers went flying everywhere, but she covered him as the grenade went off. He was useful, important prey. No sense having him blown to bits before she got her revenge. ::. .:: She remembered the scream like it was yesterday. Medvedev! He hadn’t seen the attack coming in time. He’d been watching out the door for others, and… but there was no time to worry about him now. There was a smoking, gaping crater in the floor where the grenade had exploded. Lia pulled Herr Doktor up by his collar. ::. “It is a shame you were always so devoted to your work that we never got to talk face to face. I assure you, I could have shown you techniques that would have had you cowering in your sleep just thinking of them.” .:: A slow, terrible grin grew on her face. ::. “In fact… I think I shall.” .:: She called up her Fleshcraft at a moment’s notice. Viciously, she wrapped her fingers around the skin of his nose, pulling the cartilage outwards. A sickening series of snaps echoed around the room as the skin fell to the side, loose. The good doctor only got out one scream before he passed out cold from the eviscerating pain. She grinned, and with a strength that did not at all fit her willowy feminine form, slung Herr Doktor over her shoulder, and disappeared through the hole that had been blown in the wall, into the misting, rainy night just outside of Dachau, the air still thick with ash from the smokestacks. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: Shadow stirred. Vaguely, she had the idea that she was somewhere unfamiliar, but then again, her mind had been somewhere unfamiliar. She knew that she’d been privy to another hallucination, but as usual, she didn’t know from whence it came. This time, it had been so involved, but still, it was nothing she had ever experienced before, nothing that made any sense to her. The smell of smoke lingered in her nose, just like that night at Dachau. Then, she reminded herself. ::. “Du varst nie zu Dachau.” .:: She sat bolt upright, realizing that she had just spoken German. The only problem with that was, she didn’t know how to speak German, never had. The best she knew was French, a smattering of Persian, and English. Cold fear ran through her. She hardly heard the voice speak from next to her. ::. “Ahhh, my goddess awakes.” .:: She narrowed her eyes, and turned her head. Next to her was none other than Gideon de Medici, stretched out on his side, regarding her calmly. He was shirtless, and a wine bottle no doubt containing vitae was clasped in his left hand. He shook a strand of hair out of his face, smirking at her. She jumped away from him. ::. “Gideon, what the FUCK am I doing here?” .:: He shrugged. ::. “Fate wants us to continue our association, my dear. I met you on the street, and we came back here. You confessed your lust for me, and the rest is history.” .:: She growled under her breath, aggression rising to the surface. ::. “Don’t bullshit me, charogne.” .:: His expression didn’t change at all. She wanted to beat that cocky smirk off his face with an axe, but unfortunately, she wasn’t equipped to do so. ::. “Such strong words from such a pristine goddess.” .:: She looked like she was going to kill him if he didn’t tell her. So, he told her, with just as little tact as he told her anything. ::. “I came upon you in the streets, literally standing stock still as though lifeless, or in suspended animation. So, I was a gentleman and I brought you back here so nothing would happen to you.” .:: He dropped the act for a moment, and showed his concern. ::. “Another vision?” .:: She nodded, answering almost without thinking. ::. “So involved this time… It was during World War II, and I was in Stalingrad, and then… then, I was somewhere in Germany, outside of Dachau. Mengele, and… ugh.” .:: She shuddered. It had been so vivid, so real that she still, even now, smelled the stench of the charnelhouse lingering in her nostrils. It made her ill. ::. .:: It made her ill precisely until she realized where she was, and with who. She sprang out of his bed, indignant and angry once again, remembering the night before. ::. “What the hell are you playing at, Gideon, or whatever the fuck your name is?” “I never play games with beautiful women. Especially not you, Lia. I intend to keep you as long as I can.” .:: He stood up on her side of the bed, running his hand down her arm a little too familiarly. She jerked it away, not because he touched her, but because he’d called her Lia. She’d never told him THAT either, just like she’d never told him she was working for Julian Falcon. She snarled at him. ::. “Cut the bullshit. You’ve been digging up information on me from somewhere, and I don’t trust you one goddamn bit. For all I know, you could be setting me up, maybe even for that stupid ass—“ .:: She caught herself just before she spilled the details of the entire LaBatte assignment. Her eyes widened. What the hell was wrong with her? She didn’t even know this smarmy little shit, and she’d almost compromised the security of her assignment to him! She had to leave, she had to leave right now. Gideon looked at her imploringly. ::. “Gideon, if you say one more word, I will rip your face off and have it for dinner!” .:: She didn’t wait for an answer. She stormed out of Gideon’s tastelessly Bohemian flat and rampaged down the stairs, in a flurried state of anger. Gideon didn’t follow her. There was no point. He stepped out onto his balcony, his fine, dark features lit up by the moon-drenched sky. Down in the street, he could make out her lithe, dangerous form, stalking away. He shook his head as the breeze wafted through his long brown locks. ::. “I’m trying so hard, Lia… and we were so close this time.” .:: ::: ::. It was almost dawn, and she had to find somewhere to go. Sunlight wasn’t too tempting a prospect, even though she was very much starting to wish she could just burn herself to a crisp and get this craziness over with. ::. .:: Confusion racked her body. An hour before, it had been cold fear. Once she’d left Gideon’s place, it had overcome her, by inches. Her feet had pounded across the pavement as she ran, running from what she didn’t know, but running nonetheless, harder than she’d ever run in her entire existence. Her terror had carried her all the way to the edge of the city, far from Riven, and far from Falcon, and far from LaBatte and Gideon too. ::. .:: Slumped in this alley, paranoia slunk through her veins insidiously. Was everyone wanting to see her fail? Falcon pushed her too hard to do everything on his time. Riven pushed her to accept a past that was holding her down. Gideon pushed her to accept a future that was built on dust and illusions. She couldn’t do any of those three things, and she didn’t know why nobody understood, or even tried to. Suddenly, she started to wonder if she was corporeal at all, or if she was just a wraith, drifting freely in and out of being only when it was convenient for someone to call her forth. Serving at their beck and call—that was her lot. ::. .:: So throwing it all away is the answer? Another voice railed at her. What are you going to do, give up everything you’ve worked for in PTC and WMW just to make other people happy? Other people who will ditch you as soon as you’ve served your purpose and you’re not useful to them anymore? Fuck that, Shadow! You’re not a puppy. You’re not rotten leftover food. They can’t get rid of you, they have no right! That’s why you have to stay with WMW. That’s why you have to push yourself as hard as you can possibly go to get past Jonathan Sail in GTT4. Maybe nobody thinks you can do it, but nobody matters but you. Taking that view every once in a while doesn’t make you selfish, it makes you normal. You have to look out for you, because otherwise, people will just rip off your head and shit down your throat without a second thought. ::. .:: Yeah, said a third voice. You have to be realistic here. Have you seen Sail? He’s doing the same thing you are, struggling towards the same goal. In fact you might even say that you guys are following similar paths. He got distracted by something that tore him away from what he loved, his original path, and you got pulled away from what you deserve by a past that people won’t let you forget. But he’s letting go of his past. Why can’t you? If you don’t do it too, then you’ll be fucked with a capital F. Sail isn’t going to go easy on you, especially now that his focus is coming back. Get it together, or this is it for you. What are you going to do, be an Assassin all your life? Dime a dozen. Make a name for yourself, kid. ::. .:: Reassurance started to surge through her. What did it matter if her visions were real or not? They felt real enough to be troubling, that much was true, but the other truth was that they were more useful as messages than memories. Wherever they were coming from didn’t matter, but what they were telling her did. She had to do this, and she had to do it for her. ::. .:: But before she could become convinced that was the point at all, one last crushing voice weighed down, silencing all the other voices as though they were tiny bugs lying with carapaces cracked in half on the dirty ground. ::. .:: Shadow, it said… nobody cares who you are. Nobody has ever cared, and maybe it’s time you realized why. It’s because you mean nothing. Ever since you showed up in PTC, all you’ve done is take orders from somebody. What makes you so special? It doesn’t take talent to take orders. It takes talent to forget about that shit, but the sad part is, you’re not strong enough to do that, even though you’re deluded enough to deny it. ::. .:: People pump you up, but they pump you up hard for a fall. And that’s what you’re about to take this time, with Sail. A fall. He’s not a tournament favorite for no reason. You know you’re going to lose, so why are you sitting here, wasting your time preparing like this is a matter of life and death? I mean, let’s put it this way: If it IS a matter of life and death, you might as well consider yourself burned to cinders in the pretty morning sunshine. Let’s just face facts: you don’t have what it takes. You got into this business because you had something to prove, and you’ve done nothing but wallow in mediocrity, for years. ::. .:: She tried to protest, but the voice silenced her. ::. .:: What, do you want a feather in your cap for it? Do you want a trophy for being neither good nor bad, and being terribly efficient and turning nobody’s head but your own? Because, I mean, I could sit here and tell you that you’re perfectly wonderful, and you don’t need anything but yourself to get through this match with Sail, but we both know it’s a lie. You can’t even keep your damn focus long enough to figure out what the fuck you’re doing. You don’t know up from down or left from right. So why do you expect anyone to take you seriously? There’s nothing to take serious. What you need isn’t advice from Amir or Vaclav, or Riven or Gideon. What you need isn’t a decent break, and a clean chance to prove yourself. ::. .:: What you need is a miracle, and that’s why this is such a sad case. You know why? You’ve never believed in anything. You’ve always been too much of a coward to trust yourself or anyone else, too much of a yellow-belly to stand up for what you love, or fight for what you believe in. Everything that’s slipped away from you in this live has slipped away because of YOU. Y-O-U. No other reason than that. ::. .:: There’s no chance in hell you’ll beat Sail. Let’s get that straight right now. You better get used to it, but don’t kid yourself. You’ll probably just lose to him, and get disappointed like you always do, and then sit there, wallowing around in your own slime for a few weeks before you get up and give it another go. You know, perseverance might be a great trait, but there’s also something to be said for knowing when to just throw in the towel. Uncle, Shady. ::. .:: A crushing despondency sank down over her entire being like a fog. She wrapped it around her like her shroud, letting it choke the life out of her. Riven told her one thing, Gideon another. Vaclav told her yes, Amir told her no. The only true thing lay somewhere in between the two extremes, and that was what she had just told herself. ::. .:: With war-weary limbs, she stood up, in the back alley of this seedy, worthless Italian restaurant. She slipped in the back door of it in the inky 4am darkness, down the stairs into the wine-cellar. They opened their doors at 7 every night, but she’d be long gone by then. She’d be gone as soon as dusk fell. She sank to her knees in the back of the cool, dusty room, casks of wine stacked in a barrier around her. Bottles of wine slumbered in their racks. ::. .:: If she were a praying woman, she would have prayed. But every prayer she’d ever been forced to memorize suddenly escaped her memory. ::. .:: I told you, I told you it would come to this, said the voice, her voice. ::. .:: Her weary spirit surrendered. The black river of Fate swirled around her in the darkness, whispering her name seductively. The idea of defeat weighed heavily on her shoulders, stooping her like an old crone. It shouldn’t have. She should have been used to it by now. She had been dealt blow after blow handed defeat after defeat, but had still stood up again, like a martyr. She expected defeat now. But whether or not she would, or could choose to raise her bloody brow again for the next battle was something she wasn’t sure of. She had no choice—the river called to her still, relentlessly. ::. .:: It was the final act in her passion play, she knew that now. Jonathan Sail was going to put an end to whatever she had proven thus far, and that was going to be the final curtain. She’d be forever snuffed into the oblivion of nothingness that awaited her, with gaping maw. She could resist no longer. Her final act of dignity was one of choice. She wouldn’t play Russian roulette. If she was going to be obliterated by some unseen hand, she would choose the time and place. ::. .:: And so the final curtain fell, the guillotine of God. ::. Fin.
