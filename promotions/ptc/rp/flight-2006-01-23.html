Flight

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-23 I have no way of knowing whether I’ve actually killed anyone. I know that I’ve been responsible for a few deaths, and have certainly shot men who have then gone on to die, but whether the bullet that changed it from an injury to a fatality was from my gun… I will never know. All I know now, all that I can think of is getting back to a place where I can meet Shanty Jones again. Knowing that he placed the bomb, knowing that he was the one who killed me and nine others, I cannot rest here in whatever strange state of limbo this is. I have been here ever since the nails tore through my head, on their way to wreaking destruction throughout the police station in Basra, Iraq. I have been able to see into the past of Shanty Jones, and to be told, piece by piece, the story of his life. There’s been no order or sense to it, but I now know more about him than I probably do about myself. Little by little I saw the jigsaw coalesce, moving towards a point where something would have to be revealed, and the purpose of the charade would become clear with it. I have now seen all but one of the parts of his life, the solitary instance when he was 17 and converted to Christianity, a spirit which he would obviously grow to lack. Last week that moment of revelation came, and it was shown to me that Shanty Jones had somehow planted a bomb that killed men from his own unit. Until then I thought he had been a friend, and a good man. Now I know differently, and it burns my soul, because I cannot do anything about it. Or at least that was what I once thought. Now I feel differently, because I have not only been seeing Shanty Jones. I have been given visions of others, such as my brother Artemis, and Shanty’s absurd manager, Doctor Curiosity. That is not all, as I have seen things that have never happened, and can only be in the future. I have seen Shanty and I in a room together, arguing, and I know that this is not from my time on Earth. This gives me hope, because I know that this can’t be coincidence. This has finally given me an answer as to why I, Steven Atkins, have been taken through someone else’s life and given what can only be a second chance. I know that I am witness to these moments because I am the one who will right the wrong. I will find a way back, and from on high I will swoop down on Shanty Jones, a chorus of joy filling my ears as I strike him down, an angel of vengeance sent from the dead to come and claim him as one of our own. I will kill Shanty Jones, and pure happiness will fill my veins. Only then will my time be complete. *** Until then, my search goes on, looking for a way to exert influence across the great divide between life and death. I still don’t seem to have any control over where I go, or what I see, and it’s as likely to be Shanty doing something inconsequential as it will be another clue as to how I can escape, and complete my divine mission. So I can see the murderer, the terrorist sitting back on his bed in his hotel room, looking relaxed and refreshed. His match against Kasidy Drake had not gone as he had expected, and instead of facing a motivated and deadly competitor is was almost like someone going through the motions. Jones had won with ease, and now he was staying in a slightly more expensive location, the brief fame afforded to him allowing for an improvement in conditions. There was also the small matter of him having been banned from the previous hotel, because of… "Jah!" said Doctor Curiosity, from the other side of the room. Shanty rolled his eyes and lay back, staring up at the ceiling. Having to spend time with this demented man was starting to really get on his nerves. It had begun as a favour to Paul Goodman, the doctor’s incarcerated brother whom Shanty had gotten to know whilst inside prison. From there it had appeared as if Curiosity was trying to recapture his glories from GTT4, constantly hanging around Jones, despite the obvious animosity between the two. It had all come to a head just before the third round match, with Curiosity confusing a judge enough for him to sign a restraining order… but one that prevented Shanty Jones from actually leaving the doctor’s company! "Jah!" said the doctor again, as a test tube of bubbling liquid caught on fire. Shanty closed his eyes. It had been the madcap doctor’s experiments that had set fire to the previous hotel room, and he had very little interest in a repeat occurrence. "Stop that now or I stop it for you," murmured Jones, his light Welsh accent easily distinguishable. "Stop? Nein! I am near a breakthrough! I vill soon be able to turn gold into lead!" "You’ll be… what?" asked Shanty, sitting up and rubbing his hand over his tight crew cut. "I vill be turning gold into lead! Jah!" "Shouldn’t it be lead into gold?" "Nein! I perfected zat art years ago! Ze real challenge is turning it back." Sighing, Shanty stood up, walked over to the miniature laboratory desk that Curiosity had brought with him, and crushed the test tube under one of his large, combat issue boots. "Enough," said Shan. "But…" Jones fixed Curiosity’s eyes, and for once the doctor ha the wisdom to remain quiet. *** From there I was whisked away, to be a witness to something different. I was at the prison, the one where Shanty and Paul "Eegor" Goodman had spent a lot of time together. Paul had been the only person that Shanty had been able to get along with, and they had worked themselves an understanding that was mostly to the benefit of Jones. As a man serving a mere twelve months in jail, as opposed to a life sentence, it had been quite the break to have someone of the reputation of Paul Goodman look out for you. Quite why it had happened puzzled Shanty, but never enough for him to question his friend. Now though, there was a different mood. Something was happening. Paul was standing in his cell, but around him various people were scurrying around like rats aboard a sinking ship. Something was happening, but before I could fathom any of it, the familiar tug pulled at the base of my stomach, and I knew I was headed to somewhere else… …but just as my presence left there, I could have sworn that Paul Goodman looked up at me and smiled. *** On to Artemis, and the constant shifts were getting wearisome, but I had to trust in what was guiding me, trust in whatever force was creating this mission for me. Artemis Atkins was my brother. He’s got his crazy name whilst I was saddled with the very normal ‘Steven’. I’m not sure what my parents were trying to do, but it might have involved a large supply of illegal narcotics. That would certainly have explained my younger brother’s lethargy and general disdain for the world. He lived in London, England, putting in just enough effort to his office job to avoid getting fired. There are thousand like him, millions perhaps in the world. They feel disenfranchised by the corporations and the high flyers. It’s as if the world they’ve grown up in has changed from being about fun and games to being about survival of the fittest; a new Darwinism fought in boardrooms and trading floors instead of arenas and hunting grounds. This wasn’t to say that Artemis was any sort of bad person. He was kind to others and tried to make the office a lighter place, even when he was avoiding work and feigning illness. I did love him, and I still do. I keep on telling him this, but he doesn’t believe me. I guess that’s understandable, considering what has happened. *** I was watching Artemis watch television, which is not the most exciting thing to do, especially with his taste in useless reality television and frankly disturbing pornography. I was trying not to pay attention to him, but it was hard. I felt like someone was constantly turning my head to point at him. Perhaps there was something that the powers, or God, or whoever wanted me to see? I tried to look away, but my eyes, or the recollection of my eyes, for I was not corporeal, were pried open. I drifted, staring, towards him, and that’s when I noticed something. Time had stopped. The television was static, frozen on an image of an F-List ‘celebrity’ trying to win the hearts of the public. I was the only thing moving, and I drifted towards my brother with at first a sense of fear, but then something that grew to elation. Was this it? Was this my conduit back to reality? "Arty!" I tried to yell, to let my brother know that I was there, but it had no effect. I was drifting towards him and then I was inside, and it was the strangest experience of my life. And I’ve died. If you’ve ever heard the phrase that says some people are not comfortable in their own skin, then imagine how it feels to be in someone else’s. It started off as a numb feeling in every extremity. I was confused and disoriented, but only for a couple of seconds before the blast of emotion hit me. "whoareyouwhat’shappeningwhatthefuckgetoutgetoutgetout!" He seemed to pick out what was happening so quickly. I have to say that his screams made my blood run cold, or rather, made his blood run cold. I was just borrowing his body, just for the one last job; to make amends. Artemis was still there as colour seeped into my eyes, as time restarted and I stared out from my new home. I’d been expecting something of a struggle, a need to enforce myself over his personality, to subdue him and fight in some way to take over, but in the end it was easy. His voice and his anger still bit at me from somewhere between the back of my head and my heart, but I was immune to it. I was alive again, in possession of a body and the very ability to feel took my breath away. Having been a floating entity for so long, I had forgotten what it was like to be able to touch things, to be able to caress the dirty fabric of a never-washed sofa, to smell the vitality of food, or plants that were also alive. I was so overcome I went straight to the kitchen and ate practically everything in there. Even the food past its expiration date (and there was a lot of it) tasted marvellous. I just had to sit back, patting my new stomach, and revel in it all for a few minutes, before my mission again came back to my mind. Ignoring the pleas from my mind, and assuring Artemis that I was doing this out of necessity, I found his wallet and then dialled a couple of airlines. I got a decent deal on a flight out to America, to Washington DC. After all, I didn’t want to waste too much of my brother’s money. I packed a small bag and waited impatiently. I didn’t have to leave for another ten hours, but I was impatient, knowing that I was on the right path, following the correct trail that would lead me to my betrayer, to the terrorist known as Shanty Jones. I’m not sure how long I was just lying down on the bed, smiling at the thought of my vengeance, but at some point I must have fallen asleep, and things got even stranger. *** I could see the room of Shanty Jones, and could hear Doctor Curiosity making worried noises from inside the closet, where he had been locked. There were candles in the room, and they cast what seemed like an unnatural glow across the prosaic room fittings. In the flickering light I could see Shanty drawing on a piece of slate. He was putting down some lines that intersected at regular intervals. I couldn’t get a clear sight but it looked like a pentagram. Shanty put down the chalk and silently walked over to a large, leather bound black book. He opened it to one particular page and mumbled some words that I didn’t quite catch. As he did so, the handle of the door turned, and with a creak the portal was open. I was standing on the other side. *** I awoke with a jolt, surprised at what I had seen, and then equally surprised to find myself still in control of my brother’s body. Checking the nearby alarm clock, I saw that it was time for me to get ready and head off to the airport. As I heard my brother’s frustrated screams echoing in my head, I couldn’t help but remind him that at least he wasn’t going to be returning to the office again any time soon. For some reason, it didn’t seem to ease his pain. The rest came as a blur; the ride on the Underground and then on a train to get me to the airport, and then the waiting, even that passed me by quickly. Within what seemed like minutes I was sat in my chair, browsing through the in-flight magazine and waiting for take off. During the flight I was nervous as to whether I could legitimately fall asleep again or not. The scenes I had witnessed in Shanty’s room were genuinely frightening, and I wasn’t sure that I wanted to see them, or anything like them, again. On the other hand, the information could be incredibly valuable. I was entering the lion’s den with no preparation and no weapons other than my body. It was only then that I realised that would be a problem. Before I died I had been in fantastic physical shape, kept lean by the fanatical army training regime and a diet of terror and bullets. My brother was quite the opposite. Whilst not genuinely obese, he was carrying a few pounds that he shouldn’t have been, and didn’t seem to have worked out in a few months… or possibly years. In the end I decided to drift off, because forewarned is forearmed, or whatever your choice of cliché is. *** I saw Shanty in his room again, as I slept, but there was nothing forthcoming. The book was nowhere to be seen, and the only light came from the wash of the strip lights in the ceiling. Doctor Curiosity was in the bathroom, probably trying to turn toothpaste into C4 explosives, and I made a mental note not to discount him entirely. If I was going there to avenge my death, and the death of everyone Shanty betrayed, I need to be sure of myself. Sometimes the ones who look the most innocent will be the ones who lead to your downfall. I had fallen foul of Shanty’s deception once, and would not fall to any man’s deception again. As they served the food and I was brought out of my slumber, I smiled as I thought of Shanty, sitting in his room, preparing for his match, utterly unaware that high above the sea, fifteen hundred miles and closing, vengeance was hunting him down. *** I arrived at the hotel the following day, with a large knife in my shoulder bag, and butterflies in my stomach. Contrary to what the movies would have you believe, you can’t just walk up to the nearest convenience store and buy a gun, not even in America, and especially not if you’re officially on vacation there. Instead, I had decided that buying a few racks of kitchen knives and then selecting the best couple would do the trick instead. I had one short knife in my pocket, and the other was to be brought out only when I placed my other hand on the door and opened it up. The elevator seemed to take forever to go up, but I knew exactly where I was going, and I knew that time was not going to be an issue. I would make it to his room, open the door, and deal with Shanty Jones once and for all. He’d be bigger than me, but I would have the element of surprise, and a couple of handy tools with me. In retrospect I had thought of bringing a couple of crosses and some holy water, just in case, but even now, knowing what I know, I have trouble believing in all of that kind of thing. It seems crazy, but I’m still not sure I believe in God. I believe in something, that something which has given me my new gifts, but who or what it is seems immaterial. All that matters is that I cross that threshold and kill Shanty Jones. I reached the door to the hotel room and I opened it. Just as in my dream it opened, and just as in my dream Shanty was there, the book opened to a marked page and the slate with the drawing on it at his feet. I was expecting a quizzical look, but he glanced up with a smile, and said two words that drove a dagger to my stomach. "Hello Steven." I was confused, shocked, and suddenly very, very frightened. This was a man capable of murder, who was willing to die to make sure that I died too. This was a man who seemed to be in league with dark forces, and whose manager was a well publicised practitioner of occult arts. It wasn’t just that he could see within, and knew that it was me. It was that he had been expecting me. "I’ve come for you," I said, just managing to get the words out of a tongue that felt fat in my mouth. "I’ve come to pay you back for what you did." "Basra," said Shanty, nodding and admitting his crime. "You murdered innocents, Shanty. You betrayed your own kind and killed them as if they meant nothing to you. I’ve seen it all, Shan, and I’ve got to pay you back. Your blood will be spilt and your life ended as payment for your sins." "For my sins?" asked Shanty, seeming almost amused by my question. "You are not one to talk of sins." "And you are?" I half screamed. "I’ve done some things I’m not proud of, but I never turned my back on my friends! I never killed them, the brothers who I had sworn an oath to protect! I see you have no remorse at all, Shanty. For all that is good and holy in the world, you must die." "For all that is good and holy…" he echoed. There was a moment of silence, and then Shanty Jones fixed me with his eyes, and spoke in a whisper words that stopped my heart. "What makes you think you’re the good guy?"
