Welcome to Camp Cocytus: The End of Anarchy

17 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-17 This was supposed to be his time, damnit. It was the dream that even dreamers wouldn't dare entertain. Here was a man - a man who had slit his soul for the people he loved, the people who in turn showered him with nothing but hate - who had conquered the world in mere months. He had become the Global Champion, a feat that a handful of talent in a landscape of thousands could lay a claim to, and he had upheld its image with every fiber and strained sinew both his mind and body had to offer. And still they hated him. He had done the impossible, calling out the one man who inspired mortal fear within most sane and rational hearts, throwing the gauntlet at Angelo Deville's feet and beating him in his own tournament, the tournament he had since coined into a household name. Seymour Almasy, the man who had made the Global title an icon for the business, just another victim to the man that men and women knew as The Renegade. And still they hated him. In less than twenty-four hours the most anticipated match of Rich Rollins' storied career was scheduled to go up in flames as the single biggest disappointment in the history of professional wrestling. In the same tournament in which he had dispatched the champion of GTT3, he was destined to meet the protégé of the champion from GTT4; the poetic bookends to an incredible winning streak that knew no equal. Then perhaps it was poetic justice that saw Rich Rollins marching to his death with a smile on his face. ~<>~<>~<>~ Three days earlier… For a man who considered himself a recluse by nature, sitting in solitary should have been the most enjoyable punishment Rich Rollins had ever been forced to endure. With nobody to scream at him or spit on him or socialize with him in any way, it should have been the perfect means in which to spend the last four days of his time on this earth. The only problem was, it was the last four days of his time on this earth. Packed into a big metal box scarcely larger than a telephone booth, Rollins had spent the first five hours of his mandatory seclusion in silence, staring out of the mailbox slot his jailors used to slide him food. Or they would have anyway, had Warden Hammond not instructed every officer on his staff to ignore Rollins until further notice. Instead he watched, gazing with impunity through his one tiny window to the world, watching his fellow inmates enjoy what little freedom they were allowed without giving in to the base emotions his tormentors were trying to use against him. They wanted to break him before they bought his ticket to the next world, but Rollins would be damned if he gave Hammond that kind of satisfaction. By the twelfth hour he had started to notice the smell. The uncomfortably cramped quarters that he now called his home had but one plastic bench and a metal drain pipe that led into the soil, and it didn't take long for Rollins to figure out what the drain was used for. Nobody came to check on him, not even bored guards trying to fuck with him while walking their patrol, so nobody was around when The Renegade needed to do what most people did at least once a day. Between the appallingly barbaric excuse for a toilet and the unrelenting sun perched inside its cloudless sky, the feculent funk of his own waste began to sizzle in the oven his world had been reduced to, forcing Rollins to tear his prison uniform into small strips in order to both clean himself and provide a barrier that he could hold against his nose. By the time the sun had finally set he was slumped over in nothing but his pants, boots and his sweaty bandanna, the faded heat becoming a blessed release. Sleep came slowly and in intervals that denied him any true rest. He almost didn't want to sleep because it would be too easy; his last living moments may have been within the confines of a steel box with nothing but his own feces to look at, but damnit, they were his last moments. He didn't want to waste what little time he no longer had, but he couldn't fight it forever. Exhaustion finally won over. "Rollins, I want to ask you something." He jerked awake, eyes bloodshot and wild, stealing a glance at the mailbox slot in confusion. Two eyes peered back in at his small and smelly hell, and at first The Renegade wasn't sure if he were still dreaming. The eyes spoke again, and Rollins knew it was for real. "The Warden doesn't want to grant you the right to your final meal, but a lot of us think that the way he's treating you is criminal, no pun intended." It was Priess, the only prison guard Rollins hadn't grown to hate. "I've talked to the cooks and they're willing to fix you anything you want, anything at all, and I'll sneak it out to you the night before… you know. So, what do you want?" Rollins' throat felt like a blender that had puréed raw sewage, but he still manage to work his jaw enough for coherent conversation. "Anything I want?" "Absolutely." The eyes smiled at him; the kindness nearly evoked a rare emotional moment from the soon-to-be barbequed Global Champion. "I don't suppose I could bum a smoke while I'm at it?" he asked, only half joking. "It's not like I have to worry about dying of cancer, you know?" They shared an awkward laugh, neither man finding the remark particularly amusing under the circumstances. "No problem," Priess said as he stuffed a pack of Newports through the slot. "I'll even throw in a lighter so long as you promise not to set yourself on fire. Cancer would be a blessing compared to what Hammond would do if he knew I'd been here." It was then that something clicked in Rich Rollins' brain. "Fenton," he whispered before he could stop himself. "The old man?" The eyes turned quizzical as they tried to adjust to Rollins' dark world. "What about him?" "Nothing," Rollins quickly amended. "I just… could you ask him to see me out here before I go? Just want to say goodbye to the roommate I never had." "I can try," said Priess. "Do you want a day to think over that meal?" Oh he knew what he wanted all right. It was suddenly as clear as the hatred he held for the man who had put him in this shit barn, born from his most primal survival instincts. "A t-bone steak, medium rare," he said with a grin. "Maybe a side of noodles with some alfredo sauce and a cold beer if you can manage." "I can get you the beer but it may not be cold," Priess replied. "The rest is no problem." Rollins sighed with relief. His future wasn't looking nearly as dim as it was five minutes ago. "I can't tell you how much I appreciate this, Priess." The eyes smiled again, genuine as leather. "It's the least I can do. My kids love you." When Priess disappeared The Renegade picked up the Newports and lit his first cigarette in nearly five days. His devilish grin split his face from ear to ear. He was about to eat the most liberating meal of his life. ~<>~<>~<>~ Ten minutes until execution… People were always saying that they wished you could walk a mile in their shoes. As he walked what he supposed was Camp Cocytus' own version of the "green mile" - the expanse between his final holding cell and his date with 'ol Sparky - Rich Rollins wondered if anybody would have been willing to walk this mile and see how it felt. It wasn't the most pleasant experience to have an audience cheer you on as you danced a tango with eighty thousand volts of electricity. Officer Luke Barks was the one to escort Rollins to his fate, and it didn't surprise him in the least that Hammond chose the most heartless asshole in the entire prison to be the one to throw the switch. It was more like a distant nightmare for Rollins than an actual execution; the once-vibrant blues and yellows that adorned Cocytus' walls seemed somehow faded, held behind the barrier of Rollins' suspended belief, refusing to submit to the fact that this was actually happening. But he knew it was. The minute he walked into the room itself the entire scenario sunk home with such force that he wanted to throw up the remains of his t-bone steak. At least the food had been spectacular. The first thing he noticed as he entered that room were the faces of his victim's family, a mournful collection of bleary eyes and runny noses that squared on Rollins like a searing salvo of heat-seeking missiles of shame and regret. Detective Noel Newton's sister and her husband sat in the first of seven neat little organized rows of folded chairs, followed closely by Cynthia O'Doyle's parents and a man who Rollins couldn't even try to place. They all looked at him as if he were Satan in the flesh, built from the blood of man and sent to earth specifically to murder their loved ones, but there was one face that hurt him most of all. Seymour Almasy sat quietly in the back, arms folded, his face frozen in an expression that defied human description. It were as if Rollins' very presence had suddenly unlocked a demon in Almasy that only reared its ugly head when he was around, bristling with a scorn so pure and distracting that it made the hairs on the back of his neck stand on end. Shuffling in with his shackles clanging against the colorless tile like funeral bells, Rich Rollins allowed Officer Barks to lead him towards the main attraction. The chair itself was an ominous construct that brought a chill to Rollins' lungs. Clamps were stationed for both his hands and ankles, attached to the rigid oak of 'ol Sparky with bolts the size of butcher knives. Barks unlocked his chains and sat him down with a harsh thud, clicking the chair's restraints into place before he had the chance to pull anything stupid. Rollins had no intention of making a run for it, but Barks was the sort of man who pushed the weak and helpless around for a living. The officer's eyes were alight with an unholy sort of satisfaction as he stepped away, taking his position behind the wooden booth that held the almighty breaker box. Officer Priess conducted the ceremonial obligations. "Richard Rollins, you have been condemned to die by a jury of your peers, sentence imposed by a judge in good standing of this state. Electricity will be administered into your body until you are dead," and he paused, trying to keep his voice from wavering, "at which time your remains will be buried at a location you have selected. Do you have any final words?" "Just a few," Rollins replied, taking a deep breath to calm his racing heart. He turned towards the Taylor family, Cynthia O'Doyle's parents, doing his best to deflect their murderous stares with the knowledge that they never knew the truth. "I loved your daughter, Mr. and Mrs. Taylor. When she told me she was pregnant and that the child was mine, at first I didn't believe her. I was an arrogant, selfish asshole who didn't want to believe that I possessed the love of a father, but she taught me that I've been second guessing myself for way too long." He gulped, trying to keep the frog in his throat from croaking. "I never meant to see her hurt. Believe what you will, but I did not murder your daughter. My selfishness did." Barks snorted from behind his barrier. "Any last requests?" he snapped. Both Priess and Rollins stole a sour look in his direction, but Rollins answered quickly. "Yes, as a matter of fact I do." Turning back towards Priess, Rich Rollins bowed his head as if in solemn prayer. "I want you to leave my bandanna on. I lived under this damn thing, and I want to die under it with my head held high." He did just that, locking in a glassy and poignant gaze with his secondary executioner. Priess nodded slowly. "We can do that, Rich." He took a ragged breath, picking up the black hood that would single-handedly transform The Renegade's secular world into one of blind faith. "It's time." Rollins sighed, leaning his neck against the hard back of the chair. The hood came down over his head with care, and the last thing Rich Rollins saw before everything faded out were Seymour Almasy's lips mumbling a silent prayer. Priess moved with a deliberate hesitation in his step, swinging the deadly headgear down like a beautician's hair dryer, brushing Rollins' long brown curls off of his shoulder blades as he strapped the apparatus into place. Rollins winced as the intrusive prods poked the sides of his face through the hood, crashing down on the crown of his head like a bulldozer. He tried to swallow his immediate panic. Stepping away, Officer Priess tossed Officer Barks a curt nod. "Roll on one." Rollins clenched his teeth as the switch came down. ~<>~<>~<>~ Two days ago… "One of the suits said you wanted to see me?" Fenton's grizzled old eyes narrowed in the empty expanse of Rollins' peek hole. "Jesus it stinks in there. Did you kill a cow, too?" A lazy bead of sweat rolled down The Renegade's temple as he squat next to the opening. "I need to ask you a really big favor, Fenton." He licked his lips, absently swatting at the horsefly that had been pestering him all afternoon. "Do you think you can help me out?" "Well, that all depends," Fenton said conspiratorially. "What's in it for me?" Rollins grinned. "My eternal gratitude in the form of a cold beer. Interested?" One of those aged eyebrows raised with just such an interest. "You serious?" "Well," Rollins said, "it may not be cold, but I can get you a good brew from one of guards. They're givin' me a last meal behind the Warden's back. Ain't that sweet?" Fenton laughed, then coughed, then laughed again before saying, "Fuckin' sweet, man. So whaddaya want from me? Make it quick, if the guards catch me lingerin' over here too long they'll stick me in there with you." Rollins swat at the fly again, finally smashing the filthy little fucker with his palm. He grinned. "How emotionally attached are you to your wig?" ~<>~<>~<>~ The moment of execution… The lights flickered out for a second as Barks flipped the first switch. An electric buzz filled the room like a swarm of angry bees; to Rollins it was an otherworldly hum that vibrated in his spine until he had to clench his teeth to keep them from chattering. His breath was hot and sour inside of the hood as he sucked the cloth in and out of his mouth, gripping the armrests of the chair with twin palms of sweaty steel, clamping down on them like a vice. "Roll on two," Priess commanded. Barks flipped the final switch. The current came like a speeding train, striking Rollins with the full eighty thousand volts of his dubious debt to society. At first he jerked forward in shock as the alkaline tang of electricity assaulted his stuffy nostrils, feeling a static surge lift the tiny hairs on the back of his neck with a jolt that pimpled his skin. His eyes bulged behind his mask, terrified beyond reason. Then he blinked. He didn't feel a thing. It fucking worked! He nearly cried, but his overwhelming relief quickly dissipated. The people wanted a show, and by God a show is what he was going to give them. Rollins thrashed like his life depended on it, and in all honesty, it really did. The buzzing appendages of the headgear grated through until he could feel the pressure on his scalp, gyrating in a wild spasm that began to rub his restrained wrists raw. He let the charade continue for nearly half a minute until he slowed his pace, moving his thighs in a sluggish parody of death. Finally he allowed his throes to come to an end, and shortly thereafter Officer Priess called for the current to be cut. "Check him," Rollins heard Priess order. A doctor had been waiting against the far wall when Rollins had come in, a man who now held a stethoscope to The Renegade's breast. The instrument was cold - they always were - and Rollins had to keep from laughing hysterically while the doctor conducted his examination. Dr. Ortega offered Priess a sad frown. "He's still alive." Ten seconds later the maddening thrum of electricity returned, but this time Rollins tried to keep his thrashing to a minimum as if he were quickly losing strength. The current continued to wrack the chair nearly a minute after Rollins had gone completely still, and once again the good doctor was called in for the elementary examination. This time Dr. Ortega was flabbergasted. "This man is still alive," he declared. "His heart is beating quickly, but not abnormally." A restrained gasp filled the observation area as people began to murmur amongst themselves. Rollins tried to tune their confusion out. He needed to concentrate. "Again," Priess ordered once more, sharing a bewildered glance with Barks. "Hit him again." This time Rollins really began to jive and wail. A woman shrieked when he began to move so violently, which would have made him laugh were he not scared out of his mind. One slip up and somebody - Barks he reasoned - would realize that something was most defiantly out of order. So long as he created the illusion of agony, simulated the act of a painful and prolonged death, nobody could say a damn thing. How could they possibly know? When the current was cut for the third and final time, Rich Rollins made sure that his body was as rigid as the stick that he imagined was shoved up Warden Hammond's ass. His extremities were numb from the lack of circulation, so it was really only a half-lie. When the doctor examined his pulse for the last time he nearly dropped his stethoscope. "My God," he cried. "This man is not dead. His heart is… he's alive!" Rollins heard the sound of chairs tipping over, likely somebody in the audience who had passed out. The muted slam of a man's fist against something hard filled the room as Barks said, "That's impossible! I'll fry that fucker until I see smoke pouring out his ears!" "No you won't," said Priess, a hint of wonder filling his voice. "We can't zap him again. It's inhumane." "Inhumane my ass!" Barks barked. "This man is going to die today if I have to-…" "It's against the law Barks, and you damn well know it." A dangerous air had settled over the room as Priess stated very matter-of-factly: "He's been under the shock for nearly seven whole minutes. It's not constitutional. As far as the state of Illinois is concerned, this man has just served his debt to society." More slamming. Rollins imagined that Barks was throwing a hissy fit that could put a spoiled three-year-old to shame. He allowed himself to smile. "Get him out of that chair," said Priess, the barest hint of relief overtaking his professionalism. "Get this man into the medical lab immediately." >From somewhere in the back of the room, The Renegade could hear the mystified reaction of Seymour Almasy: "I don't fucking believe this." Believe it, Rollins mused in triumph. You'd better fucking believe it. ~<>~<>~<>~ The night before… It was something that Angelo Deville had said in an interview prior to their first round encounter, a phrase that had been indelibly etched into Rich Rollins' psyche: "I don't follow in anyone else's footsteps," said The Deville. "I blaze my own trail, streaking across the sky, and I expect others to do the same." The first time Rich Rollins had participated in a PTC tournament he had wanted to carve a gash through the wrestling world so deep that the wound could never be closed, not with a hundred pressure clamps or a thousand stitches, tearing through the competition like a man possessed. His journey had been killed prematurely at the hands of Skylar G., the blind man who had at the time - almost poetically so - had named himself The End. It had signaled the end of Rollins' dreams, and instead of reveling in the sweet glow of victory he had been relegated to the sidelines as he watched Deville win it all, establishing an unshakable lordship over the wrestling world that should have been his. The loss had consumed Rich Rollins to the point that he had completely lost his focus, and when the time came to amend his defeat during the course of GTT4, his blind ambition got him eliminated by Luna Morbid in the first round. It was not a mistake he was ready to make twice. "Dinner is prepared," Officer Priess whispered through the cubby hole as The Renegade sat up eagerly. "T-bone steak with fettuccini alfredo, and a Bud Lite just for you." As soon as the food hit his waiting hands the smell totally extinguished the taint of Rollins murky surroundings. His chops drooled in anticipation at the mere glimpse of the tender meat, but when Priess tried to pass through the beer, Rollins put out his hand to stop him. "The beer isn't for me," he said. "I'd owe you more than I could ever repay if you put that in Fenton's cell. He was the only friend I had the chance to make in this shithole." "Can do," Priess assured. "Don't forget the utensils." Oh Rollins had most certainly not forgotten the utensils. It was the entire reason he had ordered a t-bone steak. You needed a decent knife to cut through a decent steak. As soon as Priess passed through the fork and knife, he stuck his nose back through the hole. "Now I can trust you with that thing, right? You're not going to try and slit your wrists or anything?" He paused. "Not that I think you would, but I had to ask. Hammond would have my ass if he found you dead with a knife in your hand." "Yeah right," Rollins said, already shoveling the heavenly slab into his mouth. "Like I'd actually cheat you the honor of sendin' me off proper." They shared a good laugh as Rollins continued to inhale his food. "You'd better go, Priess. That beer ain't gettin' any colder." Priess' eyes furrowed through the slot, focusing on Rollins one last time. "I'm going," he said. "But I just want you to know, I don't think you killed that woman. I've known a cold blooded killer or two in my day, Mr. Rollins. You just don't fit the bill." Rollins didn't even have words. Granted, his mouth was literally oozing rivulets of tender steak juices and alfredo sauce, but even still he felt genuinely touched by this man's candid honesty. All he could do was stare, but his eyes said it all. "You're welcome," Priess replied. Then he was gone. Rollins began to slow down a little, taking his time with the beautiful meal that had been so carefully and meticulously prepared just for him. It was without question the most satisfying meal he had ever eaten, which he assumed was what your last meal was supposed to taste like, but he was content all the same. He cut into the thick slab of steak with ravenous delight, savoring every morsel as if it were his last, because he knew that it very well might be. It wasn't until he had completely finished with his meal, licked the plate clean and hidden it in two pieces down his drain, that he began to finger the steak knife. It took nearly five minutes to muster up the courage, but the thought of bursting like a popcorn kernel in front of Barks and his merry band of assholes strengthened his resolve. Taking a deep breath, Rich Rollins began hacking off handfuls of hair that he hadn't cut in nearly ten years, slashing and sawing near his own scalp until nothing was left but a lopsided mess of blood and fuzz. Stabbing at the rubber lining that coated the tiny bench seat, The Renegade began placing small strips of the quarter-inch material into Fenton's wig, insulating the entire thing like a water cap. When he slipped the wig over his sore scalp the rubber rubbed angrily against the nicks in his skin, but he had just the thing to keep it all under wraps. His bandanna had never done him wrong. And now it was about to save his life. ~<>~<>~<>~ The aftermath… The doctor had never once tried to remove his bandanna while he laid out in the infirmary. Some part of Rollins thought that Priess had known all along, maybe even passed along a whisper or two to those who he knew wouldn't ask questions, and before he could say "unquestionable pardon" Rollins was walking out the front gate of Camp Cocytus. A free man. The second he stepped foot on open soil a legion of news reporters were already waiting, having already gobbled up the story. It wasn't every day that a man was lethally electrocuted three times and live to tell the tale, especially a man of such public notoriety as Rich Rollins. Since the story had broke people had begun asking questions, wondering how his execution date had gotten moved up so quickly whereas most people sat on death row for years. Warden Hammond was more than likely sitting in his office right now, screaming into the ear of any guard stupid enough to wander into his warpath. It made Rollins smile the happiest smile of his life. "Are you feeling any aftereffects of the attempted execution?" the reporters asked. "Did you actually request to be put to death so quickly?" "Are the rumors true? Are the living conditions in Camp Cocytus below public health standards?" "What do you plan to do with your newfound lease on life?" It was the question he had been waiting for somebody to ask him since the day he had been sentenced to death. "What am I going to do?" Rollins repeated, embracing the mind-numbing flashbulbs like a long-lost friend. "First, I'm going to win a fucking tournament." He paused, grinning broadly. "Then I'm going to sue the shit out of one Mr. Wesley Hammond." The cameras were rolling and the reporters were eating it up like so much chickenfeed. "What are you suing him for?" somebody hollered. The Renegade looked straight into the television camera, offering his worldwide audience a roguish grin. "About twenty-seven million dollars, to be exact." Let the new Era begin. ~Fin~
