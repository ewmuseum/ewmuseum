The Quest for a Quest Leads Nova Back Home

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-20 Turtle’s Tavern, New Orleans, LA…March 23rd, 1998 It’s getting late. No reason not to dim the lights. "Enhancin’ the ambiance," he’d say to customers. "Savin’ on the electric bill!" he’d bark at employees who bitched about the darkness. Bert "Turtle" Barringer stands behind the bar, polishing glasses and observing the few remaining patrons in his establishment. His neck cranes out, shaved-bald head cocking to one side as he checks around the left side of the bar for stragglers. In this moment he looks every bit his nickname, broad shoulders and stocky frame supporting a long neck and oddly small head. Jing-a-ling-a-ling…WHAM!! His head snaps around as the bells on the bar’s main entrance door jingle and the door quickly slams shut afterwards. Turtle stares up in cautious confusion at a skinny white kid with long blonde hair and wild blue eyes that appear terrified at the moment. "That door cost a lot of money, kid," Turtle growls, already unimpressed with the stranger. The young man appears not to notice Turtle’s informative introduction, eyes darting around the room hurriedly as though he’s either looking for something important that’s lost…or a place to hide. "HEY!" The latter is confirmed as the kid around to the side of the bar and bounds over the swinging fence, much to Turtle’s chagrin and vocalized protests. "Kid, what the hell are you…," the befuddled barkeep begins, but the young man raises a hand as he ducks down behind the countertop, a pleading look on his face. "Please…," he whispers, "please don’t tell them I’m here, alright? I didn’t do any…" Jing-a-ling-a-ling…WHAM!! Before he can finish, the door opens and slams shut again, the bells heralding the arrival of three of New Orlean’s finest, looking less than thrilled and more than winded. One of them approaches the bar. "Hey, Turtle…you…you seen anyone come in here, look like a kid, late teens, long hair, kinda stoned, fucked up like he’d smoked a lot a’ reefer or somethin’?" the cop asks. Turtle turns away from him for a second non-chalantly as though he’s cleaning something on the wall behind the bar that absolutely has to have his undivided attention at this exact moment. His eyes creep down towards the young man, sitting on the floor pressed flat against the bar. The kid slowly shakes his head, making his opinion clear on what the answer to that question should be. Turtle stares down at him for a moment, eyes narrowing as he makes an on-the-spot character appraisal… …before turning back to the policeman and setting down a bottle of bourbon. "I ain’t seen anyone like that, Tom," Turtle says with conviction, grabbing a few shot glasses and sliding them over in front of the officer, "sorry. You boys want one for the road?" "Nah, not tonight," Tom replies, obviously disappointed, "we gotta go give this block another sweep, see if this kid turns up somehow. If he’s not an idiot, he won’t. But I got the sneakin’ suspicion this one’s right under our noses, ready to be spotted." "You’re probably right," Turtle agrees, his voice flat. "Good luck, boys." Tom nods, and he and the other two file out of the bar. An audible sigh of relief is heard from the floor, and then a plume of smoke is exhaled as the young man lights a smoke. Turtle looks down at him in a mixture of curiosity and annoyance. "The hell did you do to bring the fuzz down on yourself, kid?" he asks, lighting his own cigarette and leaning against a support column attached to the countertop. The kid shakes his head. "Listen, d00d…and I appreciate that shit, by the way…I’m way away from where I’m supposed to be staying, I’m fuckin’ starving…I just wanted to scrap a joint somewhere and take the edge off, dig? Fuckin’ pigs…" "Where are you supposed to be staying?" Turtle inquires, cocking an eyebrow and exhaling into the air. The young man scratches the back of his head and winces. "Actually, I don’t know where I’m staying yet. I’m, uh, with an independent wrestling promotion and I just got in tonight." "Sleeping arrangements might take precedence over scoring ganja, don’t you think?" Turtle asks before waving the question off with his hand, "so what’s your name?" "My name’s Chris Vega," the kid replies as a mischievous grin spreads over his face, "my wrestling name is Supernova…Nova for short." "That grin…Christopher always had that grin that said ‘I may have broken a window with that baseball, but it was a home run.’ He never lost that smile that made it terribly hard to stay mad at him." – Mrs. McCardle, Nova’s grade school Social Studies teacher Turtle’s Tavern, New Orleans, LA…October 19th, 2006 "Another G n’ T there, Nova?" Nova pushes the ice cubes in his glass around with his straw for a moment before looking up at his old friend and shaking his head. "Nah, Turtle…I think I’m alright." The aged bartender appears surprised. "When are you ever alright? Something the matter, superstar?" Turtle doesn’t get a grin out of the Rising Star, who simply shakes his head a second time before continuing to stir the cubes in his glass. There’s a moment of silence, and then Nova leans back in his stool, shuffling a cigarette out of his pack and lighting it. "I dunno, man…there’s this huge tournament, right? Probably the biggest in the sport…" "Yeah, I saw your name on some list a while back," Turtle replies, "Congratulations! I know you been wantin’ to give that a go, right?" "Right," Nova replies without his friend’s enthusiasm, "but I dunno, man…I’m starting to wonder if it was such a brilliant play to toss my hat in after all." "Why?" Turtle asks with that expert bartender counseling tone as he grabs Nova’s glass to refill in spite of objections. "Because it’s making me depressed as hell, d00d," Nova explains, "it’s like…it’s like everyone involved in this thing is on some kind of a quest, some kind of personal journey or some shit. Something epic like that. It’s weird, this tournament just does that to people. But I don’t feel that. At all. I just feel like it’s a match, not Round One of the rest of my life." "So?" Turtle says with a decidedly unsympathetic shrug. "So," Nova replies irritably, "what’s that say about me? Judging by the looks of things, I should be experiencing some kind of crisis, or wake-up call, or something. I just feel something." Turtle grins and sets a fresh gin & tonic in front of the Rising Star. "You drink this, and you’ll feel something." "Ha, ha, ha," Nova offers sarcastically with a roll of his eyes, "Prick. I’m being serious." "Nova, how’s your ex-wife? How’s your daughter?" Turtle asks, his tone becoming instantly more serious, and yet softer at the time. "Huh?" Nova utters, confused. "Your ex-wife. Your daughter," Turtle repeats as though he’s explaining something to a child, "I remember when you used to be more interested in talking about them than the business." Nova snorts bitterly. "Well, I still have the business." "And not them?" Turtle comes back, a frown replacing his grin, "I know you’re not stupid enough to really believe that shit." Nova shrugs his shoulders and takes a swig from his drink. "Whew…that is stout." Turtle rests his forearms on the bar and stares at his friend. "You want some direction for your life? You want a quest? Here, allow me to direct you, Odysseus…go home. Go. Home. Don’t sit here in some dingy New Orleans hole-in-the-wall, sucking back gin and talking to me about tournaments, kid. You got a baby girl in Detroit and a woman who’s gonna pretend she hasn’t thought about ya every day since you guys signed those papers." Nova rolls his eyes again. "Oh yeah, Turtle, Ariel’s a closet Nova fanatic. That whole cold, impersonal, ‘I’m done with you’ thing? All a front!" "Oh, no," Turtle says, chuckling, "I never said she was any fan of Nova’s, no sir. But I think he liked that Chris fella alright." Nova runs a hand through his hair and takes another drag from his smoke before snuffing it out in an ash-tray. He grabs his glass, knocks back the rest of the concoction, and sets it down forcefully on the bar, scooting it over to Turtle with his fingers. "We’ll see. Let me have another there, Turtle, old sport." The aging barkeep stares at the Rising Star for a moment, and Nova holds out his arms defensively. "What? We’ll see, alright? Now fill me up." The House Formerly Known as Vega, Detroit, MI, the next day… A taxi pulls up at the foot of the gravel driveway leading to the house Nova used to call home. The man himself steps out of the cab, his own ride having recently been rendered inoperable, and gazes on his old home for a moment before shaking the cobwebs clear and walking up the driveway. He climbs up the steps to the porch and approaches the front door. He smiles as the muffled squeals of his daughter at play inside float through the walls. His hand curls into a fist but hovers inches from the door, unable to make the knock. It had been a long time. Knock, knockknockknock knock, knock knock. Nova takes a deep breath, but it’s futile because all the air rushes out of his lungs as soon as the door opens, revealing Ariel Martiarra, long brown hair (much longer than he remembered) pinned in several places on her head in a way that could almost be random if it didn’t look so good. She’s wearing a white tank top and khaki cargo pants. And her eyes…oh, wow, her eyes… …are about as wide at this moment as they’ve ever been, it would appear. "…Chris?" Nova’s attention is diverted as a small figure zooms behind Ariel past the view of the door frame. The blur comes screeching to a halt and runs back over to her mother, unabashedly standing next to her and staring up into Nova’s face. "Daddy!" comes the excited cry, and she springs forward, arms outstretched. Nova quickly bends and catches her, hoisting her up and letting her wrap her arms around his neck. "Hey…hey there, little Lulu." His eyes move back to Ariel, who unfortunately isn’t offering him the same receptive attitude. "I…I’m sorry, I just…this is completely unexpected," Ariel says, flabbergasted. "Umm…come in, come in…" Fifteen minutes later… As Lulu sits on a rug in the living room beautifying her dolls with Crayola makeup, Nova sits at the kitchen table, staring across it at Ariel, who’s leant against the counter. "Do you not want me to be here?" he asks, anger building in his voice. "I’m sorry, Chris, but I’m just forgiving as our two-year-old daughter," Ariel replies, arms folded across her chest, "I’m not going to throw my arms around you just because you decided to drop by and peek your head in. And it was your decision, wasn’t it? No call? No ‘what would work for you?’ We haven’t seen you in months!" "I should have called," Nova concedes, "but if you scowl at me any harder, my fucking beard is going to catch fire." "Maybe you can watch the language around our daughter," Ariel scolds, "and that beard needs to be set on fire. You look like a lumberjack." "And that’s okay, I work all night, and I…sleep…all…," Nova begins, but he pulls the plug on the sing-a-long when Ariel’s glare only intensifies, "look, I just wanted to come by. I know it’s been way too long. I’m sorry. But can’t we just, I dunno, enjoy the visit? I don’t have anything else to do today." "Well, I do," Ariel replies irritably, "we have lives too, Chris. We can’t just drop everything because you decide to show up for a family reunion." The ability to brush off nasty jabs is something Nova has honed over years of a volatile professional and private life. "What do you have to do today?" "I’m fixing up the house," Ariel answers. "I need to call a few repairmen, I need…" "You’re WHAT?!" Nova cries, scooting back his chair and standing up, "Repairmen?! Awww, no! I’m Mr. Fix-It, remember? I’ve done TONS of work on this house…" "And it’s all fallen apart in the meantime," Ariel interrupts, grabbing up her phone and dialing. "I just want everything to be done with." Nova rushes around to the other side of the table and puts a hand over the phone. "Oh, come on, let me help you. It’ll save money, and like I said, the only reason I’m in Detroit is to come here. Come on, please put down the phone. That’s right. Go on. Puuuuut it down." Ariel slowly lowers the phone, and sighs in defeat as Nova pumps his fists in the air. "I know that by the end of the day, I will regret having agreed to let you help us." "Oh ye of little faith!" Nova exclaims confidently, "I can feel it all coming back. The house itself is trembling in fear…it knows that I have returned." "‘Trembling in fear’ is definitely right," Ariel replies, thinking back to the time Nova cut the electricity cables running to the house…or the time he fell through the attic roof and down through Lulu’s cradle…or the time he felled one of their trees on top of the neighbr’s car. "But I suppose if you’re itching to help that badly, there are things to be worked on around here." In the first-floor hallway bathroom a few minutes later… "See? The handles on the tub barely turn at all," Ariel explains while crouched over the tub as Nova stands at the entrance to the bathroom. He takes a step forward, pulling the door behind him, but Ariel shoots up, arms outstretched. "NO!!" she yells, and Nova practically jumps out of his Spalding’s. "That door locks from the outside, Chris…we can’t close it." "Ah, I see," he says, pushing it back open, "well, glad I caught myself before it was too…" Suddenly, a shrill scream of delight interrupts him as Lulu toddles down the hallway, an enormous rubber ball gripped in her hands. She runs directly into the swung-open door, the ball bouncing her back away from it and causing the door to slam shut. "…late," Nova finishes, staring at the now-closed door. Ariel is frozen, staring in horror at their blocked escape route. For his part, Nova shrugs and turns to his ex-wife, a wide grin spreading over his face. "Well, looks like we might be here for a minute. Let’s have a look at this bathtub." He ducks swiftly as a bottle of shampoo whizzes past his head.
