Closure

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 "Forget your name. Forget who you once were. Leave your old life behind. You are Kimbusa, and soon you will be the most feared weapon of the Yakuza…" Shindo's words resonated in Kimbusa's skull, trailing his every thought as he trudged through the dank alley. He couldn't shake them; they were ominous yet empowering, both forboding and uplifting in their finality. He was startled by how quickly he had embraced his new life; indeed, he had already forgotten his old name. Once he found himself struggling to recall it, but snickered with delight upon realizing that it simply did not matter. Kimbusa. The mighty samurai warrior had returned to reclaim his place in the world. Presently, Kimbusa found himself sloshing through black puddles of rainwater and alley sludge, slowly creeping towards his first assignment. Ahead of him was Matsu, one of Shindo's most "valued," to use his term, employees. A slender Japanese man of about thirty, he currently held favor with Shindo for his ruthlessness as well as his cunning. Matsu wore a dark black shirt that clung to his skin from the rain, and his charcoal hair hung over his face, hiding his vigilant, icy stare. He had not greeted Kimbusa with great enthusiasm; in fact, Kimbusa had immediately received the distinct feeling that Matsu might move against him if the opportunity presented itself. And yet, despite the possibility of having a career killer turning his malice against him, Kimbusa felt invigorated. A surging rush of adrenaline had quickly overpowered him, to the point that he was almost giddy with intensity. It was the rush of casting aside the rules, refusing to obey an archaic code of conduct. Or maybe it was the rush of starting anew, in a place where the same primal instincts that had caused him to be shunned in his past life would be celebrated. Matsu did not look back at Kimbusa, but nonetheless Kimbusa felt as if Matsu was sensing his every step. For his part, Kimbusa did his best to look the part of the nervous newcomer, but his eyes remained subtly fixed on Matsu. Behind him were their backup, though Kimbusa had been quietly wondering what the hell good they would be. Two young boys, who Kimbusa placed at no more than nineteen, who chattered loudly and ceaselessly in sloppy Japanese about booze, women and whatnot. Their pronunciation stabbed at Kimbusa's ears like a million furious pinpricks; Kimbusa had been raised to value language and its inheent beauty, to always be aware of precisely what he was saying and how he was saying it. Behind him, the two hyenas cackled on obnoxiously, lugging behind them uzis that looked too big for the scrawny kids to even hold level. And so Kimbusa watched Matsu, exilerated with the anticipation of his first assignment. The alley was cold; it was a blustery early November night, and a chill ran across Kimbusa's flesh. He tightened his trenchcoat in an effort to shut out the breeze, but quickly caught himself and opened it again. Got to keep that sidearm ready, he thought to himself, pleased with his cunning. The gun felt heavy in his belt; in his experience as a sumo wrestler, Kimbusa had never been forced to carry a weapon. But they had quickly outfitted him with a nine millimeter pistol, and Kimbusa couldn't refuse. In truth, Kimbusa didn't forsee himself ever having to use his pistol; after all, just over a week ago he was heralded by the civilized world as the most fierce, animalistic force in the country. Just because he'd traded in the respectible life of the sumo for the Yakuza didn't make him any less dangerous. If anything, it made him more lethal; for now he was truly unleashed. Never again would he be told to hold back, never again would his true nature be supressed. Taking a deep breath of frigid air, Kimbusa felt truly free for the first time in his life. He had found his utopia. Matsu glanced over his shoulder at Kimbusa and grunted, motioning around a corner. He then promptly disappeared in the shadows, ducking to his right with a silent step. Kimbusa's eyes narrowed, and he quickened his pace to track his guide down. He felt uncomfortable having lost sight of him, even for just a brief moment. He still couldn't shake the disconcerting feeling he had about his guide, and he wasn't about to be caught off guard on his first night. The two young boys toting the uzis fell behind as Kimbusa sped around the corner. Immediately he realized his mistake; Matsu was inches away, standing with his back against a towering brick wall, staring directly at him through wide, fell eyes. "Try to keep up," Matsu spat with disgust in blistering Japanese. Kimbusa sneered, both taken aback by Matsu's chiding and embarassed by the way he carelessly turned the corner. Matsu had seen his momentary weakness, and Kimbusa was aware enough to know that a single moment's lapse could be the difference between life and death. He quickly regained his composure as Matsu lit a cigarette and began walking down the next alley. Kimbusa followed him as another wave of exileration wafted over him; the adrenaline rush had hit him, and suddenly he found himself swooning with intensity again. It was difficult for him to keep the smile from his face, as he found himself thoroughly enjoying his new life as an outlaw. He couldn't imagine what great task Shindo had set before him, what honorable deed he would do to prove his worth. Matsu passed beneath a flickering silver light, its struggling beams dancing on the wet pavement below. Kimbusa had never seen anything like this in Tokyo; he had been such a fixture in high society that he only knew the high rises, the contemporary office buildings, the nicest restaraunts. Finding himself in the dregs of the city's underbelly, Kimbusa suddenly felt an overwhelming wave of disgust for his former life. All that time spent being harrassed by sponsors with agendas, being courted by big businessmen and dining with government officials; it suddenly seemed so painfully trivial, so trite. What was he doing in that penthouse suite when there were places like this in his own back yard, just waiting to be explored? For the first time in his life, Kimbusa felt alive. Abruptly, Matsu halted a few yards ahead, dropping his cigarette and stomping it out with his boot. Peering through a sheet of dark hair, Matsu appeared to fix Kimbusa in an icy stare, before the two young boys finally arrived behind him. Matsu flashed Kimbusa a mischevious smile. "We're here." Without a moment's preparation, Matsu walked to an inconspicuous door set in a decaying brick wall, illuminated only by a lonely flickering light overhead. The door was so subtle that Kimbusa hadn't noticed it. Matsu pounded on the door with the flat side of his fist, and Kimbusa observed that he appeared to be particularly relaxed. Whatever their goal was, it didn't seem to be giving Matsu too many worries. After a moment's pause, the door finally swung open with a hideous creak. What manner of villan would be found in such a place? Kimbusa wondered to himself, straining his neck to see inside. What he saw shocked him, as an absolutely stunning woman, sporting a dangerously revealing red dress, looked directly at him and beckoned his party inside. Matsu winked at her and slipped through the door, and Kimbusa quickly followed. He sucked in a deep breath of air, trying to bring himself back to his senses after being stunned by the tantalizing vision before him. Behind him, the two young boys made no motion to suggest they would be joining him inside, instead taking a spot in the corner and snickering under their breaths. When Kimbusa stepped inside, it was as if he had stepped into another world. Located in this vile, forgotten alleyway was what appeared to be a gentlemen's paradise. It was dark and quiet, but bustling with activity. The floor was beautiful hardwood, the walls adorned with fine art and the ceiling offering breathtaking chandeliers. Kimbusa paused in the doorway, the woman in the red dress standing beside him, taking in this unexpected new frontier. Amid the subtle chatter of the powerful looking clientele was the unmistakable clatter of dice, the twirl of roulette games, and the clatter of chips. It was an underground casino, undoubtedly under Shindo's influence, located in the most unlikely of places. Suddenly Kimbusa became aware that he was gawking, and the young woman in the red dress brought him back to reality with a tap on the shoulder. Kimbusa noticed that Matsu had already made his way to the back of the room, where he was alternating speaking with someone in a shady corner and giving Kimbusa an impatient glare. Kimbusa smiled politely at the woman in the red dress and made his way through the sea of gamblers to Matsu. Matsu was speaking sharply with an older gentleman wearing a tuxedo. The man sat in the back of a booth, flanked on either side by bodyguards. One of the guards had a briefcase sitting on the table before him, with one hand on the case and the other suspiciously hidden beneath the table. Kimbusa's senses sharpened as he began to understand what was happening. Showtime. Matsu sighed and stealthily removed a small parcel from his jacket, tossing it on the table for the man in the tuxedo. The man looked up, and for the first time made eye contact with Kimbusa. Kimbusa did his best to look threatening, even if his mind was racing in a desperate search for understanding. The three men at the table shifted in their seats, exchanging nervous glances beneath the shadow of the beheamoth now hovering over them. Their discomfort emboldened Kimbusa, and he even shot one of the bodyguards a menacing snarl, causing the man to nearly wilt on the spot. Shindo knew exactly what he was doing when he brought Kimbusa in; somehow, his mere presence here had sent this powerful casino operator into a panic. The tuxedoed man opened the parcel, trying to calm his trembling hands, spreading out the brown paper to reveal three plastic bags filled with a white powder. The man opened one of the bags and slipped his index finger into the powder, then brushed it over his tongue. After a moment, the man smiled and slid the briefcase across the table. It wasn't the first time Kimbusa had seen cocaine, but it was the first time he'd taken part in a large transaction. But he was surpised at his lack of concern over the situation, even as they appeared to be making a drug deal in clear view of the patrons. Of course, it soon occured to him that those patrons were likely to be the ones to make use of his delivery. Seeing the briefcase within reach, Kimbusa acted quickly, snapping up the briefcase before Matsu could move for it. Kimbusa popped open the case a half inch and peered inside; the briefcase was filled to capacity with American dollars. Flashing Kimbusa a suspiciously friendly smile, Matsu spun about and headed for the door, leaving Kimbusa behind. Amused with his newfound powers of intimidation, Kimbusa paused to flash one more threatening stare at the table, earning another pathetic tremble from the men beneath him. Satisfied, Kimbusa kept the briefcase subtly tucked at his side and headed for the door. Matsu had already disappeared outside, but the woman in the red dress was waiting for him with a smile. She cut Kimbusa off as he moved for the door, wrapping a sultry arm around his massive chest. Leaning in towards his ear, her dress fell open just enough to offer him a view of paradise. "I hope you come back to see us soon," she cooed into his ear. She then abruptly released him and began walking away, her stunning figure disappearing into the darkness of the casino. Shaking his head, Kimbusa stepped back into the cold rainy air outside. His heart was pounding after the incredible events that had transpired over a span of no more than three minutes. He had been the muscle on a drug deal, been flirted with by a stunning young woman, and was now holding thousands of dollars in his right hand. Fate had lead him down a strange road indeed to go from honored sumo wrestler to outlaw in a span of one week. Kimbusa's idle reflecting was abruptly cut short by the cocking of a pistol. "I'm afraid your job is over, Kimbusa-san," Matsu whispered from somewhere in the shadows nearby. He spoke the word Kimbusa with particular disgust, almost spitting the word out with a snakelike hiss, and adding a sarcastic -san to punctuate his hatred. Scanning the alley, Kimbusa realized that the two young boys had raised their uzis an had them pointed directly at him. Both grinned confidently, knowin that even tihs massive beast before them would be cut down by their firepower. Matsu's voice had come from behind him, and he dared not turn to search for him. Instead, he held his arms to his sides, mind racing for a solution. He had let his guard down, and now he was going to pay for it. His own pistol suddenly began feeling very heavy in its holster, and Kimbusa felt his blood run cold as the notion that he might actually need to use it became very real. He had fired guns before, but never in a life-or-death situation; and never with the two uzis aimed at him. Even faced with these two puny kids, he knew a gunfight would not end in his favor. He figured he could take one or two shots from Matsu's pistol before going down, but Matsu had the drop on him. This wasn't going to be easy. Kimbusa sensed Matsu approach, hearing the splash of his boots sloshing through the murky water collecting on the pavement. "Just hand me the money," came the voice again, closer this time. Matsu finally stepped into the light, gun held steady and aimed at Kimbusa's head. He was to Kimbusa's right, walking in from the side, slowly heading for the case clinched in Kimbusa's right hand. "No sudden moves, and everything will be fine." With no recourse, Kimbusa's mind began grasping for anything that might help him. Somehow he found himself recalling his sumo training; The successful sumo maintains superior strength, leverage, and positioning. Positioning. Surveying the situation, Kimbusa shocked even himself with the boldness of his next move. As Matsu's hand stretched for the briefcase, Kimbusa suddenly bolted to his right. The kids with the uzis aimed their weapons, but hesitated. Kimbusa now stood directly between Matsu and the uzis, and the kids froze for fear of hitting their mentor. Matsu swung the gun about and popped off a round with a terrifying explosion of sound. The bullet ripped past Kimbusa's shoulder, missing him by the narrowest of margins. As the bullet cracked through a brick wall nearby, Matsu leveled the gun at Kimbusa's head. But he never managed another shot. With a sudden, unadulterated ferocity, Kimbusa thrust his palm into Matsu's face, sending him to the damp pavement in an unconcious heap. Spinning with frightening agility, Kimbusa rose to his full height, facing down the barrels of two trembling uzis. The two young boys found themselves staring at a terrifying vision. Chest heaving, spackled with Matsu's blood, the massive form hulking in the alleyway appeared capable of destroying their bodies and their souls. Paralyzed by fear, unable to manage anything but a wimper, the two young boys kept their uzis leveled at Kimbusa's chest. Stepping into the light, Kimbusa's sadistic sneer came into view, and the boys clamped their eyes shut in an attempt to drive out the horrifying vision. He had taken on another form; somehow he had grown larger and his features had faded away, so that he appeared to be a mere shadow, a malevolent spectre staring at them with the face of the devil himself. Kimbusa stepped towards them, his soul blazing with a sudden, unexpected rage that had seized his senses and dispatched his enemy. With a quick backhand, Kimbusa knocked the uzis free, letting them clatter harmlessly to the ground. Kimbusa raised a massive fist and unleashed a bloodcurtling battle cry, prepared to strike down the boys with malicious abandon. But a cold wave of realization seized him, halting his fist before he could bring it down upon the boy's face. He found himself unable to breathe, unable to move; he finally managed to suck in a breath of putrid alley air, shocking his senses back to action. He dropped his clenched fist, a small trickle of blood running down his fingers after breaking his own skin. Unsure of what to do next, Kimbusa managed to hiss a single order to the petrified boys. "Leave now before I change my mind." That was all they needed to hear. The boys bolted away, rounding the corner with tears in their eyes. Kimbusa forced himself to focus, turning back to Matsu. He still lay motionless in a puddle of blood and black rainwater. Looking down, Kimbusa realized he was still clenching the cash-filled case in his hand. The gun began to weigh heavy on his side again, almost throbbing in protest. A chill ran down Kimbusa's spine, a solemn reminder of just how close Kimbusa had come to being shot. But the thrill; the thrill had struck him again, harder and more potent this time. Kimbusa paused and closed his eyes, feeding off the pure, primal surge of power. Walking to Matsu, Kimbusa tried to blink the vacant stare from his eyes, desperate to come to terms with the sudden violence that had just transpired. Despite the gunshot, nobody inside the casino came out to investigate. He was alone. He was still flying with adrenaline, but a quiet rationale began to take over. His first instinct was to run. He had just been involved in a drug deal, was carrying a massive amount of money, and had just devastated his new employer's prized enforcer. And then something changed within him. It started slowly at first, like the low rumble of an approaching storm. Then it grew; voices swelling up inside him, a cacophony of enraged and empowered consciousness igniting his very soul. Surveying the alley once more, Kimbusa understood for the first time what Shindo had seen in him. He had given him the name of Kimbusa, the great samurai warrior reborn to reclaim his glory in battle. Here, Kimbusa had been sent on a mission, and he alone remained standing. He had accomplished his goal and felled all that stood against him. Matsu had surprised him, had his gun aimed directly at Kimbusa's head, and yet Matsu now lay face down, choking upon his own blood, unable to move. He had won the battle; and the thrill of this bloody, wreckless victory was greater than any he had won before. Reaching inside his jacket, Kimbusa produced the pistol and tossed it to the ground. He wouldn't be needing it. He was the most powerful weapon in Japan. ---------- Steven Caldera sits in his chair, eyes wide and heart racing, riveted as Kimbusa finishes his story. They sit in Caldera's dimly lit office, but all Caldera's world had shrunk away as he was absorbed by Kimbusa's tale. It was the first time Kimbusa had ever shared stories of his time with the Yakuza, and Caldera can't help shake the feeling that he was in the presence of a truly tortured soul. "Kim, that's…" Caldera begins, for the first time in his life unable to find the words he's searching for. Kimbusa, sitting perfectly upright upon the large guest chair, stares at Caldera from behind his dark squinting eyes. His chest heaves from the power of the memory, and both men pause to catch their breath. Caldera finally breaks the silence again, sitting back in his chair with a deep exhale. "I still don't understand why you went back there. How could this possibly help you in a wrestling tournament?" Unflinching, Kimbusa's face reveals deep wounds but no hint of his plan. "I'm not certain why I share this with you now," Kimbusa says, his voice trailing off as if he were getting lost in thought once more. For his part, Caldera was in awe. Caldera had trained Kimbusa, had managed him to glory in the third GTT tournament. But the two had a severe falling out, to the point that they engaged in a bloody war that eventually drove Kimbusa into hiding in the hills of Japan. So it was with great trepidation that Caldera had welcomed Kimbusa back into his office, unsure of exactly what to expect. But Kimbusa had skipped over the small talk and suddenly delved into one of his most personal memories, the likes of which Caldera had never heard before. Now, after all these years, Kimbusa was starting to make sense. "What can I do?" Caldera asks, taking care not to sound overly sincere. He knew Kimbusa was in need of his assistance, but that Kimbusa might be too cautious to ask for it outright. A smirk appears on Kimbusa's face as he returns to the present. "Mr. Caldera, I do not ask for your help. I know precisely what I am doing, and there is nothing you can do but sit back and watch it unfold. But for some reason, a reason I do not fully understand, I was drawn back here, to you." Caldera's brow bristles with confusion. "What are you looking for, Kimbusa?" he asks knowingly, his eyes penetrating and genuine. A moment of silence passes, where only the intense beating of the two men's hearts can be heard. "Closure," finally comes the answer. "I'm back because it's time to tie up all the loose ends. I cannot hide any longer. I must set everything right. Only then can I be at peace." Suddenly nervous as to what exactly Kimbusa's plans for him might be, Caldera loosens his tie. "Kimbusa… tell me more. I'm too damn curious. What on earth are you planning?" His eyes trailing off towards the window, where a pale stream of moonlight greets his face, Kimbusa manages only a whisper. "To truly understand, you must hear the rest of the story." Caldera can't help but grin. He was hoping Kimbusa would say something like that. "Tell me."
