Communist Conspiracy Part 4 - On the run

6 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-06 We are not retreating - we are advancing in another direction. -General Douglas MacArthur Granddad George English sprinted away from the direction of the arena. He was running for his life. It was getting to be a habit. If he started doing it regularly on the promenades of California a new craze in elderly fitness might be established (Lord knows he needed a new investment plan after the Geria-Tricks over 80’s skateboard league had bombed at the last X-Games). Fragmenting the tranquil reflection of the moon, his dirty hobnailed boots kicked up spray from the puddles as he blundered along the uneven sidewalk. Thankfully it had stopped raining a few hours ago leaving a clear night sky overhead - great if you were an astrologist, not so great if you were a fugitive fleeing from the Russian Mob. George remembered a time back in London, round about the era of the Victorians under the stranglehold of the Industrial Revolution, when you could’ve relied upon a thick foggy smog to hide you from the prying eyes of anyone further than six feet away. A bit like LA in the summer. The two withered balloons in his chest that comprised a set of lungs frantically inhaled the cold night air; the chill crispness stabbed at his sides leaving the cantankerous git gasping like a fish outta water. He was too old for this shit. Too old to be fleeing from communists with one collapsed lung and a mild case of bronchitis. Too old to be involved in conspiracies involving Lenin’s severed head hidden in the Magic Kingdom plotting the rebirth of a new Soviet Superpower. And definitely too bloody old to be wrestling. Initially Granddad George English had been kidnapped by Red Ivan, an *associate* from the Seventies during the Cold War. A fortnight ago they had been reunited in a chance encounter as George English evaded Colonel Archibald McSpiffy, his nemesis from the United Nations War Crimes Division. The big Russian and former KGB Director turned Mafia Don still remembered the roubles English had readily, even greedily, accepted from Mother Russia to act as a sleeper agent. A capitalist regime change amongst the onion domes of the Kremlin may have been so -- hell, Ronald Macdonald was firmly entrenched in Moscow selling Big Macs on the Bolshevik’s doorsteps -- but in Red Ivan’s kooky fruitcake version of reality, George’s slate hadn’t been wiped clean when the USSR had gone bankrupt. Instead the hapless Brit was still indebted to the cause...and now Ivan was cashing his ass in. He tripped on a paving stone; the flickering orange street lighting did little to illuminate the way. Flailing at the last second the geriatric troublemaker grasped the solid cold metal of a lamppost. Cheating his joints out of a painful fall, he gratefully leant back in a halo of light against the street furniture to catch his breath. They’d entered him in a wrestling tournament despite the fact he wasn’t a wrestler...he’d managed to foil their plans though. George had lost to Dr Curiosity, the scientist so pivotal to the Soviet’s plans - for this was the man they had picked to complete the complicated, even insane, operation of reattaching Lenin’s head to a new uber body. If allowed to succeed, their old leader would no doubt establish a new Communist Republic. George’s bloodshot eyes darted from side to side as he suddenly felt vulnerable under the spotlight. He was all-alone in the still, glass fronted, commercial district; the shops had shut up for the night, metal security mesh locked down over their windows and doors. As a quarry, this feeling of isolation was what he wanted wasn’t it? Well yes. Unless the Russians found him, then he wanted to be in a mass of people. After all, it was human nature to seek safety in numbers. It was via a swarming throng of people that the elderly Brit had managed to give his captors the slip an hour ago back in the arena. Despite losing his first match to Dr Curiosity, George’s Russian benefactors had plied more silver into the palms of those hands that mattered and reinserted him back into the tournament with the aim of earning an eventual rematch. First he had to fight his way back to the good doctor, so they matched him against the second oldest man in the tournament, Joe Black. It was made clear that this time defeat wasn’t an option. And by the grace of God, with very little actual wrestling, a vast amount of cheating on both sides, and George feigning a heart attack, a cheap win was obtained. His grandson would’ve been proud. Instead of returning backstage and into the arms of Red Ivan’s cronies, Granddad English seized an opportunity to escape by making like The Sandman and exiting through the crowd. His departure had been shrouded by waving signs and large Americans enjoying the carnival atmosphere. At least something positive had come of the obesity epidemic. In the distance was the welcoming glow of a twenty-four hour corner-shop. Just past that, the placid commercial district turned off into a wider, seedier, avenue that was now just coming to life, festooned with triple-X neon lights and streetwalkers. The pornographic glow looked more enticing than this empty impersonal mecca to capitalism and besides, what better place than a sleazy neighbourhood for a dirty old man to hide in? Grimy George English’s would be two a penny on that strip. Just as he made his decision to lay low in a whore house (it was a hard decision to come to), a van turned into the street from the illicit would-be haven, heading towards English and forcing him to reconsider his options. For the vehicle was baby blue in colour with the sinister mouse-eared logo on its side, slowly patrolling, looking for something. Looking for him. George pushed off from the lamppost turned on his heel and began to hobble-run back from where he had just come from, as fast as his decrepit frame would carry him. The lighting either side of the street dispersed any shadows to shelter in. Locks barred any access to the shops. Public planning authorities had neglected to install any alleyways for retreat. The van drew inevitably closer so that it’s headlights projected George’s stumbling shadow before him. Maybe they wouldn’t hurt him too much, just rough him up a little; the Commies still needed him for the rest of the tournament. “Argh!” George let out a short cry; this time he stumbled heavily, his knees and elbows getting that introduction to the sidewalk the old man had so rudely refused earlier. However, the fall had not come from uneven paving, this time he’d been deliberately tripped up. Standing over him were the legs of the perpetrator attached to the shiniest pair of shoes Granddad George English had ever seen. He remembered back to the first time he’d set eyes on those polished shoes... FRANCE 1944 George English sprinted away from the direction of the enemy trenches. He was running for his life. As melodramatic as that sounds it was in fact getting humdrum - just lately he spent most days running for his life. In fact most people did, well, apart from the dead. Fragmenting the less than tranquil reflection of antiaircraft fire and tracer rounds, his dirty hobnailed boots kicked up spray from the puddles of intestinal juice as he blundered through the uneven battlefront. Thankfully it had stopped raining a few hours ago leaving a clear night sky overhead - great if you were a Jerry machine gunner, not so great if you were a British Tommy recently ordered to capture the enemy entrenchment upon the ridge. George remembered a time back in France, round about the era of the Great War in the fields of the Somme, when you could’ve relied upon a thick foggy cloud of mustard gas to hide you from the prying eyes of anyone further than six feet away. A bit like LA would be like in fifty years time. Bullets zinged overhead and underfoot as George tried to sprint to safety, impeded by barbed wire, stumbling over falling comrades; the compressed air of a shockwave threw him off his feet when a shell detonated less than thirty feet away. It was hard to tell whether the payload had been hostile of friendly, although when this close up George could testify that friendly fire sure seemed a lot like the hostile kind. The crack of rifles and thrum of machine gun nests grew more distant, the cries of wounded men remained ever present. He picked himself up out of the sticky mud, well he hoped it was mud, and renewed his dash for the tree line where the men of his regiment had naively emerged twenty minutes earlier. Something grabbed at George’s ankles, almost tripping him up. “HELLLP MEEE!!” the thing gurgled through one of the holes in it’s broken face. George’s disgust rendered him speechless. He tried to back away, but the injured man wouldn’t let go of his foot. Then another set of damp cold hands grabbed his calf’s from behind, slowly creeping up to the back of his knees like an icy toed spider. “HELLLLLP!!” cried the new voice. “MEEEDIIIC!!” pleaded another. Ladeez and Gennelman, on tour, for one night only, at a field in France near you: Heeeell’s Chorus! And lucky old George English found himself with front row seats. The venue was a septic shell hole that the casualties of war had crawled into for refuge. Bloody, wet hands pawed at him, tried to tear him down amongst their ranks with beseeching hands. He unshouldered his rifle, holding the barrel in his hands he swung the butt back and forth tattooing their injured bodies and trying to end their painful symphony in which he wanted no part. Because for now, in this madness of no man’s land, he was but one man against the world, any recognition of comradeship he had once shared with these downtrodden sub-humans of the orchestra had been broken at the same time as their grisly faces. And despite George’s desperate plight, the hail of Nazi bullets never ceased, the one with his name on still relentless seeking him. With renewed urgency the self-serving Brit shattered the shattered man’s shattered face even further. It was fair to justify that the guy wouldn’t have been winning any beauty contests anyway. With a pitiful moan, and more coercion of the hobnailed booted kind, the living dead relented their clammy grasps from English’s thin cotton trousers. He dove from the pit of madness and made a final drive for safety. The tree line got ever closer, even the wounded chorus grew fainter to his ears. He could smell the pine now, almost taste the trees...well on account that bullets were shattering their woody trunks launching splinters into his dry, panting, mouth. One last step, one last stumble after that, and one last seven-foot drop into soft mud and chilled human excrement. He was safe - well in relative terms for a war zone. And that’s when he saw them; amongst the mud, blood and shit...the shiniest pair of shoes George English had ever seen. BACK IN THE PRESENT, 60 YEARS LATER Even now those shoes glowed; reflecting back the light of the stars in the evening sky...and the headlights of the approaching Disney van. It was sure to be loaded with communist henchmen. Armed with nets. And possibly tridents. “Get in the car!” the man hissed. George knew who it was without looking past the footwear: Colonel Archibald McSpiffy of the United Nations War Crimes Division. It seemed his whole mission in life was to pursue Granddad English throughout the decades and across the continents. Predictably he was wearing an impeccably fitted pinstripe suit replete with pocket watch and Panama hat. “Well if it isn’t the man from Del Monte, you come to check on me pineapples? I gotta tell you they feel a little bruised. So what’s the verdict gonna be, eh, he say yes?” George retrieved his own battered leather hat and stooped up. “I won’t ask you again - get in the car.” “Look Colonel, I’m not that kinda guy...maybe if you bought me a bunch of flowers first...” Click. The unobtrusive noise was that of the hammer on a 1938 Wembley pistol cocking back. What pissed George off more than having the business end of the barrel shoved in his face was the fact that even inside the breach the gleaming gun had been spotlessly cleaned. The Colonel opened the front door of the 1940’s Rolls Royce Phantom and prodded him in, then McSpiffy sat in the back. Both old codgers ducked down as the Disney van patrolled past. George held his breath for what seemed minutes but was only seconds - gradually the noise of the engine receded down the street. Amazingly the Red Menace seemed oblivious to the vintage British car glossed with its twelve layers of car wax. “You know what McSpiffy I’ve read about people like you - OCD it is, Obsessive Compulsive Disorder. I bet you wipe yer arse so hard it glows red like the sun. I mean who the hell polishes the insides of the ashtrays?” “Shut up and drive - key’s in the ignition.” “What is this eh? Driving Miss Daisy?” George winced as the gun was jabbed into the back of his skull, he turned over the engine and pulled out into the street. If he was lucky, the police might pull them over - this ostentatious ride could easily have been mistaken for a pimpmobile. “Something like that old boy; now turn left at the end of the road and keep going until I say otherwise.” even his pronunciation was clipped and precise, Archibald could’ve been a newsreader. George would’ve preferred that. “So where we going then? You gonna turn me over to the United Nations - you damn well know I didn’t mean to earn that Iron Cross.” “No? How about impregnating Winston Churchill’s niece?” “Getting a woman up the duff ain’t illegal - even if her uncle was the Prime Minister.” “It is when she’s underage chap. Fifteen years old!” “Hey, she was half bald like him - looked at least thirty five! How were I to know she were jailbait?” “Then there’s the thirty-seven counts of desertion from the frontline...stealing of German national treasures...” English glanced into the rear view mirror, “Hey, the Nazi’s stole those Van Gogh’s from the Louvre fair and square, I just perpetuated the vicious cycle of karma by nicking them back...just ain’t never got round to returning them to the Frogs is all.” George considered speeding up and attracting the attention of the police, surely McSpiffy wouldn’t shoot him when faced with a couple of gun happy Yanky cops. He floored the accelerator and the Rolls Royce Phantom chugged into action. The needle crept to twenty five miles per hour...then to thirty...thirty five! It peaked at the ungodly speed of thirty seven miles per hour. “Are you mad!?” cried the Colonel. Both of the old shites could feel g-forces playing havoc with their frail internal organs, it was at times like this that George was thankful for his colostomy bag - the farm fresh contents warmed the underside of his right armpit. “If you don’t slow down I’ll shoot you now!” “Yeah right, pull the other one. Do that and we’ll both die.” “Well you’ll definitely die, at least I have a slim chance of surviving.” When expressed like that George could see the merits of the Colonel’s argument. He decelerated to the more sensible speed of ten miles per hour that most elderly motorists deem appropriate for such horseless carriages. “I guess you’re right.” George needed to come up with an alternative plan that avoided harm to himself. McSpiffy laid on the smugness, “That’s why I’m the a Colonel working for the United Nations and you’re an old waste of space English. You may mock my attention to detail but look where it’s brought me, I’ve achieved something with my life.” “By kissing a lot of military ass I bet.” “Sucking a lot of cock actually.” “Ughh, I didn’t need to know that.” Uneasy silence. “Well if you’re such a military big shot how come you’ve spent most of your life chasing me...no one cares about war crimes from a conflict they can’t remember; so if this is all for a date you’ll find yourself bitterly disappointed.” “You know why English.” George knew all right. “Naah mate, I don’t have a clue.” “Don’t play coy with me. You have the *artefact,* from the Eagle’s Nest, now I want it.” “The *artefact*?” “The moustache.” “Ohh that. That’s my pension fund, you seriously expect me to hand it over?” “You don’t have a choice.” FRANCE 1944 George got off his cold and slimy stomach to survey the man with the shiny shoes. His uniform was spotless, George would’ve sworn it was made of Teflon...but that hadn’t been invented yet. He had the stripes of a sergeant, the winged bugle of a courier and a name badge that read McSpiffy. “You George English?” “Me, uhh, no, I think he was killed with all the other lads out there.” They glanced across the battlefield, it was fair to say that the Allies had just got hammered. McSpiffy looked back at English’s shirt, caked in dried mud, or blood, or both. Any nametags stitched to this Tommy’s garments were hidden in the filth. “Looks like you were lucky old boy, the only one left alive.” In truth luck had nothing to do with it, George English had nailed down survival to a fine art form. The trick was to look enthusiastic when charging Jerry with all the other guys, develop a limp, let the boys run past you, then leg it back to base until everyone had died. “Well I have these orders for you soldier: Top Secret.” Churchill’s personal courier shoved the manila envelope, fronted with the scarlet lettered warning, into George’s hands. “They told me you’d be the last one alive.” George English held the astonishing record for most battlefront participation and survival of any soldier. Not that he actually participated much...in fact he hadn’t killed anyone so far, he considered himself more of a, well, spectator. “My reputation precedes me.” “Indeed it does.” Said McSpiffy with an ironic smirk; he’d already steamed open the government documents for reading material while crossing the English Channel. It seems 1945 was to be an interesting year for Mr English. George looked down at the documents ordering him back to England. Apparently his outstanding talent for hanging on to life had been noted. Such a resource couldn’t go unexploited and he’d been ordered back to join the Prime Minister’s Special Operation’s Executive for retraining. In short, he was to be part of an Allied mission of glory to infiltrate Hitler’s Command Bunker, the Eagle’s Nest, and capture the Fuhrer before the Russians seized Berlin. Reading between the lines, English concluded they were basically testing his abilities of survival by sending him on a suicide mission. He also concluded that shagging Churchill’s niece hadn’t been the wisest career move one could make - especially since the bald politician’s signature authorising his approval at the bottom of the page seemed to have been signed with a jovial flourish...
