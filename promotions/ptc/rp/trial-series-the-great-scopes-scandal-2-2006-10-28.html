Trial Series: The Great Scopes Scandal (2)

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 Bogotá City. A sense of solitude transpires amongst such a barren land. Citizens find themselves enclosed within their homes, fearing the usual terror brewing on the streets of their beloved city on such a chaotic night. Intense winds howled through the night sky, accompanied by deeply toned roars of thunder, all of which united together to create an awry scene. Those who knew better dare not step unto the streets on such a night. No, on these types of nights, the streets were ruled by terror. A span of corrupt government alliances and crummy criminal organizations all culminated into what we have today. Bogotá City, where the inmates run the asylum. Once a bold place brought together by the mutual joy of it's citizens, Bogotá met a doom in which it found itself too weakened to survive. Power hungry tyrants and crime organization leaders found themselves positioned in the cities government branch, completely swaying the cities direction until it fit a bill in which they approved. By the time the state government decided to step in, the damage had already been done. This was the end result of said damage. Through the turmoil and conflict abroad, however… The citizens still believed in their city. And for that city, they'd continue to fight. But now the city was reaching a new wave of destruction. In addition to the corrupt governments, they also found themselves unable to trust their arch-bishops. Many blamed the death of Corbin Esposito on Vangelus Olsig, figuring that he'd hired someone to complete the job since it'd benefit him the most. The fact of the matter was, however, that without Esposito's testimony, the city had no case. The end result? Olsig was acquitted of all crimes. The city was in uproar. But not all things were as they seemed. -- "This is surreal…" Walter Ellis III, defense attorney in the trial of Bogotá versus Vangelus Olsig, sat patiently on the cold wooden chair, watching as the franticed Morris Essex paced back and forth, desperately trying to figure out the events in which had just unfolded and how he was going to get around them. "Not a single innocent strand in his case, and yet we're forced to acquit him in favor of some idiot who decided to put a bullet in the head of our lead evidence." Essex stated. "Amazing, isn't it." came the reply from Walter who simply smiled at his oppositions struggling to snag an answer. "Amazing is the fact that during the midst of all this animosity, you have yet to dispel the obvious." "The fact that Corbin Esposito is dead?" he questioned innocently "No…the fact that you killed him." Essex shouted, stopping dead in front of Walter in order to point a finger directly into his face. "Oh come on, Morris. Surely a good hearted soul as yourself shouldn't get involved in blind finger-pointing. We're both lawyers…we know what it usually results in." "It's not blind finger-pointing. You're the only right-minded person in Bogotá who even remotely believed Olsig to be innocent. Every man, woman, and child knew he was guilty…you were the only one willing to fight for the contrary." "And that makes me a bad person?" Ellis stated as he pushed himself out of his seat, placing his hand over his heart in a gesture of innocence. "No, but when you're the only soul willing to do so, it surely makes you look suspicious." "Do we truly know that for fact, Morris, or are you just going by what you believe? Have you placed every citizen of Bogotá under oath in order to come to the conclusion that not one…not even one believed my client to be innocent? Millions of people in this city, and you're going to sit here and tell me that not one believes my client to be innocent? Are you truthfully going to tell me that, Morris?" Ellis was right in the face of Essex now…something that the heel attorney did not take acceptance to. "It's not a case of numbers, Walter. I'm looking at this thing from a ground perspective." "Oh really? Then tell me why you've attacked me with such a transparent argument." "That's not my argument. We all know how greedy and self-absorbed you are, Walter, and let's face it…even if you paid off the judges and jurors, there was no way they would've granted you the victory in this case. This city would be burned down from the inside out if Olsig were declared innocent. You knew that better than anyone else, and thus decided to save your own hide by killing our lead evidence. Now we have nothing. Nothing at all. And because of that, we have a criminal walking our streets." this time, Essex was in the face of Ellis. Tension was reaching an all time high between the two counterparts. "As far as I am concerned, there is only one criminal freely walking the streets, and he is located within this very room. I am not one to judge myself." he snickered to himself. "Me? How am I a criminal?" "Look at the way these people fell before your feet when you entered this city. The only reason they did so was because they're feeble-minded. When this city was full of citizens who could think for themselves, Morris Essex was nowhere to be found. It was only then that they stopped thinking for themselves that you arose and became their hero. You've hidden truths and lead these people to believe things that were not accurate. You've taken advantage of weak minded citizens, even turning them against innocent souls much like myself. If you ask me, that is the greatest crime of all." a smile crossed the face of Essex as he watched his opposer struggle for a solution to the scenario presented. "You've got to be kidding me. These people worship me because they recognize my prowess and good-natured abilities, not because they can't think for themselves. If they couldn't think for themselves, they wouldn't recognize a crook like you." "What makes me a crook?" "You defended Evil." "And Jesus defended the Satan-influenced prostitute…didn't make him a crook, however." Essex shook his head in shame, weakened by Ellis' desire to always get the last word. "Did I just stomp the almighty Morris Essex?" "No you j-…" "I'll relish in my victory a bit longer. Now if you don't mind, I have a client to free." "Yeah, you do that…" And with that, Walter Ellis snatched his coat from off of the coat rack and made his way out of the conference room, leaving behind a beaten attorney. "…crook." -- It was a stormy night on the outskirts of Bogotá. Winds were howling like wild wolves, accompanied by thunderstorms and pelts of rain that were as cold as the winds themselves. It was the perfect scenery for the events in which were unfolding. The perfect scenario for the citizens of Bogotá, all of which wanted to see Vangelus Olsig prosecuted…but yet only saw him acquitted. Olsig, still bewildered by the events in which had saved him from prosecution, struggle to loosen the grasp of the security guard who violently dragged him out of the facility, tossing him out unto the flooded streets. Vangelus Olsig was free…but his expression could not proudly display that fact. Olsig desperately fought through the onslaught of the rain in order to push himself to his feet, but ass soon as he managed to do so, he was met with a kick into the back of his head. Olsig fell face first into the concrete street road. He knew someone was attacking him, but knew not of who it was. Suddenly, what began as one person's attack turned into that of several as he felt boots plant themselves into his frail body. One of the attackers began to violently choke him with their boot before placing that same boot into his face. A light illuminated the area…the headlights of a sharp limo. As the limo approached, the attackers yanked up Olsig, opening up the back of the limo and tossing him inside. Olsig crashed into the inside of the limo. It took him a moment to regather himself, but upon doing so he glanced over to see the very figure in which he'd never expected to see. …Carlos Boss. Notorious Bogotá Crime Lord. You've got to understand something about Carlos Boss…he basically rules Bogota, not with an iron fist, however. No one ever saw him nor barely heard of him, but when the corrupt government placed their corrupt notions into effect, you knew he was behind it. Some called him the silent assassin, so to speak. Which made his presence all the more strange to Vangelus Olsig. "I take it my boys didn't beat you up to bad…you're breathing." Carlos stated, sick grin planted upon his face. "Wh-…what was that even for?" Olsig asked, holding his aching arm as he struggled to fight through the pain. "Come on, Vangelus. If we would've simply approached you, theres no way you would have gotten in this limo on your own free will. Not you. Maybe some average joe, but not the combatant Vangelus Olsig. He would've fought." "Was my presence that important that I had to be beaten within an inch of my life, though? As a matter of fact, why am I here?" "You're here to return a favor." "…a favor?" the look on Olsig's face told the story. He was totally taken back by the words in which Boss was spewing his way, but yet intrigued at the same time to hear his reasonings for said words. "Oh come on. I get you acquitted…take out the man who was going to have the court crucify you, and you can't even bother to return a simple favor?" "You're the one who shot Corbin Esposito?" Olsig shouted, nearly jumping out of his seat in shock. "Not so loud…someone might hear you and we'll both be in the slammer." "But..why?" "Vangelus, a man of your caliber should know not to ask too many questions. Right now, though, I'd appreciate it if you returned the favor." "What could you possibly need me to do for you?" Boss reached deep within his trench coat pocket and retrieved a chrome .22 shotgun. He placed it in the lap of Vangelus Olsig who picked it up, surveying it with deep concern. A Notorious Crime Lord and a gun? This couldn't be good. "He goes by the nickname 'sharker'. Real name is Tony Olsig. You should know him." "Of course I know him…he's my uncle." "And he's also the very guy who's trying to knock me off of my pinnacle at the top of the mob mountain…so to speak. Two bullets is all you'll need to get him out of my life and pay off the debt in which you owe me." "You want me to kill my uncle?" Olsig asked with great concern present in his voice. "Not kill him…save him. Because if I have to do it, I'll make sure he suffers greatly. You wouldn't want that for your beloved uncle, would you?" "Out of all the guys you could hire to do this…why me?" "The odds are in my favor with you pulling the trigger. As soon as he croaks, the finger will be pointed towards me. Everyone in this city knows how bad I want him dead, so when he finally does die…of course I'll be to blame. I wouldn't have committed the crime, however. You would have. They will never expect you. Why? Because it's your uncle, first of all. Secondly, because in two weeks you'll be flying back to the states for that wrassling thing you do, a flight that I'll charter if you do a good enough job. It's rather simple, I'd say." "You're insane. No way I'm pulling the trigger on my own uncle." he responded, obviously irritated at the proposition made by the crime lord. "You owe me, Vangelus." "No…I would have owed you if I asked you to kill of Corbin. I did no such thing, therefore I owe you shit!" Suddenly, Boss throws a sharp elbow into the face of Olsig, forcing him to drop the gun in which Boss immediately snatches up. He places the gun unto the side of Olsig's head, pressing it hard so that Olsig knew he meant business. "You sure about that? I took one life, I have no problem taking another." "…alright, alright! Just…put the gun down." "I knew you'd reconsider. Now, just take this and get the job done. Savvy?" Olsig didn't say a word. Boss pressed a button on the panel of the backseat which shot the door open while they were yet driving. With force, he pushed Olsig out of the car and unto the sidewalk with the car still in motion. And as he lie there with the gun in hand… He struggled to find a solution to his dilemma. A chance at winning GTT. Or the grave. He could never seem to win, could he? -- I am not supposed to win. I am not even supposed to be here. But yet, here I am…facing the manifest which is known as Doctor Curiosity. The critics have weighed me down with their comments, killing any amount of momentum I could muster coming into this bout. Of course the odds are against me, but that's a situation in which I conquered a long time ago. When I was first born, the odds were against me. When I first entered wrestling, the odds were against me. When I first one the PRIME Intense Championship, the odds were against me. This situation has yet to distinguish itself from others. But this time, I even doubt myself. How can a man so lowly as myself rise above the ashes and defeat such as threat of Doctor Curiosity? In my mind and in that of others, it can't be done…not from a probably sense, atleast. But in the name of triumph, I'll continue to fight. Even if I am forced to relinquish my pride in the process. No more illusions. No more gimmicks. Just…fight. TBC
