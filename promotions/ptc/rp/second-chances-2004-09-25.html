Second Chances

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 “…say that one more time…” The words were spewed forth with hostility and anger. “Yes, sir. Sebastian Moraco was eliminated in the first round.” The reply was filled with uncertainty and fear. The kind of response you’d expect a child to have when he’s admitting to doing something wrong. He knows he’s wrong, and he knows he’s going to be punished. [name withheld] feels that way now. He put his pride and, more importantly, his money on a young rookie sensation out of New York. Not that Sebastian was a total failure. He drew commendable aspirations from the crowds, but he lost. And in a sport where losing and winning is the difference between life and death, that is a big negative. “You put our business on some virgin who loses in the first round? What the hell are you trying to do?” he snarled. He was a big burly middle-aged man. You wouldn’t have guessed he was fifty by looking at his body. There were two things about Ross Fletchall. The first and foremost was that he had to be perfect. Everything around him had to be perfect too. If you weren’t, you’d better be training your ass off for it. The second thing was that he had to stay in shape. It helps him keep up with the youth of today. Jeff tugged at his collar as he watched Ross sitting uncomfortably in the darkest corner of the room. “This isn’t a bad situation, Ross.” “Oh no?” Jeff shook his head. “No, as luck would have it, Sebastian’s been offered a second chance in the tournament. The tourney committee was so impressed with him, that they offered him a second chance. They believe that being matched up with someone like Inphino Blitz in the first was tough luck. He could have gone farther if circumstances would have been different.” Ross nodded in agreement and understanding. “Then things are going as planned. Tell me, did Sebastian accept the offer?” Jeff’s mouth gaped open and more a moment looked like he was about to swallow flies. “I’m not sure.” Mile High Sebastian slouched back in his seat on American Airlines Flight 228. He wore a white collar t-shirt with black pants, and timberland boots. The two seats next to him were empty on this flight, so he decided to keep his carry-on next to him as well as move his unwanted pillow and blanket over. Sebastian looked outside his window down to the Hawaiian Islands down below. He stared down at the beauty of Mother Earth from above, as well as gaze down on the Pacific Fleet harbored in Pearl Harbor. One month had gone by. Hawaii was a lot of fun for all three of them: Aaron, his girlfriend Stacy, and Sebastian. Aaron and Stacy had left Hawaii a week earlier due to Stacy’s classes beginning a week earlier. Had they had time, they would have stayed the week trying to console Sebastian over his early departure in the fourth annual GTT tournament. Sebastian stayed the week in Hawaii alone and spent as much time trying to train for Beijing’s Olympics as he did drinking alone. Sebastian pressed the flight attendant intercom and motioned for one of the flight attendants. “Can I have a glass of ice water?” He asked the flight attendant when she finally arrived to his seat.” “Yes sir.” She replied ever so politely. “Would you like anything else? Another bag of peanuts, maybe?” Sebastian sighed. “Sure, why not. I’ll take some peanuts as well.” Sebastian opened up his bag of salted peanuts and lazily picked at them. Around him, everyone was either sleeping or had their eyes glued on the in-flight movie. “A Beautiful Mind” was playing. Thank God, Sebastian thought looking at the screen. He was afraid the airlines would have been showing another rendition of the Time Machine. Nevertheless, this was a good time for him to just catch up on some sleep. The winner of the match is “The Black Dragon Master” Inphino Blitz Sebastian woke up in a sudden manner. That is if you consider closing your eyes for a second to be sleeping like so many high school teachers seem to think. Sebastian leaned against the window and sighed in disgust. He couldn’t forget that loss. He was so close. He had Blitz on the run, and was about to make tournament-breaking history. There he was, a rookie, an amateur-wrestling college student, on the brink of taking down one of the best fighters in the world. Just one more second, one more count, one more word, and Blitz would have lost. Then he would be here answering loads of questions from awe-struck fans, and not be here sitting alone mellowing in his own failure. Yet, that one more second never came. Blitz turned the situation around and before Sebastian could recuperate, Blitz took control. That was the end of it. That was the end of Sebastian Moraco. He got lucky. That’s all there is to it. I could tell he was afraid. He was scared. Right now he’s probably thanking his lucky stars he got pass me. I saw how he looked at me when I entered the ring. I could see the look of overconfidence and disgust in his smirk as the announcer introduced me. There he was a renowned fighter having to do battle with an unknown like myself. How could he dirty his hands so? That was his mistake. One more failure, and it would have been his undoing. I could see the desperation in his eyes as he fought valiantly to stay alive. Who the hell was this kid? Who the hell was Sebastian Moraco? He knows now who I am. He got lucky, but he won’t get lucky again. “Hey bradda, you that guy that was fighting right?” a voice woke Sebastian up. Sebastian opened his eyes lazily and looked over in disgust and anger at the guy’s poor manners. The guy was an overweight Samoan who just happened to be passing by the aisle. “What?” Sebastian replies sheepishly. “You the braddah who was fighting the other night, right? Against Imphino Blitz, or something like that. Man, how do you guys get into those things?” Sebastian shrugged. “I don’t know. I was invited.” Just then the Samoan turned around calling for someone he was traveling with. “Look at this guy, bro!” He shouted out being rude to the passengers. “He was the guy we saw fighting the other night.” His friend walked by and nodded in agreement. “That was some tough luck, bro.” His friend replied. Sebastian sighed. “It’s okay. I moved on. We all move on.” The Samoan eye’s lit up as if he had just remembered he left his meatloaf in the oven. “You know I heard a lot of those fighters were given second chances. Did you get one?” he asked Sebastian. Sebastian shook his head trying his damnest to ignore them. “No, I’ll be fine.” He answered them. “Well you should,” he said. “Oh yeah, I meant to ask you, where is the fighting taking place?” “It’s in….” Thump! “Sorry sirs, you’re going to have to get back in your seats!” the flight attendant called to them. The two guys said their goodbyes, but not before they could get an answer. “Where is the fighting at?” Sebastian sighed. “It’s taking place in…” Thump! “We are experiencing some heavy turbulence, sorry for the discomfort. Everyone return to your seats and we should be in clear skies in a few minutes.” The pilot said on the PA system. Sebastian buckled up and fell back to sleep amidst the rocking of the plane. “GTT is being held in…” Thump! Oh fuck it. Just leave me alone. ”A lot of fighters were given second chances.” Yes, they were. A few select fighters were given the opportunity to redeem themselves because they had been so impressive to both the committee and the audience. A few blotched spots in the tournament brackets left some open vacancies for some fighters to try to redeem their early departures. Sebastian Moraco was one of the fighters. His close call in the first round proved that some draws were just unlucky. For that, the committee decided to give him a second chance in the tournament. Sebastian realized this and to reclaim his pride accepted. However, he didn’t know that elsewhere [name withheld] was praying he would accept. Except his matters were a bit more different than pride. I’m not going to make the same mistakes this time. Inphino made the mistake of underestimating me and it nearly caused him the match. I won’t do the same with my new opponent, P. Vetra of PRIME fame. I don’t know you very well, Vetra. But I do know enough about you to dismiss you. Who are you Vetra? Are you enough for me to not dismiss you? Or are you simply another obstacle in the way of immortality? Getting in touch [name withheld] sped along the H-1 highway on his way to the Honolulu Prince Hotel, where Sebastian was staying in. Little did he know that Sebastian was already packed up and in mid-flight to New York City. Jeff picked up his cell and began to redial Sebastian’s number for the umpteenth time. “Fuck, answer the phone you ignorant fool!” He shouted at no one in particular. He was desperate to get Sebastian back into the tournament. “Might as well call someone else.” Jeff exclaimed as he dialed a number he had gotten by some underhanded methods. Jeff looked at his surroundings making sure he hadn’t skipped the exit. Much like Sebastian’s phone, this phone rang until the answering machine picked up. “Good day to you Mr. Inphino Blitz.” Jeff answered in a mixture of anger and delight. The kind of mixture you get when you’re trying to plan something underhanded. “Don’t answer our phone do we? That’s ok, Mr. Blitz. How did it feel nearly losing to a guy who was fighting in his first match, ever? Pretty good wouldn’t you say? I’ll tell you what Mr. Blitz. You’re a damn good fighter, but you got lucky this time. You won’t get lucky the second time around. Mr. Moraco was given a second chance in the tournament, and this time he won’t lose to you. But perhaps you and I can come to some arrangement. I’ll be waiting to hear from you.” Jeff hung up. He sighed and breathed out slowly. He was lying and he knew it. He had no idea if Sebastian had taken up the offer to compete again, or not. And in a few minutes, he was about to find out that Sebastian wasn’t in Hawaii anymore. Touch down. Sebastian slung his carry-on bag over his shoulder and grabbed his suitcase off the baggage claim. He walked towards the outside where Aaron and Stacy were waiting for him. School was about to start soon for some of them. It was going to be a whole new year of satisfaction and disappointment, and of losses and wins. But for Sebastian, there was going to be much more at stake. Sebastian passed by the revolving doors at LaGuardia Airport. As Aaron and Stacy made their way towards him, Sebastian’s phone rang. He dug it out of his pocket and before answering the phone realized he had 20 messages pending. He answered the phone and not to his surprise heard [name withheld] on the other line. “I’m so sorry Jeff. I meant to call you to tell you I was going back to New York.” Sebastian claimed falsely. He smiled. “Yeah, I know. I’m okay.” Sebastian motioned for Aaron and Stacy to give him one minute. “About that tournament, yes. Well…” He began as he looked at his friends. “I’ve decided to take that second chance.”
