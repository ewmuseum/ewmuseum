Opening New Doors

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-15 What do you do when your whole world changes? What do you do when everything you’ve known for most of the past two years suddenly evaporates? What do you do when the team you’ve worked so hard to make successful is taken apart, even if it is only temporary (and willingly)? What do you do when, for the first time in eight months and only the second time in thirteen months, you no longer hold the XUW tag team championship belts? What do you do when you find yourself facing a true singles career for the first time in nearly two years? Do you go on a vision quest, out to the desert to smoke some peyote and find yourself? Do you say screw it and find yourself another partner? Do you sit on your ass and wallow in false misery? Some people might. But the Latch Key Kid isn’t some people. He doesn’t do any of those things. Rather, he does what he’s always done when trouble has arisen in XUW and when he needs to find some respite. He goes to get coffee. Frank’s Coffeehouse is an unassuming place from the outside. It fits in well with the surrounding buildings and it doesn’t draw attention to itself with flashy signs, frou-frou drinks or cheap gimmicks. It doesn’t need to. It already has a steady flow of customers, some regulars and some not, and that’s all Frank’s needs to survive. Nobody begrudges Frank’s for not following the trend of corporate coffeehouses. In fact, that’s why they go there in the first place. They know what they’ll get: fresh coffee, friendly service, a place to sit and relax and play chess or read poetry or whatever they want to do, and the satisfaction that they’re giving their business to a pillar of the local community rather than to some corporation whose locations seems to spread faster than the predatory kudzu vine throughout the southeastern United States. And then, of course, there’s Frank himself. If you were to ask him, Frank would fancy himself a bit of a philosopher, a sage, a poet, a lover of life, a bit of a renaissance man. But of course, nobody would ever ask him that, because everyone knows that Frank doesn’t like to talk about himself. He’s much more interested in the lives of his customers and their problems. And he’s always willing to lend an ear and some advice. This has been the case quite a lot in the past two years, as two of his biggest customers have become regulars, even acquiring their own table in the establishment. The Latch Key Kid and Doughboy, collectively known as the Lunchroom Patrol, have been frequenting Frank’s since their days in XUDF, XUW’s development federation. What once started out as a simple trip to grab a cup of coffee soon turned into a regular hangout for the Patrol, though back in XUDF they were friends and at times opponents, as they did not form their tag team until both were called up to XUW. Frank happens to be working behind the counter at the moment. Just general work, really, helping with upkeep and cleaning and whatever he can find to occupy his time. The door opens, and in walks the Latch Key Kid. He waves as he approaches the counter, and shakes Frank’s hand. “How are we doing today, Frank?” “Not too bad, Brad. And yourself?” Frank has always preferred to call Latch by his real name. He does the same thing with Doughboy. “Could be better, could be worse. Looks like the place is doing pretty good for a weekday.” “It comes and goes, you know? It’s been pretty steady today, though. Always nice to see that. So come on, let’s go grab a seat.” Frank pours two mugs of latte, and hands one to Latch. They make their way to the Lunchroom Patrol’s usual table, and take their seats. Latch sips his coffee while Frank looks Latch over and rubs his chin. “Okay. Spill it. Let’s go.” “Spill what?” “Spill the beans, Brad.” “No way, Frank. You just brewed them. Great coffee, by the way.” Latch gives Frank a thumbs up. “I’m not talking about the coffee. Something’s troubling you, Brad. I can see it in your eyes. Now, out with it. It won’t do you any good to keep it bottled up.” “I’d rather not, Frank. Besides, I trouble you with enough stuff as it is. There’s no need to put this one on you, too. It’s not your job to sit here and listen to me.” “Funny. For as much as I actually do sit here and listen to you, you’d think it was my second job. If you really don’t want to talk about it, I can’t make you. But you know that’s what I’m here for. Well, that and brewing quality java.” “I guess you’re right. It’s just, well…this whole thing with Doughboy leaving. We’ve gone through some rough stuff in the past month or so. First it was Mr. Webster insisting that our long title reign was ruining his organization, much to our dismay and his pleasure. So when we did vacate the belts, I thought it would be over. But no. He had to send his goons after Doughboy and his girl. He couldn’t leave it alone. And now, Doughboy’s gone. I don’t know when he’s coming back to XUW, and I don’t know if he’s coming back to XUW.” “You mean you haven’t talked to him?” Frank asks with a frown. “I was in the hospital, Frank. Remember? All that stuff with Henrik? That’s why I couldn’t help Doughboy and why I wasn’t in the locker room to stop that attack on Nicole. It’s partially my fault. If I hadn’t taken it to Henrik like that, I wouldn’t have wound up in the hospital in the first place.” “You don’t have to blame yourself for anything. You’re not responsible for what they did to Nicole. You didn’t take it to Henrik, you just fought back. It’s not your fault he baited you like that. You know, if you ask me, it sounds like you need some time off. Just walk away for a little bit. Do what Doughboy did. Take a little break. Go back home and clear your mind. It’ll do you some good.” ----------------------------------------------- Home. Galveston, Texas. A pleasant city, if not a bit run-down in parts. An island, actually, just off the coast of Texas, roughly an hour drive from Houston. It was here where Brad Jameson was born in July of 1976. He was one of the hurricane babies, those select few who were born as Hurricane Brad pounded the island with heavy winds and rain, and knocked out power to much of the island. He was named for the storm, actually, which suited him early on in life. His father walked out on his family when Brad was young. His mother was forced to take a second job to have the means to provide for Brad and his sister, Courtney. As such, Brad’s mother was around even less after that. And thus was born the Latch Key Kid. Growing up as a latch key kid would be tough for any child: parents working all day, forced to take care of yourself at home after school. But for a child such as Brad, quiet, meek, non-confrontational, it was even worse. With nobody to pick him up from school, Brad walked home everyday. His walk often took him near a pack of bullies, who spared no expense in exerting themselves and making their presence felt. And they often exerted themselves against Brad, picking on the loner. He fought back one day, though, when he body slammed one of the older bullies on the sidewalk. Brad was later asked to replace the boy in the gang, but he refused. From that day forward, he made it his goal to fight back against the bullies. His quest eventually led him to XUDF, and, after a few months, he was the first home-grown XUDF wrestler to receive a call-up to XUW. Home. Galveston, Texas. No, just as Thomas Wolfe once wrote, he could not go home again. Not just yet, at least. ----------------------------------------------- “No can do, Frank. It would be counterproductive.” “How do you mean?” “I mean, that’s the last place I need to be right now. For all the memories home holds, both good and bad, there’s no way I would be able to unwind and relax the way you think I should. Besides, it’s a little late for that now.” Frank hesitates, and then frowns. “Brad, what did you do?” “You remember that tournament Dough and I were talking about a while back?” “You didn’t.” “Oh, I did. I went ahead and tossed my name in the hat, Frank. And now I have a match I have to get ready for. More than one, hopefully.” “This hardly gives you the rest you need, Brad.” “I know, Frank, and I can accept that. But haven’t you ever wondered what it would be like to try something completely different than the usual?” “I did wonder that once.” ”And what happened?” ”I went into business for myself and opened this coffee house.” “Point taken. And that worked out pretty good for you, I’d say. But now, I’m wondering what it would be like to do something different. A year and a half, Frank. That’s about the length of time Doughboy and I spent as a team. Don’t get me wrong, now. I don’t regret that at all. In fact, it was great to be known as the most dominant tag team in XUW. After a series of setbacks, of course. Dough was the best partner I ever could have hoped for, and I have no doubt that we’ll team together in the future again. But somewhere, in the back of my mind, I’ve been starting to wonder what it would be like to be known as a singles wrestler. Not as a tag team wrestler who sometimes wrestles in singles matches, but as a singles wrestler first and foremost.” “But why? Why change a good thing?” “The Lunchroom Patrol had run its course, Frank. The tag division was stale, Webster’s wish was coming true, and then, well, you know why Doughboy walked out. I don’t blame him one bit. The Lunchroom Patrol isn’t dead, Frank. It’s just on a hiatus. But until that hiatus is over, I’ve got to find out how I can fare in the singles ranks. I’ve got to scratch that itch that’s growing in the back of my head.” “And you’re doing that by entering this tournament?” "Exactly. No matter what happens, I’ll get some answers to my questions. Am I good enough to make it as a singles competitor? I think so. But I’ll never know unless I test myself. And what better test than this tournament?” “I guess you have a point there, Brad. Just be careful, though. You might not like what you find.” “How do you mean? If I find out I’m no good at this singles thing, that’ll be it. I can accept it. But if I do find out that I’m any good at it, I can accept that, too.” “That’s what I’m talking about, Brad. What if you find out you’re better at it than you expected?” “Huh?” "What if you find out that you’re too good at it? What then? That might spell the end for the Lunchroom Patrol for good.” “Oh. I get it. You’re worried that Doughboy and I may never get our team back together. I wouldn’t worry about that too much, Frank. Dough and I will always be friends, at the very least, and it’s only a matter of time before we team up for that one special match down the road.” “I hope you’re right, Brad, I really do.” “Thanks, Frank. And speaking of matches, I’m late. I hate to run Frank, but I need to go meet someone.” “Not a problem. I enjoyed our visit, as usual. And hey, tell her I said hello, will you?” Latch pauses. “How’d you – " He stops when he sees Frank scratching his chin. “I always know, Brad.” Latch nods at Frank and turns and walks out of the coffee shop. ----------------------------------------------- An attractive blonde female sits at a table in front of a sidewalk café, patiently glancing at her watch. The expression on her face betrays a look of hope and excitement, and her fingers tapping on the table rhythmically pass the time. Katherine first met Latch after he spent some time in the hospital. She worked in the flower shop at the time. Doughboy met her when he stopped in to buy a gift for Latch, and after Latch was released, Dough set the two of them up together. For the past sixteen months or so, she and Latch have been together, though there were a few months in there where she broke off contact, ironically enough, because of the way Latch was treating the evil DarkSeid, who at the time was experiencing fits of amnesia. Still, she waits for Latch today, eager to once again see the man she cares for. And then, a smile appears on her face. She stands up and gives Latch a hug when he reaches the table. They sit down and she stares at him with a pleasant gaze. This is their spot, their café. She speaks first. “I’m glad you could meet me here today.” ”I’d have it no other way. Sorry if I’m late. I stopped by Frank’s to say hello, and he got to talking my ears off. But what else is new. If I had known I’d be so long, I would’ve just come straight here.” “No, no, it’s okay. Frank’s a good friend, and I’m sure he had a few good bits of advice for you. Besides, the waiting just made me look forward to this more.” The waiter approaches then. “Hi there, folks. What’ll it be today? The usual?” “Could you give us a few more minutes, please,” Latch leans over to read the waiter’s name tag, “Johnny?” “Sure thing. You got it. I’ll just be right over there. Call me when you’re ready.” Johnny walks off, and Latch looks at Katherine. “Do you ever wonder what it would be like if we went somewhere else?” "Huh? What do you mean?” “What if we didn’t eat here? What if we just got up, walked out, and went somewhere else?” “Somewhere else? Something different? I don’t know, Brad.” Katherine calls him by his real name, too. Can’t really expect your girlfriend to call you by your wrestling name, so you may as well take what you can get. “Think about it. All those restaurants out there, and yet, every time we get together we meet at this café. Don’t get me wrong, it’s a nice place, good food, all that. But there’s more out there, and I just think maybe we should check it out.” “Are you really that curious about what’s out there?” "You bet I am. Let’s go.” Latch stands up and drops a few dollars on the table for a tip, even though they didn’t order anything. He grabs Katherine’s hand and she stands up, and they walk out of the café together. ----------------------------------------------- “Wow. That was great.” “So you enjoyed it as much as I did?” “Definitely, Brad. Maybe even more than you did.” “I’m not sure that’s possible, Katherine. I enjoyed it an awful lot.” “Well, then, you should be congratulated on your choice. It took a lot of guts to walk out of that café and try a new place. But I must say, you picked a good one.” “And here you were making fun of me for being so curious about what else was out there.” “I wasn’t making fun of you, Brad. I was just asking to be sure that’s what you wanted.” We find the happy couple now in Latch’s house, enjoying a refreshing glass of iced tea while lounging on the sofa. “Well, it worked out well for both of us, I suppose. So next time, we’ll have a tough choice to make. Our old café or the new place. But it’s a win-win situation, really.” “I guess you never know what the other option holds in store until you test it out.” “That’s true. And that’s exactly why I decided to enter the tournament. I figured it was time to test my worth as a singles wrestler and – " “Wait a minute. What’d you say?” ”I said, I figured it was time to test my – " “No, before that.” “Oh. That’s exactly why I decided to enter the tournament.” “That’s what I thought you said. Why, Brad? What could you possibly have to gain from participating in this thing?” "A world of experience, that’s what. Literally. With so many wrestlers in one tournament, there’s bound to be a clash of styles. And a clash of styles means exposure to new styles. And exposure to new styles means gaining experience. And – " Katherine looks at him, with a worried expression on her face. “But you don’t need that. You know what works for you; it’s what’s worked all along. You and Doughboy had an amazing run as XUW tag team champs. You’re a great team. You’re great tag team wrestlers. You know what you excel at, so why not leave well enough alone?” Latch’s face turns serious. “What if well enough isn’t the best there is, Katherine?” "How do you mean?” “Well, let me put it this way. That café was well enough, right?” "Sure.” “And we messed with that, right? What if we had left it alone and eaten there? What if we had never found this new place? Then what?” “Well,” Katherine nods, “I guess we would’ve missed out on something really special.” “Exactly. Now with this tournament, I’ll be messing with well enough. Leaving behind the tag team scene and venturing off on my own.” He had often wondered about this very thing, secretly. Even as he and Doughboy were lengthening their already-stellar tag title reign, even as they were running through the competition like a parasite through a gringo tourist in Mexico, the Latch Key Kid had thought about what it would be like to be a singles wrestler. He had been there once before, but it was so long ago that he wasn’t sure it counted. And it was only for the few months he was in XUDF. Once Doughboy joined him in XUW, the two friends joined forces and formed the Lunchroom Patrol. And ever since then, Latch had been known primarily as a tag team wrestler. A great tag team wrestler, with a great tag team partner, but it’s human nature to ponder the grass on the other side. Is it truly greener, or just disappointing? What lies on the other side of that door? What other possibilities are out there? Just how big is the world outside of the normal? “So you see, this is something I need to do. I need to know what the other side holds. If I have any luck in this tournament, great. I’ll know that I can hack it as a singles wrestler. If I don’t have much luck, then that’s fine, too. I’ll know what works for me, and I’ll be able to build on it. But I can’t just sit by idly and let opportunities like this pass me by. This tournament really has no bearing on what happens to the Lunchroom Patrol. Doughboy is already gone for who knows how long, and that has far more effect on our team than this tournament.” “So you don’t know how long Doughboy will be gone for?” "No. And you know, he didn’t really tell me. Frank said I shouldn’t beat myself up over not being there to help him and Nicole, but part of me can’t help but think that I may have been able to make a difference. It’s little things like that that make you wonder, Katherine. At least, they make me wonder.” “It seems to me like you’re wondering a lot about things lately. You shouldn’t do that so much. It’s not healthy for one’s disposition.” “Plenty of things aren’t, but that doesn’t seem to stop me. Besides, like you said, if I were really thinking about my disposition, I wouldn’t have gotten anywhere near this tournament. As it is, I’m in, so I may as well make the most of it.” "And Doughboy?” "You know, it’s odd. I swear I thought I saw his name in the brackets. I wonder if this is some sort of swan song for him.” “You’re not in the same bracket, are you?” "No, thank goodness. We may very well never meet up in this tournament, either. But you never know, Katherine. A few things here, a few things there, and the Lunchroom Patrol could be facing off against each other once again.” “You don’t think you’ll really have to face him, do you?” "Like I said, it’s a big tournament. Lots of things would have to go right for that to happen. Doesn’t mean they won’t, just means it would be pretty difficult.” “Well,” Katherine replies, “it sounds like you’re facing this tournament with determination and a positive attitude. That will be important. Who’s your opponent in the first round, anyway?” “Guy by the name of Jonathon Banner. I don’t know much about him, really. It sounds like he mainly wrestles in indy feds on the west coast. It looks like he prefers more of a technical style, like me. Other than that, I’m not really sure.” "So how are you going to approach this match?” "Same way I approach every match. With caution and optimism. I’ve had plenty of success in XUW, Katherine. I know I’ve been primarily a tag team wrestler for a while now, but I think I’ve still got it in me. The one major difference is that I won’t have a partner to tag in. But then again, neither will my opponent.” “I’d say that’s a good way to approach it. Caution and optimism. Careful but confident. I’m sure you’ll do fine, Brad. Now how about a refill on this iced tea?” Latch laughs and takes both glasses into the kitchen. As he refills them with the carafe of iced tea, Katherine calls out to him from the couch. “So while we’re talking about trying new things, have you ever thought about going anywhere else for your cup of coffee?” Latch walks back into the living room with the glasses, shaking his head. “I’m not that curious, Katherine. Besides, Frank would kill me.” She laughs as Latch hands her glass to her and sits down next to her. She leans into his shoulder and puts her legs up on the sofa. Fade out.
