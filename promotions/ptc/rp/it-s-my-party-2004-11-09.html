It's my party...

9 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-09 "Oh shit!" The female voice echoed off the cold stone walls. "Oh shit, shit, shit, shit and shit!" The despair etched into the trembling voice was thrown into contrast by the giggle of the other inhabitant of the room, Doctor Curiosity. "Something wrong, my dear?" he asked. "Shit!" ---------------------------------------------------------- Entering the fifth round of GTT4, Doctor Curiosity had two things that he was determined to keep intact. The first, most obvious of these was his winning record. He had surpassed the efforts of George English, Jade, Alex Grey, and most recently, the Muppet Man. Next in line between Curiosity and glory was to be Sebastian Moraco. He was not a man that the good doctor had heard of before, but that wouldn't stop him from being a challenge; a challenge that Doctor Curiosity was intent on overcoming. To do so he would have to try to keep hidden from the world the other thing he was intent on maintaining. His secret. For all his foolishness both inside the ring and out, there was more than met the eye to the good doctor. His strange inventions and love of abusing his sidekick, Eegor, served only to hide the truth. A riddle wrapped in an enigma, the doctor had been manipulating both his subservient brother and his scheming wife, whilst negotiating his way past a series of very talented wrestlers. It was beginning to seem like Doctor Curiosity was perhaps every bit as clever as his foolish persona proclaimed to be. However, were this to be discovered by Collette, his gold-digging wife, the consequences would surely be terrible. Fourteen hours before a stream of curse words would turn the air in the Curious Laboratory blue, the doctor sat on a hard-backed wooden chair and stared at the two people he had been stringing along. To his left sat his brother, Paul Goodman, known to the wrestling world as Eegor, the deformed assistant to the doctor. His thinning hair was brushed every which way, and he wore a large black coat that was covered in dust. Eegor's face bore something between a snarl and a grimace; the face of a man who is in the presence of someone he detests, but who is powerless to take any action against them. To the doctor's right sat the source of Eegor's dismay. Collette Goodman was looking as lovely as ever, her short dark hair perfectly framing her delicate English features. She was, for once, dressed in casual clothes, her austere business suits consigned temporarily to a curious closet somewhere nearby. Like everyone else in the room, though, her appearance was nothing but a façade for her to hide behind. This was no prim and proper English beauty, for Collette had been estranged from her husband for two years prior to their recent 'reconciliation', and she was back for one thing and one thing only; money. Her plans to help Doctor Curiosity win GTT4 seemed to be going well, but once he was established as a successful, and what's more, as a lucrative star of wrestling, she would divorce him and half of it all would be hers. Eegor fumed silently as Doctor Curiosity made them both wait to hear what he had to say. The assistant to the doctor had learned of Collette's plans, and had tried to tell his master, only to be thrown out of the Curious Laboratory for daring to suggest that Collette had returned out of anything other than love. "Now, vhilst I realize zat you two do not seem to get on zat vell, I tzink it vould be in my best interests to have both of you around," said the doctor, in his fake German accent. Eegor and Collette exchanged glances. They had once been at one another's throats in an entirely different way, their affair being one of the main reasons behind the split between the doctor and his bride. Eegor reflected that it had been a mistake, and a costly one too; he was still paying his brother back with his servitude. "To celebrate my defeat of ze Muppet Man, I tzought zat I could get ze pair of you together vith me, and ve could have a fun time," continued the doc, his eyes gleaming blue behind his cracked wire-frame glasses. Collette groaned. The plan was going well, but recently she had started to wonder if she shouldn't just buy a lottery ticket instead. It would certainly have been less painful than having to be around Curiosity and his brother all of the time. "To patch everytzing up, and to make ze two of you get on better, ve vill be having a party!" "A party?" asked Collette. "Jah! A party! I have bought some vine, and some party snacks." "Will there be funny shaped balloons?" asked Eegor. "Only if you tzink round is funny." ----------------------------------------------------------- "How… how did this?" Collette left the question half-formed in the air. Doctor Curiosity smiled as he lay back, either unwilling or unable to answer it. "I don't remember… there was wine, and we did… oh no, we did karaoke." Curiosity laughed at this, as Collette grew even more frantic, memories assailing her of the night before. "Then it was too late for me to go back to my hotel, and I was drunk, so you said I could stay… and then… oh God. Please tell me… we didn't… I mean. Did we?" Collette looked pleadingly at her husband, as she clutched the white sheet to her body. He sat up in the bed and blinked his eyes before nodding his head. "Jah. Ve did it." Collette's jaw dropped. "Tvice." … "SHIT!" ----------------------------------------------------------- The party was going swimmingly. Both Eegor and Collette were headed towards inebriation at an alarming rate, and Doctor Curiosity himself was as sober as a judge. Taking the occasional surreptitious sip of his wine when necessary, the doctor was mostly managing to dispose of the liquid in other ways. His plants, the doctor reflected, might need some Tylenol in the morning. The sound of the Village People burst from the speakers, and Eegor's dyslexia seemed to be affecting his performance of the requisite dance moves, unless of course the song was indeed about staying at the 'CMYA'. Curiosity watched him with a kind of clinical detachment, his mind scheming new ways to use his brother's dedication and unwavering loyalty to their advantage. The good doctor did genuinely dislike having to use his brother in such an unwitting way, but what he didn't know was unlikely to harm him. Collette was having slightly more trouble lightening up, but her hair was being metaphorically let down. She’d not got up to do a song yet, but at such a small party, there would inevitably be a time where she would have to grab the microphone. “Is nobody else coming then?” she asked of the doctor. “Who else do I know?” he shrugged in response. It was a flippant response, but underneath the veneer of insouciance lurked a deeper truth. In his previous life as a research scientist, Dr Curiosity had had many colleagues and associates, but precious few friends. When he went off the rails following the messy end of his marriage, there were few shoulders to cry on. As his seclusion increased to the level associated with hermits, people stopped calling, until the phone finally became redundant. His self-imposed exile from the rest of the world had never bothered him. Even now, after working both with and against other wrestlers from his stint with fWo and OCW, Doctor Curiosity never considered anyone he had teamed up with to be anything more than a tool, no more useful or precious than a piece of scientific apparatus. Some might be more easily worthwhile than others, a particle accelerator as opposed to a test tube, but all were the same. The concept of friendship had grown alien to the doctor. Collette knocked back another shot of tequila and sat back in her seat. She studied Curiosity and watched him as he looked on at Eegor. She didn’t know the full extent of the doctor’s withdrawal from society, though she could have had a good guess at it. She tried to think if it was perhaps her fault; that she was maybe to blame for changing a good man into what she saw before her. It might have been the alcohol in her system, but she felt some warmth towards him still. She shook her head. What the hell had brought that on? She was the wronged one here. Sure, she had cheated on the doctor, and with his own brother no less, but he had brought it on himself. He’d been the one that had always been working late, without the money that might justify such an activity. He’d been the one unable to conceive a child, and the one that had hidden away from his responsibilities. She’d had no choice but to end it, and if the manner in which everything came to a head had been distasteful to him, then whose fault was that? It was his, and that’s why she was doing what she had to do. The world could be a tough and unforgiving place. Collette had learned that it was kick or be kicked, kill or be killed, and dupe your husband along and steal all his money or be duped by your husband and have all his money remain within his own account. If she had to put up with his irritating company for a little while, then she could do it, and this tequila would certainly help matters. She threw another measure of the spirit down her throat and smiled as it burned within her. -------------------------------------------------------- "So, vas it good for you?" asked the doctor. Collette screeched wordlessly and began to pace across the room, dragging the bed-sheet behind her. It amused Doctor Curiosity to see her so nervous, so worried about what might have happened. The sheet slipped at times, offering glimpses of her toned body, but it did nothing to arouse the doctor at all. In fact, despite his teasing, Doctor Curiosity was not at all attracted to Collette. His mind was as clear as a crystal stream, and in it was fixed the image of his wife cheating on him, and her slowly manipulating her way back into his life. That image hung in his mind, much as it had done the previous night… …the previous night, when he had let Collette stay, because it was late and she was too drunk and tired to go back to her hotel. The previous night, when he had let her rest on his bed, but had not slept with her. The previous night, when he had slipped under the same covers, but on the other side, not touching nor even coming close to her. Curiosity smiled. He held all the cards now. She had no clue what had happened, and as such, he could invent anything and she might believe it. Collette, in marked contrast, was frantic. Her bare feet slapped down on the cold stone floor, and she started to notice that she could do with some footwear. “Where the hell are my shoes?” Curiosity giggled to himself. This was going to be fun. “Vell, ve vere in ze shower, and zen you kicked zem off as ve moved to ze kitchen…” “We did it in the kitchen?” asked Collette, appalled. “Jah! It vas your idea!” Collette reeled at the news, and looked at the doctor. “Get out.” “Vhy?” “I need to get changed back into my clothes.” “But I saw you vith no clothes on just last night, my dear. Vhat is ze matter? Don’t you love me any more?” replied the doctor, with a smirk. Collette hesitated. She was trying to con Curiosity into thinking she did indeed love her, but the thought of being naked in front of him now was unbearable. She had to think quickly. “I… ummmm… I need to… keep it special for you, darling. Yes, if you are to see me like this too often, you might take me for granted. So, you should really leave the room. Can you? Please?” she said. “I vould, my darling, but something holds me back.” “What?” she demanded impatiently. “I too am naked.” --------------------------------------------------------- The party had really kicked off, as far as any party with three people who mostly dislike one another can. The stereo system was still pumping out the cheesy party classic tunes, and Eegor sung “I Come from a Land Down Under” with great gusto, as Doctor Curiosity tried to convince Collette to join in with the party games. “Collette, it vill be fun. Ve can all play together, ze three of us. Eegor, come over here and stop your ridiculous dancing!” The doctor beckoned over his assistant, who stood almost still, a vague swaying motion remaining from either the music or the alcohol. “Now, ve must play a game called Pin Ze Maracas on Ze Sebastian!” The stunned silence spoke for itself (other than the sound of Men At Work in the background). “What?” asked Eegor and Collette, in unison. “Pin Ze Maracas on Ze Sebastian! It is much like ze game of Pin Ze Tail on Ze Donkey, but transformed into a vay zat I can mock ze stupid Spanish fool Sebastian Maracas at ze same time, jah!” Silence reigned once more, before Eegor broke it. “Ummm, he’s called Sebashtian Moraco, and even if he was called Maracash, why would this help you in any way at all?” slurred Eegor. Curiosity looked between Collette and Eegor, and dragged his assistant off to one side, mouthing the word ‘tactics’ at his wife as they moved out of her earshot. Collette bobbed up and down expectantly as the men talked in hushed tones over to the side. “I’m a little drunk, mashter, but I think that thish ishn’t the best idea you’ve ever had.” “Oh really?” replied Doctor Curiosity, without a hint of either drunkenness or a German accent. “Well maybe I know better than you this time… Paul.” Curiosity turned back to Collette, and left Eegor’s befuddled, drunken mind trying to grasp what it has just been informed of. Paul Goodman, Eegor, had always known that the character of Doctor Curiosity was a shell, a mask behind which his brother, Chris, hid. He’d always known that in times of stress, or when he was feeling emotional, the mask might slip and he’d get a glimpse of the man beneath; a man hurt, but wanting to carry on somehow, if only to wreak revenge against the rest of humanity. What he had not known was that he could seemingly pick up and drop the character at will. Eegor’s mind raced, even in its drunken state. If he was really planning all of this from behind a façade of idiocy, then maybe he wasn’t as helpless as he sometimes seemed. It all finally clicked. Curiosity had known about Collette all along. Eegor’s relief was the first emotion to hit, but it was quickly followed by joy, and then glee. If they both knew about her schemes, they could have all manner of fun… ---------------------------------------------------------- “Okay, so I’ll turn my back and you can get up, put some clothes on and leave the room, so I can in turn put clothes on and leave… right?” said Collette. Curiosity nodded, and motioned for her to turn her back. She did so, adding, “Oh, and no sticking notes on my back asking people to kick me!” The doctor put down the pen and paper, and cursed quietly as he rolled out of the bed. In doing so he revealed that he was not naked, but was in fact wearing his usual tweed trousers, complete with his thin leather belt. He grabbed his shirt from the floor, knotted his bow tie with the ease of a man practiced in the art, and pulled his lab coat from the back of the door, twirling it on to his shoulders with surprising grace. The cracked glasses were picked up from the dresser, and all was back as normal. “You may turn back, my dear” he said. Collette turned, clutching the sheet close to her chest. Her head was pounding like there was a marching band in there, but she’d never give her husband the satisfaction of knowing it. Besides, he’d been just as drunk, hadn’t he? “Can you leave now please?” asked Collette. He acquiesced, and with a flourish of his arms, Doctor Curiosity walked out of the door, to go and plan further the downfall of both his wife and his next opponent, Sebastian Moraco. --------------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity emptied his glass, of what appeared to be vodka, and then put on a blindfold. Collette and Eegor spun him around ten times, and he stumbled towards the large cardboard effigy of Sebastian Moraco. His cohorts egged him on, and it looked for a moment as if he might indeed pin the maracas to the Sebastian… but at the last instant, the doctor swerved in apparent drunkenness, and the pin found a very different home. “Aaarrgghh!!” yelled Collette, in whose thigh the pin had become embedded. The doctor and his assistant laughed together at the misfortune of the woman they were getting their own back on. “This game is stupid! Now give me more wine and let’s play a game that’s less rubbish!” screeched the English woman, all reserve now thrown to the wind. “Zat is fine my dear, I have a piñata shaped like Sebastian Maracas too! I vill fetch it… oh, and Eegor… get me ze Curious Mallet. I really vant to hit her… I mean zis piñata really hard!” The two brothers laughed. They had the upper hand. They were no longer pawns in Collette’s little games. They were united… and together they might prove unstoppable. Surely nothing could now go wrong? ---------------------------------------------------------- Collette found her bra hanging over a wardrobe door. She’d been looking for her jacket, but it was a welcome addition, since her clothes seemed to have been strewn around like some badly organized treasure hunt. The wife of Doctor Curiosity hooked herself into her undergarments, and entered the Curious Bathroom. “There must be something here to help me. Paracetamol? Aspirin? Alka Seltzer?” she muttered to herself as she rifled through the cupboards and shelves, looking for something to make the hangover more bearable. Her vision was slightly blurred, and despite the last few years she was still not used to the American brands of painkiller. She searched through them, but in the end her frustration caused her arm to lash out, sending everything on one shelf flying through the air. With a sob she dropped to her knees and went to pick up some of the various pills and hygiene products that she had knocked into the trash can. “Reduced to pulling stuff out of bins? Nice work…” she continued to mutter. “He’d better win this bloody tournament after all this!” She stood up and placed a few items back on the shelf. Bending down again, she put her hand into the trash, and came out with a piece of paper. Her intrusive instincts kicking in, she unfolded it, and what she saw before her eyes took her breath away. It was, ostensibly, a shopping list. In fact, it was the receipt for the promotional pictures of Sebastian Moraco. Yet it wasn’t the ticket that she was concerned with, but the drawings in the margin. In Doctor Curiosity’s handwriting were a few words, and a few drawings. The stick figures were crude, but were clearly of Eegor, the doctor and herself. The words were just as simple, but they cut deep. ‘Kill stupid Collette. Kill her in her stupid head.’ The receipt was dated for the previous day. Her blood ran cold. Curiosity knew why she was there. For a few seconds she didn’t move at all, but soon a veil of serenity passed on to her face. She smiled, crumpled the receipt and dropped it back where it came from. They thought they could play games with her, did they? Collette laughed out loud and wandered back to the bedroom. It was time to turn the tables.
