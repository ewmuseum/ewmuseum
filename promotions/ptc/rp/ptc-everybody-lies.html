"Everybody Lies"

26 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Everybody Lies. No matter how much evolution has progressed in the last few million years, it’s still a deeply embedded basic human condition. It has a certain inevitability about it that cannot be ignored nor altered. The only true variable in the whole proviso is what a person lies about at any given opportunity. 9:03am. Monday 20th March 2006. New York City. Leant against the freshly waxed hood of the car, I reach through the open window and slam down hard upon the horn. Passers by, startled at the sudden brash clamour, glare at me angrily. I shoot each of them an arrogant smile, pissing them off a whole lot more. In the fifth floor window of the building I’m parked in front of, the drapes are pushed aside and a man wearing a simple white vest appears in the window with a broad smile casually painted across his face. I shake my head in disgust and settle back down against the hood of the car, noting the rather sizeable scratch I’d somehow made right under the windscreen. I didn‘t much care. It isn‘t my car. I’m told it’s the standard issue employee transport that my illustrious employer, Mr Stakiri, utilises in the majority of his less-than legal business ventures. Whether this information is accurate is another matter entirely. I have no use for cars. Don’t need them. I can make it to any part of the city faster on foot than I can by car and that’s not just a topical indictment of New York traffic congestion. It’s something else. If I told you, you wouldn’t understand. I glance at my watch. 9:05 am. Five minutes behind schedule. Why Stakiri hired this as clown - or Bridges as Stakiri called him - over the years is beyond me. No, that’s inaccurate. I knew why. Bridges was a ‘yes’ man. He got things done. He dealt with a good portion of Stakiri’s dirty laundry. I guess punctuality doesn’t matter much if you‘re good at your job. But times have changed. I extract my mobile phone from my suit jacket pocket and flip it open with one smooth movement. No missed calls. No text messages. No e-mails. Predictable, but that’s not what I’m looking for. My fingers glide over the key panel and a few buttons later I’m looking at the date in my calendar. I got the call from Primetime Central just after getting back from Stakiri’s office last night. They informed me of another opportunity to prove my worth. The Infinite Gauntlet. The wrestling event for the ages. The constant fight of physical and mental superiority over all others. My kind of battle. My kind of war. My kind of disappointment. My reluctance. But with all that’s happened to me over the past couple of months can you really blame me? I’ve had my faith in my own wrestling ability truly shaken to the point in which I’m not sure that I can compete with the very best anymore. It all began at the GTT5. Before then I was having one helluva time tearing up the WWA roster like they were nothing more than glorified hobo’s, paid by the bookers to fight - and to lose - against me on any given night. I dominated them all like a violent alcoholic father routinely dominates his wife and children. They feared me. They hated me. But unlike the alcoholic father analogy, the WWA roster secretly wanted to be me. Then the GTT5 came along. I wanted to compete with the best the wrestling industry had to offer and what’s more I did. Mostly. I lost in the first round, quite convincingly, to Seymour Almasy and I couldn’t look myself in the mirror for a week or more afterward. Don’t get me wrong, Almasy was - is - a worthy opponent to anyone. More than that, he’s a legend, but my loss to him in the first round should’ve indicated to me that I simply wasn’t good enough to compete with the best of the best. Yet, in my naivety, I accepted the tournament booker’s offer to return in the second round despite my loss. I should’ve turned the offer down. I was beaten by just one of the outside favourites to win the thing and that should’ve told me right then and there I shouldn’t have gone onward. But my pride and my ego got in the way of my rationale and for a time I thought my decision was truly vindicated. I reached the final sixteen with many other of my WWA colleagues, but that’s where it ended. My dreams of success were completely obliterated by superior men. Since then, everything has gone downhill. My undefeated streak in the WWA was firstly undermined by a tainted draw for the current WWA World Champion and then destroyed soon after with an equally tainted loss at the hands of WWA mainstay Khaled. They both needed to cheat in order to get their respective results, but that’s not the point. In the past I’ve been able to adapt to any given situation. It’s my skill. It’s how I’ve been able to survive for so damned long when my memories were wiped almost two years ago. Khaled and Ember shouldn’t have been able to get anywhere near me, even with cheating on their side. I should’ve been able to adapt to that situation like I’ve done so many times before. But I didn’t and now my wrestling career has reached a plateau that I can’t seem to escape. Despite all of this. Despite all of the reasons I’ve tried to convince myself precisely why I shouldn’t enter the Infinite Gauntlet, I find the very same reasons are precisely why I should enter the tournament. My wrestling career is at a stand-still at the moment. That much I’ll freely admit and allow my critics to have their time in the sun. But that’s all I’ll admit. It’s not over for me by a long shot. Never will be. I’m going pick myself up and face whatever the Infinite Gauntlet has to throw at me, without fear... “Sorry about that,” the voice violently prised me away from my self-indulgent reverie. It was Bridges. He’d finally got dressed into a dapper business suit eerily similar to my own and now stood at the front of his apartment block, a stupid wide grin etched into face. “I was up late last night with my woman.” I remain silent and push myself up from the hood of the car. Bridges probably wants me to converse with him. He wants me to pry into his personal life and then pretend I’m interested in what he has to say. That isn’t going to happen. I reach for door to the drivers side of the car, but I’m unexpectedly beaten to it. I look up to see Bridges with the same stupid grin plastered across his face like a ten foot neon sign. “I’ll drive sport,” he said. I glare at him, not even attempting to hide my distain for him. “I think not,” I mutter at him. Bridges lowers his head as though he didn’t quite what I said and so I take matters into my own hands. I grab his wrist and clap down on it as hard as a vice. I notice his face screw up in agony no matter how hard he tries to mask it and in the end he concedes. Letting go of the door handle he allows a grimace and rubs his wrist, as if his other hand has magical healing qualities. “Quite a grip you have,” Bridges admits, making his way around to the other side of the car. I smile, sensing something sinister lurking somewhere underneath his words, but I shake the sensation off, attributing it to my overly active paranoia. No need worry about such possibilities until they transform into eventualities. I get into the car and in one smooth movement I start the engine. “Can you believe it?” Bridges declared once I’d pulled away from the curb. I make no movements and definitely do nothing to encourage his mindless rambling. “My girl accused me - ME - of cheating on her! I couldn’t believe it! As if I’d do such a thing.” Bridges pauses, perhaps expecting me to join in the banter. That still isn’t going to happen and after a minute or so in silence he realises it. “She told me she’d seen me in the park with another woman,” Bridges continued. Oblivious to the fact that I wasn’t showing any signs of interest. Likes the sound of his own voice this one. “I told her it wasn’t me. I wasn’t in the park that day, I was all the way across town. In the end she accepted it.” Bridges stops again and glares at me. I pretend I don’t notice and the look the other way, wishing the day had already ended long ago. Again he continues regardless. “Of course it was me, but what can I say? I’m not a one woman man. Never will be.” He pauses and holds his hands up defensively. “Don’t say it. I know. I’m a real bastard. But remember, everybody lies!” I think about shaking my head in disgust, but I can’t pull it off without him seeing it and if he did, he’d be sure to want to talk about it. As if I give two shits about his crappy life. Bridges shakes his head. “I was told you’d be like this. Didn’t quite believe them.” People have been talking about me. Not sure that’s a good thing. “Until now of course.” I grunt something under my breath, hoping that his warnings also included the part where he shuts the hell up. However, if they did tell him about this, he blatantly chose to ignore it. “So what’s this all about then?” He began. “We going to whack someone important?” I curse under my breath; I have to talk about work. Stakiri told me it would be necessary. Damn him. “It’s a simple shake down.” Bridges raises an eyebrow. “A shakedown? Why would I need you for a simple shakedown?” “What do you mean?” I ask, confused. “I know the score,” he tells me. “You’re the big gun Stakiri uses when things really go wrong. You don’t have to sugar coat it for me, I’ve been doing this a long time.” I shake my head. “It’s not like that.” “Then what’s it like?” He asks. I pause for a second. A number of plausible excuses automatically force themselves into my mind. I choose the one I perceive to have the greater chance of success. “It’s someone big, someone very familiar.” “One of the rivals?” Bridges asks, totally engulfed in the lie now. I nod in response and he develops a thoughtful expression, probably trying desperately to determine which of Stakiri’s major rivals it could be. I did nothing to deter his ponderings. Less attention on the real issue. Bridges isn’t stupid, he’ll figure it out eventually. Hence my true involvement in all of this. We’ve ventured out into the industrial sections of the city now, near the dockland area, full of warehouses both abandoned and still functioning, but equally squalid. I park up around the corner of a warehouse with a less squalid demeanour. It has a massive red sign, with high yellow lettering on it, going by the name of Offshore Trading. It’s just another one of Stakiri’s many subsidiary businesses, not a particularly good earner, but it provides a location for him conduct some of his less than legal business ventures. Bridges raises eyebrows again. “Stakiri persuaded this rival of his to meet him here?” I produce a toothy smile. “I don’t think Stakiri gave him the choice.” Bridges smiles at this and reinforces it with a lethargic nod. “Sounds ‘bout right.” We enter through the rear entrance to the building, which is already propped open for us with an empty beer crate. I make sure Bridges enters first, not wanting to turn my back on him for a second. Although I’d probably be able to deal with him with minimal effort, it wouldn’t pay to get careless. Bridges hadn’t become one of Stakiri’s top assassins for nothing and I’m pretty much certain he’d provide a few surprises for me if it ever came down to it. A few flights of stairs later, we come across two massive green fabric padded doors of the utmost opulence. I’m taken aback slightly by the sight considering the surroundings, but I quickly recover. I step forward to heave the massive doors open and then gesture for Bridges to enter. At first Bridges doesn’t see the guns. He just sees the many men stood behind them. I know from Stakiri’s bragging earlier in the day that most of them were his friends and colleagues, but I have no pity for the man. He’s done the same many times before to his friends and his colleagues. Even he knew it’d come back to get him eventually. To my surprise Bridges doesn’t try to run, instead he develops a reluctant smile and walks into the room, his head held high. He knows precisely what’s going on, but still he takes the time to confirm his suspicions with me. “A simply shakedown, eh?” He asks, the same reluctant smile plastered across his face. I can from his eyes that he’s put it firmly in place to hide the underlying fear; he knows what’s going to happen to him. I shrug. “Everybody lies.” He nods, smile wide now. “I guess so.” I turn my back on him and close the padded doors behind me as I leave the room. The gunfire shake the very foundations of the room, but I hear next to nothing thanks the sound and bullet-proof padding interlaced with the walls. I make my way out through the back exit, idly wondering when it will be my time to meet the firing line and what extravagant pile of bullshit I will be told to get me up here. I very much doubt I will face my death as gracefully as Bridges. That’s just not my style. END
