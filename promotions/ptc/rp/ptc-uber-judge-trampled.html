The Saga of the Uber Judge : Trampled

13 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It could be said that the Uber Judge was master of his domain. From the compulsive desire to clean, from the attention to detail that motivated this particular individual, his home reflected this: the neatness, the scrupulous desire for order. In every room, every stick of furniture within his abode was a calculated choice. Almost. A certain haphazard feel to the items remained, almost as if the Uber Judge chose each individual piece on his attachment to it rather than sinking into a synchronicity of theme or style that allowed for a uniform sense of comfort. Instead, each room of his abode spoke of his eccentric tastes, while the individual pieces of furniture appeared quite ordinary. In any case, the Uber Judge knew his home backwards and forwards, his muscle memory in cleaning routines knowing each particular nuance of content almost better than his actual memory did. He was on intimate terms with his home. It’s likely that the Uber Judge could hear the sound of the blades of grass on his lawn bending under a footstep. Any footstep. Well, almost. One person had lived there longer than him. His landlord was the exception that proved the rule. You might have thought that the Uber Judge lived alone. He’d like you to think so. His house actually was a duplex, with his landlord occupying the other half. Of course, the Uber Judge took care of the entire yard back to front, and actually tended to clean the common areas of his landlord’s space too. The Uber Judge’s landlord, in direct contrast to the obsessiveness of the Uber Judge, floated through life with a certain lacy grace and effortless panache. He was not a stranger to the house. In fact, the house seemed to recognize him as if it were alive, like a pet recognizes and welcomes his master. While the Uber Judge’s effort to arrange his home was a strenuous struggle to maintain order and tidiness, his landlord had a way of compelling things to fall into place just by the very merit of his presence. While he was unsure, the Uber Judge could swear that when his landlord left a room, the house would courteously turn the light off for him. When the landlord entered, the floorboards, instead of creaking under his weight, would rise to meet his steps, allowing for perfect silent passage. In a house where the Uber Judge could discern where any person was through the sounds the house made in response to their steps, his landlord was the perfect interloper. The Uber Judge imposed his presence on his home; his landlord didn’t have to. So it came to pass, that one morning, while the Uber Judge sat on his couch and regarded his mail (Thankfully the mail was not nearly as interesting as the day the letter from the PTC came, oh how long ago that was! Oh how much had changed!), minding his own business, (quite accurately) he was caught completely by surprise when his landlord spoke. The landlord said, “You need a sidekick.” If the Uber Judge were wearing shoes he would’ve jumped out of them (the Uber Judge was fond of the sensation of bare feet on hardwood floors). He turned to regard his landlord and said, “Ahh! You have to stop doing that to me, and no, I do not need a sidekick.” However, the landlord had already made his way to the kitchen, where he had opened the refrigerator and was evaluating his choices. “Nah, the Uber Judge gimmick has hit big. You need a sidekick now,” said the landlord as he decided on Simply Orange, opening it and drinking right from the bottle. (Poor Uber Judge! He took your favorite beverage!) “I do not!” asserted the Uber Judge, who was growing quite irritated at his landlord’s arrival, antics, choice of beverage, and line of conversation. The landlord tipped the bottle back, belched, and then asked, “Who was the last GTT winner?” “Doctor Curiosity,” said the Uber Judge right away. He’d been part of that tournament in his old administrative capacity. “And Doctor Curiosity had Eegor. What’ve you got? A gavel?” “Outrage!” said the Uber Judge, “Doctor Curiosity’s victory was because of that wily man’s ingenuity and intelligence, not because of his sidekick. He does not establish a precedent of sidekicks being necessary for success in the PTC, regardless of how one might wish to draw such a conclusion. And further, this is the Infinite Gauntlet! GTT5 hasn’t even started yet!” “But,” said the landlord, “this round is also a special qualifying round for GTT5. So it does connect to it.” “In the loosest sense!” retorted the Uber Judge, “It’s a qualifying round before the tournament even begins!” “Ah!” replied the landlord, “but it looks to me like we’re looking at a few changes for this year’s GTT. Earning a place in GTT5 might be a much more difficult feat to accomplish.” The Uber Judge crossed his arms in stubbornness, “I do not need a sidekick.” The landlord returned to the living room where the Uber Judge pouted and said, “Well, listen to this. I came up with this last night.” “I'm listening,” said the Uber Judge, though his tone suggested he was listening under protest. “The Under Miner!” exclaimed the landlord, moving his hand across his field of vision as if he were seeing the name in lights, or across a marquee. “The Under Miner?” exclaimed the Uber Judge in absolute disbelief. “Yes! The Under Miner! I could get some of those tinted goggles, one of those hard hats with the light on it, a pair of overalls, work boots, and a pick axe. What do you think?” “The only thing you are Under Mining is me!” said the Uber Judge with derision (but also a small amount of satisfaction for thinking up that turn of phrase.) “Well, of course,” said the Under Miner, “that’d be part of the gimmick. You’ve got to admit that the Uber Judge is pretty silly.” “I am not silly!” shouted the Uber Judge, who hopped to his feet and stamped his foot on the floor in emphasis. The Under Miner cocked his head to the side while observing this behavior and said, “You could’ve fooled me, man.” The Uber Judge steamed. The Under Miner smirked (but in a sympathetic friendly manor) and said, “Look, you’re fun and funny as the Uber Judge. It’s good. But to make it work you need someone to play off of. It’s like a comedy dou, you’re the perfect straight man, and I’ll be the funny part.” “No you will not!” “Well, okay, you can be the funny guy and I’ll be the straight man. However you want it. But you need, me, Uber Judge, whether you like it or not.” “No! Get out of my house!” As the Under Miner was pushed towards the door, he said, “Actually this is my house, you’re just renting it, but I’ll give you some time to think it over anyway. Think of it, the Uber Judge and his sidekick, the Under Miner, it’s great stuff. You should really think it-” And the self proclaimed Under Miner was cut off as the door was slammed. The house of the Uber Judge was quiet again. He resumed his seat on the couch and prepared to finish with the mail, but not before a distinctive crow’s voice shouted, “Caw!” from the top his prized elm tree in his back yard. The Uber Judge’s face squished up in distaste and he shouted, “Who asked you for your opinion anyway!?” = Late in that evening, the Uber Judge’s Ford Mustang GT pulled into the driveway. Stepping from the vehicle, the Uber Judge displayed a gruesome countenance: one of his eyes was swollen shut, his nose had bled, and he had a fat lip. What had happened? Well, in a nutshell, the Uber Judge had arrived at his first XUW show, the PTC federation he’d signed with, and was dismayed to discover that he wasn’t allowed in the building in spite of the fact that he was supposed to be there. While the actual events that took place would be displayed on XUW’s show (to the Uber Judge’s absolute horror and embarrassment), it’s worth mentioning that the Uber Judge found himself on the wrong side of a near legion of security ninjas who beat the living snot out of him. The Uber Judge went inside his home and went right for his refrigerator, where he removed a bag of frozen vegetables, and applied them to his sore head. With his comfort in place, he flopped onto his couch with all the dismay of someone who’d just come home after being beaten up very badly. “My big debut in an honest to goodness PTC federation: ruined,” said the Uber Judge feeling more than a little sorry for himself. “Tah dah!” shouted the unexpected voice, prompting the Uber Judge, since this time he was actually wearing shoes, to jump right out of them, dropping his frozen vegetables on the coffee table. It was his landlord, decked out as he’d described: hard hat with light, goggles, overalls, workboots, and a tool belt in addition. “I am the Under Miner!” shouted his landlord, who after observing the Uber Judge dropped character and asked, “Whoa, what happened to you?” “I got clobbered by a hundred well trained security guards.” “Ouch. Well, I'm sorry your first show didn’t go so well.” “Ha!” said the Uber Judge (though it was a very sardonic `ha!’ with not even a hint of actual amusement), “if I were to describe the experience as one that did not go so well, I’d be making a very serious error on the side of understatement.” “Well,” said the Under Miner (who was honestly very sorry that it went badly), “I'm sorry just the same.” “Excuse me,” said the Uber Judge (who realized that he was being a little harsh considering the Under Miner was being sympathetic), “But I'm a little upset.” “It’s okay,” said the Under Miner (since he was the type of person who didn’t let things bother him too much), “I suppose this isn’t a good time to try out my Under Miner shtick.” With the sigh, the Uber Judge caved in to obligation, “You might as well, I’d planned to spend most of tonight at the show rather than here.” “Okay!” said the Under Miner, “get ready!” The Under Miner proceeded to walk around the couch to the other side of the coffee table in front of the Uber Judge’s seldom used television. He focused for a moment, and then looked up. “So, Uber Judge, I’ve been wondering something.” The Under Miner’s voice was the same as it normally was which confused the Uber Judge who thought that the Under Miner had not begun the act when in fact he had. “Yes?” replied the Uber Judge. “Well, I didn’t want to say anything really, but some people have been asking me about your gavel.” “Oh, my ridiculously oversized gavel?” said the Uber Judge, who produced the monstrosity of the object that could be said to be a little larger than artists’ renditions of the Hammer of Thor (which is pretty big). “Yeah, they’ve been curious about it,” said the Under Miner. “In what regard?” “Well, it’s pretty big. The peen on that is a little unwieldy, and the shaft of it…” “Yes?” said the Uber Judge with some suspicion. “They’ve been wondering if you’ve perhaps been compensating for something, you know, carrying that big hammer around.” The Uber Judge blinked, his mouth opened and closed but no sound came out. “And there’s also the Mustang you drive,” said the Under Miner, “You’re approaching middle age, and we’re thinking that it might be a midlife crisis kind of thing.” “What?!?” exclaimed the exasperated Uber Judge. “And there’s this whole gimmick too, the Uber Judge. Now, you and I know that you’ve been a wrestler for a while…” “Stop stop! Do not speak of such things!” “… And I got to thinking: the car, the gavel, the new gimmick with the secret identity, your age, the only thing missing is that you haven’t picked up some eighteen year old …” In a moment of honest candor among the sarcasm, the Uber Judge said, “I’ll say that’s missing.” “… and it looks like you’re trying to reclaim something that you might think you’ve missed. You know, with the funny gimmick, when you used to do only straight up serious stuff.” The Uber Judge sighed, “Okay. You’re a sidekick. You’re the Uber Judge’s sidekick. Now, just stop, please.” “Really? Already? But I had so much more material there.” “No,” said the Uber Judge, “you win. You always win.” “Hey man,” said the Under Miner, “you know I'm your buddy, not just your landlord. You’re a good guy. You’re the best tenant I ever had. You think I let anybody live in the same building as me? Come on! Look at our lawn!” “My lawn!” protested the Uber Judge from between the fingers that covered his face. “Okay, okay, it’s your lawn, you worked so hard on it after all. Anyway, what I'm saying is that I don’t want to just be your sidekick for the fame and all that. I just honestly think you need someone out there to help you. And you know, I’d like to do that.” With some irritation, the Uber Judge said, “I already agreed to have you as a sidekick, you can stop trying to persuade me.” The Under Miner smirked, “I know a couple people who have a hard time actually asking for help. I worry about you sometimes, man. Anyway, I'm going to head home. You take care.” As the Under Miner went to the door, he stopped to look back, “Seriously, if you need anything, ask.” == In his bathroom, the Uber Judge gingerly removed the wads of tissue he’d stuffed up his nostrils. His shiner would go down in a few days, and his lip would be okay. In truth, what did he have to worry about? His identity was hidden under a mask when he competed, so it made little difference the state of his face. “What purpose do I serve?” asked the Uber Judge of his reflection. He looked himself in the eye for a long time before saying, “Well, I don’t know the answer anymore, but I'm working on it. I'm finding a purpose. I'm doing what I can, with what I have available. I'm working for XUW, even if apparently not everybody there knows that yet, but I am. With a few matches under my belt, I’ll even get to represent them officially in the Infinite Gauntlet. If I do well in this round, I’ll be able to get into GTT5, and then I’ll have a chance to really earn some …” And the Uber Judge stopped. Some what? What would he be earning? Some fame? Some respect? Were those the things he really wanted? He searched himself, but before he could find an answer he was interrupted. Somebody was walking across his yard (I told you he could hear the blades of grass bending!), and it sounded like they approached the slightly smaller elm tree in his front yard. (It was smaller than the one in the back yard anyway, though both were planted at around the same time. The Uber Judge suspected that car exhaust from the street stunted it a little.) The Uber Judge stealthily crept down to his front door, and looked outside, seeing the small orange glow of a cigarette. He breathed a sigh of relief and went out. “Hey Steven,” said the Uber Judge, “how’s it going?” Steven jumped. He lived across the street from the Uber Judge, the teenaged son of the family there, who had just entered into that rebellious period, and as a result had taken up smoking. Steve said, “Oh hey, I'm just sneaking a smoke out here. My parents are looking suspicious, so I thought I’d hide by your tree.” “It’s okay, Steven, just throw the butt into the street when you’re done.” “Cool, thanks Mr. _____, how are you doing? Whoa, is that a black eye?” “Yep,” said the Uber Judge. “The wrestling business has its share of hard knocks, eh?” asked Steve with a toothy smile. “Oh yeah it does,” said the Uber Judge who couldn’t agree more. “Heh,” said Steve who took an inexperienced drag of his smoke, “Is that really you in the PTC, Mr. ____? The Uber Judge?” The Uber Judge nodded, “Yep. It is, Steven.” “Coooooool,” said Steve, “Does anybody else know? I mean, apart from me?” “Oh, a number of people know I figure, but only a couple know for sure, and most people don’t really care.” “Oh, I think that’s bullshit, Mr ____, excuse my language and all, but I think it’s really cool. I’d tell some of my friends but-” “They probably wouldn’t believe you, right?” “Yeah, that’s what I mean. The Uber Judge is really funny and shit, but you’re-” “Not a funny guy?” “Well, I wouldn’t say that. Well, actually, I guess I would. I didn’t ever think you could do the funny stuff as well.” “Well,” said the Uber Judge candidly, “it’s a lot of fun.” “Heh,” said Steve again, “so that’s what it is. Anyway, that was a good match with Rollins. I thought he almost had you for a minute there.” “Oh he did,” said the Uber Judge, “he very well did have me, but I got a few lucky shots and that was that.” “Cooooool,” said Steve, “Well, I hope you do well, Mr. _____. It’d be awesome if you had a really long streak for the Infinite Gauntlet. It’s just cool to think that my neighbor would set a record like that, you know?” And at that, the Uber Judge was touched. So he decided to present something to Steven in return. “Steven,” said the Uber Judge, “do you do your own laundry?” “No, my mom does, why?” “Oh, well, Steven, I think your parents, or at least your mom, already know you smoke.” “What?” said Steve, his eyes opening wide in horror at the mere possibility. “Yeah, because your clothes reek of it. So I’d suggest that you do your own laundry if you want to keep hiding it. Maybe they’ll think you’ve quit. Or… just tell them or something.” “I dunno, Mr. ____,” said Steve, already looking quite uncomfortable with the direction of the conversation. “Well,” said the Uber Judge, “it’s up to you really. I just wanted you to know.” “Cool, thanks Mr. ____, I gotta go,” and with that Steven hurriedly ran across the street, tossing his cigarette butt to the side as he went. After he left, the Uber Judge looked over his lawn. It was quite dark out, but the sky was clear so stars and moon provided at least some illumination so that he could see Steven’s footprints on his lawn. “You’ve been trampled,” said the Uber Judge to the grass, “but you’re not crushed. You can get back up, stand up straight.” After a moment of breathing the night air, listening to the sounds of the occasional cricket, looking up at the evening sky, and thinking about things, the Uber Judge said, “We’ll do it together.”
