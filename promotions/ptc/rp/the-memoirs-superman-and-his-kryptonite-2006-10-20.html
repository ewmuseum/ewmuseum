The Memoirs: Superman and His Kryptonite

20 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-20 The year is 2056 and our scene opens on the image of a car pulling into a house overlooking the Italian coast of the Ligurian Sea, along the outskirts of the city La Spezia. This region has grown by five-fold in the seventeen years since the end of the third World War (2032-2039) when the western powers of Europe and the United States took on and defeated the Middle East region, the majority of the time in conflict with an ever more dangerous Iranian power. The end of the war also ended an oil crisis that had hit a slope upward in 2015, though the beginning was often noted by historians as having begun early in 2003 when prices began rocketing upwards. Now the west is patrolled by a United European Union and the United States of North America; an area made up of the former USA, Canada, Mexico and parts of the former Central America. The east is controlled, if one can call it control, by the Chinese Empire as well as India despite continuing terrorist regimes throughout that sphere. The home in view is known locally as the Casa di De Luca and has been in the hands of the De Luca family for many generations dating back to the mid-1950’s when it was built by the family’s patriarch Antonio De Luca after he moved away from Savona and went further east along the coast before arriving near La Spezia. The car approaching is a silver color and built similarly to an early 21st century Porsche though much less expensive since the new wave of lighter, stronger and more economically friendly solar-cars came out to the general public nearly fifteen years ago. The car stops in front of the home’s stately entrance, the door opening as Cameron Stevens exits from the driver’s side. Cameron is twenty-five and among the last generation born pre-WWIII. He was born in North America just outside of Dallas, Texas where his father before him was born and raised. Cameron himself is in Italy to visit family; one member in particular with whom he has important news to share. As he comes up the steps towards the front door he recalls the last time he had been here, for a family Christmas in 2054. The Stevens’ family rotated Christmas between Dallas and the Ligurian region every other year so that those on one side of the Atlantic would not have to constantly cross what had previously been a very war-torn region and eventually the practice became more routine than necessary. Cameron adjusts the black button-down shirt and black slacks before clearing his throat and calmly rapping one fist against the solid oak door, the sound reverberating as he glances around and once more inhales the clean Italian air that blew in off the coast just a few miles east of where he stood. A moment passes before the sound of a lock being unhinged is heard and the door opens to show a woman that was obviously a stunning beauty even a decade previous but age had turned her beauty into a more grandmotherly attractiveness as she had passed her seventieth birthday just a year earlier. She is dressed elegantly and gazes out at Cameron, a moment passing before recognition sets in and a smile crosses her red lips. "Cameron!" She exclaims, the thick Italian accent so pleasant to the young man as he steps into her embrace, kissing her cheek gently as his six-three frame towers over hers of five-two. "Hi Grandma Nat, I’ve missed you." He says as their embrace parts and Natalia Stevens looks him over with a smirk, backing against the door to allow him entrance as she speaks. "You have missed me? I have missed you dear, my goodness you are getting so big." She says, shaking her head in amusement as she shuts the door behind him. "Please…I’ve been the same height for six years." "Then maybe this old body is just shrinking." Natalia says with a smirk, "I am so glad you made the trip, your grandfather and I spend far too much time in this old house. I think he has forgotten that we have a yard." "Is he here?" Cameron asks, glancing around with interest as his grandmother nods. "He is. How about I go tell him you are here and then I can fix us all a cup of lemon tea?" "That sounds wonderful Grandma, your tea alone is worth the trip." Cameron says as Natalia leads him into the large family den. "You charm like your grandfather dear. That could get you into trouble." Natalia says with a laugh before turning away, "Make yourself at home dear, I will tell him you are here." Cameron nods and steps slowly into the den, looking around with heavy interest at the furniture and the walls and everything that made this old home so breathtaking. Along the far wall is the family fireplace and above the mantle is a very large portrait taken in April of 2009 at the wedding of his grandparents. In the picture were all the friends and family of the Stevens and the De Luca’s. The children were all seated along the floor while the adults stood in a semi-circle around the bride and groom. Natalia’s dress was a gorgeous white lace and she looked literally heart stopping in it. Cameron’s grandfather Samuel Stevens wore a simple but powerful black suit and tie. The best part about the portrait was that the groom’s eyes were locked on his bride. Apparently the photographer had taken a dozen shots and each time Samuel’s eyes ended up locked on the beauty of his new bride, finally it was decided to simply pick the best picture of the ones taken as it seemed he would never be able to look at the camera lens long enough to take the traditional photo. Cameron continues to stand in front of it, his eyes moving from the bride and groom to the others in the photo. His great grandparents on both sides stood immediately behind the couple. His ‘Uncle’ Anthony was the best man and, while never really Cameron’s uncle by relation, Anthony D’Ambriosi had been his grandfather’s tag team partner off and on for over a decade. The Hughes’ were also in the circle along the groom’s side of it. Jason Hughes had been a world-renowned wrestler and had trained Cameron’s grandfather. Jason had won seven world championships across a twelve-year career as well as Hall-of-Fame accolades. Sadly the living legacy of Jason Hughes had ended three years earlier when he passed away in his sleep. Beside Jason stood his wife Veronica and even Cameron had to force his eyes off of her beauty. In 2009 Natalia had been a ‘ten’ but somehow Veronica surpassed even that, her crimson hair and deep eyes completely unique to a portrait of otherwise all browns, blacks and grays. Following her husband’s death she had moved to New York City and lived about a ten-minute walk from her daughter’s family. In front of the Hughes’ were their two children; then-four year old Cassian and two year old Gabrielle. Cameron smirked at the cyclical ways of history as much of his reason for making the trip involved the man that grew out of that four-year old child. "God I’ve gotten old." The broken silence causes Cameron to spin around, seeing his grandfather a few feet in front of him. The man is dressed in a gray suit, looking as though he just stepped off a magazine cover despite the rarity in which he left the home. A smile comes to Cameron’s face as his grandfather, Samuel Stevens extends his hand. "How you doing Cameron?" He says simply, his voice and the way he carried himself being very distinguished as the two shook hands. "I’ve been ok grandfather, but I…" He begins. "Oh please, I hate that ‘grandfather’ stuff. Told ya before, Sam is fine. We’re both men here, no need for stupid titles that just make me feel old. My bones make me feel old enough as it is." Samuel says, almost snapping as he points to two chairs separated by a small round table near the fireplace. "Take a seat and talk, we don’t get near enough visitors to this old house." "Alright gra…Sam." He begins, taking a seat as Natalia reenters the room with a tray of tea in hand. "Here you are boys; nice lemon tea and a slice of lemon cake." She says, smiling as she sets the drinks down and hands both men a slice of cake before turning and exiting to allow the two some time to talk. "So tell me Cameron, what brings you here?" Samuel asks slowly, sipping his drink as Cameron leans back and decides to simply come out with it. "I’ve decided to become a wrestler." The words come from Cameron’s lips with a surprising ease, himself expecting this to be a much more difficult confession to make. He waits quietly as Samuel watches him for a moment, continuing to slowly sip his tea before setting it aside and simply muttering his response. "Why would you go and do something as stupid as that?" "Well, it only took two minutes of talking for me to regret coming here. Sam, I knew you’d be against it but don’t call it stupid, after all this was your career for what? Fifteen years?" "Exactly, and that’s how I can tell you that this is a stupid idea that is going to end up killing you. Who did you convince to…" Samuel begins, pausing as a smirk crosses his lips and he just nods before continuing. "…right. And how is Cassian Hughes? I haven’t spoken to him since, wow…Jason’s funeral actually." "He’s doing fine Sam, and I didn’t have to convince him about anything. He’s built his promotion into one of the best in the country, which should have been damn near impossible considering that wrestling as a whole was all but extinct a decade ago. It’s coming back and that interest, well it’s literally in my blood." "Some would say that stupidity is also in your blood." Samuel says sardonically. "Isn’t that a bit cynical? You haven’t even seen if I have talent yet and already you’re…" Cameron begins, reaching for his tea as Samuel interrupts him. "Now, I don’t need to see you to know you’ve got the talent. You’re a Stevens, you could wrestle a broom to a four-star match. I’m not talking about the wrestling. It’s everything else that is going to end up killing you." "It’s amazing how easy you can say that. I’ve been life-long friends with so many wrestlers; you, Uncle Tony, Cross, the Hughes’ family, Reno…not one was killed doing this sport." Cameron responds, stopping to slowly drink his tea as the two men sit in silence for a few moments. After a minute Samuel calmly sets his plate and cup aside and leans forward, his hands cupped with the left inside the right as he rests his chin against his fist and speaks. "Cam, I’m not saying you couldn’t be great but you have such a bright mind. There are so many better things that you can do than beat your body up every night. Maverick, Jason and I all had children while we were in wrestling and not one of us were able to watch them grow up the way we would have liked. Hell, I couldn’t do something as simple as tuck your father in 280-plus days out of the year. This is on the off-chance you can have a family. Junior and Jacob never had the chance for family; Maverick and Jason lucked out by having met their wives in the promotions they worked. Your grandmother, well it took me nearly hitting rock bottom for the two of us to connect." Samuel pauses, his eyes having not moved from his grandson’s face during this. "I’m not saying you wouldn’t be great, but…there are things you’ll miss out on. And the pain you’ll be in every night…it’s not always worth the few cheering fans who will remember you." "Sam, I know what you’re saying and I appreciate it but…I want to try and I can handle the pain. I’ve been thrown around a lot in my li…" "Don’t be a damn fool." His grandfather says snappishly, his hands un-clamping as he points to the wedding photo. "That’s one great picture Cameron, but I honestly was damn lucky to be alive to see my wedding day. I was two years out of rehab when that picture was taken and the urge is still…" "Rehab?" Cameron interrupts, confused at having never heard about this in all the stories his grandfather used to tell him about his days as a wrestler. "Yeah, it was early in my career and without Natalia, Jason and my other friends I don’t think I’d be around today." Cameron’s face registers surprise as he sits back, "I…you never told me you were an alcoholic." "…that’s because I wasn’t." Samuel says with a smirk, taking a sip of his tea and continuing. "Addiction can hit a man in so many ways Cameron. Alcohol for some, gambling for others…for me it was pain pills. Hydr…Hydrocodone." He says, choking up at the word even after all these decades. "I was a rookie at the time. Hell, I hadn’t won a title yet and had barely seen a nationally televised match. Here I was, twenty-three maybe twenty-four and I was the student of Jason Hughes. This was months after his retirement so he was still widely-hailed as the best technician ever and I was coming onto the scene and I just expected doors to every major promotion to open for me. "Instead, I was struggling in the indies and every night I’d be getting suplexed on gym floors and against rings with no give. I remember one night I went up top and the damn fifty-cent screw snapped, the buckle broke and I fell back and cracked my head against the guardrail. I fought stiff motherfuckers who didn’t know how to work, they’d come in and think it was a damn boxing match. I’d get back to the locker room after the show and my face would be black, blue and orange…at the same time. "Beyond that I was so damn anxious to get noticed that I’d take matches with anybody. I remember weeks in 2006 where I’d be in Chicago and St. Paul on Monday, Washington on Wednesday, Phoenix by Thursday and then a stint in four towns in Texas on the weekend. That schedule and the risks I would take in the ring early on…I’d come home each night with pain and pills were the answer. I remember having fourteen doctors across the country prescribing me small doses of pills…they all thought that they were the only one I was getting a prescription from. There was a match in September of ’06 in Dallas against this kid Arik something-or-other. Boy busted me up real bad. I won but I sure as shit didn’t look like the winner. Last time I worked that promotion but I went home and took twelve pills in three hours. Looking back it’s a fucking miracle I woke up the next morning." Samuel pauses to take another sip of his tea, Cameron shaking his head in disbelief. "Sam, how did it start?" Cameron asks. "Long story, but I suppose the easiest answer is just one pill at a time. The thing with pain pills is that they’d get rid of the pain so you’d take another and so on. Eventually the pain you feel is no longer from a leg or a back problem. It’s your brain in pain from not getting the pills. That’s all it takes for the addiction to hit. You associate the pill with feeling good and eventually you’re taking the pill to feel better when you’re already fine." Samuel says casually, setting his tea down. "How did you get better?" "I basically hit rock bottom in my personal life at the same moment I was at the height of my career to that point." Samuel says, leaning back in the recliner as he looks at his grandson, his seventy-plus years taking a toll on his body as exhaustion hits him. "You ever hear of the GTT?" October 14, 2006 The Dallas, Texas apartment of Samuel Tobias Stevens [The scene opened to the apartment of Samuel Stevens, circa 2006 along the outskirts of Dallas, Texas. The apartment was fairly well-furnished and our narrator is seated in the center of a four-seat sofa made of black leather. His eyes and facial features are much the same as they would be fifty years down the road but he was young and with the chiseled body of a wrestler in the prime of his career. Dressed in a pair of jeans, he was seated in front of a small laptop computer, set atop a small wood and glass coffee table. He punched the keys with a slow deliberate stroke of his right hand, his left holding a glass of scotch while set aside from the computer is a single pill of pain medication, the half-full bottle that it came from now set at the edge of the table.] The GTT was an annual tournament held by Primetime Central, an oversight committee for about eight of the most premier wrestling promotions on the planet. The best known ones were PRIME, Atlantic Wrestling Club, Global Wrestling, the WWA and FUSE. Every year they would invite the best wrestlers from around the planet to meet in a single elimination tournament to crown the year’s GTT Champion. The actual amount always changed, going from 90 all the way to a 256-person tournament, but one thing always stayed the same. Every year somebody in the business was made a star by winning or doing well in this event. People like Angelo Deville, Seymour Almasy, Rich Rollins, Dr. Curiosity, Kimbusa, Archibald McGregor and Karina Wolfenden. These people are remembered today to the extent that men like Hulk Hogan were revered and cheered for in 2006. The GTT of 2006 was a six-bracket event featuring 92 of the world’s best wrestlers and I…I of all people, was drafted among the first fifty. This was amazing, I was a two-year old rookie in the business and my best moment to date had been a battle royale I took part in for CSWA. I was no Rollins, I was no Deville, I was certainly no Almasy…but just the same, I was in an event they had all helped to make famous. I was scared out of my mind. [The silence of the room faded as Stevens’ voice was heard, audibly reading the email he had in front of him.] "‘Samuel T. Stevens, congratulations on being the 48th pick in the GTT6 draft of stars. Your scheduled opponent for the first round match on Friday October 20, 2006 is Saij of the PTC member promotion, XUW. Good luck and thank you for participating. Sincerely, the governing board of Primetime Central.’" Stevens does not say a word for a moment, cradling his glass in one hand as he reaches over and slowly picks up the pain pill from the table. "Perfect." [Stevens just shook his head, slipping the pill into his mouth and following it with a swig of scotch as he shut the computer and leaned back against the black and white striped pillow that adorned the couch.] I should have declined the invite, I know that now, but back then I was so damn ready to be a star that little things like bodily health and mental stability could be set on the back burner. I’d been going full speed for so long that I was so close to hitting a wall mentally. Every match just seemed to take a little more out of me and now I was in this tournament against a guy as accomplished as Saij. He and Jason Hughes had been the two biggest stars in wrestling from Michigan in recent memory; Saij had made a Hall-of-Fame career in XUW and had won a couple previous tournaments so he knew what to expect from the arduous journey we were set to begin. I had no idea but I knew it was a ‘one loss and you’re out’ style tournament. I wasn’t ready to be forgotten this quickly. The pain though, the pain in my back and legs was becoming unbearable. That had to be forgotten if I was going to succeed, and the only cure I knew of was in the form of a tiny yellow pill. [Stevens’ short moment of rest was interrupted as a slow rapping was heard from his front door. He groaned and after a moment the knocking continued, rousing Stevens from his position. He set the glass of scotch down and slowly pushed himself to a standing position, his hand against his back as he again felt the jarring pain from a suplex taken across concrete from a few nights previous. He crossed the room and opened his front door to see the smiling face of his neighbor, Natalia De Luca.] "Samuel," She began, a smile coming to Stevens’ face as it did every time she called him that, "You are late." "You know…I’ve told you that ‘Sam’ is fine." He began, the two of them having this same discourse every week. "And I like ‘Samuel’." She says with a smirk, her accent seeming to coat every word she spoke in some sort of audible sexuality. "You are still late." "For?" Stevens began, his mind already in a fine haze. Natalia’s face turned sad and she glanced down for a moment before looking at ‘Samuel’ again. "I…I thought you said tonight would be…" She barely got the first part of her sentence stated before Stevens’ mind wretched the memory from the recesses of his pill-induced fog. "Dinner!" He said with a start, quickly opening the door so that Natalia could enter. "I am so sorry Natalia, my mind’s just not been all there today." "All where?" Natalia asked quizzically, her grasp of uncommon American statements still a bit slow. She stepped into the living room, giving Stevens a full view of her attire. She was dressed in a pair of black slacks that hugged her hips tightly and a gray sweater that came off the shoulders. Her hair was done up and she had the barest hint of perfume on. "Nevermind," Stevens began, "You…you look great." No response came from Natalia, though the slow blush in her cheeks showed that she had heard the words he spoke and that they mattered to her. "Anyway, make yourself comfortable. I just need five minutes and we can head out, ok?" He said, backing towards the bedroom until he saw her nod. He made a mad dash into his room immediately after seeing that, begging to any and every god he had ever been told about to allow there to be a single clean shirt somewhere in his closet. As he disappeared the scene refocused on Natalia, seated now where Stevens had been earlier. She glanced at the bedroom and noted that he was still behind closed doors. She slowly opened the laptop, seeing the email he had read moments ago. After a moment the door to the bedroom reopened and Stevens stepped out, now wearing a pair of dark khakis and a black, button-down shirt with a water bottle in hand. He crossed the room to where Natalia sat, glancing at the laptop before speaking. "You always snoop in other people’s things?" "I…" Natalia began, quickly shutting the laptop and standing. "I am sorry…I was just…" She paused when she noticed the smirk of Samuel’s face. "It’s ok Nat, I don’t mind…besides…I like you." Samuel said casually, reaching down and picking up the bottle of pain pills. He calmly took one before setting the rest down. Natalia watched him for a moment as he took the pill and followed it with a drink of water. "Are you in pain today?" She asked, having seen him take pills before. "A little, but don’t worry…it’s the first one I’ve had all week. I have a match next week and my back’s been a little sore. That’s all. You ready?" He says, the speech being the same one he told her the week before. Natalia does not say anything at first, finally smiling and nodding. Samuel smiled back and led her out of his home, shutting the light off as they exit to the hallway. Before he shut the door Natalia turned and touched her hand to his shoulder, leaning up and brushing his lips with hers before speaking. "I like you too Samuel but promise that if your back does not feel better you will slow down." He looked at her for a moment before nodding, flashing a smile as he shut the door with his last comment. "Don’t worry about me Natalia, I’m Superman." May 19, 2056 Casa di De Luca, La Spezia, Italy "You lied to Grandma?" Cameron asks, his eyes and mind focused on the first part of the story his grandfather had just told him. "I did, I’m not proud of it but I did. Even with all the times I told myself I was fine, I always lied about the pills…that should have been my first clue about my own addiction. You want to hear the rest of the story or judge me about one lie?" Samuel says with a slow annoying tone to his voice. "No…no, keep going but I do have one question." "What’s that Cameron?" "…did you win?" Samuel reaches over and calmly picks up the glass of lemon tea, taking one final sip before setting it down and speaking. "Actually I…" The scene quickly fades to black and then… Static. To Be Continued…Or Not
