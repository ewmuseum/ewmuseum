GTT5 — Roleplay archive

Esclydepades

16 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-16 It’s been only a few measly days since the GTT5 tournament officially began, dashing the hopes of sixty-four wrestlers in a single night, and already Clyde’s receiving fan mail. Turns out, no one figured he would win. I mean, come on…Clyde Walkins versus LeStatt Knight. That’s like sending a twelve-year-old boy into Neverland Ranch. Things just aren’t gonna be the same after something like that. But somehow, inexplicably, Clyde pulled out the win. He slew the vampire with his own two hands, and thusly moved on to the second round to face Drake Coleman. Now he’s seen as the favorite. A week ago, he’s just some unknown goof that managed to pick up a few fluke wins in PRIME. Today, he’s some known goof that managed to pick up another fluke win in PRIME, as well as a victory over GCW’s greatest champion of all time, and could be looking at another one against Coleman. Need I continue to tell you why he’s getting fan letters? And as for Clyde, well…he’s in a celebratory mood, let’s just say. Walkins: BUSTIN’ MAKES ME FEEL GOOD! The Ghostbusters theme playing obnoxiously loud, Clyde walks around the room much like you’d imagine a sumo wrestler to if he only ever used that funny little stepping movement they do at the start of matches, ‘dancing’ to the beat of the music. Trying to sing along with the song and failing miserably, he squats in place and thrusts forward in perfect timing to the rhythm, somehow getting that part of it right. Well, not so much ‘right’ as…you know, I really don’t know what to call it. But thankfully, James Farwell puts a stop to it before it can go any further. Without Clyde noticing him, and still dancing obliviously along as the music loops, James hits the eject button on the CD player, sidestepping the disc as it’s propelled from the stereo and straight into the wall, exploding into a million tiny pieces. Clyde, unknowing of what just happened, spins around to face James, a look of fear on his face. Walkins: I ain’t ‘fraid of no ghost! Farwell, left knee covered in bandages, looks down almost as if he’s been hurt. Meanwhile, Walkins recovers from the near ghost attack, trying not to show he was scared out of his wits at the prospect of a poltergeist seeking vengeance on Ray Parker, Jr. Farwell: That sentence is so incredibly grammatically incorrect. Oh, what has television taught our children?! Walkins: That it’s cool to be a Ghostbuster, just so long as you aren’t black or crippled? Cut to the entire Ghostbusters squad sitting around at their headquarters. The leader looks at the silent phone, sighs, and then turns to his compatriots. Leader: Listen, I just got a call and…we’re gonna need you to head out to that old haunted white supremacist museum. As he’s saying this, he looks right at the black guy. And no, I don’t know the names of the ones from that gay Ghostbusters Xtreme cartoon, but it’s what I’m parodying. So keep up. Leader: Apparently, there’s a lot of spooks there, so just go around prodding anything that looks suspiciously white. Like a sheet. The black guy looks at his friends for a moment, then drops his head in quiet defeat, before leaving. The leader then turns to the cripple. Leader: And you. I need you to get me something from the second floor. The guy in the wheelchair, unaware of any upcoming wrongdoing, obliges and happily wheels his way over to the elevator, even going so far as to crack a smile at finally being of use. Leader: Uh-uh-uh! Use the stairs. And now back to The Forsaken. Farwell: Why the hell are you dancing around in your underwear, anyway? I thought we talked about this. Walkins: But it’s a special occasion! James rolls his eyes, muttering under his breath. Farwell: You’re a special occasion… Rather than put on a pair of pants, Clyde moseys on over to the computer desk and pulls a stack of letters off the top. Walkins: All of these, mine! From people all around the world! Amazed, James wanders over to Clyde in a state of disbelief, looking over each of the letters as Clyde smiles giddily. Farwell: Wow, Clyde. There has to be at least thirty or forty letters here. Walkins: Twenty-seven! I counted. Clyde grins from ear to ear, and James just gives a half-assed smile, his eye catching one of the envelopes in particular. Farwell: Clyde…did someone send you…are these panties?! Walkins: Yeah! Isn’t it cool? Someone named P. Vetra sent them to me, along with this letter filled with a bunch of naughty words. Like balls. His eyes nearly bugging out of his head, James grabs the letter and panty-filled envelope out of Clyde’s hands, hurling them into a nearby wastebasket before lighting a match and tossing it in shortly after. Farwell: Burn in hell, spawn of evil… Turning back to Clyde as the fire in the trashcan burns down, James puts a hand on his shoulder, but pulls it away when a nearly-nude Clyde smiles and winks at him. Farwell: Uh…well, Clyde…I think that, in the future, you should let me look at all the fan mail you get before you open it, just in case it’s something foul like that. Now, I have to go change these bandages, thanks to you. Walkins: You’re lucky it was just a flesh wound. Farwell: I’ve heard that more times than I’d like to count. Now, I’m going to change these things, so if you could put on some damn pants, for the love of God, I’d really appreciate it. Walkins salutes as Farwell heads up the stairs. Walkins: Yes, sir! James just lets out a little groan, then keeps walking. Walkins: Oh, and James? Farwell: Yes, Clyde? Walkins: What’s a reach-around? The next day, Clyde receives more mail. And the day after that, even more. By the third, he has almost two-hundred letters. And all the while, James has to hear about it. Farwell: You know what he did now? He’s got all the letters in his room, and he’s swimming in them. I kid you not. He’s using his bed as some sort of obscene diving board, and he’s leaping into hundreds of pieces of paper. It’s maddening. Sipping a cup of tea, James sits across from a guy in overalls and a stained t-shirt, who taps his pen against the table and ponders the situation. The tag on his shirt says Bill, and so we’ll call him that. Bill: I agree, it must be maddening. Nevertheless, remember that all fame fades with time. The walls of his disillusion will come crashing down, and his swimming pool of attention will be reduced to dust bunnies and smelly stuffed animals. Farwell: Yeah. You’re probably right. It’s just so hard putting up with him in the meantime. Bill: You might find it helps to involve yourself in his activities. His pleasure can be yours, if you can only let your pride rest for a few hours. Now, how do you want to do this? Should I take the couch, or the TV? Farwell: The couch. I think a cat peed on it. Bill: I’m taking the TV. Standing, Bill places his pen back in his pocket, grabs the dolly next to him, and walks over to the TV in the living room. A few seconds later, he wheels it out to his repo van before driving off into the sunset. Farwell: I hope they take Clyde next. 24 Hours Later… It’s been hours since Clyde received that strange delivery, and he still hasn’t come down from his room. After a while, James begins to let his imagination run wild, with the final product being something you’d see in a cheap horror flick, except one directed by Clyde. That’s right. More quasi-homosexual dance scenes involving men playfully lathering up other men in an innocent fashion than you can shake a stick at. Or so it would seem. So it would come as no surprise that James wants to see for himself what Clyde has in his possession. Or if he’s dead. Maybe then people would stop going on about his winning streak. After scaling the stairs, James stands outside Clyde’s bedroom door, wondering for a second whether or not to knock. As he listens to what takes place behind the closed door, James hears the distinct sound of cartoons playing on the television. But that can’t be right. They don’t have a television anymore… Farwell: Clyde, open up. We need to have a talk. Inside his room, Clyde scrambles to turn down the noise and hide the source, throwing it under some clothes on the floor. Walkins: J-just a second…I’m masturbating… James knocks harder on the door. Farwell: I heard a TV in there! Where the hell did you get a TV?! And again his fist connects with the door, even harder still. Walkins: There’s no…I-uh…no speaky English! Finally, the door gives way and James bursts into the room. Farwell: Ha! Caught you with your pants around your hips! Now, where’d you hide it? Admitting defeat, Clyde breathes a heavy sigh and pulls the portable DVD player out from under the pile of filthy, filthy clothes. Beside the bed, the case for Pokémon 4Ever lies open. Farwell: Where’d you get this, anyway? Walkins: Your credit card. You’d be amazed at all the great things you can buy with it! And you said we were poor. Farwell: We ARE poor, you reject. The great thing about a credit card is that you have to pay it back, and we can’t afford to pay it back. Thusly, this DVD player needs to go back. So hand it over. Once again forced to admit defeat, Clyde says a quiet goodbye to it, before tossing James the brand-new player. Except instead of it being shiny and new, the case is cracked and scratched, and the screen is all marred to hell. Staring Clyde in the eyes, James just stands there, waiting for an explanation. Walkins: I took it down an alleyway. Farwell: I…I don’t understand. Have you been riding it? Ignoring James’ question, Clyde’s tone suddenly becomes serious. Walkins: Look, James…I know what this is about. Farwell’s eyebrow piques. Farwell: Do you, now? Walkins: Yes. The unwarranted cruelty, telling me I can only swim in my bedroom, taking TV away time and time again, refusing to order me a pizza, all of it. Farwell: …I…see… Walkins: Just admit it, James. You’re jealous. Farwell: Jealous?! HA! Jealous of what, exactly? Walkins: Jealous of me! After all, who’s been getting all the fan mail? Who’s been getting in PRIME’s Top 5? Who’s been on the hottest winning streak since David slew Goliath? Cut to David standing over the corpse of a scraggly little midget, a large red welt protruding from its head. A loaded sling in his hands with pride in his eyes, David places his free hand on his hip, while the wind blows his cape behind him. David: Let it be known that on this day, I, David, slew the Giant, Goliath. Meekly, a villager steps from the surrounding crowd, politely raising his finger to comment. Villager: But sir, his moniker was simply a joke regarding his diminutive stature. Surely he’s done nothing to warrant such a cruel demise? David: Well, you see…the thing is…I…LOOK OUT! ANOTHER GIANT! And then, suddenly, David lunges forward and brings the sling down across the villager’s head, falling him with the stone inside. Then he turns to the others. David: Let it be known that on this day, I, David, killed the Giant, Goliath. And then this guy had a heart attack. Back to The Forsaken. Walkins: And who’s the guy that won the OSW Tag Titles for us? Or the RPW Tag Titles, for that matter? How about, who just beat both LeStatt Knight and Chris Collins in less than a day? Farwell: Ok, Clyde. Enough. I get it. Walkins: See, you’re just jealous of all I’ve accomplished. It’s just too bad you can’t be happy for me. After all, at least one of us doesn’t suck. Hey, maybe I could even wrestle by myself against Teh Greatness at PRIME’s Great American Nightmare and win the titles for the both of us! James’ right eye twitches while he sits there, staring straight ahead for a moment. After a few seconds of silence, he turns his head to Clyde. Farwell: You know what? You’re right. Walkins: I am? Farwell: You are. I have been jealous of all your success, and I’m just sorry I haven’t realized it until now. But it’s not too late. Walkins: It isn’t? Farwell: …No. Listen, I saw an ad earlier for a science contest, and I think you’d do great in it. I’d really like to be your coach, if you’d let me bask in your success. Clyde beams at the proposal. Walkins: You don’t know how long I’ve waited to hear those words. Of course, Jamesy! Farwell: You’ve been waiting to hear me say I want to be your coach? Walkins: Oooh! Sends chills down my spine just thinking about it. Once again, Farwell’s eye twitches slightly, but he regains his composure. Even Clyde’s creepiness won’t stop him now. Farwell: But we’ve only got a couple of days, so you’d better hurry up and get to it. In the meantime, I’ll register you in the contest. Walkins: Thanks, James. You won’t regret it! As Clyde dashes out of his room and down the stairs, James stands alone for a second, his fingers forming a tent as they press together, and a grin slowly spreading across his lips. Farwell: Methinks I shan’t. Over the next seventy-two hours, Clyde works feverishly, digging in garbage cans and crawling through open house windows in order to gather various electronic devices, working or otherwise. Phones, cameras, MP3 players, you name it. And at the end of it all, just a day before the science fair begins, Clyde unveils his prized invention to James. Farwell: What the… Cut to a female judge standing in a gymnasium, staring at Clyde’s brainchild. Judge: …hell is that? Walkins: It’s my invention! Picture this: a flip-open cell phone on each side, old-fashioned dial telephone on top, portable MP3 player hanging off the back like some sort of twisted appendage, one single computer speaker glued to the front of the busted up portable DVD player Clyde obtained through credit card fraud acting as the face, and a Dell computer tower with a plutonium rod stuck inside it for a core. Around the bastardized creation, children stand proudly next to their own projects, and a banner clearly reading Children’s Science Fair hangs in the background. Meanwhile, the judge just keeps staring at Clyde’s invention, unable to grasp the proper words. Walkins: Beautiful, isn’t it? Judge: Er…ah… The judge looks around for an out, and finds one. Judge: Yes, it’s wonderful. Now, if you’ll pardon me, I think I see a student mixing ammonia and bleach. Taking off in the first direction she sees, the judge quickly disappears in a sea of parents. Clyde turns to James, giving a thumbs-up while smiling. Walkins: I think she liked it. Cut to five minutes later, as the same judge comes back around after awarding the first, second and third prize winners, pinning a ribbon on Clyde’s shirt. Thanks For Participating. Clyde just looks down at the ribbon with a saddened expression on his face, while everyone around him claps because they think he’s retarded. Parents, the elderly, children, other retards…everyone. And through it all, James can’t help but bust out into a hearty laugh at Clyde’s expense. Back at the house, Clyde’s monstrous creation sits proudly atop the fireplace mantle in the living room, and pinned beneath it is the ribbon that only he received. Lying on the couch facing it, James looks up every once in a while and has a good chuckle, before returning to his newspaper. After several minutes, Clyde enters the picture for the first time since returning home. Walkins: James, we have to talk. Folding his newspaper, James takes off his reading glasses and sits up, looking at Clyde. Farwell: Alright. But we have to do it in front of the abomination. Walkins: That’s what I wanted to talk about. Walking around the side of the couch, Clyde props himself up onto the arm and turns to face James. Walkins: I really thought you wanted to be my friend, and then today…I worked so hard on it, and you just laughed at me. And then everyone thought I was stupid…and it hurt me, James. It hurt. I never thought you hated me, even after everything you’ve said to me, and all the times you were mean to me. I knew that was only because of who you are. But today…now I know, James…now I know you hate me. Clyde breathes heavily, his head hanging forward as his eyes stare into the fabric of the couch. As James sits in stunned silence, Clyde lifts his head and looks straight into the eyes of the man who’s always been his best friend, a single tear rolling down his cheek. Walkins: …Wh-why…James… Hardly able to choke out the words, Clyde lets his head hang once more, now openly sobbing at the loss of his only real friend. And just as he believes he’s truly alone, Clyde feels a hand on his shoulder. Through the darkness, he hears a voice. Farwell: I’m sorry I embarrassed you today. You were right, I was jealous of you. You’re getting a lot of attention that I never have, but now I realize that it’s deserved. You work your ass off to overcome the odds each and every time, and this week is no different from last week. We’ve got Oxios and Rampage, and you’ve got Coleman in GTT5, but we’ll get through this just like we always have. And we’ll do it because we’re a team. A damn good team. Clyde stops sobbing long enough to look up at James, who returns the gaze with a warm smile and a ruffle of the hair with his hand. Farwell: I don’t hate you, Clyde. I…you’re my brother. I have no choice but to love you. Suddenly, just as James finishes his sentence, Clyde springs up from depression with a wild grin on his face. Walkins: HA! I made you say you love me! You’re gay for me! Farwell: What the hell?! Walkins: Jamesy and Clyde, sitting in a tree! K-i-s-s-i-n-g! First comes love, then comes marriage, then comes-AHH! Cutting his rhyme short, Clyde backs away just in time to avoid James’ choking hands aiming for his throat, turning and taking off at full speed around the couch and up the stairs. Knowing he has no chance of keeping up with Clyde, James mutters a curse under his breath and kicks the side of the couch. Farwell: Dammit! Causing the leg to break, of course. Farwell: Double dammit! With no hope of living down the fact he said he loved Clyde for at least a month or two, James figures it’s best to get a bit of shuteye before the next day. Because, undoubtedly, it’s going to be a doozey. Up the stairs and around the corner, James passes Clyde’s room, once more hearing sounds coming from inside. Except these sounds aren’t as much like cartoons as they are music. More specifically, "Eye Of The Tiger". Curious, but understandably wary, due to all of Clyde’s previous mishaps in his life, James hesitates at knocking on the door, opting instead to simply open it. And within the room, he finds Clyde working up a sweat as he wildly throws punches and delivers sloppy kicks around the room, knocking shit down all over the place as Survivor plays on. Farwell: What the hell’s going on in here? You haven’t been up here more than five minutes and already you’re Clydezilla, King of the Morons. Hearing a reference to something copyrighted, Clyde immediately takes up a prehistoric role. Period: Late Cretaceous. Subject: Tyrannosaurus Rex. Target: James. Walkins: RAR~! Clydesaurus Rex is ready to take on anyone! He’ll eat Drake Coleman, Teh Greatness, LeStatt Knight again, and even you! Lunging forward, Clyde takes an unexpected bite out of James’ arm. Farwell: CHRIST! What’d you do that for?! Walkins: Clydesaurus is hungry! Grr! Feed my insatiable appetite! Farwell: We have peanut butter and a brown banana downstairs. It’s yours, if you want it. Clutching his bitten arm in pain, James turns to let the raging Clydesaurus through. Walkins: GWOOOOAAAAAAR!!! Holding his hands up to his head in order to make horns out of the index fingers, Clyde lowers his head and rams into James’ chest, before careening off into a wall and then down the stairs. Not under his own power, either. Lying at the bottom of the staircase on his back, Clyde kicks the air like a dinosaur would if you pushed it onto its back, rolling around and making weird noises as a coup de grâce. Walkins: GWOAR! GWOOOOAAAAAAR!!! Farwell: That’s it…this is too weird for me. I’m going to bed. Turning and heading to his room, James shuts the door and the light almost simultaneously, leaving Clyde at the bottom of the stairs to fend for himself. Sometime in the midst of the night, as James sleeps in his bed, and Clyde sleeps on his back at the bottom of the stairs, occasionally kicking his leg like a dog, a storm gathers near their home. A bolt of lightning strikes the ground outside, then another hits the satellite dish on their roof. As the smoke sizzles outside, a blue light appears in the living room. Something’s there. On the mantle, the abomination glows. And on the abomination’s screen, two simple words remain. Simple, yet indicative of humanity’s demise. Receiving Signal…
