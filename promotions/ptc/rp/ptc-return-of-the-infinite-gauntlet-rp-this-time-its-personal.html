Return of the Infinite Gauntlet RP, this time it's personal

10 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The light was red, flashing on and off as he watched. So much like a stoplight in the dark lonesome hours after midnight and before dawn. It was supposed to tell him, that someone had left a message on his phone, but all he could think about was those stoplights, off and on, acting as a stop sign. Sure you could just roll right on through, but chances are you’d get busted for it. He wanted to pick up the phone, listen to his message, but he couldn’t. He’d heard the phone ring, saw who the caller was, and had shut down. Sheena, calling from California, where she’d stayed by her brother’s side in the hospital. He didn’t want to hear the news, good or bad, he’d still have to listen to that voice so full of caring, on the other end. He’d have to face that he’d lied to her. A drink would have helped, always did in situations like this, but he still hadn’t been able to touch a drop. Not since that night, he’d bought a couple bottles here in there, but when push had come to shove, he’d never been able to drink. He refused his own crutch when he’d brutalized someone for there’s. Apparently his hypocrisy knew certain limits after all. Outside the sun had risen a few hours ago. He’d slept, fitfully, and had awoken before dawn. As he’d stared out the window, he’d tried focus on the beauty of the fire show in the heavens, sadly it hadn’t worked. Everything was still too screwed up in his head, the guilt over everything was tearing him up inside. Soon he imagined he’d simply waste away, devoured from within by his conscience. He should be leaving, heading towards the airport, off to his next destination. The constant travel helped a bit, if he was always rushing to be somewhere new, he didn’t have to think too much about what he was trying to get away from. However, in the times like these, when he could move at his own pace that the whole situation struck him hardest. He felt like a shark, forced to be in constant motion, lest he drown. It wasn’t a comforting feeling, but then again he wasn’t deserving of comfort, so he believed at least. Not for the first time he considered calling the brother-in-law, Jeff. Having done some research he found out the guy was pretty respected in the shrink circle, and he was one of the few people that already knew the situation and the lie. Despite it being a conflict of interest, due to their relationship he’d agreed to talk with Stevens in a professional sense. It probably would have been good for him, but try as he might Stevens couldn’t bring himself to talk to a shrink like that. Prejudice he supposed, the idea that only crazies and whiney people would didn’t have the balls to deal with their own problems. Of course he was beginning to feel more and more like one of those whiney people who couldn’t deal with his own problems. It wasn’t something he’d ever really had a problem with before, only recently had things all come to a head. It all started with the Pope’s death, everything since then had pretty much been downhill. At least personally, strange how his professional life had never been more on the rise. As everything fell apart around him outside the ring, he’d managed to become one of the hottest commodities going in the wrestling world. Oh well, life’s funny sometimes. And still the light on the phone flashed, taunting him. Why did it have to be red? Red means, fire, red means stop. Most of all red means warning, tread carefully, and that’s exactly why he couldn’t pick up the phone, couldn’t return the call. He just couldn’t shake the feeling that little red light was telling him not to call back. Running a hand over his chin he felt the stubble that had developed from two days of not shaving, and attempted to work up the courage that had thus far eluded him. The knock on the door startled him nearly out of his skin. It had to be Alex, checking on him, the big man had been really good about doing that lately. That he was worried about Michael was clear enough, it was written all over his face constantly. What was slightly harder to see was the weight of the secret crushing him as well. Sheena had been a true friend to Alex, the two had stuck together in those dark times after Michael had abandoned both of them months ago. In that time the two had grown closer than ever. There’s wasn’t a relationship tainted by lust and false promises like Michael’s relationship with her, it was something better, something pure. And Alex had spat all over that purity by helping to cover up for Michael’s actions. He didn’t deserve such loyalty and devotion. It’s a wonder he still inspired it after all he’d done. “Come in” The door swung open silently, behind it the giant of a man that was Alex Morgan. He’d obviously tried to hide the dark circles under his eyes form a lack of sleep but he’d done a horrible job. Alex always wore his heart on his sleeve, and his most inner thoughts on his face. The smile lines around his face had been replaced by the darker, deeper lines of worry. His blonde hair was starting to turn gray at the temples, the man seemed to have aged years over the last few months. It was a sad sight, to say the least. “The plane leaves out of Sea-Tac, in a few hours Mike. We need to leave pretty soon to get through all the security.” Sea-Tac? That’s right, they were in Seattle, with everything going on each destination had been a blur. Although how he could have forgotten one of his old homes was beyond him. If they were in Seattle, that meant there was someone he could talk to, someone who’d understand almost exactly what he was going through. He was reminded of a line from one of his favorite movies “get busy living, or get busy dying”. He couldn’t just roll over, and die so he’d best start living. Stevens stood from his chair, filled with more energy and excitement than he’d felt since that night. Scooping his phone, cigarettes, and keys for the rental car off the table, he stuffed them all in his jeans pocket. He smiled, and it felt good to simple a genuine smile. “Actually, Alex, why don’t you call and see if you can get a later flight, maybe something tomorrow or the next day. I don’t give a fuck how much it costs, just get it done, because we can’t make it today.” The exuberant display took Alex aback, and he stared at Michael with a strange look on his face, as if trying to figure out what exactly had just taken place. It wasn’t surprising that Alex would be shocked, after all Stevens had done nothing but mope since that night. He’d been mostly quiet and sullen, probably hadn’t spoken more than a hundred words or so in the last two weeks. “Why not Mike, what’s wrong?” “Nothing’s wrong Alex, don’t you see, we’re in Seattle! I’m going to see an old friend.” “Who?” “Drake, I’m going to see Drake.” “Oh, him.” ************************************************************************************* It wasn’t hard to find the place, he’d lived there for a while after all. Certainly it had been years, but some of the best times of his life had been spent there. Walking up the gravel drive towards the house he had to chuckle at the sign outside. It read as it always had “Nutt House: If you lived here, you’d probably be crazy too”. The place looked like it hadn’t changed a bit, maybe sometimes you can go home again, at least he hoped that would be the case. He’d parked a couple miles away, not wanting to announce his presence until the last possible moment. Plus it was a nice day for a walk, and he needed some time to organize his thoughts fully. He’d tried to put them together during the drive, but he was too busy trying to decide if he’d made the right decision. It was a silly thought, of course he had, this man more than any other he knew would understand the situation. There couldn’t be another who understood more about hurting someone important to you, than Drake Hyde. Yet, he hadn’t seen or spoken to Drake, in at least two years, and they hadn’t exactly parted company on the best of terms. The two had met on the independent circuit, and had become fast friends, along with Drake’s tag team partner DeAngelo Boss, better known as D-Struction. Drake and D, were a big hit on the tag team scene eventually signing to the American Wrestling Alliance, and forming a stable around themselves called The Nutt House Lunatics, or NHL for short. They were one top of the world, until one night of revelry brought everything crashing down. They were celebrating their latest retention of the AWA’s tag team titles, and Drake had been stupid enough to get behind the wheel. Nature took it’s course, and both men had ended up in the hospital, Drake in a coma, DeAngelo didn’t make it. Once Drake woke up, he’d sunk into a deep depression, Stevens had been one of the people to help him out of the funk. So yeah, if anyone could understand it would have to be Drake, hopefully he could look past everything that came later, and return the favor. Arriving at the door, he raised his hand to knock, and realized it was trembling slightly. Come on Stevens, get yourself together man, you don’t want to look like a total wreck as soon as the door is opened do you? Desperately he struggled to regain some semblance of control, as he was flooded with memories. Everything about this place was exactly the same as he’d left it, he wasn’t sure he could handle even walking in the door. Gritting his teeth he waiting for his hand to still, and once it stopped, he pounded on the door. Probably a little too hard, he didn’t want them to think he was trying to break the door down. Letting his fist drop down to his side, he waited. Nervously he shifted his weight from one foot to the other, chewing a bit on his lower lip. He imagined how silly he would look if Drake opened the door and saw him doing either, and tried to stop. It worked, briefly, but as the wait grew longer so did his anxiety. Maybe no one was home? Or maybe Drake had seen who was on his door step and decided not to answer. Neither would have actually been that surprising. Drake was anything but a home body, and had been known to hold a grudge for a very long time. As the seconds passed into minutes, Stevens began to get the idea that no one was going to answer. Still he had to try again, had to make sure he wasn’t going to get an answer, there was always the chance that simply the original knock hadn’t been heard. Lifting his hand again he rapped sharply on the door three times. He could hear the hollow thud echoing through the cavernous insides of the place, and thought he picked up on the muted sounds of movement, but he wasn’t certain. Digging them out of his pocket he lit himself a cigarette, deciding to focus on the burning tip and smoke. Anything to look composed when that door opened, if it ever would. Again the seconds stretched into minutes, and he began to feel the heat from the cigarette on his knuckles. Tossing the spent butt aside he gave it up for a lost cause. He’d been standing in front of the door nearly ten minutes now, and it hadn’t opened, looks like he’d either have to wait for someone to get home, or try again. If Drake was indeed inside, then waiting would accomplish nothing, but he didn’t exactly have time to waste, he was already pushing his schedule to it’s limits as it was, he couldn’t afford to just come back tomorrow. Maybe he just wasn’t meant to see Drake, perhaps his sins were so vile that the world had chosen to punish him by removing the one person who might have offered some sort of relief from his guilt. Or he could just have bad timing. There were just too many variables in the situation, and Stevens couldn’t make up his mind as to what to do next. He thought back to the hotel, that moment of clarity that had brought him here, everything had seemed so simple and easy then. He should’ve realized it wasn’t going to be that easy, life never let things be too easy. Deciding he’d just drive himself crazy if he continued to stand here, he turned away from the door, preparing to walk away. Seeing Drake just wasn’t in the cards, and that was probably for the best anyway, he didn’t deserve comfort. As he stepped from the porch and headed back towards the gravel drive he heard a noise behind him, the sound of a door opening. He couldn’t believe his luck, he was going to see Drake after all, maybe he DID deserve a little piece. Turning quickly a huge smile on his face, he found disappointment. It wasn’t Drake who stood in the doorway after all, but a young woman in a bathrobe, her hair twisted up in a towel. She was vaguely familiar, probably one of Drake’s old flings. The guy had always been a ladies man. She smiled at him, a smile that held something more than just general niceness, he could feel her eyes scanning him up and down. Apparently she was impressed by what she saw. “Can I help you?” “Yeah, actually, well, maybe, I’m not sure. I’m looking for Drake Hyde.” Her smile turned into a flirtatious pout, and she adjusted her posture subtlety to reveal the tanned skin beneath the robe. On any other day Stevens might have been caught in her web, but he wasn’t interested today, he just wanted to talk to Drake, and if he wasn’t here, she was just wasting his time. “So you’re not the stripper I hired huh?” “Afraid not. Look if Drake isn’t here, I’ve got to be going, sorry I disturbed you.” She twitched an eyebrow, clearly she wasn’t used to being shot down. Upon realizing he wasn’t interested her expression shifted once again, to something more like a bored detachment. “No, he’s here, I suppose I can get him, who’s calling?” “Just tell him it’s Spitty, he’ll know who I am.” Recognition dawned in her eyes as he spoke. Suddenly she was full of energy bounding from the porch, and down to the grass. Leaping up she threw her arms around his neck, and planted a kiss on his cheek. Not wanting her to fall he threw his own arms around her, and stumbled back a step from the unexpected weight in his arms. Suddenly he realized with whom he’d been speaking, Melina Duran, sister to Drake’s primary girl, Leena Duran. “OH MY GOD! Mikey, how are you?! I can’t believe I didn’t recognize you, but you look, different somehow.” “Yeah, I didn’t recognize you either Melina, sorry. But really, I need to talk to Drake, it’s important, ok? Maybe we can catch up later?” “Sure, no problem.” She dropped back down to her feet, smiling all the way, motioning for him to follow she headed back towards the house. “Come on Mikey, I’m sure Drake’ll be really happy to see you. Oh yeah, how’s Sheena?” ************************************************************************************* He’d somehow made it through all the small talk. It was nice seeing Drake again, and Leena, and Melina looked as good as they ever had. Still it was obvious to everyone from how he spoke that there was a dark cloud hanging over him, something he really needed to talk about privately. It was just like old times with Drake helping to steer the conversation away from topics that didn’t need to be discussed in the open. Stevens himself had outright refused to talk about Sheena, something the girls weren’t too happy about, although they’d quickly gotten over it. There was a lot of old history, thus, a lot of talking and reminiscing to be done. After about an hour of catching up with the exploits of Drake and crew, the man himself had excused both he and Stevens for a private conversation. Stevens met with a grateful sigh, as the two men had retired to something of a lounge type area. Drake had immediately slid behind the bar, and started making himself a drink. Once that was done he pulled out a bottle and displayed it to Stevens. “Whisky on the rocks, right?” The offer was tempting. He wanted a drink desperately, that golden elixir to help drive the demons away. Drake should’ve been the poster child for sobriety after his experiences with liquor, but he continued to drink, although in moderation. Maybe he had been looking at things wrong, and liquor wasn’t really a crutch, just something that tasted really good. Still he couldn’t get past that block. “Nah, I’ll pass, I think I’m giving up drinking.” Clearly the sentence was more telling than he’d meant it to be, as Drake sat the bottle down on the bar, and stared hard at Stevens for a moment. Drake had the kind of piercing eyes that could tear right to the heart of any matter, and looking into the soul of whomever he watched close enough. “Ok, that’s just weird. What the hell happened bro?” “Well, I did something, bad. I hurt someone.” “Shit, you’re still wrestling, of course you hurt someone, it’s part of the job.” “No, I mean this is different. I hurt someone on the outside, I just snapped. I beat the kid up really bad, and I’m not sure what to do about it. He just pissed me off so much I couldn’t handle it anymore and went off.” “How bad?” “Coma bad.” “Oh, fuck.” “Yeah.” Collapsing into a chair, Stevens buried his face in his hands. It was all he could do to keep from breaking down in tears. This was the first time he’d actually talked about what he’d done since that night, and everything that he’d been trying to push down was boiling up to the surface. Drake moved out from behind the bar, and put his hand on Stevens’s shoulder. His grip was strong, reassuring, and it took Stevens back when he’d done the same thing for Drake when he’d confronted his guilt about DeAngelo. “Who?” “That’s the worst thing man, it was Sheena’s brother.” “I didn’t even know she had a brother.” “Well, she does, and I fucked him up, right proper. And now he’s just lying there, and I feel like a piece of shit.” “What’s Sheena think of it? I mean she’s probably pretty pissed off, but I know you man, he must have done SOMETHING to push you that far. She must understand that, right?” And there it was, the fact is she probably WOULD have understood. She would’ve been angry, would have screamed and cried, but she would have forgiven him for doing it. Only he’d been too big of a coward to tell her, and that made him the lowest of the low. “She doesn’t know.” “Wait, what?” “I didn’t tell her, I lied. She was pretty messed up when it happened, I thought it’d be better if she didn’t know. Now I can’t even face her, she’s been calling me all night, and I haven’t been able to answer the phone. I don’t think I can handle hearing her voice right now. I beat her brother into a coma, and then I lied to her about it. I’m a fucking monster.” “You’ve got to tell her. Seriously bro, it’s the only thing you can do. You fucked up, it happens to everyone, but she’s got to know the truth or it’ll always be there between you two. And worse than that the guilt from the lie will just pile on the guilt from what you did, and it’ll eat you up inside. You’ve probably already felt it, and it’s just going to get worse. Look bro, I know about this stuff. The guilt, it’s a killer, believe me, you know I understand. For a long time I couldn’t face my guilt, I wouldn’t tell people what had really happened that night when D died. I just couldn’t face it, but YOU helped me see that I had to face it. And once I did, I started to feel better, it’s the only way to start healing.” “But how can I tell her man, how am I supposed to tell her that in addition to hurting her brother, I lied and covered it up? It’s just too much man, she’ll hate me, and I need her Drake.” “I don’t know Mike, but if you don’t you’ll hate yourself even more. Just call her bro, I’ll stay right here with you while you call if you want me to, but you’ve got to do it. Plus he could come out of it. He could end up being alright after all, maybe a little worse for wear, but alright. Hell, I’m proof of that.” “I know, but what if he isn’t alright, what if he doesn’t come out of it?” “Then you’ll get through that too, somehow.” “Alright, I’ll call.” He didn’t want to, but there was no way Drake was going to let him get out of it now. He was the type of guy that would force you to do what was best for you, and if you didn’t do it for yourself, he’d do it for you. And the last thing Stevens wanted to happen was for Drake to tell Sheena everything, but he’d do it, he was that good a friend. Digging the phone from his pocket, he saw again, that flashing red light. Still a stop sign, still a warning. He started to flip the phone open, when it began ringing. Alex, he probably had some trouble changing the tickets. Anything was better than calling Sheena, so he decided to answer. “Hello?” “Mike, you need to call Sheena, now.” “What, why?” A knot formed in his stomach, and his mouth dried out. He could feel a cold chill of sweat forming on his forehead. Something in Alex’s voice told him that he hadn’t misjudged the blinking light, and it had been a warning after all. “He’s gone Mike, they say his brain just faded away.” “No.” ~Fin~
