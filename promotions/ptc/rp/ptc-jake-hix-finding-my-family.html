Jake Hix: Finding My Family

20 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The World Outside Outside, the world was going on as if nothing had changed. The streets were filled with revelers celebrating all manner of joys. The University Of Texas victory over Ohio State was being celebrated, new romance were enjoyed and some people were just looking for a small relief from the stresses of their daily lives. The sounds wafted up from the street through his open window to greet him. He was not celebrating. The live bands were out, with Main Street in Dallas shut down to accommodate the crowds and foot traffic. Deep Ellum would see plenty of money flow into the bars and clubs from the young adults trying to meet each other, and the couple wanting to enjoy an outdoor concert the one-week the Texas heat had finally relented from its excessive onslaught of the Metroplex. He was not in the mood. All of the distractions and activities his wealth afforded him meant nothing to him at the moment. His mind raced, and he had nothing to offer back except worry. He needed to know something about his family; he needed to know what Sid was arranging to get his revenge; and he needed to protect his family once again. He knew across from his building in the alley concealed within the mix of people were the disciples, watching him to ensure wherever he went was quickly reported back to Sid. They wanted him to try and find his family, to lead them right to where they were taking refuge. Jake had no idea where his family could be at the moment. The last he heard the bullet-ridden boat had been located, but there were no signs of his family. The good news was there was no blood on their boat, but there was plenty of blood on two other ruined boats in the area. “More bodies for Katrina…” All of the delicate planning had been for naught. Finding sacred ground to conceal their presence from the dark forces working to kill them had been difficult. There were churches, but they could not hide in one for an extended period of months and years. Their celebrity meant that they could possibly be recognized, and the disciples would be scouring the Internet for any rumors. Jake laughed, knowing he had misled them more than once by starting false rumors on blogs and message boards. He would use their own methods against them whenever possible. Those times were becoming scarce though. He would have traded everything he had for a call from the family, but they might not even be able to call him at the moment. He had several throw away phones to prevent anyone from listening in, but he still had thirty minutes to go before he was scheduled to call Stephen. Until then, he was left with his own fears about what had happened. Cloaked in the low lights of his den, he looked like a morose motherfucker at the moment, and he was. The fan spinning overhead combined with the air conditioning kept his bare chest cold, as the fierce wrath underneath threatened to burst out into the world. He forced himself to get up out of the overstuffed chair after pushing the ottoman aside with his feet. He walked over to the bar, his bare feet slapping the cool wooden floor. He pulled a fresh glass from the bar, and dropped some ice slivers in before pouring the Macallan 18 Year Old Scotch into the glass. He swirled everything around in his hand, and took a long sip. For several weeks he had put the liquor up, making sure it didn’t interfere with his training. None of it mattered now. What did the matches matter at the moment? He couldn’t be the Big Dawg anymore. That was a trivial concern in light of these circumstances. All he did lately was train and talk on the phone to find out where his family might be hiding out. The scotch kept him cool and even…for the time being. Sick of the waiting, he moved over to the window and looked down in the street. He noticed the car parked in the alley across the way. They would be there, at least two men who looked as normal as could be on the outside. Within, their black souls would cut his throat in an instant if they thought the attack would be victorious. Searching In The Aftermath For the modern world, heat had become something annoying until you could escape it into air-conditioned buildings. The advent of the air-conditioner was often blamed for the ruination of Congress because they were comfortable when deciding important legislation, so blubbering on for too long wasn’t a big deal. Here, in Louisiana after the hurricane, things were different. Going inside barely provided relief, and the stale air sometimes suffocated someone as much as the heat. John “Hawk” Peters was resting against a tree that provided ample shade during the day, but at night it was simply a place to lean his back against. The cane he carried was not ornamental, but pain passed. The cane helped support a body ravaged by the road and the ring. His hair had long since gone silver, but he still had it all. He kept it slicked back as though he were always ready to impress the ladies, but now he was just another refugee sweating through his clothes to any passers bye. He had promises to keep to an old friend, just like the man he was waiting on. Driving and walking among the aftermath had taken o toll on both of them. The devastation looked more like the remnants of some long ago war rather that the reminder to man that nature was still in charge. Trees had been ripped from the ground as simply as a man would weed the garden, entire communities were laid waste until only a few pipes and poled marked where entire subdivisions once had been constructed. Perhaps worst of all, there were the bodies left to rot in the sun and the water. Bloated bodies infested by the insects and bugs feeding off of the offerings of sacrifice from Katrina. They had seen death before, but not like the landscape painting in her fury. Stephen had gone inside to speak to the Reverend while John took a rest bit. They were hoping for news on what happened to Xavier, Pitt, Alexandria and Bella. Their old friend was in misery, and Hawk wanted to know where his first friend in the Hix family was. They were like brothers, and he had to know what had happened to Pitt. They had started it all…so long ago. Stephen came walking out of the church. He looked worn down and ragged; with his trademark toned body looking ragged and weakened. He was sweating like everyone else, but his sweat came from something other than the heat as his gaunt face made him look like he was one of the victims of Katrina. Their state helped them blend into the environment was all he told Hawk, still trying to hide his own pain. Reed came over, and put his hand against the tree. Hawk wondered if the tree was strong enough to support Stephen in this state, “What did you find out SSR?” The old moniker brought a smile to Stephen’s face. The name was a sign of respect from the days when he was called Sensational Stephen Reed, and was an active competitor. Those days were long gone, and for him the scourge inside was slowly eating him alive. “They were here. Xavier was in a bad way, but they wouldn’t talk about what happened. He sent them off along with the other refugees to Houston. Not to the dome, but to one of the halfway houses. They’re on the way now.” “So we need to get to Houston?” Hawk considered another trip. He was broken down, physically and emotionally like everyone else here in the state. Hawk looked out at the darkened sky with sad eyes, “He’s sure? He recognized the photos and he was absolutely sure?” “He was sure, but we can’t know which one they went to.” Stephen was sucking down gulps of air, having a hard time keeping himself even. He hadn’t taken his medication until just now inside the church where Hawk couldn’t see him. The medicine would go to work, but he would suffer another half hour before relief set in. Hawk was looking his friend up and down. He heard the labored wheezing of his breathing, “Are you okay? You sound awful my friend.” “All the shit the hurricane stirred up is playing havoc with my allergies. I took some medicine; I’ll be okay soon. We need to start heading to Houston before Sid’s men get there.” Stephen forced himself to take control of his breathing. He was able to slow it down to a huffing pant. The allergies were a quick lie that would serve him, for the moment. “Well, the time is almost here. Is your cell phone on?” Hawk asked. “I’m just waiting on Jake’s call.” Stephen pulled it out and checked the time. He hadn’t missed any calls, but it would be coming in just a few seconds. “How do you think Jake’s handling all of this?” “He’s a caged animal, and once that door is open I just don’t want to get in the way.” Hawk shook his head, knowing nothing good would ever come from all of this in the end. There was still more blood to shed, and more suffering to endure. The cell phone rang, and SSR answered it. “Hello?” “This is Jake.” The voice was choppy and direct. He wasn’t happy sitting on the sidelines at the moment, “Tell me where we’re at.” “There’s a Reverend here who helped the family. He sent them along with a group of evacuees down to Houston.” Stephen began know Jake wasn’t going to like any of this. “Houston? They’re part of the Astrodome crowd?” Jake spit the words out like ash polluting his mouth. “There are cameras all over that place! He might as well have painted a target on everyone’s back!” Stephen could feel the cough creeping up on him, but he fought it off. He only had to hold on a little longer, “Jake, calm down. He didn’t send them to the Dome, he sent them to a set of halfway houses they’re using until more permanent arrangements can be found. We’re going to head down there to round them up.” “Trying to get a flight out will be impossible. That flight is booked solid, I guarantee.” “We’re driving.” Stephen realized the mistake as soon as the words slipped past his lips. “The Hell you are!” Jake had heard enough, and he refused to be a prisoner of his fears any longer. “You can make it to Shreveport, right?” “Of course.” “Good. Then I’ll meet you there with a plane. We’ll fly down to Houston and bring everyone back home.” Jake had his mind set, speaking in iron tones. “Jake, is that safe?” SSR needed to bring him back down to the reality of the forces they faced. “We tried hiding, and it didn’t work. I won’t hide them anymore. We have to fight Sid and his little army of malevolent shits.” Jake hung up the phone. Stephen did the same, and realized the pain was becoming too much to fight away, “We need to meet him in Shreveport. He’ll fly us on to Houston.” “That flights only an hour!” Hawk didn’t understand Jake’s rush. “They’re traveling by bus, we’ll beat them there no matter what.” “Five hour drive versus a one hour flight. Where do you think he came down? Besides, it’s halfway between here and there, so we’ll fly the second half.’ Stephen took a few steps toward the car, but he could feel it overcoming him. “Can you drive us to Shreveport? I’m not feeling too good.” “Sure.” Hawk looked him up and down with a wicked little grin, “You don’t look so good either.” Stephen laughed, enjoying even a brief moment of levity, “Bite me gimpy.” Escape Into Chaos The flight arrangements had been made easily over the phone. He would pay a premium for the private plane, but he didn’t care right now. He would sacrifice the entire family fortune to save the family it was meant for in the end. As he changed into jeans and a black tee shirt he reflected upon the dichotomy of the situation. Twin brothers, one destined to save the world and the other destined to destroy it once and for all. He still didn’t understand the history of the change, and even the few brief glimpses into what had happened all those years ago in Japan weren’t enough to let him figure it all out. Sid had become the agent of destruction on Earth, and Xavier had sent him back to Hell once before. “Now it’s my turn…” He finally found the black, custom-made cowboy boots in his closet. He pulled them on and slung the backpack over his shoulder. Jake made his way up the stairs to the rooftop. He left out the door and walked to the back of the building. There was no fire escape here, such requirements missed when the building was originally built. However, he grabbed hold of the steel ladder and climbed down the stories to the alley behind his home. Jake entered the code in the electronic keypad and moved into the garage. He grabbed an old, worn down baseball cap from the rack and put it on his head. Mounting his Iron Horse, he pushed out of the garage, closing it behind him. The rotten stench of ruined beer and vomit greeted him as he started the bike up and took off down the alley to Malcolm X Boulevard. The smells were repulsive to most, but they were home to him. Back in the city he loved, a brief respite from the world. He circled around and found a parking spot two blocks over from his apartment. Jake walked with his backpack still slung over his shoulder. He pulled out his cell phone to check the time. He needed to wait just a couple of more minutes. He snuck into the alleyway where his watchdogs waited for their pound of flesh. The dumpsters concealed his presence as he waited, watching the two men inside the sedan. “What is he doing up there?” Corey as he lit up another cigarette. He was nervous, feeling exposed and vulnerable out here in the open. The parking was legal, but two guys sitting in a car could attract all sorts of attention. Michael ignored the complaints. He worked with Corey because the Dark messiah had instructed him to, but he didn’t like the man. He was moody, impatient and too impertinent about the work for Michael’s liking. “I mean, we could at least have rented some space around here or something.” Corey continued complaining. “I know this is club land and all, but there’s some empty retail space here and there. How hard would that be?” Michael unscrewed the top of the water bottle. He took a long drink, before turning to his companion. “We do as we’re instructed. Why do you always have to complain? This isn’t exactly difficult work, you know? We just sit, watch and report back.” “Yeah, well I don’t like it!” Corey shot back with an annoyed look as he looked back up at the lit windows of the building. “Bet he’s got plasma and satellite.” “Yeah, and you don’t. Get over it.” Michael rolled his eyes, sick of the little man. There was a new tradition in Deep Ellum. With permission from the city, fireworks went off in the night every now and then. They weren’t the loud popping fireworks, just the bright lights without so much pop and screaming. They didn’t go high into the air, rather they were maintained on pinwheels that spun around on each corner on the roof of one clubs. Jake Hix pulled out the pistol with the silencer. Moments later, people on the streets stopped and looked up into the air. They stopped walking; watching the pinwheels rotating quickly as sparks flew off like some fireflies enjoying brief flights of freedom before dying out. In that moment, with the attention of the world elsewhere there was a terrible rash of violence on the alleyway. Small flashes of light and the sound of broken glass were the only indications of the violence. Those signs were hidden by the regular breaking of beer bottles, and the bright lights from the fireworks in the sky. The bodies wouldn’t be noticed for many hours to come because people didn’t tend to notice a parked car where no one could be seen, even when the windows were cracked and broken. He walked back down the alley, tossing the weapon into the gutter. A light rain began falling, not hard enough to stick around beyond the rise of the scorching Texas sun tomorrow. The rain would wash away a little of the heat for the evening, but it was only a temporary reprieve. The wide drops fell across his face as he headed back to his bike. Jake didn’t care much at all anymore. Feeling the cool water he thought of the postman’s saying. "Neither rain, nor hail, nor sleet, nor snow, nor heat of day, nor dark of night shall keep this carrier from the swift completion of his appointed rounds." His rounds involved delivering messages of a different kind. Shreveport The flight would be smooth and tranquil as always when he took a private jet like the one he relaxed in at the moment. He hadn’t been on one since Rome, and he didn’t think he would fly this way anymore because of the costs involved. The price was exorbitant, but he could afford it thanks to the family business. He hadn’t brought much with him for the trip. He had friends in Houston who could provide the needed items to ensure his family’s safety, but right now he was staring at a few of the things he held most dear. Alone on the road, when no one could see him, he would often stare at the pictures. The unknown nephew and niece Pitt had fathered without knowing were strangely dear to him. Along with that picture, he had a picture of his parents he had lost, and the larger picture of the entire extended family. Jake was often at a loss for words to explain what he was doing. Saying he was protecting his family was the easiest statement, but really it went beyond that. He saw the family’s past and the family’s future cut down by the monster. He wanted to end the Hix family line here and now, and be the last memory of the family on Earth. Jake found his inspiration to continue to fight against Sid in the photos. The captain announced their descent into Shreveport, so Jake put the pictures away. He couldn’t be sentimental at the moment. All of his reservations and uncertainties had to be set aside. He needed to lead the men down to Houston, and leadership could not be established on a foundation of fear. The landing was smooth and easy. The captain came out, “We’ll be ready for the next flight in ten minutes sir.” “Excellent. I’ll find our new passengers.” Jake stepped down the stairs into the humid air of Shreveport. He walked over to the small terminal. Stepping inside, he enjoyed the cold air rushing over his body. There was no one in the waiting room. He saw a phone behind a counter and moved to it. He dialed the cell phone/ “Hello?” “Stephen, it’s Jake. Where are you guys at?” Jake looked around nervously. Something about things didn’t seem right. “We’re here at the airport, but you never told us what gate. I’m over here at…” Jake walked around looking for a sign. “AJ8 is where I’m at. Come on over, and we’ll get moving here shortly.” “We’re on the way.” Stephen answered before hanging up. Jake knew the hour was late, but usually there was at least one person in a place like this, especially with the doors unlocked. He walked around to the other side of the wall where there were a few offices. They were empty as well, but one of the computers was turned on and there was a half typed email on the screen. Jake turned to head back to the main waiting area when he heard the front door open with the standard electronic ring. “That was fast…” Jake came near the end of the wall when he heard voices from the other side. “We just got the call. They seem to be heading this way.” “Good, we’ll take them just as they come in. They won’t know what hit them.” “What about Jake?” “We were told not to touch him. We can’t harm him.” “You believe that?” “I do as I’m told.” Jake knew what had happened. Whoever had been here was gone, either subdued or worse. These men weren’t coming for him, but they would try and kill Hawk and Stephen. Jake looked around for something to use. There weren’t any obvious weapons, but then the shiny metallic cube caught his eye. “I can make that work.” Jake grabbed both pieces and moved back to the end of the wall. They were still visiting about nothing at all. Trying to act calm and cool before they went to work. These weren’t professionals, just fanatics. The Big Dawg took a deep breath, and then stepped around the corner. The two men were standing around, relaxing because they assumed they still had a few minutes before any action would be going down. “Big mistake boys…” They both stiffened up as he approached. Jake was holding the stainless steel coffee maker by the power cord in his right hand and the glass coffee pot in his left. Jake threw the coffee, having boiled all day long into some horribly acidic concoction to tear stomach lining, into one man’s face. Jake welcomed the scream as the man dropped his pistol to grab hold of his face. The Big Dawg swung the glass pot into the other man’s face, shattering the glass as the man let out a quick whelp of pain. Jake dropped the handle of the pot, and swung the coffee maker overhead. The steel construction slammed into the top of the man’s head. He folded, falling down not unconscious but in a world where little blue birds circled his head. Twirling the coffee maker a couple of times, Jake twisted his hips as he slammed the coffee maker into the screaming man’s head. He was knocked sideways, but the open gash only increased his screaming. Pity was not something Jake had a great stock of these days. He let two stops to the face extinguish the screams for the moment. He turned back to see the man pushing himself up to a sitting position as he blinked his eyes while opening and closing his mouth slowly to reorient himself to the world. Jake grabbed the man’s long hair as he approached, and drove a swift series of strong knees into his cut face. The crunching was beautiful in its own way…it meant no one would be hurt. He let go, and the man fell down onto the thin carpet of the terminal. Jake took all the man’s weapons and sat down on the sofa against the wall as he waited on his friends to arrive. While he waited, he thought about everything about to come up in his professional life. While his personal life was in shambles, he had to maintain the brave exterior for the world. He had just finished Whiplash, and now he had a huge series coming up by entering the PTC’s Infinite Gauntlet. The competitors would be the cream of the crop from all of the strong federations around the globe. He had entered on the advice of his managers, and to bring some attention to Global. “Are you ready for this old man? You going to be Jake, or are you going to be the Big Dawg? Where’s my killer instinct? I can feel it there beneath the surface. I keep letting it out for the family…but why don’t I let it out for my career?” Jake’s ponderings were broken by the headlights coming into the parking lot. He saw the lights on the opposite wall as he was tucked into a corner, keeping one of the pistols handy in case it wasn’t his friends. The headlights could simply be some additional back up sent by Sid. He saw the slight limp as Hawk leaned on his cane and the worn out form of SSR as they approached the door. SSR held it open for Hawk, and they both went into action mode upon seeing the bodies. Jake remained seated as he spoke, “They won’t be any trouble now boys.” They turned to see Jake relaxing. They remained alert as Hawk took the lead, “What happened?” “A little greeting party for you two actually. I overheard when I realized no one was here.” Jake tossed the pistol aside and smoothed his hair back behind his ears, “We need to find whoever was working tonight. This is a fucking airport, and if they’re dead then you know the feds will be looking into a murder on airport property.” “I’ll look around.” Stephen took off. Hawk looked the two men over, “A coffee maker?” “It was good to the last drop.” Jake shook his head as he looked at the damage, “I may have screwed us here.” “We’ll handle it.” Hawk tried to calm him as he limped over to the couch. Jake’s doubt turned to concern, as he looked his friend up and down, “You’re limping a lot.” “Lot of walking lately.” Hawk sat down beside him on the sofa. “Looking forward to some down time in the air. I can lay out and stretch.” Jake couldn’t look his friend in the eyes. He locked his cerulean lenses on the blank wall, “I shouldn’t ask you guys for so much help.” “You didn’t ask. You’re the high profile man of the moment, and you’re the man who has to face Sid. This affects us all, and we’re happy to help. They were our family too Jake. We always said, family is about choice, not blood. You’re father took us all in, and we owe your family a lot.” Hawk gripped his cane a little tighter. The memory of the bodies was still just as fresh months later. They would stay with him for years. Stephen returned, “I found the girl working tonight. She’s alive, but shaken up. I’m going to talk to her to take care of these things, but those bodies have to go.” “We’ll put them in the trunk of the car, and I’ll arrange pick-up and disposal.” Hawk knew some people. “What are you going to tell her?” Jake looked up at Stephen. “We need clean ties back.” “These assholes weren’t very nice, and she’ll understand. Especially after a check arrives. I know what to do Jake. Hell, no one knows you were here technically because we flew you under an assumed name.” Stephen left again. Hawk put his cane down, “Once more in the breach.” “Exactly.” Jake had to laugh. Hawk always tried to be so damned proper in his way. They each grabbed one of the man. Jake left the lighter man to hawk in deference to his back. They carried the men out to the car, and deposited them in the trunk. They also collected their weapons, and headed back to the car. “We can’t have them screaming.” Hawk said flatly. His voice was so cold and emotionless Jake had no doubt what the man meant. “You go back to the plane. Stephen and I know what to do.” “Trunks leak. It has to be non-bloody.” Jake warned. Hawk didn’t smile or grimace. His face was painted as emotionless as his words, “I know. This isn’t my first dance Jake.” There was a barely perceptible nod between before Jake turned to head to the plane. You had friends because humans were social animals, in need of companionship. You had close friends because people needed to trust each other. You had family, brothers, because you never turned on one another. You were always there for them like they were there for you. Jake needed his family now. The rest of them would be in Houston. He had a family to rescue and a message to carry back to Sid.
