Questions

7 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-07 Steve Watson peeled off his mask as he quickly ducked out the back door of the arena. The sweat, still clinging to his bare torso, made the chill of the night feel more like the dead of winter than that of mid-Autumn. Steve looked around hastily, fighting back the sense panic trying to climb its way up from his gut and choke off his throat. The police had made there presence know just minutes before his match - Detective Smythe amongst them. Steve had recognized the detective’s voice as he headed to the Gorilla position when he overheard him conversing with a road agent about their need to question a few of the nights competitors in regards to a suspected "motor vehicle accident" last weekend. Apparently the police had made so little headway in finding a suspect on Forbes hit-and-run so far that they were figuratively chasing down every lead they could find in the hopes of blind ramming into something of substance. Steve knew that as "The Masked Marauder" he would be on their list. And he also knew that once Smyth got a look at his face behind the mask, all hell would break loose regardless on his complicity in the matter or not. So it was with some relief that Steve took in the agent’s insistence that any questioning must take place after the matches of the participants in question. At least that bought him a little time to figure things out. Thus forewarned and forearmed, Steve made his way through the curtain for his match against "The New Shit" Bobino. And, as he walked down the ramp and saw the two blue uniforms take their seats stoically by the announce table, he knew exactly why they were there – and it wasn’t just because they were die hard FUSE fans. No, they were there for him - waiting to escort him back to the locker rooms after the match to where Detective Smythe most assuredly awaited, praying for some kind of break in the case that had so far left him nothing but dead ends. And Steve did not intend to be that break. There was far to much more at stake here. Steve got lucky in how the match went down. No one in the arena expected it to end on a backslide reversal of an underhook suplex; least of all "The New Shit". And while Steve would normally never have considered himself the type to take advantage of a boot on the bottom rope, these were hardly normal circumstances. So Steve figured he’d find a way to reconcile himself with it. The three-count seemed to last forever; but, once the final slap to the mat hit, Steve immediately took evasive action. Without even waiting for his hand to be raised or the bell to be rung, he rolled out of the ring on the side away from the officers and - before they could even think to react - he had leapt the barrier and disappeared into the frenzied crowd. …………………………………………………………………. Steve shivered slightly in the crisp night air. He could feel the police locking down the building behind him. His only hope was that they wouldn’t realize that he was already out until he was safely back on the plane and in the air back to Colorado. But how was he going to get back to the airport - especially with his keys and his wallet sitting back with his clothes in the same locker room that was to have become his interrogation chamber. Steve stopped in his tracks as soon as this thought hit and he realized that his brief flight to freedom was in all likelihood just that. All his "secrets" – both real and those to be imagined by the cops – would come crashing back down upon him as soon as the police pried apart his locker. Steve cursed himself and his folly, as he searched for something – anything – that could bring him solace. Any brief glimmer of hope that life as he knew it was about to change drastically. He wondered almost aloud how prison food would impact the South Beach lifestyle he had become so accustomed to. It was then that the white limousine pulled up right in front of him. The door flew open. "Get in, Steve" Steve hesitated. "Quickly now. I have no intent of sticking around long enough to if the local law enforcement was the wits to connect my car’s plates with your mysterious escape." Steve peered into the darkness on the limo interior and saw Frank Hainesworth leaning out to beckon him in. In a snap choice that Steve was sure he would regret in short order, Steve decided that his boss was right now probably the lesser of the evils presented to him and slid into the vehicle with his corporate superior. "Here, put these on," Hainesworth said as he shove a pile of clothing at Steve. They were Steve clothes – the clothes from the locker – the exact clothes he had worn to the arena that night. "Frank, what are you…?" Steve started. "If anyone is entitled to be asking that question," Hainesworth replied, "I believe it would be me, Steve, rather than you." Steve flashed back to the previous night. He had arrived in Denver late in the evening and immediately checked into his hotel room at the Airport Hyatt. Then, after a quick call home to tell Elise he had arrived safely, he’d fired up the lap top to finish up a few emails before heading to bed. Given the exhaustive, full speed sprint that his weekend looked to be turning into; Steve felt a great urgency to make any headway he could into his investigation when he had even the slightest chance. As Steve opened his Outlook and logged into the companies Webmail, he pondered how he was going to make it all work. Between the Wassermans and GTT6 and the expense report discrepancies, he figured he was going to need to be awake at least 32 hours a day to make it through to Monday. But such preoccupations were quickly washed away when he saw a couple of rather odd looking emails in his queue. The first was "DemoUser1418"and appeared to have been sent directly from the Konica-Minolta Color MFP in the IT Hallway at Heckert, Bagley, & Dannon. "Odd", thought Steve, "I thought we had disabled the anonymous scan functionality on those machines when we installed them." And Steve thought it odder still when he read the text up the message. It was a simple request for him to shut off his pop-bocker as soon as he got the message. Then, without really knowing why - which was perhaps oddest of all - Steve shrugged and did so. The second email was entitled "A Funny Joke about The Manning Brothers" and had been sent from Lisa Somers in Marketing. Steve, as a rule, tended to delete most additions to Lisa’s endless stream of assorted jokes, chain letters, and prayer request forwards as soon as they hit his In-Box; but, given the recent connection of September’s Manning Bowl with his on-going expense investigation, Steve decided to open the email instead – the contents of which consisted of a single hyperlink that read "CLICK HERE" Against his better judgement, Steve clicked. And, as soon his Explorer window made the connection, the LCD Screen on his laptop started to fill up with pop-up ad after pop-up ad after pop-up ad; probably close to fifty-two in total. Cursing himself as much as Lisa, he rapidly started to click them shut. Good-bye, online casino. Sayonara, Free Computer. Au revoire, punchable monkey. And then one of them caught his eye. In fact, if he didn’t know any better the advertisement might have seemed to be addressing him directly. "Wait Steve! Slow Down! You need to read this!" He clicked it shut. But then the next one spoke to him again. "Steve. The Company Email Is Not Safe." Curious, he clicked this one shut as well. "They are watching you. They are watching all of us." Click. "Please enter a private email address in the below slot and click to send." After a moment’s consideration, Steve started to type in the Hotmail address that he’d been using for his communications with the GTT6 offices. Then thought better of it and instead decided to give the one he’d created for scheduling his rare indy bookings he might make a few of the less scrupulous "X-treme" style promoters. Heck, they paid fairly well and every so often it does a body good to have a fluorescent bulb cracked over its head. And if he was giving out info to some ingenious spammer, his garbage fed account was as good a place as any to collect the internet equivalent. He typed quickly and clicked to send. As soon as it processed, a new pop-up appeared. "I will be in touch soon, Steve. Thanks for everyting you are doing." Steve chuckled as he himself was not all that clear what it was he was doing that might be thankworthy. Click. "Now clear your history and your cache. Shut down your maching completely and go to sleep. You have a long week ahead of you." Steve did his best to do what the pop-up ads had suggested; but his night turned out to be quite fretful. So much was going on all at once. But Steve could handle it. He knew he could. Afterall, he had always been a truly splendid multi-tasker. The next morning, Steve checked his garbage hotmail as soon as he arose. But there was nothing there yet. So he headed out to the weekend Meet and Greet luncheon with the Wassermans, knowing that in all likelihood he would not get a chance to check again until late that night when he arrived back in Colorado from the tournament. "STEVE! Wake up! Did that ‘New Shit’ character drop you one to many times on your head in there tonight?" Hainesworth’s words snapped Steve back into the present. "Oh…Hey Frank. I am sorry," responded Steve, "I was just…" "I asked you a question, Steve. What the hell do you think are you doing here?" "To be quite honest, Frank," said Steve. "given the fact that you knew exactly where and when to find me tonight; I’d hazard that you know precisely the answer to that already." "Do you think it is fun to play games with me, Steve? Especially with all that is at stake right now." "What’s at stake? Look Frank, I am fully capable of both closing stuff up with the Wassermans and wrestling in some stupid tournament in my spare time," said Steve, "Granted, I didn’t expect to have to be fleeing from Inspector Gump in there…" "Look Steve, I sent you away to protect you," said Hainesworth, "Things are about to go down at HB&D that you don’t want to be a part of." Steve paused, Steve was sure this had to do with the September expense discrepancies and he wanted to gauge how much Frank might already know. "Really?" "Yes. Really?" "What kind of things?" "Things you have gotten a little too close to already. Things that certainly people don’t want to get out," answered Hainesworth. "Steve, I like you. And I can help. But I need you to lay low for a few days." "So I guess I can bother you for a ride back to the airport then? I see my tickets are still in my pocket." "I am not sure that Denver is truly safe anymore either, Steve. If I knew you came back here, I can promise you that they know it as well." Steve replied to this news with, "I should at least fly back to the Hyatt and get my luggage and stuff. My whole investigation is back there on my laptop." "Steve, if you have stuff still back in your hotel room, trust me, they have it in their hands by now." "So, Frank. Who is this mysterious and all knowing ‘They’ we keep talking about anyway?" " ‘They’ are the people who – whether you knew it or not – you were about to expose. People who have been doing certain thing in certain ways in for a very long time that they would rather not be caught at. They are powerful people who know how to create and keep that power." "Powerful people within the company?" asked Steve. "Yes. And without," came the sober response. "And you know who all of these people are, Frank?" "Steve. You need to stop asking these questions," said Frank. "You need to back away. Because if you don’t, there will be no way I can continue to protect you and your family." "My family?" "Yes. You. Your wife. Your unborn child…" Steve’s jaw dropped in shock. How could Hainesworth have known about Elise’s pregnancy? They had just found about it themselves a little over two weeks ago and had promised not even to tell their own parents until after the two of them had seen the first ultrasound and could show them pictures face to face. "Steve, please" said Hainesworth, "Let me just protect your family the way that I couldn’t protect mine." That was when it all finally started to make sense. Steve knew why Frank was helping him. Frank knew exactly what Steve had stumbled onto because he had stumbled onto it himself six years prior when he held the same position within the company that Steve held now. It all came flooding down upon Steve now as he remembered how Frank Hainesworth had lost his teenage soon in a suspicious car accident just one month prior to the day that his promotion to CFO. Only Steve now knew that is was no accident. "Oh my god, Frank," Steve blurted out with his realization, "I am so sorry." "Shit happens, Steve," said Frank bluntly. "And then you recover and try to make the best of it. And, in my case, the best of it consisted of a mid six figure raise and a gold plaque on my door. But, in your case, they are six years more deeply involved and I sort of doubt they will be feeling quite so generous. And that is why we need to get you into hiding some place safe post haste." "Hiding? Where? For how long?" "Just until things cool down," said Frank. "And exactly how long do you figure that could take? Days? Weeks?" asked Steve, "I am a numbers guy, Frank. I need some specifics here." "You should be back out in time for your next match, if that’s what you are asking," said Frank. "That’s not what I was asking, but…" "At least, I hope it’ll be cleared up by then. I’ll need to talk to a few people. Make sure calmer heads are prevailing. Assure them that you are no threat." "Why don’t we just go to the police?" "You’re pretty funny guy, Steve. I’ll make sure to forget to tell them you suggested that," Hainesworth snorted derisively. "But speaking of the police…" It was then that Steve noticed all the flashing lights outside of the limo. They had – after a short loop through downtown – arrived back at the arena. Detective Smyth must have called in half the force (or at least 10 additional squad cars) to assist in the search for the Mysterious Masked Marauder. "Why are we back here, Frank?" asked Steve. "Give me your mask, Steve," responded Frank. "Huh?" "Just give it to me. If you are going to trust me, you are going to have to trust me." Steve handed Hainesworth the Marauder mask and the older man immediate passed it up through the divider window to where the driver was sitting. "You know what to do with this," Hainesworth said to the driver. The driver nodded and quickly laced the mask up over his head. He then pulled off his shirt to reveal a muscled physique and slid out of the car. "Where is he…?" started Steve, until Hainesworth hushed him with a glance. The two of them watched as the driver snuck through the darkness towards the arena using a rather circuitous route before ducking behind a shrub. He then waited until he saw a back of officers with nearing before he jumped up with his hands in the air to turn himself in. "Money can buy lots of things, Steve," said Frank Hainesworth, "You remember that." "Noted and absorbed, Frank" "Now you climb on up into the front and drive us to the Super 8 motel on Columbia Avenue in Paramus, "said Hanesworth. "Your room is number 113. Make sure no one sees you and don’t go anywhere or talk to anyone until I come back to get you." "But what about Elise. You said she could be on danger, too." "Don’t worry. You already emailed her about an hour ago to tell here that things were getting a bit "nutsy" in Denver and suggested she take a long weekend to go see her folks - and perhaps tell them about the baby," said Haineworth. "She though it was a great idea and left about three hours ago." Steve slid into the driver’s seat and shifted the limo into gear. He couldn’t help feeling a bit like a coward as he steered the vehicle on to the main roads and made his way towards Paramus.
