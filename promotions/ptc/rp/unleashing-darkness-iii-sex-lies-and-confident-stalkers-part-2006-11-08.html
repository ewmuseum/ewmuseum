Witnesses stated he was ‘having an argument with thin air’ and further suggested that he’d ‘lost the plot’. Other, less articulate spectators, simply implied that he’d ‘gone mental.’ Not long into the diatribe, the police arrived and took McKail into custody, promptly releasing an official statement. ‘Jacob McKail has not been arrested. We have simply taken him into custody in order get him the help he needs.’ The question is, what kind of help does Jacob McKail actually need? Has he ‘lost the plot’ as some of the eye witnesses suggested? Will he make it out in time to face Andy M. in the first round of the GTT6? At this time, both Jacob McKail and GCW CEO Steven Caldera, have refused to comment on the matter. Read More >>> ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Psychiatrist. The Offices of Dr. Phillip Cassidy, PhD. The Bronx, New York. Today. "So Jacob," The psychiatrist began, removing his thickly brimmed spectacles. He blew on the lens’ and polished with a handkerchief. "Should we talk about what happened at Orchard Beech?" His name was Dr. Phillip Cassidy and he’d been needlessly appointed to Jacob McKail by the police after the incident at Orchard Beech. McKail’s attendance there was mandatory by law and thus far he’d found the entire affair a complete waste of time. Dr. Cassidy was middle-aged man, with greying jet black hair and each time McKail had seen him so far, he’d worn the same tweed jacket, with leather padded elbows. He looked more like a destitute high school teacher than a shrink. "No," McKail responded. He noted an old style Victorian clock, just above Cassidy’s left shoulder and promptly willed its hands to move faster. It was two fifteen and his hour was up at three. Fuck… The clock fit in perfectly with the majority of the room’s decor, finely carved wood filled the room and its walls, with the only exception being Dr. Cassidy’s patient chair which was coated in luxurious brown leather. The thing not only reeked of grandeur and opulence, but was just about the most comfortable chair McKail had ever lazed in. In fact, it was the one thing he looked forward to when coming in for an appointment -- he certainly was there for the conversation. "And why’s that?" Dr. Cassidy asked, his voice laced with soothing undertones. The effects were next to useless on McKail. "Because it’s none of your damn business." Dr. Cassidy sighed. "Now Jacob we’ve been over this. I’m here to help you. If you don’t talk to me, I can’t do that. I‘m--" "I neither want nor need your help," McKail interrupted, his eyes still fixed upon the clock. Two seventeen. Faster, damn you! Dr. Cassidy smiled. "Yes. But that’s not what the police seem to think. Need I remind you, you’re in my care at my discretion. If I don’t think you’ve made sufficient progress in the next few weeks, then I’ll see to it your time with me will be extended." McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. "You can keep me here as long as you like? Bullshi--" "Enforced by the law," Dr. Cassidy interrupted, with what McKail took to be a smug grin. "It’s within your best interest to cooperate with me, Jacob." McKail sighed and remained silent, defeated. "But if you don’t want to talk about Orchard Beech, then I‘m not going to force the issue." Dr. Cassidy paused and scribbled something down upon his notepad diligently. McKail couldn’t see what. "Should we now move on to what happened at the police station?" McKail shook his head. "No." Two nineteen. Hurry the fuck up! Dr. Cassidy sighed. "Well, that only leaves one issue to discuss." He paused and observed McKail intently. "Mary." McKail’s eyes widened and his mind raced. How the hell does he know about Mary? McKail hadn’t mentioned anything and the police didn’t even know about her. Dr. Cassidy noted the confused etched upon his face and smiled. "After our first session, my interest was peaked, so I obtained the notes made by Dr. Flannigan." McKail winced at the very sound of her name. Dr. Flannigan? Fuck… "I believe she was the psychiatrist assigned to you after the death of your wife?" "I sure as hell don’t want to talk about her," McKail told him. Two twenty-two. Oh, come on… "Well, what would you like to talk about?" Dr. Cassidy asked. McKail smiled. "My stalker." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Horn: "Information Leak?" Read More >>> ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Confident Stalker. Some Motel Room. Dallas, Texas. Two Weeks Ago. McKail lay on his bed and glared up at the ceiling. It was another sleepless night for him and lately, they were becoming more and more frequent. It’d been six months since the death of his wife and he still found it difficult to sleep without Mary lying next to him. Instead of sleeping, he allowed the reassuring certainty of the rain pounding down upon the thin roof of the cheap motel to wash over him soothingly. However, the breathtaking dance of each droplet was inexcusably interrupted whenever the residue water clustered together, built up to unmanageable levels and then fell downward, pinging against the motel window at random intervals in the most irritating fashion. McKail was preparing himself for the next ping, when there was a knock on the door. McKail frowned and sat up in his bed. Who the fuck…? McKail stood and wandered over toward the window, peering out of the curtains at the door. He couldn’t see a damned thing. Who the fuck could this be? It’s the middle of the night and nobody even knows I’m staying here. So help me god, those bastard’s at GCW Headquarters better not have leaked my whereabouts again! He stormed over towards the door and opened it violently, just as the perpetrator’s delicate hand was about knock for a second time. McKail glared at her and sighed. It was the confident stalker. At least, that’s what he called her -- he never did catch her name the last time. Her face lit up with a smile that could’ve illuminated the darkest of hearts and McKail fought back a grin of his own. "Jacob." She brushed past him gently and sat on the edge of his bed, leaning back gracefully, desperately trying to mask an overwhelming yawn. She’d shown up at his motel room in much the same fashion a couple of months back and took his bed for the night. It didn’t concern McKail too much at the time because he’d received a serious head injury earlier in the night and had spent the majority of the evening conversing with a figment of his imagination as well as sousing himself with bourbon. When he awoke the next morning, she’d gone and he hadn’t seen her since. "Umm, hello again, I guess," McKail responded, unsure of what to say. "You don’t remember me, do you?" She asked. "I remember," he told her, closing the door. "I just never caught your name, is all." "I’m so fucking stupid," she muttered, blushing. She stood and held out her hand. He took it and shook. "My name’s Kate." "Hello, Kate," McKail greeted. He fought back a smile for the second time and then glared at her with all the sternness he could muster under the circumstances. "How the hell did you find me?" "I was at the show tonight." She was referring to GCW WorldWide Forty-Four and McKail winced at the very thought of it. He’d snapped and assaulted the referee --not one of his proudest moments. "After the show I hit a bar and then came to the nearest motel. I recognised your car parked outside from the last time our path’s crossed and decided to see how you were." McKail nodded in response. He was forever the sceptic, but the story seemed plausible enough to him. The last time he’d seen her was in Kentucky and she’d told him then that she toured the GCW shows whenever she could. God knows how she paid for it all. "So I see you’re not drunk this time," she noted, studying him carefully. He smiled and sat in a chair across the way from her. "Been sober for a while now. Perhaps too long." "I don’t know," she said, "you seem a lot more lucid than last time around. You had the opportunity to jump me back then and you turned it down." She let the sentence hang in the air for a moment and developed a seductive smile. "You going to let that happen this time around?" McKail’s eyes widened and he sat back in his chair. "You’re just about as direct as I remember." "And you’re just about evasive as I remember," she told him. "What’s the matter? Am I repulsive to your eyes? Or are you some sort of fruit?" "No," McKail answered her, firmly. "It’s complicated, is all." Kate sighed. "Okay. Whatever. But can I at least stay the night? Seems a waste of money for me to get another room…" McKail sighed. "I guess." Kate’s face illuminated again and she lay back in the bed, gently patting the vacant space right next to her. McKail shot her an hesitant look and remained still. She scowled at him. "I’m not going to jump you, okay? Sheesh. I’m direct, but I’m not that direct." McKail still had reservations about that, but removed himself from the chair and implanted himself beside her regardless. She draped her arms around him and he became soothed almost immediately. That night, he had the best sleep he’d had in a long while. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Horn: "McKail Saddened To Rage?" McKail glared at him questioningly and shook his head, words failing him. Dr. Cassidy didn’t wait for an answer and continued regardless, "And why do you think that was?" "What the fuck has that got to do with anything?" McKail responded, eyes wild with rage. "I think the question holds relevance, Jacob. I assume you have no attachments now and haven’t since your wife died. A girl offered herself to you on a plate and you declined that offer," Dr. Cassidy explained, tapping his pen against his notepad tediously. "Why do you think that was?" "I don’t fucking know. I just didn’t feel like it," McKail explained, his voice adopting a noticeably lower tone. "You didn’t feel like it?" Dr. Cassidy asked, with a raised eyebrow. "Next you’ll be telling me you had a headache." McKail sighed. "It’s complicated, is all." "I’m sure it is," Dr. Cassidy agreed. "I think it’s complicated because you haven’t gotten over the death of your wife yet, Jacob. I think you’re clinging on to her memory for dear life and you’re refusing to let go." "That’s bullshit," McKail disagreed, even though deep down he knew Dr. Cassidy was correct. "I don’t think it is bullshit," Dr. Cassidy told him. "I think I’m right. I think you’re having trouble letting go." He leaned forward in his chair and attempted to make eye contact with McKail, but McKail turned his gaze downward, once again defeated. "You need to move on, Jacob. Can’t you see that?" "I don’t want to see it, Cassidy and I certainly don’t want to discuss it." Whilst Dr. Cassidy scribbled his notes down upon his notepad, McKail caught a glimpse at the Victorian clock again. Two forty. Twenty minutes to go? Fuck… "Jacob we’re making real progress here I--" "No," McKail interrupted. "We’re not going to explore this issue any further." Dr. Cassidy sighed. "You’re going to have to talk about it someday, I--" "Doubtful," McKail snapped. Dr. Cassidy removed his glasses, rubbed his eyes and sighed. He’d hit a brick wall and he knew it. "Okay. Perhaps you should just finish you’re story then." McKail nodded gratefully. Anything to change the subject. To Be Continued…
