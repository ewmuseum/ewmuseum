Stand The Fuck Up. (A Back Alley Brawler Noir)

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 "Another….keep em comin’ till that twenty is no good." The Global Title Tournament. 128 wrestlers, legends and unknowns alike, were preparing themselves for the grueling battle that was set to come. Whether the previous 4 winners were respected or loathed, the path to being called the ultimate victor was never an easy task….a task that required a tempered body and a sound mind. Some were living in nearby gyms, turning flesh into steel to prepare for the fight of their careers. Others had become reclusive, having no need for the company of people and their ability to eat at your mind with scathing words. 128 competitors with confidence in their hearts…all wanting the same dream….the same glory and willing to pay the price with their bodies to be called "champion". While 127 wrestlers knew in their souls that victory could be theirs, one could only wallow in his own self-doubt. He had been in O’Malley’s since 10 o’clock that morning and only left his bar stool long enough to take a piss and come back. Shots of Jack Daniels were his only sustenance since that dreary 10 o’clock morning, the time now 7 o’clock as he knocked back another burning shot, listening to the erratic melody of rain slamming on the roof. While monks had their temples to collect their thoughts and reflect on the outside world; the Back Alley Brawler had the bars of the Bronx to do the same. The smattering haze of Marlboro, marijuana, and Newport smoke slowly slithering into his lungs. The shady talks at the booths, ranging from crack deals to meeting places for a night of pure lust. The Brawler’s ears had become trained to pick up these talks, often his only source of entertainment at the bar…unless he was fighting. The songs like "Hotel California", "Margaritaville", and any Johnny Cash song (R.I.P.) that made for perfect companions to finish your bottle of choice. And right now, he would need the bar more than ever. The proud New Yorker refused to leave its doors until he figured out the question that brought him there. And no, it wasn’t if he could win the tournament. No, it was if he could even finish the first match without collapsing. "Another Joe?" The Brawler looked up, his hardened face scrunching up so he could focus on Glenn, the owner and bartender of O’Malley’s for nearly as long Joe had been wrestling…which was safe to say a long time. Glenn was the perfect bartender, knowing when to cut off the liquid courage before you made stupid decisions and having a face too kind for a bar setting. Joe fought his slurred sight long enough to take notice of Glenn’s snow white whiskers and pronounced wrinkles, flashing a smile that it was him and not God he was staring at. The Brawler may have been in a confused state, but he wasn’t ready to see his maker yet. "Yea…anotha. Keep it goin." It had been 3 months since his knee injury in Global Wrestling, a mere day after he won his first major title, the Global Wrestling TV title. A mid-card title to some, it was the highest achievement the 40-year old Brawler had ever claimed in his 20 year career…and it was snatched from him because his body couldn’t take it, it failed him when he needed it the most. Months of rehab and a knee brace later, obnoxiously protruding underneath the right knee of his Dickies work pants like a tumor, the Bronx Barbarian had come to this bar wondering if he could still handle the pressure. He was by no means a Greek Adonis BEFORE the knee injury, but the fact that he was only walking due to semi-mechanical aid did no settle his worried soul. -clink- He looked up and saw another glass of his beloved Jack Daniels before him, Glenn looking to the Brawler with a hint of concern in his aqua eyes now. The Brawler attempted to wrap his huge paws around the drink, but Glenn’s frail hand refused to relinquish the grasp on it. First he needed answers. "Joe…you’ve had ya days. I remember when Karen left ya and took the kid, ya went on a 24 hour drunk like I ain’t neva seen. Shit, I remember letting you sleep in the restroom, bro. I just don’t wanna see you like that again and you’re doin it now. What’s wrong? Is this about your knee? The tournament?" Glenn only served to remind the Brawler about the other casualties in his long life of painful memories. All the drinking made his visions clear of the sterile hospital room he called home for months, unable to move and relying on the aid of crutches and walkers to get him by. A simple skill people take for granted, walking, the Brawler had to earn all over again through weeks of rehabilitation. Every step was a knife grinding into his muscles and bones, but he fought the pain and was able to stand on his own two feet…. ….That should have been where he quit. He gained a major title. He main-evented a Jolt pay-per-view. He proved to the world that he something left to offer at his age and shape. It was the time to walk away and rebuild his life with Karen and his little girl, Carmen. Then it came…. ….The very same envelope he used as a coaster for his drinks since he came to the bar, the Brawler valuing the hardwood counters more. The slick, Photoshopped blue and white envelope with the letters "GTT" on it and "Back Alley Brawler" imprinted in prim and proper lettering just below it. Just when he thought he was out, those motherfuckers found a reason to pull him right back in. He lifted his current drink, melted ice being the only remnant of the previously served drink, and picked up the invitation. It brandished a wet "O" in the center where his drink rested, the Brawler glaring at it like it had called his little girl a whore. "Ya see dis’ Glenn. Dis’ is whats got me fucked up, bro. Afta I hurt mah knee, I wanted to just say da hell wit it….I did what I said I would and then sum, I coulda just stopped. Then those FUCKIN’ MONKEYS gotta send me dis’. They know my decre….decrep….BROKE DOWN OL’ ASS CAIN’T RESIST. If I walk away…then I’m admittin’ I can’t handle da heat…." "And if ya stay, you’re afraid you’ll get hurt worse." Silence befell the two, the other patrons in the bar not even mattering at that point. They both shared a awkward stare at one another, the Brawler begrudgingly nodding. "Let dat drink go Glenn…I need it." "No you don’t. You need to make a choice and stop tryin’ to drown out reality Joe. Stop running away…" "Glenn, I’ma tell you one mo’ time…getcha hand off dat fuckin’ drink." He had seen it before, but not to this degree. Glenn could see in the Brawler’s pain-soaked eyes that he was shackled to the sauce. It was his excuse to forget about the tough choices in his life instead of facing them. Joe was more than just a loyal patron to Glenn, he was family. And family doesn’t watch family destroy themselves. "If you want this drink, you’re gonna have to knock me out Joe. But I’m not going to have you taking the bitch way out about this. You be a fuckin’ man and decide right here right now, are you going to walk away or fight one more time?" "I don’t wanna think, I wanna drink you rotten bastad(bastard)! GIVE ME DA FUCKIN’ JACK!" Glenn, 15 years older then his "younger" protégé sitting at his bar, displayed the courage of a Spartan warrior as he looked into the eyes of the 6’8, 330 pound Back Alley Brawler. Fear was not an option, saving his friend was. "Make me." The Brawler raised up from his stool, he and Glenn still met at the hand for supremacy over the drink and most of all…Joe’s very will. The air turned thick between the two, even the patrons looking on and whispering amongst themselves. They saw the storm coming and awaited the consequences that would follow. "Yous got 3 secons’(seconds), Glenn. 1…2…: "3. Do what you’re gonna do Joe." The Brawler took in a deep breath, his drunken state making the air hard to intake as he raised his fist sluggishly and made a swing for Glenn’s face. Glenn’s heart felt heavy, knowing what he had to do. A blind man could see the swing coming, Glenn ducking as the meaty right hand that had crushed many a jaw passed over him with room to spare. He then followed by taking the drink the two of them were in a stalemate for and throwing it’s contents in the face of the Brawler. The potent drink burned his eyes, causing the behemoth to stumble back…it’s then that he felt it. He never had known just how heavy he was until his right knee buckled, the brace not enough to support him as he reached out for anything to keep him standing, but finding nothing. Joe knew what was to follow and could only brace himself as he was now just a drunken, ebony avalanche set to crash to the hardwood floor. Even if anyone wanted to help, there was no time, his fall so sudden as he toppled backwards and slammed his head into a table on the way down with sick thud. He could feel the crimson trickling from the back of his head before ultimately slamming back first on the floor, a jolt of searing pain racing through his body. His hand reached up to the heavens, outstretched and open. "Hel….help…hel…." He could hear the creaks of a few chairs, a patron or two possibly willing to aid him to his feet. His aid was cut off abruptly with the powerful voice of Glenn, everyone knowing his word was law in O’Malley’s. "Nobody help him up. He’s only got himself to blame for this. If he gets up, he gets up on his own." He was right back to the feeling he had 3 months ago, a man of limitless power and too helpless to make any good on it. The proud New Yorker had fallen and couldn’t get up. It was funny in the commercials where the elderly lady was unable to make it to her feet, but he didn’t realize how crippling that feeling was until now. Glenn came around from the bar area, now standing over the Brawler with the sleeves rolled up on his Polo shirt, reeking of Courvoisier and Newport smoke. No matter how condescending He may have seemed, his face was one of serious concern for his comrade. "You may have hurt your knee 3 months ago, but you’ve been on crutches your entire life Joe. You used wrestling to explain why you have no family. You used the fortunes of others who made good on their chances to be a bitter sack of shit. And now you’re using the sauce and my bar to keep from choosing between family or wrestling, when you know you can’t have both!" The Brawler reached for the table he had struck on the way down to pull himself up, but Glenn easily walked over and kicked it from his reach. The table toppled over in a heap by the 7 o’clock crowd of construction workers that always frequented the bar, among the dozens of spectators watching this sad sight. "YOU WON’T USE ME TO AVOID YOUR PROBLEMS, JOE. And you won’t use anything else to pick you up this time. You’re going to pick your ass up and prove you’re a man, Joe. If you can’t do that….you don’t deserve a family or a career." Even when his daughter was born, the Brawler never had an urge to cry the way he did at that moment. If he had nothing else, he always had his manhood and now it was in question over a simple task like standing on your own two feet. "You think you’re going to beat anyone in that tourney on your back, bro? You love your family, but you want that tourney….if not, you would have tossed that invitation when you got it, not sulk over it. You want to prove you got what it takes? Prove you’re worth a damn? Peel your lard ass off my floor and stand up! Get up!" It was becoming sickening to watch, the patrons having to witness a 40-year old man unable to reach his feet. Like a child begging to be put on his feet by a negligent parent. Glenn noticed a random patron looking to help Joe up, but an icy glare was all that was needed to put him back in his place. "Anyone that helps him is BANNED you hear me?" Joe gritted his teeth, thinking to all the times he was knocked down and thought he could never get up. He survived four failed marriages, only one that really mattered. 20 years of "never-was" status and terrible career decisions. Some of the most sickening matches and bumps known to man. It would not be standing to his feet that would finally beat him. He could feel his knee threatening to buckle, but this was his manhood…his dignity…his respect that was on the line. Glenn was right…he wanted in the Global Title Tournament…and he wanted to win. But you can’t win drowning yourself in sorrows and Jack Daniels. The man who entered this bar was not the gladiator who pulled out the Global title victory months ago, he was a confused man scared of his destiny. Reaching one knee, he realized that he could show no weakness no matter how broken down he was, locking eyes with Glenn as he began to ascend to his feet. "Do…me….a fava…Glenn.." The bar began to crack into smiles, some begrudgingly but smiles nonetheless. Just getting to his feet was like a 20 mile run for the Brawler, but he could not stop until he had reached his feet and towered over the bar once again. He wanted to fall back to his back, but looking in Glenn’s intent eyes, he couldn’t let him down like that….he couldn’t let himself down like that. With one burst of energy, he reached his feet, lunging for the counter and grabbing a hold of it desperately. Glenn went to him, smiling and patting him on his back as the Brawler heaved heavily. Taking the invitation in hand and looking back to his elderly friend, only one thing could be said. "Help me get ready fo’ dis’ shit. Bum knee or not, I gotta show em dey can’t fuck wit da Bronx." Glenn smiled, the patrons smiled. He may have not been the spring chicken he once was….but he still had the spirit of the Bronx Barbarian of old. For some, the tourney was about titles. For The Brawler…it was about walking into battle and still being able to do just that. Walk out.
