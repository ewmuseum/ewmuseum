The College Chronicles #23: 'Knucks'

20 March 2005

Date: 2005-03-20 14th March 2005 12:09 It had been a hectic day. Dashing through an extraordinary Albany rainstorm, desperate for the sanctity of my halls room and a nice warm shower, only to be intercepted but a hundred yards short of my goal by the girl of my dreams, Kate Barnard, in a real state. The history of our relationship? To be honest, we hadn’t had much of one. My crush on Kate was really no more than the daft word used to describe it would imply, yet at times it had meant more to me than the feel of the warm sun on my face and the cool relief of breathing in the life-giver that is oxygen. You might turn your nose up at this – well, go on. Sneer all you will. For although it seems self-indulgent to compare something so basic as my affections towards a girl to two of the most romantic and wonderful feelings life in this world has to offer, I must get over to you that this was genuinely more than an adolescent crush. To start with, there was the basic factor of time. Two years, closing in on three, is a heck of a lot longer than the time-span given to your usual whimsical teenage fantasy that burns red-hot for a couple of months then dies totally as the realisation of ideas above your station sets in. Then, I must call as evidence the precise nature of my feelings. My love – can I call it that? Who knows? – for Kate was not based on the ‘wow’ factor. Although, in my opinion, Ms Barnard was stunning, this realisation would come slowly for most, as indeed it had for me. There was a difference between the galvanic effect on entry to a crowded room that someone slim and pretty and blonde and showing plenty of skin and wearing plenty of make-up would have on the focus of people’s attentions, and the more slow-burning effect Kate’s entry would induce. Kate’s beauty was less noticed; she could fade away into the corner of a room unless called to the spotlight; this was why I prized my feelings for her so much. Kate was my superstar, my catwalk model, my drop-dead gorgeous object of my lust. Yet that final description is inappropriate. Whilst many of Harbrook’s male population did indeed treat the opposite sex as objects, I respected Kate as a person and not just a pair of breasts. She was quiet – she wasn’t one of the way-too-cool-for-you cheerleading crowd – she dressed sensibly and was one of those rare female specimens whose private lives had not been put on show at some time or other. In this way, Kate did not find herself as one of those ogled over by the football players. Instead, she was friendly with most and generally known as a pleasant personality, her only failing being her complete inability to comprehend jokes and puns. Kate was conservative, nice, smiley, approachable. Nevertheless, I’d resisted that approach-urge for so long. Meeting new people was certainly not my forté, and the hypothesis ‘the longer you leave it, the harder it’ll be’ has definitely been proven correct on my evidence. If only, on her first day at Green Hill High, I’d been brave enough to go up to her and introduce myself. We might have become friends... more than that... lovers. True to form, I’d kept my mouth shut for years. In retrospect, I guess she must have construed something from constantly catching me watching her, but to her credit, she’d never looked down on me with scorn or turned away disdainfully as our eyes had met. Because she was a nice girl. Such a rare thing to find. And once such a treasure has been identified, it is hell to let it go. But let her go, I’d had to, albeit after a shining beacon of hope had punched a Kate-shaped hole in the monotony of my daily life. It hurts to bring this back; at the same time, it helps. This all seems like it had happened so long ago, but not even four months had passed since that fateful night. It had all started when Jake Scott – in summary, a former jock exposed as an emo/wrestling geek and rejected from the ‘cool crowd’ – had befriended me (to a rigidly-defined point; you might find this difficult to believe but it was I rather than him who had limited the development of our friendship; he could be downright annoying) and invited me to a little party in his room in Main Halls East. This had been a big step for me: a party? Tim Shipley? What have you been smoking? Back then in November, my mind had been confused beyond imagination. Artemis, a Core Wrestling diva-turned-manager, had taken me on in more ways than one. Later, I was to find out she’d been simply using me as a client to prolong her TV exposure, and in hindsight it is incredible that I had not guessed this earlier. Although there had been doubts, there had been suspicions, I’d never been strong enough to flat-out ask her what the hell she was doing teasing and torturing a 19-year-old virgin freshman when nearly half of the United States dreamed about her nightly. Without Artemis’ interference, I might have dealt with the whole Sammie situation better. Sam Farrell – a freshman at Harbrook, just like me – was a love interest at the time; I still pursued her affections today despite a very recent rejection whose sting had not yet receded. But that’s enough about now – it’s what our relationship was like back in November that you need to know about, and I’ll do my best to explain. Infrequent, nervous and short meetings since we’d first met in early September had left me on tenterhooks as to what to expect from this girl I barely knew. What I did know was that finally I’d set my sights on something potentially achievable. “Kate could wait” was how I’d put it at the time, and that indeed was the manner in which I’d approached this potential relationship. To someone not knowing the full background, this might come across as somewhat harsh, but I hadn’t been about to give up two years of hoping and waiting and dreaming and dreaming and dreaming, all for a new interest with whom a relationship might or might not work out. I’d done my best to handle it all, but really I’d been floundering; a beetle on its back with its legs waving uselessly in the air. Flustered as Sammie had commented on our similar choice of sandwiches at our first lunch together in the canteen. Hopeful as Artemis had run her manicured finger across my chin on our first meeting. Powerless as my manager had pinioned me on my bed and removed her top. I’d never had a girlfriend before Artemis, and she’d hardly been the conventional example of a female partner for a teenage boy. Forward, sensual, experienced, always wanting to hurry things – I’d never really understood her intentions. Had she felt a rush with the prospect of taking the virginity of an innocent boy (young man, even) who knew no better? Had she enjoyed her dominance and superiority in every situation we’d encountered during our short time as a couple? Had it been a fancy of hers to pull the wool over the eyes of a pathetic, bumbling Englishman, out of place and out of mind in America? To cut a long story short, Sam had walked into my room just as Artemis and I had been ready to consummate our pathetic illusion of a relationship. Getting the wrong end of the stick, she’d assumed Artemis to be a prostitute, and it had taken no end of assurances and pledges to calm her down. My revelation to her that I was a professional wrestler had sent her into an almost scary state of mind – I was clearly a liar! – and her flailing fists had landed many a blow on my rapidly bruising chest before a genuine video of me in action on PRIDE 6 had turned everything around in her muddled head and led to a perfect, glorious kiss between the two of us. Unfortunately, it hadn’t taken her long to realise that there was still half the story she didn’t know, and it seemed as if she hadn’t even wanted to know it as she and her remarkably annoying room-mate, Maddy, had forced me to leave. That had been November 5th; nearly two months had passed before we’d spoken again. So I’m sure you can imagine that two weeks after that night of revelations and uncertainty (virginity still intact), my mind had still been in such a muddle that I had been barely in control of my thoughts and actions at Jake’s party on the 20th. Adam Brent, a long-time foe and the captain of the football team, had suddenly taken offence to my being employed by Core Wrestling – perhaps a touch of jealousy? One will never know, as one (er, me) will never dare ask. A fight had ensued – encouraged, I am ashamed to admit, by my sudden rush of blood to the head. It had been idiotic to lower myself to the most primal of levels, yet somewhat unpredictably, the fight had finished pretty evenly. After landing four consecutive blows to the bridge of his nose, I’d stood up to signal an end to the brawl, but the cowardly Brent had risen up after me and delivered a devastating uppercut to the underside of my jaw. I’d gone down in a heap and made no effort to get up. The next thing I’d known, the person kneeling over me had been none other than Kate. Kate Barnard. The girl I loved hopelessly. The girl I’d never properly spoken to. She’d really been there: leaning over me, touching my head, telling me how brave I had been and how Brent was such a bully. But best of all, she’d kissed me. A moment of madness. The actions of alcohol. However you justify it... whatever you put it down to... it had happened, and that was that. Naturally, I’d wanted more, but naturally, I’d been too petrified to even go near her following that particular episode. When we had finally broached the subject, over a month later on Christmas Day, it had predictably been Kate who had initiated the conversation – but not at Harbrook; a chance meeting outside Spearman Heights, the apartment block where my parents lived. It had turned out that Kate’s parents were inhabiting an apartment in a block just down the road at Astoria. Away from the crowded corridors of Harbrook College, away from the gossiping rabble, Kate had finally been ready to talk. And full credit to her: she’d let me down gently. We had, as everyone does, pledged to be friends, but of course that hadn’t really happened; although I felt I knew her better than anyone in the world, the fact still remained that I’d only ever spoken to her on a few disjointed occasions. The worst of all – the killer blow to my misguided disillusions – had come at my very own birthday party, on February 5th, the day before my birthday proper. Kate had got together with none other than my good friend and tag team partner, Tony Aliso. Although it hadn’t been as serious as I’d initially thought – the assumption that they’d slept together in my parents’ bed had turned out to be false (well, technically it was true; they had slept together, but it had gone no further than the literal meaning would imply) – I was still not entirely comfortable that Tony, within hours of meeting the girl I’d adored for years, had managed to charm her to the extent that she’d been quite willing to kiss and cuddle up with him as they’d slept off the night’s inebriation. So, some history, huh? That’s not the half of it, but it’ll do just for now, because what I am about to explain is very serious and could be a matter for urgency. Earlier today – twenty-five minutes earlier, if you wish to be precise – Kate had come to me looking despondent. After no end of sensitive yet guided probing, I’d managed to extract two vital facts from her: one, it seemed that Tony feared for his life; two, something or someone by the name of ‘Knucks’ had something to do with it. What (or whom) this Knucks was, I couldn’t imagine. I’d run it through Google already to find a diverse selection: -A game involving two children cutting up each other’s knuckles with the use of a flicked coin or marble -An online superstore for pet supplies -Various definitions from online dictionaries -A member of some cryptic discussion forum going by the username ‘Knucks’ -A poem by Carl Sandburg -A chain of massage parlours Correct me if I’m wrong, but to me, none of these seemed particularly frightening. Tony Aliso was not one to cry wolf. A hardened man formerly of the LAPD, he knew the distinction between the trivial and the dangerous. If he felt threatened by something, then this was serious. But so far, all I’d drawn were (surely) blanks. I heard a rattle at the door. Presently, Dr Kasidy Drake entered the room, clad in a knitted maroon jumper and well-worn jeans, ducking to avoid contact with the top of the doorframe. “Hi, Kasidy,” I murmured, still distracted with worry for Tony. “Hey,” he breathed as he sat down heavily on his mattress. Kasidy had been rooming with me for a while; I wasn’t really sure why, and it was quite a risk to flout the regulations at such a disciplinarian institution, but I didn’t really see any way the authorities could find out. No-one ever saw him enter or exit my room (and of course no-one ever visited my room except the two of us; that goes without saying). Most students just assumed he was some kind of caretaker. It’s convenient how young people are usually so preoccupied in their own dilemmas that they fail to question the validity of what goes on around them – a flaw in the juvenile mindset that Kasidy and I were exploiting fully. He looked exhausted. I did worry about just what he got up to when I wasn’t around; although he was nominally researching for a thesis, I never saw him do anything of the sort. As far as I knew, he hadn’t any papers or working equipment with him whatsoever. And all too often, he would return to the room looking drained of all his energy, as if he’d just run a cross-country race or taken an exam. However, the very flaw in students that we were exploiting was just as, if not more so, valid for myself. I had a whole lot to worry about, and I wasn’t going to add Kasidy to that long list at the expense of some of the more important items, like my education. He didn’t seem to be in any immediate danger – Tony, on the other hand, certainly did. “Kasidy,” I began. The PhD holder’s eyes met mine. I didn’t think he would know anything – he and Tony weren’t at all close, but they did have connections through Core Wrestling, for which Kasidy was an on-and-off backstage interviewer, and so he might know something. There might also be the unrelated chance that he had encountered some kind of organisation or person (or maybe even a place?) going by the name of ‘Knucks’. “Has, er – has Tony said anything – strange – at all to you – recently?” With a frown crossing his features, Kasidy shook his head slowly. “Okay, forget that,” I said. “Also, have you heard of anything – anyone – anywhere – called ‘Knucks’?” Again, Kasidy shook his head. I sighed with disappointment and massaged the back of my neck as I cradled my head in my hands. ”What’s going on?” he asked slowly. I shook my head and sighed again. “I can’t explain it... I don’t even know myself,” I told him in despair. “But Tony’s in some kind of trouble and he needs help. “Oh...” Kasidy intoned uselessly. “Where’s PRIDE on Wednesday?” I asked suddenly, my head jerking up. “The Coliseum, Greensboro, North Carolina,” Kasidy promptly recited from memory. I smiled. He certainly had his uses. “Can you do me a favour?” “Sure,” he replied doubtfully. “Phone Core for me and get them to change my flight. I need to get to Greensboro right now,” I told him hurriedly, already searching in the wardrobe for my suitcase. “What!” he stood up. “Why?” I looked over at him and snapped heatedly: “Tony’s in trouble! For Christ’s sake, he’s worried about being killed! He’ll be in Greensboro later today – he always flies out early. I need to talk to him soon – before it’s too late...” I choked and buried my head in the wardrobe. I was putting a hell of a lot of faith in Tony getting to Greensboro a day early. Little did I know it, but right then, due to what was going on in a certain airport on the other side of the country, it didn’t look as if Tony would make that flight at all... “Fine.” Kasidy held up his hands, somewhat miffed at my irritable response. I sighed. “I’m sorry. I’m just a bit – short-tempered right now. But I really need you to make that call so I can start packing. It could be a matter of life and death.” Satisfied that Kasidy would finally have got the message, I furiously pulled my case out of the wardrobe and threw it open. Sensing Kasidy still staring at me, I whirled around to glare at him. “Life and death!” “Okay, okay,” said Kasidy, jolted into action. He must have hit the speakerphone button by mistake, because our small semblance of a scuffle was halted abruptly as a loud dialling tone echoed throughout the room. The two of us froze, and waited for Kate to pick up. Hope against hope against hope combated fear, unease, anxiety. The phone rang again. And again. And again.
