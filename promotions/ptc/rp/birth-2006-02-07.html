Birth

7 February 2006

Date: 2006-02-07 "SEEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEY-MOUR!" Most people look at Japan as an other. After all, Japanese culture is radically different from our own. The language is different, the food is different, and the customs are different. It is that same culture though, that ‘other’, that attracts so many people to it. Japan is mysterious. Japan is whimsical. Japan is downright fun. It produces more technological gadgets than any other nations on Earth, and is the cradle and lifeblood of the video game industry. Is it truly any wonder that Seymour loves the Land of the Rising Sun so? In America, Final Fantasy will never be as popular as Grand Theft Auto. His beloved Aeris Gainsborough will always be considered inferior to that big-breasted floozy, Lara Croft. And his obsessions with all things RPG make him a social pariah. "SEEY-MOUR! SEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEEY-MOUR!" In America, Seymour Almasy is a fan favorite, loved for his embracing of the good and just, and constant struggle against evil. He is the quintessential good guy. But in Japan, he is far more than that. Seymour Almasy is mainstream, because his loves are mainstream. He doesn’t have to hide loving anime and RPGs. They’re accepted. So, when GCW announced its tour of Japan, saying that Seymour Almasy was looking forward to it would have been the understatement of the year. "SEEY-MOUR! SEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEEY-MOUR!" Welcome to Tokyo, Japan. Welcome to the beginning of the end. ------------------------------------------------------- No matter how often Seymour Almasy came here, the reaction never ceased to amaze him. The limousine had barely come to a stop before it was already surrounded by eager Japanese fans of both genders, buzzing excitedly at the arrival of their hero. Meeting the Japanese fans was always a thrill. Because of his gimmick, and because of the fact that he spoke fluent Japanese due to years of import gaming, Seymour was always sent on publicity tours in advance of a company’s arrival. Japan was, in many ways, like a second home to him now. "Yevon, they love me here…" His tone was flattered as he looked out the tinted windows of the limousine, at the utter crush of humanity surrounding it. Finally, security stepped into place, beginning to push people back to give him room to get out. As thrilled as he was today, he was also nervous. In addition to his autograph signing, he had a meeting with a man who was, as far as Seymour was concerned, a veritable God. That man was the current president of Square Enix, Inc., Mr. Yoichi Wada. Wada-san was the man in charge of Seymour’s favorite video game company, ever. And while usually, meeting the man would produce an utter plethora of fan boyish stammering, Seymour couldn’t resort to that today. No, for he had a proposal for Wada-san. A proposal that was both audacious and elaborate, but one that Seymour wanted to see through with every fiber of his being. For now, though, he couldn’t worry about that. Not with a throng of loyal Seymour fans to hang out with. "Ready, Almasy-san," the limo driver asked, in nearly flawless English. Reaching for the door handle, the Final Fantasy nodded. "Hai," he said, before stepping out into…madness. There had to be hundreds of them out there, Seymour thought, grabbing at him, trying to touch him. There were all sorts, too. Young and old alike stood in the crowd, chanting his name, trying to get a glimpse of the American wrestling legend with the love of all things Japanese. "Wow," he said, utterly stunned as security helped him make his way to the autograph table set up in front of the massive headquarters building. "There are people cosplaying me." Seymour was used to cosplayers at his autograph signings. He had met Clouds and Aeriths, Edgars and Terras, all the way up to Yunas and Rikkus. But never before had he seen anyone dressed up as him. And some of the costumes were downright incredible. One caught his eye as he continued his walk. The young man wore mock leg armor made from foam, and had inscribed Seymour’s signature chest tattoo roman numbers with what appeared to be magic marker. Inwardly, Seymour laughed. "Heh, bet they don’t suspect that’s what I did in the beginning until I got drunk and got the real deal," he chuckled, under his breath. "Thank Yevon I was hammered, or I would have screamed bloody murder…" As he ascended the platform where the table was, he waved out to the masses, who were being herded into something resembling a line. The Final Fantasy waved, with both hands, prompting that familiar cheer to rise from the masses. "SEEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEY-MOUR! SEEEEY-MOUR!" A Square-Enix employee handed Seymour Almasy a red Sharpie. Seymour looked down, to see the giant stack of PTC 8 x 10 promotional photographs, ready to be signed. From past experience, he knew people would have their own items to sign as well, anything from scraps of paper to the case of Square-Enix’s newest offering, Kingdom Hearts II. And sign them all he would. For, unlike PTC’s Global Champion, Seymour Almasy didn’t see autograph signings as a chore that made him more money. He saw them as a chance to thank the people who made him what he was. Really, he was five foot nothing. One-hundred nothing pounds. Sure, he could fly, but so could hundreds of other wrestlers. Seymour owed his success to two entities besides his own ability. One was the throng of fans that stood before him, loyal Final Fantasy fanatics that were emblematic of his followers around the world. Perhaps more importantly, the second was the corporation behind him that let Seymour Almasy continue to exist. For Seymour Almasy had been here before, at Square headquarters. Proposition for Wada-san or not, this trip could not be any more stressful than that last one had been. ------------------------------- November 26, 2002. Tokyo, Japan -- Lobby of SquareEnix Headquarters "I’m dead. Dead, dead, dead." Somehow, Seymour had hoped his first trip to the headquarters of his favorite videogame company would be for a happier occasion than copyright infringement. Unfortunately, life rarely gave a man what he wanted, so he stood in Tokyo, looking up at the looming building, wondering what his future held. Grandpa Cid couldn’t make the trip with him, and Aerith was back at home nursing the flu. That meant that Jason Seymour Wilson had to face down the bigwigs of a powerful corporation all by himself. He chucked nervously to himself. Maybe he could get off the hook by pretending the Final Fantasy games were real, like he did in the UWF. If he went with that excuse, he reasoned, there was no copyright infringement. After all, how could anyone copyright the world he lived in? "Yeah, right. Like that’s going to work. Face it, Seymour, this is the end. Your career’s going to be over after three months." It was just about three months ago that Seymour had debuted for the Universal Wrestling Federation, and begun a meteoric rise that had seen him finish in the top sixteen of the third Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, and capture the UWF’s Television and Shoot Wrestling Championships. Then had come the cease and desist order. While the UWF could ignore such orders from crackpot organizations like People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals, the National Organization of Women, and Amnesty International, it was harder to ignore people with actual copyrights. They could sue. He’d already been told by the receptionist to go to the fifth floor. That had been twenty minutes ago, and he still stood in the lobby, a gaijin in an unfamiliar land, scared shitless that his career was about to end. No, more than that. Scared shitless that he’d be sued. Sent to prison for copyright violations. And all because he was a fan who loved the games so much that he based a wrestling persona around it. His march to the proverbial scaffold was done with slow, plodding steps. Nerveless fingers darted out to push the "UP" elevator button. Unfortunately, the elevator dinged, and opened up. Charon was there for him, right on schedule. So as not to keep his executioners waiting any longer, he jabbed at the "5" button, before slumping back against the wall. While the space of five floors was not the river Styx, Jason Seymour Wilson was having difficulty discerning the difference. *Ding!* And there he was. "Fucking cheerful elevator," he groused. Of course it was cheerful. It didn’t have emotions or worries. Technicians worked on it to make sure it ran properly. It was a machine, designed to ding at the exact same pitch every time. Automated to perfection. Just like corporate executives whose purpose was to dictate the company line, a company line that Seymour knew included "no violating our copyrights". He looked at himself as he walked, dressed in a black suit, tie, and neatly polished shoes. It was so very not him that it made him want to scream. "I should be in the ring now," he grumbled, "entertaining the fans, and doing what I love. Instead, I’m here, about to have everything I’ve worked for and strived for wiped out. No one cares about Jason Wilson. Everyone cares about Seymour." But he had no time to worry about that. Zero hour was here. Adjusting his tie, Seymour stepped through the proper open door, to come face to face with about seven suits who were going to decide his fate. "Sit," one said, in a firm tone of a voice. It was a command that Seymour had no option to resist, and so he sat in an admittedly-comfortable chair, watching as seven pairs of eyes trained themselves on the anomaly in their midst. He almost laughed. Without even thinking too hard, he knew they were staring at his purple hair. As silly looking as it could be at times, it was his trademark, one of a half-dozen things that distinguished him from everyone else. "Let’s get straight to the point," another executive boomed, in a voice that made Seymour blanche at its ferocity. "You violated our copyright on the Final Fantasy series, in addition to naming several of your moves after characters or attacks in our videogames." Well, when they put it like that, what the hell was he supposed to do? Deny it? He couldn’t. There was no way. They had evidence in the form of matches and interviews. "We should," he continued, "enforce this cease and desist order. Perhaps even follow up with legal charges." Again, it was difficult to argue the point. As much as Seymour disliked it, they were well within their rights to do so. "However," a softer voice from the other side of the room continued, "we have come up with a better idea…" What? "…one that we believe will be mutually beneficial to both sides." "Huh?" The executives chuckled at Seymour’s confusion. Clearly, he’d been expecting the worst, and any chance of a reprieve at all had pricked his ears up. "The Universal Wrestling Federation has television in Japan," a shorter, thinner man said, "and you have a sizeable fan base here. Furthermore, considering how much you talk about our video games, and your move names…you’re essentially a walking billboard for our company. Free advertising, as it were, and in an area where our games simply don’t sell as well as they do here." "Tell me about it," a suddenly more relaxed Seymour said. "You have no idea how much you get mocked in high school for having a crush on Aeris." You hear that sound? It’s silence. Uncomfortable silence, at that. Fortunately, one executive decided to forge ahead anyway. "What we are offering you, Mr. Wilson, is a chance to work with us. To be a spokesperson for our product. You would be placed on the company payroll, and in addition to wrestling, do occasional commercials, autograph signings, and things of that sort." Seymour’s jaw dropped at the offer. At the same time, though, he was more than slightly dubious. "What’s in it for you guys," he asked, scanning each executive’s face for signs of insincerity. None existed. "The free advertising," came the reply. "You know how much it costs to produce a regular ad?" Almasy shook his head no. "Well, it costs a lot. Far more than it would be to put you on our payroll. Now, do we have a deal?" When they put it that way, where could he sign? ------------------------------------------------------- That seemed so very, very long ago. Now, he was a superstar, known throughout the world. Then, he’d been a terrified kid, three months in the business. "Mr. Almasy?" He’d lied about his past for his whole career. So effectively that at times, he forgot that it was a lie. Never once had he actually believed RPGs were real. Sure, his mother had been a neglectful bitch, his father had died in a car accident, and he had played more RPGs than was healthy, he wasn’t some freak of nature psychological case. He’d gone to college for two years, hated it, and dropped out to pursue his dream. Before that one fateful day when he’d been playing Final Fantasy VIII at two in the morning in his dorm, he’d been Jason Wilson, mild-mannered independent wrestler and college student. The games had changed his life. "Mr. Almasy?" Perhaps the worst part about his lying and his cover-up was the simple fact that there was no reason to do so anymore. He was established. Had been ever since those first three months. "Almasy-san! Baseball Magazine!" He was snapped from his thought by a reporter from the aforementioned magazine. Somehow, due to the combination of signing a copy of a Final Fantasy X strategy guide and pondering his past, he hadn’t heard the man’s first two calls. "Yes?" "You were defeated in round four of GTT5 by Kimbusa. Any thoughts on how you’re going to go about defeating him this week?" "Well, for starters, hopefully I can avoid letting him sit on me." The Final Fantasy chuckled slightly, patting a fan on the head, and beginning to autograph a circa 2002 UWF Seymour Almasy action figure. "In all seriousness, though, he’s one of the most capable competitors I’ve ever stepped in the ring with." Loath as he was to admit it, the ex-sumo posed an absolutely enormous problem for the slight Seymour. "He’s immense. I’ve never faced a man who weighs as much as he does. He’s got me by about 320 pounds. It’s not like I’m going against a guy fifty pounds bigger than me who I might be able to suplex or slam out of adrenaline. I’m not getting Kimbusa off his feet by lifting him, period." That was, all things considered, a Kimbusa-sized understatement. "How is your relationship with Miss Tessa Windsor going?" Over the course of answering the Kimbusa question, Seymour had signed the action figure, a t-shirt, and a pair of trading cards. At the reporter’s question, he put down the foam finger in his hands, before turning to look at the man. "No comment. Next question." Seymour Hated reporters. Hated them with a capital "H". As long as they stuck to wrestling questions, they were tolerable. He could answer questions about moves and opponents, matches and federations. That was his public life, his professional life. Fine. Part of the job description. "But Seymour--" "I said, no comment. Next question." The firm tone of his voice seemed to startle both reporters and fans alike. Softening quickly, he turned back to the fan with the foam finger, smiled, and continued to sign the item. He could see the disappointed look on the reporter’s face, but a line had to be drawn somewhere. Reporters had ruined everything. They’d been there when he’d gotten married. They’d been at his doorstep when he went to go get a quart of milk, when he wanted to relax with Aerith, when he was trying to sleep in his hotel. Always with the same questions. Never giving him a moment’s peace. And it got to him. More importantly, it got to Aerith. Yeah, there was another lie about Seymour Almasy’s life. He hadn’t broken up with Aerith. She’d broke up with him, leading to the sad state of affairs that was their now-tattered and ruined friendship. All because of those fucking reporters and their desire to spread his most personal thoughts and moments to the four corners of the Earth. Okay, so maybe he DID have a reason to lie about everything. It threw those damned reporters off the trail… Slowly, he shook his head, trying to rid his mind of those thoughts and keep a smile on his face. Jason Seymour Wilson or Seymour Almasy, he still loved his fans more than almost anything in his life. As he signed a program from GCW’s last WorldWide show, however, he heard one of the last sounds he expected to hear. Somber notes were coming from his waist. Without looking down, he knew that there was only one person he had programmed that theme to. Laura. The very same Laura that he was still honestly rather annoyed with. There was absolutely no reason for her to call. That very fact was the only reason that he DID pick up, asking the audience’s forgiveness with a quick sweep of his hand. Almost unbelievably, the crowd quieted, allowing Seymour to take the call. "Sey-sey--" He didn’t need to hear any more than that. Something was wrong. Drastically and horribly wrong. Laura was an unbelievably strong woman. She didn’t cry much, but she seemed on the verge of sobbing now. "Laura," he asked, suddenly forgetting all protocol in his worry, "what’s wrong?" "Cid," she managed to blurt out, trying to keep the sobbing down just long enough to get out the horrible truth. "He’s dead!" The color drained from Seymour’s face at that. Cid was his grandfather. The one sane, living member of his family. The only person who had supported him on his quest to be a professional wrestler. A man that he was closer with than any other. "I’m in Japan right now," he said, eyes brimming with tears that threatened to ruin the resolve building within him. "I am going to the airport right now, and I will be on the first flight back to La Guardia. Meet me there. Whatever’s screwed up between us…I…" "Shhh," she said, voice clearly breaking. "I know. You don’t need to tell me. I’ll be there, I promise." And with that, she hung up. And with that, Seymour stood up, turning to the Square representative with panicked eyes. He searched for the words in Japanese to express what had happened, before thought failed him and he spat it out in English. "My grandfather has died. Please, explain to Wada-san and everyone here what has happened. I’m going back to the airport and getting home as soon as possible." The man bowed to him, placing a hand on his shoulder. Sympathetic eyes met Seymour’s panicked ones, as the rep did his best to be reassuring. "I will," he responded, in slow, accented English. "May your journey be a safe one." Security kept the confused fans back as Seymour jogged, and then ran to the limousine. Behind him, the announcement was made to the masses in their native language. All eyes turned to the man somberly entering the limousine. Before he could close the door, however, a young child who had managed to avoid security approached, looking at Seymour with sad eyes. "Gomen nesai, Almasy-san." One guard stepped forward to pull the boy back, but Seymour waved him off for the time being. Leaning down, Seymour patted the child on the head, and responded with a whispered "domo arigato". Looking out sadly over the crowd, Seymour closed the limousine door. Then, and only then, did he allow himself to cry. (TBC.)
