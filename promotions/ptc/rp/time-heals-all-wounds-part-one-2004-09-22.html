Time Heals All Wounds... (part one)

22 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-22 “I never fathomed what it would actually be like to be alone, I mean, it’s one thing to say that you want to be left alone. Let’s face it, our daily interactions in society ensure that none of us are really alone. Think about it for a moment, I mean, honestly, picture yourself in a dark room. What are you doing? Using the computer, perhaps chatting online? Are you reading a book, doing a crossword puzzle? Playing video games, watching a DVD, or wrestling on television? Perhaps even talking on a cell phone. Not good enough, still outside interaction, not alone. Remove all of it, so you’re sitting alone in the dark, with nothing but your thoughts to keep you company. How long would it be before you broke, and had to say something, or do something, just to shatter that deafening silence? Sure, we’ve all longed for that silence, someone once sang a song that summed it all up… “Make the World Go Away”, I think it was called. Really, that’s what I longed for my whole life. Silence. Solitude. To be free of the bondage of society. So, why, you ask, did I follow all their rules? Why did I allow myself to be played like a violin in the hands of Stradivarius? I guess, as much as I hate to admit it, I had grown as adept at playing the game of life as any of the rest of you. It was a role, but something more like instinct, to get up in the morning and go to work. I had my careful routine, my perfectly planned existence, carefully controlled. They told me once I had obsessive-compulsive disorder. Who doesn’t? I mean, is it a fucking crime to want to see things neat and orderly? That’s what I did, I created order from chaos. I don’t fancy myself a God, I don’t create worlds. Nor do I destroy them, although, I probably could if I wanted to. Words. Knowledge. Those were my weapons. I was the gentleman spy, James Bond at his best, a cunning linguist, no pun intended. Although, women were never a problem, until her. See, even now, I don’t understand why everything comes back to her. Why, after all this time, can’t I get her out of my head? Why, of all places, have I followed her into the wrestling ring? The sport of the upper echelon, fought in Coliseums past… the glory of battle, the Olympic games… now reduced to the sport of arenas present... for the entertainment of the unwashed masses. Not much has changed. And they call this civilization? Barbaric. Don’t get me wrong; I am no novice to fighting. Just ask my teachers, and if you could find the bastard in hell, my father could likely tell you. I had a choice, get tough or die. When your father is a sadistically abusive Marine, more specifically a deranged Vietnam vet, you learn to take the punches. I knew how to take a fall, and for the good of me, stay down. That was when I was a kid. When I didn’t know any better. I had anger issues, still do, really… never learned how to properly channel this rage. Wrestling. Something I sort of fell into, by accident really. The memory is muddy now, like most of them are, diluted. It seems the older I get, the better things seem like they used to be, even though the scars on my body prove otherwise. So, back to where I began, being alone. Absolute solitude. Total separation, Christians call that hell, total separation from God. Then, perhaps this is hell on earth, what my actions have created. I am alone, in every sense of the word. There are no others, I killed them all. The watchers, and their strict code of non-violence… of simply observing and never interfering… kept them from doing what they should have. I left an open invitation… and they didn’t take it. I wanted them to come after me; I awaited the challenge. Hell, I was disappointed with the outcome. Yet another meeting, that’s all. Sad, really, that all my military training amounted to this. Recruited from the CIA training program, into the CID. I could have become something better, followed in my father’s footsteps… but why? The fucker wouldn’t have been proud of me if I single-handedly won a war, and cured cancer at the same time. I knew that now, but I didn’t know it then. Who knows anything, really, at the age of nineteen? Eleven years later, and I didn’t have a clue. But perhaps, now, I could feel the kernel of self-realization, or perhaps that was simply the last of my sanity slipping away. Six of one, half a dozen of the other, did it really matter much? Sane or not, I was still here…” He sat at ringside, a pair of dark sunglasses over his eyes, watching the action. So, it all ended here, in Dallas, Texas. Amid a few hundred screaming fans in this underground fight club arena, he still felt like an outsider looking in. This is what she chose, over him? This? She’d traded his life, and his love, for this? Fighting in some dingy shithole, to satisfy the bloodlust of the crowd, and the wallets of a bunch of bookies? Sad, really. She was here, in Global Wrestling's proving grounds... trying to break into the business. He watched her every move, feeling the impact of the punches and kicks, tasting her blood as it spilled over her chin from a split lip. He wanted to leap over the barricade and jump into the fray, obliterating her opponent, saving her. Instead, he turned his attention back to the bag of popcorn he held in one hand, tossing the kernels in his mouth, one after another. He'd taught her every move, and every blow she landed, every perfect suplex she executed, was like a knife twisting in his chest. “She doesn’t even know you’re alive,” he muttered to himself between mouthfuls of the dry, salty snack. “and perhaps that’s for the best. Leave the past where it belongs.” “I’d always been a good person, or so I considered myself. I had morals; I adhered to their rules for ten long years, my surrogate family. As surely as Dante had become my brother, Matthias was my father. A much better replacement for the sadistic freak who sired me. I have done the same thing, over and over, and each time, I can’t help but laugh at myself, looking back on it. Always looking for a new daddy, a dominant male figure, someone to overshadow me, and someone I could look up to. Someone that I could impress… be it with my mind, or my body. Athletics. Mathematics. Languages. Firearms. A master of them all, a modern day renaissance man. But, I was still essentially empty. Something I have never understood, just why I feel this compulsion to fill that empty ache. Broken beyond repair. Even now, as I sit here, cold and alone, I can feel it. Emptiness. I know they say when you stare into the abyss it also looks back into you. I am that abyss… I am the darkness now. Yet, some last shred of humanity remains inside this shell. A soul, still tethered to this empty, deceased body…” He sat in the shadows, the short tumbler filled with scotch held in one hand, tight enough to break the glass. He watched the ice melt, watching it fade away, even as he watched her. From across the room, he may as well have been across the country, as vast as the gulf between them was. She had killed him… his Judas, his lover, his last mission gone terribly awry. And he loved her still… she had cut out his heart and served it up on a silver platter. She had shot him in the chest; in cold blood… she had watched him die. She’d cut him out of the picture… “So why,” he mumbled into his glass, “why can’t I let her go?” “Because, Mac, that’s the way we’re built.” The bartender stood there, holding the bottle of Johnny Walker Red, his light blue eyes filled with compassion. “Whatever.” Jackson tossed back the last of the glass, grimacing as the liquor burned a path to his stomach. He could already feel the impact of the scotch, but he gestured for the bartender to fill the glass again. He raked a hand though his tangled hair, smoothing the strands back from his face, only to have them flop back over his eyes. Not long enough for the ponytail he wore almost all the time now. He needed a haircut, it was nearly shoulder length now, unkempt, but he knew it was pointless, it would simply return to the state it was in. It was best to leave it be. His jawline was dark with stubble, a muscle twitching in his jaw, betraying his tension. “You wanna talk about it?” the bartender, early forties, six-foot-two of pure Irish muscle, asked Jackson. Jackson hesitated, considering really if that’s why he had come here, to find a confessor. To find a peer, if even only for a moment. He downed the glass in one shot, slamming it down on the bar before turning his eyes to those of the barkeep. Equal measures of sadness and anger rode in his indigo eyes, but that was nothing new to John. He inclined his head, indicating the woman in the booth beside the jukebox, finally speaking, the words almost dragged past his chapped lips as though against his will. “See her?” John looked, really looked, and what he saw made him shake his head with wonder. She was slim, with long dark hair hanging over her shoulders, a few strands hanging over her eyes. She threw her head back and laughed, amused by something her male companion said. He saw the shape of her neck, the smooth whiteness of her skin in startling contrast to her pitch-black hair. She was pretty. Even a blind man could see that. He turned his gaze back to Jackson. “Oh yeah, who doesn’t see her?” “She broke my heart.” He took the bottle when John offered it, taking a long pull from the neck. He didn’t say more, nor did he have to. “Ayuh. That’s what they do. They don’t understand us, Mac. They say we’re complex… nah, we’re simple. All it takes is a smile, or a wink… or even that look, the one they give you when they catch you looking… and we’re hooked. That’s how they get you. It’s the sex, oh they say it isn’t… but look at it like this, Mac. It’s evolution. It’s still 99% procreation, and the other 1% mental perspiration. They want the mental game, we just want to do the horizontal tango, if you get my meaning.” “Yeah, I hear you.” He let his eyes wander over to her table again, watching her every move, his eyes reflecting his pain. “But this wasn’t just about sex… at first it was all about a job.” “Yeah?” John O’Malley’s reddish eyebrows shot up into his thick, red hair. “She a hooker?” “No. At least I sure as hell hope not,” that brought a chuckle past his lips, softening the severity of his expression. The sound was devoid of humor, sounding more forced than anything else, it was almost rusty from misuse, almost as though he had forgotten how to laugh. “no, she was a thief.” “Most women are.” “Not like that,” he upended the bottle again, taking another bitter pull of the amber liquid. “Not talking about her stealing my heart. I’m talking about her profession. She was a professional thief… high-priced items. She was a relic hunter… that’s what she was when I met her. I still don’t know a damned thing about her… it’s been almost a year.” “How long has it been?” “Since what? Since she cut me out of the picture? I… I’m not sure. Time’s lost all meaning to me now.” John’s look was one of pure sympathy. “Yeah, it’s like our happiest times are seconds long… and the painful ones take years…” “It’s not that. It’s that… when your heart stops beating in your chest, you kinda lose sense of the rhythm of the world around you. How long have I been here? I don’t even know… all I know is that I had to see her again. One last time.” “You still love her, don’t you?” Jackson set the empty bottle back down on the bar, and bent his head, letting it touch the cool polished wood of the bar. His sigh made the following words somehow bitterer. “I would be damned to hell for committing the worst sin ever if I denied that. Let me tell you something about love, my friend. Love is something I never really wanted out of all this. I was just doing a job. I was supposed to get close enough to her to get back the… things she stole. I never bargained on losing my soul to her… I never bargained on losing my heart… or my life to her. She killed me… and she is still doing it. Can’t you see the knife in my back, slowly twisting as I sit here? She’s with someone else… she doesn’t even know I’m alive. Love when it happens is unexpected. It’s a blessing in disguise. And it’s death. Slowly, it eats away at you, like a cancer, like a poison, changing you into something else… from an individual into something else. A couple. That’s why it’s so liberating when it blooms… and so devastating when it dies.” “Hey, Mac… you gonna be ok? All this talking about dying… you ain’t planning to go back home and eat a gun, are you?” More laughter, this more chilling than the last. Jackson put his hand on top of John’s, letting him feel the dead weight, letting him feel the strangeness of his skin. It was then, looking into the stanger’s eyes, that John realized what had been odd all along. For all the emotions that reflected in that gaze, there was one thing missing. That spark of life that everyone had, this guy didn’t. They were flat, like the eyes of a lifelike doll. These were the glassy eyes of a corpse. He jerked his hand back, rubbing it absently with the other as he openly stared at Jackson. “Now you see, I can’t go home and kill myself, much as I would love to. You see, John, I’m already dead.” “Yeah, right. What are ya then, Mac… the Ghost of Christmas past?” “Nothing so garish, John. I’m just another lonely man, drowning my sorrows in a bottle. Nothing more than a shadow of something that never was, and probably never should be.” He reached into his pocket and withdrew a crumpled wad of twenties, tossing them on the bar before sliding out of the seat, as steady as a rock, even after all the alcohol. “Hey, Mac… where ya going?” “To the gym, my friend… to the gym. Tomorrow begins the next chapter of my life. Say hello to wrestling’s newest superstar… it seems I will be competing in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament this year.” “You,” John stumbled over his words as he began to polish the bar again with absent motions. “You’re a wrestler?” “Among other things, yes. Rhodes scholar, former CID agent, doctor, lawyer, Indian chief… take your pick. I have been many things… to many people. And today I have become something for you as well.” “What’s that?” “Your savior,” he held up his hand as John opened his mouth to interrupt. “I was planning to kill you when I first laid eyes on you, John. There is a lot of darkness in your soul. At first, I didn’t see the light. But then, you sat here, and listened… you actually listened to me. That means more than you could possibly understand. I…” John reached out and squeezed Jackson’s arm, making him pause. “Buddy, if I didn’t want to listen, I wouldn’t be in this business. Alcohol has a way of loosening tongues, even on dead guys. You looked like a man that needed a friend, or maybe a priest… I was just doing my job. No thanks needed.” When you grow up with a father incapable of showing love, a man reaching out to you has the power to do one of two things. Make you freeze up, or make you melt. Jackson looked away, his eyes filling up with tears. “Thank you, John. Thank you for helping me see the light. Thank you for giving me faith in humanity again. Bless you, my friend.” “Sometimes, I wonder if there will still be a final accounting for me… a time when I have to take the fall for my actions. A time when everything I have… and haven’t done is added up and tallied against me. I wonder, if my soul will sink then, back into the pit that spat me out… or if it will rise to the other place. If heaven means being without her, I will not go gently into my good night. I will follow her to the ends of the earth, true love is immortal… ageless… and has power that extends beyond the grave. Or perhaps I truly am insane. Six of one, half a dozen of the other. Apples and oranges. Still fruit. Still the same, although different. It’s all about perception. You see, what I want you to see. A wrestler. A man. Nothing more, nothing less. That is my disguise… the mask I wear. My burden. Surely, time heals all wounds, even those that kill.”
