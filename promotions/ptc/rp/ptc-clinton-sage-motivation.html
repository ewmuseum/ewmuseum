Clinton Sage: "Motivation"

24 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * * * February 11th, 2007 After the countless hours of interrogation by the FBI and the local Pennsylvania Police Departments, Clinton Sage was set free. For every question they had, he had a perfectly plausible answer. In the adjacent interrogation room, Reggie Hanes was crying. The poor fat bastard had a 14-month-old daughter who would grow up thinking her father was a cop-killer. His gun had slain an FBI Agent, and even though he had saved Clinton’s life in the process, every crime that Clinton had committed would now rest on the shoulders of the officer he had been paying off for months. But, he wasn’t permitted to return home. It was still an on-going crime-scene, and the bodies of FBI Agents Brandywine and Hamlin were still decorating his floor with arterial red. He was given a hotel to stay at, and since Nathan Hollinger had stolen his Corvette, Clinton was forced to use a taxi for the first time in years. He shuddered at the thought. Public transportation. The police called a taxi, which pulled up ten minutes late. Clinton hopped into the back, and ordered the cab driver to the hotel the police had recommended. He’d play there game for a week, possibly two, until the heat of the crime had died down. Besides, if all went as planned, in little over a week he’d be wrestling Angelo Deville, Doctor Curiosity, and Kimbusa for the PTC Global Championship. Clinton knew he would win the match, and he knew that in the process, he’d become the biggest name in wrestling. It would almost be his alibi. About ten minutes into the taxi ride, Clinton’s mind started to wander back towards the events that took place during the afternoon. He kept seeing a flash of Isis Hollinger holding the Vekoma meteorite, falling backwards as Clinton speared her. He could feel her ribs cracking and popping against his massive shoulder. The thought brought him enjoyment, but what really started to bug him was the fact she wasted such a precious wish. She brought back her husband, Nathan Hollinger. What a waste. A man who can’t remember anything before 1998, and a man whose only claim to fame was defeating Clinton himself in the qualifiers of GTT5. All that, hiding behind a mask. ‘The Seventh Sin’ started to wonder if perhaps she had wished for something else. Maybe, if by some miniscule chance that her mind wandered as she held the meteorite, that Clinton’s ability to transfer his own injuries unto Nathan’s body had stopped. It was time to test the theory. Looking up at the cabbie, Clinton asked in the nicest and most sincere voice possible, “Hey man, do you happen to have a pocket knife? I’ve got one of those hospital bands on my wrist, and I can’t seem to tear it off…” As the cabbie navigated the roads, he stared back at Clinton in the rear-view mirror with concerned eyes. Undoubtedly, he had heard such excuses before. But perhaps it was the fact that Clinton had been picked up from the police station, the cabbie answered and seemed to have a hint of trust in his voice, “Yeah man, sure. One second. The next red-light, it’s yours…but you’ve got to give it back right away.” “No problem. It’ll be a quick snip, and this stupid little wrist-band will be gone.” Clinton responded. They rode in silence for another minute or two, before finally rolling up to a red light. Gently handing the small pocketknife back through the porthole in the partition between the front of the taxi and the passenger section, the cabbie nodded at Clinton as he handed him the knife. Clinton Sage flashed a smile that betrayed his true intentions. He grabbed the small knife, pulling out the tiny two-inch blade from the centerpiece. She just wished for him to come back to life, is all he thought. Clinton Sage pressed the sharp edge of the knife against his forearm. As with every injury since he had gained the “ability” to transfer all his injuries to Nathan Hollinger, the pain was just as intense as it would be for any normal person. But within a minute or two, usually the less severe the injury the quicker it would transfer, the pain would dull with the closing of the wound. The steel was cold, clammy. With the jostling of the cab, Clinton didn’t want to slice too deeply into his own arm, per the miniscule chance that his power had died with the resurrection of Nathan Hollinger. He waited a moment, until the cab slowed for a sharp turn into the hotel parking lot, before taking a quick flick of the wrist and gashing his forearm. Instantly, he lost the feeling of the cold blade against his skin, drowned out by the steady flow of blood pouring down towards his wrist. He wiped the blade against his jeans, and winced at the wound. He tapped the glass with the edge of the knife, and the cabbie reached back through the porthole to grab the small knife without even taking his eyes off of the road. “Alright sir, that’ll be $24.65, without tip.” The cabbie said. Pulling to a stop, the taxi idled outside of the hotel. Clinton looked down at the wound on his forearm with intense concentration, not even paying attention to the cabbie’s demand for the cab faire. The wound hadn’t closed yet, and it had been nearly a minute. Clinton could feel the blood trickling down towards his fingertips, and the soft patter of it hitting the plastic floor mat. “Hey man, you ok?” the cabbie said, turning around and staring at Clinton. “Fine, how much was the fare?” “$24.65” Pulling out his wallet, Clinton Sage produced thirty dollars and stuffed it through the porthole of the partition. The worried look on Sage’s face garnered the attention of the cab driver, and when the driver looked down and saw that the money had stains of blood on it, he immediately ordered Clinton Sage out of the taxi. Dripping blood from a wound that refused to close, Clinton Sage demanded his exact change from the cabbie. When the cab finally sped off, the wound still hadn’t closed, and the sudden realization that his ‘power’ had been extinguished hit home. What the fuck did that bitch wish for? he wondered, gritting his teeth and clutching the small gash on his forearm. It would heal, just a lot longer than he had hoped for. * * * * * February 12th, 2007 It had taken Isis half of the trek across Pennsylvania to finally awaken. When she started to stir, Nathan pulled Clinton Sage’s Corvette to the side of the road, in preparation for the potential shock that would ensue. He didn’t know exactly had had happened, but he was positive it had been months since his wife had last seen him. The clock on the Corvette dashboard had a date of February 11th, 2007. The last thing Nathan remembered, it was a day after their wedding in late November, 2006. Something was amiss, and as soon as Isis was conscious again, facts would get straightened out. When she woke, turned her head and laid eyes on Nathan, there wasn’t a sudden shock. There wasn’t a scream of horror, or a burst of affection in the form of a big kiss or a big hug. Instead, she smiled and said, “I knew it’d be you.” The kiss followed, which Nathan had never experienced a kiss filled with such passion and longing in his life. (And his life only spanned from 1998 until 2007, at least to him.) And over the course of the rest of Pennsylvania, all of Ohio, and some of Indiana, Isis had explained to Nathan the exact timeline of the last three months. He remembered their wedding day, and his hands suddenly burning themselves to tatters. He remembered the hospital stay, with the word SAGE etching itself into his chest, the shattered kneecap, and the dozens of small incisions on his calf. What he didn’t remember was his head exploding, being buried, and then being exhumed by Clinton Sage and kept in a preservation cylinder for the last two weeks. His own memories jump from laying in the hospital bed until waking up on the floor of the Sage mansion, watching Clinton Sage land a vicious closed punch on Isis’ face. Isis told Nathan about the meteorites, and the powers they granted. But all the while, there seemed to be gaps in her story. Nathan noticed, even while driving, the vacant look in her eyes at every mention of Clinton Sage. Her lips seemed to curl in a primeval snarl at the thought of Sage. There was something she wasn’t telling him, but he didn’t press. It would come out eventually. He was driving to Colorado, and made it as far as the western border of Indiana before deciding to pull into a motel for the evening. That night, they made love for the first time as a married couple. Three months and two weeks after taking their vows, and three months after Nathan’s head exploded in a hospital in Denver, they had finally consummated their marriage. But, again, Nathan knew something was amiss. As they became intimate, he had turned off the lights in the hotel room to set the mood, but Isis had immediately demanded that they be turned back on. When he slowly crept up her body with a series of kisses, sometimes Nathan could swear that her body was quivering out of fear, more than ecstasy. And when they were finished, and she had retreated to the bathroom, Nathan swore that he heard her sobbing over the flushing of the toilet. When she returned to the bed, they lay together naked and silent. He cherished the warmth of her body, and long after she had fallen asleep in the safety of her arms, he remained awake going over the details of what she had told him. He had evaded the dark, cloaked arm of death. He just didn’t know it would be swinging back into his life, sickle in hand, within a month. * * * * * February 26th, 2007 The entire limo ride was made in total silence. Lance and Travis Reed sat across from Lane Stevens and the ringleader himself, Clinton Sage. Clinton Sage was dressed in a black suit with a dark gray shirt underneath and matching tie, while both Reed brothers wore dark gray suits with maroon ties. Lane Stevens, bless his redheaded heart, was wearing a tuxedo t-shirt and was puffing on a chewing gum cigarette. Clinton’s stare never left the passing blur of the Pennsylvania countryside as it blazed past the window of the limo. When the limo came to a rest outside of the cemetery, all four men silently left the limousine and followed Clinton past the swinging iron gates that kept people off of the property. Known as ‘The Affliction’ to all of FUSE Wrestling and a growing contingent of the PTC community, usually their style was verbal assault as well as the physical assault that followed. But today, all four men were eerily silent as they approached he grave. Slung over Clinton’s right arm was the PTC Global Championship, which he had won the night prior from Doctor Curiosity and Kimbusa and Angelo Deville. After walking a good distance through the rows and rows of headstones and unmarked graves, Clinton turned to his minions and waved them off, making sure these last few steps were taken alone. The Reed brothers nodded, while Lane continued to puff his candy cigarette. He pretended to adjust the tie that was printed on his shirt. Clinton Sage knelt down before a pair of graves: Maxwell Sage 1947 – 1993 Lauren Sage 1949 – 1994 Clinton knelt next to the graves in silence for a moment, gathering his emotions. Behind him, the Reed brothers looked a little uncomfortable at the thought of being here. They were curious as to Clinton’s demand of their presence, but didn’t’ question it. Lane Stevens leaned in towards Lance, “Clinton had the headstones changed a year after his mother was buried, removing those sappy-as-fuck epitaphs that are etched into them.” Lance Reed looked down at the red-haired FUSE LiveWire Champion with curiosity, “How do you know that?” Lane Stevens winked, “I read it in a fortune cookie.” And then all three members of The Affliction went suddenly silent as they heard Clinton speak, on his knees next to his mother’s grave. He held the PTC Global Championship out towards the headstone as if the ghost of Lauren Sage was using it to see from the afterlife back into this one. “Do you see this, mom? Do you see it? This is my legacy. Both you and Dad were cowards to never tell me that I wasn’t your own, instead lying to me my entire life. You took my love for you, and you just crushed it on what was supposed to be one of the happiest days of my life.” He took a breath, paused, and sniffed back some tears. “Keep your name, is what you asked. Well, you stupid bitch, I kept the name Sage. I kept it, alright. Now it’s my name, not yours. Now all the good that you and Dad had done over your lives will forever be wiped clean, because when people think of the name ‘Sage’ they will only think of one person: Me.” Once again, Lane Stevens leaned in towards the Reed brothers, who were growing uneasy with each passing moment. They just didn’t seem to understand what was going on, “You see, Clinton didn’t know he was adopted until his 20th birthday. We may be bad men, but bad men all love something, and he wasn’t always this way. In fact, I’d say his parents made him this way by not telling him that he was adopted until it was far too late.” Travis responded this time, “How the fuck do you know this?” Lane Stevens remained calm and collected. He didn’t answer the question, instead turning an ear back towards Clinton. Sage crept inches closer to both headstones, waging an inner war of hatred and affection. He was fighting back the tears, but also trying to calm the growing tremors of anger that were starting to quake his body. “A mother’s love isn’t supposed to turn their child down the darkened road. A father’s love is supposed to send their son off to play baseball or teach English class. So why did I end up with four parents who all ended up in the ground far earlier than they should have? Am I cursed?” His voice trailed off into a whisper, “Am I cursed?” He sat silent for a moment, tracing a finger across the top of his father’s headstone. Quickly, he shot a glance back towards his three generals of war, before returning the glance back towards the matching graves of “mom” and “dad” “I hope the both of you are happy, up in heaven or down in hell. God knows you both should be in Hell for the lifelong lies and betrayal that you committed against me. WHY COULDN’T YOU JUST TELL ME? WHY?!? Instead all the love you had given me was suddenly replaced by this rage…this unending rage and aggression…and I don’t like it, Mom. I don’t like it at all.” The tears finally started to stream down Clinton Sage’s cheeks. To the three men in attendance, and the hundreds of corpses beneath the rotting ground, it was the first time they had ever seen Clinton Sage cry. “But it’s who I am. I can’t escape it. Your name has become my name, and my name will forever be etched into the minds of anyone I come across. This belt may hold my name, but soon it will be my name that holds this belt. I'm going to carve a legacy unlike any other in this business. It will forever be associated with me, Dad, with me, Mom. ME! Clinton Sage. You both worked so hard throughout your lives to do nothing but bring honor to our name, where I'll stop at nothing to bring hatred and disdain towards it.” Clinton stood, knelt down and placed a weeping kiss atop each headstone before kicking them over, one by one. “There are those who live their entire lives searching for a meaning, but you gave me mine over a decade ago.” Clinton Sage turned from the matching graves of his adoptive parents, and stormed past his three Affliction members in silence, wiping a stray tear from his cheek. Lance and Travis Reed shared a brotherly look of confusion, before Lance finally spoke, “What was that all about?” Lane Stevens kept his stare on the graves of Lauren and Maxwell Sage, immediately responding in a hollow and honest voice, “He wants us to know what happens to those who betray him.” The three men moved in silence back towards the limo, where Clinton Sage was waiting to leave these forsaken lands. The tears had been wiped from his cheeks, as if they had never been spilled. * * * * *
