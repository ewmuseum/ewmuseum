A Shattered Soul - Part 2

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 A Shattered Soul - Part 2 After a night of utter hell spent in Parker Centre's premier overnight facility, aptly nicknamed ‘The Night-Night Room’ by the officers stationed there, Tony Aliso awoke in an instant. Though he was now alert, he still felt fatigued after what happened the previous night. The death of Becky hit him hard and it was only then, as he clumsily pulled his jeans up his legs, that it occurred to him that he would never again hear her sweet, angelic voice. Never again would he touch her soft skin. Never again would he feel that touch that made his toes curl. He shook his head and let out a long sigh as he stood up, ready to take whatever the day ahead would bring. The room he stayed in overnight was cramped but Aliso, being a veteran of Parker Centre due to his long-time association with the L.A.P.D., found it comfortable after the tragic events of last night. He got that mental image that he must have gotten at least a hundred times in his dreams; Becky's battered body being hauled away in the green body bag. He asked himself the same question that had crossed his mind multiple times since that moment - Why? Aliso rubbed his eyes and took advantage of the facilities available to him in the cramped room, preparing a steaming hot cup of coffee. The machine was fresh out of blueberry muffins, a food stuff which had become a trademark of the L.A.P.D. Instead, Aliso stuck with the coffee and sat down on his bed. He took a prolonged sip of the hot coffee. It sat well in his stomach. There was a knock on the door. Aliso said nothing in response, hoping the person would simply come in uninvited. The door creaked open slowly. A head peeped around the doorframe. It was the man in charge of last night's affairs outside Garrison's, an L.A.P.D. veteran and coming close to snatching the Deputy Chief position if he played his cards the right way. Aliso only knew him as ‘Bob’, for some reason never taking the time to remember his full title. It wasn't like Bob cared, though. “Tony, buddy, hey,” he began, visibly cautious entering the room, “You mind if I come in? I'd like to speak to you a little bit about the events of last night, if that's okay with you.” Aliso felt like telling Bob where to get off right then and there but decided against it. After all, Aliso was once in his shoes - chomping at the bit for a spot in the top leagues of the L.A.P.D. where all the money was. In a peculiar way, Aliso could empathise with Bob even as he sat down with a stern expression on his face, just waiting for Aliso to dish out the goods on what had happened. Aliso knew the guy was only doing his job but despite that, it bothered him that just hours after such a traumatic experience, he would have to backtrack in his own mind and give his version of events. Role-reversal wasn't something Aliso was fond of, to say the least. He favoured being in control of any given situation. Not this time. Bob shuffled his feet a bit and waited as Aliso sipped down more of his coffee. Aliso placed the Styrofoam cup on the floor and turned to Bob with a cold expression on his face. Aliso was sapped of all his energy. All he wanted to do was get some rest and head home to think about it all and locate the person who had done it. Still, despite his determination for revenge of his own kind, he knew he might need some help. If it had to come in the form of Aliso's old buddies in the L.A.P.D., then so be it. “Thanks, Tony. Okay, I guess the first thing I need is a pretty detailed description of the events of last night. If possible, right from when you left your home. It's possible you could have been tailed by somebody, maybe someone knew you were coming,” Bob said, trying to sound as robotic as possible in his approach. “I left my home at around 8:30pm so I could get a head start and be there before Becky was. I think she would have liked that. Problem was, I got caught up in traffic on the freeway for quite some time and hell, I checked the clock in my car and without me even knowing it, about twenty minutes had passed,” Aliso began, with a thoughtful expression. Bob gave Aliso a serious look, wanting to stay ‘in character’, so to speak. “What time did you actually reach your planned destination?,” Bob prodded. “I distinctly remember, actually. It was... ten minutes past nine. I admit I was a little tardy but I've given you my excuse. Oh, by the way, there's no need for you to go on asking me questions, Bob. I worked in the L.A.P.D. and I'm pretty well learned on the interview etiquette,” Aliso pointed to Bob's coat pocket and motioned towards it with his head. “To give you an example, I know, without a doubt in my mind, that you're hiding a tape recorder in there, recording this conversation. You might wanna rewind a bit so your superiors won't hear what I just guessed when they listen to it,” Aliso said through a knowing smirk. Bob reached into his pocket and abruptly stopped the tape, then showing it to Aliso to prove he had put its recording to an end for the time being. Aliso's interviewer took on a more relaxed and friendly approach now. His body language less tense and more easy, he prepared to ask a couple of questions. “Okay, now we can both be cool around one another. Here's the million dollar question, Tony. Do you have any idea who could have done this? Could it have been, say, a group based in L.A. who are intent on knocking people off? Someone you used to have ties with? Maybe even a friend of yours? Often here in the L.A.P.D., we find -- ,” “ -- That it's the one you least expect,” the two men uttered simultaneously, their voices echoing together in unison. Aliso nodded and furrowed his brow, staring down at the cold white floor. After a pensive moment, Aliso met Bob's gaze once more. “I don't care what I have to do, Bob, I'm finding out what kind of sick, twisted bastard did this to her. Sure, you guys may not understand how someone can become so attached to some random woman over a couple of nights, but with Becky, I felt content... all the time. That's something I don't feel often, see what I mean? What's worse is how it turned out. She was pure of heart, Bob. The girl didn't have to die. She shouldn't have died...,” Aliso whispered, barely audible to his interviewer. “How about you get yourself ready to go, Tony? It's probably best for you and for us at this point in time. I've got your cell number and you've got mine so give me a call if anything comes up. Okay?” Bob said it genuinely and honestly and Aliso knew he wasn't one of those famous ‘don't give a crap’ officers that Aliso had dealt with in the past. This man was real and that's what Aliso understood. Bob gave Aliso a pat on the back and left the room. Aliso was alone again with his thoughts, a prison he didn't want to be in right now. He finished off his cup of coffee and gathered his things. It was time to leave at last. *** The following week, things returned to normal, for the most part. Although the image of the body bag being carried away still remained vivid and very real in Aliso's mind, he knew himself that sooner or later, he would have to cast aside what he once felt for her. Life had to continue even though her life had been lost. Aliso's reverie was interrupted by his cell phone's ringing on the kitchen table. He picked up after a few rings. “This is Aliso.” Aliso always started phone conversations as briefly as possible. He wouldn't exceed four syllables, if possible. The only exception to this rule was when speaking to the opposite sex, of course, out of common courtesy. “Tony, hey. It's Bob from the L.A.P.D. Uh, we'd like you to come into Parker Centre this afternoon for a meeting. Just you, me and the Chief. It's about the post-mortem,” Bob informed Aliso in a slightly reluctant tone. “No offence there, Bob, but has it really taken you whiz kids down at the L.A.P.D. over a week to finish a post-mortem? I mean, I've waited a long time for 'em before when I worked there, but never did I have to wait a week,” Aliso said. He couldn't help but feel smug with himself as he awaited Bob's response. “Cut the smart talk, Tony,” Bob replied, almost berating Aliso for his backtalk, “just be here at Parker Centre for about four, okay?” “I'll be there.” Aliso pressed the red button on his phone, cutting off the call. He pondered for a moment on the little information Bob had given him. The post-mortem had taken place. The big boys wanted to see him for whatever reason. Perhaps the L.A.P.D. bright sparks had found something out about the killer, assuming there was one. Aliso didn't want to believe Becky did the job herself, though. He firmly believed that there was a killer. Maybe he was blinded by his selfish search for revenge or maybe it was the truth. Either way, his gut feeling told him the latter. Aliso was in no rush to get to Parker Centre. He had some time to chill out before he made the trip. He took some time to prepare a quick lunch of a club sandwich filled up with the basics of all good sandwiches - mayonnaise, salami and tuna, to name but a few - and watched some television. Two hours to go until the meeting. Aliso couldn't help but wonder what it was about. Whatever it was, he would find out soon enough. *** Aliso cut off his Beemer's engine and sat there for a moment in the front seat, again just curious about what the results of the post-mortem were. If anything, he desired a result of some sort. A conclusive piece of evidence to prove that someone had killed her. He swung the car door open and stepped out into L.A.'s mid-afternoon sunshine. He strolled over to the Parker Centre across the road nervously, like he had a dental appointment he couldn't get out of. The elevator brought Aliso up to the fifth floor, where he knew the meeting was being held. He figured that whoever the Chief was, he was probably a tight ass. Because of that, it was advisable to play it cool around him and don't try anything. He checked each door down the hallway and eventually came to the last door on the right. He peeked in the window and spotted Bob perusing through some sheets on a table. Aliso pushed the door open and treated the room like it was his own. Bob shook Aliso's hand, though he was clearly confused as to why the young Aliso looked so confident. Then he saw him. The man who must have been the Chief sat there with his arms folded at the desk. He seemed to have every little thing you could possibly want on a day in the L.A.P.D. scattered all over his desk. The Chief's ramrod straight posture got Aliso making jokes in his mind, none of which he shared with his present company. The Chief shook Aliso's hand, too. Aliso didn't want handshakes, though. He wanted answers. He wanted information. “If you'll just grab a seat, there, Mr. Aliso, this will be over in no time,” The Chief said, though it sounded more like an order than a request. Aliso sat down on a comfy leather seat, followed by Bob. Bob began sifting through a file. Aliso wasn't ignorant, of course. He noticed whose name was on the file - Becky Connelly. Aliso turned his attention to the Chief once more, putting the file in the back of his mind. “I'm glad you could come along, Mr. Aliso,” the Chief said, considerably more friendly than before. “It's not a problem, Chief. I'm just hoping that this doesn't turn into a full blown interview deal. This is supposed to be a meeting, right? I'm not here for the chit-chat, just the news. So, tell me, what happened to Becky?” Aliso heard himself say the words and they haunted him for some reason. Perhaps it just hit him that the next words to come out of the Chief's mouth would be what he had spent the last few days racking his brain over. Finally, he would learn who had killed her or how she had been killed. He waited with baited breath and involuntarily leaned forward. “The post-mortem is telling us two things. Number one; we know somebody is responsible for this. Ms. Connelly did not kill herself. When we checked the body, we found a considerable amount of internal bruising which indicates someone beating her pretty badly. No amount of self-inflicted damage could cause that much bruising, let me tell you,” the Chief said, taking a deep breath and preparing to tell Aliso the second piece of critical news. “The other thing we found on the body was fingerprints.” Aliso nearly fainted upon hearing this. Not only did the post-mortem reveal that somebody had carried out the murder of Becky, but also who had done the job. Aliso couldn't believe the killer would be so stupid to leave their own fingerprints on the body, intentionally or not. Everybody knew rule one of murder - leave no trace behind. Whoever had done the job had given the L.A.P.D. just what they needed - a path to put this scum behind bars. “There's something else, Mr. Aliso,” Bob butted in, “the prints, though they weren't exactly perfectly clear, were checked on the department's computer system. We got a conclusive match. Would you like to know who -- ” “Spit it out, already,” Aliso snapped at Bob with a tone even Aliso had never heard coming from his voice before. “Tony... it's your brother. Pete Aliso killed Becky Connelly that night,” Bob informed the impatient Aliso, almost like he was delivering the last rites. Everything stood still. Whatever hope Aliso had to rid himself of the feelings he had for Becky had just disappeared. The man responsible for the terrible act of ending her life was none other than Aliso's own flesh and blood. His brother, who he grew up with, spent countless days with and ultimately became the greatest of friends with, had killed the only woman he ever felt love for. Real love, that is. Aliso said nothing for several moments, prompting the L.A.P.D. men to leave the room, giving Aliso some time to gather his thoughts. It all seemed to fit together now, in a strange way. Aliso didn't want to admit it but that letter he read through tears the night she was killed made so much sense. He recalled its words exactly: “Pull out from your shot at glory, Tony. Pull out from everything you love. Give up while you still can... Or this bitch won't be the only one who dies. End it, Tony. Kill your dreams... or else.” Surely it was Pete Aliso, Tony's own brother, who had placed that letter for him to find. Pete wanted to draw attention to himself. Over the past few years, he had been treated as the forgotten member of the family, in a way. Never securing proper employment or security of life, he was seen as the outcast. On the other hand, Tony was praised for his efforts in both the L.A.P.D. and in the wrestling world. Jealousy's claw had consumed Pete's pure soul. He wanted to end Tony Aliso's shot at his dream of becoming the single greatest professional wrestler on the planet, a dream every man who wrestled for a living held close to his heart. There was no telling what this sick man who Aliso used to know as his loving brother would do next. Aliso stared down at the lino floor in the room and thought of the final words written: “End it, Tony. Kill your dreams... or else.” No matter what happened, Aliso would never pull out of Golden Turnbuckle Tournament 4. It was his calling in life at that moment in time. Wrestling was his driving force and even when he held the gorgeous Becky in his arms, he thought of it. For without it, Tony Aliso would not be the man he is today. To kill his dreams would be to kill himself. That wasn't on the schedule for Tony Aliso. It was time to give his brother a long overdue visit. *** Tony Aliso fought for a lot in his life. Whether it was his shot at fame, his father's respect and admiration or the woman of his dreams, he fought with everything he had. He wasn't a man to bet against when something needed to be done, either. Over his years in the L.A.P.D., he learned a few tricks, to say the least. The night before, Aliso had made a few calls to some old friends in the department about a small-scale operation he was carrying out. For the most part, they were intrigued, but unfortunately none of them had the time to help Aliso get it done. It didn't matter much, though. For Aliso, all he needed was himself for this job. Aliso drove in silence down Whittier Boulevard with only one thing on his mind - to rid himself of the brother he once trusted and loved. He was taking a huge gamble on visiting Pete's home on Union Pacific Avenue. Chances were that Pete, if he was smart, had high-tailed it last week before the results of the post-mortem had come out. There was a part of Aliso that hoped he was still there, however. A part of him wanted to see Pete squirm as Aliso executed one of his many cuffing methods. Still, this wasn't for show. It was for justice. It just so happened that on this cold, October night, Aliso was justice. He pulled up outside [detail withheld], a house which lacked any colour or depth whatsoever. The only admirable feature was an owl made of mahogany perched beside the front door. Pete had once told Aliso its meaning - it symbolised the ever-watching eye of God, as Pete was a very pious individual. Aliso always found the notion of it quite childlike and never acknowledged it. Tonight, for the first time, he did. Stepping up onto the grass which probably hadn't been cut in about three weeks, Aliso stared a hole through that owl. The intensity had taken over. Aliso broke out in a cold sweat, unaware of what was to come. There were no visible lights on in the house. Aliso peered in the glass but saw nobody. Maybe his visit was to ultimately end in vain. He was fully equipped for action, however, and wasn't about to squander this chance. Pete Aliso was his brother, but what he did deserved the highest form of punishment. In Aliso's book, that was a lifetime behind bars. No communication and no access to the outside world meant no harm could be done. Aliso patted his pockets and his holster, ensuring that all his equipment was present and accounted for. It was, and he calmed down slightly. After a moment, Aliso went around the side laneway and into the extensive back garden of Pete's home. It stretched for approximately twenty-five metres back, though it wasn't nearly wide enough. A small stream adorned the edge near the wall. The garden didn't look far off something one would see in a famous piece of art. Aliso tiptoed his way over to the screen door and attempted to pull it open. He had no idea why, but it opened. Had Pete been aware that Aliso was coming the whole time? Was this just a set-up? Aliso would soon find out. He stepped into the dark kitchen but was yanked backwards from behind suddenly. He lost his footing and tripped, falling hard on the concrete patio tiles. His assailant was obscured in the darkness but Aliso knew full well who it was. Pete Aliso knelt down beside his brother and gave him a hard slap across the head, as if to punish him for something he had done. Tony Aliso fought the searing pain in his back from the fall and pushed Pete back violently. Although he didn't fall, he turned around and swept some imaginary dust off his jacket, as if to send a message that he was untouchable. “Seems you've still got a lotta spunk left in your young ass, Tone. Still the same old brother I once knew,” Pete said, speaking for the first time. He began walking in circles around Aliso, trying to intimidate his younger brother. “I bet you see yourself as the main man in the family now, huh? You're like the man I never became, Tone. I gave everything to you, Tone. Everything you needed in life. Remember your first girlfriend, Amy?” Pete was prodding at Aliso's emotions now, a wise plan of attack. Aliso knew before he even considered coming to face his brother, though, that this would be the kind of verbal assault he could expect. Pete stood still in his tracks now, standing like a soldier at attention. Pete gave Aliso a once-over and shook his head in disbelief and disappointment. “I'm disappointed in you, Tone. You let yourself go when you stopped listening to me. And look at yourself now! You've ended up a professional wrestler!” Aliso was baffled. How had he let himself go when he stopped listening to his brother? Clearly, Pete had some serious issues with Aliso and his success. Aliso listened intently to Pete as he continued. “You've got the sweet life, Tone. Now... I want you to go back to the way you once were. No more wrestling, Tone. None. Just you and me ridin' high like we always used to.” Aliso thought about it but instantly dismissed the option. There was no way he would even consider forgiving Pete for what he had done. The heinous act he committed against another human being was simply outrageous. Aliso wanted this chat to end. He had to take action. “Pete, you're here preaching to me about my way of life? I think you ought to check yourself out, bro. I came here tonight to your house to put you away for the criminal act you committed,” Aliso took a cautious step over to his brother, “Put your hands out facing me and don't move a muscle. Everything's gonna be alright, bro. Just cooperate with me, okay?” Pete took a surprise swing at Aliso but the younger of the two brothers managed to recover and elbowed Pete in the sternum. Pete bent over in agony screaming like a mule. Aliso took his chance and snapped the first cuff onto Pete's wrist. Then Aliso stopped. Before he cuffed his brother completely, he had a final thing to say. “You know I'll never forgive you for what you did, Pete,” Aliso said, sternly, “We're all human and hell, we make a lot of mistakes but your breakdown has only made me see the light about you. Your jealousy has taken over you. You... are a shattered soul, broken like a mirror into a thousand fragments of the person you once were. Pete Aliso, you're a disgrace!” Aliso heard himself say it and realised that it was for the best. For everyone. Aliso snapped the other cuff onto Pete's right wrist with a violent force and shoved Pete towards the side entrance of his house. Aliso watched his brother walking awkwardly down the driveway to Aliso's BMW like he was on death row. Aliso opened the passenger door and Pete clambered in. Before Aliso closed the door after Pete, he said what he knew would his final words to Pete for a long time. “Pete, you tried to make me give up my dreams of wrestling to be the best. Bad move. In truth, no matter what you did, I wouldn't have even thought for a split second about retiring or opting out of Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. Not a damn chance, Pete. So, in a couple of days I get back into the ring in the third round. You, my friend, have got a ringside seat to watch everything that goes down,” Aliso snickered to himself, considering the move to allow Pete to be there to watch his match to be an uncouth yet deserving payback. As Aliso shut the door and got into the driver's seat, he thought of the thousands of fans who would, he hoped, be firmly behind him for his third round match. It was going to be one hell of a battle. And would fight for her. Her perfect gleaming eyes would look down on him with the sweetest intentions, guiding him. Though Aliso knew Becky would never come back, he also knew that her presence would never die. And in the past week since she had died, a new sense of determination sprung up in Aliso's mind, stronger than ever before. Tony Aliso's shattered soul was now rock hard and willing to brave anything that could possibly come at it. A new, more confident Aliso drove down the street lined with palm trees, staring deep into the eyes of his brother in the backseat, sad and alone. “Better get going, Pete. We've got a long drive to get to the arena,” Aliso remarked, grinning. There was no response from his handcuffed brother. The silence let Aliso think about everything, though. The past few days had been unlike anything experienced before by him. And come his round three match, a whole new experience would begin.
