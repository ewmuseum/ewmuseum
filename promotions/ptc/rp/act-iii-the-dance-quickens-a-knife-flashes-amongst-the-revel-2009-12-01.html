Act III: The Dance Quickens; A Knife Flashes Amongst the Revelers

1 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-01 “For some of you, what we will be undertaking comes with a heavy heart. To seek the extinction of your own kind is to pursue an enterprise without precedent or measure in the history of man. But know this: if there were truly such a thing as righteousness, our endeavor is just that. Our masters are the only true ones that exist in this bleak and desolate cosmos, and they have decreed that we prepare for their arrival. We, the finely-honed blades of the Old Ones, shall open the wounds on the mortal collective, and mankind will drown in its own poisoned blood. Through our message comes the promise of power, and in that knowledge comes the energy that feeds Their return to this world. As man comes to hear our gospel, and entertain the idea of embracing our great dogma, Their power will continue to grow. And through my victory, through my domination of their broken idols and false heroes in the wrestling arena, the power we channel for their resurrection will increase exponentially. Already the signs have begun to manifest across the globe; we’re witnessing tumultuous shifts in weather, disasters that rock society to its core, insanity taking root and flourishing worldwide, and blood-chilling phenomena that baffle religious leaders and atheist scientists alike. Some say the Apocalypse approaches, that Hell is preparing to widen its maw and devour the world. I say far worse is in store for those who refuse to bend knee and echo the appropriate reverence in light of Their coming. Wash the doubt from your souls, my children, for when the Earth is purged of the beast called humanity, you shall be cleansed of your fears and misgivings. Think no more of the heretics that turn their back on what you believe! Grieve not for those have cast you from their civilization of hypocrisy and hollow faith! By dooming them you shall be guaranteeing your own place in paradise, rewriting the damnable fairy tale of Genesis and helping to build a new Eden. And that Eden will be a place of beauty, eternal and undying and resplendent beyond your wildest dreams, its glory nourished by the very blood of the gutted gods that right now wish to see us fall! Rise to your destiny, and ready to partake in the masquerade that was ordained so long ago. We are the damned, the most blessed of pariahs. Their fist, Their blade, Their divine reckoning incarnate. And within us, Their wrath will stalk the land and prey upon the enemy. And if you‘re not prepared to accept that truth, you will die alongside them. Just like the interlopers that have trespassed amongst this congregation today. The acrid smell of lies follows and betrays you. Kill them…” ---Bruce Shanahan, sermon dated 9/20/2009; two Blackhelm Coalition operatives lost their lives that day, they will never be forgotten for their sacrifice Slade’s weapon of choice, a modified RPD assault weapon, was often the butt of jokes within the team. Even Major Stryker had scoffed at his hulking comrade’s custom-made firearm, deeming it excessive and once stating it would ‘be more effective as a boat anchor on the open sea’. Tonight he was eating his words, for it was the thunderous roar of the sergeant’s heavy weapon that prevented the Crying Seraph from swarming through the windows and swallowing the elite Blackhelm fire team in its teeming, bloodthirsty mass. The floor was blanketed in sizzling-hot shells as they fell in a steady stream from the mammoth gun, windows long since dissolved under the firepower of Russell Slade’s armament as he unloaded on the legion of fatigue- and shadow-cloaked shapes that raced between areas of cover beyond. Luther Ridley, surrounded by Stryker’s men, had long since fled back into the cover of darkness after the briefest of bullet-marked exchanges with his rival. The way the mercenary commander had seemed to dance between the streaking bullets did nothing to ease the major’s doubts about Ridley’s rumored pact with hellish benefactors. Millen and Webb were deployed upstairs, the steady rattle of fire from their position as equally crucial in stemming the flow of mercenaries as Slade’s customized special as it poured fiery lead into the chill autumn shadow, shadows that seemed alive amidst their shifting and thrashing. “How’s the ammo, sergeant?” asked Stryker, a bullet punching into the wall only a foot from his ear. He felt a certain dread at the answer. “Depends, Marcus,” replied the other in the gruff baritone he’d come to know so well. “If I’m picky about targeting, or lucky, another ten minutes or so. But I wouldn’t plan on standing much longer than that, sir.” “Hell.” Stryker grumbled. He thumbed the tab on his earpiece as the giant opened up with another deafening barrage, and somewhere in the darkness, a tree collapsed under the deluge of steel. “All units report in! Millen and Webb, status?” A shotgun blast rang out on the upper floor before a half-panicked growl penetrated the voice comm. “Tangos attempting to penetrate through the windows, using the trees. We’re still dug in near the door, ammunition supply is stable. Christ, major, they’re walking the branches like fucking spiders! How the hell can men move like that?!” “Hold as long as possible. Wilder and Grange, report!” “All steady here, Marcus,” Vanessa sighed, breathing heavily. “Basement still clear but they have tunnels beneath the structure. Afraid it’s only a matter of time before they make another run at us. We have barricades in place after their initial breach attempt but I wouldn’t depend on them holding too long. There’s a lot of these guys and they’re fucking good.” Stryker couldn’t help but feel guilt. The idea of leading his squad into a death trap didn’t hold well with him, intel failings be damned. The night had seemed too quiet, the task too simple. He should have listened to his gut and now his allies would be paying for his arrogance. “Major!” Ridley howled from somewhere in the darkness, his voice tinged with the mad glee of a killer smitten with his own work. “I’m impressed with the spirit of you and your team. But do you realize how futile this is? Surely you know you‘ve seen your last sunrise?” Marcus had tried to suppress the thought, regardless of how much it pushed itself back to the front of his mind. But he knew enough about the skill of Ridley and the Crying Seraph to acknowledge his team may die here in the remote wilderness of the Pine Barrens. It happened to everyone, and even sooner in their profession than most. It was the gambit one takes in the dutiful protection of brother, nation, and in their case, species. He paused a moment as a spent banana clip clattered to the floor, another abruptly slapped into place to resume the other’s duty. There would be no reinforcements, no timely extraction, no extra lives or continue screen. Death waited in those trees, and it would take a small miracle to get them out alive. If they didn’t feel the blessing of that divine intervention, Bruce Shanahan would have his head as a trophy before dawn painted the world again… Something else clattered to the floor. They recognized it, but it was too late for either Stryker or Slade to avoid. At the same time there was an explosion downstairs, and a clatter at the front door. As the flash bang grenade’s blast sent the tandem’s senses in a tizzy, Ridley’s crew of killers breached the opening and swept inside, kicking their foe’s weapons out of reach and pinning them under the barrels of their guns. It was done with such precision Stryker was sure not even the most proficient of SEALS could have believed it. They toyed with us , Stryker thought silently, fighting back a heartsick rage in the face of Luther’s masked infantry. He had the manpower and equipment to stop us in the beginning. Why didn’t he?? Heavy boots marked the stride of Luther Ridley as he marched victoriously into the room, brandishing a nickel-plated revolver. Slade made a last gasp effort for the closest firearm, Stryker’s semi-auto, but a swift kick to the jaw emphatically ended his desperate lunge. Through the turmoil shrill in his voice comm, he knew Wilder and Grange had met a similar fate. Even with high ground, Millen and Webb wouldn’t last much longer. The hammer of Ridley’s antique sidearm clicked in place. Stryker, defiant through the fury in his baleful eyes, waited for his execution. With an outstretched hand, Ridley lowed the rifle muzzle of the closest soldier. He smiled. It was playful, like a cat with its paw on the throat of a captured mouse. “Shanahan will be at Sterling’s home in two days. Along with Rourke and Mizakawa. It may be your best chance to take him. Gather your men, pull out, and be there. “Why?” hissed the major. “The thrill of the hunt. And the desire to meet a rival in battle one more time, pure and outside the standard tactics of war. No ambushes or traps. Two men with the hearts of soldiers, together in the trenches, locked in mortal combat. I’ve heard tale of you, Major Stryker, and of your martial prowess. If there is a man that will end my command of this unit, I prefer it to be you. Otherwise…” He perused the ranks of his own masked legion. All wore black hoods, their faces veiled. Most curious to Stryker were the infernal sigils dotting their uniforms. He could feel an evil force dominating the room, and with a look at Ridley‘s uniform, noted that it lacked the same blasphemous symbols as his underlings. A realization overtook him. A realization of a lie. “…it’s hard to say when HQ will issue my final orders.” He wasn’t positive of the sensation he felt right then; the duty of a soldier toward his misguided own, or the tug of a greater fate. But he had another job to do, perhaps as great as his Blackhelm allegiance. “Semper fi.” * * * * He had expected something far more ornate than a rundown sanatorium as the model for a gateway into the unknown. But in the occult world that the Blackhelm Coalition forever was a bridge to, Maury supposed nothing was as it seemed. Especially not as it seemed in the aspect of appearance. But this was it, the place that one of the head council claimed was ‘where the truth of the universe found its way in’. Dawson couldn’t help but wonder what other locales held such gateways between the worlds, doors breaking the illusion spun to protect humanity from itself. They stood in a semi-circle behind him on the asphalt lot leading up the stairs. Still they were masked by drawn-up hoods, and as they spoke quickly in turn, continued to carry the eerie monotone speech of one voice intonating from several places at once. “Within these walls rest our great secrets. The source of our knowledge we use against the enemies of mankind, the enemies of God…” “…Never has an outsider violated this place, or even come to know of its existence. Even Shanahan still believes it to be little more than legend. Quite fortunate for us…” “…Fortunate indeed, for were it to fall, the Blackhelm Coalition would be crippled, perhaps destroyed. Within this place, this fortress, lies assets that even we have been hesitant to put into play as this war with the dark ones has raged across countless ages…” “…It would shift the balance, and the equilibrium must be maintained. For otherwise to happen could tear apart the fabric of the great cosmic, and doom multiple time-realities and dimensional spaces.” “Let us enter, Maury Dawson. If you are to lead us in this fight against Bruce Shanahan’s evil, we must show you what is at your disposal. Just as a general must know the details of the legions in his battlefield arsenal, so must you know of the arcane agents to deploy in this conflict.” The inner foyer spoke volumes of the institution’s current state, a rusted and decaying relic lost to time. There was more primer than paint in view across the walls, pipes crisscrossing above his head stained with the rust-brown color of neglect. In a half-broken wooden chair, its ancient red paint peeling away in strips, a child looked up at him. Dawson was puzzled but couldn’t immediately say why; the boy was normal, his clothes that of any child his age, around nine years old to Dawson’s estimation, and a warm smile of greeting settled on his lips. In his hands he twisted at the axis of a Rubix cube, lowering it to his side as he peered up at Maury. “Hello,” he said, blue eyes twinkling as he fumbled with the cube. He crouched to eye level with the boy. The youth shied away, retreating a step while his grip tightened on the toy. “Does your mother work here?” He shook his head. “No, sir. My dad does.” He pointed over his shoulder. Curious, Dawson pivoted to face what the boy was indicating. Five of the robed council had brought him to this sacred place, and as he watched, one lowered the hood that had veiled him ever since the moment of their introduction. The figure revealed beneath the sinister grey robe wore a pleasant visage, offering a gentle smile at the boy as he knelt to pat him softly on the cheek. Dawson had expected something horrible beneath those hoods, not unlike the life-draining wraiths of rustic myth. But he was no kin to the image of Death; the menacing Blackhelm councilman had the idyllic face of a man who could be working for an accounting firm, golfing on the weekends between family outings. “Go along and play, Milo.” The child turned and raced off down the hallway, brushing by a smiling woman orderly as he ran. His steps reverberated down the corridor and he turned to face Maury Dawson. “Not what you expected, is it?” he asked with a grin. “You don’t have to say it. You envisioned a face so covered in wrinkles I’d look like a prune. Maybe a skull, tatters of flesh still hanging from bleached-white bone?” Dawson couldn’t suppress a grin of his own. It wasn’t all that far off. “Yeah, kinda. It’s sort of an intimidating appearance.” The other grinned. “I’m Gabriel. I‘m fine with being called by name. The others…” He looked back at the others, none of which had followed suit in revealing themselves. “Well…I’m the youngest of the council. The new guy. And they said you and I may identify with one another.” He paused, glancing amongst those filing through the lobby. None seemed to pay them attention. It was as if they were merely spectres observing, gliding among the living. Ebeneezer Scrooge and his Christmas ghosts. “You’ve been chosen to lead this fight, Maury. Are you ready to see what lurks on the fringe between worlds? The hidden resources of the Blackhelm Coalition?” A nod. A grin in return from Gabriel, speaker of the council. “I’m pleased. And so are they. Please, follow me.” He took Dawson down a long hallway, equally unkempt in its maintenance. Cobwebs wallpapered the ceiling and the linoleum floor was spotted with red, brown and green stains. Maury didn’t want to know what the stains were and chose not to ask about their origin. A scream split the air as they walked. Gabriel seemed unaffected, as if it were commonplace. “Not everyone in these cells are tools of the Coalition,” he explained, passing a door with a plate-glass window and a tortured face pressed against it. “Many are truly unfortunate lunatics lost to friends, family, and society. A majority have been incarcerated here for decades, nothing but their insanity for company. Ah, here we are.” He pulled a silver key from his pocket and unlocked the chamber. Through the porthole Dawson saw a lonely man sitting on a bench, hair a greasy and uncombed mess. As the door opened on rusted hinges, he looked up. His eyes were cold, tired. “What the…?” The man’s flesh was a veritable mass of scar tissue, as if not an inch of his flesh had ever escaped the cut of a razorblade. Even through the weary-eyed expression and exhaustion, he proffered a weak smile and pulled himself to his feet with the help of a rail on the wall. “G-Gabriel? What a surprise.” “Hello Kerry. How are you holding up?” A meager shrug, scratching at his shoulder. For a moment it looked as if something stirred under the skin of his shoulder, scurrying away down his back as his fingertips caressed the surface. “Pain. It never goes completely away. But the sedatives give me relief. It all feels numb. Praise be to God for his blessing in that.” “This is Maury Dawson. An agent of our organization.” “Oh?” “He’s joined the fight. And we thought it was important to show him just what our guests can do to help him.” There was a flash of something on Kerry’s face, a strange hybrid of both anguish and adulation. He nodded and held out a terribly mutilated hand, some of his fingernails completely gone from his fingers. “How much do you want to know?” “Tell us of what’s occurring now in Wyatt Manor. Which of his lieutenants are present, how many of his followers patrol the grounds. What sort of devils protect that blackened kingdom in Vermont.” He held out a blood-stained razorblade. Dawson, stricken with horror, watched as the man took the instrument and ran it quickly down his arm with a grunt and a cringe. It took only a moment for blood to begin pouring from the laceration, spilling onto the floor in a broadening puddle. Though Kerry’s face went pale, he sighed and began to speak through closed eyes. “Faulkner and Griffin watch over the infernal flock. Fifty, perhaps sixty.” He faltered for a moment, held up by Gabriel whose hand gripped the slashed arm. Dawson, repulsed, felt faint as he watched the other stagger under the loss of blood, wavering like a flower in the breeze. As it continued to flow in a crimson stream, his breath never left him. “Dark Young lay dormant in the wilderness. A cross, heavy with the robes of the wicked…trees, pine trees, shredded by gunfire…angels falling to Earth, crying that accursed name…Cthul…” “Get the nurses!” demanded Gabriel, holding up the limp inmate as he collapsed into the councilman’s arms. Maury turned, waiting for one of the other robed wraiths to retrieve help. None did. It was as if they were elsewhere, oblivious to the world and Kerry’s suffering. “Bastards.” Dawson snarled at them, racing to the desk to get medical help to that rubber-walled cell. It was only seconds before white-garbed personnel raced inside, attending to him as he lay still in a pool of his own blood. “He’ll be fine,” assured Gabriel as they left the doctors to dress the wounds. “That was only an inkling of the knowledge he can find. The rest comes when the stitches are removed. The larger the wound, the more stitches taken away, the more Kerry will know. He has been a great lexicon for us, and in some cases has interpreted entire texts for us. Better yet, a catalyst for stopping massacres and genocide, having intercepted and deciphered messages from Shanahan. As for what he began eluding to before he lost consciousness, I can‘t say. Sometimes he sees other things, perhaps prophecy. Or so it seems.” “…Sometimes wounds open all their own, when God wants us to know things we don’t know to ask. It is His way. It‘s a miracle.” The other shuddered, still fighting the urge to wretch at the sight left in their wake. Kerry was part of the ’arsenal’, but could he bring himself to order self-mutilation for the sake of his duty? “I want to know something. It’s something I’ve never fully understood.” “And that is?” “Why all of this? Bruce is a monster. Culling him from the world surely would save us this kind of suffering.” Gabriel continued his march, but addressed the question. “Shanahan is a force. Or at least, what resides in him is. The decision not to kill him has nothing to do with virtue or moral obligation, and it’s not fear of any ambitious lieutenants who could cause more damage than he could. None of them are as aspiring or as cunning as he is. Quite simply, Maury, there are people in this world with particular destinies, souls of an extraordinary nature. Why is this? Even we don’t know. They just are. “…We can only inhibit his nature, contain his evil until he meets the destruction that has been preordained for him. To do otherwise would be to compromise the equilibrium. And maintaining the equilibrium is our mission, above all others…” “…We guard humanity from its foes, internal and external, but should it endanger the equilibrium, mankind will be extinguished. Such is the path we are to follow. Even if much of it is so alien to us.” Gabriel had halted. He reached for the door and paused. Maury, an observant man, knew he was trying to hide his reluctance, as if he could predict the other’s reaction before even opening the cell. “This one has visions in a mirror. What he reveals to us is still cryptic, but often much more discernible than Kerry’s revelations. We refer to him as ‘The Viewer’.” He opened the door, not needing a key to do so. Dawson reeled. It was the boy, Milo. His ‘cell’ could hardly be called such; it lacked the rubber walls or restraints of so many others nearby, and was decorated like any child‘s bedroom one would find in the outside world. A television, a game system, child-sized bed with colorful linen, movie and music posters lining the walls, a body-length mirror. As they entered the room, the boy looked up from the brightness of the video game he was playing. Just like when Maury had first met the child, he welcomed them with the same innocent smile before looking up at Gabriel. “Hello Mister Gabriel.” “Hey Milo. I’d like you to meet someone. This is Mister Dawson. Can you say hello to him?” Cocking his head to one side slightly, he waved his hand. “Hello Mister Dawson. It’s nice to meet you.” He turned back to his game. “I’m under the impression you’re not actually his father?” “No, I’m not his real father. But when I come here, I treat him as my own child. His family was killed about three years ago. Torn to pieces. Our guess is that whatever he saw in the mirror at the time gazed back, and came through. We had those memories erased so as to spare him a lifetime of nightmares.” “…You had his memories erased??” A nod, as if it were nothing unusual. Standard clean-up, like washing dishes. “Some of our psychics in the east wing performed the operation, if you want to call it that. We brought him here, and we’ve treated him well ever since. We’re not going to undermine his childhood anymore than is necessary for the good of the world. Milo is treated by some of our staff like their own blood, like extended family. We care for him. However, we still recognize he’s vital to our duties, and as such we have no choice but to restrict his contact with the outside world.” “Milo cannot be compromised,” spoke the first of the wraiths. “For him to die young may be nearly as cataclysmic as the untimely demise of Shanahan…” “…The boy’s innocence is key. He is yet uncorrupted, and cannot be misguided while under our protection. He sees through a child’s eyes. There is no hate, no perversion to obstruct what he views through the mirror. What Milo sees is truth.” “Three men have been assigned to attempt to witness his viewings,” Gabriel recalled. “Two went violently insane. The other was torn apart, as if some invisible entity swept into the room and dismembered him. Like a little kid tears the wings and legs off of flies. Cameras see only Milo’s reflection. We have no way of knowing exactly what he sees, or even why he‘s immune to whatever malevolence lurks on the other side. But we fear when his innocence slips away, and he comes of the age to question the world he knows, that immunity will die.” What Maury Dawson felt wasn’t empowerment or wisdom. It was trepidation, a sickness tightening in the bowels of his stomach. He saw manipulation, imprisonment, and, in his eyes, a dark perversion of faith. He shook his head, staring at the happy boy innocently playing his game in his prison. “This is wrong, Gabriel. It’s madness. There‘s nothing good or right about what‘s happening here. It‘s twisted, it‘s vile…” “Please don’t condemn us. You may call it a curse, but we call it a blessing. This is what has to be.” “They have been sent to aid us in preserving humanity. Is that really an affliction worth lamenting, Maury Dawson?…” “…They are the chosen children of God, and He has bestowed upon them a sense of His heavenly power. They are gifted with knowledge, and integral to our protection of this world and the equilibrium.” He still had questions. There were other things he needed to see. Was this really the truth behind the illusion, the energies and bleak actuality concealed from men for their own well-being? How much was just a manifestation of distorted faith? Gabriel read the horror on his dubious agent’s face. Sympathetically, he laid a hand on his shoulder. “I know. You expected bright lights, the soothing touch of God, healing and love. But that isn’t always the way of things. I’m sorry, Maury, but at times the ways of Heaven appear to border on the ways of Hell.” “Do you think it’s really worth doing this to a human being, simply because you see them as tools in some cosmic war?” “Isn’t is a rhetorical question?” “Not if you’re human.” Gabriel bit his lip, sighing mournfully as he watched the boy work his game controller. The others showed no such sign of reflection, only turning their heads to gaze at one another quizzically in turn. To them, Maury realized there was no such musing. Their own humanity had long since fled their mortal forms. To ask them to think like a human being was like asking a dog to think like an elephant. That simple fact terrified Maury Dawson more than any diabolical scheme that would ever swell in Bruce Shanahan’s mind. * * * * The feast was resplendent, if not eccentric in its course selection. But such was expected of Wilhelm Von Krauser, a schizophrenic once called the Suave Sadist for his personality in the wrestling world. A ruthless debonair, charming yet merciless, he was Bruce ’Violence Jack’ Shanahan’s premier choice for overseer of the Sect’s growing European empire. A rivulet of wine coursed from the goblet and onto his hand. Shanahan quickly wiped it away, hoping his host hadn’t seen his breach of manners; the obsessive-compulsive and perfectionist German would be mortified. “I’m curious, Brother Shanahan,” Krauser started, elegantly biting into a morsel of meat. “What will the GTT crown mean to you if there isn’t a world left to appreciate your strength in earning it? There is no glory in a prize the world will never see.” “Some will. Besides…” He bit into a piece himself, stifling his initial reaction to the heavily-salted entree. “…the glory isn’t what matters. The Old Ones will ultimately receive the laurels. We are but their arm of redemption, an instrument. My pride comes in knowing their plan comes to fruition through me as their vessel.” Wilhelm grinned, snapping his fingers as more wine was poured into his glass. “The world will become aware of Them through our gospel, and in our dominance with each mounting triumph,” the American madman continued. “It’s a question of the energies and how they are transferred through the cosmos, through the dimensions in which They rest, feeding our lords. The science isn’t for me to know, only that it is the reality we’re set to bring into motion. An ant doesn’t question gravity. Who am I to question Their ways?” Krauser smirked and chuckled in a low bass. “Well now, it’s good that you didn’t lose your touch.” “I was reigning champion of JUST Wrestling prior to the competition. I defeated two world-class opponents. I’d hardly refer to that as ring-rust, Brother Krauser. But I digress. How progresses the European theater of operation? The indoctrination process?” Krauser opened his mouth and began to speak, but all sound had been drained from his senses. His heart began to race. There was no outward reason for it. But it held precedent, and triggered alarm. It took Bruce Shanahan only a second to react. “Down!” Bullets poured into the room. Priceless artwork was shredded beyond recognition, furniture reduced to a pile of splinters. Krauser’s decadent feast rained down on them as if poured into a mincer above their heads. It was a momentary barrage. Sirens began to blare as the security system kicked into gear, summoning the German’s own wealth of guards. As they trickled into the mansion and across the property, Shanahan spotted the would-be killer. He was poised just beyond the lengthy car that Krauser employed as his transport, garbed in fatigues. But his most standout feature was the veil. “That mask….?” breathed Violence Jack. Krauser was stunned to see that even the American mastermind seemed dumbfounded. “This can’t be!” The attacker had donned a pallid mask, its perfection broken by yellow slashes. He lingered just long enough for his target to observe the mask before racing to make his escape. “You look as if you’re just seen a ghost.” “No. Far more than a ghost.” “Elaborate.” Krauser’s security force had poured into the room, semi-automatic weapons at the ready, surrounding the two VIP's. “Craven. Randal Craven.” “Surely you’re wrong,” commented the German. He knew well of Craven, Shanahan’s pious ex-friend and now a priest in Philadelphia. Randal was a man incapable of homicide in the mad German’s view, an individual who believed in the sacred stature of holy judgement long before ever seeking his own. “A trick of the light perhaps.” “No. The pallid mask with the yellow slashes. As I sent him when the masquerade commenced. There is no duplicate of it.” “Gamble? One of Mizakawa‘s Yakuza assassins?” “Gamble’s not that stupid. I hardly think he has the gall to attempt to kill me. He lacks the killer‘s edge. And I highly doubt Mizakawa‘s clan of jackals would try it. They have no way of knowing I‘m here. It‘s Blackhelm. They‘ve finally done it.” Wilhelm Von Krauser nodded. Things had escalated far more quickly than they had calculated. “They made their first attempt on my life. Discretion has fled their feeble, corrupted little minds. And it has condemned every member of their despicable organization.” * * * * Click. “Yes?” “I can give you Bruce Shanahan’s location two days from now. Your boss, Tatsuya Mizakawa, will be there with him.” “Who is this??” “Shanahan’s enemy. Which, by proxy, makes me your friend.” “Why should we believe you? What evidence do you have that you’re telling the truth?” “If you want to topple the Sect of Black Wisdom, and utilize the relic for the good of your people, it’s in your best interest to follow my instructions.” “I see. Go on.” “Bring the guns. All you can muster in the next 24 hours. He’ll be present at the home of Marion ‘Hemlocke’ Sterling on the outskirts of the New Jersey Pine Barrens. His own men will be present on the property, but they’re undisciplined rabble. You’ll also find Mizakawa. A third party will be joining us as well. I suggest you treat them with the same impartial hostility as Shanahan’s den of mindless dogs. Wipe out everyone in sight and we‘ll both be happy.” “Forgive my curiosity, my American friend. But why do you want to see Shanahan taken down? Who are you aligned with that seeks his death?” “He’s already been damned and I want to see the job finished. I won‘t see the masquerade end at his behest. When the music stops, I‘ll have his empire, and the immortality that comes with it. That should suffice for now.” “Very well. Shall I presume any further business partnership?” “Unlikely. Give no quarter.” Click.
