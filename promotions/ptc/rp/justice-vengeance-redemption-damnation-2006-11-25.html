Justice & Vengeance, Redemption & Damnation

25 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-25 It seemed appropriate that the day was cold and rainy, an icy shower pouring from the skies like the tears of some grieving god. But if so one actually existed, and as loving and benevolent as the holy books proclaimed, his worshippers would pray he react more proactively than merely shedding the fruits of his sorrow. What transpired across His dominion below could easily spell not only the end of his flock, but of his Heavenly grace as well. Such was the ambition of his most fanatical human enemy. The machinations of a sociopath, a zealot devoted to none but the most abominable of gods. A mortal seeking the destruction of immortals. Bruce Shanahan's schemes would not be easily quelled as he continued to reel in Armageddon. "I trust you're adequately prepared, Brother Tully? The other only nodded, a peal of thunder echoing in the silence. "Jacob McKail shall be dealt with most decidedly. I've never felt more confident in my ability to destroy a man. His body, his mind, his soul…I'll crush whatever must be to see him fall." A grim smile from the mastermind. "Where the great masquerade failed, so shall the Old Ones see our triumph on this occasion. With victory this world will come to see our power. Their heroes vanquished and spirits crushed. The faith will grow and, in due time, the nonbelievers shall become the heretics and the outcasts." "Sometimes the most successful plans are the simplest. Our roles require no masks to ensure victory." The room was barren but for two creaky chairs and the metallic table separating them. Doctor Rourke had given them license to speak amongst themselves unsupervised at the behest of his most valued patient. Being the project that could make or break a man's career had its perks and Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan wasn't too prideful to use whatever avenues were granted to him. With the rain continuing to pelt the roof of the Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane and thunder rolling ever closer in the distance, his plans continued to unfurl. Shanahan, the consummate mastermind, pursed his lips as he thought. Pessimism was a necessary evil at this pivotal point in the scheme. No mistakes could be afforded out of vanity. "This depends on your ability to get the job done, Horace. If so it happens then you will have done two things I have failed to do in my time both as a wrestler and a vassal for the masters. You will have won the GTT tournament, and you will have brought us one step closer to the cleansing." He smiled like a proud father. "The cataclysm that will bring about the end of this wretched race will come about by your hands." "Ah yes. A horrible tragedy it would be, eh?" Tully smirked, his expression radiating all the warmth of an Arctic wind. He was as soulless as his lord before him. "The only tragedy is that they've persisted as long as they have. Too many centuries of senseless war and conflict have passed without our interceding." The smile vanished and his expression became grave. "We will overthrow their God, their beliefs, and all they hold true and faithful in this world of lies. But it depends on your success. Jacob McKail is but one part of the plan and an element equally vital to the others. The world must be a witness to our ascension. In faith, in spirit, and in battle." He did nothing to veil his concern. His subordinate would see right through such a display anyhow; they'd come too far to feign certainty when every caution must still be taken. "You once said that Brother Knight had exceeded me in the Sect. His ambition had overtaken my own. Why now do you grant me such a boon, Father Shanahan?" Lightning flashed beyond the barred windows, thunder bellowing over the hills. "That was past. We all have roles to fulfill as we await Their coming, Brother Tully. And yours is at my side as the most trusted of my advisors. "Long has the world seen my failures in this particular forum. With your win, in their minds, comes the passing of the torch. Where I was weak, a stronger force rises to take my place. Millions will see it and come over to the side that sits on the cusp of domination. And not even the Church, now even the Vatican in all its fear with its cloak-and-dagger tactics, will be able to stop us. They cannot stop a world that willingly gives itself over to the new gospel. They would only damn themselves and the transparent God they've spent centuries spilling blood and spreading lies for." He smiled, a morbid gesture warped by the vision of a shattered world. "My brother, in time you shall become their messiah." * * FLASH * * The blond man removed his sunglasses, folding them before placing them gently into his pocket. His eyes were dark, matching a cold expression forged of stone. His host smiled almost fondly and offered a meager bow. Merely a token of politeness and the grace he'd been so long used to. There was resentment on both sides, for varying reasons. "Ethan Knight," breathed the latter. "Clinton Sage. Pleased to finally meet you." Chill and baleful came the greeting, a compliment to his stare. "I'm not here for idle chat, sir. I'm here about…." "Business. I'm well-aware of that and I've been expecting you." With a flourish, the wealthy homeowner beckoned for his guest to follow. "Not you necessarily, but someone from your organization. Seeing as Shanahan himself is predisposed it was natural that one his advisors would come." The home was still in veritable ruins, literally a shell of its former glory. Ethan wagered that repairs were just now beginning but Sage didn't seem the least bit uncomfortable dwelling amongst the wreckage of his old castle. Hated as he may have been by the locals, he had the cash to ensure his own security. "And you knew of this…how?" Knight found himself a tad puzzled by Sage's insight. He'd assumed only the Sect and the Coalition had been searching for the long-lost relics and surely no one from either side had tipped off Clinton. Blackhelm didn't even know the last remaining tie to the Eyes of Fear still drew breath, and until two days ago neither had he. "A hunch. I may have been eliminated from GTT competition but I'm well-aware of the situation you and your associates are in. My sources are….vast in both number and radius, Mr. Knight. Your high priest is in the asylum in Weirsburg while Horace Tully competes in his place. And you are tasked with overseeing the labors of Shanahan's plotting. He's well-studied in the Eyes and their bloody legacy." He stopped momentarily, looking back with a dry grin at Ethan. "My informants are pretty good." Knight admired the fauna of Clinton's mansion as they traversed the carpeted corridors, what remained intact in the aftermath of whatever had razed the back rooms. In his opinion, twisted though it was, the artwork lacked the vision of what he was accustomed to back in the Sect's chilling Vermont abode, but it had its quirks. Sage paid his guest's curiosity no mind as he took a chair in the sprawling den, eyes pressed into the back of the other as he examined a large painting on the near wall. A recreation of the war in Heaven, the battle in which Lucifer was cast from the edge of Empyrean into the abyss with his fallen angels. It was macabre and more to his liking than the more abstract portraits garnishing the halls and decor of Sage's mansion. "Impressive piece," observed the cold-blooded Sect lieutenant. "Though I must admit it seems an odd selection for a man whose parents were supposedly slain by mad zealots." "It was a bargain worth much than its listed price at auction. Difficult to turn down. It has its aesthetic value but will fetch an even prettier penny in time." Clinton Sage commented on the portrait somberly, his mind obviously resting on greater things than the ornaments in his home. "But, and forgive me for being rude, didn't you say that you were here on something of a time constraint?" Knight smiled. "Of course. No apology necessary, you're correct." He pulled himself away, sitting on a soft chair opposite his host. The other leaned back with hands foldedon his lap. "You came about the meteors. I'm well-aware of that. Ask your questions. Tea or coffee?" Knight replied with a shake of the head. "No thank you. This visit will be too brief I'm afraid. Even for a cup of coffee." "As you wish," Sage shrugged. "Very well then. You're obviously in a hurry. So I'll tell you what I know to save you time." The Sect lieutentant would never admit to being excited over the prospect offered here, but doing so would be much greater than any white lie he'd ever told. Sage was said to be the caretaker of relics that the Blackhelm Coalition, its brother and sister organizations, and even the Church itself would kill to lay their hands on. How he had hidden such a fact for so long was amazing and bordering on genius considering the brand of resourceful cutthroats that had long hunted them. Only scraps of information existed now, the Eyes of Fear long dispersed and the archives detailing their exploits gutted by flames, but not even what the Sect possessed contained the sort of cosmic power long hinted at in these rocks. 'Gifts from the Old Ones' are what Father Shanahan had said, gifts proffered long before he had ever so much as entertained such ideals as he now preached. "I know nothing, Ethan Knight. Not a wretched, trifling detail that would help you or justify your trip here." His guest's heart plunged into his now-tumbling stomach. "When Harlan Raines rode the lightning this summer, any true knowledge of them died with him. I can offer you nothing more than to say that they're an instrument of pain and destruction. There's no secret code to awakening their powers and no process to enrich us about who or what sent them to Earth in those decades past." It was a combinstion of rage and disappointment that held Ethan Knight immobilized in his chair, frowning and dumbfounded. Which was greater not even he could say. "A blind alley," he mumbled to himself at least, brushing his nose with one gloved hand. A nose red with anger and impatience, shamed in frustration. "A goddamn dead end." Clinton said nothing as his forlorn visitor sighed, himself turning to the steaming cup on the wooden stand to his back left. The other's hopes were dashed but perhaps it was better that way. He was well instructed as to the exploits of the deranged Sect of Black Wisdom, it's sinister father-figure and his crew of mindless dogma junkies. And that was nothing to say of the likes of Ethan Knight or Marion 'Hemlocke' Sterling, his well-refined assassins and suitors of Death itself. This was the best way. He couldn't bring himself to aid in genocide. * * FLASH * * "What of Stryker and the mongrels he commands here?" Tully sniffed. Shanahan was well aware of his adamant distaste for Marcus even if Violence Jack held a professional respect for the man and his conviction. "He came calling, just as you claimed he would. His suit-wearing dogs were well in tow, strapped as expected. The stench of the Vatican was all over them. The Church's most elite corp of killers, I'd wager. They value what Marcus seeks." "As is to be expected. And?" He already knew the answer but inquired anyhow. Marcus was predictable in his pursuit of justice, the self-righteous prick he was and always had been. It didn't require prodding from his employers in Rome to guarantee that. "Was he as forthcoming as I said he would be?" "He promised that he'd burn down the manor and torch any of us inside, if that's what you're asking." Tully shook his head, but whether it was in disgust or amusement was indiscernible. "Honest for a Blackhelm weasel. They tend to play politics much more. It's not just a job to him, he hates us." Shanahan's eyes wandered around the room for a moment before falling back tot he table in front of him. Not confined in the jacket for once (another favor granted of the courteous Doctor Rourke), his hands fell upon the surface and his fingertips drummed in rhythm with the falling rain. "It was never a job to Marcus Stryker. Though I believe his brush with death a few years ago caused his tunnel vision to narrow even more." "Turning on one's savior seems rather lacking for a man who claims to be so noble," scoffed the other. "His only savior now is Jesus Christ, Brother Tully. Serve the Lord and his house on Earth and Marcus will be at your side even when death is imminent. Turn your back on the Church and you may as well rank among the princes of Hell in his eyes. I would never put it past him to kick down the gates and lead a force there if his masters in Italy demanded it." Another crash of thunder, lightning brightening the room like the mid-day sun for the most fleeting of moments. Fire raged in Tully's eyes, the passion he'd taken in the time elapsed since his GTT appointment. "I'm not worried about Stryker as much as I am Dawson. He feels scorned after last year. But he's not a blind advocate of the Church like Stryker or his men. He's in things for himself. He's a wild card." Bruce smiled. "I trust you know the feeling well. Just as you hold the key to what may pass in this GTT, so does Maury hold the final hand in this situation. All of us have revealed our cards except Maury. I've long called Marcus's bluff, and as such the situation with Janssen and her disciples seems well in check. Even Clinton Sage and the well-secreted knowledge he possesses seems to be holding true. But Maury will adhere to Stryker's brand of justice only so long. He's seen things Marcus only thinks he knows. He paused as if a thought had just now popped into his head. Calm and collected as if he were asking about the weather, he smiled at Tully. "The situation with Clinton Sage? Brother Knight has seen to it all?" His lieutenant smiled. "Taken care of." * * FLASH * * Sage lowered the coffee cup in his hand, drips of the beverage dripping onto the saucer. He smiled as a familar click echoed behind him. "Malicious as I expected. You and your bastard mentor will stop at nothing will you?" Knight shook his head, eyes jabbing angrily into the rear of the other's head. He knew lies when he heard them. Sage may have been truthful in his claims of knowing little but the Shanahan's ex-right-hand man knew a lie when he heard it. There was still more to this puzzle than he was revealing. "You know more than that. Tell me." Insistent and determined to take any knowledge he still had to the grave, Sage declined the offer with another 'no'. * * FLASH * * Shanahan drew himself up, staring vaguely out the window at the skeletal trees that dotted the hilled landscape. "I am the maestro, and this is my symphony. A symphony that a year ago serenaded a masquerade of altered roles and subterfuge. This year things are not so complicated. It's redundant. With times comes wisdom, and my eyes have been opened to the true path." Tully remained in his chair. The madness of his mentor now gleamed in his own dark sockets, a twin no less malicious or convicted. Their time was dawning. "I once promised to awaken the world. And this is the time. We cannot be stopped. No man, no church, no government will prevent our revelation from sweeping the globe." * * FLASH * * Sage showed no fear as he turned to face the sleek, silver barrel of the .38 directed between his eyes. Nothing would rattle him. Not even the face of death, the reaper known to so many as Ethan Knight. "Then it goes with me to the grave," he said almost silently. "I won't condemn the world with even the limited amount I know." Ethan only grinned as he fingered the trigger. "A pity," he hissed. "Then you've served your purpose. Farewell for now." He pulled the trigger. * * FLASH * * Shanahan smiled as lightning struck again. Not even God in all his might would challenge him now. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan would see his Heavenly throne toppled. "And thus comes the darkness. Thus comes the truth of the universe."
