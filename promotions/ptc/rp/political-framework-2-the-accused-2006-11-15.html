Political Framework 2 - The Accused

15 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-15 In the Sawyer and Associates office in Los Angeles, California, private investigator Richard Holmes had his chair reclined and his feet propped on his desk. He was on his third cigarette of the last hour, and the pile of paper in front of him wasn't getting any smaller. In its physical form, the paperwork was very little compared to another burden that rested on his shoulders. His boss had been killed almost a month ago, and after spending two weeks in Spain, Richard had returned to the office in California with no further suspects or leads on the case. Sawyer had been poisoned, that was certainly the cause of death. His number one suspect from the get-go was the maid, whom he had questioned relentlessly during his time in Spain. Holmes was skeptical that she had actually put the poison in the drink, but her fingerprints were found on the glass, so there was no doubt she delivered it. Was this enough to take it to court, and put the poor woman in prison? Of course it was. If anything he had learned under Sawyer's wing, Holmes was well aware of one of the prime rules in detective-work, and that was to always look behind the obvious. The woman had claimed that she mixed the drink under no awareness that there was poison involved, and Holmes had somewhat believed her. At the very least, he was considering the option, something he considered absolutely necessary. That wasn't to say that it would stop him from putting her in jail if it came to it, but Sawyer had a lot of enemies, and the lowly maid didn't make the long list. There were others out there who had much more incentive to murder the hated man. He took a long drag of his cigarette, burning the tobacco down to the filter. He put it into the ashtray, which already held a pile of butts that looked dangerously close to toppling over. Resisting the temptation to continue his bout of chain-smoking, Holmes propped his elbows on the desk and put his hands in his face. The dark circles under his eyes and upset hair were indications that he had been awake for an irregular number of hours. He reached for his phone, picked up the receiver and punched a single button, then reclined in his chair. "I need to see you in my office," he requested grimly, without a greeting. A knock came from the door of his office almost as soon as he hung up the phone. "It's open," he invited. A man entered the room – an employee of Sawyer and Associates, who clutched a newspaper in his right hand. He approached the desk and stood uncertainly, as if worried about Holmes' intentions. "Sit," offered Holmes. The employee did, and the detective across from him got right to the point: "I need you to arrange me another meeting with Heather de Lorme, the maid involved in the Sawyer case," he instructed. The last part of his sentence seemed surreal to both Holmes and his inferior emplyee. A month had passed since the death, but the silence that had overtaken the office continued to linger among the cubicles. The employee frowned. "I don't know--" "Just do it!" Holmes commanded, his fists instinctively pounding his desk. "Okay, sir," the employee replied. Before his departure, he said, "You might want to look at page 3." He handed the newspaper in his hand to his boss, then left. Holmes looked at it, interested. He opened to page 3, where he saw a bold title that screamed at him: PRISONER ESCAPES DURING TRANSFER Holmes sighed and ran a hand through his untamed hair, then reached for his cigarettes. * * * Miss Heather de Lorme, former maid to James Sawyer and suspect and witness to his murder, stood anxiously outside her apartment building in Leon, Spain, on the night of November the fifth. She reached an unsteady hand into her purse, feeling around the contents in search of a particular object. Barely clinging onto the strap with a shaky hand, her patience was running short and as a consequence her searching hand dug deeper and more aggressively into the leather satchel. The strap slipped from the nervous fingers of her other hand – the open purse plummeted to the sidewalk. Its contents spilled everywhere, which prompted an annoyed groan and tempered curse from the beholder. Her quivering breath shook through the brisk night air – all she wanted was a damn cigarette. She crouched and began gathering her belongings. Her intent seemed almost frantic, as her eyes moved cautiously from one pedestrian to another, as if she was paranoid of a sudden movement. This area of town was certainly not for the weak, and having your personal objects scattered across the sidewalk at night was certainly undesirable. Having gathered half the purse's contents, she reacted in fright to a stranger in a thick coat and toque who knelt across from her and reached for the Mascera. She screamed in result of his presence, backing away and stumbling to the ground. She managed to break her fall with her hands. "Get away!" she cried, attracting the attention of the surrounding people. "I'm sorry, Miss … I'm just trying to help," the man said very politely. He sounded Spanish. She looked at him uncertainly, then joined him in picking up the scattered remains. Once this task was complete, they both stood up. The man put his hands in his pockets as Heather removed her pack of cigarettes from her purse. She stuck one of the cancerous sticks between her quivering lips, and in realization that she lacked a lighter, she looked to the man. Noticing her concern, the man pulled out a book of matches from his pocket and ignited one, then transferred the flame to the cigarette. Heather took a satisfying drag to calm her nerves, the blew out a cloud of smoke. "My name is Juvian," the man said. She looked at him curiously before taking a second drag of her smoke, then greeted, "My name is Heather." A lull in the conversation followed as the two naturally fell into a walk that lead away from the apartment block; they accepted casual conversation. Heather continued to smoke her cigarette, letting the tobacco burn quite quickly. "Would you like to grab a cup of coffee?" the Spaniard piped, sounding nervous. "I'd like that," she responded. She sounded much more relaxed, something very uncommon ever since her employer's death. She wasn't sure if it was a result of his kindness or the cigarette, but accepted it nonetheless. Juvian smiled as the two kept walking. His demeanor suggested that he was quite the friendly stranger, but Miss De Lorme was a very vulnerable woman at this point in her life, and her trust was easily misplaced. She had come off a hectic week of being hassled with questions from detectives and interrogators, whom held her as a suspect to the murder of James Sawyer, an event that had transpired less than two weeks ago. She had since dropped her duty as a maid in the tourism sector of Leon, and suffered, with depleting funds that required her to take residence in a shady apartment. The power involved had certainly not forgotten about her, either – she was bound to get a call or a visit from them at any given time, hence the reason her phone remained off and her door was always locked. There were uncertain and unknown circumstances that concerned her innocence and temporary release from custody. Things that she didn't dare question nor pursue as more than what they meant to her – the difference between guilt and innocence; between shamefulness and pride; and maybe even between life and death. Her sleepless nights were continually haunted by such horrific ideas and choices that were to be made – she lacked the will power to make these decisions, and instead remained hidden like a coward. Her lack of closeness with her deceased employer contradicted her attachment to his death, but she commiserated regardless. Juvian, however, was a man who played with a different deck of cards. His link to the Sawyer case was, if possible, more clouded than Heather's – but she didn't know that he was connected in any way at all. As far as she was concerned, he was a friendly stranger, which was exactly what he wanted. In due time, that familiarity would change drastically. What Heather didn't know was about to be found out. Juvian looked over his shoulder at the sound of a rumbling engine. He grinned at the sight of a black van that approached them on the sidewalk. This was his cue – as the van came to a halt next to the curb, the man swiftly used his arm to take a hold of the woman beside him, clasping his hand over her mouth to muffle a scream. She squirmed in his grasp but was powerless against Juvian's muscular arms. He dragged her towards the motor vehicle as the side door slid open, revealing a man clothed in black. His rugged appearance and involvement in the act made it apparent he was a criminal, and this was confirmed as he helped Juvian wrestle the scrappy woman into the van. The kidnap was successful – there was work to be done, but the villains had the evil conscience required in altering this young woman's claims in her involvement with the murder of James Sawyer. Juvian jumped into the van and glanced at the surrounding area, which was deserted. His sly grin disappeared as he closed the door, and the van rumbled off down the road. * * * Steady rain fell down on Los Angeles, California, as Cedric Riley jogged through the few pedestrians that huddled or walked underneath umbrellas. The only thing shielding him from the rain was a thin rain jacket, so he was glad to reach his destination -- a long, clichéd diner on the corner of the street called "The Ruby Cafe". Walking into a wave of warmth and the smell of hamburgers sizzling in the kitchen, he scanned the rather busy diner, eventually spotting a familiar face in Alex Barnett. He walked over and took at seat on the bench seat. His facial expression suggested he wasn't happy about the meeting, but gladly hooked a finger in the mug in front of him. He took a long drink of the hot, soothing coffee. Alex was looking at the paper for that day and sipping at a coffee of his own. Cedric set his cup down and looked at Alex. "I'm starting to get tired of these meetings, Alex," he said forwardly. The lack of a greeting made it seem like a common occurrence. Alex looked over the top of the wide newspaper, then set it down. He took another drink from his mug and replied, "I would have thought you were grateful to have the company of an old friend." "Yeah, well … my life is different now." "I'm not taking that away from you. I'm just the messenger," Alex defended. "You haven't really been giving me any messages, have you?" Cedric accused. "I still don't know any more than what I did a month ago …" "Be careful what you wish for…" Alex warned. "What the hell is that supposed to mean?" Cedric shot at him. "I don't know if you read the newspaper, but two weeks ago, the prisoner that Sawyer helped put behind bars escaped," Alex explained. "And that has what to do with me … ?" "Two days ago, Sawyer's maid from met with Richard Holmes the detective that is working on Sawyer's case – fittingly, one of the higher-ups at Sawyer and Associates, the dead-man's detective firm," he continued. "I don't know where you're going with this, Alex …" "I don't know what happened in that meeting, but something did. The maid came all the way to America to see him …" Alex said. He didn't seem convinced of anything, but kept going. "And the prisoner that we talked about earlier, Juvian, has fled the country." Cedric closed his eyes and ran his hand through his hair. "I'm not getting any of this, Alex …" "Neither am I …" "Then why are you telling me?" Cedric said shortly. Alex took a sip of his coffee. "Because it's the right thing to do. I know you're innocent, Cedric …" "Of course I am, so why the hell am I involved in this?!" he shot at his friend hotly. Alex's expression remained neutral. "You know as much about it as I do …" Cedric sighed and reclined in the booth, thinking over the situation. "This maid you mentioned … what do you know about her?" Alex shrugged. "I've heard some … things. Her name is Heather de Lorme, I think … Sawyer's maid … she gave him the drink that was poisoned, claims she doesn't know the mix was poisoned. She's the number one suspect and witness in the case, incidentally … as it stands, they have enough to take her to court, but Holmes is holding off. Says that there's more to the case than that. He's a stubborn son-of-a-bitch … he never settles for the obvious answer." "Does she live in Leon?" Cedric wondered. "I think so … but she's in LA right now, as far as I know." "Do you know where she's staying?" Cedric said, as if he was working up to this question all along. Alex paused in the midst of bringing the cup of coffee to his lips, and his eyes locked on Cedric curiously. "Why does it matter?" Cedric had leaned forward by this time with his elbows on the table, keen on hearing each of Alex's answers to his questions. He retreated now to the back rest and shrugged. "I don't know … just interested." A faint smile grazed Alex's lips before he took a sip of his coffee. "I'm the best bullshit detector in the city, Cedric. You might be a CIA agent, but I'm a damn good lawyer … and I know that you're lying." Cedric didn't respond, and instead averted his eyes to the newspaper on the table. A lull in the conversation followed, until Cedric broke the silence. "I don't know what I'm supposed to do. That doesn't happen often." "I can see that." "I have a life now …" he began. "No one disagrees with that, certainly not me," Alex said. "But I'm not going to feel sorry for you." "I'm not asking you to," Cedric defended. Alex finished his coffee and got up from the booth. He grabbed his jacket and put it on, then reahed into a pocket and threw a bill on the table. "I have other things I need to attend to … good day to you, Cedric," he said, then handed a small business card to the ex-agent. Cedric looked at it, then looked up at where Alex was formerly standing, but he was gone. On the back of the card was a room number; the front, an advertisement for a downtown motel. Cedric finished his coffee, put some money down, grabbed his jacket and left the diner. He had a date with Miss Heather de Lorme. * * * The Orchid Suites in downtown Hollywood ran for a rather cheap $110 dollars a night, and for a good reason. While its superb location made it a considerable choice for visitors to Los Angeles, it was more like a converted apartment building than a hotel. Some of its former patrons referred to it as the roach motel, and complained mostly of the uncleanliness of the place. The old saying "you get what you pay for" rang true to most of the visitors that opted for the motel on North Orchid Avenue. Miss Heather de Lorme was staying in room number thirty of the forty rooms that the motel offered, and didn't even have to pay the low price to stay there. She had been posted there in anticipation of further questioning after Holmes made calls over to Leon with what she had told them – that she had seen Cedric Riley in Sawyer's vacationing home on the day of his murder. Was it a long shot? Yes. Would it get her off the hook? Probably not, but that wasn't the intent. Those that actually had done the deed had not been caught yet, but the police were not ignoring them after Juvian had escaped from prison only two weeks ago. After fleeing the country, a warrant for his arrest had been put on him for that very reason – Richard Holmes, however, wanted to talk to him for a different cause -- to see what he knew about Sawyer's death. To tell him how convenient it was that Sawyer was murdered less than a week after he put the escapee behind bars. The web involved in this case had become completely tangled. In the midst of it all Miss Heather de Lorme, whom could be sent to jail in a second. Her fingerprints were all over everything that had to do with Sawyer's death. But they were using her to find out more – to dig deeper into the web. And she had finally come to them with something they could use. Was it a lie? Of course. She had been forced by the very men who had poisoned the Scotch to tell the detective the lie about Cedric that would avert their attention to him. The murderers were very well-informed and despite no evidence pointing in Cedric's direction, they figured he was the prime choice to put the blame on. The maid didn't know why. She just did what she had to do in order to stay alive above all, but in the process, make it less likely that she didn't end up behind bars. It was out of human instinct. She was returning to her hotel from her second meeting with Holmes. She stunk of cigarettes and alcohol (perhaps from a short stop afterwards) as she approached room number thirty, careful not to stumble or awake the neighboring visitors. She struggled to extract the key from her jean pocket, but did so eventually. Inserting the key into the hole was even more of a struggle; she missed frequently and cursed, but eventually the lock clicked open and she turned the handle. The door swung forward and revealed her room, which she dragged her feet into and closed the door behind, turning the deadbolt when it closed. Little did she know that the somebody who intended on breaking in had already done so. She walked towards the bed down the short hall that led to it, and a man stalked her from behind. Cedric Riley had come from the bathroom and swooped the palm of his hand over the former maid's lips, muffling her scream. She squirmed in his arms, much like she had not too long ago when Juvian had captured her. "Don't scream. I only want to talk, and I want to do it in a civil manner," Cedric instructed quietly. Heather stopped squirming and her muffled screams were gone; was she playing coy? "Now … can you do that for me?" She nodded, and Cedric removed his clasped hand. The distraught maid moved away from him and retreated onto the bed, bringing her knees up to her chest and wrapping her arms around them. "What do you want with me?!" "There's no need to worry, Heather," he said. "My name is Cedric Riley." She gasped, and reacted in a whimper, "I swear--" "I'm not going to hurt you," Cedric began, "But I do want answers." He allowed himself to pause if the former maid had anything further to say. She didn't – she sat with her knees up and arms wrapped around them, still frightened. "I know you're involved in this Sawyer debacle, and I want to know what you've been telling them," Cedric began. There was a reason he didn't specify what he wanted to know, because he wasn't yet sure how the maid was connected to him – it was his intent to find out. "I – I can't … they'll kill me …" she stammered. Perhaps she would have been better off having not said anything. Cedric's blank expression was intimidating, and he bore a hole through her as she tried to hide her fright of him. "Listen, ma'am … I'm going to let you in on a little secret, okay?" the ex-agent suggested. The maid didn't respond, but was listening. "I'll take that as a yes," he decided. "I am a former CIA agent." He paused to allow her to soak that information in and form any sort of reaction. She didn't seem fazed, so he continued. "What that means is that I'm to be trusted, and any information you give me will do more good than harm." She averted his eyes, and bit her lip nervously. "The Spanish man forced me to do it. Him and his … friends … don't want to be suspected in this. They have a colorful history with Sawyer, and an alibi to commit the murder. They did it … I know they did …" "The Spanish man? … he forced you to do what, exactly?" Cedric asked. "What? … I thought you knew already," she admitted slowly. "You thought I knew what?!" the ex-agent raised his voice. BANG. The door busted open and slammed against the wall as two police-men rushed in, their guns raised. "PUT YOUR HANDS UP!" Cedric turned to them. A faint smile flickered on his face as he raised his arms in the air. "Gentlemen … what took you so long?" One of them grabbed Cedric's arms and cuffed his hands behind his back. The other one announced, "You are under arrest for breaking and entering. We're taking you into custody." Cedric looked smug. "Good. I've been waiting to talk to that Holmes character." The police men shared an uncertain glance, but remained professional. The man that cuffed Cedric led him out of the hotel room. The other looked at Heather, who remained motionless on the bed. "Miss, I will continue to keep tabs on the motel, and insure your safety," he announced professionally. "It didn't stop him from coming in …" she accused. "I don't know if he told you, but he actually is a former CIA agent," the policeman responded. She looked away from him and out the window. "Unfortunately, he did." * * * It was getting late, and Richard Holmes was still at his desk. The Sawyer and Associates office was on the 10th floor of a sky-scraper on the edge of the Los Angeles business sector, and behind Holmes was window that spanned the entire wall. It was a beautiful view despite the dark sky and looming gray clouds, as the city had come to life, lights scattered across the plain. The steady flow of traffic provided a stream of natural and red light, creating something almost magnificent. It wasn't enough to distract the detective, whose eyes continued to glance from his stack of papers to the clock, which read 10:30 PM. The only light in his office was a lamp that lit up his work space – folders, loose papers, pens, and various other items littered the desk-top. Holmes had met with the maid earlier that day for the second time in the last week. She had provided him with information and although it contradicted what she had told him during his investigation in Leon, he was using it nevertheless. Any suspect was worth looking at during this inning of the ball game – he was still skeptical about putting de Lorme in court. She had no alibi in murdering his former boss. Her claim that she had seen Cedric Riley sneaking around the household the day of the crime had tapped at an event in Holmes's memory. Sawyer had told him about a past occurrence between two; the former CEO of the company Holmes worked for was unwilling to discuss what had transpired between himself and the ex-agent openly. He had been unable to contact the FBI, who were watching over the case – apparently, they had information on Riley that pertained to his relationship with Sawyer. Holmes was sure it had to do with one of his former boss's cases; if that were true, there would have to be some sort of record that detailed what had happened. The very prominent possibility that de Lorme was lying had crossed him many times in the past week. It was suspicious that she would embrace her role as a witness so abruptly. The detective was aware of this, but needed to consider all possibilities in order to suck the truth out of somebody. He had tried finding it on his own – all obvious evidence pointed to the maid. Sawyer's mantra continued to ring through his head, and he hoped "looking beyond the obvious" was his best bet. Holmes grabbed the next folder in his stack, which was tagged "Nathan Aspinall Grand Theft Auto". He skimmed through the contents – Sawyer had been hired to investigate a mass operation that involved auto-theft. The criminal at the top of the scheme was Nathan Aspinall, who was transporting the stolen cars to Europe where he had a buyer for them. There were no significant names or events that jumped out at Holmes, so he reached for his cup of coffee and took a swig. The caffeine was the only thing keeping his eyes open, which he turned back to the documents in front of him and continued reading the closure. Apparently, a CIA agent by the name of Charles Kensrue had stepped in and used his ties to save the kids ass. Richard Holmes put the tip of his pencil over the name 'Charles Kensrue', as if he recognized it. After a moment of thought, he reached for another folder – a different colored one off to the side. It contained Cedric Riley's file. He opened it up and skimmed through the papers, something he had done numerous times. He found what he was looking for – Cedric Riley had not one, but two past identities. The first was what he remembered: Charles Kensrue. The second almost caused his jaw to drop: Sean Aspinall. He hurriedly looked back to the Nathan Aspinall case and quickly saw what had happened: Riley – at that time, Kensrue – had used his ties in the CIA to help his brother out of trouble. That was the only sensible hypothesis the detective could come to. Holmes sighed and leaned back in his chair, running his hands through his hair. He had finally got something out of his painful study – but it lead to nothing. It wasn't enough for a murder alibi. Riley would need more of a reason for murder than that, considering he had succeeded in letting his brother free. A thought struck him and he looked at the stack of folders to his left that he had been going through, and found a very recent case that was again tagged with 'Nathan Aspinall'. He rushed to open it up as if he already knew what he would find – he worked for the firm when Sawyer had emarked on the case. Summer, 2006, less than half a year before that very day, James Sawyer had put Cedric Riley's brother behind bars. Thinking he had enough to take Riley into custody, Richard closed the folder. He gathered a few of the files and put them into his briefcase, which he closed and took off the desk. As he was about to turn and leave, his office phone rang. Holmes paused in the midst of his departure, looking at the phone as it rang throughout the office. After being in the office for almost 20 hours, he was ready to leave. His dedication to his career, however, forced him to reach for the receiver and put it to his ear. "Hello?" "We have Cedric Riley at the station. LAPD." He hung up the phone, not needing to stop; he didn't even question why Riley was being held at LAPD. He shut the lamp off and was gone. * * * The holding room at the LAPD station proved to be nothing special: a small space enclosed by brick walls, with a one-way window glassed across one side, and a single table set up in the middle of the room with metal folding chairs on either side. The only aspect of the room that was particularly interesting was its occupant: Cedric Riley of AWC, who sat on one of the chairs, leaning forward with his arms folded on the table. The only thing missing from the scene was an interrogator sitting across from him, other than that, it was right out of NYPD Blue. The door to the room opened, which slightly peaked the suspects interest. In walked detective Richard Holmes, in a dress shirt and slacks, his tie loose and suit jacket draped over his shoulder. "Well don't you just look lovely," Cedric smirked. The detective returned a faint smile as he put his jacket on the back of the empty chair. "Your sarcasm sounds so … bright? … for such a grim situation, Cedric." The suspect shifted in his chair. "I don't have time for this." "And you think I do?" Holmes said calmly. "I've been working for twenty hours straight." Cedric looked away. "You're not going to get my pity." "I wasn't asking for it, however, I do hope to get your cooperation," the detective explained. "As a former CIA agent, I'm sure you know how painful these processes can be …" "It would do you good to inform me of what is going on before you interrogate me as a suspect," Cedric suggested, playing Holmes' head. "And who says you're a suspect?" Richard asked curiously. "Why else would they send a top-dog detective down here for some little breaking-and-entering dilemma?" The detective took out his pack of cigarettes and pulled one out. He put it in between his lips and lit it, then removed it after his first drag. "Mind if I smoke?" he asked, the second hand toxin swirling over the table between them. No response. Holmes continued, "Alright." He paused for another intake of nicotine. "I've been looking at your file, Cedric …" "Pretty damn impressive, isn't it?" Cedric interrupted. He got a shrug in response. "You were always a handful for the CIA, weren't you? Always had to have things your way … just like you always had to avenge your kid brother." That stuck a nerve and Cedric was soon attentive. "What the fuck does this have to do with him?" he said slowly. Holmes shrugged. "You tell me." Another drag of his cigarette. "He isn't my brother. Not anymore," Cedric said slowly. "Your file says differently," Richard challenged. "Says that somehow you got his sorry ass out of a twenty year jail sentence for grand theft auto." He allowed for a pause, and received no reply. "And then, Sawyer puts him behind bars. Two months later, BOOM. Dead as a doornail. Because some fucker had the gall to poison his scotch and threaten a poor, innocent woman to deliver it to him," the detective told sequentially. "There's no alibi against me. No evidence, and a single witness who can't fucking tell the truth if it snuck up on her and clamped onto her ass-cheek," Cedric sneered across the table. "And above all, I didn't fucking do it." The door to the holding room opened and both men looked towards the opening. An officer peeked his head in and announced, "Someone posted bail on the breaking and entering arrest … said his name was Alex. You can go." "About time," Cedric said, and got up from his chair, beginning to leave. "I'll see you around, detective," Cedric shot at him. "This isn't over, Riley …" Holmes said firmly, rubbing one temple with his thumb and the other with his pointer finger. The door slammed close. Detective Richard Holmes looked exhausted. "This is far from over …" 'AgentDash' Cedric Riley, meanwhile, had little time for the detective's clichéd claims. He was on his way to kick Killean Sirrajin's ass in the bracket semi-finals, bitch.
