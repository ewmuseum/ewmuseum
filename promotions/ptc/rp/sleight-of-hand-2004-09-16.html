Sleight of Hand

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 The jangling of keys penetrated the gloom, followed by muffled throaty laughter. A thump against the door inspired a flurry of short, elated moans, then the key slid home and clicked the lock. As the lights flickered on, washing the ramshackle hotel room in a dim orange glow, a woman sashed through the doorway, pulling an obviously inebriated man along behind her by the waistband of his pants. Her clothing was colourful and vibrant enough for any circus, and the mess of makeup marring her face would have had any clown frowning enviously. The man with her did not fancy himself a clown, however. Watching the not-so-subtle gyrations of her flesh beneath the nearly transparent ensemble, he grinned with the smug arrogance and familiarity of someone who has played this game a time or two before. She guided him halfway through the cramped room before turning around to eye him lewdly, taking a few extra steps backward before stopping and pressing their bodies close. "Make yourself comfortable," she breathed into his ear, then pushed him away and scampered off to the bathroom with another throaty giggle. The dull hum of the fan sounded briefly before she closed the door behind her. Chuckling to himself, he pulled the top of his jogging suit off over his head and tossed it on the only chair in the room. The physique he revealed was nothing to gawk at, but he did a bit of narcissistic flexing just the same. Hair and pimples fought for supremacy across his upper chest and shoulders, and his pale muscles sagged limply beneath a layer of baby fat he'd never been able to shed. He told himself that it was deliberate, that he needed to look haggard in order to succeed. Belching drunkenly, he bent and removed his pants, stumbling and almost falling only once, then straightened and smoothed his hair, which was dyed black and pulled back into a ponytail in a vain attempt to hide its thinness. He kicked off his shoes and sauntered over to the bed, tilting and wobbling precariously, as if he was walking a tightrope. Flopping down on to the bed in just his socks and boxers, he scooted back to lean against the headboard and waited for the hustle to come. There was no doubt in his mind that the woman who had picked him up outside the bar was a professional. She had the look and the mannerism down to an art, not even mentioning the price or even letting on that there was one. His lips twitched up in a skewed smile as he thought of how surprised she'd be when he turned the tables on her. When he out-hustled her. If she thought she had landed some liquored-up yokel ripe for the fleecing, she was in for a rude awakening. He made his living robbing her and her ignorant brethren. They all fancied themselves hustlers, but next to him they were mere carnival barkers. He knew that on their best day they wouldn't be able to put anything over on him on his worst. He was the real deal. The fan suddenly died and the door banged open. Sucking in his gut and adopting what he fancied a seductive pose, he poised himself for the dance to come, revelling in the swerve he would unleash. She was in for a big surprise. He opened his mouth to begin lulling her into his trap, but that thought fled before it could make a sound. It was not the hooker he had gone up there with that materialized in the doorway and strode over to the foot of the bed without a word. It wasn't even a woman. Or a hustler. Decked out in an immaculate black suit with smoky grey pinstripes running its length, the newcomer stared down at him from oceanic blue eyes that swirled with a perpetual joke he had no intention of sharing. His short blonde hair was slicked straight back, accentuating the sharp angles of his stony face, and the smile he proffered hardened the look rather than softening it. In truth, it was more a baring of his teeth than a smile. The hustler sat bolt upright, eyes wide with indignation, but the other man nonchalantly raised his hand in warning. "I have no desire to kill you, hustler," he said smoothly, his voice cold yet melodious and refined. Spreading his open hands before him, he gave his head an almost imperceptible shake. "I simply require some information." "You can take your requirements," the hustler slurred, "and shove them up your ass, Deville." Rolling forward on to his knees, he raised his chin defiantly and glared down the length of his nose. His blotched chest puffed up with pride. "You ain't gettin' shit from me." Deville blinked, then gaped. "You know who I am?" The words were thick with incredulity, but the question was rhetorical. Frowning, Deville shrugged. "Funny. That usually makes things easier." The hustler sneered. "My father warned me about you, told me to stay away from you, but he's always been too cautious." Though still laced with the occasional hitch, his face and words were coming smoother. The situation was sobering him up. "I'm not like the others, pal. They may fear your coming, and hope it never comes to pass, but not me." Impossibly, the sneer grew. Reaching behind himself, he rummaged around for a moment before producing a shiny black beretta that glistened in the tainted light. He thrust it unwavering at Deville, pointing it at his chest. "You don't worry me in the least." Deville couldn't believe it. Where the man had pulled this weapon from was beyond him, but sure enough, he had been beaten to the draw. He stood there, helpless and empty-handed, as the hustler pounced off the side of the bed, never letting the gun or his gaze drop from his would-be victim. Both stayed trained directly on Deville, weighing him, measuring him for a casket. Deville didn't fear death -- he knew it would come from time to time -- but he tried to avoid it if at all possible. That he had overcome it in the past and could do so again did not make the prospect any more endearing. It was a hassle, coming back from death. Yet there he stood, mere moments from suffering it again, just because he had delayed. He had underestimated this two-bit hustler, given him the opportunity to make the first move, and now he was going to pay dearly for his mistake. "The great Angelo Deville," the hustler mocked, strutting across the room, waving the gun with feigned worry. "Unkillable, they called you. Unbeatable." He snorted loudly, still a little shaky from the drink. "And now here you are, at my mercy." Apparently he wanted to give Angelo time to reflect on his mistake. Time for regret. "I've been waiting for this day, Deville. Waiting for the chance to prove that no man is untouchable. As soon as I was warned against you, I knew it was my destiny to kill you." He was enjoying himself, sauntering around Angelo in a slow circle. Angelo eyed him warily from the corners of his eyes. "The great Angelo Deville," he snorted again, "killed by a relative unknown." He pushed himself nose to nose with Deville, nestling the gun under his chin. "You thought I'd just be a pushover? Someone you could just steamroll over without a fight? You were too damn arrogant. Did you really believe yourself unbeatable? You could never beat me, you son of a bitch. Never." Idly, Deville wondered if the hypocrisy was intentional. The hustler's face contorted, as if he wanted to spit. "Let this be a lesson to your ignorant a--" Interrupting the premature gloating, Deville snatched at the gun and wrestled it from the hustler, spinning him around and twisting his arm up behind him. The man yelped and tried to struggle, but the pistol came crashing down against the base of his skull, collapsing him to the ground. Delivering a hard kick to his ribs, Deville sauntered around to his head and mashed his face with another one. Again the hustler cried out, grunting as he scrambled up to his hands and knees. "What's the point of having a weapon," The Deville inquired wearily as he turned the gun over in his hand, "if you're not going to use it?" The man mumbled something unintelligible in response, something along the lines of "fuck you", and Deville shook his head disgustedly. He clawed desperately at Deville's leg with one hand as an Italian dress shoe crushed the other one. "Whenever you have someone at your mercy," Deville began, taking a shiny black cylinder from his breast pocket. Nonchalantly screwing it onto the end of the gun, he continued in his lecturing tone. "Only show it to them if it gains you something." The silencer snapped into place with a click that was deafening in the overbearing silence, and the elongated barrel bored into the back of the hustler's head, freezing him in place. Keeping the gun locked there, Deville stepped back, removing his foot from the man's hand. His other hand leapt over and cradled it, vainly trying to rub away the pain. "If they have nothing worthwhile to offer you alive, you kill them." The man shivered as the gun lifted, but dared not look up to see where it was pointed now. "Because if you don't, you die." When the gunshot exploded in his ear, muffled as if the gun was fired underwater, for a moment he thought he was dead. Then he felt the pain and saw the blood pooling beneath his face. His head rang from the words as much as the gunshot, and he couldn't make sense of the situation. A wave of nausea crept over him, and he spread his hands out to keep from crumpling. The pain flared up enough to make his vision focus, and he gaped down at his hands. Each one bore a hole the size of a nickel, ripped straight through the meaty centre of the palm. Damaged nerves and the sudden pain had caused his hands to clench, as if that might somehow keep the blood from flowing. The two blackened, bleeding pits made his fists look like a pair of dice eerily proclaiming his fate. "Now keeping that in mind," Deville roared without shouting, only raising his voice enough to drown out the man's whimpers. "Listen carefully to the question I have to ask you. If you answer to my satisfaction, you'll prove yourself worthy of my mercy. If you don't . . ." The Deville let the silence hammer the threat home. When the hustler nodded fervently that he understood, Deville asked him the question. There was no hesitation. No arguing. No bartering or haggling. The answer spilled out of his mouth in such a hurry that Deville had to make him repeat it twice before it unravelled coherently. The Deville nodded appreciatively, thanking him for his cooperation, then shot him in the head. ~><>...<><~ I've made mistakes. Lots of them. I've also killed more people than I care to count. Sometimes, after the fact, second thoughts steal into my mind like a burglar in the night, invading my sanctuary, raping my security, and lifting priceless pieces of my identity. They rob me of my wits and leave me vulnerable to the petrifying scourge that is doubt. They make me second-guess my every thought and action. People say a lot of the things I do are evil. Unconscionable. Sins to make the devil himself pale with impotent disbelief. They say I should spend every waking moment prostrated on the floor, begging for forgiveness and salvation I can never deserve. They say eventually my time will come, that ultimately there will be a balancing factor that strips me of all I have and leaves me as I have left so many. Their eyes gleam as they say it, as if they're tasting the delicious prospect, smacking their lips with the inevitable satisfaction it will provide them. They count the days off on their calender, knowing that someday their shaky X will mark my end. These people want me to die. They wish I was already dead. They wish I never existed in the first place. They say it would be better that way. Sometimes, when I'm weakened by the doubt and the lingering fog of a mistake, I find it hard to argue that they're wrong. In fact, in a very real way they're right. ~><>...<><~ Standing in the alleyway like a statue, eyeing the ominous iron door carved into the fortress of brick before him, Angelo Deville dry-washed his hands and glanced towards the road. It wasn't the first time his eyes had nervously drifted towards the comfort of anonymity he was about to abandon. Reaching into his pants pocket, he once again produced a large key and raised it tentatively toward the door. His eyes flickered to the gleaming metal and scrutinized it for a moment. The key itself hadn't been hard to procure -- even high class professionals were cheaply purchased -- but he just wasn't sure it was a door he wanted to open. He wasn't sure it was a door he was ready to open. Steely resolve washed over him, setting his jaw and forcing his hand the rest of the way. The key went in smoothly and effortlessly, then turned just the same. Scooping up his briefcase with his free hand, The Deville swung the door wide and marched stoically into the artificial light. The abrupt heavy silence and smoke-choked air of the room assaulted him, but he pushed his way in and let the door slam shut behind him, cutting him off from escape. He knew right away that it was the right place, but the room was a hole. Though it was a decent size, and the walls were freshly-painted and unmarred, there was no furniture to speak of and not a single lamp. The only light came from above, resulting in a comforting but inadequate dimness. A large steel refrigerator hummed coldly in one corner. In the centre of the room, standing out like a stalactite of crystal in a gloomy cave, sat a group of five men around a polished oak table. They ranged in age from younger than Deville to older than his deceased grandfather, but each one sported a dapper suit that instantly provided a distinguished air. Three were heads of large Families, while the other two were the next best thing. Normally they were all at odds with one another, vying for supremacy but pretending not to, and by the overflowing pan- sized ashtray in the middle of the table, this occasion was no different. They were all trying to blow more smoke than the others. All the trust in the room wouldn't even fill the eye of a needle. They glared as one at Deville, however, then two of them -- the two oldest -- rose to their feet with much scraping of chairs and groped angrily at their chests. Their guns came quickly to practised hands, and both trained on the centre of Angelo's forehead. For his part, Deville eyed the group coolly, casually, as if he was a wolfhound in the company of lesser dogs. The eldest snarled. "You've got a lot of nerve traipsing in here like you're one of us." He barked a dry, humourless laugh that no one else shared, then contorted his mouth to continue, but the man farthest from Deville, at the head of the table, cut him off with a blank stare. The same level look inspired the other to put his gun away, then both men were sinking back down to their seats, heads down and faces flushed. The man at the head gave an almost imperceptible nod of satisfaction, then turned his attention on Deville. His arched eyebrow was as good of an opening as Deville was going to get. "Believe me, gentlemen, I'm not here in search of trouble." Looking around to meet each man's eyes with a steady and open gaze, Deville smoothed his face to an emotionless mask. "Quite the opposite, in fact." Proffering the suitcase before him, he marched to an open space at the table and laid it down, then took a step back. "A peace offering," he clarified when no one moved, gesturing to the black box. "And a sample. I know we haven't always seen eye-to-eye in the past, and there have been a few incidents that set us against one another, but a renewed allegiance would be beneficial for all concerned." The men at the table exchanged suspicious glances, but said not a word. "I know you are well aware of the current lull in business," Deville went on, "the lack of energy and innovation. If I were a betting man," he paused to flash a grin, "I'd wager that is what this meeting is about." He didn't wait for either argument or acknowledge. "That's why you're here today, and as luck would have it, that's why I'm here today. I can solve our problem." One of the men, the eldest again, snorted. "This," Deville said, gesturing to the briefcase once more, "is merely a taste of what is to come. Open it up and you'll be pleasantly surprised." None of the men made any move for the briefcase. The head of the table, the boss, shook his head. "You open it." With a shrug, Deville stepped forward and spun the briefcase around, then snapped it open and spun it back, pushing it across the table. The boss eyed it for a moment, then trepidaciously pulled out one of the immaculate stacks of hundred dollar bills, running a finger along the tip, fluttering it like a fan. He frowned approvingly at Deville before tossing it back into the case. "That's quite a gesture," he said, more than a hint of sarcasm in his voice. Angelo made an effort not to grind his teeth. "It's also an apology." He forced himself to breathe normally, to relax. "I'm sorry for the strain I caused, and those that were lost because of it. It was never my intention to bring anything down or cause undue pain. I only wanted to live and succeed without interference." One of the men seemed ready to curse, but the boss beat him to the punch, stating in his driest tone, "I hear that Salvatore is dead." Deville took a deep breath and closed his eyes. He had known this was going to come up, but thinking of being confronted with it and being confronted with it were two entirely different things. Murdering Salvatore, the man most would argue had raised him into the world, was something The Deville would never regret. He wasn't even sure he could convince himself to lie about it. Even if he wanted to. When his eyes opened again, all five men were regarding him smugly, as a chicken they intended to eat momentarily. "You knew I was going to do that," Deville stated calmly. "You know that I had to. You know why, and you even know that it was for the best. If that's going to pose a problem, let's just drop this right here." He wasn't going to get into a pissing match with them. It caused fires more often than extinguished them. "Drop what?" The boss asked, leaning forward. "What exactly is it you're proposing?" "Cooperation," Deville answered matter-of-factly. "The past is nothing. We need to stop dwelling on it and look to the future. We need to work together, instead of against one another. You, of course, get the tribute you deserve and a better handle on our world, while I get rein to do what I do best." None of them needed to ask what that was. "Everybody wins." He glanced from one dour face to another. "Now what say you?" Looking solemn, the boss closed the briefcase and furrowed his brow, looking around at his suddenly unreadable associates. Finding no help there, he answered how every head of every boardroom in the world would answer. "We'll need to give it some thought." Deville smiled and inclined his head. That was the best he was hoping for. For him to say they'd think about it meant the plan was unfolding exactly how it should. Deville waved away the insincere offer to return the briefcase, knowing it was staying in the room one way or another, and fought to restrain his laughter. Things were going perfectly. Before anything could spoil it, Deville offered an overly-pleasant "I'll leave you to your thoughts," and turned for the door. He half-expected them to call him back and grill him about something, to try and pry some ulterior motive from him, but they remained silent until he was back in the alleyway. Sighing with relief, he leaned up against the wall and began rummaging through his inner breast pocket, muttering all the while. What if you made a mistake? the familiar yet alien voice in the back of his head taunted, trying to get a rise out of him, but he ignored it as best he could. He knew he hadn't made a mistake. He hoped not, at least. Retrieving a small black object the size of a peanut and placing it in his ear, he fished around in his pocket again before coming out with a cigar. His spirits seemed to pick up as he placed the Cuban in his mouth and went about lighting it, listening undetected to the room he had just left. Planting a bug in the briefcase was simple, but effective. Hustling those guys out of money was a task no man wanted to take on if he was smart, but hustling them into money was one of the easiest things in the world unless you were a cop. Deville knew that as soon as they came in contact with the money, they wouldn't part with it without bloodshed. No matter how much money they had, they always wanted more, and they hated giving it up for any reason. They were tighter than the skin on an apple. Once again a wave of panic that he had made a mistake washed over him. He knew he wasn't impervious to mistakes. He expected them, and tried to prepare for when they would come. Tried to figure out how to turn them to his advantage. Usually, a mistake was a blessing in disguise if you took what you needed from it. It was an opportunity to learn. Without making the mistake of it once, Deville would never have known the folly of mercy, perhaps the biggest blunder possible in his line of work. If you got the opportunity to take out an enemy, or even a potential enemy, you took it, period. There was no such thing as honour in war, nor quarter. The opponent you turn your back on is the one with the best chance to kill you. He should have known that all along, but it took a knife in his back for it to ring true. The cigar was blazing now, and Deville puffed at it contentedly as he made his way from the alley, stepping back into the traffic of everyday life. It didn't take long for him to hear what he was waiting for, the damning words "kill him" broadcast over his private radio. Deville allowed himself a wry, knowing smile. He knew he hadn't made a mistake. He reached into his pants pocket and wrapped his hand around a steel cylinder about the size of a lighter, then depressed the trigger on its top, not even wincing when the briefcase's inner lining of C4 blew all the windows from the first story of the building with a ferocious roar. People all around him stopped and gaped, some screaming in terror as the black smoke wafted out from the charred holes, a symbol of the carnage and devastation that burned within. The silence, echoing from inside, from death's door, drowned out the screams and drove home the finality of the deed. Unperturbed, The Deville disappeared into the crowd as if it had never happened. As always, he was not playing around. This was not a gamble. It never was. There would be no mistakes. There would be no mercy. There would only be victory. ~><>...<><~ It would be better if I was dead. For them, that is. For them it would be better if I was dead. That way, they wouldn't have to perish in the futile attempt to kill me. For me, things are a little different. For me it would be better if they were dead. That's what this competition, this game, this gamble, is about. Life and death. Killing your opponent so that you may live to fight on and succeed. We're all in the same worn boat, traversing the same rocky stream, seeking the same mystical prize. We all need to kill in order to live. The difference between us is that I don't try to disguise that fact behind false morality and remorse. I don't pretend that what they're trying to do to me is any worse than what I'm trying to do to them. I just call a spade a spade and play my hand to the best of my ability. If I need to bluff, I bluff. I do whatever is necessary. I make sure I kill you before you kill me. You, naturally, want me to feel bad about that. You think I should be sorry. You think I should repent. And you call me ignorant. The only thing I'm repentant for is leaving my dominion unchampioned for so long. For allowing these second-class losers to flourish and stagnate, befouling what I worked so hard to build and polish. For letting you forget the grandeur and prestige that is true eminence and lordship. I'm sorry I allowed you to put the likes of Seymour Almasy beside me, to compare us with a finger to your lips as if uncertain what the outcome of our meeting would be. I'm sorry I let you believe that someone like Snake Eyes might upset me in the opening round, simply because he makes it known that that is his intention. I'm sorry I let you forget who I am. What I am. I'm sorry I let you grow blissfully ignorant of reality. For that, I am truly sorry. It was my mistake, and I regret it with every damning word that reaches my ears. Now the time has come for me to make things right. The time has come for me to repent. And if you've forgotten how it is I do that, I'm sorry for that as well.
