PTC — Roleplay

Just An Average Joe

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
His fifth grade math teacher, Mr. Johnson, called him Joseph. His friends, however, called him Joe Average, which had been a very fitting name for him. Joseph Franklin had the American dream: a suburban house, a beautiful son and daughter, a lovable dog named Mr. Floppy Ears, a caring and attractive wife, a manicured lawn, and a good job. He had been just like every other neighbor on Willow Drive. Maybe that was the problem. On the other hand, maybe the problem began when Joe didn’t want to be average anymore. Suddenly he wanted to become great. Joe wanted the bright, blazing lights, the big, pumped crowds, and the roars of pure excitement. That was getting ahead of us, however. Joe needed to enjoy his morning cup of Maxwell House before he had to face the nine-to-five ahead of him. With a final sip, Joe kissed his wife goodbye and left as she got ready to try to hurry the kids off to school. Stepping outside, Joe took a moment to bask in the warm sunlight. His neighbor, Bob, waved to him. Joe gave him a friendly smile and trotted off across his lush green lawn. At exactly 8:45, the sprinklers went off to water the lawn. Inspired by the gorgeous day, Joe decided to leave the car at home and walk to work. Joe only lived about ten minutes away from his work, so walking would not be too bad. In fact, the only reason Joe even took the car to work was to show off the Benz he had bought just the year before. As Joe walked, he took in the local flavor. It was a refreshing change from the stressful battle with traffic. He could take his time and listen to the bird’s songs, occasionally interrupted by the sound of a horn in the distance. Joe checked his watch, 8:50 on the dot. He smiled as he continued on time to work. With how things seemed to be going, it was going to be a great day. Halfway on the way to work, Joe passed, as he usually did, an old, wooden phone pole. He had seen it a hundred times before, passing by it quickly as he went to work. Various flyers and advertisements covered the pole, so much so that it made the pole appear to be made out of brightly colored paper rather than solid wood. “Yard Sale: September 12th-14th, Cedar St” “Lost Dog: Reward, Black German Shepherd” “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” Something in Joe reacted, a strange sensation that he had never felt before. As if he had just been enlightened to this all-new world he had never known. For a few minutes he stopped and just stared at the flyer, not quite sure what to make of it. If this tournament boasted the biggest names, then why are they advertising on a phone pole in the middle suburban America? Without really thinking, Joe grabbed the flyer and ripped it off the pole. He stuffed it into his back pocket and brushed off the entire incident. “Wrestling? Why, it is nothing more than overgrown men in tights parading around as if in some stupid ballet.” For some reason, the appearance of that flyer had severely dampened Joe’s day. Maybe it wasn’t the flyer though. Maybe it was the fact that he had spent so long staring at it that he made himself late for work. Granted he was only four minutes tardy, but at Big Bob Browskies’ Super-U that was a slap in the face of corporate America and everything it stood for. “Oh Joe, can we talk?” Ken, his boss, asked him as he came in through the doors. Joe didn’t really feel like talking. He felt like telling Ken to take that pen he always kept in his pocket but never used and—well, anyway, Joe merely gave Ken a weak nod and stepped off to the side to allow customers to pass. “Joe, I busted my watch. Do you think you can give me the time?” Sadly, Ken thought this was cute, this messing with his employees. That arrogant grin on his face spread wider as if he knew he had Joe just where he wanted him. Joe, of course, looked down at his watch and read off the neon numbers flashing at him. “Nine-oh-four.” Ken’s grin grew broader. “And what time were you suppose to work today?” Oh how clever Mr. Fenson was, how clever indeed. Not now. Joe had not wanted to go through this now. Not over four stupid minutes. “Look, I’ll just stay a few minutes over if need be, it’s no big deal.” There was no negotiating with his boss, however. Before Joe knew it, Ken had gone into a long monologue about how coming in late hurt productivity and cost the company money. “Blah, blah, blah.” It was all Joe heard. The more Ken’s lips flapped the more a weird buzzing seemed to fill Joe’s ears. A strange urge started to overcome Joe. He wanted to punch Ken right in the mouth! To stand over him and tell him to take those four minutes and shove them! “Yes, Mr. Fenson. I’m sorry, Mr. Fenson. It won’t happen again, sir. I promise.” Middle management claimed another life. Somewhere in the distance, a baby cried. The rest of the workday seemed to pass uneventfully. At regular intervals, Joe would stop and press his hand on his back pocket where the flyer was. At times, he would even sneak away into the bathroom to look at it. He never got any new information from it, just the same few words. “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” It wasn’t until his second trip to the bathroom to secretly look at the flyer, as if it was some hidden map to a long lost treasure, which Joe had finally realized that he had absolutely no clue that any of the “big names” in wrestling were. The only wrestler he could even remember was Hulk Hogan and surely by now the man had to be in his eighties, Joe thought. In fact, Joe had never thought about it before, but up until this point he thought that PTC stood for the Parent Teacher Council. Surely, they don’t hold wrestling tournaments. That, or Joe had been going to the wrong meetings for the past three years. “Wait, what if I HAVE been going to the wrong meetings? Nah…no. I mean that’s just silly. Right? Damnit, I bet they had beer.” So it Joe decided on it. Joe had no idea who any of the wrestlers were that was going to be there and he had no idea what this PTC was. With a quick flush and a thorough washing of his hands, Joe was able to go back to work and finish the rest of his shift. Joe walked back home, only stopping to look at the pole again. There was an eight-by-eleven hole in the wall of paper revealing a small glimmer of brown polished wood. Joe stared at the pole, hoping it would give some answer to his strange attraction to the advertisement. However, after standing there on the sidewalk for fifteen minutes, none came and Joe was forced to walk the rest of the way home in shame. When Joe reached his front door, the sweet aroma of meatloaf greeted him. Ah, the joys of Meatloaf Mondays, what greater day of the week was there? Well, besides Taco Tuesday. Joe loved him some tacos. Joe did his best to slip into the house quietly and made his way to the kitchen. Once in the kitchen’s threshold, Joe took a second to ensure that the coast was clear. Seeing no signs of life, he tiptoed across the ceramic floor toward the cooking dish resting on the countertop. He hovered over the cloth-covered glass dish like a vulture ready to scoop down on a dying prey. Slowly his hand moved toward the corner of the cloth, he did his best to steady his shaking hand as water formed at the corners of his lips. WACK! “Don’t even think of touching that meatloaf before dinner, mister.” Joe turned to see the stern face of his wife, equipped with large wooden spoon in hand. Damnit, foiled again. Soon, meatloaf…soon you would be Joe’s. Defeated in his attempt for a pre-dinner snack, Joe retreated to his basement den to read the paper. Per usual, Joe sat in the den reading the sports page. Joe was the type of person who always read the sports page and loved to chat sports but never had any idea what he was talking about. Ninety percent of his conversations were quotes taking from the newspaper or ESPN and the other ten percent were wild assumptions based off said quotes from the newspaper and ESPN. Midway through an article about how baseball is a far cry from yesterday’s game because of out-of-control drug use (something Joe would pretend like he was an expert on tomorrow to his coworkers), Joe started to shift in his seat uncomfortably. He leaned over and pulled the flyer out from his back pocket. Joe stared at it, confused. He had actually forgotten it existed for a few minutes. Looking around to make sure no one would see him, Joe opened the top drawer of his desk and placed the flyer face up so that anytime he opened the drawer he would be able to look at it without having to take it out. Joe stayed locked away in his den spending most of his time trying to remain focused on the newspaper, all the while stealing glances at the flyer. With each passing hour Joe spent staring at the eight-by-eleven sheet of paper, the more confusing things got. It was as if he sat before a great puzzle without clue how to start it. What secrets would this flyer unlock? What great treasure hid between the bold letters? “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” What was PTC? Or GTT. And why were there five of them? Who were these names? (Consequently, Joe was now convinced Hulk Hogan would not be at this event and that the PTC in question did not, in fact, stand for Parent Teacher Council.) “Sweetie!” his wife called from upstairs. “Dinner is ready. We’re having meatloaf.” She stated that as if Joe did not already know it was Meatloaf Monday or the fact that she had caught him earlier trying to steal a bit of mentioned meatloaf. Ha ha, meatloaf! Your time has come. Enjoy your last few moments baked in ketchup, for they will be your last. Joe closed the drawer and left the flyer as he made his way up the stairs. His children were already seated and waiting patiently with empty plates in front of them. Joe took his place at the end of the table as his wife started to pass out plates. Joe smiled ear to ear when the plate of meatloaf and mashed potatoes was placed in front of him. His meatloaf was the only thing Joe looked forward to on Mondays. The rest of the dinner passed uneventfully. The children, Little Joseph and Ashley, ate their dinners, talking about the daily events of school. His wife Elizabeth nodded her head as the children’s “exciting” stories and would respond with “that’s nice” or a simple “ah uh.” Joe purposefully neglected to tell his family about the conversation with his boss and arriving four minutes late during the dinner when he spoke of his day. His family (something Joe oddly expected as if the news about this event was widespread) made no mention of the flyer or this GTT5. After dinner, Joe debated the idea of going back downstairs to his den to inspect the flyer or if he should join his wife in bed, who was reading one of those trashy romance novels. As exciting as the idea of lying in bed half-naked while she read was, Joe decided he would try to unlock the enigma of the advertisement. And so he kissed his wife and slipped away to the basement, claiming that he was going to make sure the checkbook balanced out. Once in the safety of his den, Joe set to work to try to figure out what these PTC and GTT places were. He searched through the paper for any mention of it. After going front to back and cover to cover twice, he gave up and switched to a different media. Joe’s in-laws had given him a thirteen-inch television for his den since they knew he spent much time down there reading and such. It was by far not the greatest television, it only got the basic channels, but at a moment like this, it was better than nothing was. “If this GTT is such a big deal then surely they must advertise on public television. It reaches the largest audience,” Joe rationalized with himself. Hour after hour of mind-numbing programming passed but there was still no signs of any PTC organization. Joe started to get depressed. It was as if the only proof of the existence of PTC and GTT rested in the top drawer of his mock oak desk. He turned off the television, gave a heavy sigh, and turned back to his desk feeling like a defeated man. With a last ditch effort, Joe opened the drawer and peered in, just hoping with that last glimmer that the words would reveal some greater truth. “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” Joe cursed those typed words. He was starting to resent the encrypted message the words hid. “Hunny!” his wife called out. “It’s getting late. What are you going?” He sighed again and closed the drawer, making sure to lock it after it was shut. “I’m coming up right now.” ~~~~~The Next Morning~~~~~ Today was different. Where yesterday the sky was clear and bright, today was murky and grim. Even the weatherman could not find a reason for the sudden sour turn in the weather. Joe knew why the weather was so poor. He didn’t tell anyone, but the weather was poor because of the PTC flyer. It sounded strange, even idiotic. Joe knew this, but he also knew he was right. He had woken early so that he could run down to the den and fetch the flyer from his desk before anyone else woke. He currently sat on it as it rested in his back pocket. Midway through his bowl of oatmeal, the rain started to drop. “Oh dear, this day sure does seem to be going downhill quick,” his wife had commented as she stared out through the kitchen window. Joe only nodded. He had been expecting the rain. The world was simply not allowed to return to normal until he locked the riddle of the flyer. Joe had argued with himself all through breakfast if he would walk to work or drive. On the one hand, he wanted to stop and look at the phone pole, he had found the flyer, but he knew he would be soaked head to toe if he attempted to do so. Ultimately, his wife decided for him when she gave him a kiss on the cheek, handed him the car keys, and wished him a good day. The engine purred to life. Just once Joe wished the engine would roar and he could speed away into the distance like a world-renowned racecar driver who just won the cup. Joe backed out of his driveway. He gave his family a friendly wave goodbye, watching his wife help ready the children for school, and headed off to work. Traffic was worst than usual. More people had decided to drive to work rather than walk or take a bike. The drivers were also being more cautious as they drove slower and came to frequent stops, even for the slightest things. Joe started to approach the phone pole where he found the flyer. It was just his luck that the entire way there the traffic had been stop and go, yet now the flow of cars were steady and he would have little time to try and look at the pole. His head titled to the side and he could see the hole removing the advertisement had made. The ink of the other flyers, Joe noticed, had begun to run and was becoming illegible from the rain. He kept his attention on that empty spot. Part of him had expected a new flyer to fill the hole with a new clue to the mystery, some crucial evidence Joe would need to finally solve it. However, there was nothing there. Empty, like the feeling in his gut. CRASH! The sound of metal against metal tore through the September rain like fingernails on a chalkboard. In his investigating the pole, Joe had failed to notice the car that had came to a stop just a few feet in front of him. The sudden jerk had pulled Joe back to reality. Panic started to set in and he slammed onto his brakes. It was too late, however; the damage had been done. The two cars pulled off to the side of the road and both drivers exited their vehicles to inspect the damage. Joe had accidentally rear-ended one of those PT Cruisers; Joe never liked those cars anyways. This one even had the fake wood paneling on the side. Joe wanted to rip that ugly paneling off. “I’m really sorry, I just had my attention away from the road for a second,” Joe told the woman as she studied the little damage to her car. The paint had been a little scratched up, but outside of that, everything seemed to be just fine. Joe pulled out his wallet and opened it up. Last Thursday was payday so his wallet was still thick with money. He pulled out a few bills and held them out for the woman. “Here, you can get the paint touched up with this, and again, I am so sorry.” For another few moments, the woman said nothing and just stared at her car as if it was the first time she had ever seen it. ‘Just hurry up and take the money, woman,’ Joe thought to himself as he held his arm out for her. “Everything seems to be in order,” she finally said. She looked at the wad of cash in Joe’s hand and took it. “Thank you, I’m sure this will be enough. What is your name now?” “Joe, urgh, I mean, Joseph Franklin.” He really wanted to hurry, he could not stand the idea of being late for a second day in a row and having to again deal with the annoying lectures of his simpleton boss. Joe looked down at his watch; if he hurried, he could still make it to work in time. The rain continued to bounce off his shoulder as he became increasingly soaked. Hadn’t he decided to drive to work instead of walking to avoid this? He watched as she turned around and walked back to her car. When she finally shut her car door and drove off, Joe was able to breath easy again. Until he turned back to get into his car. Despite the minimal damage to her car, Joe’s Benz had managed to bend the front fender in. His lights were all broken or falling out and the front had been pushed in. How had his car sustained so much damage when he was hardly traveling over twenty miles-per-hour? His car was in no shape to drive to work, despite being only seven or so minutes away. His clothes were becoming heavier by the moment as they absorbed the raindrops. Every few minutes he would check his watch and cringe at how late he was. After he discovered the piss-poor condition of his Benz, Joe had called a tow truck. Immediately after that, he called work and let them know what had happened and how he would be late. Forty-five minutes later the tow truck arrived and Joe was able to spend the next twenty filled out page of page of paperwork to prove the car was indeed his (yes, because Joe just loves to steal wrecked cars; it was his second favorite hobby next to standing in the rain waiting for the annoying late tow truck). The car was loaded onto the truck and Joe was able to watch his new Benz towed off. This being of course after the tow truck driver had managed to hit the curb and bust one of Joe’s back tires. The polite thing would have been for the tow truck driver to give Joe a lift to his work so he did not have to walk the rest of the way in the rain. Speaking of which, the rain had started to fall with a vengeful pace now. By the time Joe was able to see the bright neon glow of the Big Bob Browskies’ Super-U sign, Joe felt like a cold knife had been cut through his bones. He was soaked head to toe and his hair clung to his forehead, afraid that if it didn’t it would freeze off. “Welcome to Big Bob Browskies’ Super-U!” the door greeter bellowed in an overly cheery voice. “Oh, it’s just you, Joe. Wow, you look horrible. What happened?” Joe ignored the urge to sucker punch Juliet, the door greeter, and went to go clock in for the day. It was now 10:26, and Joe was nearly an hour and a half late, the latest he had ever showed up in his seven years of service. Before Joe could even make it to the clock to punch in for the day, he heard the one voice he had been hoping to avoid. “Oh, nice for you to finally visit, Joe,” his boss said with a chuckle. Who was this man to criticize Joe’s attendance record? In his seven years of work, Joe had only been late four times while this man had been late countless times in the past few months over the most self-absorbed reasons. Joe was employee of the month compared to his boss (or at least he would have been if the boss hadn’t given the award to his nephew last month). Joe looked down at his dripping clothes; a small puddle was starting to form under him as they spoke. “I was involved in an accident this morning. I called Susan and asked her to let you know why I would be coming in late,” Joe told him, hoping this would be the end of their little conversation and that he would be able to finally get to work and off his poor day. Ken looked over his papers he carried with him “No,” he said calmly, “I don’t see any note about you coming in late. Now are you sure you actually called?” This was not happening. Joe knew he made the call. He quickly looked over Ken’s shoulder to the desk where Susan sat. She had the phone on her shoulder with her head tilted to the side to keep it in place. All the while, she is filing her names and rolling her eyes, as if having to do her work was some great intrusion on her valuable time. His boss looked Joe over, surveying the pitiful wet mess Joe was. Ken just shook his head. “You’re soaking wet, and are in no condition to work Joe, just take the rest of the day off, and think about the importance of arriving to work on time.” Just like that he was sent back home. It wasn’t until he stepped outside and back into the cold September rain that Joe realized that his car had in fact been towed and he would have to walk back home, causing him to further be soaked by the rain. At that moment, he just wished a massive cold would hit him and take him out of his misery. Joe felt so miserable that he didn’t even bother to stop at the pole to see if another flyer had been posted. He just wanted to get home and out of his soaked clothes. When he was at the end of his street, he could see another car parked in his driveway. Maybe one of Elizabeth’s friends stopping by for a visit. Hoping whoever the visitor was wouldn’t bother him when he tried to relax, Joe opened the door slowly. It was comforting to have the warm heated air brushed against his shivering cheeks. He let out a grateful sigh and took off his coat, placing it carefully on the rack so that none of the other jackets got wet. He thought to call out for his loving wife, but decided against it. If she had a guest over, he did not want to be rude and interrupted them. Sometimes it would good to be rude. The drenched clothes were heavy upon his bones. Joe wanted nothing more than to lift the weight from his shoulders. He grabbed the bottom of his shirt and pulled it over his head, as he pushed open the door to his bedroom. Joe threw his damp shirt into the corner and when he finally focused his eyes, he froze in horror. Before him was his wife, in the nude, with another man. It was his neighbor Bob, the one that lived to the right of his house. Ever fiber in his body wanted to rip Bob piece by piece, to unleash the pent up frustration and anger he felt at the world. Joe, however, merely turned around and left the room quietly as his wife and neighbor hurried to cover their naked bodies. Approaching the stairs, Joe turned right and entered through the door heading down to his den. After witnessing what he had just seen, and enduring the day he had, he just wanted to go down to his den, curl into a ball, and die. Dropping into his chair, Joe finally realized he still had the flyer in his back pocket. Panic flooded Joe as he hurried to take out the advertisement. “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” The words were somehow calming and comforting, like a warm security blanket wrapped around him. Luckily, the rain had not damaged the paper much. The edges were dampened and there were clear raindrops that had fell on it, but outside of that, the words were clear and still legible. For the next two minutes, Joe read and reread the advertisement, silently going over the words in his head. “Hunny,” his wife called out from the top of the stairs, “are you alright?” There was, to her credit, a strong sense of guilt in her voice. Joe folded the flyer up and slid it into his back pocket where he had retrieved it. He did not want to deal with this, not right now. Regardless of this fact, Joe did pull himself from his seat and walk up the stairs to the kitchen where his wife was waiting for him. She had her bathrobe wrapped around her small waist as she did her best to try to look composed. He asked the question that had been eating at him the most: “So how long has it been going on?” Elizabeth’s hand moved to the saltshaker and she spent the next few moments fiddling with it. “Almost a month now.” Joe cringed but Elizabeth tried to go on as if she had not noticed him, “He would leave his work and meet me during his lunch break when we knew you would be at work.” Thinking about it now, Joe felt stupid for not noticing the car to begin with. “Can we please just put this behind us? Just pretend it never happened that you never came into the bedroom?” She pleaded with Joe now, having abandoned the saltshaker for Joe’s wrist. Truth be told, Joe would have loved nothing more than to pretend it never happened and to forget the images he saw. However, he knew it was impossible. Anytime he closed his eyes, it would hurt. Anytime he looked at her, it would hurt. Anytime he breathed, it would hurt. Joe gave his wife of fifteen years a reassuring smile. “We can try.” He did not mean it. It was too hard to believe it when he knew it would be impossible for him to ever put it behind him. But Joe was the universe’s whipping boy and he played the part all too well. He would again suck it up and ignore it as others walked all over him. At long last, the rain was beginning to yield as the afternoon sun was reaching its apex. Joe turned away from Elizabeth and grabbed an old dirty baseball hat from the rack on the wall. He grabbed the doorknob. “Where are you going?” his concerned wife asked. “Out.” He left it at that and closed the door behind him. His arms trembled at his sides. Bob’s car was gone now and his driveway remained empty. Having no other place to go, Joe went to the only place he could think of: the wooden phone pole. It was becoming some sort of sanctuary for him. Despite all the hardships that had fell on his shoulders in the past two days, Joe felt strangely at ease here. Joe leaned back against the pole and closed his eyes, letting the first rays of sunlight hit his face for the day. The light started to become brighter and brighter. So much so that when Joe opened his eyes to see what was going on, he had to cover them with his right hand. “Excuse me, sir,” a polite-looking Australian said, as he approached the pole. Joe rubbed his eyes for a minute to get them to focus again. He had never seen this person in the neighborhood before. The man nodded his head in the direction of the pole and Joe finally realized that he was blocking his way. Joe excused him and stepped to the side. Then something marvelous happened. The Australian pulled a sheet of paper out from under his arm and taped it to the phone pole where the PTC advertisement had been. It was a simple act, but it gave Joe renewed hope and life. He knew what the flyer would say before he even laid eyes on it. “PTC’s Annual GTT5! November 30th, Watch the Biggest Names in Wrestling Battle it out” It was a godsend. The Australian took notice of Joe’s wonder of the flyer. “Yeah, darnedest thing, the other one disappeared.” Before he could utter another word, Joe snapped toward the man with a wide-eyed glaze. “You mean you know of this thing?” “Know of it?” The Australian laughed. “Oh, course I do! In fact, I’m looking forward to the next open round of the Infinite Gauntlet. The winner not only gets a guaranteed spot in the tournament, but he also gets one for a friend. Signs are still going on for the round.” This man was God. Joe had officially decided that. “Well it was nice meeting you…” “Joe…urgh, I mean, Joseph. Joseph Franklin.” “It’s nice to meet you Joe, my name is Argy…” The Australian looked down at his watch. “Ah shoots, would you look at the time? I have to go. But I hope you tune in and watch the tournament!” The man hurried off past Joe, dropping another piece of paper in the process. Joe bent over and scooped it up, looking at it. “Infinite Gauntlet Round 13: Application” Was this the Infinite Gauntlet the man made mention of? “Hey, sir!” Joe called out, but it was too late. The man had disappeared. Joe looked all around but there were no signs of the Australian man. “If that was God,” Joe thought to himself, “then he sure has lost some hair. Running the universe must be stressful.” The idea of a thinning hairline made Joe bring his hand to his own brown hair. Still there, thankfully. “Next time God manifests himself before me,” he told himself as he started to walk back to his house, “I hope he manifest himself as a top-shelved blonde.” Joe folded up the application and placed it in his back pocket with the advertisement. He even hummed a little as he walked. When he entered his house, however, his good mood soured. Boxes, tons of them, were stacked upon each other filled to the brim with various clothes and objects. Joe made his way past the litter of boxes to the master bedroom. There he found his wife packing away the last on her clothes into a plain brown box. “What’s going on?” Joe asked, trying to survey the situation. His wife turned to him, finally noticing he had come home. Tears were rolling down her face. By the looks of it, she had been crying for a while. Seeing her husband enter the room, Elizabeth broke into a deep sob and proceeded to cry even harder. “What’s wrong?” pressed Joe, as he stepped toward her. Elizabeth pushed Joe away as he tried to approach her; she backed herself into the corner. “I can’t do this anymore, Joe!” Tears rolled down her cheeks even harder now. Joe stepped forward, both hands up to try to ease her. “You can’t do what?” he asked her softly, his words almost coming out as a coo. Sucking up the rest of her tears, Elizabeth threw her arms out, “all of this! The lies, the guilt, everything! I packed up the children and we’re going to my mother’s.” The words took Joe back a step. He had started to move away from Elizabeth, hoping that if he got far enough away from her that it would make her words untrue. “…” he opened his mouth but his words failed him. “Oh,” she stated rather matter-of-fact, “I called our lawyer and filed for divorce.” She brushed past the stunned Joe, and took the last bag with her. Just like that his had marriage ended. He knew he could run back and try to reason with her. He knew he could talk her into stayed, even if for a bit longer. Maybe with counseling they could make it work, in time. No; he knew, somehow, that it was better this way. Joe closed his eyes and wished for a single moment that he could be a neutron bomb and for once could explode. It wasn’t until he had heard the car leaving that he finally moved again. His first movements were to reach in his back pocket and pull out the folded up pieces of paper he had stuffed away. He moved over to the nightstand on his side of the bed and opened the shelf. He laid the application on the nightstand flat and pulled out his favorite pen (it was one of those pens you saw in the fancy cases and wondered why they cost over a hundred bucks. It was Joe’s favorite because for some odd reason, people were impressed Joe could spend a hundred on just a pen). Joe froze. “What am I doing?” he thought, “I’m not a wrestler. I’ve never even seen a wrestling match.” He shook his head at his moment of stupidity and returned the sacred pen to its velvet blue case. He threw the application and flyer into the drawer with the pen and flopped down into the bed. He spent the next few minutes staring at the ceiling. Joe was about to fall into a peaceful slumber when his growling stomach woke him. He cursed at his own human needs but after his stomach argued with him, Joe decided it would be best to order a pizza. He had just recently been paid so he would be generous and give the driver a big tip. Joe figured if he did a good deed for someone else, just maybe something good would happen to him. Picking up the phone, Joe dialed the pizza shop (he had Pizza Hunt on speed dial. It is useful when your kids have you order it once a weekend). After ordering his Meat Lovers pizza, Joe rolled to the edge of the bed to grab his wallet. He flipped through his wallet but something seemed wrong. He had just been paid, so why did he only have forty-two dollars on him? Joe went throw his pockets, thinking he must have accidentally dropped the rest of his money. Even after his pockets had been emptied, all Joe found was another dollar in change and enough lent to kill a dryer filter. Panic started to set in.
