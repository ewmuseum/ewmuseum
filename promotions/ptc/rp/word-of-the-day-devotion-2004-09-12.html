Word of the day: Devotion

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-12 “Thank you for coming today mister Lexicon.” “Please, call me Johnny.” The large man leans over the small woman’s desk to shake her hand before settling himself into one of two chairs. The office is lavishly decorated in natural hues of oak and mahogany, the chairs covered in a black leather and streams of light languidly slipping past the cracks in a large bay window’s blinds. It is an expensive looking office, and considering the amount of money Johnny paid for his daughter to attend this school, it better be a nice office. “Let’s get right down to business then Johnny.” “Yes, let’s.” “We’ve received several reports concerning your daughter Claire, it seems she has taken to bullying several of the other girls in her classes.” “Are you serious? She’s only been here four days.” “Believe me, I am fully aware of this fact Johnny.” “I don’t know what to say, I’ll have a talk with her when she gets home tonight.” Johnny’s hands come up helplessly. The principal shakes her head sadly and removes her glasses. Johnny notices for the first time she is actually rather attractive. Her hair tied back in a bun and a crisp business suit, he speculated she had a well proportioned body hidden beneath that official exterior. He himself had chosen to appear casual in a pair of black track pants, white sneakers and a plain blue t-shirt. “It says in our files that you are a professional wrestler, is that right?” “Yeah.” Johnny says with a bit of a grin. “And I assume that takes you away from the home for long periods of time.” “Uh yeah, I do a lot of work in the states, so sometimes I’m gone for more than a week at a time. Claire stays with my parents while I’m gone.” “I see. I’m forced to question your devotion to this little girl.” “I am a devoted father.” Johnny puts in defensively. “I’m not trying to insult you mister Lexicon. But as the male figure in Claire’s life you impact her behaviour more than you might know. She sees you wrestling and assumes that violence is an acceptable form of settling differences.” “Look, I said I’ll talk to her alright.” The niceties had dropped like a bad habit, Johnny rose from his seat and the woman across from him did also out of courtesy. And without another word he turned away and left the office. Later that day Johnny took refuge in the one place he could always flee to clear his mind. His sanctum of sorts, where the only other people would not bother him with questions or attempt to give him all the answers. He’s been coming to the Rocky Mountain Gym since he was fourteen, since he decided he was going to be wrestler. Those around him either knew who he was, or accepted him as an anonymous body builder. Either way he was left to work through his routine in silence. But no matter how much peace he found on the outside, inside was an entirely different matter. His mind paced over the earlier conversation and attempted to find a conclusion without seeking assistance from anybody else. This is the place I’ve always placed my devotion before. I’ve always found comfort within these concrete walls, surrounded by strangers and steel. Working with the tools of my trade. It’s strange that I find ease here instead of with my daughter and my family. But if you consider how long I’ve done this, and how long I’ve been a father, perhaps it’s not so strange after all. Am I a devoted father though? Every week I leave this little girl with my parents to pursue that which makes me happy. I place my own desires before those of Claire. I never had to face these doubts before a month ago, before this little girl was dropped in my lap out of nowhere. I thought I could split my time and effort between wrestling and raising a daughter. But maybe I was wrong, maybe I have to give up one to have the other. Can I give up the sport I’ve devoted over twelve years of my life to? The one thing I’ve been so dedicated to that I wake up for it every morning, I dream about it every night. Whatever it is I decide, it will be after this tournament. I have to focus now more than ever, this is bigger than most of the matches I’ve ever walked into and not one ounce less dangerous. At least in 3w I can suffer a defeat and come back next week, rested and prepared to kick it up that extra notch needed to pull the victory. But here, I have to function on that extra notch every step of the way, I can’t conserve myself for the later rounds or I may not make it to those later rounds. This is do or die the entire way. I know what I’m capable of, possibly one of the very few wrestlers in this world that can say that. I have found my inner strength and come to terms with my short comings, I understand and exploit my strengths. I look at the names listed on this tournament card and in a heartbeat I can name over half that I can beat. Without a doubt in my mind, I am better than the majority of wrestlers signed into this thing. The only thing left to do is prove this fact. If I do this, I’ll be that much closer to my final goals. To be remembered forever not as one of the best, but THE best. To become a hallowed member in the annals of wrestling history. That is what I am devoted to, and I can’t turn away from that path no matter how narrow or riddled with obstacles it may become. My devotion is my strength. And I can’t allow that to waver for even a moment, if I allow a seconds worth of weakness, somebody will exploit it and I will be broken. I can’t allow that to happen, it won’t happen. “Hey Johnny!” A voice calls from halfway across the gym. Lexicon sets the weight bar on its supports and sits up. Every muscle through his upper body is tense and worked, sweat dots across his brow. Looking to the source of the voice he spots the gym’s owner waving a hand at him, a phone gripped in his other. Nodding in understanding he collects a towel and gets to his feet, wiping his face and neck before dragging it across the weight bench and turning away. He weaves through the maze of people and equipment until he approaches the front counter. “Who is it?” He asks the owner. “I don’t know, but this isn’t grand central kid and I’m not your secretary.” “Blow me Tony.” Johnny snaps with a grin and snatches the phone away. “Hello?” He says and listens to the other end. “Yeah I’ll be around in a few mom, keep her there.” Johnny hangs the phone up and shakes his head. “Problems with the kid?” Tony asks, leaning on the counter that separated the two men. “Hey, you don’t worry about that old man. Get back to work.” “Punk.” The two jest as Johnny turns around and heads for the change rooms. An hour later Lexicon sits at his parents coffee table fuming after the retreat of his little daughter. She’d thrown a typical ten year old tantrum and stormed to her room, the prospect of a child ranting at him was nearly enough to send the wrestler over the edge. “She’s like what, sixty pounds and she’s throwing glasses around. I have a mind to go spank her ass blue.” Johnny comments as his mother settles into a chair. “Oh we both know you won’t do that.” She replies. “Does she do this all the time?” “Actually… Only when you’re here.” Johnny shakes his head, bringing the palms of his hands to his face and jamming them into his eyes. “You know I gotta leave to tomorrow right?” “Where to this time?” She asks in a monotone voice. “Boston for a couple days, then over Washington for the tournament, and Hartford for the 3WA Invitational.” “Another week, two maybe?” “Look we’ve been over this a hundred times mom, you want me to hire a nanny? You won’t have to take care of her.” “You need to devote a little more time to her.” “What is this the word of the fuckin day?” His mother turns away in that casual disregard that means she’s really pissed off this time. “Shit… Mom, why don’t you fly her to Washington for Friday, I’ll take her with me to Hartford we’ll be back Tuesday morning.” The elderly lady turns around and smiles at her son, patting his hand comfortingly she nods and rises from her seat. She takes a few steps out of the kitchen, turns down a hall and stops before a young milado girl who seems to be waiting with heightened anticipation. “What did he say?” She asks excitedly. “We’re leaving Friday.” She smiles. “I’ll teach you how to do that someday.” Said with a wink. Around the corner Johnny leans back into the kitchen with a chuckle and crosses his arms. But these family affairs had to take a back seat for a little while, bringing her to Washington was only a temporary solution to the problem. A problem that would be dealt with later, there was too much to worry about at this point in time. I’ve scaled a forty foot steel X, fighting the entire way against three other men for a second rate belt. I was the only man to walk out of that match, and the only one to ever wrestle again. I stood in the centre of a ring of cars, nearly blinded by headlights as the man who dominated an entire federation grinned at me. For a long long time I held the record as being the only man to ever defeat Draven Masters, that night I didn’t need a belt to be called the greatest. Nine. That’s the number of careers I’ve ended in a single year. One year and I’ve put a stop to that many dreams, funny thing about dreams, see they’re just like glass. When they break, they shatter, and they never go back together quite the same. Xtreme cage matches, steel cage matches, triple tier cage matches. I’ve done them all and I’ve stood victorious, I’ve lifted belts high over head and been bombarded by cheers so loud it rattles the brain. Fearless have I been in my pursuit of the goals I desire, unyielding in the ring and a genius behind the scenes. But don’t mistake this for arrogance. Oh I’m an arrogant bastard, this is a well document aspect of my personality. But I have done these things, with my hands, my talent, and my dedication. I have performed some of the greatest feats in professional wrestling, survived matches deemed suicidal before their very conception. I’ve beaten the best that have been put before me, played head games with supposed masters of the mind, I have gambled with the devil for my very soul and took the house for everything it had. But this, this tournament is a bigger stage than all the ones before it. Here I can test myself against the best of other federations, perform for an audience of billions. There are no cages this time, no hardcore rulings, no gimmicky stipulations. Single standards against the people who fancy themselves a league above my own. Whether the people on this docket have watched what I can do or not, they have no idea what I’m bringing to this tournament. I am to wrestling as ice cream is to warm apple pie. You can certainly have one without the other, but they are so much sweeter when combined. None of these people know it yet, but I am the next Golden Turnbuckle Tournament winner. Lori Equinox stands as the trial grounds for my round one showing, the night of unveiling, when mysteries are put aside and all the trash talking comes to rest. The best shall advance as the losers make way and move into the audience seats. One match at a time, first Equinox; then Cyrus Skar or John Winters, and then who knows. One match at a time. Until only one match remains.
