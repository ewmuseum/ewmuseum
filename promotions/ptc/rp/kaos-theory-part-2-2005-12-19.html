Kaos Theory Part 2

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 Cause and effect. For every action there is a consequence, no matter how small. Some people plan rigorously, meticulous to the last detail. Others act on impulse, not caring to think ahead and look into the future they are creating. There are arguments for and against each way of living. Some say that the cautious type leads a safer life with lower risks and has a higher chance of living longer. But, is that what living is about? Or should we trust instinct and go back to our primal urges to do what feels right? How can you feel alive if you don’t take a risk? It seems that some people need that little kick of danger to help them get what they want out a life, an exhilarating buzz. A feeling of adrenaline pumping through the body as that choice they made nearly cost them their life. Another reason to tell themselves they are worth something, and that they should be grateful for who they are. On the other hand, people ignore the cause and effect principle for the complete opposite. Because they believe they are worthless and have nothing to lose. No matter what the consequence, to them it doesn’t matter if they die for they don’t feel alive anyway. Suicidal is not a term that can really be used to describe them as they don’t want to die. They just don’t seem to have a reason to live. Cyrus Skar, a man without purpose, without meaning. He’d wake up from his broken sleep most mornings thinking ‘Why?’ Why not some other ‘Average Joe’ working a boring nine till five job? Why did he have to be so unfortunate in what cards he got dealt? Why was it that when he was dealt five cards he ended up with all jokers? All his life he’d tried to second guess why he was such a freak, why he wasn’t like ‘The rest of the kids’. What he couldn’t figure out was that it was just another case of ‘Cause and effect’. Only, it wasn’t his actions that did this to him. It was just one of those things. Wrong time, wrong place. Life could be a bitch like that but Cyrus had learnt not to try and place blame. He did his best to accept what he was and work at it to make himself normal. But that just wasn’t good enough. His problems were escalating and it wasn’t just affecting his social status, it was spiralling out of control in all aspects of his life. Although Kaos wasn’t directly interfering in Cyrus’s career, he was doing enough outside of it to wear him down and to bring his body to a dangerously low level. Some days Cyrus went to train feeling like his limbs were made of concrete and the air was null of oxygen. He knew that sooner or later either he was going to lose or Kaos was going to. Though right now, he felt hopeless so he did what he’d done best when his Dad used to beat him. He tried to put it to the back of his mind. He tried to ignore it and push it away; only this time the problem wasn’t going anywhere. It was getting worse by the day and sooner or later it was going to explode. ----- Cyrus spluttered viciously and tried to empty the contents of his lung as his body convulsed. He wasn’t sure why he’d passed out, but he had a funny feeling he knew who was to blame. The little problem he just couldn’t get rid of seemed to have risen to the surface again. Kaos was seemingly taking control at will now and Cyrus feared he was going to do so in his match against Ember. For once he feared for his opponent’s safety, he knew if Kaos did take control that he wouldn’t stop at a victory. He’d stop once Ember was totally immobilized. A sudden sharp pang made him snap out of his day dream as he opened his eyes. Thick black smoke clouded his vision and stung as he tried to make sense of what was happening. He desperately tried to think, to recall what had happened after he’d been unconscious but he couldn’t dig anything out of his memory. His primal instincts kicked in as he stopped trying to be so logical about the situation. His house was burning down and he needed to get out. He couldn’t make out what room he was in due to the black fog covering the room. He placed his arm out cautiously in front of his body and slowly stepped towards the centre of the room. He reached down and felt something that was not in the slightest bit comforting. The soft silk cover of his bed felt divine underhand but held such a sinister meaning. He wasn’t in the kitchen where he knew he should have been; somehow he’d ended up in his bedroom. His mind started to whir, action plans formulating in his head. He had to think on his feet otherwise he was going to end up in hospital or even worse, dead. He needed to find out how far the fire had spread, judging by the smoke it’d hadn’t been raging that long but it was only a matter of time before it made it’s way upstairs. Did he dare open the door? Did he risk a fireball knocking him back inside and possibly endangering his life more? Or did he jump out of the window and risk breaking something? Was his life more important than his career? Wrestling was Cyrus’s life. It was time to make a decision; if Cyrus left it any longer he wouldn’t have a choice to make. He’d already be dead. He dashed to the door; at least where he thought the door should have been and reached around for a handle. Wrong side of the room. ‘Got to run, got to keep moving.’ He nearly tripped hurrying to the other side, grasping around through the smoke trying to find the door handle. ‘Where the F**K is it?’ Eventually his fingers grasped the brass handle and immediately recoiled as the heat burnt his mammoth hand. He cursed loudly and put his size fourteen boot through the door, causing it to go crashing to the floor. More smoke came bursting through the door like an uninvited guest. Cyrus held his breath for a couple of seconds and when no fireball came he let out a short sigh of relief, followed by a sharp cough. He had to get out before the smoke did some serious damage to him or it wasn’t just going to be his health at risk, it’d be his career. His eyes peered out from the doorway, desperately trying to see if the fire had made its way upstairs. Time was running out as his clutched at his chest, his lungs were working overtime to get him out of this situation alive. He was going to have to take a risk, he didn’t have time to think things through properly. But when did he ever really stop and think? When did he ever consider the consequences? Now wasn’t exactly the best time for Cyrus to have his epiphany but it came to him like a flash of light. Suddenly everything seemed so clear. He wasn’t going to get out of this one alone. He’d thought about this before but dismissed it straight away, for once he’d considered the consequences and he didn’t like them. But now he had to dispel those fears. He had to let go of all reasoning. He had to let Kaos take control. Within seconds Kaos had become the dominant personality, for the first time in Cyrus’s life he had volunteered his body for Kaos’s amusement. Oh how Drake felt empowered, even more so than normal. The danger around him didn’t matter anymore, for he would walk right out of this burning building a victor, the hero and he would be the one that remained. Cyrus stupidly thought he’d be able to overpower Kaos after all was said and done, it was a consequence he didn’t think out thoroughly. His desperation to get out of the house alive had cost him his body. It may as well have cost him his life. Kaos spent little time daydreaming at his new found status and started to run towards the stairs. He started down them and the smoke became thicker causing him to choke on the awful fumes from the various materials ablaze below him. As he got to the bottom of the staircase he poked his head around the corner to see that the fire was contained in the kitchen, the door was shut. It wasn’t going to hold for long though, the amount of smoke meant the flames were just about ready to make an appearance. It was only a matter of seconds before the door stopped holding and the fire swept through the rest of the house. But Kaos didn’t care. None of the elements on this planet could stop him, not wind nor water and especially not fire. He was invincible, he was a living legend and soon the rest of the world would know his name. He turned his back on the kitchen and kicked down the front door. The natural pure air felt so good to his dirty lungs. He enjoyed the sweet smell of victory as he walked unaided from the house. He started to collect his thoughts as he put one foot out onto Cyrus’s beautifully paved driveway. He wondered how long the fire had really been burning. Cyrus had only been awake a few minutes before he let Kaos take over. Cyrus had thought it might have been burning awhile, but it hadn’t. Then Kaos realized, it hadn’t even broken out of one room, it’d only been burning a few minutes if that. As Kaos turned round the force of the explosion sent him flying back towards the road, all the windows of the house shattered, glass shimmering as it showered down upon the world below. Bricks and mortar flew up into the air colouring the sky in clouds of dust. Kaos was out cold lying metres away from the rubble that was now the remains of Cyrus Skar’s house. Neighbours flocked from their front doors to see what this mighty noise was, some noting the seriousness of the matter; others were being nosey looking for the fireworks. Most just stood around staring in shock, a few ran inside to ring their friends and spread the gossip. Everyone had gotten what they wanted. But with so many enemies who was to blame? When everybody wanted you dead, how do you formulate a list of suspects? Kaos knew that a contract had been taken out on Cyrus’s head to stop him from competing anymore. But why would somebody go to such lengths? Why would anyone want to stop the only thing keeping Cyrus sane? But that was just it. They didn’t want to keep Cyrus sane. Cyrus started to come round; he didn’t know how long he’d been unconscious outside his house. He didn’t even realize his house was gone till he opened his eyes. He felt lucky to even be in control again. He knew there was a chance that when he rolled the dice he could have lost it all. Fortunately he’d only lost his possessions, not his body. Now all he had to figure out was who didn’t want him competing. But where did he start? It could have been anybody with a grudge against him. Kaos had caused so much havoc it could have been far too many people to even fathom. Maybe he’d just have to cross that bridge when he came to it. Kaos might not have gone forever, Cyrus couldn’t be sure of that. He’d have to take each day as it came now, he wouldn’t even try thinking about consequences and actions. Just what his life was, and at the moment that consisted of competing in the GTT and at a lower level, Ember. He walked over to his wreck of a car and picked a cracked wing mirror up from off the floor and gazed upon his dirtied face. ‘What a mess.’ Though he knew he was lucky to survive the fire with only minor cuts and bruises, it could have cost him so much more. All he knew now was that he could only trust himself. Or could he?
