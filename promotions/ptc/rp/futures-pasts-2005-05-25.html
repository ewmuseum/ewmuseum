"Futures & Pasts"

25 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-25 * * * * * A week had passed since the flesh-and-blood apparition of Archibald Matthews walked into Archibald MacGregor’s life late one evening, and walked out of it the next morning bearing a warning: The other side is going to throw their sales pitch your way. It’s inevitable. During the course of the week that followed, MacGregor simply lived his life by a regular routine – all while keeping an eye out for anything out of the ordinary. When the sun peeked over the horizon, he rose from bed and ate his breakfast. During the day, he would occupy himself with chores around the house. The lawn was mowed, the exterior now held a new coat of paint, and his prized car – A Nissan 300ZX – now shimmered in the sun with a fresh waxing. As the week marched on, life became tougher. In fact, Archibald’s body seemed to be rejecting the vitamins he was pumping himself with, regurgitating the food that he ate each day, and ignoring the effects of the nightly sleep. He appeared gaunt, with bags under his eyes large enough to rest his own head on. This wasn’t The Devil’s bidding, however. It was Archibald’s own doing. His body needed the alcohol he was withholding from it. He hadn’t taken a drink since the ghost of Archibald Matthews had left him. Each day, he told himself that he wasn’t going to be going to Hell, and that he was going to make his life better. And that all started with his most glaring vice: alcohol. He even went to the local card shop, and purchased a bouquet of flowers that he would give to Heather Davis when they bloomed in a few days, along with an “I’m sorry” card, complete with a heartfelt apology. He was turning his life around, so he might as well try to get back the girl as well. He would need her on this journey. Archibald also scheduled a visit with his ailing grandmother at her nearby Retirement Hospice, where she had been bed-ridden since the death of her daughter – Archibald’s mother – nearly two years prior. Her health was on a steady decline, and before she passed sometime within the next year, Archibald had to find out some information that was suddenly pertinent. If he was a Generation Five entrant into Hell, as Archibald Matthews claimed, he needed to find out the motives and fate of his predecessors. He knew how his father had passed, and his grandfather…but before that, it was a mystery. He wanted to know why his bloodline was so tainted and dark, seemingly destined to give Hell an advantage. * * * * * The Retirement Hospice, on the outskirts of Oxford, was small and quaint. It wasn’t a Retirement Home, for this was a place where constant care and medication needed to be administered to the patients. Much along the lines of a Hospital, appointments needed to be arranged for the patients that are staying at Oxford’s local hospice, and Archibald had to wait nearly a week before he could see his grandmother. When he entered her room, her head slowly turned towards Archibald, and a small smile curled from the outer-edges of her mouth, “You still look a lot like your mother, no matter what she used to say.” The thought made Archibald smile as well, if only for a moment. His heart quickly sank, because her statement was just a reminder of the fact that his mother was gone. “How you feeling?” he asked, gently sitting on the edge of her bed. Archibald’s gaze drifted around the room towards the various machines that monitored her health, and slowly back down to her eyes, where he found her gaze fixated upon him. “Don’t worry about me, Archie Allen. The tubes are keeping me alive.” Archie Allen was his nickname from his grandmother, condensing down Archibald Allen MacGregor into a more grandma-friendly name. He only allowed Heather and his grandmother to call him Archie, anyone else usually suffered a deathly stare and smart-assed remark fired back at them. He pursed his lips together, unsure of how to ask the questions – or more importantly what questions to ask – and if his grandmother could give him any sort of answers. “Grandma, I need to ask you something important. Lately, I’ve become interested in our family history, and I was wondering if you could shed some light on the subject for me.” He paused a moment, because he felt like he was walking a fine line between decency and indecency, “I’ve looked around my place, and I can’t find anything on our family past Grandpa.” He watched as her aged eyes slowly drew closed, and he could hear the nearby respirator fill with air. Above her head, the EKG monitor danced silently arm-in-arm with her heartbeat, which apparently was weak, although quickening noticeably. When she finally spoke, her voice held more resolve than it had before, “Oh Archie Allen…the only place you will find information on our family is at my place. The last I heard, it was being maintained by our former housekeeper. All of the information you need should be in the attic.” He gently pushed her silver hair away from her face and tucked it behind her ears, and then adjusted the pillow on which her head rested. Her face held a look of concern. “Is everything OK, grandma? You look worried.” She faked a smile, “Oh, I’m just wishing you would come around more, is all. I think we’re the only two members of our entire family left, if memory serves me correct. Which, lately, it hasn’t been.” The statement was true, although Archibald didn’t reinforce it by acknowledging her. Their bloodline was coming to a close, unless Archibald managed to find a girlfriend and father a child one day. “Don’t worry, I’ll be sure I bring you flowers next time. But I really need to be looking up stuff at your old place. How about I stop back tomorrow, and we’ll talk for a few hours?” The concern and worry across her face was erased by delight. She smiled, and raised one hand up to Archibald’s, “That would be wonderful. You’ve grown into such a strong man.” Once again, Archibald MacGregor sat silently. He didn’t have the heart to tell her about his alcohol problems, and he was confident in the fact that he knew she was just being nice. His grip on hers was weak, not because of his health or her brittle nature, but because his own body was in an extremely weak state from his withdrawal from alcohol. He stood up, and made his way towards the doorway. He turned and smiled at his grandmother, who probably hadn’t smiled this in many years. As he made his way out the door, he could have sworn he heard her voice, barely above a whisper, bear a warning: “Be strong, Archie Allen. It won’t be easy…” * * * * * Over the ages, he has been known by many names. Satan. Lucifer. Legion. Abbadon. The Devil. Beelzebub. For now, he has settled on a new moniker: Typherion. Hell isn’t jagged rocks, covered in flames, with lava and souls of the dead. Nor is the Devil a horned beast, with red skin and a tail that sharpens into a spear at its tip. The mythologies can paint their own version of Hell any way they like, but for the time being, Hell was quiet and calm. Almost peaceful, if not for the specifics. He stood at the end of a long table within a large marble room, the massive arched roof being held up by countless black pillars. Where red skin and black hair should be, there was tanned skin and sandy-blonde hair. Where perfectly black eyes should be shimmering eerily, a perfect pair of green eyes glanced around his lair. Typherion chose to embody himself in an image that most of Hell’s occupants would envy, and that is the form of a perfect male. One of the seven deadly sins, Envy leads to many things. You envy what you want, or can’t have, and ultimately it builds towards greed or hatred. Hell was founded on these principals, and continues to thrive on them. Most men wanted to look different, and most women wanted their spouses to be something else. You hate what you can’t have, and what better way to run Hell than to be the quintessential essence of what everyone wants, but can’t have. As Typherion stood at the head of the table, he softly fingered through what appeared to be a portfolio. With the slow and careful movements of transferring a newborn to it’s mother, he turned the pages and looked at the photographs. When he spoke, his voice was soft and commanding, “He’s a fifth generation, is he? That means his father is out on the battlefields now, commanding millions under him, all while awaiting my command.” Around the table, three identical men stood side by side. Each had their hands clasped together across their respective midsections, and each wore white suits with a white tie. Their hair was slicked back, and their eyes never left their master. They spoke in unison, “Yes, m’lord.” Typherion turned towards the far wall, which was an enormous opening leading out towards what appeared to be a balcony. He glanced past the balcony, and past the miles and miles of rolling hills, past the millions of soldiers under his command. His gaze breached the battlefield between Heaven and Hell, and across the millions of soldiers under His command, and towards the palace of the Almighty. The Palace of God, across the far reaches of Hell, The Battlefield, and Heaven stood His Palace. He knew at once that Heaven was trying to recruit Archibald MacGregor. Being the manifestation of evil has its perks, and he knew that his equal in Heaven had sent a messenger to Earth in an attempt to keep Archibald MacGregor on the correct path. Where Heaven can walk the Earth to persuade, Hell is forbidden. Through indirection, events, dreams, and anomalies does Hell do it’s bidding. “He’s already being directed away from us,” Typherion paused, with his gaze still cast across the great plains of Hell and Heaven, undoubtedly searching for a the matching gaze of God, “I want you three to do whatever it takes to pull him back in. Our ranks are slowly diminishing, we need him.” Once again, the three spoke together, “As you wish, m’lord.” Typherion finally turned back towards his three underlings, who were generation sevens themselves, and issued his final command with such intensity that the air around him cracked and popped with jagged spikes of electricity. “Through indirection…break him.” * * * * *
