Supernatural Conversation It was one of those days. The sun shined high in the sky as if held by some invisible wires. The sky was devoid of any rain clouds and the grass seemed to gain even more color every second he looked at it. It was moments like these that made him feel awe for the power of nature. He looked at the kids as they jumped around and laughed out loud, without any worry whatsoever. He longed to feel like they did. The lines of worry had already been drawn on his face and it seemed like they would never leave. The innocence and ignorance of a cruel world was something he yearned for. Death had been following him everywhere. He sometimes had to get out and look at the world, even though it was through desensitized eyes. It sometimes felt like he was floating and looking at the world from above. He couldn’t help but feel like he didn’t belong. The park was bustling with people of all ages. Dogs were running around, Frisbees were flying and couples shared kisses on picnic sheets. It was like a normal day in the life of mortality. He had always wondered why they had to die in the first place. It was not until he met Death that he understood. Life had never meant as much to him as it did now. He knew that this scene he was watching intently was special and he would never see it again. It was as if he was able to discern every little detail that made it different. Something he had taken for granted. Death had explained how the world works to him. He eyed a small bench a few feet from him and walked towards it. He could see the ducks flapping about in the pond. He sat down. Time seemed to pass unmercifully. His life seemed to be falling apart with every passing second. A finger tapped him on the shoulder that startled him. He looked at the wrinkles in the face of the old lady and calmed down. Her grin was wide and friendly. “Young man, do you mind if I sit here? My legs aren’t what they used to be,” she said as she patted her legs softly. Antonio motioned for her to sit down. She did. She held an abnormally big brown purse in her lap and a black hat that almost covered her whole face. She looked almost comical. After gathering information on her whereabouts, she looked over to Antonio. “What brings you to the park, young man?” It took Antonio a little bit to realize that she was talking to him. His immersion in watching the scene before them was one of almost scientific research. “What? Err… I’m just here to get some fresh air,” he answered without looking at her. “Young people today…” she sighed. “They have so many troubles on their minds. When I was a young woman, the only trouble was when you would get married and that you knew how to fix up the house for your husband. They were much simple times,” she said now turning completely towards him, expecting him to do the same. “Simple times indeed,” was his only answer. There was silence for a few minutes as they Antonio watched everything that was happening in front of him and the old lady watched him intently. No frown or half smile went unnoticed by her. It was as if she could trace the lines etched on his face and read him like a book. “What is it that has got you so worried, young man? I have been around long enough to know that something has you troubled.” She waited for his response intently, actually worried for him and whatever ailed him. “I’ve been thinking a lot about Death,” he said expecting the usual answer that anyone would give a comment like that. What followed was something he hadn’t expected. The old lady smiled. “She is a wonderful young woman isn’t she? Beautiful, eh?” she asked him innocently. Antonio was startled by her answer. He turned over to her for the first time since she sat down besides him with surprise on his face. She laughed gently. He looked at her, really looked at her for the first time. There was something about her, something different. He could see hundreds of years of experience in her eyes. “If you know her, then you must be one of them,” he said, trying to understand what was happening. He looked worried for a moment. He wasn’t used to meeting people like her just walking around. “Of course, we all know her. I was wondering why you knew her though,” she said, putting a comforting hand on his shoulder. “It is not usual for a mortal to meet Death and survive to tell the tale.” He sighed. He knew the truth of that. For months, he had been witness to Death doing what she does best. The conflict within him was too much to bear though. He didn’t know if he loved Death or hated her for snuffing the life out of all those innocent people. Knowledge that she did it only because that was her existence and not because she wanted to made him feel a little better. The more time she spent with her, the more he understood the way the mind of a god works. “She needed help,” was all the answer he gave her. It was enough, he thought. He looked back to the people enjoying their lives, their worries mostly held back in order to enjoy these few hours of total release. “So, who are you?” he asked her. He was interested in knowing more about this old lady who obviously approached him with something on her mind. “My name is Ceridwen. I come from Wales. I’ve spent my whole life regretting my mistakes until now. I have decided to live whatever is left for me to live. I will tell you my story,” she said as Antonio listened intently to her. “Long ago, I had the most beautiful daughter you could imagine. Her beauty was unrivaled by any on the small area we inhabited. Her name was Creirwy. I also had a son. For some unfortunate reason, he was nowhere near the beauty of my daughter. I tried every magic trick I knew in order to give him beauty but there was nothing I could do. I felt sad.” He could see the sadness in her eyes as she recounted her story. I decided that is he wasn’t going to be beautiful; he would have as much wisdom as possible. I created a concoction that would give him that wisdom. I had hired a young boy to stir the cauldron where the potion was boiling. The first three drops from that potion would give him unrivaled wisdom and the rest would be poison. While stirring, the young boy, Gwion, accidentally splashed the three drops on his hand. It burned him and he quickly put his hands in his mouth, giving him wisdom and knowledge,” she explained. Antonio was spellbound. He had been around gods for a while now, but he had yet to hear one of their stories from their own mouths. All he knew were the stories he had learned from books and in high school. “I chased that poor boy for days. We turned into every animal imaginable as I chased him until I was able to catch him and eat him. I became pregnant after that. I knew that Gwion would be my offspring so I decided to kill him. I think I had spent so much time around mortals that some of their ideas rubbed off on me. When he was born, I threw him away but didn’t kill him. He became one of the greatest bard’s in the history of Wales, Taliesin. I wish… I wish I had raised him as my own,” she said almost in tears. The memories of her past ruffled her otherwise confident nature. Antonio was the one who put his hand on her shoulder now. She stifled a cry and sighed audibly. She gazed into his eyes. She looked deep down and saw the real Antonio. She saw the man that he had become and she smiled. “I think you and her would be perfect for each other,” she told him with her returning smile. “Who?” Antonio asked perplexed for a moment. “You and Death,” she said as she stood up. He looked away from her. He knew a relationship or anything with Death was not possible. He knew he was hoping for the impossible. “It’s not possible… my mortality… doesn’t let me,” he said as he hung his head in defeat. “There is always a way. Your mortality makes you who you are. Never forget that. You will find a way,” she said. When Antonio heard this, it didn’t register at first. There was a way for him to be with Death? Did she know a way? He turned towards her, but the old lady, the old goddess was already gone. In the distance she saw a soaring eagle that seemed to call to him. He smiled. She had put it all into perspective and now it was time for him to find a solution and stop wallowing in defeat. He looked one more time at a sight he would never see before. The awe he felt for the world would always be with him. Life had new meaning to him and he intended to live his life to the fullest. His love for Death would keep him going.
