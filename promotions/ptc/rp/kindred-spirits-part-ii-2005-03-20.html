Kindred Spirits? (Part II.)

20 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-20 “I went beyond ‘gentleman’,” Emilo insisted, tactfully. “Giving up my window seat runs the risk of borderline ‘knightly,’ dear.” “Ha!,” retorted Maya without missing a beat. “So you think sitting in the seat assigned to you by your ticket entitles you worship-class status? Well hone, that’s just downright clever. If I didn’t know any better -which I don’t-” she added, smiling, “I’d say the five minutes and forty-five seconds you spent in the restroom were used to come up with that, am I right?” Emilio’s cheeks flushed red. He leaned back against his seat just in time to feel his ears pop. “Now descending to an altitude of 25,000 feet,” remarked the captain, on cue. “Expected time of arrival: twenty-five minutes.” Emilio callously reached up and wound the air vent dial to high. The cool air felt good on his aching head, blowing fresh air through his curly, sweaty hair. Unfortunately, the vent was angled somewhat more in Maya’s direction, and the pressure simply blew her long hair back in her face, causing a slight frown to weave across her tired lips. “Stewardess, ‘nother scotch, please.” Emilio reached up and rubbed the sleep out of his eyes. “Oh, and shaken, not bitched.” Maya rolled her eyes. “Look hone, if you can’t take a simple joke...” Emilio waved away her negativity with an open palm as if chasing an annoying fly. “I’m sorry, babe. I’m just a little...exhausted, that’s all. I mean,” he added, leaning back in his seat to crack his neck to the left, “you know my schedule...” “Yes dear,” she said, letting out a slight sigh, “I know your schedule.” And indeed, she did. In complete honesty, however, the Global Title match was the furthest thing from Emilio’s mind. The moist poignant issue at hand was actually the love of a certain woman, sitting a mere foot away from him. Late at night, after hours of making love, Emilio would sit on the hotel bathroom counter and stare at himself in the mirror while his new love, Maya, slept. Instead of contemplating the future of his career in professional wrestling, he dove into the task of forming mental columns of “pros” and “cons” of a potential relationship. He store in the mirror at his reflection and smiled. His father once told him that since people were backward creatures, you could only see a man’s true soul by looking at them through the lense of a mirror. But the reverse image of a dryly, phony smiling Emilio was nothing more than an odd looking man, approaching his 40's, with a semi-functioning body, and a head full of crazy ideas. All that peered back at him, really, were a pair of deep brown eyes with a subtle hint of fear, but maybe a small spark of confidence. Maybe. For the last two nights he had maintained this dirty ritual; Literal self-refection and evaluation until the wee hours of the morning. A myriad of conflicting thoughts would riddle his mind until it hurt, and he’d rack his brain some more, poking at it ‘til he drew mental blood. Soon, the tepidly dangerous ritual would subside, and he’d pass out on the bed next to her as if nothing had happened. Maya’s alarm clock would go off two hours later, and she’d wake him at 8:00 PM with a kiss, and roll him out of bed so he could make coffee for her. She didn’t see the tired in his eyes. As amiable a figure as she appeared, her sensibilities when it came to reading Emilio, were beyond reproach. Didn’t she see what she was doing to him? Or did she...and just didn’t care? Emilio couldn’t bear the thought. Too much time had been spent already, shoving his career aside, in hunt of this rare diamond. ~ Emilio tried to close his eyes, blocking out the chatter of the airline conversations. “What’s that?” some man asked excitedly on a cell phone a few rows back. “Why, that’s great news! ...Honey,” Emilio could hear the sounds of quiet ruffling, the man shaking his wife awake, followed by, “honey, it’s a boy!” A slight round of applause followed as he made this announcement publically, warranting a respectful response from the nearby passengers. Emilio simply sighed. Leaving his hands to rest, he clapped with the bashing of his eyelids, dying to get the sting out of his dry, bloodshot eyes. “Can’t get your mind off the match, can you, honey?” His companion’s sweet words merely infiltrated his inner-ear, stirring him awake, irritably. Maya reached over and hugged Emilio’s shoulder, oblivious. “Yes, honey. That’s it,” he whispered, letting out a yawn. “I just can’t get my mind off the match.” But of course, he was not; Thinking about the match merely led to more self-inflicted doubt. The loss at the preliminary match -a takeaway at the hands of Mal Somers- was already unbearable. Before the colossal failure of an event, his friends had told him the odds were in his favor and his fans expressed their lucid excitement. And PRIME, well, PRIME was counting on him. But he failed them. He failed all of them... He promised himself he wouldn’t let that happen again, he wouldn’t let the emotions of the moment choke his spirit and render him helpless when the need to be strong arose. Besides, getting too worked up for a to-do long before it happened was something Emilio always compared to wheeling around a load of bricks for a chimney today that he planned on building tomorrow. No, he wouldn’t drain himself of all excitement like he did last time. He wouldn’t drive himself into physical vulnerability. Last time, he walked down the aisle with the acid in his stomach, a cyst of determination and nervousness, all bubbling in his cavities. It didn’t pay off. And it wouldn’t pay off now either. “Well hone,” Maya chirped in, interrupting his thoughts, “maybe you just need a good night’s sleep.” As he turned to give her the infamous “duh” glare, she met him with a warm smile. He tried to force one back at her. “You think?” he asked internally. “Yeah,” Emilio replied, faking a massive yawn. “Maybe.” She got the point. Maya turned away and bit her bottom lip. “Sorry...I was only trying to help.” “It’s okay,” Emilo said, convincing himself as much as much as he was convincing her, “I think I’m just going through a...phase, is all. I’ll...I’ll be alright.” “Humph.” Maya let out an irritable grunt. “Honey, really, if you’ve got other things on your mind...” “But...” Emilio stuttered, trying to defend his vulnerable self. But as always, his protest was useless in the face of her tirades. “If you don’t think things will work between us, then let’s just call it off.” She folded her arms gently over her chest and peered out the window. The great Pacific ocean was below them now, a swirl of dark green and blues. He expected her onslaught to be something similar...dark and deep. Instead, it was a perfect jab to his heart, flash flood of sorts. A river of emotion poured through him. And there it was. “But all I’ve been thinking about is you,” he retaliated, turning to face her directly. She remained turned away from him, transfixed on the window. He called her name again. “Honey?” She turned. She looked hurt. Meeting his fiery glare, she looked down at her feet. Emilio’s shoulders slumped. The blood began to cool. “Look,” he said, gently taking her by the cheek and directing her gaze back at him, trying to rejuvenate her trust with a gentle glare from his deep brown eyes, “why don’t we just give it a rest for a few days...and see how things go? I mean, we’ll see where ‘we’ are after the match.” “Okay.” Emilio smiled, bent over, and planted her on the cheek with a tender kiss. “But for now, let’s get some shut e-” “Ten minutes until we’re over the Western United States,” the captain informed over the intercom. Emilio shook his head. His languid body slunk down in the chair, and he fantasied about falling asleep, a sleep so deep that he would have to be waken up the stewardess after they landed. Exhaustion took over. They planned on getting a hotel. When they got off the plane they’d catch a bite to eat, maybe have a quick drink -and then they planned on getting a hotel. A sunset view suite with a nice, relaxing Jacuzzi. He’d crawl along the floor with her, wrestling on the soft rug. They’d feed each other over-ripened strawberries, dipped in chocolate fondue. Maybe open a bottle of port wine... PRIME would be paying for the entire endeavor, so why not? Maybe for once he’d get to bed on time. In fact, he could get up early the next morning. Yes...maybe get in a few hours of training in before a nice brunch with Maya. He could even repeat the process for a few days. Live the good life, take it easy right up until the match... A slim smile spread across his pink lips as he breathed in deeply, the air pressure blowing softly across his face, tickling his nose. Maya curled up next to him, content to let her man rest until the plane finally ascended to earth. It appeared that her man was finally going to get some rest- however little. She smiled. ~ Emilio’s eyes shot open. “What is it, dear? Hone?” Something primordial kicked him in the gut. A sensory gland under his skin sent a drop of adrenaline to his heart. The blood rushed through his veins and his muscles tensed. Although he was in the plane, he was back in the jungle, an M-16 around his aching shoulder, bruised and tired, a pink line of flesh from where the strap was. A frag grenade was clipped to his left pant leg. The sticky sweat of fear emanated from the hollow jungle ground. No, no he wasn’t. He reached over and felt Maya. “Out of my way!” A tall beefy man made his way down the aisle of the plane, shoving startled passengers back into their seats. He had short curly tan hair, a plump rounded gut, and an awful, crooked smile. His teeth were the color of morning piss. Emilio imagined an appropriate stench emanating from his breath. “Excuse me, pardon me.” He seemed to be in quite a rush. And when he was finally a row in front of Emilio, he narrowed his eyes in on the professional wrestler, and Emilio had no choice but to peer back at him. Emilio’s heart sunk in his chest; A knot tightened in his gut. “Stay back,” Emilio whispered, turning to Maya. “Huh?” He gave her a gentle shove, forcing her protectively deeper in the seat. “Damn it Emilio, what’s wrong with you!?” The man stopped. He ran a hand through his hair, and nervously grabbed a handful of curls. Emilio cringed. ”He seems like the mob type, really.” The man was slightly bent over now, resting an arm on a seat to catch his breath. ”Apparently, out of shape, too.” Finally, he stood up. Emilio quietly undid his belt. As the man neared, Emilio could see the bulge in his side pocket. A bad omen for an already downhill experience. “And why is he heading toward the cabin?” A wave of emotions flustered Emilio out of his seat. His heart began to throb in his chest. The man looked over and gave him a coy grin. Emilio’s muscles tensed. ”What is it he’s reaching in his pocket for?” “Hey buddy,” said the man, in an all-too-friendly manner. He tightened the grip on the object in the inside of his pocket, and said, “why don’t you just relax and take a seat?” “Over my dead body.” So be it. Before Emilio could so much as contemplate the four-foot leap from point “A” to “B,” the man had unsheathed his weapon. And Maced Emilio in the eye. Emilio curled up in the fetal position, sprawled out across the aisle as passenger’s screamed, and the hot burn tore at his retinas like a wild dog dragging down a deer by the legs. A cold shudder -the kind you have with “gross” pains- went down his spine. “So much for the hotel.” ~ But by the time the plane had landed, the cuffs were off. “I’m sorry,” explained the cop, motioning toward Emilio’s face, “but we had a suspect fitting your general description.” “ ‘General Description?’ ” asked Emilio, gritting his teeth and letting the anger seethe through. “And was this alleged terrorist anywhere near seven feet tall!?” “No,” said the cop as he stepped back and lit a cigarette. Emilio sighed. Instead of the hotel, they’d be sleeping on their next flight. The incident has cost them the first one, and the next plane wouldn’t arrive for four hours. “Have a good day, sir,” the cop said in an almost mocking tone as he turned on his heels and left. When he was almost out of sight, carrying his pudgy frame down the looming airport tunnel, Emilio instinctively gave him the finger. ~ Just as the sleeper cop, Emilio’s paranoia had gotten the best of him. His internal brainwork was still ticking...but it made sporadic jumps. He felt disoriented, disillusioned, betrayed. Behind him was a long list of embarrassing defeats. And in front of him were two inevitably separate roads: one with Maya, the other with his wrestling career. According to the map, they weren’t congruent, and at no point on the trip did they cross paths. Basically, Global Title endeavors meant dying poor and lonely, and the path to Maya meant dying without the Global Title, or the PRIME Universal Title. At that point in time, there appeared to be no compromise. The only third option, it seemed, were taking neither paths. Unacceptable. ~ Halfway through the second flight, without thinking, Emilio mixed the two drinks, the vodka and the whiskey. He tipped the shot glass up in the air and downed the vile liquid in one excruciating gulp. His throat burned. “Miss,” he said, directing the gaze of the passing stewardess toward his empty shot glass, “that drink is disgusting,” he informed her, unhappily. The waiter narrowed her eyes in on him before looking over on to arm rest. She saw the two empty minis and frowned. “I’m sorry,” she said, “ but I’m not a waitress,” all the wail keeping the phony smile intact. “Don’t mix the wrong drinks, and you won’t have a problem.” Emilio set ashamedly down in his seat. “Either that, or don’t drink,” she finished. Burn.
