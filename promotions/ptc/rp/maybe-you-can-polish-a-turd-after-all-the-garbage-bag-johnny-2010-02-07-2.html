Date: 2010-02-07 Maybe You Can Polish A Turd After All: The Garbage Bag Johnny Story Chapter 7: The Helix A Garbage Bag Production "I wish I didn't have to shoot you." -Dina Triggerfinger JANUARY 16, 2010 I sat there on the hard, flat bench in the holding cell, and I could hear Dina's son John talking to the officers in the other room as he waited for his mother to arrive. He was telling the cops about how I took him to meet some of the wrestlers backstage, and even the ones who played bad guys on TV were nice to him. Apart from me, he told them he liked Lane Stevens best. He thought Stevens was funny, even if Lane taught him a few words he wasn't supposed to know that I had neglected to accidentally teach him. I couldn't see any of them, but I could see their long shadows bending around the walls as they conversed. The paint on the bricks was yellow back from when they let people smoke in the cells. They didn't let you do that anymore. I knew they couldn't keep me there too long before deciding what they were going to do with me. They said I could make a phone call. I told them to use it to call Dina and tell her where her kid was. They said they were going to do that anyway, so I hadn't used the call yet. I didn't know any numbers, so I asked to see a phone book. They declined. At some point later, I asked if I could have a magazine. I figured I could tear out the staples and try to pick the lock with them. I could sneak out for a cigarette and be back in the cell before anybody noticed. They said they didn't have any magazines to give me either. There was a stretch of time there, too, where nobody was saying nothing. I figured that John got tired of talking or they had him distracted with a magazine. I knew they had to have at least one lying around. I could smell the print, fresher than baked bread. God, I was hungry. "What are they going to do with Johnny?" talked the shadow with the child's voice. "We're not sure yet." A deeper one answered. "We'll take everything into account figure out something real fair for everyone, alright?" "Alright." The larger shadow patted the smaller one on the head and walked off, growing exponentially in front of the light before shrinking back down and wavering off to oblivion. Then some more time passed, and I couldn't tell you how long it was. The moments didn't seem too different from one another. Nothing much changed, really, except the sizes and positions of the shadows on the square of wall across the way from the steel door through the three sections of space around two bars. It just stuck there unchanging, and I didn't feel it was my responsibility to try and nudge time along. It hadn't occurred to me to try and push it back. Most people would be lucky to break even, but none so wise to turn down a tiebreaker. Then, after a while, some more time passed again. A door burst open loud, and it let a bunch of commotion in. "Where's my son?" Dina's shadow pulsated in the square as she buzzed around. "Where? Thank god, John. There you are." Her shadow embraced her son's, and the two shadows melted into an amorphous eclipse for a long time. The shadows and light in between the two bars shaped into a candle. I laughed at myself for wondering if it meant anything. Of course it didn't. "Did he hurt you?" "No, Mom. It was fun!" He was energetic, aware that the situation around him had a certain gravity to it. I thought of how excited he was to go backstage and watch the matches from a suite. When it was over, he was tired, and he wanted to go home. I don't remember much else about it, except that I didn't feel like I was there. I couldn't take him back home and face Dina again, so I turned myself over and let the authorities handle it. "I got so many autographs!" "Not now, sweetie." Her shadow shifted, and I pressed my face against the bars. I saw her walk up to the oak counter. It looked more like a bank, really. There might have been an officer on the other side. I couldn't see that far. I could see the long yellow wall, and in the sliver of room after it ended, I could see Dina standing there, John at her side. "Miss Triggerfinger, I have a few things for you to sign, and then one of our officers will need to ask you some questions if you feel up to it." The accent drawled with a charcoaled friction from the throat. "Uh huh," she nodded, her hands fishing around at a clipboard she wasn't looking at. "Did he hurt my son?" "Not as far as we could assess, ma'am. There were no signs of abuse." The clerk's speech was peppered. "If you'd like to request a medical examination, we certainly would be able to point you in the right direction." "Um, no. That's fine." I heard scribbling in the pauses. "Is that all of them?" "Sure is. Officer Fernandez will have you for questioning now if you're ready." The officer was too tall for me to see his face, but I saw him guide her by the shoulder, and they came towards me. When she saw me, I withdrew, but I still watched as she turned away and didn't look back once she'd passed me. I don't know what the look was that I saw in her eyes, but it sure made me feel ashamed. I retreated back to the bench and sprawled across its levelness, hoping it would level me, too. It wasn't long enough for my whole body, and I couldn't find a comfortable way to lie on it, so I gave up. I couldn't sleep, but I lied there long enough to drift off into thought. For some reason, the only thing I could think of was a conversation I had with Dina that kept replaying in my head. It was back about nine or ten years ago on the day before Dina and I conned Randy's money from him and spent it on a car. I had been thinking of ways to con him for a while, at that point, but Randy never showed me anything I could use against him without a helping hand. Monster Truck Doug had been an informant for the county cops in Arbuckle. The town wasn't big enough for its own force, so county patrols stopped by once every few days and let Doug catch a break in exchange for dirt as long as he didn't fuck up anything too bad. Major property damage was something they couldn't sweep under the rug once the absurd story grew some legs, and he took off to Mexico. Sometimes, the cops would interrupt the parties I threw at his place to look for Doug. One of them, who I knew Doug used to deal with, tried to get some dirt from me. I told him what I'd heard, and apparently, bringing him back would've been too much effort for them. If word got out that Doug was in Mexico, and they didn't have the means to pursue him, his department would become a laughing stock among the county forces across the state, so he offered me double the reward money to five thousand dollars to bring him back myself. I was just one piece shy of being a dirt salesman. I just needed a set of wheels. I knew Randy kept enough money on him in his coat. He didn't trust banks and kept his whole stash on him in case an opportunity to buy some real drugs came along so he could cut them up and sell them out. Sometimes he had good money on him, but he always had some money on him. There wasn't enough money in it to invite him along. There wasn't enough money in the world for that. I just needed somebody else he wouldn't expect. Randy was always suspicious, and he was always more suspicious of the ones closest to him, knowing himself best of all. I was sitting on the couch, and there were some kids around, but they were all keeping together in groups, even the ones on the couch with me. I was smoking my own joint and not paying much attention to anyone when I heard a sharply articulated knock at the door and realized I had almost forgotten what the sound meant. The main door was open, and I could see Dina through the glass screen. She had left most of her stuff around the place, and she had the rest of it with her. "Shit." I opened the door. "I haven't seen you in like two weeks. Where you been?" "I've been grounded. My parents wouldn't let me leave." She stood out there, seeming reluctant to come in. "Is it alright if I stay here for a while?" "Of course." I looked around at everybody there, wasting themselves away. "Ain't no such thing as permission here." "Technically, I'm still grounded." She peeked her head inside and looked at all bands of kids there playing adolescent games, nodding off in piles, in bordered hazes of smoke. "Word gets around pretty fast in Arbuckle." "It doesn't get around that fast." Her eyes were lost and clueless, and all I could think about was Randy's stash. "Look, if you need to lay low here for a few days, that's fine, but this isn't the kind of place you can stay at forever." "You've been here a while." I had, and I didn't know how much longer I could hold out without silence or privacy. I was stuck in the party, and it whirled around without exits. "I'm looking to get out." Dust was starting to web itself around everything and everyone. "Soon." "Then I'm coming with you." She said it without thinking it over, pulled on my hand and ran her fingers down my arm. We each had the other by the throat, and at the time, there was a certain innocence to it. "I can't let you come with me." In truth, I needed her, and she could see it in my face. "Wherever I end up going, I don't think it'll be so easy." "I don't care." I could tell there was something wrong. Dina never liked Arbuckle, but that's what kept her there. There was something about being in a place she didn't like that fit her. "Anywhere's better than here." "Suppose you're right." I looked off, over her shoulder. She still hadn't come in. "But I haven't even told you where I'm going." It made her laugh a little, and then she just brushed a hair away from her face and looked up at me, and my knees started to jelly up. "Alright." Her smile widened and when she shined her teeth at me it made her lips look cherry red, even without any lipstick. "There's something we got to do first." It hadn't occurred to me then that she was pregnant. Someone described it to me once as feeling like love, and that's why I never really got too heavy into them.
