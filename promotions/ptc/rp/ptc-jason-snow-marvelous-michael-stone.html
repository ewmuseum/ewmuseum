"Marvelous" Michael Stone

13 May 2007

May 13, 2007 (There are moments to go. Mere moments. Jason Snow stalks a circled path backstage, flexing his fingers and firing wild-eyed animal glares over his shoulder at the camera from time to time. He walks in predatory strides, like a man much larger than his 6'1, 230 pounds. With his golden blond mane and unshaven face, he quite resembles a lion, as always he has, both in looks and in form. He is Jason Snow. He is the Primetime Central Unified champion. The magnificent title belt is displayed proudly over his shoulder, glistening beneath the backstage lights on the background of his open black leather jacket. Glinting gold and diamonds catch each and every eye in the viewing audience, and it seems far larger than any championship has a right to be - regardless of its prestige. In that way, perhaps, it’s much like its champion’s ego. Much like his boasts. Somewhere in this building, another championship is held under lock and key - smaller than his own, but not without its own prestige. It is the Infinite Gauntlet championship, and tonight, Jason Snow will look to drape it over his right shoulder, to compliment the one already on his left. He stops finally, his eyes locked on something away from the camera - always angry, those eyes. And then suddenly they snap on us, staring blazing blue laser beams into the living room of every viewer. His jaw is locked. His voice is steady... Dripping with what can be called nothing but sheer charisma...) Snow: “The Primetime Central Unified Championship...” (He looks down at the championship and lightly touches the word “Unified” with the fingers on his right hand. Rough fingers and rough hands, touching the delicate jewel-work of PTC’s highest prize.) Snow: “Not two weeks ago, I walked down a ramp, just like the one out there, stepped in a ring, just like the one out there, and in front of a brazilian filthy, disgusting, screaming peasants, I walked through the best that PTC had to offer, super-kicked Clifton Sage’s ass straight to hell, and walked back up that same ramp the first ever PTC Unified Champion! (As intense as ever, Snow’s eyes flare.) Snow: “That... is... G-reatness!” (He pauses for a moment, letting his glare linger, before finally letting it break and resuming his earlier pacing.) Snow: “And now here we are, two weeks later, about to do it all over again. And again, the result will be the same. In front of a combined twenty-thousand peasants with a combined seventeen teeth... In front of wenches holding their vaginas in hopes that the damn things don’t explode - for it is known, that the mere SIGHT of me has been KNOWN to cause ORGASMS! ...I’ll be walking back a newly crowned champion...” (And then suddenly - in fact, shockingly - a cocky smile appears on Jason Snow’s face. He shakes his head in long, slow motions...) Snow: “But it’s not like last time. This is Infinite Gauntlet - where the peasants come to play. Where the plebes take their shot, hoping desperately that maybe - just maybe - they’ll get lucky, and someday have a chance to fight for this...” (Again, he taps his gaudy championship...) Snow: “A real championship.” (Still smirking...) Snow: “So I’m sure that right now, between stuffing your fat, greasy faces with nachos and animal lard and signing your welfare cheques, you’re wondering: ‘Villain, why in the hinges of hell would you lower yourself to fight for a such a worthless championship?’ It’s a good question, and to be honest, it’s one that I even ask myself from time to time.” (With his right hand, Snow strokes the stubble of his chin, a faraway look in his blazing blue eyes...) Snow: “The truth is: yes, the Infinite Gauntlet Championship is far beneath me. By Greatness, it’s nothing more than the Beer-Belly Covering Championship of the Wooooooooorld!!! Nothing more than scap metal! A piece of trash! And I’m Jason Snow, damn it! Not some bottom feeding plebe with grease stains on my shirt and footprints on my ass - I’m looking at you, Dust.” (He pauses, his hand instinctively moving to the gold on his shoulder...) Snow: “So why then?” (The smirk returns, though his eyes never soften - they burn a hole through the camera, straight into each of our living rooms, as if, perhaps, he’s capable of leaping through the screen himself. Always there is an air of danger around Jason Snow - always.) Snow: “Because the other truth is, that damned Beer-belly Cover is the only sure way to get a shot at this...” (He smiles down at his Unified Title, sparkling magnificently on his shoulder beneath the hot lights of the arena’s backstage area...) Snow: “...something worth winning... Something worth defending, even if I have to come down here to the trek through the mud and the plebe piss to do it. Let it be known, plebes, that I, in all my Superiority in G-reatness, will follow thistitle all the way to the gates of hell. And I’ll do anything... ... ...anything... to keep it right here where it belongs. And if that means putting that filthy piece of trash, the Beer-Belly Covering Championship of the Wooooorld, around this perfectly sculpted waist... then so be it. Somewhere in the last near-decade, he’d learned to fall so deeply into the character of “Jason Snow,” that often it was difficult for him to climb back out. The character was an entirely different person, after all, but over the years it had become Jason’s bread and butter. Moreso, probably, than even his ability in the ring. “Great stuff, champ,” the camera man said as he disassembled his own bread and butter. Jason did nothing more than nod; he stayed quiet most of the time around the staff and crew, when the cameras were off. They all thought of him as “Jason Snow,” the self-absorbed catchphrase machine, and he didn’t care to spoil their illusions. It was better this way. Easier. “Good luck out there,” the camera man waved casually and headed off, his tripod slung over his shoulder and his camera already in its case. He’d put the thing away with startling quickness, but Jason supposed the man had done it so many times now that it had become automatic. That wasn’t a hard thing to relate to. Not for Jason. The steady roar of the audience down on the main floor pulsed through the arena walls, sending shivers up his spine as always they did. He took a seat on a nearby bench and leaned his head against the wall. He could feel its vibrations on the back of his skull from the noise of the crowd. They’re waiting for you, he thought, closing his eyes. For you... Minutes from now, he’d be among them - above them - risking his body and ego for their amusement. But that was all part of the gig. First, there was the irony that the roster was hell bent on tearing each other apart; and second, any one of them could break any of the security guards in half at a moment’s notice. “There’s someone here to see you,” the security guard went on. Members of an underpaid crew, along with several wrestlers, were running this way and that, hurrying to set up for tonight’s show. “What the hell ever made you want to be a wrestler?” “I need the money,” Jason replied bluntly, taking in his surroundings for the first time. “Money?” Dave laughed. “Came to the wrong place for that. Dave wrestled under the name Damien Jones, and he’d been around the business for twenty years or so. Later, Jason would learn that he’d come close to making the big time in his younger days, and now, in his middle forties, his body was predictably falling apart. So he had decided to buy a small time promotion with big dreams of putting it in every living room in North America. Five years later, and he’d managed a local television deal and a couple of failed Pay-Per-Views. “I’ve still got my job at the plant,” Jason shrugged. After high school, he took a job at the local cement plant and saved enough money for he and Raven’s small wedding. “The wife’s in school,” he told Dave. “I need the cash to put her through.” Dave looked shocked. “Jesus Christ! You’re married? How old are you?” “Nineteen.” Dave stopped walking and laughed. “When I was your age, I was out banging college kids between gigs. You’ve got to experience life when you’re young, kid. Ain’t nothing left when you’re old and broke all the fuck like me.” He shook his head again and then set their pace again. “So can you fight?” “Only thing I’ve ever been good at.” “Good,” Dave nodded, “because at the end of the day, that’s what matters. Doesn’t matter how flashy your moves are or how good you are on the mic. None of that means shit if you ain’t no good when the chips are down.” “I’m not much for talking in front of people,” said Jason. A wrestler passed by with a ringpost over his shoulder. He was in his early thirties, if Jason was any judge. “Never was either, myself,” Dave shrugged. He stopped in his tracks again and then brought Jason to a halt with a hand in his chest. Dave’s eyes traced him up and down. “‘Bout how much do you weigh?” “Two hundred,” Jason shrugged. “Two ten, maybe.” He’d become heavier in the years that followed, as he grew and became a fanatic when it came to training. “We’ll call it Two-twenty in the show,” said Dave. And then, without warning, he turned his head away and bellowed: “Mikey! Mikey, come on over here for a second.” Jason was confused. A moment later, a man that looked younger even than himself bobbed over. His blond hair was brighter than Jason’s own - obviously bleached - and he wore it long and wild, down past his shoulders. Jason nodded a greeting, and Mikey returned a bright, vibrant smile. “Jason Snow,” Dave put his arm around Jason’s shoulders. “I want you to meet your new tag team partner - ‘Marvelous’ Michael Stone.” May 13, 2007 Mikey looked like shit. Dark, heavy circles anchored his eyes, and his forehead was streaked with deepset lines. He was two years Jason’s junior, and yet seeing him now, he could easily pass for a man ten years older than that. His hair, once flowing bleached blond, was still the same length, but dark now, as it was naturally, limp, and thinning in the front. He’d been so handsome once, Jason remembered. They’d been paired together in the IWA for just that reason. “You guys are going to be the heart throbs of the organization,” Dave Johnston had told him once, and they’d even toyed with the idea of naming the team that. Instead, they settled on “Ruthless N’ Reckless” - or “Rn’R” for short. And in those days, for as handsome and teen magazine-worthy as Jason had been, Mikey had been also. He had been sculpted to perfection, as Jason was (and remained), but Mikey had lost weight in the years since Jason had seen him. He had never been an overly large man, considering his profession. Dave Johnston had called him two hundred and ten pounds, which meant he was probably closer to about one ninety-five. But now, he’d be lucky to go one-sixty. And standing a shade under six feet tall, Michael Stone, at twenty-five years old, had become an average man. Jason stared at him for a long time, his mouth parted slightly - his eyebrows high in curiosity. Had he not been so close to Mikey in those first few years of his career, he thought he might not recognize him now. He was dressed in a t-shirt and jeans, and the half grin on his face was a cocky one. Finally, it broke into a full smile as he shook his head and he said: “Jesus Christ, Jason, it’s been a long time.” It took a moment for Jason to respond, as he was lost in the moment, studying his old friend. “A few years,” he said finally. “Too long,” Mikey’s smile broadened. “But Jesus, look at you. I always said you were going to make it. Didn’t I tell you? Didn’t I?” He had. Even in those early days. “Don’t matter what they say to you, Jason,” he remembered Mikey saying one night after a match, while they shared a beer in the empty ring. It had been their job to take it down that night. “There ain’t nobody you can’t beat. You know it, they know it. Everybody knows it. The rest of this shit doesn’t matter. At the end of the day, it’s all about who can kick whose ass, right? That’s the way I see it anyway.” Mikey could never fight worth shit. He could make the crowd “Ooo” and “Ahhh,” but he was all flash and no substance. Dave Johnston had told Jason that right away. Mikey knew it too. “Some fucking belt,” Mikey quipped, and then, without warning, he came close and embraced Jason in a full on bear hug. That was just his way. Michael Stone had always been of a happy nature - on the surface, at least. He pulled away from Jason after a moment and shook his head, staring at the title belt. “Fuck me... you must get tired lugging that motherfucker around.” “It’s not so bad,” Jason half-smiled. “Yeah, I’ll bet,” Mikey laughed. Not a bad night’s work, huh?” Dave Johnston beamed in his makeshift office as he stuffed the wrinkled cash into an envelope and handed it over to Mikey. Jason’s own, identical envelope, was sitting beneath his hand in the pocket of his denim jacket. He couldn’t remember the last time he’d touched so much cash - perhaps he never had. He and Raven’s first anniversary was coming up, and the gears of his mind turned to what he might be able to buy her. But then he thought of rent coming due, the electric bill, and her tuition in the fall, and thought maybe just a nice dinner and a small gift were in order. Not that it mattered either way to Raven, but that only made him want to buy her something bigger and more expensive. Wrestling was an unpredictable business, and he’d learned that lesson fast. He couldn’t be sure when a night like this might happen again, but it did lift his spirits that Dave, a well-noted pessimist, had brighter eyes than any of his roster. “I’m telling you guys,” he went on, “fucking stroke of genius putting you two together. Genius.” He pointed at Mikey. “With your mic skills” - and then at Jason - “and your ring prowess, we could have the hottest ticket in all of wrestling! And damn it, I mean all of wrestling! There was no man - no man - in the IWA that could even compare to himself in the ring. It didn’t matter how big they were. He pounded on the little wirey guys with brute strength and he danced circles around the big ogres with speed and agility. It came naturally to him. It had always come naturally. Meanwhile, Mikey was the go to guy when it came to pumping the crowd up. They might only have been working crowds of two and three hundred (or seven hundred and fifty-two, tonight - a new record), but the man had a real gift for putting them into a trance. But the thing about Mikey was, he couldn’t fight worth shit. Not that it mattered. Jason was confident that he could pick up the slack in that area. “And don’t think there ain’t more where that came from,” said Dave, gesturing to the envelope. “I’m having t-shirts printed up. Raven hated Jason’s drinking, so he hid it from her as best he could, and he supposed that curbed the habit somewhat. But Mikey... his attachments to women lasted a night a piece, and his only commitments were to the IWA and his tag team. He supposed Dave suspected the same, but Mikey and Jason were his meal ticket - his last chance at getting to the international stage, even if it was vicariously. He didn’t say shit to Mikey. I can’t take this company to the top no matter what I do. It ain’t the industry, it ain’t the system - it’s me. But you... you got something real special in you, kid. Everybody on the roster knows it. Fuck - Mikey knows it better than anyone. I never had what it takes to make it to the big show, but you do. And I ain’t talking about SIW neither - that’s just for starters. And...” he hesitated for a moment, suddenly unable to look Jason in the eye, “...I already sold the rights to you anyway. They bought out your contract.” Jason froze. “So you mean...” “I mean you have to take this deal, kid,” Dave pushed the contract forward, “‘cause you can’t come back here. It’s for the best.” “You sold me before you even talked to me about it?” “I got bills to pay too,” said Dave defensively. “Child support, fucking alimony... I got fucking gambling debts wedged so far up my ass I can taste the God damn things. And just because the RnR merchandise is selling hot doesn’t mean the rest of this shit is. Fuck, I got a storage closet out there filled with t-shirts and coffee mugs - nothing more than constant reminders of the fucking money they cost! Derrickson offered me a good deal - a fucking great deal - and I took it.” They stared at each other a moment, one man at the end of a disappointing career and the other at the threshold of a legendary one. SIW would be the first step - but only the first, and in the scheme of things, a relatively small one. But in the moment, all he could feel was a pain in his stomach. He’d have to move to the states, probably - what would Raven think of that? She was already talking about kids... But then again, what choice did he have? “Look, kid, I’m-” “Fine,” Jason interrupted. He pulled the contract close, picked up a nearby pen, and found the place to sign. In that motion, he secured himself financially, and would never have to worry about money again, and yet the hollow pain in his stomach was, perhaps, just as permanant. May 13, 2007 “I can’t thank you enough,” Mikey said, staring down at the cheque in his hands. “Don’t mention it.” “You’re a good friend, Jason,” Mikey went on. He folded the cheque neatly and slipped it into his pocket, taking a moment to smile meekly. He always looked embarrassed after taking the money. This must have been the fifth or sixth time in the years since they’d been a tag team. Always there was a different excuse, and always, Mikey professed to be clean. But every time he came back, it was more of the same. And yet still, somewhere in Jason’s stomach, an invisible ulcer set itself at ease. “I’ll let you get ready for your match,” Mikey said with another smile and a nod. “Good luck out there tonight.” “Thanks, Mikey,” said Jason, returning to his bench. “I hope this new gig works out for you. Really.” It wasn’t a lie. Jason had always hoped - prayed even - that one day he might see the name “Marvelous” Michael Stone on his television screen. For his own sake, more than Mikey’s. February 2, 2000 “You look really nice,” his wife had said to him earlier that night while she was straightening his tie. It wasn’t often that Jason Snow wore a suit. Dave had told him to wear one tonight “to show the guys what a real star looks like and give them something to remember.” So he obliged, with the necessary help from Raven. “Makes me actually want to go too,” she had said a moment after, taking a step back to get a better look at him. “The last time I saw you dressed up was at our wedding. So handsome...” But Raven always hated wrestling - the violence of it - so she had stayed at home and Jason was here now, just weeks before his SIW debut, watching an IWA event. To call it an event was a stretch, after what Jason had seen while accompanying Trevor Derrickson to an SIW show. They had lights and lasers - a television announce team. The IWA had Maury the ring announcer working for them as a side gig when he wasn’t driving a Humpty Dumpty truck. Jason felt out of place and overdressed as he slipped through the darkness and found a near-empty bleacher to take a seat. He saw Dave speaking with one of the crew at the side of the ring. He was pointing at the lights overhead that shone down on the ring area - a purchase that he’d made just a month before Jason signed with SIW. Someday I’m going to miss these guys, he thought to himself with a sigh, though in the years that would pass, most of them would be nothing more than faint memories. Except for Mikey. Reckless Life, by Guns N’ Roses blared without warning through the P.A. and perked the crowd up - a couple of hundred kids looking for something to do that night mixed with a couple dozen real wrestling enthusiasts. Jason looked to the curtain that lead backstage, and a moment later, “Marvelous” Michael Stone popped out, a cocky smile smeared on his young, handsome face as if it was painted there. Jason couldn’t help but smile to himself as well, and he glanced around at the booing audience. These were mostly casual wrestling fans, many of whom had never seen Mikey before in their life - and yet they hated him. It was like he could plant it right into their souls. And at the same time, he supposed there was a love mixed in there as well. Mikey slid in the ring and struck a pose as soon as he hit his feet, prompting more booing from the audience. Jason was in awe of the man. But the best part hadn’t even happened yet - that came when “Marvelous” Michael Stone, decked out fully in his snake skin boots and jet black leather pants, raised the cheap, crackling microphone to his smirking lips. “Alright, plebes, shut your damn mouths while I address an urgent matter of public safety,” he said over the boos, “I’ve got to tell all you fat, balding, inbread low-lives to reach over and cover your wives’ eyes - for, by G-reatness, it has been well documented... that the mere SIGHT of me has been KNOWN to cause ORGASMS!” They booed him even harder then, though it was mixed with laughter. They hated him, or at least they pretended to. Some of them, Jason thought, seemed to hate him deeply and honestly, not just as a wrestler. And yet they were each entertained by him as well, and no one - not one of them - could pry their eyes away. “These days,” Trevor Derrickson’s voice rang in Jason’s head, his eyes still glued on his former partner, “wrestling is as much about entertainment as it is about fighting. You start showing us a little charisma, and I can make this contract look like peanuts.”
