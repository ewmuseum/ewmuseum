As Crazy As Is Necessary

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 It’s been said I’m out my mind to the fullest Alone in the crib, pluggin’, fuckin’ with Russian roulette with six bullets -Celph Titled Luther Ryxn’s not the only one in this tournament suffering from a personality disorder. Besides the four hundred other wrestlers that suffer from the same affliction (isn’t that weird how it’s so common among pro wrestlers?), Chandler Tsonda has a similar psychological disease. It’s rarely talked about, but the disease may be killing Tsonda as we speak. The strain he’s got is rarely seen in humans and the effects are always regrettable. It’s such an unknown in the medical world that it has no name. For the sake of the GTT, we’ll call it SPS: Single Personality Syndrome. You see, once in a while an individual comes along whose singular psyche is so full of vitriol, such pure fury and venom, that it has adverse physiological and mental effects on the victim. But if you doubt these claims, let us diagnose the subject. * * * * * Saturday, 12/03/05 The airport was already ruining my Saturday morning. First, my agent calls me to tell me all about my draw for this tournament and that I’m booked for Tuesday, which means less then twenty four hours between this and a Wednesday showdown with that fuckbox Face-Eater in PRIME. But at the moment the only details pertinent to my life are those in the hand of these remedial school dropouts at the desk. I’m on the "colicky baby and fat women" flight. To make matters worse, the assholes at PRIME won’t spring for me to fly first class to a tournament (despite the fact that I’m the emblem of the goddamn federation, along with Deville, Sirrajin, et al.), so it’s coming out of my pocket. I’m listening to my iPod and trying to avoid touching any of the cave people in the terminal. It starts off with one of the squirrelly looking airline fuckups taking down the flight time from the big board where it’s sitting. In the place of "12:30 P.M" goes "1:30 P.M." The herd lets out a mighty, gaseous groan, having already gorged themselves on Sbarro and fake Chinese food. I walk up to the fake podium-desk. The glorified stewardess awaits my words with a faux smile. Yes, she’s a stewardess. She’s not a "flight attendant," she’s a female steward. We’re all attending the flight, aren’t we? So am I a flight attendant? No, I’m just a guy who wants to go somewhere that I’m not right now, preferably via airplane. "Hello, sir, can I help you with something today?" Why yes, you could let me punch you directly in the right eye, you thick cunt. There’s really no reason not to say that one to her face, but I’ll let her be for the moment, if only because I need to get on this plane. Her outfit is hideous; rarely have I seen something as ill-fitting to the female form as the stewardess costume. She’s plain and not-all-that-bad-looking, but she’s already too cheery and probably thinks going down on a guy is degrading to women. Fuck those girls (that’s right, those petty little sweater-wearing Alanis listener feminists are girls, not women); if it weren’t for the fine head-giving brigade of women who continue that wonderful tradition that our nation was built on, I’d probably commit seppuku on the spot. "Listen, I really need to get out of here. I’ve got somewhat of an important appointment. If I don’t show up for this thing, things will go very badly for me." If you’ll condescend to me, I’ll do it to you, you dolt. People tell me that there’s always venom in my voice, but don’t realize they’re the reason why I have to be so goddamn clear about what’s what. If it takes me spitting carbonic acid for everyone to understand what I need and when I need it, then so be it. "Sir, everyone here is trying to go somewhere and since you’re all on this flight, you’ll all get there together. I can’t really do anything for you. Perhaps you’d like to visit our food court, which has a Sbarro and-" See? This is what happens when I try to be a reasonable and lucid human being. So fuck that, I’ll be the guy you see on TV. I’ll be the worst of humanity. "No, I don’t think you understand. I don’t want Sbarro, I don’t want to travel altogether with the fat, smelly people on this flight, and I certainly don’t want you lying to me about the fact that there’s nothing to do. One or two people look up as I raise my voice at Thundercunt, but inevitably the airport does this to some people. I look her straight in the eye and can see her cowering, afraid of little old me. But I haven’t even dropped the bomb yet, the one thing that I know will send her heading for the hills. "I want to switch flights and be out of here within the hour. I know there are other flights, so just find me one." "It’s not that simple, sir." Oh but it is. You go clicky-clicky on the keyboard so the nice, good-looking man at the desk can go on his merry way. Looking more closely at her face, I can see that she’s breaking out on the sides of her face, although it’s covered up well. It’s probably stress, which only occurs for people who aren’t good enough at their job for it to be easy. On the other hand, I rarely break out. I also happen to be very, very good at what I do so there’s a nice little correlation to everything. "The plane is stopped in Houston for refueling and IAH has heavy traffic today, as we do, so refueling will take almost an hour." I find it immensely entertaining when people think that the details of why something is going wrong cancels out the fact that it is going wrong. Call me old-fashioned, but when I buy an airplane ticket that has an exact time on it, I expect the plane to be ready to be leave at that time; I guess that’s irrational. "Listen, ma’am, the fact that you happen to work for a bunch of unreliable assholes doesn’t make you exempt from the problems that this situation poses." And now the bomb. "I’m a professional wrestler and I have deadlines that don’t apply to mundane, ordinary person like you. Find me a goddamn flight." So now not only am I a rotten bastard, but I beat people up for a living and hey, I’m liable to go apeshit if I don’t get my way right now. She thinks I’m a ticking time bomb; that I might go ‘roid rage on everybody so load up the Quaalude gun just in case. And fuck her if she doesn’t believe that because it’s what I’ve been selling this whole time; if she can see through that acting job, she’s too smart to wear that dumb blue costume. She grits her teeth and I know it kills her but she buys it. She clicks away on the keyboard, saying nothing, but breathing loudly. At least she smells good, like strawberry and some other tropical fruit. But rarely should one trust a woman that smells very good; it’s like animals with warning coloration. The good smell/bright colors at first attract you, but then that survival instinct kicks in and says "hey, I shouldn’t eat that." * * * * Thursday, 12/04/80 It was my birthday, my mother told me. She said my father, the American, was coming to visit me. He was coming to see his son. He had been in the military; he had impregnated my mother over in her country. I wore a small blue shirt with a little red tie and black pants. My mother said that if I looked like a handsome American boy, father might stay with us. But he wasn’t coming until dinner time. I played on my bike in the dirt, waiting for a man I’d never seen before. I imagined that he would wear his navy whites. He would kiss my mother on her long bronze forehead and pick me up in his arms. He would tell us that he had come to live with us and he had brought us an American car. We wouldn’t live in the house that smelled like xứp rau anymore. That asshole didn’t show up. I saw him once, three years later, when he came to see my mother to tell her to stop sending him letters. I understood exactly what was going on. My father, the Italian-American hero soldier, said that I wasn’t his son. He said he only vaguely remembered my mother and that he was sorry for her, but that I couldn’t be his. He had an American son named Jack who was a year younger then me back at home. He patted me on my head before he walked out. * * * * Sunday 12/10/04 I wait behind the curtain. These people are ready for the main event. I want to take a win, grab my check, and go home. I’m sick of everything; sick of waiting for feds to call back, sick of turning on the TV and seeing former colleagues, brown-nosers all of them, making big bucks elsewhere. And I’m here, a locale which could only be described as a dive joint. I step out and a couple hundred of my closest friends greeted me with cheers. I’m the most famous person to walk into this little indy dump Having been blacklisted by every asshole from here to Piscataway after Pro Wrestling Circuit shut down, I’m in purgatory. Ron Starr can get knives shoved into every orifice on his body, as far as I’m concerned. For all intents and purposes, I’m down for the figurative, clichéd count. Twenty-nine year olds in the indies don’t get big breaks. The match is a blur. I stay simple and to combinations I’m familiar with: snapmare to leg drop to camel clutch. The guy is working stiff as a board so I throw in some ad-libs both to keep him on his feet and to show him who’s running the show. He smells like a sewage processing plant, too. I see my spot and take it; Runway Vault, Model Citizen. Onetwothree and Bob’s your uncle. So I start celebrating, playing to the pinheads in the crowd who don’t understand how fleeting fame really is. But I look back at the guy and he’s not getting up. I look to the ref, who’s clearly about to break character and give the eyes to him that say no, but he’s just looking back at me with this blank look. He looks like he’s about to faint, but he reluctantly holds on while the guy’s just sitting there. I don’t look back but I start walking towards the curtain. All of a sudden, the crowd goes real quiet, but I don’t turn around. "Holy shit, I think Greider’s seizing!" But I don’t turn around because in the end it doesn’t make a difference. If he dies, then I’m an asshole, but at least I can step back into the ring some day, instead of being the guy who broke kayfabe to go load his opponent onto a stretcher. I wouldn’t blame somebody who did, but I’m not a hero and I’m not a good person and I don’t have a heart that tells me the right and wrong thing to do. My heart tells me what’s good for me and what’s bad for me and not a whole lot else. Maybe if it hadn’t been ripped out my whole life I’d turn around but I don’t. And if he dies, I’m the one putting the gun to his head and pulling the trigger, but it’s funny because I’m the one who won’t pull the trigger. I won’t break that fourth wall for safety’s sake. Two EMT’s come rushing out because that’s all that this place can afford. With the ref, they load the kid onto the stretcher. I’m watching all of it from right behind the curtain. * * * * Monday 12/05/05 The pharmacy is a common destination for me now. Talk down at me all you like, but if I want to compete in that tournament next week, I’m in desperate need of some painkillers. Like the mega-fucking-awesome Gregory House said, "I don’t have a pain management problem. I have a pain problem." That just about sums it up; I could go au naturale, ignore the reoccurring pain, and take time off every time something just didn’t feel right. Or I could coast along painlessly and just count the days until my knees snap off. This life is fleeting. I’m in an unfamiliar city, but I’ve got my prescriptions in hand. I look at the guy behind the counter, his long white lab coat flowing behind him as he tromps around. His face is long and weathered, but not aged. He’s one of those young guys who just won’t realistically make it past sixty because of how much time they’ve wasted hating life. I tell myself everyday not to hate life, despite the awful people I’ve encountered; despite the awful person I can be. I move on because there’s too much life to be lived and not enough time to be spent on self-analysis. So I wait for my pills. The PRIME doctor has me on a weekly supply of Vicodin; it’s small and it doesn’t interfere with my life. I’m not on the emo treatment; that is to say, I’m not trying to dull all the terrible things in life. I’m just trying to dull my pain so I can do all the terrible things I need to. Scientologists can go fuck themselves; so can Christian Scientists. If you don’t want to use modern medicine, then I don’t give a shit if you die. The guy walks up to the counter and butchers my name. "Chaundler Sonday?" That’s typical bad guy schtick, but I’m now over the fact that Americans can’t say my name right. So the only remedy is to get so famous that they have no choice but to hear my name hundreds of times a day. That’s the whole reason I’m at the tournament: glory. "Yeah, that’s me, guy. Whaddya got for me?" "Your prescribed medicine. Obviously, read the directions and only take the prescribed dosage. In addition, don’t try to operate any-" "It’s alright," I mumble as I pick up the orange canister. "I know the gist." So now I’m off to slay giants, quell underdogs, or break Cinderella’s slipper. Every cliché in the book, that’s what I’ll do to win the tournament, to know that I’m the best and there’s nothing you, Joseph K. Somebody, can say about it. The old young guy gives me a slight look of disapproval, cocking his unkempt ferociously sprawling eyebrows at me. They’re little fuzzy caterpillars on drugs that passed out on his forehead. Actually, now I can hardly believe I didn’t see them before. But no rest for the wicked; I’ve got a match to win. * * * * Luther Ryxn, multiple personality sufferer. Chandler Tsonda, just generally out of touch with reality. Looks like the nuts are running the nuthouse at GTT5.
