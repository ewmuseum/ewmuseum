IG 29 - The Phantom Republican - FA

10 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Gordon Oliver Powell never really read comic books when he was younger. All his time was spent watching the WWF or other various cartoons on the TV or basically farting around with his chemistry set. He was a lad who didn't have time to read non-school related things. It wasn't until he left college and became a wrestler that he developed a taste for the graphic novels, and even then, it was only one character in particular... "Jesus Christ, you're how old and you still read those?" Richard Powell lifted up his son's can of seltzer water and placed a coaster underneath it as his son diligently reads the latest edition of his favorite hero's comic on the bed in the guest room of the house he shares with his life partner, Anton. "I don't understand you. I see you on the television, and you speak so eloquently in that ring. You throw around men twice your size and earn more money in one purse than both myself and Anton earn in six months. You're a grown man yet... blech..." Richard picks up a pair of Stars 'n Stripes boxer shorts off the floor of his guest room, holding them in the very tips of his enclosed thumb and forefinger. "Yet you read comic books, drink soda all day and live in your father's guest room." "Dad, one, it's not my fault that New Era is having financial troubles right now. If they were active, I'd be on the road and not here. And before you ask why here and not my own house, I told you, I'm always on the road, and I don't have my own house. Besides, Anton makes the best spinach and gouda mini-quiches." "Those were for brunch, asshole." "Such language towards your son." "Yeah yeah, a grown son who reads comic books..." "First off, I only read one comic book and that's Captain America, the greatest fictional American hero of all-time, and a close second to Ronald Reagan. I just got Volume Five, Issue 25, and I heard something really big happens in this one. Secondly, it could be worse. I could be a stinking liberal who doesn't do anything for a living and sponges off your trust fund you set up for me." "I never set a trust fund up for you." "And I appreciate that you chose to raise me as a working class hero until mom drove you away by becoming the neighborhood bicycle seat." "Hey, she may have slept with everyone on our block but me, but she's still your mother, and you owe her the respect of raising you right." "Fine." Gordon shook his head, picked at his mask (yes, he does wear it all the time :p) and went back to reading his comic book. Leafing through the pages, Gordon's eyes were glued to every page. The drama, the suspense... his face, well at least the parts you could see, beamed like a child reading his favorite series with a flashlight in his treehouse at night. That look, the innocence and joy, drained from his face as he turned the next page and saw what had unfolded. His eyes opened wide and mouth went agape. He dropped the book and gasped. "What? Gordon?" Richard rushed into the guest room to see what was wrong with his son. Gordon sat on the bed, motionless, like a statue even. His father approached him. "Gordon, what's wrong?" Gordon turned his head slowly to face his father. "They.... they killed... Captain America." -- To numb the pain, Gordon had Anton fix him a dry martini. Then he took a shower. Then a walk around the neighborhood. Then had Anton fix him another dry martini. And another. And another. Then he logged onto the Internet. Surfing the Web on his father's Sony Vaio laptop with a wireless connection, he came across several fan reactions, and most of them weren't what he expected. "Yeah, I'm bummed they killed Cap, but he was getting lame anyway." "I'm glad they killed Cap cuz he represented everything bad about America. He's the encapsulation of why the rest of us hate you." "Hey, I'm willing to wait and see what story they tell. Maybe we can learn a lesson here." Gordon sat at the computer, staring at these messages for hours on end. He said nary a word, nor did he even move. He just sat there stunned, not so much that they killed Captain America, but at the apathy towards the development and the antipathy towards the things Cap stood for. "Don't look so glum chum." Richard sat down next to his son and shut the laptop in hopes that Gordon would snap out of his daze. "Seriously man, it's only a comic book..." "He's the embodiment of everything good and American in life. They killed everything good and American in life." "You do know that this whole storyline was a pointed critique at your buddy Bush." "LIBERAL WRITERS CANNOT TAINT THE LEGACY OF CAPTAIN AMERICA!" Awkward silence. Gordon sulked and his father couldn't find the words to console him. Anton, however, could. "Gordon, shoogar, how old are jou?" "Uh, 27." "Jah, and jou're crying over ze superhero, no?" "But..." "Answer jes or no, comprende?" "....yes." "What is wrong wit jou? If ze loss of a symbol of zis great country is so important to jou, why don't jou do someting about it?" "Like... what?" "Ah Dios mio, do I have to do everyting for jou? Jou're in ze public eye, and jou already represent someting American. Why don't jou..." "You're right! You're absolutely right! I shall become the new symbol of all that's great in this country! Oh Anton, if I wouldn't catch the gayness, I'd kiss you..." "GORDON!" "Oh, right, no gay-bashing. Sorry dad." The next day, Gordon got up early. Eating a hearty, all-American breakfast of Wheaties with whole milk (skim milk is for Commies), bananas, wheat toast with butter and fresh Florida orange juice, Gordon made sure he was energized enough to take on the task of unifying a country. Richard emerged from the master bedroom and grabbed a yogurt from the fridge. "Mornin', dad." "Good morning, son. I see you're up early." "Yep! I have to go out and galvanize the country!" "About that... what exactly are you going to do?" "Well dad, fancy you should ask! Look over in the living room, on the coffee table." Richard turned his head to find a megaphone. "Megaphone?" "Yep! I'm going to go out and rally people to my cause, to get behind me." "Get behind you... okay, I'm lost. Do you actually have a point here?" "Sure! Oh, I must have forgot to tell you. After Anton gave me that wonderful speech last night, I logged onto the Internet and signed myself up for the next round of PrimeTimeCentral's Infinite Gauntlet." "You signed up for another company?" "No, no, this is sort of a blanket corporation, kind of like how Disney owns all those companies." "Including the chemical warfare labs in the third world?" "Have you been reading the LaRouche site again, Dad?" Richard glared at his son. "Anyway, it welcomes folks from all over to compete. And get this, the winner gets a chance to wrestle in the Unification match for the PTC Global and Extreme Championships." "And what does that have to do with your plans for today?" "Do I have to spell it all out for you? I'm going to position myself as 'America's wrestler,' and get the nation behind me as I fight the unsavory criminal elements, foreigners and whiny emo kids to become the undisputed Unified Champion in all the PTC... I'll be America's Champion! Today, I start that campaign grassroots style, by getting people's attentions through that megaphone." "I see..." Richard popped the top on his yogurt, grabbed a spoon out of the drawer and sat down next to his son. "Well son, I wish you luck." "Thanks, Dad." Gordon set up shop outside a pizzeria on Boca Raton Boulevard. He eschewed his normal business-casual dress for blue jeans and a t-shirt so he could connect with the common man. He got some stares from passers-by due to his mask, and they pierced his nerve momentarily. Overcoming those jitters, Gordon gulped, lifted the megaphone to his mouth and started speaking. "Hello America!" People stopped momentarily. "I am the Phantom Republican..." At those words, the passers-by groaned and kept walking. "...and I have come to be a unifier! As you may or may not know, Captain America has been killed! The great symbol of our will to fight was needlessly murdered by an intelligentsia that doesn't care if this country is unified or bickering all the time! However, it was a blessing in disguise, becuase now, it gives a chance for a new hero to rise up! A TANGIBLE hero to galvanize the country and unify it under one cause... to bring the PTC Unified Championship home to America!" One passer-by stopped with a ludicrous look on his face. "Hey, hey, hey, are you serious dude? You're trying to spew all this jingoistic crap for a fucking wrestling match?" "Hey, it's not just some wrestling match, alright? This is where all the best wrestlers in the world are competing to decide who reigns over them all..." Another walker came up. "Yeah, and why should you represent America? You only represent one party, less than half of this country! I'm a registered Green Party member, and I'll be damned if someone like you represents me, even if it is in some fake-ass sport like wrestling." "Hey! It's not fake, and..." Suddenly, a dark-skinned man with a thick black moustache came out of the pizzeria. "What's all the commotion in front of my shop?" Before Gordon could speak up, the second walker spoke. "This fascist is trying to push his views on us." "Hey! One, I'm not fascist, and two, I'm just a wrestler trying to galvanize the nation into support behind my quest to bring the richest Championship in the sport to America!" "Yeah, probably by discriminating against my folk," said the pizzeria owner angrily. "You know, I'm tired of your party and I'm tired of your business portraying my people in a bad light. I mean, you had some Italian guy parading as an Arab, and then when you put him up against the Undertaker, you buried him and fired him!" "That's not my company and I..." "Shut up! Now, you get your fascist ass from in front of my store, or I'll call the police on you for a noise violation!" "But..." "Now!" Gordon sighed and moved one shop down. He was about to put the megaphone to his mouth, when the pizzeria owner shouted at him again. "Get away from my store completely!" Gordon turned the corner and left that site, looking for a new one. A few blocks later, he found a nice park with no storefronts with owners to push him away. He set up shop and began to rouse the rabble again. This time, he got a few people to stay there and listen to him. The few turned into a dozen, and then a couple a dozen. Gordon felt like his grassroots approach was taking hold, the exuberance swelling up inside of him that almost made him float away. Just as he felt his best... WHHHIRRRRRRRR! The sirens and flashing lights broke the pensive attentiveness of the crowd and scattered some of them away. A good amount still remained as the Boca Raton police officer approached Gordon. "Officer Horan, Boca Raton Police. May I ask what you're doing out here?" "I... I'm just holding a grassroots rally! See, I'm a professional wrestler, The Phantom Republican, and I'm just..." "Do you have a permit?" "I thought you didn't need a permit..." "Answer the question, do you have a permit?" "...no." "If you don't have the proper noise permits, I can't allow you to carry on with this megaphone display. I got a complaint from the local pizza joint and a few other citizens about your ruckus." "But..." "No buts. Cease and desist, or I'll arrest you and throw you in jail. Is that understood?" "No! I'm going to stand here with these American people who are going to stand with me in defiance, am I right?" Gordon broke his stare with Officer Horan and looked around. No one was left. "Right?" he said sheepishly. Officer Horan was unflinching. He wasn't impressed. "All right, you win. I'll stop." "Good. Thank you for your cooperation." Officer Horan got back into his squad car and drove off. Gordon sulked towards his father's home, dejected and defeated. How could the people be so callous towards his cause? Didn't they like being American? He sighed as he opened the door to the house. His father, sitting on the living room sofa, stood to greet him. "Hey son. How did it go?" "Terrible... I got chased by a store owner and then was threatened by the cops because I didn't have a noise permit." "Oh..." Richard sat back down. "I'm sorry to hear that son. It's a shame, your campaign got derailed before it even began." Gordon shot his head up and stared at his father. "No... it didn't. I'm not done yet." "Huh?" "I just need to think of something different... for next time."
