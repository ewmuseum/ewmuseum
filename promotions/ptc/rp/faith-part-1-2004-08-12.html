Faith Part 1

12 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-12 It’d been a while since Cyrus had stepped in the LOW ring, but for due cause. He’d been due a short vacation so he decided he would take it. He hoped that the mess with Profusion and the Shadow Riders would be forgotten when he came back, only to realize that it was only just beginning. Cyrus would finally get a chance at revenge on the man who cost him his precious World Title. Luke Justice was a man that Cyrus did not like. Cyrus was not afraid to show this, he’d said in many conferences and interviews how he despised the Shadow Riders and everything they stood for, but Luke was the one who made his skin crawl the most. He saw Luke as just being the little child desperately trying to hold onto his parent’s success. What had Luke Justice ever achieved? Nothing. Luke Justice was a failure who used the Shadow Rider name to try and give himself credibility. Cyrus could see through Justice’s façade and it repulsed him when he discovered what was underneath. A mere coward hiding under somebody else’s shadow. Cyrus would show him how pathetic he was, Cyrus would humiliate him in front of thousands of people. Then he would learn… “Three things are necessary for the salvation of man: to know what he ought to believe; to know what he ought to desire; and to know what he ought to do.” Saint Thomas Aquinas The dark interior let almost no light in, only speckles filtered through the shield between him and the other side. He knew that somebody was sat on the other side because he could hear their breathing, though he could not see who it was that he was speaking to. It was his job not to disclose any information that was given to him and that the person’s identity was allowed to remain secret from him. But he was dying to know who was behind the other side of that wooden prison. “What would you like to confess?” the Priest’s voice was soft and gentle and it soothed the beast on the other side of the wall. Then there was a pause, neither man spoke. The silence seemed to fall heavily onto the Priest’s chest; as he was the first to break it. “Are you ok?” the man seemed to snap out of the trance that he was in and turned to face the barrier that blocked him from the Priest’s view. He opened his mouth and nothing came out. He took a few moments to gather his thoughts together and to think about what he was going to say. A tear trickled down his cheek as the memory arose to the top of his mind, bringing itself to the forefront of his consciousness. “I’m as good as I could possibly feel.” The man’s voice did not seem tainted in any way with what emotions he was now feeling. He took a deep breath to fill his lungs with precious oxygen and ended up spluttering due to the dust that was congregating in the chamber. It had obviously not been used for a long time; people didn’t need to cleanse themselves any more. Most people believed that they were pure, that they didn’t sin. Unfortunately this was almost a sin in itself; ignorance to moral laws that did not need to be written down in stone by religion. This man was certainly not Catholic, but he was in the church asking for his sins to be forgiven. It did not matter of what faith he was from, it mattered that he had the guts to admit that he had done wrong. “What is it you wish to confess?” once again the Priest eased the man into a state of calm. He’d done this a thousand times before and he dealt with people far more emotional than the man whom he was dealing with now. Though, it’d had been a long time since he’d sat in that old chamber. The cushion was covered with dust, neglected by age. He’d nearly lost his faith himself but somehow managed to convince himself that he was doing a worthwhile job. That the world had not lost religion and that religion had lost them. He had to try and lighten the dark skies that were falling upon the earth; he had to keep fighting for justice and peace. He knew it was all a puny effort to restore himself as a good person and that really he did everything for his own benefit. But now he was back in the hot seat he remembered why he started this. He remembered why he put that collar on for the first time. “To help people…” “I don’t think I’m ready yet, please forgive me.” His voice seemed weak as he apologized to the Priest. He did not feel ready to relive the awful memory he had retained inside his mind all this time. Others shared it too, but they were not responsible for it. They did not create it. They were not God to it. Slowly he arose from the dusty cushion he’d been sat on for the past few minutes. It was time to leave; though soon he would return, maybe sooner that he thought. He needed to do this. He opened the door of the small compartment and stepped out into the painful light. It trickled over his body, carefully engulfing him in a purity he did not deserve. He had to leave this place now; he could not stand to be here whilst he could not muster the courage to admit his sins. He’d taken a few steps when a hand was placed upon his shoulder. He turned round and went to hit whoever had dared touch him. His perfection would be effected if people touched him, he could not allow mere pathetic idiots to feel his power. The Priest was an exception; he was a man of morals and laws. He did not try to steal the strength that resided within the man’s body. “Please, come back soon; you were very close today and I feel like we’ve made progress.” Once again the Priest did not change his tone, he spoke so calmly and quietly nobody else in the church heard what he said. This felt good to Cyrus. A man he could trust to keep things safe. He could entrust all of his sins to him and be cleansed. This site would be the beginning of the resurrection of the fighting champion Cyrus once was. When Cyrus lived upon the streets he fought not for the love of fighting, but because it was the only way to survive. He did it because he needed to just to stay alive. He was a born with an incredible physique and gift for brawling which he toned into an incredible weapon. People feared him when he did not demand it; Cyrus demanded nothing from anybody back when he was young and naïve. If he earned respect he was grateful, but he did not need it. Not like now… Now Cyrus was fuelled by the desire to be looked upon in awe for the monster he was. He did not care whether people liked him; he just wanted them to fear him; to have their hairs on the back of their neck stand on end when they saw a picture of the sadistic man they loathed so much. He used to think that he was a freak and that they would just laugh and mock. He did not believe that a mistake like him would be able to command authority just by doing their job. But he was wrong; he had seen the error of his philosophy and had corrected it accordingly. The fact he was a monster worked in his favour, people would fear him more when he commanded them to. They would realize that they should not have laughed and pointed at him. They would realize why he was put on this Earth. His sole purpose was to be the best that he could be at fighting, wrestling if you will. The fact that he competed within rules and regulations worried him, though eventually he would leave that competition. He would break away from the structure lifestyle he led, once he reached perfection he would dive underground. Breakaway from the norm and enter illegal fighting circuits. Then he would fit his purpose, he would do what he enjoyed so much. Fighting without having to think of the consequences. When he was in the ring he had to hold back, once he reached his best he would not have to worry about that. He would be able to do whatever he wanted, until the day he died. ---------- “Three things are necessary for the salvation of man: to know what he ought to believe; to know what he ought to desire; and to know what he ought to do.” Saint Thomas Aquinas Cyrus new what he believed. He believed that he was the creation of a spiteful creator, though he did not hold it against him. Cyrus new what he ought to desire. Perfection. And he would stop at nothing to attain it. Cyrus knew what he ought to do. To follow the path he was walking was the only way. Any roadblocks in his way had to be dealt with. He couldn’t let anything stand in his way on his conquest for perfection. Cyrus did not care about salvation, though it would come to him when he reached the highest point he could reach. He didn’t really follow any religion other than his own, with his own beliefs in right and wrong. He saw the need for religion, to let people have hope and he did not want to take that away from them. He was not trying to teach other people what to believe, he just wanted to show the world who he was and what he was. The beast inside him was being nurtured and the next step was to cleanse it of all the sins it had helped to commit. He would get the Priest to forgive all the sins he would make, then Cyrus would have no regrets, nothing holding him back from what he was capable. No restrains. Cyrus would be free.
