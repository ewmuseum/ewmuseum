Just Add Water...and Sugar...

19 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-19 What happens to dreams when they die? Where do they go? Do they disappear forever, or are they reborn in the form of similar dreams? Alternatively, what do people do when something they love is taken from them? Do they seek out happiness and satisfaction in other ways? Do they become vengeful, spiteful, hateful? What happens, then? What happens when life throws you lemons? ------------------------- Frank’s Coffeehouse was bustling with life as usual. The poets were snapping, the students were studying, the soothing music was playing, and the coffee was brewing. Frank, of course, was making the rounds. Just another typical day in the life of Frank, and as of late – “The Latch Key Kid!” It was the same poet kid from last time, who again tried to hide his embarrassment by cracking a joke with his friends, to no avail. They still pointed and laughed at him. Frank spun around from across the room, where he was making small talk with a group of customers, to find the Latch Key Kid walking into the establishment. He excused himself and walked to the front of the shop, and shook Latch’s hand, at the same time pulling him towards the counter. Frank pulled out a barstool and motioned for Latch to sit. “It’s good to see you, Brad,” Frank started. “Likewise, Frank. Good to see everything’s still the same around here.” “Indeed it is. Now, would you like to tell me what you’re doing here?” “Why, Frank,” Latch replied, “it’s time for my shift to start. I was just going to pull on an apron and get to work. After having a fresh latte, of course.” “I’m afraid, Brad, that you’re out of luck. I can’t help you with that today.” “Oh, Frank. You’re not out of latte, are you?” “Heavens, no,” Frank said, as he looked around the room and quieted his voice. “Can you imagine having to tell a room full of caffeine freaks that there’s no latte left? Heck no, I’d never let this place run out of that.” “Well then, what were you apologizing for?” Latch asked as he scratched his head. “It’s gone on long enough, Brad. You’ve had your fun, you’ve helped me out around here, and you’ve made some friends. But I think you know as well as I do that it’s time for you to move on.” “You’re not suggesting that I seek employment elsewhere, are you?” “Brad,” Frank chuckled, “you never were employed here. You’ve been volunteering. And while I appreciate that, I’ve kept you on just about as long as I can. Don’t take this personally, please, but we’d both be better off without you working here.” Latch’s facial expression turned sullen. “How can you say that? How do you know what I’ll be better off doing?” “Because,” Frank said with a fatherly tone, “I know that you have a lot to worry about now. You’re still involved in the GTT4, Brad. You’re in the semi-finals of your bracket. It’s time to get serious about the tournament and not worry so much about the coffeehouse. It’ll still be here when the tournament is over. But for now, you should just focus on taking out your next opponent. You do want to win your next match, don’t you?” “Of course I do, Frank,” Latch perked up. “I’m facing Nick Persia, you know. He’s in UWF, and you know how much we XUW guys dislike those UWF fellas.” “Actually, Brad, I don’t know.” “Oh. Well, yeah. We don’t like them much. So I want to beat Nick Persia for the good of XUW almost as much as for my own good.” “Well that’s a pretty unselfish thing of you to say. It’s refreshing.” “Oh, come on, Frank,” Latch said with a hint of displeasure, “would you expect anything less from me? I mean, I come in here, bust my butt for a week or so trying to help run this coffeehouse, all while trying to prepare for a tournament, and now you’re surprised to hear me say something unselfish? What have I been doing, Frank?” “That’s just it, Brad. We don’t need you to help run this place. It practically runs itself. There’s barely enough for me and the others to do around here. We appreciate your help, we really do. But for now, I think it’s best you find something else to do with your time. And I think you know what that is.” Latch looked down at the floor for a moment, and then popped up with a smile on his face. “I sure do! I’m going to go apply at Moonbeam’s down the street. Thanks, Frank!” Latch hopped up off the stool. Frank reached out and grabbed his shoulder, and spun him around. “That’s not really what I meant, Brad. I was talking about getting ready for the tournament and your opponent. I want to see you do well, Brad, just like always. Nothing’s changed between us. Just do the best I know you can do in this tournament and you’ll be fine.” Latch smiled and shook Frank’s hand. “Will do, buddy.” And with that, Latch walked out of Frank’s. Frank watched the door close behind Latch, and slowly shook his head. He turned around with a glum look on his face, and surveyed the room with his eyes. “Well,” he muttered, “I’d say that went well.” ------------------------- Latch sat by himself at the table, reading the menu for the umpteenth time, while twirling the straw in his glass of iced tea. A shadow suddenly appeared over his table, and he spoke without looking up. “Yes?” “I knew I’d find you here,” Katherine replied. Latch looked up and saw her standing there, looking lovely as always, and a smile fought to creep across his face. It was mildly successful. “May I?” she asked, and Latch gestured toward the chair across the table from him. Katherine sat down and met his eyes with hers. “So what brings you here, Katherine?” “I told you I figured you’d be here. I came looking for you,” she said with a hint of concern. “I stopped by Frank’s, and he told me what happened, and I knew it must’ve hurt you, no matter how much Frank tried to smooth things out. And I know that when you feel hurt, you come here.” Indeed, Latch had gone to the sidewalk café that he and Katherine have always favored. “It’s peaceful here, you know? They don’t bother me, they don’t pester me about ordering food, they just let me sit and think.” Katherine looked around the outdoor café. “Brad, the place is practically deserted, that’s why.” “Not true. They’re good people here. Anyway, I don’t really care what the reason is. They leave me alone, which is good. And do we really have to talk about Frank right now?” “Yes,” she said, as she reached across the table and put her hands on top of his. “He told me that you threatened to go to Moonbeam’s and apply for a job there. So, did you?” “Did I what?” he asked. “Did you get an application? Did you carry through with your threat?” “No,” Latch replied, “the only thing I walked out of there with was a sub par cup of coffee. Why can’t the rest of them make it like Frank? I guess that sign Frank has hanging in his place is right. Corporate coffee does suck. If I can’t drink their coffee, there’s no way I’d ever be able to work there. That idea’s out the window. But don’t tell Frank, okay?” “I won’t,” Katherine promised. “But you have to do something for me, okay?” “What’s that?” “Promise me that you won’t let this get you down. You need to turn yourself around, Brad. You have too much going on right now to be able to allow yourself to mope about not being allowed to work in Frank’s anymore. Besides, you’ve got Nick Persia to worry about. Which is more important to you right now? Advancing in the GTT4 or trying to get your fake job back?” “Right now, as in right now?” he asked with a longing look in his eyes. “Yes, right now. And don’t even think about saying that job. You’re capable of much more than that, Brad. You’re capable of advancing to the next round of the tournament, and after that, who knows. Don’t sell yourself short, Brad. Nobody else does, we all believe in you. Just feed off of that. Take a moment to realize what you’re accomplished so far.” “Well, let’s see. I spent a week at Frank’s, helping out, cleaning up, making coffee and new friends, and -” “That’s not what I’m talking about,” Katherine groaned. “There you go again. I was talking about this tournament. Sure, you lost your first match, but somebody realized what you were capable of and took a chance on you. You made good on that opportunity, and won your next match. And the one after that. Now you’re in the fourth round. One more win, and you’re in the finals of your bracket. Think about that, Brad. You weren’t even supposed to be in the tournament after the first round. But thanks to the strength that somebody saw in you, you are. And you’ve held your own. Your good enough to pull this off, you just need to stop thinking about the coffeehouse long enough to realize that.” “You really think so?” he asked. “Of course I do. And you better think so, too. Or else, why even bother? If you don’t think you’re good enough for this, then stop. Turn away, forget about the second chance you were given, and walk away now. There’s no sense in putting yourself through this if you’re not confident you can pull it off.” “You’re right, of course,” Latch smiled. Katherine saw the sincerity in it and she smiled as well. “You’re always there when I need you, you know that? It’s almost as if you’re a guardian angel. You always know how to bring out the best in me.” Katherine laughed. “A guardian angel, huh? Then who’s God, Frank?” Latch smiled. “Now there’s an interesting, and frightening, thought. Though he is full of wisdom and advice. You never know, I guess. I never pictured God running a coffeehouse.” They shared a laugh. “So, what do you know about Nick Persia, anyway?” Katherine asked. “Well, he’s in UWF, and you know how those of us in XUW dislike those in UWF. And, well -” “Actually,” she interrupted, “I do know. I’m sure that’s probably giving you some motivation in this match. It’s nice to see you put the pride of XUW up next to your pride.” “You really did talk to Frank, didn’t you? But yeah, I want to get him if for no other reason than he’s in XUW. Other than that, I don’t see much wrong with the guy. He seems like a pretty good guy, who has good intentions at heart.” “You’re right, he does sound like you. Only -” “Only what?” “He isn’t you. And you’ve got that going for you.” “What do you mean?” Latched asked with a puzzled look on his face. “Well, would you like to face, well, you?” Katherine smiled. “Good point. You know,” Latch said, “all this talk about getting past the whole thing with Frank has given me a new idea. And I think it can help me get a better grip on what I can really do if I set my mind to it.” “Uh-oh. What are you thinking about this time?” she asked cautiously. “Well,” Latch grinned, “what do you do when life throws you lemons?” “Uhm, I’m not sure what your answer to this is. But most people would say, make lemonade.” “Exactly. There you go.” Latch nodded in agreement. “I’m not sure I understand.” “Oh, but you will,” Latch said, as he squeezed Katherine’s hand. “Are you sure? Lemonade?” “Lemonade,” he nodded again. “Come on, let’s go.” Latch pulled out his wallet and dropped some money on the table to cover the iced tea and a tip, and then he and Katherine walked out of their favorite sidewalk café.
