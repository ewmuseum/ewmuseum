Date: 2009-12-02 Puppy Love November 25, 2009 New Mexico Adelina DeAngelis is sitting across from Jasmine Munoz, sifting through her salad looking for another olive to stuff into her mouth. She has been at the facility for over a month now, and though this is not a lifestyle she can see herself living for the rest of her life - she knows that there is nowhere she would rather be right now. Josh has overseen her training for the last few months, helping her whenever she felt she was struggling. She isn't sure if she had become attracted to him because of the way he held her close to his body whenever he helped her learn a particular counter in her close quarters combat training, or the fact that he just looked like he should be on a billboard advertising clothes for Abercrombie and Fitch. She was sure it didn't really matter, because the attraction was there regardless of the reason. “You thinking about him again?” Adelina lifts her gaze away from the soggy croutons and smiles at Jasmine. “Depends on which him you're talking about.” “You know who I'm talking about.” She turns her head toward the table Joshua Salmons is sitting at, then shakes her head. “I don't know what you're talking about.” Truth is, apart from thinking about making Antonio Gambillini feel worse than she did when she lost her father, she could not stop thinking about being next to him. “With all the beautiful girls that come through here, I doubt he even notices me.” “I've seen him sneak looks at you, girl.” Adelina ran her hand across the back of her hair to smooth it out - a nervous tick she never seems to notice - adjusting the tortoise shell clip she bought at a Wal Mart in Clovis. They usually shop as far from the facility as possible, it helps that the store is open twenty four hours. “No he doesn't.” “You should go over there and talk to him.” “I'm not going to make a fool of myself,” she steals another glance in his direction before stabbing her fork through a cherry tomato. Finally Alone November 25, 2009 New York It had been seven months since I had informed Salvatore Fennucci that I would not be taking control of the DeAngelis family empire, trusting that task to him instead. I had made the decision to distance myself from the criminal activities that the position would demand, and give my full attention to my new wife Cynthia and a child that had yet to be born. Seven months later, I find myself in the exact same situation I vowed to never return to. Unfortunately, there are circumstances that pretty much demand I step back into a life I was bred to lead. When I was young, I had been sent to learn things that most children would do with plastic replicas. There were very few nine year olds that knew how to handle a throwaway special – what we called a .38 caliber revolver in the neighborhood I grew up in – much less carry one on a daily basis. The gun was mainly for protection, you never knew when someone was going to get brave and try to take advantage of one of us kids; the most popular choice for a runner in the numbers game. I didn’t run numbers for too long, because someone had bigger plans for me. Don DeAngelis knew that he wanted his family out of the business on his son Michael’s fifth birthday; that was the day he wished he would grow up and be just like his father. The Don wanted his son to grow up and have an honest career, something that did not consist of glancing over your shoulder to make sure the person behind you was not holding a knife. No, he needed someone that did not have anything to begin with, and guess who fit that profile to a tee. My father – one of DeAngelis’ main Soldados - had been shot in the back of the head on his way home one evening, so I already spent most of my time at DeAngelis’ house anyway. It was that, or living on the streets or in a foster home. If I knew the way things were going to turn out back then, I would have given foster care a longer look. The Don introduced me to a lifestyle that I thought I wanted to live. The respect, power, and fear that the Don was given was easy enough to get drunk on, and I swallowed it by the barrel full. I wanted to live the life of a Mafioso; it wasn’t as if I had anything else to live for after I lost my father anyway. Don DeAngelis preyed on this weakness and turned it into a strength that followed me all of my life. Well, until this past year when I realized that I could actually care for someone the way I cared for my father when I was a kid. Something about having someone to love me the way my father did, opened up the heart I refused to open up for so many years. When Cynthia told me I was going to be a father soon… Well, I knew that the life I always dreamed of living would turn out to be nothing more than a nightmare, because the life I had would be a lot simpler. I realized too late that Michael was the perfect fit for the position. I also knew DeAngelis would never allow me to live happily ever after. He resented me for the decision his father made, as if I had stolen the one thing he ever wanted. While I was being groomed to take the reigns of the family, Michael had been spoiled and pampered in the best schools money could buy. He despised the life his father wanted for him, demanding his father’s highly skilled guns for hire teach him the way of the Mafioso. It was in his blood. And that is what it always comes down to, isn’t it… Even now, as I stand here and stare out the window at the city that never sleeps, I find it hard to believe that I can ever truly walk away from this. Especially not now, with my goddaughter in the hands of a group of people that have proven to be far worse than anything I could ever dream of doing. I didn’t know much about the Pierotti Foundation, just that they had a way of convincing people to do what they wanted. After finding out from Cynthia’s father – Daniel Berkman – that they were the ones that abducted Adelina, I rushed over for a meeting with Salvatore; since he was the one that dealt with them. He told me then that they were also the people responsible for Michael’s disappearance, something I would have known if I had not asked him to leave me out of the loop on that side of the family’s business. “Are you okay?” I didn’t turn around, it was hard enough to keep this from her without looking into those eyes that seem to be able to read me like a children’s book. I just shake my head and blame it on what I always do – my job. “Just have a lot on my plate right now.” “Why don’t you just tell Lisa that you need some time off?” I want to say because Lisa Tyler is a vindictive bitch, but I just chuckle under my breath before I say anything. “I took one Revolution off and she damn near had a cow. Imagine how she’d be if I told her I needed to take off King of Kings.” “You’ve been fighting non stop since you went back, what’s a few shows to spend time with the baby going to hurt?” “Well Snow had been out on personal leave, so I’m really the only champion she has that she can depend on to carry the show with Sloan still in the Jewel in the Crown tournament.” “You kind of sounded like him right there.” I finally turn to face her, a bit confused by what she said. “Sounded like who?” “Well, Gamble,” she laughs as if it should have been obvious. “You sounded full of yourself, as if there was no one else that could carry the show if you weren’t there.” “I didn’t mean for it –” “I’m kidding,” she walks over and wraps her arms around my waist before lowering her head onto my left shoulder. I can smell the cucumber melon shampoo she’s been using lately; it was a scent I was starting to relate with being home. I thought it was foolish at first, but it actually makes me feel safe and secure knowing that I am home and my wife is next to me. “I may be the only one who will ever see the difference, but I know it is there.” “No,” I run my fingers through her hair as I wrap my right arm around her waist. Her body feels warm pressed up against mine, a feeling I doubt I’ll ever tire of. “Arriana will see it too.” “That’s why I love you so much.” She turns her head and kisses me gently on the neck, so I let my hand drift down below her waist and cup one of her cheeks to pull her harder against me. The moan that escapes her mouth sends a warm breath across my neck, something she knows from experience will arouse me; it’s one of my spots. She pivots her hips to grind them against mine, and I waste no time grabbing her thighs and lifting her up – carrying her to the couch since Arriana’s crib is in the bedroom. I catch a glimpse of myself in the mirror above the couch as I lay her on it, and as hard as I try not to think about it… I can’t help but wonder how long I can keep flipping the switch between the man I am, and the man I pretend to be. Do I even remember which one is the real me? Her First November 27, 2009 New York Daniel Berkman unclipped the leash from his golden retriever’s collar, then stood there and watched as the grumpy old dog hurried off into piles of leaves that the wind had gathered against the tree trunks. He lifted his head and let the warmth of the sun spread across his face for a moment, then made his way down the path Ginger – his dog – had taken. The park was empty for the most part, though given the day he figured as much. His wife left the house around three in the morning, and she felt she was running late. That was par for the course on black Friday though, and he made it a priority to stay as far away from department stores as he could. He saw Ginger pawing away at a small pile of leaves and figured he found a small rodent to toy with, so he took a seat on a bench close by. He closed his eyes, then just sat there and listened to the wind whistle past his ears. The sounds of nature comforted him like a bottle of wine would an alcoholic. It was the whimper from Ginger that woke him from his reverie. He stood up and scanned the area, but she was nowhere to be found. He headed off in the direction he had seen her last, but there was no sign of the dog. He heard the dog whimper again, this time he noticed that it appeared the whimper came from behind the men’s restroom. He rounded the corner and saw a few drops of blood that left a trail into the men’s room, his heart suddenly felt as if it was going to rip through his chest. He stepped in and saw that the trail continued toward the last stall. He pushed open the other stall doors as he passed them, certain that this was some sort of ambush. One by one, they slammed against the wall as he shoved them, but they all turned out empty. The only one left was where the trail ended. He felt as if his heart had lodged itself in his throat when he placed his palm against the door. The sudden pinch in his neck took him completely by surprise. He hadn’t even heard anyone walk up behind him, but as his knees went limp and he stumbled into the door, he noticed the lithe shadow of a figure behind him. Ginger’s lifeless body broke his fall, and the warmth of the blood on his face reminded him of how the sun felt on it a few moments ago. “Hello, Daniel.” He couldn’t place the voice, but the face seemed familiar for some odd reason. He couldn’t place it, but he swore he had seen her somewhere before. “I don’t know how long you’re going to be conscious – considering I injected enough Vecuronium to drop an elephant into your blood stream – but I’m hoping you’re awake long enough to know that I’m going to go after your daughter next.” It was when she knelt down in front of him – and he could look into her eyes – that he realized who she was. “It’s a shame you conceded your election to mayor,” she giggled. “We really could have used someone like you to try and curb the criminal activity in the city’s parks.” At least he knew who she used to be, because there was no way this was Adelina DeAngelis. “Oh well, too little too late. I’ll tell Cynthia you said hello.” He never heard the word hello.
