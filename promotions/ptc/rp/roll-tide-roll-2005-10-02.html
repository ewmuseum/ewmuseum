Roll Tide Roll

2 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 The sea: as fierce as she is beautiful. Many men have been taken down to her depths never to be rescued. Piercing blue with the relentlessness of a lion. As deceptive an entity as there ever will be is she. The spray from the water impacting the rocks splashed onto his face. He could taste the salt atop his lips as he stood- hands in his pockets- looking out across the vast body of water that is the Atlantic Ocean. The sun was out, shining in all its glory- not a cloud in the sky. His hair blew around a little as the spray continued to plaster itself onto his skin. Alex Ross stood along the Alabama coast… free from that God awful hotel room in St. Louis. He was back where he belonged. Behind him, his black Jeep Grand Cherokee sat neatly placed in its parking space. Alex had made the journey down from Tuscaloosa at the request of a friend. His house had been hit hard by the hurricane, it was salvageable, but some of Alex’s things that had been stored in his garage were taking up space. The drive hadn’t been too bad; traffic was relatively clear. He hadn’t hesitated to make the journey south as it was what he figured to be one of the final steps in getting his life back on track after both of the natural disasters held the Gulf Coast at bay. He looked out across the vast ocean surface to where a boat zipped across the surface of the water, gliding ever so smoothly, cutting the canvas like a warm knife through butter. The faint roar of the engine in the distance tailed off as the boat crept further away from the watchful eyes of the American Poster Child. The spray let up for a moment as Alex’s attention reverted down. He turned slowly began his walk back to his car, he wasn’t in any type of hurry to make it to his destination- this was obvious. He climbed the wooden fence that surrounded this part of the Alabama coast and walked toward his car. To be truthful, he was a little disgruntled he had to make the journey today. Alabama was playing Florida this afternoon, and, although he was a former Longhorn, nothing tickled his fancy more than a good game between two top 25 teams. Alex sighed as he closed the door of the SUV. The ignition turned over and engine fired up. Alex glanced down at the radio, making sure he had it tuned to the station where the game was being broadcast. After about fifteen seconds of commercials Alex got word that Alabama was indeed winning. “Awesome,” he commented under his breath. “Top ten here we come.” He put the car into reverse and backed out of the stall and onto the road. Heading west and making a right turn he took one last look at the ocean before his car rolled out of sight. *** “Hey Craig,” Alex said, stepping out of his Jeep Grand Cherokee having arrived at his destination. “Hey,” the well-built figure said stepping out of the garage, where many boxes were piled up, some having faired better than others in the flooding. Alex smiled walking toward his friend. “What exactly’s here anyway?” He said chuckling a little, “I really don’t remember all the stuff I left behind.” Ross’ former Longhorn teammate smiled along with him before responding. “Some football stuff actually, believe it or not.” “Oh,” Alex responded, half-surprised. “Some clothes and what not… I figured you’d probably want to take it back.” Alex nodded in agreement… what Craig was really implying of course was that he wanted it out of his garage. “You got the game on?” Alex said, beaming. Craig laughed a little. “You know it.” Alex walked deeper into the garage… he saw about eight or nine boxes with ‘Alex’ marked on them in Sharpie marker. The writing was a little faded, and a few of the boxes were rather tattered. “Oh well,” Alex said under his breath. After all, it could be far worse. “How’s the house fairing?” He asked of Craig, genuinely concerned. “The damage wasn’t too extensive… the roof, a little bit of the carpet. Nothing a little money can’t replace.” “This area actually held up surprisingly well…” Craig nodded in agreement, “I know, we’re damn lucky.” Alex grabbed two of the boxes and began to walk them back to the SUV. Craig helped out, grabbing two more. “You know… I’m not that far away if you ever need some company,” Alex said, reassuringly. They dumped the boxes in the back of the Jeep as Alex paused. “Thanks man…” Craig began. “Don’t mention it, it’s the least I can do.” “No, really… thank you. That means a lot to me.” Alex nodded as he reentered the garage. “You want’a pop inside and watch the rest of the game? I know we’re from Texas but how can you not cheer for ‘Bama when you live here, right?” The two shared a laugh as Alex nodded. “Yeah… let’s do that.” *** He shut his car door and backed out of the driveway slowly. Craig stood at his front door, peering out into the Alabama night. Alex put the car into gear and sped off. It wouldn’t be a couple hours before he was back home again, but Alex Ross could feel like his life was once again back on track and things were finally… sane again. No more hurricanes to contend with… just life as usual and all of the problems it presented. After all, personal issues were hard enough without having to deal with natural disasters. Alex Ross’ life was finally back on track, and he couldn’t have been happier about it. And to make a good day even better: Alabama won. Roll Tide Roll. *** It seems that in our darkest hours we find the brightest light. Sometimes, blessings are in disguise, and the worst things imaginable can actually bring us closer together with those we love and those we care for. It’s a scary thing to think that through our greatest tragedies our relationships can be solidified. Natural disasters will forever haunt our planet and the people that inhabit it, as will the problems of every day. It’s how we respond to those problems and how we work around them that make us who we are. Although we can never replace the loss of life, or for that matter, ever prevent it, the way we battle the tragedies such as these that present themselves is what makes us special. We may never fully recover from the tide of crimson that rolled through the Gulf Coast but one this is for sure: nothing can ever take away what we have accomplished as a society in regards to cohesiveness and compassion for human struggle. Through this tide of hope we now know that we can overcome any disaster or situation that stands in our path. Roll Tide Roll.
