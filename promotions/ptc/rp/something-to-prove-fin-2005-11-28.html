Something to Prove: Fin

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-28 The commercials fade away, back to a familiar set with a trio of video monitors in the background. Each screen is divided in half, the upper half shows James McFarlane’s hand raised in victory…while the bottom half shows his daughter, Dawn, lying on the mat, or leaning against the apron, or pushing herself up on wobbly hands and knees…clearly signs of defeat. The dark silhouette again fades into the now familiar form of Marcus Geoavanti "And welcome back to our third and final installment of ‘Something to Prove.’ For those of you just joining us, we’ve covered small pieces of the family and professional life of the localized phenomenon known as James McFarlane. We’ve taken a brief look at his triumphs and failures both inside and out of the ring. Of course one can’t think about James without thinking of the tremendous example he’s left behind; the sheer height he’s set the bar to makes it difficult for anyone in the world of pro wrestling to actually follow in the footsteps of such a champion…especially his children." The screens in the background change to three different pictures of Dawn McFarlane. The first shows her as a young girl, asleep on the couch next to her sleeping father. The Second shows her as older, dressed in pink spandex wrestling gear, and giving a thumbs up from beside the wheel chair of the (now weakened from cancer) James McFarlane. The last picture shows Dawn standing alone, her hands jammed into the pockets of her now trademark duster, strawberry blonde hair hidden underneath a raven black dye job with dark blue streaks. "Though the death of James McFarlane was felt literally around the world in the various federations he competed in, there is no place on earth it was felt more strongly then in the heart of his eldest daughter, Dawn." "The oldest McFarlane sought to emulate her father’s example, breaking into a sport that is traditionally dominated by men, and making a splash even at a young age. Under her father’s tutelage Dawn achieved both singles and tag team fame in many smaller ‘Indy" federations, cutting her teeth at a tender age, and acquiring a taste for victory." The screens change to show images of Dawn’s progression through the years, from valet to curtain jerker, low carder to mid carder, high mid carder to PPV participant…and then to a question mark where the picture of a world championship would be. "Yet despite an obvious talent for the sport, and years of strong teaching from her father and his cohorts, Dawn McFarlane has consistently failed to achieve the one thing that had become almost synonymous with her father’s name…a major championship title. Dawn languished in smaller indy feds after the death of her father, finding no real success until she was invited to join (then PTC member) WMW…where she would also find no success. After just over a year Dawn quit the largely sexist WMW and pursued intensive training overseas, seeking tutelage from the famous "Japanese Typhoon" Toshiro Mifune, and nearly legendary Luchador, Luis Sehra." The screens in the back change to show Dawn in the middle, flanked on either side by smiling images of the two men mentioned previous. "But even with that extra edge, Dawn has found that ‘real’ success has continued to elude her. Though crowned the very first Mistress of the Mat, Dawn was unable to successfully defend either of her first two titles during her first defense, and was also unable to capture the World Title her Masters of the Mat victory allowed her a shot at…" "But how much of success or failure is determined in your own mind? Certainly Dawn has never achieved a world title, but she has led a career that many find exemplary, capturing several other titles and earning the respect and notice of those around her." "At what point does following in the footsteps of another lead you astray? Maybe sometimes, no matter how great they were, it is best to leave the path set our by those who came before you…and strike out in your own direction. Shinsei once said ‘For the Coward there is no life, and for the Hero, there is no death." "Perhaps only the courage to find your own path will lead you to the same greatness of those who came before? Only time will tell. For ‘Something to Prove’, I’m Marcus Gioavanti…have a pleasant evening."
