Breaking The Cycle Breaking The Cycle. Written: 2nd December, 2004. "Sick, and tired, and sleepless, With no-one else to shine for. Sick of all my distress, But I won't show I'm still poor..." - The Cardigans. I - All I Ever Wanted, All I Ever Needed... Is Here. Five minutes. It’s all she ever asked for. From her friends, her family, and those who stayed true to her in arenas all across the country. And they’d give just that to her. The only thing they asked for in return, is for her to stay true to them. Yet, five minutes was five minutes too long to others. Five minutes gave some the time they needed to prove the five foot four beauty that was Tessa Windsor, was nothing but a product of pure hype. All you had to do is look at her career, and it would be proven. Fed wise, she was unstoppable. Just when you thought she was down, she’d find her way back to the top. Without any ties, she was useless. Josh Starwind, William Curr, and John Adams. They were lucky. All of them, who would have never been given a chance before, suddenly were given that chance to succeed… all because of one thing. They beat her. They provided all the proof that anyone who’d ever doubted her needed. Maybe she really was overrated. Maybe she really was on top of something else besides the company she was contracted to. Even those close to her had begun to wonder how all of this could be so. However, they could wonder all they wanted. It wouldn’t be long before the daring diva would go out there and prove them otherwise. It was exactly what she was planning on doing, as her eyes danced back and forth between the fine print that sat before her eyes on the kitchen table. “Blah….blah….blah.” Caring less about the fine details, she continued dragging her index finger down the page, in search of only the important part of the document, her signature. After two years in this business, she knew what to expect. Page upon page of legal documentation and fine print wouldn’t change any of that, or her mind, for that matter. She needed this. But what she also needed was a willing participant, to stand by her side and help her out in her time of need. Unfortunately, at the moment, there was only one person who fit that description. And it wasn’t the bleach blonde egomaniac that often clung to her side the minute help was needed. Instead, it was someone she knew could be trusted… especially when it came to pulling things off in the ring. Karina Wolfenden. She was the perfect person for Tessa to tag with. Not just because of their friendship, but because of their abilities when together in the ring. Anyone who tuned in to OSW or RPW for even a second when the two were in the ring together could attest to that. Reaching over with her right hand to the pen that rested beside her propped up elbow, she exhaled with a sigh, as a tinge of guilt overcame her, causing her to pause with her hand just inches away from the pen. “I really shouldn’t be doing this…” Pulling her hand away from the pen as if it were alive and ready to bite her for being so selfish , she instead decided to use her hand to help hold up her heavy head, which was being weighed down further by this one decision. It just didn’t seem worth it. “Mom, what’s wrong?” Until then. Then, instantly every little bit of guilt disappeared. She was sure Karina would understand how much this meant to her. And if she didn’t, all she had to do is look into pair of two pairs of eyes that were locked on Tessa constantly, especially now. To them, she meant everything. They’d do anything for her. Sadly, Tessa had nothing to give back to them. Until now. “Nothing sweetie, I’m just filling out some paperwork.” As quickly as her head hit the palm of her hand, it had shot back towards the pen, only this time she actually grabbed it. But the guilt failed to cease. It only increased, as she looked over at her youngest child who stood beside the table, looking down at the documents with saddened eyes.. He knew exactly what this meant. “Does this mean you‘re going to be gone even more?” he questioned, resting his head on his mother’s shoulder hoping he could sucker her out of it. Stroking his light brown hair, she tried to do what any mother would do, and calm her child’s fear. But it was impossible considering this fear wasn’t just one built up in the mind, it was reality. “Only for a bit.” She took a moment to look away from his pleading blue eyes, to look back at the paper. How could this really be worth it? More time away from her kids. Dragging someone into something without their permission. She would have never done it before. But her kids needed a real person to look up to. Someone who actually proved the odds were worth defying. And she hoped to be that person. “I thought you said that last time,” he protested back, without yelling. Rather with something a bit more effective, a soft hurt. The sunlight that shone through the kitchen windows that afternoon, only added to the affect by glittering in his watery eyes, which had yet to form a tear. By now, he was practically desensitized to announcements of this sort. “You said that you would try and find a job that would let you stay home more.” “I know…” His words stung worse than any wound, even with salt applied. Being only twenty three made the situation even worse. She’d never had any parenting classes. She was barely an adult. Yet somehow, someone above felt she was capable of raising these two kids on her own. Even without a blood relation. Regardless, her and the kids shared a bond of love that couldn’t be broken regardless. No how tumultuous things got to be with the people that walked in and out of their lives, they always seem to understand each other. Which is why, despite the hurt he too was feeling in his heart at the moment, little Evan put aside the pain, and cheered Tessa on with a loving embrace. “Don’t worry mom, I know you ‘re only trying to help.” So many times she had told him that, and he always listened, even if things never seemed to change in the end. It was no wonder she felt she had to return them the favour by giving them someone they could look up to, after all the crap they had been through, they deserved at least that. There was Christa, who in reality was her niece, but became hers after her father, Tessa’s brother, had passed away in a tragic car accident. She was the smaller version of Tessa, practically a carbon copy. She had the same slender figure, flowing brown hair, and sparkling blue eyes that could light up a room. The only thing missing was the smile. But it must have been impossible for her to smile when she barely got to see the one close person she had left. And then there was Evan, who she had supposedly rescued by taking into her family after a vicious crime committed by his father, that left his mother dead. He was supposed to be adopted into the perfect family for him. An older sister to pave the way for him, and two parents that were financially stable to rotate time with the kids. It wasn’t really Tessa’s fault that the supposed perfect family managed to fall apart within a matter of months, because of a selfish man that only wanted what was best for him. But still she felt responsible With only her around the help raise the two how much good was she really doing him by barely being home herself? Even foster care seemed like a better alternative at times, when Tessa thought about it. But still she persisted on. She knew she could make everything right with time. That’s all it would take. It might be a little more than five minutes this time, but it would be time well spent if it made everyone happy. “I’m so sorry, but I need to this Ev, or we won‘t have a home,” she said aloud, thinking the same in the mind as the tears finally forced their way out of her baby blues. “I promise you once this is over, I’ll try and make some more time.” Placing a reassuring kiss on his cheek as he too began to cry, Tessa hesitantly pulled away from the embrace. She didn’t want Evan to feel like she didn’t care, but she needed to get this over with before guilt took over. “I can do this.” She repeated her own encouragement inside her mind, as she used up all her will power just to drag the pen over the signature line, and finish the job. There! “Is that it?” questioned the six year, as Tessa tossed the pen to the side with a sigh of relief, before looking down at him with a smile filled with admiration. “I don’t know, what do you think?” Poking the child playfully in the chest, she managed to get a smile out of him, knowing that for at least now, they could live normal life. “I think you need to fax that before you change your mind,” he teased, which managed to get a giggle out of his mother. “Good idea.” II - As The World Goes Around. When life is lived at Karina Wolfenden's pace, sometimes she just needed to sit back and watch the world catch up. Sat on an unremarkable patch of grass, her discarded sweater acting as a barrier between herself and the morning dew, Karina sat cross-legged, facing the sun. Without even the merest hint of a breeze to waft an icy chill over her lightly freckled skin, the rapidly ascending sun bathed her in it's restrained warmth. With her hair tied back into a tight ponytail, there wasn't even a fringe to filter out the rays. Most of the other people who were up and about all seemed to have somewhere to go, walking on the brick paths around the grass, suited and booted ready for a day at the office. Without the discomfort of formal shoes or the itchiness of a dress-shirt, Karina had instead opted for a simple white vest and jogging bottoms, while her sandals were discarded to leave her barefoot. Without pushing her body to the limits, and with no music blasting into her ears, Karina just let her body relax. With eyes closed, and nostrils having no scents to register, Kari focussed on her breathing. As each breath was sucked in by her diaphragm, she could almost feel the oxygen diffusing into her blood, to then be deposited into her tissues. Since returning to the ring, signing to PRIME, her body had been tested like never before. Where before she'd exert herself to the point of collapse, the K-Wolf now didn't stop until she hit the floor, gasping and unable to crawl back up. Before refuelling herself, she wanted to know that she was well and truly emptied out. But to really appreciate the feelings of burning lungs, perspiration-saturated skin, and engorged muscles, she needed to relax beforehand. The calm before the storm. A prelude to exhaustion. Still, solitary in silence, hair tamed, and devoid of the bright colours that usually adorned her, only the pink fringe made her recognisable as Karina. A face as plain as hers could easily have belonged to anyone, and her tall, flat figure was far more forgettable than memorable. With eyelids enjoying their rare feeling of closeness, Karina didn’t see, and thus didn't care, about the odd looks she was receiving. For some reason, the sight of a woman, clad in all white, just sitting in the sun's rays was something to stare at... and odd choice for something to notice, in a world of such natural splendour. But then, those who weren't looking had their attention turned her way as the ring-tone of Karina's cell phone shattered the sedate environment she'd immersed herself in. "Speak," was her blunt command, not even looking at the caller ID. "Well, good morning to you too," replied Tessa Windsor, taking the greeting as a K-Wolf-ism rather than rudeness. "Are you still in Boston by any chance?" Managing to locate Karina on the planet was never an easy task. As if she could never get comfortable, always fidgeting and itching, Wolfenden felt the need to hop from destination to destination... taking in new scenery and cultures. Luckily for Tessa, Karina was stuck in the United States, doing something far more tedious and mundane than taming virgin snow in New Zealand, or rock climbing in Tunisia. "Yeah," Kari replied with more than a little curiosity. She was always wary when others wanted to know where she was, or where she would be. "I'm just about finished helping my brother decorate his place here... to his tastes, unfortunately for any visitors." "Fancy meeting up for brunch," Tessa then enquired, feeling more than a little guilt at having already signed her friend up for the match. "I have something... well, I have a favour to ask." "Okay..." Kari responded, knowing that Tessa was often a little bashful, but not always that hesitant in saying what she needed to. But, anyway, she was currently pre-occupied with doing something even more relaxing than doing nothing... so a curt farewell was in order. "Call me in two hours. Bye." Despite not being the shortest woman in the world, Karina Wolfenden had a way of sneaking up on people. A kind of natural stealth, that allowed her to be the girl that nobody really noticed. With a plain face and unremarkable body, it was usually only her choice of clothing that snatched passers-by's attention. "Morning," Kari beamed, watching an already nervous Tessa nearly jump out of her skin, before snaking her slender figure into the cramped booth seat opposite her fellow PRIME member. "You buying?" "Yeah, I guess," Tessa answered, already on the back-foot due to Karina's at-times up-front personality. "I'll probably grab something to go when we're finished then," Wolfenden replied, "turns out Max's fiancé has way better taste, so wants the whole of the upstairs repainted. And while I admire her colour-schemes, I don't exactly relish another day of fumes and paint-thinner." "Oh, if you're in a hurry, we can do this another time," Tessa mumbled, her reservedness about going ahead with the match starting to permeate through her false, calm exterior. "It can wait, it was nothing, really..." Wondering why Tessa was acting a little odd, Karina over-acted a glance to the spaces either side of Tessa, making Miss Windsor's gaze follow her lead. "Well, it was enough for you to off-load the kids on someone to come here," Kari suggested. "So it can't have been that small. C'mon, dish it up." Tessa's fingers were tickling one another's tips as they fidgeted above her coffee cup. Beside it, a half eaten muffin lay demolished into pieces, where a nervous Tessa had picked and picked at it, waiting for Karina to breeze in. Under the table her feet were tapping away, while the skin of her upper lip looked red, where she'd nibbled at it. "Okay, but promise not to laugh, okay?" she asked of Karina. "You're not really the best joke-teller I've met," the K-Wolf aimed to keep the mood light with, "so I think that's a promise I can keep." "Well... here's the thing," Tessa hesitantly began, wondering whether or not to divulge to her friend that the paperwork had already been faxed through, and the confirmation e-mailed to her just 2 minutes before leaving the house. "As you know, I haven't really had the best of luck around PTC... which I know shouldn't matter, but it just seems like until I win one, I'm always going to be remembered as the girl who didn't." "It's not like I've won any either..." Kari offered back, not quite seeing the big deal. "Yeah," Tessa conceded, annoyed that she couldn't have Karina's blasé attitude to the situation. "At least you're making it past the first round. In the Cruiserweight Tourney, I go first round, you make it to the final. In the GTT, I go first round, you make it up to the bracket final. But now, well, there's a chance for me to at least get a shot at a belt... a good chance." As Tessa spoke, Karina just sat back, and observed as much as she listened. Tessa's words were laced with regret, as if the events had really gotten to her. While her body language was all over the place, digits always seemingly needed something to occupy them, while wide, jumpy eyes couldn't remain focussed on one thing for more than a few seconds. "So where do I come into this," Kari wondered. "I'm not exactly the best person to give people confidence boosts, and my training methods aren't ones I want to go through with anyone who has people depending on their health." "I want you to be my tag team partner," Tessa then divulged, starting to speak the second Karina ceased. "O... K..." a rather surprised Wolfenden acknowledged. "Er, one question... why the hell would anyone want me as a tag partner?" "It needs to be someone from PRIME," Tessa answered, still holding back about the fact they were already entered. "And I haven't really been there that long, can't say I've gotten to know anyone... at least not anyone I like that much... so you're the only person I can ask." "Ah," Karina sighed, realising the icy demeanour she kept up to keep people at bay hadn't been seriously breached. "So it's really desperation then? I can live with that." "You have an odd way of making nothing sound very flattering..." Tessa commented as Karina turned her words into a reasonably fair reflection of the truth. "So, anyway, are you interested? It's only a singles match to start with, but I needed to name a partner for if I win and get the shot at the Shadow Ride-" "If you win?" Wolfenden then cut her off with. "I thought you were here asking me to fight, not cheerlead." "Well, yeah," Tessa tried to explain... without making it sound like she wasn't exactly fully trusting of the K-Wolf. "But I know you're usually not around other than for PRIME bookings, and this is important to me, so I was goi-" "How about this?" Karina once again interrupted with, an air of enthusiasm about her. "I take the singles match, and I'm in. Deal?" Tessa sighed, sliding deeper into her seat. She should have realised the chat with Karina Wolfenden was going a little too easily, devoid of the usual frustration she managed to brew up inside of people with her admittedly self-centered attitude. "This is important to me, Kari," Tessa pressed home. "I just don't want you, y'know..." "Fucking it up?" the K-Wolf casually bounced back. "It's just that I know how you live," Tessa tried to explore a diplomatic route with. "You pick up more knocks, bumps, and bruises in a week than both my kids do in a month... I just don't want you taking the match, getting hurt, and meaning we have to forfeit." "You worry too much," Kari commented. "And you don't worry enough," Tessa hit right back with, rediscovering her assertive side. "So if you're in, promise me that you're not going to do anything stupid between now and Sunday?" "Okay, okay, I promise, nothing stupid," Karina brushed the seriousness off with. "So, got the forms for me to sign?" "I... Er... they're at home," Tessa just about managed to answer with, trying to catch up to herself as the words came to her. "I can fill it all out though, don't worry. You go and get back to the decorating, I'll handle it all." "Right..." Karina mused with narrowed, curious eyes, wondering why Tessa suddenly got all jumpy. Sliding out of the booth, Karina straightened herself up, pulled down her vest where it had ridden up, and gestured to the counter. "I'll just grab a coffee and a Danish, charge to table 7?" "Yeah, that's fine," a relieved Tessa nodded, her heart still skipping the odd beat as she feared the truth would still find a way to crawl out. "And thanks... really. It means a lot." "Hey, at least you asked me first," Kari shrugged, oblivious to the large injection of guilt she’d just pumped into Miss Windsor. "Not like RPW when they decided to enter me in for stuff without telling..." As Karina grabbed her coffee and pastry, she flashed Tessa a wave, which was just about reciprocated, before heading out the door. As soon as Karina disappeared out of sight, Tessa exhaled, as if she'd been holding her breath the whole time... finally letting it all out. With elbows on the table, Tessa ran her hands through her hair, then used them to prop her had up. Staring down into the empty coffee cup, Tessa started to wonder whether Karina knew, and if her parting comment was her subtle, infuriatingly vague way of saying so. A groan was then expelled. Tessa Windsor wasn't feeling good about herself. "Why do I care this much...?" III - We All Fall Down. When you hear the words 'free run', you could be forgiven for thinking of vast, wide-open expanses. Empty fields home only to grass and weeds, ploughed only by the pounding feet that churn the top soil. But for Karina Wolfenden, the free referred to the crowds and obstacles that she had to navigate, all fixed to the ground or being drawn towards their workplace. They were locked in motion, trapped on their path, while she was free to weave through them on her way to nowhere in particular. So, on the streets of Boston, where the native Tessa Windsor would normally juggle her two children and the shopping they'd nagged her for, Karina showed that she was the care-free, responsibility dodging member of the team. "Be careful," Tessa had warned her, feeling the need to re-enforce her concerns with a phone-call, as if Kari were the third child-at-heart that she'd adopted. "Just try not to injure yourself of anything." Despite the sounds of Guano Apes blaring from her earphones of her seemingly less-than-personal MP3 player, Karina wasn't around any one person long enough for the tinny blast to be an annoyance to them. At a little over the 6 foot mark, Karina's long strides cleared nearly 5 paving slabs with each bounding stride. Just like the birds she so often tried to emulate, a walking Karina was often an awkward sight. Tall, sleek, and with a high centre of gravity, she only achieved a natural grace when in the air, or opening up her paces into a run. "Oh, please," was Karina nonchalant remark in reply to Tessa. "There's nothing I couldn't heal from between now and Sunday." Although a reckless attitude, it was one that Karina's physiology could back up. Having adopted her daredevil nature at a young age, slowly expanding from clandestine Kick-Boxing lessons, her body couldn't help but adapt to her pursuits. After a few months, the bruises which lingered from hard body strikes and kicks barely lasted the night, while the bones around her joints seemingly fused, fighting any twists or sprains her wayward landings might cause. When she was young, Karina had often contemplated what was the highest fall her unsuspectingly resilient body could absorb. By the beach where the Wolfenden family holidayed every August, there was a flight of stairs chipped out of a rock face, overlooking the beach. One morning, before her parents and brother had even dreamt about waking up, Karina approached them with defiance. Off the 5th step she dropped, into an easy landing. Then to the 6th, for the same. By the 8th, she was above the height that she was to grow into, and with a crouched landing, she landed both effortlessly and painlessly. Then to the 10th, with the same landing. While her body absorbed the impact, her brain began to reason... if she could make he 10th, then she could make the 11th, which was only a little taller. So if she made the 11th, why not the 12th. If the 12th was possible, then so must the 13th. And so on, and so on. "Karina!" the booming sound of her father's commanding voice halted her experiment with. "Get down from there... immediately!" Ever since then, Karina lived her life as a continuation of that experiment. If she could make one jump, then why not one just a little further? If she could push her body to its outer limits, then why couldn't she push it to the limits of those limits? If she could leap off the top rope into two and a half twists, then why not two and three quarters? To sum it up, Karina Wolfenden was the woman who was always looking to go that little bit further, just to prove that she could. In many ways, it was no different to Tessa Windsor gunning for a PTC Tag Title... wanting to go one step further than she had before. "Hey!" came the objection from one of those who were happy living their life at their own pace. "Watch where you're going!" But every bit of free energy Karina had was already being utilised, and she didn't even acknowledge his words. Eyes of burning emerald scanned her path, trying to devise a route through the human cattle that strolled in all directions ahead of her. When it had been worked out, legs of scorching muscle propelled her willing body forward. Arms pumped at her sides, while a mane of blonde and pink hair followed in her wake, ravaged by the air being sucked into the pseudo-vacuum her motion produced. Despite the un-even sidewalk, its overlapping and poorly laid slabs a lawsuit waiting to happen, Karina's balance remained perfectly poised. Thighs acted as shock absorbers, while hips made miniscule corrections to keep her at ninety degrees to the surface. While passers-by broke off to go around a vacant bench, Karina ripped herself from the concrete and glided onto the wooden planks that comprised it. With bounce in her step, Karina hopped onto the back of the bench, rotation through 360 degrees before flinging herself off and back down to the ground, via the make-shift stepping stone of a trash-can. Turning the corner, Karina's body leant into the bend, almost to the point that her right hand might have needed to shoot out to prevent her collapse. As her body straightened, Karina's strides widened once more, and fallen leaves were sent flying as her sneakers ploughed through the fallen leaf-litter. Out of her peripheral vision, Karina saw the steps she needed to go down, and so threaded a fine needle of two cyclists to lock her course onto them. While others walked around the rail to descend them, Karina approached black painted steel with contempt, vaulting over it with her right hand as a pivot. With only 6 steps left to the bottom, Karina took them all in one leap, consolidating her landing with a crouch, before shooting up and towards the next flight. Having landed just over halfway down the first flight, Karina told herself that she could take a whole flight. Her body had taken far more wicked drops, to the point that the concrete almost seemed spongy underfoot. Bounding towards the rail, Karina again hopped up over it, only to then look down. "Oh fuck..." was all the K-Wolf could exhale. With the architect not exactly a fan of evenly sculpted steps, Karina found herself plummeting down nearly 10 feet further than before, and even then she was aiming to land only two thirds down them. Attracted to the daredevil sight, a young child rushed to the rail and looked through the gaps to see Karina make contact with the concrete. "Mommy!" he called out. "The lady fell!" Adjusting her body in flight, Karina managed to compose herself and prepare for the landing. The loose clothes she was wearing pushed tight to her front as the blustery conditions of her fall took hold of them. In a half gulp/half swallow, Karina braced herself for impact, and managed to land into a crouch on one of the steps. "Thank fu-" Karina went to remark, a split-second too soon. Her left foot, which had landed on the thin borderline between on and off the step, was pulled towards the latter. For a brief moment, Karina had been complacent, and before her landing was assured, Karina was sent tumbling down the steps. "Ugh," her lungs expelled as the back of her head thumped against the wall, dulling the pain as her left knee swung limply against the harsh edge of the steps. "Go limp," was all Karina could repeat through clouded, almost panicked thoughts as she felt each step push it's cold angle into her tumbling body. Then, just as she'd done on the previous flight, the K-Wolf's body, at the mercy of painful gravity, cleared the last few steps and crashed down onto the concrete at the foot of them. Luckily, her head avoided any sick impact, bouncing off the rather more cushioned surface of her forearm. Barely able to take in what they'd just seen, the onlookers took a few seconds to come to life, before they either rushed towards the fallen Wolfenden, or slapped their hands up onto their cheeks, aghast. "Someone call an ambulance," the most direct and composed of those rushing to Kari's aid demanded. "What are you waiting for?" "Is she alive?" the slightly more unsure woman crouching down beside him enquired. "Was... was she trying to kill herself?" "Has anyone called that ambulance yet?" the man demanded to know, kneeling beside Karina and taking charge of the situation. "Hello? Can you hear me? Do you feel your legs? What's your name? Blah blah blah? Blah blah...? Blah..." Barely holding on to consciousness by her bleeding fingertips and splintering nails, the K-Wolf eventually slipped, losing the anchor of the man's concerned words. There, at the foot of the steps, Kari lay surrounded by the crowd, all chattering like crickets. With her left leg crumpled awkwardly under her, hands rubbed raw, and a trail of blood tricking down from her mouth, Karina may have finally found out that every step makes a difference, and that maybe her body had finally found its limit. IV - Admissions. Tessa's blood had been boiling ever since she'd taken the call. "Hello?" the unfamiliar male voice asked down a crackly line. "Who is this?" "Who is this?" Tessa asked right back, juggling preparing her childrens' lunch with taking the call, having to wedge the phone between her chin and shoulder. "After all, you're calling me." "Do you know a girl with blonde and pink hair?" the man then asked, causing Tessa to almost take the tip of her thumb off with the bread-knife. "Kinda tall and skinny? Yours was the last number rang on her phone." Right then, Tessa knew what she should be thinking. She should have been thinking, "oh my God, is Karina okay?" But instead, all she could do was blurt out, "what has she gone and done?" "Yes... yes I do," Tessa followed up with, half hoping the man didn't hear the pervious words slip out. "Karina... Karina Wolfenden. Why? What's happened? Where is she? Is she hurt?" Once the barrage of questions had been satisfied with answers, there was only one thing on Tessa's mind; get to the hospital where Karina was taken. In an act that would probably sicken her later, and cause lavish gifts of guilt to be bestowed upon them, Tessa left her children with her neighbour, and headed straight to Wolfenden. Far from the usual concern that most people felt when going to see an ill or injured friend, all Tessa could feel was frustration. She'd told the K-Wolf how important it was to her, and Karina had even asked to take the match for herself. She knew what it meant to Tessa, but she couldn't tone her lifestyle down for just one week. "I thought I asked you to go easy?" were the first words Tessa spoke, angrily, as the bemused nurse opened the door to Karina's room for her. "All I asked is that you don't do something stupid, and then next time I see you, you're in hospital!" To say that Karina wasn't really in the mood would be an understatement. The grazes on her hands, arms, and face were quickly healing but not without discomfort. Her head felt like someone was ringing a bell somewhere deep in her inner ear, while her left knee was tightly bandaged up, and obviously swollen. But even with all those ailments to bug her, there still seemed more to the K-Wolf's annoyance. "I see you didn't bring grapes," she quipped back in an act that only angered Tessa further. "You told me," Tessa stated in almost mechanical fashion to the K-Wolf. "You told me that you'd take the match. I faxed the form off, with your name ticked as the one who'd fight the match, and now it's too late to switch. I said I wanted to, you made sure you did... and now look? You KNEW how much it meant to me. You KNEW that I was worried you'd go and injure yourself again." "Believe me," Karina grumbled back, feeling more than a little sorry for herself. "I didn't MEAN to go flying down a flight of concrete steps. Not even I can appreciate the fun there is to be gained by that..." "That wasn't funny," Tessa hit back, dryly. "You could say I'm dealing with it my own way," Karina threw right back. Almost in disbelief, Tessa just stood there, looking right at Karina, who's emerald eyes were focussing on nothing in particular, as long as it wasn't Miss Windsor. "You know what?" Tessa pressed, pacing back and forth in front of Karina, who in a rare lapse, almost had an apologetic look on her lightly freckled face. "Maybe I shouldn't have asked you. I mean, politely asking you not to go and do something stupidly dangerous was a stretch too far. Tessa began to think that maybe she could take a few tips from Karina Wolfenden. Yes, she'd wanted to show PTC what she was capable of, and finally capture some gold... but she didn't want to do it at the expense of a friendship, or worse, herself. The gold was her aim because she wanted to prove others wrong, not to change the way she thought, felt, and acted. As frustratingly apathetic as the K-Wolf could be, Tessa admired the fact that she was who she was. Unashamedly, and without apology. By the end of the second, she was getting itchy feet, and kicking her heels. But by midday, Karina felt like she was melting into the furniture. She'd try and run the injury off. It had always worked in the past, so why not now? Her body would never heal if she sat around doing nothing, physically comatose on the couch watching trashy TV. If it worsened, she could just tell Tessa her leg gave way when she was getting out of bed, right? "Don't look at me like that," Karina sighed, causing Betsy to break off and hop down from the couch's arm. "I... am... SO... bored!" As headstrong and frustratingly stubborn as she was, even Karina began to doubt that she could run off the swelling. Although she lived a life of few obligations, caring little what those close to her felt of her actions, she was at least human enough to realise that it wasn't the same case for Tessa. She thought the world of them, and they thought the world of her - their mother - in return. That their Mommy was one match away from bringing a belt home for them. And although Tessa was the one who'd feel that the burden had been lifted, that she'd been validated in some way... maybe Karina would also feel good about herself for playing her part.
