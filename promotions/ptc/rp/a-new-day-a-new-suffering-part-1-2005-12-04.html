A New Day, A New Suffering: Part 1

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 We all know that life is hard, but we all still hope that it won’t be as hard for us. We seem to have that ability to hope for good things for ourselves and forget everyone else in the process. It’s nothing new, in fact, it is quite natural. The instinct of survival kicks in and we worry only about ourselves and what we can do to survive. Sometimes that is not enough. That is why man is a social being. We can’t survive without other people to surround us and protect us. It doesn’t mean that you have to go out there and find as many people as possible to be your cushion, because you will be slightly disappointed. It doesn’t work that way. It is those few people that will always be there that are important, those few friends that will help you without you asking them. Not because they owe it to you, but because you deserve it. With these people around you, you need not fear the impact of the fall, because they will not let you fall. That is why they are so important in your life. The catch is that this is reciprocal. If you are not there for your friends, you are no friend at all. Life likes to test you. We all go through these tests but not all of us pass them successfully, there are a select few that just fail. They make the wrong decisions in life and then they pay for them. You can see them to this day, walking the streets, sleeping under bridges and in those nifty little places we call cemeteries. Those are proof of why you must strive to make the right decisions. No one is an angel; nobody is the perfect good person. Perfection doesn’t exist in this world; it is just a word, an idea. So we can’t strive for perfection because we really don’t know what that might entail, but we can strive for justice. Justice for the classes, the races, the sexes and for all, because everyone deserves justice. But most especially those few women who find themselves being abused by their husband, or boyfriend. Those women need justice. Men need to look at themselves and forget everything that has been said to them about women. Most are taught that women belong in the kitchen and in the bed. These men will probably grow up to become abusive. The screaming, the hitting, and the breaking of things… it all gets to them. The emotional breakdown that follows, the lives of the children forever changed. Transformed from a happy environment to one of hatefulness and violence. We can’t let this continue. It is our duty to try and curve the idea that man can do whatever he wants, and when we see something like this hit a friend of ours, we must go to the rescue, or we are no friends at all. --- The last thing he expected was to find a happy face in between this time of despair. The tension he felt right now was insurmountable. He knew that she must have been feeling strange at that moment, standing right in front of him. He didn’t dare to speak. He could see the sadness in her eyes even though she was smiling. She hugged him tightly, he head resting on his shoulder a little bit as he hugged back. It had been a long time since he felt anything remotely close to love, but he didn’t want to rush into things yet. It had only been a few months. Yet he wished he could just give himself to her just like she had done early on. The silence dragged on for a long time, he didn’t want to be the first to speak. He was sure she had seen the interview. She finally let go of her grip and took a step back. She searched for the man in him, looking him in the eyes. She knew just by looking at his eyes that he would never dare to hit her. He knew that he could never lay a hand on her; it was not in his nature. He… he lo… not yet. He didn’t want to commit to it yet. He would be there for her as always. No matter if they were together or not. She had gone through some hard times with him and stuck with him, he would do the same for her. It was hard though, not saying the first words. He was feeling the walls he had spent years building crumbling down at this moment. Their eyes were still locked and he could see her now straining to hold it all in. She was strong. He had not met many women as strong as her. It was something she got from her father. It wasn’t easy to hold it all in, but she could do it. Never show your emotions, because they can be used against you. That is what her father used to say to her. In a way, it is smart advice, but there is always someone that you should be able to go to, someone to listen to you when you need them. He kept waiting for her. He started to think about what this situation right now could entail. Was he unconsciously testing her? Seeing if she would give in to him? He felt bad about it, but he thought it was important. He would be there for her even if she didn’t open up, but this was the time where he made his decision. It had already been close to minute when her walls broke down and the first tears started spilling down her cheeks. He moved closer to her and took out a handkerchief from his pants back pocket. He wiped the tears from her eyes, the crystalline glow of her blue eyes accentuated by the tears now covering her irises. She broke down at that moment. There was no way for her to hold it all in now. She started sobbing now, and he went over to her and hugged her tightly, comforting her with his touch. He knew that he loved her already. He loved her more than anything. She stopped sobbing for a bit and released her hold once again. The man moved his long red hair away from her face and wiped it with his handkerchief one more time. "What can I do to help you?" She looked down, not knowing what to say, what to ask for. She did not want her father hurt; she didn’t want anyone to get hurt. Someone else would get hurt if nobody confronted him though. The battle raged on inside her head. What to do? What to do? She sat down on a black leather couch that stood right behind her. She looked to her right and saw a picture of her family, all together, smiling, having fun and most important of all, happy. It wouldn’t be the same again. She covered her face with her hands, trying to forget and to forgive, but it was too hard for her to even forgive her own father. She kept thinking how it had gone down. Veronica and her little sister had been waiting for their mother to finish getting dressed so that they could go out shopping. It was a tradition for them. Her father had left early in the morning to buy some supplies for the nice dinner that he was planning to do. Instead, he hit the bar early in the afternoon. They were about to go out now, that is when his father came through the door. The stench of alcohol could be felt from a mile away. He threw the bags he was carrying down with a thud. His eyes red from the cigarette smoke. He could not stop shaking. The shaking. It was horrible. He blocked the path for the girls; they could only wish that their older brother was there. Someone who could stand up to their father. He was too big for them. "Where do you think you are going!? You stay here! I need this house clean, right now!" He screamed. He screamed in a voice that they could not recognize, their father was not there anymore. "We are going shopping with mom; you know this is a tradition." He moved forward with a vengeance, every step resounded in the ears of his daughters. His eyes had become red, but this time not from the smoke but from the hate, a surprising hate that his daughters could not know if it was being directed at them. He stopped just in front of them and when he saw his wife, he went berserk. He leaped at her as she finished walking down the stairs. Surprised, she tried to move backwards and tripped on the stairs. Veronica and her sister jumped in front of their dad, but he didn’t listen. This man was not their father anymore. He took a hold of them both by the arms and shoved them both aside. Veronica fell on the floor, surprised at his father’s anger. Her little sister did not suffer the same fate. She hit the edge of the wall, when she fell, the blood started to pour. Veronica saw this but she didn’t know if to go after her mother or help her little sister. At that moment, he saw an angel. It was the form of his brother on the outside. She rushed to the door and opened it. He had heard the screams as he was reaching the front door and knew that something was wrong. He rushed towards his father and tackled him down. Not an easy feat. Veronica rushed towards her little sister. Now, remembering it and telling it felt like it was only a nightmare. It wasn’t though, and when she remembered that, she started crying once again. Antonio Vargas, better known to everyone as Digital Mortality, sat down besides her and wrapped his hands around her. He had been a part of the family these past few months, being a very good friend of Veronica’s brother as well. He knew that their father was a strong character and he had heard the stories about it, but he had never seen him actually doing it. The twenty-five stitches on her head proved it. The smallest of the family had felt the wrath of this man, this man who after everything he did said he couldn’t remember exactly what he did. Digital felt dirty. He felt like every man was just an animal waiting to get out, he didn’t want to believe it, but when he watched the news, he saw that this was not some isolated event. He saw that this was just life for many women. He felt disgusted. "I will go and confront him, but you have to be with me. You need to say what you must, you can’t keep it all in or it will corrode you from within. I will protect you. You know that I would never let anything happen to you. You know what, forget the tournament. I will stay here with you." He meant it. He would give up his chances at winning the biggest tournament of the year to be with her in this hard time. "You can’t do that! I wouldn’t be able to bear it if you gave up this chance because of me." She wanted him there with her, but what she was saying was also true. It would be too hard if he just left the tournament after he had gotten in. "It doesn’t matter to me, babe. I have already won a tournament with you at my side. There is no need for another one. I am happy just to be with you and to see you happy again. There is no need for me to win another one. I know it is a great opportunity to cement myself again, but it is not necessary." Digital stood up and started pacing around the room. She stood up and went over to him. She held him and kissed him tenderly. "I will go with you then." "No." He answered quickly. He couldn’t let her leave her family at this stressful time. It wouldn’t be right. He wouldn’t leave her here like that. "I want you to go. I will be alright here; Christian will be here to protect us. He did really well that day. He really stood up to dad. So, I want you to go, no questions asked." He knew there would be no arguing about this. She had set her mind to it and there would be no changing it. She was stubborn, just like her father. "I want to ask you a favor though," Digital said in a very low and serious voice. "Yes, anything." "I want you to promise me that if you ever see me act like your father that you will do everything you can to slap me out of it. I want you to promise me that at the mere sight of it, you will tell me so." He lowered his head. He felt ashamed of what men could do to the people who they claimed they loved. It didn’t make any sense. The ironic thing is that if you went to a prison and spoke to the men that murdered their wives or their daughters, they would tell you how much they loved them. How can you hurt someone you love that much? It is hard for anyone to just watch someone you know crying, how you could purposely hurt them. She saw the sadness in him and knew that he would never hurt her like that. He would never raise a hand at her in the same way that her father did to her and her mother. She felt like this would be it. This guy would give his life for her and she knew that she would do the same for him. "Of course, babe. Don’t worry about it, because deep down inside I know and you know that you will never become that. You are a good person." At that moment, the door to the room opened and in walked a man in black pants and a blue shirt. He walked over to Veronica and hugged her. She returned his hug affectionately. In the process of meeting the man of her dreams, she had met his best friend, and a guy who she considered a great friend as well. Burton had been with Digital for a long time. She felt good having people that she could trust and that she could rely on. She wasn’t used to it. She more or less had completely cut relationships with almost everyone she had known from college because she didn’t really care for any of them. They weren’t real friends. These two were worth everything. "How are you feeling?" "I’m much better, thanks." Digital knew he had to leave now if he wanted to get to his business meeting in time. His new business venture ran into some personnel problems, but such were the demans of the business world. He didn’t want to leave her like this, but he had to. He wouldn’t be far off, but it was still too far away. "Goodbye, Vero. I’ll miss you. If anything happens, call me. Tell your sister when she wakes up that I will be here later today with a gift." They hugged once more, he kissed her and he left. She smiled. She had found him, the one person that would make her happy. She just wished that he felt the same way. How would you feel if your father suddenly turned into a monster? If someone you loved suddenly treated you like you didn’t exist and like he did not care for you. Even if afterwards he says he did not remember. It’s hard to watch someone you love lose it, but when that person does, you must try your hardest to help him. That is what Vero wanted to do, help her father before everything got out of hand. Her family would never be the same again though. How could it? After what happened, something must be wrong for him to go off like that. Something is bothering her father enough that he would go out in the afternoon and get drunk, come back to the house and almost kill his own daughter. Vero could only think about how her sister would feel once she got back up. She wasn’t saying much even though she was conscious. What did her sister feel? Was it the same as her? The urge to reach out to her father? Life is hard, but we sometimes wish that it is not that hard for us.
