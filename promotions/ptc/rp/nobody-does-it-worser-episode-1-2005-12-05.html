Nobody Does It Worser - Episode 1

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 OUTSIDE JIMMY BONG’S RESIDENCE – LONDON ONE PARTICULAR DAY NOT TOO LONG AGO SOMETIME O’ CLOCK IN THE MORNING, OR POSSIBLY THE AFTERNOON A pair of elderly gentlemen sharing a bench at a bus stop stared curiously at the scraggly, malnourished young man sitting on the pavement across the street and leaning against a dumpster. A vacant expression marked his greasy, dirt-smeared face. His mouth hung agape, though his unkempt mane of facial hair made it difficult to discern such. Every few moments he plunged the neck of a brown-bagged bottle between his lips and downed few gulps of who-knows-what. (The two gentlemen had already engaged in an unnecessarily spirited debate over whether it was rum or whiskey, and agreed to wager bus fare for the winner if they ever found out which.) Other than that, the only activity he engaged in was gazing blankly into a deep skyward sea of azure set above London, on what was an unusually beautiful day. The weather was a complete contrast to the young man’s foggy mood, evidenced by the occasional sigh of what was, unmistakably and undeniably, grief. A document had been posted on the front door of the building next to him, bearing the harsh declaration of "EVICTION NOTICE" in big bold letters. After all, every good landlord knew that if they wanted to figuratively castrate their deadbeat tenants, using big bold letters certainly did the trick. And indeed the young man looked quite emasculated. The mail slot, which was stuffed with overdue bills and final notices, had a name scribbled across the front: "Bong. Jimmy Bong." The young man wore a tattered tuxedo. To the elderly gentlemen on the bench, there was no mistaking his identity. "Poor lad," one sighed. "Terrible thing, seeing misfortune befall someone with such great talent." "Quite right," the other mused. "I still remember as if it were only yesterday. I’d never even heard the name Jimmy Bong until he entered that wrestling competition over in America last year." "He did so well, too. Made it far, if I recall." "Indeed, quite a good showing. I heard he was sacked from his regular employment after he was defeated, though." "Sacked? Why that’s rather harsh! I’d have been thrilled for him had I been his employer! Given him a raise, even! What sort of dunderhead would fire a remarkable man like Jimmy Bong? Where did he work, anyway?" "Not sure, exactly. My best guess would be some sort of haberdashery specializing in formal wear. After all, he never wears anything but those tuxedos." "Indeed, capital observation." As it happened, the two gentlemen were quite off the mark. Jimmy Bong was not very talented at all, nor was there anything even remotely remarkable about him. And he certainly had not been fired from a men’s clothing emporium—he was fired from the British secret service. Before he had entered GTT4 the previous year, Bong knew life only as Agent Double-Oh-Nothing, an elite British spy working for On Her Majesty’s Catering Service (heretoforth OHMCS). Exactly why the British secret service was given such a daft name was never really explained. Neither was the reason why anybody in his or her right mind would hire Jimmy Bong to be a British spy. He was, for lack of a more diplomatic term, a complete idiot. His only success was that he never failed to be a complete failure. He dropped the ball on every assignment that was given to him (even the one assignment where all he had to do was literally hold on to a football for five minutes—he dropped it after three seconds. Twelve people died as a result). To think that the international terrorist community was disappointed about the news that Jimmy Bong had been fired was not out of the question. No government agent in history was worse than him. The final assignment Bong had been given was to hunt down international terrorist (and part-time hairbrush salesman) Johannes Frederick Sebastian Billy-Bob Garfeld, the World’s Most Fiendish Fiend™ and, incidentally, Bong’s archnemesis. He entered GTT4 under orders from his superior, N, and used his progress in the tournament as a cover to try and discover Garfeld’s whereabouts. Unfortunately, his premature defeat led to another failed mission, and ultimately his dismissal from his post. He had no job, no other skills, and from the looks of things, no money and no home. The bottle was now empty. There was an angry growl followed by a CRASH as it was thrown from its paper bag and shattered on the street, the pieces scattering toward the gentlemen at the bus stop. Part of the label had landed where they could read. It said "Jack Daniels." "HA! I told you so! You owe me bus fare!" "Blast it. Well, fair’s fair I suppose. Speaking of which, where the blazes is our bus, anyway?" "I don’t know, but it’s certainly running late. Excuse me sir, do you happen to have the time?" The question was directed at another man who had just taken a seat on the next bench over. The stranger’s face was completely obscured by a copy of the London Financial Times, which he had opened up and seemed intensely focused upon as he spoke nary a word. "I say, sir! It’s quite rude to ignore someone when they politely ask for the time of day! What the hell makes you think—" The elderly gentleman paused in mid-sentence as he noticed the stranger peek out from over the top of his newspaper, staring intently across the street. "Excuse me! I am speaking to you, sir! Bah, forget it. It’s impossible to get any sort of respect these days, I tell you." "Well, it’s possible that he could be one of those deaf fellows you always hear about." "Oh, don’t start on that again! You always come up with excuses for people who are being rude! ‘The man isn’t ignoring you, he’s just deaf!’ ‘That woman didn’t kick you in the groin, she just has a nervous twitch!’ Honestly, one of these days you’ll realize that…" "Come off it, will you? At any rate, the bus is coming, finally." They both looked down the street to see their tardy transportation coming around the corner and heading their way. BANG! The sudden noise startled the two elderly gentlemen, causing them to nearly jump out of their skins. "Good heavens, that was loud!" "Yes it was! I thought I was going to have a heart attack for a second!" "Me too! That bus should really have its engine looked at." "Yes, that’s probably a good… hang on. Do you smell that?" "Smell what?" "It smells like… like… gunpowder!" They turned back around to look at the bench next to them, where the stranger with the newspaper had just been. He was gone. Then, they looked across the street… and what they saw horrified them. "OH MY GOD! JIMMY BONG IS DEAD!" "HE’S BEEN MURDERED! SOMEBODY CALL THE POLICE!" They both ran down the street (well, as fast as two old men can run, anyway), flailing their arms wildly and screaming for help, leaving the tuxedo-clad body lying lifelessly on the ground across the street. A moment later, the bus came to a halt where the two gentlemen had just been. The doors opened up. A clean-cut young man wearing a tuxedo stepped out onto the sidewalk. It was Jimmy Bong. "Good heavens," he said, looking down the street. "I wonder what all the fuss is about. Those two old men sure took off in a hurry… well, as much of a hurry as two old men can make, I suppose. Goodness, I really should avoid repeating what was already pointed out in the narration… and breaking the fourth the wall as well." Bong shrugged to himself as the doors closed and the bus drove off. He whistled nonchalantly as he strode across the street to the front steps of his home, completely failing to notice the dead man lying next to the dumpster. At that very same moment, another man approached those very same steps. He was portly, middle-aged, and sported a desperately receding hairline. His disposition was, understandably, grumpy. As one would imagine, nobody in his or her right mind would be pleased to see Jimmy Bong. This man, however, was particularly displeased. He was Jimmy Bong’s landlord. "Bong!" he shouted gruffly as they met at the front door. "What the bloody hell do you want? I tossed you out on the street! You don’t live here anymore!" Bong smiled. "Oh yes, I know that. And how can I blame you? After all, I haven’t paid you any rent for the last three months…" "Four months." "Goodness, four months?" Bong asked, amazed. "My, how time flies! I do apologize for my tardiness, but I would like to ask you one more time if you’d reconsider the eviction." "HA!" the landlord barked. "You’ve got to be joking! I’m here to change the locks, you dolt! I only lease these places out to tenants who can actually pay their rent, which includes NOT YOU!" "But I can pay my rent," said Bong cheerfully. "Balderdash!" came the landlord’s incredulous reply. "The only way I’ll let you stay here, Bong… the only way… is if you can pay me four months’ rent, and rent for the next four months… in currency… right now." Bong reached into his coat pocket and procured an envelope stuffed with money. He leafed through the contents and pulled out a considerably large wad of bills. "How about for the next year?" he asked with another smile. The landlord’s jaw dropped open as his eyes froze on the money in Bong’s hand. "Where… how… but…" He looked back up. "How in the hell did you get all that money, Bong?" "Simple! I’ve entered another wrestling tournament!" Bong replied. "Oh that nonsense again," snorted the landlord. "Don’t you have to actually win those things to get any sort of stipend?" "I guess not," Bong said with another shrug. He stuffed his money envelope back into his pocket, and pulled out another envelope containing a letter. "The other day I received a very kind offer from some company based in Chicago—they said they’d pay for all of my bills and living expenses!" "What? Let me see that!" The landlord snatched the letter out of Bong’s hand and looked it over. The bold letterhead was colored navy and goldenrod: MAYBE ENTERPRISES "Building a better tomorrow by innovating the future today." The letter read: Greetings, Mr. Bong: I am writing to you for two reasons. The first of which is to applaud you for your tremendous effort at last year’s GTT4 wrestling tournament. You certainly made it very far and turned many doubters into believers and fans, including myself. In fact, I daresay that I am your biggest fan. Congratulations on your success! The other reason I am writing to you is more important. I would like you to enter this year’s GTT5 tournament as the official wrestler for my company, Maybe Enterprises. Naturally you would be compensated quite handsomely. In fact, to prove that I am serious about this offer, I have enclosed a check with this letter. You have been without employment for the past year, if I am not mistaken. The amount should be enough to cover your most pressing expenses. Additionally I would like to extend to you an invitation to fly out to our Chicago headquarters and participate in a new training regimen that our Sports Division is currently developing. This regimen will revolutionize sports medicine and change the future of athletics around the world. We are particularly interested in your participation, as this program was designed with people of your body type in mind. Just imagine—it could be something that leads you all the way to ultimate victory this year! Since I am confident that you will accept my offer, I have also enclosed a first-class ticket to Chicago. A limousine will be waiting for you at O’Hare airport to transport you to our offices. I look forward to meeting you. Together, we will leave the mark of Maybe Enterprises on the entire world! Sincerely, Stuart Maybe, PhD CEO and Founder, Maybe Enterprises Inc. "You’re kidding me!" exclaimed the landlord. "Why the hell would the CEO of a corporation extend a personal invitation to someone like you to be their sponsored wrestler in GTT5?" "Beats me," Bong answered. "It doesn’t seem very probable, does it? But there you go, I suppose." "And how can a workout regimen be ‘designed with people of your body type in mind?’ You’re a freaking twig! Look at you! You have no muscle, and you’re as skinny as a rail! You probably couldn’t rip a piece of paper in two!" "Hey, that’s not true!" Bong started indignantly, snatching the letter back from the landlord. "I can too rip a piece of—" Bong cut off in mid-sentence as he attempted to tear the letter. He struggled mightily with the sheet for several moments to no avail. "Goodness, you’re right!" said Bong finally, smiling once more. "You couldn’t be more accurate about my physical condition if you tried!" "At any rate, I suppose I shouldn’t really care where you’re getting this money," grumbled the landlord. "All I care is that you’ve paid off your past rent, as well as enough rent to last for another year or so." The landlord reached up and tore the eviction notice off the front door, then ripped it in half. "Hey now, there’s no reason to show off!" Bong said, slightly irritated that the landlord had just shown him up in the strength department. "But I wasn’t… bah, never mind," said the landlord. "I’ll just be on my way." But before he could leave, a squad of police cars suddenly pulled up. Several officers poured out and converged on the side of the building, where the dead body had been sitting the entire time. Accompanying the police were the two elderly gentlemen from the bus stop. "There, there’s the body, officer!" squealed one voice. "Alright, alright, stand back!" shouted one of the officers. "We’ll take care of this situation." Bong and his landlord were quite curious about this sudden turn of events. They walked over to see what was going on. "There’s Jimmy Bong, he’s dead!" the other gentleman cried. "I can’t believe somebody would murder such a nice young man as him!" "I wouldn’t believe it either," said Bong, arriving on the scene. "After all, it wouldn’t be very nice, now would it? And quite painful, I’d imagine." Everybody froze. They turned to look at Bong, who was grinning stupidly back at them. He was, as ever, completely oblivious to what was going on. The officers and the elderly gentlemen were too shocked to speak. "Goodness, things got quiet all of a sudden," Bong chuckled. "You’d almost think we were at a funeral or something." He looked to his right and saw the dead body of the young man in the ragged tuxedo leaning against the dumpster. "Blimey!" he gasped. "Somebody has murdered Jerry!" "Who… who’s Jerry?" another of the officers asked finally. "Oh, Jerry is the neighborhood hobo," Bong replied. "I threw out one of my old tuxedos the other day. He must have found it in the garbage. And I must say, he looks rather dashing! Or at least he did, poor fellow. It’s a shame really, he was only a few weeks away from retirement." "Retirement?" repeated Bong’s landlord. "From what? From being a hobo?" "Well of course!" Bong replied earnestly. "I mean, what else would he retire from?" "Being a hobo isn’t a job, you dolt!" growled the landlord. "He didn’t get paid for it!" Bong scowled. "He didn’t? Outrageous! Don’t his employers realize how much sacrifice he put into his work? He was on duty twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week! He worked out here in all sorts of conditions! It’s not glamorous work by any means and he should have been paid handsomely for it! I ask you, what nobler work can a man do, than to enrich our lives by becoming a hobo?" There was a long pause. Everyone was quite dumbfounded by the sheer insanity of what Bong had just said. It was par for the course with him. "Now," he continued, "Do we know anything about why somebody would kill poor Jerry?" "Well, we have two witnesses who originally thought that it was you who had been killed," said the first officer. Bong raises his eyebrows. "Me?" he asked in surprise. "Madness! Who would want to kill me?" Everyone raised their hands. "Oh." "Mr. Bong, it is entirely possible that… erm, Jerry… was not the killer’s intended target," the officer continued. "According to these two gentlemen, the killer was most likely an unidentified young man who was sitting with them at the bus stop. He disappeared just seconds after the murder occurred. Unfortunately, neither of the witnesses got a look at his face. His nose was buried in the newspaper. Anyway, just take a look around. The victim was sitting outside your home, wearing one of your tuxedos, and there was an eviction notice on your door…" "Not anymore!" Bong smiled. "Although it wasn’t very sporting of my landlord act like such a braggart." The landlord made a motion as if he was going to strangle Bong to death, but thought better of it. "Well, at any rate, it would have been easy for someone to think that this man was actually you," the officer motioned to the dead young man. "You know, mistaken identity and all that. And if that’s the case, I would suggest laying low elsewhere for a few days. There’s no telling whether or not the murderer has realized his mistake yet." "Not a problem there," said Bong. "I’m off to America soon to get ready for GTT5! Anyway, I’ll leave you officers to your business. I must go inside and pack." He turned around and walked once again to his front door. As he stuck his key in the lock and turned it, he muttered to himself: "You know, it’s rather odd how there always seems to be a spike in homicides around the world whenever a big wrestling tournament takes place…" It was odd, indeed. Who really wanted Jimmy Bong dead? Oh sure, he pretty much annoyed everybody he ever came in contact with, but nobody truly wanted him dead. Probably. At any rate, he was right. In a couple of days, he would be on a plane to Chicago. He was going to take up Professor Maybe’s offer and participate in GTT5 under the sponsorship of Maybe Enterprises. He would be safe… or so he thought. It was a strange turn of events indeed. One day, Jimmy Bong’s life was uninteresting and not at all prosperous. The next day, he found himself once again plunging headfirst into the world of professional wrestling with the financial backing of an up-and-coming corporation. At least this time, entering GTT5 was his own choice. But there were so many other able-bodied wrestlers that Maybe Enterprises could have chosen. Why did they choose Bong? And who was it that tried kill him? The questions were starting to build… and only at GTT5 would Bong find the answers.
