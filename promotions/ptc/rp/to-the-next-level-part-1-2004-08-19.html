To the Next Level: Part 1

19 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-19 "What is it like to see the face of your own stability suddenly look away, leaving you with the dead and hopeless?" Last week, I found out. With the trust of my comrades slipping away, what do I have left? What do I have left to work for? To live for? With my mentality as low as ever, I am forced to continue, forced to battle on, week in, week out, each battle pushing me to my very limits. I feel my body being ripped away from me, even betraying me. I feel that I can not go on, that I should simply drop off and fall, and keep falling and falling forever more. Suddenly, in the midst of troubled and uneasy times, I find myself faced with the greatest challenge of my professional career. I have been thrown into the mix with 3 other misfits to represent the WWA in an inter-fed survivor series of sorts. I dislike, even hate the men I have to team with. How can we be expected to work as a unit when we can't even stand to talk to each other? And what am I fighting for? The WWA, the vessel of my hatred. It fuels my anger, my hatred for the world around me, and the obstacles that keep popping up in my path. Why must I fight for it? Why must I represent it? Because it is me. Without the WWA, I would have nothing. Without the WWA, I would be nothing. I must uphold my honour, and the honour of the Devil's Own. I must. But... can I? ------------------------------------ Location: Unknown. Date: Unclear. Sometime in mid August. Time: Late. A swift flash of lightning illuminated the dingy, grimy, dark street below. Dark, more than anything. Dark, a word with a definition so broad, perhaps more so than any other word in the English language. It is used so often that it has become vague, almost stereotypical. However, it is the only way to describe that street, that night. Dark. Steadily greying clouds clustered overhead, indicating that there would be rain soon. That was fine. Not many people went there anymore. There wasn't really a reason for that, it was just one of those places that people tended to stay away from. Since no one ever came by, the shopkeepers had all fled. No one lived in the houses anymore. It was desolate, occupied only by the occasional passer by, looking for a shortcut to their destination, or something like that. One particular occupant of that street at that time was just one of those people. However, he wasn't looking for a shortcut. He wasn't looking for anything. Actually, that wasn't strictly true. As the Muppet Man paced down that street, he wasn't really there at all. He stared fixatedly down at the cracked concrete below his feet, yet he saw nothing. The wind bit at him, snarling and spitting like an angry dog, yet he felt and heard nothing. The world around him was one big distraction, intent on throwing him off course. And it was succeeding. He cursed himself under his breath, returning to reality with an unpleasant jolt. He stopped in his tracks, looking up to the sky. The moon was barely visible; the cloud cover above saw to that. He began to walk again. The weather had a tendency to compliment his mood. This time, though, he didn't notice. He saw, felt, heard nothing, save the pictures and voices inside his own troubled mind. He certainly didn't notice the man walking in the opposite direction to him. His right shoulder connected with the other man's, and the shock of it was so great that he was violently ripped out of his trance-like state. He kept his footing, but barely. He still had some scraps of dignity left in him. The other man was basically unmoved. The world was coming back into the focus for the Muppet Man, and he turned to look down into the eyes of the man, as he was a good few inches taller. They radiated power, and he suddenly felt as if he was in the presence of a much greater force than himself. He felt tiny, an insect to this goliath towering above him. In a flash, though, this image was gone, and he was again looking down on this man. An old man, who Muppet estimated to be in his sixties. He was shocked that a couple of seconds ago, he was dwarfed in the presence of this apparently feeble man. "Don't you apologise for hitting an old man on the street?" asked the man, with a hint of superiority in his voice. To the Muppet Man, it was like he was mocking. But he couldn't be. "You walked into me," Muppet replied coolly. He really didn't want to waste his time with this right now. "Get out of my way." He began to walk past, but the man put up a hand, stopping him. "You will apologise." Muppet closed his eyes, and chuckled slightly. What the hell was this? "Make me." As if the matter was settled, he set off again. In a flash, the old man darted backwards, and was in front of him again. Muppet did a double take. "Believe me," said the old man in a low voice, "you don't want me to do that." The Muppet Man almost laughed at him, right there, but instead contented himself with a smile. "Get out of my way, old man, or I'll make you." The man didn't as much as flinch, staring up into the eyes of the WWA wrestler. Almost subconsciously, Muppet recoiled. He growled, attempting to make up for his error by, quite simply, knocking the man's lights out. The blow never landed. Before the Muppet Man could as much as think, his blow was brushed away effortlessly by the man's left hand, his right flying forwards with an insane force, hitting Muppet Man square in the chest. That was all it took. The force of the blow was tremendous, staggering. It crashed into the Muppet Man like a freight train, or a sledgehammer. He was literally launched backwards through the air, and his back landed with a sickening crack on the edge of a garden wall, before he toppled over into the plants on the other side, which did little to break his fall. A couple of seconds later, his mind realised what had happened, and he let out a blood curdling shout, shattering the peaceful silence. The old man looked up, and saw a pair of birds fly away from their perch on a tree. He tutted, and turned to leave, but he was stopped by a sound from behind him. The Muppet Man crawled back onto the pavement, hardly able to keep himself up. With all the willpower he had left, he climbed slowly to his feet, using the wall. The man admired his bravery, but sighed at his stupidity. Muppet tried to utter some words, but nothing came out. All of a sudden, his feet buckled and he fell backwards, a soft note of panic escaping him. "Shit..." ---- "Can you hear me?" The voice echoed through the brain of the Muppet Man, as he lay in complete darkness. He tried to speak. "Huh...?" He attempted to move his limbs, but found he couldn't. The voice spoke again. "Hello? Can you hear me?" He summoned up some strength, and managed to reply. "Y... yeah." The man, whoever he was, tutted. "You were pretty foolish back then." Muppet was confused, and tired. Very tired. He longed to fall back asleep, and rest his aching limbs. Or did he have limbs at all? Everything was very fuzzy. "Back... then?" The man chose to ignore him, apparently. "Come on, wake up." "Huh? Back then? Who are you?" He suddenly felt fully awake, and felt that he could move his limbs, if only a little. "Come on! Wake up! Up! Up I say!" He opened his eyes. He immediately had to squint, to block out some of the glare that burned his eyes as soon as they opened. As soon as the glare faded, he took time to take in his surroundings. For the first time, he felt the soft bed underneath him, keeping him from falling into what seemed an endless abyss. It wasn't expensive, but it was enough. The actual room was small, and sparsely decorated, with the odd oriental statuette or model here and there. Apart from the bed, the only other piece of furniture in the room was a small table next to the bed, with a lamp on it. Leaning over him was an old man. As his eyes focused, he saw that it wasn't just any old man. It was him. He sat up with a jolt, but the excruciating pain in his back brought him right back down. He groaned in pain, and if he wasn't afraid of looking soft in front of this old man, he probably would have cried. The man could see this, and simply smiled. This angered Muppet, who momentarily forgot his pain to speak up. "Where am I?" This seemed the most logical question to ask. "You are in the spare room in my dojo." "Dojo...?" "Yes." The man was taking it slowly, knowing that Muppet's brain wasn't fully working yet. "I own a small dojo here, and I am it's teacher. I am a Yi Quan master... among other things. My name is Jason Masahiro." He extended his hand, and the Muppet Man shook it, nodding, a wave of understanding crashing into him. He never stood a chance. He had been so preoccupied with his own little problems... and he paid for it. What an idiot. What a fucking idiot... "And... why am I here?" That was the next logical question. "Well, I couldn't exactly leave you there, could I?" he said, and seeing the confused expression on Muppet's face, he continued. "I have a code of honour to live by... unlike you, it would seem. This is strange to me. You are a trained martial artist, are you not?" The Muppet Man heard this, but didn't quite believe it. How had he known? "Yes, but... how did you know?" At this, the old man chuckled. "Well, you're identity isn't exactly a secret. I mean, the mask is a dead giveaway." Muppet reached up, and felt his face. At least the old man hadn't removed his mask; he was grateful for it. But, he had no intentions of hanging around here any longer. He had a match to prepare for, and it wasn't just any match. The man seemed to sense his intentions, and spoke. "I was just about to have lunch, actually. Hungry?" "Thanks... but no thanks. I must be going." He wasn't going to let anything distract him. Again, he sat up, and again, he felt the pain in his back. For a few moments he simply sat there, fighting with the pain, before overcoming it. With a sigh, the pain seemed to lessen, and he found himself able to move freely. So, he got to his feet. He felt quite dizzy, but again, this would not halt him. Nodding curtly, he left the room. A couple of seconds later, Masahiro followed him. Muppet moved directly into a large hall of sorts, and couldn't help but look around. It was obviously an area of training; there was a pile of mats in the far corner, more than enough to cover the whole floor, and an array of punching bags and the like. The floor itself was hard, and wooden. Memories of his own training flooded back into Muppet's brain. He remembered lines of boys doing knuckle press ups on the wooden floor, the master pacing back and forth, occasionally offering words of encouragement. It was so hard. They had been doing it for so long... He shook his head, flashing back to reality. Muttering something under his breath, he made his way towards the exit. "Wait!" Muppet Man was stopped in his tracks by the shout from behind him. Turning slowly, he saw Mashahiro striding towards him. For the first time since they first looked at each other, he could feel that extreme power. It was unsettling. He felt compelled to obey, and cursed himself for listening, for now he had no choice but to stand there and hear out what the man had to say. He left it plain. "I can help you." At first, Muppet Man was a little taken aback, and he didn't hide it. He replied, but even as he said these words, he realised what Masahiro meant. "I don't know what you're talking about." But he was too tired, too stressed out, too panicked to conceal it properly. Yes, he was panicking. Who wouldn't, in his situation? The best Primetime Central had to offer stood before him, and who was there to help him? No one he could trust. But, then again... could he trust anyone? Quietus was doing a good job in proving that. "I think you do," said the calming voice. To Muppet, it was not calming, but venomous, tempting. He was faced with a snake, and he had to resist. "I know that you are troubled, and I know why you are troubled. I can help you," he said again, driving the message home. "You don't know what you're dealing with," replied Muppet coolly, "You don't know the half of it." With this, he turned, attempting to make a dramatic exit, but he felt Masahiro's hand on his shoulder. "If you go now, you will lose. Not only will you lose, you will be broken, and you will fall. After that, you will never stop falling. You will be finished. This is too much for you to take alone. I can help you," he said for the third time. "I want to help you. Please... don't go now." He spoke these words softly, and gravely. The Muppet Man did not turn. Instead, he closed his eyes, and gave in to the temptation. "Damn it," he muttered under his breath. ---- Mental strength was not something the Muppet Man had an abundance of. Over the years, though, he had learned to mask this glaring weakness with a threatening exterior. Physically, he was in near perfect condition, and his in-ring technique was top notch. But, as he he had learned over the years, that was only half of it. You had to back it up. All this itme, his disguise, his shield had protected him. Now, it was being ripped apart. Muppet fell to his knees, staring down at the hard wooden floor beneath him, panting. His body was filled with an overwhelming sensation of pain, but that wasn't all. He had been working for over an hour now, and he was making no progress... or so he was told. "Stop trying to hit me and hit me!" The voice of Jason Masahiro rang out, loud and clear, quoting Morpheus from the Matrix almost jokingly. But he was not joking, not at all. For the last hour, he had been shouting instructions at the Muppet Man, and screaming at him when he got it wrong... all the time. In fact, he wasn't getting it wrong even half of the time, but Masahiro believed that you shouldn't let your students get too pleased with themselves. And he was right. For what seemed the thousandth time, the Muppet Man got to his feet, his whole body shaking with exhaustion. He darted forwards, and Masahiro altered his stance, putting his arms up in front of him as a sort of barrier, or at least that's what it looked like to Muppet. Immediately, Muppet backed away as Masahiro took a step forwards. He began to hop, still in stance, to hide his error, but it was plain to see. He had no idea what it was, but something the master was doing made him instinctively back away. He had no choice in the matter. As Masahiro stepped forwards, he leapt backwards, cursing himself out loud. "What am I doing that's making you back away?" "I... I don't kno..." "WHAT AM I DOING?" "You're... you're controlling your chi?" "Exactly." Masahiro was impressed. "But... everyone knows that's bullshit." Masahiro didn't bite Muppet's head off as Muppet was expecting. Instead, he smiled. "Is it?" Muppet pondered over this for a second or two, and that was all it took. In this lapse of concentration, Masahiro was on him in a flash, and before Muppet could react he hit him square in the shoulder. To the naked eye, this was nothing but a tap. To the Muppet Man, it was as if he had been booted by a mule. Muppet Man fell to the ground, clutching his shoulder, and before he could even speak, Masahiro's boot was over his neck. He smiled, helping a dazed Muppet up to his feet. "Shall we try again?" he asked politely, with no attempt at mocking him. Muppet inclined his head, and took his stance. Immediately, Masahiro flung out his wrist, sending a shower of playing cards straight at the Muppet Man. He did it once more, and Muppet put up his hands, trying to deflect them. Masahiro took two steps in, squared his hip and shoved down on the back of the Muppet Man's head. Muppet went tumbling to the ground, and Masahiro's boot was back at his throat. "What the hell?" Muppet growled. "That's not fair." "Fair?" Masahiro laughed. "I beat you, my friend, again. I thought you of all people wouldn't care about how victory is attained." "But this a wrestling match, not a street fight." "I don't claim to be an expert on the matter, but how many matches do you see where all the rules are abided by?" Muppet had no answer. Now, he was getting angry. Angry with Masahiro, but moreso, angry with himself. He was Devil's Own. Since when did he care about rules? He would win, and it didn't matter how he did it. He would attain victory at all costs, or be shamed. Right now, he was being shamed. And how. He made his way back to his feet, again, and flung out a fist in anger. It missed. What the hell? Masahiro hadn't moved, yet the punch had missed his face by an inch. Or... had he moved? "What the..." Masahiro cut him off with a booming laugh, something you wouldn't expect from someone his age. "Who says you can't have fun while you train?" he commented, still laughing somewhat. Muppet Man was not amused. "Shut up," Muppet said, "and let's go again." Masahiro stopped laughing. "As you wish." Muppet span on the spot, attempting to surprise Masahiro with a spinning heel kick. Obviously, it was blocked, but Muppet wasn't counting on it being caught. He wrenched backwards, and his foot didn't move, so he lost his balance. Masahiro spun him around in a circle with his foot before hitting a knife edge chop, shouting 'WHOOO' and laughing again. "This is a waste of time," Muppet said sourly. "We move on. Now." "We're not stopping or moving on until you hit me." "You're too good," Muppet spat. "You can hit me. And you will." "Fucking fine with me," Muppet thought, his expression not changing. "No way I can do this... so no point in holding back." He leaped, aiming a kick at Masahiro's head, and it was brushed away. Landing, he quickly spun and went for a leg sweep. Masahiro jumped it, and Muppet was back on his feet in a flash. With a huge shout, he powered a punch straight for the head of his trainer. It was blocked and held. "Don't let your anger control your movements. Channel it... and control it. Only by doing this can you..." He never finished. Muppet Man flung himself forwards, his whole body weight crashing down on Masahiro. At least, that's what would have happened if the move had been completed. Masahiro, with an insanely fast improvisation, altered his weight and monkey flipped Muppet over his head. However, Muppet kept hold of the hand that Masahiro hadn't released when his punch was blocked. As Masahiro got up, he yanked Muppet with him, and Muppet took his chance, sending his foot out as fast as he could. It connected. Both men were momentarily shocked, but Muppet Man struck first. He tried for a straight punch, still dazed, not knowing what else to do, and it was brushed away once again. Masahiro sidestepped, and drove his fist straight into Muppet Man's shoulder with all his might. Muppet let out an abrupt cry of pain, before falling to his knees. "Master... eh? You... talk too much..." Muppet said through gritted teeth, smiling, before falling over... unconscious. "Not bad," Masahiro muttered, a hint of surprise in his voice. "But... he's still got a long way to go." To be continued...
