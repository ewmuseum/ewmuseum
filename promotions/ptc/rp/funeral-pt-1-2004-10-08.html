Funeral Pt.1

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 Alone. That is what it had now come too. That was what I had become. Alone. There was no one. There was no single thing. When this happens what does one do? What can one do when there is no one and nothing out there left in the world for him? Simple. He goes home, yet unfortunately for me on this day. Going home wouldn’t be the simple thing to do. You’ll ask yourself the question, “What now?” The low grey overcast seemed to accentuate the general mood of the day perfectly. Not that I had expected the mood to be any different though. Funerals were generally not a fun thing to be part of, especially in my case that day. I had questioned myself as to whether or not I should I attend. In the end I had felt I must if not to respect the dead, regardless of the feelings that might erupt from the source of my presence. There were some things a person just had to do. When a person dies it is the right and honorable thing to do by attending their funeral where their life is remembered. This is especially true when it is your fault that the person is gone. I had asked myself in the days before whether I was a murderer or not. I finally decided that I was not. Luckily the local District Attorney agreed. ‘A simple accident’ he had called it and nothing else. It might have been a simple accident to him, but to the teary eyes family I had seen in the front row it had been much more. It had bee so very much more to the now motherless son and daughter. It had been much more to the now former husband. Feelings of contempt were in their hearts at the moment, I was sure of it. Interwoven between the feelings of sorrow and regret there would the feelings towards me. None of them could be good. I had thought of all these things before I ever started to dress myself before the ceremony. It was just something that I had to do, something I must. I don’t know if I’ll ever know how much I hurt them. Knowing what I did to them still hurts me everyday. I sometimes just wish I could rewind and take back everything. Unfortunately none of us have that luxury and we must all live with the actions that we make, so is the way of the universe and it’s mighty laws. I had purchased a bouquet of flowers for the affair. It seemed to me that it would be right. I had to scour the last remnants of my pockets to come up with the money however. The unemployment checks had long since stopped and my small bank account was not nearing the single digits. It wouldn’t be long before that to would be long gone, just like everything else. I sat there and listened to the speakers of the event. They all seemed to mime each other: ‘She was a great woman; she really cared; she was a wonderful mother.’ Those were the many things said by the many speakers. I tried not to look towards the front row where I knew that a son was struggling not to cry and a daughter surely had the marks of tears dried out by the cool breeze that had began to move into the area. As the speakers droned on about the kind of woman she was and what kind of life she lived I began to think of the night barely a week ago, the night where the wreck wasn’t the main thing on my mind. The wreck of course had been something that I would never wish upon any other living soul. It wasn’t the fact that the wreck had happened, but more of the fact that when I finally came too and exited my small battered truck I came to find that my tuck for the most part was fine except for the dent in the front left side. I finally came too and after a couple of minutes of me trying to get my bearings I decided to make the journey outside of my truck to check on the occupant of the small red compact. My truck door creaked open as always and I took my first steps out onto the now muddy grass in the small ditch beside the road. I went to stand straight up but the quickly went tumbling down into the mud. My head had still not quite recovered from the beating it had just taken. I took a moment to let my mind rest and brought myself to my feet, not worrying about the fact that I was now covered in mud. It was now raining harder than ever and I sensed that the lightning spectacle that was dancing across the sky was only bound to get worse. I took a step up the ditch to try and get a better look at the destruction that I knew lie ahead. The darkness was making it hard for me to see but the remaining headlight of the compact was helping out as much as it could. I walked over to the driver side door from behind to make sure the driver was ok. I knocked on the window to no avail. My view inside was blocked from the fact that the windows were tented, so I went ahead and opened the door. I was amazed to find when I opened the door that no one was in the seat. My first thought was that maybe they got out. Maybe. It was then that I noticed the water pouring through the windshield through a huge bloody hole. I froze up at that moment because I feared what I may find at the front of the car. I slowly shut the driver’s door and proceeded to walk the short length of the car to the front. As I came around the front of it I saw what lie there. It was an image that would haunt my nightmares forever. I grimaced as the pain I had felt that night forced their way back to the front of things. It’s not everyday you see a lifeless body, especially one in the condition I did. Though death had become an almost to common thing in my life, that night and what I saw would still be burned into my memory for a while longer and I knew it. It was now time for me to pay my last respects to a woman I had never met in life. I can say that it was then that I began to stiffen. Not from fear or from some illness, but from the realization of what had happened. It had only just dawned on me that I had ended a life. In a twist of event where my life should have been taken, it was spared and hers was taken instead. I had to start to gasp for breathe their in the farthest back row. To me everything else was now starting to lose focus. At that moment I didn’t care about the fact that I had no money or the fact that I had no family or friends. It especially didn’t matter to me that in just one day I would once again make my way back to Washington, D.C to take part in the grandest tournament in all the lands, The GTT. No, none of it seemed to matter to me at the moment. It just didn’t seem fair to me at the moment, me sitting there with my hands in my pockets and my heart in throat, alive and well. What crimes has she committed deserving such penance why couldn’t she have just been five more minutes later. She never even had a chance to cry for help. She died there on that cold wet road, alone. Alone. Just like me. The rows in front of me had emptied and it was now my turn as the last guest to walk up and my respects. Yet, as I stood there and came upon these many realizations I knew in my heart that I couldn’t walk the center aisle. There was no way that I would be able to pay those respects that I had come to offer. To face those left behind in the wake of it all. It just wasn’t fair. Just not fair. Why did life have to treat people in such ways? Why did her family have to suffer and I not. Why was I here and she not? It just wasn’t fair. It was then that a thought came into my mind. It should be fair. Then of all the things that could happen, a grin came upon my face. It would be fair. I would make it so. I stood up as the dark clouds now began to move into the area. Yes, I would make everything right. Though I knew this wouldn’t be easy. I took my turn finally and walked up the center aisle to the where the casket lied. I sat the pretty purple flowers that I had brought on the some how dampened brilliance of the oak finished casket. I took a moment to reflect on it all. How it was not fair. How it would become fair. I began to hear the whispers behind me. They were from the family I was sure. I could have sworn I heard the son say to his father ‘is that him dad?’ I still have no idea to this day what the father answered, though to me it still matter not. I turned slowly around to where the family was sitting and just stood there with my hands now back in my pockets and my heart once again in my throat. Warm tears began to rolls down my face. “I’m sorry” I sputtered. Not sure of what else to say. The father just acknowledged me with a nod of his head, clearly trying to be the male figure by not crying. Though I could make out the swelling eyes to which tears formed. “It’s just not fair.” I said in a low tone as I was forced down by the seeming less weight of the world. I went down to my knees as my tears began to come out in streams now. I looked up through my tears and saw that the son had began to cry now as well and that the daughter had her face down in her hands balling every drop her eyes would allow. It was then as the first few raindrops fell that I made my promise to them. The one for which I would reflect upon for the rest of my life. “I will make it right. It’s just no fair.” There was only one thing I could do to even the score of life. The thing most precious tot hem had been taken. So something of mine would have to be taken as well, and seeming as how I had no money or friends or loved ones that just left one person. Myself.
