The Girl: People Like Us

26 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-26 "I have no particular desire to live. I have no particular desire to be killed. It is a matter of indifference to me. I do not think I am altogether right." - Albert Fish. * * * Prologue: Floating Along Off the strip, life was a little less hectic. There were several Las Vegas diners that you wouldn’t know about unless you were a local, or had accidentally stumbled across it on the tail end of some kind of cruel bender. When your ears had been bombarded with the dinging of slot machines, and your eyes had seen so many lights that you could not close them without colors streaking everywhere, this was the sort of place you found. This particular establishment was called Monty’s. You could get a meal or a cup of coffee here 24/7. You could sit at the counter, or in one of six booths. If Regan Guest were here, she would testify that this was the vision she had for her diner, before it closed. However, Regan was not here. It was 3:30 in the morning, and there were only three humans in the building. One of them was Frank, a cook who was passed out in the back with the latest issue of Maxim on his lap. The second was Sheryl, a blonde waitress in her mid forties. There were cigarette burns on her arm from when she had come home late on ladies night, thus drawing the ire of her live-in boyfriend. Her white uniform (with the Monty’s logo across the chest) could have covered these burns if she so chose. But she did not. Secretly, she hoped someone would bring it up. Maybe a handsome man that would take her away from all of this? Was that really too much to ask? At this point she would have settled for the pity of a co-worker. "Could I get another?" "Sure thing sweetie," Sheryl replied, and put down the news paper. The third person in the establishment was seated at the counter, and his name was Miles Cavanaugh. SCCW fans would know him as Lane Stevens’ childhood friend, and current stooge. Desade put him on the payroll months ago. He was certainly handsome enough for Sheryl’s fantasy, but he had not been nearly talkative enough in the last few hours to inspire her to hope. Miles features were soft, and when his blonde hair hung in front of his eyes he looked like he could be the cover boy of an US Weekly magazine, or perhaps a model for some kind of clothing line. When his hair was brushed aside, the bitterness in his eyes was almost startling. He and Lane shared the same dark appraisal of the world. Only one of them was able to laugh it off. "Here ya go!" she chirped, and handed him another cup of coffee. "Thanks," Miles said politely. She studied him as he took a sip. There was obviously something on his mind, but she couldn’t imagine ever understanding. From this vantage point, it seemed like too great a secret. And then, without warning, the flood gates opened. "I’m good at a lot of stuff. Just not life." At first she said nothing, as if she was trying not to spook an exotic creature before she could get her camera out. "First time I saw Young Guns, I copied Emilio Estevez’s laugh for a month. Just never felt right in my own skin, it’s like I never took the time to become…real. Does that make any sense?" "Sure," she lied. "And now it’s just like…I’m going with the tide. Just floating along. This isn’t a moral crisis, I mean fuck, I’ve done some bad things. Not really sorry about any of it." "I understand." "Don’t you say that empty shit to me," he said quietly, and she shuddered. This wasn’t a conversation, as much as a confession. "Most trouble I ever got in was for some shit I didn’t even do. Served jail time for him when were just kids really. Had a bunch of pricks rat on me for selling drugs I never even touched, just to save his skin. They all thought Lane was the devil, wouldn’t have crossed him for the world. It was just smoke and mirrors." It never even occurred to him that she didn’t know who "Lane" was. He had been in the public eye for months now, in a strange connected universe. It was weird for him to talk to people outside of it. "In jail I saw someone OD from the same shit that I was falsely accused of selling, and I actually felt guilty. Can you imagine that?" Miles actually laughed. And the waitress, this poor woman, didn’t know what to do. An hour earlier she fantasized about him holding her tight, and now she just wanted him to leave. "I was who he wanted me to be, and I still am." When he set the coffee mug down, part of a white bandage came into view on his upper arm. "Are you…are you hurt?" she asked meekly. "Huh?" Miles asked. He followed her eyes to the bandage. "Oh, no," he reassured, "Just a new tattoo." * * * Las Vegas, Nevada – Two Weeks Ago The sliding automatic doors of the Spring Valley Hospital slid opened with a whoosh, and two people emerged onto the sidewalk. "I think my dick is bruised." "Certainly one of the greater human tragedies of our time." "I hope you like sucking on bruised dick." By now, you might have some idea who one of these people is. Lane Stevens’s face was dotted with bandages, and as he remarked, there were several bruises on his body. Most of them were covered by his t-shirt which read: "The Only Impulse I Can Resist", and sported a picture of the wrestler by the same name. Randall Knox was lying flat on his back. Clad in a lime green shirt and jeans, Regan pushed him in a wheel chair that wasn’t entirely necessary, but one the hospital had insisted upon for the ride to the parking lot. "I used to get pretty tanked in college, might have happened before," she mused. This situation had managed to completely freak her out, but having Lane in good enough spirits to joke around helped her mood slightly. He had been beaten, and beaten quite severely, at the hands of people that seemed way more interested in scaring her than anything. It had worked. "Look, I just want to say…" "Don’t," Lane said sternly, the humor had evaporated from his face suddenly. "What if I was going to suggest tacos?" "You were going to apologize. Not a lot of things more useless than an apology," The River Rat offered. She couldn’t decide if he was mad at her or not, for getting him into this. But then again in the few months she had known him, he didn’t really seem to get mad. She had told her friends that he was either amused, or unconscious. But that wasn’t exactly right. There were moments like this one, where with a head swimming in painkillers he gazed at the sky and saw something remarkable there, a great secret that he would never divulge. And there were nights that she pretended to be asleep, and he actually cuddled up next to her. This monster that had taken aim at cancer-ridden Alanna Marshall, this thug who had tried to break Jared Sykes’ leg, liked to cuddle! Her train of thought was broken by a black stretch limousine that pulled up along side them on the sidewalk. At first, Regan didn’t stop. She continued to push him, and even picked up speed a little. After what she had seen on this evening, she did not know the face of her enemies, much less their vehicles. "Stop," Lane said, and Regan did. The backseat window of the limo began to slowly roll down. "Desade, I don’t care what the Jews say about ya…you’re a sweet gal!" The River Rat chirped. The face that presented itself was not Alexandra Pierce at all. "Get in the fucking car, Lane," Clinton Sage said softly. Lane’s brain began to fathom various hypothetical situations, and scanned over every variable. Sage knew what his old partner-in-crime was doing, in the old days he had counted on Stevens to consider such things. And to make the situation even more tense, Lane knew that Sage knew it. The River Rat settled on a smile. "Come on sugar butt, let’s go with the nice man." "Just you, Lane. I do not wish to involve the girl." Regan looked down at Lane and cringed. "Just take the car, I’ll meet you at home," the River Rat whispered. "It’ll be okay, if he was gonna hurt me, he would have gone about it a little differently than this." She didn’t believe him. "Now," Sage reiterated. But she believed Sage. It wasn’t anger in his eyes. There was no rage, jealousy, or venom. No, it was something else. It was the complete absence of hope. * * * Two Days Ago Sunlight crept into bedroom like a burglar, or at least that was how Lane thought of it in his whacked out state. The River Rat watched its progress across the floor with no particular emotion. He sat on the edge of his bed, still wearing yesterday’s clothes. Thanks to a combination of vodka/red bulls, and aderol, he had not yet gone to bed. The evidence of the former was on the night stand, and his breath. Evidence of the latter presented itself in the way of a constant twitch. He had been up all night playing online poker, and writing a bizarre meandering note to no one in particular about all kinds of depraved shit that he couldn’t really make any sense of. Naturally, he deleted it before anyone else could have a look. In the bed, Regan slept. In between poker hands he had been watching her breathe. When you were on as many uppers as he was, one of the only ways to calm yourself was to watch something relaxing, and repetitive. Her chest rising and falling might have been the only thing that kept him sane. Having finished his session, Lane closed the laptop and put it on the night stand. He walked over to a pile of clothes, and found an old Iowa Hawkeyes hoodie. He discarded his white t-shirt, revealing heavily taped ribs. He gingerly put the hoodie on, cringing as he did so. He had taken quite the beating, after all. Before Stevens left he wrote a brief note, and laid it on top of his lady friend. Regan was a light sleeper, but even as Lane bumped into a lamp as he tiptoed out of the room, she did not wake from her dream. * * * In the weeks prior Regan had some rather bizarre dreams. This one might have taken the cake. There was noise, all around her. She could not escape from it. And the people. Where did all these people come from? She was in the middle of a large crowd, impossibly large. It was a warm spring day. Beyond that, the setting was unfamiliar to her. All of the people were facing a large white building. White steps lead up to a podium, and at the base of these steps reporters shouted and cameras snapped pictures. She cheered along with the crowd, not really understanding what was going on. For whatever reason, she felt obnoxiously happy. The feeling came out of nowhere. And then, the decibel level doubled. A man had emerged from the building, and was walking towards the podium. She was not close enough to see intricate details, but she did know his face was being blurred out the way a breast might be on cable. "What?" she said aloud. It was the only way she knew how to react to the blurred face. But everyone around her was much too into the moment to even notice her presence. Could they even see her at all, she wondered? "Thank you, thank you!" the blurry faced man said into the microphone, and the crowd let loose another titanic cheer. After a few minutes of the man waving and crowd finally began to settle down enough for him to speak. "It’s been a long road, but by god…we’ve done it!" And suddenly, everything froze. Every man, woman, and child in the crowd stopped what they were doing and stood perfectly still. Even birds flying overhead lingered in the air. There was no noise, except for Regan’s frantic breathing. "Dreaming, dreaming, dreaming, dreaming," she mumbled, closing her eyes as hard as she could, in an attempt to wake up. Things were turning bad, she could feel it. She could feel a distinct chill in the air. And that smell? It reeked of rotting meat. She opened her eyes for a moment and even the sky was darker. It was gray, with a faint hint of red. It didn’t seem like weather to her. It was as if someone had sabotaged the sky, or drawn on it. She closed her eyes again. "Dreaming, dreaming, dreaming, dreaming…" And then another voice confirmed it. "Sure are, pilgrim." Her eyes snapped open, and now the crowd vanished, save for a few. She could still see the man by the podium far ahead, still frozen. And directly in front of her, sitting on a park bench sat a tall and broad middle aged man: a sad smile on his face, and a black cowboy hat atop his head. "You," she said, unable to run. "Me," he agreed. When this…man had loaned her money, before everything had gone to hell, he went by the name Dennis Darkhat. But he seemed to have discarded that name, the way a snake might shed skin. "What is…" "Something you weren’t supposed to see, I’m afraid," the Cowboy cut her off, and shrugged, exposing both of his palms. "Don’t suppose it matters much now. The ball is a rollin’, as they say." "What the fuck are you?" The first thing that came to mind was a Ricky Nelson tune. "I'm a travelin' man, and I've made a lot of stops." She looked beyond the Cowboy, to the man at the podium. Suddenly she felt her legs return to her, and before she was even aware of it Regan had begun sprinting towards the podium, towards the blurry faced man. "Whoa there Miss," The Cowboy called after her in his southern drawl, "maybe we should talk about this." But she did not stop. Even though it was a dream, she could feel her lungs burn as she gained ground on the podium. And then… * * * She was awake, breathing heavily. Regan sat up so fast that the note Lane had written her fell off the side of the bed. * * * Two Weeks Ago In the back of the limousine, Sage poured Lane a glass of wine. Outside the tinted windows, Las Vegas and all of its absurdities flew by. "Wouldn’t give me a drop of booze in there," The Warlord said, eagerly taking the glass out of Clinton’s hand. "You mean the hospital?" Clinton asked, and shot his former stablemate a strange glance. "Yeah, fucking assholes," Stevens answered, and took a sip. Life on the road with Lane Stevens was starting to come back to him. "To life!" Lane toasted, and lifted his glass in the air, the bright lights of Vegas momentarily illuminating his scarred face. In response, the man wrestling fans used to call "The Seventh Sin" passed on the toast and simply took a drink from his glass. Lane retracted his hand, and slammed the rest of the glass. They rode in silence for over ten minutes then, the driver looping around the same parts of the city repeatedly. Stevens’ eyes scanned his former boss up and down. Sage wore a dark suit, which fit him just fine. The frown, on the other hand, did not. When he pictured Clinton, he thought of triumph, he thought of a satisfied smile. It was then that a terrible thought struck Lane. Was there someone out there that knew how to take away his smile, too? He would prefer death to that. The Seventh Sin clutched a mangled wedding ring that he wore in the form of a necklace, subconsciously rubbing it with his thumb. Stevens almost commented on this, but decided to let it go. Let it be known that even this man has the ability to show restraint every now and again. Eventually, it was Clinton who broke the silence. "You know where this is going, right?" "Not sure if I get your meaning." "You are either the smartest idiot I know, or the dumbest genius. I can’t decide which." "Dude, they had me on pain meds I never even heard of," Stevens started, "You’re going to have to be more specific." "The girl," Sage barked, "I’m talking about the girl." "Regan?" Sage’s eyes grew wild, and for a second Lane recognized his old associate. "Do you know what’s after you? Or more specifically…what’s after her? Do you have even the faintest idea? This is not a man who is concerned about you hitting some valet with a chair. This is the real world, where things do go bump in the night. Not the absurd funhouse that you and your kind have built in Sin City. And if you plan on antagonizing this situation, as I’m almost certain you do…" Lane chuckled. "…then you’re going to find out what I did, that there are no happy endings for men like us. I will not call it karma, because I know you would never believe in such a thing. I’ll just call it…probability." "You’re so gay now," Lane offered casually, as if it wasn’t an insult. Without another word Clinton was on the other side of the limo, with one hand wrapped tightly across The River Rat’s throat. With his free hand, he produced a pair of small pictures from the pocket of his shirt: one of them of his wife, one of them of his daughter. "One of them is dead. One of them is in a coma," Clinton seethed. "Is this a…this a quiz?" Lane gurgled, the words barely recognizable. Sage released him, and straightened his suit jacket before retreating to his side of the back seat. Stevens coughed and wheezed, and as he did so a smile managed to creep across Clinton’s face. "You know, that’s why I picked you originally; I needed a special something for the work we were going to do, or maybe I needed a lack of something. I don’t think you’re actually a human." "Oh, that’s where you’re wrong, Clint. I am humanity personified…no filters, no apologies. Free as a goddamn bird." The River Rat held out his wine glass, asking for a refill. * * * Two Days Ago Regan had spent most of her morning reading the paper and relaxing on Lane’s balcony, her slender frame sporting one of his signature "Get Fucked" t-shirts and sweat pants. There was something about bumming around the house in his clothes that made her feel safer. She did not know where he was, but this was not surprising. He tended to wander off every now and again for a bender or a high of some kind, often without saying a word. She didn’t feel entitled to know yet, because even after days when she felt strangely close to him, she would awake the next day to find that this feeling was nothing but an illusion. He would be distant, and treat her as if she was simply a prostitute. And in a sense she was. They had made a deal that if Lane got her out of trouble with a loan shark, she would pleasure him sexually. She could not decide if it was that simple anymore. When she returned to the bedroom to tidy up from The River Rat’s mess from the night before, she saw the note sitting on the ground. She looked at it for a moment before picking it up, afraid it might be poisonous. Even before she unfolded it, she had a bad feeling. "I’m going to fix it," Lane’s handwriting said. She rushed to the living room to find her cell phone. First she tried Lane’s phone, and got no answer. If Lane would have picked up, what she would have told him was: "I’ll fuck you anyway, you don’t have to do this". What Lane probably would have said was, "I’d have done this for free." Next she tried Miles Cavanaugh; a high school friend of Lane’s that Desade had hired on to be his muscle. If Miles would have picked up what she would have said was, "I know you’re involved in this." What Miles would have said was, "Yep. Fuck my life." * * * The Town House was a small bar, just off the strip. At one in the afternoon on a weekday it was typically empty. In that sense this was a typical day. The bartender had stepped outside for a smoke break ten minutes earlier, and still had not returned. The only person in the establishment was a man in a wheelchair guarding a door towards the back of the bar. Someone had posted a sign that said, "STAY OUT" on said door. Rumor had it that there were illegal poker games in the basement, filled with high rollers whose income sources were not always legitimate, hence their desire to stay off of casino cameras. It was these rumors that had lured Lane Stevens here weeks ago, and led to his beating. The fact of the matter was there were illegal things going on in that basement, and poker had nothing to do with it. The fellow in the wheelchair, as you might remember, was named Stew. He was a pudgy man with a buzz cut and a wife beater who on this day was entertaining himself by reading Guns & Ammo Magazine. He chewed tobacco, and would occasionally spit into an empty Snapple bottle. It is worth mentioning that on his upper arm there was a tattoo of a black cowboy hat. Stew heard the door to the bar open, but paid it no mind. It was probably just a local here for a cheap drink and a burger; god knows this bar wasn’t pricey. When the footsteps got closer, he didn’t think much of it either. It was probably someone that needed to use the bathroom, as the men’s room was close to basement entrance. When the footsteps stopped, he finally looked up. "Hey," was all Stew had time to get out. * * * Two Weeks Ago In the back of the limo, Sage and Stevens were having another glass of wine. "So what are you going to do?" Clinton asked. Despite the new leaf he had turned over, despite the changes he had made in his life, part of this excited Sage. It was as if he could hear a part of himself he had buried deep within crying out. "Pretty sure I’m going to throw a handicapped man down a flight of stairs. That’s really all I have thought up so far." "Oh," Sage remarked. * * * Two Days Ago In the basement of the Town House, there was a meeting. Or, rather, an initiation. Covering the walls were newspaper articles from all over the country, and the world. They were arranged in a way that might not make sense to the common eye, but pay it no mind. This isn’t about that. In the middle of the room was a table, and on one side of that table there were two chairs. In one of those chairs was Darren Hayward, ex high school basketball phenom, current lackey for a man and a cause he will never grasp. Despite not playing competitive sports in over five years, he was still in incredible shape. A plain white t-shirt hid the tattoo on his upper arm, the black cowboy hat, but he could always feel its presence. It itched and burned constantly. His knuckles were still a tad swollen from the number they had done on Lane Stevens’ face. Sitting next to him was a man who called himself Drudge. As far as physical appearances go, he was Darren’s opposite. He was a lanky man with weathered skin. His teeth were crooked and his hair was long, on this day kept in a ponytail. Unlike their employer’s southern accent, his was real. "Well I must admit," Drudge started, "I was surprised to hear about you coming down here. Didn’t get the heads up, but that’s the way it goes with Him sometimes. But you have the mark…" Drudge gestured at their new recruit, who sat across the table, and his exposed upper arm. "It goes without saying, but this ain’t the kind of shit to be talkin’ about," Darren said. "What my negro friend here is trying to say," Drudge gestured at Darren, "is sometimes it’s real tempting to try to impress some dumb cooze at the bar, but I would not recommend that." "I’m not too chatty," the new recruit offered, and cleared blonde hair away from his eye. "Yeah, I noticed," Drudge responded with a sneer, and was about to produce a manila folder for discussion when they were interrupted by a noise. The door to the basement opened, causing Darren’s brow to furrow. "Goddamnit Stew," Drudge yelled, "we’re in the middle of…" And then they were interrupted by other noises. Thump. Thump. "AAAAAAAAH!" Thump. "AAAAAAAAAAAAAAH!" Thump. Thump. "What the fuck?" Darren cried out, and shot up from the chair. At the bottom of the stairs, Stew was unconscious. Which is probably for the best, really, seeing as he suffered five broken bones during the fall, and the pain would most likely be excruciating. A few seconds later his electric wheelchair came soaring down right after him, and bounced into the wall. They heard a voice say from the top of the stairs, followed by wild laughter. "It’s like my Mom always said, to make an omelet…you have to break a few cripples." The new recruit handed Drudge a baseball bat, and kept one for himself. Darren took a defensive position. The old stairs creaked as someone made their way down. When that person reached the bottom of the stairs, Drudge’s face contorted into disgust. "You fuckin’ serious?" he asked. "Well I’m never all that serious," Lane Stevens replied, now standing about ten feet from the three men. Curiously, he held no weapons at all. You wouldn’t know that by his smirk though. If you were to look at that, you’d think he had a bazooka and there was a cavalry behind him. In the heat of the moment, all Darren can think about is Lane’s Hawkeye hoodie, and how he almost went to the University of Iowa to play basketball. Drudge, for his part, just shook his head. And their new recruit hit Drudge in the spine with a baseball bat. Drudge didn’t cry out, or scream. Instead there was a low guttural noise that escaped from his mouth, as he writhed around on the floor. Darren’s eyes grew with surprise as he looked back and forth between the downed Drudge, and the man he thought was on his team. "I’m taking like a month off," Miles Cavanaugh said, uncharacteristically animated. "Do you know what I went through with these creepy assholes? Jesus fucking Christ! Also, I think the fucking tattoo is infected!" "Yeah, yeah…you can get a parade too," Lane responded, by this point not even paying attention to his associate. Instead he was taking a few steps toward Darren who, despite his size advantage over Lane, was backpedaling slightly. "Fuck man, I’m sorry…" "Few things more worthless than an apology," Stevens said, and grinned fiercely. * * * Two Weeks Ago The limousine had come to a stop in front of Lane’s residence. "Promise me you’ll at least think about what I said," Sage pleaded, "it doesn’t need to be this way." Lane’s hand was on the door handle, but he let go for a second. "Only if you promise to think about something too." "Of course," The Seventh Sin responded immediately. "Would your family have been brutalized if you were still the old Clinton Sage, or would they have shuddered at the mere thought of attacking you?" Sage felt the weight of that question; he felt it stab him right in the heart. "Goodbye, Lane." "Goodbye, boss," Stevens said, "tell your wife or kid…you know, whichever one is still alive, I said hello." If it were anyone else, Sage might break their neck. But he knew Lane meant nothing by it, the River Rat honestly couldn’t remember who had died. For just a moment, he envied Stevens for that. * * * Now. Darkness. Complete and utter darkness. Darren Hayward was never afraid of the dark as a child; he never believed there were monsters in the closet. But this was different. At this point he would prefer to be beaten again, to more hours of waiting…just to be beaten again. For hours at a time he would try to scream into the duct tape that covered his mouth, but eventually he had given that up. At this point it was just a whimper. He wondered what his mother was doing right now, and his sister. He had done such a good job of bullshitting them, of telling them about a new business opportunity, that they weren’t even concerned. They had no idea about the world of shit he had entered. There was no one to worry about him, and that made it ten times worse. It was at the peak of his self-loathing, and his hopelessness, that the door opened again. It slowly creaked upwards, like a garage door. From the other times this had happened he had figured out that he was in a storage unit of some kind. He was tied to a chair, his arms and legs bound. The chair, as it were, was bolted to the floor. All around him were boxes. The only light that entered the room was room was artificial, as it was just after midnight. This was not surprising to Darren, as he had completely lost track of the day and time. Darren saw the outline of a man. His eyes were not adjusted yet, but they didn’t need to be, since he knew who it was. "Well, I brought you a cheese sandwich and some water." Lane Stevens was in a good mood, or least appeared to be. From the things he had said to him, the things he had confided, Darren figured it was just the latter. The door began to close again, and Lane turned the light on. The Hand’s Warlord was carrying a gym back, and a plate. A bottle of water was indeed under his arm. He set all of the items down, and ripped the duct tape off of his captive’s mouth, causing Darren to gasp and spit. "Please just...stop all this." And beg. "I appreciate the politeness of your request, but I will respectfully decline," Lane answered, and rubbed Darren’s head. "Now eat." He held up the cheese sandwich to Hayward’s face, and watched with a whimsical expression as it was devoured over the course of two minutes. When that was complete, he grabbed at his gym bag and produced a mock title belt that appeared to have been polished recently. "Since we’ve done so much talking the last few days about my work and everything, thought you might want to see this. Just had it custom made." "…a belt?" Darren asked. Making small talk with Stevens tended to keep him occupied. "Oh, not just any belt. This is the World Championship of Beating Impulse! After this next match, I figure the records will show that I’m pretty fucking good at it!" At a time like this, he wanted to talk about pro wrestling. This had mystified Darren before, but the more he talked to Lane, the more he began to understand that it was all part of the same comedy show to him, all part of the same punch-line. "So this goody-goody waste of space got knocked out of the tournament…waited by the phone, praying for someone else to get the stomach flu and no show. Some real faggot shit right there, if you ask me. But hey, no one did. Oh well. All these people, all these grown up children using words like courage, adversity…blah, blah, blah. All they want is to win! They want people they want to high school with to read about their great success in the paper, they want to impress girls, or boys or…both. You know, Aimz is a freaky bitch, but that’s another story for another time." Lane pulled up another chair, and the screeching sound it made against the floor was almost unbearable for Darren. "Even Lance Marshall, deep inside that big heroic man, is just a petty insufferable twat who is tired of being overlooked, tired of all the people talking about Jonathan Rhine and all the other sexy heroes of today. I mean, you and your folks really went out of your way to give me a beating, give my girl a hard time. I got to hand it to you, well done." "Once again, appreciate the politeness but…" Lane gave a thumbs down, and a mock sad face. "What do you want to know? There’s got to be something…what do you want to know?!" "I want to know: how much does this hurt?" Stevens grabbed a hold of Darren’s left hand, and lifted the hammer in the air. Mercifully, we do not linger here. * * * Later on that evening, Stevens would crawl into bed next to Regan, and wrap an arm around her. "You make things better, or worse?" she would ask. "Huh, never really thought of it that way." * * * Epilogue: Cause and Effect He had worked so many jobs. To be honest, he couldn’t even remember them all. Many years before he was a loan shark, the man in the cowboy hat (he called himself Bernie Blackcap now) had traveled the country performing at children’s birthday parties. In those times he had gone by the moniker "The Magic Man". Simple tricks and slight of hand illusions amused him to this day. So on this night, or any other really, it was not surprising to find him with a deck of cards. He walked the alleys at night because had a knack for knowing where the road went. And as he walked, he shuffled his cards. By his side was perhaps his most faithful employee, or rather servant, Drudge. It should be noted that Drudge walked gingerly, a souvenir from his earlier altercation with the River Rat. "He took Darren, don’t know where he went. He knows we won’t call the cops." "Hmm," the Cowboy offered in response. "Personally, I’d have said good riddance to that fucker myself weeks ago, but you seemed to have a fondness for him." "Well, I’m not sure I’d call it a fondness but…I reckon Darren is important, maybe real important." The Magic Man stopped at an intersection, and seemed to almost sniff in the air, the way a dog might. This was a part of Vegas that you couldn’t find on any brochure. "Then how come you don’t seem mad?" Drudge asked, and looked over his boss’ face. It looked younger today, but he dismissed that thought as lunacy. "Because just because I told you to keep Darren safe, doesn’t mean that I wanted him to be kept safe." "What?" Bernie Blackcap pivoted to face his associate, and looked upon him with a bitter annoyance. It made Drudge feel sad, perhaps even frightened. For some reason it reminded him of how he felt as a young man, watching the Challenger blow up. "Oh, my dear friend," The Cowboy said, and Drudge instantly felt warm again, "cause and effect, ebb and flow…you did what you were charged to do. Understanding what you were charged to do, that might be a little out of your league." "Yes sir." Drudge didn’t take the time to wonder where his boss’ southern accent had gone. They lingered at the intersection for a few more moments, before the Cowboy made a decision. "Left it is," Bernie Blackcap announced, and Drudge followed. "Where are we going?" "To cause some more effects, naturally," was the Cowboy’s response.
