Sins of the Father, Book 1.5 - 2

22 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-22 I was happy with my career. I should probably start with that. “Nice one, Knox,” said someone who I didn’t recognize. Thanks, I replied, Xander was right on his game, too. Between the Fans Wrestling Organization and New Frontier Wrestling, I was in the middle of the mix for a pair of World Tag Team Championship belts, and the current standing Television Champion. As a matter of career, I never wanted to wrestle anywhere else. Miss Ivy keeps me from getting complacent during the downtime, and Eddie Mayfield may have the world in the palm of his hand waiting for Crash 50 to drop, but he’s at least taking the time to do it right. “When you hit the corner chest – first, I thought you were hurt,” said Rosie, as she entered the backstage area of the arena a half step behind me. Naaah, I replied, a little sore but nothing out of the ordinary. With all that’s either happening or on the horizon in my home promotions, why am I wrestling in a gym in Westchester on a Wednesday night? Why am I involved in two independently sponsored tournaments? “Good match, Knox,” said Xander Scott, as he finally walked through the curtain as well. I shook his hand and gave him a brief hug, “Listen, I know you guys wanted to go back to the city tonight but I’m exhausted, I’m gonna get a hotel room somewhere and take the train.” No worries, sir, I said, as I shook his hand, Pleasure as always. The reason I’m here tonight is because there are exactly two things I want to do: wrestle, and spend time with my lady. Since Rosie has been coming with me on these short hops lately, I can do both at the same time. In this case, my opponent was my tag team partner, Xander Scott, and the scene was a one – off that kept both of us sharp for whatever’s coming next. Rosie was right, however – I hit the turnbuckle a lot harder than I wanted to, and I’d be feeling it for the foreseeable future. Xander and I had agreed to wrestle here tonight because we enjoyed wrestling, we had good ring chemistry, and, let’s be honest – why not? The show was early and, amazingly, running on time, so here we are, ten – fifteen PM, with the rest of the night in front of us. We’d be out the door by eleven and in the city by midnight with plenty of time to hit the block and see what’s happenin’. What’s happening tonight, I asked. “V has a gig tomorrow,” said Rosie, “but I really have no idea what’s happening tonight. If we hit up one of the usual spots I’m sure we’ll find some interesting people or happenings.” She was right, of course. If there’s one thing I can say about our friends, it’s that they never seem to sleep. Rosie with her gig as manager of TC’s is the closest anyone has to an official job – type job. Man, if the usual suspects at the bar could see her now. A good ninety percent of the regulars and semi – regulars have only seen my girl in her work clothes, which consist of a t-shirt or a tank top, jeans or cargo shorts, and a pair of black converse that I think she’s had since she was sixteen. Compare Rosalyn Callasantos, bartender, with Calico Rose, manager slash valet slash Rosie hasn’t decided what she is yet, and the populace will go with Calico Rose ten times out of ten, in her light slipdress, calf – high boots, and bouncy hair. Me, I think they’re both hot. I already got paid for the night, old trick Miss Ivy taught me. If you ask for your money at these indy shots ten minutes before your match time, they don’t have any choice but to pay you. For the longest time I refused to believe promoters operated this way, but all it takes is one questionable fat man in a flair – collared shirt to give you a bum check and you get very protective. I’m not a prima donna, I’m just a food and shelter addict – I like to eat every day and sleep indoors, and I can’t do that if I don’t get paid. The point is, I didn’t have any stop to make except the locker room, to shower and change, and me and Rosie could jet. That was the scenario, at least, when we walked into the locker room without a moment’s hesitation. “Something was making noise in your bag,” said one of the other wrestlers. I don’t remember his ring name but his real name was [name withheld]. Some raw talent, if he got some direction, he could make some noise. Thanks, I said, and I dug through my bag for the only thing it could be: my phone. Sure enough, Momma Knox had left a message. ”Hi Randall, it’s Mom.” She was the only one who ever really called me by my full first name. ”You’re probably getting ready right –ow so I’ll b-- --uick.” And her phone, or my phone, was getting terrible reception. ”I talk-- --our father today, he’s in –own. He asked me where you’re living and I d—tell him, but he—pro—ly going to be waiting for you at Ivy’s bar. I’m n—telling you –ou have to see him, but if you go, please –e nice.” Well. That was unexpected. ”Also, if –ou do go, please don—tell him that I’ve been dating. You know how he –an be. I love you, call me this week.” I clicked off the phone and told Rosie what the message was about. She listened, the way she always did, and put a gentle hand on my shoulder after I was done, sitting, taking off my gear. “Are you okay?” asked Rose. Course I am, I told her. What’s not okay? I’m not a kid anymore, there’s no lingering need for acceptance. I turned around and pulled her down, sitting on my lap. Seriously, I said, I’m fine. For some reason, I don’t think she believed me. “Seriously,” said Rosie again, “We can just go home.” Are you tired, I asked her, I’m not. I’d just like to go to my favorite pub, have a drink, and see what else might be going on tonight. Do you want to go home? This had been the standard faire since we got in the cab. Rosie really didn’t want my father and me to be in the same place, especially if we were both going to be drinking. I understood her concerns, but they were moot, since I honestly didn’t hold any resentment towards him. Do I wish things were different? Of course I did, but when you wish for things to be different, eventually you forget to enjoy what is, and I don’t want to do that. “I want to go where you are,” replied Rose, as she leaned against me. I put my arm around her and kissed her on top of her head. “You married?” asked the cab driver from behind (in front of?) the Plexiglas divider. “No,” said Rose, “we’re not. We’re just together.” “That’s no good,” said the driver, “You youngsters fall in love, you get married. That’s the way it s’posed to be.” Rosie and I looked at each other. Hasn’t worked out too well for our families so far, I said, so we’re doing what works for us. He didn’t say anything else, but I could tell he didn’t think much of my opinion. No matter, it was a nice, quiet ride back to the neighborhood. Six years ago… give or take. “We are the masters of the universe.” My best friend Lou put an arm around my neck and pulled me head close in, and held his camera out. I smiled and held up some ‘rock ‘n roll’ horns, and my sunglasses slipped down a bit. He swung the camera back around and we took a look at the display. Well, we look dapper at least, I said. And we did, in our dark blue graduation caps and gowns. Less than a half hour ago, we got our high school diplomas, and walked out of the gymnasium for the last time. “Congratulations guys!” Maybe there was something to come back for. Rose and my mom both approached us from the crowd of people. Mom gave Lou a hug and kiss, and Rosie leaped a bit to wrap her arms around my neck, and squeezed. In hindsight, I probably should’ve seen it all coming. Regardless, sixteen year old Rosie, with two more years to go, pretty much guaranteed we’d be back. “Where are your parents?” asked Mom, as the four of us walked towards the street. “They were here,” said Lou, “but they had to get back to work.” “Well alright then,” said Mom, “You’re coming to our place for dinner tonight. Rosie, sweetie, do you and your mother want to come as well?” Rosie pursed her lips and shrugged, “Sure, I’ll call her and see what she wants to do.” As if on cue (and with a heaping dose of cliché), Mom’s phone rang. “Hello?” she said, as she opened it up without looking at the number. Someday she’ll learn not to do that, as her mood suddenly shifted. Badly. “Yes, Max, he’s right here, which is where you should be.” “Who is Max?” whispered Rosie. “I’ll see if he wants to talk,” said Mom, as she handed me the phone, “It’s your father, Randall. You don’t have to talk to him.” Time seemed to freeze around all of us, as the phone hung in the air like the elephant in the Bronx that nobody wanted to address. Sure, I said, and I took the phone. Everyone else seemed to back up. Hello, I said. “Hey, champ,” said the semi – familiar voice on the other end, “congratulations, college man!” Thanks dad, I said, Where are you these days? “Believe it or not, I’m up in Albany,” replied Dad, “But I’ll be down in the city in the next few days, I thought I’d take you out on the town to celebrate. How’s that sound, slugger?” Great, I said, Looking forward to it. We hung up, and I handed the phone back to Momma Knox. So, he said he’ll be in town in the next few days and he wants to hook up, I said, Should be fun. “Yeah, it should,” said Mom, “C’mon, kids, let’s get out of the heat.” Of course, he never called. I wasn’t surprised, and I wasn’t disappointed. In the four years since my parents got divorced, I’d seen neither hide nor hair of the man. It didn’t bother me because you can’t change what is; you can only control what will be. Deep, huh? Ten years later, I stood outside TC’s, with my father apparently sitting inside waiting for me. We paid the cabbie and got out, and had about six feet to walk from the curb to the door. For some reason, I stopped. “Just take a breath,” said Rosie, “we can go in, or we can go home.” No, I said, this is what we do at the end of a night out – we have a drink with Val or with LA, we leave early or hang out until closing, depending on what else might be going on, wash, rinse, repeat. “Stop,” said Rose. Stop what? “You don’t have to be the Zen Master all the time,” said Rosie, “If something’s bothering you, you should talk about it.” Seriously, I said, nothing’s bothering me. Let’s go in. Oh Billy by Sirsy was playing on the jukebox when I opened the door, which told me that Valerie was tending bar before I even saw her. She was leaned up against the bar, talking with a smile to what looked like a middle aged man. Let me guess. Hey Val, I said, who’s your friend?” She looked up at me and Rosie, and smiled. “Hey guys, what’s up?” said Valerie. We both walked to the bar and hugged her. I looked at her new friend. Hey Val, I said, I see you’ve met my dad. Her face went through a series of emotions, but none of them were disgust. Evidently, pops had behaved himself. “Hello, Randall,” said my dad, “Good to see you.” Dad, I said, This is my girlfriend Rose. They shook hands. “Nice to meet you,” said Rosie. “Likewise,” said my dad, “Listen, Randall, can we talk in private?” I’m good, I said. Of course, Rosie and Valerie took that moment to make a judicious retreat. So what’s happening, I said. “Oh, I’ve been traveling,” said dad, “Working towards that next big payday, you know?” Yeah, I didn’t know. Sounds interesting, I said. “How about yourself?” he asked, “Your mom told me that you were a professional wrestler. I never thought I’d see the day.” Yeah, I said, just lucky I guess. So what’s going on? “Nothing much,” said my father, “I’m in town on business, I thought I’d stop in and see how you were.” Why, I asked. “Excuse me?” Why now? I repeated. I mean… what’s different about tonight? Pops drank down the rest of his drink. I eyed Valerie, who pointed at the glass with her eyes. Yes, of course, I was paying for his drinks. “Nothing’s different,” said my dad, “I’m just trying to make up for lost time, is all.” Lost time, he said. Maybe he meant it. Maybe he even thought he meant it. What was I supposed to do? Tell him no, I don’t want to talk to you? Someone else probably would’ve, but that’s not my style. So, I did the only thing I could. I sat down next to my father, ordered a beer, and started a conversation. Crazy world, right?
