13 May 2005 Hunt or Be Hunted .:: ::: ::. Two months ago, you might have said that Shadow had everything a woman could have wanted. On the surface, at least, there was a settled marriage, a successful career with a good amount of recognition and respect, and money enough to live however she wanted to. She did upper-middle-class America proud. Of course, if you looked a bit deeper, the picture was more complicated: a marriage that, to the male partner, was quite settled, and to the female, was simply a means to the end of revenge, while she had the real partner she wanted on the side; a career that was falling apart because the recognition and respect all came from people who were on the outside looking in, because everyone in WMW wanted her eradicated; and, of course, enough money to do as she wanted to do, yet all this time, she never had a single stable place to live, and often ended up crashing in Riven’s burned-out sewer haven, the smell of his ashes thick on the air. Nobody ever said that it was easy being Shadow—mentally, at least, she was probably one of the most confusing beings that you could have chosen to be. Duplicitous was the name of the game from the word go, and duplicitous was something Shadow could live up to. In some people’s eyes, she might have fallen far short of everything else that a woman should have been, but when it came to being a two-faced turncoat? She had that one in the bag. Things were… different now. That was a nice way of saying that they weren’t going so well, but she didn’t like to admit that, not after how hard she had worked to make sure that all the necessary apparatus was in place for revenge; yes, as much as she tried to walk away from Kindred games like that, revenge was still one of the number one concerns on her mind, at least when it came to her bastard ex-husband (or at least that’s what he was now). She hadn’t fully achieved the objective, but at least she had made him look like enough of a fool that she was partially satisfied. But she was still alone. That grated on her more than anything, that after all this work, after all these discoveries, finally remembering who she was, and discovering what she wanted… she was still alone. The payoff of everything she had done over the past few months had been limited, and had she allowed herself to feel, she would have felt quite unhappy. The one thing that she could say was that she wasn’t quite the pariah anymore. Now, there were people who appreciated her, people who wanted to see her succeed. Well, maybe that was going a bit too far—but Hunting Grounds seemed like they were pleased to have her around, especially Christian Zenith. It had been his idea in the first place—a chance meeting backstage at that stupid WMW event Ides of March. One hand on the door was all it took—she recognized his face from the Global website, and she didn’t care who or what she was letting in the door. All she cared about was the knife she was ramming into WMW’s back by letting him in. The reward for that kindness, of course, had been well-deserved. She had jumped ship, as they said, to Global that very night, walking out after losing the Ace Superior Championship to Umbrage with a smile on her face, because she knew that where she was headed next was far bloodier a battlefield than she had ever hoped for. The best part, of course, had been Zenith’s immediate approach to her, asking her to join Hunting Grounds. The specs he gave on the group had been amazing—a group with no real leader, that simply acted out of loyalty to one another, and to the common goal that they all shared: excellence. Excellence at any price. Hunt or be hunted. Sure, they might not have liked her. Vivica rolled her eyes a lot, and complained about Shadow’s fangs being “disgusting”; Tessa flinched away when Shadow got too close; Dawn just seemed overly distant, even more so than when they had been in WMW together; and Zenith evidently amused himself by bribing her to stay with the group. But, Hunting Grounds wasn’t a popularity contest—if they could work with her while turning up their noses the whole time, more power to them, so long as they didn’t cross her. She supposed, and supposed with a smile on her face, that these people were the types of people she had always wanted to be around. Ruthless, calculating, adversarial, rude, violent, and best of all… bloodthirsty. Even with this seeming “dream team” things still were not uncomplicated. Nariko posed an issue—well, not Nariko. Shadow had scented her out as Dawn from the moment they passed in the hall, her senses overwhelmed by that small hint of perfume that Dawn always wore, and the underlying scent that was only Dawn, something Dawn could not have detected herself. It was something about the way Dawn’s blood careened through her veins that made the smell intoxicating and overwhelming. Oh yes, Shadow had known. But she had said nothing at all. On top of all that, there was the matter of Soma. Leinad Soma had been a presence in her career for several weeks now, and for the life of her, she could not figure out why he was there. First he taunted her, then he hinted at seduction, and now he wanted to face her in the ring. She would have allowed herself to be overcome by confusion, save the fact that she knew, in the back of her mind, that this was some game, some twisted little game of vampiric chess that would be answered soon by a checkmate from one side or the other. If possible, she was determined to make that defiant move herself, to once and for all fence herself off against outside interference. His gift was another matter entirely—on her person at all times she wore the vial that he gave her, hung around her neck on a leather thong. A vial of his blood, or so he said—but if he were really so powerful a being, would he go giving it out as a gift? Shadow had her doubts about that. Plus, she wasn’t even sure of Soma’s feelings towards her—he liked her, he disdained her, he lusted for her, all at the same time. With feelings that mixed, she wouldn’t have put it past him to be trying to poison her with his vitae. Worse than that, she wouldn’t put it past him to be an agent of Corvus, working deep undercover in an attempt to keep up these silly games that had distracted them both ever since they had discovered their spouses still living and mutually taken up the battle standard. But did that really matter? Did it really matter at all? She was days away from Wrestlecade—days away from a match with Leinad Soma that was no doubt going to turn out brutally, in the most beautiful possible way. The adrenaline surged through her veins as she thought about it—blood, sweat and tears. Wasn’t that what Kine said these days? She didn’t care about the other two, considering for her at least, they were all the same thing—sweat a thin, filmy red miasma on her forehead, tears just counterfeit red droplets that were stolen from someone else before she decided to use them. Blood. That was what it all boiled down to in the end. And it wasn’t just Wrestlecade that it applied to—it was this Extreme Title match against Nova as well. Shadow wasn’t a stationary being. She’d enjoyed traveling to PRIME’s last show to do the interview, at least until Nova opened his mouth. Then again, Shadow had never found herself too fond of most people she’d ever sat across an interview couch from, so there was no conceivable reason why Nova would have been an exception. And of course, there had been the typical “you’re a woman, you’ve got a femi-Nazi chip on your shoulder” routine, which was something that rather amused her. Telling a woman who had lived through the Dark Ages that she was a femi-Nazi—how much was there that this cocky little ass had missed in his high school level history classes taught by the football coach? No, she didn’t have a chip on her shoulder because she was a woman in a predominantly male business—anyone could look at the likes of Karina Wolfenden, Tessa Windsor, and Dawn McFarlane and say that it didn’t matter whether you were a woman or not in this business, and perhaps that would have been true. No, what Shadow had a chip on her shoulder about was that after as much work as she’d put in, she was still just the femi-Nazi, just the newest joke in the back, the newest woman for guys to go “hey Toots” at, the newest subject of locker room pranks. That had nothing to do at all with the fact that she was a woman, and everything to do with the fact that people still acted like she was some rookie who had stumbled into a wrestling ring by mistake and somehow magically pinned the likes of Joey Vendetti and Dave Harley to get here. A woman who had somehow just accidentally entered GTT4 and run through some of the names that everyone was predicting to take brackets—Tsunami Oni, Jonathan Sail… but the past didn’t matter, and the truth was that it never would. She just wasn’t the favorite, and she never would be. She wasn’t anybody’s pick to win—always the dark horse, always the long shot. No matter the opponent, nobody ever thought she had it in her. But Shadow was not a woman to simply sit around and let things like this be rumblings of discontent in an already over-crowded mind. This was a chance—she wasn’t stupid, and she realized full well that in order to ever get what she deserved, she would have to start from scratch and build herself up brick by brick, stone by stone. So nobody thought she could beat Nova—so what? Nobody thought she could get to the GTT4 bracket finals. Nobody thought way back in WMW that she could have ever beaten Noah Hanson for the Ace Superior Title. What that mystical “everybody” thought was to her just a phantom, haunting her whenever she had doubts, trying to pile on the self-hatred so thick that she would just lie in bed all night instead of getting up and training. For too long, Shadow had taken a back seat. For too long, it had been all eyes on the Global Title and its prestige. Right now, the playing field was hers. This was her chance to prove what sort of champion she made, her chance to prove that those other wins weren’t flukes. In the Extreme Title Division… that all started with blood. How much she was willing to shed herself, and to what lengths she was willing to go to bloody her opponent beyond recognition. That was what won matches in the ETD, and that was what had won wars for countless ages on this little planet. No holding off, no backing down. This time around was to be an all out full-armored battle. She’d been waiting on a chance like this for a very, very long time. If Nova wanted something to complain about, she’d give it to him, though perhaps not exactly in the form he expected. .:: ::: ::. Shadow’s eyes narrowed in annoyance. Airport security always had a way of pissing her off—it was something about that presence that she gave off that seemed to make most of these irritating little Kine nervous. Nine times out of ten, they’d scan her bags multiple times and take extra time to search over her with their irritating little handheld metal detectors. She didn’t claim to know how any of this technology worked, but all she knew was that every time she went through a damned airport, she could smell fear from these little puppets as they rummaged through her bags. They knew something wasn’t right about her. “Alright, looks like you check out, ma’am,” said one of the security guards, with a hickish twang that grated on her. Didn’t people know how to speak anymore? She leaned down to pick up her bag, as he looked at her curiously. “Have a nice flight,” he said, scrutinizing her a little too closely for her liking. “Piss off,” she hissed, shouldering her bag with one swift, rough gesture. As she stalked off, she could still feel his eyes on her back. She turned back to give him a nasty glower, and noted his shudder of dread with a thrill of delight that reverberated through her entire being. It had been much more convenient to do this traveling thing when she had access to a private pilot who’d keep his mouth shut for a right price. But, that was the past, and there was no sense lamenting it now, was there? No, this time, she’d have to suck it up, and just deal with the fact that she’d be flying commercial air, irritations and all. At least she wasn’t having to pay for it this time—somebody had come up with a nice compensation for her having to go so far out of her way to first sign the match and make arrangements, then fly back to Global for Cade, and then back to PRIME to defend the ET for the first time. PRIME, PTC, Global, somebody—she didn’t know who—but it didn’t matter, so long as the hassle was off of her back. She’d been informed, of course, that this first trip to the PRIME offices was only a formality, but it was something important. A contract signed in blood didn’t have quite the same effect when it was faxed, and that probably would have made quite the mess anyway. The clock above her head read 9:32 pm. As she passed under it, she grimaced slightly. Because of her little hang-up at the security checkpoint, she had about ten minutes to rummage for her gate before the plane started boarding. She picked up her pace slightly. There was no way she was going to miss this flight because right now, the most important thing to her was the mission that was set out in front of her, and accomplishing it. She was bound and determined to both walk in and leave as the PTC Extreme Champion, because this time, just like every other time, she wasn’t going to succumb to expectation and lose. That would be too easy. That would be what everyone wanted. A hand shot out of nowhere and caught her by the wrist as she passed a bank of pay phones next to a dark, shabbily carpeted hallway. She turned, her eyes practically glowing with fury, making a concerted effort not to snap, and bite off the head of this unfortunate interloper. ‘I swear to God, if this is some down-on-his-luck kid asking for change, I’ll break him in half, I don’t care who sees me,’ she thought to herself. “Whoa, Shadow, chill out,” said the familiar voice, dropping her wrist. She was assailed by a familiar pair of cold blue eyes; although the skin surrounding them was rather light itself, that did nothing to take away from the piercing effect of his gaze. The familiar form of Rune Hvalstrøm towered over her. After backing up to give her some space, he resumed his usual collected demeanor and raised his cigarette to his lips. She wrinkled her nose at the odious smell. “Rune?” she asked, raising an eyebrow. There was no sense asking what he was doing here; smugglers had cause to be a little bit of everywhere, especially in these days of global politics. He gestured with his head for her to step over to the corner where he’d been lurking before, and she followed. Most other people would have gotten a hail of curses for their trouble by now, but she liked Rune—she trusted him. He’d ferried her across the Atlantic to Prague on a moment’s notice, and what’s more, never made a peep about the nature of the undead cargo he was ferrying to anyone. Whether that was because he was a good smuggler, or just because he didn’t care what he carried so long as the money was good, she’d never bothered to figure out. “What are you doing here?” he asked, refusing to answer her implicit question. She shrugged. “Flying to a PRIME show to sign a match for the PTC Extreme Title against some smarmy prick who’s decided that I’m the end-all be-all femi-Nazi, and that’s all there is to me.” Rune looked down, running his fingers through his oddly cropped, shaggy blonde hair. He scoffed at her, a gruff noise emanating from deep within his throat. “You still holding on to that piece of tin?” She rolled her eyes. Rune’s detachment could be quite frustrating. “It’s not just a piece of tin, it’s… it’s mine, Rune. It’s something for myself, outside of all the other bullshit that people try to involve me in. It’s something I’ve earned.” He nodded. “Guess I can understand that. That’s why I hang on to the Jædr, no matter what a bucket of bolts she’s turned into by now.” He paused, and the silence danced awkwardly between them with the grace of a three-hundred pound woman indulging her delusions of being a prima ballerina. There had to be some reason he’d talked to her—Rune never just talked to fill space. His blue eyes dug into her once again. “So what flight are you on?” he asked. She couldn’t help but feel that it was a loaded question, so she arched an eyebrow at him. “United 538, why?” Without warning, Rune grabbed her by the wrist and pulled her into the darkened hallway that he’d been standing in front of. His large form blocked any glimpse she might have had as to why. “What the—“ she started, but was quickly cut off when Rune clapped a hand over her mouth. “You might want to shut up right now.” As soon as he was sure that she would, he turned from her and peered out of the hallway. For a few moments, he was quiet; still as though he were stalking the frozen wilds of Norway, every muscle along the back of him that she could see rigid and taut. The tension broke as he let out a quiet breath; she doubted anyone could have heard it but her. He turned to look at her, with more emotion in his eyes than she had ever seen him possess, and gripped her by the shoulders, looking down at her. “Listen, okay?” She nodded her assent, surprised by this sudden burst of compassion from stoic, immovable, emotionless Rune. He lowered his voice to a low hum. “You don’t want to get on that plane. There’s been a lot of activity in this neck of the woods lately that you don’t want to get mixed up in—“ “I’m not scared of smugglers,” she butted in with a hushed whisper. He tightened his grip on her. “Neither am I, but who said I was talking smugglers? This place is crawling with members of the Society of Leopold.” A cold fear suddenly began to crawl down her back. The Society of Leopold, as any vampire worth their salt knew, was just a kinder name for what had been the bane of their kind back during the Long Night—the Inquisition. “Looking for me?” she asked, perplexed. “Hell no, not looking for you. They don’t even have a clue who you are, and you’d best hope it stays that way. But you know they’ve got about a million different gadgets that they can pull out of their ass at any time, and you better believe they’ll get bored on that plane ride and start scanning for Kindred. Bam. You’re nailed.” “What the hell are they doing?” Rune rolled his eyes, slightly annoyed. “Who cares what they’re doing? Nobody knows what they’re on about half the time anyway. Just stay out of their way. Don’t get on that flight.” Her shoulders tensed. “Rune! As stupid as it is, I can’t walk out on this formality… I’ve got to be at the offices to sign this match tomorrow night,” she said, exasperated. “Shadow… Do you want to be bull-headed and end up shipped back to some London Headquarters in several pieces? Or do you want to fax a signature to PRIME?” She growled at him. “I can’t, and that’s final, but if you don’t want me taking THAT plane, the one that you’re about to make me miss,” she said as the announcement for boarding came over the speakers, “then you better be providing me with some alternate transportation.” Rune winced away from the sharp fingernail that she stabbed into his chest. His facial expression blanched as he thought of the rather sensitive load of cargo that was resting in the belly of his plane. “I… I can’t.” She growled at him, her eyes flaming up with what Rune could only guess was that famed temper of hers that he’d heard so much about, but never had the misfortune of encountering. For a few moments, there was a twinge of emotion—not fear, but at the very least, mild discomfort. “Rune… I’m going to get on that flight. If anyone tries to lay a finger on me, I’ll make their ribcage into a hat.” She started to push past him, going to grab her bag, which she had dropped some time back, just outside the lip of the hallway. For a few moment’s, Rune’s mind was rife with indecision, but then he whirled around, a chunk of blonde hair flopping over his left eye. “Fine! Shadow, fine, I’ll go clear out the Jædr, and I’ll fly you there myself. I’m not letting you put yourself in jeopardy.” She smiled slightly. “See, Rune… I’m not so difficult to deal with.” His face faded into blandness once again. “Get your things together. Give me fifteen minutes to shift around some cargo, then come out to the extreme west runway ready to go.” Without any words, Shadow stepped back out into the hallway and picked up her bag. Though she hadn’t planned things this way, at least this took care of a few things that she’d been concerned about—and hey, it wasn’t her money she’d be wasting by taking a cargo flight anyway. Comfort wasn’t an object—in between flying in business class with a bunch of Society of Leopold ideologically brainwashed fruitbats, and being stuffed beneath the floor of a plane like a crate of oranges in relative safety from crazies and sunlight alike, she’d choose the latter any day. She lingered casually in the terminal, trying not to attract too much attention until fifteen minutes could pass—she really didn’t want Rune to know how nervous this whole situation made her. So instead, she thought about her match. As Rune slipped out of the hallway and towards the terminal exit, a cigarette in his mouth, she was already lost in her thoughts. The cocky little grin of Nova tumbled around in her head like some spur to action; she had a great desire to tear it forcefully off his face that only grew every time she thought about the prospect. Slowly, confidence began to grow. She’d been down on her luck until she made the decision to go once again into his territory, even if just for a symbolic visit. But now, there were ideas in her head—ideas that once again, nobody’s favorite dark horse could pull off the upset, and put Nova back in his place. She didn’t care what he had at stake—her natural stubbornness was taking over, and she was more and more hell bent that Nova would not walk out of that match with a win. As Rune hit the air-strip, he already had his phone open. This conversation wasn’t going to be pretty, and he knew it. “Ambrosi.” “Virgil… you got a minute?” “Ahh, Rune… si, what do you need?” Rune sighed. “Listen… the shipment’s going to be a day late,” he said matter-of-factly. There was a moment of deathly silence in which Rune could just imagine Virgil planning his death. “Why?” “Something came up…” At that moment, Virgil snapped. It wasn’t like Rune to fudge on his responsibilities, but this was a VERY bad time for him to pull something out of the ordinary. “La Puta Virgen!” he swore, “Are you at all aware that this is my culo on the line and not yours?” Rune winced. “I know… believe me, I know, but I can’t help it. Something personal came up.” “El Niño Jesús,” he muttered under his breath. There was a moment of silence again. “You owe me an explanation for this,” Virgil snapped. “I can’t… I just… I owe someone a favor, alright? It’s not something I can exactly say no to.” If Rune were a praying man, he would have prayed that Virgil didn’t recognize a lie when he heard one. “You’re lucky I like you,” was the growled response—and then there was nothing but dead air. Rune sighed, running his fingers through his hair, which only served to tousle it into even more of a gigantic mess. He shut his phone with his other hand and slid it back into his pocket, wondering exactly what he’d be in store for next time he looked the Spanish vampire in the eyes. It almost inspired dread, save the fact that Rune had long ago steeled his nerves to dread. “You don’t know when to quit trying to be the hero, do you?” he mumbled. Honestly, he wondered why, with everything else that was going on in his life right now, he was still smuggling, and more than that, still ferrying vampires around in the middle of the night. Back in Norway, his marriage was falling apart, his wife was sleeping with his friends, and his baby girl, Astara, was being caught in the middle. He hadn’t seen the child in a long time, but could lay bets that Heidrun had poisoned the two-year-old against him already. Home hadn’t been home in quite some time. The Jædr was home. He sighed, reminding himself of this fact; peering on through the night-time skies as they neared Las Vegas, he resumed his task of navigation. He was so absorbed in his thoughts that he didn’t hear the door to the compartment open. Evidently it had, however, for a few moments later, he found himself in the company of his cargo. Shadow cast a sidelong glance at him, accompanied by a smirk, before turning her own sharp eyes out the front window. Rune didn’t say anything; often, being around Kindred made him quite nervous, but he wasn’t exactly in a position to be rude about it. “Sorry if I’m bothering you,” she said, “It was getting a little boring back there by myself.” He nodded. “We’re almost in Vegas airspace anyway. It won’t be much longer before we’re on the ground.” ‘And after that,’ he thought, ‘I can get back to letting Virgil chew my ass out for screwing him over.’ He clenched the stick a little harder. He really didn’t want to think about that right now. Shadow crossed her legs, watching him curiously, which is what she did most of the time when she was close enough to humans to study them. Rune shifted uncomfortably under her gaze—mostly because he didn’t exactly know what the purpose of it was. He could never tell exactly how human these creatures were; it wasn’t as though he could deny that he found her attractive, but to him, that was almost like finding a marble statue attractive—sure, it was nice to look at, but you didn’t try to make a move on it. “Rune, what do you do when you’re at home?” she asked, point-blank. “Ummm…” he responded. He wasn’t quite sure how to answer that, considering how suddenly the question had sprung out of nowhere. She smiled slightly, and looked away from him. “I argue with my wife, mostly,” he responded. Shadow could hear him swallow hard, and clench his jaw. She didn’t intend to press further, counting on the fact that he would open up anyway. However, Rune surprised her. Most people, under the influence of Kindred, would have become open books, but Rune was stoic, continuing to pilot the Jædr, no matter how hard he was swallowing at the moment. “Does she not like what you do?” Shadow pressed. Rune laughed, a dry sound. “If Heidrun knew what I did, she’d probably divorce me instead of fucking all my friends.” Shadow arched a brow. For some reason, she felt oddly protective of Rune, and the idea of his wife abusing him that way irked her. “You must not have very good friends.” “I don’t care. Heidrun is a bitch, and the only reason I’m still married to her is Astara.” “Your daughter?” she asked. Rune nodded. Shadow grimaced slightly, but luckily Rune didn’t see that. They were nearing Vegas now, and he had to concentrate, considering how many other flightpaths he would probably be crossing in order to land. He was about to have to get some contact going with the tower, and right now, Shadow’s presence was making him uneasy, but he would have considered it rude to ask her to leave. Shadow leaned over to rest a hand on his forearm for a moment. He glanced at her, and she could see a bit of consternation on his face, and smell a bit of fear—it always had that sweat mixed with adrenaline smell. She smiled slightly. She hadn’t expected that Rune would ever fear her. He glanced away, back out the plane’s front window as she spoke to him in a soft, low alto tone that was quite soothing, surprisingly enough. “There are times when we have to leave things behind, Rune… no matter how much they may mean to us, we are better off in the end alone than trying to cut off our noses to spite our faces.” She stood up, keeping her balance admirably, unlike his more… well, human… passengers, and started back towards the cargo hold. “I’m going back under, since you probably need to get in touch with the tower, right?” She didn’t wait for him to answer, but merely disappeared. A few moments later, he heard the shifting of the heavy metal door that led to the secret compartment, and then the quiet click that meant she had latched it from the inside. Her words resonated in his mind for a few moments. They made a lot of sense—Rune couldn’t deny how pissed off he was every time he came home and saw the familiar face of Heidrun, with some new issue to be on his ass about every time they talked. Worse still, perhaps, was the way his friends acted more and more distant; they obviously weren’t interested in his side of the story, because they were getting cheap ass from Heidrun out of not listening to their friend. No matter how much he tried to convince himself otherwise, though, there was always Astara. A part of him sincerely wished that Astara was not made up of half Heidrun’s blood, and was all his—the fact that Astara had some of her mother in her terrified Rune. What would happen if she rejected him when she was old enough to understand, just like Heidrun had? He couldn’t take that chance. In that vein, Shadow was wrong. He’d be around until Astara made her own choice—that was the only way he’d ever leave her life. His motions took on reflex as he picked up the radio and radioed the tower. They cleared a small spot on the runway for him to land, and from there, instinct took over. Rune guided the Jædr to another soft landing, taking a moment when the engine was finally turned off to clear his half sweat-soaked dirty blonde hair from his eyes before sighing. He was so tired—but a few hours of sleep would have to suffice before he woke himself up around 2 in the morning to fly the shipment to Virgil in Seattle before sun rose. Once again, the presence of Shadow in the cockpit startled him; he jumped slightly at the hand on his shoulder. She smiled down at him, something that still looked eerie and threatening no matter how genuine it was. “Thank you, Rune… I know this was not exactly what you had in mind for your week.” Her voice didn’t contain any hint of ire or sarcasm. Rune slipped off his headphones, wiping another few droplets of sweat off his forehead before looking up at her and nodding quietly. “It wasn’t a problem, really. I couldn’t let you get yourself in any kind of trouble getting on that plane with those crazy Society pricks.” “Where to next?” she asked, tilting her head slightly as her blazing green eyes practically paralyzed him. She nodded. “If you can stomach it… drink that before you have to get back in the air. You won’t even need any sleep.” “What the hell is in it, cocaine?” he quipped weakly. She smirked. “Ancient Chinese secret.” With that, she stepped out of the plane.
