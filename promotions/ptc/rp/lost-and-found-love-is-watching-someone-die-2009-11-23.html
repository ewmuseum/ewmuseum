Lost and Found: Love is Watching Someone Die

23 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-23 All Figured Out It’s either your insecurities or your age, but you’ve never been that guy who has everything figured out. You came into the wrestling business with the impression that you’d pick it up on the way. That you’d learn as you go and by the end of your first year in FUSE, you’d understand not only how to succeed in the ring, but you’d understand more about your life in general. All you understood was that you still had a lot to learn. You still achieved success, and you could say that you learned that understanding and success aren’t necessarily correlative. You did well in the ring, and you were generally happy, but you still didn’t have everything figured out. You still hadn’t learned everything you could. And you still weren’t sure about Katie. That was probably the biggest of your concerns, and an understandable one at that. No matter how well you did in the ring, that was something that came naturally to you. Having a well established and mature relationship wasn’t. And it’s taken you three years, two breakups, two affairs, and a lot of heartache, but you think you might finally have most of it figured out. You’ve always had trouble figuring out your relationship with Katie because you’ve always had trouble figuring out exactly what it is you want. But now, after thinking about everything you’ve been through and listening to the good and bad advice your friends have given, you realize what you want. You want stability and reliability. To be blunt, you want that boring part of your life. The kind that isn’t exciting or glamorous, doesn’t give you the same excitement as standing in the middle of an arena with thousands of people chanting your name, but still provides you with satisfaction anyway. You know that you want Katie, and that means it’s hard to keep a smile off your face as you drive home to tell her you’ve figured it out. What’s Wrong Jonathan Rhine is only a few miles from home, but he’s coasting. Riding a few miles below the speed limit, Jon takes no notice as the cars honk and zoom by him, shouting out of their window as they pass. Every once in awhile he lets the car drift into the opposite lane, providing some near misses and more honking. None of this can take the smile from his face as he cautiously looks over to his American Eskimo Kody sitting in the front seat. "This is going to be a big day for you, boy. Your first day being part of a family. Well, at least, being part of a happy family." Kody pays no mind as he sticks his tongue out, closing his eyes in the force of the wind. Jon laughs and risks removing a hand from the wheel to pet him. "I feel good, you know?" Jon says, ignoring the silliness of talking to a dog. "It’s a really nice day out. You know what’d be a good idea, boy? If we all went out to the park. After I settle things with Katie, of course." He sits in silence for a few moments, a smile on his face. After a moment, he reaches for the bag sitting on the armrest. "We’ll see what she thinks about the idea." Risking his safety, Rhine takes both hands off the wheel to dial his phone and give Kody a rub on the back. Bringing his hand back to the wheel, he smiles idly as he waits for his girlfriend to pick up. She doesn’t, and Jon nods as he sits through the voicemail message. "Hey, Katie, it’s me. Listen, I…well, I’m sure I’m going to tell you this in a few minutes anyway, but I figure it won’t hurt to have audio record of it too. I’m sorry about all the fights we’ve had lately, but I realized that none of it matters. What matters is I’m coming home, and when I do we’re going to work at being a family. Today we can go to the park and talk, and after that…well, after that we can just keep going. Well…you know I’m not really good with voicemail messages, so I hope the real life speech is better. I’ll see you soon, and I lo—" He is cut off by another beep on the phone, and he brings the phone up. Not recognizing the number, he grimaces and switches the call over. "Hello? "Yes, Mr. Rhine?" "Yes?" "Hi, this is Phillip Meeks with Honeywell Alarm Systems. You live at [detail withheld], correct?" Jon slowly tightens his grip on the wheel. "Yes, that’s my house. What’s wrong?" "Are you at a safe distance from the house?" "What’s wrong?" "Well, sir," the operator says calmly, "our emergency alarm systems have alerted us that your house has been triggered. We’ve sent fire trucks to your house now but this is a courtesy call to make sure you and your family are safely out of the house." "I—" Jon’s foot begins to push further on the pedal and the car bucks slightly, jarring Kody and sending him towards the edge of the seat. Jon looks over at his dog but doesn’t do anything except for drive faster. "Thank you for calling." "Mr. Rhine, is the rest of your family with you?" "Thank you for calling," he says again, and hangs up. Putting the phone down, he makes one more risky move while driving. He closes his eyes. Reason to be Happy "No. No, no, no, no, no." The engine is barely cut when Jon leaps out of the car half a block away, sprinting towards what had recently been his newly built home. Kody, who only sees Jon running, lets out a loud bark and follows him. When Jon stops in front of the house, Kody continues to bound around him, yipping in joy. There is no joy on his master’s face. Jon remembers first being afraid of fire when he was fourteen. He was at his first boy scouts retreat, and he was watching the leaders and other scouts feed the fire. He began to get frightened, and it took him a few minutes before he figured out why. It wasn’t the fire itself, not the heat nor the danger of it catching onto his clothes. It was the smoke. The smoke was thick, uniform, nearly solid, and to fourteen-year-old Jon it looked like a force that no one could stop. Even when the fire was put out, the smoke still rose to the sky like an angry, persistent army, and the scene affected Jon for years. This smoke is worse. If the smoke then was an army, this smoke is an entire nation. Pillowing out of every window and newly-created break in the wall, it moves horizontally to the mass of people that have formed outside the two-story home. People shuffle backwards as the flames get doused by water, the bulk of the fire being over but the damage being done. And even with the conquered fire, the smoke still persists. Jon begins walking forward when an officer puts a hand out and hits his chest. "Step back, sir." "No. I live here." The officer nods and walks Jon to the bulky fire chief, who is holding a little boy to his side. Soot-soaked and shivering, but Connor Nackedy, the son of Jon’s trainer, is alive. "Connor!" Jon shouts, and scoops him up, wrapping him in a big hug. "What happened?" he asks the fire chief. "There was something in the oven that caught, most likely cardboard or paper. The oven fire caught on the curtains and it spread through the walls. This little man was able to get out in time." "Aunt Katie saved me," Connor says lowly. "She carried me out." Jon turns from Connor to the chief again, his face falling. "Katie?" That’s when he hears the quick whoop of a siren and sees the ambulance pull off. He takes two steps towards it, shouting her name, but the chief clamps a hand on his shoulder. "Mr. Rhine—" "No!" Jon says, putting Connor on the ground and looking at the ambulance as it peals down the street and out of sight. "Is she…?" The fire chief sighs. "She’s not looking good. She inhaled a lot of smoke, and she was hit by a beam on her way back out. Couple that with the burns, and…well, the doctors will do everything they can." "It was an accident," Connor says, sniffling. "It’s my fault." Jon bends down to face Connor. "It’s not your fault. Connor…why did she go back in?" "I don’t—" "This is important," Jon says, giving Connor a little shake. "I need to know why she went back in the house after she saved you." Connor looks down. "She said a lot of things. She said something about her computer. She said something about a flash. She looked really scared, and it scared me too. She said she couldn’t lose everything." Once again, Jon turns to face the chief, who is looking older and older with every passing moment. "Were you able to get the laptop?" "Yeah, it’s over there," he says, pointing to a pile of debris in the grass. "Slightly charred, but the hard drive is probably safe." Nodding, Jon walks over to the pile and grabs the laptop. It’s warm, and the end of the top cover is burnt, but it’s in otherwise good condition. "Why, Katie? What were you trying to save?" he asks, lowly. "Where is he?" someone shouts. Jon turns around to see his trainer and manager in SCCW, Foster Nackedy, running up. As soon as he sees Connor, Foster wraps him in his arms. "Connor, tell me you’re okay. Tell me you’re all right." "I’m okay, dad," says Connor, trying to push free from his dad’s hug. Foster puts him down, then sees Jon standing over the pile of salvaged items in the grass. "Hey Fos," Jon says, not looking up. He bends down and grabs a photo album from the pile. Slowly turning the pages, he shrugs. "I don’t really smile in pictures much, do I?" Foster leans over his shoulder and looks at the album, immediately seeing the picture Jon is referring to. The backdrop is the Louisiana Superdome, and Jon has his arm wrapped around Katie Malick. While Katie’s smile threatens to break the camera, however, Jon’s lips are barely curling upward. A glance across the page confirms that this picture is not an aberration. "Lot of guys don’t. Most of us are insecure about our teeth. I know that my front ones stick out a little too much, see?" Foster opens his mouth, pointing to his teeth. Jon doesn’t look, and after a moment Foster Nackedy nods. "But you don’t think it’s that." Jon shakes his head. "I know it’s not. It’s because I never felt I had a reason to be happy. Ever." Foster laughs. "I call bullshit. What about when you won the title?" Jon finally looks up, and his sincerity stops Foster’s laughter. "Especially when I won the title. I always wanted something more. It wasn’t enough. Nothing was. I didn’t smile because I didn’t think I had enough in my life to smile." Foster nods again, knowing what this is about. He puts his arm around his friend and says, "But now you know that wasn’t true." Jon looks down again, dropping the album. "I had more than enough. I hope I still do." What Sarah Said Jon Rhine has been at the hospital for hours, and he hasn’t heard anything. The doctor has poked his head out several times, but it was always for someone else. So Jon sits, his eyes on the ground, and he does the only thing he can think of doing at the time. Before SCCW, before FUSE, before all the titles and belts and everything else, there was just a kid from outside New Orleans who wanted to be a singer. He had been in a few bands, but because his father pushed him to wrestle, by the time he was sixteen he had little time to concentrate on anything else. But he never lost the passion to sing, and he never lost it as a coping mechanism either. So as he sits in the waiting room, he sings lowly to himself the only song he can think of, a song that is painful in its appropriateness. "And I rationed my breaths as I said to myself that I’d already taken too much today…" As he sings, the door slides open and Shweta Kallemullah walks in, looking around wildly. When she finds her wrestler, she walks towards him. "As each descending peak on the LCD took you a little farther away from me." "Hey, Jon," Shweta says, looking down on him with concern. He doesn’t look up. He’s too busy with the next verse. "Amongst the vending machines and year-old magazines in a place where we only say goodbye…" "Jon." She says, snapping in front of his face. He looks up, sees her, and looks back down. She taps his shoulder. "Jon, stop it. Look at me. Tell me what’s happening." He finally breaks the song, and when he looks up again it is with impatience. "We’re in a hospital. Take a guess." Shweta nods, then sits down next to him. "I’m so sorry, Jon. Was it an accident?" Jon, who still hasn’t gotten all of the sarcasm out of his system, rolls his eyes. "No. She set the house on fire on purpose. She thought it’d be funny." Again the former soldier of Desade nods. "Have you heard anything yet?" Jon shakes his head, and Shweta sighs. "Come on, Jon. Don’t close yourself off. It will be okay." He looks at her again and shakes his head. "Why do people insist on saying that?" Rather than get defensive at Jon’s continuous aggression, Shweta shrugs her shoulders. "Well, what are we supposed to say? I can’t just sit here and tell you it’s all going to turn out badly, can I? That’s not what a good manager tells her client." She thinks, then puts her arm around him. "It’s not what a good friend does either." "A good friend?" Jon asks, standing up. "A good friend doesn’t tell someone to break up with his girlfriend after a fight or two. A good friend doesn’t then come and offer sympathy for someone she clearly didn’t like. Why are you here, Shweta? Are you here to tell me to break into the burn ward and break up with her right now?" He looks down at her, breathing heavily. "Why are you here?" He asks again. She sighs. "I’m here for you, Jon. That’s why I’m here." After staring at her for a moment, he shakes his head. "Can’t take this. I’m going outside. Do me a favor and give me a few minutes alone, okay?" Without waiting for an answer, he grabs his hooded sweatshirt and puts it on, then walks through the automatic doors and steps out onto the ambulance entrance. Shweta sighs, bowing her head. Outside, Jon leans against the column for a moment before sliding against it to the floor. Putting his hood over his face, he continues what he started inside. "‘Cause there’s no comfort in the waiting room…" With the hoodie still on, he doesn’t see the door to the ICU open. He doesn’t see a tired doctor amble out. He doesn’t see Shweta stand up to meet him. "Just nervous pacers bracing for bad news…" He doesn’t see the short conversation that ends with Shweta bowing her head, then looking outside. "And then the nurse comes round and everyone lifts their heads…" He doesn’t see the hesitation as Shweta waits in the room, looking from Jon to the doctor. He doesn’t see her consider staying inside and giving him space. "But I'm thinking of what Sarah said…that love is watching someone die…" And he doesn’t see her ultimately make her decision and walk towards the automatic doors. "So who’s gonna watch you die?" A single tear escapes the hood as the doors zip open. "Jon?" All Figured Out (Reprise) You don’t have anything figured out.
