Christian Darke: Volume One

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-15 LVPD Detective Erick Matthews When I enter the room he is just sitting there; sitting with his eyes wide open, taking in his surroundings, staring at the empty white walls, the cold steel table in the middle of the room, the mirrored window on the wall. I catch his gaze almost immediately as I enter the room. The soft click of the metal door behind me assures me that for now it is just him and I. I stare back at him coldly, he hasn’t caused any problems since he’s been here, instead he’s been almost spacey. Almost as if he isn’t worried in the slightest about being here. Sitting there in the hard metal folding chair, he sits slightly slumped over, almost defeated and somewhat dejected. I walk to the middle of the room and place the large file folder; his case file, in the middle of the empty stainless steel table. I may have added a little extra emphasis on the end, a bit more of a slam than a place. I choose not to sit, not yet, I’m still trying to size him up, still trying to figure him out. His eyes never leave me as I pace around the small interrogation room. I give him the silent treatment to start. This works with the really weak ones. It’s a natural thing for human beings, we all hate silence. We’ll do just about anything, say just about anything all in the name of avoiding that awkward silence. Something tells me that he isn’t a weak one. Something tells me that this won’t be that easy. Sure enough after a minute or two, I lose the attention of his stare, and after a few more minutes of pacing behind him, he still hasn’t said one word. He sits as calm as ever in his chair, simply staring off at the wall; soaking in the lonely white of the cement walls. It almost makes me sick to my stomach to think about. If this guy is who his record says he is, he has left a wake of blood and bodies behind him. Yet sitting here today, his face is blank of anxiety, his body language empty of any emotion. Either this is the most cold-blooded, heartless bastard we’ve seen here in a long time, or he isn’t our man. I make my way back towards the table and pull out a small metal chair similar to his. There is an ear-piercing screech as the metal drags across the cement floor. I nearly have to cover my ears, but he doesn’t even flinch. He just stares straight ahead, those dark brown eyes of his taking in everything, but not budging. I take a seat across the table from him, but even still I sit in silence, examining him for a few minutes. His stare makes its way to meet mine and he looks innocent enough. The bags under his face tell the story of a man who is tired, of a man who is overwhelmed. And then in an instant everything in that stare changes. Maybe I’m crazy, maybe I’m seeing things. But his soft chestnut eyes, turn into a cold black stare, and then in an instant it’s back to how it was. I shake the thought from my head, probably just my mind playing tricks, trying to put together things that aren’t there. I’ve got to remain objective on this one. I reach my hand out towards the table, and drum my fingers on the surface of the table. Like I said, anything to break the awkward silence. Finally, I reach out and grab onto the file folder and begin to open the manila file folder up to reveal its contents. I think that I see a slight smile spread across his lips, believing that he has won this battle. And perhaps he has, however, it is a long war, and I am in control. I’ll show him that, I’ll show him who’s in charge. Of course, it could have been nothing, could just be my mind playing tricks on me again. “Well Mr. Cobaine, looks like you’ve been quite busy since you woke up,” I begin, all the while staring across the table, trying to gauge his reaction. Not necessarily to my words, but more to the crime scene photos that I am non-saliently thumbing through. He remains silent and offers only a mere shrug of his shoulders in response. He adjusts his black baseball cap, pulling a few loose strands of his black hair out of his face as he does so. He is dressed very casually, blue sweat pants and a black Under-Armour t-shirt, not exactly the look of a mafia hitman. Yet his body of work over the past week certainly seems to make him appear to be just that. “It’s only been a week since you woke up from that month long coma that you put yourself in, but you’ve been very busy. Look at everything you’ve accomplished since you woke up. All the death you’ve brought to this city.” I stare across the table, still hoping for a sign of recognition. What I’d really love is an admission of guilt right now, but something tells me I won’t get that. Hell, right now I can’t even get a reaction out of him. “Five known affiliates of a large organized crime syndicate, all of them found dead. All of them brutally beaten, tortured, mutilated and murdered. And then to top all of that off, a police captain, also found beaten and murdered. A POLICE CAPTAIN! The head of the gang taskforce no less. Seems like a bit of a coincidence doesn’t it?” Across the table I am still met by nothing but his empty stare. This time he doesn’t even bother to offer me a shrug, he simply looks at me blankly and then turns his attention back to staring at the wall. Clearly this isn’t going anywhere; looks like it might be time to play hardball. I grab the crime scene photographs, all of them horrible, grotesque, macabre images of pain and suffering. Carelessly I toss a handful of them across the table, they land right in front of him. His stare hardens as he looks at me, it’s almost like he’s waiting for something. I gesture towards the photos, scattered in front of him and he finally takes the cue and begins to inspect the images. I search his face, waiting for a reaction, waiting for some kind of recognition, but I don’t get one. After a few minutes of examining the pictures, he mutters his first phrase. “That’s pretty fucked up. Why are you showing me?” His arrogance is almost nauseating. The confident smirk on his face enrages me, but I do my best to remain calm. I can’t let him get the best of me. “Are you saying you don’t know these men? You don’t recognize their faces?” Again I am met by nothing but silence, a blank stare and a slight shrug of the shoulders. “Jonathan Davis... Sage Sabbott... Mani Shigeru... Jude Johnson a.k.a Silent Wind... Quartz Davis... all high-ranking members of the organized crime syndicate known as the Nevermore. All of them dead. We know you were connected to the Nevermore. We know all about your work for them, it’s all in the file. We have it all.” “What you don’t have his any circumstantial evidence,” his voice was harsh this time, confrontational and almost emotionless. He didn’t bother to elaborate, didn’t bother to add anything. He was keeping everything short and to the point. He knew how these things worked, he knew exactly what I was trying to do. He knew that I was trying to goad him into something, but he was doing everything right, he wouldn’t be tricked into saying something that would implicate him. Of course, I should have expected that. My background information tells me that he should be very familiar with these kinds of interrogations. “You’re right. We don’t have any evidence yet. But we do have is an extremely short list of suspects; namely you. And what I have is an extreme desire to bring whoever did this to justice. That police chief? He was like a father to me, and I am going to do everything in my power... EVERYTHING, to bring his killer to justice.” The smile was returning to his lips. I had let myself make this interrogation personal, I had let my emotions shine through. He knew he had me beat. He didn`t need words, he didn`t need a big show, the smirk on his face said everything I needed to hear. I win Matthews, you lose. I stand up and turn my back to him. Turning to leave. I do my best not to turn around and simply cold clock that cocky bastard before I leave, so instead I simply clench my fists and bite my lip as I stride towards the door. I give two loud hammer fist thuds onto the door. “It’s Matthews. I’m coming out.” There is another soft click on the other side of the metal door as the lock clicks itself off. I grab the handle and swing the door open. I turn to leave but he interrupts me. “When do I get my phone call?” the arrogance is almost radiating out in his voice now. I turn back and contemplate simply ignoring him. By all rights, in this case, I should give him a phone call, but he didn’t ask when he was read his rights, I wonder what’s brought about the sudden change of heart. Maybe he’s slipped up. His medical file indicated that he might be suffering from some short term memory loss from the effects of the coma, perhaps he’s still vulnerable because of it. “Who would you call? You have no one. No one to love, no one who cares if you live or die. You’re all alone Mr. Cobaine.” “I want my phone call. I want to talk to my fiancée, I want to talk to my son.” His voice is almost pleading now. For the first time, he’s shown some sign of emotion. For the first time, he’s shown a bit of weakness. “You’re fiancée is dead. So is your son. You killed them. Right before you tried to end your own life and put a bullet in your brain. Unfortunately, you missed, and it didn’t kill you, instead it put you into a month-long coma. You woke up last week. I know you remember.” I stare at his face, and this time I get a reaction. From him it’s the last thing I expected to see. His face washes over. Gone is the cold stare and the look of arrogance and invincibility. Now it’s been replaced by a look of helplessness and confusion. Clearly it’s something he doesn’t remember, or he’s been blocking out. Either way, his shoulders slump and defeat, and the look of confusion continues to grow on his face. I can tell that he is beginning to disbelieve, thinking that I’m only playing games with him. This last one should shatter him completely. If he didn’t remember or wasn’t sure, this would surely seal the deal. “I know you don’t believe me. You don’t have to. Check out the file. The pictures are in there.” With that I turn and exit the room, slamming the door behind me. And in the room I leave him, silent, defeated, confused. I take one last look through the mirrored window and watch as he reaches his hand out towards the file folder. As I walk away only one thought crosses my mind. I win, Cobaine. You lose. Unfortunately, you missed, and it didn’t kill you, instead it put you into a month-long coma. You woke up last week. I know you remember.” Melissa.... Cody.... Dead?! My shoulders slump and I feel my heart begin to sink. It can’t be true. How the hell could they be dead? Who would want to kill them? He’s lying... he has to be. He hasn’t been able to get anything out of me this whole time, now he’s making up stories, he’s trying to squeeze something out of me. It can’t be true... It can’t be. “I know you don’t believe me. You don’t have to. Check out the file. The pictures are in there,” his voice is flat and the look on his face shows a look of disgust as he turns and slams the door behind him. I don’t want to give into his games, I don’t want to fall for his tricks. But I have to know. I have to know that they’re okay. I have to know that my family is still safe. When I agreed to this case, I was assured that they would be under constant surveillance and protection. They swore to me that they would be safe! I can feel the anger rising up through my entire body. I can almost feel it as my face begins to redden slightly and it’s almost as if I can feel my blood boiling underneath my skin. I reach out towards the file folder on the table; I need to know. The folder is filled with crime scene photographs, surveillance photographs, psychological evaluations from my past life, and then inside the folder, there is a small envelope. The envelope is marked ‘Cobaine: Double Murder, Attempted Suicide.’ Slowly, I take the envelope out of the folder and place it in front of me. I deliberate for a few seconds, simply staring at the envelope, before I finally gather the courage to open it. Inside the envelope are dozens of crime scene photographs. The first few show no bodies, they simply show the area surrounding the crime scene. It appears as though they are from a large warehouse, somewhere near the outskirts of town, almost overlooking the desert. My mind begins to race, the warehouse is almost familiar to me. ”Follow us inside Christian,” a man’s voice says softly. “There’s only one last thing we need from you before your initiation is complete.” I look up at the massive warehouse. Its brick facade is dark and the warehouse almost has its own presence and personality as it stands there, the last sign of humanity’s touch before fading into the barren wasteland of the Nevada desert. Several of the windows have been broken and are boarded up. I’m not sure what they want me here for, but I’m sure I’ll soon find out. I shake my head, trying to shake away the thoughts. It’s been happening a lot lately, ever since I woke up in the hospital last week. There have been voices, there have been visions. I don’t know exactly how to explain it; it’s almost as if there is someone else living inside of me. An angrier version of myself, desperately struggling to take over. His voice is sadistic and full of rage, his visions are full of hatred and suffering, whatever this other presence is, I have to keep control of it. I continue to glance through photographs, there are a few more of the area surrounding the crime. Finally, I realize the grisly truth. There are several pictures with the bodies in them. The first, shows a beautiful black haired woman; my fiancée. At least she used to be beautiful, only a complete sadist could find any beauty in this image. Her clothes are ripped and torn away from her, her hair is matted with blood and dirt, her face covered in bruises and cuts. The look on her face triggers something, I can almost remember her calling out to me. Almost remember how helpless her voice sounds, as she begs me for help, as she pleads for her life. ”Aaron,” she manages to mutter in between sobs. “Please Aaron help me...” The tears are streaming down her face. Tears of pain, tears of suffering, tears of helplessness. Her clothes have been ripped away, she sits there in front of me almost completely naked, begging for my help, but I can only watch. I’m paralyzed, frozen in space. I don’t know what it is that’s holding me back, but something stops me from reaching out to her. A large shadow looms over her, and blocks out all of the light. I look up and stare at the monster of a man, this colossal giant. I stare into his eyes, his cold, piercing blue eyes. I see no regret, no remorse for everything that he’s done and everything he will do. Instead I see satisfaction, I see a hint of delight as he pulls something from behind his back. The metal gleams in the silver moonlight, the cannon in his hand raises up and points towards his target. “You were good bitch,” his voice thunders, echoing off of the walls even in a quiet whisper. “Made me feel all good, but now you have to die.” I hear her sob again. My Melissa. The sobs quickly turn into tears, I don’t know if it’s what’s happened that is causing her to break down, or the knowledge of what’s about to happen. Either way, she says nothing. She turns to me, her eyes still pleading for my help, her body trembling, her lip quivering. “I love...” BANG. The gunshot sends shockwaves reverberating through the entire abandoned warehouse. I look up at the monster, who allows a small smile to creep across his horrific face, before he holsters his weapon. He turns his back to me, and begins to walk away, whistling a soft song as he lumbers back into the darkness where he appeared from. I try to reach out to her. I try to hold her one last time. But I’m still trapped. Still helpless. All I can do is stare at her lifeless body. She still looks beautiful. She never deserved any of this. As I stare, I know this image will be burned into my mind, it’s something I’d rather forget, something I’d love to block out completely. But something tells me, this won’t be a night I’ll ever forget. I can almost feel the tears coming, feeling a rush of emotion washing over my entire body like a tidal wave. I do my best to choke down the emotions, to swallow the sadness. The memories... no the visions; they’re too vague to be called memories, they are so real. Everything about them, it’s so, gut-wrenchingly real. I do my best to shake the thoughts from my mind, to swallow my emotions. That’s when I hear the voice again. You know what they did to them. You know that’s why they deserved to die. I look around the small interrogation room and it’s almost like the empty white walls are beginning to close in around me. A quick scan of the room assures me that it is just me in the room, and the voice is definitely inside my head. Although my hands have started trembling slightly; an effect that is beginning to occur over my entire body, I am still clinging tightly to the crime scene photographs. I look down once again and debate whether or not I can even bear to continue. If I can face the grisly reality that might be shown to me on the next photograph. My curiosity overtakes my good judgement and I flip to the next picture. I instantly regret my decision. I nearly fall out of my seat at the sight of the image. My son... my only son... Cody, his face battered and bruised, transformed into a mix of black and blue. His small hands, his innocent hands are crossed over his crimson soaked t-shirt, blood oozing out of bullet holes in his three year old sternum. The image begins to register in my brain and my mind begins to wander. I struggle, doing my best not to relive this moment. If I have any memory of this, I never want to relive it, never again. But, I can’t subside it. Can’t stop it. He’s crying. He’s reaching out to me. He calls my name, yells for my help. “Daddy! Daddy! Help me! Please! I don’t want to go with this man!” I hear my own voice reply. I almost cringe at how helpless I sound. “Don’t worry Cody, just be strong. Daddy, will be waiting right here when you get back.” I struggle with the binds that keep me helpless and paralyzed. They say when a child is in danger, their parent can gain superhuman strength, can call upon the untapped human physical resources and accomplish great feats. I say that’s bullshit, because there is nothing I wouldn’t due to protect that little boy that’s getting lead away by that monster. And there is nothing I want to do more than break free and kill that bastard with my bare hands. He leads my son out of sight and for a few moments there is nothing but silence. My fiancée has already been taken from me, what is there worth living for if my son is taken from me too. The silence is broken by the harsh thuds of flesh against flesh and instantly I can hear him crying. Crying in pain. Crying out for help. I want to scream, but this time I can’t, I can only watch. Watch as the monster leads my son back into my view, watch as my son cries in pain clutching at his bruised and bloodied face. I see an all too familiar glint in the moonlight, the glint of metal against moon. I try to scream again. Wanting to do anything to help him, but there is nothing. There are several loud crashes, the gunshots echoing through the otherwise silent night. The crashes that my son would probably describe as thunder and wightning pierce through his body at over 1200 feet per second and drain the life from his body just as quickly. The look of mistrust and pain on my son’s face breaks my heart, and I try to look away. Instead something keeps my gaze fixated there. It won’t allow me to look away, won’t allow me to close my eyes, instead I’m forced to burn that image into my brain. Burn it so that I’ll never forget. I shake the thoughts from my mind, but nothing can stop the pain and emotion that washes over me now. I break down and I can feel the tears welling up in my eyes. Once again I can hear the voice in my head call out to me. Now do you see? Now do you see why they needed to die?! I scream out. Whether it’s out of pain, frustration or convalescence, I don’t really know. But for now it’s keeping the images and voices out of my head. I thrust myself to my feet and push myself away from the tables. I throw the photographs back onto the table, not wanting to look at them any longer. I continue to scream out and I’ve surely got the attention of the officers now. But I don’t care. I just want the voices to stop, I want the visions to stop. I just want to know exactly what is going on with me. I kick the steel chair back towards the table. It folds itself in and crashes against the steel table; the loud crack of metal on metal echoing throughout the room, before the chair crumples and crashes to the ground. I back away from the table, backing into the corner, I stare straight ahead. I don’t know what I’m doing, but right now, I’ll do just about anything to get out of this interrogation room. And with that, the lock on the door clicks and the door swings open.
