Father of Lies

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 He was not alone in the darkness. All around him there was movement, both subtle and exaggerated, the bold yet tentative deathmarch of an unseeing army moving inexorably toward a precipice they knew nothing of, and would not discover until it was too late. He could sense their stumbling steps and searching, outstretched arms, feel their doubt and trepidation. He could smell their fear and uncertainty. Some rushed headlong into the amorphous abyss, boasting loudly and wildly to mask their fear and doubt, while others hung back in silence, hoping the mere passage of time would somehow deliver them to victory. A select few cut fluidly through the throng, planning and scouting ahead, avoiding struggle where necessary but dealing swiftly and mercilessly with any obstacles that blocked their path. Fending off death as long as they possibly could and devising ways to best avoid it, they were completely oblivious to the futility of their efforts. They would be the last to die, but death would come for them as surely as the world would continue turning without their presence. Deville admired their vigilance and their tenacity, their refusal to go gently, but at the same time it offended him, violated him to his core. This was where he was born. This was his home, his sanctuary. The limbo between life and death was his domain, and no one danced it better. When these lost souls gathered, trying to discover his secret and usurp the power for themselves, Deville couldn't help but feel invaded. They were trying to steal what was rightfully his, to claim his kingdom as their own. They would fail, Deville knew, but it still irked him that so many would try. It galled him even more that he would have to wait to shatter their dreams, and that he wouldn't get to deal with each one personally. The best way to end a threat was to annihilate it from an angle it didn't expect, and that's exactly what Deville had in mind for those few unfortunate enough to stumble into his path. Striding purposefully through the void, Deville imagined he could see them struggling blindly through the syrupy blackness, edging ever closer to doom no matter which way they stepped, trying to fool themselves into believing they belonged in the darkness, that they could master it with time. The ignorant determination on their faces brought a smile to The Deville's lips and a twinkle to his eye. They thought they could conquer death by avoiding it. By ignoring it. They hoped that if they paid it no mind it would reciprocate the courtesy. Eventually they would realize their folly, and come to understand that death is inescapable. When they fought it, some would perceive with their dying breath that it could not be sidetracked or vanquished. It was a tireless hunter with no conscience or remorse, no intelligence or reason. It simply came and devoured, and all who opposed it were swallowed without remark or prejudice. It could not be stopped. None among the throng understood that, and so they would dash themselves against it like waves against a rocky shore, their imperceptible impact forgotten half a second after their appearance. Death could not be worn down, distracted, or defeated, and because of that, it would effortlessly consume all opposition. Every last man and woman huddling in the darkness feared its arrival and the battle they would inevitably lose. Deep down, they knew that as soon as it reared its ugly head, they were done, and they were more terrified of that -- everything coming to an end -- than the end itself. It was that terror that prevented them from conquering death. They loathed their own God-given limitations too much to explore beyond them. They feared their own end too much to ever embrace death, mistakenly believing the two phenomena interchangable. They were too afraid to welcome it and see what lay beyond. They would fight against death tooth-and-nail, and moments later when it devoured them, that would be the end. Shift. Reality tilted, rotating on its inconceivable axies. Deville hadn't moved or thought to move, yet he was suddenly elsewhere, as if the Universe had decided it was time for a change of scenery. The teeming masses of walking corpses were gone, yanked unceremoniously into oblivion, while the oppressive darkness loomed on as before, permeating everything with the stench of death if not decay. The black, however, was no longer absolute. Deville could make out a series of shimmering rectangles, like dominoes lined up for battle, beckoning him from not far off. Pallid silence rimmed with malice pushed in at him from all sides as he walked towards the glow, stuffing his head with panic and doubt, trying to turn him back. The air -- if it could be called air; it certainly didn't taste like air to Deville -- flickered and trembled, as if straining. A hum picked up deep within the nothingness, soft and low at first but gradually rising to a fever pitch. Deville stopped and bent over, both hands clamped down over his ears to defend against the head- splitting noise. If anything, it worsened. Sagging to his knees, Deville tried to wonder at where the sound was coming from, his ideas shattered by the siren in his mind. It was like the final screams of a thousand victims all joined in unision, caught and preserved at the acme of their terror. They bored at Deville, drilled into his heart and his soul, ripped at his thoughts and mind. He felt like he was being torn apart. "Stop!" The cry was out of impotent desperation and frustration more than anything, yet the wailing abruptly halted. Climbing unsteadily to his feet, panting with suspicious relief, Deville wondered if it had been a coincidence, if the sound had been in his mind all along, or if it had stopped because he ordered it to. Continuing on, he doubted he would ever know. Unburdened by gate or fence or any form of security beyond the pained cries of its inhabitants, the graveyard sat unmolested in its private nowhere. The rectangular tombstones were all of the same shape, cut, and size, and each glowed with a dim otherwordly light. Red inscriptions seemingly burned into the ethereal material wavered under The Deville's scrutinizing glare. Aside from the scripted name marking the upper edge of each tomb, every unpoetic elegy was identical: "Killed, defeated by Angelo Deville." Floating slowly through his private wasteland, Deville silently scanned the names as he moved along the single row of graves. Kimbusa was there, as was Frankie Bones. Xavier Kannon. Syko Arsonist. Skylar G. Ivan Stanislav. There were plenty of others nestled into their eternal slumber, but those ones stood out the most to Deville, filling his head with the sweet memory of victory, each allowing him to relive his dominance for a fleeting instant. The next tomb, the same as the others though coalescing perhaps a touch more vigorously, abducted all thoughts of triumph. Deville glared down at the name etched there, the unadulterated hatred in his azure eyes rivalled only by paramount respect. Six feet beneath him, undoubtedly turning over and over, lay the man who had made all of this possible for Angelo. It was an unintentional gift, a scheme that was meant to rob him instead of reward him, but the man had underestimated the resourcefulness of The Deville. He had believed himself more evil than The Deville, more at home in the darkness. He had thought to use that to his advantage, to rid himself of the only conceivable heir to his throne. In his unthinking greed he managed to steal a single narrow victory over Deville, foolishly exchanging it for his life and soul. Deville couldn't blame him for taking it to that extreme -- it was the only hope he had of victory, however fleeting -- but he did feel pity. The man had thought to secure himself the legend of immortality by robbing Deville of everything he had. When he realized it couldn't be done at all, he tried to convince himself it simply required unconventional measures, and set about the most devious scam he had ever schemed. In the end, to steal Deville's life, he had to forfeit his own, damning himself to an eternity of shame, loathing, and futility. With his limited foresight, he knew it was something that had to be done. He knew it was his only hope. It availed him that once, his thoughtless gift, but it gained him nothing, causing instead what he sought to prevent. "Alan Kriegman," the stone burned fiercely, angrily. "Killed, defeating Angelo Deville." Alan Kriegman. The man who had witlessly given Deville the gift of death. Wherever he was, Deville hoped Kriegman was suffering for what he had done. The grin stealing onto his face betrayed very well that he knew it was so. Stretching on down the line were a few just freshly covered graves, the black "dirt" bumpy instead of smooth like everywhere else, and beyond them lay a whole slew of waiting holes. Immediately before it all, right beside the tomb of Kriegman, was another tomb different from the rest. It was the same size and shape, and it had the same eery glow, but a line of purest dark zigzagged down its center, cutting it smoothly if crookedly in half. The "dirt" in front of it was strewn all about in wild piles, all pushed away from the centre, as if something from within that black wasteland had clawed its way out. The epitath blazed with something hotter and more sinister than its molten makeup. "Angelo Deville," it seared, "Defeated, killing Alan Kriegman." Shift. Reality spun sideways once more, finally disspelling the darkness. Deville blinked in the sudden light, squinted at the beautiful landscape, abounding with myriad orchards and gardens of exotic, sweetly scented flowers and fruits. There was no sun in the immaculate white sky, and little need for one; everything seemed to radiate its own brightness. Unseen birds sung happily, with each variance of note a little more intricate than the last, as if they were engaged in a competition to produce the most compelling sounds. Idly, Deville started to wonder which section of Hell, or limbo, or death, would house such a breathtaking spectacle, but before the thought was completely formed, it fled from the apparition now filling Deville's eyes. His breath caught. The man was as tall as Deville, though not quite as wide, and garbed in a spotless white suit of silk, perfectly fitted to hug and accentuate every subtle nuance of his body. For a moment, staring at the angular face carved from pride and mischief, set around two blue eyes sparkling like gems, Deville imagined he was seeing his clone, a perfect copy of himself. Then he saw that the man's hair was darker and winged with gray, and his face held a maturity Deville had never before seen in the mirror. Seeing Deville, the man smiled fondly, baring what could have been the pearly white gates of Heaven. "Dad?" Deville barely managed to choke out the word, his eyes welling up with tears. He took a hesitant step forward and reached out doubtfully, stretching for the father he hadn't seen since he was six years old. Stretching for the father he had never truly come to know. Need waged a vicious battle with disbelief within The Deville's head. "Angelo," his father intoned, his voice rich and mellifluous, a more distinguished version of his son's. His smile beamed as he came forward, legs unmoving, floating over the grass as if such a simple thing as walking were beneath him. Of all the luminous artifacts of beauty and splendor in the valley, Deville's father was by far the most brilliant. Angelo surged forward just before his father was upon him, crashing into him with the force of twenty years worth of lost embraces. His father's deep lilting laughter created a symphony with the birds as they hugged, each Deville seemingly trying to crush the other before they could be separated again. "Dad," Angelo whispered, nuzzling his face against the pristine white shoulder, as strong and comforting as he remembered it. He didn't know when he had started crying, but through the sheen he could see tears splashing onto his father's shoulder and being absorbed. He didn't even know why he was crying, except that he felt a surge of relief and belonging so strong, so comforting, and so foreign that he couldn't do anything else. From the age of six, he had come to terms with the fact that he would never see his father again, and he had spent his entire life trying to make up for it, as if it was his fault. Everything he had ever done was with his father at the forefront of his mind, always fretting over whether it was smart or clever enough, always worrying if it was what his father would do in his place. "Shhhh," his father cooed gently, slowly rubbing his hand up and down Deville's back, "it's alright." He had never come to know the man while they were both alive, but he had a very unshakable idea of who his father was, pieced together from his own memories, the journal he had stolen twenty years ago, and the stories he heard from various people in both their families. It didn't give him a complete history, but it gave him enough to speculate and create images. All he knew for certain was that his father was the greatest man who had ever lived, the pinnacle of what one should strive to become. Deville remembered it, people had told him, and it was plain from the inner thoughts pledged secretly to paper before he died. His entire life growing up, people had told Angelo he was the spitting image of him, physically and mentally. They would smile when they said it, a gleam of fond remembrance sparkling in their eye, then they would tell Angelo one of the many heroic adventures that seemed to pour from his father as easily as breath. Angelo would grin proudly, nodding excitedly for them to go on whenever their flare for dramatics made them pause, and inside, at a conscious level, his soul would rejoice at finding another marvelous facet of his father's life and personality. At a subconscious level, though, it all bogged Angelo down, saturating him with doubt and anxiety, crippling him with a desire to fill those shoes. Since he had been a boy, Angelo had been vainly trying to live up to the achievements of a God. The most frustrating part of it, for him, was that he believed it would have been a glorious cakewalk had his father been around to guide him. Instead, though, he died, and his mentoring duties fell to Salvatore, whom Deville learned later had been the one to kill his father, out of greed warped enough to make Machiaveli blush. Deville knew he could never be his father, or anything near, because of the taint of Salvatore raising him and a lack of true guidance, but for as long as he could remember that's all he ever strived for. To take his father's place and make things right. To make his father proud. When he saw his father standing there beaming at him, as radiant and perfect as Deville had crafted him in his mind, all that pressure and agony built up over so many years dissolved, leaving behind a scared six year old boy who wanted nothing more than to be lost in the powerful and protective embrace of his father. "It's okay, Angie." His father's hand rose comfortingly to the back of Angelo's head, burying itself in his mane. "I know. It's okay." Hearing his father tell him that it was okay should have set him on cloud nine, but it had the opposite effect. There he was, blubbering like a girl because he had made a mess of everything, because he had failed, and his father lied to him to make him feel good. It was not okay. Deville wished it was, but it wasn't. He knew he had let his father down. He jerked free, stomping on the tears so they'd stop coming and wiping away those that were already there, trying to pretend they never were. His father's eyes widened just a hair as Angelo backed away, his face contorted with misery and dereliction. "I-- I avenged you," Angelo muttered weakly, stammering for the first time in years. "I killed Salvatore." His father gave him a weak smile and nodded, his face beginning to reflect the sadness in Angelo's. "I know." "I'm sorry it took so long. For a long time I didn't know he had done it, and by the time I figured it out he was practically untouchable. It took planning, years of planning . . ." Angelo hung his head. "I should have done it sooner, the second I--" "Angelo." His father didn't shout, or make a face, or lunge forward to stop Angelo from rambling. He just said his name quietly, in a voice wrought of concern. "Never again imply, or take it into your head for even the idlest consideration, that I could ever be disappointed with you. You've done more than any man has a right to, and without a doubt more than I could have done. If anything, I should be apologizing to you, for dumping such a mess in your lap. I should have known it was coming, and stopped it." "But--" "Don't you dare tell me I'm wrong, either." Still he didn't raise his voice, but his tone had that fatherly quality of impatience, a timbre that stated quite plainly that he would brook no nonsense or argument. "I've had a long time to think about this, and the benefit of talking it over with someone who understands. If I had acted on my instincts about Salvatore, perhaps we . . ." Trailing off, his father stared absently at something inside himself -- or perhaps something beyond both himself and the place he was in -- before steeling his features and going on in a crisp tone. "Listen, Angelo. None of that matters now. You paid far too high a price for my revenge, an endeavour I now wish you would have abandoned altogether, and we need to deal with that." "Abandoned?" Deville blurted, disbelieving. "You think we should have let him get away with murdering you?" Even addressing his father, he was all scandal and outrage at the suggestion. "Yes, Angelo, you should have abandoned it. I know your motives were pure, and that you believed you had a right to Salvatore's life, but revenge, I've discovered, is not a solution to anything. All it does is add more problems to the mix. Evidenced by your presence here." Angelo couldn't help but sneer. "How is having power over life and death a problem? My presence here is an indication of strength, not weakness. I am unkillable, father." His eyes burned with a feverish, maniacal light. "There won't be a Salvatore to blindside me as he did you, because I've managed to move beyond the trivial issue of being killed. I can return to the land of the living whenever I please, no matter what happens." His father hadn't twiched an inch. "No, you can't." "Yes, I can." Angelo's father sighed. "If that is so, that you have power over life and death, why is your life not your own when you return?" Angelo eyed his father suspiciously, wondering how much he knew. "What do you mean?" "You know what I mean." They stared at one another for a long moment before his father clarified. "The voice in your head, Angelo. You need to get rid of it." "What do you think I'm here for right now? Satan's not going to kn--" "I know what you think you're doing here right now. That's why I'm here. To warn you." Angelo blinked, then shuffled his feet uncomfortably. His father affected not to notice. "Satan is more wily than you think, Angelo. True, you are a master of cunning and deceit" -- for the briefest instant Deville Sr.'s eyes twinkled -- "but you're up against the beast who invented the craft. He's been practicing forever, and time has no meaning for him. His only interest is pain and chaos, and he'll do whatever it takes to achieve it, however long it takes to achieve it. Demand a hundred forms of proof if he tells you the sky is blue, or that water is wet, because he'd change either one if it benefitted him or hurt you. He's always scheming, and even his scemes have swerves." Angelo smirked loudly. "He's as transparent as clear glass. All he does is try to order me around and drive me crazy, ranting about nothing and making perverse images cloud my mind." He paused, as if considering Satan's ulterior motives, then shook his head dismissively. "Believe me, father, I have this under control." His father was as soft and yielding as granite. "Believe me, son, you're in more trouble than you've ever been in before, and you're digging yourself deeper by the second. You need to get rid of the voice." He took a step toward Angelo, bringing them mere inches apart. "Before it becomes more than a voice." For a long moment Angelo was lost in his father's eyes. He could see timeless wisdom there, overflowing, trying to reach out to him and cocoon him in safety. He had never thought his father could be wrong before this, and more than anything he wanted to nod his agreement and have his father smiling once more. But his father was wrong. "I need Satan for the time being," he said matter-of-factly, making his voice cold. "If I can't put a stop to the voice here and now, at least I know I can put up with it." He ran a hand through his hair and rubbed it against his face. "I have to. The Devil is my only lease on life." "The Devil," his father said heatedly, "is nothing more than hype. He has no true power over life and death; only what you give to him to use. Any trust and faith put in him is trust and faith lost and destroyed." Slowly, his father began fading, along with the scenery around him. It shimmered, as if struggling to remain constant but being destroyed from somewhere else. As if it was being erased. "Save yourself, my son. Put your faith elsewhere, Angelo. "Please." Shift. The shimmering beauty was suddenly gone, as if another reality had been dropped over top of it and smothered it. All around him now great columns of flame licked up from a molten floor to snatch at a molten ceiling. He looked down and discovered he was standing in lava, or more accurately, on top of lava. Somehow, it didn't melt his body. He just stood on it as if on a floor of sturdy marble. The heat was overpowering, however, and in half a second Deville was soaked with sweat and panting. The air roasted his lungs with every breath he took in, and every exhalation threatened to explode into a fit of coughing he would never recover from. Strangely, no imps, demons, minor devils, damned souls, or anything else was about to learn the folly of tormenting him. It was only The Deville and the fire. And The Devil. Angelo hadn't seen him at first, camouflaged against the red inferno, but he saw him now, sitting on a throne of fire, lording over his domain with great majesty. His eyes, purest darkest red, the colour of spilled blood, were locked hungrily on Deville. That gaze paralyzed Angelo where he stood, struck him down. An icy hand gave Angelo's heart a squeeze, threatening to crush and shatter it at the same time. If he could have run right then, he undoubtedly would have. The Devil rose from his fiery chair slowly, leisurely, uncoiling to his full height with a sadistic grin. Flames danced around the outline of his feet, like water splashing up from a puddle, as he took careful steps toward Deville. Angelo struggled to remember what it was he had planned to say, what he had planned to do in order to bend Satan to his will. He had a plan for this, he just needed to find it and execute it. All that would form in his mind was fear, and accusations from his own inner voice that he was a coward. He didn't want to be a coward -- he would die before he became one -- but what could he do? His mind, his sense, had fled him as soon as he saw Satan, and now they weren't returning his desperate calls. He wondered helplessly what Satan would do to him, and unbidden, thoughts Satan had fed him before, when they had shared Deville's mind, ravaged him. It took all of his will and concentration not to fall on his face and grovel, to beg for forgiveness, for one last chance. He expected The Devil to burn him to ash where he stood, one nanometer at a time. "Angelo," Satan said instead, his voice thick with apology. "I'm sorry about the misunderstanding." Clasping his hands behind his back, Satan looked at the ground as if shamed. "We normally get on so well, I thought you'd welcome my counsel in your head. I never meant to drive you to the brink, or to make you take such drastic measures. Forgive me." For a moment Deville's mouth worked soundlessly, then finally he managed to articulate his thoughts with a profound "What?" Satan sighed, again apologetically. "It never dawned on me that you didn't want my guidance until you pulled that trigger. I thought you were playing around, trying to screw with me." He looked up at Deville hopefully. "It is something you'd do, you know. But until you actually killed yourself, subjecting yourself to the trauma of death once again, I didn't realize it truly bothered you." Deville wanted to tell him off, to tell him that he didn't believe and that it didn't make sense, but brushed that idea aside, knowing it would only make matters more confusing. That Satan was lying, twisting the truth as idly as a piece of taffy, went without saying. Deceit with the devil was a foregone conclusion. "Dying and returning to the land of life is too much of a strain on the both of us," Satan went on, "so let's try to avoid it if at all possible." "Stay out of my head," Deville interjected, surprised by the vehemence in his own voice, "and I won't have to blow you out." Frowning, Satan shook his head. "Unfortunately, my friend, that is impossible. Like I said before, the only way for you to return is to take me with you. But!" The word held a lot of emphasis, but Satan did it one better by thrusting a finger up in the air. "This time things will be different. I'll lay low and keep quiet, and let you go about your business. I'd have done it from the onset, had I known it was what you wanted. I only wanted to-- I'm sorry, Angelo. We can return as soon as you're ready." Deville eyed Satan suspiciously. He knew this wasn't right, but he knew just as well that he wouldn't get to the bottom of it standing around in the pit of doom, trying to coerce the truth from the father of lies. There was nothing for it but to move ahead and be prepared for whatever might come. Bracing to share his mind once more, to sacrifice his privacy for another taste of life, Deville nodded firmly. "I'm ready." He had no other choice.
