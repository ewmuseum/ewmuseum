IG 30 - John Covel: The Fire Burns

29 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Few people in this world can truly say that they give back to their community. Some people help with a car wash to help the local tee-ball league or make a sweet treat for a church bake sale, but nothing measures up to offering your life in addition to your time. That’s the life of a firefighter in small communities. The men and women that commit themselves to volunteer fire departments do not go un-appreciated in villages such as John Covel’s hometown of Mazon, Illinois. Some people wonder, “Why do it for free?” Why risk your own well-being for nothing? You are getting something out of it; pride. Being one of the brave individuals to step inside a burning building for a chance to save someone is more self-gratifying than anything in the world. John Covel knows that. He’s lived it. March 10th, 2007 3:39 P.M. CST Saturday mornings were glorious to John. It meant sleeping deep into the morning, never getting out of comfy clothes and normally working on his 1968 GT 500. That’s exactly what he was doing. Ever since he had found his baby in upstate New York three years ago, John had cherished that car. He had just shut the hood after an oil change when the black box on his belt came to life. Beeeeep-Beep-Beep-Beep-Beep-Beep “Mazon Fire Department, Morris is requesting mutual aid for their structure fire at 3900 Industrial Park, Thorton Industries. Respond with two squads, an engine and a tanker. Once again, Mazon Fire Department, mutual aid to Morris at Thorton Industries with an engine, a tanker and two squads. Time out, fifteen thirty-nine hours.” In a split second John darted through the side door to his garage and hopped into his car. His dash lit up with a rotating blue light as the engine roared to life. A mile outside of town, John sped from his driveway, reaching for the radio sitting on the front seat. He switched it on as the car’s tires squealed onto the pavement of the road leading into town. “South dispatch from thirty-four-oh-one; Page received.” A familiar voice exited the radio. John’s father had been chief of the department for as long as John could remember. Cars pulled to the side of the road as John kept the accelerator at the floor, racing ahead at close to seventy miles per hour. With speed like that, a mile disappears quickly and before he could think, John was rounding a corner and pulling up to the station. A few others were already running into the station. John stepped out and began trotting towards one of the opening bay doors. As he stepped inside, one of the other firefighter’s voices became audible. “This thing is HUGE. I saw it on my way home from work.” He shouted. John reached his locker and kicked off his shoes as he grabbed his bunker pants and slipped them on. The four men already dressed headed for the engine as John grabbed his coat and helmet before he followed. Butterflies filled his stomach as he climbed into the back section of the crew cab, but it was normal. Five of the six seats were filled in the brand-new engine; all they needed was their driver. It wasn’t long before he had climbed in and pulled the engine from the station. “South Dispatch from thirty-four twelve; put us en route with six.” The driver said into the microphone. John reached up and grabbed a hold of the wire connected to the roof. A tug on it sent the booming sound of the air horn echoing against the homes at the main intersection in town. John settled into a comfortable position still grasping the cord. It was his job to blow the horn whenever needed as they trekked the ten miles north. March 10th, 2007 3:59 P.M. CST Whoooooosh The air brakes sighed as the engine came to halt just a few hundred feet from the blaze. All four doors shot open and the men began piling out. Sixty pounds of steel are now suspended on each man’s back. They gather around the back of the truck, getting their air masks hooked into the tanks as the driver rushes to command for assignment. The five men begin laughing as they look to the towering flames. “Anybody got some marshmallows?” One of the men joked. It was Harold, John’s brother-in-law. As serious of a job as fire fighting was, there had to be a tension breaker. If left alone in silence, fire fighters would begin to think about all the bad things that could happen. “Damn, you can feel the heat from here.” Rang the chief’s voice. John’s dad sauntered up to the men taking a puff from his cigarette. He flicked it away as the driver trotted back up with another officer in tow. John sighed as he cracked his knuckles, listening to the assignments. When ever he went on mutual aid calls he hoped for front line. It wasn’t very fun to stay back and fill air tanks or make water runs. He was a fire fighter; he wanted to be INSIDE the fire. “You three take some two and a half and head in through that service door right there. We’ve got it knocked down enough on this side that we can start heading in to attack the center. Just be DAMN careful, we’ve had…” KA-BOOOOOM The commanding officer’s voice was cut off by a booming explosion that rockets a handful of fifty-five gallon barrels high into the air, followed by a billowing mushroom cloud of fire. “…explosions almost non-stop. Be safe. You three, come with me.” He finished. John was ecstatic at the assignment. In no more than five minutes, he’d be twenty feet into the warehouse, staring at a wall of flame that stretched from floor to ceiling. Crazy? Yeah you could call him that. He sprang into action, grabbing the end of hose hanging from the back of the truck. He began to run forward, draping the hose over his shoulder. “Grab the twenty-five hundred PSI nozzle!” Harold barked as he grabbed onto the hose. Mark, the other man, rushed back to the truck as his colleagues pushed forward, pulling the second length of hose from the truck. By the time Mark rejoined them, they were only fifty feet from the door. Mark and John connected the nozzle to the hose quickly. KA-BOOOOOOM Another explosion rocked the building, shaking the ground and jarring the nozzle from their hands. They caught themselves before falling over and changed gears, prepping themselves for action. Each of them tightened the air mask over their faces and took one final deep breath of impure Illinois air before locking their regulator into place. Pure oxygen began coursing its way through their lungs as they pulled the Nomax hoods over their masks. With every inch of skin covered by fire-retardant material they place their helmets on their heads and head towards the building. John advanced to the door, side-stepping long enough for Mark to bust it open with an axe. John opened the nozzle as he stepped inside, releasing two hundred pounds of water pressure onto the blaze. “Left!” Came the muffled yell from Harold. John looked left and targeted a burning barrel no more than five feet from the men. The dancing flame dissipated and fully disappeared in seconds allowing John to focus on the orange mass in front of him. As the water rained down on the hell-fire the three men slowly advanced further. John’s heart was pounding. There he was, staring fifteen hundred degrees of evil right in the face. The water’s force began to knock completely charred walls to the ground, providing a surge of oxygen for the fire to breathe in. It spit its own version of Carbon Dioxide out in the form of small flame clouds momentarily. “Up!” John barely heard the voice of Mark, three feet behind him. The fire crackled loudly as it ate up wooden crates containing more barrels. John looked above his head and spotted the flames advancing back towards their escape route. The water was soon soaking the men as it shot straight into the air. They fought to back up on their knees as quickly as they could to catch up with the mindless beast. After a near three minute struggle, the flame above their heads died. A smirk hidden by his mask appeared on John’s face as he crawled his way forward, blasting near-by objects with water. Just as John settled into a somewhat comfortable position penetrating the orange wall in front of him, a tap came on his shoulder. He turned and saw Harold pointing up a staircase. “Office! We should go check it!” He shouted. John nodded and turned to his right, spraying the metal staircase with water. Steam began to mix with the toxic smoke as the metal cooled at an alarming rate. Just as John planted his foot to get to his feet, he felt the ground begin to shake. KA-BOOOOOM Another barrel ignited across the warehouse and shot straight into the air, through the roof and up. John took a deep breath and began making his way up the stairs, spraying the area in front of him. A loud bang signified the graceful landing of the aforementioned barrel on the metal roof. It startled all three men enough to slow their forward movement. Chuckles could be heard from Harold and Mark as they took a few more steps up-wards. The heat was unbelievable. It was like nothing John had ever experienced. The heavily insulated fire gear created enough body heat of its own, but sweat poured down John’s face with the addition of the flames. The three advanced three more steps, finally reaching the cat-walk like second level. John peered across the structure, looking for the best route to the office. “Left or right?!” He shouted. He knew it was his decision as the nozzle man, but it didn’t hurt to ask for advice did it? He surveyed his surroundings for a second, still showering the blaze with water. After a minute’s thought he motioned left and began advancing. They crawled across the mesh walkway, inching their way towards the office. KA-BOOOOOOM Another barrel ignited. This time it didn’t get enough thrust to penetrate the roof. Instead it began to ricochet off anything it touched. John halted Harold and Mark, watching the barrel fly right underneath them and crash to the ground. Just a part of the job John thought. Now over the center of the warehouse, the heat intensified. Fifteen feet below them was a mass cardboard boxes and wooden crates incinerating in the heat. John saw another hose unit attacking the demon from the ground, so he ignored it. Suddenly, the water ceased to exist and the hose team retreated. Confused, he turned to look towards his Lieutenant for answers. A shrug of the shoulders was all he got. “ALL UNITS EVACUATE THE BUILDING. 5000 GALLON LP TANKS HAVE BEEN LOCATED NEAR THE CENTER OF THE BUILDING, ALMOST FULLY ENGULFED. EXPLOSION IS IMMINENT.” John sighed as he heard the radio transmission and looked a few feet further. He saw the tanks being gobbled up by the deadliest of Earth’s natural elements. He kept a hold of the hose line as he pushed past the others, set to lead them back out. They moved as quickly as they could safely, clearing the area in front of them with a barrage of water. Now John was faced with another decision. Left or right? The left was clear of debris, but his hose went to the right. “Never abandon your water line. It’s your life-line out.” He thought to himself. He nodded and took a small crawl forward. Suddenly, time began to slow down. The area around John began to darken. Suddenly a bright light appeared, but it wasn’t the luminosity from an explosion… FLASH March 10th, 2007 2:23 A.M. CST Just a few feet from the steps, John heard a hissing and felt the metal begin shaking. KA-BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM One of the tanks had gone. The explosion rocked the metal structure as flames rose up to engulf the exact spot they occupied just minutes before. The heat rose to nearly three thousand degrees, weakening the thin steel walk. It began to buckle and within a few seconds it collapsed. John grabbed the railing of the staircase to hold himself but found he was still laying on a flat surface. He sighed and slowly let go of the railing, making sure it was steady enough to move on. After he was sure he was safe, he looked behind him for Mark and Harold, but saw nothing. He quickly scanned the area in front of him as he crawled forward. Finally he spotted a hand clenching the grated floor to the walk. He scrambled forward to the hand and peered over the edge, spotting his two partners dangling over a mass of flames that danced at the bottom of Mark’s feet. “Are you guys hurt?” John inquired. They both responded they were fine, but losing strength. John knew he had to act quick, so he reached out. He began to panic as he couldn’t find a way to help Mark get back to safety. Grunts escaped from Harold’s mouth as he tightened his biceps and pulled with everything he had to lift the two hundred fifty pound fire fighter up. Finally able to reach him, John grabbed Mark’s outstretched hands and pulled. After fighting exhaustion for twenty seconds, Mark was finally able to swing a leg up onto the walk, pulling himself the rest of the way up. Both men turned back to Harold, now grasping the grating with both hands. The fire below began to billow higher and higher as John and Mark each grabbed one of Harold’s arms. Just as they prepared to pull, the structure began to shake again… KA-BOOOOOOOOOOOM The second LP tank lit up with thunderous force. It hurtled itself into the air, striking the crippled structure and jarring the three men. The two safe men did all they could to hang onto Harold’s hands as they fell, but it was too much. His glove began to slip off as John struggled to regain proper position. It was no use. Harold’s hand fell from the glove and he plummeted fifteen feet down, landing in a mass of burning barrels. John gasped as he sprang awake, sweat dripping from every inch of his body. The nightmare he had just experienced slowly began to fade from his present memory, allowing him to calm himself and lay back down. Hopefully he’d never remember that. Actually, it’s a good thing he did. FLASH March 10th, 2007 4:30 P.M. CST Time regained momentum as the flashback ended. It was then John knew he had to go against his training and instincts. He dropped the hose nozzle and began crawling left, the opposite way he had gone in the dream the night before. “What are you doing?!” Harold shouted. “Just trust me!” John answered. They crawled forward a few more feet before the structure began to shake. The same hissing noise John had heard the night before rang out again. He knew it was coming. He halted the men and covered himself. KA-BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM The tank erupted, jarring the structure like it did the night before. John looked to his right and saw the walk-way collapse just shy of the staircase where they would have been. A sigh of relief comes from the lungs of John Covel. The three men climb to their feet and begin racing across the walk, completely ignoring the standard policy crawl. The relief that had over come John was short-lived as he felt a rumbled under his feet just a few yards from their escape staircase. His feet sank further into the melting metal with each step. He lunged forward, flying through the air and grabbing onto the railing as the structure gave way. He began to panic before the rumbling had stopped, looking around for his comrades. He was happy to see Mark lying at his feet, getting to his knees. But where was Harold? John watched as Mark turned around and reached into a void in the walk. It had happened again. John rushed forwards and saw his brother-in-law dangling from a piece of railing a few feet below. John’s heart rate sky-rocketed as he realized he was living the nightmare. “What do we do? Mark asked. John wasn’t sure. He knew there was no protocol for situations like this, so he had to improvise. After ten seconds that seemed like an eternity, he scooted past mark and laid on his belly at the edge. "Support our weight!” He yelled. Mark nodded and followed suit, lying on his belly and wrapping his legs around the railing. He grabbed hold of John’s legs, allowing John to crawl over the edge and hang. He looked at his brother-in-law and reached out, asking for his hands. John had never seen fear in Harold’s eyes, but at that moment, it coursed throughout his whole body. Harold released his grasp on the railing and grabbed one of John’s out-stretched hands. KA-BOOM Another barrel below them ignited and shot up into the air, colliding with the railing before Harold could prepare himself. The railing was peeled away, leaving him dangling from just John’s right hand. “Just let me go, John.” Harold muttered. John felt the grip of Harold’s hand loosening. He wasn’t going to lose him. John clenched the hand even tighter and motioned for Harold’s other. Harold sighed and pushed through his exhaustion, pulling his arm up and grabbing John’s free hand. “PULL US UP!” John yelled. Slowly, the two dangling men began to rise up, inching their way back up to the walk. After an excruciating minute passed, John felt his knees back on solid ground, allowing him to gain leverage to pull with all his strength. Mark approached once John was stable and grabbed Harold’s air pack, yanking him the final two feet up. As the Lieutenants body rolled away from the edge, they all collapsed to their backs, thankful the ordeal was over. After only a few seconds rest, they got to their feet and proceeded down the stairs and out the door. Just as they exited, all three of their air warning alarms went off, signaling the emptiness of the bottles. John began to unhook his gear as he walked away from the blaze, towards their engine. He sat on the back of the truck and tossed his mask to the ground as he felt the ground rumble once more. KA-BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM It was gone. Each wall of the building fell inward in rapid succession. They had emerged just in time. John buried his head in his hands for a moment before he felt a hand come to rest on his shoulder. He looked up and spotted his brother-in-law standing there, smiling. John got to his feet and extended his arms. Harold accepted, embracing him with a hug. That was the brotherhood of the fire service. That was what it felt like to give back to your community.
