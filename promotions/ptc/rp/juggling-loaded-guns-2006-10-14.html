Juggling Loaded Guns

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-14 What do you do when you’re staring down the barrel of a loaded gun, being told "Do it, or I’ll pull the fucking trigger"? "This is bullshit!" "No, it’s plain English… right here in your contract." Marc Avit shoved the stack of papers across the cheap laminated desk top. Several of the sheets slipped away, swirling around and floating to the floor… but the important page, the one that mattered… found its place in front of Matt’s face at the tips of his fingers. "How did this happen?" "I was hoping you could tell me, Matt." Marc leaned forward from his tattered leather chair, the smokey grey of his shirt stretching as a patch of it stuck to the duct tape across one of the longer gashes. "Tell you? You’re my agent. You’re supposed to know what the hell my contract contains. So you tell me why I have to wrestle in PTC’s GTT?" The volume of his voice increased with every syllable of his last sentence. "Because if you don’t, your contract with PRIME will be terminated and you’ll be fair game for a lawsuit… breech of contract. Do you understand what that means?" "Yeah." From somewhere in his lungs, a long sigh rose through his body, saturating in frustration along the way, escaping his lips in depressing form. He had been hoping for an answer as to how and why the clause had manifested itself in the contract… not what penalties accompanied any sort of ‘non-compliance’ as the official wording labeled it. Sometimes, Marc was a lousy agent. But, he was an entertaining personality and his service came at a fair price. ‘Cheap’, as the un-official wording labeled it. "This had to be Nelson’s doing. He had to have slipped it in their when he took over PRIME and I became the number one thorn in his side." "It makes sense. You never exactly hid your desire to work a limited schedule. By adding in a GTT competition clause, he had the perfect opportunity to get rid of you. Probably assumed you would refuse and he could fire you." While he rambled on with his thoughts, Marc moved his hands about as if her were some annoyed junior high teacher bothered by having to re-explain some simple concept to a ‘stupid’ class. The mannerisms didn’t do much to help cool down his increasingly enraged client, but he continued oblivious to the fact. "Worst case scenario, you’d agree and he could at least get you out of his hair for a couple months while you focused on the tournament." "How did you let this happen, Marc?" "Me? Might I suggest that if you want to avoid this sort of thing in the future, you not intentionally butt heads with the man who signs your paychecks. For God’s sake, the moment he became CEO of PRIME, you declared yourself ‘The Anti-PRIME’. You interfered in his matches, haunted him in the Dual Halo, and just generally vowed to make his life hell. I’m afraid this isn’t my fault." Marc kept his composure in contrast of Matt. He gathered some of the pieces of paper that had sprawled across the desk earlier, pushed them into a tidy stack, and folded his hands across them. "Of course, that’s all assuming Tyler Nelson is behind this. It’s quite possible he’s not. How and why its there are rather insignificant. The bottom line… it’s there." "Mary’s not gonna like this." The words hung in the air, completely lacking the weight they deserved. Matt ran his fingers through his hair, tugging a bit as he reached the back of his head. ‘Not gonna like this’ was more accurately ‘hate this with every fiber of her being.’ It was hard to pinpoint the exact reason that she would be furious… probably because it was actually a multitude of reasons… but there was no doubt that her reaction would be a disheartening mixture of seething anger and disappointment. Two and half years ago, when he had held her hand as they’d slushed through the heavy soaked sand on the beach in front of their vacation home, he had promised her that he would retire, trade in his life as a wrestler for one as husband and father. For over a year, he held true to that promise the same way he had held her and kissed her forehead and convinced her all his words were honest. But when PRIME had laid an offer on the table for him in September of 2005, everything he’d promised shattered into jagged pieces and cut them both. For the better part of the last year, he’d been strange and estranged from his wife. Eight, nine, ten thousand months had past with them not speaking. He wasn’t sure exactly how long the silence had lasted… only that it had lasted. In early August, just days before he had captured the Universal Championship from Killean Sirrajin at PRIME’s Colossus III event, an apology had escaped his lips… an apology that Mary must have considered long over-due. The end result was some sort of shade of grey. They had moved back in together. They talked at dinner, laughed during lousy movies curled up on the couch, but there was still a discomforting silence every time he packed a bag and hit the road for a PRIME show. The truth was, they were fighting to find some middle ground. Mary still didn’t want him wrestling, in the perfect world, but perfection wasn’t something to be attained; it existed solely as a thought to be struggled towards. So, she accepted the next best thing. She smiled and said, "Ok, wrestle" and he smiled and said, "Ok, no more house shows". Compromise, it seemed, was a suitable replacement for perfection. "Matt," Marc’s words snapped his client back from the state of zoned-out thought. "Think about this thing. It’s the perfect opportunity. All the rumbling, all the grumbling, all the rumors said you weren’t interested in PTC. Now’s your chance to go out there with the best of the best." "I don’t need to make a name for myself, Marc." He shifted his wait in his chair, then immediately did so again, not finding that spot of comfort. "I’ve spent the last five years working my ass off to make sure…" "I know… you’ve won multiple world titles, wrestled on national TV week in and week out… I’m not talking about that stuff, Matt. Look at it like college sports or something. You’re king of your conference, an incredibly talented and difficult conference, but this is your chance to take on the greatest from every other conference. You see what I’m saying?" "Yeah, I understand both the concept of college athletics and metaphors." Matt glanced down at the solitary sheet in front of him. Yet again, he read the stipulation in his contract that was causing this nightmare. "Marc, are you sure there’s no way out of this, no way around this?" "It’s my job to know. And I can safely say, that there’s no way over, under or around, and the only way to get out of it, is to violate your contract and suffer the penalties. Honestly, Matt, this is your chance to get out there and put it all on the line, and maybe walk away a bonafide living legend." Marc leaned back in his ratty chair and held out his hands with a shrug of his wide shoulders. "What’s the big deal?" "My family hangs in the god damned balance!" In a sudden eruption, Matt leapt out of his chair and threw his hands apart, shoving the contract off the desk. His left hand flew uncontrolled into the small lamp on the corner of the table, smashing it on impact, breaking it into jagged pieces that cut him. "I just managed, by god know’s what luck, to patch things up with Mary and work out an agreement about my wrestling, and just when it seems things were settling down, this shit levels me out of nowhere. And now, I have to go home and try to explain this to my wife and sit there and watch when she probably walks out on my ass again! THAT is a big deal, Marc!" There was a moment of nothingness. Matt stood over the desk, the slow drip of blood rolling down the crevices of his left hand the only sound in the room. Marc sat in a bit of shock, too afraid to speak, not that he knew what to say in such a situation anyway. The crimson puddle that formed on the corner of the desk beneath Matt’s palm grew steadily larger. Marc watched it with a hypnotic gaze. "It’s a big deal to me." It was all Matt said, and though it fixed the awkward silence, it did nothing to help Marc find the right words. "It’s going on three o’clock. I’m starving. I’m gonna grab a late lunch." Marc pushed himself out of the chair and grabbed his jacket from the coat rack behind him. "You know your way out." The agent headed across the office, disappearing into the small lobby area outside the doorway. Matt took one last look around at the mess he had made. He kicked the contract on the carpet, then stared at the shattered glass on the desk. With a grit of the teeth, he began to pick up each piece, but paused. Changing his mind, he dropped his forearm across the desk and brushed the pieces off the side, letting them fall to trashcan below. What do you do when you’re staring down the barrel of a loaded gun, being told "Do it, or I’ll pull the fucking trigger"? You act quick, knock away the gun if you’re brave enough. If you’re crazy, you can make a distraction, turn and run, hope their aim is off. But most people aren’t that brave, and most people are only that crazy till they’re face to face with death… then… not so much. So what do you do with someone pointing a gun in your face? You do whatever the hell they tell you to do… …unless someone’s pointing a gun at the back of your head telling you not to. The heavy stitched corners of the curtains tip-toed across the soft blue walls courtesy of an Ohio autumn breeze. Matt stood looking out the open window, noticed that the leaves were falling from the tree in the backyard, and rubbed his neck in exhaustion. The only thing that kept his head from sinking was the smell of spaghetti sauce that danced through the air. He was battling when to break the news to Mary. There was a part of him that wanted to get it off his chest as soon as possible, and it would have likely been the easy decision had it not turned out Mary had invited her parents over for dinner. "Matthew, close the window." Despite her words, she took the action into her own hands, slipping past him and pulling down the pane of glass. "I told you I don’t think Georgie has been feeling well. Do you want to give your baby girl a cold?" In typical over-protective mother form, Mary walked over to the crib and pulled the blanket up around Georgie, tucking it beneath her chin. "Sorry." "It’s ok. Just remember… Georgie is one… she can’t tell you she’s cold or get up and close the window herself." "I know." It was a silly conversation. One that didn’t need to take place. Those kinds of conversations had been popping up more often between the two. In a way, both were still adjusting. Being together didn’t seem like something that was hard to do, until they were apart for nine months. "Mary, there’s somethin’ we should probably talk about." "Is this gonna take awhile? I’ve got some garlic bread I need to be pulling out of the oven in just a minute." "I’m hoping it’ll all be over soon." "Matthew," She pulled herself away from Georgie, turning her attention fully to her husband for the first time since she had entered the room, "what’s going on?" "I was talking with Marc today and he found something in my contract that you’re not gonna like." "What are you talking about? You’re not making any sense, sweetheart." "Somehow, I dunno what happened, Marc doesn’t know what happened… but somehow, there’s this clause in my contract that says I have to compete in PTC’s upcoming GTT event." Now he waited for the response that he’d already played through in his mind a hundred times. "What?!" "Yeah…" "No way. I don’t want you to do this. No." "If I don’t, I could lose my job with PRIME…" "Would that really be such a terrible thing?" The look she shot his way let him know that his answer wasn’t necessary, it was included in her question. "… and I could be sued for breech of contrast. Could spend months wrapped up in legal battles, continually draining money. I mean, I don’t want to do this, I ju…" "Then don’t do it, Matt." "It’s not that simple. I told you, God only knows how much it could cost us financially if I don’t compete." He made his way over towards her, but she was already rounding the crib, making an exit from the room. She had a dinner to finish, and garlic bread wouldn’t wait for the same old nonsense. "We’d be fine." She fired him a series of looks. He didn’t catch them all, but the important one… the one that mattered… found its place in his eyes. He saw the hurt. "We’d be fine and you know that." Mary was out the door before the words were finished. They met his ears with a harsh sting, an unpleasant contrast to the aroma of Italian sauces that filled his other senses. Alone in the room, he looked around for no reason, then kissed his fingertips and softly touched them against the forehead of his baby girl. From the rest of the house, he could hear conversation of a far nicer variety filling the kitchen and dining room areas. He headed in the opposite direction, making his way to the den. In hapless form Matt fell into the leather couch, allowing it to swallow him whole. As he sat there, he occupied his time till dinner hating Tyler Nelson, hating Marc Avit, hating his contract, hating himself, the fans, Mary, GTT… whatever came to mind. "Am I interrupting something?" "No." Looking up through his self-inflicted emotional haze, Matt saw his father-in-law standing in the doorway of the den. "Just taking a minute to loathe everything capable of being loathed." "I would assume this has something to do with what my daughter just finished telling me all about." He motioned towards the sofa. "Scoot, son." "Sure thing." Without hesitation, Matt lifted his ass and pushed himself over some, clearing a spot to his left. He even did so with a brief laugh. Something in George’s voice just encouraged it. He remembered back to the night Georgie had been born. The two had gone out for a bite to eat while Mary’s mother had stayed at the hospital with her and the baby. While munching away on some fries, George had winked and said "You’ll always be ok in my book, kid. Afterall, ya did agree to name your first born daughter after an ol’ man like me." Since then, Matt had always felt at ease around George, despite the firm, straight face he always sported. "You know…my daughter loves you very much." "Yeah, I know. She probably shouldn’t." "No, she should. When she went off to college and developed a dangerous drinking habit, you stuck by her. Through all the lies, through all the drama, through a trip to the emergency room at two o’clock in the morning… you stuck by her. And when she quit her job out of nowhere to try and write a novel… you gave her your undying support. So why shouldn’t she stick with you now?" "Because, she made things right in those situations." Matt rubbed his eyes and let his head drop. As easy as it was for him to talk to his father-in-law, he was finding it hard to look him in the eyes. "And you’ll make things right too." George gave his son-in-law a hard pat on the shoulder. Matt knew well enough to know it was his form of embrace. "Matthew, do you remember that time Mary threw out her back and was laid up in bed for a couple days, and then she came down with a cold. One afternoon she asked you to bring in a movie for her to watch, and a glass of orange juice… and her favorite book, and a box of tissues. And you, trying to be the superhero husband, gathered her laundry list of requests into your hands and tried to get everything into the bedroom in one spectacular trip." "And I dropped it all and ruined her book as it pages marinated in juice, and every time she blew her knows for the next two days, she said it smelt like oranges." "Hahaha, that’s what I’m talking about." "So what about it?" "You may throw a helluva punch, son…" George turned his head in Matt’s direction and the movement forced his son-in-law to lift his gaze. "but you can’t juggle, even though it might be convenient." Several seconds past by while he digested the comment before it occurred to him to ask what it was he should do. Juggling and failing seemed the only option. But, before he could form any question, their conversation was brought to a hault. "Matthew, grab you car keys. It’s Georgie… she’s really hot. Something’s wrong." Following his daughter as she bolted out of the room, George jumped from the couch, his old knees springing to life with the venom of a college linebacker. Matt did the same, lacking the same youthful burst, but charging as quickly as he could into the bedroom. With a quick look around, Matt spotted his keys on the nightstand and snatched them up. He didn’t wait for anyone around him, just made his way straight out to the driveway and into the car. A turn of the key sent the engine churning, giving it life just as he saw his in-laws come bounding out the front door, Mary just a step behind them with Georgie in her arms. He clenched his fists, grinding into the steering wheel with his palms in fear and anger. His daughter was sick and so was he. So what do you do when you’re staring down the barrel of a loaded gun, being told "do it, or I’ll pull the fucking trigger", while someone stands behind you, burying cold steel in the back of your skull, crying, "don’t do it, or I’ll shoot you." What do you do? You catch a bullet with your brain.
