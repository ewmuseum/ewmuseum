Date: 2006-11-11 The Horn: "Policeman’s Brawl?" Posted @ 10:56am by Daniel Pena October 13th 2006 ProWrestlingBugle.com Reports have come in this morning suggesting that GCW superstar Jacob McKail was attacked by a police officer yesterday whilst awaiting arrangement of mandatory psychiatric counselling. Yesterday morning, Pro Wrestling Bugle reported that the superstar was ‘taken into custody’ by the police after witnesses claimed that Jacob McKail had ‘lost the plot.’ It appears that a few hours later, a police officer cornered the GCW superstar and physically assaulted him. Sources indicate that McKail ‘didn’t put up a fight’ and ‘the aggression was all one way.’ ‘We regret to confirm the rumoured acts of violence against one Mr. Jacob McKail, are indeed accurate,’ the official police report stated. ‘We wish to extend our apologies to Mr. McKail, however, our initial judgement of his mental state remains firm. Therefore, we still insist he is remanded to counselling for the time being.’ Jacob McKail has declined to comment on the matter. Read More >>> ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Companionship. On the Road. Approaching Houston, Texas. Two Weeks Ago. "So where did you get the bruise?" McKail took his eyes off the road for a second and watched Kate track purple-blue line across his neck with her index finger. McKail sighed. "It’s a long story." He didn’t want to think about that damned police precinct again, never mind talk about it. What happened in that cell will stay with him until his grave and that’s what he told the other police officers during the investigation too. But he saw the eagerness in her eyes for the story to be told and realise then such a dismissive comment wasn‘t going to dissuade her in the least. So he added, "You don’t want to know." Truth be told, it probably made things worse. McKail was somewhat surprised that Kate was still lying next to him come the morning. When he awoke, her arms and legs were wrapped around him as though her life depended on it, and for the first few moments of consciousness, before his memories came flooding back to him, he thought it was his wife. For a few blissful seconds, life was bearable again. He forced the thoughts out of his mind, he didn’t want to go there. Not ever again. The last time Kate showed up wanting no-strings-attached sex, and was unceremoniously shunned, she’d disappeared once the morning arrived and McKail half-wished that she’d done the same this time. He didn’t mind her company too much and he tolerated her more than he would most, but she wasn’t much of a morning person; McKail had found out the hard way. It was noon and she’d snapped at him three times already, even though he offered her a ride to the next GCW show in Houston. However, after three mugs of coffee when they stopped for breakfast, she’d begun to mellow out some and after an hour or so of silence, words were finally being exchanged; much to McKail’s annoyance. "I don’t seem to remember you taking many shots to the throat in the ring," she said, still observing his wound carefully. McKail remained his usual stoic self. She was trying to gauge his response in order to worm further information out of him and he didn‘t intend to take the bait. "And I seem to remember reading something on the ‘Net about you being assaulted by a police officer." McKail glared at her warily. He hadn’t read the Internet dirt sheet’s in years; he didn‘t have the time to squander. "You don’t want to believe everything you read on the Internet, Kate." "If it isn’t true, then it seems kinda strange to me. I read somewhere you were attacked by a police officer and now I see you with a bruise around your neck…" she trailed off, watching him like a hawk. McKail didn’t react. He kept his eyes on the road and his mouth shut. If he confirmed anything at this point she’d want to know more and then he’d have to experience that hell all over again. I don’t fucking think so! After a while, she sighed defeated. "Fine. Don’t tell me, see if I care." McKail couldn’t tell whether she was genuinely upset or whether it was one final last-ditch effort to siphon the information she wanted. Either way, McKail didn’t much care. He wasn’t forthcoming at the best of times and he certainly wasn’t going to explain away his life story to a complete stranger. Then she grinned. "You’re really not going to tell me, are you?" McKail shook his head. "Nope." "You’re a real bastard, Jacob McKail." She told him, trying desperately to hold back a smile. "You don’t know the half of it." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Dissection. The Offices of Dr. Phillip Cassidy, PhD. The Bronx, New York. Today. "Interesting," Dr. Cassidy muttered. He turned over a page in his notepad and scribbled some more on the fresh one. Once done, he glanced up and realised that McKail had stopped talking, waiting for him to follow up his train of thought. Dr. Cassidy obliged him, "You seem to have a tendency to deflect questions regarding yourself, and your past, with great proficiency." Two-Fucking-Fifty. The fifty minutes had seemed like an eternity and he didn’t know whether he had enough left in him to finish the last ten. "Yeah," McKail responded, not really listening. "Seems like." "Why do you think that is?" Cassidy asked, setting aside his notepad and pen. "I told you," McKail responded. "Because I don’t like complete strangers knowing my business." "That’s not the real reason, Jacob, and we both know it," Dr. Cassidy told him. "Oh it’s not the real reason, you say?" McKail asked, with an undeniable sarcastic tone poorly embedded within his words. He leaned forward and faked an expression of bewilderment. "Please, enlighten me." Even though Dr. Cassidy’s faced remained placid, McKail could see the anger thinly veiled in his eyes and fought back a smile. "You’re cutting yourself off from the world and you’re neglecting every kind of human interaction. And do you know why this is?" McKail couldn’t tell whether it was a rhetorical question, but he didn’t respond regardless. "Because you can’t let go." "You’re bringing this around to my dead wife again?" McKail asked, shaking his head. "Man, you’re just getting boring now." "It is about Mary’s death, Jacob," Dr. Cassidy told him. "It has affected you more than you’re letting on." Dr. Cassidy grabbed at a brown cardboard file resting upon his table and thumbed open the pages. "Dr. Flannigan indicates something called, and I quote, ‘the promise’? You explained to her about some sort of promise -- her notes don’t go into specifics -- you made to Mary on her deathbed? Dr. Flannigan had pretty much come to the conclusion that this promise you made had begun to rule your life." He paused and placed the file back where he found it. "That was six months ago. I’d hate to think what it’s doing to you now…" McKail glared at him in silence for a while and Dr Cassidy simply glared back. Remarkably, it was McKail who broke the silence first, changing the subject entirely, "So, my stalker and I rolled into Houston at around dusk…" Dr. Cassidy shook his head and sighed, reaching for his notepad and pen. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Horn: "McKail’s Return to the Ring." Posted @ 17:00pm by Conrad Wilson. April 28th 2006 ProWrestlingBugle.com A small independent wrestling organisation based in New York City yesterday announced the shocking return to the ring of former independent superstar, Jacob McKail, after a lengthy absence from the business. He has been pencilled in to appear tonight, wrestling against firm fan favourite Davis Fury, in a fifteen minute bout that’s scheduled for one fall. Last week, Pro Wrestling Bugle reported that Jacob McKail tragically lost his wife to cancer and had taken time away from the industry to ‘see her through her last days’ (as quoted on McKail‘s official blog). When asked why he’d decided to come back to the ring so soon after her death, McKail refused to comment. We at Pro Wrestling Bugle are glad to see him return to the ring and wish him luck for tonight. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Closure. On the Road. Approaching Houston, Texas. Two Weeks Ago. McKail wasn’t much for small talk; he didn’t like to speak when he had nothing to say and often found himself surrounded in silence most of the time. Usually, he felt comfortable in such an environment, but riding with Kate into Houston, the silence was getting even to him. It’d been an hour since they’d last exchanged words and Despite their conversation ending in somewhat of a upbeat mood, McKail suspected that Kate was upset with him regardless. But how can I talk about it? He silently questioned. I find it painful to even think about it, never mind put it into words for someone else to fully comprehend! Kate sighed; the silence was getting to her too. She fumbled with the opening mechanism of the glove box and glanced at him questioningly. "Got any CD’s in here?" "No," he told her. It only just came to him what he still had locked away in there and didn’t want anybody to see it; least of all Kate. "You probably shouldn’t look in there." McKail noticed Kate’s expression turn to that of curiosity and knew then he’d said precisely the wrong thing; he’d always had had something of a knack for that. She fumbled with the opening mechanism some more, but with more effort and lines of determination were etched into her skin to all to see. Finally, the thing burst open and Kate stared at it’s contents puzzled, then directed her bewilderment at McKail. He reached across her, trying not to take his eyes off the road, and slammed it shut. Her gaze did not falter and it was beginning to make McKail nervous. "It was my wife’s," he finally admitted, with a sigh. Kate nodded and reopened the glove box in one smooth movement this time. She removed the gold diamond ring and glared at it intently. "I didn’t know you have a wife; how come she isn’t wearing this?" McKail wanted to be angry with her, he wanted to scream at her and to hurt her just for making him remember all of the pain all over again. But it wasn’t her fault; she couldn’t know just what in the hell she was opening up and quickly realised as much. McKail choked back the emotion that seemed to build in the back of his throat and forced out his words, "Because she died." Kate glared at him, water building up in her eyes and her faced engraved in sorrow. "I’m sorry, I didn’t know." "It’s okay," McKail told her. "You couldn’t have known." "How long?" She asked, unsure of her words. "How long has it been?" "Six months," McKail promptly responded, fighting back the tears. He stared straight ahead; he didn’t want Kate to see him like this. "Six months?" Kate needlessly responded, sniffling. "It--it all makes sense to me now." "What does?" McKail asked, eyes remaining forward. "Your life on the road, staying in crappy motel after crappy motel, without a home to go to. Your lack of friends. Your determination and focus." She paused for a second and placed a gentle hand upon his thigh. "Rejecting my advances." McKail shook his head. "You have an exaggerated opinion of yourself, you know that?" The insult had no effect, instead her caring eyes dug deeper holes in him. "You haven’t moved on," she said, "and you don’t intend to," she removed her hand from his thigh and glared at the ring sullenly, "do you?" McKail thought about lying to the woman, but then asked himself, what would be the point? He had nothing to hide and felt no guilt nor shame over the path he’d chosen to walk. "No." "Don’t you know this is ruining your life?" Kate asked, desperate to instil some sense into the stubborn man. "Can’t you see the downward spiral you’re in?" "I know what I’ve gotten myself into," McKail told her. The conviction in his voice made her shudder. "The consequences are meaningless to me as are your opinions of what I should and shouldn’t be doing with my life." "Okay, so my opinions don’t matter." She tried to establish eye contact with him, but failed. "What about Mary’s? Do they matter?" He glared at her fiercely; he knew where she was heading with the conversation and he didn’t like it one bit. He remained silent, reluctant to give her anymore ammunition than she already had. "If her opinions do matter to you, then consider this," she paused, edging closer. "Do you think Mary would be happy with what you’re doing? Do you think she’d approve of what you’ve become." He’d like to say he hadn’t thought about it, he’d like to say he hadn’t considered the implications of his self-imposed path of self-destruction. But then he’d be lying. He questioned himself everyday and everyday he’d fought to convince himself that what he was doing was right; he was fulfilling the promise he’d made to her on her deathbed regardless of the consequences. Everyday the battle got harder and lately he’d been questioning his own convictions. Maybe she’s right, he considered. Maybe, I do need to move on. Maybe… When Kate realised that he didn’t intend to respond, she turned away from him disgusted and McKail lowered his head, almost ashamed of himself. Almost. "Pull over," she told him. He’d assumed that the last portion of their journey would be submerged in more uncomfortable silence and he was almost glad to hear the words leave her mouth. It was only when the words took meaning a second or two later that his opinion changed. "But we’re in the middle of the country," McKail glared around. They were surrounded my nothing but plush green fields, the likes of which McKail hadn’t seen in a long while, for there were no cotton fields in the Bronx. Hell, there no fields the Bronx. "There’s nothing around for miles." She sighed. "Just pull over." McKail shrugged and shook his head, veering the car off to the side of the road. They came to a halt smoothly and McKail turned the engine off, glaring at her questioningly. "So, what now?" She looked him in the eye and held out the ring on her open palm before him. "You need closure and you need to move on." McKail developed a dumfounded expression and noted the agitation in her eyes, that she was desperately trying to quell. "You can start by throwing this ring away." She paused, gesturing toward the open fields surrounding them and the scenic beauty that could swallow his past whole. "Somewhere out there." McKail looked at the ring in her hand and then looked out onto the distant green pastures on the horizon. He snatched the ring out of her hand and stepped out of the car. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Conclusion. The Offices of Dr. Phillip Cassidy, PhD. The Bronx, New York. Today. Dr. Cassidy noted something in his pad excitedly and leaned forward. "So what did you do?" He was on the edge of his seat, buying every word McKail fired at him and then coming back for more. "Did you hurl the ring into the field? Did you move on?" McKail chuckled and glanced at the clock. It was three and his hour was up. When McKail didn’t answer, Dr. Cassidy followed his gaze toward the clock, developed a surprised expression and then checked it against the readout on his cheap digital watch. McKail stood and scooped up his black leather jacket from the back of the chair. "Not so fast," Dr. Cassidy told him. "I have the next half hour free, I’d like to delve into this --" McKail held up an open palm. "I think not. I have places to be." Dr. Cassidy looked down at his shoes, this time it was he who was defeated. Yet, he came back for more, "Tell me, Jacob." He paused and fixed his eyes upon McKail. "I have to know. Did you throw the ring into the field? Have you let Mary go?" McKail winced at her very name and then whispered, sullenly, "Yes." Dr. Cassidy reeled back in his seat, his face breaking out into a smile immediately. "You’ve consciously made a decision to let go of everything that’s holding you down and making your life a misery." He stood, took McKail’s hand and shook. "You’re making real progress here, Jacob. Next week should be exciting!" McKail rolled his eyes. "Yeah. I’m sure it will be." The sarcasm entrenched deep into his words were seemingly lost on Dr. Cassidy as he went back to furiously scrawling notes on his pad. McKail shook his head and made for the door. His fingers buried deeply into the pockets of jacket, he felt around nervously for the little metallic object, becoming instantly soothed as it slipped into his hands. It was Mary’s wedding ring, it was still there and it still belonged to him. Nothing would take it away. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Epilogue. Some Motel Room. The Bronx, New York. Later. "So why’d ya do it, kid?" It was McKail’s hallucination, in the form of Burgess Meredith or Mickey from the film Rocky if one wanted to be pedantic. It’d come back to haunt him shortly after stepping foot in his motel room. McKail figured it sensed he needed to talk with someone; someone who knew him better than himself. He sat on the edge of the bed, his leather jacket lying next to him and the diamond encrusted gold ring held out before him. He’d been staring at it for the better part of an hour already and had only been half-listening to Mickey ramble on. McKail tore himself away from the ring and gently placed in the top breast pocket of his shirt. "Why did I do what?" "Lie ta tha man," Mickey responded. "Why didn’t ya let him help ya?" McKail smiled and turned toward the window, feeling himself calm watching the droplets of rain, course haphazardly across the surface and well up in the crevice at his underside. The rain had stopped pounding down upon the cheap motel room roof a little while ago, but its influence upon the area still held firm and wouldn’t clear up for some time. "I had to let him believe he was helping me," McKail muttered, trying to quell the seeds of self-doubt that had already begun to grow inside him. "It’s the only way." "It’s tha only way fer what, kid?" The figment of his imagination questioned. "To get the hell out of there and get back to living my life." He paused, reflecting upon his words. "To get back to following my dreams." Mickey nodded. "Yer nearly did it, didn’t ya, kid?" It asked him. "Yer nearly threw that damned thing away…" McKail didn’t respond. Instead, he forced the thoughts out of his head and once again took to watching the rebellious droplets of water stream down the window pane. The End
