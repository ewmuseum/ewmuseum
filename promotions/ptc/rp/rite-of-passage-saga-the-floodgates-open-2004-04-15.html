Rite of Passage Saga: The Floodgates Open

15 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-15 ---NOTE: A section of this roleplay contains a scene of morbid and graphic violence. For those with overly vivid imaginations and weak stomachs, take caution. It may not be as bad as I personally visualize, but this is a warning nonetheless.--- I am Ethan Knight. And what I have just done is the beginning of what is yet to come. It is something that the compassionate side of me regrets, but I have been assured that it is something that will fade away over time. Compassion is weakness, so says the teachings of the Sect. And it is a weakness that will be purged given enough conviction. When I am cleansed of that weakness, none shall stop me. And none shall prevent the insurrection of the Sect of Black Wisdom's worldwide regime. We fight a fragmented enemy. The Catholic Church is lost, swallowed in its own depravity and damned by none by its own transgressions. The Protestant-Christian contingent is divided amongst itself and unable to even agree on simple doctrinal notions, let alone wake up long enough to take action and oppose us. The Islamic faith is warped, distorted by hate-driven jackals and twisted into an pseudo-sacred path of vengeance that not even its conservative leadership can save. The followers of Buddha are too passive and peaceful to take any action to impede our march to domination; the doddering fools are too occupied with spiritual ascension to concern themselves with the fate of their world. Scientology.....heh....well, suffice to say its nothing more than the deluded machinations of a dead fiction author. Purely insane, as are its practitioners. None of the others have either the organization or ambition to take a stand against what shall soon ravage and enslave the denizens of this planet. We, of the Sect of Black Wisdom, are unified and prepared to do whatever it takes to bring the Great Old Ones back into this world. When that day comes they shall proceed to sweep across this unhallowed Earth, sanctifying it in the blood of the enemy. The aftermath shall be a paradise for the faithful and undying torment for the disbelievers. The Old Ones and their respective followers rank many, and by the efforts of myself and my brethren a new theological empire shall be established. And if need be, it shall be done under the noses of those loathsome faiths of which I've already proclaimed as inept. The Brotherhood of the Beast, the Starry Wisdom Cult, the Sect of Black Wisdom, the Hand of The Living Flame, the Esoteric Kin of Tsathoggua. They and every other growing union of the Great Old Ones shall be united under one banner and toward a lone purpose: the revival of our cosmic masters. This week I confront a man who considers himself a god. Tragic irony. I fight for an individual and an organization who most consider mad for merely believing in entities outside the mainstream of ignorant human thought, yet the one called Raijin is embraced for his brash claims. Funny how the wheels of fate turn is it not? Even in the wake of a string of miracles, humanity mocks and crucifies its asserted messiah. Yet the moment some brainsick lunatic wanders out of some field in Japan, proclaiming himself a holy peacekeeper from the dominion of the immortals, his lies are swept up and accepted by a spiritually-ravaged populace. I'm not overly familiar with the culture or lifestyle of the Japanese; Father Shanahan spent many years on their island country and can reason this much better than I, perhaps. In the coming weeks many will wonder why I have done this. The reason, however, is simple. If this fool lingers anywhere even close to the celestial tree of divinity, then it will take a vital sacrifice to upend him. That has now been offered, and I pray it shall satiate the hunger of the Old Ones. An enemy of my greatest teacher slain with the goal of sanctification. A sacrifice to appease the gods, for the purpose of defeating a god. The gospel shall spread, and I shall be among its greatest vessels. I am Ethan Knight, and this is my ambition. Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn....... * * * * * "I understand you're upset, Marcus. We all are. But I don't think I need to advise you that becoming a rogue entity within a rogue entity isn't the best way to go about getting retribution." Captain Marcus Stryker only glared longingly out the window into the rain. Streams cascaded down the outer pane, the deluge of the downpour spilling from the roof of the church onto the sidewalk. Not unlike the tears that had run down his face only days ago after the calamity. "I'm sorry about Jago," continued the voice of the man behind the desk, tone and body language genuinely mournful. "All of the Blackhelm Coalition grieved his death when it happened. He was a good soldier, a good operative, and a great man above all else. But what you're proposing.....Marcus, I simply can't understand the logic. It's sheer madness." Stryker abruptly pivoted to gaze into the eyes of Father Randal Craven. He knew he stood in a house of God, facing a man not only counted among his friends but also as one of His appointed priests. Anger, however, still gripped his heart and he felt almost patronized by the Church's dual standing on the matter. His comrade, and friend, had been murdered by a known enemy of the Church and they seemingly discounted it as 'just another tragedy'. "Exactly what's wrong with my logic, Randal?" he growled. "What's madness?" Craven, a portrait of Christ hanging behind him, pursed his lips. He knew his long-term sentiments weren't currently reflected by Captain Stryker. "What would Jago do if it were you instead of him that was killed, Marcus?" appealed Craven. "How is throwing your life away going to redeem Lieutenant Sensaki's sacrifice?" "It wasn't sacrifice!" roared Stryker, his rage flaring out of control. "He was fucking murdered by a brainwashed drone of Bruce Shanahan!! That is not sacrifice!" Randal pulled himself upright, sighing deeply. He adjusted his glasses, peering disappointedly at his enraged counterpart. The priest didn't like the look in Stryker's eyes but it was far from an alien sight these days. The events of last year's endeavor at Enoch Lodge had been a crippling blow to the collective psyche of all involved and Marcus hadn't been the same since. "Might I remind you to please watch your tongue in this house of God?" rebuked Craven, keeping his tone calm. "Shall I refresh your memory, Father?" Stryker spit out the last word with disdain, taking Craven somewhat aback. He had come to fear the gleam in the eyes of his friend, and knew that given the circumstance it was best to let the distressed man just vent. Surely even God could overlook it this one time. "He was drawn out into some wretched cabin on the outskirts of nowhere by someone claiming to be family. He got there and was killed in cold-blood by someone out to spite not only the Church, but you and me personally. We both know it's not conventional for a member of this organization to be randomly lured into a trap and killed. Not with the security measures the Coalition takes with our identities." Randall displayed his disagreement with a shake of the head, anticipating the thoughts of his long-time acquaintance. "During the time of the murder Bruce was away. It's been looked into by both the police and our operatives. He was fulfilling his wrestling obligations. He had an alibi and it's air-tight, we checked with the management of his employer." Stryker, frustrated, turned away to again stare into the torrential rain. His eyes narrowed. "It wasn't Bruce. It was one of his new guys." The revelation was a new one to Craven, but he did well to hide his surprise. His eyes deviated to the paperwork on his desk. "Which one?" "Knight. The one they call Ethan Knight. He did Bruce's dirty work. It's a rite of passage of some sort." Randal only shook his head, fighting the temptation to offer up judgement to his estranged comrade. He has never been able to figure where Marcus got his supposed 'informants', let alone any sources privvy to information even the Coaltion psychics and top spies were unable to attain. In all truth, maybe it was better not to ask. "There's no evidence that either Bruce or any of his followers are behind this," denied Father Craven, his gaze again falling on his counterpart. "The story about Jago being 'lured' into Nebraska by someone impersonating a distressed family member is hearsay andf you know that, Marcus. Besides, I really believe that Ethan Knight would be one of Bruce's last resorts for a crime like this if he were involved. Knight's specialty is diplomacy. He's the one Bruce turns to for negotiation when the Sect deals with other organizations of its ilk. Tully, Sterling, maybe Griffin. But not Knight, he's not a killer. I can't accept that as an explanation. Check your sources better, my friend." "I had a dream last night, Randal." The cool demeanor suddenly marking Stryker's words virtually chilled the room. Judging by the bead of sweat that had appeared on his forehead and the quivering of his extremities, Craven knew that Stryker didn't wish to relate the experience but deemed it necessary to make a point for his case. The priest could only look on in morbid fascination as detail of his vision crept through trembling lips. "I opened my eyes, and I arose in the middle of some abyssal chamber. Didn't know how I had gotten there. I was naked, and my body was inscribed with terrible symbols and markings I've only seen scrawled in the pages of the Church library's most feared and sealed away tomes. Before I even lifted my head, I could hear the screams. Awful cries, Randal, pleas for death in a world where death is just a grand illusion and empty hope. I could hear and smell things I can barely speak of or place even now; the vile rending of flesh from bones, the putrid odor of searing flesh mingled with the stench of rotting meat. From all around me I heard horrid utterings, the maddening chattering of demonic entities spawned from some unholy pit that God never intended to exist. I didn't want my eyes to open, I didn't want to behold what I already knew lurked in that atrium of the damned." Craven could only imagine the pain Marcus was putting himself through recounting such madness. It had driven Bruce Shanahan insane two years ago, and the toll was heavy on any man. Agents of God were not exempt. "But I looked up. I looked up, Randal, I opened my eyes. And my sanity has suffered for it ever since. I saw legions upon legions of men suspended from chains and hooks stretching into an endless wall of darkness, some with the flesh flayed from their bodies and others even worse. The ground I stood on was a rug of crimson, stained after God knows how many unhallowed eons of depraved torment. And that wasn't the worst part. The ground, the walls.....Jesus Christ, Randal, the walls were breathing!" Stryker's vision was nearly identical to ones Bruce had told him of in the past before his alliance with the darkness, but this didn't surprise Father Craven; the atmosphere was typical of Nyarlathotep's dream-sendings, one of the entities responsible for Shanahan's corruption. Marcus's breathing had become heavy and erratic and thus Randal feared he would begin to hyperventilate. The trauma of such a nightmare and recent events were a terrible burden for the human mind to bear. "They constantly spilled out noxious fumes, convulsing with a rhythm like the walls of a heart," continued Stryker. "The room itself was alive and I'd swear it took conscious pleasure in the suffering of those therein. I walked what had to have been three hundred yards, and still I could see no end to either the shrieking damned or that dark netherworld. I watched as men were torn apart by unseen things, gutted like fish and eviscerated by invisible talons. And there was no death to save them. That was when I stumbled on the worst part of the ordeal. That's when I came upon Jago." "Please don't do this, Marcus....." pleaded Randal, seeing the anguish on his friend's face as he struggled to recall all of what he witnessed. Even then, he could see it was to no avail. "Let me finish." A sigh was all that the priest of St. Augustine's Chapel could offer in response. "He was hanging like all the others, drenched in his own blood and suspended by a combination of barbed hooks and gore-covered chains. His face.....I can't even describe how it was contorted, the pain I could read in his eyes. I wanted to save him, get him out of that ungodly abyss and personally guide him to the gates of Heaven. But I couldn't. Something was stopping me, some wicked and unseen force guiding my movements. A humanoid shadow crept from the outer darkness, something clutched in both hands. The laugh it uttered before it stepped into the light.....my God, that hideous laugh!" He paused, on the verge of breaking down. Even Randal's hand had twisted into a white-knuckled fist by now. "And then, there, I could see him. Blond hair stained with blood, cackling with mirth. Ethan Knight. The son of a bitch set his eyes on me and gloated as he went about his work, shredding Jago's skin systematically with a black glaive. I watched one of my best friends repeatedly slashed and impaled by a maniac who took glee in the torture of my brother-in-arms. He was suspended over some sort of black pulpit that dipped inward like a funnel. Jago's blood dripped.....no, spilled into that funnel, and was channeled into an archaic chalice set beneath it. It was engraved with some infernal runes I don't even want to know anything about, and as it filled to the brim with my friend's blood those runes seemed to glow. They pulsated almost in rhythm to the gore spewing from Jago's wounds." Stryker was a learned man, an avid reader in his spare time and fan of such brilliant minds as Poe, Hawthorne and Dickens. Assimilation of such broad vocabulary made his account of his nightmare all the more dreadful and macabre. That was his intention; he wanted to sway Craven into supporting him. "I watched as Ethan Knight took up that goblet and drank Jago's blood, an act I know is symbolic of something. My friend hung there, still racked in undying agony as that monster drained the chalice and set it back down under the pulpit, his face cold and stoic. He sacrificed a man I considered the next thing to a brother for God knows what exact purpose. And didn't bat an eyelash. "But you realize that wasn't what really happened," asserted Randal. "It was a nightmare. Ethan Knight didn't torture Jago Sensaki, and he didn't slowly kill him with some Hell-forged weapon. The death was instanteous, you've read the coroner's report." Marcus had actually studied it several dozen times, but he wasn't up for offering a response. With rage ever-brewing in his heart, the officer continued to peer into the soaking rain beyond the chapel walls. "It was a dream. A nightmare, at the most a terrible mental broadcast from the enemy. They try to drive you mad, Marcus, that's their game." Father Craven held his position for a reason. He knew what was best for the organization and was well-suited emotionally for setting aside personal matters for Coalition business. The Vatican had personally delegated him to that stead. "At this point only three factions even marginally stand in their way, Marcus. Us, the Wilmarth Foundation, and Delta Green. We're all are rogue administrations, Marcus, we don't have the means to prove them responsible for any of the crimes we suspect. We have only prophecies, illegal intel and suspicion to work with. We don't have the resources to pose any great threat right now to a veritable cult conglomerate like what Bruce is assembling. If what he proposes is established, they'll go totally unchecked and untested." Stryker, eyes red with the tears that had flowed as he reaccounted the terror of his dreams, turned to face Father Craven. "We can't allow that to happen, Captain Stryker." Marcus nodded, fully grasping the purpose of Randal's words. Addressing him by his military rank was a sign that Craven was all business again. "We can't allow vengeance to take hold of one of our most experienced operatives. If it was Ethan Knight, he'll pay for his crimes. I assure you of that; I'll personally delve deeper into the matter. But if we falter, and give in to our own transgressions, what will be unleashed into this world is a force that nothing of human design can stop. So for the final time, no. I will not allow you to go to Wyatt Manor and I won't assemble a force to go there either. It's suicide and it's a sacrifice we don't need right now. Less than ever." Marcus Stryker nodded his agreement. He wouldn't use or manipulate Coalition assets to seek retribution. "I'm glad you agree, captain," Craven said, issuing a half grin before Stryker departed the cathedral office. "That's all for now." But that's not to say he wouldn't seek other measures against the boy....... * * * * * Lightning flashes, a gaunt figure standing silhouetted against the flash of simulated daylight. On the ground, sprawled out and facedown, lies a contorted male form entrenched in thick mud. A blade is clutched in the hand of the former as he gazes down on the fallen, images of the preceding struggle consuming his thoughts. His breathing is still raspy and erratic, hands soaked in blood with the hammering rain doing little to wash it away from his still-trembling fingertips. Shaky, his movements begin to steady as the whippoorwills' song floats through the skeletal trees. His mind slowly nearing serenity via the birds' naturally sedative call, the shadowy individual rolls the hilt of the knife over and over in his crimson-stained palm. He had hoped the killing would bring about a sense of self-instilled gratitude for doing the work of his masters. But regardless of his arduous mental training and adopted dogma, humanity still was the essence of his being. Guilt still lingered in his soul. The disposition of the mortal soul was not so easily overcome, a difficult obstacle even for an apostle of the Old Ones. Lightning again flares in the distance, a searing bolt bathing the land temporarily in the light of day. The abrupt radiance having dissipated, the figure only gazes far to the horizon where it had presumably struck. He had heard the hype and the allegations, entertained them though it sickened him that they were taken with anything more than a grain of salt. A solitary word enters his mind. Raijin. Allegedly such a phenomena had been the omen signaling his arrival in the Earthly realm, and such an irony struck home with the shadowed one. Was it possible he spoke the truth? Perhaps the forces of Heaven truly were mobilized to strike down the Sect's insane resolutions. Perhaps the Japanese crusader was truly blessed with divine powers. And perhaps pigs could fly. The figure, faintly silhouetted by the dim bulb dangling above the cabin porch, kneels to touch the jacket of the fallen. His hand slowly caresses the smooth leather, ascending to the chill flesh of the dead man's face. An expression of shock is still imprinted on his twisted countenance, surprise marred with stark horror. His cold, dead eyes stare directly into those of his killer. The latter could only wish that same indifference were reflected in those glassy orbs. Raijin represented all that he had come to believe was wrong with this realm. A harbinger of false salvation, riding in on his white horse under the guise of a paladin and guardian of righteousness. If this was truly was his identity, the edict was for him to be exterminated. He had descended into the world of mortals, and as such had himself become one. He had abandoned his lone advantage in the bout to fight on even ground. A noble ploy, but a misguided one. Studying under the tutelage of Bruce Shanahan for so long he had come to study many ancient texts, those of human and inhuman conception alike. This brings to mind the revelations of The Art of War, and subsequently a smile across his face. Raijin's blind quest for honor would prove to be his downfall. Everything was slowly falling into place, like some great celestial puzzle he felt destined to complete. He had overcome Rocky Adams. He had defeated Tessa Windsor. And now, to achieve the goals of the cosmos, he would conquer his very humanity. Trudging back into the remote and desolate cabin, he places the curiously-designed knife into a black shroud embellished with innumerable runic characters. Wiping a streak of blood from his bruised left cheek, he places the artifact onto a rickety table in the center of the bare room. Beside it lays a simple journal already opened to several pages within the cover. He nonchalantly draws up a chair, picking up a black pen as he exhales a slow breath. Lightning flashes again. He grins and casts a final glance back into the swampy driveway before setting to work. Let the Heavens rage. They would be quelled soon enough. I am Ethan Knight. And what I have just done is the beginning of what is yet to come.........
