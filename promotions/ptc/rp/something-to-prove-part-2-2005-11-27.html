Something to Prove: Part 2

27 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-27 After a lengthy commercial break about the usual tripe, familiar inspirational music plays as lights illuminate Marcus Geoavanti standing before the same three video screens. "And welcome back to "Something to Prove". Before the commercial break we gave you, the viewer, some brief insight into the professional history of James McFarlane. Through a series of video clips, and some interviews with co-workers and fans, we hope that you’ve had a chance to see at least some of the phenomenon that made James the power he is in the ring." The screens in the bathroom show a quick montage of James’ victories, flashing to several scenes of him holding one title belt or another over his head. Interspersed between are shots of him signing contracts or shaking hands with Managerial big wigs. "But the professional side of James makes up only half, maybe less, of the whole man that many came to love before his untimely passing. He was also a dedicated family man, a man devoted entirely to his loving wife and two lovely daughters." The first screen changes to a photo of a tall young woman in a wedding dress. Blonde hair and sparkling green eyes make up just two of her many striking features, and the video tag under her name says "Patricia Anne McFarlane". The second screen changes to a young girl, with rosey cheeks and the chubby cuteness that is normally seen in children below ten. Straight red-blonde hair hangs in shaggy locks around her face, and the video tag says "Dawn Catherine McFarlane. Finally the third screen shows an even younger girl in a pale blue jumpsuit, with the familiar family curls starting to sprout along her head. Though obviously younger, she’s the spitting image of the slightly older Dawn and the video tag says "Katelyn Rosalind McFarlane." "James married at the young age of twenty, having already found the woman he knew he wanted to spend the rest of his life with. Family and friends around the two lover birds were often amazed at how gentle and loving the massive McFarlane could be wherever his blushing bride was concerned." The scene fades out to a much older man with thinning grey hair hanging around his largely bald head, and wrinkles crowding around the warm pits of his green eyes. The video tag beneath him says "Peter Abnett, Father in law." "I’ll admit I didn’t much like James when I first met him. I didn’t care for his flashy car or the laid back way he talked to his elders. But one thing I liked from the start was the way he treated my daughter, Patricia. It was pretty obvious, even to me, that he had fallen head over heels for my little girl and that he would do anything in the world for her." Peter shifts a little, smiling a tired smile. "I remember one of the first times he came over, I was working on our new porch in the back yard. It was too expensive to hire a contractor on my salary, so I had just decided I was going to make up the porch myself. Well that fancy car of his comes rumbling up in the driveway and a few minutes later he walks into the backyard, since I was the only one home and obviously wasn’t answering the front door. He takes one look at me, rolls up his sleeves and just jumps right in. Didn’t ask any stupid questions, didn’t ask if I wanted a drink or anything, he just knew what had to be done and did it. That’s one of those things I always liked about him, he just knew what had to be done." The scene fades to an older looking, but not elderly man, in a pressed black suit and gold rimmed glasses. The video tag says: "Michael Abnett-Martin, Brother in law." "Unlike my dad, I got along with James right from the start. I didn’t care that he was dating my sister, all I knew was that he drove a flashy car, he wrestled on TV and he didn’t treat me like an idiot just because I was little. Even when he was only coming over to see Patty, he’d always stop to say hi to me or ask me what I was doing or how school was going. Even when he was…was diagnosed…he was always asking after my health…" Michael becomes visibly broken up, taking off his glasses to wipe away tears. "I’m sorry, can we take a break? I, I just can’t…" The scene fades to a young woman with blonde hair and brilliant green eyes, only a hair shorter and several pounds lighter then the more TV famous Dawn. The video tag under her name says: "Katelyn McFarlane, youngest daughter." "I was still ‘fairly’ young in the grand scheme of things when Daddy passed away, so I didn’t have as much of a chance to know him as some of his friends or Mom’s family did. Being a pro wrestler he wasn’t home a whole lot, but when he was home he didn’t let little aches and pains stop him from spending time with us, especially when we needed him. The uh, the first night of my biggest recital, I remember Daddy had a huge fight with someone on the phone, he was yelling and when he finally hung up he tore the phone right out of the wall he hung up so hard." "I didn’t know it at the time, but his promoter wanted him to come in and work on the day of my recital and daddy refused. So when that promoter told him if he didn’t show up he’d be fired, Daddy quit on the spot…and uh…he came to my recital anyway…I wish…I wish we’d had more time…" Katelyn breaks into tears, cupping her face to her hands. The screen fades to a spliced picture of James McFarlane and Dawn McFarlane each in a different ring, holding up a different title. The caption says: "Living in the Shadow?" And "Stay tuned after these messages."
