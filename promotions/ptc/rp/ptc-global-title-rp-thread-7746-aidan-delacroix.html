19 July 2004 The Changes of the Unchanged – Part I Shattering the Walls of Secrets In the mist of my success, a new awakening was brewing, unknown to my eyes. Something would happen in the days that followed one cold February night where I had been foolishly led to believe I would be released from my plague, that would change the way everything seemed to my eyes. Something and someone would come along, that would make my imminent success as the Prime Time Global Champion change every single breath I took. But to understand how I have been aligned for the future, you must first understand my previous tracks in the past… And so, another tale begins… - - - - - - So what does a person do when he is shackled to the walls of despair as I was? You cannot hope, for hope is but a strange word to you. You cannot dread, for dread is nonexistent to you. You cannot wish, because wishing is but a mere word without meaning to you. What could I do on that cold night? I had been robbed of my veritable chance of escaping. Peering down upon the lifeless body of my latest victim, I did not feel anything that I should have felt. Whether it would be regret or simple anger, nothing came up to me. I just looked down at her and was enveloped in the cold night air that enveloped me, as a mother would wrap her child in a blanket. I knelt down before her and looked at the shock that had stayed printed on her facial expression. I took certain pleasure in drawing out the lines of fright in her face. You must understand that I did take pleasure in certain things, but simply not the right ones. While you could enjoy the rising of the sun, the warmth of a fire crackling in your houses, all I could feel was the pain I had inflicted on others before robbing them of what made them feel. My frost bitten finger tips caressed every single curve in her face, closing her fear-infested eyes and caressing her lips. My eyes closed as my breathing became heavier… I grew great pleasure in simply looking at my victims and drawing out their expressions of pain, a pleasure I would venture to compare to orgasmic. She had taken too much of my time, I thought to myself. I stood up and turned my back to her and her infant. The night would prove to be long, but for now, I longed for something I had been deprived of and would get no chance of having. I walked and walked and walked until the snow had ceased to fall, and the sun had begun to rise above the cloudy mountains. Unforgiving, the cold air had become arctic and more violent, lashing out cries of hate as they sprung passed me. Torment began its violent warfare within the confines of my head as I took steps closer and closer to the room I had allocated at a local Inn. I would be there soon, but before I could reach it, I would need to look inside me for things I wish I had forgotten. Why? One reason: the Prime Time Central Global Title. Too long had I not had the human sentiment of need, of passion. Too long had I been deprived of any warm emotion that could be conjured up from any sort of event, good or bad. Too long had I not felt veritable lust for something, and then … it had appeared in my line of view. It was an object like none I had ever seen before. I had seen larger amounts of gold and better craftsmanship before, so it was not its aspect of richness that lured me to it, but what it produced within me. I remember sitting and watching as a man named Hessian raised it over his head as he sat above a ladder. The look on his face was one I had never seen before. I witnessed as every single facial expression of his changed and told a different story than one I had once imagined. And then, a month passed before a repeat of history, played with different actors. My eyes locked on a man named Saij, a man I would consider an enemy of mine in the future, I watched him as he would raise the honor above his head, and as he radiated with his share of emotions, totally different to those of Hessian’s, yet saying the same things. For once in a very long time, I began to feel something close to envy. I wanted to stand above the Gods and look down on them with a hint of mockery as the greatest of the great. I wanted to take from Saij what he valued as much as his own soul. In him, I saw great opposition, I saw a man that would be worthy of dealing pain to me. In him, I saw the man that I wanted to be… a man in jubilation over what he felt. I was fascinated by him, just as I had been by one Hessian before hand. As the night set sail, allowing daylight to reign above all, I pushed the Inn’s main door in and walked to my room, a floor above. The wooden floors creaked under my weight for every step I took. I entered my deserted room and lay fire to a candle next to the bed. In my solitude, flanked by the emotions of envy and lust, I began to reminisce on my past, to the last time I had been victim to emotions, the only treasure I craved for. I wish I had not started to think of it, as I felt veritable pain on as I looked up to the badly insolated ceiling of my room. Memories came crashing into me at the speed of a train. My hands tightened as my head fell forwards. I thought back of the day where my innocence and my desire to live were stolen from me, and I was transformed into the monster that I am now today. Of the day where the greatest pleasures and simplest were hidden behind a veil and I was forced to march into a world of ugliness and despair. Suddenly, events that had been marked out of my memory by the Council came back to me. Events that I was lead to believe never happened returned to my memory. Fueled by my lust for the PTC Global Championship, the veils were ripped away, and for the very first time, I remembered the last moments of my mortal life, and the last feeling that I felt. I remember the day where Seekers came to my house, and murdered my Mother. An Undefined Amount of Years Previous “Hold on to me son,” her loving voice said. That horrific sight will forever be imprinted in my mind. On that one night, my whole life was stolen from me, though I was later given immortality, the price was too high for the reward that I was given! Straddled in her bed, blood dripping from her nose and from the numerous cuts in her visage, mother looked at me. The fiends had just finished with her, raping her of all her dignity and robbing her of all her will to live. They stood in the corner of the room, laughing at the whimpering boy that I was. Putting their pants back on, they had a feast of laughter at the action they had just perpetrated. My mother’s caring hand lent forward and soothed my face. Her nearly crippled fingers wiped away my tears and lured me towards her. “I love you Aidan,” she said between sighs of pain. “Mother… don’t leave me, I need you,” I whimpered, begging her to clutch onto what I once grasped myself. “Don’t leave me, I beg of you” Tears welled around her eyes, then fell, following the route of the curves on her face and mixed themselves into the open cuts and the bloodied visage that once was so soft and so clear. I threw myself upon the bed and wrapped my petite hands around her battered body, resting my head upon her now exposed breasts. I shook my head in denial, refusing to believe what I was seeing and feeling. Behind me, the cackles of pride came from the dirty filth augmenting the building rage inside me. “Run, boy. I cannot hold onto the breath of life much longer, and if you stay here, they will do to you what they have done to me,” she whispered in my ear. My head rose as she began to talk and I looked deep into her eyes. I was so sad. I was petrified with shock and horror. She was the only person that had ever mattered to me, and she been taken from me, stolen. How could a person as pure as Mother be treated the way she had been? How could she have been taken away from me? Teary eyed, I shook my head in denial, holding on tighter to her body. “Don’t say that,” I protested as I kissed her on the lips. By that time however, her protest of the pain had died down and the whimpers had ceased. My eyes set on the one purpose that was worth more than all the gold in the world, at the only prize that I had ever wanted. “Should they feast on her?” I heard one of them say. Those words were enough to stray me away from my shock of having lost Mother to these vile stains in the fabric of society. The tears had begun to dry on my dirty skin as I slowly turned around to face my mother’s attackers. Three of them. I knew they were not men of the village, not only by their attire, but because I had never seen them before. “Yes, that is why we were sent in the first place, seeker,” the one in middle replied, paying no attention to my fiery gaze. Feast? On Mother? I could not let such a thing happen. I had no comprehension as to what they were talking about; however, I knew far too well that I would not let them disgrace the bearer of my Salvation any longer. She would stay with I, and I with her. She would drift away as the winds of time passed her by, and so would my sorrow, but we would be together, alive or not. “Mother will stay with me,” I whispered with ferocity. The man in the middle reached for his side and retracted a wonderfully crafted iron sword from its scabbard. The sound of the blade grinding against its metallic interiors came to me like a most powerful wave, knocking me to the side, forcing me to cover my ears. This could not be happening I thought to myself. I was nine years of age; boys did not lose their mothers at such an age. “What of the boy?” came the foreign voice of the third member. “His soul is not strong enough for the Council, he may stay here with her drained body,” retorted the leader of the clan between cruel chuckles. And slowly, the sound of their leather soles against the wooden floor that we had in the house grew closer and closer. I refused to look, choosing instead to wrap my arms around my mother’s neck and bury my tear-masked face into her neck. I felt the cold hand of one of the men clasp around my ankle and let out a scream of despair, trying to kick free. The hold was too strong, and sooner than later, I found myself on the floor, my body slumped against one of the close walls. I looked on as one of the men took my mother’s arms without care and placed them to her side. “Leave her alone,” I complained as I rose to my feet and ran at the man that had thrown me off my resting place. He had omitted to pay me any attention, and for that, he felt the cold kiss of my teeth entering the flesh of his calf muscle. He winced as he turned around and laid the back of his hand with great intensity upon the side of my eyes, knocking the back of my head into the wall. I slumped to my behind, and became witness to a sight that haunts me to this very day, over two decades after it happened. The man planted the sword into my matriarch’s heart. I found myself on the brink of losing consciousness. The events had sent my head swirling and my heart plummeting. Before my eyes closed, I saw something seep out of the wound and wrap itself around the sword, some sort of aura … one I would hunt for later on, in hopes of one day being able to escape the curse to which I was made a slave too. That was the last time I ever saw my mother, alive or dead. To think that the last image I have is that of her bloodied and disgraced, choking on her own blood as she suffers from a sword to the heart. It is a sight that shall haunt me for my eternal life, and the last thing I shall think of if I am to die… And that is how it all began. Before long, I had been forced to be branded with the Council of the Thirteen Ghosts’ scriptures. Before long, every desire I had ever had as a little boy was taken from me, as I became a mindless slave to my mother’s murderers. Can you begin to imagine what came of me as I sat on the bed in my rented room? The word destruction would come off as gentle next to what became to brew inside of me. It was on that night that I felt my very first emotion, the ONLY one that I would feel until I had gained what I craved for. Hate. I hated the Council of the Thirteen Ghosts in every breath I took, yet, every breath I took was for them. I could not turn my back to them, or exact revenge, for my life was bound to them. I knew far too well that I was powerless before them, and that I truly was but a slave. Granted, I had grown to become the Commander of Seekers, however, to a Council of Immortals, I was replaceable. I stood up and in a rage, began flailing my arms wherever I could. My knuckles made contact against the wooden walls before my hands threw whatever they could grip. In my madness, I found myself clueless to the fact that I had knocked over the candle when I had stood up. Before long, a solitude filled room transformed into one of the pits of hell, as fire took over every single inch of the room. I stood in the middle of it, a smile drawn up to my lips. I could hear the screams of worry coming towards the room, probably those of fellow travelers and the inn keepers. Before they could take the ten-foot run and open the door, I had already vanished, courtesy of the open window. I fell from the window to my feet and slowly, began my walk towards the forest before me. The sun was high now, it must have been noon. I had walked away from the fiery image and stood at the forest’s doorstep. Before I could even enter, a voice called out to me… “Though you are still bound to them,” came a cold chilling voice “your soul and fate now belong to you, Seeker DelaCroix.” I spun around my heels and found a cloaked man holding onto a rather large walking stick. Slowly, he became unveiled from under the hood and his mere sight sent me to my bottom. Before me was the man that was the single greatest enemy of the Council of the Thirteen Ghosts for over two decades… before me stood the Sage known as Worlack the Merciful. He alone had banished seven of the Thirteen Ghosts to a life of never ending suffering. He alone had power far greater than I could ever imagine. Was this the end? At the eve of my greatest quest? “No,” I said, stumbling through my words “no, this cannot be happening… you cannot be real” What can be so terrible that you can make a man that has never felt fear tremble? His power was unlimited, truly a God amongst men. They spoke of him as the Banisher of Evil and Ray of Light in the Dark. He was of a holy alignment, one that thought of others before him, one that cursed all evil beings to the deepest cavities of hell. They spoke of him as untouchable and destructive if he saw fit for you to be destroyed. No one had ever survived his attacks, even our Elders in the Council. We as Seekers were beneath them in terms of power and knowledge. They were the kings and queens while we were only the soldiers in action. I stood in front of a man that even Ghosts feared. For the very first time I felt yet another “human” emotion, just like I had done earlier on in the now charred up Inn. Now, I felt veritable fear. I never cared much for the life I lived, and the aspect of death came to me as being as dull as a stone used to throw in a lake. Death was death, life was life. I did not fear when death would come, nor how it would come. I did not dread the suffering that would come of it, mostly because I thought I would never die, and because I cursed every breath I took, but what I did fear though was the man before me, and what he could and would do to me. His human body was the reflection of an elder man, but his posture suggested that of a man in his prime. He stood tall on his two feet, his body firm of muscle and untouched by weakness. He had shoulder long white hair and a rather long white beard. Every inch of his vestment was as white as the result of a new snowfall. No spec of filth ever touched him. White was the color of purity, and purity could not be dirtied. Every single finger had a band around it. Whether it be silver, gold, platinum incrusted with diamonds or not, all of them were dressed with signs of richness. His right hand held what I once believed to be a walking stick, but now identified as a staff. I had heard tales of what he could do with this unlikely weapon. Engrossed in the hard wooden staff were many scriptures that I could not read or comprehend. The head of the staff, which he palmed, had been carved out to look like a soaring phoenix, the rising wings caressing both sides of the holding hand. I looked up to him in fear, in respect, and in hatred. For all my existence, I had been taught that he was the enemy. I had read, heard and had been told how he would find me one day, and how he would take away from me what made me breathe. He would banish me to a life of excessive suffering and make me repent for my previous murders for eternity. Within mere milliseconds after laying my orbs upon him, all thoughts of the Global Championship had disappeared. All sentiments of envy towards Saij had vanished. The prize that I looked at as salvation now was as pointless as my entire existence was. I had forgotten about any possible future, as I foresaw my end. Worlack was there to do me as he had done others before. As Commander of Seekers, and possibly the most notoriously known Seeker within the underground wars, it was only fitting for my reign of tyranny to be ended by the opposition’s greatest. My fingernails pierced into the earth’s skin as I tried to pull myself away from him and into the nearby forest. The ground was hard under the cold given to it by the winter’s snow. My tentative escape was without positive outcomes. Finally, his voice came to my ears. “Will you not stand Seeker DelaCroix?” His voice came to me as a challenge. I would have to fight. Though fear made every limb in my body tremble, I would fight until the very end, until my very last breath.
