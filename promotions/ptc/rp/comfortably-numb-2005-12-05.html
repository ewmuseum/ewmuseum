Comfortably Numb

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Damn did he need a drink. Normally, he wasn’t really the kind of guy to crave a drink like a smoker craves that first puff of nicotine filled bliss. He wasn’t a heavy drinker really; he controlled his urges and never really abused alcohol. But this time was a little different. This time, he absolutely craved it. After searching through the cupboards and every shelf in his quaint little loft in Winnipeg, he didn’t even come up with one drop of booze. Even the shelves in the refrigerator were bare aside from a lime, two colas and a jug of milk. "Must’ve been from that party the other night…" he thought, still conducting a hopeless search. As he slumped down on the cold bar stool, his eyes caught glimpse of a vodka bottle standing beside the large stereo system by the window. He pulled himself off the stool and slowly made his way over, hoping that he would find the clear gold that he wanted. But his hopes faded when he picked up the bottle and not a single drop was found in it. Angrily he flung the bottle across the room and it shattered against the wall near the other window. He slumped down against the side of the couch and caught his head on the arm rest. He moved his hand up to the bloody bandage that was beginning to fall from his forehead. He was ready to get up and walk out the door until his mind woke up and became aware of the millions of glass shards that littered his wood floor. In most cases he was a clean person, but he left the broom and dustpan where they were in favor of his coat. He made his way to the door, in search of the nearest bar or commercial establishment that served alcohol. Killean walked down the slippery sidewalk that was covered with a thin layer of fresh snow. A small bit of snow continued to fall as it felt like it was getting colder the longer he was out there. He walked with his hands in his pockets and a continued gaze at the concrete. Missing were the trademark sunglasses that usually adorned his face even in the winter months. The laser surgery he had had a few months earlier helped his sensitivity to light quite a bit. He still had to wear his specially designed wrestling goggles when in the ring but the sunglasses were becoming less and less of a necessity in everyday life. His eyes caught up with him as he stopped in front of The Woodbine Hotel. It was a small hotel in the heart of Winnipeg but it had gone downhill years ago. It was now a hangout the housed some of the socially delinquent individuals that lived in the many small and rundown hotels including this one. But it was the closest bar, only a few short blocks from his loft. He approached the door and pulled on the handle. The door squeaked as it opened, seemingly releasing the odor of vomit and marijuana smoke from the inside. It came quite close to making him sick, but he really just wanted a drink and thought he could handle the foul stench. It was nothing but dusty, disgusting and damp inside, almost making him want to turn on his heel and leave. But he chose this dumpy place over the chilly air outside. Walking inside, he unbuttoned his leather trench coat as he made his way over to the bar. A couple older men were sagging against the backs of their chairs having drunk themselves unconscious. A few other "colorful" individuals were sitting just in front of the bar area. Only one older man sat at the far end of the bar, whereas the other end was vacant making it the most appealing area to sit. Not bothering to remove his coat, he took a seat on the creaky stool right in the corner near the possibly broken jukebox. Seconds later, a middle aged man from behind the bar peaked around the corner. "Whatcha havin’ buddy?" He asked, obviously not enjoying his job in this kind of establishment. "Just gimme a beer for now." He sighed and plunked a twenty on the bar making it clear that he intended to order more after. The bartender took a beer from the cooler behind him and snapped off the cap with a bottle opener and placed it in front of Killean. The first sip was like heaven to him. The cold beverage danced in his mouth when he swallowed, gathering to make it’s way down his throat. Another few gulps followed and he sighed, finally getting the simple taste of something that was so hard to find in the last fifteen minutes. Given he was only in the bar less than three minutes, it was rather surprising that two of the patrons began to create a ruckus already. As they began to get rough, another man rose from his seat. He pulled out a small pocket knife and held it for the other two men to see. "Get outta here before I have to throw yer asses out!" The fat man said, not having made it clear that he was the bouncer. Being almost as drunk and dirty as the rest of the crowd had done well to hide his identity. The two men pushed and shoved their way outside, probably ready to brawl on the front street which prompted the bartender to call the police. Killean turned back to the bar counter, glancing at the television which only showed an image of a newscast between the lines running up and down the screen. The older man from the other end of the bar made his way over to sit one seat over from Sirrajin much to the former Universal Champions dismay. His voice was rough yet inviting. "That TV’s on its last legs it is." He said in a spurt of broken English. It wasn’t that he was from another country, he was just from a different era altogether. It was an era that Killean didn’t much care for because it reminded him of his parents who abandoned him and his late sister so many years before. Killean just nodded in response to the comment fired his way. His lack of reply did not discourage the man. "That’s some bandage you have around your head son. You gets into a fight or sumthin’?" Killean turned to face the man after the comment. He was about to reply when the man recognized him, much to the wrestler’s chagrin. "Hey, you’re that guy from that there rasslin’ show. Killean is it?" His lack of hesitation made it quite obvious that the man was at least a fan of the wrestling scene. Less than impressed that the man could recognize him, he just nodded in response and turned back to take yet another swig of the cold beer. But the man wouldn’t stop his pursuit of information. "I saw what happened to you the other night son." Killean smirked a little and let the man continue. "It’s quite understandable that you’d come here and drown your sorrows after sumthin’ like that. I know what it’s like." That final comment peaked The Supreme Machine’s attention. Turning to the old man, he searched his eyes for feeling. But Sirrajin was still doubtful that the man knew how he felt. "I don’t know if I should ask, but how would you know how I am feeling? I have fifteen stitches in my forehead to close a gash that is still bleeding a little. I got betrayed for the countless time by someone who I thought was some type of friend. What exactly could be worse than that?" Killean was confident the man would keep his mouth shut and mind his own business. But to his surprise, the man persisted again and this time it surprised him that he was so fast to respond. "I kinda figured you’d ask. Son, look at my clothes, do I look like a guy who’s been down on his luck?" Killean held up a finger when the man finished, gulping back the rest of his brew and signaling the bartender over. "Jack and coke next please." He then turned to the old man again and looked him over closer. The old man had hair that was combed over, poorly hiding the bald spot underneath. His clothes were clean and wrinkle free and he had a scent of cologne on him. He was hardly the image of a man who had difficulties but there was still the past. "I can’t read your mind old man. I presume there has been something." Killean took the safe route. He didn’t doubt the man nor did he confirm anything. He took a sip of his new drink that the barkeep left in front of him and faced the man again. "Well I’ve had my ups and downs. I may not look like it, but I’m a veteran. ‘Nam was the name of the game. I had my whole team with me. When I walked off the battle field, not a single man was standing out of the bunch. Twelve men fell that day. I lost all my confidence and I was certain that I’d be next when I next went to battle. But I was demoted to lieutenant and I had this hard ass Sergeant on my ass the rest of the way. He was the born leader and I was just the follower. That was how it should have been from the beginning." Killean took a few more sips as the man talked. He found himself interested in the man’s story and was listening, not bothering to notice that a couple females actually set foot in the grubby looking bar. They sat at the other end of the bar where the old man originally came from. He finished his drink as the old man continued on. He listened to stories of lies, deceit and then victory. Thoughts ran roughshod through his head. Killean actually felt bad at this point for sounding like his situation was really all that bad. Reality was; it was nothing in comparison to what this man had been through in his life. "After I left the war, I’d also lost that same Sergeant. But it was that man who taught me the most important lesson. Once when we were resting in our bunker, I got to talking with him. He noticed that I was down and really had no push. So he told me, ‘If you remember anything from this experience, remember this… when you don’t think things can get worse, imagine what your loved ones would feel like if you had died in this war. Don’t let that happen. Also, music can be the cure for anything. Pick a song and when things get bad, concentrate on that song. It can be the medicine you need.’ I took his advice and I have used it to this day. It took me years to find a song that I could listen to." Killean had finished his second drink by this point and signaled the barkeep for another of the same. The old man made his way over to the jukebox beside Killean and looked for the song while he fished for a quarter in his pocket. He pulled the coin out and slipped it into the machine and pressed a few buttons. Nothing happened. "It takes a kick or two sometimes." The old man gave the machine a kick and it jumped to life. The lights came on and he was quick to select a song. He walked back to his seat as the song started and the tune was unmistakable. It was Pink Floyd. "Comfortably Numb… that’s the song I chose son." Killean and the old man sat and listened to the song. As the song was approaching the end the old man began to sing the tune quietly so the rest of the bar wouldn’t hear him. Killean however heard him loud and clear. "When I was a child I caught a fleeting glimpse Out of the corner of my eye. I turned to look but it was gone I cannot put my finger on it now The child is grown, The dream is gone. I have become comfortably numb." The song completed and the old man sighed. Killean could sense that he was relaxed and free of stress. Sirrajin himself only wished he could feel that way right now. Contrary to what he had thought, the alcohol was not doing it. "You understand what I was aiming for son?" The man questioned as he pushed the beer bottle he had to the back of the bar. Killean had finished his third drink and pushed his glass forward with the twenty, leaving the money for payment and tip. "Sort of, it made me feel that my problems were not that important in comparison." "It’s not that your problems aren’t important, it’s just that you should be able to overcome them with simple therapy, such as music." The man was right. Killean had done that before but the urge for alcohol seemed to overcome him. That was something he didn’t want to make a habit of, last thing he needed was to become addicted. Killean nodded as the man made his way over to the nearby coat rack to grab his leather coat. He slipped it around his frail arms and made a final comment to Killean. "Well son, I hope I was able to help in some way. My wife hates it when I am out longer than a couple hours at a bar. Gotta go." Strange as it was, Killean had enjoyed talking to the old man. It was time to take his personal problems by the scruff of their proverbial neck and squeeze the control out of them. "Thanks guy. Say, what’s your name anyways?" Killean asked as the man turned to leave. "Name’s Tom son. Nice talking with you." And he turned and walked through the bar and out the door. Killean turned to the bar and realized that Tom left something for him there. Picking up the material object, it practically floated in his hand as he read it. It was a military patch. "91st Squad Infantry. 1st Lieutenant Thomas Sirrajin." Killean’s heart sank deep into his stomach as his eyes glazed over as he read the patch maybe ten times not wanting to believe what he read. How could he not see this? On top of everything, the man who disappeared and deserted him and his late sister was sitting beside him just a moment ago. He abused him as a child and Killean let him go without extracting some sort of revenge that he promised he would get for so many years now if he ever saw his father again. Now, Killean just let him walk out the door and out of his life… again. Killean sprung from his seat with the energy and spirit of a child, almost running down a drunken man standing beside another table. He burst through the door and out onto the sidewalk. He quickly looked left to right multiple times, trying to catch a glimpse of his deceitful parent. Many buses and taxi cabs drove by along with a plethora of other family cars. No one was standing on the sidewalk that matched his description and he came to the realization that he would probably never again see his father. He held the patch in his hand and looked down at it, reading it over again as if it had some sort of power over him. His mind raced and his heart pounded. He couldn’t believe what just happened. He couldn’t believe that he wanted another drink. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ An uncomfortable silence joined the cold wind that blew around the rooftops bouncing off the buildings in the downtown area. The darkness enveloped the entire scene as the camera finally came to rest on a figure standing on the edge of a building probably 8 stories high. The hood covered his head and the long trench coat was being caught up in the cold breeze, flailing at his side. "Can you hear it?" The rough voice of Killean quipped, trying to sound the least bit sober after the nights earlier events proved otherwise. "Can you hear it? The wind is howling, playing its own little tune combining with the environmental orchestra of this winter season. Everyone who hears it could hear it differently, thinking it was natures own message being delivered to them. Many could think that it was a soothing sound. Many could also think it was a violent sound. But many wouldn’t think much at all would they? Plenty if people don’t delve into a thought process that deep for something as simple as the sound of the wind. But I do." Killean stepped away from the ledge of the rooftop and walked further away from it. He had his head bowed, trying to stay covered as best as he could. "I have the deep thought process that most don’t even realize exists. I think about everything that happens to me often and with plenty of complication. I think about the ifs, the ands, the buts and even the maybes. But I tend to never think about the whys. Why do certain things happen? Why does life take me and many others in a certain direction? Why did the man whose plain image haunted me all my life enter it once again even only for a few minutes? Why did he torture me? He knew who I was. But maybe it was for the best. The why of the situation is simple. I would have killed the mother fucker where he stood had I known who he was." "Simply put, I now have to look at the whys more often. Step one will be this Wednesday at PRIME’s ReVolution 84. My supposed friend betrayed me again after we kind of had a mutual understanding. But that is first. I am thinking about the second item on the agenda. A little thing called The Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. GTT… FIVE!" Killean looked towards the camera as he flashed his face and held up his hands as if to mimic lights going off, sort of celebrating the annual tournament PTC holds. "GTT Five. One hundred twenty-eight of the best wrestlers this world has ever seen will be competing for one prize. That one prize is the right to tell one hundred twenty-seven others that you are the best. And now I look at the whys." "Why is Killean Sirrajin in this tournament?" "Simply put, it’s rather difficult to have a tournament to crown the best without the best being involved. Not having a PTC record to speak of, my PRIME record speaks for itself." "Why is Michael Benson important?" "The man who I face in round one. He is important for the all too familiar reason of being in the wrong place at the wrong time. Being chosen to face a masquerade, a man who no longer wants to know his true identity must be difficult for you. It’s like the running of the bulls. There are the runners and there are the bulls. You my poor boy… are a runner. Your entire existence in this tournament will branch itself on the fact that you cannot match up man to man, face to face. You are here to prove a point? That point will fade in the obscurity of disappointment and brought forth will be a new point, a new reality." "Why should Michael Benson be penalized and sentenced to the darkness in which sixty-four other people will linger for all of GTT Five eternity?" "This darkness, it is a realm of pain, of torture and of limitless agony. To be considered the best, you must beat the best. Unfortunately for one Michael Benson, it will not be his night. Gone is the hope that existed when you entered this tournament. Gone is the glory that you have experienced in your career. Gone is the awareness, for you are just too stubborn to accept that you will fall in round one. I don’t need the hype the PRIME brings with it. I don’t need the hype that my career brings with it. I will prove myself. Step up and I will knock you down." Approaching the door leading into the building, Killean turns back towards the camera. His eyes are wild with anger, his face contorted with rage and his stature, beaming with nothing but confidence. "You are about to battle the best. Try not to hurt yourself… kiddo."
