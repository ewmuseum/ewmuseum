Eternal: The End (Chapters 5, 6, 7)

25 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-25 Eternal: The End Chapter 5: Memory Walk The crystal blue surface of his eyes glimmered in the sunlight, as the slight breeze caused his pure white hair with sky blue streaks to cover part of his face. Kasper Sky stood there on the flawless green grass-covered ground, his legs frozen to the spot. Elsewhere, his sharp sense of hearing was able to hear the birds chirping in the nearby trees, in the midst of that quiet afternoon. He was the only person there in that particular wide piece of land, populated with white slabs sticking out of the ground. Somehow, the scenery calmed him, even as a small chill ran up his spine. It had been forever since he last came here, but it seemed just like yesterday when everything happened. He still remembered himself standing there all those years ago, yet generations didn’t seem to taint this place. He still recalled when those tears came flowing down his eyes, even as he fiercely tried to not show his emotion. With that, a sad smile slowly played on Kasper’s lips as he momentarily closed his eyes. A couple of seconds passed, its significance noted. Kasper Sky slowly opened his eyes, focusing his sight at the words on the white slab in front of him. Named Christine Milner, with the period of her life declared as unknown, she was similar to the other white slabs around Kasper, with only one difference. She was his mother. It all ended on that fateful night in Belfast, Northern Ireland. Kasper’s long blonde hair came up to his shoulders, his crystal blue eyes glistening in the bedroom light. His pale complexion seemed almost pure, as he came up to his mother, looking deep into her eyes. Kasper tugged at his mother’s right hand sleeve, slowly feeling the cotton between his fingers. His face started showing signs of concern, slowly kneeling down beside the bed. He buried his face deep in his mother’s hands, feeling the cold rushing through his warm body. “Kasper, look at me. I have to tell you something, even though I know this isn’t the right time. But you must hear me out, and try to understand.” Christine whispered warmly to Kasper, making every effort to make her words clear. Kasper looked up at his mother, and waited. Kasper kept his silence, not knowing what words he was supposed to speak. Christine motioned Kasper to come closer, wanting to whisper in his ears. “Kasper, you possess the gift of the heavens. I’ve seen it in your eyes since the day you were born. I’ve felt it within you, the pureness of your soul. But yet, only my death will trigger your eternal being. You won’t understand me now, and you won’t understand me until years from now, when you are at the peak of your life. Remember my words, dear Kasper. Remember them well.” Christine whispered those words softly in Kasper’s ear, closing her eyes, not wanting to tear. Kasper just listened without a word, and for a moment, complete silence overcame the room. Christine hesitated for a moment, before deciding to continue her words. “You have a sister, Kasper. Her name is Krystal. Find her you must. She shares the same gift; you need to let her know that. She needs to be revealed. Promise me, Kasper. And then maybe with time, the both of you will be able to seek the same answers. Answers that I can never tell you.” Christine spoke again, trying even harder this time to put the words out. Christine seemed to be in obvious pain, coughing badly, her frailty showing through her age. But yet she smiled. “Kasper turns ten next month, and this will be the first birthday that he will be celebrating without me.” Christine spoke to herself under her breath, taking a huge breath of air within her. That proved to be her last, as she closed her eyes for one final time, a tear slowing flowed down her right cheek, and right onto Kasper’s left hand. Kasper looked up, and the life of him faded away. Away into the very shadows of the night. She left the world that night, leaving a young boy to face his destiny by himself. A young boy who will later be known not by Kasper Milner, but as Kasper Sky. Kasper remembered that night all too well, maybe because it kept replaying itself over and over again in the depths of his mind. He grimaced, bringing his head down, not wanting to look at the words on the slab anymore. It was painful for him, too painful. He clenched his fists tight into a pale ball of skin, and glanced up slightly, peering at the eternal white slab from the corner of his eyes. “I promised you that I’ll return when I’ve fulfilled my promise,” he whispered softly, only audible to his ears. “Well…” “The day is finally here.” With all the sadness in his eyes, Kasper Sky still managed to force a soft smile to play on his lips. Chapter 6: A Change of Heart 72 Hours Earlier “You were outstanding in that match, simply –“ “Athena…” Kasper interrupted Athena’s words, letting her enthusiasm die off in a trail of the dark night. Athena turned her head in the direction of Kasper Sky, looking in his face in an enquiring voice. She kept her silence, not wanting to say another word. Kasper had been keeping to himself a lot lately, especially since that day when she saw him in the alley talking to himself. Something seemed to be disturbing him, but she couldn’t figure out what. Kasper ran his hand through the grass, feeling the roughness of the blade, before picking up a small pebble. Peering at it up close, he observed the texture of it. “I need to tell you something…” Clink. The pebble bounced off the glassy surface of the lake a couple of times, before sinking slowly. His eyes followed its movement, concentrating on it. “Actually, so do I…” Athena replied back, hesitation in her voice was clear. She looked away from Kasper, and stared across the lake, noticing the icy blue surface shining in the dim moonlight. “Really?” he replied back, surprised, his eyes widening. He didn’t know that Athena had something to tell him, and that had taken him aback. He had been preparing his words ever since his encounter with that mysterious girl, trying to find a balance in his words to tell Athena what he thinks is the truth. “You first then.” Clink. Another pebble bounced off the surface of the lake. And as before, it sunk after about three bounces. Athena turned and beamed at Kasper, her cheeks turning red. “Well, to continue where I left off, I think you were really outstanding in that match against Hell Itself in Round One of the GTT4. You looked completely focused on winning that match, and win it you did.” Athena continued on enthusiastically, with Kasper giving out a small comforting smile just to please her and not worry her more. He kept his silence, looking at her from the corner of his eye. He didn’t know what to say now that could help her feelings from what he was about to tell her. “Well, as long as you keep your focus for your Round Two match against Tony Stecchino, I’m sure you’ll be fine.” Athena tries to assure Kasper Sky, not aware of the other factors enveloping his mind. “Don’t worry Kasper, you will finish off what you’ve started in this tournament. I believe in you.” Athena urged on, placing a hand on Kasper’s lap, surprising him a bit. He observed her cold touch, almost icy, and looked at her. Observed her deep brown eyes, her tanned complexion was almost sparkling in the moonlight. Kasper forced a sheepish grin, wanting to get his mind off his thoughts for the moment. “I certainly hope so,” he whispered, peering at the imaginary solid surface of the lake. “You w…” “Is that what you wanted to tell me?” Kasper questioned, interrupting Athena as her sentence trailed off with a finish. She looked away from Kasper’s eyes, not wanting to gaze at him. “No, it isn’t.” She replied back, a tinge of hesitation in her voice. “Then what is it you wish to tell me?” Kasper turned away and picked up another pebble, thinking that Athena was going to take a while before she’d answer his question. “I think…that I have feelings for you.” Clink. Another pebble landed on the surface of the lake right after Athena’s words, as complete silence encased the surroundings. Kasper turned and peered at Athena, and looked down. Chapter 7: A Mystery Into A Mystery He winced at the thought at what had happened seventy-two hours earlier, when he stood up after Athena spoke those words, before muttering that he had to head somewhere. He walked away from Athena, drowning in her silence as he saw tears glazing down her cheeks, her deep brown eyes unblinking as she managed a smile. Somehow, the memory seemed to hurt him more now that it had entered his mind. But yet he managed a small smile, a sign that beautiful memories still existed in his mind. This was way before his mother’s death, before he could remember that he had a sister. The memory only came to him during his search for the sister that he never knew existed. Kasper looked up, observing the man standing in the shadows behind Krystal. He didn’t know why the man was there, looking at him. With a black trench coat and smooth black pants, Kasper didn’t like the look of him one bit. “Kasper?” Kasper turned his attention to the girl in front of him who had a sad look on her face. His sister Krystal was looking intently at him, peering at him through her brown eyes. She slowly grabbed both of Kasper’s hands and held it in hers. “I have to go somewhere. But I will see you again. I promise.” She spoke in a soft tone, trying to assure Kasper of her return. Krystal was only about two years younger than Kasper, which made her about six years of age on that day. And yes, that day was her birthday. “Why do you have to go? And who is that man? Have you told mother yet?” Kasper questioned her in a worried voice. Krystal hesitated, stealing a glance at the man behind her, who nodded his head. Kasper glared at him, anger rushing through his blood. She looked at Kasper again, “There’s something that I must do, something that mother need not know. Please keep this a secret, dear brother.” Kasper looked down, weighing his options. “Go ahead.” “I trust you in your choices. But just come back soon, or mother will become really worried.” He stressed on those words, trying to put some sense into his sister’s head. “I will. Trust me.” She spoke with such conviction, that Kasper actually managed a small smile. Krystal slipped her right hand into her pocket and produced a small silver locket attached to a silver chain. She looked at it with a small smile, and clenched a similar piece that she was wearing around her neck. “Wear this always. As long as it’s around your neck, it’ll be a symbol of our bonds of blood.” Krystal whispered, slipping the chain around Kasper’s neck, and took a step back. She smiled a sad smile, a sadness that even Kasper could not see at that time. Kasper nodded his head as a sign of approval. She hesitated but for a second and turned away, the man taking her right hand in his left, and led her away into the shadows of the night. That was to be the last time Kasper saw his sister’s face. That was the very last time. Kasper held the locket around his neck tight, his eyes closed in the midst of the silence. So beautiful, but yet so miserable at the same time. It was an amazing conflict of emotions. “Having mixed feelings huh?” Kasper turned around at the sound of a voice behind him. It was the mystery girl that he met in the street a couple of days back. He couldn’t think of how she would know that he would be here. The girl shook her head. “I see that you’re still not fond of words.” She smirked at him, her crystal blue eyes sparkling in the sunlight. “How do you know that I will be here?” Kasper questioned harshly. She gave out a short laugh, apparently amused at his anger. “You really should control that temper of yours. It might get you into trouble one of these days.” She replied with a mock innocent smile on her face while wagging her finger. “You were actually thinking that I wasn’t going to show up?” “That I’m going to let you win this game as easily as you think it will be?” “I mean, please…do not kid yourself.” She gave out another short laugh, flinging her hands into the air. Dressed in complete black, she seemed out of place on that beautiful afternoon with the birds chirping in the background behind her, but yet she seemed not to care. He wondered if she had even noticed. “What is it you want?” he continued, taking a step forward in her direction, and a step away from the white slab. The girl took a step closer as well, standing parallel to him. She smiled sweetly at him, her pale complexion gleaming. “Just for you to know the truth,” she whispered, making direct eye contact with Kasper. He was shocked. What truth, he asked himself. He glared at her, intensity in his eyes. “You know, sometimes I wish we have more time to get to know each other.” She spoke, her face serious all of a sudden. She looked down, and Kasper could have sworn that there was a soft touch of sadness apparent on her face. “But all regrets aside, I guess our relationship will be quite a short one.” She muttered, a playful smile on her lips. “What do you mean? I don’t quite understand.” “You will soon enough.” The girl pulled out a white handkerchief out of nowhere, and wiped her pale hands, smiling in amusement at the same time. Kasper didn’t quite understand. She slipped her hand into her pocket and pulled something out that he couldn’t quite see. And then from the other hand, she pulled out a Beretta from her black jacket. Kasper’s eyes widened. He didn’t know what was the meaning of all this. He didn’t even know the girl until a few nights ago. “What are you doing?” A few seconds passed, before her voice was heard again, this time in a crisp tone. “Just paying your dues for you.” “What dues?” he enquired. She didn’t answer, but instead clenched the tiny object in her left hand, while pointing the gun directly at him in her right hand. Kasper stared directly in her eyes, surprised that his blood had turned cold. “You have no reason to fire a bullet through my body.” “Or maybe through your head.” She replied, grinning wickedly. “What reason do you have?” She shrugged her shoulders and leaned her head slightly. “Enough reason to actually fire this.” She replied. Kasper froze when he heard the familiar clicking of a gun’s hammer cocking, his eyes widening. “What are you going to do, kill me?” he whispered, just loud enough for her to hear. She smiled, a tear falling down her cheek, “It would only be right.” “Life wasn’t supposed to end this way. Not this way.” Kasper resigned in a soft tone. “I agree. But maybe destiny has other plans.” She whispered back at him, “Maybe this is the end, Kasper Sky.” She placed her index finger on the trigger, applying a little bit of pressure as Kasper concentrated his sight on it. Without him noticing, she dangled the object that she clenched on her left hand, on her left index finger. Click. The birds fluttered out of the trees, as the sound echoed through the breeze, disrupting the silence of the surroundings. It all happened in less than a split second. With Kasper Sky standing there, his crystal blue eyes facing the barrel of the gun, he noticed one thing. The silver locket dangling on the girl’s finger. His eyes widened as his mind kept racing through various thoughts of his life, finally focusing on one thing in particular… The thunderous crack of a gun being fired directly at him. You know, in this world there will be moments where you wish that certain things had changed. Situations where it makes your thoughts wander back in time and question what complications would have arisen had some things been different. At times you may even wish that you weren’t part of this painful game of sorrows, that you were just an observer. Nothing more, nothing less. Wishing that you were just an ongoing passerby within the realms of reality, and not having to stand trial for the sins of your past. Sadly, not all of us have that privilege. Have you ever wondered that maybe parts of your memories have been altered to suit the determination of your frame of mind? That maybe everything isn’t as factual as you seem to think it is. Yeah, I think I am right in that sense. Sometimes our minds beg us to believe something so much, that we actually began to believe that it is true. So much so over time, we know that it is true and refuse to think otherwise. Influenced by our state of weakening emotions, we subconsciously changed what our past actually is, into something that we want our past to be. And then maybe you never knew all along that there was a flaw in your memories. That some things just don’t seem right. But yet your mind forces you to take a stand, and believe in things you don’t actually believe in. I know, it does sound really complicated. But I can assure you that this won’t take long for you to understand. What will be running through your mind, when you finally look Death in the eye and realize that your whole life was just one big mess? That you turned a dream into a nightmare even beyond your wildest waking imaginations. And as you look back within the constraints of time, you finally realized you made a mistake, but you are too late to rectify it as Death arrived too soon. I thought I would never say that. Ever. I never thought that one day time would finally turn its back on me. That whatever mistakes I made throughout this damned life, had finally caught up with me. I guess the only thing left for me to do now in this world is to look Death in the eye, and admit my mistakes. I know you want to hear it, that you have been waiting to hear it ever since you first knew me. That you were longing for the day that I had finally fallen from grace and began to drown in the depths of my own damnation. I guess now is the time to atone for everything. I guess now is the time to admit to everything. Ok then, yes, I admit it. I made a mistake. TO BE CONTINUED
