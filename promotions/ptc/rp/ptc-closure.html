"Closure"

16 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Reflection (Present) Training always cleared my mind. It isn’t actually the physical exertion of it all that cleansed my soul, it is simply knowing. Knowing that I am prepared. Fully, truly, prepared for whatever lies ahead of me. In this case, it happens to be the Infinite Gauntlet open invitational and the Super Series, all within a month. I don’t think it’s even possible to be fully prepared for such a challenge, but I’m sure as hell going to try. Block, palm strike, parry, kick… And despite it all, I can’t quite get this one out of my head. It’s strange; things like this have never even begun to bother me before and, truth be told, it’s nullifying my concentration completely. Block, side step, knee, elbow… But I know precisely why it is getting to me now. It’s her. My angel. Aeryn. It’s what she told me about my past, or rather when she reconfirmed my own suspicions Parry, side step, kick, forearm… None of that matters now. I can’t get the picture of the pregnant woman’s face out of my head. It’s haunting me. The innocence…I have to do something. I haven’t been assigned this task, but I have to – no need to – do something about it regardless. I need this issue to be resolved, so that I continue with my life and regain the concentration that I will need to succeed. I need closure. Politics (Six Hours Ago) On this occasion, Aeryn and I met upon yet another nondescript rooftop around the center of New York - not too far away from my apartment – and as usual she was late. Every time she called me to assign yet another meaningless task – at least meaningless to me – I always got a knotted nauseating feeling at the pit of my stomach. I’ve been trying to ignore it for the better part of eleven months, but I fear it’s getting to the stage where I cannot hide from it any longer. That thought alone scares me to death. I shook the thought from my head; extra baggage I don’t really need. Aeryn arrived three minutes late, moving from an easterly direction from rooftop to rooftop. She must have walked – or drove – some of the way, as she only made it onto a rooftop several buildings back. Of course, she doesn’t have to tell me any of this. I already know. I saw her; I smelt her and I heard her coming. I had my back to her when she landed on the same rooftop. She attempted to tip toe up behind me and it made me smile. She actually thought she was capable of sneaking up on me. Obviously, her genetically modified senses were not as astute as my own. “Don’t bother,” I told her, whirling around. I saw her attempt to mask the surprised look that had so obviously been spread across her face and couldn’t help but chuckle. “You’re not that good…” “What the hell are you talking about?” She questioned, keeping up with the pretence. I could tell she was feeling a little embarrassed and so decided to drop the matter. Besides, I’m here for business not pleasure. Not that I get much of the latter these days. “Forget about it,” I replied. “What did you call me about this time?” Aeryn handed me a piece of paper, I hadn’t noticed she’d been carrying before. It was a colour picture of some guy, perhaps forty to fifty years of age, white hair, overly tanned, wearing an expensive suit. I recognised him from somewhere, but for the life of me I couldn’t tell why. As usual, I don’t like where this is going. Aeryn could tell that I was having a hard time putting a name to the face and so decided to put me out of my misery. “Cornelius Johnson.” I recognise the name from somewhere too. I still couldn’t place it. I glanced at her blankly, hoping she would just go ahead and fill me in without actually having to ask. A pride thing I guess. Aeryn sighed. “Don’t you watch the news?” I shook my head. “No…it angers me.” “Cornelius Johnson is a high profile politician who may be making a run for presidency next year,” Aeryn explained. “And he needs your help.” “My help?” I asked her. “How does he even know I exist?” “Our industrious employer has very strong connections with Johnson,” Aeryn further explained. “And wishes to maintain such a connection.” I thought about the prospect for a second. From what I know of him, my employer doesn’t exactly strike me as the type of man who goes out on a limb for just anyone. He especially doesn’t risk exposing me unless it is extremely important. It just doesn’t make any sense. “But why?” I questioned. “Why would our employer go out on a limb to maintain a connection with him?” “Our employer has practically funded Johnson’s political campaign single-handedly in order to maintain his allegiance,” Aeryn replied. I noted the word ‘single-handedly’ in that sentence. To fund a political campaign on your own took some serious money. Just how rich was my employer? And why am I not being paid? “Obviously, when Johnson gets into office he will be expected to reciprocate our employers generous contribution.” I sighed. “Meaning, Johnson will be expected to pass a few laws that will aid our employer continue his shady dealings?” Aeryn grinned. “Pretty much.” “So,” I began, preparing myself for the worst. “What does he need me to do?” Aeryn handed me another picture, this time of a beautiful blonde woman. I don’t recognise this face. “It’s Johnson’s pregnant mistress. She’s been taken captive by some ‘political activists’ and is being used to blackmail Johnson.” “Blackmail? How and what for?” I asked, trying to get a clear picture of what was happening in my head. “They say they’re going to kill her if Johnson doesn’t step down,” Aeryn explained. “And obviously our employer can’t let that happen.” “I take it our employer already has the location of the woman?” I asked, idly wondering whether any detective work would be necessary. Not the greatest talent in my arsenal. Aeryn nodded and handed me the address on a torn piece of paper. “Of course.” It all made sense to me now, for what it was worth. I handed the pictures and the address back to Aeryn and made my way to end of the building, ready to climb down the fire escape. Aeryn glanced at the pictures, then at me. “Won’t you need these?” “No,” I told her, not even bothering to turn around. “Photographic memory.” Aeryn’s eyes widened. “Impressive.” “I know.” Rescue (Four Hours Ago) I perched myself across from the target building and waited. As of yet, there had been no sign of the perpetrators or the girl. The sun has just come up, so hopefully they’ll be undoing the blinds soon. Before getting here, I had to return to my apartment for the suit. I hated it. It’s a black, Kevlar-laced, stress suit – designed for the durability I need to do my job. It also covered my face to maintain my identity. The problem was, it was completely black and made me look like an extra from a rather successful S&M party. It’s also a little too restrictive for liking. These thoughts evaporated into the air, as the blinds opened and man behind them undertook a length yawn accompanied by an equally lengthy stretch. He saw me squatting on the building top across the way and froze in horror – I mean who wouldn’t under the circumstances? I managed to catch a glimpse of the heavily pregnant woman behind him, lay down on a mattress. That is all I need to see. Immediately and with great speed, I hurled myself across the open void between buildings and directed my momentum toward the designated window. In mid air, I scrunched myself up into a ball and placed my forearm over my eyes to protect them from the glass. I crashed through the window with impressive velocity, thumping into the yawning captor, knocking him unconscious on the decorative pine flooring. The impact alone broke at least three of his ribs, and the large piece of glass sticking out from his leg didn’t look to good either. I made sure it hadn’t cut a heartery, before pulling out a few nominal pieces from my forearm – the glass did not penetrate the Kevlar suit. The woman sat up on her bed, hopeful, yet obviously frightened, by both the situation and me. That is to be expected. Another one of her captors then rushed into the room a knife held high above his head. With my left hand I grabbed underneath the elbow of the knife-carrying arm, and then with my right I struck out at the man’s neck with the gap between my forefinger and thumb. He fell to the floor in a heap, coughing profusely. I held my hand out toward the woman and smiled, for what it was worth. “W-who are you?” She asked. We don’t have time for this. “A friend,” I replied. “Johnson sent me.” A spark of delight shone in her eyes and without further hesitation grabbed my hand. “I knew he’d come for me!” “Yeah,” I said, “whatever. Can we get a move on?” I led her to the window, but I could tell she was more than a little reluctant - and rightly so. “Can’t we just go through the front door?” She asked me. “No,” I replied, rather too sternly. Then I noticed the guy I took down with a thrust to the throat, stir awake. I turned, sharply; to make sure he still wasn’t much of a threat. He could barely move, I must have taken him down harder than I expected. I couldn’t help but smile. “Wait!” He bellowed. I really should’ve just left, but I didn’t. “You can’t take her back to Johnson, he’ll kill her!” The woman became incredibly agitated. “No! He wouldn’t do that!” “You’re his pregnant mistress,” the man replied. “What do you think will happen when this goes public?” “He’ll be disgraced,” I blurted out. “And it’ll hurt his presidency campaign,” the man added. “Do you think he’ll really allow that to happen?” The woman angrily strode over to the man and kicked him hard in the ribs. I was beginning to like this woman. The man desperately clutched his already damaged ribs and lapsed back into unconsciousness. This time I took the woman roughly by the arm and lead her to the window. Religion (Two Hours Ago) We had to travel slowly – well slowly for me anyhow - across the rooftops on the account of the baby an all and so we arrived two hours later at Senator Johnson’s stately home two hours later. One of his many bodyguards ushered us into his office area. I guess his wife wasn’t home this weekend. By the look of his bodyguards, I’m actually quite surprised that my assistance was required at all. As soon as we entered the man’s office area, the woman rushed into his arms and he embraced her gratefully. Not the actions of a man planning to kill his mistress and child – at least not on the surface. “For what profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world, and lose his own soul? Or what will a man give in exchange for his soul?” Johnson exclaimed, glaring at me over the shoulder of his mistress coldly. I hadn’t got a clue what the hell he was talking about, but the woman clearly had. “Matthew 16:26?” Senator Johnson smiled. “Of course, my dear. Why don’t you go and rest whilst I talk business with our new friend here?” The woman smiled and kissed Senator Johnson on the cheek. I nearly threw up in my mask. She smiled and mouthed the words ‘thank you’ to me as I left. For the first time since the incident, I wondered whether what the man told me about Senator Johnson was actually true. The Senator strode over to me and extended his hand. I reluctant grabbed it and shook. The Senator retracted his hand and rubbed it pain fully. “Sorry,” I offered. I was still a little pumped up from the action. “Forget about it,” Johnson replied, making his way over to his rather sizeable drinks cabinet. “Drink?” “No,” I told him, “I don’t drink.” The Senator seemed to be a little taken aback by the statement. “Well, how the hell do you get through the day?” “With great difficulty.” Senator chuckled at this, whilst pouring himself a generous glass of Bourbon from his stylish decanter. “I like you. Your funny.” “I’m really not,” I told him and meant it. Senator Johnson made his way to his desk and slumped down into his luxurious leather chair. “Your employer tells me that your one cold son of a bitch, huh…I never did catch your name…” Clever. He’s trying to make me slip up, trying to get my name out of me by the slip of my tongue. But he’s not nearly as clever as he thinks he is. “That’s because I don’t have one.” Senator Johnson nodded in response, realising that his little trick was ineffective on me. “Nevertheless, you’re a cold one,” he repeated. “So I don’t really think you’ll care when I tell you what I’m going to do with the girl you just rescued.” I don’t like where this is going, could the captor have been right? “Probably not.” “I’m going to kill her,” He explained, a rather sadistic smile spread all over his face. “And our unborn child.” Her captor was right. I felt a little nauseous by the Senator’s revelation, but I did not show it. “I see.” “Do you have a problem with that?” He asked, grinning maliciously. “No,” I lied. “I can’t have their existence exposed to the public, you see,” He explained. “Having this go public would ruin my political career.” “I understand,” I replied. “Therefore, I tell you, her many sins have been forgiven – for she loved much. But he who has been forgiven little loves little,” Senator Johnson quoted. “Luke 7:47. Now if you’ll excuse me I have an important meeting to attend – I trust you remember your way out?” I simply nodded in response and left the office, sick to my stomach with what I’d just heard. Mercy (Present) Finding her was the easiest part of all. The good Senator didn’t want to do the dirty work himself or have it done anywhere near him and he sure as hell didn’t want the body to be found in a hurry, so the docks was the natural place for his henchman to take her. I followed the inconspicuous white Sedan from the Senators house, managing to keep up with the vehicle by jumping from rooftop to rooftop – my preferred mode of transport. I had no idea just how fast I could go this way, either. I have to say it was impressive. When the car stopped, the hefty henchmen clambered out, but the woman did not. They all made their way to the rear of the car and began pushing the car into the ocean. Perfect. I stayed out of sight, and managed to get into the filthy water before the car did. When the car did topple over the edge, I was ready. I got to the car, ripped the car door away and grabbed the woman all within about the twenty seconds mark. She was out of the water and breathing okay before the car even reached the bottom. She was clearly upset, though. I mean, who wouldn’t be? “Why?” She blubbered. “Why would he do this to me?” I sighed, trying to think of something comforting to say. Nothing immediately sprung to mind. “Never trust a politician,” I told her. “But he said he loved me!” She screamed. “Well, he was lying,” I replied. She had no answer to this and simply continued to weep. I held her in my arms, as much to comfort her as to keep her warm. Aeryn turned up half an hour later, in a black unmarked truck. She regarded the girl crying in my arms and smiled. “Well, you’re a fast mover!” She exclaimed. “It’s not the time for jokes,” I told her sternly. “I need your help.” “You need my help?” She asked, almost bewildered. “That’s a first.” I opened my mouth to say her name, but then rethought the action. Probably isn’t the greatest idea, giving out her identity like that. “I thought you were supposed get the girl to Senator Johnson anyway?” She asked. “I did,” I replied, “but he didn’t want to risk the chance of the blackmail thing happening again, so he…” “…decided to kill her,” Aeryn filled in. “Nice.” “The Senator thinks she is dead and I intend to let him continue to think that,” I explained. “Great,” She said. “But what can I do? Don’t get me wrong I’m glad that you rescued her – more than you’ll ever know – but I’m not sure our employer would agree with me.” “Well he should,” I told her. “Because I’ve just provided him with extra leverage to use against Senator Johnson if he gets into office – an extra leverage he knows nothing about. This’ll ensure Johnson plays ball.” Aeryn thought about it for a second. “You’re right. He’ll go for this.” Aeryn turned back to her black unmarked truck and beckoned for two rather sturdy individuals to come over. They did, gently took the pregnant woman back to the car. “So,” Aeryn began, drawing closer to me. “You’re the hero, huh? I never pegged you to be the type.” I ignored her playful comments. I knew that she was happy that I’d done the right thing for once, but it just wasn’t time to make light of the situation. I think deep down Aeryn understood that. “Why’d you do it?” She asked, her tone of voice more serious now. I began to walk away; I recognised the fact that I need to be alone right now. “I needed closure, that’s all.” “That’s all?” She asked. “That’s all,” I replied and left. Perhaps now, I could gain the concentration I so fervently desired. Perhaps.
