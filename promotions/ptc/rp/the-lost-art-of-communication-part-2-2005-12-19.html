The Lost Art of Communication, Part 2

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 "Mom! What are you doing calling at this hour? It must be like 4am over there." Doreen did her best to keep her tone light and airy, but inside she was shaking with fear. Why the hell was her mother calling? She wasn’t due to phone ‘til next Sunday. "Darling, this is costing me a fortune, can you just put me on to James please?" Doreen froze, panic surging through her. She couldn’t possibly put Coach on the line. He might say something he shouldn’t… Or perhaps she could put him on? Her mother obviously suspected something was wrong, why call otherwise? If Coach rambled psychotically down the line, then surely her mother would have to come home, put everything right? Covering the mouthpiece with her meaty hand she whispered to her mentor, "It’s Mom, she’s asking for you." Coach looked up from the tabloid he had been staring at vacantly, his face drawn and wan. "Huh? Can‘t speak, just can‘t." A small part of Coach listened to himself and rattled the bars. "You idiot! This is your chance, get help!" Surprised to find herself so relieved, she cobbled together an excuse, "Mom, Coach isn’t here. He’s uh, helping Martha down at the Laundromat, the machines bust again." Her voice dripping with sarcasm, Gillian Gervais, Reader’s Digest’s Motivational Speaker of the Year, 2001, purred down the line, "It’s wonderful that he’s helping the maid with the laundry, but also quite impossible considering I just spoke to Martha, who just happens be in Los Angeles this week. What’s going on there Doreen?" "Nothing Mom… honest!" An icy sweat dripped from her brow. Lying was not something Doreen relished doing, or indeed had much experience of. But what made it all the more difficult was the internal battle that raged inside her. Half of her wanted to own up now, have everything go back to normal, turn Coach in so he could get professional help perhaps, and the other half wanted to continue with the deception, continue with the tournament. Was she being selfish, or truly looking out for her friend? "Mom?" A dial tone kicked in. God bless the Eastern European lackadaisical approach to telecommunications. Doreen had bought a little leeway, but not much. It was time to decide. GTT5, round 2, in or out? Sharifa quietly put down the extension. It wasn’t the first or last time she’d eavesdropped on her sister’s calls, rare though they were. So Mom was onto them? This was bad. The last few weeks had been great. She’d never felt so free. She’d taken full advantage of Coach being spazzed and hadn’t been to school in what seemed like forever. Of course, Doreen only knew about one or two of the days she’d played hooky; Coach had Doreen down at the gym so much that she practically had the house to herself. She wasn’t ready to give it all up. There was so much more she wanted to do, like find out more about Brian Arson! For a brief moment, she regretted that she’d told her friends she was away on her mother’s tour promoting her current book, "Fight Fat to Live Fit!". Sharifa giggled. She always giggled when she thought about her mother’s job. What would happen to her career if her adoring public ever found out that Gillian Gervais, nemesis to all lipids, was the mother of Doreen Pepperpot, fattest girl in two counties? Not even the neighbours knew. Sharifa felt no shame that she viewed her mothers dark secret as a savings bond to be cashed in some day. But in the meantime, she had more important things to do. She had to make sure her sister, her only access to the man she wanted in ways she was too young to understand, stayed in the tournament. Doreen had flipped the coin a dozen times, and it still wasn’t turning up the right answer. Maybe she was asking the wrong questions? She was pathetic! Next she’d be writing out the alphabet to make a ouija board! No. There was only one way to make the right decision, and that was to figure out what was best for her, her sister, and her friends eroded psyche. And the only way to do that, was to ask them. Doreen tapped lightly on her sister’s door before walking into the room. "GET OUT! You can’t just barge into my room like that!" Sharifa practically spat venom. "I’m sorry okay? I knocked and you mustn’t have heard me. I just wanted to talk to you, but if that’s so inconvenient, I’ll come back later." Sometimes, Sharifa made Doreen want to just pick her up and shake her until she stopped being such a bossy cow. Turning to leave, she caught a glimpse of what was on her little sister’s desktop; a picture of Brian Arson. "I’m sorry hon, I know I’ve been a bit narky. It’s not fair on you, especially when you’re trying to help me by researching my opponent. Found out anything interesting?" "About who? What the fuck are you on about, Moby?" Doreen nods towards the computer screen. "Brian Arson of course! He’s big isn’t he? I’m going to need all the help I can if I’m going to have a shot at knocking him out… are you okay?" Sharifa sat slumped in her chair, eyes wide and her mouth hanging open. It took a moment for her to regain her composure. Doreen’s opponent was Brian Arson? This complicated things. If only she’d listened before when Doreen had been droning on about the stupid wrestling. What was she going to do? Her sister couldn’t beat Arson… it would be wrong. But equally, he couldn’t beat Doreen, or she, Sharifa, would never get another ringside seat, never see him in person again! "You always ruin everything! GET OUT!" As Doreen slammed the door behind her, she realised she hadn’t even gotten a chance to ask Sharifa what she wanted her to do about the tournament. She found coach in the garage, rummaging through a tool box. "Whatcha doing Coach?", she trilled. It took a couple of minutes for him to stop what he was doing and focus on her. "I don’t know." He brought his hands up, inches away from his face and appeared to be examining them intensely. "I don’t know how it happens, but sometimes they just get away from me, know what I mean?" Doreen didn’t know. Her heart sank. He was obviously having one of his bad days. Most of the time, he could get by and appear almost normal. Unless you knew how things should normally be, that is. Most of his illness centred around his false belief that Doreen was more than a sad 17 year old with no friends. In Coach’s reality, she was an amazing sports person, with enough talent for two people. In Coach’s world, she was two people; the friend he had nurtured for years and the son he had barely known anymore at the time of the accident. "Coach? What am I going to do about Arson?" "Arson? No Doreen. Arson is wrong. You’ll get arrested and then they’ll take your title away." "I don’t have a title…" Doreen stopped herself mid-sentence. She wasn’t going to go down this road again. Reminding Coach even vaguely about the accident caused him so much pain, and he didn’t look ready to deal with it just then. The last remaining part of Coach’s mind that was clear shouted out into the chaos, Listen to her! She’s right. She doesn’t have a title. She’s not who you think she is! How can you do this to her? How can you let her believe that by living out your dreams for your son she’s helping you? All you’re doing is delaying the inevitable. For Christ’s sake James. If you care about her at all, let her go. You can’t protect her in the ring. She’s not the professional he was. She’ll be ripped apart." "Are you okay, Coach?" "Sure I am sweetie, sure I am. What are you doing out here? Shouldn’t you be in the ring practising? Arson’s not going to pin himself you know!" Doreen laughed politely at his lame joke. "I need to ask you something, and I need you to be as honest as you can." Coach quieted the voices as best he could. His son had something important to say. "Do you want me to stop this? Do you want me to call your nurse? GTT5 isn’t that big a deal. If it helps you, I’ll step down, let you get what you need done done and maybe stay with Aunt Felicia in Memphis ‘til Mom gets back. What do you think?" "Yes! Good girl, that’s exactly what I need. I knew you’d come through for me. I’m sorry for putting you through all this. I didn’t mean to harm you or your sister. You must believe that. You’re far too young, too goddamn innocent to be in the world of professional wrestlers!" Unfortunately, that part of Coach’s mind was muted by the matrix of thoughts and illusions that surrounded and protected the sensitive core of his pain, the memories of the accident, and strived to keep the status quo. The voices closed around in a cacophony of noise, eager to drown out the voice of reason. "Coach?" "What do you mean, drop out? I didn’t raise you to be a loser! You’re in this tournament to win. If you’re not going to do your best then maybe you should just leave, go your own way, and fail. You’re weak, constantly looking for an easy way out, but I’ll always push you, and one day you’ll be grateful. Do ya hear?" Was it only coincidence that this was almost the exact same thing he said to his son, last time but one that he saw him? The mind has a funny way of playing tricks. Maybe this was its way of trying to wake Coach up to the real reality, remind him what happened last time, jerk him out of it once and for all. Shocked that Coach had almost shouting at her, spittle gathered in the corner of his mouth, Doreen could only stand there and nod. "Don’t do that! Stop confusing the girl! Tell her it’s Ok. Tell her you’ll forgive her if she wants out. It’s too much for her. It’s too much for you! "If it’s what you really want, then I guess I’ll do it. Somehow I’ll defeat Brian Arson and make you proud… of both of us." As she walked away, she smiled. For the second time, she’d managed to convince herself that by doing what she really wanted to do, she was helping others. It was a tenuous conviction at best, but was all she needed. Being the son Coach had lost would somehow make him better. And by being a winner she would be a positive role model for her sister. Sharifa sat in her room, torn. There could be no positive outcome from her sisters match against Brian Arson. She was in almost physical pain with the thought. This had to be someone’s fault. And when she figured out who, she would make them pay.
