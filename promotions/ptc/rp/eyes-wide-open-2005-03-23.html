Eyes wide open

23 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-23 I was going to start of by saying welcome to my parlour, but I thought that after our last little meeting anything that brought up connotations of spiders was probably a bad thing. So how about I just say that I am glad that you could join me once more. I know that parting is such sweet sorrow, but there is no help for it. After this last foray into the realms of the little place I like to call my mind we shall not be together again like this. I know that it is a hard thing to bear, but bear it we must. We can at least be grateful for the time that we have had together, the things that we have shared. We will always have Paris. … and Paris will always have that little video diary of hers, and Daddy’s millions. So everyone is happy. Unfortunately, or fortunately I suppose for the PTC who would probably be pulled off the air faster than you can say ‘Janet Jackson’s nipple ring’, we are not going to see or hear anything as exciting as Ms Hilton’s little foray into the big bad world of one handed movie entertainment. Oh no, but instead I am going to regale you with my deeds of daring do and my cunning play on words that will have your head spinning and your eyes bulging. Hmmm … Paris Hilton and I do seem to have things in common then, just different parts of the anatomy will be bulging. Where was I? I got sidetracked for a second so you have my apologies. Anyway as I was saying while I cannot compete with Paris in the realm of cheap titillation … at least I hope I cannot, and to be truthful I would be rather worried if any of you found the sight of a six foot man encased in black leather and latex any more stimulating that a nubile, blonde nymphomania! Thinking about it though … realising that my description has put a different slant on the actuality of the facts … I suppose that there is a certain section of the community that would be interested in that sort of thing after all. It is all in the perspective after all … it is all so very subjective. I mean take this tournament, the ‘Seven of the Best’ tournament … please take it I have another one coming in ten minutes. Sorry I couldn’t resist the attempt at a joke, and yes both objectively and subjectively speaking I realise that in order to earn the title of ‘joke’ it should at least be funny, so my bad! Anyway, as I was saying, the ‘Seven of the Best’ tournament is something that is also based on subjectivity at the moment. We have fourteen people who all want to do the best that they can, who are all striving to beat of the opposition and come out on top. Victory is the goal, the Global title the treasure at the end of the rainbow. Pretty logical when you think about it really, I mean it isn’t like one of them woke up one morning and thought to themselves that they really should enter a tournament and come second, is it? Of course not. No-one enters a tournament and hopes to come anywhere but first. The old saying that it isn’t the winning that matters, just the taking part is not grounded in any reality that I know of. It is all well and good to train hard, to say your prayers and to take your vitamins. It is commendable to prepare ones selves for the upcoming battle and walk in with head held high and do the best … hope for the best … BE the best that you possible can, no matter the actual outcome. Oh yes, that is all well and good if you are living in a Hallmark movie. In the real World, however, there is no such thing as second or third place; in a tournament such as this there is no runner-up. When the curtain falls and the dust settles there won’t be bronze or silver medals and smiles from the podium while national anthems blare out proudly. Oh no, there is only one thing that matters, victory. There is only one place that matters, first. … and there is only one color, one precious metal, that matters, gold. Altruistic people will disagree with me of course. So will the liars amongst us who will claim that they will be happy as long as they know that they tried as hard as they could, win or lose. The truth is … and we all know how much I LOVE the truth … is that each and everyone of us, madmen or fools that we are, don’t want anything other than to be proclaimed the best at what we do. … preferably without having to say ‘bub’ at the end of every sentence as well. I am honest enough with myself … and with all of you … to state that I have not come into this tournament to simply ‘try my best’ or hope that my best ‘good enough’. I am not happy with just competing for the sake of battle against Scott Monroe. I want to beat him. I will not be satisfied with just making it to the final and holding my head high, knowing that I am one of the seven best competitors in the PTC. I don’t want to be one of seven. I don’t want to be compared to Karina Wolfenden, to Tsunami Oni, to Tim Shipley, Jesse Jamester or ANY of the others. … especially not my vaunted opponent in the first round, Scott Monroe, who seems to be more immersed in an episode of Buffy versus the Bible than in any semblance of ‘real’ life. I want to surpass them, I want to look down on them as their feet hit the floor as I throw them out of the ring. I want to smile as my name is called out at the end, with the fans on their feet. I WANT TO WIN! Do you know what? Of course you don’t because I haven’t told you yet. Well it is simply this … I have spoken about truth and I have spoken about fear as well. The truth of the matter is that I am NOT afraid to give voice to my intentions, I am not afraid to tell the truth about my goals. Others may call it arrogance; some may even call it hubris. I don’t call it that though I call it acceptance. I have accepted the fact that I am going to go toe to toe with some of the greatest wrestlers that the diverse federations within the PTC have to offer. I have accepted that I will be seen as an outside, as an underdog. I have accepted the fact that all eyes will be firmly on the figures of the better known people in this thing … the Karina’s the Onis for example. I have accepted all of that and one thing more. What is that? Well let me start by saying that I have watched the other thirteen. I have looked into them and I feel that I know them a little better than I did before. They say that knowledge is power after all, and that to be forewarned is to be forearmed. I have looked into their pasts; I have dug into the vaults and watched some of the matches that led them here today. I have done as much research into each and everyone of my possible opponents as I can. I know them now … in fact there are even a couple that I know very well indeed. … which is something that those thirteen cannot claim to say about me. That is my advantage, that is my edge, that is what makes me ever so slightly different from the others. That is what I have accepted you see. The fact that they may think that they know me, that they may think that they are prepared for me, but the truth of the matter is that they don’t. I am not being egotistical; I am not being blind to the truth. In fact I can see, I can see very clearly. The truth is in front of me, seen with eyes wide open. I have accepted the fact that I CAN win this tournament. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The light streamed in through the window like shafts of pure gold. Illuminating the room with an almost too intense light, cascading over everything within it like waves from a yellow ocean, the sunlight brought hints of burnished orange and golden honey to everything that it touched. Light and airy already, with only a large bed, a bedside cabinet and a full length wooden-framed mirror to fill it's space, the room had a sense of calm tranquillity about it. Motes of dust floated languidly in the streams of light, like faded stars reliving their now faded glory. The air in the room was still, though not oppressively so, and there was an edge of expectation hanging in the silence; this was a portentous day that was just waiting to begin. “Oh Lord, thank you for this day,” sighed a voice that was as dry as sandpaper, but still full of simple joy. Almost invisible among the floral coverings on the bed, and the high pillows that framed her head, Beth Georgia sighed a breath that was a bittersweet mixture of contentment and melancholy. Her small frame was barely perceptible under the covers, the eiderdown hardly swelling at all as it covered her, and the face that stared out through the windows to the clear Spring day was almost skeletal in it’s gauntness. Skin, that could have just as easily been sun-dried leather, was taught against the skull and covered by a network of wrinkles and lines that played out a road map of each and everyone years that Beth had been alive. While not the oldest woman in the World there were days that Beth felt like she was, and today … her one hundredth and second birthday … was definitely one of them. Deep brown eyes, so dark as to be almost black, gazed clearly out through the window, belaying the passage of years that they had watched. The eyes were called the windows of the soul and Beth’s eyes were a reflection of a self-less life lived well, a worthy life, a long life. In a body that was beyond frailty, beyond miraculous, the eyes sparkled with a youthfulness that brought a smile to all who looked into them. Just as it brought a smile to the face of Nina Georgia, Beth’s only great-granddaughter, as she quietly walked into the room and paused to stare with eyes full of love at the small woman in the bed. Standing at just barely over five foot three, and small boned, Nina was almost an exact image of how Beth had looked at her age. Nina, of course, never realised the small pang of fatality that she evoked in her great-grandmother because she had never seen a photo of her at that age. For Beth herself though it was like looking into a mirror of the past, showing her an image of the seventeen-year-old girl she would never be again. The age difference was never a factor in their relationship though as Beth and Nina … great grandmother and great granddaughter … were as close as two people could be. Love that transcended the generations filled them both and they knew without knowing that their souls were linked in a way that the rest of the family could never quite match. Beth … and her husband George who had been dead and buried for over forty years now, a man that Nina had obviously never known … had had seven children. Over the years her family … because no matter how large or widespread it became it would always be HER family … had grown to include eighteen grandchildren, all of whom Beth loved with all of her heart. Then, seventeen years ago to the day … because she shared Beth’s birthday … Nina was born and Beth realised that though she loved all of her family equally with her whole heart she loved Nina more, she loved her with her heart and soul. She was, literally, the apple of her eye. Not a single day had passed since her birth that Nina didn’t spend at least some time with Beth, and today of all days was no different. “You going to stand there all day child?” Beth asked without looking around, her voice rasping but filled with humor. Nina laughed as she stepped into the room, her arms clasped around the large bouquet of wild flowers that she had spent the early morning picking. Beth loved nature but hadn’t been able to leave her bed for over two years now without aid, and so could only watch the World pass by outside her window … and experience it through the small parts of the outside that Nina regularly brought inside for her. Reaching the side of the bed Nina struggled to hold the flowers together as she leant over and gently kissed Beth’s forehead. “Happy birthday Mama” she smiled gently, staring into the eyes that mirrored her own, as she used the name that the whole family … and most of the people in the small town where she had spent her whole life … called Beth. No matter the actual relationship to people Beth treated everyone like her child, the archetypal mother figure. “… and a happy birthday to you as well my darling child” Beth’s eyes twinkled with mirth, “and am I to take it that the small forest in your arms is for me?” “No, they were just lying at the door so I thought that I would bring them in and put them in the trash, I mean we don’t want them stinking up the neighbourhood!” Nina quipped, her own eyes twinkling. Moving faster then expected for someone as frail as she was Beth snaked a hand out and snatched a small white rose from Nina’s arms before she could react. Taking a small step backwards Nina laughed as Beth held the rose to her nose and took a deep breath. “Ah child if only everyone in the neighbourhood could smell the fragrance of the rose the way that I do, but if you don’t get those poor things in some water they won’t be lasting long at all” Beth said around the flower. “I’ll go get the vase now and then come back and tell you all about what Johnny is doing for me tonight” Nina said with a smile of excitement as she walked out of the room, winking at Beth who allowed the rose to fall to the pillow beside her head. As she watched Nina walk through the doorway, and out of sight, Beth allowed herself a small smile. ‘So young but already with that nice Johnny’ she thought to herself, ‘ but then who am I to worry about that when I knew that my George was the man for me at her age too?’ The memory of her husband brought, as it always did, a smile to Beth’s face and she let her head turn towards the window again, watching the sunlight illuminate the dust. ‘Little stairways to Heaven’ she thought to herself, remembering how her mother had told her … so many years ago … how the light that streamed down through the trees in the near wilderness where they struggled to simply survive was sent from Heaven to allow the Angels to come down and help people. In a life that had been filled with pain and hardship that little piece of a mother whose face she couldn’t remember anymore brought Beth comfort whenever she thought about it. “Ah but Lord above Beth Georgia you think that you would be too old for such nonsense now wouldn’t you?” she muttered to herself. “Too old to believe in angels?” The voice filled Beth’s ears with the taste of honey, it filled her eyes with the smell of freshly mown grass and it filled her nose with the colour of warm barley. Her head swam as her senses were overwhelmed and for a second she couldn’t catch her breath. For a second it felt like she was drowning, it felt like everything was slowing down, getting darker. … and then it was all so very clear again. The sunlight streaming though the window seemed more vibrant, seemed more alive than before and the motes of dust now visibly moved and danced around each other. The blue sky outside the window seemed to be an azure that she had never seen before and the small white clouds that dotted the clear blue expanse moved past with unbelievable speed. Remembering the voice she turned her head to face the other side of the room, and a small gasp escaped her mouth as she stared at the figure that stood there. ‘So beautiful’ she thought to herself as she gazed at the man … the youth … who stood gazing at her fondly. An immaculate white suit, obviously tailored to fit his lithe and toned form, seemed to gleam with a hidden backlight. The shirt that was open at the collar, to reveal golden, sun blanched, skin, was of a paler shade of white again. Blonde hair feel to his shoulders and piercing blue eyes stared at Beth gently. “Thank you Beth” the man smiled graciously at her, nodding his head modestly. Beth shook her head as if to clear it. ‘Damn you woman you are getting old, talking out loud like that when a strange man walks into your bedroom!’ she thought to herself. Even though she knew that she should she didn’t feel afraid, she didn’t feel alarmed … she only felt a quiet calmness washing over her tinged with curiosity. “You know if you have come here looking for money or anything like that you are out of luck, son, I don’t have anything of value here!” Beth said to him calmly, her gaze unwavering. “I am not here for your money Mama, but you are wrong you know, you DO have something that is very valuable here” the figure said with another small smile as he took a step towards the bed, his hands swinging gently by his side, “priceless even.” Reaching the side of the bed the figure sat down gently beside Beth, reaching out to lay one hand on top of her own and smiling at her as a look of wonder played over her face. Beth’s eyes widened at the stranger’s touch. For as long as she could remember, for too many damn years, her body had been betraying her. Arthritis had spread through most of her joints and her hands especially constantly ached … but when the stranger placed his hand over hers the pain went. … the memory of the pain itself vanished as if it had never been. Realisation began to set in and Beth’s eyes widened even more and then a small … almost shy … smile appeared on her face. “You have a beautiful smile Beth,” the young man said to her, smiling in return. “Oh shush now boy, you are far too young to be talking all sweet like that to me and I am far to old to be listening to your pretty tongue” Beth said, but her smile deepened nonetheless. “Well I am not as young as you think that I am Beth, and in my eyes you are not old at all,” the stranger laughed as he reached up to tenderly stroke one cheek on Beth’s face. A shudder of pleasure ran through Beth as she felt every pain … every ache … every swollen joint and hardened artery … relax and disappear. For the first time in as long as she remembered she felt alive, truly alive. It was glorious. Unbidden, but not even trying to stop them, tears of joy started to roll down her cheeks as she starred into the eyes of the beautiful stranger in front of her. “What was that you said a moment ago … about being to old to believe?” she whispered softly. “I asked if you were too old to believe in angels Beth, but you didn’t answer me,” he repeated gently as he stared at her, “ … so, do you?” Beth gave a small shudder, a shiver that her mother would have claimed meant that someone had walked over her grave. Over one hundred years, who would have thought it, Beth asked herself quietly. Good years, years filled with love and happiness … enough to outweigh the years of hardship and struggle … they years of racism and fear. One hundred and two years of life. Beth smiled to herself as with some effort … but not as much as she was expecting … she raised herself up on the pillows and stared directly into the young man’s face, her eyes twinkling as they reflected the pure and simple expression of joy on his own face. “What do I call you sonny?” Beth asked, avoiding the question. “I have many names Mama but you can call me Azrael if you wish” he replied gently, holding out a hand expectantly. At the name Beth gave a small sigh. She had always been a faithful woman, had read her scriptures and remembered them for as long back as she could recall. The name was known to her, as was the duty that he carried out. “Well Azrael I guess that I am not too old after all am I?” she said with a small smile, “ so why don’t you just take me home?” Reaching out she placed her small hand in his own, smiling at the look of shock that played over his face ever so briefly. Gripping his hand in her own she laughed, fully and vibrantly, her voice sounding strange to her own ears until she realised that she could hear no trace of frailty or age in it. “What’s that matter Azrael, not what you were expecting?” she asked teasingly. “Well I have to admit that I thought that you might have questions, might have concerns, might even argue …” he started to say. “I have spent one hundred and two years trusting in the Lord boy, and I am not going to stop doing that now just because he has decided that it is time to call me home. I am ready, I have had a good life and now I am prepared to meet my maker” she interrupted. “So you are ready to leave the World behind, and your family?” he asked tenderly. “Ready? No I wouldn’t say that I am ready … but I knew that this day would come and I am not afraid to take the journey, I am not afraid to leave. I accepted a long time ago that this day would come, and now it is I am ready” she said with quiet dignity. “You are an amazing woman Beth, and know that your family will be well and will always love and remember you” Azrael nodded and smiled at her warmly as he stood to his feet again. “So is it time … is it time for me to die?” Beth asked quietly, a little tremor of anxiety in her voice. Azrael laughed, holding out his hands and clasping her own in his. Pulling her to her feet he turned her to face the mirror and she gasped in shock. The reflection that stared back at her was not the frail shell of a woman that she had been for the last few decades, instead it was the girl that she remembered … the girl that she was inside … the girl that she had always been. Smooth and unblemished skin, luxuriant hair, a body that stood straight and tall. She was young again, she was herself again. “No Beth, it is not time for you to die my child … that happened before we started talking” Azrael said gently as he stepped in close to her and embraced her in his strong arms. Over his shoulder Beth saw herself … the old woman that she had been … lying in the bed, still and unmoving and placed her head against his shoulder as she realised that her life … her pain … was really over and that the conversation with the angel of death had simply been to ease her on her journey. As his arms closed tightly around her she smiled as she felt the embrace become warmer, become tighter, and gasped in surprise as she felt the tickle on her face. Wrapping his wings around her, Azrael became ensconced in a nimbus of golden light and when she was safely cocooned within him he looked out through the window and up along the pathway of light. “Father, we are ready” he said gently and then smiled as the light intensified around him. Nina gasped, both hands going up involuntarily to her mouth. The glass vase fell to the floor and shattered with a crash of glass and water flying out around the room. Flowers exploded all over the place and lay unnoticed as tears filled her eyes and her heart skipped a beat. She watched as the woman who had shaped her life lay unmoving on the bed, her chest still and quiet. She took a step forwards and reached out to lay a hand gently on the face of the old woman and a small, sad, smile played over her face. Nina leaned down and kissed Beth’s forehead gently and then stepped out of the room, looking back in wonder … not noticing the small feather that floated gently to the floor near the mirror … as she burned the expression on her great grandmother’s face into her memory for all time. With her face to the sky, and an expression of complete and utter peace on her face, Beth Georgia lay still and unmoving, staring into the Heavens with eyes wide open. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Dancing and moving and weaving the small flame revelled in it’s existence. Once faded hues, pale reflections of their former glory, became stronger and muted yellows, reds and oranges become stronger, became vibrant once more. Unheard music filled the air and a beat that could only be felt but not heard gave the rhythm that the flame moved too. Primal instinct took over and a force as old as time remembered what it was. Spark became ember became flame became fire. Growing stronger and bigger, rising up and outwards, devouring all that stood in it’s way … turning everything it met into fuel for it’s own existence the fire itself grew and evolved. Spark became ember became flame became fire … became inferno. Unstoppable in it’s ferocity, untameable in it’s desire, unbound and unfettered the inferno reached out, like wings of fire, it reached up into the sky. The flame remembered, the flame lived, the flame triumphed. End ... or beginning?
