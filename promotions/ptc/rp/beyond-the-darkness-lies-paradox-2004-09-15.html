Beyond The Darkness Lies Paradox

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-15 Monday, August 2, 2004 0333 hours, EST Jackson’s condo, Chicago, IL Gabriel Delacroix paused before the wall of mirrors, looking at his pallid face in the reflection. Spots of blood still spattered his cheeks in a fine mist, his eyes staring widely from amid a face lined with exhaustion. The dark circles carved hollows beneath those otherworldly windows to his soul, accentuating their bloodshot whites. He was tired, and frightened. He looked as terrified as he felt, and as he peered into his amber eyes, something happened, something changed. Those eyes became blue, a deep shade of blue, nearly violet, framed with impossibly long lashes. Not his eyes. Before he had time to register this phenomena, and to step back from the silvery glass, a hand reached out from the mirror, from somewhere beyond the glass and grabbed him by the front of the jacket, hauling him forward, off balance. He felt his skin pass through the mirror’s solid surface, suddenly gone liquid, malleable like pulling taffy. He would have screamed then, but the shock stole away his voice, leaving him mute… Freefall. Weightlessness. Silence. All things that were not normally ominous, but now, turned his spine to jelly, and his blood to ice water. The darkness spread all around him, velvety and thick, an impenetrable curtain. Suddenly, he had an inkling of what Alice felt upon falling down the rabbit hole. Sheer, blind terror. With luck, Jackson would be merciful, and let him die quickly. He opened his mouth to scream, and found that he still had no voice. He couldn’t see. He couldn’t hear. He couldn’t move. He was a paralyzed body, falling in the dark, forgotten. Surely this was hell. “No,” a voice replied, echoing in the darkness, everywhere and nowhere at once, “it’s not. Welcome to limbo, Gabriel. I have been expecting you.” Limbo. The word cut through Gabriel’s terror clouded stupor, instantly sobering him up. Limbo. The state of unbeing. A place of neither the living nor the dead. An icy finger of dread trailed down his spine, making him shudder, making him suddenly cold. Was he going to die? A chuckle filled the silence, reverberating in his eardrums, making his head pound. “Ah, Gabriel, if I meant to kill you, don’t you think I would have done it before?” Instead of even pondering an answer, Gabriel began thrashing, flailing his arms, trying in vain to break out of the darkness. Sweat beaded on his forehead, but his actions did nothing, a fruitless effort. He strained in the darkness, trying to catch hold of it in his grasping hands, trying to feel its essence, but he felt nothing. A hollow empty ache, but even that, was nothing. A soundless scream erupted in his mind, and he recognized it as his own. Surely, this was madness. “No, Gabriel. Dante is mad… you still possess all six of your senses, although I took the liberties of incapacitating your rather peculiar gift. Would you like to join him in the River Lethe? I could arrange that. Honestly, Gabriel, do stop trashing about before you actually hurt yourself, the aether really doesn’t appreciate this abuse.” The words only made Gabriel buck harder, his eyes glazed with terror, his teeth chattering as he whimpered in fear. Panic was setting in, he still couldn’t see anything… and he couldn’t feel anything either. For the first time, he realized how much he relied on that other sense… just how vital it was to him. The air around him seemed to quiver suddenly, like the ripples from a shockwave, and he found himself truly falling, through a space filled with light, and sounds and smells. He impacted with the hard gravel covered roof, the air rushing from his lungs, expelled by his sound of shocked pain. He lay there, unmoving, gasping, trying to draw the smog tainted air into his lungs in great draughts. The voice, again, distracted himself from his situation, and this time he could pinpoint a source. “Do you find this more to your liking, Gabriel?” He flopped over on his side, still wheezing, his chest still clenched with that fist of panic. There sat his quarry, Jackson, his back against the brick top of the elevator shaft, his eyes hidden behind a pair of dark sunglasses. The barest hint of a smile played around the corners of Jackson’s lips, and one of his dark eyebrows was cocked in a questioning look. Gabriel took his time replying, instead studying Jackson’s features, and looking for a clue. Jackson shook his head slowly, running a hand along his jaw line in a familiar gesture, his dry fingers rasping across the stubble that painted his chin nearly black. “I suppose I should be asking you why you persist in this madness, Gabriel. So, indulge me, why are you here? Do you feel lucky? Is that why you’ve come? To thank me for sparing your life? Is that it? Save me the platitudes and well wishes… I don’t need them.” Gabriel pulled himself to his knees, his unsettling gaze still fixed on Jackson. His voice was filled with sadness when he replied; his shoulders slumped as though the weight of the world lay upon his thin shoulders. “I came here to fulfill Artemis’ dying wish. I came here to avenge Matthias. I came here to find out…” His eyes filled with tears, hot and bitter as he looked up at Jackson, almost begging. “To find out what, exactly, Gabriel? To find out what made you the lucky one? To find out why you, above all the others, was spared?” The tears fell from his eyes, unbidden, perhaps drawn by Jackson’s tone of voice, perhaps simply long overdue. They cut through the dried blood, turning the trails pinkish as the fat tears rolled down his cheeks. He took a shuddering breath, and then wrenched his gaze away from Jackson, and the maddening sunglasses that hid his true intentions. He wasn’t aware that Jackson had moved, until he felt a hand touch his shoulder. He jerked away from the press of Jackson’s feverish palm, his breathing ragged, part sorrow, part anger. “Don’t. Touch. Me.” “Gabriel,” Jackson’s voice was soft, filled with hurt, with confusion. “Look at me, please.” Gabriel obeyed the request, not out of respect, more out of hatred, and the look in his amber eyes conveyed the lack of warmth in his feelings. Jackson sighed, and then took the sunglasses off, casting them aside, letting Gabriel see. A whirlwind of emotions swept through Jackson’s indigo gaze, and not all of them were negative. Anger, regret, sadness, pain. He held out his hand, and Gabriel accepted it, allowing himself to be pulled upright. The two stood toe-to-toe, locked in a heated gaze, until Jackson turned his back on Gabriel, raking a hand though his hair, so much like the old Jackson. “Bradley,” Gabriel began, but didn’t finish when he noticed Jackson stiffen at the name. He whirled around, pinning Gabriel with a glare that made him flinch. For a moment, his eyes flickered, filled with something hot and elemental, and then they were the same old blue again, albeit filled with their share of fury. “Bradley is dead. You were at the funeral, you watched them bury me. The last of my mortality went into the ground, along with that casket. I am keeping the name Jackson, as a reminder of the life I left behind…” He turned away again, walking towards the edge of the roof, lifting one leg and bracing his foot on the low brick wall, leaning forward with his elbows on his knee. His voice was low, carried to Gabriel, where he stood, on the wind. “Do you truly want answers, Gabriel? Is that what you want from me? I’m afraid I will have to let you leave empty handed, I don’t know why I let you live. Perhaps it has something to do with luck after all. What do you think, Gabriel? You, the all-seeing psychic… can you see why I let you live? Can you discern my motives?” “No. I cannot.” Jackson shook his head slowly, still looking down at the world below. “I was afraid of that. What do you see when you look at me, Gabriel? Tell me, please.” “Darkness. Nothing but darkness. Like the place you brought me beyond the looking glass. Thick and nameless, like nothing I have ever felt.” “Yes. Nameless. Darkness. Gabriel, you’re a genius! Namenlos. Duisternis. My heritage, my mother-tongue. From this day forward, that shall be my name. Jackson Namenlos-Duisternis.” Something in the air shifted, the wind swirling suddenly against him, making the long leather coat twist around his legs. He turned, and found Gabriel behind him, pointing a gun in two hands that were shaking very badly. He tsked softly, his eyes narrowing in irritation. “Honestly, Gabriel, I thought we were past these games.” The night pressed against them, silent… a heavy pause, filled with menace, almost like a slumbering boa constrictor… a trap, waiting to spring. Gabriel’s teeth ground in anger; sweat stinging his eyes as it poured down from his brow. “You thought wrong, Jackson. This game ends now, queen’s knight to rook seven… checkmate. You lose. It’s time for you to tell me, are YOU feeling lucky, punk?” Jackson shook his head slowly, the bemusement fading from his expression. He looked almost… worried. “I don’t think John Wayne would be proud of your misuse of his quote.” Gabriel snapped a reply, spots of livid color standing out on his cheeks. “It was Clint Eastwood, not John Wayne.” “Like it fucking matters! None of this shit matters. This…” he spread his hands, indicating the world in general before he continued, his tone filled with bitterness. “is nothing. It’s all a game. Life is a game. You can take your secret societies and your rules and shove them right up your ass, Gabriel… and they don’t mean a thing. Let me tell you something about luck…” “Shut up, just shut up.” The gun jittered wildly in Gabriel’s hands, sweat pouring down his face as Jackson sat down on the low wall, with his back to the open air. “Put the gun down and just listen to me, Gabriel. This is the part of the movie where the bad guy spills his guts, explaining his complicated scheme to ruin civilization as we know it… and then the valiant hero, that would be you, Gabe… gets to blow his head off at that perfect, opportune moment.” Gabriel lowered the gun slightly, his expression wary. “Fine. Speak your piece. But first, tell me where the Reaver is.” Jackson blinked, clearly puzzled at the question. “The Reaver? Ah, it all makes sense now, that’s what you’re after. Not revenge… you want a taste of the blade’s dark power too, don’t you? Can’t you feel it, Gabriel? Can’t you hear the siren song? Listen. Close your eyes and just listen. It’s calling to you…” Gabriel closed his eyes, swaying slightly, the gun still held in his hands as he gave himself over to the waves of darkness crashing against his mind. With almost superhuman effort, he realized his folly, and snapped back to reality, almost expecting Jackson to be wresting the gun from his hands. Instead, he was still sitting there passively, his dark eyes fixed on Gabriel, that infuriating half smile still on his face. “You don’t trust me, do you? I suppose that’s to be expected, I didn’t really give you a reason to feel otherwise… I mean I did just shoot the other nine elders in cold blood, didn’t I? Regardless, let me continue. I heard your words, Gabriel… and those of the elders. I know what’s in your heart. I know you want to kill me; I’d at least like the opportunity to state my case, as it were.” He paused, raking a hand through his hair before continuing, seeming almost human. “I heard your words, Gabriel. Reality. Communication. Let me clarify a point, Gabriel. I listened. I heard every last word you said... context. Word for word. Verbatim. What I also heard was the subtext... the underlying theme. I am a pro at reading between the lines... from where I come, that's standard. I’m the master of espionage here, the linguist, the shadow that creeps into the places you dare not go. I have heard things you would never want to know; yet you accuse me of being a liar. You condemn me to death without even asking to hear my side of the story. How’s that for democracy? Yet here you are, charged with your holy mission, brandishing your own version of justice, for what? Misplaced revenge? Some skewed sense of loyalty? Face it, Gabriel, you were their lapdog… their charm, like a spell, or a crystal ball, just another mystical object in the archives.” Gabriel shook his head, squeezing his eyes shut, pressing his hands to his ears. “No. Lies. I won’t listen!” “Grow the fuck up, Gabriel. You know what I say is true. You’ve known it all along. Don’t pretend this is news to you. We all lie to ourselves. That's part of existence. Denial. But what do you do, when lying to yourself isn't enough? When getting up in the morning, and looking in the mirror doesn't make you feel any better? What then, Gabriel? Yeah, I'm still living... but why? That's what I'm looking for, and like that U2 song, I still haven't found it. Life. A quest. That's what I'm talking about here…” Gabriel opened his eyes, his face lined with exhaustion, streaked with dust and tears. “What does this have to do with luck? What does this have to do with me?” “Everything. Just listen. Luck. Let me tell you about luck. How many times can you flip a coin and have it come up heads. Let’s say, you flip it fifty times. Statistics say, that it will come up heads about 49.9% of the time, rounding off to whole numbers. Likewise, tails will end up the same. That leaves a few fractions of a percent without absolute certainty. What about landing on its edge? How often does that happen? Almost never, maybe a million to one odds… but it could still happen. That’s where you were, Gabriel. That’s what luck has to do with it. Today, was your lucky day. It was your serendipity. I eliminated the odds for you, and left you with your own free will. I made the coin land on its edge. Paradox.” “Free will means nothing. There is no such thing.” “You said the same about this, remember?” He reached inside his jacket and withdrew something. A sword. No, the sword. The Reaver. The Lord of Darkness. Jackson held it out in both hands, the dark metal gleaming wickedly against his palms. A skull adorned its hilt, with dark rubies glittering evilly in the eyesockets. It seemed to pulse with evil. “Is that really… it?” He inched forward, his eyes fixed on the dark blade, his pupils huge and elliptical, like a cat’s. Jackson let him approach, and then stopped him with one word, barked like a command. “Yes. Don’t come any closer, Gabriel. You ask me about luck. About chance. And why your life was spared. I cannot answer you, at least not fully. You see, you and I are a lot alike, closer to brothers than Dante and I ever were. Paradox binds us together. Luck makes us kin. We both should have died… yet here we are, both sides of the same coin. Light and dark. Good and evil. You know what must be done, Gabriel, as surely as I do. But the question remains, who kills whom? Now you tell me, Gabriel, what happens when the coin lands on its edge…?” He stood in one fluid motion, replacing the sword inside his jacket before holding out a Susan B Anthony silver dollar between his thumb and forefinger. He flipped it, and stepped back, letting it fall. Heads? Tails? No, it landed on its edge, balancing impossibly on the ledge, only wobbling slightly. Gabriel gasped and the gun fell from his lifeless fingers, hitting the gravel with a muted thud. “This is where luck ends, and paradox begins. Chaos. Welcome to my world, Gabriel… welcome to my time. Welcome to the era of the Nameless Darkness. Your luck has just run out, my friend.” Jackson stepped up on the ledge, and then vanished over the side, silently, disappearing into the dark night. “I'm done with it. All of it. The lies... the self-denial. Unlike you, I know what it feels like to fall flat on your face and never reach the big time. I know what it is to stupidly sacrifice reality for dreams… I know what it is to die for unrequited love. And now I know, after all the reaching, it just doesn't feel as good to have what I wanted. Not after all the things I had to give up. It’s time for my payback… time for my revenge. I bring my fight to the wrestling ring, as a wild card, an unknown… but really, I’m still looking for her. You see, Gabriel... I would trade it all, still, for her. My Judas. Altruism. A sacrifice for the welfare of others. It doesn’t have to be this way… it doesn’t have to end like this. Make your choice, you are the lucky one. You are the fragment of my paradox… choose well my brother, for tomorrow begins a new day. Hunt or be hunted, Gabriel… which will you choose?”
