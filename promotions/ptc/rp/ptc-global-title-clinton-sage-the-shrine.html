To any passerby, it would appear that Nathan Hollinger died at the youthful age of 8 – taken from this world before he ever really had a chance to experience it. But to those who knew him, and his story, Nathan Hollinger was born in 1974, yet ‘reborn’ in 1998 via amnesia. After six years of incarceration for crimes he never committed, Nathan Hollinger left prison and moved to Denver, where he found Isis. In 2006, Isis Maguire was reborn, and later, she took the name of Isis Hollinger via wedlock. Their matrimony was short lived, however, because less than two weeks later she was a widow. Isis didn’t have enough fingers to wipe away the tears that started falling down her cheeks. Nathan was gone, and so was their happy future. The last two-month had been especially hard on her, considering the mysterious way Nathan had died, and accepting that his fate was fair in the eyes of God. It took an especially brisk gust of wind to snap her out of a mournful reverie, but Isis Hollinger soon realized that something was wrong. Even though she had only been at the grave once before – the day they placed Nathan into the ground – something triggered that instinctive response to inspect further. Look for details. The headstone was off center, and slightly crooked. No more than an inch, a passing glance would reveal absolutely nothing wrong at all, but upon a keen eye, certainly amiss. There were two depressions, running parallel to each other, leading away from the grave. Each track was a foot across, and sank an inch or so into the ground. Undoubtedly left behind from a vehicle of some sort. Isis remembered the day of the funeral, the hearse never made it this close to the grave. And these were recent. Still looking, Isis soon found that the topsoil above Nathan’s casket had been disturbed. There were foot-by-foot squares cut into the sod – carefully and precisely measured out. It appeared like a grid had been etched into the grass, and had she visited perhaps a week or two later, the grid would have faded by then. Jabbing at the grass with her finger, it felt soft and malleable. Like it had recently been dug up, and replaced. Hitting her like a tsunami, knocking her down to her knees, Isis Hollinger buried her face into the palms of her hands. She didn’t want to let her mind’s eye wander, but it kept tugging and tugging and nagging at her. Someone had dug up Nathan’s body. She had no doubt. She was mourning loose dirt, a shiny headstone, and an empty casket. The body of her husband was most certainly elsewhere. Why? Dear God, why?
