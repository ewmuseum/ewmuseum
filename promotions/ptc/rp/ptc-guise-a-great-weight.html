[IG29 - Guise - FA] A Great Weight

For one man, it is the beginning of everything. --------------------------------------- February 29, 2060 New York City The Apartment of John Reynolds John Reynolds sat, hunched over, in his favorite recliner. The chair was set up so as to look out over the Manhattan skyline. Even at the age of eighty-five, with his eyesight failing, the sight never failed to amaze the man. Before long, a knock came at the door. “Come in,” Reynolds replied, seemingly unworried about the knocker being a criminal, or assassin, or any such unsavory element. As he seemed to know, a teenager walked in through the door, a somber look on his face. “You wanted to see me, grandpa,” he asked, shoulders slumped. His grandfather had called earlier and asked to see him. Considering how serious his grandpa had been, the young man could only surmise that the news would be bad. “Close the door, Jack,” Mr. Reynolds said, voice heavy. “I’ve got something to talk to you about.” Obediently, the grandson closed the door, then took a seat in a chair near his grandfather. “What is it?” Reynolds took a deep breath, and closed his eyes. “What I’m about to tell you will shock you. It’s a secret I’ve been harboring for over fifty-five years. A bunch of secrets actually. I’m…not long for this world, and I can’t pass on without telling someone. My soul cannot bear it.” Grandson looked at grandfather, and nodded once, quickly. “Tell me,” Jack pleaded, leaning forward. “Anything to help you.” Slowly, painstakingly, John Reynolds rose from his chair. With short, cane-assisted steps, he walked the few feet over to the wall, where a black and silver mask was contained in a glass case. The letter “G” stood out from the front of the mask, accentuated with faux-gemstones. “I’ve told you about this mask, right?” Grandson nodded. “Of course, grandpa. It’s the mask of Guise, that wrestler on the tapes we used to watch together. He gave it to you when you went to a show in Poughkeepsie.” Reynolds froze where he stood, thinking about the consequences of what he was about to do. He was about to burden a young man, his own grandson, with the story of his life. With a tale that he had told no one. Not his parents, god rest their souls. Not his recently deceased wife. Not even his children. But if what the doctors had told him was true, he was going to die in two months. It was finally time for him to lift part of the great guilty weight that had buried him for over a half-century. And if he could trust anyone in the world, it was his grandson. “First of all, Jack…even though you’ve probably figured it out by now, I am Guise.” Jack nodded. “Yeah. It was sort of easy after a while. You kept showing me his matches, and I really didn’t believe that story you told me about Poughkeepsie.” “Thanks for pretending, though,” the old man replied. “Made me feel better. Now that you know that, though, I can tell you the rest of the story about your grandfather. I…” The elderly man’s leg nearly buckled, and his grandson immediately rushed to his side, helping escort Mr. Reynolds back to his recliner. Wincing in pain that was more emotional than physical, John spoke again. “I’ve been going to church for thirty years, and I still can’t purge the memories of my sins. You might want to go to the cupboard and get a snack,” Reynolds said, darkly, “because this tale is going to take a while.” “Thanks, but I think I’ll pass for now,” the grandson replied, deadly serious. “Tell me, grandpa. You’ll feel better.” A foreboding feeling filled the young man’s gut. He had a feeling that this wasn’t going to be a simple story about college hijinx. Looking his grandson dead in the eyes, the grandfather took one, final deep breath. This was it. No turning back now. “I guess I’ll start with my original sin, then. Back in college, I was training to be a wrestler. I was also a theater major. Specialized in costume making and theater makeup. I sewed myself together the Guise costume and mask, and sent in my name to be part of a Primetime Central event. The 29th round of the Infinite Gauntlet. The winner would go on to fight Cassandra Paragon for the Infinite Gauntlet Championship.” “Wow,” Jack responded, already becoming engrossed. “You never told me this story about Guise before.” “With good reason,” came the sardonic reply. “The day before that match, though, I had to work backstage at the theater department’s production of Henry IV, Part I. That day changed my life forever. That day, John Reynolds died…and the man I became was truly born….” ++++++++++ March 9, 2007 One hour before showtime. “There now. You’re ready to go,” John Reynolds said, admiring his handiwork. “Costume’s some of my best work, if I do say so myself.” Prince Hal stared back at John, and nodded. “Thanks, man,” the princely actor replied, looking at himself in a nearby mirror. “This is a big night for me. It’s my biggest role ever. Hoping to attract some attention.” The expression on Reynolds’ face hardened, but unnoticeably so to anyone who wasn’t paying close attention. “From Rebecca, you mean,” the costume designer asked, and the other man nodded. “Yeah. She’s like a dream. We’ve been dating four months now. I think she’s the one.” “Well, Harry,” John said, beginning to walk away from the backstage area, “they’ve got me playing usher tonight too, so I have to go get ready to escort your adoring public into their seats.” A smirk returned to Reynolds’ face. “You do that,” Harry (yes, he’s playing Prince Harry/Hal and his name actually IS Harry! What a coincidence!) said, with a grin of his own, “and I’ll see you at the after party.” With that, the Prince headed off in the direction of the growing mob of actors preparing to perform. For his part, John picked up a flashlight, and headed for the door leading to the audience portion of the theater. Though he was an usher now, tomorrow he would have his big chance in his chosen sport. He would don the black and silver costume that he had made himself, and enter the Infinite Gauntlet. Currently, though, he was mostly moping at his lack of luck. “John Covel,” he moaned, in a whisper so that no one heard him. “The FUSE Universal Champion. What next? Nova from PRIME? All I wanted was a nice easy way to make myself known, and Covel decides to show up. Figures. World loves to rain on my parade…” Shaking his head, he put back on his well-practiced happy face. With his flashlight, he was now the head usher, one of a half-dozen roles he performed for his university’s theater department. Patrons came in, and he escorted them to their chairs. It was boring work, but simple as pie. After all, he wasn’t fit for much else, it seemed. He had tried, of course, to be in the play. He’d tried out for Prince Hal. And while he’d done well…Harry had done just a little bit better. Harry ALWAYS did just a little bit better. Especially when it came to asking out the rather lovely young woman who was making her way over to him at that moment, Rebecca Reed. “John,” she said, cordially, embracing the erstwhile usher, and snapping him from his darker thoughts. “How are you?” “Fine,” he stammered. “Just showing people to their seats. Can I help you find yours?” She nodded. To John, she was an angel…an angel that he’d managed to ask out a scant thirty-five minutes after Harry had already done so. “Of course,” Rebecca said, showing her ticket to the usher/costume designer/pro wrestler/makeup artist. He nodded once, and led her down one aisle, pointing with his ever-useful flashlight. “Row four, seat B. Can’t miss it,” John told her. He was met with a grateful nod and a thank you. “Will you be at the after party,” the usher asked, only to be slightly surprised when she shook her head ‘no’. “Too much work to do,” she sighed. “Hey…if you see Harry there, could you ask him to come over? I haven’t gotten to spend any time with him in a while.” Forcing the bile back down into his throat, he nodded his head yes, and turned on a heel to walk back up to the entrance of the theater. By the time he was halfway there, an idea had sprouted in his head. A truly hideous idea. A scheme so ridiculous that it nearly defied description. But he could pull it off. He knew that he could…. +++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++ Present “I can’t explain it,” John Reynolds told his intently listening grandson. “Somehow, the idea just popped into my head.” “What idea,” came the predictable question. “I was lonely,” the grandfather said, instead. “I loved that girl. I couldn’t deal with the fact that she was dating Harry instead of me. Harry always seemed to beat me at everything. I wanted nothing more at that moment than to be Harry. That’s when I had the idea.” Jack didn’t quite know what the old man was going to tell him, but he had inklings. Inklings of something so ridiculous it didn’t seem possible. “You know the old saying, ‘if you can’t beat ‘em, join ‘em’?” asked the grandfather. He was met with a slow nod of the head. “Well, I took that one step too far.” +++++++++++++++++++++++++++ Past Flinging the door to his dorm room open, John Reynolds was a man with a purpose. Closing the door behind him, his first act was to place his makeup supplies down on his desk. His second act was to strip down to his boxers, before looking intently at his closet. Rejecting various articles of clothing, he finally decided on a shirt and a pair of pants. Yanking them free from the closet, he redressed himself. “There,” the theater student said, firmly, “that’s better.” Glancing over to his desk, he frowned, suddenly. “No,” he stated, in a low, sad voice. “It’s not going to work, not unless I…” Wordlessly, he walked over to his supply kit, and pulled out a pair of scissors. Steeling himself for what was to come, he spun ninety degrees, to face the mirror attached to the door of his dorm. The scissors did their job, first clipping the hair that hung down in front of John’s eyes. Several more snips began to thin and shorten the young man’s mane, until finally, the floor of the room was covered in hair, and he himself was sporting far less. Wiping stray pieces of cut hair from his face and clothing, he next dragged a chair over to his desk, and sat down, once again facing the mirror. With the intent focus of a surgeon, he regarded his makeup kit carefully. This would be the hardest part, no doubt about it. The task took far longer than putting on a few clothes or cutting his hair. At various points he nearly threw up his hands in frustration. He nearly gave up. But desire motivated the young actor’s quest. He was determined to make this the greatest weekend of his young life. Tomorrow, he would compete before the eyes of the world in the Infinite Gauntlet. He would take his chances against some of the best in the world. Tonight, though, he had a different goal in mind. Finally, hours of feverish work had paid off. With a glance in the mirror, he knew that he was done. With a nod of appreciation, he rose from his seat, and left the dorm, closing the door behind him. All that remained in John’s room were hair clippings, the clothes he’d worn to the play earlier, and one more object that explained everything. A picture of Harry Burroughs. ++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++ Present “Y-y-you…” The teen was finding it hard to spit out what he now knew to be obvious. “Unthinkable, isn’t it,” the grandfather managed, breaking the nervous moment. “I’d never thought of anything so ridiculous in my life. But I knew I could pull it off. My teachers had always told me I was the best makeup artist they’d seen in thirty years in the department. Turned out they were right. I looked just like Harry. It was scary.” “But…there’s no way it could have been perfect, right?” “Of course it wasn’t,” John said, nodding. “But it was good enough, for a dark night…” “You went to see Rebecca, didn’t you,” his grandson accused, a hint of amusement in the younger man’s face. Grandfather nodded to grandson. “I did. I wanted to know what it would be like to have her attention for once. That’s all I wanted…things just went a bit too far. I…heh. Don’t think I can say the next part out loud. Mind giving an old man and his memories a moment?” As Jack nodded, John Reynolds let his eyes close. Even a half-century plus later, he still remembered the night as if it was yesterday. Remembered details about her that made him smile… ++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++ Past John’s heart pounded a mile a minute in his chest as he exited the stairwell. Thankfully, Rebecca lived on the floor above him. Any longer of a walk, and he might have passed out. He visually looked the part that he was trying to play. But pulling off Harry Burroughs was more than just looking the part. John had to assume the other man’s confidence, his tone of speech, his mannerisms. In a way, it was the ultimate role: having to convince someone that he was Harry, in spite of that not being true. Moreover, he had to convince the man’s girlfriend, someone who knew Harry as well as anyone. Knocking once at the door, he took a half step back, and blinked. No longer was he John Reynolds. No, now he was Harry Burroughs, star actor and boyfriend of the prettiest girl in the theater department. The door opened, revealing a mostly dark dorm room. Rebecca stood, partially hidden behind the door, wearing little but a nightgown. The light from the hallway cast her in dim shades of gray, and it was all the young man could do to not stop breathing at the sight of her. “Harry,” she whispered, seizing John by the arm. “You got my message. John’s such a loyal friend.” “Yes,” “Harry” replied, voice low, an octave deeper than it should have been. She noticed almost immediately. “You sound so hoarse,” she worried, reaching up to run a hand through his hair. “The play must have taken a lot out of you.” The young man nodded in response. One potential disaster averted. It seemed, though, that there wouldn’t be much close analysis, as Rebecca pulled John into her room, closing the door behind him, enveloping them both in darkness. What happened next was a combination of sweetness and sin, purity and pornography. Suffice it to say that Rebecca Reed took Jack Reynolds' virginity that night, completely convinced she was spending a night of passion with her boyfriend. When it was over, and they snuggled together, John felt only pleasure, only joy. There was no remorse. Perhaps that should have told him then and there where his life would go. ++++++++++++++++++++++++ Present After about three minutes in the real world, Jack Reynolds was growing impatient. “Done yet,” he asked, tapping his toe on the ground. “Oh! Yes,” the grandfather replied, snapping his mind back from the world of the sexual that he had been on the outside of for a few decades now. “You actually pulled it off,” Jack said, bemused. “I’m going to assume you’re reminiscing about doing her. What happened after that?” “I had to leave,” the old man admitted. “As much as I wanted to stay the night, if she saw me in the light, she’d have known. Besides, I had to get sleep. The Infinite Gauntlet was the next day, remember. It was the greatest night of my life, though. Well…until everything else that happened because of it.” Jack was relatively good at putting two and two together, and here was no exception. “What happened? She get pregnant?” John Reynolds’ eyes teared over as he nodded. “She did. She gave birth nine months later to a baby that looked nothing like Harry and way too much like me. Harry and Rebecca used to joke about me having had an affair with her. If they only knew…” “Wait a minute,” Jack interjected. “If he looked like you, how did they never figure it out? Harry would have known he didn’t sleep with her.” “That’s the odd part. Harry got drunk at the after party I was supposed to talk to him at, but he still managed to find his way back to her room. He curled up next to her at some point after I was long back in my room, and too drunk to remember that he didn’t have sex with his girlfriend that night.” “So, you’re a rapist,” the grandson said, nodding. He’d never really expected his grandfather to be capable of such a thing, but who could? Who could believe their own relative could commit a crime of any seriousness? “Among other things,” the grandfather replied, “but we’ll get to that in due time. That night changed my life. Once I realized I’d gotten away with it…” “You figured you could get away with anything.” John nodded to his grandson. “I did,” he said, “but that came later. First, I had a match to compete in. Drove to the show, sweating bullets the entire time. I was still terrified someone would find out. I’d showered for two hours to wipe the makeup off. When I got to the arena, I borrowed clippers from one of the makeup guys there and cut the rest of my hair off. As it turned out, it was better that way. The mask of Guise fit my head much better.” “Did you get to do an interview,” the young man asked. With the subject back to wrestling, he was clearly more comfortable. “I did,” John replied, a smile finally returning to his face, “but for the life of me, I can’t remember what I said. I was a rookie, up against guys like the Phantom Republican. I didn’t even really know what I wanted Guise to be yet, so I just talked. Come to think of it, though, I do remember one thing. I was being interviewed by this prettyish interviewer girl. Somehow, this quote by the Marquis de Sade came to mind…what was it again…hmmmm….ah!” Drawing together his full strength, the elderly man spoke forcefully, just as he had fifty-five years ago, at the beginning of his career in the sport of kings. "Let us give ourselves indiscriminately to everything our passions suggest, and we will always be happy...Conscience is not the voice of Nature but only the voice of prejudice." Shrugging his shoulders, John went on in his normal tone. “I guess it came up because of what I’d done the night before. I did what I wanted that night…first dressing up as Harry, and then sleeping with Rebecca….so I sort of decided then and there that was going to be what Guise was all about.” “Cool,” was the only reply Jack could muster up. “What then?” “I kissed her, through the mouth hole of the Guise mask. She was surprised, all sputtery. It was about then that I knew I could make something of myself. Really…I just made Guise into the person I was becoming, the person no one knew I was.” A small groan escaped the elderly man. Such a long storytelling session had left him feeling drained. Slowly, weak hands went to the sides of his chair, and he began to push himself up to his feet. “Forgive me, Jack, but I’m exhausted. I’m going to go take a nap, and we can continue my confessions later. If you want something to eat, feel free to fix it for yourself.” Taking hold of his cane, the grandfather began a slow, shaky gait towards his bedroom, leaving his grandson to stare at the retreating man. Was this all real? John Reynolds had been known to joke about things before…but he seemed far too serious. Was the beloved patriarch of the Reynolds family really a criminal? Jack couldn’t ask. But in spite of the situation, he couldn’t help but ask his grandfather one final question. “About the Infinite Gauntlet match…who won?” John Reynolds—no, Guise looked back over his shoulder at his grandson. Smiling a friendly, grandfatherly smile, he shrugged. “Who do you think?”
