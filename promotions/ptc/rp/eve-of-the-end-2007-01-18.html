Eve of the End?

18 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2007-01-18 "The minutes tick by slowly tonight. It's as if time itself aligns with him, prolonging the emergence of what he foretells. Can a man really hold such sway over the elements of life?" Doctor Bulwark regarded Rourke with a patient gaze. His superior was a disheveled mess, hunched over an uncharacteristically cluttered desk with a styrofoam cup of lukewarm coffee at his elbow. He didn't like what had happened to Dan as of late, with the paranoia and what he could swear was a semblance of faith in the promises of the lunatic in the adjoining ward. Bruce Shanahan had vowed that some ancient city, an undersea necropolis nearly as old as creation itself, would rise from the waves somewhere in the Pacific and bring with it a black new age, complete with global chaos and hellish doctrine. He'd been in Rourke's ear for weeks, feeding on the other's insatiable curiosity for what truth lurked behind that criminal psychosis. That was how Bulwark viewed it. Rourke thought he had controlled Shanahan, using him for the research he hoped would someday form the foundation of a stellar career. But even beneath all the sedatives, straitjackets and analysis sessions, Shanahan had been the puppet master. The man had been moving the strings not with his fingers but with his mind, the possibilities he offered to any who would enertain his notions. His elusions to a sinister, veiled reality had dredged up something not only in Doctor Daniel Rourke but in the whole of the Weirsburg asylum populace. An illusionist holding an audience spellbound as he weaved a web of deception all around them. Tricks under the guise of truth. Such was the spectacle of Bruce Shanahan and his madness. "Why have you forsaken everything you know is right, Dan?" asked Leonard Bulwark. "When did you stop being a man of science and believe in this bullshit mumbo-jumbo?" Rourke didn't even glance at him. His posture denoted the focus of his intent as being elsewhere, eyes only staring ahead at the computer screen, the deep lines in his face traced and illuminated by its glowing blue hue. "I never said I bought into it. But there's something going on here and he's the centerpiece. He's the source of the phenomenon, somehow. The phantoms, the premonitions, the chills we get when we walk the halls…" "An arcane lightning rod." Bulwark scoffed, incredulous in every sense of the word. Even as a child watching magic shows, Leonard had been the type who scanned the room for the mirrors, always searching for the secret in creating the ruse. "I wish you could hear yourself right now. There's nothing rational about it. Keep it up and you're going to be in a cell yourself. You'll be having needles stuck into you instead of administering them." He grunted as a drop of his own scalding hot coffee dripped onto his wrist, hurriedly wiping it off with his hand while hissing a curse. Rourke didn't even appear to notice and Bulwark fell silent for a long moment, only the ticking seconds on the overhead clock preventing total quiet. "Alright, let's pretend for a second that there's some genuine paranormal explanation for what's been happening around here. Suppose that whackjob is telling the truth to some extent and he's tainted this institution with some kind of real and tangible evil. What does it all mean? It all points to what exactly?" "If he's right?" Rourke grinned, pallid lips and bloodshot eyes framed by a face wrinked by exhaustion. "Then this might be the calm before the storm. The final chapter for a race soon extinguished." Bulwark sighed. "Go home, Dan. You don't have to be here. Besides, you look like hell." Rourke stood up, shaking his head erratically back and forth. "No….no, I can't do that. I have to see if he's telling tell the truth." The other drew comparisons to a strung-out meth junkie as the man he called his supervisor paced about the room, running hands through his hair and muttering to himself, hushed gibberish from what little Bulwark could make out. "Don't you get it, Leonard?! Why can't you see what's happening?!" "I'm afraid I don't understand, Dan." His response was met with a hardened glare, angry and almost accusing. Rourke picked up a packed manilla folder and tossed it at his subordinate, papers dashed to the flor as the paper clip detached and tinkled to the floor. "Everything, Leonard. The paranoia, the madness, the increased violence…hell, the fucking apparitions and the disembodied chants that everyone from the inmates to the janitors to my most rational employees have reported." Leonard Bulwark hoped no one would walk by and see him in this state, else it may cost him his position. "It's like the seven gates of Hell have opened in this goddamn place, man! And none of it, not the spooks or the paranoia or any of that shit, started until that man was put in our custody." Bulward wouldn't call Daniel Rourke mad yet; his eyes had yet to reveal such a terrible change. His obsession with this patient's psychotic theories was far from healthy but it had yet to reach a realm he would call sickness. Rourke had always been passionate about his work and it was ardent fervor he saw behind those bloodshot orbs. He'd been under a lot of stress --- the state itself was very persistent in knowing of Shanahan's progress --- and everything that had been happening at the Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane had taken a toll on his composure. Rourke pointed to the clock with one quivering finger; Bulward wasn't sure if it was the excessive intake of coffee or true fear. "In under one hour, it will be January the 16th. The day that Shanahan claims his city rises from the depths and this debauched god is unchained. He says that we'll be able to see its influence from here, in those who are 'more prone than others'. The stars will be right, and evil will ascend back into the world of the living to revel amidst chaos and madness." "Jesus, now you're sounding like him…" "Yeah, well, his words for the most part. I'm slightly paraphrasing." "Nothing is going to happen, Dan. Go home. Just by being in this damn place you're allowing all of this irrationable superstition to bounce around in your head and give it some credence. Go watch a movie, read a book, make love to the wife, whatever will take your mind off of that maniac's ramblings." Rourke, exhausted and trembling, fell back into his chair. Dark circles glared beneath sunken eyes, a normally well-shaven face sprouting day-old whiskers. Running his hand over his rough cheeks and neck, he grinned. "Maybe you're right, Leonard. I dunno, with everything that's been going on around here along with what he's said and the bad feeling that's been twisting my stomach…" "It's a natural inward reaction, Dan. They've put a lot of pressure on you. Me, the staff, the state, everyone. It's been unfair the responsibility that's been hovering over your head, it'd be a burden for Sigmund Freud. Let me handle things the rest of the night, you go home and get some rest. This place will still be here in the morning, I promise." Rourke grabbed his coat and headed for the door. Shrugging into his jacket, he wrapped his fingers around the doorknob, pausing. Only the buzz of the computer and the monotonous tick of the clock permeated the quiet, the latter almost deafening to a man so preoccupied by the time, and the sentence it could impose on all of man if the inmate a couple halls down wasn't as psychotic as they believed. "Dan?" Rourke turned back to peer into his burly colleague's eyes, greeted by a smile. "Relax. Everything is going to be ok. What makes the babble of this loon any different from the others?" Doctor Rourke said nothing, it was pointless anyhow. He flashed the slightest of smiles and exited the room. Things weren't quiet tonight in the Weirsburg asylum, not in the least. With tension having escalated further ever since the murder, Rourke had feared everything finally coming to a breaking point, and he had ventured that today would reflect his theory. Though it had been much more serene than he'd been led to believe by the self-declared prophet resting within its walls, a feeling of restlessness had permeated every corner of the madhouse throughout the day. Some of the prisoners, the more vocal of his emotionally troubled wing and the paranoid alike, spoke wildly of increases in their visions and an unsetlling frequency of the phantom sightings had cropped up across the vast ward. He hated leaving the bulk of the night's afflicitions to Leonard but knew the institution was in more than capable hands. He just needed a good night's rest. His friend was right about the state board's pressure and the impact the stress of managing this local campaign for the mentally ill; he'd break if he entertained Shanahan's unholy revelations and devil-talk as anything deeper than a deep criminal psychosis. Rest and a night alone with Miranda was the medicine he needed. "Doctor Rourke!" Train of thought halted, he paused in his musing stroll and turned to the source of the impassionaed yet shaky voice. Behind the bars of his cell, Devin Nester's face was twisted into a distorted mockery of a smile. A schizophrenic who was convinced he had nightly visits from the spirits of such infamous conquerors as Napoleon, Hitler and Pizarro, the balding little man was actually a favorite of the doctor's. Even moreso for his relatively upbeat personality than the interesting stories he could commonly relate. Grinning like a jack-o-lantern and exposing a row of yellowish teeth with thinned, deteriorating gums, he beckoned his caretaker closer and pointed eagerly to the corner of his cell. "Ah, Devin. Another of your visitors?" He's come to realize that treating Nester was largely dependent on staying loose and at ease, even humoring his delusions to a mild extent. Medication had failed to affect the man's long-degenerative mind, causing to Rourke to treat him more in the vein of a dying cancer patient than one of his salvagable charges. He smiled as he peered into the corner, obviously blind to the patient's hallucination. The other retained that upbeat aspect, smiling as he nodded feverishly. "Yah, sir. 'Dis her is one a' muh new'st guests. He's her ta teach me all 'bout muh place in life!" He actually beamed, flashing those decaying incisors proudly as if he was some being offered the role of some great leader." Dan smiled kindly. Not long ago, during one of their counseling sessions, Nester spoke of how Napoleon, during one of his many midnight visits, had divulged his battle plans and military arrangements prior to his fateful date at Waterloo. He was sick but much more harmless than many of his fellow inmates. A buff for military history prior to the night he'd gone violently insane many years ago, the hobby had left its telltale residue on his current mentality, a requiem of a long-lost sanity. "And what does he say, Devin? Is he telling you about strategy on the battlefield like your normal company?" Nester shook his head in earnest. "Nah, doctuh. 'Bout mah role in duh new world. He's been a'sayin' that's comin' and right soon! So I'd a'better be ready fo' when it do!" "What kind of new world, Devin?" Daniel Rourke's interest was piqued. Nester centered himself in his cell and frowned. It was as if the thought brought him sorrow, a tragic dawn awaiting just over the distant hill. "A hard 'un, Doctuh Rourke. He says tha' those who ain't ready will perish right quickly." The crazy little man paused, scratching the crown of his hairless head with the long nails on one gnarled hand. "I just a'learnin' but he 'un told me somethin' 'bout the will of the Crawlin' Chaos an' those that wall follow 'im. Dunno 'bout you, doctuh, but I'll be a'listenin' to what he wants when 'e comes! He and duh one from duh sea! Ain't that kinda funny, Doctuh Rourke?" The words grew more chilling with every syllable; never had the doctor found the words of an ignorant rustic so ominous, so evil. "Ah this time we thought our God was in tha' sky somewheres in Heaven. But he ain't! He be in the ocean right no', but he be a'comin' back mighty soon!" Rourke felt the chills envelop him again, drowned out every mad shriek and indiscernible utterance in the cells nearby. All he could see and hear were Nester's proclamations, delivered with optimism and hope despite their insidiousness. He swallowed hard, and felt the world he knew crumbling around him. He realized he could barely stammer out a response. "Wha…what else does he say, Devin?" He braced himself for the answer, almost cringing as he posed the inquiry he feared a response to. "Who is this 'god from the sea'?" Oblivious to the evil inherent in the 'delusion', the inmate turned to his invisible mentor in the corner and grinned widely. A child accepting candy and a ride from a stranger. Poor Devin Nester. He looked back to the psychologist and pursed his lips, his face taking on the features of a man in thought. "Hay said any 'un who den'us 'tis god wall suffer. I dun like it either, Doctuh Rourke, but he's seer'us. But ta evy'un else it'll be great!" "Who is this god, Devin? What's it called?" His voice was shaking but he couldn't prevent that now. He only needed the right answer to affirm his prayer was going unanswered, that coincidence didn't exist here. Bruce Shanahan might be mad, and he might be pure evil, but he was the oracle he professed. "Cutulu, or somet'in' like dat. Ish a funnuh name." As terror spread through the heart of Daniel Rourke, he spotted a movement in the corner of the cell. He gazed past the leering, toothy grin of one Devin Nester, the unfortunate bumpkin who tonight had the misfortune of being a clueless seer of demons, and latched his fear-stricken eyes on the shape slowly materializing into view before him. Hazy and near transparent, more of a sentient haze than an entity of Earthly flesh. He clenched his eyeslids tight in refusal to behold the spawn of the abyss. "I'm seeing things," he muttered to himself through closed eyes, still resistant to the things he'd seen and heard. He wouldn't be swept up in the paranoia of the place he supervised. He'd be strong and lead them out of this like a true leader. He wouldn't allow himself to be beguiled by tricks of the light, or swayed by the deranged machinations of a psychopath… His eyes flashed open, and he stared into the fiery orbs of the unspeakable. This disembodied haze, eyes flickering and flaring with the very essence of the demonic, regarded him with a terrible fixed gaze from its spot in the corner. For a moment that felt like eternity, he was unable to move, and whether it was by some supernatural property of the thing or his own overwhelming fear Daniel Rourke couldn't say. He stood frozen in horror while Devin Nester, seemingly unaware of the nature of what now shared his cell, smiled absently, his own eyes seemingly locked on something past Rourke. Whether something else lurked in that hallway beyond was a question Rourke couldn't answer and rejected answering. "Roooouuuurrrke," it hissed with a haunting echo, an infernal, coalesced voice like two speaking at once. "He riiiiisssessss." Finally able to move again, he could bear no more. In a panic, and undaunted as to who may see him so in such a state, he raced for the double-doors that offered freedom from the asylum and whatever malevolence had come to dwell in its shadowy halls. He could feel more eyes on him as he dashed down the hall though whether they were other silent inmates or more like that fiend he couldn't say. He plowed through the exit and in a soaked parking lot, himself drenched in rainfall only moments after leaving the building. Rushing for the presumed safety of his car, Rourke lost his footing on the slick lot and crashed into a puddle that had gathered in a pothole. A rush of pain surged through the knee that had absorbed most of the fall, drawing curses through chattering teeth. It was only a moment, Rourke quickly reaching his feet and breaking back into a run. Whatever it was might be giving chase now, tracking the frightened mortal to keep him from divulging its existence to the world. From telling Stryker and Dawson, and the men they worked for. From telling their Coalition and the Church. Yes, that was his only chance. Stryker, Dawson, a church, anything. A sanctuary from the evil. Salvation from the abyss he'd stared into, and that had stared back. He fumbled with his keys in the blanketing downpour, wiping away the rain that diluted his vision and throwing open the door the moment he turned the key. Collapsing into the driver's seat, he inserted the keys into the ignition and paused, catching his breath. His chest heaved as he glanced into the rearview mirror, seeing nothing. Only the asylum's closed doors left in his wake, lights dotting office windows. Nothing was after him. He was out. He glanced at the glowing digits beside the dashboard and scowled. He couldn't shake his instincts, couldn't forget the madness in the inmate's eyes are he told of what was to come. And he couldn't shed the image of whatever hellish thing was in that cell with Devin Nester, eyes that burned with the fires of nethermost Hell, a mirror reflecting the fate awaiting civilization. Of that he found himself unbearably certain. It was all coming down. Either he was going mad himself or he was wrong. Wrong to believe in the logic and science he'd devoted his entire life to. But he would soon find out which one it was. It was 11:59 p.m. * * * * Maury Dawson knelt at the base of an oak tree, its branches skeletal and bare in the throes of winter, icy rain drenching him from head to toe. He gazed into the windows of the sprawling house with a just and feverish intent, eyes dancing across what was visible in the barren courtyard to the right, scouting for sentries or any unplanned company. It reminded him too much of Wyatt Manor, the base of Shanahan's operations in Vermont. But this had nothing to do with Shanahan right now, his vile minions had been their informants and that was that. He needed to get the image of that bastard out of his mind. Denise Janssen was as much an enemy of justice and the rational world as the one he once knew as Chaney, the veiled demon Masquerade. The Blackhelm Coalition had delved into her past and into her cult and the findings were both startling and brutal; she was more murderous than the former's nefarious entourage, the Sect of Black Wisdom, and the dogma she promoted was the very embodiment of stereotypical bloodlust. The Order of the Horned Beast had to be stopped tonight before more were sacrificed in the name of its irreverence. Denise Janssen had to be stopped before more were brought into the fold to accept her bloodthirsty doctrine. 'The Wraith Princess' she'd been called, a fitting moniker for such a soulless fiend. "Dawson. Come in, Marcus." He lowered the radio as leaves crunched under the footfalls of his squad. They hunkered down around him, under the cover of night and shadow, behind the centuries-old trees and crouched behind jagged pieces of long-corroded fencing. The night brought with it rain, cold though unseasonable, and a foreboding silence that put that cold and dread feeling in Maury Dawson's gut. He hadn't been in the field for a long time, not since his past association with the Blackhelm's Coalition had ended. It reminded him of her, of Sage Zimmerman Dawson, a thought he had to put out of his head. Reminiscing during a cult raid might get him killed. Carelessness in the past had already killed the one he loved… "I'm here, Maury," crackled the radio. "We're in position. Where are you?" "West side of the estate," he replied. "We have an area fortified in case this goes bad. This is a bad night to take Janssen, Marcus. I have one of those damnable feelings." He scowled and swiped rain droplets from the mic on his headset. "And I don't want casualties." There were bad rumors about what was happening only minutes from now, what midnight could mean for the entire world. Stryker felt that it was an opportune time to advance on the Wraith Princess and her Order, the whole lot of them shacked up in this delapidating mansion in northern New York. They'd never see it coming tonight, he said. Dawson couldn't help but admire Marcus Stryker's tenacity; he was the sort who would challenge the forces of Hell on Allhallows Eve if he thought they had a chance of surprise. Movement on the other end, a sigh from Marcus. "You never want casualties. But if they happen, they happen. As a leader you have to accept that things may not go according to plan in every sense. You were here a while back, my friend. It's kinda like riding a bike, y'know?" The days of the Salem witch hunts were long dead. The Blackhelm Coalition, the secretive strongarm legion of the Vatican, still carried out its inquisition on a worldwide scale, simply along a more rational and justifiable path. The corps of Blackhelm had disarmed extremists of every one of Hell's denominations and toppled cults before they could ever grow to challenge conventional dogma. Terrible things lurked in the dark corners of the Earth and beyond, and it was their job to see them seized and stopped before they could ever reach the light. Just because Rome publically denied the existence of demons didn't mean they actually doubted their existence. For Marcus Stryker, it was another day on the job. For Maury Dawson, it was a return to a position that had once broken his heart and ruined his life. Clouds moved overhead erratically, struggling against the wind to blot out the light of the moon. Dawson could envision the war being waged right now, God warring against the sinister forces they were here to cripple, Him offering the cover of night and darkness versus their attempt to spotlight the would-be slayers of their devotees. The irony of such a battle settled quickly on Dawson's steel-trap of a mind before a visualization of Shanahan again came to mind. A bloodied sword was raised in his hand, and hordes of savage, star-spawned beasts drooled and slavered to his rear. A general of the infernal and usurper of God, just as he wanted. Just as he's endeavored to make Maury aid him toward a year ago under the guise of a messiah. Marcus's voice again chimed in over the headset, interrupting the unwanted fantasy. Dawson wasn't one to complain. "You're thinking of Bruce, aren't you?" piped Stryker. "How'd you know?" "He's been the focus of your efforts ever since you rejoined us. And given that we're set to eliminate presumably one of the greatest threats to his own little theological regime at his behest…well, suffice to say I wanted to check in." Dawson rubbed his frigid and clammy hands together. Exhaling, the glow of the inside lights grew hazy through the mist of his breath. "I can't shake the feel that we're doing his dirty work. Killing another zealot just so he doesn't have the blood on his hands. Maintaining that damn image of his innocence." Stryker scoffed. "No. I know what that bastard wants. I know him and his wrestling endeavors too well, and that was the main reason he had his lackeys tell us about this place. I don't care about 'Raven' or Jason Snow. I just know this bitch is a cold-blooded killer and that she's murdered in the name of some type of foolish religious devotion. That's it. And that's why we're here" Dawson paused, static crackling over the line. The weapon cradled in his arms was slick with the rainfall and his exposed fingers bristled with chill of January night. "He wanted us to distract Snow to get a leg up for that damn Tully," Stryker resumed after a moment. "I've got more important things to do than run around interviewing wrestlers about their old flames. And right now, you need to worry about ridding the world of a glorified serial killer. For one minute just forget the Church, forget the cathedrals and the steeples in Rome, forget about holy wars and skirmishes between gods in Heaven and everything else the bishops consider prime material for rallying morale speeches. Think about preventing more people from being butchered by Denise Jannsen." Dawson nodded to himself but still couldn't shake the terrible feeling burning in the pit of his stomach. He looked around at those surrounding him before shifting his eyes to the ground, glowering. These men had families, lives, convictions. He couldn't let them down or lead them into the jaws of death. It was just the one question burning inside that bothered him. If they killed Denise Jannsen and found evidence of her involvement in the massacre in Providence, it would clear Bruce Shanahan and his cult of the crime. He'd be free to leave the asylum in Weirsburg, and avoid prison altogether. Was it worth it to free such a monster? Was this the justice he wanted? "It's time, Maury," came the command. He'd dreaded it ever since the raid was planned. "Let's move." He swallowed his own virtues and did as he was told. With a gesture, they advanced on 'The Wraith Princess' Denise Janssen's deathly abode. It was 11:59 p.m. * * * Darkness, then a gentle emergence of light. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan, postured much in the model of the famous 'The Thinker' sculpture, slowly and warily lifts his head. His eyes gleam with madness in the meager light, almost taking on a shine of their own, as a malevolent smile further adds to his wicked image. He's wreathed in shadow, the background pitch-black and devoid of luminance. A showcase of the darkness he so flawlessly represents. "What is law? What is virtue? And why do you hold these things so dearly? They're anomalies of the universe. All along, my ambition has been to help you realize that. When you do, you'll truly be free. A man unchained by righteousness and the burdens of this world can open his eyes to what lies beyond the veil, and behold its magnificence without the trappings of regret or pain. Such is the real meaning of existence. Without that realization, without shedding those false ideals, you only invite greater pain when it all ends here and you enter the realm of the immortal. I appeal to the darkness in us all. And by doing that, I appeal to the hunger for truth within every man. Inherent in us all is the knowledge that what we're told to believe is an elaborate fraud. It's a concoction of those following fearful doctrines wanting us to stay blind to what surrounds us in this universe. They crave our ignorance and demand faith in a magical, self-righteous dream. The universe isn't so kind. And denying it won't change a goddamn thing. Their deceit cannot go on any longer. But people won't always see truth. They refuse to wipe away the sugar-coating to get to the 'rotten' core, regardless of the necessity. They need comfort and solace. But it goes against the wisdom that time has forgotten, the lost gospels that your spiritual leaders have swept under the carpet. There is no time for pleasantry in this reality. It will only bring you pain and scorn in the next world. This is a quest for power between the gods. The God you may know seeks to regain his grip on us, the master race he supposedly built in his image before he lost us to his own apathy and neglect. You won't read that latter part in any Bible but its the truth. Those I serve are out to reclaim what is theirs: this universe, this world, and all the dominions that go with them. Their guidance far surpasses the paltry offerings you may think awaits you upon the throne of Heaven. Let me tell you, friend, nothing will await you upon that throne. The lies that your divine crown has urged must be halted. That's why I'm here. I want you to know Adam and Eve didn't give in to the sin of temptation when they ate from the tree: they were simply carrying out their original design. A design corrupted by your impudent God when he rebelled against our true masters. Follow me and help write a new Genesis. A new world will be forged from the disgraced and debauched past. Our masters will be pleased. Heavenly blood will flow, and it shall be I clutching the crimson sword that ends its tyranny.
