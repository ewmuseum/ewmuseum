"Dementia" (IG 14)

9 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Colin Gilchrist was seldom home, and when he was he spent the time either engaging in sexual acts with his wife or spent hours in his study den going over his research. There wasn’t much that science could teach him. Like a Beethoven, Einstein, or Archimedes, he was simply ahead of his times. Still, Colin liked to indulge himself in the latest of the advances in science, particularly molecular physics and biochemistry – spectrums which he still had to explore. There were times like this, however, when Colin simply wanted to relax in his chair and watch TV. Children Amelia and Ariel played on the carpeted floor beneath him. They were too young to understand or grasp the concept of an absent father – an excuse Colin would hope to use as a scapegoat. He loved them, though. But, he also loved his work and in time would accomplish his goal. Tossed on the leather couch beside Colin were stacks of paper from his lab. Colin sat back comfortably on the couch enjoying his dinner while he watched the local news. “Can you believe it?” Colin turned around to see his wife, Veronica, sit down with her tray of food beside him. “What’s the matter,” he asked her, taking a spoonful of brown rice into his mouth. Veronica shook her head while cutting her meet. “Thirteen people from this town disappeared…without a trace. How do thirteen people all vanish at once?” “Incredible.” It was indeed incredible, but for Colin it was routine. They had assumed that their primitive antics would work against a man who was far beyond their own comprehension and appreciation. Colin disposed of them easily. Police are still on the search tonight for the disappearance of thirteen men nearly two months ago. Eyewitnesses report seeing a group of men matching the description of the men police released a few weeks ago packing into several pick up trucks and heading towards the town square. “You think they’re all dead,” Veronica asked, her eyes staying glued to the television. “I’d assume so.” “You don’t think anything weird is going on in this town do you?” To this date only the trucks have been found deep in the woods with no apparent evidence. Forensics teams scaled the scene from edge to edge with no results. Police continue to scout the area and highways leading out of the town are still being monitored regularly. Colin looked at her and chuckled. “Don’t look at me like I’m crazy,” Veronica snapped back. “Look at all the disappearances. Look at what happened with that guy at your center a few months back. What was his name? Fred?” “Frank.” “Right, Frank. He lost his mind and then killed himself by stabbing his throat with a bold point pen repeatedly.” If anyone has information that may help the police in this most disturbing case, they are urged to contact the information hotline seen below. All calls will be discreet and your identity will not be revealed. Tom, back to you. “The guy went insane,” Colin replied, “it’s simple as that. I’m sure what happened to those thirteen men isn’t too big a deal. Maybe they went hunting and got lost, got into some trouble, and that’s it. Maybe they did die, but it wasn’t by anything paranormal.” “I hope you’re right. This town gives me the creeps.” **** The light bulb flickered on and off dangling from a lone thin wire. The room was desolate – paint-chipped walls and cement floors framed the small room. It seemed more like a jail cell with a view than someone’s bedroom. Miguel Stephens lay on his cot looking up at the ceiling and the stains of decay decorating the run-down slums. He twiddled with his fingers, almost subconsciously. Unable to rest soundly, he sits on the edge of the bed and turns on the television – straight to the news channel. The news is still covering the disappearance of thirteen men nearly two months ago, a case that has left the town shackled in bewilderment. Miguel’s uncontrolled shaking keeps him tapping the remote control on the steel bedpost. “I-I-I know who did it,” he whispered to himself, crouching over. “It was that doctor, G-G-Gilchrist.” “T-T-They do all kinds of weird e-e-experiments in that b-b-building of his,” he says, gripping the remote tighter and tighter. If anyone has information that may help the police in this most disturbing case, they are urged to contact the information hotline seen below. All calls will be discreet and your identity will not be revealed. Tom, back to you. “Yes,” Miguel screamed. “I got s-s-some information for you.” He threw the remote control aside – it landed hard on the floor exposing its batteries. He reached for the phone, but stopped in his tracks before picking up the receiver. “I’m sorry Sheila. I know I made some mistakes about you, about us. I should have been honest with you from the start about your brother. It’s my fault h-h-he’s dead. I k-k-know I can’t take it back, but I-I c-c-can make it r-r-right.” He picked up the phone and dialed the information hotline. After a few moments of ringing, he’s greeted by an operator who assures him that his identity will remain anonymous. Miguel said he doesn’t care, not as long as the criminal is put behind bars. “I know where they went to, those men. The day before one of t-t-them was telling m-me that they were g-g-g-going to go to t-t-that building on the o-o-other side of town – the Psychoanalysis T-T-Treatment C-Center.” Miguel kept nodding, reassuring the operator that what he was saying was true. “Y-Yeah, the men w-w-wanted to do something to Dr. C-Colin G-Gilchrist. I-I-I think something happened t-t-to t-t-them there. I know for a fact that they w-w-went there. You have to check it out.” Miguel hung up and laughed with joy. He blamed Dr. Colin Gilchrist for the destruction of his girlfriend Sheila’s brother, Luke. Miguel had learned that Luke was feeling depressed about life and had sought the doctor’s reassurance. Since their untimely meeting, Luke had slipped further away, moving on to drugs and eventually getting his sister in, too. It made Miguel furious and unable to control his actions when he beat Luke to death. Miguel nodded and reattempted to get some rest. As he lay back down he thought he saw something at his window. He looked again at the corner of his eye and saw a skinny kid dressed in a black turtle neck standing at his window. But that was unreal – his window was three stories high and he had no ledge. Miguel quickly rubbed his eyes and looked back towards the window again. There was nothing there; it was just a figment of his imagination. Dr. Colin Gilchrist walked down the main hall of his research center. Next to him, keeping up the pace, was his friend and assistant, Demetri Vieri. “What’s this about?” Colin asked, perturbed. “It’s a couple of detectives from the precinct, Colin.” Demetri said. “They want to talk to you about what happened last month.” “I suppose it was only a matter of time before they came here.” “Do you want me to handle this situation?” Colin shook his head. He knew what Demetri meant by it and there was no need for that. “No. I’ll handle this one myself.” Colin walked through the main doors towards the awaiting detectives. Colin smiled and greeted them both. “Good day, detectives,” he said. “I hope I can help you in whatever it is you came here for.” Detectives Sally Weathers and Alex Brandon had been partners for years and on the force longer. They were accustomed to overly examples of friendliness and dismissed the doctor’s charms. “How about we go somewhere a bit more informative?” Colin nodded and led them to his personal office. “So, what can I do for you?” Colin asked. Sally and Alex took their seats. Sally leaned over on the desk and Colin saw that she had been quite the eye candy in her earlier years. In her mid-thirties, her body was still in shape, but it was no scarred by the ravages and toils of her work. “We received a tip that the thirteen men who disappeared almost two months ago were seen heading in your direction, Dr. Gilchrist.” Sally said. Alex chimed in. “The fact is that your institute here has been the cornerstone of a lot of accidents within the last year.” Colin cleared his throat. “Now those incidents were proven-“ “To be accidents,” Sally said. “Don’t you think that it’s ironic that a town known for his living conditions experiences such awful circumstances the moment you arrive here?” Colin shook his head. “That’s absurd. My family and I have been nothing short of outstanding citizens here despite how the rest of the town treats us.” “Mr. Gilchrist,” Ales said, “we are not here to make excuses, but the facts don’t lie. You had several employees within the last six months – two dozen to be exact – disappear under some inexplicable circumstances.” “I-“ “Not to mention that nearly half of those were later found dead,” sally replied. “It’s a bit off that half the employees associated with you all commit suicide. All that’s missing is a gallon of red Kool-Aid.” Colin stood up from his char. “I’ve been nothing but supportive here. All those incidents had nothing to do with me or my institution. Your pals at the department had already been through this before and it was proven time and time again that I was innocent.” “How do you explain the death of Frank Smith?” Sally asked. “He was linked to one of the victims last month,” Alex replied. “His brother had been reportedly sending threats to you and your family.” “That’s right,” Colin said, nodding. “But that doesn’t prove anything. If you want, I’ll submit every member of my staff to finger-printing, questioning, or whatever you may need.” Alex and Sally looked at each other and then nodded. “That’d be great if you can provide us with a list of visitors for the past two months.” “And access to all your security videos for the past month as well.” Colin nodded. “How is all that going to help?” “Well,” Alex replied. “Whoever is responsible may not be an employee here but just someone making the most of his situation. In any case, we appreciate your assistance.” Colin opened the door for them. “There might be a way I can help you now, detectives.” “What is it?” Sally asked. “We still have the feeds from the last two months,” Colin answered. “And if we’re lucky we may still have the feeds from the night in question.” ”All right, let’s see them.” Colin led them down several stairs past many secured doors. He opened the door and led them inside. “What the hell is this place?” **** Miguel Stephens walked into a local bar called the Spitfire. Inside, the bar was decorated in a tribute to the old Western days. The floors were boarded with mahogany, the walls decorated with vintage newspaper clippings from the 1800’s (obviously fake and doctored) and dim orange lights gave the bar the autumn feel. The bar was usually busy, but on this night it was only partially decorated with tenders. Miguel took a seat at the bar. “G-Give me a shot of Vodka, keeps.” The bartender didn’t respond but in an automated fashion, conjured up the drink and slid it down the table. Miguel downed the shot quickly and asked for another. He downed that one, too. He kept asking for more and after nearly a dozen shots, the bartender refused to comply with his requests. “W-What are you doing?” Miguel asked. “Ain’t y-y-you going to give me my drinks?” The bartender shook his head. “You ain’t in no shape to be drinking no more.” Miguel got up –clumsily – from the bar. “I can drink. Y-Y-You best give me another one, if yer know w-what’s good for ya.” The other patrons remained uncaring for the event. This sort of thing happened all the time. The bar tender remained calm. “If you don’t leave here, you’ll be forced out.” Miguel took his glass and threw it at the bartender. He missed but the glass shattered on the wall and floor. “Get him out of here!” the bartender yelled, and a pair of big thuggish men wrapped their muscular arms around Miguel and dragged him off the bar as if he were a paper doll. Miguel kept yelling profanities. Miguel stopped when something on the television caught his attention: the news. Miguel begged to be let able to watch the news and that he would leave on his own terms. The bouncers looked over at the bartender and he nodded in approval. Last night the police received an anonymous tip regarding the disappearance of thirteen men nearly two months ago. The rest of the bar was no fixated on the news. The tip police received was from an unknown source claiming that he had seen the thirteen men heading for the Psychoanalysis Treatment Center, the workplace of Dr. Colin Gilchrist. Dr. Gilchrist had been linked with several incidents in the past. “T-T-That’s because he’s a murderer!” “I hope they jail that son of a bitch.” “He’s the reason my brother’s gone!” It should be noted that one of the victims was the brother of Frank Smith, the ex-employee of PTC who went insane and then stabbed himself in the neck repeatedly with a bald point pen. Police say that the brother of Frank Smith had been sending threats to the doctor and his family. “If I ever get m-m-my hands on h-h-h-him…” Miguel said, held by the bouncers. Earlier today, detectives talked with the doctor who was reported as being very cooperative. Detective Alex Brandon went on recorded as saying, “Dr. Gilchrist is no longer a suspect to this tragedy. While we do not out rule the possibility of an employee within Dr. Gilchrist’s institute, we inform the public that Dr. Gilchrist has been exempt from all charges. Earlier today, we were given several conclusive reports and access to all personnel at PTC.” Detectives have in their possession the feeds from the security cameras. The thirteen men were nowhere near the research center and Dr. Gilchrist is free of all charges, once again. “No!” Miguel shouted. He struggled to break free, but the bouncers were too strong for him. Finally, Miguel shouted that he was going to leave on his own will, and did. Outside, Miguel adjusted his clothes, still furious at what he had heard. He knew the doctor was the blame for the incident, two months ago. He knew the doctor was to blame for a lot of things. “Those s-s-stupid detectives can’t do shit,” Miguel said, walking down the street. “You c-c-can’t depend on them for shit anymore. You got to do t-t-things yourself if you want them done.” Miguel looked across the street to see a policeman walking out of his patrol car. Usually cops patrol in pairs, but this one was inexplicably on his own. He watched the cop walk into the corner store and then surveyed the scene around him. It was late and the streets were empty. Miguel took his time crossing the street, giving the cop time to walk out of the store and be in the open. When he did, Miguel greeted him at the car. “Excuse me, sir.” Miguel said. “I’m a bit lost.” “Where do you want to go?” the cop asked. “The Ramada Hotel,” Miguel replied, saying the first place that came to mind. The cop laughed. “Wow, man. That’s all the way on the other side of town.” “Fuck…” Miguel replied. “You know where I-I can catch a c-c-cab?” “Sure,” the cop replied, and then turned around to point the way. “Walk about three blocks-“ Miguel clobbered the cop with an elbow to the back of the head, knocking him out cold. He opened the door and threw the cop inside, driving off to an alley a few blocks away. With no one in sight, Miguel dragged the cop out of the car and stripped him of his uniform. The cop woke up to find himself in nothing but his socks and boxers, and gagged and tied like a hog. “I-I’m sorry about t-t-this,” Miguel said, wearing the policeman uniform. “B-But you guys c-c-can’t do anything right.” The cop watched as Miguel got into the car and then drove off. **** The sound of police sirens blared behind Colin on the highway in the outskirts of town. Colin looked at his rearview mirror and was surprised to see the police care behind him swaying awkwardly. What the hell is going on, he thought pulling over the side. But he was not fast enough for the police car bumped into his rear bumper sending him into a spin off the road. Unharmed, but a bit shaken, Colin struggled to get his door open. “D-D-Don’t fucking move,” the policeman shouted, approaching the car with a gun. Colin raised both his hands in clear view and saw that the policeman was shaking uncontrollably. “Relax man,” Colin shouted, “I got nothing just stay calm.” “I-I-I told you n-not to fucking m-move,” Miguel yelled, opening the door. “Now get out and keep your hands in the air.” “Whatever you want,” Colin said, walking slowly out of his car, “I’ll give it to you. I’m a wealthy man, I got money. You want money, you got it.” “S-Shut up,” Miguel yelled and clobbered Colin in the middle of his back with the revolver. Colin’s legs crumpled beneath him, but quickly regained composure. Miguel was shaking uncontrollably and his breathing seemed labored. Colin noticed the unusual amount of sweat pouring down his face. Whatever was wrong with him, it was severe indeed. “What do you want?” “I-I want you t-to shut up and k-k-keep walking into the woods,” Miguel replied. “You ain’t g-gonna fuck up our town anymore. Y-y-you ain’t going to be a curse dangling in our family anymore. It’s your entire fault she’s leaving me.” “Who,” Colin asked, walking straight into the woods with his hands raised, followed by Miguel. “Sheila,” Miguel replied, “she was my girlfriend. I loved her. But now she hates me because her brother died. He died because of you.” “I never killed anyone in my life!” “Liar!” Colin felt the sting of the revolver’s butt right between the shoulder blades. He managed to get a glimpse of where they were: nowhere. No one could hear him scream for help. And if they could, they’d have a hard time following them in the dark of the night in the middle of the dense woods. “You did something to her brother, Luke.” Miguel said, pointing the gun towards the downed Colin. “I-I know you did s-s-something to mess h-h-his mind, so he went to d-drugs. He had nowhere to turn to and he m-m-messed his body and m-mind with that shit and he got his s-s-sister into it.” “I’m sorry about what happ-“ “Shut up,” Miguel yelled. “He was distraught, a piece of shit, d-d-didn’t know what to do. Then he got in our way. But I couldn’t see that it w-wasn’t h-h-his fault. I thought he was r-r-responsible for everything, for f-f-fucking Sheila up, for ruining our r-r-relationship.” “What happened?” “I s-s-snapped,” Miguel replied, wiping the sweat from his forehead. “I saw him r-r-ruining Sheila’s life and I beat him to death. But you don’t understand. I-I-I thought it was him doing it. But he was t-t-trying to recover. I made a mistake and I c-c-c-can’t take it b-back. But I can sure as hell m-m-make it right.” Colin slowly rose to his feet keeping his hands high in the air. “Whatever happened in the past with you is ok. I’m not going to tell anyone. Just let me get you help. We can work this out, somehow.” Miguel shook his head. He was now almost about to cry. “There’s no turning back.” “Do you think Luke would want this?” “Y-Y-Yes!” “Are you sure?” “What are you talking about?” Miguel asked. “I got the gun right fucking here and I know Luke would want me to get revenge for what you did to him in that center of yours.” What makes you so sure of that? Miguel quickly turned around full circle in fright. “W-W-who’s fucking there,” he screamed. “Show yourself or I swear I-I-I’ll fucking put six bullets in your head.” Miguel turned to his right to see a tall, dangly kid standing by the bushes wearing black. His face looked pale, his hair tangled like a black widow’s web, and his lips lacked in color. It was Sheila’s brother, Luke. But it couldn’t be. Luke was dead. “Who are you?” Miguel asked, his attention now fully shifted from Colin to the new threat. “I’m Luke.” “No, you died.” “Jesus, what the hell did my sister see in you,” Luke asked. “Ain’t you ever hear of a ghost?” Miguel shook his head, tears dripping down his face. “You’re such a loser. You abused my sister. You didn’t care about her.” “That’s not true. I know I made my mistakes, but I loved her.” Ethereal Luke laughed. Colin stood in his spot mesmerized by what he was seeing: Miguel talking to himself. “You loved her? You let her get away, and you beat up her helpless brother. You beat him to death, Miguel,” Luke answered. “Stop it!” Miguel said, crying. “Y-Y-You’re not real. Y-Y-You’re just a figment of my imagination.” “And don’t you think your conscious is trying to tell you something?” Miguel dropped to his knees, his revolver still in his hands. “I didn’t know.” “Bullshit, you didn’t know! You couldn’t stand me. You’re a fucking loser. You just wanted to kill me to get me out of the way.” “That’s not true,” Miguel said, rubbing his head with the palm of his hands repeatedly. “I thought you were someone else.” “Typical. Even when you’re being haunted by your own conscious you still swim in denial. Well listen to this, [i[friend, I am who you thought I was. I did drug up my sister. It wasn’t anything Colin did. I did it myself.” Miguel looked up from his fetal position. “What?” “The guy you mourn over so much is a fucking junkie,” Luke laughed. “And to think you were so determined on this revenge idea makes me all kinds of happy. You’re an idiot, Miguel. You always were one and you will always be one.” Luke floated towards Miguel as he fired off two shots on him. But it was futile. The shots went through Luke, penetrating the deep trunks behind him. “G-G-Go away,” Miguel shouted, pleading. “Leave me alone.” “You’re going to die tonight, Miguel.” Luke said, cackling. “I just wish Sheila were here to see this.” Luke approaching the Miguel, cackling maniacally. “G-G-Get away from m-me,” Miguel, shouted, burying his head between his legs. “Get away from me! Get away from me! Get away me!” “Are you okay?” Miguel looked up to see Colin Gilchrist attending to him. He was still shaking and looked around for Luke, but the ghost had long gone. “Are you ok,” Colin asked, putting one hand on Miguel’s shoulder. Miguel shook his head. His eyes still glared with hatred towards Colin. Colin leaned in and whispered into his ear. “I have a confession to make…” Miguel slowly turned to face the doctor, his lips curling into a devious snarl. “…I am the one who killed your brother,” Colin said, smiling. “And I killed you, too.” Miguel shook his head. “What are you talking about?” Colin stood up and stretched. This was his favorite part: telling his victims how superior he was. “It was nothing really. Luke came to me asking for work and I gave it to him. Although, I think it wasn’t the work he wanted, but when I saw this dangly kid all alone, it was just too much to pass up. He couldn’t take the pressure and he found solace in drugs, my drugs.” Miguel was getting furious. He had been right about the doctor all along. “So then he became a piece of shit, an improvement, actually,” Colin said, laughing. “And now I have you here, but it’s a shame you’re going to kill yourself.” “Y-Y-You son of a b-b-bitch,” Miguel screamed, taking aim at Colin. “I’m going to fucking kill y-y-you.” Colin stood his ground waiting valiantly for the shot, but it never came. Miguel struggled to pull the trigger. The motivation was there, the need was there, Miguel wanted to pull the trigger, but his finger wouldn’t budge. He was experiencing a sort of paralysis. The kind people experience when they wake up subconsciously from their sleep and are unable to move because their brain’s still wiring their motor reflexes towards stop. “Did you really think you were going to kill me?” Miguel didn’t bother to answer, he kept insisting on pulling the trigger. “Luke wasn’t the only one to experiment. You were, too. Oh, don’t get so surprised. Luke poisoned your food and your drinks. You were just as much a junkie as he was. But don’t blame him, he wasn’t exactly in control.” Tears ran down Miguel’s face, but he wasn’t crying – he was furious. “You b-b-bastard. Why can’t I k-kill you?” “Because you don’t want to,” Colin replied. “Did you think that everything you did was of your own doing? The loss of consciousness, the beatings, the time you went berserk and killed Luke? You thought that was all you?” “You’re going to kill me-“ “No. It’s not worth it. See, right now you are nothing. No one cares about you. Why would I do you the favor of turning you into some kind of martyr? When the police find your body, it’ll be a suicide. You assaulted a police officer, stole his clothes, stalked me, and then killed yourself.” “W-W-What?” “You killed yourself, Miguel.” Colin said. “Such a shame it was, really.” Colin adjusted his suit and turned his back towards him. “We will miss you, Miguel.” Colin slowly walked away hearing the ruffling of dead leaves behind him. He could hear Miguel laboring hard to pull the trigger and could hear the rattling of flesh against steel. Then suddenly, he heard the sound of a click…and then a bang. For a moment everything was still, even the air. Colin looked down at his suit, still clean. He turned around and saw Miguel lying on the floor in a puddle of his own blood. The revolver he had bought him to exact his revenge lay just outside his right hand. Miguel had taken his own life and died – just as Luke and Colin said he would. Colin shrugged and walked back to the car, smiling. “You should’ve had a better plan.”
