"The Poem" -- Archibald MacGregor

3 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * * * The Devil himself sat behind his desk, staring down at the poem. Typherion, his most recent choice for a name, read the passage multiple times with the uneasy sensation never leaving his gut. Glancing out of the window in his chamber, he looked out across the vast battlefield that separated Hell from Heaven. Even visible hundreds of miles away, Typherion could barely make out the marble fortress etched into the Heavenly mountainside. Between the fortress he currently resided in, and His off in the distance, a battle waged. Various volleys of flaming arrows shot into the air, while hundreds of thousands of Hell’s soldiers engaged in fierce combat with those from Heaven. Occasionally, the clear glassy orb sitting in the center of his chamber would glow an electric blue as his minions “killed” the angels from Heaven. Undoubtedly in God’s fortress across the war-torn lands, a matching orb would glow when his demons fell in battle at the hands of Heaven. Typherion glanced back down at the poem once more when Kiln walked into his chamber. Standing over 8 feet in height, Kiln had been Typherion’s highest ranking warrior for nearly a lifetime. His black hair was pulled back into a ponytail that reached all the way down his back. A giant battle-axe was strapped across his broad shoulders, even though Kiln had never seen the battlefield. Kiln stopped and stared at his master for a moment before speaking, “The MacGregor child has been born.” Typherion nodded as the orb flashed blue a few more times. Kiln, never a demon of many words, stood in silence. He noticed the flashes of the orb as well, and then looked out across the rocky battlefield before finally taking a step towards the window to watch, “I see you have visited the archives, m’lord. May I ask for what purpose?” Typherion sat in his throne without answering, then finally shutting the book. He stood, brushing off the dust the massive book had ejected as it closed, and walked over beside his most trusted slave, “Nothing important, just inquiring about my predecessors tactics in battle. Our numbers are diminishing, we need to replenish our ranks.” Kiln nodded in agreement as his master continued. “When was the last time that a generation six was born?” “Fifty three years ago, but he wasn’t able to be converted.” “Does it worry you that this MacGregor child is the same generation as you?” Typherion asked, looking at his minion directly in the eyes. “Yes, it does.” “And why is that? He is a mere infant, and even if we took him now he’d be more powerful than anyone across that battlefield,” Typherion flicked his finger towards the vast lands between the fortresses of Heaven and Hell, “Are you jealous that he might arrive and unseat your position?” Kiln turned and stared down into the eyes of the Devil, who was nearly two feet shorter than Kiln himself. He chose his words carefully, “Because I know you were lying to me about reading up on battlefield tactics. And if the MacGregor child bears the mark across his back, that poem you fear so much might actually ring true. I’m afraid of what that might mean, not his supposed ascension into my position.” Typherion stood there in disbelief. Unlike his Heavenly equal, he couldn’t read the minds of his minions, so more often than not a few things surprised him. Even with more than enough power to strike fear into the billions of residents of Hell and manipulate those on Earth, he was equally as flawed in some aspects. The area immediately surrounding Typherion started to bend and distort, and as his anger escalated, rips and tears appeared in the air surrounding him. It cracked and popped as it was sheared apart by Typherion’s anger, and for a split second, his true form flashed into existence. Kiln, standing there in feigned shock, took a step away from his superior. He had apparently chosen unwisely. A moment later, he was gone. The area around Typherion started to stabilize, and he quickly shot his gaze across the vast battlefield once more, to the orb that was held in the Chamber of God. He knew it had just flashed, and God was aware that a position opened up in Hell. Killing off one of his most powerful Generals proved to be unwise in the long run, but rationale had never been Typherion’s strong suit, wrath was. The Devil remained angry and uneasy at the thought of the poem’s prophecy coming true, and he needed to make sure that the MacGregor child wouldn’t be the one to fulfill it. 300 years had passed since the last time he walked on Earth, and it was time to make another appearance. * * * * * Now that the hook is planted firmly in your cheek, let me reel you in by telling you exactly what I was told by the ghost of Archibald Matthews during the spring of last year. Much like the quote in my first chapter explains, I am wanted by Hell. But Heaven doesn’t want me there, because in the grand scheme of things, I will be a much more valuable asset to the Devil than I ever would be to God. I was told that when a person enters Hell, he would enter it as a foot soldier in the Great War that is constantly being fought in the afterlife. However, if you follow in the footsteps of your father, when you enter Hell you will be more powerful than your already present Dad. Each successive generation gains in power exponentially. So now looking back at the quote that I gave you last chapter, you suddenly realize that doing a little bit of research about my family history will shed some light on the situation. My father, Christopher MacGregor, committed suicide because he was overcome with guilt after he was too afraid to save his best friend, the aforementioned Archibald Matthews, from being slaughtered during Vietnam. My grandfather, Willis, jumped from the 20th story of a New York City skyscraper when the stock market crashed. The wealth of our family was wiped out, and he couldn’t bear the weight or responsibility, so he leapt from this world into the underworld. My great grandfather, Samuel, was an officer aboard the Titanic. He was found innocent of shooting passengers who were overflowing the lifeboats, but only a year later, jumped off of a cruise ship returning to England, proving his guilt. His father, a fresh from the boat immigrant, was mistakenly accused of murdering a Philadelphia woman in the late 1800’s. His innocent anger because guilty rage when he lynched an officer who was sent to arrest him. He was then hanged for his crimes. So you see, by the time I leave this world, I will be the fifth successive generation to enter Hell. When the ghost of Archibald Matthews told me this news, he told me that I was one of around 100 other people on this planet that are a “Generation Five” (you’d think the Devil would come up with a much cooler ranking system, no?), I didn’t believe it at first. I had to do research on my family, and the surreal events that have followed since then are all the proof that I need. The ride to the hospital only strengthened my greatest fear. The fear that no matter what I do in life, I’m going to Hell regardless. That my sins have already sealed my fate, because with all the eyes watching us as Aaron was being born, it leads me to believe that there hasn’t been a Generation Six in Hell for some time. To me, Hell seems to be getting desperate for some sort of advantage in the ongoing War. I just pray that my son isn’t what they’re looking for. * * * * * Heather Davis wanted out of the hospital immediately after giving birth to baby Aaron, but due to a complication during childbirth, she was required to spend an extra day at the hospital. After being upset for nearly an hour, she smiled and shrugged off the bad news as merely delaying her arrival home with the newborn family member. Aaron was in the infant care center, only a floor away. Archibald had returned home after spending the night with her and Aaron, and only because he had forgotten to send in the paperwork to sign up for the Infinite Gauntlet competition that would be taking place in a few weeks. ”I can see that you miss your son…” Heather turned and looked across the hospital room at the woman laying on the bed parallel to her. She looked well into her seventies and had no color in her skin. The woman appeared pale, hairless, and frail, and managed to smile as Heather matched her gaze. Deep brown eyes stared back from eye sockets that had receded into her skull. Her upper lip was cracked and dry, an old scab clung to the corner of her mouth. If Heather had to guess, she would suspect a tumor as the culprit. Heather answered in turn, “It’s a new feeling. Aaron was inside of me for 9 months, and now he’s suddenly gone. I know he’s fine…but still, I just want to do nothing but hold him.” The woman grinned once again, her dark brown eyes hidden for a moment behind the natural squint of an honest smile, “I remember my first, and I can remember that very same feeling. You’ll cherish these few moments apart more than you’ll ever know. It gives you something to compare against the moments you spend with your son.” Heather gently sat up in her bed and nodded at the thought, then introduced herself, “My apologizes, I’m Heather.” ”If I could sit up, I’d match your gesture, but the chemo has left me a little weak. My muscles feel wilted. I’m Molly, it’s a pleasure to meet you Heather.” Her voice was soft and raspy. “Oh, I’m so sorry.” ”You have no reason to be, my dear. I’ve lived a long life, and done certain things to put me in this place. I know my time is limited…but I’ve learned to love every minute of what’s left. It really is cliché, but equally as true.” Heather smiled at the thought, but was still unnerved by Molly’s willingness to accept death. She was about to reply when a knock came on the door, and a doctor walked in holding Aaron in his arms. “Who ordered one infant with blue eyes, slightly soiled? Asked the doctor, grinning from ear to ear. Immediately Heather noticed that the doctor was extremely handsome, and undoubtedly had work done on his smile. His teeth were perfect, as was his hair, cheeks, and the dimples they held. His eyes were a shade of green that Heather had never seen before, and seemingly couldn’t take her gaze away from, even with the man holding her infant son. “Oh, I’ll take him, he looks adorable!” Molly said, suddenly with more energy and enthusiasm in her voice. She remained motionless atop her bed, putting all her strength into sounding healthy for a moment. The doctor walked over towards Molly and before handing over Aaron, stopped and held the child out, “That’ll be 14.99 plus one urine topping, bringing the total to 16 dollars even.” ”Shit, I’m all out. I don’t suppose trading you a bedpan will suffice. If you ask me, the Barter system should have never been abolished…” Molly said, her voice diminishing along with her strength. Heather smiled and reached out for her boy, “I’ve got the money, I guess I’ll take him if he's all you got. I don’t suppose you have a girl to go with the boy?” ”I’m afraid not, miss” the doctor replied as he walked across the room and finally put Aaron into Heather’s waiting arms. He stared down and watched as Aaron woke up momentarily and matched his mothers gaze, before finally returning to sleep. ”I’m afraid I’ve left my wallet at home, actually. Can I give you an IOU?” Heather said, continuing the cute joke that the doctor had started, “Now who would I make this IOU out to, if you don’t mind me asking?” The doctor flashed that perfect smile once again as he bowed slightly while introducing himself, “Just call me Dr. Ty. You’ve got a wonderful son, Ms. Davis. I’m sure you and the father are very proud of him.” She nodded and looked over at Molly, who was looking weaker by the minute, but nodded in return. The doctor continued, “He checked out beautifully, Ms. Davis. In fact, the only thing we noticed that was out of the ordinary was the birthmark on his back. Other than that, you’ve got a healthy boy that will undoubtedly have a great life.” Heather once again looked up at the doctor, “Can you promise me that? I can sue you for malpractice if you don’t.” She giggled at the on-going joke. Typherion, in his medical guise, had chills running down his spine. Fear isn’t often an emotion that the Devil encounters, but it was rushing through him like a rain-flooded river. He looked down at Aaron MacGregor and ran his hand across the forehead of the child, pushing his soft blonde hair back away from his chubby face, “Yes, I promise. I think it’s safe to say that he’s going to impact the lives of millions of people, wouldn’t you?” Heather nodded, and gently swayed Aaron in her arms. “What about my future, Doc? How many more days to I have left?” Molly asked from across the room. She winked at Heather as she said it, trying to lift her head off of the pillow but ultimately failing. Heather watched as the Doctor walked across the room and, much like he did to Aaron, brushed back what hair was left on Molly’s head. He leaned down and whispered into her ear. For a moment, she smiled, but then her eyes suddenly shot open and her face contorted in extreme pain. The doctor casually walked from the room, quickly flashing one more glance at Aaron as he slept in Heather’s arms. The door clicked shut only moments before nurses barged back into the room ready to revive Molly. Heather held Aaron close as she watched in horror as the tumor had undoubtedly finally taken it’s toll on the poor woman. Aaron’s soft breathing was the only thing that calmed Heather as they wheeled Molly out of the room, towards the morgue. * * * * * The excerpt from "100 Proof Life" by Archibald MacGregor was printed with permission from his estate.
