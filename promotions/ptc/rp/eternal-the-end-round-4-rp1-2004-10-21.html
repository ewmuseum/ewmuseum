Eternal: The End (Round 4 RP1)

Date: 2004-10-21 Eternal: The End <><><><><><><><><><> The rain-swept evening felt like a new beginning, as if the drops of water were washing away the sins of the world. The rumblings of thunder heard through the dark gray clouds caused some children to cry, startling them, making them clutch the hands of their mothers in fright. The heavens seemed to have heard their cries, and the storm began to subside as the frequency of the bolts of lightning decreased. As the horns blared through the traffic-congested streets, he walked, squeezing in between cars and ignoring the glares directed at him. With his black jacket dripping wet and his similar shade of pants drenched, he moved slickly, like a shadow flirting with the light. For many, he was non-existent, just an enemy to the lines of sight. With drops of liquid slipping down from his white hair and onto his pale cheeks, he looked down and walked through the least populated areas, his hands firmly pushed down in his pockets. To him, it was just another day. And with his mind pushed down the road to overdrive, he had no interest in the weather, nor even the good deeds that mortals thought the heavens had bestowed upon them. He simply did not care. Like a snake slithering through obstacles, his movements happened within a blink of an eye. So sure of himself, knowing exactly when and how to wander through the peak hours, without attracting even the slightest of attention. An enigma to the passing eye, he remained just that, soon to be forgotten in the minds of casual bystanders, just the way he wanted it to be. The rap on the wooden door thundered through the hallway, slitting itself deep in the silence, like a wrench to the ears. The passageway to get to that fourth level apartment had been nothing short of filling him with disgust, with dirt filling every inch of that building that was located in one of the city’s poorest neighborhoods. But he paid no notice, standing in the doorway, his hands neatly clasped behind his back, his silver rings gleaming slightly in the dim lighting conditions. “Who is it?” a voice bellowed from inside the room, muffled by the distance separating them. “What is it you want?” the voice continued, disgruntled. A sudden silence then ensured, a chilling pause just daring for someone to make a response. “Open the door,” he said simply, in a clear harmonious tone. His voice may have been soothing to the ears, but there was a firm edge to it, an almost demanding request. “Who are you?” the voice inside spoke again hesitantly, noticeably in a lower touch. A chuckle was subconsciously let out of his mouth, as he mused about the question. It tickled him considerably, as he tried composing himself. “Kasper Sky.” He declared in the same tone, a smile slightly playing on his lips as he ran his right hand through his hair, smoothing it back. The response to his two words were the shuffling of feet as someone distinctly approached the other side of the door, the audible grumbling under his breath heard by Kasper’s sharp sense of hearing. That made him smile wider, clearly entertained by the person’s antics. The door creaked open as a man stood in front of him, looking directly in a set of crystal blue eyes. He flinched, and then eyed Kasper suspiciously, his eyes darting between the dripping wet black jacket and the smooth calm facial expression of the immortal facing him. “Sincere apologies for the manner of my coming,” Kasper spoke nonchalantly, referring to his wet outfit. He walked past the man, firmly pushing him aside with his shoulder. In a clear sweep of his eyes, he observed the room, noticing the empty pizza box by the television, as well as newspapers scattered all over the couch. He shook in head in apparent disdain. He raised his brow and turned his head back, a flop of white hair falling down and partly covered his eyes. “Do you have what I had come for?” Kasper quizzed, his lips set in a firm line. The man looked down as he nodded his head. “Y-Yes,” he managed to stutter out. He carried his weight past Kasper and walked hurriedly to his study table, wiping his hands against his dirty white shirt. After about a minute of rummaging around, to which Kasper got somewhat irritated, he finally brought out a small brown package from one of the lower drawers. With a vice-like grip on it, he turned towards the direction of Kasper. “Where’s the money?” The man questioned, observing his movements warily. Kasper’s response was a smirk as he rolled his eyes. “Let me see it first,” he replied, with an insincere wide smile directed to the man. He motioned the man to come forward with a wave of his hand, composed yet unyielding. Cautiously he walked up to Kasper, careful in his steps, his eyes keeping a close watch on Kasper’s movements. “There you go,” he muttered, shoving his hand forward to within a reach of Kasper’s arm. “Thank you,” he spoke with another smile, beaming sarcastically at the man who was standing nervously in front of him. With a quick swoop, he grabbed the package in a swift movement, and held it in his own hands. With a couple of deft touches with his fingers, he undid the brown paper. The man stared at Kasper’s fingers guardedly, as they delicately pulled out a Beretta, grasping it firmly like a professional. He crushed the brown paper, and threw it uncaringly behind him on the white tiled floor. The gun had an uncanny resemblance to the one his sister had pointed at him over a couple of weeks ago, he noted wryly. He felt the weight of the piece, getting himself used to its icy cold metal surface. “Now where’s my money?” the man questioned again, interrupting Kasper’s thoughts. The man crossed his arms across his chest, trying to look stern. He was standing about ten meters away from Kasper, with a wary look on his face. Kasper frowned, not appreciating the disruption of his thinking. “What is your name again?” he enquired in an innocent tone, trying to smoothen the creases on his pants, ignoring the man’s previous question. “Brock,” the man muttered, unclear as to what Kasper was getting at. He looked a bit uncomfortable, after trying to read Kasper’s thoughts but to no avail. “Ah…Brock.” Kasper looked up at him, acting as if his name rang a bell. “Aren’t you the guy who is supposed to be a big-time drug dealer and who goes around shooting people by the hundreds but amazingly not get killed yourself?” he continued, grinning at Brock, wagging his index finger in front of his face. “Huh?” Brock blurted out in amazement. He couldn’t believe his ears, and somewhere deep inside, he thought for a moment that Kasper was crazy. “What do you mean? I work down the road in the local café,” he replied, his eyes widening in utter confusion. “That’s a pity,” Kasper whispered, wiping the Beretta softly with the sleeve of his black jacket. “For a moment there, you reminded me of Token Weed.” “You know, I’m still not satisfied with this piece. It just seems so…let me find a word…” Kasper persisted and looked up at the ceiling as if deep in thought. “…Unfamiliar.” Kasper spoke with a certain of stress to the word, sending a chill down Brock’s spine. With that, he flashed a smile at the man, waving the Beretta in front of his face, gazing straight into Brock’s pale brown eyes. Brock snorted, his facial expression signaling that he had dismissed Kasper’s words as utter nonsense. “You’ll get used to it,” he mumbled, shrugging his shoulders. Kasper gave out a short laugh, shaking his head in disbelief at Brock’s complete ignorance. “I’m sure I will,” he said, his eyes turning into tiny slits as it gleamed under the dim lighting of the apartment. “What do you mean?” Several seconds and quick squeeze of the trigger, Kasper answered his question with a deft piece of movement. The bullet pierced through Brock’s body gracefully, penetrating the tanned skin, easily drilling through the flesh. Blood spurted out almost instantaneously, staining his white shirt a deep red. The noise reverberated from wall to wall, echoing through the neighborhood, but it was simply shrugged off by everyone who heard it. Kasper smirked as the man’s body slowly collapsed to the floor, his right hand gripping his chest, trying fervently to stop the blood from gushing out. A couple of minutes later, and he was dead. Kasper glanced from the gun to the man’s body unmoving body, and stared back at the gun. He broke into a grin, as he slipped the Beretta into the back of his jacket, wiping a few drops of blood on his hands with a small black handkerchief. “I guess it’s familiar enough. At least now I know that it can kill.” Kasper whispered, walking out of the door with a slight smile playing on his lips. <><><><><><><><><><> They say I stood no chance. Damning my soul to within a whisper of my ear. They wished that I had failed, that one of the previous obstacles that stood in my way had proved their promises right. Mortals that were promised incentives if they were able to stop my rising splendor, had since fallen. One by one they rise, trying to stir an uprising of my throne of magnificence. They think that they had what it takes to destroy and conquer my grandeur. They believed in every word of the propaganda around, that it would not take much to kill me. They think they are Gods. They placed Hell Itself in front of me in the first round, deeming him fit to defeat me. And when that failed, they put forth Tony Stecchino who despite his lovesick life, they thought was able to overthrow my glory. That proved another failure, unsurprisingly. Their latest hope was in Larry the Legionnaire. I don’t think I need to repeat what exactly happened there. Yet they did not stop, hoping against their very lives, that a true candidate can step up and challenge my lordship. Now they think Token Weed will be able to get the job done. That a drug dealer might stand a chance to cause me to fall into eternal damnation. A man whom people say could not be killed, but in turn had killed thousands of others in pure cold-blooded murder. And you in turn expect me to believe all that. You think that an immortal such as me will fall to a man who spreads his own propaganda that he is the ultimate killer, the guy who couldn’t be stopped. You think I should be worried. You think that I will lose. You think that my empire will be overthrown. I really don’t need to say this; but I think that once again, I will prove you wrong. But alas, I doubt that you will believe me. You will rather believe that someone like Token Weed will defeat me and relieve you of your rather anxious moments, looking behind your back at every corner, expecting at anytime to be looking at Death in his cold crystal blue eyes. Have patience, your time will come. But not right now. That’s a promise. And if you don’t believe me, then rest the hopes of your life on Token Weed. I’m sure he’ll do just fine. <><><><><><><><><><> TO BE CONTINUED
