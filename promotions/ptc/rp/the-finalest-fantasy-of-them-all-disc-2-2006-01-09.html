Date: 2006-01-09 As black as the sky had become, it could never equate to the darkness that dwelt within the heart of the one who stood before the farmer. The reporters had left, the attention which had once befallen the crater moved on to the next big story, and that’s when he emerged. Unscathed, tearing through the earthen mass that had once confined him, breathing air for the first time in spasmodic gasps. Had he been there since before humanity, waiting to be reawakened by the lightning storm? Or, had he used the searing bolts of lightning as some type of transport? Perhaps, neither. The one thing the farmer did know, however, was that a naked man stood before him, taller than any man he knew, with platinum hair hanging nearly to his knees. And that naked man looked down at him as if he were an insect. The farmer clutched at his throat, but he was too old. His hands were too feeble to pull apart the fingers of this giant. "Life." A sharp cracking of bone signaled the end of one human’s existence, his corpse still suspended in the air by the unclothed man’s right hand. "Death." Thud. - James Farwell, Clyde Walkins, and Heather Knightjesus step out the front door and into the outside world, taking in the sights of an entirely different planet than the one they know. Overtaking everything in its path as it envelopes the sky and the ground below, a black and purple darkness sweeps across the land. Still in costume, James and Clyde haven’t even the chance to turn away as the aphotic wave assimilates them as well. A swirling vortex threatens not to lift the trio off their feet, but rather pin them to the ground. The storm takes a toll on their strength, but they each manage to hold onto a hand of the other, and then silence. Around them, all looks normal, yet somehow different at the same time. Almost mechanical. They stand, brushing the dirt from their clothes, and searching the skies for any signs of the storm that no one else seems to remember happening. Running from the front yard, Heather puts a hand on the shoulder of the nearest person she sees, gasping and taking a step back as she peers into his lifeless eyes. Heather: E-excuse me, sir…but…do you…do you know what just happened here? Man: I’m looking for my son. If you see him, tell him to come home for dinner. The man then turns and walks away, leaving Heather stunned for a moment. Heather: Wha-what? She jogs to catch up with him, and is startled once again as he turns around on his heels, not as much looking at her as past her. Man: He never eats his vegetables, so I hide them in his meat. Again, he turns to walk away, but Heather grabs his shoulder, now almost in a panic from thinking she’s going insane. Heather: What the hell are you talking about?! Man: I’m looking for my son. If you see him, tell him to come home for dinner. This time, when the man turns to walk away, she lets him. Now standing beside her are Clyde and James, both equally as stunned as she is. Farwell: What was that all about? Heather: I have no idea, but it doesn’t look good. - The cellar door creaked open loudly, but no one would hear it now. The Final Plan was already in motion, and now every being on the face of the planet had become infected with the Virus. The planet itself was contaminated, and its neoteric physical laws applied only to those who were bound within the newly developed realm. But he existed outside their laws--outside their realm. He was a God. The farmer’s body landed hard atop the algid cement floor, his stripped body contorted in an unnatural position. A final glance down into the old man’s concrete grave was all the giant afforded him, before slamming shut the lid of his crypt. The diminutively sized clothes were too small for him, but apparently the farmer never believed gluttony was a sin, so the naked man felt he should be able to use them as partial cover until more suitable garments could be found. Keys hung in front of the back door, and his hand enveloped the ones that he instinctively knew belonged to the aesthetically-lacking old pick-up truck at the side of the house. Or, had he been programmed to know? Did he even have instincts? These questions only plagued him for a few seconds, until they completely fled his mind. All that was left now was to attain his primary objective. The world was to be wiped clean. - After encountering several more people with a maximum of two different things to say, Clyde, James, and Heather decide to head back to the house. Once inside, Clyde’s portable television is turned on to the news channel, with the three of them huddled in front of it for any confirmation of what they’ve just witnessed. Instead, it’s just more of the same. Sure, they know about the lightning attacks, but not of the seeming loop they’ve been caught in. "I want that, too." - The Forsaken have entered many battles before, but none quite as intense as this. After all, if they lose, they’re dead. Once back outside, Clyde is simply beaming as he slams his palm against the top of the desk, awakening the secretary from her slumber. Secretary: What do you want now? Walkins: I won, so I want my James back. Clyde pauses, then narrows his eyes, scanning the room for any eavesdroppers. Walkins: And all the BP I can carry. Secretary: I’m sorry, but I can’t say you won in good conscience. That last monster…wasn’t actually a monster. Walkins: That thing fell from the ceiling, began to crawl towards me, and gave me a threatening look. What about that isn’t monstrous? Secretary: Sir, if you can’t control your temper, I’ll be forced to call security. Walkins: Call security, for all I care! Call the National Guard! I fear no man! I’m Clyde Walkins, bitch! Clyde slams his fists against his chest, letting loose with a rumbling roar that echoes throughout the Gold Saucer. A button is pressed under the desk, and security arrives in record time, bringing Clyde down with a barrage of bean bag shots to the groin and face. Clyder-Man goes down in a squirming heap. Walkins: Ahh! Right in the moneymakers! - He had a cat on his head, for the love of God. It wasn’t like he wasn’t a spectacle enough to begin with, but then people hand to laugh at his fortunes. He tried as hard as he could, and they still laughed. How were they supposed to keep a straight face when the same nonsensical fortunes they were anticipating came from his rear end? Sure, it may not sound overly humorous, but seeing a giant stuffed animal pinch a fortune loaf right in front of your very eyes is enough to make the coldest heart warm with a laugh. Granted, the laughter might have killed him inside, but he still maintained a brave front. It was his job to create happiness, and he’d remain upbeat if it killed him. That said, he soon found a chance to both create happiness and do something to change the world around him. A locked cage, hidden away in the middle of nowhere, only seen through the darkness thanks to a glimmer of light reflecting from within. Thank God for Clyde’s glow-in-the-dark codpiece.
