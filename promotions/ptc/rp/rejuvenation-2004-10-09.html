Rejuvenation

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 Jay Phoenix… I can barely bring myself to utter those words, let alone consider how I’m actually going to bring myself together to win the match. The man is a legend. He’s been there, done that and got the foam pointy finger. I’m sure he has no problems preparing for this thing. For him it’s the same-old, same-old. Train a little, watch a few tapes and turn up. That’s all he has to do, for I hold no threat to anyone of that kind of calibre and he knows it. I’m just pathetic. My entire life I’ve proclaimed myself as Exceptional Dave Hurst, as a technical master, a person who strives for perfection in each and every contest that comes my way, no matter how unlikely my chances may be. But all along I was just deluding myself. I lift the bottle up to the ceiling, tilting back my head and taking in the last precious drops of alcoholic delight from the vodka bottle, before letting my arm fall to the floor, bringing the bottle down like a scythe and rattling it against the floor, luckily this one didn’t smash. What have I managed to do in my career? Nothing… nada… A whole career of near-misses, a life of should-a, would-a, could-a. Nearly in the running for winning an FAW world championship… but never did. A contender for a big title in OSW… but never got any. Progressing well in the UTT and named as a possible dark horse… but was knocked out in the quarter-finals. And that’s it. I found a plastic duck on the side of the road as things weren’t going my way. I saw it at the time as being something a lot more than it was. I saw it as something special, as something to be cherished, and not only that, I thought that it was real. But all along I was just deluding myself. I scan the room around me. There must be more alcohol here somewhere. There must be more of that magical liquid, the thing to make all of my worries, all of my anguish, all of my thoughts disappear. All I can see is broken glass, my own sick and empty bottles… Who talks to plastic ducks, for Christ’s sakes? There are only two types of people that would do such a thing: Loonies, and retards. And as much as I don’t want to admit it, the latter is exactly what I am: A retard. In a couple of days I’m set to compete in the third round of the GTT, against someone who should be an ex-champion of the event. Somebody with limitless ability, with the size, strength, speed and technique to match anything I could ever throw his way. I could have prepared myself. I could have said to myself over and over that I can win this. I could have gone into this match with my head held high and put my very best out. But then I’d be deluding myself again, and I’m not going to do that anymore. I hear a sound from the front porch, a soft slapping sound. The newspaper must have just arrived. As if I want to be reading the newspaper. I have my own worries to care about the rest of the world’s problems. -----=====|=====----- I really hope that Dave is okay. I know we haven’t seen each other for quite a while. I was probably expecting too much going to see him again after all these years. Yeah, he may have just changed, but the person I saw two days ago was not Dave. This is something a lot more serious. I think he may be having a mental breakdown. I hope he doesn’t do anything stupid. It’s been a while, but I still hope nothing bad happens. I still care… I still have… feelings. I lean forward, place both my elbows down on the wooden desk in front of me and rest my chin on my hands, staring gloomily at the computer screen in front of me, at the article that I had written the night before. I know I should have gotten his permission. I know he said he didn’t want to be in the newspaper, that he didn’t feel like an interview, but seeing him like that I just had to do something. So I did the one thing I’ve always been good at, my entire career. I made it into a story. With the excitement running through the city of the GTT event, with a lot of coverage of all the people still competing, it wasn’t difficult to make this a front page special. Exceptional Dave Hurst – One of Primetime Central’s most loved characters, in dire trouble. I mentioned the stress he was feeling, and him not wanting to open up to the anxiety he’s having. I even mentioned what happened after I’ve left, the alcohol, the smashing of things… I’ve had someone keeping an eye on him. Every few hours he’s been phoning me, giving the latest on the desperation. He even found his beloved Ducky, underneath a parked car after being thrown from the apartment window. The window is still open now. He tells me that he hasn’t seen Dave leave the apartment since he got there. Could he have done something to himself? I know I should have gone over there. All the time I was thinking it could have made the situation worse, but I don’t think you could make the situation any worse. I just hope my plan will work out… I look up at the clock ahead of me. 7.15a.m… the newspaper should be arriving in people’s doorsteps right about now. I hope that Dave picks it up. I hope he gets to see quite how messed up he’s getting, what he’s doing to himself. I know he has a tough match coming up, I know there are a lot of people saying that he has no hope of winning the match, but this isn’t the right way to deal with it all. This tournament means the world to Dave. He’s always wanted to do well in this competition, to put him on the wrestling map, to make his mark in the world of wrestling. He may be having a slight setback, he may have an insurmountable task ahead of him, but that doesn’t mean he should cower away into the corner. He has to go out there and fight, to achieve the one thing he always strove to achieve. That’s it… I’m going to see him. I’m not going to just sit here and wait for something dreadful to happen. I’m going to get him back on his feet and wrestling again. -----=====|=====----- Who’s that phoning at this time of the flaming morning? Have some fucking consideration. I stagger over the glass across the room to the source of my irritation. Instead of clutching the handset, because that would put me in a conversation with whatever mindless idiot decided to call, I grab the cord attaching the telephone to the phone socket and give it a quick tug, pulling the thing straight out and relieving me from the relentless ringing of the telephone. It’s fucking disgraceful. It should be illegal for these companies to call so early in the fucking morning. Well… I’m all out of alcohol. If I leave it much longer I might just sober up. And I don’t want that to happen. There’s a supermarket up the road, I’ll go over there and get some tinnies. I walk out of the living room, leaving the devastation from the last day alone and into the hallway. I reach up and grab the collar of my black coat, pulling it over my shoulder and pushing my arms through. As I do so I stumble backwards and nearly trip on something. What the flaming hell was that? It’s the newspaper… hey wait, is that me on the front…? RING… Who’s that at the door at this time in the morning? It looks like… is that Wendy…? RING… I can’t answer it. I can’t let Wendy see me like this. I can’t let anyone see me like this. RING… She moves her face right up to the window of the door, trying to look in and see if there’s anything inside. Oh shit she’s going to see me. “Daaave…?” she says calling me, “come on Dave. I can see you there, answer the door.” I guess it’s too late now. I better answer it. With utter embarrassment and disgrace, I slowly reach up and grab the door handle, pushing the handle down and pulling the door back towards me showing the hideous figure of a pathetic sweaty drunk that I have become. Wendy stands there in the doorway, not saying a word, the look of concern as clear as day across her tanned face. She reaches across her shoulder and pushes back her long blonde hair, her look never leaving, never waning. She’s been standing there for what seems to be an eternity. Why is she even here? Is she here to take another look at the one-man freak show, before he fades into obscurity? Maybe she’s here for a big scoop for her newspaper. Professional wrestler turned drunk. Yeah, that’d make a great front page story, I’m sure. “Dave…?” she begins, “…are you okay…?” What a ridiculous question that is… I take a moment to run a hand over the rough bristled stubble on my chin. “Do I look okay to you…?” I say in reply… the endless silence begins once more. “What are you doing to yourself, Dave…?” she says taking a step through the door and into the apartment, “obviously there’s something seriously wrong. Is there something you want to talk about?” I turn and walk back into the living room. She follows me taking moments to scan the carnage that has been left lying around the apartment floor. I slouch into my leather chair and look up at her, shocked at her surroundings. Why is she here? What has she got to gain from coming here? I’m hardly going to give her an interview now. She looks back at me, waiting intently to hear my life story. I must admit, for her it must be a bit strange seeing me like this. I always used to be the happy-go-lucky, goofy grinning idiot that would crack jokes and never have anything let me down, now I’ve turned into a miserable drunk. “I’m a loser, Wendy.” I begin, “All my life I saw myself as a credible wrestler, as someone that people respected, someone that people looked up to, that people considered a great talented wrestler, but I was so wrong.” “Dave… you’re not a-“ “I used to go about things blindly, smiley, happy, cheerful. I used to say the first thing that came to mind, do the first thing that came to mind. I never used to care what anyone thought of me, I never noticed what anyone thought of me… until two days ago.” “What do you mean?” “I was just doing what I usually do. I was bored on the train, so I took out the plastic duck and I started talking to it, as if it was real… and the other people in the train noticed.” “Oh Dave…” Wendy says, walking over to me and laying a sympathetic hand on my shoulder. I bowed my head and broke into tears. “They started laughing at me, Wendy. I was a big joke to them… and that’s when I started to think about it.” “You’re not a joke, Dave. Those people on the train, they don’t know you.” “But I know me Wendy. I know me now. I realised that day what I was, what I had been doing my entire life was just one big joke… I was just one big joke.” “Dave… you’re not a joke.” “I am Wendy. I’m nothing but a loser. A pathetic clown put on this planet for people to point and laugh at, I realise that now. But I don’t want people to laugh at me anymore.” “People don’t laugh at you, Dave. People love you, they find you funny. That doesn’t mean they think you’re a loser, they just really like you.” “If only that were true Wendy, but I don’t believe that… I don’t even know why you’re here anyway. Why are you here anyway? Are you here to pity me? Do you think by coming here you’re going to make yourself feel better inside, helping the charity case?” “No Dave…” Wendy says with her voice trailing off, now bowing her own head letting her long blonde hair dangle in front of her face, obscuring the view so that I couldn’t see. “I… I really care about you Dave. I don’t want to see anything bad happen to you.” “You care about me…?” I don’t want to believe it, if there’s anything that these past few days have brought home is that nobody cares about me, but looking at the way Wendy is struggling to get these words out, it’s hard to see it as anything other than the truth. “I didn’t come over the other day for an interview, Dave… You were in the same city for this tournament… I… I just wanted to see you… I wanted to talk to you… the thing is… I… forget it.” Man, Wendy is really hurting. “No… what is it Wendy…?” “I…” Wendy stutters looking up at me, her glistening blue eyes looking straight into mine, “I love you Dave.” Wow… and then the memories make so much sense. The way she always chased me for an interview, almost every week. She’d always skip the interviews with all the top superstars, just to go and talk to me; the way she had that really cute chuckle whenever I cracked a sly joke about my next opponent; the way we used to share banter as I said some really ridiculous things and she’d make fun of it. “I’m gunna go,” Wendy says as she turns and makes her way into the hallway towards the door. I dart out of my chair. I can’t let her do this. I can’t let her just walk away again, out of my life. Not this time. I walk briskly through the living room and into the hallway. Wendy has her hand on the door handle and is about to open the door and leave my life. I reach out in desperation and touch her soft smooth fingers, pulling them away from the door handle. She looks up at me, wondering what is going on, but before she can say a word, I reach my hand behind her neck into her velvety hair, tilt my head and press my lips against hers. Breathing heavily with passion, our hearts racing, we embrace in a long slow sensual kiss. I can’t believe she never told me. All this time, all those interviews, all those years of her following me and never said anything to me. Or at least if she did, I never noticed. How could I have been so blind? I bring my head back, a smile now beaming across my face. “Why didn’t you just say, Wendy?” “Hehe, I dunno, I didn’t think you’d care about little old me.” “Silly girl...” We press lips once more, continuing in the passionate kiss that I never want to end. This woman is amazing, the worst moment of my life taken into a complete flipside as she has come back into my life to save me from my depression. I don’t think I’ve ever been as happy, and that’s saying something! It’s Wendy’s turn to bring her head back. “Hey there mister, haven’t you got a match to prepare for?” “What…? Oh Jay! I don’t think I’m going to be showing up for that… I’ll just get slaughtered.” “Don’t be silly!” she says with a massive grin, “You’re Exceptional Dave Hurst! You’re the greatest technical wrestler in the Universe!” As much as I admire her enthusiasm, I have a much better grounding of reality now. I know I’m not good enough, everyone else knows I’m going to lose the match. “That’s not what everyone else thinks…” As I say that, a roaring sound is heard from outside. I’m not sure quite what the sound is… Eeeeee… Deeee… Aaayych…. Eeeee… Deeeee… Aaaayych… I clasp the handle and peer outside the door to see what the whole racket is about. Whoa! I close the door. This can’t be real. Out on the street… a huge group of people, all facing the apartment. Looking right up at me, with an ‘I told you so’ style smug smirk on her face, Wendy can’t help but say something. “That’s not what they think, Dave.” “Did you…?” Her cute little lips widen into a beaming grin. How did she organise all this? “After I wrote the story about your anguish in trying to get to grips with your match this week, falling into your deep depression, our phones wouldn’t stop ringing with people asking if they could somehow help. The people of PTC love you Dave, they want you to go out and compete in the GTT, to give it your all and win your match.” I shake my head in disbelief. This can’t be happening… I must’ve passed out after drinking so much and am dreaming. This isn’t real. The roar begins once more as they continue their chanting. You know what… I’m going to do this! I may go out there, I may get my arse kicked, but it’s a hell of a lot better than backing out. I’m not going to let all these people down. I’m going to go out there and give it my all. If my all isn’t enough, so be it, but you better be ready Jay matey, because I’m going to come out with all guns blazing. Wendy reaches over to the handle and pushes it down, pulling the door back and opening it revealing the sheer numbers out on the streets outside, and then sitting there on the bottom step, looking up at me… a small plastic, yellow duck. “I’ve spoken to him,” she says, “he’s not happy with you, but he’s willing to accept your apology and take you back.” I go down the steps, lean over and pick up the little bath toy, placing him on the palm of my hand. “Sorry little mate.” I said, “I don’t know what I was doing… forgive me…?” He’s not replying, but I think he accepts my apology. Either way, I don’t think I’m going to get a chance to watch any telly this week. In repayment I think it might be a week’s documentary channel for Mister Ducky. I might have been down, I might have been at the lowest I’ve ever been in my life, but it’s time for a big change. No more Mediocre Dave Hurst, no more settling for second-best. I’m in it to win it. I’m striving for perfection again. It’s time to live up to my name. It’s time to be… Exceptional.
