Broken Butterfly

28 September 2005

Date: 2005-09-28 Breathe in. Try to steady your arm because if you slip one more time it might be too much. You might drop it, or things might smash, and you didn’t mean for it to get this late, for it to be so soon, but in the end you were just hiding from it all. Breathe in. Hold it. Calm down. Your arm is still now. Your breathing is level; swallowing shallow gulps of air because you know it will all soon be well, if you can just hold it together for a few more minutes. The hairs on your arm stand up, prickling the flesh and sending a chill, a sense of things to come up your limb and down to the base of your spine, where it nestles and slowly dies. Breathe in. Open the bag. As your hand goes in and picks it up you feel a strange sensation of desire and guilt; of anticipation and revulsion all at once. The hatred for the man that has done this is always there, burrowed far enough into your skull that you’ll never forget it, yet the passion is tempered by the other, the part of the brain that loves this, that genuinely lives for what is to come. Take it out of the bag with as steady a hand as you can manage. It’s the last one, so you’ll have to see him again soon. You’ll have to ask him for more, like some kind of depraved Oliver Twist. You’ll prostrate yourself at his feet, looking up into his grey eyes and crying, begging for more. You’ll hate yourself for it, and him even more, but the relief as he hands them over will make up for your pathetic display. Please sir, can I have another fix? The little plastic sheath houses the needle, but you leave it on for the time being. No sense in taking unnecessary risks. Not after the time you shattered one, and he made you wait. The withdrawal was more painful than you could have imagined, and you don’t want to go through that again, no matter how delicious it was when he relented and gave you what you wanted. So you take your belt, the only thing you have than can approximate what you need, and lash it round your arm. You pull it tight, and then bite down on it, yanking with your head to cut into the veins. The leather tastes familiar in your mouth, making you salivate with anticipation. You can see your arm, but you don’t recognise it. When did the muscles wither away? Where did all those marks come from? It’s like looking through a lens and seeing something else, or someone else. This isn’t happening. This isn’t happening… but you can’t let yourself believe that, because intravenous drugs and solipsism don’t mix so well. Check the needle works, and the squirt of the blue liquid that he does for no reason other than you saw it on television. Maybe something about air bubbles, but you know deep down that you wouldn’t have been given anything that could do you harm, at least in anything other than the way it is intended. The needle plunges into the vein, and the squirt of hot God knows what enters the bloodstream. This moment, this is the good part. People don’t do drugs just to fuck themselves up, or because of peer pressure or rebellion or any other reason put out by the press and anti-drugs lobby. You know that people do drugs because they feel really, really, really good. Breathe out. Embrace the moment. The needle drops to the floor as you let go, mind devoid of anything but the pure pleasure, the ecstasy that makes an orgasm seem like stubbing your toe. It’s beautiful, and ninety nine point nine percent of your brain is flooding all your senses with joy, all the pain and illness gone away. The little fragment, that tiny decimal, it won’t be washed away for good. The guilt will come back, and then worse than the guilt, the pain. You didn’t want to become an addict. You never asked for this. Let it pass. Breathe in. Breathe out. Now is the best time, the good time. You’ve survived again. For this moment your troubles are gone. Let yourself slide off into the realm of the unconscious, carried aloft on clouds of silk. When you wake up, you’ll have twelve hours. ******** The Domicile of Darkness was many things to many people. A sort of half-place, a building that could seem to be a fantasy one minute and both very real and deadly the next. A lot of this was attributed to the personality and will of the owner and resident of the property, Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy. In his time, Jack had been the scourge of the wrestling world, forcing countless people to his will. It had seemed more important to break people than chase title to The Ripper, and it had been a theme that he had taken into retirement with him. “The papers have been prepared,” croaked a voice that spoke of many years gone past. The Ripper turned to see Sirius Smythe, his trusted lawyer and facilitator, standing behind him with a sheaf of legal documents. A slight raise of the eyebrows from Jack was as close to a smile as he would give at this time. He rose from the high backed chair in which he had been sitting and walked at a measure pace to where the ancient lawyer stood. “They are all in order?” he asked. “You even need to ask?” Sirius Smythe had been practising law for over eighty years, and his knowledge of every legal system on both sides of the Atlantic Ocean was second to none. From his past as a con artist, forging documents to let him conduct trials when still in his teens, to his more recent years in the employ of Jack Nobbledy, the colourful history of The Ripper’s lawyer was littered with misdemeanours, bribery, corruption and the use of every loophole that could be exploited in a court of law. Many would have thought that his current day to day tasks were beneath an expert such as he, but something about the place kept him there, something in the work that The Ripper was doing. There were those who said that Sirius Smythe was actually a well trained zombie, performing only the tasks asked of him by his master, The Ripper. Of course, this was strenuously denied, although Sirius did seem to have a strange aversion to rooms containing both typewriters and large boxes that can be used to store items. Jack took the papers and placed them on to a small table, next to an ornate gothic candlestick. The Ripper was not particularly enamoured with any particular style of architecture or design, but he felt that sometimes it was worth keeping up the appearances that people expected of you. “They seem to know that he’s here,” said Sirius. The Ripper nodded. Cereal Killer’s future team-mates had all been curious as to the whereabouts of the erstwhile PTC Cruiserweight Tournament Champion. After winning the tournament in a tense finale against Tessa Windsor and Archibald MacGregor he had done some promotional work for Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling, but had soon started to look drawn and tired. There were rumours about the stress taking its toll on him after a few years out of the spotlight, and when he just plain vanished from the realm of the minor celebrity, everyone just presumed he had decided to return to his previous life as a recluse from the flashbulbs of wrestling success. If only it had been that easy for him. The drug addiction foisted upon Frank, the Cereal Killer, had changed him in ways he was unable to control, or prevent. His former touchstone, his schizophrenia that made him hear voices in his mind, a hundred different characters and players cajoling and consoling him through his life, had been wrenched away and replaced with silence. For Frank, this was the worst thing that could have happened, as if one of his senses had been obliterated in an instant. The side, or perhaps even the main, effect of the cocktail of drugs that Frank had received was to make him single minded. He could focus everything he had on one task and one task only. It was that ability to zero in on a task that had allowed him to win the tournament, but it had not lasted. The other chemicals within the compound he injected into his veins were too strong to be resisted, and eventually his defences fell down in spectacular fashion. A well muscled athlete turned into a rake of a man who looked like the fashion industry’s definition of ‘heroin chic’, only without the chic. An attention span ravaged by the combination of psychotropic drugs, amphetamines, opiates and various inhibitors. Cereal Killer had become a champion, but he had paid an incredible price; the butterfly to The Ripper’s hurricane. “Yes,” replied Jack, after a pause. “They know that he is here. What they know about his state remains to be seen.” “How did they find out?” enquired the lawyer, although the question was rhetorical. There was only one way they could have found out. “The Ripper deemed it necessary. They are a disparate group of simpletons and savants, who needed something to latch on to. What they choose to do with the information is their own choice.” “You think they might come here?” “It is not a possibility that The Ripper would discount,” was the reply, with a hint of humour creeping in to Jack’s tone. “Will we let them see him?” The Ripper raised his black eyebrows and looked across the room with something like incredulity. “That would not be the wisest of decisions.” ******** You wake up in a mess, but you’re almost happy with how it’s turned out because for once you didn’t actually soil yourself. The buzz has long since worn off as you lay there, and as ever you think that it was a bit of a waste, and you wish that consciousness had stuck around longer. Then the guilt hits, and the realisation of what your life has become. It hasn’t exactly been a wonderful life from start to finish, but it’s never come close to anything like this before. Even when you were retired and living only with your imaginary friends it wasn’t this bad. It was never, ever this real. Yet it’s your own fault. You let yourself get manipulated again. The drugs weren’t an expected development, but you put yourself in the position to be abused, and he took advantage. His motives, as ever, unclear, but his effectiveness in carrying them out, immaculate. You’ve got some hours to kill, but there aren’t exactly a lot of things you can during that time. The Domicile is yours to roam, with only a few doors failing to yield to the bony hand that opens them, but it seems somehow stripped of its magic, washed out of colour, a drab backdrop of grey shades that never coalesce into something that can catch your attention. You don’t know if this is because of the drugs, your mental state, or if it has always been this way, and you’ve just never noticed before. The Domicile of Darkness. Even now it can almost bring a smile to your face knowing that the building has such a ridiculously dramatic title. It’s a large house, nothing more and nothing less, yet the grandeur of the title and of the inhabitants have always been enough to lend it the aura it acquired. Each room and artefact named in the same fashion; here the Kitchen of Darkness and there the Laundry Room of Darkness. You kick dust across the Carpet of Darkness and continue on your search. You wonder, as you often do, about some of the previous tenants here. There were so many people that lived here once upon a time, associates of The Ripper, as you yourself were once upon a time. They seemed to add the colour to it, the impression that this place could actually be a home as opposed to a prison. An unruly bunch, all of them, but perhaps they tempered something in The Ripper, gave him an alternative outlet, and now they were gone there is just one pet project of his left, and he’s seeing how far he can take it? Or perhaps this is just another game to him, but one that is slowly taking your life from you, syringe by syringe. Another door opens, and this time it’s the right one. You’re early, but you’ve found him, and it sure as hell doesn’t pay to be late. ******** “The little killer,” said Jack. “To what does The Ripper owe this pleasure?” The Ripper looked down towards the smaller man with a mix of amusement and contempt. The contrast between the two physical specimens was astonishing. One stood closer to seven feet tall than six, and his broad shoulders still looked as if they could tear someone apart. The other was a mere shadow of his former self. Cereal Killer had once struggled to get his fighting weight down to under the required cruiserweight limit, but now he wasn’t even in sight of it. Skin hung limply from his bones, and the face that had once brought him notoriety as XUW’s Teen Heartthrob was now gaunt and sallow. His muscles that gave him surprising strength were now wasting away, or already gone. He looked far, far older than his twenty four years. “I’ve run out,” said Frank, the words dropping from his lips without inflection. Had The Ripper been wearing glasses, he would have been looking over them at his former protégé. “Of what have you run out?” “You know what,” snapped Frank, and The Ripper immediately smiled, seeing the reaction of the man he knew underneath the fog of frustration and addiction. “Ah yes. Your nasty little habit… The Ripper is sure that you have used your latest supply up quicker than usual.” “I don’t want to play your games any more.” “Nobody is forcing you to do anything. The Ripper believes you were the one who came here with a request.” Frank stopped. The shakes and shivers were starting. He knew that he didn’t need the drugs for the next few hours, but his discomfort would gradually increase, the feeling of disassociation from his own body would grow larger and more uncomfortable until he had to jam the needle into his arm, and the guilt, the stigma of being a drug addict be damned. “Please…” was all he managed, a tear coming to his eye, as it always did. This seemed to satisfy the larger man. Frank was slightly surprised, looking up at his nemesis and half expecting him to grin, telling him that it was not enough, that he would have to beg, down on his knees… again. “By the time you find your way back to your room, what you seek will be waiting for you.” The Ripper turned away as if to end the conversation. This was not unusual, nor was the method of supply. Nevertheless, the ease with which Jack had given in to his request emboldened Cereal Killer. “What is…” said CK, silenced by The Ripper looking over his shoulder. “The Ripper almost forgot to tell you. There is a wrestling match happening soon. You are scheduled to take part in it. You will be teaming with three other members of your federation to take on three from PRIME, in a rematch from the aborted Survival match.” “Didn’t you participate in that originally?” “Yes.” Frank paused, and The Ripper turned away again. The conversation was over, with that revelation. No… I have to do this. “What will be there… in the syringes? What are you giving me?” It was not the first time that Frank had asked this. He’d lost count after around the twentieth attempt to get the information, but that day was different. He had no idea why, but it was different. “Some Haloperidol, Clozapine, Dothiepin, Phenelzine, and other assorted prescription drugs. Then there are the opiates, like Meperedine, Hydrocodone, and some… stronger variants. There are more, such as Lemoncleanse laxative and the Colonel’s secret mix of eleven herbs and spices.” “Really?” asked Frank. “Would it make a difference?” asked The Ripper. Sullenly, Frank shook his head. ******** Walk down the corridor, don’t run. Breathe in, and keep it even. You know he can see you, from the multitude of cameras hidden at every turn. You’ve never seen these cameras, but you’ve heard the stories. No cubic inch of the Domicile is not visible under the watchful ‘eyes in the sky’. Keep walking, because for all the times he’s seen you break, snap upon his cruelty, he will not see you this time. No matter how well he knows your inner workings, or how well he has orchestrated your rise and fall, you will not give him this victory. He may have won every single war between you, but this one battle would be yours. You will make it back to your room, and you will sit there for an hour before you succumb, and inject. So you see the door, and the urge to run to it is almost overpowering; to throw open the door and find the drugs and stab them into your arms, ending the cravings and the shakes and the damn need to be conscious in this hellish place. No. Walk. Breathe in and walk. The door handle feels good under your clammy hand, and you turn it, looking away from your own body as you always do. No guilt for the moment, just focus on surviving for an hour. Prove him wrong. Prove that when you put your mind to it you can overcome all that he’s done, if only for a short time. Open the door. Raise your eyes. Break your heart. The door closes with a click behind you that speaks of nothing but a very expensive locking mechanism. Your eyes fill with tears and your already shaking body wracks itself with sobs. The final straw has landed on the camel’s back, and you are truly broken. Your eyes close from the reality that you’ve been hiding for as long as you’ve been able. You got back to find that The Ripper had clad all the walls and ceiling in mirrors. Staring back at you is a man you don’t recognise. Some sort of fucked up kid like you’d see in the wrong sorts of movies, or in the newspapers a week before they died of an overdose. That is not you, you say, but of course it is. You’ve got a match in under a week against four wrestlers from possibly the best federation in the world today. At the moment you couldn’t even beat four kids fresh from kindergarten, and it strikes you then. You’re not a wrestler any more. You’re barely even a man. So you inject, and he wins…
