Things Need to Change

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Note: Ben granted me the right to use his Lane Stevens and Miles characters in my roleplay. Thank you Ben. October 1st, 2009 Blue Ridge Memorial Cemetery Blue Ridge, Alabama “As if I didn’t hate this enough,” Jeb grunted, a cold gust of wind cutting across the cemetery and hitting him in the face. It was the only thing keeping the Blue Ridge weather from being perfect. It seemed like anytime he came back to visit her, it’s been either raining or frigid. Maybe it’s God’s way of making Jeb aware of his presence, even in spite of his continued questioning of His existence, he thought. It had been a while since he’d been back, in fact it was becoming harder and harder to get back to visit her since he’d gone back to working for Lane, but the visits still never get easier. “I’m sorry I haven’t been back in a while Mom. Work has been pretty busy lately. Lane says hi, although I don’t think he believes much in mourning the loss of others.” Jeb snorted as he stared down at the gray headstone and studied the lettering that had been chiseled into it many months ago. His good friend, pretty much his only friend, and employer, Lane Stevens doesn’t believe in always coming here and had tried to convince Jeb that he has been wasting his time coming back to visit his dead mother. Yet he kept coming back. ” "She's dead, big fella. And dead folks, their ears don't work so well. So quit dropping all that money on the airfare and develop an honest to goodness coke habit or something."” Jeb couldn’t help the smile that cracked his face as the thought about Lane’s words came to him. Truth was that Jeb had been trying to be more and more like Lane. He’d wanted to be able to get to the point where he just didn’t care the way Lane didn’t. He figured if he could get to that point, to live that type of lifestyle, then the pain of losing his mother, the injustice he felt in her death, would all go away. Life would just be easier. Maybe. Being like Lane wasn’t always the easiest thing for Jeb, even though he believed it would make his life easier. He took a long time to really grow into doing a lot of the things that Lane would do or taking part in a lot of the things Lane did. Even now, despite the desire to just forget everything else, he’s not able to always go all out the way he knows Lane would. “Hello momma. It’s Jeb. I know it’s been a while since I got back to see ya but I’ve been pretty busy with work and all.” The truth was that while work had been busy it wasn’t why he didn’t make it back as often as he should. Jeb’s choices in life had been shady, at best, since his mother’s death and he knew it. He intentionally had been seeking out that behavior and those decisions. He knew she wouldn’t approve and in his heart he doesn’t blame her. “I haven’t been the best person lately. I don’t know if you’ve had time to look down on me. I’m hopin’ you been far to busy playin’ bridge and goin’ for walks on them streets of gold, you and the preachers always told me bout, cause I’m sure you’d be very disappointed in me.” Jeb took in a deep breath as he knelt down onto one knee, in a futile attempt to put himself at more of an eye level with the stone. He always looked directly at the stone, and her name chiseled into it, cause it’s just impossible to look at the ground. To think of her body, now nothing more than a skeleton in that casket, just turns his stomach. To look at the ground when he made these visits made his heartache even more than it normally would just thinking about her on a daily basis. “I know I’m suppose to put my faith in God, to believe He always has a purpose and a plan but I can’t see it Mom. I can’t see why a great person like you had to be sick, had to suffer, and had to die and meanwhile there are all kinds of folks out there who are terrible that get to live it up all the time.” During all her years of suffering, while sick with cancer, Jeb had never understood why she was the one suffering when there were child molesters, rapists, and all those sorts that should have been the ones to be in her place. Despite not understanding the why during those days he never gave up hope that God had a plan to make her better. He just knew if he kept his faith, believed, prayed every day, that she’d be healed. When she died it all changed. His faith pretty much died the same moment they’d turned her breathing machine off. “If there is a Heaven I’m sure you’re there and I’m sure if you’re not busy then you’re likely there worryin’ about your baby boy givin’ up on the man upstairs. I’m sorry for any worry and pain I might be causin’ ya but I’m startin’ to believe there ain’t no hope for me anymore.” Over the last several months the feeling that he is permanently lost, even if God really was in the metaphorical upstairs, had grown stronger and stronger in Jeb. He had been doing things, and taking part in things, that he’d have never done prior to her death. Terrible things. It’s true that Jeb had once been a part of The Affliction and that they often did bad things but Jeb was always spared from being part of those bad things. He some how managed to be left out of the terrible stuff they did. Now things were different. Now he wasn’t necessarily left out of those things and he really didn’t try to avoid them anymore. He’d long ago accepted that his association with Lane was one his mother wouldn’t have approved of. Lane being the kind of bad influence that she’d always warned him about would not have sat well with her. “I know you wouldn’t approve of Lane. I know he’s just the kind of person you wouldn’t be happy with me bein’ around but…” his voice trailed off a moment. He couldn’t explain why it was so hard for him to explain that Lane was the only person left in the world that he believed really was his friend. “Lane’s a good person at heart. It’s not easy to see, or to believe, but it’s there. Somewhere inside of him is the good man that he wants so desperately to bury underneath all the backstabbin’, evil doin’, and contempt for the world.” He takes in a deep breath and looks up into the sky, quietly closes his eyes. The cold wind that was annoying him before, making the day miserable, became a welcomed feeling. The cold made it much easier to push back the burn in his eyes and face. For several minutes he stood there with his eyes closed, thinking, wondering, remembering. Above all else he continued to question himself, his decisions, and how he’s become this man who his mother would never recognize anymore. Finally he slowly nodded his head, almost as if he’d agreed with one of his thoughts, before opening his eyes and turning his gaze back to the headstone. “Maybe I’m more like him than I thought.” A moment passed before Jeb slowly leaned forward to the gravestone, laying a single rose atop the wet grass, and placed a gentle kiss on the top of the stone marking. Several seconds passed before Jeb slowly rose back to full height, turned from the grave, and once more, just like hundreds of times before, walked away. Away from the only person that he knew, for certain, ever really cared about him. As he walked away he began to think about the GTT letter that was in the inside pocket of his jacket. He’d never thought a whole lot about doing anything for himself in the wrestling business, or life for that matter, but the realization that he and Lane were more and more alike was giving him reason to believe maybe it was time to change those facts. October 5th, 2009 The Spearmint Rhino Las Vegas, Nevada The loud music and annoying strobe lights immediately put Jeb in a foul mood. Well check that. It actually made his foul mood even worse. His head was throbbing before he even arrived from all the stressing over rather he was doing the right thing by entering the GTT tournament. If he had been joining for the reasons he’d normally join something like this then he wouldn’t even give it a second though. This was different. This was going to be a lot more trouble. Then to top that all off now he had to come to the Rhino. That wasn’t unusual with Lane but it didn’t make it any better. He hated coming into these places for his meetings with Lane. He understood the draw to seeing women naked, and he got why guys wanted so badly to see them naked, but he never understood, nor desired to, the draw to paying to watch a woman dance around in front of you and tease you. What is the point if you’re not going to actually get to go the whole way? He shook his head, as he slid his black jacket off and placed it over the back of the seating area he knew Lane would want for their meeting, because it’s not like he knows anything about going the whole way. Just one of those annoying things about himself that he couldn’t shake no matter how bad he wanted to in another feeble attempt to bury his pain. “Can I get you a drink honey?” a tall, blond asked with a smile. Her hair is long and blond. The tiny thong she’s wearing is the only clothing on her until you follow her long legs all the way down to the high heels on her feet. “Jack and coke with very little ice.” He responded while paying close attention to not give her a second look. He just couldn’t understand how any father could allow his daughter to become something like this. “And don’t call me that. I’m not your honey.” She stared for a moment as this giant of a man sitting before her. It wasn’t unheard of for a customer to be rude, crude, even down right cruel toward the ladies in any establishment like this but it was usually in some repulsive sexual manner. He was just cold toward her all together and that wasn’t normal. “Something wrong hon…” she caught herself and smirked. “Something wrong? You not a blond guy?” He rolled his eyes out of frustration. “Your hair color don’t matter none. I’m just havin’ a bad day is all.” She smiled at him and nodded her head. “Life can be pretty tough sometimes. Even us dumb strippers can relate to that.” She smiled again as she walked off to get his drink. He felt bad, momentarily, for snapping at her but it quickly passed. He knew he needed to clear his mind, get his focus, so that he could do what needed to be done. GTT was just around the corner and Lane had no idea that Jeb had even sent his letter in, signed, to be part of the tournament. He wasn’t sure how Lane would take the news. He could only hope that Lane, as usual, would be late. Unfortunately for Jeb things like that really never worked out in his favor. Suddenly the doors at the front of the club swung open, hard, and light flooded into t he joint for only a brief moment. But it was moment enough to know who’d arrived. Get Fucked Even if he would have been wearing a ski mask, this t-shirt alone gave away Lane Stevens’ identity immediately. He walked through the club briskly, his eyes still having time to linger on each girl individually. There was a dark humor in those eyes, a joke that Jeb sometimes thought he wished he could understand. Sometimes. But he was not alone. Miles Cavanaugh, another man whom Desade had bought for Lane’s “campaign” against the roster of Sin City, trailed behind. As always, Miles looked disinterested. Jeb wondered how much of that was for show, and how much of it was genuine. Did Miles have some of the same doubts he did about some of the things they had been asked to do? He couldn’t fathom asking. As The River Rat approached the stage, Jeb’s gaze again returned to the table. “Hey there ‘Bama,” Desade’s WMD said. Stevens seated himself next to Stewart, already motioning for the cocktail waitress. “What I like about this place, is not really so much the food, or the drinks its…well…” “It’s the exposed vaginas,” Miles completed Lane’s sentence. “Bingo!” Lane chirped. “Hello Lane. You’re in a good mood today.” Jeb responded as he nodded toward Miles without so much as a smile. At first he’d sort of liked Miles but over the months working with him, while working for Lane, had really soured his thoughts about Miles. He didn’t really know why, or care for that matter, cause it wasn’t like he was any worse than Lane. Just something about him just rubbed Jeb the wrong way. “Oh, but I’m always in a good mood,” Stevens replied, and smiled savagely. “It’s you I worry about sometimes, sour puss.” “Someone probably should.” Jeb responded quietly. Lane started to say something, probably to ask what he said, or more accurately to likely mock what had been said, but was cut off when Jeb simply waved him off about it. “I’m not exactly in the mood to be here any longer than I have to be so let’s cut to the chase. We’re here for a reason so lets get it over with.” The cocktail waitress arrived, which only seemed to irritate Jeb further but he didn’t really know why. After all she had his jack and coke he’d ordered earlier so he knew she’d be back. “Gin and tonic,” Lane said, and Miles handed her a twenty. She smiled and was on her way. “Now, where were we?” “I dunno, you tell me.” Jeb responded before downing his drink. “Ah, yes. I can give you the quick version, then. The legal case against Lance Marshall isn’t going to build itself. So what I got is the names of the officials that Lance assaulted after the PPV match, you guys can split them up. We need to get in touch with them and let ‘em know that if they should desire to press charges, they will have the finest lawyers that Desade’s money can buy.” Jeb couldn’t get over how proud of himself Lane looked. “Here ya go,” Miles uttered, and threw a folded piece of paper in front of Jeb. He picked up the paper and unfolded it to get a look at the names. They were just that to him, a bunch of names. A bunch of people who worked for or around SCCW and he didn’t have a clue who any of them were or what they did. He didn’t care. That fact was one of the many reasons that he knew he had to change. It was time to make things different. “You know we could always do this the way things were intended to be handled. Man to man.” Jeb responded as he folded the paper back up and stuck it in his pocket. “You know like maybe there is some way that you or I could get hooked up against him in that PTC tournament and just take care of this in the ring.” “Bo-ring,” Lane said in a singsong voice. “Lance thinks he’s better than you and me Jeb, that he’s some kind of goddamn super hero. I simply intend on showing him, and the rest of the SCCW viewing audience the depths that this man is willing to sink to when the chips are down. Plus I just get off on making him do the angry face.” Lane attempted a Lance Marshall impression, contorting his face into mock rage. A few moments later, his gin and tonic arrived. “You want to be the kryptonite, or whatever, to his superman. I saw we do that the right way. The real way. In the ring.” Jeb replied a little more impatiently. “The GTT provides that opportunity to destroy him, in the ring, in front of the entire world and can be achieved without all that other junk you’re talking about that sounds a whole lot like cowardice to me.” Jeb held his breath for a moment. He wasn’t sure why he chose to call Lane a coward. True that was the nature of how Lane operated but as much as Jeb wanted to get his life back on track he still thought of Lane as his friend. This wasn’t going exactly the way he’d hoped it would. Jeb took a deep breath and laid his hands flat on the table as he looked right at Lane. “Listen Lane. I’m in the tournament at PTC. ”At first Lane had no response, his eyes narrowed slightly as he stared down his soldier. Then after a few moments his grin widened, spreading across his face like a virus. “So you have…backbone now, eh? Those must be some kind of vitamins you’ve been taking. Well naturally, it’s in my nature to wish you the very best of luck.” “You’re a real sweetheart, boss,” Miles added, lifelessly. “Personally? I don’t give two shits about this tournament,” Stevens didn’t even pay Jeb the respect of looking at him. He tucked a dollar bill behind his ear, and waited for a stripper to come by. “Alexandra would like to be properly…represented, so I’ll do my thing, whatever. It’s my job. And when people get in the way of me and my job well,” Lane tittered, “the shit gets tragic, ya follow?” Jeb said nothing. There was a lump in his throat. This talk was easier in his head and now it was clearly moving in a direction he’d hoped it wouldn’t. “So if you should happen to come across Desade or I in that tournament, I’d imagine you’ll just be laying down, enjoying the view. The MCI Center really is state of the art.” “Look, Lane, I understand how you…” But Stevens kept on. “In fact, this actually works out good. You should be able to take down a couple of these guys. Kind of soften the field up a little and make winning this tournament easier for Desade.” “Lane…” The River Rat continued to rattle on, ignoring that his friend was trying to explain to him that things weren’t what they seemed. Jeb desperately wanted to explain to Lane what this tournament meant to him but Lane was making it impossible. He was off in his own little world. Creating. Scheming. Basically being Lane Stevens. “It’d even be really funny if right before we locked up I got on the mic and called you fat, and you acted so hurt by it you just fell over, and I pinned…” What happened next, no one was ready for, especially The River Rat. Jeb grabbed his gin and tonic from the table in front of him, and threw it against the wall. One of the dancers screamed a moment after it shattered. A few of the bouncers were already on their way down to the stage. Miles took a few steps back, his mouth slightly ajar. Despite what this moment had become Jeb had to admit that Miles backing up like that felt good. “This is for me, goddamnit,” Jeb whispered. “For me.” Jeb Stewart taking the Lord’s name in vain, even in these months when he wasn’t even sure that The Lord really existed anymore, was not a common occurrence. He stood there for a moment shaking. A combination of rage and embarrassment fueling his every thought at the moment before he stormed off, shoving one of the bouncers to the floor on his way out. It all happened so quickly. After a few moments, Miles took the seat next to Lane. “What do you make of that?” Miles asked. Stevens rubbed his index finger against his chin. “It’s a shame that it has come to this but, just the same…it’s come to this,” Stevens said, and flagged down another waitress. Miles didn’t know what that meant, and he wasn’t sure he wanted to.
