Offensiveness Is Almasy’s Dying Breath: Chapter 1

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 “Offensiveness? Is that even a word? His offensiveness like none other, Seymour Almasy is favored to finish in the top leg of this tournament.” I shook my head and placed the paper down before me. How fitting it is to see that my very first opponent in this tournament of all wrestling tournaments shall be none other than a hand picked favorite, a man destined to win. Unsettled, I glance down again, only this time to be greeted by his picture. There he was in all his majesty, clad iron tight in glimmering armor with a sword by his side. To say that he was a warrior would be an understatement. I ventured my eyes upward, and then it began. Again I was looking face to face with the man whom I have killed several times, the resemblance uncanny. A descendant maybe. Decades after decades, centuries after centuries we fought. However, each time in a different fashion, the result was nonetheless gratifying and ended abruptly in the same manner, the man’s death. It always ended with his offensiveness subsiding, the dying man grasping for his weapon, each time a different one, however he soon recedes, as he tries to obtain something, which he never had to fight for before. The man was no longer fighting for his life, no longer for his country. The man was now battling for his very breath. How fitting it is that it very well seems that it shall be the same way in which Seymour Almasy will end his offensiveness in this tournament. Fighting for his last breath. Yes, inside my head locked away are several great stories, each brought back to life after seeing my opponent’s picture for the first time. Seymour Almasy, UWF’s God. What more can be said of him, what less? Nothing, the man has achieved greatness, but, as I can attest, even Rome fell. And I shall see to it that he falls the same way as the ones before him, his forefathers. Need more proof? Something lacking in my words? “Listen now and I shall tell you a story, one filled with thousands of thoughts, but yet little mind. Thoughts taken from me Ash a man, or at the very least a shattered shadow of one. Complexities may arise as to my origin, but I am of flesh, however am bound to liberties beyond that which mortals cannot fathom or consciously comprehend. Relax, take it in and seek solace, knowing that you have no other choice but to do so willingly. With that I bid you adieu as I establish the setting of my account.” ‘Death was all around. It’s fetid breath filled the air. It’s putrid conquest was displayed grotesquely across the battlefield.’ I remember those words, echoed in my writing of years long gone. Looking back now I can see why it is that I have yet to get the thoughts out of my head. I remember the feelings, the mixed feelings. Feelings of joy and happiness later turned to anguish and in turn to anguished mourning. I remember a lot from the past battles, but now in particular Almasy’s ancestors; I remembered the people, their faces. Faces twisted in what was left of them shrieking out in pain, horror. Faces that looked on as if they would have a choice, a chance at salvation. The thought of the day, that to them, they believed would never come. To me however, the thought of the day in which I wish to come. Again, on those eves I broke down and lost myself. Crying, unable to find satisfaction or deliverance for what they could do, for what I have yet been able to do. Little do the Dead know that they can and do find their salvation, God’s precious gift to them, mortality. Maybe in this final meeting with Almasy I can meet mine. You see I am their master and speak out to them, the Dead. I am their lord and command them. However, I am yet their servant and bow to their every whim. I am a walking contradiction onto myself, and breath that thought with every step. The Dead the Living, it’s all the same to me. They are my reminder, the reminder of what I am. Life and Death incarnate. The embodiment of something greater, something superior to that of mortal beings. All this, and yet I wish it not so. Offensiveness has a tendency to lead us to the future as its remains lead us to the past, where our memories empower our thoughts. Yes, I remember those days for I took in Death and in so embracing it, I awoke and liberated my thoughts my memory, or did I? I believe it was merely simple letting Death in with a warm welcome, however now I see the error in my ways, as I try to contrive that which I have killed. Again, my thoughts my memory, maybe me? No, not me but simply a part of me. The part that I cannot get back, but do I want it back is the question? “You see, offensiveness is what men have come to appreciate. All throughout history they have found new ways of pushing the boundaries. Advances in technology made it all the more clear, as first the knife and spear were produced, and then came the gun. Now, today we have the Atomic Bomb, and even the ‘Death Ray’, which can shoot with pinpoint accuracy, due to satellites, across the globe with searing temperatures hotter than that of the surface of our Sun.” I would look down at the grounded man glaring, but every time he dared not to look. Did he hear me? In an attempt to conjure up what little words I could from him, I would speak again. “Man today, lives on the brink of extinction. What say you?” He would look up at me, with his bloody smile and again I would speak ever so eloquently. “I think that today as yesterday people continuously strive for the betterment of that which surrounds them. We compete for many things. Some men compete for the love of the game and others, most certainly for money. However, I think that we, as man, try to be the best at what it is we say and do and those who uphold these morals and can attest utterly to them, pride themselves in the image of being that which no other being can be. The best! It is in this type of competition that I fear man has gone wrong, as one can never be perfect, and in the pursuit of trying to do so you create flaws around you, that others immediately are drawn to. It is then that men seek competition to dangerous new heights, and hold the world as it were but a game. One such competition was the starting of the race to build the Atomic Bomb, a race that supposedly ended with the American victory of World War II, but will ultimately end with the world’s destruction.” Yes, indeed my apprentice would never listen to me nor would he choose to hear me. I stopped momentarily as I spoke of love and went back, albeit an instant. That, however is another story. Taking in my words I remembered once again why I cherish the sound of my voice echoing off into the valley, and why I do not provoke him to speak more often then he should. I always feel as the undergraduate in my students’ presence, as they tend to mesmerize my thoughts in the ways of silence. “How do you feel about war? Do you think it makes birth through Offensiveness, or do you think it is pioneered through competition? Maybe even greed?” To this day I still have not the answer. I have seen fruitless wars fought over the pettiest of things. The battle of Antietem for example, the bloodiest day in the Civil War, fought over a shoe. I was there, however it wasn’t at this battle that I saw Seymour draw in his last breaths again, instead that honor was saved for me at another special place during this war, where upon I slew him with ease. “July 1st through the 3rd in 1863, two forces fought at the Battle of Gettysburg, the costliest battle of the Civil war. Over fifty-one thousand casualties were reported. I shot a man there, and watched him die before my eyes. He had the same face as Almasy.” The account true, I had been on that field as well. Waiting for the adversary to appear. Knowing that my born over enemy would be coming for me again. As I smote him, I pondered at the thought of him coming back. I knew he would inevitably, and that again I would have to kill him. My opportunity came a while later. “Almost 11 million people died in what is referred to now as World War I. It was there that I shot down a war plane in a ‘Dog Fight.’ As the plane fluttered to the ground I watched in amazement as the very same man died again.” The same Almasy, or at least his ancestor, killed again. Being the Master of the Dead, I let him be reborn so that I may slaughter him again. Sure enough, years later I had my opportunity. “On in March of 1939, Adolph Hitler demanded that Poland allow Germany to annex the Nazi-dominated free city of Danzig, and to also allow Germany to be given control over a twenty-five mile wide strip of land between Germany and East Germany, created by the Versailles Treaty. This was originally done to give Poland access to the Baltic Sea. A compilation of countries aptly named the Allies made several attempts to persuade Hitler to negotiate with the Poles. All such attempts were unsuccessful. After signing the Nazi-Soviet Pact, which safeguarded the eastern border. Hitler sent his air and land forces into Poland on September 1, 1939, where upon command Warsaw and other Polish cities were bombed thus starting a 6 year and 1 day ordeal known today as World War II.” “I gunned you down again Almasy, at least your ancestor, only this time by machine gun fire. What makes you think your future shall be any different than his, or should I say you? He fell, as you shall, in a hail of bullets. Still breathing I walked up to you Almasy, as you grasped for breath, I shot you in the head with my Luger.” I say all of this and swallow hard thinking about the times and days that I saw the men strewn about, yes I was indeed there in that Hell. But I can’t help it, I must torture myself time and time again. It is what I am, it’s what I love, what I hate, me. That’s my nature. This is the path in which I must ride every day. This is my curse this is my destiny, that which I cannot change. Many think of it as a gift. Do you think that? Is that what you call your life, let alone mine a gift? Life eternal? Something I never asked for yet cannot refuse. I call it a curse. Some people think this life is a blessing others say it’s a nuisance. The latter are the people that know what I truly feel, those which are the Dead. Because they realize that I cannot stop these feelings, I cannot change who I am. I am the Phoenix, life everlasting. The Dead know me and seek me, for they know that they will not wake again, and laugh at my misfortune. As they know that I must arise another day with this grief, this pain. They realize that I must wake up with that which I call myself. Offensiveness has a tendency to lead us to the future as offensiveness’ remains lead us to the past, where our memories empower our thoughts. Still now I must momentarily conclude my internal reflection, as I can still see the dying man, my student taking in his last breaths. Taking time again, I glanced back over at the paper. There he was, Seymour Almasy, standing there in his armor. I think it was at about this time that I was brought back to the Spartan days, as indeed the boy reminded me of that breed. I have seen and been in so many wars over the year. I have so many tales that this face brings me back to, yet one special event, still stands out above the rest. It is still one of my favorite stories to tell. “The Spartan armies, though usually quite small, were well-disciplined and all but overpowering in combat. Each citizen soldier was encouraged with ferocious tenacity to win or die. When giving her son his shield for battle, a Spartan mother would say ‘Bring back this shield yourself or be brought back upon it,’ referring to the manner in which the dead were carried on their shields from the field of battle. Even know I wonder if Almasy was ever given the same refined outlook on war. Among Sparta's most heroic achievements was the stand taken by its fighting men at the battle of Thermopylae in 480 BC, during the Persian Wars.” “Originally engaging the enemy in August of 480 with a force of some seven thousand men, Leonidas, the Grecian king, aimed to hold a pass as long as possible so that the rest of Greece could rally their troops and navy. Xerxes, the Persian commander, did not believe such a small force would oppose him so mightily. He gave the Greeks five days to retreat. When they did not, he single handedly selected and sent special waves of troops into the pass, but each successive company wave was effectively eradicated. The Persians, with arrows and short spears, could not break through the long spears of the Greek hoplites, although it was said that the Persian arrows blotted out the sun. The six thousand Greeks defending the pass defeated the Persians in a similar manner on the second day of battle.” “After the second day, a Greek named Ephialtes defected to the Persians and informed Xerxes of a separate path through Thermopylae. The pass was defended by the other one thousand Greeks, from Phocis, who had been placed there when the Greeks learned of the alternate route just before the battle. These men remained staged expecting not to engage the Persians. The Phocians offered a brief resistance before fleeing, and the Persians advanced unopposed. Leonidas then realized that further fighting would be futile. On August 11th he dismissed all but three hundred Spartans. A contingent of Thespians, who were led by Demophilus, stayed as well in a suicidal effort to delay the advance. Leonidas also had a force of Thebans, but they quickly defected to the Persians. Although the Greeks killed many Persians, including two of Xerxes' brothers, Leonidas was killed, along with all three hundred of his men. The last Spartans were finally killed after recovering their king's body, having been driven back into the narrowest part of the pass, onto a small hill in the pass.” “A small sad story in itself, however something stuck me as odd in this contest. These men were fighting against imposible odds, and it whittled down to what? In the end, the Spartans held no spears, swords, knives, or rocks. They battled with their hands and mouths, thus the phrase ‘Battling tooth and nail’ was born. All the technology of their day at their disposal, and it ended with them battling for their lives with their very own extremities. This was their last offensive effort?” Thinking back, it was a pitty that my student stopped breathing, how rude of him to join the Dead without letting me say goodbye. I did however let out a sad sigh and uttered a prayer to myself. I can’t help but feel happy for him, because he then had the opportunity to rest. As elated as I was I still grabbed my shovel. I plunge my shovel into the soft wet soil. The ground that until that day had been spared of bloodstains. I looked upon that which was before me and shuttered at the sight. There I was in the midst of what I cannot do; join those who serve me, the Dead. But why would I wish even this so? For now I have all of eternity to torment myself over and over again about the one flaw in which I posses. I thought to myself about the man’s honor, but then decided to bury the body of that young Spartan, I dared not allow him to return home, like a hero, on his shield. I think I shall grant Seymour Almasy the same treatment as well. “You see Seymour Almasy; you can have all of the things in the world at your side. You can hold all of the prestigious titles you wish. You can have people cheer for you night in and night out. Come into this match with everything riding on your side and defeat this underdog known as Ash, the Dead Dark Phoenix. Fight me with everything you have. Hit me with every weapon you can. Bring with you all the offensiveness you want but it still won’t be enough to save you. For in the end, when it is all said and done, we will be fighting not for prominence, not for glory and not even for honor, we will be fighting for our very own lives again. We have done this all before, as we shall do once more, but this time what is left to do? I bested you with a sword, I crashed your body into the ground. I even gunned you down. I have it planned, This meeting will be the culmination; this meeting will be the end of humanity. This meeting I fear I will have to unleash, me! The Dead Dark Phoenix! Maybe then I can finally be able to rest!” This time we will again be fighting ‘tooth and nail,’ and I very well doubt he has the gull or the audacity to step up to me without that sword in his hand, the very one that he carries and parades around with him on a nightly basis. For you see, I understand that it not only accompanies him so that he may look better, but also so that he can strike fear into the fearless. “Seymour, I know that you have heard this before, in that you cannot strike fear into someone not afraid of anything, and I am not going to say that I am not afraid of anything; for that would be an outright lie in its self. As of now, I fear two things. I fear a man with golden Spartan armor, gear that seems to gleam on the twilight of every battle. For I know that it hides your true self, Almasy. Yes on the outside you are full of glitz and glamour. On the outside you are all that glitters. On the outside, that golden armor withholds what lies within, a kindred spirit. Yes, on the inside you are just like me, and that is what I fear the most.” “For you see, to me, all of the above-mentioned offensiveness in which we have taken the liberty of exerting upon one another, were all horribly equal in their atrocity and yet even when combined, still but a shadow beneath an ant when compared to that of my everyday thoughts. Almasy, I know your spirit yearns like mine, and yes I’m frightened because for me this is my everyday life story. For others like you Seymour who have yet had the horrible privilege to release the power within, all I have to offer is my pity; for the greatest offensiveness that the world has had the privilege of not knowing is held daily within the shattered remnants of my scarred mind. An offensiveness that I will unleash upon Almasy in due time, an offensiveness that will once again end, with your dying breath.”
