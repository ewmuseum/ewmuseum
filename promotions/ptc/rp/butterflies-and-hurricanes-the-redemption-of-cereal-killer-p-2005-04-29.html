Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Two: "Fights, battles have begun / Revenge will surely come"

29 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-29 “Do it!” “No!” “Frank, do it!” “No, Valerie! I’m not trying to stick an apple up my ass, and that’s the end of it!” -------------------------------------------------- It had been a strange kind of week for Frank, the wrestler better known as Cereal Killer. He’d had his idyllic life shattered by his girlfriend entering him in to a wrestling tournament. He’d been reluctant to participate at first, but once he thought back to his glory days in XUW, he decided that he had to have one more throw of the dice, one more effort, and this time he’d really be trying. You see, Cereal Killer had always been afflicted with a crippling fear of failure, which forced him to abandon any gimmick or tactic that was giving him any success. He reasoned that if he wasn’t really trying his hardest, and wasn’t really aiming for the very top, then any failure was intentional. Aiming low and hitting his target was infinitely preferable than going for the very best and hitting the same low target. Eventually the underachiever bit off more than he could chew, and the only thing he hit was a stacked of thumbtack-ridden, flaming tables. His career was ended in an instant; yesterday’s man at the tender age of 21. Yet in the hospital he had met a lovely girl by the name of Valerie Rose Dubonnet, and after a long period of rehabilitation, she entered him into PTC’s second annual Cruiserweight Tournament, where he defeated Chris Collins from Core Wrestling to progress to the second round. Cereal killer’s return to wrestling, in tip-top condition, has been a resounding success, even if he himself is a little unstable. All this we know. All this will change. -------------------------------------------------- “Seriously Valerie, I don’t even know why you have any apples,” said Cereal Killer, complaining. “I didn’t buy them,” replied Valerie. “You bought them after your match, when you were ranting about being the Fruity Fighter from Frankfurt. To be honest, I was glad you were just after apples at the time.” Cereal Killer rubbed his head, but the dull ache wouldn’t go away. “Was I drunk?” “I think you might have had a little vodka, but you were acting strangely all day. Don’t you remember anything about last week at all?” “Well, I think that…” his voice tailed off. “You think that what?” “I remember fighting someone, and I remember yelling a lot.” “Well, that’s something. Frank, I’m worried about you. You’ve acted so strangely since I entered you in to that tournament. Your ranting last week was scary. You were claiming to be all these different people. It was like I didn’t know who you were.” Cereal Killer buried his head in his hands once more. His slightly curly dark blond hair poked through his fingers as he tried to massage some thoughts back in to his sluggish brain. “Yeah, that sounds… that sounds like something I’d do.” He was telling the truth. All his life Cereal Killer, or Frank as he was known to his parents, had invented imaginary friends, creations of his own mind that he talked to, or when he was stressed, became. In recent years they had gained more and more control over him, or he had grown weaker to resist the lure of the voices. It was one of the reasons he had given up wrestling for good, or so he thought. “If that happens again, Frank… if it happens again I might have to tell the doctors. You’re not meant to be like that.” “I know, Valerie.” “I only entered you to the tournament because I thought you’d want to be happy again, and that wrestling would give you that… but it can’t come between us, Frank.” “I know, Valerie. I know.” “So bend over, and I’ll fetch the apples.” “No! What’s the fixation with the apples? Or, for that matter, with my ass?” “You have a nice ass, babe,” said Valerie, waltzing off to fetch an apple for him. “Valerie, come back here right now!” he said, perhaps a little too sternly. Her hair whipped round a second before her face. The raven locks brushed past her nose to show eyes that reminded Frank of his own. They were burning brightly though, and he paused for a moment. “Don’t speak to me like that, Frank,” warned Valerie. “Okay, I’m sorry, but really, what’s the deal?” “Okay, I’ll tell you. Do you want to sit down?” “Only if there’s no fruit on my seat!” The couple sat down on their apple-free couch, the soft cushions bearing their weight far better than a round fruit might have otherwise done. “So, are you going to explain anything?” asked CK. “Yeah. I was reading a book recently called ‘Eat That Frog’, and it’s inspired me.” “To eat frogs?” “No, it’s not really about eating frogs.” “Oh, then that’s a really misleading title.” “Look, Frank, are you going to let me finish?” CK held his hands up in surrender, and Valerie continued. “I was reading the book, and it’s all about the philosophy that if you eat a live frog the first thing every morning…” “Then it is about eating frogs!” “…then you’ll have done the worst thing you’ll have to do that day. After the frog, everything else will be easier. So it’s not just about frogs, it’s about doing something at the start of the day that is nasty and that you don’t want to do. It’ll make everything else that day seem easier by comparison.” “So you want me to stick an apple up my ass?” “If you do it, imagine how much easier beating Enrique Morales will be!” “If I do it, imagine how hard walking will be!” Valerie threw her hands up into the air, and stood up, walking towards the door. “I’m sorry, babe,” said Cereal Killer. “Look, we can still do that kind of thing, but without the apple, okay?” Valerie turned around to look at him, a tear forming in her good eye. She was by no means the most stunning girl in the world, but there was a certain something about her that kept CK interested. There was a certain innocence that reminded him of an earlier time, back when he had started in the wrestling business, when he was sure there was a great career ahead of him. “I just want to be involved with you, Frank.” “I know, and you can be.” “It’s just that last week,” she said, sniffing loudly as she tried to stop the apparent emotions. “Last week, you were strange, Frank. There was something about the way you were screaming that I didn’t like… but I still like you Frank. I still want to help you. I could come down to the side of the ring and cheer you on, just let me be involved.” “I don’t deserve someone as good as you, Valerie.” A smile creased her face, and Cereal Killer stood up, taking a step towards her. He knew that he had upset her last week when he had one of his ‘episodes’. The voices around him, the imaginary friends he had created as a child, they’d been buzzing around his mind and he’d gotten carried away. It was the kind of emotion he felt whenever he was in the wrestling ring, but outside of it there was nothing positive that ever came out of it. CK desperately wanted to keep his control when he was around Valerie. To lose her would be too much… but sometimes his mind would wander, and sometimes it would be very hard to resist. “You’re so sweet, Frank.” “I’m the Sweetest Thing… I’m the Sweet-Tooth Scoundrel. I’m Sweet Home Alamaba!” “Frank?” said Valerie, worried. “No! I mean… no!” Cereal Killer’s pulse was racing, even faster than his mind. He had to control himself; he had to stop the past from coming in and taking him away again. His mind had been altered from his time in XUW, from the dark heart that manipulated his every move; that finally sent him over the edge. Surely he’d recovered from that by now? Valerie took a step towards him, scant feet separating them. “Frank, please don’t do this.” “I won’t, Valerie. I just think… I just think that you should let me be for a few minutes, and give me a chance to calm down. I’ll be fine, and maybe we can see someone about this, but for the moment, I need to be alone… I’m a loner. I’m the Lone Ranger. I’m the Lone Gunman. I’m…” His voice trailed off as he heard Valerie start to cry again. He wanted to hold her in his arms, to caress her head tenderly and promise that everything would be fine… but it wasn’t fine, and he didn’t know if he could stop it. Faced with a loss of control, Cereal Killer turned his back an Valerie, and closed his eyes tight, praying she would go before he lost control any more. “Dammit girl, leave!” He wanted to say it but he couldn’t. All that was in his mind was a series of names, of characters he knew, of characters he could become. His cranium was full to bursting point with escape routes, names and gimmicks that could take him further away from where he started, from the place that really terrified him. From the man that had brought this all to the surface; from The Ripper. Wanting to turn around, but locked in place, Cereal Killer waited for what seemed like an eternity, before he heard the door to the apartment open. A few seconds later, it closed, and he breathed an enormous sigh of relief. It was as if all of the tension in his body has just gone in an instant. The voices vanished, their plaintive calls to be heard falling on deaf ears instead of the receptive brain of their host. CK grinned as he opened his eyes, taking in the battered furniture and homely detritus of his third floor apartment. Until one of the voices returned; a voice like the sound of a chisel hitting a headstone. “You’re going to lose her, you know.” Cereal Killer turned round, but he knew what he’d see before he even moved. Standing in front of him was the man who had plucked him from obscurity four years ago, and who had then left him to rot when his usefulness was exhausted. It was The Ripper.
