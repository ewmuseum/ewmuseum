"Air"

20 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-20 * * * * * During the night, under the steady singing of the rain, Archibald MacGregor had a very long conversation with the ghost of whom he was named after, Archibald Matthews. A year prior, Archibald Matthews had revealed himself only once, for barely half a day, but informed Archibald of his fate in the grand scheme of things. That he was more important in the after-life than he ever would be in this one. That his family tree, four generations of male MacGregor’s before him, had all earned their place in Hell and with each successive generation, became more and more powerful tools in the Great War between Heaven and Hell. A year ago, he learned he was a Generation Five and walking a fine line between earning a place in Heaven or Hell. His ultimate fate was still up for grabs. But during the night, more than likely only solidified by his bloody act at the Church, the ghost of Archibald Matthews informed Archibald MacGregor of something he already knew: There was no way he could enter Heaven, not now. And like that, perhaps as selfless as a father could ever be, Archibald cast aside his own grief and wondered about how Aaron came into the picture. And the Angel told him everything he could ever want to know about the next life after this one. About how Heaven and Hell are the same place, but merely two sides of an on-going battle. About how the polarization between the two is nearing perfection. Where the Devil can walk the Earth if he sees fit, God cannot. Where every angel retains all their memories of their lives on Earth, the demons of Hell enter with what could best be described as retrograde amnesia. Except for the two who rule. The Devil remembers every sin of the Earth, every memory of his troubled life, while God takes his seat with a total loss of the horrors of the world in order to be fair and unbiased to those beneath him. These are the rules of the after-life, and Archibald Matthews told his namesake every little detail that he could possibly imagine. But the one fact that stuck out and scared Archibald MacGregor more than anything was the one that didn’t deal with his own demise, but the eventual demise of his only son, Aaron. The birthmark that rests beneath Aaron’s shoulders, in the center of his back, and the fact that he is a Generation Six fulfills a prophecy set down through the Ages of the afterlife in the form of a poem: Bearing a mark across the infant’s back, Allows an entrance into hell with memories intact. Aaron MacGregor, if he should follow in the footsteps of the five generations of MacGregor’s before him, will take the throne in Hell upon entrance. Only those with memories realize where they are without being told, and what they are to do without question. Like any loving father, Archibald MacGregor had to find a way to ensure his son wouldn’t follow him into Hell. And there was only one thing he could ask of God, and the ghost of Archibald Matthews, in order to accomplish his goal: He needed to tell Heather everything. Everything. Archibald needed to make sure she understood that the accident wasn’t her fault, and that they needed to raise Aaron in the best possible environment to ensure his safety not only in this world, but also the next. * * * * * "She’s going to get confused, you know, with both of us being named Archibald." "I hadn’t really thought of that. But then again, we’ve never really had a problem with it. I’m the dead one, and you’re the one with a pulse. It’s not too hard." MacGregor paused for a moment, "Everyone can see you, right?" Archibald Matthews nodded. "And everyone can hear what you say?" Another nod, "That’s right. I’m just as real as you, I’ve just died one more time than you have. And I can’t die, but I can tell a few bad jokes." "Well then to avoid confusing Heather, I think you should go by your middle name, which is…?" Archibald MacGregor asked, raising an eyebrow in genuine curiosity. The angel sighed, it seems even in the afterlife the distaste for an awful middle name was still present, "Wentworth." An avalanche of laughter roared out of the younger – and much more alive – Archibald, "Wentworth? Are you serious?" Wentworth Matthews shrugged his shoulders, which were hidden underneath a faded brown tweed coat, and pressed the bowler hat firmly atop his head, "We better get going. You have a lot to explain to Heather. I’ll just be here for the parlor tricks if you need me to perform some angelic miracle or a series of awful jokes." Still smiling, Archibald turned down towards Aaron, who was awake and alert in his portable car seat, and asked, "Did you hear that? His middle name is Wentworth! Just be glad Mom and I gave you Christopher." "Unoriginal." Wentworth replied, in a voice as dry as the bones in his far off coffin. Still smiling down at Aaron, Archibald tickled the infants foot and spoke in that cutesy baby-voice that so many parents overdo, "Maybe so…but at least we didn’t name you Wentworth, right?" Aaron smacked his lips, and his blue eyes wandered around the room. He obviously could not care any less. The big colorful and mysterious world was more interesting to him than a silly middle name. Before picking up the car seat and leaving the house, Archibald MacGregor glanced around the kitchen and noticed that the glass of vodka he had glued to the countertop had fully evaporated. With the bottle of vodka only a quarter of the way evaporated, he unscrewed the cap and poured himself another glass of alcohol he wouldn’t – and couldn’t – remove from the counter. Wentworth admired Archibald’s tactic to beat alcoholism, "That’s a very different method, but it seems to be working." Archibald nodded and put the bottle down, then turned away from the glass without even a thought of drinking it. He had another bottle in mind, except this one was filled with baby formula. A moment later, Aaron was slowly feeding on the bottle and both men and the baby were stepping outside onto the front porch when a time-hardened, stern voice erupted from behind them. "It’s about time, MacGregor." Archibald didn’t need to turn away from locking the door to identify to whom the voice belonged. Standing in his naval garb, with perfect posture and an invasive gray-eyed stare was Rear Admiral Daniel Davis. "We’re just leaving for the hospital, you’re welcome to ride with us if you like." Glancing at the driveway, Daniel Davis eyed up the Hummer H2 with utter disdain, "Not a very practical family vehicle." "It’s much safer than the minivan. If someone can flip this one onto it’s roof, it certainly won’t burst into flames." Daniel Davis grimaced, turning back and spitting out the words with such venom that both Archibald’s winced, "So you think that my daughter’s accident is a joke, do you? So you go out with the money you made…wrestling…and bought yourself the biggest and baddest vehicle money could buy?" "I’ll put the tike into the truck and leave you two alone for a minute," Wentworth said, picking up the car seat and making his way past Daniel Davis, who never removed his demeaning stare from his future son-in-law. "You don’t need to speak to me like I am some sort of disobedient child." Archibald said, trying to let his anger fade away with each word. He had plenty of trouble on his plate for the day, he sure as hell didn’t need Heather’s father poking and prodding at him. "I think this country air has clouded your judgment, boy. My daughter is laying in a hospital bed, covered in one giant scab from forehead to waistline because of you. I stood in her hospital room for hours last night and listened to her cry herself to sleep…for what reason? Because of you! You are a child, and a disrespectful one." Archibald’s jaw tightened, teeth grating against each other hard enough to pulverize diamonds into a fine dust, but he remained silent. He let the asshole continue on his tirade. "And what infuriates me most is the fact that you’re leaving in a day to go wrestle again! If I could have left the Navy to be by her side, I would have…but I have a duty, son. Your duty is to my daughter, and when you put that ring around her finger in a few months, you better damned well live up to that duty every waking minute of your life!" Through the Hummer windows, Wentworth Matthews watched as Archibald MacGregor descended the steps from the porch to the walkway, coming within inches of Daniel Davis’ face. They undoubtedly could smell what each other ate for breakfast. "Now you listen, because I’m only going to say this once. I don’t think you understand me – no, correct that, I know you don’t understand me. You don’t understand the passion and love I have for your daughter, or the grandson that we created! You’ve no – " "Love? Love! Do you even know what love is?" Rear Admiral Davis interrupted in a voice reminiscent of what he might use while speaking to an insubordinate officer while out at sea. Archibald’s face began to flush with anger, his eyes widen and his nostrils flare, "How dare you insult my love for your daughter! So you don’t think I know what love is, do you? Well I’ll fucking tell you what love is…your daughter is the epitome of love!" He paused a moment, screaming at the top of his lungs, uncaring about the neighbors possibly overhearing the argument, "She is laying there in a hospital bed, – covered in scabs like you so rudely put – and refuses to take a morphine drip for the pain. And why does she refuse to ease her own pain?" He waited another moment, then continued, "Because she is fully aware of my problem. She knows I’ve been addicted to booze longer than I’d like to admit. Because she loves Aaron – your grandson, might I remind you – more than she could ever express because she doesn’t want to depend on something the way I depend on alcohol! So don’t you ever preach to me about being unable to understand what love is, Rear Admiral Davis, because you have no room to speak. A loving father would never refer to his daughter as a scab." Tears began to well up in Archibald’s eyes as he stood face to face with absurdity wearing a star on his shoulder. "You love alcohol, kid. Do you want to know what I’m afraid of? I’m afraid that sooner than later, I’ll have to watch over you and Aaron for the rest of my years, because both of the men in my Heather’s life will be addicted to a bottle! The only love of your life comes in different proofs, so go ahead and try to pretend that you know what love is, what honor is, because it’s just a crock of shit. You’re a piece of shit, and my daughter has been paying the price ever since she started dating you, culminating with her skin being melted off in a fire that you couldn’t save her from!" Boiling, Archibald could easily just snap his head forward and deliver a crushing head butt, sending the delirious man to the ground. But that would be what a lesser man would do. He kept his anger under control and pushed his way past Rear Admiral Daniel Davis, and opened the driver’s door of the Hummer H2. He looked back at Davis, who had a mocking smile across his face as if he had won some great naval battle. "Just because I don’t have stars on my shoulder, or a badge on my arm doesn’t mean I have no honor, sir. And when you come to understand what honor means to me, then we’ll talk. But until then, stay the hell away from me, my son, and my home." Archibald slammed the door closed and started the Hummer. He gently backed out of the driveway, and drove towards the hospital where he had to somehow find the words to Heather that he wouldn’t be seeing her in Heaven. Till death do us part. But there was one thing he was sure of, and that was he’d be seeing Daniel Davis in Hell. * * * * * The drive to the hospital had let Archibald’s nerves unwind, and his emotions cool. The tears falling from Heather’s face when he told her that he knew she cried herself to sleep each evening washed away any of the anger that had managed to stay with him up until that moment. "I’m so sorry I hid it from you, Archieball…I just couldn’t bear to put you through it." He sighed and leaned against the small railing on the side of the hospital bed, "I know. But I just want you to know that it’s not your fault, none of it." "Yes it is. All of it. Our future is ruined because of me…why on earth would you want to marry a mummy like myself?" Heather exclaimed, lifting up her bandaged arms and motioning towards her midsection and face. Her voice was quiet and suffocated. Archibald leaned in and smiled, kissing Heather on the lips. He then bent down and picked up Aaron in his small car seat and dangled him in the air, "Because he needs his mummy. I need his mummy." Heather let out a stifled laugh, sniffling between breaths. The tears continued to fall, undoubtedly stinging her wounds on her face beneath the layer of bandages, "Smartass." "Now, now…we don’t want to teach Aaron curse words just yet. Let him discover girls first." In the corner, Wentworth Matthews stood with his hands in his pockets and smiled. While life in Heaven was truly bliss, it was these little moments that he missed. Heather spoke softly once more, "I’m scared." "There’s nothing to be afraid of, dear. You’re practically out of the woods." "But I can’t. It’s just an overwhelming fear of you leaving me, and Aaron growing up and never inviting his friends over because of what I’ll look like. He’ll be ashamed of his own mother!" her voice quickly started to choke up. Archibald sat back in his chair, a smile from ear to ear, and said nothing. He let her continue. "And it’s just this fear that will always be with me, because these wounds will always be with me! No matter how many grafts I get, I’ll always look in the mirror and never see a reflection of who I am, but of what I’ve become. I’m afraid, Archie, and your smile isn’t helping…" "I’m only smiling because my Dad once helped me conquer my fears. Did you know that I was afraid of the dark during my childhood? Every night for two weeks straight, Dad would tuck me into bed each night and tell me that there was nothing to be afraid of. That everything was just in my own head." "That there is nothing to fear, but fear itself." Heather said, completing the thought. "Exactly!" Archibald exclaimed, and continued on with his tale, "But anyway, Dad would tuck me in and leave the room. On the first night, he would leave the door wide open with the hallway light on. Then each successive night, he would gradually close the door, maybe only a few inches, but by the end of the two weeks I had learned to slowly understand that my bedroom stayed the same each and every night. It just got a little bit darker, but everything stayed exactly where it was, and when I woke up each morning…there were no monsters. No goblins. Just sunshine and scrambled eggs." Heather sighed, trying to imagine what she’d have to go through in order to grow accustomed to a body that fire destroyed. Skin forever waxy. Fingers forever without the sensation of touch. From the corner of the hospital room, the ghost of Archibald Wentworth Matthews perked up during the story and took a step forward. By the time the tale was finished, he had matched Archibald’s smile and added an anecdote of his own, "You know something, your Dad really did love those scrambled eggs. Wouldn’t shut up about them while we were over in ‘Nam. Always complaining about how he missed his scrambled eggs." Heather perked up slightly, intrigued, "You knew Archie’s Dad?" Wentworth took off his bowler hat and bowed slightly in Heather’s general direction, "I think you already recognize me, Ms. Davis, I’m just sure that you’re just unable to explain from where you recognize me." Beneath the bandages, Heather blinked a few times. With full confidence, she replied, "I’ve seen you everyday in the park at the center of town. A statue made of bronze, in your honor." Heather’s fiancé turned back at her in amazement, "Aren’t you curious as to how you’re talking to someone who has been dead for over three decades?" "I think someone slipped me some morphine." Heather watched as the man in the faded tweed coat took a step forward and placed his hand on her foot, and introduced himself, "My name is Archibald Wentworth Matthews, pleasure to meet you. We have a lot to talk about." * * * * * As the great battle waged on between the realms of Heaven and Hell, God sat high atop the battlefield and stroked his bushy white beard. He couldn’t remember who he was while he lived on Earth as mortal, and considering his current position, he was sure that at some point during his own life he had been visited by one of his predecessor’s angels. The circle of life. What goes around comes around. When Archibald MacGregor had entered the church and called out his name, God had listened. He had sent Archibald Matthews back to Earth to help give assurance and guidance to the poor boy. The soul of Archibald MacGregor was indeed lost to the realm of Hell, but the pressing matter was the fact that the two other souls in his life were still on a path to Heaven. Heather Davis, for all her pain and suffering, would be a welcome asset when her time eventually arrives. But the boy, the boy was another matter. God was aware of the marking across the back of Aaron Christopher MacGregor, and what it meant in the grand scheme of things. How it foretold his own possible decent into Hell, and subsequent ascension to the throne of the Devil. What gave hope to the Great Lord, however, was the fact that the mark on the MacGregor child and his troubled bloodline fulfilled a prophetic poem in Heaven as well. * * * * * For the next two hours, Wentworth Matthews explained how he stood before them, what Archibald meant to both Heaven and Hell, and ultimately the much-desired fate of their child. Occasionally as Aaron’s name was mentioned, his eyes would dart around the room seemingly aware of the conversation turning towards himself, or perhaps he had simply liked the hand gestures of both the angelic Archibald, or the man he would one day call "Daddy". Heather didn’t say much during the two hours, casually nodding and soaking up everything she was being told. To Archibald’s amazement, she simply accepted what she was being told as the truth, never questioning a single statement, even when it centered on her fiancé’s eventual placement in Hell. It reached the point where Archibald actually grew uncomfortable with her acceptance of the story, and demanded that Wentworth prove that he was not of this life, but of the next. "Come on, just do something. I know you’ve got some parlor tricks up your sleeve." "Sweetie, I believe him. He doesn’t need to do anything. I don’t know why I believe him, but I do." She replied in a voice momentarily scratchy from such a long period without speaking. Archibald Wentworth Matthews raised a hand in a motion to silence both of them, and began to walk around the hospital room, scouring the floor and windows for something. After a minute of searching, he bent down and picked up a tiny object, placing it in the palm of his hand. He quickly walked over to the hospital bed, his hand outstretched. Laying belly up in the center of his palm, legs folded in a death curl, was a dead fruit fly. "I told you that Jell-O wasn’t good for you, Archieball." Heather said with a smile. Wentworth smiled, "Sometimes, with the ability of Free Will imposed upon all of you, souls are taken from this world unjustly. Before their time. While it is rare for us to interject into the matters of life and death, God has given us the ability to grant life where it might have been unjustly taken." Archibald blinked in confusion, the gears in his mind constantly shifting, "Wait, wait, wait. So you can bring things back from the dead? Isn’t that…" "Wrong?" Wentworth asked. "No, not the word I was looking for. I was thinking more along the lines of revealing." Heather’s gaze bounced between the Archibald’s in a state of confusion. "You mean because a soul might pass into Heaven, that if we brought that soul back, they might return with absolute knowledge of the afterlife?" "Something like that, yeah." "It’s impossible. What you get is the typical ‘white light’ that so many people see as they experience near-death, or in our case, a return to life. There are some boundaries that can’t be crossed, and that is one of them. The memories of the after-life don’t pass back here, to this realm." Without touching the fly or even moving his hand an inch, the fly’s legs started to twitch, and then it’s wings fluttered gently until it turned itself over, and quickly flew away from Wentworth’s palm. David Blaine, eat your heart out. Both Heather and Archibald stared in awe as they followed the fly around the room until it landed on the windowsill from which the angel had picked it up. From beneath her mask of bandages, Heather smiled. It was akin to watching a mother give birth to her child, a life being born. But this time, it wasn’t birth, but a return to life. Archibald stared at the fly with wide-eyes and an idea he knew Wentworth would absolutely oppose. Because of this amazing ability of Wentworth’s, he had possibly found a way to save his son. * * * * * Archibald MacGregor poured the final bag of ice into the bathtub, and started to run the water. He checked the temperature as it poured out of the faucet, making sure it was as cold as he could get it. He had stopped at a local grocery store and picked up six bags after he dropped Aaron off at Heather’s sister’s house. "This is absurd. There is no way I’m going to let you go through with this!" Wentworth said with a voice drowning in a growing disbelief. "Put it this way, if you leave and go back to Heaven and don’t bring me back…I’ll be gone for good. I’m doing this with you here to watch over me and make sure I come back, or I’m doing this without you and just praying that someone finds my body in time to resuscitate it." With the tub filled three quarters of the way with water and the rest with bags of ice, Archibald MacGregor started to strip off his clothes. He stared at the water with eyes that weren’t filled with anything resembling fear, but rather, determination. "You do realize that you won’t know who you are, or where you are once you arrive there. You’ll wake up in a state of confusion, and more than likely be immediately dragged out to the battle lines with only two things certain: a fear of the Devil, Typherion, and the fear of what happens when you don’t do His bidding." "Weren’t you listening earlier when I told the story to Heather?" "You can’t conquer this fear, kid. It becomes a part of you, involuntary and inescapable. You can’t think about turning it off, or trying to stand up to it." "I want him to know I’m there." Archibald said, still staring wide-eyed at the bathtub filled with freezing water. "He’ll know." "I want him to think that he’s gotten a big piece of the puzzle, because how you describe it, I’m a key piece in the war you’re fighting against him. And then I want you to yank it away from him. Let him taste what victory could be like, and then rip it out of his grasp." Slowly, Archibald stepped into the bathtub. Gently lowering himself in, exhaling sharply as the freezing water drove it’s chilling daggers into every pore of his skin, Archibald leaned back in the tub and let the water come up to his chin. He waited a minute, the color quickly starting to leave his face. He then reached out beside the bathtub and picked up the two bowling balls he had tied together with a bath towel. Placing them in his lap, he looked back up at the astonished Archibald Wentworth Matthews and smiled. "You really are fearless, kid. Stupid, but fearless." "It’s time this asshole got a taste of his own medicine. Leave me under for 10 minutes, then pull me out and pull me back. I want that horned asshole to know what it feels like to have something you cherish yanked away from you in a second." At first, the angel shook his in disbelief, but quickly found himself nodding along in agreement. "If you see him, tell your Dad I said hello." Archibald MacGregor nodded, and then sank backwards underneath the freezing water. Laying at the bottom of the tub, all he could see were the ghostly pale bellies of the ice floating on the surface of the tub, with the ceiling of the bathroom occasionally peeking through in marbled and distorted shapes that reminded him of the tall mirrors he would find in a funhouse. By the time he had let all of the air out of his lungs, the water was no longer cold. The bubbles slowly floated to the surface, once again parting the ice just enough to see a bent and distorted figure standing above the bathtub, glancing down. Archibald MacGregor pulled the bowling balls up from his waist and placed one on each side of his head, with the wrapped towel across his neck. If for any reason he should convulse or try to reach for the surface as the grips of death clawed at him, the balls should act as 16 pound anchors to keep him at the bottom. By the time the bowling balls were in place, his fingertips began to tingle and his arms felt extremely heavy. Archibald tried to force more air out of his lungs, but nothing would escape. Slowly, a fire started to burn in his mouth and throat much like a shot of whisky would as it made its trek to his stomach. A moment later, a sudden and icy rush extinguished the fire in his throat. The ice seemed to float above him and now it felt like it was floating throughout him. The two bowling balls soon carried the same chill as the water, and Archibald could feel each one of them pressing against the sides of his head. The bowling balls. They were his fathers. Christopher MacGregor loved to bowl in his years before the depression took over, and he put a bullet through his skull. The freezing chill was now completely gone, except for the two bowling balls. One ball was his, the other belonged to his Dad. Dad’s. Dad. "Dad!" Archibald yelled. "We’ve been through this for almost two weeks, kiddo. Last night was no different than the night before, and the night before was no different than the one that came before that one. The room just got a little bit darker each nigh…" Christopher MacGregor had his hand on the doorknob and was slowly pulling the door closed behind him as he left Archibald’s bedroom. He had a warm smile that immediately put the soul at ease, and this time was no different. "I love you, Dad." Archibald exclaimed, his nerves unwinding by the second. "I love you too, bud. And I promise you that you’ll be fine in the morning." Archibald smiled and just lay there, watching as his father eased the door shut. The light from the hallway had once poured in through the open doorway, but was fading faster than the setting sun, and soon became a mere sliver separating the door from it’s frame. He watched his father take one final peek into the bedroom through the crack in the door with the warm, gracious smile, before finally pulling it completely shut. And all was dark. * * * * *
