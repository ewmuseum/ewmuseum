Losing Hope Pt. I: Unknown Caller

16 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-16 "Get the hell outta my house!" Jeff barked. "You don’t have to tell me twice, you piece of shit!" Brian Arson shouted back at his cousin, who just moments ago battled him in an epic shouting match. The end result was Brian being tossed out of the house, turning a month long visit into a one week scream-a-thon. And although Jeff’s wife had attempted to persuade him into letting Brian stay awhile longer, Jeff had overruled her and ordered Brian to take all his stuff and leave immediately or else he’d call the police. Now Brian was outside his brother’s house in Connecticut on a cold December day with just his belongings and no means of transportation to get him to a hotel or airport. The mid-day sun had melted much of the snow that had fallen during the past weekend but a half a foot still covered the ground. Brian walked straight across the front yard, leaving six-inch deep prints behind him. He wasn’t too happy about the current situation, but odds are, he would have ended up leaving the house on his own accord eventually. Well, this is excellent, Brian thought to himself as he stepped onto the street, his boots covered in snow. His cousin’s house was just off the town’s main road and the downtown area was just a short distance to the north. Albeit was a small town, he believed he could find a small inn to stay the night. A piercing wind penetrated Brian’s outer layer of clothing, and he bundled up his black overcoat and began his trek north into town. He had taken but a few steps when the pocket of his jacket began to buzz furiously. "Now what?" Brian said to himself. He thought maybe his cousin had changed his mind and was calling him to apologize, but when he pulled the cellular out of his jacket pocket the number read ‘Unknown.’ Well that’s odd. Brian flicked open the phone. "Hello?" "Brian Arson." The voice on the other end was very deep and Brian recognized some sort of Eastern European accent. "Yes? And who the fuck is this?" Brian replied forcefully, knowing by the tone of the man’s voice that the conversation wouldn’t be leading to anything legal. Brian’s crass response was something he’d developed over the years in order to intimidate opposition in and outside of the ring. Unfortunately, this mystery caller seemed to be unmoved by the harshness of Brian’s voice. "Please, spare me any bullshit, I don’t have the time. My name is not important at the moment, but you will learn it soon enough. You are in Connecticut; I know this. Tonight, you will be in Columbus, Ohio and in room 657 of the Hyatt Regency downtown, no later than eleven PM." "Is that so?" Brian replied smugly, yet the man seemed to instill a fear inside him. "Well, I suppose this is where I say that I’m too busy to be pestered by a crazy foreigner over the telephone. But of course, I know how these things work, so what is it you have to keep me from denying your request?" "You will find out soon enough, but I suggest you come as this could be your last week of freedom should you choose to stay where you are now. The flights are available; see you soon." Before anymore could be said, the ‘Call Ended’ shone brightly on Brian’s phone. He flicked it shut and jammed it into his pocket in a fit of rage. "Well, shit!" Brian said, accenting the obscenity and disturbing some cardinals in a nearby tree. According to his phone, it was closing in on noon so he’d have to get into town as soon as possible and call a taxi to take him to the airport. This situation put Brian in a serious dilemma. He wanted to shrug the whole thing off as a bogus call, but the tone in the man’s voice frightened him and Brian’s instinct told him to follow orders. And so he continued his march north, hoping he would be able to catch a flight on time. *********** Luckily for Brian the Hartford taxi system was much more efficient than those back home in California, and he had managed to make it to the airport by around one-thirty. Not only that but he was able to buy a ticket for a three-thirty flight, meaning he’d be in Columbus at around seven or so. Meanwhile he’d have to wait in the airport for about two hours before takeoff so he decided to pass through security and get a bite to eat. As Brian approached the security checkpoint he couldn’t help but groan at the long line waiting to leave for the weekend. They had six metal detectors and scanners but of course only two were open just to tempt someone to go running through. These twelve hours shifts could not be any more monotonous and he was sure the guards would love it if they had a chance to fire off a round or two at any uncooperative patron. Finally, it was Brian’s turn to pass through the metal detectors and he placed all his belongings on the conveyor belt to head through the scanner. On the other side a skinny redhead was about to coax him through, but stopped him just before he passed under the detector. "Sir, if you take your shoes off, you won’t have to be checked on this side," she said with a plastic smile, trying to be as polite as possible. "I don’t want to take my fucking shoes off!" Brian snapped before he could catch himself. "Now, just calm down sir, we can’t have you raising your voice." "I am calm, god damnit! The shoes stay on!" "That’s fine, sir. I’m just going to have you step through the gate and you can sit down over there and they will conduct a further check to ensure you have a safe flight." She pointed toward a chair on the other side where a large black man was standing, rummaging through another person’s carry-on. Brian grabbed his briefcase from the conveyor belt and walked over to the big man a bit perturbed. The man reached down and grabbed Brian’s carry-on out of his hands and put it up on the counter and began shifting through it as if he were trying to find a case of pudding in a lunch box. Brian could tell he had had much experience. "Sir, I’m going to have you just take your shoes off there for me," the large man said with straining exhales as if going through Brian’s luggage had taken the wind out of him. Brian sighed and reluctantly obeyed the man’s demand. The guy continued his search for a WMD roughly the size of an M&M when suddenly his eyes opened wide as he laid his hand upon what had to be something significant. "Oh my God… sir, what the hell is THIS!?" In his right hand, he pulled out what appeared to be a shiny blue writing utensil of some sort. "Um, a pen?" Brian replied rather confused. The big man hastily reached down for his radio. "This is gate Alpha-7, CODE 13 BLUE! I repeat, CODE 13 BLUE!" Seconds later two men in white-collared shirts, sporting aviators, were sprinting around the corner of the terminal, guns snapped into their belts. They skidded to a halt as they reached the table that Brian’s bag was perched upon. After taking one look at the object in question, the first man turned toward Brian with a look of concern. "Where the hell did you get this!" "Um…." Brian began, getting a closer look at the particular pen they had begun dismantling. "I believe that one I received from a business associate back in California." "You mean there’s MORE?" The security guard looked extremely distraught at the thought of more these so-called pens. Brian still wasn’t sure what exactly the problem was. "Well, yeah, but I think that’s the only one I brought with me." "Sir!" The other guard chimed in, acknowledging his partner. In his hand he held up the outer casing of the pen, and he pointed to a set of symbols. "I’m not quite sure what to make of these, as my Arabic is a bit patchy, but I believe they reference a certain satanic Middle Eastern cult." He paused a moment as everyone looked at him in disbelief and then simply shrugged. "I read about it… in a book… Al-Quaeda For Dummies." "Those are my initials, you idiot." Brian began to stand up from the chair but the first guard stepped in front of him. "Sir, I need you to remain seated, this is no laughing matter!" Brian slouched back down into the chair and let out a frustrated sigh. *********** Thirty minutes later and Brian was still seated in the same chair. His pen had been completely disassembled and lay in pieces on top of the table. The three men from before as well as a couple others that had come a little later were speaking quietly to one another in a small huddle. Finally, the man in the white-collared shirt that had first approached Brian scooped up the pen parts from the table and walked over to him. "Er… we’re sorry about that, sir, here you go." The man dropped the pen pieces into Brian’s hand. "You can never be too cautious around the airports these days. You let someone onto a plane with a funny looking pen and then the next thing you know they’ll be sneaking ICBMs the size of pocket knives into their back pockets." "What? That doesn’t even make sense," Brian said incredulously at the moron of a man that stood in front of him. "Oh, you don’t believe me? You just wait, bucko, you’ll see!" "Wouldn’t that defeat the purpose of an ICBM?" "Well, actually… to be honest… I don’t even know what an ICBM is, I just heard about ‘em on CNN and then saw ‘em shooting things." "You guys are fucking idiots, give me my God damn bags!" Brian stood up and snatched his briefcase from the large black man’s paws. "Honestly, you guys really need to find something productive to do, or better yet, get a different jobs, or better yet, I don’t know… hang yourselves…" Without looking back, Brian stomped off, slinging his bag around his shoulder and headed toward a Max & Erma’s. He was so annoyed that he had nearly forgotten why he was going to Columbus in the first place, let alone the fact that he had a second round wrestling match coming up. He wondered how someone faced with so many distractions and petty annoyances had not gone completely insane by now. The hostess at the restaurant sat him in a matter of minutes and when the server came over he ordered immediately so as to not be late for his flight. A nice juicy burger sounded fantastic, with a side of onion rings. Two sides of rings make it. He hadn’t eaten all day and it was nearly two-thirty. The server was back about fifteen minutes later with his food, not the fastest he’d ever received, but he didn’t care so long as the food hadn’t been confiscated by airport security. Before he had even reached down to grab the burger, he saw the little person approaching out of the corner of his eye. A kid, no older than twelve, with a pencil and paper in either hand about to disturb his meal before he’d even started. "Mister! Mister! Can I have—" "Fuck off, kid!" Without even lifting his head, Brian pointed back toward the direction from whence the boy came. The kid looked incredibly frightened and went scurrying back to his father several tables away. The man looked over at Brian in burning anger. Brian saw this and simply popped up another finger for the daddy. All around, people were looking over at Brian with disgusted looks upon their faces. An older plump lady to Brian’s left shook her head condescendingly. "Oh, lady, go to hell. You eat anymore of that damn burger and you’re gonna turn into a fucking cow!" The woman was taken aback, but Brian was finished with her and turned back to his plate to try and enjoy his meal. He took a few bites of the cooling burger and threw it down onto his plate, realizing he’d lost his appetite. After forcing a few of the onion rings into his mouth, he stood up and slammed down a twenty onto the table. "Oh, for fuck’s sake, what’s a man gotta do to get some peace and quiet around here, let alone a decent meal! Fuck this state!" With that said, he grabbed his bag and scrambled off toward his gate, hoping his flight would provide him with at least an hour and a half of serenity. *********** By the time Brian boarded the plane and it had taken off toward Ohio, he had become completely drained. It was nearing four o’clock now and the sun had nearly set on the East Coast. Slumped in his seat, he had been lucky enough to get a window seat without anyone directly beside him. He gazed blankly through the glass at the grayish clouds that lay beneath him, the glowing red orb of the sun gradually fading away. Too much excitement had troubled the thirty-year old. When he entered the ring last week, he had won a match he expected to win, yet the thrill of victory didn’t seem to last but a brief moment and he wasn’t even sure who he’d been lucky enough to draw in the second round. Usually his good friend and retired wrestler, Jon Storm, would call to congratulate him and even advise him on any future opposition, but he had not received a phone call of any sort, from anyone. Until today of course. Brian was feeling alone, more so than ever before. He had no federation to turn to for support, no close friends around to encourage him, and as far as family goes, they had been out of the picture for a long time. Is this why I’m so angry? What’s happening to me? Brian’s thoughts quickly wandered from wrestling to his upcoming meeting. He wasn’t quite sure what to expect, but an uneasy feeling gnawed away at the back of his head like a dog into the bone. This could be your last week of freedom, the man had said. Impossible, Brian thought. This man was obviously calling his bluff in order to make a quick buck. The whole reason he had gone to Connecticut was to avoid all of this, and how the man knew of his whereabouts he had no idea, but there was definitely a missing piece to the puzzle. "Drink, sir?" A flight attendant popped into the row, jolting Brian up out of his seat and erasing all thoughts he had been attempting to gather. "Jesus Christ, lady!" "I’m sorry, sir, didn’t mean to scare you. Would you like a drink?" Brian groaned, will they ever go away? He sat up straight and turned to the stewardess, trying his hardest to keep his cool. I don’t suppose you have any glasses of go-the-hell-away, do ya?, he thought to himself. "Just give me some vodka." The flight attendant pulled out a miniature Sky Blue bottle and poured it into a small cup full of ice and then handed them to Brian. He took the cup and downed it in a gulp and then finished off what was left in the bottle. Ten minutes later he was fast asleep…
