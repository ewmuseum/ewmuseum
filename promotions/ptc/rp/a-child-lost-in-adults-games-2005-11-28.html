A Child Lost In Adult’s Games

28 November 2005

Date: 2005-11-28 Amy Silveira: A Child Lost In Adult’s Games The GTT, or Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, as it was formerly called. An event which gains the attention of wrestling fans across the globe, pitting the finest athletes in the sport against each other in a closely-fought spectacle of stamina, speed and general wrestling excellence. The fact that GTT5 has arrived and has already begun will be no surprise to you, then, with the event expected to take over the wrestling presses attention for the next few months. Only 128 of no doubt thousands of applicants made it into the competition, with a lot of the names well-qualified and celebrated. The likes of Seymour Almasy, Angelo Deville, Hoyt Williams and Rich Rollins will be familiar to all wrestling fans, and whether you like the guys or not, you have to respect their ability once they step into the squared circle. Of course there are weaker names in there, but there’s one which stands out like an extremely sore thumb – the young and inexperienced Amy Silveira. Silveira only turned eighteen last April, and has only five professional matches to her name. She weighs in at 116lbs, stands at a very average height of roughly 5’7", and if she had been born in the USA would still be completing her education. Despite this, she makes up part of a field claimed to be the ‘cream of the crop’ – Seems that someone misunderstood just what that term means. The facts are, really, like this. I’ve seen Silveira wrestle, and I won’t deny that she’s a great gymnast, and that she’s clearly trained in the ring. However, this doesn’t take away from the fact that she’s a young, pretty girl who would look better-placed as a valet to a Tim Shipley or Emilio Rage, than as a girl fighting men who are twice her size and weight. The girl doesn’t seem to want to be in the ring, either – her entire character is seemingly only existent because of her father, Hugo Silveira, who boldly and proudly marches her into battle every time she fights. Wrestling experts would know the man as a small-time manager back in the early-1990s, with too few achievements to even bother noting. Without wanting to make any accusations or cause controversy, I just get the sense that he is the only person that wants her in the ring. Her nervousness going into matches is no act – She’s reasonably talented, yes, but she’s clearly not comfortable when she hits her trademark corkscrew moonsault, or when she shows off her martial arts skills. I’m by no means associated with the Silveira’s, but I do feel that Amy should be in school, having wild parties and enjoying herself, instead of doing something which she seems to not want to be doing at all. If I am correct, then the fact she has a prestigious spot in such a major tournament is really not fair to the more experienced and committed competitors out there on the wayside. Moving on from the reasoning of her presence, though – Amy has a tough job ahead of her. She’s in the third bracket, and drew Headstrong Wrestling’s Jason Warr as a first round opponent. Warr is fairly new to HSW, but weighs in at almost twice her weight, and possesses a brawling style which she won’t feel too comfortable going up against. She goes into the match the ultimate underdog. I have to admit, it’s great to see females getting the opportunity to show that they can conquer the world. Successful divas such as Karina Wolfenden, Aimz and Teresa Tomas (incidentally, Amy’s potential second round opponent) have opened the door to girls like Amy. However, the girls going through the door should want to be doing so – whether Amy wants to make the top of the wrestling world, is debatable. Amy Silveira – She’s the ultimate underdog in a tournament where the big boys play, and she’s just a little girl. If she’s going to do anything in GTT5, she needs to be able to play with the big boys, and unless she finds some momentum quickly, I don’t think she’s ready.
