Death

9 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-09 La Guardia Airport Thirty eight million, three-hundred sixty eight thousand eight-hundred. Estimating the life expectancy of the average American male at seventy-three years, that’s how many minutes there are in Joe American’s life. Some of those minutes are good. Some of those minutes are bad. And some of those minutes inspire sheer helplessness, absolute terror, and an utter sense of shock. Sixty seconds have passed since Laura Almasy Winters, ex-wife of PTC legend Seymour Almasy, said five words that, combined, sent Seymour’s psyche into a death spiral. "Retire. And marry me again." He attempted to open his mouth to respond to her, to say something. Anything! But he couldn’t. Of all the things he expected her to say, this was nowhere NEAR the top of his list. In fact, he listed it rather near the bottom, right along with her offering to dress up as Quistis of her own free will. By the time Seymour snapped out of his virtual trance, she was tapping her foot impatiently, and looking at him with intense eyes. "Well," she asked, clearly expecting an answer. "How….what…you barely gave me any time to think!" he exclaimed, leaning against the cold door of the airport for support. She’d lost her mind. Yes, that was it. She’d gone insane, and in a moment of utter ridiculousness made her suggestion. Or he was dreaming! Another logical possibility. All of those dreams that he had about her while on the road had conspired against him again to create this one, one that was rapidly becoming a nightmare. "What’s there to think about," she asked. And by God, it actually sounded like she meant it. "I made you an offer. Accept it, or decline it. It’s simple." "Simple?!" Seymour looked back at his ex, mouth still dropped open. "There’s absolutely NOTHING simple about this! There are hundreds of things that play into this decision! People that I could hurt! A career where I’d be leaving my co-workers! A girlfriend--suddenly, he remembered that he did indeed HAVE one--, Laura! I’m not single! I’ve been going out with Tessa Windsor for the past three months! She took a step closer, and suddenly, Seymour was afraid. In that instant, he knew that his concerns didn’t matter. To her, at least, they were trivial in the face of love. Love that they had once shared. Love that they could share again. "It IS simple," she insisted, nodding her head slowly. "Accepting my offer means you’re willing to give all of that up. You’ve been a great wrestler, Seymour, but it’s killing you. I see how sad you are on TV in GCW. It’s not worth it anymore. As for Tessa…" She frowned before continuing. No matter how she put this, she was probably going to sound like a catty bitch. But given the circumstances, giving that she was pouring out her soul, she ceased caring. "Seymour, when was the last time you two slept together?" The Final Fantasy blushed two shades of crimson, before giving the honest response. "Um, never." "What about the last time you roleplayed with her?" Somehow, as she was speaking to him, he recalled one particularly forceful roleplay in which she’d had on a leather outfit. And a whip. "Never…" Exasperated, she glared pointedly at him, before asking the final question. "Have you ever even seen her without her clothes on?" "…no." "You’re not in grade school, Seymour," she replied, continuing to close the narrow distance between them even more. "Men and women who like each other don’t just hold hands. Passion is a healthy part of any REAL relationship. And, as I told you in the car on the way back from that slum, she REALLY doesn’t know what she’s missing out on…" Finally, there was no distance between them, as she pushed him firmly against the door, grinding her body into his. Seymour’s eyes went wide as he tried to push her away, tried to prevent himself from giving into the temptation that would be so very easy to lose himself in. "Don’t tell me people are watching, Seymour," she whispered, mouth dangerously close to his ear. "I want them to watch. I want everyone to know that you’re mine. Mine, Seymour. Not the property of some pink-wearing prude from Massachusetts." One of her hands cupped his chin gently, delicately, as she looked into his slightly-frightened eyes. "Mine to love," she continued, breath tickling his ear. "Mine to play with, whenever I want. Mine to be deliriously happy with." She pulled her mouth away from his ear, only to look him dead in the eyes once more. He knew what was coming now, what she was going to do. Laura was a beautiful, confident woman, who simply didn’t care about trivial matters like all of the people watching her, or the fact that she was about to do this to an attached man. "Mine to kiss," she whispered, to herself, before leaning forward and fusing her mouth to his. Before he could respond, her hands moved to his shoulders, pinning him against the glass door. Laura kissed Seymour brutally. Passionately, as if she would never get the chance to do so again. As if his decision hinged on that one kiss. And then, it was over. She pulled back, leaving Seymour gasping and fumbling for words. Looking at him, she smiled, and then turned around, beginning her walk to the parking garage. "You know, you’re right," she called back, over her shoulder. "It wouldn’t be fair for me to make you decide now. Meet me at our house, at five. Give me your decision then." By the time he could move again, his ex-wife and best friend was gone, disappearing in the labyrinthine passages of the garage. A quick glance over his own shoulder, back into the airport confirmed that the time was, indeed, one o’clock. "Four hours," he muttered, unable to believe what was happening to him. "Four hours to make the most important decision of my life." No one was left to help him. The person he usually went to for advice was Aerith herself. And the only other option he had was…dead? Cid In all of the hubbub, he’d nearly forgotten about his poor deceased grandfather. Other than Seymour’s ex-wife, Cid Wilson was the one person in the world who had aided the Final Fantasy more than anyone. Time was ticking. It was a commodity that he didn’t have nearly enough of. But even with Aerith’s ultimatum issued, Seymour couldn’t forget about his grandfather. Couldn’t forget about the one family member left who actually cared about him. Practically sprinting over to the first taxicab he saw, Seymour frantically began negotiating the details of his next trip. While the past few days had been nothing but travel, it seemed, there were several more destinations before his journey would be over. There was really only one place that he could go, now. Back to Long Island, to the funeral home where his grandfather lay in state. Seymour could pay his final respects to a man he respected and loved above all others. But there was another reason for his journey. One that would make no sense to most. Yet, to the open-minded Seymour, it was a perfectly plausible possibility. Perhaps, seeing his grandfather would spark something. Perhaps, Cid Wilson could advise his grandson from the grave. ------------------- Conway Funeral Home Long Island, NY A yellow taxicab sped away, leaving its former passenger standing in front of a rather somber looking building. Seymour Almasy regarded it carefully, respectfully, understanding the significance of the place. "I can’t…believe he’s actually gone," Seymour whispered. The emotional roller coaster that he was being forced to endure was such that he had truly, actually managed to forget about the death of his grandfather. He reproached himself for that. How could he forget about Cid? How? Seymour looked at the sign on the front lawn of the house. Even through the weeds and plants growing on it, he could make out the words "CONWAY FUNERAL HOME", in black, proud letters. The Final Fantasy let his head hang low. He hadn’t been forced to endure the death of a loved one since his father, all those years ago. In that, he’d been fortunate. But now, it seemed that Death was getting its revenge. As his eyes scanned the overgrown lawn, Seymour noticed that a long strip of land was grassless. It led to the stairs, which, quite naturally, led to the front door. In that instant, he wondered how many people had made the walk that he was about to make. How many people had cried here, knowing they’d never see their loved one alive again? It was almost too much for Seymour to bear. He considered retreating, until he remembered the words of his grandfather. Cid Wilson had instilled perseverance in his grandchild. "When the going gets tough," Cid would say, echoing the ancient phrase, "the tough get going!" Really, it was sort of a silly phrase. But Cid lived by the spirit it embodied. It was that spirit that spurred Almasy on down that lonely stretch of earth. "Damn the torpedoes," he heard Cid say in the back of his mind, "full speed ahead!" Cid was full of famous quotes. Unlike most people, though, he had a knack for saying them at the proper time. Even as Seymour dreaded what he would soon see, the man himself was alive in the back of the Final Fantasy’s mind, spurring him onward, motivating him to do what must be done. The steps creaked beneath Seymour’s feet as he ascended them, one, two, and then finally the third, until he stood on the funeral home’s porch. Taking a deep breath, he knew that it was now or never. "I’m coming to see you now, Grandpa," he said, screwing up the last bit of his courage, and reaching for the doorknob. Slowly, he opened the door, peeking into the sterile environment of the funeral home. He felt hesitant, at first, but then remembered what his grandfather had taught him. Seymour entered the home with a slightly sick feeling in his stomach, one that was not lessened in the least bit when he turned to the woman standing nearby, and told her why he was here. "I’m here to see Mr. Cid Wilson," Seymour said, solemnly, trying to fight back the tears that came with the statement. Merely saying it was giving in to the reality of what had happened. Like he had learned so long ago with his father, life had no Phoenix Downs or Raise spells. Dead was dead. An inexorable state. "Come with me," the woman replied, leading Seymour into one of the rooms. There, lying on a table in a coffin, next to a pile of his personal effects, was Cid Wilson. "Shall I leave you alone for a moment, sir," she asked, looking at Seymour sympathetically. He gave a solitary nod of his head, and she left, heading back to the entryway of the home, and leaving Seymour with the man who had changed his life. "Hello, Grandpa. Sorry I didn’t get a chance to see you recently." Walking around to the other side of the casket, Seymour braced himself, and then pushed open the lid. The smell of chemicals was heavy in the air, and Seymour finally managed to bring himself to look down into the casket, and confirm the horrible truth. Somehow, though, in spite of the fact that he was now ghostly pale, very little had changed about him. Cid’s proud, strong face was undiminished, even in death. Leaning into the coffin, Seymour placed a kiss on his grandfather’s forehead. Now that he had gotten over the initial shock of seeing his grandfather motionless and cold, he could accept what had happened. "You lived well, didn’t you," he asked the corpse, pulling up a chair to sit by the casket. "Better than I ever could. More nobly. More heroically." Cid Wilson had been a veteran of World War II, one of the tens of thousands of men to invade Normandy on D-Day. He’d been wounded then, and received the Purple Heart, an award he always carried with him proudly. As a matter of fact, there it was, shining in the pile of clothing and other items that had been taken here with his body. "Wonder what they brought here," Seymour wondered aloud, standing to search through the pile. There was a shirt, a pair of pants, the aforementioned Purple Heart…and a piece of paper. With Seymour’s name written on it. "Interesting," he whispered, turning the paper over to read what was written on it. He expected a last will and testament. What he got was something else entirely. Seymour, By the time you read this, I will likely either be in ill health, or deceased. It’s been a long time since you and I have had a chance to talk, so I wanted to tell you everything that I’ve meant to say for a long time now. I admire you more than you can possibly imagine. You came out of a broken, abusive home to become a success on a worldwide scale, and even so, you have never stopped being the sweet, caring child that used to come over for visits when he was three. It is truly a shame that you never had a chance to truly forge a relationship with your father. He was a wonderful, hard-working man, with whom you share many qualities. For as long as I have known you, I have asked much of you. I have asked you to be yourself, no matter how tempting it is to give in to the temptations of this world. I have asked you to excel in school, and in your chosen profession. I have no right to ask more of you, yet I feel that I must. For that, I am sorry. My final request to you, Seymour, is to journey to the land of Nalmh, to retrieve the Golden Sword of Azathar from the Dragon of Durmok. Hah hah, just kidding. You always did love it when I made up adventures for you when you were little. My true final request is for you to be happy. You have done so much to help others, that I worry about you yourself. As wonderful and noble of a trait as it is to care for others, you must also look out for yourself. Live each day as if it is your last. Leave no regrets. Goodbye, Seymour. I’ll be watching you. Love, Cid Wilson Wordlessly, Seymour put the piece of paper down. It was good advice, he knew. It would be even better advice if he actually knew what it was that would make him happy. He loved Laura, that was undeniable. But he also loved Tessa. And he also loved to compete. All were alluring in their own ways. Laura’s love was pure and true. He didn’t doubt for one second that she wanted him back, wanted to take him to bed with her and recons mate their union in the most thrilling of ways. And yet, in spite of her often sexual nature, she could just as easily be soothing and comforting and loyal. Tessa, on the other hand, was new to him. She seemed to be just as pure in her love as Laura was, but he couldn’t tell for sure. Still, the time he had spent with her had been wonderful. Windsor was capable of cheering him up with a kind word when he seemed at his most down. But then, there was wrestling. Wrestling couldn’t hug him. It couldn’t kiss him. It couldn’t be there to cuddle with him at night. The love he felt for his sport was a different kind of love. Seymour truly enjoyed what he did, for the most part. Stepping in the ring to him was a true privilege, the chance to pit his skills against the best in the world a rush like none other. The sport had also played an equal role in saving him from the horrors of his childhood. Watching wrestling had planted a seed in young Jason Wilson’s head that would one day blossom. When he was sitting in his dorm room, despairing at the prospect of having to take a desk job, wrestling gave him a way out. It let him do something different. Indeed, something he loved. Nodding to the casket, Seymour gently closed the lid. He didn’t have much time left, he figured. Laura was waiting for his decision. Finally, he had managed to make one. The Final Fantasy fully expected that it would change a half-dozen times on the taxi ride over to her house, but for now, he had made a decision. He just hoped that he wouldn’t eventually regret it. ------------------------------------------------------------------ Residence of Seymour and Laura 4:55 PM To his surprise, by the time the taxi had made it to his old house, he wasn’t as nervous as he’d thought he would be at this, the moment of truth. No, he had made up his mind. That gave him the courage he needed to drag his exhausted, sleepless body from the relative warmth of the taxi, and out into the New York cold. Seymour immediately scanned the immediate area for her. Something told him that she wouldn’t be simply waiting for him in the house. And indeed, she wasn’t. A few hundred feet behind the home, she stood atop a sandy rock outcropping, looking out into Long Island Sound. Even from a distance, he could tell what she was wearing. "That’s…the dress she wore…when…we…" The statement didn’t need to be finished. Laura was wearing the very dress she’d worn the day in 2003 that Seymour and she had been wed in holy matrimony. Her beautiful hair was done up, held together by something that glistened in the waning sunlight. She was a vision. A dream. His every fantasy about what a woman should be, wrapped up in one splendid human being. Slowly, he approached, feet leaving prints in the sand, evidence of where he was going. Evidence of the momentousness of this very day. As he walked, a gust of wind picked up, blowing his hair back. His silvery mane flew behind him as he continued his ascent up the outcropping. Still, she did not turn to meet him. She now gazed into the sun, as if waiting for something…waiting, waiting, still waiting. For five o’clock on the dot. Slowly, painfully slowly, the sun moved into the exact position that it was fated to be in at this time, and she turned around. She saw Seymour. She smiled. "Right on time," Laura Marie Winters whispered, to herself. "Just like I knew he’d be." Breath left him at the sight of her, just as beautiful as she was the day they were wed. She looked at him with shining eyes, waiting for him to say the words she hoped would come. "Was it difficult," she asked, observing him for any sign of reaction, any hint as to his disposition. "Extremely," he replied, looking back at her. God, with the sun setting behind her like that, she looked positively angelic… "I’m sorry for doing this to you," she said, sincerely. "I just couldn’t wait anymore. I couldn’t stand not being with you. Every time I turned on a UWF show and saw you with Tessa…or when you kissed Shadow as Emoticon…or when you pursued Dawn McFarlane…it made me die a little inside, Seymour. I need you…" The Final Fantasy stepped forward, and rested a hand on her shoulder. She was so lively, so warm, a stark contrast to how his grandfather had been at the funeral home. "There were so many things to take into account. So many issues, so many concerns. You know I love you, Laura, right?" "Always," she replied, nodding. "Since you were six years old," she added, blushing a little bit. He had been so cute when he was little, so shy, and yet so very nice once she actually got to know him. "Always," he repeated. "I owe you a debt I can never repay. You’ve saved my life on multiple occasions. You accompanied me to the ring. You helped me make my way through a very unfair world." "And I will keep helping you, to the best of my abilities. I love you. I thought I’d be okay without you when we broke up…but I’m not. No one makes me feel like you do, Seymour. No one." Laura dashed forward then, throwing both of her arms around her ex-husband, clinging to him tightly. It was like a fairy tale, almost. She had her Seymour back, and this time, she would not be so foolish as to let him go. "Come on, Seymour," she breathed, almost delirious in her happiness, "let’s go plan our wedding. A real one this time, with a wedding chapel, and guests, and a really big cake!" In that moment, that infinite moment, he wanted that more than anything else in the world. More than the PTC Global Championship. More than winning GTT5. More even than life itself. "…I can’t, Laura." But the harsh realities of this would-be fairy tale ending were simple. "Retire. And marry me again." As much as he wanted it to be an option, it wasn’t one. "Wh-what do you mean, Seymour," she asked, stunned, pulling away from him to look into eyes that were already clouded over with tears. "I just can’t," he sobbed, body quivering, trembling with the effort of having to say words. "I want to. GOD I want to! But I can’t!" "Why not," she sobbed back, the tears quickly overcoming her as well, and at an even more rapid rate. "I thought…we were going to be happy…again." Tears fell from his eyes, staining the sand below. He hated to hurt her, especially after she had bared her soul to him, shown him that her silly one night stand with some guy in a bar meant absolutely nothing. Shown him that it was *he*, Seymour, who meant everything. But what was done, was done. "My career," he managed, wiping tears from his face. "People look up to me. No matter where I go, no matter how down I am, or how upset I am, the fans still care. Before…before you called me about Cid, I was at an autograph…signing in Tokyo. Thousands of people came…I can’t disappoint all of them. I…just can’t." Both were sobbing freely and unashamedly now, Seymour gasping desperately for air, to try and finish speaking. "And Tessa," he choked, "prude or not…she’s suffered with bad boyfriends her whole life. She’s been left by every one of them. Mistreated…tricked…duped. How could I leave her with a straight face? How could I possibly hurt her again, after everything she’s been through?" He dropped to his knees. How could this have gone so wrong? The decision was simple, by all accounts. Take the fairytale ending, and run with it. "W-we were supposed to live…happily ever after," Laura whimpered, looking at Seymour in utter astonishment, utter sorrow. "I know," came his sad reply. "But even…even if I walked away…even if I was with you…" "You could never forget," she finished, knowing the words well. Yuna’s words to Tidus in Final Fantasy X. Telling the man she loved that she had to continue a journey that would end in death. Helplessly, he nodded. Slowly, she backed up further, moving closer and closer to the precipice. No, Seymour thought, in sudden agony, she wasn’t going to…going to… Suddenly, she stopped, and Seymour gave an audible sigh of relief. Until she pulled the pistol from a fold of her wedding dress. Until she leveled that pistol at her heart. "I’m sorry, Seymour. Goodbye." No. Not like this. God, please… No…. Seemingly frozen to the spot for the past few seconds, his body finally snapped into action, lunging forward for the weapon, even as he saw her finger reach for the trigger. He had to get there first. Fairy tales don’t end this way, right? "LAURA! STOP! PLEASE! I’LL MARRY--" *BANG* Too late. "--YOU!" Her arm jerked violently from the recoil of the weapon, which delivered its payload home. One single bullet sped from the gun. One single bullet lodged itself in Laura Winters’ chest. The force of the impact sent the white sphere in her hair hurtling from her person. It bounced once, twice, three times on the rock, before falling over the edge into Long Island Sound. Her body fell to the sand, as Seymour looked about frantically, desperately for anyone in the area, anyone to help. His eyes hit upon a pair of beachgoers, and he screamed at the top of his lungs to them in helpless desperation. "PLEASE! SOMEONE CALL 911! PLEASE! PLEEEEEEEEEEEEAAAAASSEEEEEEEEE!!!!!" Miraculously, they heard him. One pulled out his cell phone. The other began a sprint to the site of the shooting. "Laura," Seymour whispered, dropping to his knees in the sand, looking for any signs of life. "LAURA, PLEASE ANSWER ME!" He fell silent, and then, there it was. Breath. Coming slowly. Hitched. But breathing nonetheless. "Laura!" He pulled her head up slightly, trying to make her comfortable. "Everything’s going to be okay," he told her, clinging to her desperately. "You’ll make it through this! We’ll get married. I’ll stop wrestling! Anything! Just please…please don’t…" She coughed then, blood coming up from her throat, and spilling over the front of her wedding dress. Somehow, even now, she managed to find her voice. "Cid…was…right. You…do think…of everyone…else…ahead of…yourself…" He didn’t know what to say. How was he supposed to have any idea? The love of his life had just shot herself. "I’m…sorry…" she whispered, voice faint. "I didn’t…know…how important…it was. Everything…I’m so sorry…for doing…this…to…you…" "Laura," he queried, still clutching to her. No answer. "Laura!" he shouted, still holding on. No answer. "LAURA!" he screamed, still listening for her angelic voice. No answer. Only shallow, shallow breathing. One minute passed. And then another. And then sirens. An ambulance. Help. ---------------------------------------------- One day later… "Shit," a PTC official said, backstage, mere moments before GTT5’s fifth round was about to begin. "We’ve got two no-shows tonight." The suited man was panicked. One of them he’d known about. O’Shea having to pull out was unfortunate, but Paddy had told him in advance. The other was an utter astonishment. Seymour Almasy didn’t miss matches. Especially big matches. Behind him, the door burst open. "Sorry I’m late," a tired, heartbroken voice said. "It’s been a bad week." The official turned. What he saw nearly made him cry. Standing there was Seymour Almasy, in street clothes. His hair was matted to his forehead. His eyes showed signs of fresh tears. He looked like he hadn’t slept in days. "What…what happened to you," the official asked, looking at him as if he had seen a ghost. "Laura shot herself," Seymour replied, choking back the tears. "I’ve been in the hospital with her ever since. I shouldn’t fucking be here right now, but she told me to go. That was the last thing she said to me in the hospital before they kicked me out to run tests on her." "Oh my God…" "Is it go time?" Wordlessly, the official nodded. Wordlessly, Seymour passed him, headed for the curtain. I made the wrong choice. I didn’t think Laura could do that. I didn’t know her love ran that deep. I didn’t even know she owned a gun. But she did. And now, all I can do for her is pray. When she makes it out of this, I will keep my word to her. I’ll quit this stupid tournament. This stupid business. This stupid life. Amazing how a near-death experience puts everything in its proper perspective. Tessa, I’m sorry. Perhaps one day you’ll understand. I promise, I’ll let you down gently. Anything for Laura to live. Anything. I can hear my music playing, just before my battle with Kimbusa. Just before a match that wouldn’t be happening if I’d have made the right call. Before, I had no purpose. Now, I do. Now, I’m winning for her. I’m going out on top, so we can celebrate when she’s recovered. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep.
