An Introspective Exposé (Part I of II)

29 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-29 How do I know Rich Rollins? I’ll tell you how I know Rich Rollins. Life can be real funny when it wants to be. An honest-to-god riot. A man loves his woman - or so he says in any case - and it’s supposed to be that simple, cue happily ever after, right? It must give the Man upstairs a good old fashioned gut-busting giggle fit when that woman gets left alone, cold and pregnant and living in Dumbfuck Falls, New Mexico. People always say God has a sense of humor. Ha fucking ha. But I’m getting ahead of myself. Fact: In 2003 the United States seized over two hundred thousand pounds of cocaine crossing through its boarders. Fact: The ratio between confiscated contraband and that which famous football players snort off a hooker’s ass is roughly one in ten. It‘s not the government’s fault. They can’t catch everything, now can they? I would know. Pampers really makes a killing at airport terminals. They make it so convenient for traveling parents to keep their gum-toothed infants feces free right before the big flight, but that’s not the only convenience on tap when it comes to airport diapers. “Babies smuggling coke in their crack,” my mother used to say, “Oh the irony.” She’d say this, of course, while hiding behind the thickest pair of sunglasses she could find because she’d downed half a bottle of Percocets and her eyes were so bloodshot you’d have thought she popped a vessel. We buried her in one of those cheap things. I’m sure it’s the way she wanted to go, you know? Anyway, as if it weren’t obvious by now, I was one of those babies. While I was still gumming animal crackers and drooling through a pacifier, I was smuggling drugs. I was a mule before I could walk, a runner before I could waddle. Adorable little Prudence Jane, an innocent child whose innocence was stolen long before I knew what the word meant. Like mother like daughter I suppose. Only I didn’t stop at half a bottle. As I write this (whatever it is; memoir or suicide note, you be the judge) I’m looking at enough empty vials of pain killers and anti-androgens to make a pharmaceutical clerk blush. You know what goes best with a good dose of Darvocets? Fact: Jack Daniels. Fact: More Darvocets. Fact: I want to die. But I’ll be damned if I don‘t take Rich Rollins with me. ~<>~<>~<>~ Cruising along a Massachusetts Interstate… For reasons that eluded conscious thought, the myriad parade of roadside signs and billboards had always entertained Rich Rollins. He was always alone; from city to city, venue to venue, his plane tickets were always solo one-way affairs. His rental cars - usually a Honda Civic, like today - rarely had a second occupant. His journeys from one arena to the next held few pleasures, except for the billboards that struck Rollins as nothing short of uncensored Republican propaganda: Choose Life, Not Lust Followed closely by: Jesus Is The Lord And Savior Followed closely by: McDonalds: Exit 202 - 10 Miles It was almost hysterical. Rollins certainly didn’t feel like a horribly overpriced and undercooked cheeseburger. Or over-salted fries. Or a medium Coke. “You know, I could use a Big Mac right about now.” The voice came from the backseat. “A Big Mac before the big match, what do you say?” The Renegade casually glanced in his rear view mirror, scowling impishly with a bitter retort sitting just behind barred teeth. He took a deep breath. But nobody was there. Another billboard flashed by: Second Hand Smoke Kills Followed closely by: Jezebel’s Gentlemen’s Club: Exit 198 - 5 Miles Followed closely by: God Bless America “Forget the Big Mac, you need to loosen up. Jezebel’s sounds like just the place.” It came from the passenger’s seat this time, cracking like a gunshot through the maddeningly steady drone of the Honda’s engine. Rollins snapped his head around sharply, already expecting the inevitable appearance of his most unwelcome guest. Grinning like a schoolboy about to tell a dirty joke, Dave Olson met The Renegade’s challenging glare with a mocking tilt of his head. Most people stopped having imaginary friends by the time they were ten. Then again, Rollins was not most people. Besides, he wouldn’t exactly call this apparition a friend. Dave Olson was more a reason to give up drinking than an imaginary mentor. An uncontrollable hallucination. The cancerous cyst in his morbid psyche. Dave Olson was a curse to which he had no cure. “I’m serious, exit 198 is calling your name Rich. Just listen.” No cure at all. “I’m not having a conversation with a man who doesn’t exist,” Rollins declared, returning his attention to the road and its endless string of commercialized propaganda. “Too late, bucko.” Olson snickered. “You’re a crazy man. Just accept it and move on, because all this lone wolf boo-hoo bullshit is giving you gray hairs.” Rollins fought to retain even a sliver of self control, but his cheeks darkened just the same. “Fuck off and die,” he blurted. Olson snorted. “Might as well try to lose your shadow and see how far that gets you. Oops, missed your exit.” He looked out the passenger’s side window, following the turnoff with eyes that didn’t exist. With an imaginary shake of his imaginary head, Dave Olson sighed. “I’m just trying to help, man. You need to calm down, maybe snag a bite to eat. That Big Mac is sounding real tasty right about now.” Strangling the steering wheel in a white-knuckled death grip, Rich Rollins did his best to keep from grinding his teeth. “Only if you’re paying,” he spat. “In fact, why don’t you pick up the toll while we’re at it? That’d be real helpful.” “You don’t need to be a smartass,” said Olson. “All you need to do is focus on the road ahead.” “And I was doing a pretty damn good job of it until you showed up.” Rollins’ voice was getting hoarse, one of the many sleep-deprived symptoms he continued to ignore. “I’m not talking about the highway,” the apparition snapped. “I’m talking about Sunday. I’m talking about Super Series. I’m talking about your career here, Rollins. You haven’t eaten in two days, and if you think that you can walk into the main event this weekend on nothing but fumes and ambition, think again. Now pull over.” Another sign passed by, catching the Renegade’s attention: Adopt A Highway: 1-800-ACT-FAST As always, another soon followed: Adopt A Child: 1-800-ACT-LOVE Rollins couldn’t help but wonder if you got a discount package if you adopted both. It was all so fucking hysterical. “I’m telling you, without a single solid meal-…” Olson began, but it wasn’t necessary. Moving to the ramp, Rich Rollins was already taking the 202 exit. Those unmistakable golden arches weren’t far behind. ~<>~<>~<>~ You’d be amazed at all the things a woman can do without a real social security number. So long as your skin is white and your accent is native, your real identity is virtually invisible. Nobody checks the obvious. If you do want a new identity however, there’s only one way to go. You call a mortuary. You see, everyone gets a social number, but calling it “secure” is about as far from the truth as you can get. Everyone is issued a number at birth. Even the stillborns. Even the sickly heroin babies who die after the first week. All these social security numbers start to stack up, going by practically unused. They never appear on anything other than a death certificate, and if you want a fresh identity, you bribe a mortician. You’d be surprised how many dead children live vicariously through the lives of illegal immigrants. If you can get to the information fast enough, it was like the original child never existed; in his place is a true-to-life John Doe, formerly Ramirez Ortega from Cuba or Julian Sanchez from Venezuela. Or yours truly, Prudence Jane, formerly a poor girl from Houston whose mother ruined my credit history before I was even old enough to talk. Imagine the look of surprise that first IRS man must have adopted when they found out a nine month old girl was four months behind on her electric bills. My mother was a frigid bitch like that. That may be harsh, but I’m in a real loose-lipped kind of mood. Fact: Chewing prescription drugs and washing them down with half a bottle of Jack Daniels may not be the best way to go, but at least you feel like being honest. Who's laughing now, God? Anyway, thus is how I came to meet Rich Rollins. Almost a year ago to the day, I met this wonderful man who helped me discover who I was. Or who I decided to be, anyway. Don’t read too much into that; what I mean is, he helped me get my new social security number. He knew people, he had money, and I was more than eager to repay the favor. It isn’t every day that you meet a gorgeous specimen of manhood like Rollins, and I let him sweep me away with promises of a new life, wherever that life may lead. As it turned out, it led to the middle of nowhere, New Mexico. I could never understand why he wanted to stay in this little cow town, especially after all his success in the wrestling business (his words, not mine) and the life he could have afforded to live. I wanted glitz and glamour, but all he gave me was solitude and seclusion. Well, that’s not entirely true. He also gave me what certain Texans refer to as a “small case of pregnant,” but let’s not split hairs here. He never knew how close he came to becoming a father. He was long gone before I had the chance to tell him. Now he’ll never know. Fact: A coat hanger may not be the most civilized method, but it does the trick. Fact: My mother wasn’t the only frigid bitch in the family. Fact: I sure could have used that extra social security number. ~<>~<>~<>~ A McDonalds in southern Massachusetts… “Is that… hey, that’s Rich Rollins!” “Shut up Donny. It is not.” “How the fuck would you know? You haven’t watched a wrestling program in years.” “Yeah, I also got laid last night. Your point is?” “I want an autograph.” “You would. Fucking dork.” “Look who’s talking, Mr. I-still-live-with-my-parents-because-I-flunked-out-of-college.” “Hey asshole, Harvard was expensive. You don’t have to get so personal. Jesus.” “Guess you’re more of a community college kind of guy, huh?” Silence. “Go get your goddamn autograph. Dick.” Sliding out from one of the uncomfortable bucket benches that McDonalds is so notorious for, Donny Sieger snatched up an unused napkin from the table and began walking toward the concession line. Even with the dense lunch hour crowd smothering the floor from wall to wall it was all too easy to pick Rich “The Renegade” Rollins out from amongst the masses. The eighties were long since dead, and only Rollins would sport a bandana in public with such uncompromising poise. Napkin in hand, Donny politely elbowed his way through the lunch mob until he was right behind the Renegade. A sudden bout of the star-struck jitters began doing a tap dance in his bowels as Donny reached up to rap a finger against Rollins’ broad shoulder, but he swallowed it and rapped anyway. Meekly he said: “Um, Mr. Rollins? Do you have a second?” Like an enormous polar bear coming out of a long hibernation, Rich Rollins made a slow turn and blinked as if his eyes were still adjusting to the fluorescents. His pupils narrowed on the young man in front of him, then drifted down to the crumpled napkin in Donny’s sweaty palm. Rollins frowned. “I don’t do that shit anymore.” His eyes widening in embarrassment, Donny started to stutter out some half-assed apology, but Rollins had already turned back around and was busying himself with the overhead menu. The rosy tint of humiliation filled the young man’s cheeks. The tap dancing in his stomach had just become a full blown Broadway musical. Donny knew better, but he just couldn’t contain himself. People were staring. “What an asshole,” he mumbled as he began to turn back. Unfortunately, he mumbled a little too loud. “The fuck did you just say?” Rollins said as he rounded back on the dejected young Donny. Frozen in his tracks, all Donny could do was sweat. The musical in his stomach just hired a seven-string orchestra. “Oh man, I’m sorry,” Donny stammered. “I just didn’t think you’d mind taking three seconds for a simple autograph, Rich. It just slipped, I didn’t mean it.” “Are we friends?” Rollins asked dangerously, burrowing holes into Donny’s flesh with his eyes. Donny almost stopped breathing. “What?” was about all he could manage. “Are… we… friends?” Rollins repeated in a tone that made his intent none too vague. “Simple question kid. You got any friends?” Donny shot a look over to his friend at the booth, his friend who was presently too involved in his McChicken to be of any real help. “Yeah, sometimes.” “Well I ain’t one of ‘em,” the Renegade snarled. “You’d do well to remember that, kid. Now fuck off.” The line between rationality and stupidity suddenly blurred over in Donny’s mind. Being shut down by a celebrity was one thing, but being embarrassed in public by a relative stranger was something else. “Fuck you, man. All I wanted was a goddamned autograph, you egotistical prick! Have fun getting your ass kicked by half the wrestling community this weekend… Richard.” It was then that two things snapped: Rich Rollins’ composure and Donny Sieger’s right arm. Nobody moved to stop Rollins as he snatched the boy’s elbow and popped it forward with his forearm. Nobody stepped in on the kid’s behalf. Nobody budged. Everybody just gawked on in shock, still chewing their McNuggets and slurping their McSodas. The perfect McCustomer. “Not even my worst enemy would call me Richard,” the Renegade spat into the heavy silence that had suddenly descended upon the lobby. Donny’s piercing shrieks were the only other audible sound besides the sizzle of french fried fat from the kitchen. “My arm! I’ll fucking sue you! I’ll fucking sue you!” The crowd parted like the Red Sea as Rich Rollins began making his way to the door, his welcome now most definitely exhausted. “Fucking bill me,” he offered over his shoulder. And he was gone. ~<>~<>~<>~ Rich Rollins thinks he’s going insane. Truth is, he was always just a little bit crazy. I only helped speed the process along. I hear he’s going around the country trying to find the man who drugged him during that Infinite Gauntlet thing he was so proud of winning. I also hear that his employer sent him to half a dozen shrinks to help stamp out his little “hallucinations.” As if it were that easy. All it would have taken was a simple phone call. A Hallmark card. A fucking text message. Would that have been so hard? Just a little, “sorry I left you and my unborn child to fend for yourselves in the middle of nowhere with no job, no money, and no life” would have done just fine by me. He should have known better. He wasn’t the only one who was crazy. He’s looking for the man who drugged him, all right. Men can be so single-track minded. All it would have taken was a phone call. Then maybe I wouldn’t have poisoned the poor bastard. And that’s a fact. To be concluded…
