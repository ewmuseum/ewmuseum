Prologue: Change

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 It was with great nervousness that Seymour Almasy looked up at the apartment building in his hometown of New York City. For two years, that building had housed the apartment that was Seymour’s base of operations, his domicile. He had hundreds of memories in that apartment, both good and bad. But he could take no more of those memories now, and so, he had left. That apartment represented a career that no longer was, and an existence that he now was not. He had lived there with Aerith once, in blissful matrimony, back when he’d been on top of the world. But that wasn’t how things were now, and so, the apartment sat vacant. The "For Sale" sign hung on the apartment’s door, Seymour’s furniture had been put in storage, and all the Final Fantasy carried with him was a large duffel bag and a suitcase. Inside were the essentials; toiletries, a few changes of clothing, and his wrestling attire. He shook his head quickly, trying to jar loose some of the snow crystals that had attached themselves to long strands of hair. The effort wasn’t especially successful. He had resolved to himself long ago that GTT5 would mark a new beginning for him, but as he stood outside under the icy might of the heavens, he was starting to wonder if anything was going to change at all. The only question that the Final Fantasy had left after that was "what now?" It wasn’t a question he could answer in the long term, but it was certainly an answerable one in the short. All he had to do was wait. Not long, as it turned out, because the limousine sent for him by PTC officials was just pulling up to the sidewalk as he reminisced over evenings past. "You Seymour Almasy," a voice called to him, as the driver exited the limousine to open the passenger door and the trunk. "That’d be me," Almasy replied, with a self-conscious shrug, placing his bags in the trunk and entering the limo. The door closed behind him with its usual slamming noise, and soon enough, the long black vehicle was (slowly) cruising through the streets of NYC. "Traffic’s a bitch this time of day, ain’t it?" Almasy frowned. Sure, the limo driver was just trying to make idle conversation, in a friendly sort of way. But Seymour wasn’t in the mood for idle chatter. Not with GTT5 weighing so heavily on his mind. "Yeah," Almasy offered in response, with the disinterested tone of voice made so famous by Squall Leonhart in Final Fantasy VIII. Realistically, considering the somber mood and faint annoyance, the two had a lot in common right now. A few seconds of awkward silence persisted. Finally, it seemed, the effort to continue the conversation was too much for either of them, and the driver decided to cut it off at the knees. "We’ll be at the airport in about an hour, giving this traffic. I’ll let you know when we get there." With that, the man let the tinted window slide up between passenger and driver. A merciful amputation, indeed. And while the conversation died a slow, hideous, and gangrenous death, Seymour Almasy was left alone to do what he seemed to do best lately: sulk. "Another tournament. To think, I used to love these things." Sure, in public he’d put on the happy and confident face. When asked, he told people he was ready to win GTT5, ready to reclaim his rightful place atop the pantheon of PTC demigods like Angelo Deville and Rich Rollins. In reality, he was nervous beyond belief, and afraid that he would be beaten in the first round. Again. But it was more than that. So much more. It was the culmination of two years of being beaten down by life. Of winning titles, only to have them slip through his fingers days or weeks later. Of losing the woman he loved, and searching desperately for someone to care. Of watching his career degrade into the wretched state that his social life was in. Sure, most people might have been happy with his impressive 11 wins in 15 matches that he’d reeled off on GCW, but Seymour saw only negatives. In his one title opportunity, he’d only won by countout. And in the match that he’d come to GCW for, HIS match no less, he’d lost to Rich Rollins on the peak of Mount Gagazet. So much for calling Rollins out. In retrospect, that hadn’t been the smartest thing he’d done, but then again, what in the past two years had been? If he really searched for positives, he could look to his burgeoning relationship with Tessa Windsor, although the more he thought about it, the more likely it was that Tessa was simply using him to get back at her ex-boyfriend/general scum of the Earth that was Chris Storm. "Leave it to me to get into a situation where every time I kiss my girlfriend, I have to wonder if she’s doing it out of spite. I swear to Yevon, this world is messed up sometimes." Well. Thinking about Tessa wasn’t helping. Nor was his career in general. Maybe GTT5 would do the trick, to awaken his competitive urges and inspire him to greatness? "Last year,’ he muttered, stifling a yawn, "I was the target, the one man everyone wanted to beat. This year, I’m not even worth slapping a bounty on. My, how things change…" Last year, his opponent had been Ash. This year, it was Jonathon Winters of the WWA, a man with an undefeated streak in the promotion and definite designs on pulling the upset of the first round. But for once, Seymour couldn’t care less. "Wake me up when we get there," he mumbled, unaware in his exhaustion that the driver couldn’t hear him. Sleep was usually rather good to the Final Fantasy. It let him escape the doubts and annoyances of the daily routine of wrestle-travel-wrestle. Of course, fatefully, on this day, it chose to be a bit more of a bumpy ride than normal… ------------------------ Seymour woke, or thought he woke, on a rather plush couch. Looking around, everything seemed perfectly normal. He was in a nice apartment, with nice furniture. Circling the room like a model train might in other households were championship belts, dozens of them hanging on the top of the walls, testament to Seymour’s prodigious success in his career. Across the room from his black leather couch, a 52 inch television set loomed. All was as it should be. In fact, there was only one indication that something was off; that this situation wasn’t quite right, and it hung out from underneath Almasy’s Final Fantasy VII shirt. A beer gut. A rather impressive one, at that. It seemed that this Final Fantasy hadn’t seen the inside of the ring in a while, but he most certainly had seen the insides of Heineken bottles and Breyers cartons. It was that latter vice he seemed intent on indulging in, rising from the couch with the big screen TV’s remote control in hand. Walking to the freezer, he jabbed his thumb at the "up" button on the remote, only to find no result. "Fuckin’ thing must be out of batteries again…" With a disappointed sigh, he heaved the remote over his shoulder. It landed somewhere in the pile of RPG strategy guides at the foot of the TV, likely never to be seen again until Seymour decided to clean the living room. Opening the freezer, he withdrew a carton of cookies and cream. Next, he turned his attention to the sink, where a pile of dirty dishes made him frown. Reaching into the mess, he managed to come out with a spoon. Turning on the hot water, he ran the spoon thoroughly underneath, then dried it with a dishrag as he shut the water off. There. Clean. He carried the carton and spoon back with him to the television set, poking at the channel buttons until finally arriving at one of dozens of the PPV channels that satellite TV offered. When he saw "PTC Presents Super Series XVII", he gave a satisfied sigh, and returned to his soft couch. "This used to be my life once," he mused, as the Primetime Central intro began to play. Wrestling had been good to him, far better than it had to most. He was paid well for his services, and he had saved his money so that he wouldn’t need to work at a Wal-Mart once he reached his sixties. With the exception of several noticeable scars and some soreness in his joints, the business also hadn’t left a devastating physical impression on him. He was addicted to neither drugs nor alcohol. He was lucky, he knew, in most respects. But then, he heard something that made him doubt that fact all over again. "And then, folks, it’s the one you’ve been waiting for! Two-time PTC Global Champion and the GTT5 winner, Jonathon Winters of WWA, going one on one with the #1 contender from the wacky UWF, Pope Pain XXX!" To this day, even with the hair at his temples starting to gray, the name of the man who’d robbed him of his career still made his pulse quicken and his eyes flash murderous crimson. His match with Winters in GTT5 had gone pretty much as he’d expected, deep down. He was worried about losing to Winters, and so he did. It was really that simple. Sure, he’d had a few chances to win, but he’d pissed them away. Combined with Winters analytical approach to the game, it was too much for an overly cautious Final Fantasy to take. One Down to the Numbers later, and the Almasy era of PTC was over, and the Winters era began. "I still can’t believe he beat Shadow and Deville back to back to win that tournament," Seymour groused. Even as he said it though, he knew he was being jealous. After all, Winters might have had fame and fortune, but so did he. Of course, the thing that defined Seymour now was the one thing he didn’t have. Companionship. He couldn’t blame Tessa Windsor for leaving him. Not considering how grumpy and grouchy and unpersonable he’d been after getting his ass handed to him by Winters and exiting GTT5. Sighing, he thrust his spoon into the carton of vanilla and cookie goodness, raising it to his mouth to enjoy the taste of something sweet for a change. Suddenly, he didn’t want to watch Super Series any more. He wanted to turn the TV off and find a way to distract himself from the fact that, over the last two years of his career, he’d done nothing but drop the ball. And then, his butter fingers had started to bleed over into real life, and he sat on the couch, a decade after he meant anything, a sad and broken man. Once, he had laughed at the thought that money couldn’t buy happiness. Now, it was virtually gospel. "Not pretty, is it Seymour?" "…the Hell?" "Oh, I forgot! You’re still in that dream. Hold on, just one second now…" --------------------- A stereotypical bright flash of light later, and the Final Fantasy stood in the middle of, well, nothing. Before him stood an equally stereotypical old man, wearing dark robes and a long, grey beard. "Greetings, Seymour," the old man said, in a cordial tone of voice. "I am the Lord of Dreams!" The Final Fantasy only grimaced, and looked at the man cautiously. Nervously, he approached, and whispered something into the other man’s ear. "Hey, the personification of DarkStar’s gimmick isn’t supposed to be for a few rounds yet! Besides, I don’t even face him unless he gets to the bracket finals!" The old man shook his head. "Didn’t you see the bracket shuffle?" "There is no bracket shuffle yet," Seymour exclaimed. "It refreshes every time you go to the bracket. Tournament Director Wool-Knit’s already trying to drive me insane." "Oh…" Turning to go, and shaking his head slightly, the Lord of Dreams suddenly stopped, stock-still, and whirled around to face the Final Fantasy with speed belieing his old age. "Oh! There IS a reason for me to be here beyond that," the ruler of sleep remembered, looking Seymour dead in the eyes. Seymour could have figured that one out on his own. After all, people didn’t usually have dreams like the one he’d just had without there being a point to it. "Really," the bitter former Global Champion replied, "going to show me another disgusting image of my future?" "Actually, no," came the slightly acidic reply, "though if you keep talking to me in that tone of voice, I’m going to consider it. Instead, I’m going to show you a far happier vision." With a snap of his fingers, the blinding white flash returned, and Seymour found himself hurtling through the time and space of his subconscious, finally returning to something resembling reality. ------------------------------------ This time, he found himself walking back through a curtain, clad in his wrestling attire. Around him, his GCW co-workers stood, applauding him. One stepped forward, grinning, and shook the Final Fantasy’s hand. "You’ve been in this business, what, five years now, man," he said, nodding, "and you haven’t missed a single step." Seymour could only nod good-naturedly at the comment. "Thank you. There’ve been some rough patches, but this is what I love to do, and what I do as well as anyone." GCW’s World Championship rested proudly around the Final Fantasy’s waist, fresh off being defended at NC-17, the promotion’s biggest pay-per-view event of the year. "So, whatcha gonna do tonight, champ," another fedmate asked him, knowing the answer even before Seymour could say the words. "I’m going to see the woman I love." In this particular dream sequence, it was only a year later. Seymour was still mostly the same man. Certainly, his ring attire had changed a bit, and the beginnings of a tiny wrinkle were starting to form under his left eye, but it was still the Final Fantasy that fans screamed for. Making his way to the locker room, he hurriedly placed his championship belt in his duffel bag, and stripped. The shower beckoned to him, and he stepped underneath the hot water with a mighty sigh. Life was good. Really good. He was the GCW World Champion, and as such, one of the top men in PTC, just as he had always been. There was the pending fifth meeting between himself and Rich Rollins at Super Series IV for the PTC Global Championship, to settle the score between them. The shower head was abruptly turned off, and Seymour roughly toweled himself dry. He was sore, but a good kind of sore. It was the kind of sore that let him know he was alive, that he was still competing. Wrapping a towel around his midsection, he re-emerged into the dressing room, only to find he now had company. "Seymour!" It was the pleased exclamation of a female, who immediately flung her arms around the Final Fantasy’s neck. Seymour looked into her eyes, only to find them changing color every few seconds. Indeed the entire face was changing seemingly by the minute, as was the attire and hair color. "What the bloody Hell?" "Hey, I can’t predict the future! I’m just offering you a potential vision of it. Pick any of the thousand or so girls you have a crush on if it‘ll make the experience easier for you." "HEY! "You were great out there, tonight," she said, smiling at him as she fell to his side, fingers interlocking with his. He could only grin back in response, in appreciation. "Thank you. There’ve been some rough patches over the past year, sure…but I love this. I love you." A hand rose to affectionately fluff his hair, prompting a happy squeak to escape Seymour’s throat. "And to think, you almost quit after losing in GTT5…" "Okay, TIME OUT!" "Hoo boy, I figured this was going to happen…" ---------------------------------------- Stereotypical white flash. Black empty room. You know the drill by now. "Okay, mystical dream guy, I get the point. Second vision is far nicer than the first one. But what does it matter? I still lose in GTT5." The old man looked at Seymour, shaking his head. It seemed to be a staple of anyone dealing with Almasy lately. "Is WINNING GTT5 the only way you’ll be happy?!" The Final Fantasy thought about the question for a few moments. Really thought about it. "No, of course not," he replied. "Good. Then, what else will make you happy? Seymour paused, taking a slow, deep breath to focus his thoughts. "I would be happy to do well in it. To have my life calm down, to stop dealing me harsh blows…things ARE improving though." They were, if he was willing to be fair. While he would never have Aerith back, he HAD started a relationship with Tessa. Sure, at the moment, it was mainly predicated on making Chris Storm’s life a living Hell, but there was potential for it to grow. Sure, he’d lost to Rich Rollins, but he hadn’t put on a bad performance. He’d gone out there and given it his best. That night, it hadn’t been enough. Was that so hard to accept? Yes and no. "Seymour," the old man finally said, "as far as I’m concerned, you have two options…" Whatever force was holding Seymour up in the nothingness suddenly broke, and Seymour found himself hurtling down, down into the abyss. As he fell for what seemed like forever, he heard the old man’s voice following him, echoing through blackness. "Wilt thou change the world, Seymour, or wilt thou change thyself?" ---------------------------------------- "Yo, wake up man! We’re here!" Groggily, Seymour Almasy stretched his arms, eyes slowly coming into focus on not the face of an elderly man, but the slightly gruff and traffic-weary expression of his limousine driver. "Alright, just gimme a sec," Seymour replied, drowsily, still trying to get his bearings. What had just happened was a dream, certainly, but it wasn’t the sort of dream one could easily shake off. It was the sort of dream that felt like it meant something. Maybe he really WAS being too hard on himself. The foundation for regaining what he had lost was certainly in the ground. His GCW win streak proved that he could still get it done in the ring. And out of the ring, he had someone who cared, on some level. Without warning, the limo door opened, sending a rush of cold New York air into the back seat that almost immediately snapped Seymour out of his daze. He stepped out of the limo, hand going almost immediately to his wallet before the limo driver actually shook his head. Ah, he had nearly forgotten. PTC had provided the limo for him. And why? Because he was a favorite. Because when people thought of PTC, they thought of Seymour Almasy. Because he’d won two PTC sponsored tournaments before. That knowledge allowed him to come to the conclusion that the old man and his subconscious had reached long ago. "…this worrying and nervousness shit is starting to get old." While the limo driver looked at him, puzzled, Almasy simply took his luggage, beginning the walk to the airport terminal. The old man’s question had a simple answer, really. He would change himself, back to what he once was. He had lost to Craig Goat because he was afraid of losing. He was so terrified of Archibald being an assassin that he had let himself get psyched out and lose, in the first round no less. No more of that lack of confidence bullshit. It was only hurting him. So what if Jonathan Winters was undefeated in WWA? So what if held two titles simultaneously? That was the sort of thing that Seymour thrived on. Going up against competitors with great home fed records and all of the confidence in the world, and then demonstrating to the world that he was still as good as they came. There would be no more fear. Now, there would be only resolve. Determination. The will to win. Everything that he had forsaken in his past two quests for tournament glory. "I’m Seymour Almasy," he mused, soon disappearing behind the glass doors of the Continental Airlines terminal. "This time, things will be different." Indeed they will, Seymour. Does different mean better? Only time will tell.
