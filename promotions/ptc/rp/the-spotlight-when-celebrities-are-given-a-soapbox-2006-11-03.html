The Spotlight: When Celebrities Are Given A Soapbox

3 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-03 The crew bustled busily behind the scenes as they made the last few adjustments before show time. Damien Cruz sipped his coffee, and an intern was there to quickly refill the little that he had drunk. The host looked over his notes, visibly annoyed to be interviewing a professional wrestler, and not the head of state or a field-leading scholar. Instead, due to slumping ratings and the Executive Producer's desire to score a younger demo, he was about to spend an hour talking to a glorified stunt man about his make believe fight during a pre-written event. He was a far way from South Laos in 1968, and could taste the bile rising in the back of his throat. Damien, not sure what his agent was thinking when he made the booking, took another sip of his coffee, and then hid the cup from the intern. If this was walking into the longest hour of his life, he wanted to at least finish the coffee. A production assistant held a light meter in front of Damien's face without looking at Damien, and gave the director a thumbs up. Another PA clipped a small mic to the lapel of his jacket, again not looking at Damien, tapping it and the sound guy gave the director a thumbs up. Finally, the host was handed a last minute addendum to his notes, which he seemed to roil at receiving, and the director was given the thumbs up. After an awkward period of silence and near-motionless around him, the Director began the count-down, and the lights dimmed slightly. In three…two…one… "Welcome to The Hugh Morton Show, I'm Hugh Morton. Today, we continue our series on American Culture and Generation Why." The host seems to introduce the concept of what Damien does day in and day out with the same alien nonchalance that he introduced the growing trend of Pilates and file-sharing; he knew the buzzwords, but none of the meanings. "On our show, we will interview a man who has made a career in the realm of professional wrestling. The "sport" of wrestling seems to combine Circus Strong Men that wrestled ears and bent iron rods with their bands with the lights and theatrics of a rock and roll concert. A strange mix of athleticism and performance art to some, a wasteland of empty violence and hypersexualism to others. What the world of professional wrestling truly represents can be debated, but what can not be debated is its growing popularity. To help me, and I'd imagine a lot of my viewers, better understand that, I welcome Mr. Damien Cruz to the program." Damien watched the man deliver the introduction with practiced enthusiasm and mild amusement, but could sense that the last thing this man wanted to do was to talk about something that he didn't understand, didn't want to understand, and would most likely be unable to understand even if Damien spent several hours trying to explain it to him. So Damien didn't try. "Thank you for having me, Mr. Morton. May I begin by commending you on your interview with Chuck Rangel, representative from New York? I am a huge fan of his, and felt that you gave him a great platform to get his message out." The host, after a moment of confusion, let out a stammering thank you, and looked at Damien for the first time that evening. " I didn't think the topic of strong Democratic leadership would come up in any other way, I just wanted to crowbar that in. I'm sorry." Hugh Morton, after gathering his thoughts, tossed aside his notes and adjusted his tie. " Well, as the guest, I'll give you free reign on what we talk about. If Democratic politics is what you want, it's what we'll discuss. What are your thoughts about the mid-term elections, and where it will take the Democrats n 2008?" Damien smiled to himself, and began. " Well, I think that there are a lot of Democrats that are walking into this mid-term with a little more confidence than they should. By all accounts, we've seen the Republicans pull out a few big wins under the radar. Let's remember, they may have alienated a lot of their core with the what Iraq, the economy, and the war on terror as a whole has gone, but we cannot be so naive as to think that issues such as gay marriage, tax breaks, and ironically, the war on terror, have gone away. There is a substantial block of voters that will turn up and vote Republican solely based on a single issue. Say what you will about that type of political activity, but their votes count as much as those who spent the time between election cycles digesting newspapers, scouring the blogosphere, and weighing all the issues despite party lines. So as far as the mid-terms, I think that the Democrats will get a lot of headway started, but may not get the sweep they were looking for unless they spend the next couple of days campaigning like they had these past few months. And as far as 2008, and kind of going back to the mid-terms, I think that the Republicans were kind of given a gift by John Kerry's recent mis-statements about those guys over in Iraq. Granted, I think he should be commended for trying to be bolder, more assertive, and less politically safe, but he may have overplayed his stroke a bit on this one. So…yeah, I think the Republicans are going to pounce on that if he lets his remarks flounder out there for too long. And that may hurt his possible 2008 bid." Damien took his sip, almost on cue, and waited for the host to fire another one at him. " A recent poll shows that if she were placed on the 2008 Democratic ticket, that only 27% of Democrats would support Hillary Clinton as the best choice. Another 34% would support Kerry, and about 19% would support Barrack Obama. With his latest snag, that means that the strongest Democratic candidate may be in jeopardy of losing what support he had. Do you feel that the Democratic Party may be cursed by almost-there candidates who lose their footing at crucial moments?" Damien chuckled. " Not being the spokesperson for the democratic party, nor a true card-carrying Democrat, I'll refrain from giving an answer to that. All I'll say is that the Republicans have made a science out of getting people to feel more comfortable with them. Even people who have major bones to pick with them seem willing to let those gripes take a back seat when it's time to go to the booths. It's almost like how we regard our friends and family; we may have a few gripes, but at the end of the day, we know that certain loyalties still hold fast. Somehow, The Republicans have become our deadbeat dads, and the Democrats are the over anxious stepdad who wants to play catch." " Do you think it's tied to the religiosity of the Republican Party?" " They seem to have tapped into a very solid voter block just by aligning themselves with the conservative Christian community. So while I'm not ready to make that direct line, I am also smart enough to know that there is more than a loose co-dependency at work there. It further impresses upn me the irony of where this country is headed culturally." "Are you critical of religion in the political sphere?" " Yes, very. And I think that most Americans should be. Not because religion is necessarily supposed to be apolitical, or partitioned off from the moral thinking that drives most political positions. What I'm saying is that I am critical of faith and politics becoming intertwined because I feel deeply that faith, or even the lack there of, are far too personal to be transformed into laws that should govern the nation on a whole. And to be perfectly honest, I think faith and religion make it too easy to come up with personal philosophies and ethical directives. They protect the individual from the very complex process of critically desciding what is reasonable and what is practical by painting one set of ideas as wholly good and others as wholly bad. In a changing world such as the one in which we live, I find that dangerous and lazy. I think that the philosophies of Divine Authority and clerics dictating what is right and wrong according to some arbitrary system of fairy tale laws are cumbersome and poisonous to social evolution and adaptation." "I think there will be a lot of letters arguing that your comments seem strongly biased against those among us that have faith?" "Yeah, and I stand by my statements. But don't misunderstand me; I don't mean to say that religion or faith or spirituality are bad or wrong or stupid. I'm simply saying that one's faith should be a personal thing, and left away from public and political reasoning. I think the role of religion is to align the individual to their place in this universe and their ideas of self. To use that same spiritual tool as a means of legislation, and as a route to how we judge others is an affront to what religion is supposed to be. Let your superstitions and fairy tales guide how you live your life, not how I am to live mine." Impressed by the man's insights, but eager to move on, Hugh Morton breached a different topic. "Mr. Cruz, I know that you weren't born in the United States, but have made your home here for some time. From the perspective of a new immigrant, what are your thoughts on the immigration debate?" " I'll be honest, I really don't see much of a debate. Those illegal immigrants are illegal, and should not be excluded from the rules of immigration. If you are an undocumented citizen, and you are caught, you should be sent home. If you want to enter this country, do so with the proper protocol. And if you feel like your situation back in your country is unbearable, then vote for change or seek political asylum. But add to the American economy, learn to assimilate, and be a gracious guest. When you come to this country, it is like entering a home that is not your own, so you treat the land and it's rules with respect. How can a person make the argument that they came to the US because of it's great opportunities and it's superior way of life, and then undermine it's laws by breaking them, and then criticizing it when you're caught or threatened? It's a hypocrisy that really gets under my skin. I came to this country because it truly is a great nation with great opportunities. And I abide by the laws that govern it and I embrace the culture that has been borne of it. Am I blind to the faults and short-comings and corruption? Of course not. And that is why I went to the post office and registered to vote before I went to the bank and cashed my first pay check." " So how do you think your compatriots will react to your comments?" " Probably with a sense of betrayal because I'm a wealthy American now. But that's the path I took and it was no easier for me than for them, really. I found a skill, or a talent that made me a part of the American economy. I paid taxes when they were due, and I let the government know where I was at all times. If they let me stay, or if they made me leave, I was willing. So how I shaped and exploited my opportunities is not the issue. Because if I were a dish-washer or a mechanic, the same rules apply. And for the record, my views are not limited to just Mexicans. I feel that they have become the iconic portion, but there are almost as many Chinese, East Indian, and Russian here illegally. It's really about visibility and the fact that we share a border. So they've become the scapegoat. Whatever the case, I think that Mexicans need to accept that they will be picked on because of those reasons." " So what advice would you offer them?" Damien chuckled, knowing he was being painted into a corner he wasn't sure he wanted to be in. But hell, he was long past playing it safe. " I think the problems of Mexican immigrants clearly goes back to Mexico. They need to help build a political system that serves them instead of running away from it. From what I understand, even with the recent changes and the promises made in Mexico, there is a deep mistrust that makes it hard for the Mexican people to have faith or optimism that anything will really be different. I mean, 40-odd years of blatant corruption cannot be forgotten after one election." "Interesting point, and worth thinking about. But to bring the conversation back to where we began at the top of the show: Where do you think the American Culture is headed? Do you feel that this generation is more jaded, cynical, and nihilistic?" Damien laughed, and shook his head. "What ‘new’ generation isn’t? I mean, from the perspective of the generations before them, everyone thinks that the next generation is vastly different than our own. It’s often pointed out that Socrates worried that the youth of his time was going to bring about the extinction of Mankind. Clearly, he was a reactionist. But I will concede this; the younger generation is a bi more cynical because they are more media-savvy. They have seen the curtain pulled away from the wizard behind it. They know that the news is as much a commercial and a product as anything else they see on television. They also know that what’s on channel 7 has to compete with what’s on channel 2, and 4, and 15, and 9. And that the news programming is less concerned with bringing an accurate view of the worlds than they are in bringing more viewers to the channel. It often becomes a chicken and egg scenario. On one hand, there are sensational stories to report on, and there are viewers who want to see sensational stories because anything less is unable to stimulate them anymore. The same goes for the entertainment we use. Sex, drugs, violence, and sensationalism has needed to go bigger, and louder, and more in your face simply to compete with where the culture has taken us. To be honest, I wonder if 9/11 wasn’t regarded the way it was because we saw Jerry Bruckheimer and James Cameron and Michael Bay blowing up landmarks for years. I was in Brooklyn at the time, and I was watching as people rushed to the shoreline with video cameras and their cell phones. Granted, there was still an unbelievable sense of fear, grief, and anger, but the cameras were there the whole time. From the moment the planes hit, the cameras have not turned away." "Mr. Cruz, this is very fascinating, and I am grateful that you’re here today. Now if you’ll pardon the irony, we need to take a commercial break." Damien smirked, and accepted the ebb and flow of the business. "We will return very shortly." It may have been a surprise to him, but Hugh Morton was striking gold with this guy. For Damien, he was simply releasing the random, flowing, unspoken stuff that was so hard to express with chair shots and high spots. As the director signaled a return in 5 seconds, Damien took a deep breath and prepared to go further. In three…two…one…
