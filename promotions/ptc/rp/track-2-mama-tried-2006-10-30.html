Derek For nearly two years, Derek Saunders had been drinking stale coffee. You’d think the FBI would spring for something a little better than this crap, he thought for the millionth time as he added sugar to this morning’s disappointment. Across the table from him, Howard Edwards did the same. Although Howard was Derek’s superior, this was his first day in the apartment that Derek and his co-workers bitterly called "Mobwatch." Early this morning, Derek’s team got word that Justin Hattoni himself would be making an appearance. The boss… The man at the top of it all… That’s why Howard showed up. "This coffee is horrible," Howard made a bitter face. "Welcome to Mobwatch," smirked Derek. At thirty-two, Howard Edwards was nearly a full decade younger than Derek, and yet his superior in rank. It wasn’t an exceptional case. For years, Derek watched his co-workers leapfrog him in the pecking order, but he never complained - at least not out loud. His wife was sure it was because he was black, and although Derek had told her time after time that she was being silly, he couldn’t help but notice the same patterns that she did. On the street below, old Nicholas Ramone stood next to the small table outside of M&M with two men at his side. He was wearing an overcoat in spite of the late August heat - a hat too, and he was smoking a cigar. Forever the gangster, Nick, thought Derek, always thinking about appearances. Nicholas Ramone was a man in the winter of his life. He was near seventy now, but he still looked healthy and strong - tall and slim, as he’d always been, and moving like a much younger man. Everything about him demanded respect - the way he spoke, the gestures he made… And of course, he still wore those ugly thick glasses with their big black frames. The other wise-guys used to make fun of him for those glasses, and they called him "Nicky Four-Eyes" as much to his face as behind his back. But then he was made a Captain, or caporegime, and all that changed. No one would dare make fun of Nicholas Ramone now. All Palo had to do was say those two little words: Nicholas Ramone… they probably would have let him go on the spot. At the very least, his sentence would have been cut in half. All of the broken knee caps, the shady business deals; all of the murders… take them high enough and they all lead to this one man. The Don. Justin Hattoni. Everyone knew Johnny would never be the boss. Outside, the men were running through the same formalities as when Hattoni showed up. There was obvious tension between Ramone and Johnny Silver, but they went through the motions all the same. And just as had happened with the young man that arrived with Ramone, when each of the wise-guys came to Jason Snow, there was nothing but a simple handshake. Howard fixed himself in front of the window and stared down at the blond man among the black-haired and bald pates. He fingered his way through the files, some as thick as books, until he found the one with "Snow" at the top in bold, black letters. He pulled it out and flipped through for a moment until he found the photograph that he was looking for. "On November 11, 2003, Jason Snow’s wife died." "A hit?" Howard raised an eyebrow. The photo depicted Jason Snow and his young wife standing outside of their car in 2003, just before the time of her death. "Pretty girl," remarked Howard after a moment. "Pregnant or fat?" Across the street, the wise-guys and associates were settling into their seats and the small-talk seemed to be finishing up. They were getting down to business. Derek wished he could be closer - wished he could hear what they were saying. Maybe then he could finally put a couple of the big names away. Maybe he could finally put a dent in the monster known as the Hattoni crime family. He had devoted a major portion of his life to that goal, and yet, here he was at forty-one, and no closer than he’d ever been. Nicholas Johnny Silver, that piece of shit, sat smiling at the opposite end of the small, round table from Nicholas.
