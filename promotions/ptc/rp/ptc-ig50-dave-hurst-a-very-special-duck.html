Darth von Doom versus Dave Hurst

6 October 2008

A Very Special Duck (IG50 Promo #3 of 5) A Very Special Duck “What would you like, sir…?” I’m in the bar. Ducky, my little plastic companion, sits on the stool beside me. “I’ll have a bottle of Corona, please.” I ask the bartender, and he heads off to fetch my beverage. “You want a bacardi and coke then, little mate?” I ask Ducky. He doesn’t reply, disgruntled at my jab. Of course, Ducky doesn’t drink. It’s not that he won’t drink for religious reasons; it’s just that he doesn’t like the taste. He knows I’m only teasing. Ducky continues to stare into the distance. He’s got things on his mind, I know it. He’s been very quiet since we arrived at Las Vegas, it’s not like him. I wonder what’s bothering him. I know exactly what’s bothering him. It doesn’t take much thinking to work it out. He’s worried about how I’m doing. He’s upset that my training isn’t going as well as it should. My mind hasn’t been right, I need some motivation. I’m scared stupid that I’m going to have my head torn off and I can’t do anything to stop the inevitable. Since… that day… I just can’t bring myself to be put in that situation again. If all that happened to me again, I just don’t know what I’d do. I don’t know if I could ever cope with the abuse, with people in my face all day, with people screaming at my hotel room through the night because I beat their golden boy. Just don’t think about it… I look over to the barstool beside me. Ducky has stopped staring at the wall, now he’s looking over at me. He’s stuck by me through the good and the bad, and I’ve never really thanked him for it. I should. This is a very special little plastic duck. He came to me at a time in my life when I hit rock bottom. After the closure of Old School Wrestling, the wrestlers were laid off, and expected to immediately get picked up by the remaining wrestling companies under the Primetime Central umbrella, but it took a long time for me to get a job. By the time I was picked up by an independent wrestling promotion I was a mess. I was a drunken fool. Sleeping in cheap motels, moving from town to town and from bar to bar. I had no car, I had no place to stay, and I had no life. I took the job to keep me going, because I’d be sleeping rough if I didn’t get money soon. After taking the job with the wrestling promotion (I can’t remember the name, I wasn’t in the frame of mind to remember much of anything then) I went to board a bus to the town of the wrestling event. As I walked over to the bus I noticed a small plastic yellow duck lying in the snow. I chose not to pick him up and went on my way. I boarded the bus, sat down on the end and beside me sat a little plastic duck. I thought to myself, could this be the same plastic duck from outside the bus? – Impossible. The bus stopped and I stepped off, dragging my suitcase behind me. I went into the venue, to the dressing room where I was to prepare. I opened up my suitcase to get my wrestling attire, and sitting there at the top of the pile of clothes was a little plastic duck. Then it struck me that this is no ordinary duck. It could be a voodoo duck! It could be some kind of blessing from above, I didn’t know. But I didn’t really have enough time to find out, because my wrestling match was first that night and I had to be out there. I walked down to the squared circle. I wasn’t really that nervous about the fight. I didn’t expect to win. I was in no shape to compete. I was just planning on running around the ring a little, getting hit a few times, losing, taking my pay, and going on my way. The promoter said himself that I was hired to help promote the other guy. A wrestler could gain a lot of kudos in those days for beating an OSW wrestler. I didn’t care. The crowd were booing me, but I kinda expected it. This was a small town promotion. They had their favourites and they pretended that the big promotion’s wrestlers were nothing compared to their hometown boy. I climbed up into the ring, and looked on at my opponent, a 6’6” 250lb guy with a muscular frame and a menacing grin. The bell rang, and then something extraordinary happened. The drunken, out-of-shape fool parading around the ring suddenly gained an exceptional wrestling ability. The 6’6” guy tried desperately to overpower me in the collar-elbow tie up, but I had the technique to turn his momentum into my advantage, shifting his weight around and pushing him into the corner. I ran rings around my opponent, something I could never do before in my wrestling career. The crowd were astonished, and had gone silent. I put the goliath into a camel clutch, and pulled his head back as hard as I could until he eventually tapped. I couldn’t believe it. It was like an out-of-body experience. I could never do anything like that. I knew it wasn’t the booze, I was starting to sober up when I got into the venue. I turned to exit the ring and down by the turnbuckle sat the plastic yellow duck from my suitcase. I don’t know if the duck had anything to do with what happened out there, I have an idea that he had a little something to do with it. But there was something that I had realised… This was a very special duck.
