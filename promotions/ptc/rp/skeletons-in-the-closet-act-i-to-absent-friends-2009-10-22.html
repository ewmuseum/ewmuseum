Skeletons in the Closet (Act I): "To Absent Friends"

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Prelude: The Lost Night Her name was Alexandra Pierce, perhaps better known as Desade, Sin City Championship Wrestling's fearsome "Spider in the Web". However, she was also Elisabeth Pierotti and Charlotte Raymond, Emily Beckett and Melanie Lovejoy, and a whole host of others, each with their own backstory, their own tics, and their own foibles – like her brain was a mixer at an eclectic apartment complex, and everyone was invited. Which was why, when her tired, stone-gray eyes snapped open in the wee hours of an October morning, her first question wasn't "What time is it?" or even "Where am I?" it was "Who am I today?" She rolled over as nonchalantly as possible, dropping her arm out from beneath the thin cotton bed sheet, slender fingers brushing the carpet. Pale, yellow-orange light spilled from the lamp outside the window, sneaking between the slats of the blinds in dribs and drabs. Pierce blinked slowly, not recognizing her crisp white blouse and snug black, velvety vest. She eased the sheet back, hesitantly glancing over her shoulder; the bed was empty, thankfully. A relieved whoosh of air escaped her lungs – the last thing she wanted was to have someone else in bed, someone who would remember the preppy dark-haired girl Alex didn't remember. Not to mention she'd feel horrible having to explain it to her girlfriend. Trying to make as little noise as possible, Pierce eased up out of the bed, gauging her surroundings with a practiced eye. It was a hotel room, but that didn't narrow it down; she traveled so often, both for SCCW and for her sideline work, that nights she actually got to spend in her condo were few and far between. The room rode the borderline between decadent and tasteless, its king-sized centerpiece rumpled enough that she was sure something happened there the night before. There just wasn't any indication as to what that something was: she could find no blood or actual damage, and the trashcan didn't have condom wrappers in it or anything damning. She'd just come to the conclusion that it was too clean – just a pair of heavy hairpins and her no-prescription eyeglasses on the bedside table to mark her presence in the room – when keen hearing caught footsteps coming from outside the doors. Briefly, she considered what weapons she might have at hand. Figuring out just who and where she was took a backseat to avoiding what may be an ambush on the other side of the door. In the end, she selected one of the hairpins; the things were weighted weirdly, but they were still basically just fancy wooden stakes, and she was confident she could put it in the proper place if she had to. The lock clicked – whoever it was had a key – and Alex was briefly worried that there had been a man in that bed with her. Pierce breathed out, twirling the pin in her left hand as the door swung slowly inward. Alex crouched down as low as she could get (in her snug, pinstriped dress trousers, that wasn't far) waiting like a snake in the weeds. The intruder was small, probably a woman, and favored her left side as she crept into the room. Desade had already pinpointed the spot she wanted to strike when the woman spoke, her voice barely over a whisper. "Al?" Pierce could barely make out the glint of a small, silver ring through the woman's nose and a tuft of her stoplight red hair, and all of the adrenaline flushed itself out of her system at once. "Amy?" The question was soft and not at all from the direction it should have come from, so forgive the diminutive, redheaded former SCCW champion if she jumped a little. Amy Campbell's brows furrowed when she spun aside, finally finding Pierce. "You, uh... were you expecting somebody else to come back to the room with snacks?" "I don't..." Pierce frowned, just a light downturn to her lips. She'd palmed the hairpin, sliding it into the back of her waistband. "I don't remember coming back last night after the show." It was the day after SCCW's Temptation broadcast, taped during the inaugural SIN*CON event at Tucson's convention center, an event which saw Desade win the way she often did – through gross amounts of chicanery. The frown melted away with her embarrassment, but the tiny smile that slipped across her lips owed more to the other woman's presence than any sense of self-consciousness. "Have I been here long?" "You must've slipped in after I fell asleep, which was late enough to get me worried half to death. Were you drunk?" Campbell's gaze had turned a little accusing, moving away from Alex along with her body when she sat back down on the bed. "I trust you, I just... how can I know I should trust you if you can't even remember where you've been laying for the past few hours?" "I don't know." It was a simple enough thing to say, but she still felt dirty saying it. Rather than following the smaller woman to bed, Alex turned towards the bathroom. Her fingers worked at the wide buttons to her vest, like jet-black half dollars. "I can only hope you do, but then I look at myself in the mirror, and I don't know who I'm supposed to be." Pierce's reflection was a little too made-up, a little too young, like Suzie Secretary had been tarted up for an event. Her lips were rose-red, and the woman she was not frowned back at her gently. "I thought Elisabeth was at a party," she called; it was like she treated her aliases as whole other people. "There was champagne there, and you know that does awful things to me, but..." Her hands went to her hair, a black wig of human hair that looked like the real thing. "I don't like double-dipping in the same crowd, but maybe there was a reason." "I hate this." A sigh floated through the door. "Just stop talking, please. I want to try to be okay, not to nag and pry and... just stop." "I'm sorry." Alexandra was a redhead again when she emerged from the bathroom, her rich, rust-red hair brushing her shoulders. Her vest and too-tight slacks had been left behind folded atop the marble countertop, but she still wore the blouse, shirttails whispering along the tops of bare thighs. "I'm just... I'm trying to figure it all out." Amy had moved to the dresser, setting down her store of vending machine candy beside a faux-mahogany ice bucket. She probably used a little more force in her use of the scoop than was altogether necessary as she fished out mismatched pieces to fill her little plastic cup. "You're mad," Pierce whispered as she neared. Her fingers toyed with the smooth stone on her simple necklace, one of few gifts she had from Campbell. "I'm sorry I was gone." "You told me it would be in and out," Amy muttered, snagging a small bottle of lemon gin she'd pilfered from the wet bar. "Just some glad-handing and then you'd be here. It's two in the morning, and all I could find on cable was a movie where the cops had to knock on this woman's door and tell her that her husband was..." She couldn't complete the sentence, didn't want to face the possibility. "I'm sorry." Alex repeated it again, lacing her arms around Amy's waist from behind. She bent to plant her chin on the other woman's shoulder. "I should have called. I wish I had called, because then you would know what I was doing." "Just..." Amy turned in the taller woman's embrace, the cheap plastic cup dangling from her fingertips. "Just tell me you didn't do anything bad, and that you're taking the day off tomorrow." She glanced at the red face of the alarm clock on the bedside. "Well, today, I mean." "What's so special about today?" "A day that doesn't have to be anything important for Alexandra Pierce to take off." Campbell smirked. "Where you don't have to be anybody but yourself, and you get to promise me that you won't be using it to plan or research or do anything but relax." "Oh." Alex grinned a little. Her left hand remained around Amy's waist, right lifting to brush the hair away from her face. "I thought someone had remembered I kissed you for the first time just about a year ago this week." "Hard to forget when I've still got the scars from Drusilla's reaction to it," Amy sighed. "But the trophy was worth it... when she can remember who she is, that is." Alex's fingers danced along Amy's jaw line, but she affected a wounded expression. "Is that all I am? A prize you won from Drusilla Devonshire?" The smile fought its way through Pierce's mask – it was downright irrepressible in the other woman's presence. "What would you have us do on this 'day off' thing you speak of?" "Go to the beach, enjoy one of the last warm days we'll get in the year and maybe hang out with Quinn? It's an improvement over me picking up the pieces of whoever you needed to be yesterday." "The beach?" Alex's brows lifted, her hand smoothing Campbell's hair. "You know how much I burn..." "Well, if you don't wanna, I mean, I'm sure we can—" "No, it's fine." Pierce pressed her lips to the tip of Amy's nose. "We may have to go shopping, but... I think I'd like that." She nuzzled her nose against Campbell's cheek. "And I think you probably want to put that cup down." "Better idea." Amy upturned the liquor down her throat, even as her partner drew her to the bed in a series of stumbling, staggering steps. "Al?" The question hung at the back of Campbell's throat as Pierce turned, sweeping her onto the pile of sheets and pillows that had the audacity to call itself a bed. Alex's response was lost in the tumult, but Amy pressed forward anyway. "You never answered me before." Alexandra ended up on top, and her smile down had no small amount of heat behind it. "Answered you about what?" Pierce quickly became frustrated with the much smaller buttons on the blouse. "Did you do anything bad today?" Amy's voice was quiet, fragile, equally afraid of spoiling the mood and that she'd get an answer. But she had to know. The Spider's cool gray gaze met the Red Raver's mismatched one. "No," she said quietly. "I didn't do anything bad." It was a lie – there was no way Pierce could know for certain what she did or did not do if she couldn't remember who she was – but it was a relief for Amy, who surrendered herself finally to her lover's ministrations. They say leopards can't change their spots. They say you can't teach an old dog a new trick. Alexandra Eloise Pierce would very much like to prove that "they" are wrong. This is the story of how she tried and failed, and how she failed and succeeded. Scene 1: The Body They were just pulling the body out of the water as Detective Helena Maddox came up the driveway. Her gunmetal gray 1997 Saturn SL coughed in protest as she brought it to a halt, cranking the driver's side window closed. As usual, it stubbornly refused to ascend the last two or so inches, and the streetlight drew a swath of orange across the detective's face as she killed the ignition. Whirling red and blue lights illuminated Maddox's as she climbed from the sedan. She was a tall woman, but not particularly imposing; it was the kind of height that said she should have played basketball, but the kind of laidback demeanor that promised she would have been eaten alive in any sort of competitive environment. Maddox ducked briefly back into the car, emerging with the tools of her trade – a grande mocha latte (no foam) in her left hand, and her dog-eared yellow legal pad in her right. The tablet was her sixth thus far in 2009, though she'd readily admit the pages were filled with more stray doodles than actual, case-related notes. In her defense, Helena really hated meetings. Quick, quiet steps, made quieter still by her Carolina blue Converse high-top sneakers, carried Maddox (Detective Maddox, she reminded herself) to the low-slung iron fence which encircled the pool area. Before she could touch a long-fingered hand to the gate, one of the uniforms (Great, they sent Mathis and Dixon, she thought bitterly) wheeled around, barking past a mustache so thick it looked like a slug died on his lip. "Hey, Beanpole." Ah, Dixon. "You're blocking the fuckin' light!" Maddox slide-stepped to the side, tucking her notepad under her arm as she opened the latch. "Nice to see you, too, Bernie." She smiled – Helena Maddox did not grin – as she tugged a #2 pencil out of the mahogany mess she dared to call a hairstyle. "What've we got?" "Unidentified Caucasian female." It was Dixon's partner, Perry Mathis, who looked up as they set the body onto the concrete. Unlike Dixon, Mathis was cool, calm, and professional as he read off the pertinent facts. "One of the neighbors apparently complained about the noise, and the real estate agent found him during a tour about forty-five minutes ago." "A tour?" Maddox's brows rose, but she had yet to put pencil to paper. "It's a model home." Mathis glanced over his shoulder as his partner haphazardly covered the waterlogged corpse with a blue plastic tarp that was just a little bit too short. The woman's feet stuck out, red toenails glittering dully in the wavering light from the pool. "Guy says it's real high-end stuff," he continued. "They show you the place, then let you design your own dream house." "Where is this guy?" Maddox was finally taking notes – although it would be more accurate to say she was taking scribbles. "I want to talk with him." "Sure thing, Beanpole," Dixon said as he joined them. "Guy's pretty shook up, though – says he ain't seen either of them before in his life." "Either of them?" Maddox looked up from the pad, her left brow quirking. She leaned against a post, lifting her coffee from the table. "You didn't mention there'd been anyone else here." "I was getting to that," Mathis said defensively. "It's some guy." Dixon's response was at best nonchalant. "He was out cold when we got here – got a bruise as big as my fist on his forehead. Figure someone KO'd him, then did the girl." "But not him?" Mathis' frown widened the furrows on his forehead. "It's just as likely they were in some kind of struggle – she hits him, then slips into the tub. Lover's quarrel, maybe." Maddox raised her hand. "Let's can the idle speculation until we know the facts," she ordered. "Do we even know who she is?" "Not like she can carry her wallet around in that outfit." Dixon looked back to the tarp, a half-smile latching onto his porcine features. "If you can even call it that." "You do realize you're ogling a dead body, right, Bernie?" Maddox hid her smile behind the plastic lid of her Starbucks. "She looks young enough to be your daughter, anyway." "Even if that was a problem for me, up close, she seems older." Dixon shrugged briefly. "She dresses younger, sure – all those things in her hair and whatnot. But the lines around her eyes and her mouth say different. She's the kind of girl that'll be carded until she's forty – real pedo bait." "Sometimes, you scare me," Mathis mumbled. "'Sometimes'?" Maddox grinned. "Well, we can't do much until Darryl brings the meat wagon around. If they don't live here, they had to get here somehow. Bernie, I want you to go door-to-door." "At eleven thirty? Everyone's sleeping." "Not everyone. I've only been up since six," she corrected. "If they didn't see anything, get a list of the vehicles they own. Maybe one of these cars parked on the street was hers and we can find out who she is. Perry, see if her clothes are stashed around somewhere – I doubt she was walking around dressed like that." "Why do I gotta wake people up and make a list of cars, and he gets to look for lacy underthings?" Dixon wasn't pouting, but it was close. "Don't know about Helena, but I'd be scared of what you'd do if you found them," Mathis said around a chuckle. He kept his eyes on the detective, however. "What are you going to do?" She was already moving to the tarp-covered body. "I'm going to make sure you guys didn't miss anything." Soon enough, the two uniformed officers were gone, but Helena Maddox didn't care; she crouched by the head, pulling the tarp back just far enough to get a good look at the girl. Dixon may have been a pervert, but he was right – the girl did look young, no more than twenty-two or twenty-three. But this close, it looked too put-together, too perfect. The barrettes that held her hair parted were topped with little pink flowers, and the water had left only the remnants of her make-up, but it looked like she'd intentionally undersold the look – just a tad of blush, maybe a little bit of lip gloss, all in soft pastels. Put her in the right clothes and she could easily pass for a teenager. The bikini she wore were definitely not the right clothes, however. The pink, stringy thing was almost certainly not designed to bathe publically in, since the wrong motion in either direction risked arrest for public exposure. The fact that the flimsy triangles covering her chest had the gall to display Hello Kitty made Maddox feel uncomfortably like she was watching kiddie porn, and she didn't even swing that way. "Pretty girl," she said to no one in particular, drawing on a pair of latex gloves. "What happened to you?" She knew she should probably wait for the coroner before examining the body, but Helena's curiosity had outstripped her good sense since she was a little girl, and Darryl was notorious about being late to a murder scene. She turned the girl's head to the side – no sign of head trauma, no bruises or abrasions. The warm water would probably make time of death difficult to pinpoint, and they'd probably have to wait for an autopsy to determine cause. It was still a strange case, and she tapped a considering finger against her cheek as she tried to piece together the puzzle. Her eyes snapped open as soon as her gloved finger touched her cheek, tilting her hand towards the light. There was something on her glove, some kind of putty, and when she glanced back to the body, she saw the indentation of her hand on the woman's face. Curious, Maddox wiped harder at the spot. Beneath the makeup, she found a plastic retainer – the kind used to keep open a piercing without a stud. A cursory look found at least three more such hidden holes, two in the right ear and one in her left nostril. Her brows knit, looking at the body with new eyes. "Who are you?" she mumbled. "That is a rather... complicated... question, I'm afraid." The new speaker startled Maddox, but the fact that she didn't recognize his voice that dropped her hand to the firearm holstered at her side. "Keep your hands where I can see them," she ordered. The man in the long coat complied, displaying both palms. "I'm not looking for any trouble." He was entirely unremarkable outside of that ankle-length leather duster. He was of average height, with plain farmboy features – flat, cornflower blue eyes; wavy, dishwater blond hair; a thin, unreadable smile. It was just the coat, the one that made him look like something out of a spaghetti western, as if his should be accompanied by an Ennio Maricone flourish. Detective Maddox did not move her hand from her hip. "What are you looking for?" The man's shoulders blurred in some semblance of a shrug. "I had to see for myself if it was true." Something in his voice – the note of sadness, perhaps – caused her to drop her gun hand to her side. "Who was she?" The man stepped forward slowly, his loafers barely a whisper on the concrete. "She is... she was a lot of people," he said. "A friend. An enemy. A lost little girl." Momentarily, he seemed to be somewhere else, eyes drifting past the girl's body. "Do you..." He cleared his throat to dislodge the frog. "Do you know yet what happened here?" Helena's mane of hair shook with her head. "Not until the autopsy. If you have information..." He straightened, his smile turned rueful. "You wouldn't believe me if I did." "If you interfere with this investigation, I will put you in handcuffs and take you down to the station." Her hand inched towards the pistol again. "That would make us enemies, Detective Maddox – and I assure you, neither of us wants that." "I never told you my name." "It is my job to know these things, Detective." The smile thinned into an unreadable line again, the hint of teeth showing. "A job I am quite good at, believe me." "What's your part in all of this?" "Right now, I am merely an interested observer." She thought he looked sadder somehow as he looked down at the woman's body. "Eventually, I will be the man who answers your questions." "I have questions now." The man looked up, and that enigmatic smile hadn't quite crawled back onto his lips. "The questions I have answers for are ones you have yet to even consider." "I've had about enough of your 'I know something you don't know' act." Maddox's police-issue sidearm came free into her hand, but she kept it at her side, pointed to the ground. "Either you tell me what you know now, or we can do this the hard way, and you tell me at the station. I know you think you're amusing, but that girl is dead, and you haven't even told me your name." The man crossed the distance between them swiftly. "I have been unconscionably rude, I know." He dipped his hand into the coat's inside pocket. Maddox still pulled her pistol up, but she didn't point it at him. "I am sorry about that," he continued, only a ghost of his smile left on his lips. "Just keep your hands where I can see them." He lifted his hands into the air, both empty. "Sometimes, it doesn't mater what you see, but rather what you don't see." He flicked his wrist, and a small cardstock rectangle sprouted between his first two fingers. She would have sworn on a stack of Bibles that it had been empty. "What's that?" It was a silly question in retrospect; of course she knew what a business card looked like. The man did not insult her with an answer, merely extending his hand to allow her to pluck it free. "Jonas," the card read in simple, 12-point Times New Roman font. "[detail withheld]." Her first thought came, unbidden – where the hell is area code 246? Detective Maddox looked up from the card, her left brow quirking again. "That's it?" Jonas smiled wanly. "You'll know when to use it." "How do I know I can trust you?" "You don't." For some reason, the idea amused him greatly. "But you don't even know what you think you know." His grin tightened. "But they say the first taste is free, so you may want to check her hair." "Her hair?" At this rate, Helena's brow was going to break in half. Jonas simply stood aside, gesturing towards the body. After a moment, Maddox relented, tucking her piece away as she passed the man. He watched impassively as she knelt beside the body, replacing her latex gloves. It did not take long for Maddox's search to produce results. At first, it was just a single bobby pin, but then she found a second. A third and a fourth quickly followed. Soon, she'd amassed a small collection – right before those blond locks came loose in her hands. It was a remarkably well-made wig, probably hand-crafted from human hair. Beneath, the girl wore a nylon wig cap, which Maddox slipped off as well. A tumble of shoulder-length curls sprung from the woman's scalp, dyed a deep purple. "Who is she?" Helena looked up, both brows raised. But Jonas was nowhere to be found. "Who is she?" She shouted it the second time, the wig spilling through her fingers. "That's what we're here to find out." Darryl appeared finally, coming around the corner with a frown immediately appearing on his hangdog features. "Who moved the body? Did you people just graduate from the academy?" Helena Maddox wasn't listening, however, instead racing to the gate. There was no sign of her mysterious benefactor, only a sliver of egg white cardstock crumpled in her hand by which to remember him. She swore silently she would get to the bottom of this, if it were the last thing she did. Scene 2: The Beach The sun hung fat and happy over the beach, burning away the clouds to shed its light on the sand below. While much of the country prepared for the fall's first cold snap, the beaches in California were still livable – if a visitor knew where and when to go. Only a twenty-minute ride outside of Oakland, the secluded stretch of sand was not as crowded as it had been in the summer months, but it had a few lingering surfers enjoying the waves the breeze only blew higher, and some few spectators in it just for the view. The two women lingering outside the changing room on the boardwalk were almost certainly in neither category. "Guys, come on," the younger one whined, turning down her paperback's page corner. Her dark hair fell nearly halfway down her back when it was left to fly free, but the wind forced her to wear a ridiculous, floppy straw hat, but she would rather be teased mercilessly than comb out her hair when she returned home. The other woman was just a few years the girl's senior, her thumbs tucked into her belt loops as she waited impatiently. Despite her best efforts to hide it with a lacy cover-up, the small redhead's tattoos still peeked out along the backs of her hands and up her neck, and she squinted down the pathetic, huddled mass of shops, a retail black hole. They were Quinn Gregory and Amy Campbell, daughter and lover to the third woman, still hidden in the changing room. "God, she's slow," Campbell grumbled. "You just want to see if she'll wear it," Quinn offered. "No, actually, I just want a piece of pizza and... well, to see if she'll wear it." "She will." The lanky teen levered her sunglasses up onto her head, opening her well-worn copy of A Game of Thrones. "You asked her to." "She will do no such thing." It was the third woman's voice – a soft thing that always bordered on a whisper – that emerged from behind the propped-open changing room door first. "I look ridiculous, and I'm just going to change back." "You would've worn it to play a character," Amy noted. "And I'll bet almost anything that you don't look ridiculous, so nut up and get out here!" "If I was playing a character," the third woman whined. "I would have a blond wig, some silicon inserts, and you wouldn't be here. I am not the type of girl that wears these kinds of things, Amy. If you'll give me twenty minutes, I can get what I'd need to be that type of—" "Everybody else in the history of California has worn a bathing suit of some sort, and you're not going to be the soccer mom on the beach with a goddamn one-piece, so suck it up." Campbell sighed. "There's no 'type' of girl for this – it's not a string thong or anything, Al. Don't make me start listing the worse things you've done for me." It took a moment for the reply to come, and when it did, Alexandra Pierce's usually frozen-over tone was meek, even pathetic. "Okay, but if you laugh, I'm changing." "And if Quinn laughs, I'll punch her." Pierce didn't see the wink Amy used to put the lie to her words. The changing room door swung inward; at first, it only moved a little. The redhead outside the door busied nervous hands with her sunglasses, an outrageous pair that made her look like she was an extra from Blade Runner or Mad Max. She ended up with a pose she hoped looked casual, shades down her nose far enough that she could peer over them. Nearby, Amy's de facto daughter managed to stifle her smile at the sight of perhaps the brashest woman in her entire industry squirming. Quinn still looked over the top of her book as her mother yanked the door open and stepped out onto the boardwalk. Her rust-red hair was pulled back in a tight tail, but that wasn't what Amy was looking at. The swimsuit was simple – just a string halter top, tied at the nape of her neck and a basic bottom, both in a shade of mango orange that set off her alabaster skin well. Her blush was almost the same shade, though, running up along the sides of her neck. Campbell and Gregory were silent for a moment, and Pierce's thin lips turned down slightly. "You hate it. I knew it." "The only bad thoughts in my head right now are nothing negative toward that outfit." Amy smiled beneath her sunglasses, inching toward the taller woman. "Can't speak for Quinn, but was that so hard?" Alex slid next to Amy, her smile small. "I don't... I told you, I am not the 'hot girl in bikini' type. But... no, I guess it wasn't too hard." She still nervously scratched at the back of her neck. "I just..." She forced both hands to her sides. "Don't think you're getting this often." "You look great, Mom," Quinn said. "Really. Not in the way Amy thinks, but... you two need to remember, I'm not old enough to bail you out if you get busted for public indecency. Now..." She slapped the thick book closed. "Can we please get something to eat? I am starving." Campbell's hands replaced Alexandra's on the back of her neck. "You're gorgeous, you're at the beach and appropriately dressed for it. You don't need to be a 'type' for that... now, let's feed the girl before she resorts to cannibalism. Where's the... huh, must've left the bag in the car." "I can get it." The offer was out of Alex's mouth before she realized it. "You two go on ahead, and I'll—" "Oh, no." Amy shook her head. "I can live without, and you'll just hide in the car, which defeats the entire purpose of this exercise." "'Exercise'?" Sculpted brows rose as Pierce fished out small, functional sunglasses that contrasted Amy's showy ones. The trio began their slow pace down the boardwalk, Quinn in the lead and the two redheads following side-by-side, one step away from hand-in-hand. The teen tried not to roll her eyes too much. "Well, I know I'm getting a workout," Campbell mumbled. "I just don't know if I like the front or the ba—" This late in the year, the crowd might not have been large, but three women walking together, especially given how close the two in the rear were, attracted some attention. Amy's head snapped around, lip curling. "Hey! Hey! Eyes back in your head, buddy!" "Amy!" Quinn scolded. "He was... he was checking your mom out!" Amy's lower lip jutted out a little. "Don't you find that just a little gross?" "Amy, really." Pierce took the smaller woman's hand in hers; the motion almost immediately quieted the Red Raver's anger. "You don't have to do that. It's—" The easily recognizable guitar riff of Johnny Rivers' "Secret Agent Man" interrupted them, drawing another frown from the smallest of the three. "I thought you were going to leave that in the car," Campbell pouted. "And how do you even have a place to put it? I can't even bring my purse." The levity was wasted, however, as Alex stopped in her tracks, withdrawing her iPhone from the pouch at her side and lifting it to her ear. "Pierce," she said by way of greeting. "Seriously, you brought your phone down to the beach." She grumbled, turning to Quinn. "We'll never escape that damn phone. I can't believe..." "Uh." The girl tipped her head at her mother, her brows lifting significantly. Amy glanced back over her shoulder, just as the woman they called the Spider pressed her left hand to her ear. "Say again," Pierce hissed, taking a few strides down the boardwalk, which moved her away from the hamburger joint they'd nearly reached – but also away from her companions. Amy's features went pale, her semi-smile drowned in the moment. She regarded Alex seriously for a moment before turning back to Quinn; as good as Pierce was at concealing her emotions, something was off. "You, uh... you should head for the car. I'll meet you back there in a few, okay?" "I... can I at least get some fries?" The girl looked longingly towards the closed door of the small fast food place. She just didn't feel it, not in her heart, at least not as much as she probably should. The Hand's sports psychologist (the very same Sarah Riley consoling Kathryn Shaw) used terms like "sociopathic tendencies" and "antisocial personality disorder," but Riley still cashed the checks, so she didn't see too much of an issue. The only thing Alex knew was, all things being equal, she only cared what one person thought, and that inked-up redhead was wending her way across the room below, replenishing her supply of hard booze from the bar. Ashe started another story (Lord, not the Buenos Aires story, Pierce thought. It always seems to end with Roderick singing, "Don't Cry For Me, Argentina"), but the woman they rightly called the Spider in the Web wasn't paying anything more than cursory attention. She was caught up in the way Amy Campbell walked across the room – the casual swagger of her stride, the glance over her shoulder. The eyeroll at the white-clad River Rat as he slid a card facedown across the table to the smaller man. The tip of her head, as if checking whether the coast was clear before she slid a knee onto the barstool, snagging one bottle and then a second – just to be safe. Not to mention the way that pair of dress slacks fit as she stretched to snag a pair of glasses. Everything about the diminutive redhead entranced Pierce; she felt like a cobra caught up in the song of a snake charmer. Campbell exuded life – she sent a thrill through Alex's system like no high she'd ever experienced. When Amy stopped to embrace Kathryn Shaw (to say the two were heated rivals in the world of professional wrestling would be the grossest of understatements), the Spider felt the first tears of the night form behind her empty, gray gaze – not for the sorrow Shaw might be feeling for the loss of her step-sister, but because Amy was sad. Campbell stopped halfway up the steps, glancing back at the morose, moping lot of them. Ashe's story lost its steam when the only member of the audience who would be considered attentive, Julissa Minorez, turned her attention to the card game. Lane Stevens triumphantly tossed down a pair of red eights with a howl of victory. "Hah!" Stevens hollered at SCCW's LiveWire Champion (now the only member of the group toting around championship gold). "Now you're stuck with the Old Maid!" Lane's gaze slid to the nubile Latina in the skimpy black dress. "Er. No offense, right?" Campbell took the rest of the steps two at a time, crossing swiftly to Pierce's secluded little nook. "You okay?" Amy's brows knitted with concern – it was a question she'd asked repeatedly in the days since they heard the news. Alexandra always gave the same answer. "I'm fine," she said, turning briefly to the side to favor the smaller woman with a tiny, tired smile. Amy arranged the three classes carefully on the glass-topped table that sat before the Spider's perch. "No, you're gorgeous," It was corny, but Amy made a point of smiling at her. "But you look less than fine. You have for a while now. Too bad you don't have anybody to keep you company, or kiss and make it better." Her brows furrowed, the smile dimming to a semi-sarcastic smirk. "Or is that what I'm for? Jeez, I forget my duties in this crowd. I've somehow managed to be one of the most sober in the room, and Ashe has me beat for ugliest and... and this isn't helping at all, is it?" "It's you helping more than you know," Pierce said. "I just... I'm playing it out in my head, and I feel like I'm missing something." Her lips turn down in discomfort. "Mostly, I feel like I would rather be trying on bathing suits in dingy changing rooms for a week." She dropped her hand to Amy's leg. "Thank you. For being here. I know you and Lauren weren't... on good terms." "Is that the technical, Alex Pierce way of saying she tried to strangle me with a pair of handcuffs?" Alex dropped her head. "I'm not trying to defend her. It's just... these people knew the real Lauren, and I don't know what to tell them." "What do you think they're expecting you to say?" "That we will find out who did this. That we will make them pay." She waved a hand through the air. "Lots of 'fire and doom' stuff." "I'm no genius," Amy shrugged as she filled each of the glasses on the table overfull. "But if it was an attack on you, somebody would flaunt it. They'll eventually traipse it in front of you, try cutting as deeply as they can and pour salt on it. If *I* know that, they probably do. Just... you don't have to make them pay. They're suffering enough as killers." Pierce closed her eyes briefly. "I wish it was that easy," she sighed, flowing effortlessly to her feet. "They're looking for someone to blame. In time, it will help them get through this to be doing something." Amy stopped her with a hand on her hip. "Just don't forget that you need to get through it, too," she said quietly. "I know. Don't go far." Campbell's brief smile nod sent Pierce – now definitely in full-on Desade mode – into motion. She drifted to the wooden railing, just the fingertips of her left hand touching the polished mahogany. For all the names she used and all the roles she played, the one she was needed for now was the one she was arguably born to play: a leader. Pierce did not have to ask for the attention of those below; the simple act of raising her half-full flute of wine lifted every eye in the house. "To absent friends," she intoned. "To absent friends," they repeated. The glass came down, its stem resting atop the wood. "Lauren – Savant – was the best student I have ever had," Pierce said quietly. "She was lost to us some time ago through events largely my fault." The corners of her lips turn downward in a very small, very brief frown. "You have long ago learned to expect me to stand here and promise we will succeed, that they will never know what hit them." She paused, dropping her head. A stray lock of rust-red hair fought free of her ruthless ponytail, curling along her porcelain features, rendering them briefly girlish. "I cannot do that on this night." When she looked up, those gray eyes were glassy with tears that she couldn't figure out how to shed. "Tonight, I am just like you. Tonight, I have to admit, I am lost. I wish I had found a way to make her understand, and I want to make the world pay for robbing me of the chance to fix this. To mend what should never have been broken." The silence in the room was a palpable thing, like they could taste it. "But there is no one for us to blame. No force we might attack. No vengeance we may wreak. We are left only to raise our glass to absent friends." "To absent friends." The response was louder the second time. "We win all too often, my friends. We take and we take and do not conceive there may consequences for our actions. And so when they come, we think them just a small obstacle. That we will just win tomorrow. Perhaps we may, but tonight is not about what we will do next or whom we can hurt." Pierce's stone-gray eyes flicked to the memorial, a gift sent by Wyatt Connors, a man none of them would call "friend". "This night is about her. Lauren believed in me when no one else did. She risked everything she had for my freedom, scorning her disbelieving friends because... because she thought I was worth it." At some point while Desade was speaking, Amy Campbell slipped down the stairs, joining the rest of them. It was to she whom Alex glanced before continuing. "I do not know if she was right or wrong to believe it. I only know that all of us – each and every one – owes her this day, this moment to remember when times were different. So I raise my glass to absent friends." "To absent friends." Campbell's voice joined the others now, a strident, tear-strained voice among many. "We remember you." Alex raised the glass again, her smile a sad thing. "We honor you, our absent friend." They lifted their glasses again as one, longer now. Somewhere down among them, a sob escaped. "I'll miss you, Lori," Pierce whispered, her lips barely moving. In the wake of Alex's speech, the silence was nearly oppressive; it was like the air had been sucked out of the room. Several of them were teared up, most of them had their heads down. Amy Campbell whispered a soft prayer to any god who might still be listening, her hands knotted awkwardly at her waist. It took everything Amy had not to race up the stairs to be with Alex. Julissa Minorez found her hand in Jadian Bridden's; she would never admit that she'd put it there. Kathryn Shaw's shoulders shook with quiet sobs, a well-manicured hand lifting her handkerchief to her eyes, hidden behind the lacy cloth. Roderick Ashe wept openly, clapping his hand to Shaw's back as he crossed to the bar. Sarah Riley wrapped her arm around the veiled brunette's shoulders, tugging her close; behind her glasses, Sarah's eyes were wet, her face lifted to Pierce's perch. Lane Stevens set down his tumbler, reaching for a shot glass to replace it. His eyes never left the redhead standing above them, either, even when his guest pinched his arm to try to claim her attention. Despite similar expressions, however, the River Rat and the Good Doctor had different reasons behind their studies. Above them, Pierce herself was stone-faced, never lowering her gaze to her employees. She didn't look away from that portrait, even as the party resumed. It wasn't until Amy breezed up the steps that Alex even stirred. Campbell's fingers walked across the taller woman's shoulderblades, a small smile on the brash woman's lips. "Hey," Campbell said. "Hey." Pierce's voice was still a deathly whisper, the words nearly muttered into the mouth of her wineglass. "What you did..." Amy tilted just her head down to the assorted sociopaths, thugs, and ne'er-do-wells below, but she wouldn't move her mismatched eyes from the Spider's stoic features. "That meant a lot to them, you know." Alexandra nodded just a little, turning her glass upside-down into her mouth, but she didn't respond. "And I know..." Amy proceeded, in spite of the fact that she knew it was a bad idea. "I know it means a lot to—" "Can we just... go?" Alex said, her voice growing increasingly strained. Amy's brows furrowed a little, however unintentional, when she tried to study the Director's face. "I... yeah. Absolutely. C'mon." Pierce set the glass down on the table. "I know we should stay, but—" "Say no more." Amy raised both hands. "Front or the back?" Alex peered over Campbell's shoulder, her brows knitted so softly, an onlooker would have had to be specifically looking for it to notice – but it was one of the half-dozen or so ticks the smaller redhead learned to deal with over the last year. "Back door it is," she whispered, taking her partner by the arm and guiding her down the hall. It would be ten minutes before the rest of them noticed their leader was gone. * * * * A half-hour (and a stop at the drive-thru liquor store) later, Alexandra Pierce displayed the skills she'd survived for years in the back alleys of Budapest and the slums of Singapore to break into her own condo without disturbing her teenaged daughter. "Shh," Alex said, wincing at the click of the lock. "We don't want to wake her." "A missile attack couldn't wake her." Amy offered another small smile - more of a grin, really - when she inched closer to Pierce, pressing gentle lips to the side of her face. "Are you sure this is where you want to be right now? I won't be mad if you need to be there for them, and I hope you know that." Alex smiled briefly over her shoulder, easing open the door. "I need to be here for me." Soft, tiptoed steps carried her across the hardwood floor. Campbell stopped in the kitchen, drawing her prize from the paper bag – a bottle of Cabo Wabo tequila. She glanced to the side; Quinn's door was closed, and not even a sliver of light came from the crack beneath it. "I'll be right there," she called softly. "Just grabbing some stuff in here." Alex raised her hand without turning, padding into the master bedroom. Amy didn't turn on the light, finding the cabinet blindly. She snagged her favorite plastic cup (a collectible Return of the Jedi glass she'd bought off eBay), which she quickly filled (some might argue over-filled) with alcohol. "You know," she said as she headed into Pierce's room. "Roderick smells bad, and I mean bad, like potato skins and day-old chicken, but he seemed to really—" When she turned into the room, the Spider was nowhere to be seen. "Al?" A quiet sob nearby attracted Campbell's attention, and she darted across the room. Alexandra Pierce sat on the cold tile floor of the bathroom, her knees drawn up against her chest. Her hair fell over her face like blood from a wound, and she looked up, cheeks drawn in. Tears leaked from her eyes. "I'm crying," she whispered. "I'm crying and I don't know why and I never cry. I..." Her smile was a dismal failure. "I miss her, Amy... I know I shouldn't and she betrayed me and... and she tried to kill you, and I'm so... just so—" New sobs shook her like a leaf. Her head dropped atop her hands and she could no longer stem the flow. Amy merely nodded, sliding down to the floor beside Pierce. "Here," she said softly. "C'mere." She drew the taller woman onto her shoulder, doing her best to smooth the hair out of Pierce's face. "I'm here. It's okay... I'm not going anywhere." Sitting there on the floor, clutching the shirt of her lover, Alexandra Pierce didn't look like much, she didn't look like she could hurt a fly, nevermind terrorize a federation of talented men trained in combat. Amy Campbell wrapped her arms around the taller woman, her chin atop Alex's head, and she cried too, soft hidden tears. Right then, she wasn't the brash yearlong champion with the razor tongue. They say these two women are the most dangerous people in all of a very dangerous place. But even they have their moments of weakness, few and far between as they may be. Even they are human.
