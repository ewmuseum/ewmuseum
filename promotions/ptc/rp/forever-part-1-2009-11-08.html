Forever, Part 1

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 - 6 - MICHAEL Callie’s song filtered down through the mansion, gracefully gliding down the twin staircases, through hundreds of crystal fingers dangling from the chandelier above the lobby, softly floating through the hallways that branched off towards the kitchen, den, and bedrooms. Every father believed his daughter to be the most beautiful woman in the world, both physically and emotionally. Callie’s voice was honestly one of the few things Michael would ever label as ‘breathtaking’. Given his natural inclination to promote her voice above all others, even after much thought, he could nary think of a voice that came remotely close. Michael Mangold stood in the doorway of the guestroom, listening. He was on the floor beneath his daughter as she sang “Swing Low, Sweet Chariot” to perfection, trying to lull Arya to sleep. He had to hand it to Clinton — the mansion was designed beautifully, it seemed to only increase the harmonics of Callie’s voice. The echoes in the hallway gave her voice a slightly angelic effect, without adding any distortion to leech any of the beauty from the song. He was also thankful that Clinton stood just as tall as he did — six foot, six inches — which let Michael stand straight as an arrow and not worry about hitting his head on the doorframe, like he did at most houses. He could stand tall in the Sage mansion, never slouching down, and that was something he really admired. Admiration aside, Michael’s anger towards Clinton for leaving Callie and Arya was still simmering. He’d deal with that later. As Callie’s voice trailed off, Michael could visualize exactly what was occurring on the floor above: She was standing at the foot of her bed, carefully leaning over to see if Arya was awake or if the lullaby had enchanted the little princess into sleep. The visualization technique was something the agency had taught Michael decades earlier, and it was an old habit to break. A moment later, Callie started to sing “Hush, Little Baby” and only made it two sentences into the song before she faded into a sudden silence, followed by a quick sob and sniffling. It was at the mention of “Papa” in the tune where her voice broke a minute, before trailing off. There wasn’t a punch in the world as powerful as that moment, Michael knew immediately he needed to comfort his daughter. Callie’s sobs lasted no more than a few seconds before she started to sing “You are My Sunshine”. Michael started down the hallway, quietly crossing the marble-tile lobby floor, then up the steps that led to the master bedroom. The large chandelier had been dimmed down low, and the early autumn sunset cast the lobby in a soft purple, his own shadow ascended the staircase just ahead of him. A bar of yellow light fell across the top of the steps, slashing at the wall. The master bedroom door was only open a few inches, but it was enough to let Michael peek in and see his daughter. Just as he had expected, Callie was sitting on the corner of the bed, hunched over the small crib. A few strands of her light brown hair had fallen loose from the pony-tail, swaying freely as she rocked the crib only slightly. Michael was used to being quiet, although he was tall he wasn’t as heavy as you’d think, but all those years of spying had taught him to be light on his feet. Ugh. I’m spying on my own daughter, he thought to himself as he watched her through the slit in the double-doors. It’ll never end. Even at 62, I haven't stopped, he winced at the thought. Old habits die hard. “You are my sunshine, my only sunshine. You make me happy when skies are gray. You’ll never know dear, how much I love you. Please don’t take my sunshine away.” Breathtaking, absolutely breathtaking. At the end of the verse, Michael stepped into the bedroom. Callie looked up at once, and without breaking from the song she placed a single finger across her mouth and ‘shushed’ her father. Still keeping the crib rocking gently, she smiled and continued the song: “In all my dreams, dear, you seem to leave me. When I wake my, poor heart pains. So when you come back, and make me happy, I'll forgive you dear, I'll take all the blame.” He watched Callie realize the weight of the lyrics, and perhaps that it was another bad choice. Her eyes glistened. He could see a tear welling up in the corner of both eyes, but never falling. Michael walked over and sat beside his daughter as she checked on Arya once more. The baby was sleeping, breathing in long heavy breaths. Her black hair was twisted into curls with little pink bowties. Father and daughter sat in silence, watching Arya sleep. Michael could remember watching Callie sleep when she was the same age. She’s a good mother, just like her mom. Like her father, Michael thought, and instantly felt foolish for lying to himself. Somewhere in a Philadelphia penthouse, Carter was proof of that lie. He shook his head to clear the image of his son from it, and rested his chin on Callie’s shoulder, “You doing ok?” Her whisper matched his, “Yeah, I guess.” “He’s only been gone two days, honey. He’ll be back.“ Callie sat there, watching Arya sleep, absent-mindedly twisting the wedding ring round and round. “I’m not so sure, Dad. He signed all the money over, damn near everything. He left us...” At the moment she said ‘us’ Michael noticed that she squeezed the ring a little bit. “I’m going to find him, talk with him.” “Dad, don’t. Please.” “You’re no longer Callie Mangold, you’re Callie Sage.” Michael said, putting emphasis on the Sage. “I like Clinton, I really do. I just think this whole mess has gotten him mixed up inside. He’s confused. When someone kills another person, there are psychological effects, Cal. He’ll be back.” She sighed and didn’t reply. Michael watched as her blue eyes continued to hover around the room, then finally settle on Arya. It appeared as if Callie would find some resolution in all of the mess, but once she laid her eyes on Arya, it would all vanish. As if she pictured a future where Arya grew up without a father. She twisted her wedding band around and around. “Clinton is aware of what’s etched inside your ring. More importantly, what’s etched inside of his. I’m just going to have a little talk with him, once I find him. I promise you, sweetheart, he’ll be back. There isn’t any other man on Earth that I trust more to protect you than Clinton. He needs to understand that.” Callie flinched at the implied reference about her brother and his capabilities, “Good luck. He could be anywhere by now.” “You know I have friends, Cal. They know how to find people. I’ll just make a few calls.” Michael Mangold stood up and towered over his daughter. She looked up, her eyes still shimmering. Callie managed to smile, but they both knew it was fake. “He’ll be back by tomorrow night, or the night after. He might just need some time to clear his head.” Callie stood up and hugged her father. The embrace was long and hard, and when she finally let go he could feel the dampness across his shoulder where those unspent tears had finally found a resting place. He couldn’t take the sight of her like this, it reminded him too much of her mother all those years ago. His mistake, being repeated. He had to find Clinton Sage, and right now. “I love you, Dad. Thanks. For being here, for everything.” Michael kissed his only daughter on the forehead, cupped her cheeks in his hands and looked her directly in the eyes. He didn’t need to reply, he didn’t need to say another word. She could see the resolve in his eyes. He consoled her anyhow, “I’m going to find him. Tonight. I’ll be out late, touching base with old friends. Fire up the laptop, tap away at that fantasy novel of yours. Before you finish another two chapters, Clinton will be home. I promise.” Her smile was brief, but genuine. - 7 - CLINTON Staring down at his wedding ring, Clinton Sage could feel the word etched along the inner surface of the platinum band: three syllables, a word defined as 'a limitless time without interruption'. Sitting at the lop-sided dinner table in his tiny apartment, Clinton hadn’t even made it a year as a husband. When he first met Callie, he was filled with eagerness and joy, but they have since vanished and have been replaced with a hollow, empty feeling. A void, a black hole. The darkness. His heart yearned to be with his wife and daughter, but his mind told him otherwise, They’re safer without you in their lives. She was a little more than two miles away, probably asleep in their bed. Her bed, now, Clinton thought as he sighed to himself. He couldn’t sleep. The bed that came with the apartment was well-used, the springs too soft in the center and too hard around the edges. Not to mention it was a single, so Clinton had to curl his six-and-a-half foot frame just to fit on it when he slept. Instead of lying beside his wife with Arya snoring softly at their feet, he was sitting above a college bar in an old apartment that stunk from a mixture of cigarette smoke and various colognes that filtered up through the vents. Clinton looked away from his wedding ring, back towards the paper that lay atop the table before him: An application into the GTT7 tournament. Since the moment he met Callie, Clinton never felt the need to wrestle again. He had accomplished nearly everything in the sport, had beaten the likes of Angelo Deville, Jason Snow, and many others that some wrestlers revered as legends. Clinton remembered the title reigns, the blood, the sweat, and the misguided rage that had consumed him throughout his entire wrestling career. When Callie smiled her way into his life, it felt as if the greatest weight had been lifted from his shoulders. The desire to prove himself inside the squared circle had died. Before Callie, even with unmatched accolades and unrivaled success, Clinton Sage felt the need to constantly prove his dominance. To be the man everyone wanted to hate because not only was he smarter than everyone, but the talent matched the intelligence. Now, even after leaving Callie and Arya behind – far from the dangers of what Clinton might do and to whatever depths he might reach, the need to wrestle was still missing. It hadn’t returned when he signed over all of his millions to her. Not when he signed the mansion over, nor the cars. The need to beat other men to a bloody pulp had left him, but he needed the money. Clinton needed a way to get out of this apartment, to move far away to keep his wife and daughter safe from whatever demons still found residence deep within his soul. GTT would be starting in three weeks, and Clinton Sage would be there. For the first time, he didn’t want to be there, but he needed to be there. Looking over the application, Clinton realized he had left only one section blank. He hadn’t been aware of it as he penned in his name, height, weight, entrance music, and residence. Nor his championship history, or past affiliations. But upon quick review, Clinton noticed that he had left the section blank that dealt with his nickname. For the longest time, it had been ‘The Seventh Sin’. Wrath. Clinton knew that was a different man, someone who chose to beaten and bloody anyone in his path regardless of their intentions. A man who stood alongside that little shit Lane Stevens and ordered him to do unspeakable actions to Jonathan Rhine, Jacob McKail, John Covel, or you name it. Clinton wondered what Lane was up to these days, and from whom he was taking orders. He’s probably dead, Clinton thought. Lane always had a penchant for getting himself in too deep with the wrong people, not possessing the talent or credibility to wrench his way free without the aid of someone like Clinton, as he had done in days past. Clinton twisted the wedding band, wondering if he should leave the nickname blank. Instead, he slowly pulled the ring off and looked at the small pale circle that wrapped itself around his finger. Even when he tried to take it off, to forget the word etched inside, there was still proof of his failure on display for all to see. Clinton Sage placed his wedding ring on the table, and penciled in ‘The Seventh Sin’ on the application. Only a few days earlier, he had shown flickers of that old fury, the unmatched rage. Wrath was as apt as it ever would be — be it inside a wrestling ring or alleyway. * * * * * When someone pounded on the door to his apartment at 3am, Clinton expected to see a drunken college student standing there, mistaking his apartment for someone else’s. But when he peered out through the peephole and saw the silver hair of his father-in-law, Clinton yanked the door open immediately and stood there with his mouth agape. “Close your mouth, son, you look simple.” Michael Mangold said as he stepped past Clinton, a six-pack of Heineken in his right hand. Sleep still clouded Clinton’s thoughts, “H-how did you find—“ “I used to work for a government agency, didn’t Callie ever tell you that?” Watching his father-in-law gingerly walk across the apartment, look out through the window for a moment, before turning back and sitting at the tiny dinner table, Clinton simply shook his head. Michael nodded slightly and smiled at the notion that his daughter hadn’t talked too much about her father’s past, “Atta girl.” “CIA? FBI?” Ignoring the question, “Sit, please. We need to talk.” Michael ordered, prying the caps off of two bottles of Heineken. He kicked out the only other chair from underneath the table so Clinton could sit, and Clinton found himself walking over and sitting before he really understood the weight of the meeting. It became evident when Michael stared down at the wedding ring, which was placed atop the GTT application. Taking a long gulp of beer, Michael let Clinton get comfortable — and then uncomfortable due to the wait — before speaking, “So tell me why you left, as simply as you can.” Clinton stared down at the wedding ring. Through it, past it. Beyond the etching, back to the day when he and Callie stood across from each other and placed the bands on each other’s fingers, “I’m dangerous.” “Walking across the street is dangerous, Clinton. So is playing chicken with an oncoming car. Or wearing a Cowboys jersey to an Eagles game.” Clinton stared long enough that the reflection of the apartment disappeared in the silver face of the wedding ring, replaced by the day Arya was born. That silly Dr. Roebling, the nurses cleaning Arya, and Callie looking exhausted. Clinton could see it all replaying again, see his own smile. His happy future. “I killed two men with my bare hands. That’s the sort of dangerous that shouldn’t be around Callie and Arya.” Michael took another swig of beer, “You only killed one with your bare hands, and it was self defense. You shot the other one through his kidney and his lung. They were addicts, son, they were putting the well-being of Arya and yourself behind their need to get high. You didn’t do anything wrong.” Clinton didn't respond, instead he finally took a sip of his beer. The image in the ring was suddenly replaced by the corpse of Roman Kern, waking up from death after Clinton used the Vekoma Dagger to wish him back to life. He could feel those emotions again — the need to kill Roman with his bare hands just as he had killed the other mugger, Ronnie, rather than a bullet doing it for him. To see if the darkness inside of him would dissipate, if the anger would dissolve away into nothing. He could see Roman's death-clouded eyes, the cleft lip sucking in air as Clinton choked the life out of him again. This is why I left them, Michael. These horrible things you couldn’t possibly understand. He wondered if there was a smudge on his wedding ring from where Roman’s dead skin might’ve smeared the polish. Michael seemed to sense Clinton’s anger, “Look, calm down. I’m going to be very honest with you, and tell you things that need to be said: You’re a good man. I know all about your past, I know what horrors you’ve seen through that goddamned cult, I know all the things you went through during your career as a professional wrestler.” Clinton watched as Callie’s father readjusted himself in the small chair, and continued, “We've all looked beyond that bullshit. The cult thing was never your fault, it was just who your parents were. The wrestling…I can’t begin to fathom why you did half the stuff you did, but that’s all in the past. When Callie came down to Baltimore and told me she had fallen in love with this amazing man, like any father, I was curious. She raved, told me about how two often traded the chore of cooking each other breakfast. She told me about how you gave her time to write, even if she wasn’t getting anywhere professionally with it. And she told me these things, Clinton, with a smile across her face.” Michael had leaned forward and pointed the head of the beer bottle at Clinton when he said the word ‘smile’. His voice lowered to a whisper for no reason whatsoever, “Son, a father loves to hear these things about the man his daughter has fallen in love with. It's a little scary at first, knowing she doesn't look at me as the one to keep her safe anymore. Because I know I won’t be here forever, and someone else has to take this job.” They both glanced down at the wedding band, then back at each other. “Michael, you don’t understand—“ Michael interrupted with a raised palm, “Yes I do. Unfortunately, you and I have something in common besides our height and Callie’s affection.” Clinton felt like he was looking into a mirror in which his reflection was 30 years older. An emptiness appeared in Michael’s eyes just as it had appeared in his own over the last few days. The same bubbling hatred buried deep below the surface, the uncertainty of who you truly are. “I made a mistake, before Callie was born. Left my wife for a few months, much like you’ve done. Except this was when Carter was four, going on five. When I found my senses and came back, Sharon welcomed me back with open arms. Because she loved me, Clinton, because we were supposed to be together. Mistakes happen, I’ll be the first to admit, but mistakes often breed other mistakes. And my biggest mistake was thinking I needed to be away from my wife and son, to clear my head from the things I’d seen working for the Agency. It cost me the love of my son, and even you know where that path led.” Clinton sat there, wondering exactly what Carter Mangold was doing at this moment. Everyone in the tri-state area knew of the sway Carter had on many high-ranking officials in Philadelphia, New Jersey, and Delaware. He was called ‘King Carter’ for a reason, and had lopped off a few heads much like the kings of old. Michael Mangold picked up the wedding ring and pulled open Clinton’s hand, placing it in his palm, “Look, I know you’re screwed up in the head right now. You can take a few days, I get that. I really, truly do. But Callie is a mess, Clint. She can’t do this by herself. I’m here telling you not to make the same mistakes I’ve made, but instead to learn from them. I won’t be here forever, and you’re the only man I’ve ever met where I can say in complete confidence that once I’m gone, you’ll be the only one able to stop Carter from coming after Callie.” Clinton Sage looked down at his wedding band, seeing nothing but the shimmer of the room. His palm was sweaty. “You love her, kid, it's clear as day. Go back to her, and soon. Wrestle in this tournament, too…take out your aggression there. This...this darkness. But go back to her, please.” Michael looked haunted by the past, “I just don’t trust anyone else to protect her from Carter the way you can. The way I know you can.” Michael smiled and took another sip from his beer as Clinton slipped the wedding band back onto his finger. Clinton splayed the fingers of his left hand open, staring down at the ring, while absent-mindedly clenching his right hand into a fist. He could feel the etching inside the ring pressing against his skin, a single word and its meaning now carrying new weight with the revelation and plea from Callie’s father. “I’ll go back.” Clinton said, with stern determination in his voice. Forever. - 8 - CALLIE The sleep that once came so easily had vanished altogether, replaced by hours of tossing and turning. When Callie did finally manage to find temporary solace in her slumber, it never lasted more than two hours at a time. In one way, it was good that she was constantly awake because she would catch Arya before her little darling could really throw a temper-tantrum during the middle of the night. Every other way, Callie Sage was lying in a King-sized bed by herself: the cool, empty mattress a constant reminder that Clinton had left her. One of Callie’s self-taught techniques for writing was to think about her characters as she lay in bed. She firmly believed that her imagination ran much wilder, without conscious limitations, in the moment’s right before she fell asleep. Perhaps it was her subconscious voice finally finding its own resolve, or perhaps it was just her willingness to accept any thought that went through her mind, but she found it to be helpful. Callie lay silent, listening to Arya start to stir in the crib. Her motherly instinct told her that it was feeding time, so she closed the imagination factory’s doors for a few moments and got out of bed. The lobby was dimly lit by the chandelier, which threw small rainbows of light across the marble tile that covered the floor. The tiles were chilly under Callie’s bare feet, so she hurried to the kitchen and quickly prepared a bottle, warmed it, tested it, and then hurried back up the steps and into the bedroom in time to hear Arya’s first whimpers of hunger. Picking her daughter up and bouncing her for a moment before putting the bottle between Arya’s lips, Callie realized that during that entire trip that she hadn’t thought of Clinton once. Granted, it was only three minutes at most, but it was a realization that both calmed her and alarmed her. Am I getting used to this empty house already? She watched Arya, brushing a few curly black strands of hair away from her forehead. The sight of her daughter slowly whittling away at a bottle was relaxing, and before Callie realized it, she was starting to nod off. Her lack of sleep over the last few days had started to catch up with her. Gently, slowly, Callie pulled the bottle from Arya’s mouth. Her little girl was asleep again, so she pulled up the little blanket and placed the bottle on the nightstand next to the bed. The clock read 4:17am. She curled back up under the covers, trying to ignore the empty space beside her, and once again let the imagination factory roar back to life. Callie started to think of her protagonist, Nymar Dell’Orci, and his quest to find the reason behind the curse of sword referred to as only ‘The Blood Sword’. She wondered why anyone would touch a sword that was known to kill those who picked it up, she began to wonder why Nymar would believe he could possibly wield a weapon that killed enemies with a razor-sharp edge, and the owner with a curse. She heard a thump downstairs that sounded like a door shutting, but the clutches of sleep were pulling her deeper and deeper. Her father probably returned home, safe and sound. Nymar would do the same, as she would stop her progress on The Blood Sword immediately, Callie realized she had no idea what she was doing. She shouldn’t be writing high fantasy. Sleep welcomed Callie with a warm embrace, delicate and comforting, and the factory doors closed. * * * * * A dragging sound roused Callie from sleep. She blinked away the disorientation and quickly glanced at the clock, which read 4:49am. She’d only been sleeping a half hour. Arya was breathing pretty heavily at her feet, heavy enough to send a wave of alarm racing through Callie’s system. She only saw the naked man standing next to her when she sat up. Callie could only let out a small yelp before his hands were wrapped around her throat, and she was dangling above the bed in his ice-cold grasp. Her neck burned for a moment, and then an odd sensation of muscle fatigue hit where his hands were squeezing. He pulled her close, and only then did she realize it was the man’s heavy breathing, not Arya’s. He smelled rotten, filled with decay. A small, black tongue peeked out from behind rotten teeth and licked at the huge cleft on the right side of his face. “Where is he?” the man hissed, never relinquishing his grip on her throat. Please please please be asleep, wake up Callie, wake up! Callie’s eyes raced around the room, which was barely lit from the small amount of light filtering in from the lobby chandelier. Like any good novelist, she absorbed what she saw. Arya’s crib, and the soft rise and fall of her daughter’s chest. Thank God, she’s alive. She saw the covers on the bed, the plush aqua blue felt-like material. The bottle on the nightstand, half empty. Finally, she looked at the horror that was holding her. A skeleton barely wrapped in an impossibly pale skin. The matted blonde hair. The gaping wound in his ribcage and the dark circle of decay surrounding it. What appeared to be bruises on his throat, shadows of two hands that had probably choked him the way he was now choking her. The clouded eyes, only traces of the pupil hidden behind a milky cataract. She didn't know what was happening, or how it was happening. Callie’s vision was beginning to blur with tears, and she could only spit out one word, “Who?” She was dangling two feet off of the ground. The man pulled her forward with a strength that was inhuman, his tall lanky frame moving in jerky motions but showing no signs of strain, “He who made us.” Callie gagged on his rotten breath, trying to stifle any sound she was making. She didn’t want Arya waking up and the attention of his horrific intruder to be drawn towards the baby. The man once again licked the cleft in his lip, and when Callie didn’t respond he shook her so violently that she grew immediately dizzy, and the odd sensation of fatigue started to spread past her throat and down through her neck and into her arms, tingling her fingertips. “He who shot us. He who choked us. He who made us!” the man rasped, an odd oscillation in his voice that sounded harmonic, almost choral. It sounded like a millisecond-like echo. Like five or six voices all speaking at once. Perhaps it was something she imagined, but one sounded like it had a stutter. “I don’t know” Callie blurted, and his hands immediately clenched tighter. She felt something pop in her neck, and lost control of her ability to hold in the scream. She filled the room with a few seconds of agony and terror, then fell into sobs. Still dangling Callie in the air like a ragdoll, the decaying man roared, “WHERE IS HE, HE WHO MADE US?” Callie heard Arya stirring in her crib. No, no, please no. No no no no. Stay quiet, sweetheart, please stay quiet. The world was dimming around the edges, darkness creeping in. It all felt like a dream. It must be a dream, she thought. The oxygen in her system was quickly diminishing, “Who are…you” she managed to ask in choked gasps. Arya started to cry. The nightmare turned his head towards the crib, his milky-dead eyes searching for the source of the sound. When he found where the crying was coming from, Callie could feel the man start to thump-drag-walk his way towards the crib. She could do nothing but scream, hoping to draw the attention back towards her. The man didn’t turn his attention back towards Callie. His gaze remained fixated on Arya, crying in her crib. His voice sounded like it was bouncing off of itself half a dozen times, “WE ARE GEIST.” Suddenly, Callie was flying through the air. He simply tossed her aside like a twig. The world slowed down, and everything became unclouded once again. She saw the man reach down into the crib with one of his mangled hands. She felt herself floating across the bedroom, her right hand shattering as it crashed against the doorframe. Everything was happening impossibly slow, her flying and tumbling. As Callie twisted through the air, she saw the wooden banister explode into small wooden shards when her body crashed through it, she saw the little rainbows twirling and dancing across the lobby floor as her body smashed into the chandelier. Callie watched as the marble floor slowly raced up towards her face, the little rainbows looming larger and larger and larger as it approached. Callie Sage felt light as a feather until she slammed face-first into the marble floor. The world sped back up with bone-shattering speed, before disappearing into darkness to the sound of of a raven's wings fluttering above.
