The Saga Of The Uber Judge : A New Day Dawns

28 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
At the dawn of a new day, the Uber Judge rose from slumber. Staring forth from the window out into the fields of his well maintained yard, observing the morning light play across the leaves of trees and the blades of grass, the Uber Judge saw. And thus seeing, he responded. “Good.” Sparrows, robins, and the occasional plump and fat dove fought for space at the birdfeeders or quenched their thirst in the birdbath. But, in the top branches of the Uber Judge’s prized elm tree, one black crow had something to say. “Caw!” The Uber Judge’s pulse quickened. Reserving judgment until he had more information, he descended the stairs from the bedroom and sought his prized beverage. Simply Orange. Among Orange juices, the Uber Judge was convinced, Simply Orange was without a doubt the finest collection and presentation of the sweet nectar from the greatest gift of fruit to humanity. No concentration, no sweetening, the stuff was simply the best. Opinion? Not at all. Judgment. For the Uber Judge considered himself to be the discerning authority over all creation. With glass of premium OJ in hand, the UJ descended to his living room to evaluate the truth (what little could be found) contained in the newspaper. With a pen, he attacked the stories presented on the page, newsprint ink smudging on the bottom of his right hand. The evaluation process was an exercise, to keep him sharp. Establish the writer’s goals, establish the desire for sensationalism, establish the story’s actual merit, evaluate the presentation of information, and raise questions regarding what information is being withheld. Soon the mail arrived, dumped through the slot in his front door. As the Uber Judge stood up to receive his correspondence, he heard through the open living room window the faint sound of the crow. “Caw!” Frozen in the act of rising for a moment, the Uber Judge swallowed his last draught of Simply Orange, and placed the glass on a coaster. How unlike the Judge of All Creation! All things serve their purpose, and all things have their place! The empty glass ought to have been stored in the dishwasher, not left on the living room table! Let this signify the sense of urgency, the sense that something is quite amiss. Quickly descending the stairs to his front door (but not too quickly) the Uber Judge seized his mail. Flipping through the junk, a myriad of credit card companies promising gold and platinum, the standard bills and bank statements, he found the item of importance. A simple white envelope, addressed to him, with the return address showing the signature stamp of the PTC used in all their official correspondence. Resisting temptation, the Uber Judge returned with his mail to his living room. Pushing the paper aside, he deliberately put the PTC related envelope on the bottom of the pile. Sort. Put things in order. Prioritize. Deal with things in their ascending order of importance. These were the standards of the Uber Judge, but the signs of his desperation remained. The empty glass still waited on the coaster to be removed to the dishwasher. A feeling of tension permeated the living room as the Uber Judge classified the mail, separating what could be destroyed and disposed of from those things that would require attention later. Finally, the only document remaining was the letter from the PTC. He opened it, and read. Dear _____: It has come to our attention that despite the considerable contributions that you have made with regard to your invaluable assistance with wrestling tournaments sponsored by the PTC, that your services will no longer be needed. The present administration finds that your interpretation of performance standards to be entirely too rigid, to the point where your opinions, as valued and respected as they are, do not reflect the point of view of the PTC’s fans. By this reasoning, we have no choice but to conclude that your expertise is contrary to the PTC’s goals with regard to its tournaments, and therefore respectfully decline to have your involvement in an administrative capacity. Best of luck in all your future endevours. _____ _____ The Office of the PTC The eyes of the uber judge were frozen on the signature for a few quiet moments, discerning that like the return address, it was clearly stamped as opposed to signed by an actual hand. Outside, all the birds in the back yard took flight simultaneously, sending the sounds of rustling wings, various calls, and one distinctive “Caw!” from the crow. The first sounds of the Crow had been warnings. The last seemed to shout, “I told you so!” as a final departing comment. The Uber Judge is a rational being. But even he, a rational being, is still human and subject to emotion. The Judge of All Creation dropped the letter onto the table, leaned his head back and gave voice to his feelings. “Outrage!” == Choices. Every act of human kind begins with a choice. Even reactions, knee jerk emotional responses, include synapse activity: the mind makes a choice. Pacing back and forth in his living room, the empty glass on the coaster, the junk mail yet to be destroyed, the important mail yet to be moved to his desk, these things glaring errors of irresponsibility, the Uber Judge sought to make a decision. What was he to do? In this midst of his angry turmoil, a shred of rational thought informed him that he was too emotional to make a worthy choice at the moment, but this wise thought was disregarded. He needed something else at the moment. With a snap of his fingers, he seized the answer. Conflict. Confrontation. Only after achieving these things could resolution be gained. Leaving the morning’s business still incomplete (That poor lonely glass that once held orange juice! How it longs to be restored to it’s proper place!) the Uber Judge approached his hall closet. Judge’s Robes. Executioner’s Mask. Ridiculously Oversized Gavel. Costumed appropriately in his office of Judge over All Creation, the Uber Judge made haste to his confrontation, without even phoning ahead and asking for an appointment. == A ridiculously oversized gavel, while not being the most well-balanced of weapons, was at least quite intimidating, and very useful for smacking open closed doors. “SMACK~!” went the Gavel. “Woosh!” went the opening office door. “Hmmmm?” went the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments (VPOPTCST) with remarkable calm and curiosity. The VPOPTCST, in his years of dealing with the extreme behavior exhibited by professional wrestlers, had become quite comfortable with their needs to display violence, speak in their “outside” voices, and demand attention be paid to them during such acts. Thus, having his office door kicked open became a regular sort of event, and secretly a kind of welcome distraction from what often was tedious work. “This!” shouted the Uber Judge, holding the letter he’d received that morning from the PTC, “Is an outrage!” The VPOPTCST blinked at the Uber Judge, smacked his lips once, and said, “Yes, and?” The Uber Judge, the Judge over All Creation, tried his best not to show his deflating sense of purpose as he shouted a little louder, “I demand an explanation!” “Ahhhhhh,” said the VPOPTCST, leaning back in his ergonomic office chair. “Well, Uber Judge, it’s all quite simple really. Your opinions have become useless.” His sense of purpose completely deflated (not unlike a blow up doll with a hole in it), the Uber Judge quietly asked, “They have?” “Oh yes,” replied the VPOPTCST, “you see, after careful evaluation by our oversight committee, we’ve noticed that no one agrees with you. Your judgments are consistently flying in the face of the other judges, the opinions of our fans, my opinions, my secretary’s opinions, my wife’s opinions, and probably even the opinions of my dog. Your point of view is completely isolated from the rest of the wrestling community.” “But I am the most discerning, observant, and competent mind to evaluate any single thing in All Creation on its merits!” protested the Uber Judge. “Well,” said the VPOPTCST, “that very well might be true, but even if everyone in the business is wrong and you’re right, you’re still outvoted by a considerable margin. So in that respect, you still might as well be wrong, eh? Heh heh heh. So yes, we don’t need you anymore for the Tournaments, and don’t particularly want you either, I'm afraid. It’s too bad really. Oh! Come to think of it, could you take a half step forward please?” The Uber Judge complied. “Very good,” said the VPOPTCST, who depressed a button on his desk while saying, “Good day then.” The trap door opened. And the Uber Judge fell, his voice echoing, “Nooooooooooo!” through the dark passage before it was sharply cut off by the trap door closing. The VPOPTCST allowed himself a snicker before sighing and resuming his normal tedious work. == The trap door deposited the Uber Judge onto the sidewalk outside the building. Picking himself up, and dusting off his robe, the Uber Judge squared his feet and leaned his head back for a diatribe. “IMPLAUSIBLE! The introduction of the trap door existed within the realm of stage performances to assist the earliest known special effects, with Shakespeare’s Globe Theatre featuring several trapdoors customized to each play’s performance! The concept of a Trap Door existing in an office building as a means of cruelly disposing of unwanted visitors was introduced in the earliest Warner Brothers and Disney cartoons! Architecturally, a working trap door built into a several storied office building is absolutely preposterous, considering the load bearing capacity of the trap door itself, and the potential lawsuits that would stem from a fall producing serious injury!” The Uber Judge gasped for breath and was about to launch into the next stage of his diatribe when it suddenly occurred to him that no one was listening. Well, some people on the street were looking at him warily, many choosing to cross the street rather than pass near this person in a strange costume with the large hammer of a weapon who was apparently screaming up at the sky. Sure, they heard his pronouncement a moment ago on the implausibility of the trap door gimmick, but could they understand it? No. Perhaps the VPOPTCST was right after all, if no one could comprehend the Uber Judge, did it matter what he had to say? The Uber Judge hung his head. In being Judge over of All Creation had his mind advanced to the point where he might as well be of an alien species? “But am I a mortal man, like any other,” protested the Uber Judge out loud, “I’ve just taken this incredible responsibility of Judgment on, and I have attempted to discern all things to the very best of my ability. Is that so wrong?” No one answered. == The Uber Judge had marched back into the office building, but instead of heading for the office of the VPOPTCST, he instead went to the sign up kiosk that’d been set up in the building’s lobby. “I’d like to sign up for the Infinite Gauntlet,” said the Uber Judge. “Ah, yes, well Mr. Uber Judge, I regret to inform you that we won’t be needing your services for this particular event, perhaps you haven’t received a letter that was-” Cutting off the poor man, the Uber Judge raised his voice and said, “I have indeed received the letter in question, you simpleton! I am not signing up for the Infinite Gauntlet in as an administrative capacity …” The man at the kiosk gasped, as the Uber Judge paused dramatically before continuing, “but as … a COMPETITOR.” == “Oh,” said the young man, “in that case, we have some forms to fill out. What PTC fed do you represent?” “Uhm,” said the Uber Judge, “I'm not in any of the PTC federations.” “That’s fine, that’s fine. I’ll put you down as a free agent then. Your previous federation associations then?” “Well,” stammered the Uber Judge, “that’s a secret.” “Oh fine, fine. That’s perfectly fine. Shall we say you’re from `parts unknown’ as well?” “How about, `All Creation’?” asked UJ. “Ah a good choice. Mysterious, yet somewhat profound. Height and weight?” “Six feet, two hundred and twenty pounds.” “In Metric?” “I … I'm not sure.” “No worries. We’ll get that taken care of. A distinctive finishing maneuver?” “Oh uhm … The Implausibility Driver. It’s a Fire Thunder Driver.” “A nice choice. I’ve always been a fan of the Wrist Clutch Exploder Suplex myself, but the Fire Thunder Driver is still a very good choice.” “Do you think I should use the Wrist Clutch Exploder instead?” “Oh no, certainly not. Go with what you know, I always say. Besides, you’d have to come up with another name and all that nonsense; go with the Improbability Driver.” “Implausibility Driver.” “Yes, right that’s what I said. Anyway, sign here, and initial here. Very good. Now sign this release form. Thank you. Now sign and initial this form which permits us to use your likeness for the promotional work for the Infinite Gauntlet. Thank you. Now this is your agreement to subject yourself for a physical so we can have proof that you’re fit to compete and that you’re not using any illicit substances. Now initial. Thank you. This is your agreement to abide by all administrative decisions as determined by the Infinite Gauntlet Administrative committee, you’re familiar with all that, in the case of judges decisions, those decisions are final, yadda yadda yah, sign here, sign here, and initial here.” “Anything else?” asked the Uber Judge. “No sir, that’s all of them. Congratulations, you are now entered into PTC’s Infinite Gauntlet. Best of luck to you.” Leaning his head back, the Uber Judge let loose a loud cackling laughter, echoing hauntingly off the lobby’s walls. “Very good sir!” said the man at the kiosk while clapping politely, “I’d rate that evil laugh very highly. In fact, on the Angelo Deville scale of evil laughter, I’d put that one at a point eight five.” And with a sweep of his robes, the Judge over All Creation left to return to his home, where he’d find the lonely glass that used to contain orange juice on his living room table, and finally put the damn thing in the dishwasher.
