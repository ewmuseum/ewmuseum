Until Dawn

10 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-10 .:: ::: ::. The Den Quarter’s on the outskirts of Cleveland were full. The atmosphere swam with anticipation. Everyone present realized that they were hanging on the edge of a momentous occasion, else the Den Master wouldn’t have called them here. And Geffroi LaBatte could sense the feeling in the air. They were ready tonight, his hunters. They would hang on his every word as though he was God, and he intended to play them like a master pianist. At this moment, he stood backstage, donning his silken blue robes, preparing to go out and speak to them. He could hear them milling around outside. He could almost feel the heat radiating off of the crowd, or perhaps he was just imagining that. He’d never had such good luck with a hunt before. Typically, he just got lucky. Typically, he’d just happen to run across information on an Assamite in Cleveland by chance, but no one ever questioned whether he came across his information by chance; rather, they assumed that he was just some sort of spiritual guru who knew whenever an Assamite came into town. And that pleased him. He’d always been a lucky devil. Of course, the thought that his luck was going to run out had never run across his mind—not until this Shadow character came into town. He didn’t even remember how he’d figured out her name. That was how inconsequential it had actually been at the time. In fact, he hadn’t really cared about her, until the Giovanni’s had jumped on his case about it. At that, he was almost tempted to roll his eyes. Geffroi LaBatte detested being under the thumb of ANYONE. He hated not being in control of his own fate. And these Giovanni masters of his, they knew it all too well. They could tell just from looking into his eyes that he wanted all the power for himself. And for that reason, they did everything that they possibly could to keep him from getting it. If he could have, he would have disposed of both of them, but he needed their blood to keep him powerful. Not only that, they tended toward shrewdness, and would gladly withhold blood from him when he needed it most—until they could think of something that they wanted. This matter of Alessandro Pazzi’s death had put him in between a rock and a hard place. Everything that he had, he had because the Giovanni clan of Cleveland had seen fit to give it to him. Now, they were asking for something in return—Alessandro Pazzi had been murdered. He remembered the meeting he’d had with the heads of the clan, Cadenza and Vittorio. They’d been furious over his death, mostly because now, they didn’t have the accounting expert to cook their books anymore. Why they blamed it on him, he wasn’t sure—actually, he knew that wasn’t true. They didn’t blame it on him, they just looked to him to solve all their problems because they were stuffy dried-up shells who couldn’t handle life in the real world. He sneered. Whining, sniveling brats who couldn’t handle their own affairs, and wanted him to take charge. That was all that he thought of them as, and he figured it was a lucky thing they were too stupid and out of touch with the modern world to figure it out. As it was, he had what he wanted: Every time there was a problem, Vittorio and Cadenza would come crying to him, he’d fix it, and he’d get whatever he wanted from them, be it something to push his business, something to further his aims at dominating Cleveland’s business world on meager skills, or some more of that oh-so-precious blood that kept him going… the better he did at getting them out of a jam, the more they’d give him, so he always did his best, or at least made it seem like he did. This had been a different matter though. They’d come to him after Pazzi had been killed and saddled him with not only the task of taking over his duties, but finding and disposing of his assassin. He supposed that was right up his alley, but then again, he didn’t really care about The Den, either. The only thing that was good about keeping that bunch of idiots around was strength in numbers. Any time he needed something, he could easily coerce one of those idiots into doing it. And he supposed it was easy enough to find them a hunt now and again. Who cared about Assamites? Well, that was a silly question. HE cared about Assamites. Or, more particularly, he cared about this one, because this one was causing him to lose his clout. Cadenza and Vittorio Giovanni were furious with him, they hadn’t given him any blood, any money, ANYTHING, in quite some time. On top of that, The Den had gotten wind of the situation, and were clamoring for her blood. He’d have it, if only to reclaim his good name. How dare she interfere with his life, and more importantly, how dare she make him look bad? “Master, they are ready for you.” Geffroi LaBatte spun around, his eyes narrowing. “Klaus, must you interrupt me all the time? I’m very well aware that they are waiting for me.” Klaus’ face reddened immeasurably. LaBatte knew that Klaus hated displeasing him, and for that reason LaBatte tried to get displeased with Klaus as often as possible. It wasn’t so much that he disliked the hunched-over little German, it was just that so long as Klaus feared reprisal from his master, he’d perform better. Yes, it couldn’t be said that Geffroi LaBatte didn’t know how to play people. That was his gift. And now it was time to use it. He mounted the ramp that led from his preparation room to the Den Quarters auditorium. The lights dimmed in The Den, and he could hear them stamping and hissing and humming, their signal that they knew very well what was coming. Now, the Master would come to lead them. He stepped up to the podium. “Denmates! We have a momentous occasion ahead of us!” And it was true. He’d found out enough information to get a damn good idea of where this Shadow bitch was hiding, and who’d gotten her out of Cleveland. And it wasn’t just finding her—that damn Ravnos had sold out Cadenza and Vittorio, and filled them full of bullshit. He didn’t know what Gideon de Medici’s connection with Shadow was, but he’d pay for it. And they’d both be dead. And Geffroi LaBatte could go back to living his life normally. He took a deep breath, and launched into his speech to prepare the troops. .:: ::: ::. The bedroom was lit only with candles. Gideon had to admit, in that low light, her skin was beautiful. He usually tried not to get mixed up in this kind of mess, that is, he usually tried not to get mixed up in having any sort of relationship with anyone. Even he had a breaking point, however. He’d spent enough time talking things through with Shadow after she’d seemed to have some glimmer of hope. Of course, he hadn’t made a breakthrough with her yet. She still wasn’t to the point that she needed to be, not in any sense. He couldn’t have taken her back to Seattle in this state, because his brother wouldn’t have been happy. HE wasn’t happy with it. All he wanted was for her to see what had really been going on for the past eight months of her life, but he didn’t know how long it would take for that to happen. Shadow’s eyes were almost liquid as they looked into his own, swimming with confusion, but recognition. She was fairly certain that she knew him now, fairly certain that she HAD to know him, or she wouldn’t feel this way about him. But something was strange about their association. Something wasn’t quite right about the way that she felt about him. Something was missing. She couldn’t put her finger on it. But she’d felt comfortable enough to spill everything to him. She’d told him what she could pull out of her feelings last night. They’d just laid in his bed together, talking over everything that she could think about—every emotion she could dredge to the surface, every memory, every confusion. For some reason, she felt like an idiot confiding in a Ravnos, but she couldn’t help it. When she was around him, it just spilled out. And he’d listened. For once, he’d actually listened instead of taking in every word, analyzing it, and figuring out what he wanted to do in order to manipulate her to his ends. He didn’t want to do that tonight, and he hadn’t really wanted to do that to her anyway. Well, not anymore. Something like concern, and perhaps pity filled his azure eyes. But she didn’t want him to pity her, she just wanted him to understand. She wanted his help, but she didn’t know how to ask. She never lowered herself to that level. Maybe now was the time to start. She looked up at him, pulling on the lip piercing that ran through the middle of her lower lip. “Gideon, there’s just one thing…” “Hm?” His eyes seemed to pierce into her attentively. Yes, he was listening. She could do this. She had to. She couldn’t always do things on her own, and she was coming to the point where it would have been stupid to keep pretending she was invincible. “I feel like I’ve been lying to myself and everyone around me for such a long time… I only wish I could figure out how to feel something. You know, how to REALLY feel something, as opposed to faking it because I think I should.” He nodded. An impulse wedged itself into his head. He knew he shouldn’t follow it, but feeling was starting to take over. He supposed that women had always been his weakness, but then again… Shadow wasn’t typical. He reached out and pulled her hand into his, lacing his fingers through hers. “Some time, you have to let yourself go. You have to trust yourself again. Not everyone is out to get you, or use you for their own gain, whether Amir and Corvus were or not.” Shadow had been trying to tell herself that for years. For some reason, it rang truer coming from Gideon than it ever had when she’d been trying to convince herself of the idea. And Gideon saw it in her eyes. He leaned forward, pressing his lips to hers and from the instant that they met, something new flowed into her, lighting a fire inside veins that had been dusty with disuse. She allowed herself to kiss him back, a bit slowly, a bit hesitantly, but she realized slowly that she wasn’t afraid anymore. She just wanted to trust someone, whether it lasted more than one night or not. Her skin was cool to his touch, but then again, that gave it more the quality of perfect porcelain than a pulsing living thing. She’d never fade. She’d be beautiful forever, just as he’d forever be the dashing rogue with the blackest heart in Spain. Leave the golden god act to his brother. He was more Dionysus than Apollo anyway, and right now, the Dionysus inside him required him to drink deep. .:: ::: ::. Geffroi LaBatte refused the urge that was overcoming him to drowse in his plane seat. True enough, he’d chosen a team of his most loyal Den members just hours ago, and they’d jumped the flight out to Napa Valley on short notice, but this was the way it had to be. If they wasted a moment, they might lost their chance. Kindred were migratory creatures, and he knew that Shadow and Gideon wouldn’t stay in California for long. He couldn’t sleep now, because he was about to see the completion of his task; the completion that would mean he could go back to living his life like he wanted to live it. He pressed his hands together expectantly. Perhaps they would even make him into a clan member, finally. He’d been waiting for it so long that it was starting to grate on his nerves every time he thought about it, and he didn’t like to have to THINK about anything. But this was the solution. It had to be the solution. If this didn’t tie it all up, Geffroi LaBatte was thinking of throwing himself off a building. .:: ::: ::. Outside of the sprawling Napa Valley Estate, a crack team of Den members were gathered. These were Geffroi LaBatte’s best and brightest. People that he knew he could depend on to do their jobs, and that was what he needed right now. Paranoia was starting to overcome him. In the semi-darkness of the late night, his crystalline eyes gleamed maniacally, and sweat beaded on his upper lip. He pressed his hands together and pulled them apart, then pressed them together again. They were all hanging on his words, but that wasn’t enough. He wanted them to fall at his feet and worship him for the opportunity to be privy to his greatness. They were in awe of him. Alexis Key, Austin Hiram, and LeRoy Jenkins stood before him, practically bathed in his glow. And it was he who they looked to for guidance on this momentous occasion. None of them spoke. Speaking was not their job; it was up to Geffroi LaBatte to speak and instruct them. He was the Master. LaBatte paced. They had arrived, but to be quite honest, he had no clue how to proceed. The majority of the time, he led based on crowd response; in other words, just about everything he spouted from his perverted pulpit was bullshit, but he knew it, at least. Right now, the three young people across from him were almost completely silent, and that was becoming an irritating factor. He took a breath and moistened his lips. Alexis, Austin and LeRoy leaned forward, almost hanging on the very pattern of his breathing. LaBatte was nervous. If he fucked this up, he might as well kick himself in the balls repeatedly for the rest of his life, and kiss his dreams of immortality goodbye. Hell, Vittorio and Cadenza might even kill him in some sick, gruesome way, just to get the pleasure of seeing him snivel at their feet before they offed him. He knew them well enough to realize that they’d do something like that. In fact, he’d been witness to it a couple of times. But it was the here and now. And he had to act. “The floor plan that Klaus procured may or may not be accurate. Kindred are often quite sneaky about what information they release to the public. To be honest, we could try to infiltrate, but crossing swords in hand-to-hand combat with an Assamite and a Ravnos isn’t a good idea. From the best that we know about them, they’re old, at least old enough to easily take down all four of us.” That was bullshit, naturally. Klaus had never set his hands on any plans for this place. He was only lucky that Gideon de Medici was enough of a moron to use his name on the deeds for this place. In any case, LaBatte was starting to get cold feet. He didn’t particularly want to get mixed up in a nasty fight with these two. So, he’d do the easiest thing: just torch the whole place, leave it to burn with both of them inside. His eyes flickered maniacally fjord blue again. “The best thing for us to do in this situation is to destroy the mansion. There are no escape tunnels in places like these, so we set it on fire, and we’re done with it. It’s a surefire way to make sure that they pay for what they did.” He figured the two men, at least, would buy the lie. They were stupid enough to not see how ridiculous what he’d just said was. But Alexis started to look like she had misgivings. Normally, he liked her plucky intelligence, and had thought often about making her a ghoul once the Giovanni clan of Cleveland embraced him. But right now, he wanted to hit her on the head with something sharp. Perhaps a claw hammer. He held up a hand. “I know, Alexis. It’s regrettable that we won’t have a trophy to take back to the Den. But as you well know, Kindred bones are nearly indestructible. We’ll come back and see what’s left once the blaze burns itself out.” She still appeared to think he was bullshitting. But she was gullible enough to still believe that he was telling the Gospel Truth. The entire Den was that stupid, and despite the fact that it made them nearly useless, he was glad for it at this moment. He nodded to the three of them, and they went forward, under his watchful eye. LaBatte didn’t have to question whether or not they’d get the job done. The Den was too terrified of his knowledge and power to do anything without doing it correctly every time. He paced back and forth, waiting, waiting, always waiting. It seemed like hours, but he knew it could only have been twenty minutes at the most; he heard the muffled BOOM! of an explosion at the base of the mansion and muffled screams of “GO! GO! GO!” coming from the thick lips of LeRoy Jenkins. Moments later, they approached him, sweat dripping down their dirt-caked faces. Alexis was still eyeing him suspiciously. There was something about Geffroi LaBatte that she didn’t like tonight. Something about him seemed disingenuous, for the first time. In fact, he’d seemed a bit disingenuous ever since the business of pursuing this Assassin had started. But she wasn’t going to question him right now, not right in front of the others. LeRoy and Austin would rend her limb from limb if she uttered so much as one reservation about him. LaBatte smiled at them and thanked them profusely for their job well done, bowing his head like a wooden bird dipping its head into a cup of water on some businessman’s desk. Alexis didn’t hear a word that he said as she climbed into the Explorer with her two companions and her leader. Something was up, and she didn’t buy that this job was something LaBatte was particularly concerned about. He was losing his zeal as a slayer, and if that was the case, Alexis was determined that she would take his place at the head of the Den. No one could allow the mission to become subverted. And LaBatte had long been too lax in his association with Kindred of whatever kind was useful to him at the moment. Alexis sneered darkly. They disgusted her, all of them, and most of all LaBatte. He thought he was invincible, with his trustworthy, honest blue eyes, his perfectly groomed blonde hair, and his tailored to perfection suits. He might have thought he was the savior of Cleveland’s Den, but he wasn’t. She was the continuation of her family line, and she could guarantee that she knew more about The Den than LaBatte ever could. He thought he was hot shit. But if he screwed this up, she’d make him into nothing MORE than shit, nothing more than a bloodstained memory. He’d pay for his lax policies. He’d pay for subverting Cleveland’s holiest order. .:: ::: ::. Acrid smoke crossed Gideon’s nose. Usually, that wasn’t something that reassured him too much. His eyes sprang open, panic quickly slipping into them. Smoke filled the room, oily and gray. The first thing he thought was that The Den must have infiltrated the house. But no, they wouldn’t leave themselves to rot in this inferno as well. Perhaps they were trying to smoke he and Lia out of the house so they could kill them. But that wouldn’t work. There were tunnels networking below the house to various escape points in the nearby countryside. He had to get them out first, though. He turned to look at her, dozing peacefully on the pillow beside him. For that moment, he was caught in the very recent memory of her. For once, he saw her looking peaceful in her sleep—even for the three days she’d slept in his house previously, she hadn’t looked like this. That’s how he’d known she hadn’t been in torpor; she’d tossed and turned like a demon the entire time, tormented even in her dreams. Now, her ebon hair was splayed across the pillow like a silk fan, and her sharply contrasting ivory skin lay perfect and undisturbed like smooth milk in a narrow glass. He wanted to reach out and touch her again. Mortal women were a dime a dozen, but Lia had brought out a demon in him tonight. She’d brought out the caress of a lover who was leaving the next day. He didn’t recall ever NEEDING a woman’s touch the way he’d needed hers tonight. But that was all too much to think about. And right now, he realized, he didn’t need to think, he needed to act. Reluctantly, he shook her. “Lia… LIA… Reveillez maintenant.” The French stabbed through her fogged slumber and she stirred to her senses slowly, as though pulling through a clear, cool, oceanic pool that reminded her of what it had been like to swim in Gideon’s eyes in the few moments that she pulled away from his insistent and passionate kisses. But then she smelled the smoke, and all sense of slumber was lost. She opened her eyes, in a panic and sat up, nearly cracking her forehead against his as he leaned over her. She held the satin covers to her otherwise nude torso. “What the fuck?” Satisfied that she was awake, his brow furrowed and he slid out of bed. Within moments, he’d slid into a pair of black vinyl pants that just happened to be lying next to the bed. For once, he was thankful that he was a terrible housekeeper. “The Den is at it again, I’m afraid. I don’t know how they found us here, but um… they’re obviously not too fond of the idea of either of us continuing our existence. Get dressed… We have to get out of here before we’re fucking toast.” She slid out of bed, running on pure instinct. There was no time to question him, or question where they’d go from here. For a few moments, there was abject silence, other than the ambient noise of the intensifying fire around them. It wasn’t panic that ran through either of them, but simply a need to act—if there was one thing that became innate in Kindred after the Embrace, it was the instinct of self-preservation. Moments passed, insatiably slipping away into the raging inferno surrounding the house. The vineyards had just started to catch; the heady scent started drifting into her nose as they both finished dressing. He grabbed her by the hand and they plunged out into the hallway. Panic overtook all of her senses as she saw flames, and she tried to pull away. It was a bare instinct with Kindred, something that existed as a failsafe protection. He gripped her insistently, shouting over the cacophony. “Just trust me, Lia. Whatever courage you have, you’ll need it right now!” She bit her lip until she could taste blood, and tried to resist the urge to close her eyes. Gideon had to push his own courage to the brink. He didn’t know how in the hell he could suppress the fear that was pricking through his veins like millions of needles, but he had to. If not to save his sorry ass, to get her out of here and eventually, back to his brother. They bolted down the stairs, fire on either side of them. He pulled her through the large, well-appointed kitchen and into the stairwell. It might have looked like it led down into the basement, but it really led down into a tunnel that would get them out of here before they were turned into roast duck for some good ol’ boys hunting club. She had the urge to stop in the cool of the tunnel once they got into it and just take in the damp air of the tunnel, let it fall on her skin and erase the feel of the flames that she could almost feel licking up her back. He pulled insistently on her wrist still, but dropped his grip when he realized she wasn’t moving. He looked back at her, seeing the dazed look on her face, and he backtracked, pulling her chin upwards with his index finger. “Can’t stop now, mi sombra. We have to get to the airport.” Mi sombra… It echoed in her head, sending waves of comfort that brought her slowly back to reality. This time, it was she who grabbed his wrist and pulled, even though she didn’t know where she was going. After a few moments, his long legs caught up with her, and he took the lead again, not wanting them to get stuck in the labyrinthine mess underneath his estate. Thankfully, he thought, he was a Ravnos. And everyone in his clan was quite used to having to save their asses by the skin of their teeth. He just hoped Shadow could adapt. .:: ::: ::. Shadow slumped in the plastic airport chair. She sincerely hoped the private plane that Gideon said he’d get would get here soon, otherwise, they’d still be crispy critters. The night was waning quickly. She didn’t bother looking at Gideon right now, because then she’d have to admit that she was scared, and she wanted to fling herself into his arms. Gideon, meanwhile, was slouching against the back of a phone booth as he checked his watch. The voice on the other end of the phone certainly wasn’t pleased. He sighed. “There is no need to sit and yell at me. There’s not that much that I could have done about it. I told you I’d get her back to you safely, and I’ve gotten her away from her would-be killers twice now. Don’t you have any faith in me?” There was an irritated growl from the other end of the phone. “You know I have faith in you. Can you not understand? This isn’t about you, this is about restoring Lia to her proper place.” He nodded, butting his forehead irritatedly against the palm of his free hand. He wanted it just as much as his brother did, but he didn’t know how long it was going to take Shadow to understand. “She’s progressing. But I have to get her out of here, and I can’t bring her back to you yet.” “So where are you going?” He sighed. This was about to get ugly. “Europe.” “Europe?! Where?” He shook his head, his long hair brushing against the back of the phone booth with a rustling noise. “I don’t know. I’m going to talk to Rune when he gets the plane here.” “Tell me where you’re going!” His famous temper started to take hold. Dawn was approaching, and he was starting to get frustrated. “Dammit, I don’t know! Rune will know the safest place, and I’ll call you when I get there!” “If you don’t I’ll cut off your balls, you prick.” His brother slammed the phone down in his ear. He snarled at the sound and hung up the phone. He wondered about his brother sometimes; he’d never been a particularly kind person, but that had lessened even more after the Embrace. He wondered if it was just because his brother was being stubborn, but that wasn’t it. He missed her. And on some level, she missed him too. She’d said as much last night. When he sat down next to her again, he said nothing. She looked as though she was concentrating on something, and in fact, she was. Shadow had slowly started to realize, with the waning of the night, that the consequences of her life as of late had been nothing short of amazing. Despite the fact that she felt like she’d just been riding the wave up to this point, something had dawned on her. What would happen if she suddenly took control of her own Fate? She’d heard tales of Kindred that were nigh unstoppable once they put their unbending willpower behind their aims. Perhaps it was time to test the theory. Perhaps it was time for her to step out of the shadows of her past; this time, instead of running from the light, she’d step up and take her place. No one could stop her. The first traces of red started to come into the sky. The resembled blood, reminded her of all the blood that spattered across her history. And she smiled. It was time to make it a bit bloodier. And this time, she’d do it without her hands tied to tradition OR guilt. .:: ::: ::.
