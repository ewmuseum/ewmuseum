The Politics of Homosexuality

1 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-01 Even for the staunchest of conservatives, Fox News Channel can get pretty old after three straight days of watching. Gordon Oliver Powell, the Phantom Republican, found that out after 72 hours of living on his mother's couch. He found out several other things too, like Moose Trax being the ultimate snack food, sitting in one position for too long giving you ass-blisters and his mother having an incredible ability to zone out people who live on her couch. Gordon assumed that she had more than one deadbeat boyfriend to sort of condition her to it, like putting a mental callous on her mind. The only things that kept him from revulsing over the fact that he was becoming a parasitic leech on his mother like most Welfare cases were on society were his thoughts. He drifted in and out of attention to the conservative news network, thinking about things his mother had told him, or more appropriately screamed at him, and he started to feel some remorse. About 72 hours (give or take an hour or two) after he had settled on that couch, he drifted back out of one of his thoughts and into the television. Hannity and Colmes… again. There was only so much of Colmes' "umms" and "errrrs" that he could stomach, so he finally changed the channel. "Let's see what's on the next channel up." He flicked the up button on the channel changer, and it was public access. Some college student in a hoodie railing against Bush for his fascist pot laws. Click. Next one up from that was Lifetime; he didn't even stop to see which typecast battered wife was in this movie of the week, Valerie Bertinelli or Judith Light. Click. Next channel up, and it's Springer. This particular show's title was "I cheated on my cousin… with my cousin!" Gordon went to put his finger on the "up" button on the remote, but strong, invisible urges kept it from pressing down. His eyes were transfixed on the screen as the 300 lb. redneck woman argued with her 140 lb. redneck cousin she was sleeping with because he cheated on her with the 295 lb. cousin on the other side of his family. Gordon put the remote down and put another handful of Moose Trax in his mouth. Forty-five minutes later, Gordon was hooting and hollering in unison with the crowd on TV. "Oh no he didn't! Oh no he didn't! Shaniqua, you better slap him. Slap the shit out of him before I slap the shit out of him through the TV! Do it!" Gordon jumped from his seat on the couch. As the crowd on TV started their famous chant, he joined in with them. "JERRY! JERRY! JERRY! JERRY! JERRY!" The next sound Gordon heard was the rumbling of feet down the stairs. In a blue terrycloth flash, his mother appeared at the bottom of the stairs and scowled at her son. "What the hell is all this ruckus about?" Gordon looked at her sheepishly. "Mom, I was… I just… I was just watching Jerry. It's so addicting!" "Oh yeah? Well, go get addicted to it at your own place. You've done nothing since you were here except watch that damn TV. Hell, have you even gotten up to do anything other than get something to eat, take a dump or go outside to yell at the paperboy for peddling our newspaper? "It's a liberal rag, mom." "He's only 12 years old!" "I know! They're starting young!" "You're unbelievable…" "Mom…" Gordon took a step towards his mother. "I… I know I haven't been the best son since I left for college. I feel really bad, and I've been thinking about what you said, and you know what? You're right. I do need to pay more attention to family." A smile started to form on his mother's face. "So, what I'm going to do is go down to Boca and see Daddy." "What?" "Yeah, after I'm done wrestling in that GTT6 match I have with Angelica Dominique. What you said got me thinking, and I really never get a chance to visit him. I only get to call him, and that's not right." Gordon picked up his bag and headed for the bathroom. If looks could kill, the expression on his mother's face would have launched missiles to destroy the walls of her downstairs commode and incinerate her son. "What about me? Huh? Where's my love?" "Mom, don't be silly," he said from the bathroom. "I spent an entire three days with you." Her anger dissipated into a frustrated sigh. After summarily dispatching Angelica Dominique from GTT6, Gordon hopped on the first flight to Boca Raton, FL. Boca was a quiet town in November; seniors, like Gordon's Aunt Stella, dominate the landscape, living out their autumns and winters in great comfort as they enter the autumns and winters of their lives. Spring Break and summer vacation tell a different story, but for now, the town is peaceful. Gordon walked excitedly up the walkway to the door of Aunt Stella's condo. He hadn't seen his father since his high school graduation party. He was always closer to his father in youth, but when he moved out, they grew apart. That didn't change the fondness Gordon had for his father, but they surely didn't speak as much as they used to. Then he moved to Boca Raton. He made a few visits here and there, Christmas, birthdays and the like. After that graduation party, Gordon went to college and his life never slowed down enough for him to visit. He didn't have time. That was all about to change though. Gordon was going to reconnect with his father. He knocked on the door vigorously. After a few moments, it creaked open, and his elderly Aunt Stella answered. She took one look and was taken aback… "AH! ROBBERS! HEEELPP!" "Aunt Stella, Aunt Stella… settle down, settle down… it's me…" Gordon lifted up his mask mometarily to show his aunt his face. "Oh my stars, Gordon… gosh, you take that wrestling thing seriously." "Yeah, it's my job… is dad here?" "Oh… oh no, sweetie. Your father hasn't lived here for four years now." Four years… "But I send him Christmas Cards here every year and he gets them just fine." "Oh sweetie, that's because I forward all his mail. He moved out… well, he just didn't want anyone to know. But you… well, he'll be thrilled to see you. C'mon in, I'll give you directions to his place." Gordon was relieved. His father at least still lived in Boca Raton. He hopped back in his big, red Explorer and followed the directions all the way to his father's house. He parked on the street and walked up to the pathway. The lawn was expertly cut, almost with the touch of a manicurist. There was a garden filled with exotic flowers, and a weeping willow hunched over the walkway to the door. Gordon walked on the concrete path all the way up to the door and rang the doorbell, stomach churning with over 15 years of waiting boiling and bubbling inwardly. The door creaked open, and a mustachioed man dressed in khaki pants and a Hawaiian shirt peered out. He too jumped back. "AAH! ROBBERS! HEEELLLP!!" "Dad, dad, no, no, it's me. Me, Gordon." His shock turned to utter delight. "Oh Gordon… dammit, do you have to wear that mask everywhere you go? Oh nevermind, come give your dad a big hug." Gordon rushed in and embraced his father. "I missed you so much dad." "I missed you too…" As they let go, Gordon looked around the house. The interior decorating, he thought, was exquisite. "So I guess Aunt Stella told you where I lived." "Yeah dad… why did you have to keep it a secret?" "Well… umm…" Another voice came booming from inside the den. "Oh Richard, silly, who came in?" "Anton, come on out and meet my son!" "Jour son? This is a glorious day!" From out of the direction of said den, another man, hair slicked back wearing white pants and a white "wife-beater" shirt. He traipsed into the living room and put his arm around Richard. "Uh dad… who's this?" "Oh, it's Anton, he's my life partner." Gordon took a step back. His lower jaw dropped like the invisible cords that were keeping it shut and fastened to the rest of the skull snapped instantaneously. "Gordon… there's something I have to tell you… I'm… well, I'm gay." "No… no…" Gordon started inching away from his father, walking backwards ever so slowly and carefully the way he came. "Gordon, why are you looking at me like that?" Shock. "Gordon, please, stop that." Shock. "Please, understand…" Horror. "When your mother filed for divorce… I… I was beside myself." Horror. "I had nowhere to go, I was desperate… I…" "I've heard enough… I… I have to go." Gordon turned tail and bolted out of his father's house. Richard Powell's normally broad shoulders slumped in utter disappointment. Anton nestled his arm around them even tighter. "Oh Richard, what did jou expect from Capitan Republican? Come on, I'll make jou mohitos. Jou want some mohitos?" "No, I'll just take a shot of Jack, thanks." It was a good thing Gordon had an FOP card, or else he would have been ticketed for speeding twice on his mad dash from his father's house. Local traffic laws be damned, Gordon had to get away from there to someplace where he could be alone. He didn't want to leave Boca, but he didn't know where else to go. He had already visited most of the losing candidates from the massacre known as the 2006 elections, and he couldn't go back to his mother's house either. So he went to the only place he could think of: The Boca Raton Holiday Inn. He checked in and carried all his bags hurriedly up to his room, paying no mind to anyone else. He almost broke down his hotel room door, then slammed it shut. Locking the door, he looked around the room, making sure he was alone. Then, with steps as slow and steady as an elephant crossing the plains of Africa, he plodded across the room towards the chair in the corner. He turned around and plopped his butt like a television thrown out a four story window by a jilted girlfriend at a cheating boyfriend below. Then, he did something he hadn't done in years. He cried. Two days passed. Gordon ate nothing, drank nothing, didn't take a shower, change, do anything else than sit in the same position and let his ass-blisters grow to epic proportions. The pain of those callouses paled in comparison to the pain in his heart, the crushing blow that his father, his only strong male role model that wasn't the President of the United States or the World Wrestling Federation Champion, was a liar, a sneak and worst of all, a homosexual, an abomination before God, the United States and the Republican Party. His own father… how could he betray me like this, he thought. After two days, the phone rang. Luckily for Gordon, his chair was right next to the nightstand where it was located. Gordon picked up the phone. "Hello, Gordon? It's your father. I…" Click. He didn't want to hear it. He didn't want to hear anything from the man who just crushed his entire world. The phone rang again, Gordon picked it up again. Richard tried to plead his case again, but Gordon wouldn't have any of it. After the third time, he stopped picking up the phone. For the next hour, the phone rang almost non-stop. Gordon refused to pick it up. It could have been President Bush for all he cared, but he wasn't taking the chance of having to listen to his own father. After an hour though, heads tend to start aching after hearing shrill ringing noises almost non-stop. Gordon's brain was no exception. The ringing of the phone started ringing his ears. He couldn't take it. He picked up the phone with every intention of telling his father off once and for all. "DAD!" Gordon stopped. He heard something on the other end. He heard a man crying, sniffling. "Gordon… *sniff, sniff*… please, please listen to me…" Suddenly, another voice came back into his head. "Unconditional love, Gordon. That means you should love me no matter how fucked up I am. That's family." His mother was right, but God was also right too when He said that homosexuality was wrong. Gordon spoke up again. "Dad… I'm going out. I… I… I'm sorry." He hung up the phone and got up out of his chair. After rubbing his behind, finally noticing the ass-blisters, he rushed out of his room and into his Explorer. With questions clouding his mind, he revved his engine and put it in drive, looking around desperately on the Boca streets for the first place he could go to talk to someone. He looked left, then right, then left, and when he looked right again, he found it. St. Lucy's Roman Catholic Church. Sure, Gordon wasn't Catholic, but at this point, Christian was Christian. He parked the SUV and hurried into the church. In the vestibule, he noticed a message board. "Confessions today, 3:30-6:30 PM." Looking down at his watch, he noticed it was only 4:15. Gordon's brand of Christianity doesn't have the confession of sins, so he was a bit flummoxed on how the whole process worked. He thought about sitting down, but noticed the pews were made of wood. Not good for a blistered behind. He looked around and noticed someone exiting a confessional. He hurried over and went right into the box. He kneeled down, and a voice spoke through the screen. "Good afternoon, my son." Gordon didn't know what to say at first, but he remembered a movie he saw that featured a Catholic confession. "Umm, bless me Father, for I'm a sinner…" He paused, not knowing what to say. "Umm…. but I'm not here to confess." "Oh really? Then why are you in this confessional today, my son?" Another pause. "Well Father… I need someone to talk to… you see, I'm not Catholic. I'm a Christian, but not a Catholic, but that's not important. Right now, I'm having a family crisis." "Go ahead, my son. All men are Children of God in my eyes, and I am willing to lend an ear to any and all who need it." "Thank you Father… anyway, you see, I saw my father the other day for the first time in 15 years. I mean, I kept in contact with him, but this was the first time I saw him face to face. And when I got to his house, I had an awful, awful revelation. My father… my father revealed to me that he was a homosexual." Pause. "That is quite unfortunate, my son, but not the end of the world. How… how did you react?" "Well…" Gordon took a deep breath and gulped. "Well, I kinda freaked out, ran out and spent the next two days crying at the Holiday Inn." "Okay, that is an extreme reaction." "Well, you have to understand something, Father. My dad… he meant the world to me when I was a kid. He was my hero, and now, now that I've found this out, I felt like I've been lied to my whole life. And now… now my father, he's going to go to Hell, and I don't want him to, and I want God to look on him in a positive light." "I understand." Another pause. "My son… you're right. Homosexuality, it is an abomination before God. I understand that you're upset, and I probably would be too. However, my son, do you recall what The Fourth Commandment is?" "Oh, yes Father… 'Honor thy father and thy mother…' Oh! Oh, Father…" "You see now?" "Yes… well, yes and no. I mean, I do honor him. I love him. I… I… my mother has already been a huge disappointment to me, I just don't want my father to turn out like that too. I want to help him. What should I do?" "Well, my son, I can't tell you what you should do in matters like this. You have to look into your heart and listen extra carefully at what God is telling you." "Oh… okay Father. Thank you." Gordon scuttled out of the confessional and out of the church. As he sat back down in his Explorer, he thought about what the priest had told him. Long and hard. His stomach was still churning, but with dilemma instead of anticipation. Just at the point where he thought he was going to have an ulcer, he decided. He turned the key on the ignition, and off he went. KNOCK KNOCK KNOCK! "Richard, can jou get that? I'm making a Cobb salad!" Richard was sitting by the phone, his glassy eyes stained by tears. "I don't feel much like moving." "Come on, silly, it'll do jou good to move." Slowly but surely, Richard got to his feet and plodded towards the door. The knocks were getting louder and harder. "Pipe down, I'm coming," he said halfway to the door. He inhaled deeply and sighed as he got to the door. He turned the doorknob, expecting to greet the person knocking with a lifeless expression worthy of being cast in the next George Romero zombie flick. That lifeless expression turned to one of shock as he saw his son standing at the door. "Dad…" "Gordon…" Gordon looked down at his feet, almost like he was trying to avoid his father. "Dad… listen… I don't approve of your new lifestyle. I think it's a sin and an abomination before God…" "Gordon, lis…" "BUT," Gordon forcefully interrupted. "But, I'm not God. I'm not here to judge you. I'm your son, and no matter what… I love you." He picked his masked head up and looked his father in the eye. "I love you too, son." Once again, they hugged. "I'm sorry, daddy. I'm so sorry…" "It's okay. It's okay. Just come in." They let go, and Gordon looked back into his father's eyes. "You're more than welcome to stay for dinner, Gordon." "Oh… thanks, dad. What are you guys making?" "Cobb salad." "That's a little light. How about I make some steaks?" "Steaks, well…" "Did the little munchkin say steak? Richard, he read my mind, but we don't have any." "That's alright Anton, we don't have to…" "No dad. Why don't you and me go out to the Giant and grab some steaks." Richard looked in his son's eyes. "You know what, that sounds like a good idea." Father and son walked out to the red Explorer. Tonight there was going to be a steak dinner.
