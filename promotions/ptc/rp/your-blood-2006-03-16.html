Your Blood…

16 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-16 "Zis is not easy to understand, you realise," said the doctor to the man laying on the ground. "Please speak up if you don’t understand." Shanty nodded, and tried to get his muscles to respond to the pleas from his brain, without success. "Ze vorld that ve live in is governed by two sets of rules, depending on ze scale of the events that are taking place. On the one hand, you’ve got large scale events like bounding a tennis ball, or having a wrestling match. These fall under the rules of regular dynamics. You hit something hard enough and it’ll move. Something will be in a place and you can see it’s there for sure. It’s the world we live in." Going back to talking about the kind of work he had been involved in for years before he took on the persona of ‘Doctor Curiosity’, Chris Goodman’s accent started to fade until it was barely there at all. "Then there’s the other world, which is the world of quantum mechanics. Don’t worry, I know it sounds like the kind of crap that sent you to sleep when you were at school, but it’s crazy stuff, and given what it just did to you, I think you’re going to want to hear this. You see, things don’t react how you’d expect them to when you get down to the level of the tiniest particles in the Universe. When you’re looking for an electron, you can’t just take a picture of it. It defies all common sense, but you can never tell where it is and what it’s doing at the same time. It’s called the Uncertainty Principle." Shanty nodded his head, as Paul covered his eyes. "I thought you said this wasn’t going to be dull." "Shut up, Eegor!" said the doctor, briefly dropping his mental mask into place, before continuing. "Now, the Uncertainty Principle isn’t really linked to what’s happening here, and why you’re on the floor, but it just goes to show how weird these quantum effects can be. Trying to pin them down is like trying to find a couple of goldfish in an Olympic swimming pool whilst blindfolded, and with your hands cut off. Or maybe it isn’t, because we can see the effects that electrons have on the world, jah? Your radio, your iPod, your computer, whatever the latest gadget is, they’re all run by streams of electrons, but you can’t pinpoint the right one and know what it’s doing at the same time!" "If you would move somewhere near to the point, please, doctor," said The Ripper, in a tone full of civility. "It would be greatly appreciated by all who are present." "This normally takes a semester and a string of equations longer zan your arm," snapped Curiosity. "Be thankful zat I am being brief." The doctor scratched his chin. "Ah yes,now, perhaps the strangest thing about all things quantum is quantum entanglement, which can lead to quantum teleportation and…" "Woah… teleportation? Isn’t that science fiction?" asked Shanty, propping himself up on one elbow and flexing his fingers, sensing that his strength was returning. "Jah! But not quantum teleportation. You see, it is possible to link two quantum states of a system together, to entangle them." "Quantum states?" "Jah! It’s like the energy of the system… of whatever body of atoms we are talking about. The electrons, the things that bind together the molecules in our bodies, the cells that make us all up, they are governed by these quantum rules. You can, if you’re very careful, manipulate these electrons so that they are entangled, caught up in one another, even though they might not be touching at all." Shanty and Paul looked across at the doctor, with blank faces. "This might be why you never made it as an author of popular science," said The Ripper as he walked between Shanty and Doctor Curiosity. "Instead, skip all of this theorising and think of n electron, spinning big and fast in the ether. Now think of it splitting into two, so you have these two balls spinning." The huge man had removed a small sponge ball from his pocket, the kind favoured by magicians, and then with his enormous fingers moving as deftly as any illusionist’s, there were suddenly two balls where one had previously rested. He threw one to Paul Goodman, and kept the other visible. "Now, you can take these two balls to the opposite ends of the room, or to the far corners of the world, but if you suddenly make one electron spin the other way…" Jack gestured, and the ball flew from Paul’s hands. "…then the other one changes direction at exactly the same time, even though there’s no link between the two in any way." Shanty shook his head. "And you discovered this?" "No," laughed The Ripper. "This has been known for quite some time. Einstein himself called it ‘spooky action at a distance’, which was quaint, really. People have been trying to get it to work at a large, practical scale for years too. Even as recently as five years ago a group of scientists got very excited that they entangled two clouds of particles that were millimetres apart… but technology moves on so quickly these days. The conventional science laboratories are still troubled by getting anything useful from it, bar the simplest of quantum computers, but they have not possessed the necessity of the matter that The Ripper has. They have not had the motivation required to do anything in pursuit of the goal of dominion over the world of the sub-atomic." "And you have?" asked Shanty, raising himself up to one knee. "Pray to your God that you will never need the same motivation," said The Ripper, with a trace of regret in his voice. *** Frank wandered through the park as the last of the sunshine filtered down through dust and pollution in the atmosphere, casting glorious shadows from its majestic palette. His mind was clear; in fact it was clearer than it had been for a long, long time. He could vaguely remember something about a dark man, and something about being a competitor in some tournament? In some sort of competition? As soon as the thoughts alighted on his mind they drifted away, caught by a breeze in his synapses, controlled by something other than his own volition. Still, he did not notice it, and as he walked on he appreciated every flower, every blade of grass that kept the world in such a wonderful state. He couldn’t remember much, but he liked it that way. It would all come to him in good time, and after all, he wasn’t in any rush. Life was for the living. *** "So that’s what the factories were about? You wanted to learn how to work magic?" asked Shanty. "No, they were about more than that. They served three purposes, and that was one of them. Another was the very dull business of genuine torture and interrogation. Of course, you don’t get away with committing those sorts of acts within military confines without them getting some information back for it. Our techniques proved particularly effective, but at the same time it was never the point of the operation. It was never what we were searching for." "So why don’t you just tell us what they were there for and quit with the grandstanding?" "Oh Shanty," said Jack, his voice becoming soft and delicate, in sharp contrast to his bulk. "You would love for The Ripper to solve all your problems for you, and tie it all up with a nice little bow, wouldn’t you?" Shanty was back on his feet, and without even a millisecond of hesitation he charged at Jack, instinctively going for his enemy. One arm waved in his general direction was all it took. Jack’s eyes locked on to Shanty and a brief flick of the wrist sent the wrestler down to the floor again. "Your body wanted to go one way, but your electrons suddenly seemed very attracted to the far side of the room," sneered The Ripper. Shan tried to get up, but his muscles were numb again. "Do not try such foolishness again. The Ripper would hate to see you all broken and battered before you can finish the task." "You want me to do something for you?" "Of course," replied Jack. "Why else would you be here? You must simply defeat Rich Rollins in the ring and claim the GTT5 tournament victory." "What does that have to do with anything?" asked Shanty. "So many questions, little man. Perhaps one day you will have the answers, but for now consider it enough that it is a job you must complete." "Then I won’t do it," said the finalist, getting slowly back to his feet, urging life back into his muscles. "You can try to make me, but I’ll resist." "Of course you will," said The Ripper, walking away and running his hand down the sashes that hung from the bed. His face looked disappointed, but it was hard to tell whether it was with Shanty Jones or with the quality of the furnishings. "Or rather, you’ll say that you will resist, but in the end there will be an offer that… it’s a cliché… but there will be an offer that you can’t refuse." "Try me." "Very well. Should you enter the final of GTT5 and use all that there is at your disposal to win the match, then The Ripper shall leave you alone for the rest of your natural life, no matter whether you win or lose. Even the implants will be shut down." Shanty didn’t know where to start. "Th… the implants?" he stammered. "How do you think that you can control the forces if not with technology. Shanty, to turn your electrons away from you, an equal force must be applied to the oppressor. Your electrons were flipped as a reaction, and it’s all controlled right here," said Jack, tapping the side of his head. "Without the quantum processors, a useful little side effect of mastering entanglement, The Ripper would be just as affected by the change as you. Fortunately, the calculations can spread the effect over the entirety of the oppressor’s body, slipping out in tiny bursts everywhere, in places without nerve endings or bundles of sensitive tissues. It’s quite the feeling, Shan, but you can always try it for yourself." "For myself?" "You have, after all, had Paul’s DNA implanted into you. His blood inhabits your body, just waiting for you to flip the switch and turn it on. It’s all inside of you, Shanty Jones. The entangled material, the quantum processor, the ability to control it. All of it can be yours, Shanty." A look of wonder passed across Shan’s face. To be in the situation was somewhere between a fantasy and a nightmare. The power was almost intoxicating; to know that he could do what was being done to him by The Ripper filled him with a sense of duty, a sense that he wanted to perform. Paul Goodman had sinned against him, and now he was there, ready to be punished. And yet… And yet… "I don’t want to judge people any more," said Shanty. "The last time I did it was for God, and you’re really not quite up there with him. I could wrestle Rich Rollins in the final, if he somehow gets out of jail and they quit procrastinating over whether they’ll postpone the match or not… but I won’t do it for you. I’ll just do it to be free." "Yes. The Ripper will set you free, again." Shan’s eyes narrowed. Again? "Of course again," said The Ripper, ignoring that Shanty had not actually spoken. "Your nice little act of rebellion with the bomb was enough to kill Steven and several others in your squad, and it was enough to kill you too. Don’t get too delusional that you were brought back by God, for it was something far more tangible that allowed you to live on, that brought you back from the brink of oblivion." Shanty couldn’t even speak; could only stare into the face of someone who had been ahead of him since the very first step of his journey. "The other purpose of the factories, Shanty, was perhaps the true masterpiece. Organs were harvested and DNA was checked, stored and catalogued. More than that though, we created embryos and harvested the cells from within. Then, when we had the prisoners, they gave us everything we needed to move into mass production. Their bodies provided fertile grounds for our new creations, grown from scratch, from miniscule batches of cells to fully formed, beautiful products." The three men in the room looked directly at The Ripper, their minds all reeling, thinking of the different possibilities. Was this cloning? Had they grown entirely new human beings? "When your bomb went off, and you were taken to hospital, a call was made to the factory that you yourself had seen. They brought over the correct equipment to revive you, where most of the medical world would have given up. Your heart was ragged, scratched by a broken rib that was in danger of poking all the way through it. Your lungs were collapsed and damaged beyond repair. Your other organs, your kidneys and liver were traumatised to a point where they could not sustain their functions by themselves. You had lost so much blood, Shanty. Without the other equipment, you were a dead man. So they brought it in, and from the cases they took your heart, your lungs and your other organs. With due care they removed their predecessors from your ravaged shell of a body and piece by piece put you back together. Shanty Jones, you are only alive today because of the work of Jayenne Industries; because of the work of The Ripper." *** Frank sat down on a park bench as the lights finally dimmed away and dusk settled over the city. Everything took on a more menacing tone, and it was no longer the thrill of victory and the sense of achievements past that lingered within him. The thoughts were now replaced by shadows, by nightmares of being kept in one single room in a building for a year, being forced to take drugs that slowly eradicated who he was inside. He blinked, and the thoughts were gone again, chemicals floating around his brain, interrupting thought processes and obscuring neural connections. What an odd day, thought Frank, as he got up from the bench and went in search of his home, wherever that might be. *** There wasn’t much that could have been said in response to The Ripper’s revelation. Whether it was true or not was impossible to tell, though there was always something about the way that Jack spoke that made people think that it had to be the truth, that it was the only way things could possibly have occurred. Shanty’s response threw even The Ripper a curveball. "So what?" One dark eyebrow was raised towards the Welshman. "So what if you brought me back to life? I’m the one standing here now, and whether my organs are my own or you grew them off someone else, does that alter anything? Jack, I know that I’ve done bad things in the past, but I have atoned for them, and whatever you did to me should lie on your own conscience, not mine." "Interesting…" mused The Ripper. "I’m just past caring now. A couple of years ago I was just an ordinary man, and now I’ve seen things that still makes me wake up in a cold sweat, I’ve killed friends of mine and then guided their souls to one of their two inevitable destinations. I’ve seen that you are one truly fucked up psychopath and that you must be paying some hot shit scientists of dubious moral character to get you to do what you can do. I know you can kill me, but since you’ve refrained thus far I think I’m safe on that for now." "The Ripper promises that you will never be harmed any more than is absolutely necessary." "That’s very kind of you," said Shanty, his voice betraying that he had been broken somewhere inside. His soul, some indefinable part of his being had finally run out of patience with the world. He may have been given a second life, but it was still his to use. "So, does your deal still stand? I fight him and you let me go free?" "Don’t trust him," interjected Paul Goodman. "He’s got something up his sleeve. He won’t let you just walk away. He never does." "You doubt The Ripper’s kind intentions?" asked Jack, with a smile on his lips. "Well, that’s almost hurtful." Shanty had stopped trembling from the after-effects of the ‘shock’, and he stared right at the man who had irrevocably altered his destiny. "So, what’s the catch? What’s it to be? You want me to kill him for you or something?" "Oh no," smiled Jack. "Nothing as terrible as all that. You just have to go out there and try to beat him. Nothing more and nothing less." "He’s lying," insisted Paul. "He’s not," replied Shanty. "He’s just not telling us everything." The Ripper smiled again at Shanty Jones. "You catch on quicker than most." "So, what’s it to be?" "You just have to use everything at your disposal," said Jack. "Including this…" The Ripper produced a vial from the inside pocket of his long, black coat, and held it up to the light. Inside the glass was a viscous, dark red liquid that almost seemed to shimmer, to refuse to be seen in its entirety. "And that is?" asked Shanty. "This is the DNA of a certain Mister Rich Rollins," replied Jack. "I think you know what to do with it."
