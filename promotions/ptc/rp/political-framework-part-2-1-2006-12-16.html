Political Framework: Part 2 [1]

16 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-16 (Author's note: vs. Ember … good luck) November 25th, 2006: AWC was dead. One of its up and coming superstars, Cedric Riley, felt the same way. In a period of his wrestling career that was ostensibly glorified, the former AWC superstar felt like his best friend had just died, despite his recently-acquired accolades. Namely, he was in the final six as a GTT participant, and he had obtained a prize in Triangles – a heavily-contested main event match, and the final one of the promotion. These two accomplishments he would expectedly become proud of in time. His success in wrestling was contrary to his real-life affairs, though, where he was involved in a much bigger problem. While the wrestling had taken precedence, unfortunately, it didn't make his murder mess any less of a problem that he was expected to face. Eventually, he planned on taking the appropriate action to help his cause. As a logical human being, Cedric was always trying to 'help his cause' in the traditional sense of the expression. Since being suspected for murder involvement, his actions yielded a much more influential effect on his life, thus making the 'appropriate action' much more demanding. In fact, his life could very well be turned upside down with the slam of a Judge's gavel. Obviously, being sent to prison was the absolutely worse possible outcome, and to be avoided at all costs. He wished it were less realistic, more implausible, and that his problems would just 'go away'; essentially, that's what everyone wants, isn't it? Cedric Riley's persona did not fit in to the typical 'everyone' group. A former CIA agent that was contracted to AWC as a professional wrestler, he had made fruitless efforts to adapt to a new lifestyle. He was passionate about wrestling, and it was considerably less dangerous then his former career. The truth was, he wanted to cross the bridge sooner than was plausible; such a change was, while ideal, very much unrealistic. The crossover would in fact would be proven as easier said then done, with continuous obstacles being thrown in his path. He would continue to dodge them; as best as he was capable of, anyway. The former agent was a very capable man, he just needed the right motive. It was only the day after Triangles that Cedric Riley was in his hotel room, lazily stretched across the bed and staring admissibly at the ceiling. After the closing of AWC, he had accepted the room as a refuge – a somewhat effective way of avoiding the emptiness inside. It was not one of his prouder moments; he had never felt so useless in his life, which was ironic, because he had never been hotter in the wrestling ring. Grand Slam Package winner, and semi-finalist in GTT – two achievements that he never thought he would surmount to. But there he was, lying on the bed, unsure of how he was to deal with his sorrows. He still had, at minimum, two matches to wrestle in the tournament, yet he felt like he could never step into a ring again. The pride of being a professional entertainer had completely vanished, and he was left to almost nothing. Cedric didn't know why he was feeling this way. There were so many loose ends to tie up in his life, but the motivation was gone. He didn't want to move on; he wanted to stay in this hotel room for as long as possible. The presence of AWC in Cedric's life had been significant. He had chased his passion for wrestling, and furthermore, his dream, for a long time in his adulthood, yet he failed to find somewhere to fit in. That all changed when he joined Atlantic, where it was never a struggle to wrestle each night, but instead, it came naturally. This was what made it fun, and a career that he could see himself in for a long time. Unfortunately, all good things must come to an end. And what was even more unfortunate – he had to move on. Somehow, someway, he had to get over himself and get the hell out of his hotel room and face the world, because there was much to be faced. He needed to deal with the detective that was trying to put him in prison. Eventually, he had to find a new job. A surge of unfounded motivation hit him and he got up off the bed, heading towards the door. He had places to be, people to talk to, and problems to get rid of. And a door to answer, because just then, a knock sounded into the hotel room. Cedric opened the door. "Agent Jim Charles, FBI." The agent stood at over six feet and wore a fancy black suit. He flashed open his wallet with one hand, boasting a federal badge while he used his free hand to remove his sunglasses. Sharp blue eyes pierced into Cedric, who stood indecisively, contemplating his reaction. He almost looked as if he didn't care, an irregular attitude from the normally feisty and stubborn AgentDash. "Here to arrest me?" Cedric scoffed. "About time." Jim Charles smirked. "I wish it were that easy. How about you let me in and we have a little talk?" Cedric raised an eyebrow. "Why can't we talk out here?" The agent sighed, casually putting his hands in his pockets, then leaned towards Cedric and muttered, "This isn't the place for this conversation." "Alright, come on in," he accepted uneasily. He was naturally suspicious of the agent's friendliness, when it was assumed that he was here to arrest Cedric, a task ostensibly unpleasant. Jim Charles moved forward past Cedric and into the room, while Cedric took a quick glance both directions in the hallway before closing the door. "So, 'Agent Charles' …" Cedric began cynically, "If you're not here to take me into custody, what in the hell is going on?" Jim had his back to Cedric as he paced uncertainly towards the window. He fingered the curtains open and gazed outside into the dark Californian sky, taking a deep breath. "Well, Cedric …" he said, choosing his words cautiously, "I didn't come here to talk to you about this whole Sawyer mess." The agent sounded rather annoyed with the 'Sawyer mess', but turned round to face Cedric and continued anyway, "But I may as well let you in on a few facts. Richard Holmes has been taken off the case. While certainly a credible detective, his personal involvement with Sawyer made him a bad choice from the get-go. And before you ask, yes, I was the one who assigned him. I made a mistake." "Well, that comes as unexpected," Cedric replied briefly. "There must be a better explanation …" Jim nodded. "We at the FBI have investigated further, and have determined that Holmes' motivation for suspecting you was … how can I put this … unethical." Cedric remembered seeing the detective and Heather, the maid, at the airport only a few weeks before, and put the facts together. "Wait a second …" "Please, that's enough of that for now," the agent interrupted. "Needless to say, you're off the hook. The case itself is far from being solved, but we're onto a lead now. But that's not why I'm here … well, not really." Cedric frowned. "I'm not following …" "I didn't plan on being here very long, so I'll get straight to it – I'm here to offer you a job," Jim said forwardly. "At the FBI." Cedric's eyes widened. The room fell silent as the two men found each other's eyes; Jim was looking intently at his opposite, awaiting a response. Cedric expectedly didn't have one ready, other than slowly sitting down on the foot of the bed, and looking away from the agent. "I don't--" he began, trying to find the right words. His immediate reaction was that there was no possible way – was there? The excuses not to were plentiful, but they just weren't coming to him. Other than GTT, he currently had no planned future in wrestling. His girlfriend and son were of concern, but he was only 29 years old! That was much too early for retirement, especially for a man of his ambition. "While I don't follow wrestling, and I don't exactly consider it a legitimate career choice, but I did hear that the promotion you worked for closed recently," Jim said. "Yesterday," Cedric reminded him quietly. "Of course," Jim echoed. "I knew it would be a little early for this offering, but on the contrary, I wanted to inform you as soon as possible to give you time to think about it. As I understand, despite not being employed very long in the CIA, you were an asset nonetheless. You're young, talented, and even experienced -- we would love to have you work with us." Cedric nodded. "I appreciate it, Jim … I really do … but … this comes as rather unexpected. And I might be out of a job, but I still have commitments to fulfill," he added, staying true to his involvement in the inter-fed tournament. "Understood. But please, think about it," the agent requested politely, then beckoned for a handshake. "I will." He would. * * * December 2, 2006: Cedric Riley sat in the office of Agent Jim Charles, located on the second floor of the FBI Los Angeles field office. He was alone for the time being; sitting uncomfortably in his formal attire, awaiting the return of the office's owner. The door opened and Jim Charles stepped inside, greeting his visitor with a nod. He circled around to the back of his desk and sat down in his chair. "Good afternoon, Cedric," the special agent in charge acknowledged, sorting through the loose papers on his desk. "I take it that all of your admission proceedings went well?" Cedric nodded. It had been a week since he had accepted his position; exceptions had been made involving his recruitment process – given that he was in the CIA prior to this, the procedural training and tests were unnecessary in his case. He was required to go through all the legal nonsence, though, which included the new FBI agent to pen through multiple documents. It was a rather tedious effort, but one that he had been expected to do nonetheless, which he accepted. "All that's left is your badge and gun, which I have here," Jim said. He rolled his chair around to a cupboard near the back corner. He opened the lock with a key and extracted a small leather bag. He set it on his desk and revealed the contents – a sleek black handgun, a badge, and Cedric's new identification. "The rest of your equipment will be in your locker. I trust you know where that is?" Jim asked. Cedric nodded. "And I trust you remember that I have other commitments before I can fully dedicate to this position?" "I do," Jim responded indifferently. "What is it … two more of these wrestling nuances?" "At least," Cedric confirmed, then paused. "After that, I'm done." The agent-in-charge smiled. "It's great to have you on board, Mr. Riley." After GTT, Cedric had no business in wrestling. He was done. * * * December 8th, 2006: "I have your first assignment." Cedric held his cellular phone up to his ear, listening intently to his new boss as he paced his hotel room in Los Angeles. "What?! I thought I told you that I couldn't work until--" "Yes, I know. Unfortunately, we have a new development on the very case the you were targeted for. Call it a rather drastic turn of events, but given the circumstances, I think you're the right man for the job," Jim explained, mincing words. "… Okay," the new agent replied uneasily. "Where is this going?" "Juvian Cohol. Remember the name?" Jim prompted quickly. "Yes," Cedric replied, remembering back to his conversations with Alex. Juvian Cohol was the man who Sawyer put in jail just before his demise; only a short time after, Juvian escaped from prison, and was rumored to be involved with higher, more sophisticated organized crime than petty deviances. "It turns out that he was working for a man right here in Los Angeles for a few months before he got locked away," Jim explained. "Go on," Cedric urged, his instincts kicking in. The thrill of embarking on dangerous missions had evaporated from his professional life since leaving the CIA; with his new job and this new information, he almost felt … rejuvenated. "Victor Roberts. A rather powerful figure here in LA … he owns some businesses around town and brings in a very healthy profit. Turns out that he is one of the top funders in some organized crime in the city … unfortunately, we don't have any proof. Considering he is Juvian's boss … and he, like most people, had beef with Sawyer … we shouldn't ignore the possibility of involvement with the murder itself." "Well .. that's great and all that you have a new lead," Cedric acknowledged, "but where do I come in?" "Good thing you asked. He's hosting a party on his yacht down by the docks tonight, and I have two invitations. One of our undercover agents is already set to attend … and, well … she needs a date." * * * It was only a few hours later that Cedric met his 'date' for the evening as she picked him up in front of his hotel. He got into the passenger seat and greeted his partner for the evening. "It's nice to meet you, Regina. You look lovely," he complimented. "I anticipate this will be a great evening …" "Thankyou … I hope you know, however, how serious this is …" she replied. "This seems rather sudden, I know … I'm just as surprised as you are," Cedric acknowledged. "Yeah … well, unfortunately, my normal partner got caught up in some bad business today," she explained as she sped the vehicle onto the road. "Now, I have to go over the details of this assignment with you, so listen up … we don't have much time." Cedric looked at the clock on the dashboard, which read 10 PM. The yacht party was scheduled to start in only fifteen minutes. "This isn't just about bringing Victor in for questioning about the murder," she said quickly. "Of course, that's probably the main reason, but I've been tailing him for a while now, and I've been wanting to get a peak at the interior of that boat of his. Seems like a prime place to store something … oh, I don't know … illegal?" "Yeah," Cedric concurred, digging back into his CIA days and remembering infiltrating such places – wealthy criminals were renown for these kind of hideouts. "All that really matters, Cedric, is that we get his evidence. And by we, I mean you," Regina informed. "What?!" Cedric responded quickly, caught off guard. Regina nodded. "I was hesitant to agree with Charles … but it's for the best. You have far more training than I do with stealth, and that's what we need." "It's been a while, though. I mean …" Regina shook her head. "We need confidence from you, Cedric. I know what it's like stepping into a new position and being thrown at the wolves. Unfortunately, as you are a rather unique and rare recruit, we're making exceptions. Normally it would take much longer to involve an agent in a situation that calls for this amount of skill, but like I said, you're an exception." Cedric nodded in agreement and looked out the window as they sped along the highway, the Pacific Ocean glossed under moonlight to their right. "Seeing as Victor Charles is already familiar with me and believes me to be an accomplished real estate agent rather than an undercover cop, I'll try and preoccupy Victor. The party will take place on the deck of the yacht, so you have to sneak into the vessel and find the goods. We're under the impression that there is cocaine hidden somewhere in there … it's your goal to find it." "Okay," Cedric agreed silently. He was ready. "What we need is enough to warrant a search …" she added, turning the car into a side-road. It lead to a parking lot that was only a short walk from the water, where multiple docks floated. She parked the car and grabbed her purse. "Open the glove-box." Cedric did – it was rather spacious. Big enough, in fact, for two hand-guns to fit nicely. * * * A private dock that floated just off-shore of the Los Angeles coast was home to a handful of yachts. Some looked more expensive than others, however, they all gave the impression that their owner was rather wealthy. One in particular was currently bursting with activity; people in formal attire laughed and chatted as they boarded the large white boat, anxious to join the party that had already started on its deck. The call for celebration was unimportant – for the majority of these people, elegant parties were a weekend occurrence. The owner of the yacht, Victor Roberts, could certainly afford to host them with his deep pockets and impressive circle of friends. Expectedly, he was already on deck, dressed in fine, tailored threads and sipping casually at his Scotch. He was in the company a few men that looked of great importance, and their wives were off to the side, conversing casually about recent gossip. The invite-only occasion was beginning to reach its decided limit, and Cedric Riley -- "Johnathan Carter" for the night -- was one of the last few to come on board. On his arm was an older but attractive woman – Agent Regina Saunders, cover name "Elizabeth Jones". She wore a spectacular black dress and boasted a smile that could catch any man's wandering eye. It was a beautiful night and the party went well. 'Elizabeth' introduced 'Johnathan' to many important people throughout the night – men who owned banks, stock-market geniuses, and older casino owners who were never without a cigar. These men had wives or girlfriends who were considerably younger than them, and one could only presume their motivation for crawling into bed with filthy-rich old men every night. It was almost midnight by the time Cedric and Regina slipped nonchalantly into a group of people who listened intently to Victor Roberts. He was explaining how and why the LA economy was about to hit a standstill, and everyone seemed to agree. "Elizabeth!" he greeted the 'real-estate' agent with a friendly smile. "It's nice to see you, Victor," she responded as he took her hand and kissed it gently. "I hope business has been treating you well," he acknowledged. "As good as a woman could ask for," Regina joked, smiling shyly. It provoked a chuckle from her male audience, and even Cedric grinned slightly. "So, who is this lucky man with you tonight?" Victor asked, surveying Cedric closely. "Johnathan Carter," Regina introduced, "he's a friend of mine from Berkeley." "Ah … I see. What business are you in, Mr. Carter?" Victor inquired, looking towards Cedric. "I work for a real estate agency as well," Cedric lied. "In fact, that is how Ms. Jones and I met." He added a smile as he looked at his partner, who returned the gesture; the intended flirtatiousness was well-executed, and their audience noticed the chemistry. It was also Cedric's signal. "Well, it's nice to meet you," Victor admitted, showing a faint smile as he extended his hand. Cedric shook it, then asked, "This might seem like a bad time, but may I use your washroom?" "Of course! Mike here will show you the way," Victor offered, gesturing towards a man to his left. "I appreciate the offer, but I would rather not remove him from the party …" Cedric said. "No, please, I insist …" "It's fine," Cedric reassured. "It's just on the floor below, then?" Victor nodded, and Cedric thanked him. Regina continued to talk with them as Cedric moved towards a short, narrow set of stairs that led to a door. He opened it and walked into a room – one that wouldn't be out of place in a home, it was a combination of a dining room, living room, and kitchen, with all the common furniture arranged conveniently. Across from him was another door that was assumed to be the bathroom, which was not his destination. Instead, he began to search around the room, quickly opening cupboards and looking anywhere and everywhere to find any indication of drugs. A couch that was positioned against the wall was his next target. He lifted up the cushions and set them aside – which led to a wooden panel that covered the space beneath. And underneath that … Jackpot. He reached into his pocket and grabbed his cell-phone, prepared to use the camera function. "Have any trouble finding the bathroom?" Cedric turned around. One of Victor's men – Mike. With a gun pointed at the new FBI agent's chest.
