Compromise (IG22 RP)

8 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“If you limit your choices only to what seems possible or reasonable, you disconnect yourself from what you truly want, and all that is left is a compromise.” Robert Fritz Then. The referee’s hand cuts through the air, pounding down, against the canvas for the third time, with unnecessary force. Then, the bell rings to confirm what everybody already knows. I don’t even attempt to stand straight away - I already know that’s not going to happen. Instead, roll onto my back and I glace across the ring at the chaos I’ve orchestrated, drinking up all of the carnage that remains as though it were a cold beer on a hot summer's day. Still on my back, and breathing heavily, my hand is raised into the air and the ref points at me, nodding his head, as the announcer readies himself to deliver news that is already redundant - twice over “Winner of this match and NNNNEEEEEEEEEWWWWWWW Infinite Gauntlet champion...Jacob McKail!!!!!!!” I should be focused on this, but my mind is elsewhere. The world whirls around me at a increasing rate, the audience scream by me in a blur of flesh and colourful fabrics and I can’t move - it’s as though a great weight is atop of me and is pressing me down, further, into the mat and there’s nothing I can do about it. Then, my entrance - and exit - music flares up and I’m filled with a heightened sense of urgency that I can’t quite seem to shake. I want to walk out of the ring with my head held high, as I know should. This, after all, is my moment of glory. All my life I've been searching for something Something never comes, never leads to nothing Nothing satisfies but I'm getting close Closer to the prize at the end of the rope. I don’t know how much longer I can last. Everything’s getting dark and I’m finding it increasingly difficult to breathe. It seems as though all of this is out of my control, but deep down I know it isn’t. This isn’t something being done to me, this is something I’m doing to myself. Done, done, on to the next one. Done, done, on to the next one Then, as quickly as I’d thrust all of this anguish upon myself, the answer comes to me in an almost blinding flash of light. Turns out, a large part of me had been contemplating something that I couldn’t, no, refused to even fully consider and I hadn't realised a damned thing. However, as it barges into the forefront of my mind, I realise the seed of this notion had been planted some time ago, in a rather dreary New York hospital room. All night long I dream of the day When it comes around and its taken away Leaves me with the feeling that I fear the most Feeling comes alive when I see your ghost. I rejuvenated my wrestling career a little over six months ago, when I set out to accomplish everything that I hadn’t accomplished before. Back then, it was a time of cheap gimmicks and catering to the smart mark’s - I hated every minute of it. That time of buffoonery ended with my wife, Mary, being diagnosed with cancer. It was untreatable and I, understandably, was devastated - I didn’t want to continue. On her deathbed, she made me promise that I would achieve all of the glory and honour that we both knew I was capable of, but was unable to even approach before. The promise kept me going when she finally faded away, it kept me alive and prevented me from carrying out the foolish actions that my heart forever prompted me to do. It also gave me focus, made me work harder, and I wanted it that much more. Done, done, on to the next one. Done, done, on to the next one Now, I’ve won the Infinite Gauntlet and I’ve achieved so much in such a short period of time. My promise to Mary is effectively fulfilled and now I can see end on the horizon, where I can be left to grieve, peacefully, and without purpose. It’s over. I’m done. *** As soon as he accepted the thoughts without resistance, everything righted itself back to the status quo and he was allowed to rolle out of the ring, under the bottom rope and made his way backstage. It’d been an hour since the match had ended and McKail was still sat in the talent locker room, propped up against the wall, wearing his wrestling tights, just staring at the dirty beige tiled floor. The thoughts were still playing heavily on his mind and he’d now begun to desperately search for a reason to dismiss them as nothing than nonsensical. He’d been unsuccessful thus far and wasn‘t entirely convinced it was even an option. “Can’t you read?” The voice prompted McKail to glance up from his self-imposed reverie and was confronted by a young rookie glaring back at him angrily. He wore casual grey sweats, with a simple white t-shirt and stood in front of an overly large kit bag. McKail didn’t recognise him, must‘ve worked the dark matches or something. What the fuck’s his problem? “What?” The rookie pointed toward the no smoking sign placed just above the locker room door. “No smoking.” McKail developed a confused expression, then glanced at the lit cigarette in his right hand. Strange, he didn’t even remember lighting the damned thing. He hadn’t even taken a drag of it yet, he’d just left it in his hand to burn. “Fuck you...” It was priceless. The rookie's face contorted into such anger, McKail found it difficult to contain a smile. Hell, he didn’t even like to smoke, he just mostly did it to piss other people off; thus far it’d worked far better than he’d ever expected it would. This rookie wasn’t the first guy McKail pissed off in such and he sure as hell certainly wouldn’t be the last. “Mu-ther-fucker!” The rookie declared, his face turning crimson. McKail prepared himself, half expecting the moron to charge at him, arms flailing. Hell, the idiot was big enough to do some damage too. But he didn’t. He simply turned and made a quick exit, mumbling something about making a complaint and taking civil action. McKail shook his head and finally took a drag of the cigarette. “Pussy.” He stood, grabbed his heavy kit bag, and made toward the locker room door. On the way he flicked the still-lit cigarette butt into the rookie’s bag, half wanting to stick around just to see the moron’s face turn a dangerous shade of purple. The troubling thoughts had faded some now, temporarily swept away by the mild entertainment, and stored within the same vault in which they once kept. However, with this occurrence as prime example, such things never stay buried for long, no matter how much McKail would want them to be. It would eventually come back to haunt him one day and he would then have to make his decision, but that was another battle for another time. Now. Perched on a rather uncomfortable, crappy, standard issue motel room chair, McKail propped his feet on the grimy windowsill, filled with various dead bugs and stared out, into the real world, at what passed for normalcy these days. Most times it helped sooth him; normalcy didn’t play a big part in his life anymore - did it ever? - and he felt oddly comforted, keeping tabs on how the other side lived. The cars rushing by on freeway in the far distance; the various people wandering the streets, stopping to glare into a shop window from time to time, as though they don’t have a care in the world; the young couples walking hand in hand, obviously in love, doing nothing in particular, but enjoying spending the time together regardless. They hadn’t a clue about life, none of them had, not yet. Nobody really did until something popped up, out of the blue, slapped you across the face and changed your live forever. Something like finding out one day that you or a loved one has a fatal disease; something like watching your wife die to said incurable disease; or something like desperately searching for the strength to carry on in spite of such an occurrence. McKail glanced over his shoulder toward the large travel bag resting on his bed, packed and ready to go, and sighed. Have I achieved all that I able to? Or can I do more...? Hell, he didn’t even realise the next round of the Infinite Gauntlet was upon him until he finally got round to reading his mail yesterday. After subconsciously suppressing the troubled thoughtsfor so long, they swarmed back to him with a vengeance, almost instantaneously, and he hadn’t left his motel room since. He’d barely eaten or slept - although the latter was hardly a side effect of this particular problem. I’ve accomplished much in six months, should I just go out now, on a high, before anything can happen to take this glory away from me? Had such a downward spiral already begun? Yes, McKail had won the GCW Television champion in only his second match within the organisation, but he’d lost it again come the second defence of the belt. Since then, he’d picked up the odd victory here and there, but he’d also picked up some loses and he sure as hell hadn‘t secured many title shots. Could a record such as mine signal greatness for the rest of my career? Or could it signal despair...? And that brought him back around to the Infinite Gauntlet. Although he would always deny it, McKail got lucky when he won round twenty of the event. He hit the right moves, just at the right time - it could’ve gone either way. Now, he had to defend his title of gauntlet champion against two other equally talented individuals. Could I get lucky a second time, or should I get with what limited glory I’ve already obtained? *** What the fuck did you think my answer was going to be? Standing outside the arena, heavy kit bag draped over his shoulder, McKail made the decision that would define him forevermore. He stood right in the middle of the concrete terrace, by the narrow civilian entrance and watched his name flicker on and off the electronic billboard. To the passer’s by he must’ve looked like some moron in a trance, lsimilar those deranged people you see just glaring up at the sky, expecting some kind of fairy tale UFO to scoop them up and take them away. But as soon as he saw his name in ten foot high letters, his mind was made up. What the fuck was I thinking? After months of antagonising over the issue, the decision wasn’t even a decision at all. Hell, in the end, it wasn’t even an issue. Quit now? Because of what? You’re afraid you can’t do better? You’re hoping that to simply give up now will fulfil a promise on a technicality? So that when you finally do meet your wife again you can have the dignity to face her? But what kind of man will she be looking back at when that time comes around again? Could you really live with that, not knowing? Hell no. McKail had decided long ago that he wouldn’t make any compromises in reaching his dreams and it truly disturbed him, how easy he'd forgotten it. This had a facade of nobility, but when examined, it was just another compromise. Sickening really. No fear, or at least that’s what he’d told himself all of those months ago. Quitting now, overwhelmed by the possibility of losing, would betray his credo developed whilst at his very most lucid. A new credo was needed in order to run in conjunction with this, to put himself in check for possible future wavering. there coulkd be. The Infinite Gauntlet will not be the end for me - it will be the beginning. McKail grinned to himself. Yes. That’s perfect. He made his way into the arena, head held high. After all, he had a match to prepare for. END
