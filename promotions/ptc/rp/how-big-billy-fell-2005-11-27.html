How Big Billy Fell

27 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-27 So what is it you wanted to know about Big Billy Blackwell, again? Oh right, about how he fell. That’s simple enough. Big Billy Blackwell was one of the toughest, meanest, most ornery sumbitches I ever met on the circuit. He didn’t care where he had to go or what he had to do, he’d go there and do it. If it meant riding a bull in Abilene, riding a bronco in Cheyenne, or roping a calf in Billings, he’d do it. Hell, I even remember this one time, in San Angelo, one of our clowns was out with the flu. I think it was Spunky or Funky or one o’ them. Anyway, don’t matter none, cause Big Billy stepped in, put on that suit and painted his face, and went out there and hid in the barrels all the same. Now that’s dedication for you. And that’s what made it so hard after Big Billy fell. We all knew that was it for him. He knew it, too, but he refused to accept it for the longest time. In the end, though, he had to accept reality. After you’ve been out of the circuit for that long, it’s too hard to get back in the saddle again, if you’ll excuse the pun. And that was the beginning of the end for Big Billy Blackwell and his rodeo career. And that’s when he turned to the bottle. It got him through some awful times, boy howdy. What? Fall from grace? What the hell are you talkin’ about? I’m talkin’ about the time he fell off Large Larry, the biggest damn bull any of us had ever seen. Large Larry was new on the tour, and Big Billy figured if anybody could break him in, it was him. He was wrong, though. Give him credit, though, he stayed on about three seconds. And then he fell. And that was it.
