Chapter Four: Changing The Rules

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 It had been twenty-four hours since Lance Marshall’s son had been brought back to him and, if you were to ask Lance himself, he’d be unable to recall whether or not he’d actually managed to sleep for any of them. Life had been placed on hold the moment Zach had appeared at the hospital, Lance planting himself by his son’s side for every waking moment. Either together with his wife or, when it all became too much for her and she needed to get some rest at home, alone. Lance couldn’t begrudge her that. With her surgery just a few days away, she needed all the rest she could get. Lance shook his head when recalling everything his family had been through recently. Alanna’s initial diagnosis. Lane Stevens putting her health, maybe even her life, in jeopardy through his actions. His son’s abduction. Lance remembered what he had once been told, that G-d gives no person a burden too great for them to handle. He was having a hard time believing that right now. If he could, he’d tell G-d that just because someone’s back was broad it didn’t mean their capacity was infinite. People could only deal with so much before they broke, whether forever or just for right now. And the last thing Lance wanted to admit is how close he’d come to breaking these past few weeks. There had been one of the detectives present in the room at all times. Watching the hospital staff carefully, making sure that Zach was never alone in the room. Lance hated that they were even there, resented their presence in his son’s life. It had gone especially badly when the female detective had tried asking Zach questions about what had happened. No doubt because they believed Zach would respond better to her. “Mister Marshall? I’m Detective Stevens. I was wondering if I could…” “I know who you are and I know what you want,” Lance spat. “Does this have to be done now? My son has been through hell and he needs his rest.” “Unfortunately, sir,” the detective answered, keeping her voice calm and level, “it’s best if we can try and get as much information as possible as quickly as possible. I’ll try and keep this short though. You’re welcome to sit in if you like.” As if that was actually up for debate, Lance thought. Sarah Stevens gazed at the huddled form lying in the bed in front of her. Truth be told, she wasn’t expecting very much. But it had to be done and the possibility of gathering evidence outweighed any reservations she might have had. “Zach? I’m Detective Stevens.” Zach shrunk back further against the pillows. “Hi,” he answered weakly. “Do you mind if I ask you a few questions about what happened?” A slight shake of the head from Zach. “Okay.” “Do you remember anything specific about that day in school?” Zach thought for a moment. “I was outside on the monkey bars. Mrs. Konover had told me to go inside and I didn’t…” “That’s okay, champ,” Lance interrupted. “…because Greg Pilkington had said no one could cross the monkey bars three times without stopping. He said he’d give me five dollars if I could do it.” “So, after you got down from the monkey bars, what happened next?” Zach thought for a moment. He closed his eyes tightly in concentration, the effort forming small creases on his eyelids. There had been that strange smell, the one that had made him all funny. But apart from that… …there was nothing. “I don’t remember.” “Nothing at all? Not what the man who did this to you looked like, nothing?” “YOUFUCKING BRAT! You think your mommy and daddy are going to come and save you…that’s it, keep crying…Mommy and Daddy are just sitting at home right now, not even thinking about you at all…they’ve just forgotten all about you…” Without his even realizing it, tears started to pour from Zach Marshall’s eyes. “no,” he replied weakly. Zach turned to his father, his face a mask of confusion and fear. “I’m sorry, Daddy,” he wailed, “I’m so sorry!” Lance grabbed his son in his arms, holding him as tightly as the IV stuck into Zach’s arm would allow. He held him close, murmuring gently in his ear. “It’s okay, champ. It’s okay. I’m here. Daddy’s never gonna let anybody hurt you ever again…” “It’s okay,” said Detective Stevens, “we can try this again in a little while.” “Fuck that,” Lance growled. “This is over.” “Mister Marshall, we need…” “I don’t give a FUCK what you need, lady! You are not talking to my son anymore today, do you understand!” Detective Stevens simply nodded and went silently back to the chair she had been sitting in. Lance continued to whisper words of comfort to his son, holding him until he fell back asleep. Not a word passed in that room for the rest of Detective Stevens’s shift. Alanna Marshall collapsed with a sigh into the deck chair, her legs dangling over the edge. For once, the sounds of the city were somehow being kept at bay and she was able to enjoy a brief moment of total, blessed quiet. Lord knows, she thought, she could use this right now. Alanna was used to her life being busy, that much was certain. She often joked that she was not the type of person who did “bored” particularly well. There had just been so much lately…at times it felt like everything was just threatening to overwhelm her, leaving her buried underneath a pile of crap. Just being able to breathe felt like a luxury these days. She turned her head at the sound of heels upon the patio, the sun causing her to squint at the figure in a red blouse and jeans making her way across the patio. Anna Marshall made her way across the patio with grace and elegance, her grey hair the only indicator of her nearly seventy years on Earth. In each hand she held a glass of ice water. She placed one on the side table next to Alanna, taking the other for herself. “I thought you could use this, my dear,” she said, taking the chair next to Alanna. “Lord knows we’ve all been through a lot these past few weeks.” Alanna smiled at her mother-in-law, grateful for her presence. She and William had flown down from New York as soon as possible after hearing the news that Zach had been found. They had, William said, been determined to see that their grandson was well and being spoiled rotten. Alanna was sure that Zach would be happy to see them when he came back from the hospital. “How are you doing, my dear?” asked Anna, taking a sip of her water. “I’ll survive.” “I don’t doubt that,” Anna replied, “but it’s not really answering the question, is it?” Alanna sighed. “Honestly? I think I’ve gotten so used to feeling like shit that I don’t really know what it would feel like to feel good or happy. Mostly, I just put one foot in front of the other and pray really hard that it doesn’t look so obvious that I just want to scream all the time.” “It’s an understandable reaction, dear. I can’t imagine how I would have held up under similar circumstances. I’m amazed that you’re doing as well as you are.” “This is well?” Alanna asked. “I’d hate to see “doing badly”. And as bad as I am, Lance is worse, I mean, he is just looking around for somebody to kill.” “Much like his father in that respect,” Anna replied. “Oh, William likes to pretend that he’s here strictly for Zach’s benefit. But, when he thinks I can’t hear him, he goes off on one of his diatribes. Largely about the many and creative ways he learned to inflict pain upon someone while in the service. And how he’d like to use all of them on the bastard who hurt Zachary.” Despite herself, Alanna chuckled grimly. “Like father, like son.” “Oh, don’t get me wrong, dear. I completely approve of William’s plan. Whoever the horrible person who did this to Zachary is, I hope he dies an agonizing and painful death and burns forever in the fires of Hell.” Alanna did a spit take at this, nearly choking on her water. To hear a woman she’d always regarded as classy and stylish and, frankly, not a little intimidating, say something like this…was a bit of a mind blower, to say the least. Anna’s eyes twinkled, taking note of Alanna’s confusion. “We are Marshall women, dear. And as Marshall women, we know one thing: family comes above all else. We will do anything for our families. Anything.” Alanna nodded. She raised her glass of water, clinking it against her mother-in-law’s. “To family.” “To family.” Zach’s first day back had been hard. He’d spent most of the day in his room, either playing quietly or sleeping. Mostly sleeping, as Alanna had found herself checking on Zach repeatedly throughout the day just to make sure he was still breathing. But every time it had been the same thing: Zach, curled up in a ball on his bed, sleeping the sleep of the dead. If this was as bad as it would ever get, the Marshalls thought, things would be okay. They would be able to deal with this. Zach’s second day back was considerably worse. Zach had woken up early, even before the cartoons started airing. He’d stayed in bed till he knew he’d be able to watch something he liked on TV then padded down in his bare feet to the living room. His father was sitting at the counter, working on an egg white omelet, whole wheat toast and a glass of orange juice. “Hey, sport,” Lance called. “You sleep okay last night?” Zach nodded. “Uh huh.” He walked slowly over to the cupboard, pulling out a cereal bowl. “is it okay if I have cereal for breakfast, dad?” “Sure, sport.” Lance Marshall watched worriedly as his son went through the motions of his daily routine. He was doing all the right things, certainly: pouring out the cereal and milk, making sure he didn’t make a mess, cleaning up after himself. But that spark of life, that youthful energy that always used to be present in Zach…he couldn’t see it anymore. It was like his son was running on autopilot. “When do we have to leave for school, dad?” “Well, champ, you just got out of the hospital and we thought it’d be good if you just rested for a little bit. So you get to stay home today, okay?” “Okay.” Zach padded over slowly to the sofa, carefully taking a seat before pulling his bowl of cereal onto his lap. The TV firmly settled on Phineas and Ferb, Zach began working methodically away at his cereal. Lance’s worry continued to grow as he did so. The antics of Phineas and Ferb, which would normally have Zach laughing, were now met with a stony silence. “Are you enjoying the show, sport?” “It’s good, daddy. But I’ve seen it before.” Okay, maybe not a cause for worry. Lance continued eating, casting a wary eye towards Zach from time to time. When Lance finished, he moved to the sink to clean up his dishes and put them away. In the middle of rinsing his plate, he could hear the sound of bare feet padding along the floor followed quickly by the sound of a bowl crashing onto the floor. Lance dropped the plate into the sink, water still running, and turned towards Zach. “Zach, are you…” Lance stopped dead at the sight of his son’s face. Zach’s gaze was fixed straight ahead, his focus never wavering. His eyes were wide in fear. “You’ve been a bad boy, Zachary…and you know what I do to bad boys…” No, thought Zachary. Please, no. He didn’t know why this strange man was hurting him…why he seemed to like it so much. He didn’t care. He just wanted it to stop. “Please…please don’t hit me…please don’t hit me…” “Zach?” “PLEASE DON’T HIT ME!” Zach wailed. Lance jumped, numb with shock at the anguish in his son’s voice. From the distance, the sounds of feet running down the corridor could be heard. Alanna was first into the room, followed closely by his parents. “What happened?” Alanna asked. “The bowl must have slipped from his hands or something. I didn’t do anything, I swear…” “His eyes, son,” answered William Marshall. “Look at Zach’s eyes.” And that’s when it hit him. Zach wasn’t looking at any of them. His gaze was fixed into the distance, onto something else that only he could see. Wherever Zach was right now, it wasn’t here. “Where the hell are those doctors,” Lance snapped. The Marshalls had been informed by Alanna’s oncologist that they were to report to surgery at precisely 7 a.m., exactly two hours before Alanna’s scheduled surgery time of 9 a.m. What they had not been told was that they would be spending most of these two hours either filling out paperwork or waiting. Mostly waiting. At 9, they had been ushered into what looked like a central waiting area for patients about to go into surgery. Alanna had quickly changed out of her clothes and into regulation hospital wear; Lance had been both captivated and repulsed by the antics of someone who looked as if they were going through the nasty stages of detox. Alanna had then been placed on a gurney and hooked up to an IV which was feeding a steady drip of saline solution into her veins. That had been three hours ago. “Oh, thank G-d,” Lance sighed as a male nurse came up to them. “My wife was supposed to be going through surgery at nine. It’s now twelve and no one’s told us anything.” “My apologies, sir,” rasped the nurse, his voice sounding like it had been on the losing end of a battle with nicotine. “The Doctor was seriously delayed this morning and it’s thrown everything out of whack. But I’ve been told that he’s almost ready for you. In fact, that’s why I’ve come here: to give you this.” The nurse took the syringe he’d been holding and prepared to inject the contents into Alanna Marshall’s IV line. “What is that?” asked Alanna. “Nothing serious,” the nurse rasped. “Just a little something to help you relax, take the edge off of the anesthesia. It’ll have you feeling good in no time.” He finished the injection, carefully retracting the syringe. “I’ve got to go now, I’ve got other patients I need to attend to. Hope everything goes well.” “Thank you.” As the nurse retreated, Doctor Finnigan walked over to the Marshalls. “I’m sorry, Alanna, Lance. It’s been a madhouse today. Are you ready, Alanna?” “As I’ll ever be,” she answered. “Good,” replied Doctor Finnegan. To the two orderlies next to him, he added “Take Mrs. Marshall to OR 3. I’ll meet you there in a few minutes.” “I’m afraid this is where you’ll have to say your goodbyes,” Doctor Finnegan continued, turning to Lance. “The OR is off limits to non-medical personnel. Alanna will be spending her first night in the ICU, so she’ll be unable to receive visitors. I will let you know when she’s moved to her room, though. Of course, if there are any significant changes in her condition, you will be the first to know.” “Thank you, Doctor,” Lance replied. Turning to his wife, he gave Alanna a kiss and said “Love you, baby.” “Love you, too,” she answered woozily, the drugs already starting to take effect. Lance stood watching as the orderlies wheeled his wife out of the waiting area, following them down the hallway as they wheeled her towards the operating rooms. At the turn of the hallway, he stopped and bowed his head. Dear Father, please watch out over her. Please protect her. Please keep her safe. If you were to ask the doctors who had attended to Alanna Marshall’s surgery, they would tell you that she’d had a remarkably smooth operation. Despite a minor fluctuation in her life signs at one point (due to what would later be discovered as an overdose of sedative), the surgery had gone incredibly well. The tumor had been removed and all signs pointed to the surgery being a complete success. Alanna Marshall was unaware of any of this as she slowly regained consciousness in the ICU. What she was aware of was pain. She had certainly been hurt before in the course of her and her husband’s careers but this was a whole new level of pain. She found herself absurdly glad for the machine hooked to her left arm, ready to deliver a life saving dose of morphine at a moment’s notice. The second thing she was aware of was someone next to her bed. “Hello, Mrs. Marshall,” a voice rasped. The nurse from earlier, the one who’d given her the happy juice. What the hell was he doing here, she wondered? She stopped wondering as she felt the steel of the scalpel he was holding against her neck. “My, but it’s such a very pleasant evening, don’t you think?” The Voice continued. “Now, I think it’s time that you and I had a little chat..."
