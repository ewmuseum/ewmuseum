The Royal Family Of Sing

17 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-17 A sore, bruised, and disheveled Brenda Clark trudged into her office two hours later than normal. Her appearance garnered several unwanted stares. On any given day this was not unusual, as she was typically primped in rather sexy (but tasteful) attire that subtly accentuated her curvaceous figure. Today however, the scenario was quite the opposite. People in the hallway were stopping to rubberneck rather than ogle, and for good reason: Her clothes were rumpled; the areas of her face that didn’t have bruises were ashen; and upon close inspection, one would have been able to ascertain a slight limp. All in all, she looked like hell. The words "Jesus, Brenda, what happened to you?" bombarded the child services agent from all directions. She didn’t respond to anyone with much else other than a few garbled grumbles of what may or may not have been some particularly nasty four-letter words. Clearly, she had been assaulted. Clearly, she was in no mood to discuss the matter. Clearly, she was pissed. Then again, just about anyone who’d gotten their ass kicked the night before by a midget wielding a giant inflatable penis would be. -~-~- The hotel room was trashed. Clothes, blankets and sheets had been strewn about the floor and furniture. Empty bottles of tequila, Corona, and Captain Morgan lay scattered everywhere… as did several empty juice boxes, along with toys and diapers (thankfully unused). A pair of lovely young models lay in one of the two twin-sized beds, sleeping peacefully on either side of their happy customer. There was a man in the other bed, snoozing the morning away while gently cuddling a tiny bundle, keeping it close and safe. Another man in spandex lay between everyone, passed out on the floor. This was the aftermath of the previous night’s party—a party involving a grown man, his eighteen-month-old daughter, a mentally challenged luchador, and a cockfighting duck. "Ungh…" The man in the bed stirred, and sat up. He slowly rubbed his head, which by some miracle wasn’t hurting too badly. "Holy fark," he yawned, stretching, "What a night." Saij looked down at his daughter, Leanne, who was curled up next to her father. She seemed to be asleep. Saij smiled, and gave her a soft kiss on the forehead. "Daddy?" Guess she wasn’t asleep after all. Leanne opened her eyes, and looked up at Saij, who let out a low groan. He’d hoped to have at least a few minutes to himself before having to resume father duty. Maybe, he thought, she’ll fall back asleep if I give her the last few drops of Captain from that bottle on the nightstand… but he quickly pushed that idea out of his head. "Daddy, I wan’ stowy." Saij rubbed his hand through his mussed-up hair. "You want a story now?" he asked. "Sheesh, it’s only…" he looked at the clock, "…eight AM. I only tell you stories at bedtime, sweetheart; you know that." "But I wan’ stowy Daddy." "Hm… Well, what do we say first when we’re asking for something, Leanne?" "I wan’ stowy… now!" "Uh, the other one." "I wan’ stowy… pweese?" "There ya go!" Saij smiled, lifting his daughter up onto his lap. "Okay, since you asked so nicely like a good girl, I’ll tell you a story. How about…" -~-~- THE ROYAL FAMILY OF SING Once upon a time, there was a country in the far off East Lands called Sing. In this country resided a King and a Queen, and they were very happy together. The King was always off fighting wars, while the Queen stayed at the castle and tended to matters at home. Whenever the King would return from his conquests he would bring the Queen her favorite flower, for they were so very in love. It happened one joyous day that the Queen discovered she was with child, and was going to give birth to a beautiful Princess… -~-~- The two models in the next bed over began to giggle and squirm under their covers. A third, much smaller figure was excitedly rolling around with them. Saij looked over and shook his head. "Guess Plucky’s awake too," he grinned. "He’s sure getting his money’s worth. Anyway, where was I?" -~-~- "Hey Brenda, you alright?" A tall, blonde uniformed police officer stood in the doorway of Brenda Clark’s office, holding a bouquet of roses in one hand and respectfully removing his hat with the other. Brenda didn’t even so much as glance at him. She was fervently flipping through several files on her desk, frequently going back and forth between them and her computer screen, searching, a woman possessed. "Damnit, where the hell is it?" she muttered. The officer, about a year or two younger than her, stepped forward and spoke again. His voice was high and timid by nature. "Brenda? Hey, Brenda? I said, ‘are you alright?’" She ignored him and continued searching. He took another step forward. "Brenda, I—" "I heard you the first time," she growled. "Just like I heard the first time everybody else in the building asked me the same fucking question. Leave me alone, Graham. I’m busy." With a wistful sigh, the officer—John Graham was his full name—lay the flowers on Brenda’s desk. "I heard you were roughed up, so I stopped at the florist and got these for you," he continued anyway. "I thought they might cheer you up. Had to stand in line for half an hour to get them, too—some jerk was keeping the owner on the phone, complaining about lilies or something. Captain gave me an earful, too, for getting back from my lunch break so late. But it was worth it for you, Brenda…" "Found it… 408 Maplecrest." she said, ignoring him. She tore off a piece of the bouquet’s paper wrapping, and scribbled down the address she had been looking for. Graham was crestfallen. Not so much as a smile or a "thank you." No acknowledgement whatsoever! A thoughtful present of fresh, sweet-smelling roses, and all she seemed to think it was good for was taking down notes… His disappointment gnawed at his gut; yet he really shouldn’t have been surprised by her cold reaction. Ever since Graham had started working with Brenda, he’d been infatuated with her—and she was greatly annoyed by that fact. But Graham was a hopeless romantic, as well as a clueless one. Every time he tried to do something nice for Brenda, she either ignored him or blew him off. He sometimes chalked it up to her being too absorbed with her job to notice much of anything else; she did seem to be a workaholic. Other times he justified her by thinking that she just wasn’t ready for another relationship—she was a divorcee, after all. Whatever excuse seemed handy at the time, that’s what he would tell himself. So it was by choice that he endured her indifference, clinging to the feeble notion that one day she would indeed notice him, and they would live together, happily ever after. He wasn’t very bright. "S-so uh," he stammered, trying to conceal his hurt feelings, "what’s that address? You trying to find someone?" "Of course I’m trying to find someone," she snapped. "I’ve been trying to find the same damn someone for the last two weeks. Or have you been living under a rock this whole time?" "Oh… uh, no, of course not," Graham quietly managed. He may have been willing to endure Brenda’s indifference, but her sharp tongue made him feel quite small regardless. "Is it the Jeffries case still?" "Yeah, still," said Brenda, quickly standing. "I finally tracked him down and went to his apartment last night, but he got away from me before I could grab the girl." She ducked into an adjacent washroom to freshen up. "Sicked his little midget friend on me while he escaped," she added, the door half-open. "That’s where you went?" Graham was shocked. "You went to extract another child without me? Brenda, you know you’re supposed to take a police officer along when you do that! How many times to I have to tell you?" "You don’t have to tell me at all," Brenda’s voice dripped with sarcasm. "Nobody’s making you. In fact, I’d prefer it if you’d just shut up altogether." "That’s… not exactly what I meant…" Graham trailed off. "Whatever," Brenda replied over the sound of running water. "Anyway, Scott Jeffries and his daughter are long gone… to where, I have no idea. If he’s smart he won’t be coming back anytime soon. If you ask me though, he doesn’t seem very smart at all. I’d say he has the common sense of a sponge, but that would be an insult to sponges everywhere. He’ll come home, I’m sure of it… the question is, when?" She turned the water off. "And where?" "C’mon Brenda," said Graham gently. "If he’s gone, then he’s far, far out of our jurisdiction. Why don’t you just work on some of your other cases? You know, forget about him for a while and…" "Forget about him!?" Brenda yelled. "Graham, if you knew half the things about this guy that I’ve been told already, and if you saw the cesspool of an apartment he had his daughter living in, you wouldn’t fucking forget about this guy!" "I… I see." Wrapped up in her work again, Graham thought to himself. He picked the rose bouquet up from her desk. As he did so, he paused to notice a picture frame that was prominently displayed on the left side. It was the only portrait Brenda Clark kept on her desk: a three-year-old girl, with red hair and green eyes just like hers. Graham picked it up and looked at it closely. The thought of having his own children someday—maybe with Brenda—brought a smile to his face. "Put that down!" Brenda’s voice made Graham jump. "Who gave you permission to touch my things!?" "Uh, s-sorry, I…" Graham was at a loss. "I just… that’s a very pretty little girl. Is she your daugh—?" "None of your business," she hissed, grabbing the frame out of Graham’s hand and slamming it back in its place. Apparently that was a touchy subject. At least she was looking a whole lot better than when she’d arrived in the morning—her hair was tidier, fresh make-up hid most of the bruises, and her temper with Graham brought the color roaring back to her face. Brenda grabbed her jacket and briefcase, making ready to leave. "Where are you going?" asked Graham. "To the only people who might be able to tell me when Mr. Jeffries might come back to town," said Brenda. She stopped in her tracks and turned to Graham. "And… if I have to have an officer with me… then I guess you can come, too. Just keep your mouth shut, got it?" Graham nodded. In spite of Brenda’s resentment, he was quite happy at the prospect of spending more time with her. Her eyes trailed down to the roses in his hand. "I hate flowers. You know that," she said. "Oh." Brenda spun around and briskly walked out the door. Graham stood where he was for a moment, deflated. His brief spark of happiness had been snuffed out. With a sigh, he dropped the roses in the wastebasket, and went to catch up with Brenda. -~-~- When the Queen discovered she was going to give birth to a Princess, she was overjoyed. Yet at the same time, her heart was troubled. The King was a warrior and a hero, and he loved to fight. How would he be able to help her take care of the Princess if something horrible happened to him? So the Queen asked the King to stay home and give up traveling abroad to fight his wars. The King was unhappy to hear this request, for he loved the heat of battle just about as much as he loved the Queen. He and the Queen had several spats over the matter, and all the while the King continued to ride off and conquer his foes in far-off lands. This caused the Queen great distress, and one day she could bear it no longer. She asked a court Magician to cast a terrible spell that would cause the unborn Princess to die. But just before the spell was to be cast, however, the King returned home. He went to the Queen and was horrified to see what she was about to do. Yet, in that shocking moment, he understood. Overcome with love for the Queen and for the Princess, he swore then and there that he would never ride off to fight in another war again so that he might help the Queen care for the child. The Queen rejoiced, and ordered the Magician to cease his spellcasting. The King was true to his word, and laid down his sword for good. Instead of riding off to battle, he would go for walks in the castle garden every day and bring the Queen her favorite flower, as he’d always done. Months and months passed, until the day of the Princess’s birth finally arrived. The whole country of Sing was overjoyed at the news of the little girl’s arrival. The people waited outside of the castle, eager to celebrate the long-awaited moment. Inside the castle however, the mood was dark, and howls of anguish filled the air… -~-~- "OW! FARK!" Saij yelped. Something had nipped at his toe, and it hurt. It was Plucky the Cockfighting Duck, apparently finished being satisfied by his hired lady companions who were bidding him farewell as they blew him kisses from the doorway. "Quack!" he said… for you see, he was a duck. "Geez Plucky," Saij said irritatedly, "if you wanna get my attention, you could try, maybe, not trying to bite my farkin’ toes off. Anyway, whaddaya want? I’m trying to tell Leanne a story here." "Quack!" "Breakfast? Well fine, but if you’re that hungry can’t you just go get it yourself?" "Quack." "You’re a lazy bastard and you want room service instead? Dude, that’s expensive." "Quack." "Yeah, it is my hotel room, and I’m not paying for room service that you want!" "Quack, quack!" "I am not cheap!" "Quack!" "Neither is my mother! You take that back!" "Quack, quack!" "Impossible! You couldn’t have slept with my mother last night at all, because you were here this whole time! I know that because you kept me up half the damn night with all the nose you were making with those floozies! Besides that, my mother is a very classy woman, and I’m sure she’d charge a much higher rate than what you’re implying!" "Quack." "Oh. You were joking… uh… well, I knew that!" "Quack." "Shut up! But getting back to the matter at hand, I’m not paying to have breakfast brought up here! There’s a perfectly fine continental breakfast in the lobby, you can go down and get some yourself if you’re hungry." "Quack, quack quack." "What? I do not owe you for kicking your ass!" "Quack!" "No, there’s nothing in the tournament rules about paying restitution to your opponents for eliminating them! That’s the dumbest thing I’ve ever heard… and I wrestled in a federation with Jimmy Bong!" "Quack quack quack?" "No, last night’s party was not restitution. I just thought you’d like to hang out was all. We put on a hell of a match and it seemed like a celebration was in order." "Quack!" "Come again?" "Quack!" "No, I didn’t lose myself in a drunken stupor and use your silk handkerchief as a diaper for Leanne…" Saij looked down at his daughter, and to his surprise saw that her bottom was indeed wrapped in a white handkerchief of silk, with the letters "P.t.C.D." embroidered in one corner. "Ah fark, I did," Saij sighed. "Fine then, I’ll get room service for you and we’ll call it even. If you wanted it so badly though, why didn’t you just call down yourself?" "Quack." "Oh, right. I guess that was a pretty stupid question. Anyway, I’ll call down to room service real quick, and then I gotta finish telling Leanne this story…" "Quack quack?" "Huh? You wanna be in my corner if I end up facing Jason Snow? That’s an odd request…" -~-~- A police car pulled into the driveway of 408 Maplecrest and parked; out stepped Brenda and Graham. Brenda nodded in approval after taking a moment to look around. The yard was freshly cut and raked. The gutters had recently been cleaned out. The sides seemed to have been given a fresh coat of paint over the summer. The whole property was picture perfect. Whoever lived here certainly knew how to keep a home—or at the very least, keep up appearances. "So," Graham broke the silence. "Whose house is this? You never told me." "You’ll see," said Brenda, strolling up the front walk. Graham followed her. She came to a halt at the front door, on a concrete stoop that bore a charming "Welcome" mat with flower and rainbow designs. She reached out, rang the doorbell, and waited. A few moments later, the door swung open. A tall, friendly-looking gentleman stood before them; his graying hair and subtle wrinkles indicated that he was nearing his sixties, but his blue eyes seemed full of youthful vigor. What Brenda noticed immediately, however, was a very familiar goofy grin… "Huzzah!" he said cheerfully. "Hello there! How the fark are you today? Can I help you?" Brenda rolled her eyes. "Great," she mumbled to herself. "I guess the idiot gene is on the Y-chromosome." "Hey Martha, come to the door would ya?" the kindly man called back into the house. "We have guests! And it sounds like one of them is a molecular biologist!" "Oh nonsense, Stanley," came a stern, motherly voice. "Why would a molecular biologist be calling at this time of day?" "Fark if I know," said the man, whom Brenda and Graham now knew to be named Stanley. "Makes sense though, what with switching off of Daylight Saving Time, and the days getting shorter and stuff. If I were a molecular biologist, I sure wouldn’t wanna be running around after dark. Might get jumped by vampires, or werewolves or something." "Don’t be ridiculous, sweetheart," said Martha, a short, plump and otherwise jovial woman who now joined her husband at the door. "Vampires and werewolves don’t exist." "I didn’t think molecular biologists existed either, but here we are!" "I am not a molecular biologist," said Brenda, having had quite enough. "Oh," said Stanley, his grin shrinking away. "Well, never mind then. I knew it was too good to be true." Brenda turned and shot Graham an odd glance. Graham just shrugged. "Anyway, my name is Brenda Clark. I’m with the Department of Human Services; child welfare, specifically. Are you Stanley and Martha Jeffries?" They nodded. "Ah good. I’m here to ask you a few questions about your son, Scott." Stanley and Martha suddenly looked quite sullen. They exchanged sad looks with one another, and otherwise said nothing. "May we come in?" Brenda asked, trying not to sound impatient. "Uh, sure," Stanley said finally. "It’s just that… well…" "What?" Stanley rubbed the back of his neck. "Well, Scott’s twenty-four years old. He’s been living on his own for quite some time. I dunno why child services would wanna take him into protective custody now." Brenda nearly fell backward. "Uh… what?" "Don’t play coy with us!" Martha yelled, holding back tears. "You’re from child services, and you’ve come to take away our baby! Well he isn’t here, and we’re not giving him up! So there!" Brenda slapped her hand on her forehead. Apparently the apple didn’t fall very far from the tree. "No, I think you misunderstand," she said through her teeth. "I’m only here to find out some information about him, not… ugh… take him into protective custody. He’s too old for that, you see." She could feel her brain cells dying off one-by-one from being forced to rationalize the obvious. "Oh," said Stanley, looking at his wife and nodding slowly. "Well, that’s different then. What do you want to know?" Good, some semblance of progress, Brenda thought. She shrugged, "Just trying to find out where he is, that’s all." "Well, I don’t know how much help we can be then," Martha sighed. "We haven’t heard from our Scotty in over a year." Brenda blinked. "A year?" Just her luck. Her only lead, and this was looking like a dead end for sure. "Yeah," said Stanley, taking off his eyeglasses and rubbing them with his shirt. "Ever since the night our granddaughter was born. He hasn’t been the same since then, far as we can tell." "Really," said Brenda thoughtfully. "Well, I’m trying to help both your son and your granddaughter, and anything you can tell me will certainly be of assistance. May I please come in?" "Of course," said Martha. "Have a seat in the living room. I’ll bring some refreshments for us." "Thank you," said Brenda. She turned back to Graham for a moment with a hint of a smile. Graham knew it wasn’t really for him, but it made his heart leap nonetheless. He followed her inside. "So, why exactly hasn’t your son ‘been the same’ since your granddaughter was born?" Brenda asked as she stepped over the threshold. "Well, for starters," Stanley began, "his girlfriend… or wife, I guess… she died shortly after giving birth." The front door shut behind them. -~-~- Inside the castle however, the mood was dark, and howls of anguish filled the air. The Queen’s labor was a very troublesome one, and she was only able to give birth to the Princess at the expense of her own life. The King wept and wept as his beloved wife lay sleeping, knowing that she would never awaken again. The Queen had been so worried about the King staying home to help raise the Princess. It never occurred to her to think what might happen if she weren’t around to help. Although he was surrounded by his subjects and his friends, the King felt very alone. He missed the Queen so much that he named the Princess after her, and he swore on his life that he would always protect her. As long as he breathed, he would never leave her side. And he never did. The End. -~-~- "Not th’ end," said Leanne. Saij gave her daughter a quizzical look. "Not the end?" he asked. "Not th’ end," Leanne repeated, frowning. Saij chuckled. "Well, maybe it isn’t," he said after a few thoughtful moments. "After all, the King loved to fight evil in distant lands. Maybe he took the Princess along with him, and they had some exciting adventures. But that’s a story for another time, my dear. Right now, we have to get cleaned up and fly back home!" Leanne giggled and clapped. "Hooway!" she cried. "Hooome!" "Yeah," Saij said quietly. "Home. But only for a little bit… if we stay too long, that nasty old woman from child services will find us, and I don’t want anyone taking you away from me. Not ever, understand?" "Uh-huh. Icky woman!" Leanne wrinkled her nose. "That’s right," Saij smiled. "That’s right. Good girl. Now help your daddy pick up a few things, okay?" Saij stood up and stretched. He grabbed a thin gold chain off of the nightstand, and slipped it around his neck. "Daddy," said Leanne, "I wan’ meet th’ Queen. Can I meet th’ Queen?" Saij paused. He slowly reached up and grasped part of his chain between his thumb and forefinger. It was a pair of gold wedding bands, linked together. He looked up for a moment, and sighed. He knew he shouldn’t be going home. He knew the sensible thing would be to stay as far away from there as possible. But there was a promise he had to keep. "Yeah," he said at last. "Yeah, you can meet the Queen. We’re going home to see her."
