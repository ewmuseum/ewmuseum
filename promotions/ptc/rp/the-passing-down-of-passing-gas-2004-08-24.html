Date: 2004-08-24 The debate about nature and nurture is fundamental to studies of psychology. Why do individuals act the way they do? Is it because there’s something inherent in their physiological, biological and psychological make-up that pre-programs them to act in the inane ways they do? Or, is it that the environment in which they grow up, experiences had at an early age, which shape and mould the actions of people? Most individuals admit that it is certainly a mix of both. What is oft missed, however, is the penultimate role of history in structuring and determining the end goal. For, if action is predicated by some biological predisposition, then knowing the actions of predecessors is a good place to start. However, if its all about nurture, then actions of ancestors line up a long string of social contexts which in turn, affect the outcome of the individual. Why all this psycho-babble?
