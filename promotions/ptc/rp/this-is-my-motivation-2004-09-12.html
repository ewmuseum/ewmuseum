This Is My Motivation

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-12 "So, why are you doing this?" It was a question with a rather obvious answer. Yet no matter how obvious the answer was, it was the question every participant in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament had been asked at least once. Thankfully, I hadn't been asked this question until now. And it wasn’t from some snoopy reporter, or a fan, or my opponent. The words had come from the man who had brought me to this earth. And while the other half of the couple that brought me to this earth may have claimed it was a mistake just as John Adams father had said to him in the past as well, I learned to ignore that half. If it was such a mistake, then why was I the one holding this family together after my brother had passed away two years back? But that was back then, when I had also stepped out from behind the shadows and proven myself to be worthy of the credit I received in the third Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. Now, I’d have to do the same thing once more, only two years later. But the question didn’t phase me that much. Even with every ounce of concern wrapped up in it, I had been desensitized to this sort of care. Ever since I started riding the emotional roller coaster that my career had become, I stopped caring about others opinions of what I was doing, because in the end it seemed that in the end every person that I trusted, turned around and showed that it should have been mistrust that guided me instead. “Because I need this opportunity, dad.” I replied, as all the comments about me being overrated, a fallen star, and simply eye candy that doesn’t belong in the business crossed my mind. It pissed me off greatly to hear that coming from someone’s mouth. It was almost as bothersome as the sight that laid before me. A perfectly healthy fifty year old man, reduced to nothing all because of the greed of one woman. Of course my answer still didn’t convince my father that I was doing the right thing by entering this tournament. Sure, he had others taking care of him, but he understood just as well as others what a career in wrestling meant. While he understood that wrestling was something I enjoyed, he also understood the risks that came with it. His concerns didn’t lay with me being petite female. He knew that I could compete with even with someone as tall as the 7’3” “Prophet” that John Adams proclaimed himself to be, even though females weren‘t built to compete against males. The concern laid with my condition after the match, considering the last time I went up against a man the size of Adams, I barely clung onto my ability to ever walk again. “But why do you need this so much?” His words weren’t harassing in the least bit, unlike my mothers when my career was brought up in conversation. Rather, his words were so gentle they had me questioning whether I was doing the right thing, the same point the was trying to get across. To tell the truth, I wasn’t completely sure why I felt I needed this so much. I guess it’s just the growing appetite for stardom that even I, one of the most unselfish type of people you could meet in this business, had inside of me. It’s what everyone wanted, or at least it seemed that way from the many people I had encountered in this business. Everyone, no matter how humble or kind they claimed to be, was willing to stab someone in the back for that one taste of spotlight and for the simple phrase “number one” to be placed in front of their name. “I… just…do..” The uncertainty in my response was clearly present. There really wasn’t anything that made the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament something that I needed to be in. In the end it would probably backfire like all my other plans anyways. But I wasn’t going to back out now. I had made a promise to prove myself once more, and now was the perfect chance to do just that. “But you’ve got plenty. Two children, your own house, and a career you enjoy. What else could this tournament give to you?” So sincere yet so selfish. I loved my father but he was more worried about what he wanted, than what I wanted. Then again who could blame him? Nothing else went his way. His surroundings proved that. If he had his way he wouldn’t be treated like a child. Then again, if anyone in this place had their way, they’d be back at home as well, reliving their youth instead of staring at me with the jealously that hidden well behind the friendly smile and a wave they’d give every time I visited. “Well, what else could it give you?” My mind enquired as it quickly brought me through a pictorial recollection of everything that had happened to me since I started my career in professional wrestling two and a half years back. “Money?” It never bought me happiness before, or else I would have happily accepted a marriage gone wrong. “Fame?” That just meant I’d never get time to myself. “A better life?” It might put a little more power and respect into the name Windsor, but it wouldn’t bring me back to when everything was perfect a little more than a year ago. “Bragging rights?” Those would only last until the next time I got pinned to the mat for the three count. “Respect?” Yes. That’s exactly what it was that I wanted. But truthfully, the odds played against it. I’d either win or lose. If I lost I’d go home with my head hung low, proving the people who spoke out against me right. Proving that I truly was overrated. But at least I’d have the burden of keeping my name alive lifted off my shoulder, by finally killing it. If I continued to win though, I could do exactly what I had intended to do when I entered the business. I could turn heads. I could make enough to support a family without having to worry the same way I had to right now with the little that RPW paid. I could finally have the respect of those who doubted my ability. I could finally find a way to forget about the past and make a better future. But wrestling wouldn’t raise two children. Wrestling wouldn’t teach they life’s important lessons. The only thing wrestling would do is give me what I needed to keep a roof over their head without working forty hours a week in a cubical or behind a register. Even if it cost me their trust. They knew better than to expect me home on the weekends now, even if I promised it. They knew better than to think they’d ever have an actual family. They only family they had was each other, and my father knew that. It’s why his eyes remained glued to me the entire time I sat there in silent notion. “I just have this feeling, Dad. This is my chance to get out there and make things better for all of us.” I replied, knowing just how easily it could all come crashing down on me. As much as I’d like to think this was something that could be life changing, I also realized it could be something that could hurt in the end. All it took was one loss and it would all slip away. “But sweetheart…” “Mr. Windsor I‘d hate to interrupt, but visiting hours are now over.” Chimed in one of the nurses before my father could finish. In a way I was thankful for her intrusion. This would erase those second thoughts I was beginning to have now that my father had no more time to try and talk me out of it. But still, I understood where he was coming from. I myself wouldn’t want to see either of my kids take the chances I did each week, even if there wasn’t an actual blood connection to them. “Just be careful. Please don‘t do anything you‘re going to regret.” My father pleaded before exchanging a parting embrace and kiss on the cheek. Even though I knew it was out of love, it felt more like a kiss of death. Regret It was such a strong word that had haunted me most of my career. It started with trust that proved to be useless when the man I loved with all my heart walked out on me because things would never be the way they should have been, and it seemed to only grow from there. The only way to get somewhere now was to mistrust everyone I met, no matter how close I grew to them. It was the only way I could survive. Not even my fathers words were immune from this mistrust. He might have had good intentions but he also needed to learn to let go. It was time for me to grow up. It was time to prove myself once more. Only this time, I wouldn’t let my name fall back into the shadows of others. This time I was going to stick around in the limelight and prove to the world again that I could survive everything it threw at me.
