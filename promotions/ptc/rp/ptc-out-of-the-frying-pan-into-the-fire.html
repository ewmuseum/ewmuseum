Out of the Frying Pan, Into the Fire

27 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
One man sat alone, crouched down with his back against the wall, His head buried in his hands and his eyes closed, reading the thoughts that raced through his head. His body was bruised and nearly broken, but it wasn’t half as broken as his spirit. Next to him, outstretched on the floor, was the Wrestling Midwest Shoot Championship, the name Ickwa ‘The Lion’ Hamunya carved into the gold, which immediately gave identity to the man. Ickwa looked up, his face covered in cuts, the result of a painful exposure to barbed wire. He could still feel the metal and the spikes rubbing across his face, tearing into his skin, cuts that would inevitably leave scars, reminders of his vicious battle with Corvus, challenger to his belt. It was Ickwa’s first title defence, and second match in the WMW, which he had successfully won at Burnout. He should be happy, he should be celebrating, he had been in the federation three weeks and he was already building quite a reputation, but he wasn’t. His eyes opened, moist with tears which he held back, refusing to let them spill out and roll down his cheeks. Corvus had been playing mind games with him, following him to his village in The Amazon, or so it was hinted. He was left a very disturbing note outside his very door, one which threatened not only him, but the people that surrounded him, his own family. At first Ickwa brushed it aside as nothing but idle threats, but he had been haunted ever since, plagued by his worst fears, that his family would come to harm and he wouldn’t be able to protect them. He was interrupted by the sound of someone clearing their throat, coming from along the corridor beside him. He rubbed his eyes and looked up to find his friend Johnny looking down at him, a nervous expression on his face. “Are you ok?” He asked Ickwa. Ickwa nodded, picked up his belt, threw it over his shoulder, and stood in front of Johnny. “Damn, you look a mess,” said Johnny, horrified. Ickwa looked away, hiding his face. “Anyway, looks like things are moving up for you.” “Why’s that?” Ickwa asked. “Brad Johnson has entered you into a massive battle royal run by Primetime Central. It’s a contender’s battle for the PTC Extreme title!” Johnny’s eyes never bothered to hide his excitement, but Ickwa’s mirrored the exact opposite. “Why me?” “Johnson wants his very own WMW Shoot champion representing WMW over there, as well as Noah Hanson and Stevie Vertigo.” “I’ve just come from a hellacious battle with Corvus, one that has scarred my body and my mind, and he’s throwing me into this?” Ickwa wasn’t a man to show anger, but he was struggling to hold back his rage. After Burnout, Ickwa wanted a break, a break to mend his body, a break to figure things out. Now he had to concentrate on a huge event that could further boost his name. “What’s wrong with you man? I thought you’d jump at the opportunity, considering how you want honour and all that.” “Everything I do is done for my people. The honour I receive will go towards my people; it will bring honour to their name. How can I find honour for them knowing that I’ve left them behind with the potential risk of Corvus going back to The Amazon?” “What does he have to gain from it? He messed with your head in order to win his match, but he failed to do so. He is not scheduled in another match against you, and probably won’t be for some time now. There’s no gain for him from messing you up even more.” Ickwa shook his head. He wasn’t as sure of those words as Johnny was, but he refused to argue with him. He was tired and he was confused, maybe he should think about all this after a night’s sleep. “Anyway, you have a lot to prove by competing in this match,” Johnny commented. “The critics believe you’ll be one of the first to be eliminated. “Well I shall prove them wrong,” Ickwa said, half-smiling. “After all, no one expected me to pick up a title on my debut match in the WMW full roster battle royal now, did they?” They began walking down the corridor, Ickwa wincing in pain, using the wall to aid him. “you know,” began Johnny. “I think Noah Hanson is slightly confused.” “What makes you say that?” “When speaking in reference to you, he said: This is not Africa and I’m not a Gazelle you’re hunting.” “What? I’m not from Africa! And I’ve never hunted a Gazelle in my life!” “I think he believes Brazil is in Africa, and he takes you as some kind of hunter.” “I’m a warrior, one which fights battles for victory and respect. Why would I slaughter a helpless animal?” “Maybe he’s worried you’ll do the same to him in this battle royal? He might expect you to slaughter him for the helpless animal he is!” Ickwa chuckled, enjoying the little play on words Johnny had just done. He held his ribs, the pain gushing through him with each laugh, an drumbeat of aches against his body. “What about the rest of them?” “Your competitors? No one has mentioned your name. Perhaps they don’t see you as a threat.” “That’s fine; we’ll just wait and see what happens.” But was that really fine? Ickwa had stormed into the WMW, made a big impact at the awards ceremony and had won his first title defence all in three weeks. Didn’t that deserve some recognition? What did he have to do to be acknowledged as a threat to other competitors? This, thought Ickwa, was what I had to do. Win this battle royal and build a name up for himself not only in the WMW, but all over the world of wrestling. It was something to look forward to.
