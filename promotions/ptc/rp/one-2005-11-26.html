Date: 2005-11-26 Date: Sunday, September 25th Time: 11 am The oil painting was riveted to the smudged brick wall, slanted in its angle, compromising the truth that it had once cost nearly a thousand pounds. It hung there in mid-air, years on end, untouched, unwatched. Until one day, it would be remembered as a sign of despair, as a memory glanced upon in the form of tragic imagery. He stood flat on the broken tiles, staring unnervingly at the portrait of a woman whom he once believed he had forgotten, healed from the jagged edges of soulful pain by the soothing years of time. But sometimes, not even time could restore oneself from the self-destructing feeling of damnation. His creased fingers ran lightly against the smooth surface, attracting dust against it like the pull of an iron core magnet. The crystal blue in his eyes glazed, enveloping his troubled mind in the virtual world of once beautiful memories. He managed a smile, more of forlorn than that of delight. "Found what you came here to see?" Mrs. Hudson spoke from across the large living room; exasperation loomed in apparent fashion from her tone. She was the house agent for the now-vacant house, with the price plummeting in recent months, as the victim of a murder tangle involving the owner. It was a Sunday, and she was woken up from her deep sleep in the early dawn by a call from a stranger, who wished to view the house. By now, she was already sure that he didn’t come there just to offer a price. "She’s beautiful, isn’t she?" He turned his head, glancing his soulful blue eyes at her, fraying her nerves a slight touch. She ignored his question, brushing it aside with no clear concern. "Can we leave now?" she replied, "I have an afternoon meeting with a client." "She was my wife." Her eyes widened in shock, taken aback by what he just said. "What do you mean? What’s your name?" she spoke harshly, crossing her arms across her chest. He smiled, giving the painting a final glance. He turned in her direction slowly, smoothing over the creases on his leather jacket with his rough palms. "It’s apparent that I have no further use for you." She didn’t reply. She never did. She couldn’t. A sharp sound of the hammer striking the metal rang through the air, attracting the attention of a few passer-bys. It was followed in quick account by a loud thud. And then, everything else was crimson red.
