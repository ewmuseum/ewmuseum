The Worst That Could Happen

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 Two years ago Jared's idea began life as a joke he told on a friend's couch in between nervous laughter and agonized admissions. Dr. Sebastian Parker had known Jared Sykes for close to five years. The two met shortly after Dr. Parker was hired by the National Wrestling Council and tasked with observing and reporting on the behavior of Jared Sykes to the executives in charge of Hawaiian Island Wrestling. The organization had labeled Jared as a potential problem, and their hope was that this exercise would give them an opportunity to remove him from their ranks. They did not expect for Jared and "his own personal Dana Scully" to hit it off and become friends. Dr. Parker did not expect to have Twinkies stuffed into his shoes during his first night on the job. On a late fall afternoon both men were gathered in Dr. Parker's newly relocated offices on the outskirts of Raleigh, North Carolina. It was their second meeting in as many days, and it was the first in which Dr. Parker had hoped to crack the invisible barrier that Jared had built up around himself. "Are you serious?" It was a question that Dr. Parker had asked him many times. Most of Jared's ideas bordered on ludicrous, and while the majority wasn't to be taken seriously Dr. Parker had seen and heard enough of them that he could never be quite sure. "No, not really." Jared offered a small smile before leaning back into the couch. He reached his right hand into the left sleeve of his hooded sweatshirt, scratching at his wrist. "Why not?" Part of Dr. Parker's job involved reading the visual clues his clients, or in this case his friends, would leave during the course of a conversation. Judging by Jared's sudden change in expression it was clear this was not the response he anticipated. "What do you mean 'why not'? Because it's the worst idea ever, that's why. I mean, I know I've done some silly things, but I think even I would draw the line at pretending to be a blueberry. What, would I get to have a mask with little cartoon berries on the side? Maybe a lopsided crown?" Dr. Parker nodded once as he tapped his thumb on the armrest of his chair. "Well, let me ask you this: do you think you'd be ready to get back into wrestling without the mask? Do you think you're ready to be that open with your life again?" Jared's gaze drifted to the floor, his hands falling into his lap. "No, probably not." "Okay, and what would you do if not wrestle?" Dr. Parker leaned forward in his chair, resting his elbows on his knees. Looking over the top of his glasses the good doctor tipped his head forward. "What's your back-up plan, Jared?" From his seat on the couch Jared could hear the wind shifting leaves outside the office. He lost himself in the sound, hoping that if he stayed silent long enough that he'd be left well enough alone for the remainder of the session. "Jared?" Dr. Parker wasn't buying it. Jared shifted on the couch, crossing one leg over the other. He let his eyes drift to the floor where they traced a lazy pattern through the Berber carpet. "Guess I don't have one. I'm not really sure what else I'd be any good at. This is the kind of job that takes over your life, you know." He looked up from the floor but did not make eye contact. "I started when I was 19, and all I ever had before that were the usual kid jobs. No college, either." He paused to consider his options, weighing the possibilities in his head. "Yeah, Park'. I'd be kind of fucked." It was a far cry from the days of filling shoes with baked goods. "I didn't say this to upset you, Jared. I'm just trying to help you figure out the next steps, and..." The doctor paused, his attention drawn to Jared's hand. "What's wrong with your hand? What do you keep scratching at?" A little embarrassed, Jared pulled his hands apart, hiding the left in the pocket of his sweatshirt. "Ah, it's nothing. Breaking in a new watch. Guess I'm not really used to the wristband yet." Whether or not Dr. Parker was contented with the answer for the time being he would let it go. "How are you adjusting in general? Any issues being back at home?" Jared squirmed in his seat as the corner of his mouth twitched into and out of a weak smile. His eyes darted nervously around the room. Yesterday's session hadn't been as formal as this one; it was more to get reacquainted as friends. Today brought the hard questions, and with them came a fair amount of nervous trepidation. "I, umm, I haven't talked to anyone yet. Not my parents, not Merv', nobody. It's just..." He paused to clear his throat, still avoiding any form of eye contact. "I don't know. Things still don't feel right. Like, I'm back in my apartment, and I know it's home, but it doesn't feel like home anymore. Nowhere I've been recently feels right. It's like I'm uncomfortable in my own skin all the time." "That's probably to be expected," Dr. Parker reassured. "You were out of the country for a while, and then..." "Can we skip this part?" Jared interrupted, cutting him off mid-thought. For a brief moment their eyes met. "Not keen on talking about it?" "No. I'm really not. Not yet, and maybe not for a while. I've been trying real hard to convince myself that I'm actually not crazy, so can we just conveniently forget the last few months for now?" "You'll need to get it out there eventually, Jared. The sooner you address it the sooner you'll be able to work past it." "I know, but for right now it's just easier to pretend." Jared shifted his position on the couch, bringing his limbs in tighter. He never enjoyed sitting in one spot for too long, so when the silence hung in the air he took it upon himself to start fidgeting with a loose strand of thread from a nearby cushion. Scratching wispy gray hair with chubby fingers, Dr. Parker observed the scene without a word. He knew that he would need to get Jared talking about this eventually, but there was plenty of time. For now he'd try a different tactic. "So this blueberry thing..." "Is still a horrible idea five minutes later." Jared gave the string a hard tug. He'd hoped to simply snap it free but instead ended up unraveling part of the fabric. If Dr. Parker noticed he gave no indication. "I know. I know, but what if you did it?" "Then I'd probably need to think about com..." He cut himself off before he could finish the word, but Dr. Parker's eyebrows had already raised. They fell just as quickly. "To going away again." "Ah." "Well, then again..." "Yes?" "I mean, it would be easier, right?" "Easier than what?" "Than being me. At least at first, I guess. People will find out eventually." "How would they find out unless you tell them?" "You ever hear of this thing called 'the Internet'?" Jared's voice was laced with sarcasm, but Dr. Parker smiled regardless. While still a far cry from the norm it was at least better than the self-indulgent sulking he'd witnessed thus far, and if nothing else might loosen Jared up for him to dig a little deeper. "Amazing little invention, really. In addition to serving up porn by the assload - sometimes pretty literally, people actually use it to send ideas across the entire planet through an interconnected series of tubes. Really, it's a technological revolution in the making. I'm surprised you're not on board." Dr. Parker chuckled as he raised his hands. "All right, all right, I get it. You've made your point. Now can I ask you something?" "Shoot." "I get the feeling that you're not entirely opposed to this idea, otherwise we'd be talking about something else. You've said it yourself just now: this is what you do, and while I can understand and appreciate your reluctance to dive back in head-first, I think you've already made up your mind on the situation. Is that fair?" "I guess." "So tell me then, and tell me honestly. What's the worst thing that could happen?" "Aside from calling myself 'King Blueberry' and looking like a complete asshole?" "Of course." Jared shrugged. "God only knows, Park'." * * * * * One month ago "Hey, George?" George O'Donnell had just stepped past the open door of his brother's room when the voice called out. Stopping mid-stride, George turned towards the open door bearing a Metallica poster held in place by a myriad of bumper stickers. "What's up, Mike?" the elder O'Donnell brother asked as he peered around the threshold. Though they were both sick to death of hearing how much alike they looked, in truth neither of them saw it. George, 24, the older and taller of the two O'Donnell boys, was lanky and awkward in contrast to his brother's shorter, more athletic build. While one spent the majority of his time reading comic books and watching wrestling, the other always seemed to be chasing either fly balls or girls. "Can I bother you for a minute?" Mike asked, looking up from a worn textbook whose margins were filled with freshly drawn stick figures. "I'm having an issue with my homework, and mom's being kind of a hardass about it. She won't let me do anything until it's finished. School's only been open for three weeks, and she keeps telling me that she 'doesn't want a repeat of last year.'" George stepped into the room, leaning against the wall and sliding his hands into his pockets. His eyes surveyed the space briefly, taking in the cacophony of colors and textures pasted across the walls. Mom and Dad would have never let me get away with this, he thought. "Sure. What subject?" "Physics. It sucks. They put me in Mr. Boyle's class. Was he such an asshat when you had him?" George smiled as he pushed away from the wall. He stepped over to the desk and leaned forward, examining the pages and marveling at his brother's crude artwork. He took particular pleasure in a picture of what appeared to be a shark swinging a pair of hammers. "I didn't think he was that bad, no. Then again that was eight years ago. He might've changed since then." "Yeah, well he still talks about you like you were Jesus, or something," Mike said, rolling his eyes. "He even has a stupid little joke about it. 'Mr. O'Donnell, if only you could turn your potential energy into kinetic energy you could accelerate to greatness.'" He threw his pencil down defiantly onto the surface of the desk where it bounced once and then rolled to the floor. "What the hell does that even mean?" "I think it's his way of trying to motivate you." "I think it's gay," Mike scoffed. George snorted. This in turn drew a laugh from his brother who had once again occupied himself by scribbling in the margins of his textbook. George took a step back from the desk and straightened up, taking a second to smooth out a few wrinkles in his shirt. "So what exactly are you having an issue with?" "Stupid gravity." "How many problems did they give you to try and solve?" "Like ten. And none of them have the answers in the back of the book." Mike shook his head, frowning down at his desk. "Gay." "Yeah, he assigned things the same way when I had him. He never gave us any of the odd-numbered problems, because the answers were in the book. He wanted us to come to it on our own." It seemed to George that Mike was quickly losing interest. The longer George talked the less time he was actually being helpful, which to a teenager meant precious minutes of life that could be better spent doing anything but schoolwork. "Tell you what; I need to run an errand, but it shouldn't be too long. When I come back we'll go through this to get mom off your back, okay?" "How long is long?" "Maybe a half-hour to an hour tops. Nothing more than that, I don't think." "I don't suppose it can wait, huh?" "Sorry, Mike, but the sooner I get there the sooner I can come back and help you with this." "Hey, can I go with you? I have my learner's permit now, so I can drive." His eyes perked at the thought of getting a reprieve from his mother-mandated schoolwork. Unfortunately it was not to be. "Sorry, Mike. I don't think mom would let us." Mike's sigh did little to hide his annoyance. "Fine. Whatever." "I'll be back soon. I promise." When Mike leaned over to pick his pencil back up off the floor George took the opportunity to sneak out of the room, taking hold of the banister and descending the stairs. In the kitchen he found his mother, Janice, rinsing off a few dishes that had accumulated in the sink. John, his father, occupied himself at the nearby table by flipping through the sports section of the newspaper. "Morning guys," George said, stepping to the refrigerator to retrieve a bottle of water. "Hi, George," his mother said, momentarily turning away from the sink. "You're up and about early." "Yeah, I need to run a quick errand. I shouldn't be out too long." "Anything important?" "Not really, I just need to run into the office real fast and check a few shipping confirmations for one of the drivers. I just hope I don't get pulled into something else while I'm there." Janice put the last dish in the strainer and turned off the water. Reaching for a towel she turned to face her husband and son. "Did you see Michael on your way down? Was he awake? I've been trying to get him up early to get his homework out of the way, but it hasn't been easy. Last thing he needs is a repeat of last year, especially if he's going to be applying to colleges this winter." "Yeah, he was awake," George nodded. "We're going to take a look at some physics problems together when I get back." "Oh, good." Janice smiled, almost relieved. Secretly she hoped that if George spent enough time with his brother that the younger O'Donnell might absorb some of his studious nature. She never said it though, not wanting to upset either son. "He wants to go watch the football game tomorrow with some of his friends, but I told him he wasn't going anywhere until he finished his homework." "Not like he'd be missing much. Not if the Titans play the way they did against Pittsburgh and Houston." John O'Donnell didn't look up from his perusal of the news, opting instead to hide behind his newsprint shield. Unfortunately, his addition to the conversation had the unintended side effect of drawing the ire of his wife. "And you won't be doing much of anything either unless you go outside and get that barn in order," Janice barked, waving an accusatory finger at her husband of twenty-five years. "There's a lot of space we could put to good use out there, but first somebody needs to clean it." John O'Donnell was whipped. There was no denying this. There was also no denying that the force it would take to keep him away from a Sunday afternoon of football was greater than anything his wife could offer. He'd been holding out on cleaning the barn since they moved to Tennessee from Minnesota almost eight years ago. This weekend would be no different than the four hundred before it. For his part, George knew that the odds of his father doing anything productive in the yard were infinitesimal, but that didn't stop a nervous flash from surging through his skin. He only hoped that neither of his parents were astute enough to catch it. "Sure, hon. I'll get right on it." He looked up from his newspaper at his son and winked, but not before he checked to make sure Janice wasn't looking. He was whipped, not dumb. George's nerves immediately settled. Safe again, he thought. Not wasting any time, he stepped out of the kitchen and through the living room en route to the front door. It was only when he was finally inside his car did he allow himself a moment to breathe. His office was only a few minutes away from home, but that's not where he was headed. He had a blank check, he had two forms of identification, and if everything went the way he hoped it would then within a few weeks he would have a license to purchase and carry a gun. * * * * * Now He didn't notice her when he stepped into the lobby of the hotel. Instead his attention was drawn to the BlackBerry he held and the standard click-click-scroll-click-scroll that went with it. Shiny things always had a nasty habit of keeping his attention, even in the face of details he would normally treat as important. Jared stepped to the reception desk, setting his phone down on the raised marble surface. He was greeted by a pleasant smile from the mousy 20-something behind the desk. "Can I help you?" "Yeah, I'm supposed to be checking in." He let the bag on his shoulder slide to the floor, relieved to be free of its weight. For someone as easily distracted as him the need for things to occupy his attention usually resulted in packing more than necessary. "Should be a reservation for Jared Sykes." She nodded once and went about the task of pulling up his information in the computer. The confusion that seemed to be overtaking her as she looked at the screen did little to put him at ease. "And this was booked by a Mr. Larry... does that really say 'Spiderman'?" Jared grinned. He was fairly certain that his agent had been born with one of the best names ever. Larry didn't think so, mostly on account of people like Jared who couldn't seem to stop from teasing him for it. "Yeah, he's someone I work with. Apparently it's supposed to be pronounced 'speedermin'. I don't buy it, though. I'm pretty convinced he has a pair of bright red tights hanging in his closet." Judging by her response Jared was fairly certain she didn't find the idea as humorous as he did. "By any chance did he give you the printed confirmation?" "Yeah, one sec. I think I have it here," Jared said, kneeling as he opened the smaller of the two bags he carried with him. He was too distracted to notice the red-haired storm in olive jeans and a black hoodie that approached. He was too distracted to see her tiny hand reach for the BlackBerry he'd innocently left on the reception desk. When the woman behind the counter opened her mouth to say something about what she believed to be an attempted theft her gaze was met by a hard look from a pair of mismatched eyes, immediately bringing that idea to a halt. "Sorry this is taking so long," Jared continued, attention buried in his search for a confirmation number and oblivious to scene unfolding around him. "Just another sec, I think." Satisfied with the information she was looking for, Amy Campbell set Jared's phone back on the counter, sneaking away before he stood back up. "Here you go," he said, sliding a crumpled sheet of paper across the marble to a woman trying very hard not to appear flustered. "It's a little wrinkly." * * * * * The boredom was becoming something of an issue. He knew he should be training, or researching his opponents, or at least doing something to get himself ready for Seymour Almasy and Kaden Calix, but that sort of thing never suited Jared. It was one of the many habits on an endless list that his agent found annoying. Up until recently it was a point that Larry called out until Jared started answering questions on his training regimen with responses along the lines of "only the right arm". It wasn't the sort of thing Larry never wanted to hear, much less think about. Normally Jared would at least be able to find and pester Gabriel Simon, the second and current man to wear the King Blueberry moniker, but Gabriel's travel plans had been delayed. Both his wife, Jamie, and his daughter, Emma, had been sick with the flu and Gabe decided it best to take some extra time at home to help out. Unfortunately for Jared this meant he was stuck alone on the road. He had entertained the thought of constructing a pillow fort from the cushions in his hotel room. It had become something of traveling tradition as of late; a new city meant a new hotel and new cushions with which to build his bastion of fabric and fluff. Occasionally he would take pictures, uploading them to a Photobucket account that perhaps three people in the world ever looked at, but the real joy was in imagining the confusion on the maid's face when she opened the door to be confronted by a fortress of foam. Jared stood from his seat on the bed and stepped over to the couch, testing the cushions as he contemplated the mechanics of the would-be structure. He imagined himself hiding in it, waiting for someone to come to his room, at which point he would shout, "Defend the king!" and defiantly heave a knotted towel at the unfortunate soul coming to change the linens. He wondered if the exercise would get him kicked out of this particular hotel. He had already pulled the first two cushions from the couch and was in the process of testing the third for obvious signs of biohazard when he heard the distinctive chime of his BlackBerry signaling the receipt of a new text message. Bored and eager for any form of human interaction he turned and lunged forward, tripping over his own shoes and flopping onto the bed with the grace of a dying gazelle. The message on his phone was direct, albeit confusing. have a seat at the hotel bar around 6:30. and dont get your hopes up, this isnt the cute chick at the front desk. It gave him pause. He didn't recognize the number, nor did he recall seeing anyone familiar in the lobby. Nonetheless he was curious, and with less than half an hour until 6:30 he gathered himself up and headed for the door. Curiosity had taken him over. The pillow fort would have to wait. * * * * * The problem he found with showing up early wasn't in sitting alone. It was in the anticipation. Lately he'd been finding himself failing in more and more social situations, which is why he didn't wait for his unknown companion to arrive before ordering his first drink, a Samuel Adams Light. The hope was that it would help take the edge off, though he didn't think it was working. Jared kept his back to the door, mostly so he wouldn't be tempted to check the entrance every few seconds. It was only when he noticed the bartender raise his eyes to the approaching redhead did Jared dare to glance over his shoulder. Then he picked his jaw up off the floor. "Holy shit," he said, turning further in his seat. "Didn't see this coming, like, at all. How the hell did you know I was here? And how'd you figure out my number?" He swallowed hard, remembering their previous meeting and the fate of Dan Mulholland, rendered unconscious thanks to 33 ounces of Canadian maple. "Wait, you're not hiding a bat, are you?" "I can barely hide me in these jeans." And it was apparent, though she slipped into the seat with relative grace, Amy Campbell shifted uncomfortably when she finally sat. "I gained five pounds thanks to my new anger management technique. Supposedly you're not supposed to use candy as positive reinforcement for calm thinking. Fucking Nazis." "Yeah, that's some serious bullshit." He smiled nervously, glad to know there wasn't a beating in his immediate future, but still unsure of what to expect. Sure, they'd chatted a bit on recent episodes of Temptation, but that was television, a spectacle for a national audience. This was something else entirely. There were no cameras here. "Still doesn't answer my question though. Like, do I have one of those RFID things in my head that I don't know about?" Amy threw her hands up. "Hey, that's something to ask Alex, not me." "Waiter!" She continued by snapping her head toward the bar. "Tequila? Whatever you've got, no biggie... three shots, please." Smiling, she turned back to the table before her, resting an elbow on it. "You're so sketchy, I swear. The company's too cheap to pay for our hotels anymore, but they get us discounts - like I didn't assume we'd both pick the cheapest. You... ooooh, I get it. You still don't know what it looks like when I'm being nice?" "I think I'm starting to get it." He took a sip from his bottle, noticing that the beer was starting to get a little warm. "I guess I just have an... I think we'll call it 'healthy respect'." Both his grin and his tone had hints of sarcasm, testing the waters. "How bored are you that you're here?" he continued, swirling the liquid in the base of the near-empty bottle. "Alex busy kicking someone's puppy?" "Watch it." Campbell stiffened for the warning, but relaxed a moment later. "And no, she's not... that was mean. Alex is busy being who she needs to be." She corrected her posture, shifting and tugging at the waistband of her slightly-too-tight olive jeans again. "While I'm being who I always am. Just... yeah, we'll go with bored. You don't have to sit with me if you want. We can take this slow." When Jared's eyes shot up, Amy laughed. "Being friends, dumbass. We can start slowly. I'm still married and supposedly gay as Christmas, remember?" "No, you're right, that was kind of a cheap shot. Guess I'm just so used to everybody telling me that I'm doing this whole 'how to behave around people generally perceived as evil' thing wrong that I slipped." Jared downed the last of his beer and gently pushed the bottle away. He'd order a second eventually, but was in no particular hurry. The thought of having to go back to his room and do a whole lot of nothing meant that he would stay here as long as possible. "Actually, I feel like I kind of owe you one," he continued, smiling. "You managed to save me from an evening of movies edited for television and the construction of a pillow fort." No sooner did the words escape his lips did Jared clench his teeth, drawing a sharp breath in between them. "I probably shouldn't have mentioned that last part." "Right. Because the great and epic evil that is Amy Campbell would never be friends with someone who shares her love of pillow forts." She snickered. "Big, scary Desade only just last week convinced me to deconstruct the castle of boxes I made in her living room. I even went nuts on the main entry with a Sharpie. Sadly, Aimztopia was torn to the ground and recycled. I'm also immune to the heartwarming hijinks of those lame TV movies -- except that they turn the holiday season into Ovarian Boohoo Sobfest EVERY goddamn year for me." Before Jared could order, Amy did so for him - asking for another round and a fourth shot with it, alongside one more of 'Whatever pee beer this guy just had'. She continued with an infectious smirk and wiggling of the eyebrows not unlike what he would have seen on TV just before she verbally - or physically, in most cases - assaulted somebody. "So don't worry, Assorted Hotel Linens Boy, your secret hideout's safe with me." Jared practically collapsed onto the table, burying his face in his arms. His laughter was hard enough that it shook both his body and the table. "Wow, I... wow." He leaned back, rubbing his eyes with the palms of his hands. "Ovarian boohoo sobfest. I don't even know how to fucking follow that. Normally I'm supposed to be the one saying the goofy shit. And for the record, if someone takes Linens Boy and spins it into a gimmick I will totally have had nothing to do with it, regardless of my track record." "The only dangers are sitting at this table, and I'm actually more worried about me using it, considering that I'm eventually need to get over the universe thinking I'm either the devil, or sleeping with her." "Well, for what it's worth, I don't actually think you're the devil. I just have a nasty habit of running a perfectly good joke into the ground. Plus," he paused, the gears in his head turning into place. "Wait, what was that second part?" Amy's brows quirked again. "You and everyone else probably think Alex is pure evil, and I'm... well, we're... anyhoo... are you busy tonight, or am I gonna have to watch the shitty Gerard Butler movie I ordered alone?" "I've got nowhere to be, but..." His voice trailed off, a result of the new set of drinks placed before him. He eyed his own for a moment, gently turning the bottle between his fingers as he debated whether to ask a question she most likely didn't want to answer. "I mean, wouldn't you rather watch it with her? And feel free to tell me to shut up, or whatever, but the last few times I've talked to you there've been comments you've made about her. Is everything okay?" "I told you, she's busy." The answer was stern at first. "She's not in town until tomorrow, had to take care of some business in Oakland. I stay out of it, because... well, you know a little bit about her. You know what I'm up against. I've fought about her enough over the past few months, especially lately, so I'd rather not fight over who she needs to be to keep her people in line or what she has to get invo--y'know, probably not the best topic. Everything's okay and the ship's at no risk of sinking or springing a leak, the tide's just a little unforgiving sometimes. Aaand I just sounded so Atlantic Canadian there, wow." Campbell shrugged, "Yeah, I'd rather be with her - no offense, of course - but being with her doesn't mean I forfeit the right to have friends or some semblance of a social life. I party alone a little too much, and going to bars has traditionally been more than a little bad for my health and criminal record, so watching The Ugly Truth and eating extra-buttery popcorn with Jared Sykes will have to do." She smiled, deliberately brighter than she felt after delving a little too far into her distaste for Pierce's work. There was no need to run Sykes off, but poking fun at him was a tiny joy she'd never tire of - like a warm, cross-eyed kitten. "Are you in, or do I have to fish through the hotel records to find someone on this roster with a bigger vagina?" "I don't know, dude," he said, taking a healthy swig from his drink. His own smile was wide. "Word on the street is that mine's pretty cavernous. Might be tough to find bigger. It'd be like..." And then everything came to a grinding halt. It was as if his brain had gone non-responsive, and as hard as he tried to think of a way to end that thought nothing came to him. There were few things in this world Jared Sykes feared as much as a failed punch line. Nothing else left him as vulnerable. He'd rather be naked in public than bomb a joke. "Shit," he sighed. "I've got nothin' for that one. Gerard Butler for the win, I guess, but you should know that if it sucks, and I have a feeling that it most certainly will, you can expect a running commentary." Amy's last words were about as prophetic as they came with her, spoken just before she filled her mouth with more tequila. "If it sucks, we'll be drunk by the end, so who cares?" "Fair enough, but if she kills me in my sleep tonight I am so coming back as a ghost and haunting you for the rest of your life. You will never go in your attic again, so help me." He brought the bottle to his lips, draining the remainder of the contents before speaking again. "Hey, actually, would it be weird if you ran it by her? I'd never actually say this to her in person, but she kind of scares me, and I don't want to step on her toes." * * * * * Panic. That's what he'd initially felt when he followed her upstairs and into the room she would no doubt be sharing later with Alexandra Pierce. No sooner did she unlock and step through the door did he feel the need to divert his eyes, for fear that they might catch a glimpse of something neither woman would want him to see. Explaining to Desade about "that time he saw her girlfriend's underwear" wasn't high on his list of to-dos. Fairly early into the film, which was in fact awful, Amy had cracked open her private stash, and since then the small coffee table by the couch served as a museum for bottles of various shapes, sizes, and colors. The collection of alcohol was based on a strong foundation of tequila and gin, neither of which Jared was particularly fond. The gin was most troubling to him. His most recent experience with the volatile liquid left him hung over for two days and banned for life from a Daytona Beach hotel. Jared took it slow so as to maintain some semblance of control. A more deliberate pace followed a few early shots, if only to maintain the buzz. He felt it necessary state of mind given the cinematic catastrophe unfolding on the television. He'd once made the mistake of admitting on national television that he classified himself as a "naked drunk". That wasn't a scene he felt like explaining later on, either. "This movie makes me want to die," he said, kicking off his shoes and resting a foot on the coffee table. "Seriously, if he doesn't kick her into a pit soon I'm going to be really upset." "'Christ," Campbell exclaimed, wincing at a particularly bad - not to mention awkward - moment involving vibrating underwear. "They're gonna bill me four dollars for this abomination. And what's up with you? You're not nearly drunk enough for this! Have another." "Bad things happen when Jared gets drunk," he said, leaning forward to oblige her request. He paused mid-pour, wondering why he just referred to himself in the third person. "I have a horrible habit of taking all my clothes off. All of them. It's not a pretty sight." His face flushed in embarrassment as soon as he heard her giggling. "Wait, no, that's not what I meant." "I'll put a stop to it before that happens, don't worry. Plus, I know Alex would ki--speak of the devil!" Amy had stopped mid-sentence after an off-screen vibration. If Jared's mind immediately went to dark places (and then scrambled out of those places, screaming his innocence), he could be forgiven. She finally fished her BlackBerry out from behind a row of bottles, initially smiling (presumably when she saw a text message from Pierce), then frowning a little at the screen, tossing the phone onto the couch. Keeps people from going 'Oh hey, that's Desade from TV!' or something. She just... she kinda gets too into it." Amy seemed to be launching a liquor-based pre-emptive attack on her headache, tossing down another glass. "Not cheating or anything, but she's a good actress and... and I don't like it. Not to mention that everybody she works with thinks I'm the bad influence on her." All things considered he was holding up remarkably well. He hadn't expected Amy Campbell to summon him to the hotel bar. He hadn't expected to spend the rest of the evening hanging out and getting drunk with her. He certainly didn't expect her to delve into the extra-curricular activities of the subtlest and most secretive person he'd ever come to know. At the very least he knew enough to not keep silent. If it meant playing makeshift-counselor then so be it. "I kind of don't know what to say here." That much at least was true. "I seriously had no idea. I mean, well, that's not entirely accurate. She hangs out with enough people to field her own baseball team, and they seem to be pretty loyal from what I've seen, so it's one of those cases where you figure that something's up, but I wouldn't have guessed that." He took a minute to compose himself, trying to shake the last of the haze from his head. "Can I ask you something?" Strangely, though the effects of the alcohol were waning he found his courage increasing. "How do you live with that, like, knowing that the person you're with is doing... whatever you just said. That'd fuck me up." "I didn't tell you what she does," Amy pressed that point firmly, widening her eyes along with the jab of a scolding finger. "Her business is her business. "What makes you so screwed up, aside from liking to wear blue?" "Okay." He pulled himself out of the couch just long enough to fill his own glass with gin. He downed it in a single gulp before setting the now empty glass back down on the table. "Okay." Again he turned towards her. With a deep breath he loosened the band on his watch, sliding it over his wrist and exposing the skin underneath. There's always a watch, or a wristband, or tape, or whatever to cover it. Four people know I have it. Well, four people plus me, so I guess that makes you number five." "So there you go. Nice little memento, all because somebody thought I was actually crazy." It shouldn't have been very surprising that Amy didn't see much negative about the revelation. Leave it to you to prove that you're actually way stronger than I am, huh? That's a beautiful thing." She brought her eyes up after that remark, shoving at Jared's shoulder. "And I am not hitting on you, no matter what that drunk little head of yours is thinking, so don't get all tense and awkwa--well, I guess you can't help that part." "Not as strong as you give me credit for, to be honest," he said, letting his gaze drop momentarily to the floor. "I checked myself in." What he saw when he dared look at her again shouldn't have surprised him, but he shook his head regardless. She'd fallen asleep - or, more accurately, had passed out.
