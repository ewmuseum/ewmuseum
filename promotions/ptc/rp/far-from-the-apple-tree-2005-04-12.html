"Far From the Apple Tree..."

12 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-12 One! Two! Three! Doctor Curiosity wins the match and advances to the semi-finals! For a fighter, there is no sound more horrendous than hearing the ending of your match and no time more slothful than waiting to hear that you lost. The final ten seconds of Sebastian Moraco’s loss to Doctor Curiosity in the bracket finals of Prime Time Central’s copious GTT4 tournament seemed like an eternity. Sebastian fought with all the talent and heart that he could muster in an effort to shake the no-chance monkey off his shoulders. In the end all his training and determination proved naught as Doctor Curiosity prevailed. Sebastian, in losing, had proven his doubters right, and Doctor Curiosity had done the same. There was never any chance of Sebastian beating Doctor Curiosity. It didn’t matter that Sebastian had learned the trade rather quickly, or that he was a former amateur wrestling state champion, or that the good doctor was more than twice his age. Sebastian never had a chance and he proved everyone right. That loss was as infuriating to him as any other he had experienced. The heartache even ranked up there when he knew that he wasn’t going to be selected to represent the United States in the summer Olympics. Losing the match and having to tolerate the aftermath of the loss wasn’t the only fallback from the match. Sebastian Moraco, as was later found out, had agreed to be registered for the tournament without the consent of his parents. Sure, not telling the University that you were going to be gone for a few weeks was certainly a scar on your record, but lying to your parents was another matter entirely. Even more so when those parents had specific dreams for you that didn’t include beating up on someone you never had a reason to fight in the first place. Sebastian was welcomed home with open arms, but probably not with the kind of open arms you would expect a parent to give their child when they came back mildly successful and with a few extra dollars. Outside of the daily routine, Miguel Moraco never gave his son any more dialogue than a common “hello” or a “goodbye”. Sebastian knew that his parents were heavily distraught with his decision to leave them and go fight, risking his life, but he still went on to do it. He wanted to do something for himself for once and not just be following in the traditions of countless Moraco family men. As indifferent as Sebastian’s father had become after the incident, his mother was the real concern. Sebastian had always loved his mother more than anyone in the world and was raised very close to her during her troubled years with Miguel. In the end, it made him sort of a “mama’s boy” who was strong, yet soft and passionate. He grew up to be very respectful vowing never to be like his father when it came to relationships. Maria knew that and believed in her heart that Sebastian would have dropped everything if she had asked him to do so. Sebastian didn’t. He defied her twice and continued to fight in the tournament, much to the distress and sadness of his mother. They watched his matches with eyes wide-open and pulsating heartbeats, looking away each time Sebastian would be hurt. Sebastian returned home the next day following his loss and didn’t receive any heartwarming welcome from his father. His mother, however, was nearly bought to tears – just happy to have her only son back. That didn’t change the fact that Sebastian still defied her and still put himself at risk. All for the glory of being a fighter, something that none of his parents approved. “I know I lied to you.” Sebastian had told them as soon as he set his suitcases down. “I did it because it was the only way I could get this done without your disapproval.” “You not only lied to us, Sebastian.” Miguel scolded him. “You also lied to the University. You already failed all the classes this semester. Did you think of that?” “I wasn’t worried about the classes!” Sebastian remarked. “I could have always made them up. Taking another year wasn’t going to be a problem! I lost a chance to go to the Olympics. You both know how much that meant to me. This was going to be my second chance there, and I saw it and took it.” “And what happened?” Miguel asked the rhetorical question. Sebastian took his time in answering. Everyone knew that there was no chance of him going back to the Olympics – at least not in that way. “Everyone knows I lost.” Sebastian answered. “I lost any chance of redemption.” “Yeah, you lost a year of schooling just so you can be in some ridiculous, barbaric, and insane tournament.” Miguel insulted him as Maria just turned away biting her tongue. “No!” Sebastian yelled back. He had grown tired of being undermined for this decision. “It wasn’t just a fucking ridiculous tournament! This was my chance to make something of myself! I found something I can be successful in and that’s all that matters.” “Don’t raise your voice to me.” Miguel scolded back in a loud whisper. He had hit Sebastian before, but not anymore since the first time Sebastian decided to hit back. “You disrespected us. You disrespected yourself. You wasted the University’s time on you. You disrespected the family. What do we tell your relatives now that your chances of being a great architect were fluttered by some ridiculous tournament? What the hell do you tell them now!” “Maybe I never wanted to be architect!” And just like that…the awful truth had been revealed. For as long as he could remember, not that his parents would let him forget, Sebastian’s family had always had a tradition of maintaining professions within the locale. The tradition was more than just being a successful mechanic, architect, or other professions it was also about maintaining that profession within decent distance of the family. None of the male or females had traveled outside of Spain with the exception of those let’s-kick-back vacations. Over time it became to be more than just a tradition, it was almost a rule – an axiom of sorts. They were born in Spain. They were raised in Spain. They will continue the cycle in Spain. For the most part no one ever questioned it. There was never a requirement to stay in Spain; it was only highly recommended that you did. So when Sebastian’s parents announced that they would be leaving Girona, Cataluna Spain for the United States, there was no surprise that a maelstrom of worry ravaged through the family. Everyone took solace in Miguel Moraco’s, Sebastian’s father, promise of returning to the homeland once Sebastian had acquired his degree in architecture. Even Maria Moraco, Sebastian’s mother, spent the majority of her good-bye conversations putting down any rumors of them leaving Spain for good. Spain gave me life and is in my blood, she would say. Little did they know that the decision to educate their son in the United States was going to be a catalyst for him to go on a path not already set. It certainly wasn’t going to be one set by his father. His mother and aunts had raised Sebastian during his parents’ tumultuous married days. Miguel, at the time, had been away on business frequently, months at a time, and when home was nothing more than an adulterer and abusive. He blamed his behavior on his job, of being an architect, and having to travel too much to see his family. Maria endured it, perhaps because she could not see her son growing up without a father. At the very best, she hoped, Miguel would be a role model for him to not idolized. That wish slowly came into fruition as Sebastian got older and vowed that he would never be like his father: disrespectful, unavailable, abusive, and an adulterer. Miguel had, on numerous times, hit Sebastian out of anger or as a method of inducing punishment. Hitting a child to discipline him was normally accepted, so no ruckus was ever made of that. That form of tough-love punishment would be induced on Sebastian for most of his childhood. That was until he decided to hit back at the age of sixteen. If there ever was a time to mark as the beginning of Sebastian’s distancing from his father it would have been that day. Sebastian had been caught shoplifting from the store and Miguel refused to believe that Sebastian would have done that. So he resorted to the only punishment he knew: psychical abuse. Sebastian endured the belt shots, and in one quick moment struck his father. However, the blows from the belt and his lack of any real fighting made him a sluggish puncher and the punch rolled off his father’s shoulder. There was a moment of silence there and the disciplining that came afterwards was harsher. “Que te paso a ti?” Maria had asked him when she got home that day. She saw her son was red with anger and the back of his neck was red. “What happened to you?” She asked. “Nothing…” Sebastian muttered staring out the window. “No, don’t tell me that.” Maria insisted. “Let me see your back.” “I’m fine, mom!” Sebastian yelled at her biting back his pain. “Let me see!” Maria insisted as she slapped Sebastian on his shoulder. Sebastian yelped in pain and quickly went over to the other side of the room. “I told you it’s nothing , mom. I’ll be fine tomorrow, just had an accident that’s all.” “An accident?” Mom inquired. “In town. A couple of friends of mine…we all went down to the park and I fell down the cliff by the Lussano’s park and scraped myself on the way down.” Sebastian lied to her trying to get her off his case. “It looks pretty bad from here, even with your shirt on.” Maria continued to be concerned. “Come here so I can have a look at it, make sure it’s not infected.” “It’s not, Mom.” Sebastian lied again keeping his back away from his mother as she stepped in closer to him.” “I think it is, Sebastian.” And with that Maria grabbed Sebastian’s armed pulled him from the wall and lifted his shirt. Sebastian stood paralyzed knowing that there was no more point in lying. Maria looked at his back for a split second and then stepped back and gasped after she saw all the bruises and the dried up blood on her son’s back. “Oh…my…God…” Maria began to weep. Sebastian put his shirt back on. He didn’t dare look at his mom, and talked to her looking away from her face. “I’ll be fine, Mom.” Sebastian whispered. “When did this happened?!” Maria demanded to know. “Today…” Sebastian said. “A couple of kids from school were making bets to steal from Pedro’s store and I got caught. Pedro told dad and…” “I don’t care what you did!” Maria cried as she hugged Sebastian’s head tightly. “He shouldn’t have done this to you! We need to get you to a hospital.” “But I’m fine!” Sebastian protested. “It doesn’t even hurt that much anymore.” “You’re not fine!” She continued to weep. “Just look at yourself. You’re just a kid.” “No!” Sebastian cried. This time the tears were in his eyes. “I’ll be fine because I will be nothing like him! I won’t be like him! I won’t be like him! I won’t be like him!” The apple had fallen far from the tree… Maria grabbed her son and hugged his head tightly for long period of time. She wept with him and, while the tears rolled down his face, Sebastian did not weep. He clenched his fists and stared into the wooden floor as if attempting to burn a hole through it with an attempt at pyrokinetics. Sebastian was never going to be like his father, he vowed. He knew all to well the kind of man he was and wanted nothing to do to resemble him. He knew about the fights his father would have with his mother. He knew about the girls his father would sleep around with when his mother was sick. For every good thing his father did, such as having a very successful job, he did something equally as negative as being unfaithful and unavailable. But Sebastian’s distancing from his father was more than just because of the man he was inside the house. Sebastian wanted to follow his path, avoiding the axiom tradition that his predecessors had set for him. He did not want to become an architect. He did not want to stay in Spain. When he was younger, Sebastian had no say in the matter of his schooling. It was all what his parents decided for him, and what they decided for him was a very technical-induced curriculum. So, one can only imagine the kind if opportunity that was presented to Sebastian when his parents decided to move to New York City. But not even Sebastian could have predicted that the move to New York City would have been a catalyst not just for his career, but for his life as well. In Spain, or in Cataluna, the most common game to play was soccer. Sebastian’s cousins, brothers, and friends would always play down by the local field every day engaging themselves in some scrimmages. Whenever there would be a party or a reunion of sorts, there was always a highlight game to be played as everyone would cheer for their son, brother, and sometimes even sister, or daughter. Soccer became almost a tradition as set in stone as the others that had been set for Sebastian. However, this one he did not mind. He played along with the trend, sometimes getting into fights himself. He was a decent fighter, having won only half of his fights. The strangeness of it all, was that he enjoyed the fights. Sebastian even went as far as to assume that he would be a great boxer, having tried out for the boxing classes at school under anonymity. That never got off the ground, but it was the start of a beginning. Sebastian’s first year in the United States was as jumpy and tumultuous as any other. It was even more hazardous considering he was living New York City and spoke very little English. He went through the last two years of high school in New York City trying to make the best of it. But patience and tolerance can only go so far. Sebastian got into the first fight of his short time in New York City in the summer of his junior year. He was called a “Spick” and lunged at the man who had insulted him. A surprise right-handed cross caught the juvenile’s jaw and sent him straight to the pavement. Sebastian leaped on top of him and behind wailing at his head cursing out Spanish profanities. “Maricon!” “Puta!” “Miedra!” Seconds later, that juvenile’s friends stormed Sebastian from behind and proceeded to stomp on him. Sebastian had gotten into his first fight and had lost his first fight. It was not going to be the last fight he got into. Sebastian Moraco got into many more fights the last two years of high school. His parents would always be concerned of course, but for different reasons. His father was worried about him expelling himself from school; his mother was worried about his health. But Sebastian was fine. He was almost enjoying it. He finally got to prove how much he enjoyed it when he moved on to Polytechnic University in New York City. Polytechnic University was a predominantly Division II school – especially when it came to soccer. Sebastian Moraco scouted the soccer team and felt it was too inadequate to join. Besides he had set his eyes on something else: wrestling. It wasn’t the kind of wrestling he saw on television that consisted of soap opera like storylines and moves he had never seen before. This kind of wrestling was all about physical fitness and skill. While Sebastian had lost more than his fair share of fights growing up, he had become better with each fight and by his freshman year in college was in pretty decent shape. At best, he was in the best shape of his life and more so than that of his peers who spent their time by playing video games or sitting at front of a computer for hours. Sebastian tried out for the team and was accepted. His talent and skill continued to develop and, by the end of the first year, was co-captain of the team and instrumental in the college winning the state championships for the first time in a decade. That win marked off a remarkable run, and put Sebastian in line for a shot to represent the United States in the Summer Olympics. That shot never materialized, however, and Sebastian got his first real heartbreak in the United States. For the first time, he had found something he was good at and enjoyed. It was taken away from him in a manner of seconds. When the second year began, something incredible happened: he was offered a chance to fight for money and glory. His mentor, [name withheld], urged him to sign up for something known as the Prime Time Central’s GTT4 tournament because of all the good it did for him. Sebastian reluctantly signed up for the tournament not too sure of his chances. These fighters were all professionals, having fought for much longer than he had. They were in better shape, more experiences, stronger, faster, and knew how to fight better. Sebastian’s training during that copious tournament consisted of nothing more than hitting the gym to increase his strength and stamina, and watching WWE videos to learn a few moves to mimic. The end result was spotty at best. His lack of experience showed in the ring several times, but his determination and quick learning showed as well. That training, though inadequate by most standards, proved well for Sebastian as he managed to win match after match after match against someone he had no business beating. That was until he ran into the roadblock known as Doctor Curiosity. Once again Sebastian went into the match with little to no fanfare. This time the end result was expected: He lost. Match done. Tournament done. Washington D. C., done. Lies, done. Sebastian returned home the next day to his parents after he had lied to them about his whereabouts. He was bruised and beaten on the outside, but inside he was as joyous as ever. He finished out the semester in college, knowing that he would have to stay one more year, for already missing four weeks worth of material in each class. Time passed and Sebastian could not stop thinking of the tournament and how great he felt fighting against other people to see who was the best. He could still the savor the roaring of the crowd as they saw two great fighters give it their all. He wanted it. He just wasn’t sure how much he wanted it. For the next few months, Sebastian laid low, only wrestling at the college and under the direct supervision of his new coach. His mother would always argue with him to make sure that he never ran off like that again. She was worried that her son was going to continue that violent path and get himself hurt, or worse, killed. Those fears came into fruition a few weeks ago when word was bought to Sebastian that Prime Time Central was in the midst of hosting another tournament. This time it was going to be CW2. A small scaled, yet highly anticipated, tournament limiting the entrants by setting a weight limit at 225 pounds. Sebastian was well within the limit and signed up for the tournament as soon as he could. Greatness in the tournament was going to be guaranteed. Getting in was not. Thus it came as a surprise for Sebastian Moraco when he found out that he had been accepted. He was shocked. Sebastian could not believe that he had been given a spot above so many other gifted fighters and was going to be fighting in the same arena as the defending champion, Seymour Almasy, and Ethan Knight, one of the previous year’s final four competitors. Sebastian wasn’t the only one to receive word of his acceptance. During one of her daily cleaning routines, Maria Moraco found a crumpled piece of paper underneath Sebastian’s bed. She opened it up with curiosity, thinking perhaps it was homework he was neglected. What she found was not homework, but the acceptance invitation to CW2. She was crushed. Her son had defied her once again. Maria sat on his bed and waited for her son to come home… When Sebastian came home that day, he did not see his mother anywhere. He walked into his room and saw his mother on his bed. “Don’t go.” Was the first thing she said to him. “I’m not going anywhere, Mom.” Sebastian feigned. Maria let out a force chuckle that was quickly replaced with worry. “Don’t lie to me. You are a very bad liar when it comes to telling me the truth, Sebastian.” “But I’m not lying.” Sebastian insisted, putting down his backpack. Maria opened up the letter in her hand and read it loud. “We are pleased to inform you that your application has been accepted for entrance into the Prime Time Central’s second annual cruiserweight tournament. Your first round opponent will be the sixteen year prodigy from the DRWF, Silvio Fiore.” “I know…” Sebastian surrendered. “I know how you feel about this. But you cannot set the rules for me anymore. This is what I want to do.” “For what?!” Maria cried throwing the paper at him. “To fight? To get yourself killed? To end up like your father?!” That hurt. “No…” Sebastian muttered turning away. “I told you I won’t be like him. I won’t be like anyone.” “But you don’t have to do it this way, mijo.” Maria pleaded with her son. “I know how you feel about everything that has happened to us. I know you don’t want to be what everyone else has been before you. But please, just listen to me, you can find something better to do.” Sebastian merely shook his head. “Goddamn you!” Maria cried out loud. “You’re going to fight a sixteen year old kid, Sebastian. Sixteen years! Do you remember what happened to you when you were sixteen?!” Sebastian breathed out loudly. He remembered it all too well. It was the time he first began to distance himself from his father. “I know. I’m not going to let the same thing happen to me again.” “Then don’t let it happen to someone else.” Maria said, caressing her son’s face. “He’s just a kid. Don’t do the same thing to him.” “Silvio Fiore isn’t a kid.” Sebastian explained, yet he knew that nothing he could say would persuade her. “He doesn’t live a normal life. He suffers from an illness that prevents him from experiencing any pain. He’s fought before and will probably fight again. Nothing I do to him is going to hurt him or make him worse.” “That’s still no reason to fight him!” Maria scolded him. “You’re better than that. It’s time you realized it. Don’t go…” “Stop it!” Sebastian protested. “You’re not setting the rules for me anymore. I don’t care about what you don’t want me to do. I’m not going to end up like the man you married!” Slap! That was the first time Maria had ever hit Sebastian. The two stared at each other still unsure of what had happened. “I’m sorry…” Maria began to apologize. “I’m going.” Sebastian professed. “I’ll be fine.” I’ll be fine. Maria didn’t let Miguel know about what had transpired that day. Miguel never found out. Maria and Sebastian never talked about the incident, or CW2, when Miguel was around, but they did argue about it everyday until it came time for Sebastian to leave. Prime Time Central had already sent over their invitations, reservations, and travel arrangements for the competitors and their guests. Sebastian wanted his mother to attend the tournament with him. He thought that perhaps having her by his side, he could not only do well, but also perhaps change her mind that he wasn’t really in any danger. Maria refused the offer and begged him to resign once again. Stubborn as always, Sebastian refused and the debate went unresolved. Sebastian was set to face Silvio Fiore, hailing from Hays, Kansas. Silvio was a unique individual who felt no pain. Sebastian knew that the only way he was going to beat him was to knock the wind out of him, or break a bone. He had hoped that he would be successful with a quick pin fall, or a surprise move like a small package and get the win that way. At sixteen years of age, he did not want to cause Silvio any serious harm. The same could not be said perhaps of Silvio and his feelings towards Sebastian. No one could know what he truly felt. Sebastian knew that, and he knew there was a chance he could be hurt. But he could take care of himself. The day soon approached when Sebastian had to leave New York City for the tournament. He never told his father anything. He never even bothered to make up a lie about being on a field trip like he had done with the GTT4. He knew that he had very little trust with his father and anything he said had little to no credibility. Instead, all he could hope for was that Miguel never found out, and that he would never take out his anger on his mother. Maybe he was being selfish, but Sebastian was sure that when this was over he would decide and know for sure what he was going to do the rest of his life. On the morning of his departure, Sebastian snuck into his parents’ room to say goodbye to his mother. But he was shocked to find that his mother wasn’t there. Maria had been awake in the living room all night long, just waiting for him. “Are you still planning on going?” Maria asked him as he stepped into the living room. “Yes…” Sebastian replied back. “Please don’t go.” Maria pleaded with him once more. “I…I can’t.” Sebastian tried to explain to her. “I already made up my mind. I’m sorry for this.” “So am I…” she said. “I promise I’ll be fine, mom.” Sebastian explained to her. “I’ll be fine.” With that he hugged her tightly. I’ll be fine. We are now boarding all passengers onboard American Airlines Flight 412. Please have your boarding passes ready with a form of identification. Sebastian Moraco slung his carryon over his shoulder and stood in line at the gate. He walked obliviously as the line moved, looking outside the glass to the city he was leaving behind for a few weeks, or as long as it would take him. It wasn’t the city he was concerned with leaving, it was his mother. He did not want to leave her in that state of worry. Sebastian got inside the airplane and took his seat by the window. He stared out the window wondering, not of the tournament, or of Silvio Fiore, but of his mother. “I’m not going to be like him, mom.” Sebastian muttered under his breath. “I’m not going to be unfaithful. I’m not going to be an abuser. I’m not going to lie to you anymore. I’m not going to be unavailable.” Sebastian turned away from the other passengers, not wanting them to see his eyes redden. “I won’t be like him!” he muttered under his breath. “I won’t be like him! I won’t be like him! I won’t be like him!” Sebastian had always defied his family’s traditions. He didn’t want to travel the road that was paved for him. When the elder tree dropped the apple, the apple fell far from its grasps. Over time, the apple continued to roll further and further down its own path, until finally you couldn’t tell from which tree it fell from. Sebastian had always wanted to distance himself from everyone else, and this time he was finally getting what he wanted. He had taken the road less traveled and for the first time felt really alive. The airplane took off and Sebastian Moraco looked out the window one last time. “I’ll be fine, mom.” I’ll be fine…
