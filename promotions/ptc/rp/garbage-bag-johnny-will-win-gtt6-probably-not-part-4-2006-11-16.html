Garbage Bag Johnny WILL win GTT6 (Probably Not) Part 4

16 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-16 Nova and I planned to leave the hotel early that morning after careful deliberation over our next move. Walking down the hotel hallway, we were decidedly wary to go back to his house for fear that Julius Jones and his men would be there. Like I said, Julius was like a ghost. He could walk through walls- perhaps not literally, but he just seemed to kind of appear in rooms. Neither friend nor foe would hear him enter, and Julius could stand there for as long as he wanted to stand there until he wanted to make his presence loudly and suddenly felt. "I bet he’s just chilling in there right now. He’s probably sitting on the couch with his boots all up on my coffee table, just waiting for me to waltz right in there." The few restless hours of sleep we had gotten did nothing to calm my nerves. We had made the decision to go back to Nova’s place to survey the scene. At the least, we’d take his conversion van, and if the coast was clear enough, we’d run in quickly to grab his stash of illicit substances and some money he had hidden away. "We’ve got to at least go back for the van. It’s the only chance we have at a successful getaway." "I think we need some good disguises," I added. This was upsetting Nova a great deal. He claimed that I was becoming so afraid of getting shot multiple times that I was becoming irrational. Nova had no intention of dying either, but I thought he had a little too much confidence in his survival skills. "The wrong disguises could muck up a perfectly good plan. If there’s one thing I don’t need right now, it’s you getting us shot because someone mistook you for a bear or a gorilla or a samurai. I know if I saw a samurai parading around the streets with his unsheathed katana, I’d shoot him right between the eyes." "I’m just afraid of being recognized, you know? Julius has eyes working for him all over the city. Hell, some of those eyes are probably watching us right now." I looked around nervously, but Nova wasn’t concerned. I stopped walking, a step behind him. "Plus, I think it would be really swell to dress up as a samurai." Contrary to Nova’s rationalizations, I figured that if I was going to go down, I was going to go down dressed as a fucking ninja. The media never cares to check facts. The stations would all report that I died using my fluid karate training to battle gangs. You never hear the damned liberal media badmouthing ninjas. Nova turned around a few steps down the hall, noticing that I had stopped. "What is it now?" "Nothing," I replied. "I’d just feel a lot better about all this if we weren’t walking around in broad daylight, completely undisguised!" "Come on," Nova unenthusiastically beckoned, "or I’ll leave without you." To illustrate his point, Nova started walking, slowly at first while looking back every so often. But then, his pace quickened, and he stopped looking back until he got nearly to the stairway, and I called after him. "They’ll take me down for sure if we split up, man. Do you want that on your conscience?" Nova stopped, and I could see his muscles tensing in reluctant loyalty. He just stood there, though, refusing to turn. Instead, his shoulders perked inquisitively. "Besides, Nova, if they torture me, I’ll probably end up getting you in trouble. Not many people know this about me, but torture is my greatest weakness." I saw Nova’s sides begin to tremble as he was withholding laughter and shaking his head. He still refused to turn around, even after I had called his bluff. "Look, Johnny. You can wear a costume, but you’ll have to make it yourself. I can’t waste my money buying costumes when we may need it for something more important wherever we end up. There are some sheets back in the room that you can probably steal before we check out." With my disguise options limited to ghost or Roman Emperor, I ran back to the room, and I could hear Nova’s slow footsteps walking back towards me. I went to work, savagely fashioning eyeholes in a sheet with one of my keys. The key to making a decent ghost costume is to make the eyes just off of center longwise on the sheet. This guarantees that the sheet hangs evenly on both sides. When I was done fashioning mine, I put my costume on and went to work on Nova’s. My costume fit perfectly. "Hurry it up, man," Nova urged upon reaching the doorway. "How long does it take to steal a sheet?" "I’m almost done," I told Nova, who apparently must have seen that I was already in costume. "Alright, you got your costume on. You’re practically incognito. Let’s go." If I didn’t know any better, I would have thought that Nova was starting to get a bit impatient. "I have to finish your costume first." I worked diligently while talking, boring holes in the thin cloth where I’d imagine Nova’s eyes would be. "My costume?" Nova questioned almost accusingly. "Sure," I said, having thought things through a bit more thoroughly. "Julius saw us leave together at the tracks. If Julius sees you standing next to a guy in a ghost costume, I imagine he’d be able to put two and two together. A disguise isn’t much of a disguise if he knows I’m wearing it. I can’t just be myself underneath a sheet. I need to be a convincing ghost, and the only way for me to be a convincing ghost is if you play along, alright?" Nova had no choice but to acquiesce. I could see the cogs in his head turning over themselves as he weighed the futility of argument over the absurdity of wearing a sheet over his head. It brought to mind an old cliché I had heard once about choosing your own battles or something. *** We hid in the bushes across the street from Nova’s house for a good while just watching for any sign of movement around or within the house. The coast seemed clear, but there was much talk about binoculars as we waited. We were still disguised as ghosts, and so far, nobody seemed to notice us. Still, neither of us was able to muster up the courage necessary to come out from behind the bushes. It had been an hour or so, and I was getting tired of crouching. "Do you think it’s safe to go in yet?" I asked Nova, who seemed like he wasn’t as tired of crouching as I was. The son of a bitch was showing off his crouching skills; flaunting them at me. He could crouch all day if he wanted to, and I felt his crouching display was growing a bit inconsiderate. "Not yet," Nova said, still not convinced that it was safe to cross the street. "Maybe we should throw a rock at the house to see if anyone comes out." It was a good idea, so I went along with it, and the two of us searched the dirt under the bush to gather sizeable enough rocks. We amassed a small collection, and Nova tossed a pebble over the bushes. It barely cleared the street, only flying half the distance it needed to fly to hit the house. Nova tried again, but he only threw the rock a bit further. "You throw like a girl, you noodle armed sissy!" After I shouted out at Nova, he glared at me while putting a finger against his lips, alerting me to keep my voice down. I grabbed a rock and carefully weighted it in my palm. As I was calculating for wind and other variables against accuracy, I was interrupted by a voice that stealthily cut behind me. "Agent Dash, FBI. Which one of you is Garbage Bag Johnny?" Nova and I both turned in our ghost disguises, me with the rock still in hand. The FBI agent was young and his sharp suit seemed like it hung off of him rather than fit on him tightly and imposingly. He was a bit smaller than both Nova and me, and he wore a pair of black rimmed glasses. I could tell he was anticipating an easier apprehension than he had in store. Usually, I would have been cooperative, but under the guise of the sheet, I was feeling more than a bit ghoulish. Ghosts, you see, have a disdain for humanity, and I was starting to feel that disdain along with a spectral playfulness. I felt like trickery and disturbance. The dead have a small bit of influence over the living world. It’s nothing major. It’s just enough to slowly make a man go crazy. I had some antics up my sleeve to do just that to this Agent Dash fellow. "He is!" Nova and I both said, pointing at each other with our hands poking through creases in our sheets that hung to the wrist. Agent Dash carefully examined the situation, and I could tell from the twists and wrinkles in his face that he was trying to think of the most clever way to handle the situation. The one thing about the feds that I never liked was how they always had to be so clever. I guess that’s why I pegged him with a rock. It was all nothing more than some knee jerk reflex, but I got him good. It hit him right against the temple, and he fell like a brick. "What the hell was that?" Nova asked me underneath his sheet…or was I Nova and did Garbage Bag Johnny ask me? "That, my friend, is how you throw a rock." "I think you just killed an FBI agent, dude. That is not cool!" "Nah, he’s still breathing. He’ll be alright." The second the impending peril sunk in and we truly realized what kinds of deep shit we’d be in for maiming an FBI agent, we forgot all about Julius Jones, and we were sobered from the apprehension to leave the bush and make our move as quickly as possible. "What should we do with this Agent Dash guy? We can’t just leave him here. Someone might find him." "You’re right. I’ll put him in the back of the van and get the engine started. You go in the house and get the wooden box that’s in my closet. It’s buried underneath a pile of dirty laundry." I started towards the house, and Nova grabbed Agent Dash under his arms and tried dragging him across the street; the asphalt was none too kind to Dash’s suit. As I was just about in the door, Nova was lagging behind a bit. "Johnny, help me load the agent into the van. Then I’ll get the box." I ran back and hooked Dash’s legs under my arms, and the two of us carried him to the conversion van and dropped him in the back. We both stared at him for a while, just kind of in a pile there in the back of the van. He’d slide and flop all over the place if we didn’t tie him down. "There should be some bungee cords in the back. See what you can do about tying him up so he doesn’t crash around back there." With that, Nova ran back into the house to search for his box while I did my best to clamp Agent Dash against the wall with an assortment of bungee cords stretching across his body and hooking on whatever I could find. By the time I was out of cords, I was pretty proud of what I had done, and if the faith I had in my design would translate to its applicability, Dash would be strapped in too tightly to even think about bouncing around back there. "I got the box. You ready to go?" "Yeah," I said after Nova had startled me with his sudden reappearance. I had forgotten he was dressed as a ghost. "I got the fed strapped up pretty good back there." "Alright, so where are we going?" "Right now? As far away as possible. How many borders do we have to cross before we’re home free. What’s the law these days? Seven borders? Nine?" "I don’t know. Just get in the van." "Let’s go with nine to be safe." "Alright, get in the van." I got in the passenger’s side and Nova started the large, clunky vehicle. He put the box in my lap and told me to open it, which I did, to happily find Nova’s stash of money and drugs. He had quite a bit of each, enough to last us quite some time. So with an unconscious FBI agent propped up against the wall by a tube full of bungee cords and a box full of illicit drugs and all the necessary tools for abusing them, Nova and I were heading as far away from everything as we could get. I rolled a joint. *** We had been driving for quite some time now and had long ago decided that the ghost disguises were no longer necessary for our survival. The space between things began to grow, and we were long out of the city and its suburbs, and there was half a long stretch of highway until we got to the next city. The inside of the van looked like we were baking clams, which really meant it looked like nothing at all since the smoke was opaque and I couldn’t even see Nova sitting next to me. Luckily, we could still communicate through the van interior shaped smoke insulation. "What are we going to do when the agent wakes up, man?" Nova’s voice sounded louder than I expected it to sound. He seemed so distant in the smoke, but his voice was a reminder that he was inches away from me. The end of the joint was the only light that pierced, very red, through the smoke. The voice, though, squished the sound of rolling highway beneath it. "I don’t know," I admitted. I hadn’t really given much thought to what would happen when the agent woke up. "Maybe we should just off him; dump his body off the side of a bridge." "We’re not offing anyone!" Nova shouted, the van veering in and out of our lane from his channeled anger. "We’re in enough trouble as it is, and I don’t even know what we did to get into that trouble." "Alright, but can we still dump his body off the side of a bridge? I’ve always wanted to do that." "Are you out of your mind? He’s going to be pissed enough as it is when he comes to. He’s going to be pissed that you knocked him unconscious with a rock and then dragged him miles and miles out of the city." "Exactly," I interrupted. "We’re his only way back. He can’t do anything to us, or he’ll be stranded." "That’s not how it works," Nova corrected. "These FBI agents, man, they’re all trained in like twelve degrees of Tae Kwan Do and some of them know sorcery type shit." "Nobody knows any sorcery, Nova." "I wouldn’t be so sure, man. What do you think they do in all those secret government facilities? They have wizard schools and shit. I read it in some book." His logic seemed seamless. "Well, if it was in a book it’s probably true. We’ll just have to play it by ear, and if that doesn’t work…" "We’re not offing anyone or dumping anything off the side of a bridge." "That’s not what I was going to say." "Bullshit it wasn’t!" "I swear I wasn’t going to say that." "Fine, then what were you going to say." I could feel the van swerving again with that channeled rage. "I forgot," I defended, only half-lying. It just so happened that I did forget things rather easily. I’m sure that if I had wanted to forget what I was going to say, I could have done so with relative ease. "Like hell you did," Nova shot back before breathing deeply to calm himself down and coughing because he took a deep breath of smoke. Then he started laughing about it, and I started laughing about it too, and for a while, we forgot we were in such an explosive situation until we heard Agent Dash mumble out some awakened sound. Then we both stopped laughing. All the while, smoke was slowly rolling out the now cracked open windows. "Where the hell am I?" These were the first words to pierce through the smoke from behind us, and Nova and I immediately fell silent at the voice of the FBI agent who was now awake in the back of the van, trying to figure out why he had woken to find himself trapped to a wall behind a slowly clearing smoke. "Hello?" he called out from the back. "Is anyone up there?" Nova and I had no idea how to respond, so we kept quiet for the time being. Lacking the cool composure of an FBI agent, however, Dash kept rambling on in the back. "Excuse me, but I’m tied up and I don’t remember how I got here. If you can hear me, please help!" Nova and I were caught off guard. We communicated in whispers after Nova broke the initial silence between us. "I think he might have amnesia, but then again, it could just be a clever ploy. What do you think, man?" "There’s only one way to find out, man. Is there a bag of ecstasy in your drug box? I think the pills will make our buddy back there a little more willing to spill the beans if in fact there are beans to be spilled." "Drugging an FBI agent- are you sure that’s such a good idea?" "Nova, I’ve never had a better idea in my life." "That’s what I’m afraid of." "You want to roll, too?" I asked Nova, holding up the bag of multicolored pills. To me, a bag of ecstasy can be not all too different than a bag of candy. Before you know it, you’ve eaten all of it. This is why I try to stay away from the stuff completely, but there have still been many times when I found myself inexplicably listening to crappy, pulsating techno beats while surrounded by a ton of Asian kids blotting their mouths on paper towels in between the irksome but somehow mesmerizing twirling of glow sticks, but that’s beside the point. "Don’t you think we should be in a clear state of mind when we interrogate him?" "Yeah, you’re right. I’ll give the agent a pill and then I’ll just pack a bowl. That should get us nice and clear." "Don’t give him the pill yet. He’s not just going to take any pill that any stranger gives to him. We’ll have to stop off at a diner and slip it in his drink. If he’s playing some kind of FBI mind game, we’ll just have to shrug it off until we get to the next off-ramp with a greasy spoon." "Right," I agreed. "But we’re still packing that bowl first, right?" "Oh, of course," Nova quickly clarified. "What fun is there in eating sober?" He was onto something. Without an illegal chemical agent, eating had lost its flavor for me, and I preferred not to do it. If I wasn’t high most of the time, I’d most definitely wither away to a skinniness that would leave me susceptible to windstorms and eventually, unseen cracks in the pavement. We were done whispering, though, and we ended the conversation with a semi-blind high five through the smoke so that our hands almost missed. Still, the sound was enough to perk the ears of Agent Dash, who was still asking for assistance from the back of the van as the smoke became almost thin enough for him to see us. "Hello? Is there somebody up there?" I was getting pretty sick of it. For a man who had gotten maimed out of consciousness by an impressively accurate rock throw, strapped to the wall of a conversion van with a tube of bungee cables, and dragged out on some ride with no destination but away, the guy complained an awful lot. If I woke up in his predicament, I would have thought nothing of it. I probably would have just gone back to sleep. "Just sit tight for a second. We’ll pull off and cut you loose," I informed him, trying to quiet him down a bit as I picked out the seeds and stems from Nova’s baggie and placed them in one of his empty cup holders. *** We had been at the diner for quite a bit already, and all three of us had finished our meals. I had ground and slipped the pills in Agent Dash’s cola- two to be safe- and he didn’t notice anything, but the pills were starting to kick in. He had excused himself to go to the bathroom, and he had been in there for quite some time, which gave Nova and I ample time to discuss strategy. During the meal, nothing much was said out of care for the predicament. It was a small, quiet diner. There were a few regulars speckled at the counter, mostly farmer types who wore denim everything. Families with young children sat in some of the booths, though the majority of the booths were empty. They had too many booths, and I’m sure that there was never a time when all of them were being used. It was kind of relaxing, though, to be in a place with so many booths. It means the owners of the restaurant didn’t care as much about their business model as they did about their customers. It also meant that we were able to find a booth back in the corner where the uncorrupt folks of whatever backwater town wouldn’t be exposed and offended that we had arrived. Nova and I were both still stoned out of our minds, and when you’re as stoned as I was at a restaurant, you tend to pick up on things you would otherwise disregard. For one, the waitress, who was getting older and a slight bit plumper in her waning middle years, had the most charitable demeanor I had ever encountered. Her kindness was unparalleled, and it set Nova and I on edge. People can only act this kind if they know something about you, after all. All these farmers- all of them knew something. People who woke up at sunrise were not to be trusted. I felt the farmers were waiting. They were a patient breed who talked endlessly about their crops and the best way to grow them. The farmers all knew that the best fertilizer was decomposing human corpse, but with murder being illegal, the subject was taboo, so they discussed the next best fertilizer without mention of corpses. Still, there were sometimes murderous opportunities that arose, and nobody asked questions in such cases. The nice waitress would keep her eyes locked on mine or Nova’s when she took our orders, and then she would go back behind the counter and lean in real close to the farmers, and they’d talk and laugh. "So what do you think?" Nova asked me. "Well, the eggs were a bit runny, and the hash browns were overcooked, but the biscuits and sausage gravy more than made up for it." "I mean about the FBI agent. Either he’s a tremendous actor who is immune to drugs or he’s telling the truth about his amnesia." "I think he’s telling the truth, but to be sure, we better feed him a story and keep him strung out on a healthy diet of drugs." "Yeah, maybe you should go check on him now. He could be doing some secret agent shit right now. Either that, or he’s rolling pretty hard in the bathroom." Nova was right. He had been gone a long time. I figured the food might have gone right through him. It was pretty greasy. It was too risky, though, to give him his privacy, although after you hear another man in a public restroom, you can’t really look at him the same way again unless you know each other on a gastrointestinal basis. I pushed the oak door open, luckily it was unlocked- another sign that he honestly had amnesia. If he was conducting FBI business in there, he would have almost certainly locked the door. And if he was conducting other sorts of business, I would have hoped he would have locked the door as well. It was just a small bathroom with a urinal, a toilet, and a sink. Instead, my jaw dropped when I saw Agent Dash shirtless at the soap dispenser, filling his palms with soap before rubbing it all over his bare chest. It was on him in such large gobs that I could still see the pinkish soap hue gelatinously dangling off of him in many places. I simply stared at him, and he grinned widely. The sides of his mouth stretched almost painfully across his face. "I…love soap! It feels so good." I grabbed him by the wrist, feeling that it was all soapy as well and dragged him out of the bathroom. "Look, man. You can’t be walking around in public like this. Let’s get you to the van, get you cleaned off, and put your shirt back on." I cradled his dress shirt and suit jacket under my right arm while guiding Dash with my left hand around his wrist. Before I left, I doubled back to take a wad of paper towels with me. We walked back towards the table and as the farmers noticed us, I could tell they were in shock. They had never seen anything quite this strange, and strangers like this would make for good fertilizer. In their eyes I could see both appalled repulsion and murderous desire. We had to get out of there fast. "Stop touching things!" I commanded the rolling agent. He was feeling the stools at the counter and dipping his fingers in water glasses; basically touching everything within reach. I was mortally frightened that he would start rubbing a farmer, and if there’s one thing I know about farmers, it’s that they take offense to being rubbed by other men. That this particular man was half naked and covered in soap would only exacerbate the offense. I could feel the eyes on my back as I brought Dash back to our table. "Nova, we need to get out of here. We’ve made the farmers angry." Nova looked around, having to make an effort to peel his eyes off the soapy Dash. The farmers in the background came into focus rather quickly for Nova, and he saw they were all staring at us as well, scowling. I didn’t have to tell Nova about what the farmers would do to us if they got us alone out in the middle of a cornfield where there would be no witnesses besides the scarecrows. He could see it in there eyes. And I could see it reflecting in his eyes, imaginatively, as the three of us were all environed by farmers wielding pitchforks. As they stabbed downwardly at us, their faces contorted. Where the skin was once leathery and worn and covered in stubble from farming, it now greased a sleek red. They turned into devils as they stabbed, pointed tails ripping through overalls and goat hooves tearing through shoes. The legs too, tore the pants to shreds, growing in girth with muscle and fur, and with the lower halves exposed, the farming goat devils unfurled their devil goat balls, their crimson penises growing erect from the arousal of murder. "Yeah, you’re right. Let’s go," Nova monotonously advised as he slapped his money on the table, his eyes watching the farmers as he backed out of the restaurant behind me as I led Dash out the door. In the back of the van, I made Dash wipe himself off with the paper towels I took with me. He seemed to enjoy this even more; the way that paper rubbed coarsely against his skin, and I couldn’t bear to watch anymore. Nova hit the gas and peeled out of the small parking lot as I rolled a joint and tried to answer the blabbering FBI agent’s questions about who he was, who we were, and what the hell we were doing. "First off, man, your name is Dash, and as it turns out," I told him, "you’re the third member of our team. We travel around the nation in this van, examining paranormal activity and hopefully putting a stop to it. You took a nasty bump on our last call. I think you got clubbed by an ogre." Nova punched me on the shoulder. "That’s the best explanation you can come up with?" he whispered with a harsh staccato in his tone. I signaled for Nova to relax. "Does any of this ring a bell?" Dash thought for a while, still wiping soap from his body. "It sounds vaguely familiar," he admitted, albeit incorrectly. "But if we’re a team, why’d you have me tied up to the wall?" "Uh, we needed to keep the blood from rushing to your head. I didn’t want your brain to bruise up." "Oh, right." And we were off down the road as the sun began to set. The horizon looked beautiful over the stretches of land, but we knew that waiting there in every field was a farmer full of coffee and bloodlust. "What’s that noise?" Nova asked. "I don’t hear anything." "It sounds like an uneven thumping…ah! There it is! Did you hear it?" "No. It’s probably nothing. You’ve just been driving too long, and it’s getting to your head." "Yeah, you’re right. I’ll pull over once we get somewhere safe; away from all these farmers." Unfortunately, we never got quite that far.
