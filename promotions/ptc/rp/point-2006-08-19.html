Point

19 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-19 Click! Blinding white filled every inch of the horizon, bleeding from a slim and sinuous line into a sucker-punch that served her perfectly in the jaw. "AH!" she exclaimed, turning her face away, closing her eyes to get away from the feeling that her retinas were searing straight through. Her face was twisted into a grimace unbecoming her lovely features, animal reflex overcoming the need for composure. It had not felt like the sun; it was too artificial, too white. This light was not nurturing warmth, it was something entirely more monolithic and without personality, without knowledge that it beamed itself onto a sentient being who writhed under its harsh gaze. She groped for the nightstand to turn it off, but found instead the unforgiving wooden railing of a chair. She was about to ask whoever had turned it on to do her the courtesy of switching it off again, but breath caught in her throat when the echoing tap of dress shoes on cold linoleum gave the lie to her delusion that she was in an unfamiliar hotel room somewhere in the Pacific Northwest. "Are the theatrics really necessary?" The voice was even more alien than the surroundings, like nothing she had ever heard before, coming to her out of a nightmare. She was, she noted a moment later, probably just overreacting to the two stimuli, following one another like a one-two to the gut--it was no more abnormal than any other voice, after all. The crisp diction and cold, businesslike tone were just what she would have expected out of any associates, or enemies for that matter. "…Theatrics?" she returned in a half-gasp a moment later, "Who's acting? Those lights are fucking bright." A muffled murmur from somewhere in front of her caught her attention, but it was wordless and formless, a single aural gesture of disapproval. "I see. Well, do you think you could perhaps open your eyes?" She peeled one eye open, then the other. The light still glowered at her in all its omnipresent indifference, bringing tears to the rims of her eyes. It was not the order she had received that drove her to keep them open, but the curiosity as to whether or not she had somehow curiously stumbled into some modern Spanish Inquisition. "Is that adequate?" she retorted snippily, beginning to feel like a sullen teenager. "Quite. At the very least, Your Honor, we can get this underway now without having to watch the defendant's histrionics." Reality shifted into a farce for a moment, as the impossibility of the situation descended from on high like a god finally deigning to speak his truth to the insects crawling below on the planet's face. Defendant?! The word tasted acrid and strange as she tried to spit it out in the form of a question. It would not come. No answers came from her surroundings--obscured first by the great white halo of light whose source seemed only inches from her face, the only concrete perceptions came in the form of a multitude of hazy outlines, between which no distinctions could be made. "You can start whenever you're ready, Counselor." This was a different voice, coming from above her somewhere. She strained to look, but could only turn her head so far before it became painful to try to see. And still, there was the light, making the whole scene a surrealist fantasy. "Honorable ladies and gentlemen of the jury, today I'm here to tell you about a defendant who just… doesn't know when enough is enough. About a woman who would hold on to her own delusions no matter how much hurt that would bring to others, even others she claims to care about." There came a brief pause, in which hard-heeled dress shoes beat a marked staccato against the floor. "You're about to become acquainted with someone who, to put it simply, has absolutely no grip on reality. Someone who has, for far too long, gone on doing whatever she wants to, irregardless of who was put in harm's way by the doing of it. Someone who must be brought to justice in front of all of you because finally, it is time for such a thing." There was another pause. She picked up the sound of a glass grating across a wood table, followed by a singular swallowing sound that made her nauseous as it replayed itself in her mind. "There are many, many, many examples of what I've laid out before you just now, but in my opinion, the best example comes from simply letting the defendant herself talk. You'll hear this story straight from the horse's mouth, and I don't think it takes much for any reasonable person to see my point. Without further ado, Your Honor, I will begin my questioning of Ms. Vlaszy." "Proceed," came the reply, which sounded gruff, and slightly impatient. "Ms. Vlaszy," came the Counselor's voice, a little closer to her now, "tell us where you come from." "Zdice, Czech Republic," she answered, fairly automatically, after having been asked the question a thousand times by wrestling reporters. "And could you tell us a little bit about your hometown?" "Small," she spat tersely, "… a fairly rural population, as it has always been. Its value comes mostly from its historical significance, as it houses an extremely old monastery at the edge of the town." "A monastery, say? What year was it built?" She shrugged her shoulders, discomfort beginning to steal into her limbs. "Around 600, I suppose. I wasn't keeping track." "And what year were you born?" His line of questioning was beginning to make her nervous. The answer to that question was one that she could not give honestly--and yet she was bound to give it, by the simple fact that she was apparently under oath right now. Her silence was shattering. "Please answer the Counselor's question, Ms. Vlaszy," came the judge's voice from above her, reminding her painfully of the stories of Christ sitting in judgment with his father come the day of reckoning. "It's alright, your honor… If Ms. Vlaszy can't remember her own birthday, I can help jog her memory," the Counselor said, and she could tell thereafter that he had turned to her by the shift in volume of his voice, "Ms. Vlaszy, the biography on your employer's site simply lists your birthday as January 18… but after doing a little digging through the pro-wrestling news media, I've come up with this article, which appeared in the May 2005 issue of the Cincy Wrestling Observer in which you state that your birthday is in fact January 18, 783. Do you deny that?" The same tenseness that always rocketed through her in situations like these caused her jaw to clench, nearly right down on her tongue. "I do not deny making that statement," she said, her voice tight and drawn. How else could she have answered? "Do you deny its veracity then?" "No." A shocked noise went through the room. She couldn't tell how many observers such a noise could have come from, but in her already agitated state of mind, it sounded like hundreds. "That would make you what, about 1200 years old? You don't look a day over 25." She snarled, and she knew it would be visible to all who cared to see it beneath those hellish lights. Yet cornered, how was she to react, what was she to say? There was no way out of this question. "Well?" the Counselor pressed, his faceless voice suddenly sounding like that of a cat with a goldfish in its mouth. She bit her lip, turned her head away from the light. "This is one of the most dangerous features of her psychosis—as you can see, when confronted with evidence to the contrary, she can’t handle the contradictions in her own nature. And let’s be honest, what that nature is… is obviously up for debate. No one in their right mind could honestly act the way that Ms. Vlaszy does and yet maintain such an insistent claim that she is completely stable. It is an inherent contradiction to think that way, two polar opposite behaviors that cannot exist outside of a very, very sick mind." "Bullshit," she growled, the word slipping out unbidden. "Language," came the voice from above her, "Ms. Vlaszy, we are attempting to keep some form of decorum in this courtroom, and I would appreciate it if you could observe those rules yourself." "Well," came the response to her outburst, sounding exactly like the words smarmy prick, "Let’s take this another direction then. If your mental health is not an illusion, Ms. Vlaszy, then why do you continue to pretend to be something you very obviously are not?" "Why do you assume that anything out of your narrow little paradigm of what’s ‘right’ is pretending?" A condescending chuckle. "Why should it even be necessary to bring some sort of wounded sense of individualism into it? At the barest levels, the notion of pretending to be a fantastical creature, but refusing to admit that you are not one is considered unhealthy, dipping into schizophrenic mental territory. If you were as ‘well’ as you claim you are, then you would have no qualms admitting that you’re pretending… that you could stop whenever you wanted to." "If you knew as much about me as you seem to think you do, you would realize that portraying a so-called character is part of my job. You are aware of that, I’m sure." "Portraying a character is one thing, Ms. Vlaszy, but don’t you think you take it a bit too far?" She couldn’t answer that either. It was all another trap that wound down to opposing themes—one the right answer, the other wrong, but which one was which depended on whether you were an unwitting participant in this circus, or its director. "And since you bring up the business aspect of your ‘so-called character’ as you call it, now would be an apt time to get into that, I think. Ms. Vlaszy, while under contract to Global Championship Wrestling, you are expected to stay ‘in-character’ while at each arena, and at any company-sponsored events, correct?" "If you must put it so crudely, yes," she responded, pushing back with her heels against the front of the box that enclosed her chair. The chair did not move, the effect of her efforts being only so much more wasted energy. "Then why is it that when in public, you behave similarly? Why do you extend the charade as far as you do, when it is not necessary as per the contract that you have signed?" "Because one could, at any time, run up on a fan, Counselor, that is one reason for it." A clipped laugh bulleted through the darkness and grazed her chest. "You act as though your success depends on whether or not you act out this character at every waking moment. If I may, I’d like to bring in some comments about just that… these are anonymously polled PTC wrestling fans, mind you, so these are the people Ms. Vlaszy is concerned about impressing. For example, one young man stated ‘I’m tired of seeing these goth chicks… they’re all the same. What is this, a year-round Ozzfest? Can nobody come up with anything better?’" "Basing a statement like the one you’re making off of one person’s opinion is ridi—" "Ms. Vlaszy, you’ll have your turn!" "This is not just a single example in a sea of positive responses to Ms. Vlaszy, Your Honor. In fact, I have another statement here, which is an example I have pulled from many which may be accessed via PTC’s website. This fan states that Ms. Vlaszy is ‘a fake-ass Hot Topic goth… the only people who could stomach that act must be likewise.’ As you can see, it’s not—" "It’s not fair to stand out there and crucify me based on personal choice! I could research and selectively pull quotations that say everyone who wears a suit is an asshole. I could bring up the thousands of lawyers-go-to-hell jokes, and that would prove about as much as that shit that you’re flinging at me right now does!" The murmur in the courtroom roiled into a din, the crowd incensed by her boisterous response as she leveled a finger at her still-unseen accuser. Bang! Bang! Bang! "That is QUITE enough. Counselor, I have been exceedingly patient with your line of questioning, but at this point, you are devolving into pointless ad hominem arguments that are wasting both your time and the court’s. I suggest you find a different line of questioning to prove your point. As for you, Ms. Vlaszy…" The voice had turned downwards. "This is the last time I will warn you about your behavior. One more outburst that is an aside from this series of questions, and I will hold you in contempt." The rebuke burned ember-bright inside her stomach, searing through the lining and outward into her limbs. The scorn in the air was palpable, these people looking down on her from on high and spitting on what they saw. "Now, this next set of questions… I’m going to indulge in the idea that, perhaps, our defendant really is a vampire, as she claims to be. If that were the case, Ms. Vlaszy, then what would be the reason for a vampire to deign to enter the mortal world of wrestling?" Through slitted eyes and gritted teeth, she slid out her answer like a defiantly uneaten prisoner’s meal. "The same reasons anyone else would. Perhaps it’s a sport I like, and enjoy competing in. Perhaps I have a personal vendetta to work out. Maybe I’ve just got a temper, and enjoy kicking the hell out of someone in a setting where I get paid for it." "But," countered the Counselor, taking that superior tone of voice once again, "that doesn’t in the least answer the question I am posing. Vampires, in general, are superior in strength, agility, and speed than mortals are, are they not?" "According to traditional legend, yes." "If that is the case, and they could easily beat any mortal who stepped into the ring with them, why would they want to wrestle? Where would be the challenge, or relish? Or are you implying that a higher race would really take a route that involved complacency and being satisfied with maintaining a bare minimum of success over opponents that are like sleeping fox-cubs to a hawk?" "In some cases, perhaps they would." "But why? Why, Ms. Vlaszy, would it be necessary? Don’t vampires have better things to do?" Her knuckles bleached whiter on the rail of the box. She couldn’t see the hue-change for herself, but could feel the blood running out of the skin, and the concurrent cryptlike coolness creeping into her fingertips. "Do you consider, perhaps, that the life an immortal might lead is a lonely one? If it’s really so difficult to understand, consider being forever an outsider on the fringes of a society that you once inhabited. Consider being ejected by a choice that may or may not have been your own, and told that you have to always be away from the ones you care about and grow close to. Consider repeating that pattern, ad nauseum, for centuries." "What would be the point? Why not just live in solitude if it’s that painful?" "Have you ever stared at a car wreck?" "So… what you’re saying is that this is basically a dressed-up version of an animal playing with its food?" A low chuckle filtered through the court in response to the question, stifling any response she might have had to its idiocy, idiocy that seemed to be incontrovertible, worst of all. "I’m sorry, I just find it hard to settle on an idea like that as truth. You offer reasons for it, but they’re all reasons that hinge on buying a myth, or reading one too many Anne Rice books. You are aware of that, aren’t you?" "There are more things on earth than are dreamt of in your philosophy," she said, defiantly biting the words off into the shadowed darkness. "Pithy Shakespeare quotes don’t prove points, Ms. Vlaszy. Even if they did, there’s still another issue…" There was a lengthy pause, and the sound of shuffling papers. It was as though he had saved his best point for last, and was giving enough spare time for the jury to hang on the drama of the moment, enough time for Lia to acutely feel the sword of Damocles hanging over her head. "Ms. Vlaszy… if you are indeed a vampire, then can you explain to me why opponents over the course of your career have been able to beat you? How can you explain the fact that you are claiming to be a being with incredible supernatural powers, yet lowly earthbound mortals can overtake you in the ring?" "What does that prove, my fallibility? I can make mistakes, does admitting that automatically invalidate everything else that I say?" "It invalidates the claim that you are a supernatural being with preternatural powers, yes. It gives the lie to the claims that you are still willing to stand up and make in front of this court without any other proof other than what you say." She fell silent. Like the festering dawn of the apocalypse, it became apparent that there was no escaping this. Cornered, she was turned into a farce. "Your Honor, ladies and gentlemen of the jury… my line of questioning today has proved one thing—it’s harmful to allow delusions like this to continue without any sort of consequence. As you have seen, the defendant had to be restrained in her chair by most ridiculous means because otherwise, she might have posed a danger to the people in this courtroom." Those dress shoes tapped impeccably against the floor again, the approaching footsteps of Mephistopheles offering a deal to the zealots hungry for witch-flesh. "This is all very necessary, you see, lest our defendant think she has permission to leap at the throats of common people in the street to suck their blood. And as laughable as it may be, one only has to look to New Orleans, for example, to see an instance of so-called ‘vampirism’ getting out of hand enough to cause murders. This case, of course, is not so extreme—or it hasn’t yet been so. But at any point in time, this mindset can erupt into dangerous psychosis." There was another pause, as water wet those serpentine slimed-lips. That clear angel’s voice with its slithering nephilim-like undertones continued. "It isn’t just the defendant endangering herself or becoming a public nuisance, either, but simply that her behavior is counter-productive. Her justification for it, that it is what her employers wish her to do, is proven false by the text of her contract with them, which we have already discussed. Further, it is counter-productive as the fans that she claims to be protecting by her actions are at best indifferent, and at worst unimpressed by her behavior. Consider this last quote…" The throat cleared. It should have been rubbed ragged by the acid spewing forth from the vocal chords buried beneath the skin, but mercilessly, it pounded on, driven by a sinister force hiding behind a mask of benignity. "Still from the PTC website—and I must note that this quote is edited slightly due to its language. ‘This Dies Irae ‘expletive-deleted’ is wack. Nobody wants to see an ‘expletive-deleted’ Lovecraft story in the middle of a wrestling ring. Shadow worst of all. If she’d just give it up, GCW would be better off. When DI comes on, I change the channel.’" The knife sank further, twisting through sinew and bone. "When it comes to the bottom line, the sentiment of most involved with the PTC community is simply that Ms. Vlaszy is playing a dangerous game, one that brings no benefit to her, nor to the community as a whole. Encouraged with promoting wrestling talent as they are, what does it do to their credibility to promote a group full of supernatural creatures, and in particular the supposedly all-powerful vampire who leads them? Yet, strangely enough, how far has it gotten her? She clings stubbornly to an idea that the community as a whole plainly rejects, and because of it, not only is her own progress retarded, but the whole community suffers. How concerned could PTC truly be about realism if it allowed a fairytale to come to prominence within its fold? What would it offer, aside from ridicule and disinterest from the fans that support the organization?" The slight noises around the courtroom seemed to be noises of agreement. That was perhaps the most damning fact of all. "Counselor, you’ve gotten us to an excellent stopping point for the day… In the interest of keeping the proceedings from running too late this evening, I’m going to request a recess until tomorrow afternoon, one o’clock. Do you have any further points?" The noise that echoed within the fortress walls of her psyche was a thousand cobras all at once, a noise that made her want to clap her hands over her ears. And within, the octava bassa of a voice that was at once familiar and alarming. "No, Your Honor," came the voice, borne over windswept deserts from a thousand years past, "I have nothing further." Bang! Bang! Bang! "We stand adjourned until tomorrow, one PM." There was the demon-like screech of chairs pushed back across the protesting floor. Bodies exited the room through the portal of light that gleamed at the end of the long aisle leading to the door. As soon as it had come, the light was gone, and her solitude in the room mocked her isolation. No explanation sufficed, for even an explanation would have been a cold and empty shell, as were all words of those who were damned from the moment of their birth. She felt gutted, alone and hung on a hook of her own making, the inanimate object of ire in someone’s imagined village square. But the pyre was all too real, as were the thirty pieces of silver jingling in some non-descript pocket somewhere outside the hellish realm that bound her hand and foot.
