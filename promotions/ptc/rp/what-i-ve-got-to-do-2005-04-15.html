What I've got to do

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 You know, I've seen all the rumors. The predictions, the smark-sheets and all that I'm the underdog and I'm okay with it. Really... At least that's what I try to tell myself, especially when I start to feel guilty about not training "properly" for what's coming. There's definately a flare that goes off somewhere in my mind every time I hear about my predicted early exit from this tourney. I know that it's more an indictment of my lack of market exposure than my wrestling ability; but regardless of how I look at it logically, it still burns. I guess that's really why I'm in this. There's still that drive there to make something of this "career" I've gotten myself into. I also know that basically this is "it" for me, whatever that is. I don't know how to explain it. I guess some would call it "do or die" time, but I find that phrase to be grossly inappropriate (I find a lot of things inappropriate, which is really strange considering my demographic). Maybe "now or never" is a better cliche. My point is, I'm ready to make a stand. I don't think that I'm out of my head on this one. I'm a good enough guy; maybe not as interesting as that guy in the bar of the bar chain smoking camel straights and tossing back shots of rye, but definately more interesting than anyone who ever brought a sixer of Zima to your party. I know I can do my thing in the ring, the trick is getting you to give a damn about the guy doing it. In all honesty, everyone's got a story worth telling if you only take the time so listen. Yes, even you. Maybe you can tell it to me sometime, but I'm on the clock here so hopefully you won't mind if I go first. First there was a really big bang....... Ok, I'll skip a bit. I was born, so I can't complain too much. I have a close knit and loving family (which is really odd for a wrestler I guess) who've always been there for me. A brother, a sister, mom, dad, grandparents, a cat, a dog, and 2000 acres of family farm. (Yes, that's like Connecticut but with a fence around it) I guess that's the beginning of where your and my "ordinary" lives differ. It was a lot of "up with the sun" and hard work, but a lot of freedom and open space to mess about in. Lots of building tree houses and catching fish eventually gave way to dirtbikes and fireworks. Times were good, weather was bad, the 90's sucked and dad ended up selling about 800 acres just to keep us out of bankruptcy. I never really understood how hard it was on him till years later. How could I have been so naive? I got through school with a B average that I barely tried for and a lot of nagging about something called "potential"; but I was happy with chasing girls and pitching for the baseball team. I was pretty good, could get it up around 85 or so, and that was going to be my ticket. Baseball scholarship to a local D-III school. Get a degree, get signed to the Cubs and live the high life. And then everything changed. It did for a lot of people. In New York & Pennsylvania & D.C. I know that most people wouldn't do what I did. Hell, most would call me an idiot; but I went and chucked it all for Army green. It's not something I'm going to stand before you and try to justify it any further than saying I had to do what I had to do. Maybe you would have too, if you'd had to look into the eyes of my father or my grandfather. Grandpa was in an Artillery Battalion during the Korean War, and dad was an ammo troop/bomb loader during Vietnam. They weren't heroes or anything like that. They were happy just to do their part, and I could tell that each of them wished like hell that they were in my shoes, so they could do it again. I can't tell you what it is that made them feel that way, but I knew it was a part of me too. There was an emotion as strong as anything I've ever felt. It's hard to describe. It wasn't like hatred or vengence or anything like that. More like a sense that something had to be done, and that I'd be damned if someone else was going to put their neck on the line without me doing my fair share. I didn't get through training in time to be in the first push. Went out 6 months later for a tour, mostly counter-insurgency ops and some raids in the mountains. I'm not going to BS you. I was scared. Not of dying, not really. It's OK if you get yourself killed. You just tried as hard as you could not to screw up and get one of your squad dead. That was what scared me. That I'd screw up and someone else would pay for it. That's the hell that lives inside you. Not fear for yourself, but for the man next to you. There's a part of me that was almost happy when I got hit. I mean, it was aweful and horrible and a hundred other words to describe pain; but it happened to me. As sick and wrong as it sounds, thank god it happened to me and not one of the other guys. Thank god. It was a long road back to something resembling normal after that. I know I got lost a couple of times between rehab and separation and coming home. About half of it's a blur, but somehow I've made it this far. My opportunity is right in front of me, I just know it. I've got that feeling. It's not quite the same, but maybe it's as close as I'll ever get to it again. I've got to do what I've got to do.
