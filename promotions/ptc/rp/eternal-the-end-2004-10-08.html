Eternal: The End

8 October 2004

Date: 2004-10-08 Note: I’ve cut my chapter system, but this is still a continuation of the series that I’ve started since Round 1. Just a note to readers and judges. Eternal: The End <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Larry would have wished that I’d lost. Held his breath in anticipation of news of my defeat. Thinking to himself that just maybe, I would go down in the second round to someone like Tony Stecchino. With his hands clasped together bonded by beads of sweat, he awaited. Listening intently to the broadcast of the match, trying to pick up signs that it was to be my end. Sadly, he was disappointed. Sadly for him that is, that I walked out basking in my glory, knowing fully well of the outcome of the match even before the first bell had sounded. But alas, damn the word ‘hope’. For Larry, he clung onto that word with his life, hoping that I would fall within the constraints of my own splendor. It was to be his fate that he should be disheartened. But I hold no remorse for him. For in this game, there will be only two outcomes. It is always about being victorious over the person standing opposite you, doing whatever is necessary to ensure triumph. Against all hopes, we all know that it was never to be my end. Along with his hopes, it ran alongside my destiny. Conquest was never meant as a gift for the weak, pinning their lives on the very word of hope, wishing fervently that lady luck would continue smiling on them, pulling them closer to what they dream would be their turn of grandeur. The innuendo that such an event would come to reflect reality, clearly is mockingly amusing. What? Oh, I didn’t know that. I had no idea that Larry the Legionnaire was supposed to be an amusing character, trying to instill laughter in his fans to further him along his career. That’s a weird coincidence, isn’t it? Then again, maybe not. I’ve always found something odd with all his talk regarding his “Legionnaires”, at least now you’ve proven my instincts right. You know, frankly I don’t even care. He can lead his band of “Legionnaires”, or smurfs, or even Devilles to try and amuse the audience. At the end of the day, we all know what the outcome is going to be. It will be a defeat for all to see. That no matter what game plan you come up with, I will pull up something better. That however you wish to try and defeat me with your band of amusing friends, I’ll just make you relinquish your hopes of conquest. Quite simply, there will be no end for me. However this time around, things will be played slightly different. Instead of sitting back, enjoy the ride, and then defeat you – I felt that it was getting a little bit too dreary. So you ask what it is I am going to do? Don’t worry; I will still defeat you. That is already an unquestionable outcome. The only difference will be the manner of your defeat. I’ve grown weary of placing everything on the line to the risks of opinions. This time, I will just quite simply destroy you. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Present Day/Time It seemed like the perfect setting. With the sun burning a deep orange behind the altitude of the hills, exuding a mystic glow that seemed to illuminate the surroundings in every shade of the spectrum, the small brick house lay quietly at the foot of the smallest hill. Unnoticed by most, it was far from spectacular – deeply stained white walls covered with dust, and cracked concrete floors plastered the whole surface area of the residence. To most, it would seem that that place of dwelling had never existed, being such a minute detail in a vast piece of unoccupied land in the depths of Belfast, Northern Ireland. The last time someone owned that place; it belonged to a young lady by the name of Christine Milner. Rumors had it that her husband disappeared into the night on the evening of the birth of her second child – a girl. That happened generations ago, long forgotten by even those who visited her from time to time, including her family and friends. You see, her marriage wasn’t blessed by her parents, who insisted that the man that she married held some form of black magic that caused him not to age. In the early stages, deep inside she hesitantly agreed. Until she found out that he was an immortal. While she aged, he never did. He was eternal, just like how his children would one day grow up to be. However, she never told either child of their gift, or some would say curse, not even when Krystal disappeared on the night of her eighth birthday. Kasper was the first to know the truth when he was ten, although at that young age it was understandable that he wasn’t quite able to comprehend the magnitude of it all. She never knew what happened after that, for that night was to be her final in this world. But neither did Kasper. For years after that, his memory failed to exist. He couldn’t remember his past, but only for the various pieces of memories that appear in his delusional mind from time to time. It was like as if he was dead. So you ask how exactly were all these facts known? Simple, it wasn’t. This was how Kasper believed in his own fragile state of mind of how everything actually happened in the past. But for a fact, no one knew for sure. It was like a fairytale, but without a perfect ending. However, some may argue that the end hasn’t arrived yet. “Why did you bring me here?” Kasper questioned in a soft tone, looking back at his sister, Krystal. She stood behind him, with Athena by her side, both with a grim look on their faces. Krystal crossed her arms across her black jacket, and stared back at him. “This was where everything began,” she replied, a slight hesitation in her voice. “I know this place, I’ve been here before in my dreams.” He whispered back, a touch of caution audible. There was a rough edge to it, as if unsure. “It was our home,” Krystal replied, her soft tone sending chills down his spine. She turned and looked across to Athena, and nodded her head slightly. Kasper noticed the gesture, which added to the confusion that had been heaping on his mind for the past few days, ever since Krystal broke the news that he had been the one who took Athena away from him all those years ago back in Athens. He turned back with an exasperated look on his face, an expression of clear tension. “What has this got to do with what happened back in Athens?” he spoke again, evidently uncomfortable to be in the presence of his former home. Yet he couldn’t understand why. “This place has everything to do with Athens. This is where our lives evolved and separated in two different directions.” Krystal declared, her voice wavering slightly with a tinge of sadness in her eyes. “This has nothing to do with Athens,” Kasper said spat out the words angrily, while waving his hand at Krystal in a violent manner. And then he subsided, surprised at his show of anger for bringing him back to the place where he saw his mother dying in her bed. “I saw mother dying in her bed, while you disappeared a couple of years before that.” He muttered, hanging his head. “That was when she told me to seek you in Athens, to find a sister whom I was trying to forget,” he continued with a bleak look on his face, growing paler every second, “And that was where I found Athena, crying in the streets, and took her in.” Kasper looked up with a desperate expression on his face. He covered his face with his hands, his silver rings gleaming in the sunlight. “I really don’t see where you come into the picture. I’ve never met you until about a week or two ago, when you pointed a gun at me.” He glanced at Krystal wryly, that piece of memory still clear in her mind. “Is that how you think it all actually happened back in Athens?” Krystal questioned, placing a lingering doubt in the air. She looked at Athena again, whom had tears welled up in her eyes. “Tell me, what exactly happened?” he replied with dread in his voice, a part of him not really wanting to hear what Krystal had to say. He shoved his hands into the pockets of his black jeans, as the wind caused his white hair to cover part of his face. “You had a bullet pierced through your body, Kasper. That’s what happened.” Krystal muttered softly, looking at him straight in his glistening blue eyes. Kasper took a step back as light finally dawned on him and a sudden influx of memories flooded his head, his eyes widening. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Location: Athens, Greece Timeline: About ten years ago Wrestling was his life. Well, for the most part of it anyway. Christopher Alexandria lived and breathed wrestling. That was the main reason his wife left him years ago, leaving her daughter as well. But he didn’t care. He was happy living his life with Athena, training young ones who had dreams of becoming professional wrestlers. At forty-five years of age, he was still as strong and sturdy as ever, always with a smile on his face. And today was just like any other day. With people walking in and out of the gym, and him showing them moves in the ring located in the middle of the room. Knock. The door rapped loudly from the corner of the room, causing Christopher to turn his head in that direction. “Ouch!” he shouted, wincing slightly as he caught a blow from a wrestler practicing moves in the ring. The young man quickly apologized, but Chris knew that it wasn’t the boy’s fault; he was the one who was distracted by the knock. He wasn’t expecting any visitors today to visit the gym, so he was particularly curious as to who was behind that door. Athena wasn’t due to arrive at the gym until hours after. Thoughts were running through his head as he exited the ring, and made his way to the old wooden door. “Hi.” The young man outside stammered, shifting his feet slightly. His crystal blue eyes glimmered in the light, catching Chris by surprise. “What brings you here?” he questioned. “Well, I read the sign outside your door and it said that you are allowing people to stay in your gym as a form of temporary accommodation.” He spoke in a hopeful voice, a smile playing on his lips. “Only if you want to be trained in wrestling,” Chris replied suspiciously. “Sure, why not. My name’s Kasper.” The young man spoke with a smile, while putting his hand out as a sign of respect. Two weeks later “I need a break,” Kasper muttered, heaving a huge sigh and collapsed on the mat. He didn’t expect wrestling to be this rigorous; all he wanted was a temporary place to live in while he continued his search for his sister. Chris threw a towel down on Kasper’s sweaty body, and walked out of the ring. He smiled at his daughter Athena, who was watching them since just now, and went to get himself a drink. Kasper turned his head and smiled at Athena, while trying to pull himself up. He wiped himself clean, and rolled out of the ring. “Let’s head to the jetty. I need some fresh air to get all this wrestling out of my system.” Athena nodded her head in approval, and smiled back. “So how’s everything going for you so far?” Athena asked him, trying to create a conversation while they were walking along the pathway to the jetty. Kasper was taken aback by her question but managed to compose himself. “It’s been alright, I just wish I can take a break from wrestling all the time, and try to get some free time to roam around the city.” “I’ll talk to dad for you,” she beamed, her face lighting up. Kasper grinned at her, and shrugged his shoulders. “Thanks,” he whispered back. Approaching footsteps then caught his attention and he turned around, only to find a young blonde girl with her crystal blue eyes looking directly at him. She seemed distinctly familiar, but he couldn’t remember where he had seen her before. The girl looked away and murmured, “Mr. Alexandria is looking for you back in the gym.” She turned and walked away, stealing a glance at Athena who nodded her head back at her. Kasper rolled his eyes, dreading another wrestling session. “I’ll be back later,” he said, trying to force a smile. Kasper grumbled on his way to the gym, cursing for the simple fact that he chose this place as his temporary living quarters. He was supposed to search for his sister, but instead he had to waste time day in and day out wrestling in that stupid ring in the gym. He sighed to himself, not able to believe his luck. The old wooden door creaked open as he pushed himself against it, not even bothering to use his hands. As soon as he stood by the doorway, he saw a strange man leaning against one of the walls in the corner of the room, dressed completely in black. Strangely enough, he had crystal blue eyes as well, along with light blonde hair. The man raised a brow when he saw him, and Kasper noticed him clenching his fists. Kasper ignored the man’s behavior, and noticed that the girl who had approached him just now was standing beside him, her head down. He looked around, and walked over to where Christopher Alexandria was standing, situated in the other corner of the gym. Somehow he felt relieved that he didn’t have to walk to the corner where the strange man was standing. He pushed his thoughts out of his head when he saw Christopher’s grim face, wondering what exactly was the problem. Chris always had a smile on his face, even if it was a bad day in training. He knew him well enough from the two weeks that he had spent there in the gym. “Why did you call me?” Kasper questioned with a smile, trying to liven up the atmosphere. He failed badly, as Chris didn’t even show a sign of emotional expression. He was cold, utterly ice cold. “I want you to stay away from my daughter, Athena.” He spoke grimly, his voice echoing through the walls of the now-empty gym. That was like a shot to Kasper’s head, he didn’t understand the sudden piece of news. “B..bb..But why?” he stammered out the words. Chris ignored his question completely. “And I want you to leave this place now. Right away. I don’t ever want to see you in this gym ever again.” His cold voice stung Kasper, but his blood turned cold when Chris produced a handgun and pointed it straight at his head. “Please leave. Now.” He whispered in a stern voice, motioning Kasper to the door. Chris stole a glance at the man standing in the far corner of the room, and nodded his head slightly. Kasper anticipated this, and took opportunity of the moment, and jumped on Christopher. Taking hold of his hand that was holding the gun, the two men struggled on the floor for a couple of seconds before a loud noise boomed around the gym. And then there was silence as Kasper heaved himself up, and stood there over Christopher with blood staining his face and clothes. He looked down and stared at Chris’ broken body, with thick red liquid pouring profusely from his chest. “No!” The blonde girl cried from the other corner of the room, as the man started running towards where Kasper was standing, with clear anger in his eyes. Kasper didn’t wait for the man to catch up to him, as he bolted out of the gym door. With his footsteps bouncing off the gravel, he sped in the direction of the jetty. It was only a couple of minutes, but at the end of it all Kasper had grabbed Athena and pushed her into the boat that belonged to the gym much to her confusion. Especially with blood all over his shirt, she couldn’t think of what to make out of it all. “What happened?” she managed to blurt out the words as Kasper frantically started the engine. Kasper couldn’t think of anything to say. “A man appeared, I think he is somehow related to that girl who called me to the gym just now.” He spoke in a rushed tone, not looking at Athena, who had a sense of recognition in her eyes. “He shot your father,” Kasper continued, looking at Athena’s reaction but for a split second. Athena tried to rush out of the boat as soon as she heard that, but Kasper grabbed her and pushed her back in. “He’s dead,” he screamed at her, trying to get that thought into her head. He steered the boat out to the sea, directing it to ride across to the nearest island. “Look!” Athena screamed in front of Kasper, looking over his shoulder. He turned back and saw the man waving a gun at the jetty, but too far away for him to swim and catch up to the boat. Kasper’s eyes widened as the man slowly pointed the gun in his direction. Kasper turned away, as a loud noise erupted through the air. Athena screamed, as Kasper collapsed to the floor. With her hand on the steering wheel, she started crying, fear in her eyes as she stared back at the man as Kasper lay there on the ground staining the whole deck red with his blood. <<<<<<<<>>>>>>>> Present Day/Time “You were there, weren’t you?” Kasper questioned his sister as he looked up, his face strained. Krystal kept quiet and looked down, as Kasper continued on. “You were that girl who called me out to go back to the gym.” She kept quiet, as a tear flowed down her face. “Who was that man that was with you?” Kasper questioned in a low but harsh voice, as his face tightened. But somehow deep inside he already knew who the man was. And that surprisingly filled him with dread. A part of him didn’t want Krystal to tell him who the man was, and not wanting her to mention his name. Krystal kept up her silence, her blue eyes meeting his. She just nodded her head in despair.
