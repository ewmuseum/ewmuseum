Act II: Music Starts, Faces Veiled

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 Act II: Music Starts, Faces Veiled “Evil does not exist. The theories of good and evil are a malicious lie fabricated to give men comfort, a false picture of what they could never grasp, a seat for their vanity, self-righteousness and natural tendencies toward judgment and persecution of their own. The truths we seek terrify them, and for that we are labeled evil men. And why? Because our methods often entail pain, misdirection, deceit? Because our doctrine is taken from the work of men that were considered mad and treacherous? Cast from your minds the feeble dogma of humanity, for it will be their hypocrisy that dooms them all as we bask in the light of revelation and immortality. We shall end them, and we do it for their own good. In a sense, we’re martyrs, the collective scapegoat for a world too afraid to look in the mirror and truly peer into their own souls. The discipleship embodies their innermost frailties. The difference? We embrace our nature, whereas the heretic dogs would as soon die than admit what they are. We are the damned, and we do so willingly. In the damnation of our own comes our salvation.” --Bruce Shanahan, sermon dated 3/18/2009 The frescoes and sculptures lining the interior of his host’s mansion always caused Bruce Shanahan to marvel. Most men saw only suffering, blasphemy and horror in their subject matter; in the eyes of the common beholder, they depicted only the darkest machinations of human creativity and design. Terrible demons of the underworld in their most morbid construct, the graphic portrayal of war, the aftermath of epidemic, the ruins of cities shredded by bombardment. The mastermind of The Sect of Black Wisdom appreciated their beauty and truth. It was a shame he was so singular in recognizing that voice. As he approached the main parlor for his audience, he caught the muted tones of a symphony. Moving down the hall, footsteps ringing in the broad atrium of the ancient home, he silently reveled in the music serenading his march while perusing the ghastly artwork. Few had ever partaken in the macabre gallery, for the owner allowed few guests. It took a moment for the other to recognize his presence. An ornate wine glass in hand, filled with the dark red of some memorable vintage, his German host reclined with eyes closed amidst an immaculately decorated den. Shanahan managed to pass halfway into the room before he saw a smile twitch into being on the other’s face. “Amadeus was a tortured soul. His brilliance was underscored with a pain that we often overlook. Such a pity that men ignore the inward darkness that blights us. I find that we learn so much more of ourselves if we search the dark corners of our history and minds.” “You preach to the choir, old friend. Men are fragile and ignorant creatures. Wiping them out is the best thing for them, really. How have you been, Brother Kinzler?” The other abruptly sat up, something malicious briefly flashing in his eye. Beyond that, his expression was blank and remotely pallid, like a man who’d heard the name of the long forgotten dead. Shanahan grinned, chuckling as he fished papers from his jacket. “My apologies, Brother Krauser. In my defense, I’ve been assailed by many things as of late and these things slip my mind.” Of course it was a lie. He simply found himself amused by the other’s refusal to answer to his given name. The German found it tainted after centuries of the weakness and degeneration sown by his predecessors. To him, Oliver Kinzler was the ghost that haunted his maturation as an occultist, and only Wilhelm Von Krauser was capable of meeting the ends he endeavored to reach. The greatest irony was that Krauser was truly insane, a schizophrenic that longed for a past age where his noble bloodline existed as more than a footnote in local history. He resented the commoners of the world, the uncultured oafs and cretins that they were, and few could appreciate the beautiful nuances of the world that he could. Shanahan, a man he considered enlightened in the arts that few could ever grasp the most minute concepts of, was among them. And it afforded the American, and his organization, the resources of a wealthy madman. Krauser smiled, lowering the volume of the music. He appeared to have forgotten the perceived offense. “My sources indicate that the Blackhelm Coalition is mobilizing, Brother Shanahan. They appear to take offense at your endeavors to end their world.” The other only grunted. Bruce Shanahan felt legitimately offended that others of his kind would be so shortsighted and seek his destruction, regardless of how long he‘d put up with it now. “And I hear you’ve come to employ the services of the Crying Seraph?” He chuckled, an undertone of pity in his voice. He sipped from the intricately molded glass, eyes intent on his voiceless guest, studying him. Shanahan felt more like a lab experiment than a houseguest in that uncomfortable moment. “Tsk tsk. They say I‘m mad, and even I would hesitate to hire those maniacs into carrying out my bidding. Luther Ridley bows to no man, perhaps to no god. Some claim his shadowy little brigade are demons he personally summoned into this world, and that his soul was the price for his avarice. The ringmaster of Hell‘s circus.” “You know Hell doesn’t exist, Wilhelm.” “Of course not. Only an expression passed on by his past employers. None of his targets have survived to take up that saying.” “They’re the best. The murder of Xavier Cross was a thing of beauty. I believe you would have truly appreciated their art.” “Life is a delicate and fleeting thing. I believe there’s a certain magic in the moment it’s extinguished. But enough on your mercenaries, old friend. To what specifically do I owe your visit?” He passed the documents to his benefactor. The German perused its contents and reacted with the detachment that the Sect priest knew so well when business was presented. “Intriguing. It appears war cannot be prevented.” “It can,” Shanahan replied. “But only if we abandon our plans, and our cause. I won’t balk at their threats. Besides…” He paused, pouring himself a drink from the wine bottle. “…you know we’re far too powerful to fear anything right now. It would take the winged armies of God to sweep down on us to strike even a tinge of doubt into our hearts.” Krauser sat back, musing as he analyzed the documents. Even if he trusted the other implicitly, even he knew the world would ooze with blood by the time the conflict was over. “The Yakuza want your hide. Blackhelm, Wilmarth, and all of their allies are marshaled and prepared to die for this paltry illusion they call a world. You may have outdone yourself this time.” “Shall I consider that a compliment?” “You’ve enticed half of the world’s zealots into taking up arms, perhaps with a vigor we haven’t seen since the Crusades. I’d say that registers as a compliment, Brother Shanahan.” “Do I have your support?” “Give the word, and I’ll personally raze the Vatican to the ground.” “Excellent. For now, I have other pressing business to attend to. Forgive the limited window of negotiation, but I didn’t feel I’d need long with one as convicted as yourself. Shall we speak at greater lengths this evening?” “Of course. Dinner, to discuss the minor details.” With that, the prelude was at an end. With nary another word exchanged between them, Shanahan swept from them to leave Krauser to pore further over the letters. As Mozart penetrated the silence once again, Krauser lay back and smiled. Atop a desk only inches from his handrest, a mask lay. The mask he has donned for years in his public appearances, a metallic cannibal mask. He sighed, already thinking about the wonders soon to come. It had been too long since he’d played the game. “A new masquerade. Glorious.” * * * * “I want this one done by the book, people. No mistakes.” Stryker clicked off his headset, nodding in the direction of his partner. Russell Slade was a mammoth of a man, as was the heavy weapon he carried, but his smile was almost gentle as he read their surroundings. The country evening was chill and quiet, a chorus of crickets broken sporadically by the call of an owl. Leaves rustled beside him and a shape crouched by his ear. Even without the perfume she wore outside of duty, he recognized the nubile outline of Vanessa Wilder. There were special forces scouts lacking the talent of his prized team member. “What have we got, Vanessa?” “Intelligence got it right. They’re in there. But don’t ask me for exact numbers because I can’t provide them. There are men on the second floor and men on the first floor. Armed.” Stryker shook his head. Something didn’t add up. “He doesn’t arm that mob of his. You rarely see them carrying more than a pocketknife.” “Things are changing, major,” Slade rumbled. “They know as well as we do what’s happening right now. Maybe he’s taking extra precautions?” “Bruce is shrewd. I don’t want to gamble. No funny business, make this one clean and fast. I want to know why they’re here.” New Jersey had seen very little in the way of the Sect’s sinister designs. But reports had a branch of the cult basing itself in a cabin on the outskirts of the Pine Barrens. Local churches had been destroyed, men of the cloth murdered as the fled their burning sanctuaries. And the nightly chants and bonfires had spooked the locals, some even now whispering about dark, ancient things unleashed from God knows where that stalked the woods. Thankfully, they had yet to materialize as Stryker’s team watched the dimly illuminated cabin from the trees. “Willis?” “Go ahead, Marcus.” “What do you see?” The sniper paused on the other end. His vantage point afforded him a perspective over the cabin and the compound around it, negating any advantage the others may have in surprise. “Two on the top floor. Movements downstairs, but unclear on number. Looks like three, maybe four.” “Alright. Willis, wait for my signal and take the firsts hot the moment it’s available. Let’s go.” The rest of the team began to move at the other end of the compound, moving between shadows before veritable pouncing on the cabin with surgical precision. A distant shot rang out, punching through the second floor window. The hulking slade kicked open the side door and scrambled inside as the dismayed cultists nearly staggered in shock. The interior was lit up by gunfire, the thundering rumble of semiautomatic weapons sending tumbling shadows in a cascade across the peeling walls. Bodies collapsed in a heap, Shanahan’s minions permanently taken out of his wild schemes. The leader was poised in the center of the room, not having moved even as the giant mowed down his kin, as if inviting his apprehension. His face was veiled by a mask, blue with red crosses. “Reveal yourself,” Marcus barked, weapon leveled at the unknown cell leader. “Gladly.” The mask came off, falling almost clumsily from his fingertips. A face that Stryker had seen only fleetingly in person appeared, its features now skeletal and ghostlike. A caricature of the man he had been in his past. “Ah, Marcus Stryker. I’ve heard tale of your recent exploits. It’s been a long time.” “Rourke? Jesus…” Doctor Rourke, ex-director of Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane that had disappeared three years ago, stood before him. His health seemingly had taken a bad turn and the man looked like a corpse in comparison to how he had been running the asylum years prior, but there was no mistake it was him. “What the fuck is this?…” Rourke smiled. Painted on such gaunt and sallow of a visage, it was truly a terrifying expression. “I’m so glad you could make it. We were told you’d be coming.” He eyes darted over Marcus’s shoulder. Stryker pivoted, and at his side Slade cursed. Where the other had come from was a mystery. Judging by the military garb, he had an inkling about the intruder’s identity. And given his abrupt appearance, he began to wonder how much truth there was to the stories. “My name is Luther Ridley. I fear we’ve never been acquainted.” “Yeah. The psychotic captain. I’ve read your history, bud. I think you’d be proud of the bio we’ve managed to put together on you since you came under Bruce’s hire.” Ridley smiled, a finger running the length of his weapon barrel. “I’m told Blackhelm spares nothing in its reports. I’m actually flattered that the Vatican’s personal killers have me on their radar.” Stryker growled at the comparison. Like Maury Dawson, he had no love for the Catholic kings. He was an instrument of a greater good than the politicians in Rome. “So the stories…are they true, Captain Ridley?” “That I’m a shepard for some flock of Hell’s exiles? I wouldn’t go that far. My men are human, as mortal as you and me. They don’t sprout wings and sail over the battlefield, they don’t hurl hellfire in combat, and they bleed the same blood as all men. They’re simply…superior at what they do.” “Why would you want to stop such a glorious return, Marcus?” asked the wraithlike Rourke, almost imploringly. He had been brainwashed, another cog in the machine that was The Sect of Black Wisdom. “When the Old Ones come, everything will begin anew. A dawn of discovery, wisdom and power. The stoic Ridley seemed completely uncaring in regards to the raving lunatic who had been behind a mask only moments ago. His focus was completely on the major. “My understanding is that this has been coming for a long time, major. Shanahan went on about omens, visions, and all that other nonsense. I shut it out after a while. I’m only a soldier. And while I admit to holding bias, my greatest prerogative is dollar. And my employer has that in spades.” “The Crying Seraph killed Xavier Cross, a man who worked for us. And we know you’ve assassinated other members of our organization, and our brother and sister organizations. By the power vested in me as a ranking agent of the Blackhelm Coalition, I hereby place you under arrest.” Ridley chuckled, tugging on his black gloves. His eyes locked with Stryker as Slade swung his colossal weapon toward the window, where shapes began to slink about in the shadows. Death was encroaching, and no member of the Crying Seraph had any intention of leaving under Blackhelm authority. “Aren’t you going to read me my rights, Major Stryker?” Luther seemed genuinely amused at the prospect of the other attempting to place him into custody as his own stable of killers prowled the darkness outside. A scoff was his reply. “No. The other part was only a formality anyhow. We all know you have no intention of going anywhere.” “Oh but I do. I intend on leaving here with your head, and delivering it to my employer.” “I like my head where it is.” His brow furrowed as more movement outside caught his attention, and abruptly he heard the shattering of glass upstairs. Only a split second later, a shotgun blast. Millen’s close encounter special was introduced to the fray, and Marcus only prayed the rookie could handle himself. They had their own dilemma to attend to as a wave of shadow-guised murderers surrounded the remote cabin, with no reinforcements for hundreds of miles. “I do hope you like to dance, ‘major‘,” said Ridley with a sneer, caressing the grip of his sidearm. “Because you and I are going to waltz straight into the depths of Hell.” “The music hasn’t started yet, ‘captain‘,” snarled the other. “And you and your witty little jests are coming with me.” A shrill scream from the basement. It was Vanessa. Ridley shrugged. “Ah, and there it is. I hope you can keep up.” Rourke had long swept from view, his disappearance having eluded Stryker as the confrontation with Luther Ridley was drawn out. Cursing, he motioned to Slade, and the big man nodded earnestly in reply. He needed to be found. “Time to die.” * * * * He didn’t know where he was, or even why he was here. But upon agreeing to meet with the council behind the Coalition, Maury Dawson had left fate in the hands of the God he trusted. Blindfolded, he was taken an undisclosed location, and there he had waited. It seemed like hours in that cold darkness before the blindfold was lifted. And there he sat, in a concrete chamber, surrounded by the veiled faces of his organization’s masters. Cloaked completely in hooded gray robes, like the members of some esoteric society, they peered at him as though studying a test subject. When the time came, they spoke in turn, but in a cryptic monotone almost as though voiced of one entity in myriad places at one time. “Agent Dawson, we’re taking you to see the heart of our operation. Consider yourself honored. Few men have ever been allowed access to the menagerie.” “It is the center of all of our knowledge. There will see things you would otherwise think impossible. But what you’ll find is a place where the truth of the universe finds its way in, and offers us a look into the forbidden vistas beyond man. God’s gifts to us, and perhaps the key to preventing Bruce Shanahan from releasing the apocalypse.” “You have been chosen to lead the war against our most powerful and ambitious enemy. Are you ready, Maury Dawson? Can your mind handle the secrets we wish to show you? And will you deploy what you see for the protection of His creation?” There was no other answer to give. He had taken on the mantle and was determined to see it through. And part of that responsibility was peering into the areas of the universe that he had long feared, that he had once wanted to maintain ignorance to. “I’m ready.”
