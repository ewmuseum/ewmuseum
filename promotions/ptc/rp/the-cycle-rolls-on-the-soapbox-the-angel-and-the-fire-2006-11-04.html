The Cycle Rolls On: The Soapbox, The Angel, and The Fire.

4 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-04 Part II: Furthering The Cause of Ego "We’re back with professional wrestler, and impromptu cultural commentator, Damien Cruz." Damien sat back in his seat, ready to take the interview where he wanted to go. He was tired of talking shop, and was happy to be given a chance to step out of himself for a moment and pretend to someone important. Gratefully, his host was very open to the idea. "Before the break, you commented on where you believe the American culture has gone. Now I want to ask you a more personal question. Who shaped your culture? Who influenced you growing up?" "Being a skinny kid in a dusty Mexican town, it was a very close-knit community, so our mentors and our role models were the people we saw everyday. Teachers, clergy, our parents, the baker, the butcher, and those men and women who woke up early every morning and went to bed late at night just trying to keep food on the table and a sense of pride in the household. Honestly, it wasn’t until I was about 16 or 17 that it occurred to me that there were people larger than Cielo Azul. When I left my very sheltered life, I literally traveled to the other side of the world, and took it all in with one motion. I bought a plane ticket that Tuesday, a book about Japan on Wednesday, a laptop that Friday, and a book of Western philosophy for the plane ride. In true Damien Cruz form, I did not dip a toe into my awakening; I dove in head-first." "So what have you learned?" "Everything. Literally everything. I learned how very small this world truly is. I learned that there is a fine line between Us and Them and You and Me and Here and There. I had the Brechtian experience as I traveled. Here I was, a naïve little brown hick who had never learned the art of cynicism or egocentrism looking out onto a world full of both. But I learned. I grew and I adjusted, and I made the adaptations needed to stay fed, and stay focused. I think a large part of why I allowed wrestling to inhabit such a large part of life comes from the fact that it was my universal language. It seemed that no matter what nation I was in, what language they were speaking, or what cultural nuances I had to acclimate to, wrestling become my key to being a part of it. From Tokyo to Toledo, I was able to make sense of the world within the four corners, and that was a sense of security that I embraced whole-heartedly. It also gave me an access to the individual communities and an excuse to be the fly on the wall. Most time, I was treated like the side show spectacle; meanwhile, I was staring right back at the spectators." "And what did you see?" "I saw something primitive, but altogether familiar. I like to use the analogy of the developing human fetus. When you take that fetus at its earliest stage, it’s a single-celled organism. Simpler than a paramecium by all accounts. And slowly it divides, and develops, and changes, and grows until it slightly resembles a near-microscopic plankton, then it looks almost amphibian, then a reptile, a bird, a rat, a pig, an ape, and then a human. Now I’m taking some poetic and biological liberties, but for the purpose of my analogy, it’s forgivable. I saw the same level of atavistic primordialism in the emotions of these people who were very different from myself, and very different from the other audiences. It truly is incredible to see how raw human curiosity, wonder, anguish, and enthusiasm is so familiar even if you see it in peoples that are sworn enemies in the political, or cultural stage. There is a fraternity that I know I am not the first to point out. I mean, we’ve all read the Eckman and Friesman study about the universality of facial expressions. There was also Izard, Maslow, Rogers, even Darwin noted the trend; so I’m not a pioneer or anything. But what I’m referring to is that epiphany I had in my personal views that helped me to see that emotion is as central to our humanity as our physiology. I saw it first-hand and it opened my eyes to the hypocrisy and irony of these arbitrary divides we create among ourselves. I guess if I’ve learned nothing else, it was that our roots are all tapping into the same soil at the end of the day." "I hate to sound like a nay-sayer, but that type of hippie rhetoric is hard to take seriously. Let alone from a guy who, if my notes are correct, participated in some of the most violent matches in your sport. How do you marry those two halves of yourself? Can you?" "Yeah, I do everyday. What I want to make clear is that I am no different than a lot of people out there. I harbor no messiah fantasies nor claim enlightenment. I am not the Buddha. I am simply a human that holds certain truths to be sacred and act in a way that is almost contradictory to those truths. But in reality, the interconnectedness I se does not dull the edge of my sense of competition and dedication. On a very deep level, I respect each and every opponent I face. But on the pragmatic, tactile level, I am in that ring to win matches however I feel I must. I won’t bore you with my take on Bushido, or the Warrior’s Code of Ethics, but I feel that defeating my opponent, and smearing his blood across the ring; if I need to do so to win, is not a betrayal of that high regard I hold for them. I understand--" " Have you ever faced an opponent you’ve genuinely disliked?" "Many times. Many, many times. Don’t misunderstand me; I am not a Samurai or a wandering warrior monk. I am prone to the same visceral emotions that any one else is. There still is a long chasm between my Utopian ideal of who I think I should be; how I think I should behave; and what I should be doing, and who I am today. I’m twenty-six years old, so I still have a lot of really fu-uh-screwed up aspects of myself that I have to sort through." "You almost slipped there", The Host pointed out, smiling. "Exactly my point, Hugh", Damien returned. "Do you think of yourself as a role model? It’s a typical question asked of celebrities, but I am often surprised my the answers I get." "Well, like I said earlier, my idea of a role model is based on the role models I had as a child; so it may be different than the idea that you and your audience may hold. However, I will say that I do try hard to set an example for how I think people should be outside of what I do in the ring. I feel that I have given back to my community, and that I did so because of the loyalty I felt toward Cielo Azul. So if I can inspire loyalty and generosity in someone else, than I am proud. Beyond that, I just try not to pretend that I can live without consequences. I am successful, and I am blessed to be where I am in my life, but I am still susceptible to the slings and arrows that anyone else is. And at the same time, I don’t dress up what I do in my career as anything but what it is. I am a wrestler. I inflict pain, I endure pain, and my goal is to inflict enough pain as to incapacitate my opponent before he beats me to it. Really, there is no poetry that can make a silk purse from a sow’s ear. I am a lucky dude just trying to not embarrass himself too much." "After these messages, I’ll continue my interview with Damien Cruz. Stay tuned." The director gave the sign for 30 minutes, and Hugh Morton flipped through his notes, smiling at Damien. "I have to admit that I wasn’t expecting this; but I’m grateful for the great answers you’re given. Now is there anything you’d like me to lead with to let you finish up strong?" Damien finished his coffee, and when the intern came to refill it, he asked for water instead. "No, I’m okay. Just ask what you think your audience would to know." The host agreed, and when the show returned from the commercial break, Hugh Morton fell back into character. "I am here with FUSE Superstar, Damien Cruz, and we’ve been talking about almost every subject available. Now I will open the phone lines, and let the viewers have a chance to pick his brain a bit. We’ll start with Paul from Augusta. Paul?" "Hello? This is Paul. I wanted to ask Damien a question." "Sure, Paul, what’s up?" Damien answered, eagerly. "Hey, Damien, I’m a big fan. So what do you think your chances are of taking home the big cookie at GTT?" Damien briefly explained to Hugh Morton what GTT was, and then answered the question. "You know, Paul, I don’t think any one ever enters a tournament like GTT without having a strong belief in their abilities. No one will ever answer that they feel they’ll have a strong showing, but ultimately sputter out by the second or third round. If I didn’t feel like I was at the top of my game, in great physical and mental shape, and hungry to take home all the chips, I wouldn’t bother to show up. If I’m being overly optimistic, so be it. But my blind optimism is making me sharp, it’s making me confident, and it’s making it very hard for me to hold back. So, Paul, if I don’t get the Big Cookie, I’m going to die trying. Anyway, to answer your question; I think my chances are great of taking it all. I honestly respect every single name on the roster list, but can’t pick a one that I think wants it more or is willing to go as far as I am to get it." The Host goes to the next caller, "Martin from Orlando, you’re on with Damien Cruz." "Mr. Cruz, I’m a father of two boys, and I have a bit of a bone to pick with you. I want to know why it is that you’ve made a career out of doing what you do, knowing that there are impressionable young kids out there that don’t know it’s all a game, and don’t know that you’re just an actor. The other day, I found my oldest son standing on top of his bed, about to jump onto his brother. I know where he saw it. I want to know how you live with yourself knowing that hundreds of kids are killed and injured every year copying what you do and get paid millions for. And the way you promote violence…you should be ashamed." Damien Cruz, not sure how to respond, shrugged. "I am not responsible for raising your kids, Martin. I have no duty to protect your children from imitating what they see on TV. My job is to entertain the fans, not offer moral lessons. Now if you don’t want your kids watching wrestling turn off the TV and read to them, play with them, or teach them how to put a worm on a hook. If you don’t want them jumping off the couch and imitating whoever it is that you think they are imitating, I’d suggest you supervise them and give them alternative ways to spend that energy. Frankly, Marty, I think you need to be a parent to them, and not my parent. I hate to break it to you, but if they’re not copying a wrestler, they’ll ape a race car driver, or a fireman, or a rapper, or a boxer, or a Power Ranger, or Bruce Lee, or Johnny Knoxville, or Tony Hawk, or a ballerina…they will copy and mimic whoever it is that is filling the void that you’ve left for them. So if you don’t want me to be the most influential figure in their lives, why not offer up some competition. I can guarantee that your kids would rather follow in your foot steps than in mine if given half a chance." "Don’t tell me how to raise my kids." "Then don’t tell me how to earn my living. Deal?" Hugh saw no easy reslution, so he thanked the caller and hung up. "One more?" Damien jokingly rolled up his sleeves and prepared himself. "Pete from Kansas City, go ahead." "Hey, Damien, I saw you in Seattle a few years back. Great show. So I wanted to know if you are going to be in any movies or anything like that. I see John Cena and like The Rock, and them doing it all the time." "You know what, I respect the hell out of guys who have learned to parlay their success in one medium into another. I think that they are doing what works for them, and that’s great. But I just don’t have that type of drive or talent, if I can be honest. I am a wrestler, and not much else. At best, I am a okay dancer. But that’s where it ends for me. So I don’t think I’m going to cut a demo or write a screenplay or anything." The host offered a follow-up question. "Have you thought about life after the ring?" "I made a promise to myself that I will leave the ring at 30, or when I have my first child. So if I leave, for either reason, I think I may start a new career as a talent agent, or maybe a lawyer for some of the young guys who are really being sold out. I saw what this business can do to a kid who doesn’t know better, and I’d like to maybe help those guys out. But that is still a few years off, so I give it only passing thought." "I’ll say this; I’m glad you stopped by and am eager to do this again. Thank You, Mr. Cruz" "Thank You." With that, Damien concluded his interview, and paid his dues to the Media Gods. Hugh Morton offered him a cordial handshake and an empty promise about doing it again, and Damien left the studio and re-entered real life. Part II: The Little Things She lay back against him, and the warm water covered them both. Damien wrapped his arms across her chest, and playfully squeezed a small fold of skin along her sides. "I love this…right here…this is why I love you." Sylvia swatted his hand away, whining that she was going to get back to the gym as soon as they were fully moved in. "No, no, I wasn’t being sarcastic. I like my women with a little bit of plumpness." "I am not ‘plump’, Damien. It’s just my hibernation weight. As soon as the holidays are over, I’m getting right back on the wagon." Damien kissed the top of her head softly, and promised her that he was just teasing her, and that she had nothing at all to worry about. She was amazingly beautiful, and he had nothing to complain about. "Okay, but what if I put on fifty pounds? Would you still be saying that?" "I wouldn’t care. I’d live you no matter what. But since I know you, and know how you are, I’d imagine you would be miserable. So for that, I’d be concerned. But for all I care, you can gain as much weight as you want. Besides, you’re a Latina. And I am a Latin man. Let us not forget that." Sylvia was Puerto Rican and Irish. Damien was Puerto Rican-Mexican. Part and parcel to that fact was their love of good food; among other passions. "I might hold you to that. I won’t tell you, I’ll jus start pigging out and see how full of crap you are." "Go ahead." "I will." "Good." "Dare me?" "What? Are you ten? Do it." "Then I will." "Good." Sylvia turned her head and kissed Damien on the chin, then settled back into his arms. "Nah, I’m not going to do it. We barely fit two of us in the tub as it is." "Well, the fact that I’m six-two doesn’t help." "I think that’s sexy." Damien laughed, "You’re too easily impressed." Sylvia nodded sarcastically, and rested her head against his shoulder, closing her eyes and enjoying a rare moment of closeness. "I saw you on TV last night." "On Hugh Morton? Yeah, I was making an ass of myself." "No, I thought you were good. He sucks as an interviewer, but you did good." "I did okay." "You make me mad, Damien." He chuckled, "Again?" "Yeah. You’re so smart, so charismatic. You have a good head on your shoulders and yet you are out there banging it against a wall every night. I don’t get you." Damien hugged her, and let the statement stay unanswered. Sylvia said nothing more about it. She simply drew a handful of warm water over her fiancée’s exposed leg, and changed the subject. "So I think I might get the job down at Concordia General. They were really interested in me, but were not sure they could meet my salary. I told them that I would be willing to take the job for what they were offering so long as they could get me a good benefits package. So I should here from them by Monday if I got the position." "Isn’t there a Nursing shortage in this country? Why are they being so selective?" "It’s not that easy, Baby. In this day and age, it’s a business. Maybe they are hoping I’ll get impatient and come down a little more before they offer it to me. You know how that works." "You’re a good nurse, Sylvia. You shouldn’t sell yourself short." Sylvia turned on the hot water with her toe, and Damien laughed. "I’m getting chilly." Damien pulled her into himself tighter, and she was grateful. "I like the place, and with the money you’re making, I feel like I’m free to go for the jobs I want as opposed to pulling double shifts in a shithole just because the rent is due that week. Thank you for that, by the way." "I love you, Sylvia. I hope I’ve made that very clear to you." "Well, you’ve had me nervous a couple of times, but I think I am sure of it now." "Really? So there were moments of doubt? That makes me ashamed." He was only half-joking. There really was a part of him that felt embarrassed by how long it took him to make that room in his life for her. The desire was always there, but the logistics weren’t. For a time, his priorities were directed at offering her what he thought she wanted, not necessarily what she was actually asking for. Damien had taken a nasty fall at a local bingo hall event. He was carried into the ER of St. Vincent’s with a shattered patella. It was Sylvia who made sure that he was given a private room. It was Sylvia who sat with Damien and sorted out the billing. And when Damien began to heal, and his rehab began to look more optimistic, it was Sylvia that Damien sought to help him celebrate. She, perhaps eager for a change in her life, or charmed by his almost child-like affection toward her, allowed him into her world, and they feel rapidly in love. As his injuries healed, Damien began to think about the ring again. His mission went from learning how to walk again to getting back into a ring. And so, when he was able to, he sent his tapes out, he performed where ever they let him, and he began the road back to where he left off. He did it for them. Even when he was gone for weeks at a time, he did it for her. When Damien was young, and as he made his way through his day-to-day life, he understood success in a relationship as a measure of what can be offered. Not entirely in a materialistic way, but in an archaic version of being the one who offered a stable home, a full plate, and an environment where the bills were paid, there were two cars in the garage, and they could live like newlyweds for years. In Damien’s view, arguments over how the lights were to be paid for were more damaging than arguments over a wandering eye or a lost of romance. Nothing kills a romantic evening like the thought of the past due water bill that is hanging on the fridge. To nip it in the bud before it became an issue was what made Damien go out into the circuits, and get signed to a new company as soon as his injuries allowed him to. At the same time, Sylvia was growing lonelier. She was finding herself sacrificing more of herself. And in the end, she gave him back his engagement ring, and leaving the fairy tale behind. But that was ancient history in that moment. A lot of those old wounds had healed. Not forgotten, but healed neatly. That was where Damien wanted to be. He wanted to be in his new home, with his fiancée, naked and distracted from that plane ticket that sat in his coat pocket; a plane ticket back to GTT, and back to a world that she had uneasily accepted as necessary. "I’m just teasing you, Baby", she assured him, then kissed his forearm where it lay across her shoulder. "If you say so…" Sylvia closed her eyes again, and allowed the hot water to warm her. "I promise." The two sat in the tub until the sun went down, alternating between moments of quiet appreciation of their physical intimacy and talking about nothing in particular, but enjoying every word of it. The bath was followed by a nice dinner, and then they drifted off to sleep on the couch, watching re-runs on Nick at Night. It was boring, it was predictable, and it was far from the Rock Star life of Pam and Tommy. It was everything Damien Cruz loved, and nothing that The Latin Assassin represented. It was perfect. Part IV: Coda Damien Cruz sat in the locker room, willing his muscles to life, and cycling through a thousand and one scenarios that could arise during his match. He thought of every angle, every hard surface, every inch of that ring, and the ways in which he could exploit them. Damien Cruz pictured his opponent, and the many dangers that Nova brought to the fight. He thought of the way Nova moved, the way Nova walked, the way Nova fought. He tried, as best as he could, to think about the way Nova thought, the way Nova planned, and the weaknesses that Nova tried so hard to conceal. Like feeling his way through the dark, Damien Cruz gripped any bit of information he could observe, remember, or invent, and used it to guide him. Damien Cruz, with his eyes squeezed shut and his heart racing in his chest, prepare for his match as he had prepared for the thousands in his past. And when he opened them, The Latin Assassin was ready. His breathing deepened, his muscles relaxed, and there was the sensation of liquid warmth throughout his body. It was a moment of clarity that he relished and greedily embraced. The Latin Assassin pulled tight the last bit of tape across his fists, and waited. He waited, and meditated, and cycled through a thousand more scenarios, and counted a hundred more ways to use the ring, and imagined a dozen more vulnerabilities to target, and allowed his thoughts to ignite a fire in his temples. And when the moment arrived, and he was called to his task, he could see nothing else. It all came together.
