The Sun is Burning: Part II

Date: 2006-02-20 The sun is burning in the sky Strands of clouds go slowly drifting by In the park the lazy breeze Are joinin’ in the flowers, among the trees And the sun burns in the sky ~<>~<>~<>~ "Cynthia," Rollins stood staring at the woman, his lips twisting a bitter halo around his teeth. "This isn’t funny." "Do I look like a fucking comedian?" Her eyes became moist as she shook her head ever so slowly. "I missed my period, Rich. I’m moody and irritable and find myself craving unmixed concentrated orange juice." Rollins snorted, deflecting what appeared to be an obvious bid for attention with dry humor. "I thought those were signs you were on your period." She didn’t laugh. Prying herself off of Rollins’ bed, Cynthia began caressing her stomach as she stood in front of a cracked floor-to-ceiling mirror settled against the steel support column running perpendicular to the far wall. Gleaming green eyes glistened with uncertainty, blinking rapidly in trepidation; her pupils shrank to the point where the irises all but disappeared, her corneas swelling with anger and abandonment as she stood swathed in humiliating fear. "Did you see a doctor?" Rollins pried, even now refusing to bend over backwards for her amusement. He still assumed it was a ploy of some kind, a trick to keep him from leaving her. She shook her head, smudging a tear from the corner of her eye. "No." "Did you try a pregnancy test?" He stood now, drawing away from his desk and his own autographed picture he had been mulling over all evening. "Have you confirmed any of this with anybody of authority?" Suddenly she spun on him, lashing out in a raspy tone that literally stopped Rollins in his tracks. "That’s what I thought I was doing with you, Richard! A woman knows, damnit! A woman knows when a life has begun to grow inside of her, and if nothing else she knows damn well when her monthly visitor misses his flight. I’m pregnant, Rich." Inhaling a breath deep enough to rob the ozone layer of its oxygen supply, Cynthia didn’t stop. "What am I to you, some sort of fuck toy to use and abuse like a life-sized Barbie doll? Are you my anatomically correct Ken? Do we pretend like this is an honest relationship in the bedroom, but when it comes time to be responsible and loyal and own up for our actions, we run away like it’s fucking preschool and I gave you cooties?" "Did I say anything about leaving you?" Rollins had in fact been getting ready to end the affair before something like this reared its ugly head, but Cynthia didn’t know that. "Jesus fucking Christ, Cynthia! Now? You wait until now to spring this bullshit on me, and you expect-…" "Oh, so it’s all bullshit to you," she hissed with a smack of her lips, throwing her arms in the air. The Renegade had come close enough to smell the sandalwood and caramel of her Thierry Mugler Angel, a posh perfume he had bought for her on his last trip through Paris. He could hear the jangle of the diamond bangles he brought back from Amsterdam. Her lips sparkled with raspberry Machu-Picchu. Ears glittered with twenty-four caret studs. Legs and arms moisturized with Kiehl’s crème de corps. A Barbie doll, indeed. "You’re married, Cynthia." Slamming his palm against the steel column, Rollins towered over her like a monolith of unshakable stone. "How the hell am I supposed to believe the kid is mine? For all I know you got to drinkin’ one night with that couch doctor you call a husband, and boom, the baby batter flowed more freely than the breeze between your legs. Or you’re lying." Her face froze like a fawn staring down a train. "Fuck you," she whispered. Reaching into the ribbed pocket of her designer Dior denims, Cynthia produced a small rounded piece of white plastic. The only thing that struck the ground harder were her tears as she tossed the object on the floor, about-faced, and calmly walked out of the room. Trying not to scream himself hoarse, Rich Rollins bent down to retrieve the tiny pregnancy test. Math was never his strongest subject, but he knew enough as he looked at the indicator to know that a pink plus sign equaled big trouble. ~<>~<>~<>~ In the lair of a troubled mind… It was officially midnight in the garden of good and evil, but in Frank Jacob’s flower patch, the edges of morality blurred to the point where he just didn’t care anymore. His garage was starting to reek of spoiled cheese. It nauseated him to the point where he had begun wearing a paper air filter over his nose and mouth whenever he was working on the van, spraying on the decals to his new death machine with all the care and precision of a neurosurgeon. Not even the metallic tang of silver spray paint could mask that awful acrid stench; the van had become a big seeping wound made of rubber and steel, courtesy of its macabre cargo. Frank was slowly learning that compared to a rotting corpse, year-old fruit smelled like yellow roses. It was definitely not the garden Frank had envisioned for himself, but vengeance was hardly the most aseptic of obsessions. The shriveled remains of Margaret Rollins lay in a heap under a thin blue tarp within the van. Frank had been rushed for time the night he had exhumed the body, as it had been virtually impossible to take the casket with the corpse. A little innovation and a titanium stomach were all it took to get the remains in his van, and before the caretaker had time to notice the gate had been jimmied, Frank and his deceased victim had disappeared into the night. It didn’t even make the nightly newscast. As far as Frank figured, who the fuck really cared about the decomposed remains of an old whore? Other than her son Rich Rollins, of course. Pulling away from the side of the van, Frank pulled the mask off of his ears as he stood back to appreciate his handiwork. A smile crept across his bushy and unkempt face as the new logo beamed back, the gray and blue letters outlined against the black body of the ‘96 Chevy Astro. It was yet another message directed at the Renegade, one more point Frank wanted to drive into his heart before he killed the man who killed his daughter. Named after the man who could have given Rich Rollins a run for his money but instead ran for the hills, the newly-christened "Paddy Wagon" looked ready to purr even with the keys in Frank’s pocket. This time, Frank mused, Rich Rollins’ good luck is about to bite him in the ass. The unshakable stench finally forced Frank to don his mask once again as he tossed the spray paint aside, looking for something to eat. All this morbid scheming was giving him quite an appetite. ~<>~<>~<>~ Downtown… "We hit the honey pot, baby. We’ve got an address and a warrant and a whole day to put two and two together." Slapping the official court order down on his desk, Lt. Detective Noel Newton clenched his fist around a black ballpoint pen as he flashed a perfect Princeton smile, leaning back in his chair with as much confidence as a man of the Blue can summon. As he put the pen aside to interlock his fingers just under a chest so puffed out he could have put the Goodyear blimp out of business, Rich Rollins decided that the good detective looked more like a stuffed animal than a cop. A big, obnoxious stuffed nerd, complete with eerie eagle eye stare and pocket protector accessories. It made the Renegade laugh. Detective Newton didn’t seem to catch the humor. "What’s so funny?" His confusion only fueled Rollins’ amusement. "Goddamnit, what’s so fucking hysterical? We found the man who stuck a gun in your face before stealing your dead mother’s corpse. I could think of a lot of emotions I’d be feeling in your shoes right about now, but unrestrained hilarity sure isn’t one of them." "Relax, Sherlock." The Renegade tried to wipe away an ear-to-ear grin with the back of his hand, but it was a chore. "It’s just that you accomplished… what, exactly? The same shit I could have done on Google? I’ve had the fucker’s address for a week now." "What?" Detective Newton began to chew on the inside of his cheek, trying to hold on to some shred of badge-imbued superiority. "Why the hell didn’t you give it to us, then? You didn’t go-…" "No, I didn’t go smash down his door," Rollins interjected, his mirth officially melted. "I already knew the place, and it wasn’t a Chicago residence. The fucker went around and modified his information, trying to set somebody up for a pretty nasty fall." The detective’s brow became canyons brimming with doubt and apprehension as he regarded Rollins carefully, aloof and unrevealing. "What do you mean you ‘know the place’?" As Rollins leaned over to take up the warrant sheet, the detective jerked ever so slightly as if to protect the damning information. "Relax Fido, I’m just makin’ sure you found the same bullshit I did." Rollins ignored his perpetual indignation, an expression he couldn’t help but wonder whether it was a case of Botox gone wrong or if Newton was just born to look like a whimpering lapdog. "Yeah, that’s the same one I have. That’s a New York penthouse, chief. You know who lives there?" Detective Newton’s glower was anything but inquisitive. "No, but I’m sure you’re about to tell me." Sliding the sheet back across the mahogany surface that Newton no doubt toiled over to unhealthy extremes, the Renegade cocked a satisfied eyebrow. "It’s Seymour Almasy’s address." The look on the good detective’s face almost sent him into hysterics again, but Rollins knew he needed to maintain. "What the hell?" Newton snatched at the warrant report as his eyes narrowed in confusion. "Why-…?" The Renegade shrugged. "Beats the piss outta me. Maybe our tomb raider here wanted to do more than fuck up my personal life. I think he’s trying to ruin my professional career - not to mention the sport’s image - by taking out two of the biggest players in the game." Shaking his head in amazement, Rollins clicked his tongue out of rare nervous habit. "Can you imagine the outcry? Seymour Almasy, one of the most beloved ass-kissers in professional wrestling, convicted of stealing the remains of his most hated opponent’s dead mother. Then what if I end up dead? Ain’t no jury in the world who would buy his ‘but I was raised on RPGs’ bullshit." "It takes more than a personal computer and a web address to forge public records like this," Newton replied quickly. "You’re talking about a full blown conspiracy theory here, Rich. It’s just not feasible. I’ll have to check our sources and verify this Almasy business, but until then, for all I know you stole the woman’s corpse yourself." It was a hit below the belt, and Newton knew it. Rollins’ eyes focused immediately on the detective’s emotionless façade, his tone suddenly very dangerous. "She wasn’t just a ‘woman,’ bucko. She was my mother. And why the fuck would I do somethin’ like that?" Now it was Newton’s turn to shrug. "Makes some damn good publicity for this little tournament of yours, doesn’t it? Paints you up like some kind of vigilante rebel while you hunt down this twisted bastard." "Yeah, and I tried to shoot myself in front of five hundred wrestling fans," Rollins spat sarcastically. "Or did you forget there were witnesses when this guy tried to blow my brains into confetti?" Holding his hands up with a noncommittal flourish, the detective frowned. "I’m just saying I’d believe that before I bought into some national conspiracy against the wholesome empire of professional wrestling is all. I mean, wouldn’t you?" With a countenance that spoke volumes for an enmity he didn’t even try to hide, Rich Rollins looked deep into Newton’s pupils with conviction. "No," he said roughly, almost too forcibly. "I’m calling bullshit, Newton. Either help me catch this fucker or get the fuck out of my way. While you’re sittin’ here twiddling your thumbs and chasing shadows, I’ve got other things to attend to." As the Renegade came out of his chair and headed for the detective’s office door, Noel Newton shot him a quizzical look. "What the hell could be more important that catching this creep?" Rollins stopped at the knob, cocking his head just enough to catch Newton’s expression. He smiled. "I’ve got an appointment with my mistresses’ husband." Detective Newton obviously didn’t get it. "Huh?" "He’s my shrink," Rollins replied, flashing a mischievous lopsided grin that split his face from earlobe to chin. "Catch you later, dick." As the door slammed behind the PTC Global Champion, Detective Noel Newton shook his head in frustration. "It’s ‘detective’ to you, asshole." ~<>~<>~<>~ The offices of Dr. Paul O’Doyle, PhD… To tell or not to tell, that was the moral question. For nearly six months, Dr. O’Doyle’s feng shui sky parlor of an office had been a second home to Rich Rollins. The good doctor, a young and energetic new age philosopher with a perpetual tan and a predilection for pro wrestling had been nothing but a good luck charm. Whenever the world seemed too big for the Renegade to swallow on his own, Paul O’Doyle was there to talk him out of climbing the nearest clock tower with a rifle and a six pack of Miller High Life. He could keep the beasts at bay with little more than a click of his Bic and an insightful anecdote about that one time when his wife tried to stick a box of popcorn in the microwave while it was still inside the plastic wrap. To which Rollins could respond with his own anecdote about that one time he stuck his penis in Dr. O’Doyle’s wife, which was ironically enough also wrapped in plastic. Somehow, Rollins figured the good doctor would miss the humor completely. Such was his dilemma. He had tried calling Cynthia all day, but it always went straight to her voice mail. Rollins cursed under his breath as he snapped his phone shut yet again, unable to reach the woman who may or may not be carrying his illegitimate child. And here he was, about to sit down on her husband’s couch and complain about his own petty problems while Paul sat and listened and ranted in kind about the price of rice in wherever. As morbid as his sense of humor usually was, for once Rollins was not in the mood to crack a smile and laugh at his own misfortune. If Cynthia was carrying his child, his life was about to change in more ways than he was ready to think about. If it was Paul’s baby on the other hand… But Rich Rollins didn’t know for sure as he gave a curt nod to Paul’s redheaded secretary, who immediately bade him enter into the psychological sanctum of Dr. O’Doyle’s working penthouse. "You’re five minutes late, Rich. I was beginning to think you had canceled." Sitting behind a lavish pine desk veneered in tropical teak, Dr. Paul O’Doyle offered his favorite patient a warm smile from the other side of his multi-tiered dance studio that had been transformed into the lounge office of a lifetime. The buzz and bustle of downtown Chicago filled the floor-to-ceiling windows behind O’Doyle as he rose, gesturing for Rollins to pick from one of the many ornate couches in the room. "Guess we should get started then, eh?" Slipping his black leather duster off his shoulders and tossing it on a circular coat rack by the door, Rollins walked deeper into the massive space. "Sorry doc, I had some business to take care of with Chicago PD. You understand." Even though he felt welcome in the doctor’s presence, he couldn’t bring himself to make direct eye contact with O’Doyle. Paul nodded knowingly as he followed Rollins to his favorite furniture collection, the jungle camouflage loveseats with Spanish silk pillows. "That’s a murky affair, with your mother and all. How are you holding up?" "The usual psychotic bullshit," Rollins replied as he sank back into the pillows, kicking his feet onto a matching ottoman. "I get the urge to hurt small animals while violating your wife." The Renegade froze. He didn’t mean to say that. But Paul only laughed. "Lord knows she could use it. You know we haven’t been intimate in weeks? I think she’s avoiding me." Rollins was too stunned to speak. Here he was, blatantly blurting out this man’s wife’s infidelities, and the good doctor comes back with a bombshell: They weren’t sleeping together. Guess that answers that, Rollins thought as a bitter weight settled in the pit of his stomach. Rich Rollins was going to be a daddy. Paul O’Doyle was going to be one upset shrink. "Look, doc-…" but Rollins didn’t get the chance to come clean before Paul launched into his own diatribe of double-speaking psychobabble. "I envy you, Rollins. Really I do," the doctor gushed, and Rollins really began to wish he wouldn’t. "You live in this constant state of melodrama, but it’s like it all gravitates toward you like moths to a porch light. You’ve got a coping mechanism that I simply don’t see in anybody else. You work an extremely physical and emotionally strenuous job, are one of the best if not the best in the world at it, and because of this you come across people who only wish you harm on a day to day basis. They discredit you on national television. They point guns in your face, chant for your blood and steal your mother’s corpse, and all the while you just push forward like nothing was out of the ordinary. You’re a strong fellow, Rich Rollins. Most men would crack under such pressure." It was then that the Renegade realized why he enjoyed his sessions with Paul O’Doyle. Unlike most everybody else - the mindless sheep of the wrestling world who spat at his feet and wanted nothing more than to see him crash and burn - Paul stroked his ego in much the same way his wife stroked more private things. It all worked so well that Rollins figured they should take up practice together. One head peeper, one whore. It was the perfect tandem, at least in Rollins’ mind. Instead of taking the high road and exposing the truth, the Renegade said: "You’ve just got to keep it all in perspective and expand your dominance one step at a time. My personal life doesn’t interfere with my professional life…" and he paused, because the moment he spoke the words he knew they were a flat lie. O’Doyle caught the subtlety. "Something on your mind, Rollins?" About a hundred somethings actually, but Rollins only gave him one. "Well, that’s not entirely true. About my personal life interfering with my professional life, that is." Lord, this was proving to be harder than fellating a diseased elephant, not that Rollins would know first hand. But it was still difficult to say. "I sleep around a lot, doc. I don’t even have to go lookin’ for it most the time, the pussy just… finds me. Especially recently, as there are one or two women who I probably shouldn’t have gone with, but, what can you do?" Paul clicked his tongue thoughtfully, leaning back in his own camo recliner. "You’re not afraid you contracted something are you?" God bless him, the good doctor didn’t even see it coming. Rollins suddenly felt very sorry for the man. "No, I’m afraid I might be a father." And right on cue, Dr. O’Doyle’s cell phone began to ring. Checking the ID, Paul sighed as he held up two fingers. "I’m sorry Rich, it’s my wife. Two seconds." ~<>~<>~<>~ She blotted her mascara-smeared eyes with another tissue, trying desperately to calm the warble in her voice as the line continued to ring. When her husband finally picked up, Cynthia O’Doyle inhaled a deep, ragged breath. "Honey?" she whimpered. "We need to talk." ~<>~<>~<>~ "I’m in the middle of a session, sweetheart. Can’t it…? Yes, he’s sitting right here in front of me," Paul said as he shot Rollins an odd look. The Renegade immediately found himself watching the doctor’s facial expressions like a hawk, the hairs on the back of his neck now standing at full attention. "Oh shit," he whispered under his breath. "Baby, you need to slow down. I can’t understand… wait, what?" It was then that the warm smile behind Dr. Paul O’Doyle’s eyes trained on Rich Rollins, but that smile was dead. "What? Is… is this a fucking joke? Cynthia, stop playing games with me! Stop it! You’re fucking pregnant?" Rollins started pulling himself out of the love seat. "I can see this is a personal call, doc. I’ll come back-…" "Don’t you fucking move," O’Doyle spat, stabbing a finger at Rollins’ chest. "We’re not even close to finished, you lying sack of shit." The Renegade held up his hands, trying to calm his now ex-shrink down. "Look Paul, I was trying to tell you-…" "No. No excuses. You sit your steroid-strung ass back on that couch and wait until I’m done talking to my wife, understand?" He did, and even though Rollins knew he could snap O’Doyle in half, he couldn’t argue with the moist anger that had filled the doctor’s eyes. He owed him that much, at least. They didn’t talk long. Five minutes later, a flush and shaky Dr. O’Doyle snapped his cell phone shut and slid it back onto the belt holster he was so fond of using, his breath coming in shallow spurts that made Rollins extremely uneasy. A handful of agonizing heartbeats passed between them in silence, Rollins looking at Paul looking back at Rollins. The Renegade felt he could suffocate on the tension. When O’Doyle finally spoke, his voice was hollow, soulless and unreadable. "How long?" Rollins began to shift uncomfortably under his gaze. He wasn’t used to being on the other end of that look. "Awhile," he offered, trying to keep his voice neutral. O’Doyle slowly stood, his fists clenching into white-knuckled balls. "I asked how long have you been fucking my wife? I’m speaking plain English, bucko. I don’t give two shits who you are, what you stand for, or how many people you’ve destroyed over the years, Rollins. I’m a man of my word, and so help me Christ if you don’t give me the answers I want to hear I will fucking end you, understand?" The Renegade sighed helplessly. It was too late to sugarcoat anything, not now. "About six months, doc. Since my third session with you, or somewhere thereabouts. You introduced us, after all." And it was true. Cynthia had been swathed in a green silk gown the evening she and Rollins had first met. He had invited Paul to attend his private Hall of Fame induction ceremony at the Eden Palace ballroom in New York prior to GCW’s Annihilation. She had come along by proxy, and as a result they ended up having their first sexual encounter under the eyes of God in the unused chapel. As he related all of this to a disturbed Dr. O’Doyle, it occurred to Rollins that he hadn’t been this honest with anybody in years. It felt good to be this candid. Too bad the feeling didn’t last long. "I want you out of my sight," Paul decided. His face had turned a shade of red that Rollins didn’t even recognize, his eyes glistening with unshed emotion. "I can’t believe you’d stab a friend in the back like this, Rich. She was my wife, damn you." His words had lost all of their passion. Now there was only sorrow. "Her womb was not yours to violate, and you’ve taken that from me. Now get the hell out of my office." He almost tried to apologize, as if his words meant anything at this point, but Rollins thought better of it. Without anything else to say Rich Rollins stood, offered his former psychiatrist a respectful yet pained nod, and made his way for the door. Paul’s tears began to flow as he walked away, his soundless sobs wracking not only the doctor’s body, but Rollins’ conscience. Collecting his long leather coat, the Renegade closed the door behind him. He was fifteen paces down the hallway when the gunshot rang out the final act of Dr. Paul O’Doyle. ~<>~<>~<>~ O’Hare International Airport… "I trust you had a pleasant flight," the bright-eyed stewardess gushed at the man who stepped out of the private terminal. "Will you be staying in Chicago long?" He wasn’t in the mood for small talk, but even so the white-haired man shook his head. "Only as long at it takes," Seymour Almasy replied. "I need to have a chat with somebody, and something tells me it’s going to be a fairly short conversation."
