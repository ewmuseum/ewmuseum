the love that i found

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 don't want to no-show and don't want to resign so i'm throwing up what i have. enjoy! While the shower may have been running at a scorching temperature for the last fifteen minutes, Jessica O'Grady has yet to manage getting inside of it. Much of the time she's spent has been dedicated to gathering what courage she still has. After fulfilling the dauntingly awkward task of aiming her stream of urine onto the edge of a small stick, she planned on entering the shower for a nice, relaxing distraction while awaiting the arrival of the All-Telling Line(s). But she can't stay away. She can't occupy her mind with even the most mundane of tasks; instead it is firmly entrenched in the possibilities that present themselves. Jessica just allows the water to run, adding 'shower' to the top of her queue, while collapsed atop the closed lid of a toilet seat. She ominously glances towards the sink counter-top where the pregnancy test sits. After a few more moments of staring at a blank field, she hands her head in defeat; aware of her slavery to time. Though she feels guilty for doing it, she'll keep on waiting even if it takes fifteen, thirty more minutes. Hell, she'll wait another couple of hours of she has to. It's not like Jack will notice her absence, anyways. He has more "pressing matters" to attend to. She can't hear me from this far, hidden as well as I am, but I'd like to tell her anyways. I'd like to tell her, that she shouldn't feel ashamed. You shouldn't feel ashamed, Jessica. You shouldn't punish yourself for having that dedication and passion for maternity that comes naturally for all women. It's a beautiful intention and not one that should be neglected so easily. It's Jack who should understand that he's approaching this situation all wrong. In fact, he probably does understand, and it's only a matter of when he'll man-up to apologize to you for how he's treated you. But we both know that he never will. Jack is never one to rectify his faults or put forth an effort to fix the damage he's caused. He's victim to his own self-destructive cycle and he doesn't appear to care. Someone so closed-minded, so ridden corrupted by his tunnel vision, does not deserve such a prestigious accolade that accompanies victory in this tournament. Someone so determined to succeed seems destined to lose from the outset: Murphy's Law, and all that jazz. Jack knew our final bout was on it's way and he couldn't be ill-prepared for it. So while I'm making preparations of my own, unique kind—to pull that rug out from under him, so to speak—he has been plagued with the intention to destroy me, once again. After all, this match does have all the makings to be a score-settler, with our record on each other standing at 1-1. He's determined to take another victory on me. No, he's downright obsessed. He's so obsessed, that he's forgotten how great of a woman you truly are. Dare I say, he's psychotic? Although I am ashamed to say it, Jessica, I too have committed such a crime in the past. When I first employed you as my manager, I admit that my intentions were to affect Jack Murphy, much like my intentions here today. It was not to capitalize on your skills as a manger, but to capitalize on his jealousy. But this time around, while I am here for Jack Murphy once again, things are somewhat different. During the past week or so, it was never my intention to discover how truly genuine of a person you are. Observing you from this great distance—catching sight of your raw actions, at times convinced you are alone—has shown me a sight of you that I never expected to see. When I first noted it, I didn't think it'd affect me this greatly. Just considered it a minor quirk in your personality that I may be able to take advantage of. But I guess my frame of mind couldn't contend with the way you present yourself. It's different from anything I've ever taken to before. It's admirable to see you working so hard to achieve something so precious; something that people don't understand and often times becomes an anchor in their lives. But you don't let it weigh you down. Instead, it serves as your own personal step closer to the heavens. I'd kill to have a woman like you at my side for the time I settle down. Instead, I get Ellis. When Ellis was pregnant, she didn't go to the fucking bookstore to get a book of names. She went to the liquor store for some bourbon, or whatever the hell it is she drinks. She didn't once go to look at cribs or nighties or breast-pumps or car-seats. She went to a dealer's and looked at Ari and Humboldt and B.C. and Amsterdam strands. Ellis didn't go to the doctor—not once. She didn't even have the decency to go to the adoption agency. Or the abortion clinic. She wouldn't even talk to me. But here you are: not even pregnant, and you've already got the kid's first and last name picked out, whether it's a boy or girl. You've even got similar sounding names just in case you have twins. You've been to those stores in the mall that sell clothes with growing room for your belly and already picked out the cutest little outfits. You've even had three visits with your doctor in the past two weeks. Glancing at the counter, Jessica notices there is some change in the field on the test and assumes that enough time has past for a viable reading. She'd kick herself later when a friend would tell her that E.P.T.s take less than a minute. She doesn't know how to read it at first, so she digs through the trash and examines the box for instructions. After a moment, she reads the outcome clearly. It'd taken so much time that led to all that anticipation, and all of it was for nothing. The test reads that she is not pregnant, for the second time in the past two months. She refrains a reaction for as long as she can, holding back the instant swell in tears, but she can't do it any longer. A mix of emotions all come to a head with her in such a fragile state. Her shoulders shrink and her legs cross, her face collapses in the pit of her hands. I'm sorry, Jess, but I can't let it happen. Not yet. She attempts to pull herself together, but I know she won't be able to bounce back from this. Jessica thin--. She knows something is wrong. It'll be a shame to see her find out what it is, but it's all necessary. All she has to do is hang in there. It's hard, I can relate. But you can stay with him for a little bit longer. You can make it work. But when the time is right, Jessica, you'll realize that you never had to worry. So please, try not to, because I'm going to give you your baby. She slowly but surely will pull herself together, as she's the strong Irish type who doesn't tend show off her feelings; especially to a man who's more interested in himself at this time. Halting the tears Jessica slides out of her bathrobe and into the shower, shutting the curtain behind her. She must notice that the hot water has ran a bit warm and now she must turn it up. The window begins to fog, but that totally doesn't matter at this point anymore as I've seen what I need to see. And while she may have stopped crying before getting in, I can almost guaranteed she'd cry at least two more times before going out there to tell him, if she'd tell him at all. It is because of these situations that I totally love my fucking job.
