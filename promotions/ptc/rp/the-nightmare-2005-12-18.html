"The nightmare"

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 * * * * * 24-hour diners are lifesavers, if you ask me. They're always open, and someone else is always sitting alongside you at the oddest hours of the day, which to me, is a reminder that I'm not the only crazy person in this world. Immediately after moving out to Denver this past August, I hunted down the closest 24-hour diner in hopes of finding my place of solace and tranquility. And delicious home fries. I needed to have that one place I could escape to, somewhere to relax and be in the company of complete strangers. The last few years of my life have felt oddly alone, and I'm trying to rectify that, if only one tiny step at a time. Today, it’s 24-hour diners…tomorrow, maybe a mall. Lately, I've been making a lot of trips to this particular diner for all the right reasons. Great sausage and egg omelets, and not to mention a certain waitress whose company I happen to enjoy. Tonight, however, I just need to be around someone. Anyone. The nightmare returned, those same shimmering green eyes once again haunting the darkest corners of my sleep. * * * * * The trip from home to the diner takes no more than 10 minutes, even with a fresh snowfall. The all-wheel drive on my Nissan Pathfinder makes even a few inches of snow seem somewhat less than intimidating. Located down the hillside a few miles, the trip to the diner often is more treacherous than the trip leaving it. More often than not, snow is blown across the road in huge white drifts of powder. Beautiful to look at, deadly to speed through. The diner itself is simply called "DINER" with no fancy signage, unless you count the oft-blinking neon-tube sign across the entrance "fancy". An odd feeling of being watched came over me as I pulled the Pathfinder into one of the many vacant parking spots, and stepped out. The parking lot was lit by two fluorescent street lamps, which turned the brilliant fresh white snowfall into stale, yellowish heaps. The uneasy feeling quickly enveloped my stomach and moved up into my throat, tasting acidic and bitter across the back of my throat. The nightmare has never given me the dry heaves, or any kind of heaves for that matter. Nor have I ever had such an overpowering feeling of being observed, yet a quick scan of the surrounding area provided no peering and inquisitive eyes, let alone many places for this curious apparition to hide. Quickly, I made my way for the entrance, and heard the slight electronic buzz of the neon lamp as I entered. The feeling of being watched was still crushing me, even as the door shut behind me, the bell overhead singing the note of my entrance. Leaning against the counter, twirling her light-brown hair around her fingertip in what seemed to be a nervous habit, was Isis. She looked as alarmed to see me, as I was to see her. Evidently she noticed the horror painted across my face and the brisk nature of my entrance, because she started to make her way over towards me. The weight of being watched was still crushing, and it just got about 110lbs heavier. I'm sure she'd say 120, though. * * * * * Even though she was barely 5'3", she carried herself around the room in such a fashion that everyone noticed. I've often attributed her success as a waitress to this feat, because everyone seems to turn their heads as she gracefully glides past them. As she got closer, I could tell that her nervous habit seemed to also stem from distress, much like my entrance had. Her face -- usually calm, rounded, and always smiling -- seemed tight and shaken, her eyelids heavy under the weight of sleeplessness. "Well look at this, you couldn't wait until the morning to see me, could you?" she said to me, in a voice that was slightly scratchy from years of smoking. She smiled, but knew instantly that I recognized it as being somewhat forced. I shrugged my shoulders, and smiled at her. She read my feigned smile just as I had read hers. We both knew something was up, because it was nearly 3am, and normally she works the morning shift from 7am until noon, and I'm there usually between 10 and 11. Our presence at the diner, while normal, was hours ahead of schedule. "What's wrong, Arthur?" she said as she sat into the booth beside me, patting the seat. She doesn't know my first name, only my middle. It will stay that way for a while. I sat across from her, still feeling the weight of unseen eyes crushing me in their gaze, and tried to explain as best as I could, "Just having some trouble sleeping. And I'd be hard pressed to believe you have no idea what I'm talking about." "I want a drink." she said, never one to hide the fact that she's a recovering alcoholic. "We've talked about this…" "I know, and each time you lecture me how I shouldn't go back to what I was. And each time, I don't…so I lose a little sleep over it, big deal," she said with a shrug of her shoulders, "So there is no point in discussing my problem. Why are you here, and why do you look so skeeved out?" I figured, over the last few weeks she's peeled back the layers of her personality, revealing her very troubled core to me…it's time I started to reciprocate, even if only slightly "I had a nightmare. Except I've had this nightmare for a few months now, and it's always the same." Isis motioned to the waitress for two coffees, and then returned her attention to me. Her eyes were as light as her hair in color, a chestnut brown that was light enough to see the striations in her eyes, but also dark enough to lend some mystery to her already intriguing name. "Spill it. It's obviously harsh enough to bring you down from your mountain-top palace, m'lord." This time, the smile was genuine. I wish I could have returned the volley. "It's always so vague, so dark," I started, pausing for a minute to try and re-imagine the horror I've endured dozens of times, "I can't see any details, only this blackness that seems ominous and unbreakable. It's as if the night swallowed the day, the stars, and any source of light that could give me a clue as to where I am. But there are noises…" I paused as the waitress brought us our coffee, and asked if we'd like some food. Isis waived her off with a flick of the wrist, and then started to tear open the dozen or so packets of sugar she likes to soak up her coffee with. I continued, never touching my glass, "I can hear these hollow footsteps, it’s a sound that I know I've heard before, but I can't quite place. They get louder and louder as they draw closer, and in the dream I try to stand up and move, but I'm bound to something, or by something. And that's when I start to panic, because these chorting breaths keep getting closer. No matter how much I move or how much I wriggle, I can't pry myself free. And that's when the eyes appear." "Eyes?" I couldn't look at her, I felt so ashamed. Here I am, a grown man who fights other grown men for a living, and simply recounting a nightmare to a woman I obviously have a crush on is making me stumble like a baby taking its first step. "Yes, eyes. Green and shimmering, always unblinking. They look right at me, and it just feels like someone ramming a knife into my chest, and twisting. The eyes are the only color in the dream, and right now I’m praising the high-heavens that your eyes are brown." She blushed slightly, helplessly wading in the waters of my discomfort. I continued, "Over the last few months, I’ve had this dream over and over again. I don’t know what it means…but each time, I wake up with my heart racing and sweat covering my brow." "Awww, poor Artie…you sound like a child," She giggled and took a sip of the suger-laden coffee. I could tell she was trying to lighten the mood, but didn’t know how. So I smiled again, this time feeling a little less forced. "I’m almost afraid that since my other dream came true, that this one will come true." I said, a note of embarrassment singing through my voice. I felt foolish, but it was the truth. And this pressing feeling of someone watching me wasn’t helping. "Well," she started, "I honestly doubt that there are disembodied green eyes hovering around out there. So I doubt that this particular nightmare can come true, unless Stephen King is penning the story of your life." I finally chuckled at that statement, only because if she did know the story of my life, she probably wouldn’t be sitting in the booth across from me. * * * * *
