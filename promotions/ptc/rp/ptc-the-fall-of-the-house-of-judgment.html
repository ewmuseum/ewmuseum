The Fall of the House of Judgment

22 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
~<>~<>~<>~ CNMRI Center (Chicago Northside MRI)… “What does it look like?” pried Steven Caldera, his eyes scanning the computer cut-out in front of him with obvious concern. “Until he’s finished, we can’t say much,” the rather rotund radiologist replied, “but at a glance it doesn’t appear to be a tumor. We can be thankful for that.” Caldera released a bitter snort, the doctor’s placating attitude obviously not hitting home. “I would be even more thankful if one my most bankable talents wasn’t so fucked in the head,” he mumbled aloud before he could stop himself, causing the white-coated radiologist’s brow to furrow. “You say you found him where, exactly?” the doctor inquired, turning his attention through the thick radiation glass and into the chamber beyond. The MRI scanners within emitted a soft hum that was only faintly audible through the supposedly soundproof window, encasing its patient in a field of ultrasonic vibrations and magnetic waves that were already hard at work on a diagnosis. “A locker room at the All State Arena in Rosemont,” Caldera answered, his attention likewise focused on the thrumming machine beyond his touch, beyond his control. He released a ragged sigh. “He was alone in his room, babbling to the wall about some outlandish cult conspiracy.” “Has he sustained much trauma to the head?” “Are you kidding?” Caldera chortled as he shot the doctor a frown dripping with condescension, “He gets whacked in the skull on a weekly basis. That’s what I pay him for, sort of comes along with the territory.” So many questions, and far too few answers. They went on like that for half an hour as the scanners inside the antiseptic observation chamber continued to circle around the pull-out cavity that entombed its patient, swarming like vultures closing in on a wounded fox. At least, that’s how Rich “The Renegade” Rollins had come to think of it. Unaware of the conversation taking place on the opposite side of the radiation window, Rich Rollins tried to relax as much as possible. With the lower half of his body jutting out the front of the massive machine and his top half crammed inside of it like a sardine, The Renegade simply closed his eyes whenever the constrictive vice of claustrophobic paranoia threatened to grab his reigns and ride. He wasn’t usually the kind of guy who got spooked by close quarters, but this was decidedly different. He had never had to prove that he wasn’t a complete loony before. That made it very different, indeed. After all, he had the scars to prove it all really happened. He had the memories of men whose faces haunted his dreams - refusing to be forgotten - because they had been just as real as the blisters on Rollins’ feet. Men with families. Men with jobs. Men who were dead. Weren’t they? ~<>~<>~<>~ A house show in Rosemont, Illinois… The small black rubber ball ricocheted off of the white plaster wall, its pliable round surface creating a steady rhythm with each impact. …bom-thwack…bom-thwack…bom-thwack… With one leg propped up against a chair, Rich Rollins absently sent the ball on its back-and-forth journey with calculated practice, his ring attire still stuffed into the large black duffel on the bench beside him. A myriad display of unspoken emotions threatened to burst through the cold intensity in his eyes, simmering just under the surface of his normally cool and serene attitude. The simple repetitive rhythm of the black rubber ball was an activity he had indulged in many times over the years; the smooth motions kept him centered, the soft sound of the steady impacts slowly lulling his mind into a hypnotic trance. …bom-thwack…bom-thwack…bom-… His wrist still carried through with the motion of catching the ball and tossing it, but the ball never made the return. Snapping his attention back to his immediate surroundings, The Renegade practically snarled with impatience as he met his impromptu bright-eyed visitor, who held his little black ball in one hand and a wrapped bundle of cloth in the other. “Damnit Olson, you think you own the fuckin’ place?” Rollins hissed, rising to his feet and snatching his ball out of Dave Olson’s hand. Depositing the toy into a large side pocket in his wrestling duffel, The Renegade ignored the man as he began rummaging for his ring attire. “Can I help you?” he offered over his shoulder. Gnawing on the remains of a soggy toothpick, Dave Olson casually leaned his weight into the door of a closed locker, a devious grin marching across his lips. “I have a present for you,” he said with the faintest hint of sarcastic glee. “Here.” Turning around just in time, Rollins managed to catch the little bundle Olson had been carrying. Shooting his NCAA rival a quizzical look, he quickly pulled the cheap plastic band off and unfolded its payload of black fabric. As the simple tee-shirt tumbled into existence, a decidedly dark scowl immediately took up residence on his brow. The shirt contained only three characters, two carats separated by a dash, but the simple design was an image that Rollins was familiar with: ^_^ The symbol of Emoticon. “You think you’re funny?” Rollins alleged through tight lips, chucking the shirt back at Olson. His visitor merely laughed, tossing the remains of his toothpick into a trashcan in the corner. “Oh, relax Rollins. It’s just a joke.” Slinking around his leaning post against the locker, Olson snatched a folding chair from the wall and set it down, planting himself in front of Rollins’ bench and kicking his feet up directly in The Renegade’s way. Biting back a bristling bout of anger that was quickly creeping up his spine, Rollins spun on his heels and kicked Olson’s legs off of his bench. “You do realize that some of us actually work for a living, right?” Olson’s grin only grew. “You need to learn to lighten up,” Olson offered with a shrug, “These bouts of silent suffering you have going, they aren’t healthy.” The Renegade scoffed, turning back to his duffel and producing his favorite red bandanna, ignoring Olson’s blatant admonition as he proceeded to tie the precious scarf around his scalp. “My point exactly,” the NCAA prodigy mused. An exhausted shudder rippled across The Renegade’s broad shoulders as he gently lowered his head, equal parts of frustration and fatigue speaking boldly through his body language. Releasing a weathered sigh, Rich Rollins finally sat down on the bench and faced Dave Olson, his expression grim. “What do you want?” he asked bluntly. Olson’s good humor quickly melted, giving way to something that Rollins thought he recognized as sympathy. And he hated it. “I want you to tear down whatever it is you’ve got bottled up inside,” Olson said with candid sincerity. “I need to hear about your Trial, Rollins. And you need to let me hear it.” And there it was. The request was openly smeared across Olson’s smooth expression, a look that struck Rollins as a blatant pity ploy to get him to spill his guts. They had shared an experience, these two men; the horrors of their Judgment at the hands of a merciless cult of crazed madmen was something that would dwell in Rollins’ thoughts for the rest of his life, but that didn’t mean he cared to share his story. His ribs were still sore from the ordeal, but that was a wound that only time could heal. Everything else would take care of itself. But the look in Olson’s face said otherwise. Finally, Rollins conceded defeat. For the last time they journeyed back into the halls of the macabre reality of their memories, wandering within the confines of the living hell that had changed their lives forever. For the last time, Rich Rollins and Dave Olson went back to the House of Judgment. ~<>~<>~<>~ The Trial of Richard Rollins and Aleksei Leonid A damp chill rolled up Rich Rollins’ bare backside as he looked across the open expanse of the stone cellar, his stern expression doing nothing to belay the sense of urgency in his eyes. Likewise dressed down in nothing more than a pair of scruffy jeans and his own steady conviction, the large Russian soccer player Aleksei Leonid stared right back, the soft torchlight dancing across his rocky features like a maddening ballet of whispery flame. A lone Judge in the center of the bare cellar looked to each captive in turn, a large steel ball clutched in either black gloved hand. Where the firelight skittered across both men’s faces with eerie motive, the light didn’t so much as reflect itself off of the Judge’s faceless mask, as if the very emotionless piece of fabric was itself an impassable black hole. Readily the Judge spoke, his voice booming in the confines of the chamber. “Each projectile,” he began in a chilling monotone, holding the two ball bearings aloft, “are weighted at thirty pounds apiece, and will be your only means of salvation in this Trial.” He dropped both balls at this, letting the resounding clang ring in their ears for a few moments before continuing. “The first man to land three blows on his opponent will be declared the victor.” Scooping up the thick folds of his dark robes, the Judge allowed himself one last methodical glance at both Rollins and Leonid before taking his leave to stand witness against the uneven wall of stone and sod. Like a sentinel the Judge remained, his arms crossed in front of him with bleak finality. “Begin,” he commanded. They didn’t need to be told twice. Hitting a dead sprint, both Rollins as his Russian adversary threw themselves at the twin steel spheres, each man grasping for an immediate advantage. Simultaneously descending upon their targets, both men scooped up an orb and instantly backtracked, sizing up the distance between them. It was the Russian who launched first; Leonid’s desperate first strike sailed through the air as he spun like an Olympic discus thrower, catching The Renegade before he could put ten feet of space between them. The ball slammed into Rollins’ side, forcing him to drop his own sphere as he doubled over in staggered agony. With the wind knocked out of him, he knew immediately that something had likely been cracked as a dull pain began spreading through his sides. He tried taking a deep breath, but a sharp sensation across his ribs quickly changed his mind. “Are you okay?” Leonid asked, his concern painted plainly across his face. “Mercy is for the Weak and Innocent,” the Judge scolded with impunity, “and you are neither. Continue.” Gritting his teeth, Rich Rollins climbed back to his feet as he scooped the steel ball up, rolling Leonid’s weapon across the floor with his foot. Just as the burly Russian bent over to retrieve his sphere, The Renegade retaliated with a shot of his own that struck Leonid on the lower back. The Russian toppled like a great oak, sucking at his teeth to keep from crying out. And thus they traded the two steel spheres back and forth by the flicker of torchlight, the heavy clang-clang-clang of the balls ricocheting off the floor and walls with such force that a few of the torches managed to bounce out of their perch and scatter in blazing trails across the ground. Sweat dripped off the end of both man’s nose, matting down their hair and stinging their eyes. They began limping from corner to corner, dancing and dodging the projectiles and returning in kind, doing their best to make sure it was the other guy who took the final fall. Five minutes later Rollins was hit again, this time a full-on impact with his stomach; he wasn‘t sure who screamed loudest in protest, his ribs or himself. Coughing and wheezing as if his bowls had just burst - and they very nearly had, at that - The Renegade rolled onto his back and gazed up at the sod ceiling with eyes that would no longer cooperate. His field of vision blurred, melting light into shadow, creating a decidedly sinister reality that his consciousness began accepting as his own personal version of Hell, complete with the ever-watchful eyes of the Judge to criticize and mock his very existence. He barely even noticed as the broad frame of Aleksei Leonid slowly hovered over his fallen body, one deadly ball bearing held trembling in his hands. “Finish this,” Rollins all but whispered, his vacant stare somehow finding Leonid’s conflicted face. With hands that quivered in betrayal of their owner’s outwardly composed façade, Aleksei Leonid slowly raised the weighted steel sphere above his head, looking upon Rollins as the proverbial ant about to be crushed by a big round rock… But he hesitated, his eyes catching Rollins’, and a startled unease nestled itself behind the big Russian’s eyes. “Finish it…” Rollins pleaded, praying for a release from his own heartbeat that pounded daggers into his side. But it was not to be. Leonid finally turned to address the Judge, letting the ball bearing drop to the ground. “I will not kill this man,” he said firmly, meeting his captor’s eyeless attention with one fortified with fiery conviction. Silence. “You would intentionally disregard the conditions of your Trial?” the Judge intoned darkly. Leonid nodded. “I won’t lower myself to the rank of murderous barbarian for your amusement,” he replied. “And frankly, you can take this Trial and shove it up your ass.” And again there was silence, but it didn’t last long. The mortified shrieks that were ripped from Aleksei Leonid brought Rollins back to his senses just in time to watch the Judge utilize the scathing lash of his leather whip one last time, sending the Russian into howling hysterics. It was that exact moment that Rich Rollins knew it wasn’t a release he sought; no, what he wanted was something far more satisfying. Revenge. ~<>~<>~<>~ Returning to Rosemont… Dave Olson sat quiet as Rollins finished, his eyes downcast. A handful of heartbeats passed in silence as the experience drove home, a strange mixture of bitterness and sorrow floating between them. Finally, Steven Caldera spoke. “I’ve heard enough,” Caldera said from his place in the open doorway. He regarded The Renegade carefully as Rollins jerked around in total shock, an infuriated glare frozen on his unshaven features. “Doesn’t anybody knock around here?” Rollins exclaimed indignantly, rising to his feet. “You can’t just b-…” “Who were you talking to?” Caldera cut him off, his face strangely neutral. The Renegade released an impatient sigh. “Steven Caldera, I’d like to introduce you to Dave Ols…” But he stopped. The chair in which Dave Olson had occupied mere moments ago sat empty. In his place was another face, a symbol that drove knives into Rollins’ heart: ^_^ “We need to have a talk,” Caldera stated matter-of-factly. But no words were forthcoming on Rollins’ end. The room began to spin, and before he knew it, the world had descended into blackness. ~<>~<>~<>~ CNMRI Center… “Can you clear him to compete?” Steven Caldera was in no mood to beat around the bush. The radiologist, however, was taken aback by the bluntness of his request. “Compete?” the doctor almost choked, “I shouldn’t even let him walk out of this room! He’s tested positive for masteron and stanozolol, not to mention a type of medical sedative that zoologists usually only use on elephants in labor, for Christ‘s sake!” “Are you accusing my talent of steroid abuse?” Caldera warned dangerously, sending the radiologist back a step. “No, not necessarily,” the doctor was quick to correct himself, “but it is rather suspicious. Combining those levels of testosterone with a powerful enough sedative can create an incredibly psychoactive mixture that frankly… it’s amazing he’s still alive.” “But can he compete?” Caldera repeated. “No,” the doctor replied with brusque finality. “Very well then,” Caldera nodded, encompassing the radiologist’s palm in a firm handshake. The crinkle of green-faced Benjamins touched the inside of the doctor’s hand, and a knowing twinkle danced in Caldera’s pupils. “How about a second opinion?” Despite the guilty lump that had suddenly materialized in his stomach, the radiologist grinned. “He’s fit as a fiddle.” Smiling broadly, Steven Caldera gave the chubby little doctor a firm slap on the back. “Good man.” ~Fin~
