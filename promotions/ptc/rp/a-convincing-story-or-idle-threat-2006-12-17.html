A Convincing Story, or Idle Threat.

17 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-17 As we live our mundane lives on a day to day basis, all around the World there are people in the wrestling business busting their asses in order to become the absolute best. These people struggle through their lives in order to do what they feel is right, because they know they can accomplish anything if given the proper opportunity. As we live our lives the only way we know how, opportunities fall into our laps and wait for us to fulfill them. They wait for the right person to come along and take advantage of absolutely everything they can. When a certain opportunity arises that can prove yourself worthy to all mankind, you know damn well you're going to take it, whether you believe in yourself or not. If you're the only person believing in yourself, though, it tends to throw your confidence off-track. You don't focus as much as you should. You can't help but think what other people are thinking about you, if you're being laughed at behind your back. Are people gathering around in a little circle, saying 'this guy hasn't got a chance in the world, no matter how hard he tries'? People are the meanest of the mean around this so-called World. Sure, they're nice and perky to your face, because you're nice to them, but once you turn around, you can feel the knife digging in inch by inch. You can hardly wait for the knife to come out so you can feel better, so the pain can be a mere afterthought… but that's just it, the pain's never gone. The unbearable tolerance stays with you for a very, very long time. The longer you've been around this business, the more you realize it. There's no doubt that you see more, experience more, and – overall – learn more. You see more backstabbing in one day than bumps in a wrestling ring. You truly begin to see the two sides of every person, some you can trust, and others you aren't so sure about. But that's life, and there's nothing you can ever do about it. Humanity is strange, but it's how they act that really unbalances you. Which begs the question: Why do we even bother? ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ If there was one man who had a lot riding on Sammy Brown's success in GTT6, it was the man sitting behind his desk, sipping from a cup of coffee. His desk was a few yards away from Sammy, who was propped up against a pane of glass that covered the entire wall. "Nice of you to join us, Mr. Brown." The man was courteous, though everything seemed to be a blur as Sammy slowly came to his senses. "I hope my boy 'Knuckles' didn't hurt you too much." Those unfamiliar with Sammy's Brown story will not recognize the man seated behind the desk, Don Michael DeAngelis is the name his father christened him, but others have seen fit to call him a bastard from time to time. A custom tailored suit was fitted perfectly to his proportions, enhancing his already broad shoulders. To his left stood the man he had called Knuckles, a dark gray collared three button shirt was left untucked over black slacks that draped over spit-shined pointy black shoes. Sammy shook the cobwebs out of his head, though it caused him even more pain as it began to throb from the blow that had knocked him unconscious earlier. Blotches of red, blue, and yellow tinted his view, skewing his sense of reality for a brief moment as they adjusted themselves to their new surroundings. "I took the privilege of ordering you some macaroni, I hope you don't mind." Sammy was still oblivious to what was actually going on around him, but at least his face wasn't pressed up against the glass anymore; a streak of saliva inching it's way down the glass was the only evidence he was there in the first place. He also doubted his host would really give a damn if he minded or not, he knew he was just putting up a front. Finally up onto his feet, his eyes more accustomed to the two men across from him, he staggered like a drunk to a chair placed in front of DeAngelis' desk. A plate of Fettuccine with grilled chicken sliced on top steamed within arm's reach, a glass of red wine was placed not too far from there. "Who are you," Sammy asked. He stumbled into the chair, and nearly knocked over the glass of wine. DeAngelis shrugged slightly, almost as if he expected Sammy to just know who he was. "I'm a friend of Stevenson's, maybe he's mentioned me…" He leaned closer, his hands now pressed firmly against the desk as he pushed himself up slightly. "Michael DeAngelis." Sammy watched as the man nodded his head; though the movement was so slight it would have been missed, were Sammy not paying so close attention. "I've heard of you," Sammy replied. He couldn't help but notice how casual the Don was acting, as if the two could be old friends. "You're his bookie, right?" Offended, the Don held his hand to his heart. "Ouch, that one really hurt." He then shook his head, as a smile flashed briefly before he corrected the grappler's mistake. "I wouldn't consider myself a bookie, per se. Though I have been known to dabble a bit in the problems people have with gambling. It'shard not to take their money, it's like stealing candy from a baby." "So you're a thief too." Knuckles stepped forward, upset at the lack of respect Sammy is offering to his employer. Mikey is quick to put a halt to his aggressive advance, he knew that the shot he had dealt Sammy before was nothing compared to the stories he has heard from other 'jobs' he has performed. "Bradley was right, Mr. Brown." DeAngelis pressed his palms together, but there was no chance he was about to pray. "You don't seem to value your own life. It's quite disappointing." "Why," Sammy asked. He jabbed his fork into the pasta, the sound of it as it scraped against the bottom of the bowl made his host shiver. "Does it make killing me less of a challenge?" "I'm not sure what Stevenson has told you about me, Mr. Brown." His tone was soft, almost apologetic. "But I can assure you that killing you would do nothing to help me in my affairs." "Then what do you want," he wondered aloud. He hoped that the answer would end the fear that had been stalking about in the back of his mind, but he highly doubted it. "I just ask that you reconsider your decision to drop out of that wrestling tournament you were doing so well in." It couldn't be that easy, could it, Sammy thought as he stuffed a full fork of the Alfredo sauce covered pasta into his mouth. "I know you aren't young anymore, and me offering you two million dollars would make no difference when you could have an accident and die tomorrow." Something changed, there was a sudden sparkle in the corner of DeAngelis' eyes that made it seem as if he was leading up to something. "I bet that gentleman right there would love to have two million dollars," he pointed toward the large pane of glass, through it to a family seated together having dinner. The woman had her a white linen cloth pressed against her face as she laughed, but the moment she pulled it away Sammy's jaw dropped. A wide grin creased DeAngelis' features, as he heard the memories of a distant love uttered in a single word. A name. Her name. "Margie." ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Margarette Olivia Parker was the proud mother of two wonderful children; her eldest was a twelve year old daughter named Samantha, while her youngest was a six year old boy named Jacob – named after his father, her husband. She lived the life she always dreamed of, and was the type of mother other children always envied. She had married Jacob after dating for a few months, and for the most part they have been happy. There was that one occasion with the secretary they don't talk about anymore, but other than that life has been peachy. Her husband provided for her and the children, kept a roof over their head, and always kept the refrigerator stocked with weeks worth of food. He was dependable, but not the type to go out and splurge on things they did not need. Which is why she found it rather odd when he came home and told her he had made reservations at such a fancy restaurant, pricey is what she meant to say. Of course, that was all explained when he told her about the one hundred dollar gift certificate he had won. You get to know someone pretty well after eleven years of marriage, and she knew exactly how frugal he was with money. Even his Christmas gifts were practical. Still, she saw how happy it made him feel to be able to take them to such a nice place, and who was she to turn down something so elegant. Not to mention the fact that the kids had never eaten authentic Italian cuisine, since frozen lasagna and packaged spaghetti was as close as she could ever get to cooking Italian cuisine. "Is something troubling you, dear," she heard her husband ask. She was so lost in her own trail of thoughts, that she had not noticed the waiter holding the plate of food at her side. "Sorry," she apologized, as she leaned back in her chair. "I just had this really strange feeling that someone was staring at me." "Well look at you," came her husband's reply. "Who wouldn't want to stare at such a beautiful woman?" "Eww," screamed little Jacob, as his nose scrunched up and his eyes narrowed. "Get a room," Samantha blurted out, as she watched her parents lock hands and blow kisses to each other from across the table. "Excuse me," Margarette said, her eyes wide with surprise. "And what do you mean by that?" "It's a joke, mom." "Well, it's not an appropriate one," she scolded. "I don't know where you heard that from, but it sounds like I'm going to have to block some channels on your TV." "Like that's going to do any good," came her daughter's quick response. " I heard it on Zack and Cody." "And what's that?" She was clueless about television shows, but she was about to write a letter. "The Suite Life of Zack and Cody, mom!" Her head practically jerked away from her neck, as she turned to see her six year old son humming what she figured to be the theme song. "You have him watching this filth, too?" She was practically in a loss of words. "Which channel was it, so I can complain. I think they should be showing more children oriented shows, if you ask me." Her daughter smiled, not a regular smile… It was a smile when you already know the other person's reaction to what you're going to say, and you love it. "Disney Channel." Now she was definitely at a loss of words. "I… I-" she was pretty much hyper-ventilating. "Relax, dear," her husband tried to settle her down, he even waved his own napkin in front of her to give her extra air. "I'm sure it was innocent." He turned to face Samantha, then shook his head with a smile. Daddy's little girl? ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ What in the hell, he thought as he pushed himself up off the floor, beads of sweat seeping through his pores as the fear – of what he imagined DeAngelis had in store for the only woman he ever cared about – sent shivers up and down his spine. The hair on the back of his neck stood at attention, as the small bit of pasta he had eaten earlier tickled the back of his throat. His mouth watered as the sour taste of the bile trickled onto his tongue, a reflex reaction that caused a bit more of the vomit to fill his mouth. He was scared out of his wits, and he was sure his host was enjoying himself. "Don't you dare hurt her," he threatened. As empty a threat as it was, the veins in his eyes swelled to rivers of blood as the white of his eyes changed to a slight shade of crimson. "Margie has nothing to do with this." "Oh, I have no intention of hurting her directly." His grin had deeper conviction behind it, as if there was still something more. "No, you see, there are other ways to make someone feel pain. To have to live with a pain so heavy you would want to kill yourself in order to be free of it's restraints." Sammy walked up to the glass, as a waiter walked up to her with a plate of food in his hand. She was still as beautiful as he remembered her to be. There was no way he could let DeAngelis get away with this, he had to stop him. "Do you remember when you left her, Sammy," he inquired. "What was it, twelve – thirteen – years ago?" "I'll do whatever you want," defeated, Sammy stared out at the life he could have had. A family that should have been his, if it were not for his damn stubbornness. "No, you need to hear this, Mr. Brown." He was insistent on telling his story, about driving the blade deeper into Sammy's back. He wanted to be sure that Sammy understood the severity of even thinking about crossing him. "I want you to know how much you hurt her, how much pain she went through when you turned your back on her…" Sammy tried to tune him out, to ignore his cheap scare tactics, but he couldn't ignore him. Especially not after he finished his sentence. "…and your daughter." ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Why do we even bother? We bother because we hope that something will change. That somehow, if we can make some kind of mark, some sort of difference, it will get easier. That we won't have to struggle to make ends meet. That we won't have to worry about where the money for the rent will come from, or if there will be any electricity when you get to the place you call your home. We do everything in our power to make sure we provide those we love with the proper attention they deserve, no matter the cost. Independent wrestlers put their livelihood on the line everyday, not because of the money… but because of the opportunities that lie ahead if they manage to catch the attention of someone important, who just happened to notice something special in the double corkscrew plancha off the top rope Jose did in his match. For some though, people like Sammy Brown for instance, you do it for the sheer joy of watching the fans jump out of their seat to hoot and holler when the good guy overcomes all adversity to win the match. Or at least he did. Now, as he stands behind the curtain – waiting for it to be drawn back – he does it because he has to. He does it because he doesn't want someone special in his life to be hurt, even if they have no clue as to who he is or what is going on. So as the curtain draws back, the spotlight warming his skin as it shines down on him from afar, he steps out onto the ramp that leads to the ring for what he hopes is not the last time. And he hears the cheers of thousands of fans chanting his name, but it still does not drown out the one name he cares about at this moment. The daughter he never knew he had. Samantha. It just kept repeating over and over in his mind, marching him toward the ring. One more match to win to make it to the Finals, and he he'd be damned if Jason Snow was going to just waltz through it. He beat High Flyer when no one thought he would, he dropped the Giant Killer from his perch when he beat Vangelus Olsig, and he took out The Phantom Republican without much of a fight. This one wasn't going to be easy, he knew that much, but this meant more to him than it ever could for Jason Snow. He knew at least that much. If he knew anything at all.
