Weapon of Choice: I

21 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-21 GTT5 Mrs. W. Bracket Finals Seymour Almasy vs. Kimbusa "Ugh…" Really, when Seymour Almasy thought about it, that wasn’t a significant expression of pain considering he’d just been drilled in the face with a Yakuza Kick from a man who outweighed him by over 300 pounds. He could feel it all slipping away. Kimbusa had trained for this day, prepared himself for his chance to become the best in the business, his chance at retribution against his former life. The massive sumo wrestler grabbed the downed Almasy by the arm, and dragged him to the corner. It didn’t take a genius to see what would come next. The Bomb. The Banzai Drop. By any other name, if Kimbusa dropped his 500 pounds on Seymour’s chest cavity, that would be end game, beyond a shadow of a doubt. Slowly, the former OSF World Champion ascended the ropes, poised to drop The Bomb. "SEY-MOUR! SEY-MOUR! SEY-MOUR!" …nothing. No movement. No motion. Seymour was done. Kimbusa knew it. Steven Caldera knew it. Most importantly, Seymour Almasy knew it. "SEY-MOUR! SEY-MOUR! SEY-MOUR!" It was nice to have the fans cheering for him, even at the end, Seymour thought. Why, in a few seconds, Kimbusa’s gigantic form was going to squash him like an insect, and the referee could count to 1,003, let alone three quick slaps of the hand. Kimbusa began bouncing on the ropes, gaining momentum. His moment was at hand. His time was now. Until the crowd’s chant…changed. It started low, among the fans that knew what had happened through various news reports and wrestling magazines, growing in seconds to rise to the rafters. "AERITH! AERITH! AERITH!" Seymour’s eyes shot open. Kimbusa came off the second rope. Nobody home. Seymour had willed himself out of the way, the name of the woman he knew he loved enough to inspire his exhausted, unprepared, nearly destroyed body out of the way. The rest of the match was a blur, really. Seymour threw himself at the wounded sumo, kicking away with every strike in his arsenal, going to the top rope again and again, before finally throwing himself into a 450 splash, rotating through the air until he finally crashed down onto Kimbusa’s bulk. At 10:35 PM, the referee’s hand hit the mat for the third time, signaling the end of the contest. Seymour Almasy had won the Mrs. W. Bracket of GTT5. At 10:35 PM, the dreams of Mitsuhiro Kurokame and Jason Seymour Wilson died simultaneously. ----------------------------- Backstage… Seymour disappeared behind the curtain a few minutes later, the deafening cheers of the fans still resonating in his ears. Tonight, though, there would be no reveling in the cheers, no celebrating perhaps the biggest win of his career. There would be getting his gear and taking a red-eye flight to Long Island so he could be with Laura in the hospital again. Congratulations were offered by wrestlers and staff alike, but Seymour barely nodded in their direction. Usually, Almasy was friendly to a fault, but today was understandably different. He made it back to the locker room in near silence, tearing into his bag for his cell phone, to see if any messages had been left. None had been. "Don’t worry, Laura," he whispered, looking down at the phone with near reverance. "I’ll be back soon, I promise." The resulting ring nearly made him jump out of his boots. Seymour threw the phone open and slammed it to his ear with almost unnatural velocity. "Seymour Almasy," he said, face slowly going pale. "Who’s calling?" "It’s the hospital," a voice said, on the other end of the line. Seymour was no voice-reader, but he could tell that the other man’s voice was trembling nearly as much as his own. Something was wrong. Very wrong. "What happened," Seymour asked, a sick feeling radiating out from the pit of his stomach. In truth, he didn’t know how he’d got the stupid question out. Obviously something bad had happened. It was only a matter now of just how bad. "She’s…flatlined. I‘m sor--" By that point, however, the phone was in flight across the locker room. Seconds later, it smashed into the concrete wall, shattering into a thousand pieces that sprayed seemingly everywhere. Seymour watched the cellular telephone’s destruction with fascination, until it had been accomplished, and there was nothing left for him to do but accept reality. "No…" This wasn’t how it worked. Period. Everything had always worked out before! When Jamie Shock hit her with a crowbar over the head, she’d been sent to the hospital in a coma, and she’d gotten out of that. When, in a drunken act of stupidity, he’d slept with Alyssa Sanders in Lights Out Wrestling, she’d forgiven him. Their love had even survived their breakup, making their friendship closer than most people could ever fathom being. But now, she was dead. There was no coming back from dead. As he’d so bitterly discovered when he was young, life had no Phoenix Downs. No Life spell. No priest who could bring a party member back to the realm of the living for a small donation. Laura was gone. Forever. And the worst part? "It’s…my fault. I..could have, should have--" He cut himself off. Yevon, how could he have miscalculated so greatly? All he had to do was say ‘yes’ to her and none of this would have happened! Laura and he could be getting married right now, flushed with happiness at the occasion and Seymour’s continued success. Ah yes, he thought bitterly. His continued success. Beating Kimbusa meant he’d have ANOTHER match to wrestle in this Godforsaken tournament. Tournament had once been Seymour’s greatest joy. Now, it was his greatest annoyance. If nothing else, though, it could at least temporarily distract him from the dark thoughts that were starting to swirl through his head. The thoughts that told him to just stop thinking. Stop doing. Stop being. Laura, though, would have wanted him to go on. Wanted him to think, to do, and to be. So he would. For at least one more round. But what to do until then? It wasn’t as if he could just pretend nothing had happened and go train for the semi-finals of the biggest tournament he’d ever been in. Laura was gone. She wasn’t coming back. Well, if that was so, the least he could do was go back to the scene of the crime. To the place where this all started. Maybe he’d find some closure. Maybe. ------------------------------------ Long Island, NY As he got out and paid the driver, Seymour realized that he was growing really fucking sick of taxicabs. They were taking him everywhere lately, it seemed. They took him here a few days ago to watch his wife put a bullet in her chest. They took him to the arena where he found out that she’d flatlined. Now, it was taking him back to the symbol of everything that was once good about their relationship. A symbol perverted by the yellow police tape and the mess that Seymour knew was likely still in the master bedroom. In spite of all of that, the house itself was still as lovely as he remembered it. For a few moments, he planned to go straight to the site of Laura’s suicide. That was the plan, at least, until he realized that he couldn’t do it without sobbing like a child. Taking a slow breath, he dropped to his knees, looking up at the hill where the deed had been done. Judging by all the police tape and investigation equipment around, no one save the cops had been there since he’d watched the woman he loved fire a deadly piece of metal into her chest. Morbid curiosity finally got the better of him, and he plodded slowly towards the taped off part of beach, stepping over the yellow "DO NOT CROSS" barrier to arrive at the outcropping. All he could do at first was stare. Chalk outlined where her body had fallen, where he had clutched her desperately. Considering she hadn’t been dead at the time she’d been taken away, Seymour supposed the chalk had been placed down when she’d died. Or perhaps not, he didn’t know how police investigations worked very well, anyway. Lying to the side of the chalk outline was the pistol, the damnable pistol which she had somehow procured and used to end herself. Before he quite knew what he was doing, he had walked up to it, and scooped it up, holding the gun in his hands. He’d never held one before. It felt foreign to him. From movies, though, he knew how to open the chamber, to look inside. Five bullets remained in the gun. That was more than enough, he decided. The Final Fantasy suddenly rolled to his back, lying down in the chalk outline. It was taller than him, prompting a slight chuckle. "She always did get a kick out of being taller than me," he whispered, sadly, before opening his mouth, and inserting the muzzle of the pistol. Yes, it was the coward’s way out. He knew that. But he didn’t care. This way, he’d die and get to see his Laura again. His finger went to the trigger as he thought of Tessa. Of Cid. Of Laura. Of everyone who’d been tied up in this goddamned mess. The thoughts broadened to everyone he’d met in the sport. To Tsunami Oni and Max Queue. To Shadow and Dawn McFarlane. And finally, to Rich Rollins, his next opponent in GTT5. The man he’d be letting off the hook by pulling the trigger. Snorting bitterly, he waited. And waited. And hoped that somehow, the gun would go off, because he was too much of a fucking coward to even pull the trigger. Only as he pulled it free from his mouth did he notice the coppery taste in his mouth and look down at the barrel, at Laura’s blood that had splashed on it when she pulled the trigger. With all of the signs of a man utterly broken, he placed the gun on the ground, and began to sob. ------------ Night had begun to fall as Seymour lay crying. Staying out here much longer was not an option, he knew. So, reaching into his pocket, he came out with the key to the home, and began the short walk to the side door. Each step felt like daggers as he left the site of the suicide, only to move towards what had once been his Disney World, his happiest place on Earth. Seymour had always preferred the peace of the Long Island home to the hustle and bustle of their Manhattan apartment. Now, there could be no peace here anymore. Nonetheless, the key fit into the lock like old times, and Seymour stepped into the house. The Final Fantasy took a deep, slow breath, before sinking into the familiar couch. His limbs felt heavy, which was understandable, considering he’d barely slept a wink on his flight. Sleep was not Seymour Almasy’s friend recently. Sleep meant nightmares. It meant seeing Laura shoot herself again and again and again until he woke up. Seymour stretched out on the couch, looking up at the ceiling. How could everything have exploded so violently in his life? With the notable exception of the tournament, nothing at all had been going well for him lately. "First Laura goes and sleeps with some random guy…then my mom snaps at me," he mumbled, to himself, working his way through the list of negativity. "Then Cid dies, then Laura kills herself. It doesn’t make any sense!" Life rarely did, Seymour knew, but in this instance, it made even less sense than usual. He slowly sat up, looking at the painting on the opposite wall and shaking his head. "Cid died of a heart attack. That’s the only non-suspicious thing that’s happened so far. Mom hasn’t so much as said a word to be until a few weeks ago, and Laura doesn’t own a gun OR go clubbing and sleep with strange men." Given everything he knew, there was only one conclusion that he could come to. "Something’s up," he said, knowing it in that moment to be gospel, just as he’d known something was wrong when he had been here with Laura just before discovering her dirty little secret. This house was full of surprises, it seemed. He couldn’t quite ascertain why, but Seymour now knew that there was something here that he was missing. A piece of the puzzle that HAD to be here. But he knew every figure in the story. Aerith, his mother, Grandpa Cid…there was no one he didn’t-- "Him." It was a single word, but it came from the depths of Seymour’s soul. Him. Of course. The man who’d slept with Laura. He had to know something. Maybe not anything substantive, but anything was better than nothing. Perhaps he had left something upstairs? Wordlessly, the Final Fantasy rose, power-walking towards the stairs. That room was going to help him get to the bottom of this mess, he was sure of it. Why, he wasn’t quite sure. Most likely, it was because that was the way it always happened in the movies. The hero found the one clue needed to crack the mystery open and get retribution on whoever was trying to ruin him. Or, for that matter, in Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney, his new videogame obsession. Hey, Seymour doesn’t JUST play RPGs. He also plays weird niche shit from Japan. With head held high, he ascended the stairs, steeling his will to the possibility of seeing the room still in tatters, with a condom still spilling semen over the picture of Laura and himself. With a trembling hand, he reached for the doorknob, pulling it open to reveal…a perfectly clean room. In that instant, he felt rather silly. "Of course it’s clean, you dumbass," he reproached himself, "Laura’d only been LIVING here for a month since then." He winced at his accentuation of the word ‘living’, as it was sadly not the current case for the woman he loved. If the room was clean, though, how the hell was he supposed to find anything? Laura was a very good housekeeper. Well, very good except… "Jackpot." Seymour smiled as he walked into the bedroom. When Laura was busy, he KNEW her favorite cleaning technique. All of the couple’s clothes found themselves in one very special location. The closet. Floor thereof, to be more specific. And sure enough, when Seymour Almasy opened the door to the closet, clothing of all sorts practically came spilling out. Now, the hunt began. He waded his way through a bunch of perfectly normal pairs of slacks, then on to several of Laura’s more revealing outfits. Seymour felt like blushing and crying simultaneously as he remembered particular incidents attached to most of them. Shoes were also strewn into the mess, as he continued digging. "Hey," he suddenly exclaimed, unearthing something from the giant pile, "it’s my favorite Final Fantasy VII t-shirt! I must not have gotten it back during the initial divorce…" Being sure to put the shirt aside for safe keeping, Almasy continued sifting through the pile. More lingerie. More shoes. More shirts. More everything. "Good Yevon, how much clothing does this woman have?!" Out of frustration, he buried his hand deep in the pile, grabbing on to something in annoyance. He thrust his arm out of the morass, to see what unimportant article of clothing he’d pull out. Seymour blinked. Once, twice. He looked at the object in his hand again, wondering if it actually was what it appeared to be. "What." "The." "FUCK?!" Finally, Almasy’s eyes allowed him to focus on the object. It was smaller than most of the articles of clothing, but also entirely different. Seymour was holding it by a knot in the back that kept it tied properly. He turned it over in his hands, looking at it with utter disdain. He sniffed at it, detecting the powerful odor of sweat, before wrinkling his nose. He knew this particular scent. He’d certainly smelled worse in the wrestling ring before, but what this scent meant was enough to make him lose control. Seymour swerved violently to the side and vomited, throwing up what little food and drink he’d eaten over the past day. Chunks of throw-up clung to his chin as the second wave poured forth from his throat, staining the carpet irrevocably. For at least two minutes, Seymour’s body shook as his stomach violently emptied himself. Mercifully, after the first minute he only dry-heaved, until he collapsed, facedown in a pile of his own vomit. With trembling arms, he pulled himself up, reaching for the fabric he found to wipe his face clean as he held his breath. "That…son of a…WHY?! HOW?! WHEN?!" There would always be more questions than he could ever answer. The enraged Seymour Almasy whirled on his knees to face the door, accepting the horror of what he saw. His own pale form staring back at him, clutching a vomit-stained red bandanna. ------------------------------------------------------------------------- LaGuardia Airport "Here’s your ticket, Mr. Almasy," the young woman said, handing it to him. "I hope you have a pleasant flight." Seymour didn’t respond. He was too focused on what he was going to do when he got to the security checkpoint. His palms were clammy as he dragged a bag of luggage behind him. Suddenly, with his oversized jacket on, it was entirely too warm inside the airport. He approached the checkpoint slowly, melding into one of the lines cooly, calmly. Precise timing would be needed. The man in front of him stepped through just as the guard turned his back, and Seymour followed immediately. *beeep!* At the sound of the metal detector going off, Seymour quickly stepped in front of the other man. The startled security guards looked up, seeing the flashing lights and the unfortunate man Seymour had used as a decoy. The man was called back, and run through the detector again. All was clear, and they let the man go. In the guards minds, nothing was wrong. That was primarily because they didn’t know that they’d just let a man with a loaded firearm through security. And with any luck, they never would. Seymour reached into the inner pocket of his coat, fingering the weapon that Laura had used to take her life. Now, it would serve another, far more satisfying purpose. The Final Fantasy felt inexplicably relaxed. The first difficult part of his plan was over. Now all he had to do was get on Flight #2455, non-stop to Chicago, Illinois. As far as Seymour Almasy was concerned, the semifinal matchup of GTT5 between Seymour Almasy and "The Renegade" Rich Rollins wasn’t going to happen in a ring. It was going to happen in a back alley somewhere in the Windy City. Because, while he couldn’t prove anything else, he knew one thing. Rich Rollins had slept with Laura. And on its own, that was an offense that justified the wild, ludicrous idea he was considering. He kept the even expression on his face as he handed the ticket to the flight attendant, who tore it, before handing it back. "Have a safe trip, sir. Will you be in Chicago long?" Seymour shook his head. "Only as long at it takes," he replied. "I need to have a chat with somebody, and something tells me it’s going to be a fairly short conversation." Chuckling to himself as he got on board, Seymour wondered just how many more times he’d have to say that this trip.
