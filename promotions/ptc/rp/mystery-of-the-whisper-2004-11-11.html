Mystery of the Whisper

11 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-11 .:: ::: ::. The sun dawned over Napa Valley over the ashes of Gideon de Medici’s estate. It hadn’t taken long to burn, and burn to the ground. A rain had come up shortly after the vineyards had started to burn, but most of the sprawling property had been annihilated. Now, there was just a steaming pile of ruin where the beautiful house had once been. Geffroi LaBatte drove the Explorer down the dirt rode that wound through the Napa Valley countryside. He’d gotten no sleep, and had spent most of the evening biting his nails, trying to figure out whether or not this little trick had worked. It had to have worked. Mostly, it had to have worked because he didn’t know what he’d do if he’d botched the job yet again. Not only would Vittorio and Cadenza Giovanni be furious with him, but The Den might start losing faith in him as a leader. If this hadn’t worked… he didn’t want to think about that. They approached the estate from the south side, smoke still rising from the damp vineyard. LaBatte tried to take his time as the nerves rose within him. He even looked ill-groomed today, Alexis Key noticed. Her sharp sea-foam colored eyes picked up on the rather unconfident look in LaBatte’s crystalline eyes. It didn’t matter how much he tried to get rid of it, she knew it would stay there forever, especially if he’d screwed this up. Inside her head, she seethed. She’d been excited to be selected for this elect team, but she’d been completely blown away by the incompetence with which the Den Master had handled the entire situation. She stepped out of the Explorer moments after he parked, hanging back for a moment. Alexis didn’t want to jump the gun, but she just knew they’d walk into the ruins of that house and find nothing. It was an instinct, a premonition, jamais rien, if you will. Geffroi LaBatte’s heart was pounding as they started the trek up the dirt path that led to what remained of the house. It wasn’t safe to go up here, he thought. At least, maybe he could tell his patient disciples that, and maybe they wouldn’t want to go. No, they’d never buy that. They’d been through more dangerous things before than tromping through the shell of a burned out house. He couldn’t make excuses. This was his moment, and he’d brought it upon himself. Either he’d be a shining example, or a glaring failure. He wondered if it was too late to start a personal relationship with God. But then he realized that he didn’t even know how to begin praying. He cursed under his breath. LeRoy Jenkins turned to look at him. “What was that, boss?” He tried not to sneer in irritation. “Nothing, just… just talking to myself. I have a lot on my mind.” Of course he wasn’t going to tell them that what was on his mind was making sure that this little Assassin was dead so that the Giovannis wouldn’t kill him. They proceeded to the broken foundation of the house. He had to admit, they’d certainly done a good job taking care of this. If he hadn’t felt like his neck was on the chopping block, perhaps he would have been a little more appreciative of their efforts. Nothing jumped out at him from the ashes. He kicked through the closest portion of the house’s foundation with the pointy toe of his Italian leather shoe, and was alarmed at its tenacity—it would reveal no secrets to him. The four of them stood, looking over the ruins from a distance. Nothing appeared to be at all out of the normal; no acrid scent of Kindred flesh pursued them; no bleach-white bones stood out in the red earth and black ash. Austin’s face looked perturbed. “Well, I’m not just gonna stand here. If y’all want to help me, I’m gonna look through the ruins over on the west end of the house.” Austin trudged through the ash, and Alexis and LeRoy followed him. LeRoy seemed a bit concerned, and the look on Alexis’ face could have melted steel. LaBatte quickly caught up with them, particularly Alexis, who was trailing at the back of the group. Despite the fact that it was not particularly hot, sweat had begun to bead on his face. He smiled at her, a nervous tic starting at the corner of his mouth. “You look a little down, Alexis. No need to worry, Austin is probably right. The layout of these houses often has the bedrooms at the west end. They were probably there.” Alexis nodded, but as he looked into her green eyes, he could tell that she knew he was lying. That made him even more nervous. Sweat beaded over his lip. He wondered how he could possibly come up with some way to squirm out of this. No need to get so upset, he told himself. You don’t even know whether they’re dead or not. How could they have possibly escaped? “God damn!” Austin and LeRoy had reached the west end of the house before Alexis and LaBatte—Austin had dug through a steaming pile of ashes with the toe of his boot, and had come up with nothing. LeRoy’s curse, however, came from a discovery. At this end of the house, the dirt had fallen down over the top of a structure. It didn’t take much of a glance into the place, now illuminated by sunlight, to figure out what it was. A tunnel. An escape tunnel. Cold fear reached its hand through Geffroi LaBatte’s chest and squeezed, hard. Austin’s head was shaking back and forth, and LeRoy’s lips were pushed forward into a perturbed pout. Alexis was oddly silent. LaBatte was terrified to look at her. “Ain’t no big deal… we haven’t covered everywhere yet. We better check in these tunnels and out in them vines.” LaBatte was tempted to tell them no, mostly because he would rather that they just assume Shadow and Gideon were dead in the tunnels than actually go look and find nothing. He’d never been a man of instinct, but he guessed that the rock inside his stomach meant that Shadow and Gideon had escaped in one piece. Alexis knew that they had gotten away too. He could feel her standing behind him, a rock hard mass of seething, lavalike anger. She knew he was a failure. Now, it was up to him to come up with some way to save his ass. He had to figure out where the hell the two of them could have gotten away to, because if he didn’t kill them, he’d never be able to show his face in Cleveland again. Panic was starting to take hold. He got the feeling that he’d seized upon something that he could never defeat. And for Geffroi LaBatte to feel like his success was in jeopardy meant that something was very seriously wrong. Geffroi LaBatte played games, but he never lost. Dripping with sweat in the sun, he swooned, dropping to the ground like a sack of bricks. “Mister LaBatte!” Austin cried, dropping to his knees. The look on LeRoy’s face betrayed great alarm. “Shit, we gotta get him some help!” Alexis’ arms were folded over her chest. She looked down at them coldly. “My cell phone is in the car. I’ll go call for help.” They wondered why she showed no concern. They wondered how she could stay so calm in this situation, but then they supposed that was why she was one of The Den’s most promising young hunters. She stalked off toward the Explorer, her ADIDAS tennis shoes kicking up a storm of dirt that came to rest on the legs of her jeans. They couldn’t see the expression on her face, and they were more concerned about LaBatte anyway. Not her. Alexis Key couldn’t care less if Geffroi LaBatte died. He was already dead to her anyway—the most complete failure that he possibly could have been. .:: ::: ::. A light rain was falling in Trondheim, Norway as the plane taxied to a stop. This air strip wasn’t really an air strip at all, Rune thought to himself. At least it was past dark, though, so his passengers would be safe. He couldn’t recall how long he’d been hauling around Kindred, or how long he’d known… what was he calling himself now? Gideon. Yes, he couldn’t remember how long he’d known Gideon either. But he didn’t supposed that mattered. Gideon and Rune had a work relationship. They weren’t exactly companions, because they didn’t see each other often enough to be that well acquainted. In any case, Rune knew that Gideon found his tales of life as a human completely uninteresting. He found most of Gideon’s tales rather tedious as well, truth be told, considering the only thing Gideon ever talked about were his conquests. The engine of the plane slowly ceased its spinning until it reached a standstill. Rune looked out the front window. Yes, Trondheim would be an excellent place for them to hide out for a little while. There was no way anyone would ever think to look for a Ravnos and a… what did he say she was? He didn’t suppose that mattered, either. Rune didn’t have the best memory in the world, but what mattered was that he got his job done. The two of them would be safe here, so long as they didn’t do anything stupid to make waves among the locals. Gideon said he had a cabin up in the hills. Rune wondered where in the hell Gideon found the resources to have a place to stay in just about every country on the entire planet. But that was just another question that Rune didn’t ask, because… it didn’t matter. Gideon said that Rune could stay there and relax as long as he wanted since he’d come on short notice, and Rune planned to enjoy that. He needed a vacation, if not just to get away from his unbearable wife for a little while so she could cheat on him again. He figured that was the only time she was happy. He stood from the pilot’s seat and tapped lightly on the false panel that covered the cargo hold of the plane. His plane didn’t look big enough to have such a hold, but it was necessary, at least if he was planning on carrying around Kindred for any length of time. There was a muted groan, and he assumed that they’d been asleep. Did they sleep during the day? Did they sleep at all? He supposed they did. Moments later, Gideon emerged, offering Shadow a hand up the steep steps out of the plane. She’d never been to Norway, she said. He hoped she’d like it. Honestly, though, he could hardly remember what his own place in Norway looked like. He’d never used it much, save when someone had really been on his ass. This was a good occasion to revive the place. He just hoped it hadn’t fallen apart. Stepping off the plane was a breath of fresh air, sense-wise at least. He didn’t think his brother would be pleased if they stayed too long in Trondheim. But oh well. His brother would have to deal with it. He wanted Shadow to be as mentally prepared as she could be the first time his brother set eyes on her again. Well, that plus the fact that he wanted some time with her too. A slightly crooked smirk graced his face, and mischief danced in his eyes. After the night of the fire, plus the trip over here, he was beginning to see why his brother liked Shadow so much. “What are you smirking about?” Shadow addressed him with nothing short of an innocent smile on her face, as she pulled the folds of the one coat she’d managed to salvage last night around her. His smirk split into a grin as he looped an arm around her waist. “I never give away my secrets.” Rune rolled his eyes as he pulled his bag out of the back of the plane and locked it up. God, was he really going to have to endure this much schmoozing for the next few weeks? Maybe his vacation wouldn’t be quite as long as he’d planned it. As he watched his two passengers flirting with each other like schoolkids, he couldn’t decide which was worse: going home to his bitch of a wife, or watching these two paw all over each other while Gideon played Don Juan. Either way, he’d be spending a lot of time alone. .:: ::: ::. Shadow had to admit, Gideon’s place was rather impressive. Then again, his place in Napa Valley had been rather impressive too. For a moment, guilt weighed heavily on her for the fact that it was her fault that he didn’t have his Napa Valley estate anymore. But he’d told her several times on the way over the Atlantic that he really didn’t care. Of course she believed him. He didn’t care about much of anything. She’d spent the last half hour or so taking a long soak in the deep stone bathtub in the rather ornate bathroom connected to the master bedroom. This place wasn’t a sprawling mansion like the other place, but it was nice, and rather well-appointed. They’d chosen to give the guest room to Rune, but that meant they had to share a room. Not that she was opposed to it, although he’d offered to sleep on the sofa. She wouldn’t have any of it. It wasn’t like they had any secrets from each other at this moment anyway. At least not that she knew of. With her hair tied up in a large white towel, she slipped into the wood-floored bedroom. She wanted to go find Gideon, but right now, it was best for her to take some time on her own. All this excitement had gotten her distracted from her true purpose of the moment: the GTT4 tournament, and her match against Kasper Sky. She sat down on the bed, pulling out the one material possession she’d managed to salvage from the house, and then, only because she kept it in her coat pocket: a tournament information book, with pictures and biographical information on all of the competitors. The page was dogeared. She flipped it open, and there he was. Kasper Sky, staring out at her. The glossy finish didn’t do his crystalline blue eyes justice, or so she guessed. He looked like… well, he looked like a real asshole, if she had to judge. True, there might have been plenty who would have said otherwise, plenty of people who would have said Kasper Sky was one of the best and brightest lights of professional wrestling today. But they were wrong, and short-sighted, and every other negative epithet she could come up with. Since she’d seen Kasper Sky, even before she’d known she’d have to face him, he’d gotten under her skin. Has there been anyone since the start who’s believed in me in this tournament? I really don’t think so. But then again, when has someone believing in me mattered all that much. When have the opinions of other people figured into what I’m doing with myself? No, I’m not going to lie. It matters what they say. It matters that they never pay attention. It matters that Kasper Sky is the savior of the wrestling world to them, and I’m nobody, except someone who managed to get lucky. But you know what? Maybe it was luck getting into this tournament in the first place, and getting put into the position that I’m in. But everything past that? I’ve beaten Raguel, Shaman, Tsunami Oni, and Jonathan Sail. Everything past just getting my name into a bracket is all me. All MY talent. I don’t care if they figure me as an underdog until the very moment that I triumph. They’ve done it since the beginning of the tournament. It’s become a relationship between me, and all the dirt sheets. New brackets come out, and everyone’s pointing fingers and making predictions. Nobody believes in me, nobody thinks I’ll make it past this round just because they think I’m marginalized. They think I’m nothing. But nothing’s stopped me yet. They predicted me to lose against Shaman and I stomped him. Tsunami Oni was everyone’s favorite, and I made sure to send him packing too. Jonathan Sail? What can anyone say about that? I beat Jonathan Sail not because I’m lucky. I beat him because I’m talented. I beat him because from the very beginning of this tournament, I’ve had one thing on my mind: winning. Whether or not I’m always focused on the tournament has nothing to do with it. Even if I have something else on my mind, GTT4 has always been lurking. It’s been the reason that I’ve fought so hard to find my own way through the bullshit of my personal life—it mirrored the tournament. If I’d listened to tradition, I would have already been eliminated from this thing. If I’d listened to what everyone else told me to do, I never would have even had the courage to fill out an application. But I’ve hewn my own path, and I’ve walked it, no matter how hard. Look where I am now. Do they really think an ego is going to stop me? Although she dwelled inside her head at the moment, her green eyes narrowed into hard jade once again. This was a woman, both outside and in, who was determined not to take no for an answer. Kasper Sky just doesn’t seem to get it. From the very beginning, it’s been easy to tell that he only wants one thing: Deville. He can’t stand the idea of not getting to Deville. And thus far, that determination has served him well, hasn’t it? Thus far, he’s managed to just toss his competition out of the way like somebody’s dirty laundry. What he doesn’t realize is that he and I are coming from completely different angles here, and I’m something he’s not prepared for. There is no quitting. I can’t just give up now. I’ve gotten this far, and pushed myself farther than I ever could. I knew I could handle myself before, but I’d never had the chance to truly go out on a limb on my own. Now I know that I was right. Now I know that I’m a force to be reckoned with, that all those times I said that previously, I wasn’t just lying through my teeth in order to intimidate my opponents. Maybe I thought I was. But I realized that I can push myself a lot harder than I thought. I realized that even though I thought I was lying to myself, I wasn’t. And I’ve realized that all those people who told me no in the past didn’t know their ass from a hole in the ground. Corvus? Amir? Maybe they were just scared of what I have to offer. Maybe they were just scared that one day, I’d wake up and realize how all the motivation and talent that I need to do what I want to do is right under my fingertips, because it was all mine in the first place. But my eyes are open now. Kasper Sky isn’t thinking about me, I can just tell it from the way he acts. Just like everyone else in this tournament. Just like no one else has ever given me a second thought, other than being surprised when I manage to knock someone out. But it’s not like I’m just a child of luck, going through this tournament with my head up my ass. No. I’ve been purposeful and serious from the very beginning, and I’ve done what I set out to do. Maybe I haven’t always believed in myself. To think that even while I thought I was nothing, I was still succeeding as I have… well, that’s quite amazing to me. But the real difference comes now. The real test is how much I can raise the bar against Kasper Sky. I know he’s a tough opponent, but I just don’t think that getting to Deville will be enough motivation to get him past someone who’s just determined to prove that she’s not a nobody. Yeah, I don’t come with tags on my name, like Global Champion, or GTT2 or GTT3 winner. But don’t they say it’s the quiet ones that you always have to worry about? Don’t they say that you always have to watch out for the dark horse? I came into this tournament knowing that nobody knew my name, and thinking that I’d just do my business and not care. But I do care. I do care because now, I’ve made it this far, and not even my opponent is focusing on me. Hell no, I’m not Angelo Deville. I’ve never claimed to be God. I’ve never claimed to be someone so unstoppable, so unbeatable that the grass itself lies down in submission under my feet. But that doesn’t matter anymore. The blinders are about to be stripped away, because I finally see the light of day on the other end of this tunnel. It’s finally my big chance—if I take out Kasper Sky and they still don’t believe in me, then they’re just stupid. This is my golden opportunity. After years of searching, I’ve finally found the Grail. Does he really think that he can take it away from me? Does he really think that he can ponder on what he wants to do once he gets his hands on Angelo Deville this time and still stand a chance in the ring against me? Nobody else is able to see what I see when I look in the mirror. My blood surges strong through my veins. It’s the blood of a champion. It’s the blood of a woman who won’t be told no, and who will never turn away from something she wants without a fight. And I want this. I want this more than I could describe to anyone. More than Kasper Sky wants it. Because I don’t just want to face Angelo Deville so I can go down in a burst of flames. My scope is wider than that. Once I’m out of that ring, win or lose, you’re all going to remember my name. And if I have anything to say about it, the golden boy will go down in flames. Icarus is getting a little too high on his own dreams, without realizing that the foundation is melting right out from underneath him. It’s mine. But can I take it? She flipped the book shut and tossed it across the room. She was tired of looking into Kasper Sky’s honest blue eyes. She was tired of thinking about him, tired of getting more and more angry because he just couldn’t see what was right in front of him because he was so busy looking past her to Deville. He was about to run into one hell of a brick wall, and he had no clue that it was coming. .:: ::: ::. Gideon looked reflectively into the sanguine liquid that was in his wineglass. He rarely found himself without a glass full of vitae when he was just lounging around, perhaps because he felt that it gave him the elegant, refined appearance of a debauched dandy. He was better at it than Baudelaire, if he could have offered an opinion. But right now, for once, he wasn’t thinking about himself, he was thinking about Shadow. The situation was so strange; since he’d gotten her out of Cleveland, she’d pulled almost a total one-eighty. There, she’d been paranoid, disagreeable, and always sneering. But she seemed so much freer now, and happy, especially by Kindred standards. He didn’t have any delusions; he knew that it wasn’t him that had provoked the change. Perhaps, though, the change meant that she was finally coming around, finally seeing the truth. He wasn’t going to tell her—he and his brother had already talked over the fact that she needed to have this revelation on her own. He couldn’t push her into it, because she’d never see it then. He sighed. She’d seemed, a few nights ago, to be moving towards recognition. But it had stopped short of what it should have been, and now there was a sort of plateau. He wondered if he was supposed to expect that sort of thing. He took a sip of his vitae and mulled it over. Right now, she wasn’t quite stable. She was caught somewhere in between the present, and the real truth, and it was entirely possible that she’d realize it very soon. She didn’t need to be alone when she came into that realization either. He bit his lip. Yes, that meant he’d have to play steward for a few more days. He’d even have to accompany her when she had her match in GTT4 against Kasper Sky—maybe even her match with Scott Vyper too. He didn’t know how far he’d have to carry this, and that was frustrating. Was he willing to make that commitment? He didn’t figure that he had much of a choice. That frustrated him. It wasn’t that he disliked her, or didn’t want to spend time around her. It was just that… well… he was himself. And he didn’t normally deal with this much personal contact, especially with a woman. But if he didn’t get over that, he’d find himself in a rather large pile of trouble. If his brother felt at all like he’d abandoned Shadow during this period of her life, he’d be dead—deader than he was right now, and that was alarming. And he didn’t suppose he’d blame his brother. He wanted Shadow back, back in her rightful place, doing what she’d been doing before… well, before whatever had happened. He really had no clue what had gone on, and his brother couldn’t really tell him specifics either. That was why he didn’t know how to deal with the situation—nobody could tell him what had really happened. Nobody except Shadow, and he expected that she didn’t remember. But he was in this for the long haul. He had to admit, he liked her more than he already had. He wasn’t going to let anything happen to her, so she had a permanent companion, whether she liked it or not. He only hoped, for his sake, that he didn’t get too attached to her while pretending to be attached to her. That would create a rather large mess, one that he’d rather not clean up. He heard her footsteps in the hall behind him, the hall leading out to the front porch of the cabin where he sat. So, he wiped the frustration and confusion off of his face as best as he could, and prepared to pour on the charm. Luckily, it was natural, like turning on a faucet. .:: ::: ::. He made her so happy, so at ease, that it was almost difficult to function normally. It wasn’t so much that she didn’t want to be happy; she just wasn’t used to it. Whether she was used to it or not, she felt like she’d been doing nothing but laughing since she’d come out here to talk to Gideon—and making her smile, much less laugh, was no easy task. She couldn’t put her finger on what it was about him that made her so happy, and so at ease. She hadn’t felt this way around Corvus, and she’d certainly never felt this way around Amir, either. Being with Gideon was just easier—there was no bullshit, no drama. They could talk freely with each other—she felt like she’d known him for years. In fact, she couldn’t shake that sense. At the moment, there was a comfortable silence between them. They’d stopped joking and/or flirting for long enough to actually pause and think. And that’s what she was thinking about—how much he’d managed to make her open up in just the past few days. It was like she was a diary, and he was the long-missing key. “What are you over there musing about, amor?” he asked her. A few days ago, she’d shied away from him calling her that. But now, she didn’t want to. “Oh, I guess I’m thinking about GTT4 a little bit… worried about my match, since everyone’s saying that I can’t win.” Gideon didn’t look too interested. He’d never shown much interest in her career, but he shrugged, smiling slightly at her. “I don’t think what anyone else says matters much. Any question of superiority will be decided in the ring. Kine just like to run their mouths and pretend that they can predict the future.” For a moment, her brow furrowed as she considered that thought. Slowly, she began to nod. That was pretty sound reasoning, she had to admit. And he was probably the last person she would have expected to hear it from. “You know, Virgil, you’re pretty insightful for a Ravnos.” She smiled at him slightly, but his eyes widened, and he sat up in his chair. “What did you call me?” She paused momentarily, wondering exactly what had him so worked up. But then she realized that she HAD called him something different. She looked up at him, half-confused, half-afraid that she’d said something wrong. “… Virgil?” She felt like she was looking at him with a new set of eyes. That was right, somehow, she knew that was the right name, but she couldn’t have explained how she knew it. He leaned forward, nodding. “Si, si… that’s right.” He looked at her expectantly, as though he expected her to say something else, and suddenly, she felt like she was letting him down. Anxiety welled up inside her chest, tight like the constricting of wet leather. “I… I don’t know how to explain how I know that. I just do…” He nodded. For a moment, he looked away from her, running his fingers through his hair, then he turned back to her. She thought she could sense frustration, but it wasn’t in his eyes when he looked at her again. “It’s okay. It will come to you in time, okay? Don’t try to force it.” She was confused as hell. What did he know that he wasn’t telling her? She’d known from the very instant she met him that he was familiar, and that he’d known more than he let on. But she just figured that was a Ravnos for you—they had connections and could figure out things that they’d never explain. This was more than intuition. She felt like she’d infringed upon some great secret. But he hadn’t been keeping her from it—he’d been waiting for her to recognize what was going on. And now that she had? Now that she realized there was something behind her connection to him? She didn’t know what to say, or what to do. He stood up, and reached for her hand. “Don’t worry about it right now. I’m the least of your concerns.” She stood up, her brow furrowing in confusion. He wasn’t going to explain, but apparently, he wanted her to come by the knowledge on her own. She’d try, if just to make him happy that she’d figured it out. But right now, she was drawing a blank. She looked up at him. “Will you come to my match with me?” She didn’t even have to ask twice; he would have come even if she hadn’t asked, but her requesting it almost made him feel as though she actually did prefer being around him. “I wouldn’t miss it.” He wanted to say something else, but he wasn’t sure if he should. Regardless, he’d never been one to keep his mouth shut when something was on his mind. He smiled at her. “A lot of people are watching you, you know… when you pull off this win, you’re going to make them very happy.” She didn’t know what he meant by that, but she trusted him, and it made her happy to realize that she was something more than just a lone Assassin, that she was something more than just the one person who always got lost in the shuffle. She didn’t exactly know what he meant, what she was getting into. But she believed it would all work out—and she almost looked forward to untangling the mystery of this connection with Gideon—no, with Virgil. The first step, though, was taking Kasper Sky down. And she felt like with Virgil’s support, she could do it ten times over if she had to. For the first time in her life, things were falling into place, even though she didn’t know why—yet. That would come in time, just like he said it would. .:: ::: ::.
