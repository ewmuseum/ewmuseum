Love Lost

10 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-10 Even at the age of 16, Xian Phillipse was a solid figure. Sure, he wasn’t as tall; the boy was only 5’9 at his age, but he made up for that in physique. At 175 he was already toned, a body that his peers envied, women of teen and even older age desired, and even some grown men wanted his body… just not in that homosexual sense that made it creepy. All the coaches up in Bronx wanted him for their team, be it football, soccer, wrestling, track, or basketball. He wasn’t good in any of those sports except wrestling really, but they knew that if they worked with him hard enough he had the potential to be one of the best on the varsity squad. But Xian never joined any of their teams… he always told them he got enough of competitive sports at home. Xian Phillipse sat on the subway traveling through New York City, but this was not the GTT dominating XP of today. This is pre-GTT, pre-WWA, precursory to his wrestling career entirely. No, the Xian Phillipse that sat there was the Xian Phillipse of the Bronx, a teenager with short cropped blonde hair attending to Frederick Daniels High. He stuffed his hands into the pockets of his black pilot’s jacket and tilted his head back, looking up at the dingy white paneling of the subway car’s interior. "Are you all right?" A soft, gentle voice floated into his ear from the left of him. He gave a short shake of his head, exhaling deeply with his eyes locked on the ceiling above. "No… well… yeah. Yeah, I’m all right. Just thinking about everything going on; that’s it." He felt a small hand slip into his pocket and squeeze his tightly. It was warm, despite the chilling climate around them. He looked over at the owner of the female voice, finding deep hazels staring up at him. He could fall into those eyes of swirling caramel and lose track of time itself. "You shouldn’t worry too much," she said with comforting smile. That smile… Xian wasn’t a happy person by nature, but he felt a spark in his chest every time he saw that smile. It was as if he had not truly lived life until he saw that smile. "Heh, you’re probably right." Looking into that smile, everything always seemed all right. She nuzzled her thumb against his, leaning over and resting her head on his shoulder. Xian kissed her on the top of her head, smelling lavender and mint in her blonde hair. Everything about her was refreshing. Everyone and everything around them didn’t matter anymore. All that there was to life then and there was him and her, his heart. He didn’t want to do anything but make her happy; this was something new in his life. For as long as he’s know, he’s been alone bearing with the pressures of living with no one to share his pain. Now he felt that as long as she was by his side, he could do anything in the world. He felt invincible, as if no man could strike him down… because the love he had for her would protect him from harm and repel pain and death. The brakes squealed, jerking the car forward as the subway car began to halt in front of its destination. The platform began to pass by them, blurred visages of people standing and waiting to board. As the people around them began to congregate in front of the doors, XP and the girl remained sitting. She and XP had been waiting for this moment since they stepped onto the subway; it was the moment that they would have to part ways. But it was only for the night. Tomorrow, they had the rest of their lives ahead of them. For right now, however, they wanted things to remain the way they were as long as they could keep them. They wanted that moment to last forever. But as the car jerked a second time, it woke them from their beautiful dream. They came back to reality, and the fact that they had to part ways returned into their minds. The two jean-clad teens stood up slowly as everyone started to pile out, their hands latching together tighter than before. Everyone that was getting on was off the car… but XP’s girl was still there. "Remember what I said, okay?" XP looked down at her again, watching her nod at his question. She was holding back tears, staying strong in the face of her love, but he knew how she felt. He felt the same way. "I won’t forget." People began to enter the car, meaning that their time together was to an end. They moved towards the doorways, and as pedestrians flooded in around them, they shared one last kiss, a kiss that only the likes of Shakespeare could describe in words, and such words would dwarf the love and passion described for Romeo and Juliet. Their hands finally unlocked, and the white jacketed girl stepped out onto the platform of the 14th and Richland Street underground NYC subway. Chimes rang overhead, signaling for the doors to close. Before they slid together, the girl said one last thing to Xian. What was said became unclear as Xian Phillipse was jarred from his dream. Don’t you hate it when that happens? Love Lost / Lust Laden Lying in your bed, looking at the ceiling, waiting for something to happen and knowing all the time that you were meant for something better… feeling it… wanting it… but uh, how much can you take? How much can you take? How much can you take… before you snap? Jack Nicholson’s voice echoed in his ears as Yonderboi’s "Before You Snap" played on a much older Xian’s mp3 player. It wasn’t Jack’s rich, sarcastic tone that brought him out of his unplanned nap, but the nudge on his shoulder that did the trick. His eyes snapped open, and brief glimpse of the coffee mug in front of him before looking up at the brunette waitress standing next to him. "Ah, sorry," she said, gripping the edge of her serving tray tightly with black nails. Though her voice was firm, it was apologetic. "I didn’t mean to wake you! I just thought you couldn’t hear me." Her eyes went down to the floor, checking past her black slacks to the black non-slip sneakers she had on shuffling beneath her. "Or you were ignoring me… I wasn’t sure." He was still a little groggy, but XP gave her a sleepy smile, shaking his head at her. "No, I wasn’t ignoring you. I passed out for a moment. Guess I’m more tired than I thought." The green eyed girl gave a nod, her ponytail bobbing behind her. "Rough day, huh?" "I wouldn’t say that. I’ve just been a little worn out lately. I’m not from here, and staying in a hotel really isn’t comfortable to me." "Oh, where are you from?" "South Carolina." He turned around halfway, holding his oversized green cappuccino mug in hand. She started to loosen up, her kung-fu grip on the brown serving tray lessening. "Really? You don’t sound like you’re from the South." "I’m not. I live there now, but I’m originally from New York City." XP pointed at her, his left eyebrow cocking at her. "Now… you sound like you’re from… uhhhh…" She could see that if she didn’t give him a hand, he’d be trying to attempt a guess all night. "Texas, Houston, to be direct." "Ahhh, a Texan. Good western stock, huh?" He raised his cup up halfway to sip, but he didn’t complete the journey as he listened to the girl speak. "Not really. I’m Puerto Rican and Cuban, but my parents moved to Texas before I was born, so I grew up around a bunch of rednecks. Lucky for me, I didn’t catch it." He smiled at the joke, but she only gave a soft scoff, like she it was only faintly amusing to her by now. He put a halt on his coffee drinking, lowering it to the table to converse more with the black polo-fitted girl. "Yeah, that is a lucky deal. I’m pretty tired of the southern accent myself. Like I just want to stab myself in the eardrums with an icepick because it’s so annoying." He had plenty of experience with the southern accent from Vana Zell. The only time her accent wasn’t annoying was when she was moaning… and that wasn’t really something to be discussed with a complete stranger… or anyone at all. The brunette nodded, the expression on her face saying she agreed with him. She looked back over her shoulder at the coffee counter for any customers before returning to XP. "So what are you doing in DC?" "Two things, really, but the main reason is because I came in for a funeral." "Oh, I’m sorry," that apologetic tone crept back into her voice. "Well it wasn’t anyone in my family, but yeah, thanks anyways. Just some distant relative in my best friend’s family. We came up here to attend the funeral, and we’ve been in town dealing with the deceased’s belongings. That’s where my friend and her mom are now." "So what’s the second reason you’re here?" He grabbed his mug and raised it to his lips. "Having a few training sessions with a friend of mines." He skewed the cup to drink, but when his lips received no caffeine enriched goodness, he looked inside to find his mug empty. So much for being aware of things around him. He held it out to the waitress, who only looked down at it. "I’m off duty, now." "Ohhh," he sounded disappointed, but his spirits were lifted back up when she took his cup anyway and walked back to the counter. He looked back to the books he had on the table, literature sprawled out and open to various joint locks and holds from different martial arts and wrestling techniques. He had wrapped things up with a bow nicely for Wildlife and Peter Vetra, but here he was facing off against Ember. He was a multiple WWA heavyweight champion, and XP had only been a US champion. The two of them never really spoke to each other in the WWA locker room, but then again, most of the long term WWA vets didn’t really speak with the rookies. They probably didn’t expect them to last too long, and for those that managed to last longer than the others, they would be accepted, but not welcomed into the inner circle of talent that WWA held. Even as Xian beat established star after established star, they just wouldn’t accept him or his style. He remembered the remarks he got from the booker’s secretary. Your style just doesn’t fit into our main event scene. You still need more work to be a top WWA superstar. We think you should stay in the lower scene for a while longer, until you find your niche that makes you into a Jimmi Zion, Brian Arson, or a Jack Griffin quality star. …But those were the same people that XP had beaten with his ‘unorthodox’ style. XP often wondered if they just didn’t want an 18 year old kid coming in and showing grown men how to do their jobs. That would be embarrassing, losing to someone who couldn’t even buy alcohol yet, someone fresh out of high school, who never even went to a legitimate wrestling school. There were probably voices in the booker’s ears, rewording their fears into more suitable, acceptable words like ‘He’s still green. We can’t have a noob fucking up during the main event with millions or billions of dollars spent. He could make the fed look bad if he slips up just ONCE." Words can hurt, but XP would show Ember just how much his kicks hurt more. Another vanilla hazelnut became the sole property of Xian Phillipse, courtesy of his kind, eyebrow pierced waitress. She stood there and watched as he tasted it and gave her the nod of approval with a wink. Setting the cup down, he reached into his pocket of his jeans for the fee. "How much do I owe?" She shook her head at him. "Don’t worry about it, XP." His interest peaked in her suddenly. She knew him, but he didn’t know her. "Ah, so you recognize me, huh?" She nodded, looking a little embarrassed to be found out as a fan. "Just a little. I watch a little wrestling over the years. I wondered what happened to you when you dropped out of WWA a year or so ago." "Oh yeah, most people don’t know what happened. I just didn’t really want to sit around in WWA anymore. I had a feeling that they wouldn’t use me properly if I had remained in the company. You know… loads of potential, but since I was new, I’d have to pay dues before I’d be given the chance to prove I was a champion." "That’s bullshit." She covered her mouth, mumbling an apology before replying ‘That’s unfair." Chuckling, XP took another sip of his drink. "Hell, you’re right. It was bullshit. But I was satisfied with the amount of money I had made from them in the short time I worked there. I didn’t go nuts with the cash, and I saved nearly all of it, so I put it away so I could go to college." "Oh, that’s smart of you. You don’t know how long you’ll have in the wrestling business. One bad bump and your career could be over." "That’s why I wanted to have a backup." He smiled at her, holding his hand out to make his acquaintance with her. "Since you know who I am, it’s only fair that I know you, ain’t it?" She placed her hand in his, giving him a soft, warm handshake. "Demona." Sitting down, she folded her arms across her chest while looking at her watch. "So what are you doing here, by the way?" He thumbs over his shoulder, "I was training with my buddy DDR at a gym around the corner. You know who DDR is right?" She searched her mental databank for the name. "I’ve heard of her, but I’ve never seen any matches." "Download you one. She’s pretty damn good. I’m more ground and pound, and she’s more high flying and acrobatics, so we learn from each other." "That’s how life is most of the time. We’re all learning from each other, no matter if it’s good or bad things." She looked down at one of the books, flipping through the pages nonchalantly. XP took a gulp of the warmed coffee, cracking his knuckles when he put the cup down. "Well then, Demona. I’ve got to head back. Which way are you going?" She points to the left side of the Barnes and Knoble’s store. "I’m taking the bus around the corner there." Nodding, XP wraps his black and white plaid scarf around his neck before the black leather jacket goes over his black Ramones tee. "I’m heading that way, to the Browning Avenue metro. Want me to walk you?" "That’s cool of you. You’re really are a nice guy, huh?" As she stood up, she grabbed the tail of his scarf and felt the material. "Wow, this is a nice scarf. Where’d you get it from?" The normally pleasant XP dropped a few levels, his smile fading as he looked at her holding the scarf. She realized the change in his demeanor and she stared at him blankly. "Did I say something wrong?" "No… it’s just that… I got this scarf from someone that meant dear to me that passed away a long time ago." "Oh, I’m so sorry." She let go of the scarf right away, backing up and grabbing her black sweatshirt. "I didn’t know…" The awkwardness must have overloaded her, because she suddenly ran out of the bookstore, leaving X to the P standing there looking pretty awkward himself. He just stood there watching her run out, people looking up at her before returning to their books. She had some neurotic issues about her, indeed. -=-=-=- XP stood there on the subway platform alone, his hands tucked into his pockets as he listened to A Static Lullaby rip rock greatness into his ears. People began to gather around him on the platform as the floor lamps began to flash, signaling the oncoming train. The train came to a halt, the doors sliding open directly in front of him. People began to pour out of the car, XP standing there tapping his foot as he looked off with a distant look on his face. He wasn’t paying attention to anything at all around him, so when people began to enter the subway car, he didn’t notice the brunette dashing off the car and wrapping her arms around XP’s neck. Before he knew it, he was sharing a kiss with her… one that held the passion of Christ Himself within it, so much love that it was not humanly possible. The chimes rang out, signaling the doors to close. The girl backed up into the car, looking at Xian teary-eyed with a smile. As warm and comforting as that smile was… it sent chills down Xian’s back. He thought back to his dream of the girl back in NYC. He remembered the words she said to him… and now they came back to live, and in living color. It was like an echo that took years to finally catch up with him. And as Demona spoke, the firmness disappeared, and he heard a soft, gentle voice speak to him once again. "Keep me in your heart forever… okay?" He stood there lost in the swirling caramel of her eyes… frozen to the bone even as the doors slid shut, and the train took off. The gust of wind from the train wasn’t enough to make him break his focus on the girl in the car. His heart seemed to sink into his chest like a sledgehammer hit him. The scent of lavender and mint floated with the wind into his nostrils.
