GIANT

15 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-15 There are some days a man can never forget. There’s his 18th birthday, his wedding, the day he graduates, the day his first born comes into the world…all landmarks. These things are usually uplifting, always bringing a smile to your face. One of these times, though, was miserable. I met a man, a man that was desolate, incomplete, and full of nothingness. He was evil and pure at the same time, happy and sad…a case of a total mix of everything. This man made an impression on me that, to this day, I cannot forget. This man is Drake Coleman. It was just the other day that I saw him in my Church. I’m the reverend of a member less place of worship, and it really made me lonely. I stopped getting people coming to my sermons years ago, and now I just came there to see something that once was. I usually spend all my time at the church as well, being that I’m a lonely reverend. My wife died years ago and I haven’t heard from my children since the funeral. I’m getting old now, and company was hard to come by. That’s where this all falls into place. I was in the Church, sweeping the dust under the pews on a Sunday afternoon when the door creaked open. I didn’t bother to look up, figuring it was somebody lost or some kind of cop telling me something was overdue on the building. The steps were heavy as he drew close, and when I pulled my head up, I saw the silhouette of a man in a trench coat. I couldn’t make out any figures…I couldn’t really make out anything…until he came into sight. When you see Drake Coleman, it really is quite a shock at first. The most notable thing about him is the bandages. From head to toe he is covered in the off-white sutures, leaving no skin showing except his hallow black eyes. His trench coat swayed behind him as he walked, the pants tightly around his legs to match his black boots and wide-brimmed hat that hung over his face. At first, I thought it was the devil. He walked slowly and gingerly through the pews, coming closer and closer to me. I could see the evil in his shallow eyes, burning a hole into me as I stared back. I was frozen, not being able to sweep. His stride was confident yet shy, his legs and arms moving with a brisk force that was nervous as well. At first sight, he was evil. Without stopping, he marched right on past me to the front of the church, standing right underneath the cross that hung above where I used to preach. Pulling his trench coat and hat off, he dropped them on the ground next to him and stared at the stain glass of Jesus Christ behind the sermon area. I was bewildered with this man. “May I help you?” I forced out, the words stuttering from my mouth. He stood there silently, seeming to ignore me. He was broad shouldered, his body apparently well built underneath all the bandages. He stared continually at the stained glass, remaining stone cold. I stood there, wondering what was going on, whether God was going to end me right now with this warrior from hell, when suddenly, he spoke. “You know, I haven’t been here in years.” The words that came out of his mouth had the saddest tone I have ever heard in my life. I could hear the tears forming on his tongue, the letters of the word coming together with caution. I was now confused…how can this devil looking man be so…be so…be so innocent sounding? “Well,” I started, “I’m glad you’ve come…it’s a while since someone other than me has been in here…are you trying to find God?” He laughed at that, still keeping his eyes on the stained glass. He stretched his arms out in the air and yawned, slowly turning around to face me. His eyes had changed…the blackness now had a sense of loneliness and innocence in them, wanting something unknown. “Father,” he said, “In case you can’t tell by this, I haven’t believed in God for a while.” He motioned to his bandages, forcing a smile onto his face that was timid enough to force a small one out of me as well. Upon saying this, he sat on the steps that brought him to where he was standing and looked at me, keeping his eyes sharp and focused. I put down my broom and walked towards him, choosing my steps carefully. “First of all,” I said, “You don’t need to call me ‘Father’. I’m a reverend, a protestant, we don’t have titles like that…I’m happy with ‘reverend’.” I smiled at him when I said this, trying to sound warm and comforting. He kept the same look on me, so I continued. “Second…its not too late to find God…I mean…” “I’m not here to find God,” he said, cutting me off, “I’m here for…help…advice…” I could now see what he wanted. Drake was a lost man, a man who needed guidance in his life. Drake was confused…but about what? “What is it that discomforts you…um…” “Its Drake.” He said, sticking out a hand which I met with a shake. “Drake it is…what is it that you need help with?” He sat there quietly, not looking up at me and rather at the floor. I could tell it was something hard, something he couldn’t discuss. He quickly looked back at me, seeming to have found the courage to talk. “Follow wrestling?” The question was simple and precise. “No.” I said with honesty. “Well, I am a wrestler. I’ve been wrestler for years now…been here and there and everywhere…but, recently…I don’t know…things have been happening…” “Like what?” I was genuinely interested now, sitting down next to him and looking him in the face. “Well, I’m in a tournament…I’ve beaten two people, and in the third round, I am facing a woman, Tessa Windsor…” “If you can find the courage in yourself, then…” “No, no!” He said, interrupting me as fast as he could. “Beating her, or anyone, isn’t the question. I can beat people with no problem. Its just…its just…strange things are now happening…” He now stopped speaking, staring straight at the ground blankly. I was looking at him, confused and feeling pathos towards him, when I forced a question out. “What…what kind of strange things?” Slowly, he looked up at me, the insanity bursting into his eyes. He looked scared at what he was about to share, so I kept myself ready and cautious. “I am…I am seeing things. Things beyond comprehension. I see me when I was younger…I’m seeing my former flame before I went off into war…frightening things.” He was now staring at me with that same face, looking for something out of me. Way back when, people used to come with me on advice for this sort of thing, back in the 70s…but it’s been a while. I was unsure of what to think about it or what to say. “What are these things doing?” I almost didn’t want a response, afraid of what Drake might say back. He had a look to him that made him seem like anything could happen in his life. “They are stopping me…demanding me…persuading me…persuading me to…” He stopped right then and then and buried his head in his hands, the tears flowing out. This man who looked so fierce was so unstable now that, apparently, the thought of whatever these hallucinations were or are must be threatening to his well being. I put a hand on his shoulder and leaned in close. “Tell you to do what, Drake?” He lifted his head out from his hands, slowly turning it to me and staring back at the me, the dears dropping down his cheek. His mouth opened first, and then the words came out. “Telling me…to kill myself.” He looked at me coldly and blankly, his mouth eventually closing again. His eyes were now desolate of emotion, the tears stopping and the blankness returning. I kept a confident face, even though on the inside, I felt beyond bad for him. “Drake,” I started, “We all go through…rough times in our lives. We all become sad, become happy, feel different about everything…its all part of life. Is there anything that makes you happy?” “Nothing..” He said, turning his head away from me. He looked straight ahead, keeping his eyes fixed on something. I looked to see what he was looking at, and saw nothing. He was now watching something, though I didn’t know what it was. “I find that hard to believe. I mean, there has GOT to be something you like. Personally, I like writing in my journal. Its very peaceful, and…” “No,” he quickly said, “There is nothing that makes me happy. I have been through years of pain, years of loneliness, years of desolation. During the Vietnam war, reverend, I was injured badly from a burn…it brought me to a bed for years, recovering by myself in a full body cast. I was alone and, often, in the dark. I started thinking too much, my mind started rushing…and I forgot everything in my life. There was no joy in the bed, reverend, and when they let me out, there was no joy in the world. Nothing makes me happy.” I was stunned. Drake was confessing his life to me, telling me everything, and I finally saw the man trapped inside of Drake. He was a man looking for out of this world, these visions he was having were actually his thoughts, his wants. He was so delusional with the pain of life that he wanted out of it, any way possible. A tear dropped down my face. “Well,” I said, “What about wrestling? You must like wrestling.” He got up from sitting quickly and walked to a pew, standing away from me before he spun around. “I hate it.” He said, the explosion from his mouth putting me back. “I hate the violence, the pain, the suffering, the agony other innocent people suffer in the ring because of me…I hate the sorrow and misery I instill into other people, I hate the desire to win, I hate how I need to be a warrior, how I need to defend myself. I hate how everyone wants a piece of you if you’re on the top, and no one knows you if you’re on the bottom. I hate the people who don’t understand that there’s more to life than that, that will bury you as much as possible in the ring. But I do it because I am good at it, because it earns me money in which to live. But now, suicide is a friend, suicide is a buddy that I play cards with on the weekends. Money is no longer a problem, because life is in question. So, why do I keep on wrestling? Is it for the pride? The honor? Most likely…but on the inside, I know exactly what it is…” He stopped for a moment, staring at me before he continued. I waited for his response. “It’s the wanting to make everyone feel how *I* feel, to feel the pain that *I* deal with every day. On the inside, I want the world to know what I go through, to know why I’m not like everyone else…and I hate that.” Drake stood there, silently looking at me, his guts spilt before me. I stood up and walked towards him, our faces four feet away. I felt I needed to console him. “I know that you are pure, Drake, by what you have told me…don’t stop wrestling, even if you don’t like it…we are all good at something…I know you can persevere through these hallucinations…” He put his arm on my shoulder, looking at me and shaking his head. I was confused now…why was he doing this? Was he upset or something? “Reverend,” He said, “How can anyone know me when I don’t even know myself?” The question struck me like a knife, stinging. All my work, for nothing, for a question I could not answer. He knew this as well and, without a word, grabbed his trench coat and his hat and put it on. As he walked past me towards the door out, he stopped and looked back. He stared at me for a moment, a tear dropping down his face, but then quickly whipped his head back forward again. He walked out of the door and out of my life. Even though this happened only days ago, I knew I’d never see him again. Like I said earlier, there are days that every man doesn’t forget. This day, I know will last in my memory forever. This day, the saddest man I’ve ever met walked into my world and slapped me with a thought, a thought. How can anyone know a man if he doesn’t even know himself? I pray Drake will know himself some day…oh lord do I pray. -----([([([FADE TO BLACK])])])----- “How can anyone know me if I don’t even know myself?” --The The
