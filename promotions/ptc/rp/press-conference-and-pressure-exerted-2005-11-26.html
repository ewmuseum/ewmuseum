Press Conference And Pressure Exerted

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 Before The Press Conference The old-world furniture looked like it wasn’t made to support a man of the twenty-first century. Each of the pieces seemed designed for men much shorter, much smaller than the man sitting in the antique chair. However, he wasn’t concerned about such small matters because the chair had served him well for over a decade. Great furniture makers, true carpenters from a long tradition and rich history had long ago used something that modern business seemed anathema to…. over-engineering. "Gentlemen, I can’t give away too much. There are a lot of people in the press room waiting to hear what I’m going to say about the upcoming event. I’m here to give some background so your articles are more…colorful." Jake Hix leaned back against the thin rails running up the back of the chair. The familiar creaking worried the reporters around him, but not him. "Come on!" "We’ve been good to you over the years!" "You owe us!" The Big Dawg stood up, towering over the men with their pencils and paper. Jake flexed slightly, letting his three hundred pounds of muscle strain against the black tee shirt for added impact, "Guys, you want to know what I think about Michael Stevens wait for the press conference. I promise to give you the details then, and I’ll let you have the first series of questions. Now, go have some of my brother’s famous steak and I’ll be out soon. I’ll even do another round of questions just for you AFTER the press conference." Under the groans of protest, he escorted them to the door. As they walked away, he saw the one missing reporter coming up to him. Jake shook his head as Tim Goff approached. "I thought you planned on joining us?" Jake shrugged at the man. Graying more and more quickly, he had known the man for many years, but now beneath the ashen locks he was flat, gloomy was the expression covering the smart face. Tim leaned forward, "I cam late because I have something I have to ask in private." "Five minutes before the press conference?" Jake’s eyes went wide, but Tim never looked this…disturbed. "Come on in." Jake led him inside, and moved behind his desk. Time threw a manila folder onto the polished surface. The gall of the move offended Jake, but as he looked down he saw the first picture. His heart stopped…he breathed out…and the world quit spinning on its axis for an instant. "I need you to tell me why I shouldn’t run with that." The lifeless body, nailed to the wooden support beam of the barn was one he had never forgotten, but to see it here in vivid color brought an entirely new dimension to the problem. No one was supposed to know… Jake crossed the floor to the oak liquor cabinet. He pulled out the bottle he hadn’t even unwrapped yet. A pair of crystal tumblers came out next, and he returned to his desk. He sat down into the chair, the groans of the wood cried out to him. He suddenly understood their weary pain. He didn’t know what to say. "Did you hear about the special Johnny Walker blend Diageo sold?" The question echoed through the room. "What does that have to with the pictures?" Tim demanded. He wasn’t wavering, he would get to the bottom of this no matter what it took. Jake turned, staring at the large picture on the wall. His entire family was standing around the ranch house in the family portrait, "You see, to celebrate the 200th anniversary, they put together this amazing blend. Bottles were auctioned off for twenty-five thousand each. I bought eleven bottles, and ten will be auctioned next year, with proceeds to charity." "Trying to buy me off with a drink?" Tim pushed the tumbler near him aside with two fingers. He tapped the pictures, "I’m not that cheap. This is important Jake." Turning his head back, the dark azure eyes were filled with unfettered contempt, "You should watch yourself Tim. You seer, I don’t love drinking, but I like it. I dropped that much money to taste this liquor. What you have there is about something, someone I love." Jake unwrapped and opened the bottle. He poured the gold liquid into his glass and drank it down, "Have I ever lied to you Tim?" Tim didn’t back down, "Only recently." The Press Conference Jake Hix listened to his introduction. He walked out to an explosion of flashing lights from the cameras as he made his way to the podium. Jake couldn’t see anything as he stepped up and gripped the sides of the lectern with his fingers. The bright circles of light burned into his eyes faded away. He looked over the room, and there was Tim Goff against the wall, staring intently at him. Words didn’t form on his lips or in his mind. He seemed unsteady and unsure of himself as he looked out over a larger group of reporters than he was used to dealing with. His heart stopped…he breathed out…and the world quit spinning on its axis for an instant. Tim knew everything, and the moment of revelation might be at hand. He didn’t know what Tim would do with his information, and if their long relationship meant anything next to the final, big story of his career. The awkward silence only grew as he stood…a deer in headlights…unsure what to do.
