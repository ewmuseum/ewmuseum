Date: 2006-12-02 You’ve taken everything away from me, Karla. I’ve only just begun. # Wolfgang surveyed his surroundings looking for any sign of his daughter, Angela. He continuously tapped the steering wheel of his black Land Rover, trying to recall anything Angela said that could have been a hint. But nothing came to mind. Sighing, he took a glance at the empty passenger seat next to him. He wasn’t used to this. Not anymore. He drove everywhere alone when Angela was young enough to have babysitters. But when she became a teenager, she was old enough to go on trips with him. Angela didn’t like that – the typical rebellious teenager. To Wolfgang, it was heaven though. It was his way of trying to catch up on all the years he missed with her as a child, because he couldn’t be a full time dad. Now the seat next to him was empty, and it was his fault. No, the seat wasn’t empty. Laid down gingerly with great caution was the golden mask of the Sentinel. Remembering Charles’s warning to forget about the Sentinel, Wolfgang couldn’t help but take the mask with him. The mask was him after all. How could he forget about being someone he’s been for fifteen years? "We’ve been through a lot," Wolfgang said to the mask as if it were a real person, or possessed by a spirit – hopefully benevolent. "You were the only one that knew how to deal with my loss," Wolfgang continued. "You stayed quiet." The mask lay on the seat without saying a word. "For fourteen years, I’ve worn you to matches. I wasn’t hiding myself. I was just trying to hide." Wolfgang sighed, recollecting the reason he became the Sentinel and the reason he was stubborn upon giving it up. "Charles was right," Wolfgang chuckled, glancing over to the mask. He paused for a moment, expecting a reply. "He said I was hiding. He was right. I was. I was hiding from all the people who had the nerve to tell me they knew how I felt. They wanted to help me. Well, you know what? Fuck them!" A small speed bump was the only thing that forced a reaction from the golden mask. "How the fuck did they know how I feel about losing Abigail?" Wolfgang cried out. "What makes them think that a fucking ‘I’m sorry’ Hallmark card is going to be enough to bring her back. I spent half my life winning her love and she was taken away from in one night. You call that fair? You call that life? Answer me!" If the mask could react, it properly would have reacted with a grin. Wolfgang could have sworn it actually did. "But then you came into my life," Wolfgang said. "You gave me the opportunity to hide. Hiding in your protective fabric, no one knew how much I hurt inside. No one knew who I really was or why I wore you in the first place. All they knew was that you were the Sentinel – the pinnacle of sportsmanship and altruism. Everybody loved you because you were what everyone wanted to be." Wolfgang took a right turn, having reached the end of the town. It was time for him to double back and continue his search. "But if they only knew what you really were. If they only knew you were a coward, running away from the pains of the truth. If only they knew that the person beneath your shadows was a man who was afraid to deal with reality. If they knew that, maybe they wouldn’t love the Sentinel so much. Maybe I wouldn’t be out here looking for my daughter when she should be home in her bed, talking for hours on her cell phone while I worry about boys calling her." Wolfgang stopped at a red light and stared at the golden mask of the Sentinel that had almost shifted to the edge of the seat. He wanted the mask to reply back. He needed someone to reply back and tell him that everything was going to be all right. "So, what is it going to be like now? Is it going to be another fourteen years of hiding from the truth, from my own daughter? Am I going to be in your shadows pretending to be the great savior of the Atlantic Indy Wrestling Federation, when in truth I can’t even help myself? Is that how it’s going to be?" Wolfgang shook his head. It had taken him fourteen years to finally realize what he should have realized the first time he put on the mask to hide from dealing with the loss of his wife. "No, it won’t be that way. Not anymore. I’ve hidden from the truth far too long. I owe Angela something better than wrestling, something better than GTT6. That’s right. The GTT6 may be the biggest thing you’ve ever been in. But that’s the problem – it’s the biggest thing you’ve been in. This isn’t for me. There are far more important things in life than a once a year tournament. Maybe next year, you can find someone else to hide in your shadows and win GTT7 for the sake of winning the ransom money to save their wife and kids. I don’t know." Wolfgang clutched the mask in his fists. For the first time in a long time, the mask felt a bit lighter. He crumbled up the golden fabric in his hand. "I don’t need you anymore. I’ll defeat the Phantom Republican, not as the Sentinel, but as Wolfgang Moriarty. That’s who I really am. This is the end of the road for you, Sentinel." Wolfgang lowered the window to the driver’s side and cautiously held the mask outside the truck. "I’m going to tell Angela the truth. I’m going to tell everyone who I really am. Then people will no longer cheer for the Sentinel. Instead, they’ll cheer for the real me. There’s no point in hiding anymore." Wolfgang released his grip on the golden mask and the mask fluttered to the pavement. Wolfgang watched mask be run over and over on the rearview mirror. I’ll pray that no one finds you, he thought. Then suddenly, his eyes widened. "Of course!" Wolfgang cried out, remembering the last conversation he had with Angela. "The church!" Wolfgang quickly cut a tight corner, scaring a couple of pedestrians out of their shoes. "Why didn’t I think of this before," he muttered. He knew where Angela was now. It was so simple. She was at the only place she could ever be at this point – the abandoned church house. Wolfgang quickly raced to Angela. He hoped he wasn’t too late. # Angela pushed herself away from Karla, wiping the tears that flew down her cheeks. She wasn’t fast enough. Struggling through broken sobs and whimpers, she asked, "I don’t understand." Karla wiped her own tears away. She truly deserved an Oscar. "What do you not understand?" "Dad always said that Abigail was my mother," Angela explained. "He never mentioned anyone else." "And how did you get that picture?" "I found it in a black box my dad keeps," Angela answered. "There were a lot of things there I didn’t understand." "Like what?" Karla asked, wiping her tears again. Her eyes were blood red. "But it all makes sense now," Angela said, jumping to the wrong conclusion. "He kept saying my mother was gone, but he never told me why." Inside, Karla laughed hysterically. She knew what had really happened to Abigail, and was surprised that Angela still did not know after all these years. "Go on, sweetie." "And I remember feeling so weird when I opened that block box," Angela continued on. "It was like I was in the twilight zone or something. Instead of seeing pictures of my dad and Abigail, I saw pictures of you." This was the signal Karla was waiting on to continue her seduction of Angela, "Well it would make sense. Your dad and I have been going out for years." Angela nodded. It was so obvious to her now. Why hadn’t she seen it before? Karla was her real mother, not Abigail! "Why did you leave?" "Abigail," Karla lied. "She split us up. But I regret it everyday." "I’m sorry." "No, I’m sorry," Karla said. "I never should have let that whore ruin our lives. But I’m here now, baby. I’m here and no one is going to hurt you." "You’re not going to leave me?" Angela asked. Karla shook her head. She cried once more, "I promise you, I won’t ever leave you again." Embracing Angela in a tight love-infested hug, Karla smirked behind her back. You’ve paid your dues, Abigail Moriarty. # Flashback: A little over 15 years ago "I can’t guarantee any side effects, but I can guarantee that you will have a child of your own." Abigail embraced Karla in a tight hug, thanking her over and over. "I don’t do this often and yours is the first case I’ve done in two years," Karla said. "I want you to know that this vial here is the only one of its kind." "I can’t thank you enough." Abigail repeated through her tears. "You can have anything you want." Karla smirked, "Yes…about my reward…" Abigail simmered down preparing herself for the price she would have to pay for the chance to have a child of her own. She would have been better off praying, instead. Karla grinned at her wickedly, "The child will be mine." Abigail arched her eyebrows. She wasn’t sure that she heard correctly. "Excuse me?" Karla cleared her throat and stared at her with demanding eyes. She said, "The child born of this will be mine. Once you give birth, it’s going to be mine." Abigail was at a lost for words. "Did you think I was going to make this easy? Did you think I was going to help you without anything in return?" "But….but…" "That’s the price. You want my help? You’re going to have to deal with it." "But that’s fucking ridiculous!" Abigail protested. "I can’t do that!" Karla shook her head. She was disappointed in Abigail. "How many doctors have you seen before me?" "About a dozen or so," Abigail answered. "What’s that got to do with anything?" Karla fiddled the vial in between her fingers like it was a toy. With each rotating flip, she could see Abigail’s eyes widen and heart race, afraid that she would drop her miracle and shatter her life, "You’ve seen a dozen doctors and they all told you the same thing?" "Yes," Abigail answered, submissively. "There’s no way for me to have a child." Karla nodded, "And now I’m giving you that chance and you want to throw it all away?" "But if I do accept it," Abigail explained, between her broken sobs, "I will be giving it all away! You can’t make me do this! Please." Karla was never one for emotional theatrics. When her father died, she asked what time the late night movie was playing. Other people’s suffering was not her concern. Thus, watching Abigail cry before her knowing she was helpless didn’t bother Karla at all. "If you do not want to agree to it, that’s your problem." Karla tipped the vial between her fingers and held it gingerly, teasing the vial with the concept of falling down to its shattering death. She peered at Abigail, "Then you will continue to live a wasted, barren life. You’re nothing more than a black hole – a plague among real women. You waste our time and our space with your deficiency. Go and adopt a child." "Stop it!" Abigail pleaded, blocking her ears, trying to muffle the sound of Karla’s voice. "And remember when you’re holding that child that no one loved, that somewhere some woman is more of a woman than you will ever be. And she is out there fucking and having babies, because she can. She’s sleeping in hotels, sleeping in cars, because she knows she can just give a child away, while you are sitting at home watching your daily soap operas and Oprah Winfrey specials, because you couldn’t develop a seed in that barren wasteland you call a uterus." "Stop it!" Abigail cried. "Please." "You’re barren, Abigail," Karla continued her insult. "How do you think Wolfgang feels when he’s inside you? Do you know what goes on in his mind? He’s praying that his dick doesn’t fall off. When he’s with me, he’s in heaven. I give him the life and richness he sorely misses every time he sticks his cock into your vagina, solely for the purpose of fulfilling his role as a husband." "I’ll do it" Abigail blurted out between her sobs. Karla grinned. "I knew you would." "But you have to let me be with the child for a bit," Abigail tried to negotiate. "Please don’t take the child away from me." "I’ll consider it." "You’ve taken everything away from me, Karla." "I’ve only just begun." Abigail wiped her tears. The pact with the Devil was already made and she couldn’t turn back. She could, however, change the deal. She agreed to the outlandish request on the hopes that she would just deny the child. She could run away. Yes, she could run away. She would never have to give up her child. Abigail left the office without so much of a thank you. Karla watched her leave with curious eyes. I know what you’re thinking, Abigail. But one way or another, that child is going to be mine. # Wolfgang screeched the Land Rover onto the sidewalk. It was illegal, but he didn’t care. He had arrived at the old abandoned church building. He knew this was where Angela was. Whether she was here against her will, he couldn’t say. Either way, he was going to bring her home He knocked on the door. No answer. He knocked once more, but still no answer. Wolfgang tested the doorknob. The door was open. He peered into the building. He could hear noises in the back, someone was obviously here. He had heard the sound of children playing in the backyard when he first drove up to the church. But now he was sure that there was someone inside the building. He couldn’t make out the voices, but the two voices sounded female – and they both sounded very familiar. He approached the sound of the conversation. When he reached the entrance way to the main room, he stood in silent awe. Sitting before him, hugging each other like mother and daughter were Angela and Karla. They didn’t notice him at first, but then Angela did and she quickly broke her embrace with her mother. "What the hell are you doing here?" Abigail asked enraged. She wanted nothing to do with the man who had lied about who her real mother was. "Angela," Wolfgang warned her. "Get away from that woman right now." Karla giggled. She felt like she was at a carnival and had won every prize in every game. "Why do you want me to leave my mother?" Angela cried out. "Do you want me to forget about her, too?" Wolfgang shook his head, bewildered. "Your mother?" "YES!" Angela cried out, hugging Karla. Unbeknownst to her, Karla grinned and winked at Wolfgang. "Honey," Wolfgang replied, trying to keep calm. "She isn’t your mother. That no good for nothing She-Devil is not your mother!" "Stop lying to me!" Angela cried out. "You expect me to believe you’re telling the truth now? You expect me to believe you after all these years of lying to me?" Wolfgang clenched his fist tightly. The irony pained him so. He had lied to his own daughter for fifteen years that when he finally told her the truth, she didn’t believe him. And of all the things he could be honest about, this was the most important of them all. "Angela, please, listen to me," Wolfgang pleaded, approaching Angela. Angela walked away from him in fear. "You stay away from me!" Wolfgang nodded in agreement and stood in his place. "She is not your mother. I know that I haven’t been honest with you. And you deserve to not trust me now." "Why should I trust you?" Angela demanded. "You never tell me anything." Now it was Karla’s time to chime in with the insight only a mother could possess, "Angela, darling, you should give him a chance He is your father after all." "Shut up!" Wolfgang bellowed at her. "I don’t know what the hell you told her or what you’re trying to do here, but stop it! You know damn well that you are not her mother!" "How can you talk to mom like that." Angela defended her mother, Karla. "Abigail is your mother!" "Stop lying!" Angela insisted. "That whore is not my mother." Wolfgang gasped. Inside, he cried a little. He wondered just how much poison had the succubus, Karla, inflicted upon his daughter’s mind. "Angela, please listen to me." "No you listen to me," Karla chimed in. "Did you tell her that Abigail couldn’t have any children of her own?" Wolfgang shook her head. "That was what she was sick with, wasn’t it?" Angela asked, recalling the conversation they had, had over their dinner date weeks earlier. "You wouldn’t tell me then, would you tell me now?" Wolfgang nodded. There was no more point to hiding it, "Yes, she was sterile. What does that have to do with anything?" Karla grinned. She excused herself from Angela and walked towards Wolfgang. "What’s going on?" Wolfgang’s eye grew wide. "What are you talking about?" Karla smirked.
