Bad Luck/Good Luck

22 September 2004

Date: 2004-09-22 “...So I finished shooting in Milan for an Oxygen photo shoot and I’m waiting for word from my agent about….” In one ear, out the other. The disenchantment was clearly written all over the face of Jamar Gordo, not even feigning interest in the conversation at hand. It was a constant déjà vu he could never escape from, one he tried to run from so much, he just learned to deal with the torture until it passed. How many nights could a man don an obscenely expensive Gucci suit, have a bottle of 300 dollar plus champagne at hottest dining establishment, and enjoy the company of the flavor of the week 36-24-36 supermodel before it all became a total bore. There he sat in the C’est La Vie, his ebony forehead buried in the palm of his hand as his mind focused on everything but the obvious. The Chardonnay had lost its almost orgasmic tingle. The food had nowhere near the robust flavor promised by the chef. Even the curvaceous beauty sitting across from him had lost her attractiveness, a woman that millions of men would be masturbating to come her next photo shoot release, couldn’t even get the movie star to keep his eyes upon her for five whole seconds. Any other red-blooded male would call Jamar insane for what it is he DIDN’T feel at the moment, but for him, it has always been about one thing….something he was denied in the first round of the GTT. A fight. Therein was the problem with Gordo. He was only alive when he fought. So needless to say, a win being handed to the master of All Styles by no means set well within his mind. Could he have beaten Matrix had he shown up? Or maybe, how badly could he have beaten Matrix had he shown up? Would this be a reflection on the next round? It was the questions that ate him up inside and kept him from at least faking enjoyment of the night. “Jamar...JAMAR,” called his lovely date from across the table, concern mixed in with the sternness of her voice as she leaned forward. His hand finally parted from his forehead as he looked towards her for the first real time of the night, almost perturbed that she had disturbed his train of thought. “Jamar, what’s wrong? You haven’t seem interested in anything I’ve said all night,” she stated as she tried to understand what the problem had to be, genuinely concerned at why his attention was fleeting. His index finger traced the rim of his champagne flute as he honestly took a good look at his date, the lovely supermodel Dawn Carter. The playboy in him wanted to correct the situation, citing how lovely her soft pink, flower dress was. He could have even inquired more into what little he heard about her life, like the Oxygen photo shoot. Frankly however, he was sick of being around the same stuffed shirt elitists. The classical music invading his ears drove him to the edge of insanity. HE DID NOT WANT TO BE HERE. What he wouldn’t give to feel his shin smashing into the face of a challenger, or the feel of his muscles burning after a 40 minute fight that just would not end….the urge could not be denied. The way a cannibal needed human flesh, he needed to fight. Suppressing the urge was no longer an option. Dawn asked yet again, now slightly annoyed at his lack of a reply, “Jamar, what’s wrong?” Jamar looked to her once more, but it was no longer the lifeless look he had settled into the entire night. It was now the look so many knew him for, the cocky Cheshire grin that angered people towards him before they even could know his name. The ego that even the sun couldn’t eclipse reared its ugly head as he replied to the lovely Dawn Carter, as if he had been meaning to tell her this since the moment they met. “Look Dawn, to tell you the truth, I could care less about this date. Let’s be real, I wine you, I dine you, and then we come to the only conclusion this night could have come to….your panties, G-string cut from the outline of your dress, lost somewhere in my condo after marathon fucking. After of course, I’d cuddle to be nice but then make it a point to lose your number like a Catholic school girl’s virginity at a frat party.” The onslaught kept coming, even with her mouth wide open in shock. “Shame” was nowhere to be found in Gordo’s vocabulary. “Also, piece of advice. You’re a COVER MODEL...meaning you pose for a cover, smile, get paid. Movies, please. You better hope you have a mouth like a Hoover if you expect to find some decent work in this town. Now if you’ll excuse me, I tend to handle business more pressing. Maybe a root canal or a few hours on a torture rack, anything other then this,” assertively stated Jamar as he reached in his suit pocket and threw 400 dollars down on the table as if pocket change. “There’s money for the wine and food. By the way, PLEASE don’t ever speak in front of a man again...that voice makes them lose the hard on.” He uprooted himself from his seat and like a good assassin, although in a verbal sense, never stayed to watch the body drop as he left her aghast at the table. He felt a calming release as he left an establishment of fine cigars, suits, and socialite bullshit by walking right out the door. The city air hit his nostrils, the aroma of unpredictability and danger enticing Gordo, his smile filled with child-like wonder at the hustle and bustle of fast cars and even faster people. If he wanted his night to end right... ...It had to end with a fight. Seeing as how he wasn’t always a millionaire, he knew what street life was like and he knew just where he could find a fight. The fight that could set his mind right, at least long enough to make the urge subsidize. Downtown / 1:15 am / Your Average City Streets “I told you to have the money and now you’re pissing your pants when I come to collect?!” John Wallace was by no means “Mr. Lucky”. A man of a short, stubby stature with thinning hair and “coke bottle” glasses; Wallace always seemed to get passed over when it came for the offerings of luck. He repeated the 12th grade thanks to being a credit short, strived to be a nutritionist (yet had high cholesterol), and got the first woman he made love to pregnant...thanks to a faulty condom. Flash forward to today, as our good friend John happens to be faced with the choice of life or death. With apartment rent, alimony (yes from his first lay people), and nagging bill collectors leading to him taking a loan from the burly gentleman in his company in your everyday, seedy alleyway. Did he pay the money back you ask? Then answer this question. Do loan sharks put a gun down your throat if they got their money back? Didn’t think so. Wallace was a trembling mess, the sweat racing down his forehead like a set of Olympic sprinters, unable to shake the taste of greasy metal on his palette. He watched as the loan shark, dressed in a muscle tank and baggy jeans, a mural of various scars and tattoos from past experiences. At this moment, they say the average person’s life would flash before their eyes…mostly the comforting things that made their life worth living. Romantic evenings. Grand achievements. Spiritual enlightenment. Wallace had NONE of these. All life ever gave him was a boot in the ass and a punk card he picked up time and time again. Never did Wallace get a shining moment in his life and he was a fool for thinking it would ever come to him. Deep down in his soul, a part of him wanted the thug with the gun to squeeze the trigger and let the bullet waiting in the chamber to raise through the barrel and rip though the back of his skull. However, his dilemma set in. Who in the hell would think to mourn a sad man like John Wallace? An unremarkable man with nothing of note to his name, noted maybe only for his string of abysmal luck, which he would soon become a victim of tonight. “Nod your head yes and shake your head no! Do you have our money or not,” forcefully asked the thug, the Desert Eagle planted firmly in his mouth. John’s eyes watered as he shook his head no, lying not even an option in the situation he was in. “Do you WANT me to splatter your shit all over this alley,” he chimed in angrily, making an unnecessary gun hand gesture to John’s head. Unnecessary in the fact that one was already clear and present, that gesture was merely an insult to injury. His head shook nor nodded, unable to decide whether life was worth living or if death was better then continuing his horrid existence. The alley grew uncomfortably quiet, only the sound of paper smacking the concrete streets audible at the moment. The common thug held Wallace’s life in his very hands and judging by the look on his face, he didn’t feel like holding it anymore. Enter Jamar Gordo. Walking around for what must have been two hours, Jamar felt almost dejected that he was unable to find a fight anywhere in the city. He had looked to test his skill in various clubs, gatherings, and bars all over, yet came up as dry as a dolphin in the Sahara. It had seemed as if Gordo had given up as he trekked the busy streets, merely glancing in the aforementioned alley casually, and to say he was quite intrigued by what he found was an understatement. He had seen this before. Man is unable to pay up and feels his brain matter rocket outside his skull before dying. Jamar almost found the situation amusing, looking about at the droves of people who merely pass by the alley and choose to turn a blind eye to the situation Wallace had found himself in. “Geez, this superhero shit is not my style...but a fight is a fight,” remarked Jamar to himself. Whether he saved the man or not was of no consequence to him. Just as long as he got into a good scuffle, he considered it time well spent. Almost symbolic, Gordo stepped away from the illuminated city streets, where the average person looked to bath in their safety at this time of night. Instead, he stepped into a world he was strangely familiar and enthralled with, like a woman he knew lusted for and didn’t love. He traversed into the darkness until he was showered in it, his Armani covered footsteps growing louder and louder until finally catching the attention of the thug and the unlucky Wallace himself, both sporting eyes of widened surprise at their added guest. “What the fu-,” the gun-wielding thug’s “welcome” interrupted by the strangely calm eccentric Gordo, “I believe I know what is happening right about now. He would have never stayed alive this long unless it was about money. Am I right?” As much as the thug wanted to unleash a hot round upon Jamar’s head or chest, the martial artist certainly knew what he was talking about. Wallace seemed like a man too simple to understand what was going on, choosing to settle with the gun in his mouth as the thug replied in a slightly annoyed manner, “What’s it to you? You obviously ain’t no cop and I don’t think you know this motherfucker, so what’s this got to do with you? I’d mug you if it weren’t for the fact that this bitch is more pressing. So get your Armani ass out of here while I get my mo-” He was interrupted again. This time not by words, but by cold hard cash between the fingers of the cocky martial artist. “What’s the price on his head? Let me guess? $1000? $2000? How about some dick sum like say, $5000? I bet you can’t even afford to WINDOW SHOP at the Armani store with this nickel and diming of yours. So I’ll tell you what, whatever his price is...I’ll give you 20 large. That is, if you can beat me in a fight,” challenged Gordo with his usual abundance of confidence, dangling the bait at his potential fight. The adrenaline was racing for Jamar right now, no price was too high for a fight. The offer was so enticing, the Desert Eagle finally was removed from the mouth of his victim, Wallace usurping as much fresh air as possible. With the gun aimed at Wallace’s forehead, the thug turned an eye to Jamar and skeptically questioned, “So what’s in it for you if you win? Why the fuck do you even care about this guy?” The thought made him actually ponder for a moment, Jamar even moreso surprised such an intellectual question would come from the ruffian’s mouth. Silence filled the air until his answer finally escaped his lips, “I don’t care. I’m just looking for a challenge. If I win, most likely you’ll be in the hospital. Therefore, I guess he’d go free. Now, do we have a deal or not?” “Put your money in your pants pocket then. After I knock you the fuck out, I’ll get it after.” Challenge accepted. “Get your ass against that wall, I’ll deal with you after this jackass,” ordered the thug, Wallace burying himself in the alley corner subordinately as his fate was to be decided by a man who would surely see him dead and a stranger. The thug squared his feet and his fists were at the ready, Jamar dashing aside his Gucci suit coat carelessly into the grimy alley. He couldn’t help but smile, because the beginning of a fight was like Christmas Eve for him that tingling moment of anticipation. One hand behind his back like an English gentleman and the other in a “come on” motion to his challenger, Gordo stood at the ready for his opposition. Win or lose, Jamar had found his peace at last. 5 Minutes Later… A hush had come over the alleyway, the type of hush meant for mass prayer or the ideal library setting. There he sat, exhausted and a mural of bruises and blood...Jamar Gordo had won, but you could tell he was put through hell to get it. Blood was spattered randomly on his dress shirt with low red bruises on his face. It didn’t matter however. The precious life force lost and the cosmetic damage were merely arbitrary. It was the feeling he felt right at that moment, the tingle of victory that mattered. Jamar was in his own private heaven, even with the dank surroundings about him. Victory for him was the orgasm people dreamt of. Even though it wasn’t the orgasm that came from sex...it was damn close. It was the feeling he was denied the first round of the GTT and he did what was needed to substitute it. That moment, still taking in oxygen, he looked over lackadaisically to his fallen opponent. There he laid, his bare knuckles brandishing Jamar’s blood as he laid face down on the concrete. His own blood made a tarnished mix with the water he fell in, his world assured to be dark for an unknown amount of time. Gordo’s eyes then shifted to the victim of circumstance in this situation, John Wallace, unveiling himself from behind the garbage cans now that he knew the dust had settled. To say he was aghast at what he had seen was like saying Antarctica was just “cold”. Sheepishly, he crept over to the thug who just moments ago held John’s life in his very hands, amazed at how much the tables turned in 5 minutes. He couldn’t resist the urge of tapping him with his sneaker-covered toe, the thug returning no movement or action. Both the martial artist and the unlucky Wallace had met eyes finally, the bad luck stricken man almost as shocked at the amount of damage Gordo took as he was the knockout of his assailant. It was safe to say John was the dead cat, because curiosity was killing him at that very moment about Jamar Gordo and his intentions. The urge to question what had happened was not even rebuked by Wallace as he asked, “Why? Why do this? Why help me? You seem like a well off man. Why put yourself through this punishment?” Who knew just looking for a fight, a great physical tasks unto itself, would lead to so many brain-teasing questions? Even in his weakened state, Gordo entertained the question from Wallace as he replied slowly, yet with that same cocky demeanor, ”Because I’m a very…VERY fucked up individual. Besides, you just happened to be in the right place at the right time, so if a “thank you” is coming, save it for something more productive. All I wanted was a fight, I got it….end of story. If I had gotten any of the other fights I tried to start today, I guess you’d be doing your best Jack-O-Lantern impression…seeing as how you’da had a gaping hole in your head.” Jamar made no attempts to get up at that moment, still waiting for more air to course through his veins. John felt slightly dejected by the movie star’s comments, Jamar almost able to reaffirm the worthlessness he had settled into. But something strange happened...he didn’t. Just when he was ready to curse out Jamar for being such a prick when he saved his life, John had remembered….that very same man lying before him in a Gucci suit had brought the greatest streak of luck to his life he had EVER had. No matter how cocky he had seemed or how shady his intentions were, luck was luck and Wallace’s brain matter was still safely tucked in his skull where it belonged. “Listen. Sir, you are quite possibly the greatest thing to happen in my life, ever. You may not want to hear that. Hell, you may not even care. Fact is, you coming along has been the only good thing to happen by chance in my life, period,” John paused, now finally feeling a smile overcome his face as he began to fully cope with what he was saying. Luck may have booted him in his ass many times during his life, but it came through in spades that night…and it was wearing Gucci. Jamar took that moment to look Wallace up and down. It didn’t take long for his mind to settle into how worthless the man was in the mainstream of life. Gordo knew because he crushed people like him, mentally and physically, throughout his career. But no matter how much of an asshole Jamar was…he wasn’t a FUCKING asshole. He realized with the genuine sparkle in John’s weary eyes that his thought of him was sincere. That and Jamar had never been an omen of good luck for anyone before. It certainly was different from some of the other infamous titles he earned through the years. “Look in the pocket of that suit jacket over there,” Jamar instructed to John while pointing at his fallen white suit jacket, dirt speckled on it. Cautiously, John picked up the suit and dug his hand in deep, unearthing it to find a several rolls of cash...four of them, romance novel thick, and each with nothing but 100 dollar bills. “I don’t know how much that is and I don’t care. You say your luck is bad? Buy new luck,” the God of Gucci stated, with a different smile. One of a hidden kindness he chose not to show regularly, that smile substituting for his usual conniving smirk. He could have cried right then and there. In a matter of five minutes, 29 year old John Wallace went from a human stain on the planet Earth to being handed both his life and a means of making that same life better. Only two words came to mind for this. “Than...,” but they were quickly interrupted by a halting hand by Jamar. “Save it. My karma tab is still going to be dangerously negative whether you do or not. Just take care. Fucking keep the jacket too, but YOU BETTER WASH IT! I coulda paid off your car with how much that cost...cold water too.” As harsh as those parting words were, John took no offense. The smile the two shared certainly seemed like enough, both understanding what had taken place even if it wasn’t acknowledged. He turned away, after stuffing his monetary gift back in the jacket of course, and turned to leave the alley with a million dollar smile tattooed on his face. There was comfort in that smile as John joined the world again, leaving the cloak of darkness in the alley in exchange for the brilliant light of the city streets. There was comfort in the fact that maybe his prayers were finally answered. His luck had changed, thanks to a complete stranger. He may have no said it out loud, but in his mind, it resonated over and over like a syrupy chorus. “Thank you. Thank you. Thank you.” However, the story wasn’t over for the man sitting calmly, his energy spent as he sat in the alley. BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRR~! BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRR~! His eyes widened, almost surprised his Nokia phone has survived the battle from before. He wrestled with his pants pocket to unleash his phone, finally acquiring it as he waved it at the thug he had bested. “I suppose you’re not gonna answer it,” insulted Gordo, flipping the phone open as he answered it. “Yeah...wassup. Where am I at? Heh, you wouldn’t believe me if I told you. Global Title round 2 opponent? What’s the deal? Ya, I’m aware of the bracket shuffle......(pause)........hey, you know that shit called “the point”, get to it Alex.” It couldn’t be possible. Well, maybe. It depends on how superstitious you are. Is it possible for the luck of one man to transfer to another? With the announcement Jamar’s agent had gave him those following seconds, he certainly was a believer at that moment. Of all the men he could have drawn....HIM. Was it a trick by the god(s), a spiteful tactic of the booking committee, or perhaps a cocktail of both? Either way, how could you not say his luck had not downgraded drastically, especially given his lack of competition last round as compared to the daunting mountain he was given this round. “Fuck. You are kidding me right now. Seriously-,” he took in a soft breath then continued, “Violence Jack. Fucking lovely. No calls until after that match...I have some work to do. I know what I said. You don’t enter Hell and not bring water with you Alex! Take care.” He clicked the phone off, leaning his head back on the wall as he stared off into the space above him, the moon particularly beautiful that night. “Yea, I’m scared. But more importantly then all that....I’m a cocky motherfucker. Do your worst Jack, and I’ve been told how bad you can get. Don’t worry….I’ll bring mine. I swear on my life you religiously warped bastard...,” ranted Gordo in a cocktail of insecurity and anger. He pressed off from the ground, getting to his feet, albeit shaky. With a hand pressed to the wall, he wiped the blood on his face away with his shirt sleeve, turning to his fallen opposition for the night and finishing his uncharacteristically enraged spiel, as if Jack was there, ”You’ll fall to me like this weak motherfucker right here! YOU HEAR ME?! I’m the MASTER OF ALL STYLES! ME, JAMAR GORDO!!! I can beat you….I can beat you....I...can-fucking...beat...you.” He slowly made his exit for the streets much like Wallace did, his mind and his body wanting this fight more then anything. They would pull him through as they did every other daunting fight....but then there was his heart. That lingering, pulsating heart that contradicted everything Jamar stood for. It said what Jamar was too afraid to say. It said what Jamar was too scared to come to serious grips with, from the moment he found out Jack was his opponent, to the moment he made his return to the city lights. He could not beat him.
