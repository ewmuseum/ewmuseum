It's the Masked Monk-DAMMIT! Los Mucho Macho Mascaras! Pájaro de Presa... And White Tiger... Starring in... TEXAS TEABAGGING!!! It’s a cold winter day in Chicago. “The Windy City” is living up to its name as a newspaper blows by. It gets caught in the gutter and the date can be read… 2055. The wind picks up and the paper blows in front of a large arena, the sign outside declaring it the Jerrod Y. Darson Sportsatornima. A large statue of man stands out front, pointING majestically to the east, towards where the sun rises and hence towards the future. The statue looks magnificent, an athletic looking man in a sharp business suit. Then you get to the head, covered in a bird mask. And a pretty crappy looking bird mask at that. An old Hispanic man sits on the bases of the statue, a mournful expression on his face. He lets out a sigh and shivers from the cold. Just then, a group of children run by. They stop and gaze with eyes filled with wonder at the imposing statue. “Who is that, anyways?” asks a small boy. “You do not know?” the old man responds. The children all shake their heads. The old man lets out another sigh. “What are they teaching in schools these days?” the old man grumbles. “Well, gather around, niños, and I shall tell you the story of the man this statues honors.” The cold Chicago wind blows and the sands of time are swept back half a century… White Tiger, dressed his trademark cat-like mask and a yellow Mucho Macho Security bowling shirt, is walking down a hallway in the famed UWF Arena, once again wondering where the hell his partner, Pájaro de Presa, is at. He hears someone call his name from behind him and turns to see who it is. A man in a white robe and hood is running up to him, clipboard in his hand. White Tiger wonders what Dicky Flint, the wrestling Klansman, would want from him. “Boy!” pants Dicky Flint. “I had me a hell of a time catchin’ up to you, buddy! This outfit ain’t made for runnin’!” “Yeah, I guess it wouldn’t be,” White Tiger replies. “Something I can help you with?” “I got me this here petition I’d like you to sign,” declares Dicky Flint, holding out the clipboard. White Tiger glance down at it. “What’s this for?” he asks. “Well,” says Dicky Flint. “It’s to get rid of all them wetbacks we got runnin’ around here in the U-Dub-F.” “Wetbacks,” echoes White Tiger. “You know,” says Dicky Flint. “All them Mexicans all over the place! Like them Mojo Nacho Macaroni fellers.” “Los Much Macho Mascaras,” corrects White Tiger. “Yeah!” exclaims Dicky Flint. “Them folk! Sneakin’ across the border and takin’ all the good jobs like security and enhancement talent.” “Uh, those jobs require getting beat up,” White Tiger replies. “A lot.” “But they’s American jobs!” Dicky Flint retorts. “Why just the Mexicans?” asks White Tiger. “Why not the Asians too?” “I thought about that,” Says Dicky Flint. “But that there Shiatsu gal is a might fine piece o’ tail! I’d hate to see her go! Wouldn’t mind watchin’ her walk away, though, if ya know what I mean! Hawr!” White Tiger can’t argue with his “logic” there. But there is another flaw in Dicky Flint’s proposal. “Do you realize I’m one of Los Mucho Macho Mascaras?” White Tiger inquires. “But you ain’t like them other fellers,” says Dicky Flint. “Yer white!” “Uh, no.” “No?” Dicky Flint’s voice sounds confused beneath his hood. “But yer name is White Tiger!” “That’s because I wear white. The mask. The tights.” White Tiger explains. “I’m Mexican, too.” “Yer Mexican? Really?” “Yes,” replies White Tiger. “From Mexico and everything.” Dicky Flint seems taken aback by this revelation. “Well, slap my cheeks and call me Rosey!” declares Dicky Flint. “It’s a good thing I got this here on, cuz my face in turnin’ red!” "Yeah," White Tiger replies. "You'd really hate yourself then." "Huh?" "Nevermind." White Tiger glances at the petition. "What were you exactly trying to accomplish with this thing?" "Get all them illegals kicked out of the UWF," answers Dicky Flint. "That's just silly," says White Tiger. "Most of the foreign wrestlers have work visas, like me. The only one I know that's actually in the States illegally is... Pájaro de Presa." White Tiger seems to be in deep thought. "You know," he says. "Maybe I will sign it." Dicky flint seems stunned, then he hands the clipboard over to White Tiger. The luchador siGNs his name and hands it back. "Thanks!" Dicky Flint exclaims. "Whatever, " says White Tiger. "And you have mustard on your dress. Sheet. Whatever the hell you call that get-up." Dicky Flint glances down. Sure, enough, there's a yellow stain on his robe. He looks up and see Whit Tiger walking away. "Keep hope alive, my brother!" says Dicky Flit raising a fist into the air. White Tiger flips him the bird without turning around. Meanwhile, at the same time this is happening, Pájaro de Presa is standing in the office of UWF head honcho, Richard Hayes. Pájaro is trying to appear as dignified as a guy dressed like a retarded bird ca as Hayes sits behind his desk, looking at the Rudo with Attitude-o thoughtfully. "I suppose you're wondering why I called you in here," Hayes begins. "Is it to wish me luck in my PTC tag title match?" asks Pájaro. "No," says Hayes. "But that does tie in to what I have to say to you. Pájaro, the wrestling business is cyclical." "Uhhh..." says Pájaro nervously. “Does that mean I have to give you a hummer?" "No," Hayes answers. "It means it goes up and down." "So I WILL have to give you a hummer," Pájaro says. "Good thing I'm already wearing kneepads." "Pájaro," says Hayes. "I don't want a hummer from you. What I'm trying to tell is that business in the UWF is on a downturn right now and I have to make some cutbacks." "OK," Pájaro replies. "I'll stop letting Plucky the Cockfighting Duck use the whirlpool." "It's not about that," says Hayes. "Although I am glad to know where the feathers in the drain came from. No, Pájaro, I'm afraid I'm going to have to let you go." Pájaro de Presa stands there dumbstruck. Well, dumberstruck. "But Mr. Hayes!" Pájaro protests. "You don't even pay me! You just give me stuffed jalapenos and caramel shakes!" Richard Hayes lets out a deep sigh. "Ok," says Hayes. "Business isn't on a downturn. Business is great, in fact. The truth of the matter is this… you’re an embarrassment. Even more so than those idiots you hang out with. I let you do this whole PTC tag thing is to get you out of my hair. But you’ve actually been successful along with your buddy, White Tiger. Unfortunately, with that success comes notoriety. A lot of people are watching you. Hell, you show up in other organizations, making a complete ass of yourself! Not to mention that damn trial on Court TV! To save the image of this company, I have to let you go.” “But… but…” stammers Pájaro. “Don’t try talking your way out of it,” states Hayes. The Rudo with Attitude-o’s attitude changes from shock to righteous anger. “Fine, Dicky boy!” shouts Pájaro. “I’ll just go to PRIME and be with my Kare-Bear! She’ll welcome me with open arms and wide-open thighs!” “I’m afraid not,” says Hayes. “The executives at PRIME have gotten a restraining order against you. If you come within a hundred yards of one of their events or facilities, you will be neutered.” “Neutered?” says Pájaro. “Is that legal?” “Normally, no,” Hayes replies. “But the judge was willing to make an exception in your case. Not surprising since it was your old friend, Judge Hannibal Mental.” Pájaro de Presa’s shoulders slump and his head bows in defeat. “Do I at least get severance pay?” “There’s half a sandwich I wasn’t able to eat in my wastebasket,” answers Hayes. Pájaro mournfully digs the sandwich from the trash. He sighs and looks forlornly at Richard Hayes. “Don’t make me have you thrown out by your ex-compadres in Mucho Macho Security,” declares Hayes. Defeated, Pájaro de Presa exits the office of Richard Hayes. Richard Hayes sits alone in his office, deep in thought. “The birdbrain really thought I wanted him to give me a hummer,” mutters Hayes to himself. “Hmmmm…” He presses the button to the intercom that contacts his secretary. “Ja, Herr Hayes?” says a female voice with a German accent. “Miss Schtupp, would please come here?” requests Hayes. “Uf kourse, Herr Hayes.” Richard Hayes leans back in his chair. A moment later, a very attractive pair of breasts enter the office, followed a second later by a very attractive blond girl. “You vanted to schpeak to me, Herr Hayes?” she asks. “Miss Schtupp… Freida…” Hayes begins. “The wrestling business is cyclical.” Richard Hayes raises an eyebrow as he gazes upon his buxom young secretary. She sighs and takes a wad of gum out of her mouth as she approaches his desk. Meanwhile, Pájaro de Presa wanders down the hall like he was a zombie. This is how White Tiger finds his partner. "What’s wrong?" asks White Tiger. "I got the ax!" moans Pájaro. "How will I support my family?" "What family?” asks White Tiger. “All you have is a duck." "Shows how much you know!" Pájaro retorts. "There's a possum in my dumpster!" "Wow," White Tiger replies. "That's... a big responsibility." "Tell me about it!" says Pájaro. "Talk about high maintenance!" "Anything I can do to help?" asks White Tiger, much to his own amazement. Pájaro sighs deeply. "No," he says mournfully. "I just need some alone time." Pájaro climbs into a nearby trashcan. He sighs once more, then ducks down into the trashcan, pulling the lid over him. White Tiger shrugs and turns back down the hall. As he walks, he passes another trashcan. Suddenly, the lid flies off and there stands Pájaro, big dumb grin on his face. "Hola, amigo!" shouts Pájaro. "I thought you needed alone time?" asks White Tiger. "I did," answers Pájaro. "But I know just how I like it, so it doesn't take long. Gimme a hand out of here." "Uhhhh... you're on your own." For some reason, White Tiger doesn't look very eager to touch Pájaro’s hand, so the Rudo with Attitude-o pulls himself out. "I tell you what," says White Tiger. "I'll buy you some stuffed jalapenos and a caramel shake. "Sweet!" says Pájaro as he raises his hand up. "High five!" "No." Los Mucho Macho Mascaras step outside the arena and into the sunny outdoors. "Such a beautiful day," says White Tiger. "Too bad you got fired on it. Since you don't work for the UWF anymore, how will that effect our shot at the PTC tag titles?" "I didn't think of that!" says Pájaro. "This is all a conspiracy to keep me away from my sweet Negasonic Nymphomaniac! My beautiful Lupine Love Muffin! My darling Kare-Bear!" Pájaro shakes his fist angrily at the heavens. "Oh, Lord!" shouts Pájaro. "If you are a merciful God and believe in the power of true love, give this poor, humble, yet very sexy luchador a sign!" Just then, a man rides up on a bicycle, dressed like a leprechaun. He hops off the bike and smiles at Los Mucho mach Mascaras. "Are one o' ye foin gentlemen Pájaro de Presa?" he asks in the worst fake brogue ever. "I am," says Pájaro, because he is. "Then it's me foin pleasure to give ye one o' Lucky Lima's world famous Limerick-o-grams!" Lucky Liam takes off his hat and get down on one knee. "There was an old lady from Amarillo Who loved watching this Bird of Prey fellow When she kicked off at ninety-eight She left him her entire estate My, that's a mighty foin hello!" "Ouch!" says White Tiger. "That was bad." "Hey!" says Lucky Liam with no trace of an accent. "I only get minimum wage for this gig!" "Sorry," apologizes White Tiger. "I'm supposed to give you this." Lucky Liam hands a note to Pájaro de Presa. The Rudo with Attitude-o looks it over. "Holy frijoles!" exclaims Pájaro. "That wasn't just a catchy poem! A little old lady really did leave me her entire estate! C'mon, Tiger! We're going to Amarillo!" Before White Tiger can protest, Pájaro de Presa grabs his arm and Los Mucho Macho Mascaras run off towards destiny and Texas! “Hey!” calls Lucky Liam to the migrating luchadores. “What about my tip?” Something flies through the air aimed at the leprechaun. He deftly catches it. He looks down and sees it’s Richard Hayes’s half-eaten sandwich, with a few extra bites missing. Lucky Lima gives it a tentative sniff. “Ewwwww, gross!” says Lucky Liam while making a disgusted face. He tosses it into a dumpster, where it’s grabbed by a possum. As Lucky Liam gets on his bike and rides off, a huge dust cloud appears, obscuring all from view. When the dust cloud disperses, the location has magically changed to an empty stretch of highway. The road cuts through miles and miles of empty desert. The only landmark is a sign that reads “AMARILLO 15 MILES.” It seems a strange place for a bus to stop, yet one pulls up by the sign and brakes. The door opens and Pájaro de Presa gets out. Actually it looks more like he was thrown out. White Tiger follows, except he leaves by his own power. “There’s one more of us,” says White Tiger. Then a pair of webbed feet walks down the steps as none other than Plucky the Cockfighting Duck! “Quack,” says Plucky as he stands next to White Tiger and Pájaro, who is still sprawled in the dust by the road. The Rudo with Attitude-o stands up and dusts himself off as the bus drives off, stranding Los mucho Macho Mascaras, not to mention Plucky the Cockfighting Duck. “Madre de Rod!” yells Pájaro. “I can’t believe that gringo driver kicked us off!” “Actually,” says White Tiger. “He kicked YOU off. You should be glad Plucky and I got off with you.” “I still don’t see what the big deal was,” says Pájaro. “You took a gigantic crap on the bus,” White Tiger answers. “So?” replies Pájaro. “I think that sandwich I got from Dickie boy wasn’t that fresh. And when ya gotta go, ya gotta go.” White Tiger stares into the eyes of his partner. “There was no bathroom on that bus, idiota.” “Cut me some slack, amigo!” says Pájaro. “I did the deed as far back as I could! Boy, I didn’t think nuns were allowed to talk like that…” “So know what?” asks White Tiger. “Now, we walk to Amarillo!” answers Pájaro gleefully. “Only fifteen miles to Casa de Presa!” White Tiger’s draw drops. “That will take hours!” he protests. “And we’re in the middle of the desert!” Pájaro gives White Tiger a playful punch to the arm. “C’mon, Tiger!” he says. “We’re super-tough luchadores. And we’re Mexicans! We came from worse deserts than this!” “Fine.” The hours and the miles crawl by slowly. White Tiger is stumbling along, caked with sand and sweat. Pájaro de Presa looks even worse, crawling along the ground with Plucky the Cockfighting Duck perched on his back. “By the twin moons of J-lo’s sweet ass!” Pájaro moans. “We’re gonna die out here!” “But we’re Mexicans!” says White Tiger mockingly. “We came from worse deserts than this!” “Screw that!” says Pájaro. “I’m from Cancun! If it got above eighty degrees, I was in air-conditioned bars doing body shots off of American sorority chicks!” As Pájaro crawls along, his head bumps into a wooden pole. “Ow.” “Hey!” says White Tiger. “I think I’ve found the way to the little old lady’s house!” “Really?” says Pájaro, perking up. He sees White Tiger pointing to the pole that he had just headbutted. There is a wooden arrow attached to it that read “TO THE LITTLE OLD LADY’S HOUSE.” Pájaro leaps up with excitement, sending Plucky the Cockfighting Duck flapping to the ground. “Quack!” says Plucky, sounding very annoyed. Los Mucho Macho Mascaras look to where the arrow is pointing. They see in the distance a small house. “Yippee!” yells Pájaro. “Let’s go!” He runs off towards the house with incredible energy. White Tiger just walks, with Plucky waddling behind him. After a couple minutes, they reach the spot where Pájaro has collapsed onto the sandy ground. “Still… in desert…” he mumbles. White Tiger gets him up and they eventually make it to the house. Pájaro stares at the tiny, rickety abode with pride. “There it is, Tiger!” he says. “Someday soon, I shall carry Mrs. Karina Wolfenden-de Presa across that threshold!” “I think you’re more likely to carry your spleen in a paper sack thanks to Karina Wolfenden,” says White Tiger. Los Mucho Macho Mascaras open the door to Pájaro’s property. Inside is… birds. Paintings of birds. Stuffed animals of birds. Birds embroidered on pillows and blankets. And all over the place are birdcages. Unfortunately, it appears no one has been out to this place in quite some time, since all the occupants are quite dead. “Uh… wow,” says White Tiger. “It’s… wow. I guess we know why she liked you.” “Oh Rod! Oh Rod! Oh Rod!” exclaims Pájaro. He has this look of dread on his face. Well, the part you can see under the stupid bird mask. “Calm down,” says White Tiger. “Think of it as… a fixer-upper. And it’s better than a dumpster.” “It’s not that!” says Pájaro. “That sandwich is acting up again!” Squeezing his buttcheeks together with his hands, Pájaro runs off to find the bathroom. “That’s what he gets eating a sandwich from the trash,” says White Tiger as he shakes his head. Down the hall, a loud splash is heard and the voice of Pájaro de Presa calls out. “Oh thank you, sweet Jesus, Mary, and Joseph!” White Tiger makes a disgusted face. The sound of flushing can be heard. “Tiger!” cries Pájaro’s voice from the bathroom. “You gotta see this!” “I don’t care how big it is!” call White Tiger. “Just flush again!” “It’s not that!” says Pájaro. “Something loco is going on and it’s not my fault! I think!” White Tiger runs down the hall and opens the bathroom door. Pájaro de Presa is standing there, staring at the toilet. A thick, black liquid is bubbling up from the depths of the toilet. “Is it… the Blob?” asks Pájaro as he quivers in fear. “No!” says White Tiger, looking at the toilet in amazement. “That’s oil! This house must be on a huge oil deposit! It must be coming up from the water well and through the pipes! Do you know what this means, Pájaro?” “I need a new toilet?” “No!” says White Tiger. “Well, yes actually. But you can afford one because you’re rich!” Pájaro de Presa looks like a kid at Christmas. Los Mucho macho Mascaras hug each other and start jumping up and down, laughing. “I’m rich! I’m rich!” yells Pájaro. “And I know just what to do with the money!” White Tiger suddenly stops jumping and laughing. “I’m going to regret asking this,” he says. “But what are your plans for the money?” Pájaro smiles at White Tiger with a grin so wide that White Tiger looks around for nearby sharp objects. “I am going to buy…” says Pájaro. “THE UNIVERSAL WRESTLING FEDERATION~!!!” “Oh, no,” groans White Tiger. “Oh, yes!” says Pájaro. “I will have my place on the roster back and we can compete for the PTC tag team titles! I will be EL PRESIDENTE OF THE UWF~!!! And my sweet, darling Kare-Bear will be my First Chica! And you will be my Chief of Staff! I shall call you BIG CHIEF WHITE TIGER~!!!” “You can just not call me at all,” says White Tiger. “Quickly, Big Chief White Tiger!” shouts Pájaro. “We must return to Chicago!” The newly self-proclaimed El Presidente of the UWF runs out the door of the house. A few seconds later, he runs back in, covered in sweat and gasping for air. “Quickly, Big Chief White Tiger!” pants Pájaro. “We must call a cab to return us to Chicago! One with AC!” And so as Los Mucho Macho Mascaras flip through the yellow pages for a cab, so do the pages of time flip forward half a century, back to the old Hispanic man and his captive audience of children. “And that is how Pajaro de Presa became a wealthy man and purchased the UWF,” says the old man. “But why is there a statue of him?” asks a little girl. “Because El Presidente did more than buy the UWF with his money,” says the old man. “He poured money into medical research. And after many years, his scientists discovered a safe and effective medication that enlarged the breasts on women and the penis on men! If it wasn’t for Pájaro de Presa, strippers would still be spending money on surgical implants and six inches would still be considered average!” “Wow!” says the children in unison. “How do you know so much about Pájaro de Presa?” asks a small boy. The old man says nothing. Instead, he somberly pulls out a white mask with balck stripes and proudly pulls it over his head. “Because I… am White Tiger!” He looks at the children. They are staring at him with blank expressions. “White Tiger,” he repeats. “From the story. Weren’t you kids listening?” The children just look confused. Some of them shrug. “Ah, get the hell out of here, you brats,” says the old man. “I got a gun and a bullet with each of your names on it!” The children all waste no time getting up and running away from the old man. He watches them flee without saying a word. Then, groaning and creaking, the old man in the mask of White Tiger stands. He looks up at the statue and gives it a small smile. “And hasta mañana, amigo,” the old man says before walking away himself, leaving the statue of Pájaro de Presa standing proudly in the cold Chicago wind.
