Fraudulance

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 1 Money – that’s what makes the world go around to most teenagers. If you didn’t have money, then there’s no life to live. There are always two sides to a coin and for every thousand you’re out of debt there’s always another extreme, someone somewhere else in the world, suffered through. Some people have no idea the tough times that can come with the harder parts of personal finance. Some don’t even have a clue the trouble that can be brought with such a nasty word as ‘debt’. But there are some who have to learn, and it changes their lives forever. Carys Callicker was one of those people. There are some people in this world that find it truly blissful to steal from other people and live comfortably like that forever. These people with their fake credit cards, duplicated debit cards and dodgy cheques can, and usually do, cause the ruin of good standing citizens. These people can sit, plotting the downfall of people they know, hate – or even worse it can be just because of convenience. 2 Carys swept the dirt from her modest, black clothes as she stumbled drunkenly to her feet. That young babe had once been the picture of tee-total-ness, but as her career crumbled to dust around her feet, the alcohol seemed to be all that held her together, until the fresh night air hit her and her legs became more like jelly. She slowly took three meagre steps forwards and felt her knees give beneath her, to be welcomed by a rather large puddle. She was not impressed. “Are ya okay, ma’am?” a strong voice called to her from behind. Her eyes swept along the gravel that was dangerously close to eye level and gawked at the polished boots that stood uniformed and perfectly side by side. “You should get back on yer feet.” This embarrassed Carys terribly, but the words ‘piss off’ seemed like too much work for her lips to form. Embarrassed or not, she managed to swat his hand to the side weakly, however he was right, and she made an almighty struggle to get back to her feet. She knew she could stand. Why not? She was good at it! Her hand dragged at the white hood of the car in front of her. A mixture of dirty puddle water and the slightest bit of blood dripped down the black and white stockings that covered her legs. However, her resolution to stand failed like her consciousness. The last thing she felt before oblivion was her mascara stained cheek sliding down the bonnet. The policeman put her in the back of his patrol car, clicking his tongue against the roof of his mouth as the realisation of a long day hit him. 3 The manoeuvre was in its second day; the shift had changed and the men whom were clad in black fatigues lay freshly, peering through a hole in each barrel. “The eagle’s flown the roost, over,” echoed mechanically from a pocket of one of the officer’s. That same officer raised his hand and with his index and middle finger pointed the two groups of two in two different directions. The centre piece of the huddle scrutinized the warehouse for several minutes and it wasn’t impressive, he mused. The polar opposite of the impression she had left on him. He’d caught a wondering glance of the six foot athlete after she had made her way from the building to whatever errand she was running. That natural raven-black hair hung around her head and shoulders shoddily; her lightly mascara’d eyes that belied her 26 years on this earth in a good way. Domineering Hispanic lips, palely rouged, a button nose that only added to her ironically angelic face. For the briefest of seconds he was positive that she had seen him dwelling in the shadows, but her hazel eyes were as quickly hidden as they were shown. She was dressed in black: Leather thigh boots with no ankle, mini skirt and low cut hoodie with a V-cut that incorporated plenty of cleavage. “Follow my lead,” is all he said as he kicked open the wooden barricades that covered the main entrance. 4 Once the two independent teams, and the third that had the skylight for an entrance, met in the centre of the room, guns poised and ready, they flooded out into the adjoining rooms in groups of five, making two in total. The first team knocked a door from its hinges as they crashed into a begrimed room. Paper lay strewn all over the floor, chairs and some of it even against the single blacked out window in the room in the form of spit balls. The individual team leader pointed to a pile of papers on the desk, held down from the wind by a thick pen. One of the larger men went forwards holding his gun out at arms length as though the paper were really C4. When he got within eyes sight of the paper he glanced down to what was on it; nothing much, a few pages of scrawled signatures, as though it was practice. A cold, clammy feeling on the back of his neck made him spin around faster than the goose bumps could grow on his neck. He was the one who saw it first. His scream was shrill in the entirely darkened office, where the only sounds to interrupt the silence had been the crash as the door fell from its hinges. He stumbled backwards towards the back wall and away from the clearly growing shadow cast impossibly in his vision as the leg of a chair interfered with his path. The loud rattling of submachine gun fire didn’t even escape the room with the silencer that had been attached and his cry was cut off. The shadow grew to full visibility before he took his last breath and a woman stood almost growing into full figure from the hand that choked his breathing. The leader evacuated the room in need of assistance, and all he found on the floor were the soulless shells of two of his men – and another of them was nowhere to be seen. “Sonofa-” his last words were cut off as an iron pipe was thrust sideways through his trachea, the attacker was nowhere to be seen. 5 “Oh dear God!” the shouts were hysterical. The team leader strode forwards quickly, pointing to his lieutenant to cover the other side of the door as he went in fast. The lieutenant recoiled away from his superior as he fell forwards into the room. This old part of the factory was still chilled to a temperature well below freezing. The leading officer stepped back to his feet weakly and brushed the ice from his fatigues. His stomach wrenched after prodding the black clothed pieces of meat that hung up at the front of the meat locker; they were his team alright. All usual techniques were wiped from his mind all he wanted to do was: “Base, base this is Falcon, do you read me, over?” the man’s hand shook, and it had nothing to do with the temperature, however many of the girl’s men had stayed behind, it was impossible to determine. The only thing that could be certain was the fact that they were very dangerous, and unequivocally lustful for their blood. “Reading loud and clear, Falcon. What’s your position?” the radio asked, femininely. “We have 8 men down, fatal. Requesting permission to evacuate!” he whined, hoping the reaction was swift. Perhaps it was a whole deal more than he would have liked. “10 men,” Static broke after the correction and the only officer left in the building span on his feet, gun already locked in a fit of fire, but all that was left there was his only other man gargling on blood as his radio was lodged in his throat. The party leader was never to be found again. 6 The cell was pretty typical; three bleak grey walls, finishing the box with black steel bars, a commode blocked out crudely by half inch thick walls on either side, finished off with a cot spread against the back wall, a girl sprawled uncomfortably upon it. Carys sat up when the hot guard came into the room. Her hair was heavily dishevelled and swept to the side of her head in a pony tail in a black mess of dye and hair. She was wearing paper clothes that invited in all of the cold air of day that rustled annoyingly with every unimportant move that she made. The guard sat next to her with breakfast on a cold steel tray. He slid it optimistically across the clammy plastic mat and watched the seventeen year old scoop up the scarcely warm food and shovelled it into her mouth. “Hunger’s finally caught up with ya, huh?” it was barely a question and more like a statement to break up the uncomfortable silence inside the cell. Carys looked up from the pile of crap food that lay in her lap, still scooping it into her mouth in copious quantities. “No shit, hangover’s a bitch. Oh, and just for future reference, don’t accuse anyone of grand larceny with a bad head,” food flew from her mouth in a great slop as she spoke and chewed. The police officer flinched away from the food that hurtled towards him, but still ended up wiping scrambled egg from his eye. “Anyway, I brought this,” he said as he handed over a few pieces of folded paper, a small smile spreading over his face at her eating habits, one that was soon caught and wiped off as something crossed his thoughts. “Bail’s been paid? Who the hell pays $10,000 for me to get out of jail?” the answer crossed her mind and she stood up where she stood. “I’d leave my clothes here for your lot to wank over, but I’d have nothing to go home with, then,” the mischievous smile was shared with the man as he led her out of her two day home. Soon she’d find out who was the person who had been pulling $100,000 worth of fraud on her behalf, and that will turn out to be a whole lot sooner than she could have ever hoped. 7 Carys was pissed off, and when the seventeen sensation was pissed off, there was one chick in the northern hemisphere you didn’t want to fuck with. She stepped onto the sidewalk from the bus and made a bee-line for the factory. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] Her arms and torso moved as though in a well practiced regime as she approached the front doors of the factory, giving one last fleeting look towards the address on the bail slip and banged her way through the front doors that barely rested on their hinges. There was a time in the recent past that Carys wouldn’t have even noticed the money being drained from her accounts and credit, but now she stomped around an abandoned building, aching to find the person who was responsible for her financial headache. The sheet crackled in her hands, unbeknownst to her, her hands were white with rage and she had to stop walking and make a visible effort to loosen her hands. Slowly she moved on, looking on in wonder at the mess that surrounded her on the floors, walls and even the ceiling were caked in a black sticky mess that could have only have been ink. She rubbed a fair amount from her fingertips after she grazed the wall with her hand on the way past and struggled to get all of it off. She began to shake her hand zealously, as though keeping it on her hands would harm her. Which was ironic, because trying to take it off had her distracted enough not to see the stairs into the cellar, she collapsed in a fast downwards spiral. 8 She stopped in a heap of pain on the dank, wet floor of the cellar, whinging into her no doubt bruised arms. It felt like being hit with a hammer around the head when she tried to look up and take a proper view of her surroundings. “Bleedin’ he-” she halted her own frantic whispered when her heightened senses went into overdrive. She knew that she was not alone even with her nose squashed into the concrete, very nearly broken, and the blood that dripped from her nose, she knew. But she didn’t want to admit it, she didn’t want someone to be in the dark festering room of horrors with her, she still denied it when she heard the footsteps creaking from an alternate set of stairs. Shivers, which could have come from shock or the almost freezing temperature of the room, racked her body making her look like a typewriter at work. Finally, when the maniacal laughter filled the air she had no choice but to stand up and take note – which she did groggily. “Quiescetum,” the single guttural word cut through the air like a blade. “Quiescetum!” the voice barked, this time a lot more authority and perhaps even a little shock in the voice. “QUIESCETUM!” it was undoubtedly a command, the final time. Carys dropped to her knees as though the heaviness in her bloody eyes was travelling down to her legs, pulling like an anvil. Her body flopped backwards like all of the bones in her body had been pulled from it in an instant. 9 She took out the tiny jars that had once served to hold her make up, liquids, powders and sponges. Dirt transferred from the mirror onto her black gloves as she swiped at it with the back of her hand. The index finger from her left hand pushed at the slight bags that had formed under eyes with the past worries of fighting for pittance, and traced downwards to the generous lips that had been busted a thousand times, pushing on the tips of her sharp incisors that had sunk into muscle thousands of times to win a wrestling match, and then flicked on the grease tainted light. Jade allowed her teeth to drag lightly along the bottom lip, leaving traces of rouge on her pearly whites. An unsatisfied look rippled across her features and she turned with one of the jars in hand. Down the stairs she trod, heavily banging her bare feet down on the concrete. “Wake up, sleepy head,” she called into the bitter darkness of the damp cellar, still unable to see the empty chair that had just barely been vacated. “Wake up ya bleedin’ self, bloody psycho buggar!” a small crackle fizzled from the dark and struck the jar from jade’s hand and knocked her six feet to the right. The source of the spark stepped back into the daylight and confirmed the already roused suspicions that the English accent did indeed belong to Carys. A small neon blue dot of electricity grew in the dark. “You just can’t leave it alone, can you?” As the ball grew Carys shone in the light, anger and disdain sparked on her face and glowed perhaps more than the ball in her hand. “Now it’s going to end,” her eyes changed, made her intent clear as the electricity flickered in the lights. “Carys, no! Leave my Mom alone!” A flicker of light, as bright as the sun ripped through the darkness and staggered Carys back. The path that Carys was making that led to Jade was blocked by a bright eyed boy. “Joshua!” Carys exclaimed, a few pieces of a long unsolved puzzle fell into place, and knocked shivers into her spine. If she would have had time, Carys would have been stood there, immersed in intense shivers for close to a minute. The boy called Joshua had different ideas towards allowing things to escalate and pulled two chairs from beneath a dust cloth with his mind, pushing his mother and Carys into the chair. Carys tried to move her hand to force more energy into her hand but she couldn’t even move her index finger. Carys bellowed, but somehow the magic in the air drowned out her voice. All of her worries escalated to a peak, her neck muscles strained under the force that held her back. She knew how powerful the eleven year old was, and she knew how long he could hold out these kinds of forces, an insurmountable time. ’STOP STRUGGLING!’ Was all that Carys heard in her head, all the lines were open, but there was not a single sound in the atmosphere. She had no choice. “It’s not really a game,” Jade’s child finally decided to say after debating quietly where he stood, “not like you think, anyway.” Carys, who was still sat down in the dusty chair summoned all of her energy just to speak, and she spat out the words. “Keep outta my bleedin’ head!” Her neck muscles still hadn’t relaxed and had started to look like thick tendrils bubbling beneath the surface of her neck, as rabid as she felt. “Leave him be, he’s not the one who you’re truly angry with, pigeon. I’ll talk if you listen, and then everything will be crystal, everything on a plate.” “Speak,” she said as she rolled her eyes to the side. There was a great deal of arrogance in her demeanour, as though nothing that she could say would change the judgement that she had already made on Jade’s ignorance. Joshua nodded at Jade and allowed her to sit forwards, she smiled gratefully and took a deep breath before she started, the kind that happens when nerves keep a hold over everything that you can focus on, specifically your tongue. She coughed, removing phantom phlegm that had lodged itself in her throat, again she coughed. “Oh fuckin’ talk, bitch!” Carys interrupted, her patience already stretched by the fact that she was still being held down when the psychotic beside her dodged around the need to talk all day. Three short bursts of air rasped in the form of a dry chuckle. “Okay, okay… It’s been roughly four months since I left Joshua in your care-” she finally started. “Yeah, and the lyin’ little tard claimed that he didn’t even know who his family was! You’ve both been havin’ me for a run for a long time. Was this all some filthy fuckin’ trick to get your fingers inside my bank details?” But Carys was already shooting her mouth off, interfering every single thing said. “Shut up and let me talk if you want to know the truth, you’re rude!” Carys snorted, exasperatedly and shook her head, still shutting up nonetheless because she wanted to know the truth. “I left Joshua with you, as soon as I’d seen him! After TEN years! You have no idea how much it hurts to do something like that, and to wipe his memory so that he doesn’t remember his Mom or Dad! “His father was so angry when I snatched him from the Dead city, Necropolis. He was using our son as a crafty way to profiteer in poker, no doubt that you’ve noticed his talent for breaking into your thoughts? Well, no boy of mine will be used as a tool, and then treated like an animal when he does. He was locked up, while his drunken father wasted every penny that his offspring worked for! “They were all animals in there, Carys. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] It was pure luck that his father was sober enough to defend him when those kind of situations arose. “At the time I didn’t know what was happening, with the dreams I was having, I just had a feeling in my gut that someone was reaching out to me from within there. I went to see an old friend, a druid, to find out who it was, what it was. “After the initial shock period – and that was weeks, believe me - had passed, I made my way there on a mission to free my son, and break out. I knew that I’d have to save every bit of energy that I had in my vessel, so I made my way by plane, to the nearest island, at least. That’s where the cash I stole from you came into play. I didn’t know or care how I was going to get back out of the city that held all prisoners; I just needed him to be safe. “Once that was achieved, there was still a great threat to him – in the form of his own dad. I couldn’t rest safely until I knew someone could look after him while I finished off his dad.” “I don’t like you calling him my dad, Mom,” he said. It dawned on Carys that this was the first time that Josh had heard the story himself, and had to try hard not to feel pity for him. “Go make me a coffee, Josh? I don’t think you need to hear this story – it isn’t good for you,” she said, as thought the idea had been passed from Carys to Jade in the atmosphere. Josh nodded his head wistfully and disappeared as he had come, in the flicker of light similar to one you’d expect to see when a bulb dies, even down to the soft smoky smell that fills the air. “I killed him, and brought him back with me,” she chuckled a little maniacally, “I use him for ingredients, elves do have very useful body parts.” “You’re a sick bitch,” Carys’ look was one of genuine disdain, but she appreciated the fact that she had at least had the common sense to send her son out of the room before she shared the gruesome details, even if it did make her feel like vomiting. Then a question crossed her mind, “he’s half elven?” Jade looked up and nodded, still smiling from Carys’ crack about her being sick. “Why do you think he’s so small? He’s an eleven year old who looks closer to eight. “Anyway, back to the story. When I took him back from you I wiped your memory, you’d been a great deal of help looking after him for so long, but you didn’t want to let go, for some reason. He was like your nephew, I guess.” Carys didn’t miss the weird look of remorse that rested in her eyes, but didn’t want to interrupt the answers to most of her dismay within the last month or so. “I waited there, in Necropolis, for two months, there were enough fights there to satisfy Ghandi’s lust for blood, and it was good for my soul. “When I…I…Josh, JOSH!” Jade began to shake, feverously. Carys was still pinned to the chair so she couldn’t help. The light bulb effect went off in the room again as he came back, a pen and paper in one hand as he raised the other towards his mother. Jade flew back against the wall, hitting it without sound or pain. Josh dropped the pad to the floor and soon dropped down to meet it, pencil touching paper instantly. “Bad Jadie! Greedy Jadie! Raven’s flying everywhere!” her eyes rolled in the back of her head, and her voice increased in volume and pitch becoming more of a wail. “They don’t know about the wolf, she is the bringer of the wolf, she owns it and keeps it where they don’t see, sheep’s clothing! Master’s angry, no supper …GOLDEN, SILENCE IS GOLDEN, LIKE THE TURNBUCKLES…for it tonight, only spankings! I’ve been a bad puppy, BAD PUPPY!” She growled and began slamming her head backwards against the wall, she looked possessed. “The one who…duplicitous WHORE…steals…magpies is…everything…HOME!” The usual hazel hue of her eyes returned and she fell down to her knees, hands in her hair, holding her temples wearily. “Woah, Drucilla!” Josh turned his head to the side and his forehead creased in confusion. “Y’know, from Buffy?” He shook his head in reply and still refused to get closer to his mother. “What is this world coming to?” Josh had been distracted enough to drop the hold he had on Carys, though for some strange reason she chose not to move, or do anything until the right time presented itself. Jade stood up, shrugging her shoulders as she did so, like nothing had happened, she walked towards Carys. “She’s having weird jumbled up vis-” “Yeah, visions, no shit. Why am I here? What did you have to gain from me being released on bail?” “Here’s what it all boils down to,” Jade began, “I can’t control these violent blackouts I keep having, hell I can’t even remember them once the headache passes. That’s why I have my boy here with me. “Taking the money from you was convenient, in more than one way. But that’s not why you’re here; hell money isn’t anything to me, apart from the pretty shiny stuff. I mean, I stole enough from Patti while she and I were ‘friends’ to set his grandkids up into retirement. That’s why she hates me. I don’t lie anything down to coincidence, anymore. I face her for a purpose, and it’s not to lose and give her something else to open her mouth about and make everyone want to shoot their self in the face, just listening to her – it’s to win and become a mighty warrior. “That’s what everything in my life has been building around. I might have taken a back route to get to this place, done a few dirty handed dealings to get here, tortured a few people, apparently. But it’s all been done for a reason. This is not the end. It’s simply the beginning.” “You talk a LOT of crap, Jade. Quit borin’ me with talk about your match and tell me why you want me here, so I can finally go home.” “I want you there with me.” All the hairs on Carys’ neck stood to attention. There was no way on earth she could actually be serious, was there? “What the hell makes you think for a deluded second that I will be seeing you ever again?” Josh walked back from a cupboard; in his trembling hands he held a faded leather briefcase. He floated it in the air while picking the lock and span it around to face Carys. “Money,” he answered honestly. “You want me to join some kind of jaded allegiance with a psycho for money, when she stole so much from me? You want me to sacrifice my life to help someone who has seemingly tried non-stop to ruin mine. One who has designs on killing me if I don’t – it’s all in your eyes, by the way. You think that I am stupid enough to do that? Carys dived from her chair and slammed a ball of energy into her chest, sending her flying. “Sure thing,” she smiled confidently. So did Jade. I think it’s Patti that needs to watch herself.
