The Weakness in Society

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 Humility… To be humble. What the word truly means is miles away from that which it’s positive connotation seems to imply. When one hears the word humble, what comes to mind is not it’s true example. What comes up in the mind of most people is a noble man, comfortable in his position in life. A man willing to take what he is given and take no more. What the world sees is a man willing to accept weakness, a man willing to accept a lowly place, when he can take so much more. What the world doesn’t seem to realize is these are not noble and great characteristics, no they are the ideals of a weak coward. To be humble, whether it’s with God, your wife or any other being. It is to be epitome of weakness. To show humility is to accept a level below that of another. But, a man like myself, I cannot be satisfied with that. I refuse to live with that fact. I refuse to live a life where I will be the dog to another man, where I will be the inferior. The rest of the world doesn’t seem to have a problem with being humble. The rest of the world doesn’t seem to have a problem living an inferior life. The rest of the world doesn’t mind submitting to inequality. The rest of the world doesn’t see this as a weakness. The rest of the world considers humility to be a virtue. The rest of the world has no idea how naïve they truly are. But I am not the rest of the world. I woke up panting, my breathing heavy and wheezing at times. I couldn’t tell why these dreams had been haunting my sleep for the past several days, I only know that they were sure as hell interfering with my sleeping patterns. Sleep that I was going to need. I was coming up onto the verge of my first round match-up in the GTT4 tournament. Every ounce of sleep was needed. And most of the time I didn’t spend resting up, I spent training. My opponent was someone whom I did not know, which did not help ease my worry. It had been many months since I had been inside of the squared circle. Unfortunately, it had only been days since I finished my rehab program and had received doctor’s clearance to compete. I laid back down; the sweat crawled across my body. It felt like a million bugs crawling all over me at once. I closed my eyes, practically having to clench them shut to allow me to get back to sleep. I sat and I counted. The time passed slowly, seconds seeming like hours. Finally, sleep came. But my mind was not salvaged from torment. The disturbing images from my past continued to haunt my subconscious mind. Why do they always run? Why? They know they’re going to get caught eventually. Even if they get away today, it’s not like I won’t be after their ass again tomorrow. All they manage to do is piss me off, and it only adds to the torment and suffering that they will experience when I catch them. Up ahead of me this teenaged punk kid is running away. It’s not that I have a problem with teenagers, myself only leaving that category five years ago. But, what I do have a problem with ignorance. And people that think they can just rob a guy on the streets. If you’re going to rob someone on the streets of downtown Dallas, you should at least look for a few things before doing so. 1) Is the person I am about to rob athletic? If yes, will he catch me after the fact? If he catches me, is he going to beat the hell out of me? 2) Is he the son of a famous Yakuza boss, a famous wrestler, or both and one who has been openly noted as being thought to be linked to multiple murders in his homeland of Japan. Now after answering yes to both of those questions, why you would try to rob someone at gunpoint is beyond me. I mean sure I didn’t argue when he pulled the gun, I just let him run off. Now twenty seconds later, I have him directly in my sights and I’m chasing the kid; who’s probably scared for his life, through the middle of rush hour in downtown Dallas. The people take notice that I’m chasing the kid. What they probably fail to notice is the fact that there are two twin custom made pistols in my hands. The glare will give it away to the observant pedestrian. Or maybe someone who owns a gun. I look across the street of cars, he’s building his lead on me. This kid might be a drug addict, but he sure is a speedy bastard. Probably running from the cops a few too many times. There is a break in the cars and I decide to go for it, I book it across the street in time just to see the kid run into a church. They always seem to think that will help them. But of course it never really does. Being a man who does not believe in the Catholic faith, I have no problems walking straight into a Catholic Church, guns blazing. I crossed the street, tucking my guns back into their holsters as I walked through the slightly buzzing streets. Up the stairs and into the rather large and old building I went. The outside of the church looked rundown and in need of repair. This was not the case however on the inside. Inside the church was beautifully decorated with stained-glass windows, statues and other pieces of religious value. The church gave me an uneasy feeling. It was not one of fear or sadness, it was one of being uncomfortable. I took out my two pistols as I began to walk throughout the church. I wanted to find that little punk who stole my twenty bucks quickly, and I didn’t want a whole lot of trouble about it. I walked towards the main pews, all of which were empty. Suddenly, I heard a noise behind me. I spun quickly and got ready to fire. “No!! Heaven’s please don’t shoot!” The pastor screamed out and threw up his arms at the sight of my guns. “I’m not here to kill you, I’m looking for a teenager,” I spoke, quickly hoping for some assistance from the man of God. “I will not allow you help in your task. It is against the will of God to take the life of another,” he replied. “Yeah, well, it’s against God’s wishes to steal too, and that little prick made off with my shit so I’m going to get it back!” “Please, watch your language in the house of the Lord,” he replied meekly. He was scared and he didn’t want to help me, he wasn’t going to be of much assistance. I heard a noise once again, this time closer to the door. I turned quickly and prepared to give chase as I saw the boy quickly run out the door. However as my legs started to move, the pastor grabbed a tight hold on my shirt and held me back. He wasn’t allowing me to go. “Let me go right now,” I looked down at the much smaller man, but he did not back down. “Listen, I don’t want to cause a whole lot of shit in this church, but if you don’t take your God-damned hands off of me in five seconds, things worse than hell will happen to you!” He didn’t let go. At first he just stared at me. Finally, he began to speak. “My son, there is nothing wrong with being afraid of religion. Many people are too scared to embrace the words of the Lord. But do not be frightened.” “I’m not scared. But I’m going to send you straight to this God you talk about so much, if you do not remove your hands from me in five fucking seconds!” “You are anxious young man, you must listen to me. God is the light that will show you the way. You must simply believe. Belief is your release. Humble yourself in the presence of God and He will exalt you.” ** James 4:10 Jackpot, there’s that magical word that I distinctly hate. Humble. A word that purely means to accept weakness and inferiority. My eyes lit up at the sound of the word. Don’t get me wrong I was not angry. I was disappointed, disappointed in the human race. Upset that we would lower ourselves, the dominant species of the planet. That we would lower ourselves to an artificial being meant to impose control over our early ancestors. “Listen, I’m not religious. So you can just stop that bullshit right now, if you don’t mind.” “Everyone is religious my son, every man must have his own faith,” came his reply. “I have a faith. But, it is not that of your Western mannerism, it is instead of a much different faith. One that you weak Americans could never understand. I have faith in myself, not in a fictional God.” There was a look of shock in his eyes. A look of pure shock and disbelief. I smiled at him as his grip began to release slowly. He must have thought that I was some sort of demon or something. I am no demon. I am simply a man. I am a man who is confident in what he can do, and I do not take the words of the Lord and humility lightly. I will never bow down to another man, because I will never accept that another is greater than me. When that is proven to me, I shall train, and I will continue to train until that changes and I reign atop. I turned and walked away. A large smile spread across my face, there was nothing he could do to stop me now. I had lost the kid forever, I’d probably never see him again in my life. If I ever did rest assured it would be a bad day for him. But that was not on my mind as I left. I just smiled and listened to the shocked preacher yell out prayers for my soul. “Save your breath!” And with that I stepped back out into the sunlight. I quickly put my Oakley sunglasses on and joined in with the heavy pedestrian traffic of the downtown Dallas core. My eyes shot open once again. I looked at the small clock radio on the night table. Light was beginning to seep in through the blinds covering the small window. It had to be morning. The time on the clock read 8:03 a.m. I sat up, I shook my head slightly. My hair fell around my eyes and I wiped it away from my face. GTT4 was coming up soon, and I had training to do.
