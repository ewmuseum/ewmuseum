Chaper Two: Raising The Stakes

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 To say that the Finnerton Academy had a standard procedure for these sorts of incidents would have been…well, a lie. This was, in fact, only the third such incident that had ever taken place on school grounds. The first two had involved children in the middle of bitter custody disputes; they had been resolved quickly, with minimal difficulty or harm to the child. This time, however, was very different. The child’s parents were married and happily so. There had been no notice of any relatives coming to pick him up, no sign that this day would bring anything out of the ordinary. Zachary Marshall, it seemed, was simply…gone. That thought filled Gladys Konover’s mind as she carefully searched every inch of the school playground and sports field. Even though she knew it was not in Zachary’s character to do so, she found herself hoping that Zach had simply chosen to play a trick on his teachers or was engaging them in some elaborate game of hide and seek. That she would be able to deal with. There would be repercussions, of course: harsh words, disciplinary actions, perhaps even being sent home for the day. The alternative…she didn’t want to think about what the alternative might mean. She couldn’t allow herself to think of what it might mean. The search of the playground and sports field had proved fruitless. Mrs. Konover then made her way back into the school building, a cold feeling of dread slowly building up in her stomach. She made her way to Mr. Randall’s office, calmly informing the principal that a student could not be found and, while she could not as yet be one hundred percent sure, she feared that an incident may be brewing. As quietly as he could, Mr. Randall and Mrs. Konover gathered together the school maintenance staff, informing them of the current situation and asking them to conduct a thorough search of the campus grounds. Every inch of the school was carefully scrutinized, every nook and cranny thoroughly examined. Teachers were quietly pulled out of classes, all calmly informing Mr. Randall that, no, they had not seen Zach Marshall at all since recess. There was nothing left to do. Mr. Randall informed his secretary, Annette, that all parents were to be called and instructed to take their children home as quickly as possible. No, they were not to be given a reason why, simply that it was important. Yes, he would like all of the children to be released from class and gathered together in the library. Right away would be very good, thank you. Mr. Randall sighed. This sort of thing was not meant to happen at Finnerton Academy. There were measures put into place to prevent such a thing from happening, precautions taken so that this would not happen. And yet, somehow, someone had come along and slipped past all of their carefully planned security…and now a child was gone. This day was not going to go well. Mr. Randall shook his head, banishing such thoughts. There was much still left to do. Matters needed to be taken in hand and quickly. Picking up the telephone receiver, he made the first two of what would become a series of numerous phone calls that day. The first had been to the police. The second had been to Zachary Marshall’s parents. Zach was tired and cold. The air in the box was always cold and it smelled funny. He hadn’t known where he was, at first. The strange person outside never allowed him to see very much. He kept the box dark most of the time. The first time that Zach had even seen anything was the first time he’d gone potty. He hadn’t wanted to. He didn’t want to do anything in this strange place, just wanted to go home and be with Mommy and Daddy again. So he ignored the strange person as much as he could. He tried not to answer when the strange person talked to him, only doing so when the strange person would start to yell at him. He ignored the food that somehow appeared inside with him. And he most definitely did not want to go potty. So he remained where he was, pretending that he was sleeping. Even when his stomach had started to hurt, he kept ignoring it. It was only when his stomach had felt like it was going to blow up that he had finally given in. “I need to go potty. Bad.” A light came on; Zach could see a toilet and a sink. As fast as he could with his stomach hurting so bad, Zach ran over to the toilet and made potty. Just for a moment, he forgot about the strange person outside, forgot about feeling tired and cold and (though he would never, ever admit this to anyone) scared, forgot about everything. Until the strange person began to speak. “Who’s a good little boy, then? Who’s a good little boy?” Zach hated the way the strange person made him feel, all scared and sick inside. He finished his business as quickly as possible, looking down the entire time. Once finished, he dashed over to the sink as quickly as possible so he could wash his hands. As he did, he could see what looked like a wall in front of him. Glass, maybe. Or plastic. Whatever it was, it looked tough and heavy. The minute he was done, he ran back to the bed. As soon as he got there, the lights went back out again. Zach had no idea how long it had been since this had all started. How long it had been since he had been taken from school, how long it had been since the strange person had put him in a box and started watching him, how long he had been away from Mommy and Daddy. All he knew was that he wanted it to stop. The Voice smiled as he watched Zach Marshall slip slowly back into sleep. The child had been defiant at first, as he’d expected. He’d yelled and screamed, called him rude names, made all sorts of threats regarding what his mommy and daddy would do to him when they came to rescue their son. The Voice had known, however, that this was just a little initial rebellion. Fear and adrenalin talking. That, given enough time and patience (plus the little something he was adding to the box’s air supply), Zach would start to become less rebellious. More compliant. More agreeable. And that would make things so much easier. The Voice smiled as he carefully started preparing Zach’s evening meal. It was a simple meal, purchased from a nearby fast food outlet. The sort of food that a young child would be used to. The sort of food that Zach had been rejecting for a few days now. But now, with Zach having finally given in to his body’s natural urges, the struggle would be gone. Zach would no longer have the option of not eating; his body would not allow him to do so. The Voice chuckled as he used the mortar to slowly grind the white colored pill into powder. Once done, he slipped a small amount of the powder into the plastic cup, sloshing around the thick, brown liquid inside until the powder disappeared. The Voice knew that he had to be careful just how much of the drug he gave Zach. The drug could have powerful effects on fully grown people; never mind what it could do to a six year old. And his instructions had been perfectly clear. There was to be no permanent physical damage to be done to Zachary Marshall. As far as everything else…well, he was given the freedom to “use your imagination”. And The Voice had quite an imagination indeed. It had been several hours until the police had even bothered to show up to the Marshalls’s home, a fact that Lance Marshall was having trouble understanding. His son was out there, right now, in who knows what kind of danger and he had to listen to detectives babble about protocol?! The detectives looked tired and almost irritated as if someone had kidnapped his son just to ruin their day. Lance found himself wishing desperately that the detectives would just do what they had to do and leave already. He was inches away from committing assault on a police officer and he could not deal with that on top of everything else. “Would you happen to have a picture of your son that we could use?” asked the lead detective. He was a tall man with an insincere expression and hair dripping with product. “Something that we can show to search parties and volunteers.” Alanna handed over a picture of Zach taken on his last birthday. He smile out from the photograph, his party hat lying crookedly on his head. “Here,” she sniffed, handing it over. “Thank you,” responded the detective. “We’ll pass it along as soon as we begin the search.” “Which would be when, exactly?” growled Lance. “Tomorrow.” “Tomorrow?!” Lance exploded. “My son is out there right now, in the hands of some lunatic and you want me to wait until tomorrow?!” “Mister Marshall, try to understand. Procedure dictates that we can’t actually treat this as a kidnapping until twenty-four hours have passed. We’re doing you a favor by even being here now. You understand?” Lance sighed heavily. He closed his eyes, bringing his right hand to the bridge of his nose. In his mind’s eye, he could see himself with both of his hands wrapped around the detective’s throat, squeezing the life out of him. He could feel the cracking of the detective’s skull as he rammed it repeatedly against the wall, smell the blood as it ran from his head, down the wall and onto the floor. “No,” Lance answered, “I don’t understand.” The lead detective turned his attention back to Alanna. “Is it possible that Zach could have just run away?” That was it. Lance sprang up out of his seat and stomped over to the next room, desperately trying to keep from injuring the detective. “Fuck you,” Lance muttered under his breath, “Just…Fuck You.” “You’ll have to excuse my husband,” Alanna said. “This has hit all of us very hard. And no, I don’t think Zach ran away. That’s just not like him.” “Okay, then,” said the detective, “if he’s not a runaway, then we are looking at abduction. Do you know anyone who might want to try and hurt you or your husband by taking your son?” Oh yes, Lance thought, he knew someone who would do something like this. He knew exactly who it was to blame. There were ways of finding someone that you needed to get in touch with. Phone calls, emails, private detectives…each valid and each effective in their own way. But when you happen to work for the same company? Well, then it makes getting in touch with them a whole lot easier. Lance Marshall struggled to keep the anger and fear boiling within him from overwhelming his mind, evil thoughts running through his mind as he waited outside the University of Texas at San Antonio’s Convocation Center. That Lane Stevens had the nerve to show up tonight didn’t surprise him. That he would get into the ring and make jokes about his son’s disappearance…Lance was determined that he would get the truth from Stevens tonight. That he would beat his son’s location out of him. Lance didn’t have long to wait before his target came into view. Lane Stevens and Katsidy made their way out of the auditorium, chatting amongst themselves. Their mood was light, their chatter breezy. Lance couldn’t tell what they were talking about. Quite frankly, he didn’t really care. The first either of them was aware of what was happening was when Katsidy hit the ground, the contents of her purse spilling out all around her. Lane Stevens was considerably less fortunate. He was seized by his collar and thrown through the air, landing with a harsh clang against something hard and metallic. His wheelchair followed him through the air, missing him by just centimeters. By the time he had managed to process exactly what was happening to him, the first of what would be many fists had connected squarely with his face. Katsidy collected her belongings as quickly as possible, gingerly testing her ankle as she got to her feet. Good. Nothing injured but her dignity and that had survived far worse. What she saw before her now…when she was to think about it later, she would be disappointed at herself that she was in any way surprised by what had happened. She and the rest of the Dead Man’s Hand made a habit of testing fate…it was only natural for things like this to happen. “Lance, please…,” she pleaded. Lance Marshall turned, his right hand still firmly clasped around Lane Stevens’s throat. “You stay the FUCK out of this, Kat!” he screamed. “How the hell do you think Lani is feeling right now, huh? How the FUCK do you think she feels that someone she calls a friend is standing out there and laughing about the fact that our son is missing?!” Katsidy stepped back in shock, her mouth agape as if she’d been struck. Not many understood the nature of the friendship between Alanna Marshall and the woman named Katsidy and, if they were honest, the women rather preferred it that way. Katsidy knew that Lance certainly didn’t understand it, that he almost certainly hated it. But he had never tried to dictate who his wife could be friends with, had never used their friendship against either of them. Until now. Katsidy stepped back, not even trying to get in between the two men. Instead, she dug out her cell phone and quickly dialed a number without even bothering to look at the keypad. His attention now focused back on Lane, Lance resumed his pummeling of Stevens. Marshall’s face once again hit Lane’s face and the blood began to flow from Lane’s nose. “Where the hell is my son, you sick son of a bitch?” Lance bellowed. “Where is he?!” Stevens…just laughed. Another punch. “Where is he?!” “Get out here, now. Lance is here and he is looking like he wants to kill Lane.” Stevens continued to laugh. Another punch “WHERE IS HE?!” “No, I am not exaggerating. Get out here, now!” Another punch. And another. And another. “WHERE IS HE, STEVENS?! WHERE IS MY SON?!” “I could tell you the truth a hundred times,” Stevens spat through a mouthful of blood, “which is that I have no idea where the hell your kid is. But you’d never believe me. So why don’t you just do it, huh? Just do what you’ve been wanting to do for weeks now. Just go ahead and beat me to death, show everyone what a great big hero you are.” No. That was it. That was that one step too far. Stevens had to pay for that. Lance pulled his hand back again and then… …nothing. His hand was being held fast. The grip was not a tight one but it was solid, preventing him from moving. Lance didn’t even have to run his head to know who it was. There was only person Stevens would count as an ally who would have the nerve to do this, much less the strength. “I’m warning you now, Stewart. Leave, before I do the same to you.” “I am not certain where Mister Stewart is at the moment. I would also ask you not to injure Mister Ashe unnecessarily. Good help is so hard to find these days.” Lance turned as best he could, finally letting his grip on Stevens slacken. As Stevens slithered away, Lance could see the rest of the Dead Man’s Hand gathering nearby. Closest to him (and now letting go of his hand) was Roderick Ashe. The other members stood a short distance away, one member in particular catching Lance’s attention. The she-devil. The demon in the white pantsuit. Desade. “While I know you do not believe him, Lance,” she said, “Lane is being as truthful as he can be. He did not initiate your son’s abduction nor is he directly responsible for it.” “You’ll excuse me,” Lance spat, “if I have trouble believing someone who lies as often and as well as you do.” “Understandable,” Desade responded, her voice never rising beyond a flat, professional tone. “So I do what I must to convince you of the truth. I offer you Quinn.” Katsidy stared at Desade, mouth open in shock. “What?” “I offer you my daughter, Lance, to abuse you as you see fit. Take out your revenge on her as I have done on your son.” No, thought Lance. This has to be a trick. She’s planning something, she’s got some sort of scheme…but this is Quinn. This is her daughter. The one thing she holds sacred. There was no way she would do this unless… Oh God, no. Lance Marshall seemed to sag, as if someone had let the air out of him. Desade turned to her associates, addressing each of them in turn. “Go. Go back to your hotels, go drinking, go see a film. It is of no concern to me what you do as long as you do it elsewhere. Mister Marshall and I have things to discuss and we will not be able to do so with the rest of you present.” Most of the Dead Man’s Hand made their way to their various cars, keeping out of Lance’s way as they did so. Only two held back. Amy Campbell walked over to the Director’s side, a look of concern on her face. “Are you sure? I can stick around if you think things are gonna get violent…” Desade turned to Campbell and the expression on her face visibly changed. Desade was gone now and Alexandra Pierce stood in her place. “I’ll be fine. Lance is angry and hurt but there will no longer be any violence tonight. I’m sure. Now, go. I’ll be with you shortly.” Amy nodded, uncertain but willing to go along with what Alex wanted. As she walked off, Alex turned her attention back to Katsidy. As if someone had flipped a switch, Desade was now back in control. “Go, Kathryn. I will be fine.” Reluctantly, Katsidy left, occasionally casting glances behind her. Desade walked slowly up to Lance, who had not moved since her earlier offer. His face cast downward, he looked thoroughly defeated. Stopping exactly ten feet in front of him, she began to address him. “I still remember that discussion we had, Lance. I remember your vow, that no matter how much you hated me you would never hurt Quinn. I, in turn, vowed that I would never harm Zachary as long as you kept your vow. And you have. Quinn has faced dangers due to being my daughter but none have been at your hand.” She paused briefly. “Which is why I swear to you now, I bear no responsibility for your son’s abduction. I have not ordered it done nor have I done it myself. May Quinn be struck dead in front of me if I am lying to you now.” Lance looked up now, tears streaming down his face. “Help me,” he asked, the words thick and hoarse. “Please.” “I will ask my people to look into this matter,” Desade replied. “I will see what can be done. Be warned, though, I guarantee you nothing. Now…go home. Be with your wife.” With that, Desade turned and walked away, no longer wanting to see her opponent. Under other circumstances, it would give her some degree of pleasure to see Lance Marshall as broken and defeated as he was now. But not tonight. Not like this. Zach was tired. He was tired all the time now. No matter what he did, he seemed to need to sleep all the time. No matter how much he slept, the pain never seemed to go away. He was having a hard time remembering things. Pictures would dance in front of his eyes and then…nothing. He was starting to forget what home was like, having a hard time keeping the picture of his Mommy and Daddy in his head. He was just tired all the time. The Voice watched from outside the box as Zach Marshall continued sleeping away. The drugs had been effective on the boy, allowing The Voice to have his fun. But he was on a schedule and things had to be done exactly according to plan. The game wouldn’t work otherwise. Zach didn’t stir as The Voice made its way into the box. He didn’t wake up at all, not even when The Voice carried Zach away and into a waiting van. “Who’s a good little boy,” The Voice rasped as Zach was bundled into the back of the van, the doors closed behind him. As he climbed into the driver’s seat, The Voice smiled one final time. The game was progressing nicely. Round one was nearly over and round two would begin very soon. Everything was going according to plan. Carmen Del Toro sighed as she leaned against the back of the bus shelter, the metal structure providing only slight respite against the blazing sun. Work was being a bitch, again. It wasn’t what she’d been asked to do, exactly… Carmen laughed at that. What she’d been asked to do. More like what she and about a hundred other employees had been asked to do. Whatever the case, she hadn’t particularly been bothered by what she asked to do. What bothered her was the fact was that it seemed to be going so badly. Usually, these sorts of missions went rather easily. This time, however…with every lead pursued, with every clue followed up on, they came away with nothing. The Director was not going to be happy. Ah well, she thought. A brief stop at Starbucks and back to the grind again. Carmen was about to continue on her way when, out of the corner of her eye, she saw a small body sprawled awkwardly in the grass about ten feet away. She turned and saw what looked to be the body of a child. For one horrible second she feared he might be dead until she could see the child’s chest moving up and down, lungs slowly taking in air. There was no one else around so she moved slowly over to the child. She would just make sure the boy…for now she could clearly see it was a boy…was okay, call an ambulance and then leave. She had too much to do and the Director would not be happy if… …she could see the boy clearly now. Oh shit. She quickly bent down to him, checking his vital signs. His pulse was weak and his breathing seemed labored. Every visible area of skin seemed to be bruised. Digging her cell phone out of her purse, she quickly dialed the first number on her speed dial. Barely waiting for the voice on the other end of the phone to answer, she began speaking. “This message is for the director. Target has been acquired; I’m sending you my GPS coordinates now. I need someone here within ten minutes. Out.” Hanging up the phone, she mentally ran through a list of voices she could do that sounded realistic. Settling on Puerto Rican, she quickly dialed her phone again. “911, what’s the nature of your emergency?” “Yeah, you know that kid that’s been all over the news, the one who got kidnapped last week?” “I think I found him.”
