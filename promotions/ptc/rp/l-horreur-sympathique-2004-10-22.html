L'horreur Sympathique

22 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-22 .:: ::: ::. .:: Riven held up two gloved fingers. The barkeep was attentive; he had to be, else he might find himself on the receiving end of something quite nasty, and he knew it well. Working in Club Tenebrae had made him more alert, but it didn’t make him less nervous. Kindred were capricious creatures, and you never knew when one of them was just going to decide that he was tired of you and pull your head off your shoulders with one swipe. He rummaged, knowing that Riven liked his vitae with a bit of spice, while Falcon enjoyed a bit of vodka in his. Legions of favorites filled his head, some liked it plain, some liked alcohol, or drugs mixed in… but no two were the same. That meant he had to have a damn good memory in order to keep it all together. ::. .:: Luckily for him, he’d only slipped up once, and it had been to a Toreador—too limp-dicked to do anything about it, just like most Toreador. In this case, he had a Nossie, and a… well, God only knows what clan Falcon had allegiance to. He didn’t know, and he didn’t much care. He sent one of the ghouls that worked full-time out to them with a tray containing their beverages of choice. The ghoul slipped up to the table, flaunting a bit of elegant white thigh; some vampires could still be moved to like that. Riven paid her no mind. Falcon looked, of course, at which Riven’s deep brown eyes rolled behind his mask. Falcon was a Philistine, a pig. He could see why Shadow had started to hate him so. Falcon slipped a tip into the top of the ghoul’s thigh-high fishnets as she walked off. She winked at him. Riven was forced to roll his eyes again, but he took a sip of his drink to cover that up. When Falcon quit ogling her, and turned back to Riven, his eyes were serious. Dead serious. He peered into his glass for a moment. ::. “Riven, I wish that I was having this conversation with your friend, but… it seems as though she really doesn’t want to see me right now.” .:: Riven bit his lip underneath his mask, nodding dumbly. Falcon went on. He never stopped ranting after only one sentence. ::. “I’m not entirely certain what would cause an Assamite to break a bargain. I’ve always been informed that when it comes to work, Assassins are the most dependable you can find. Perhaps you might have some information for me?” .:: Riven’s eyes narrowed. So, not only was Falcon wanting to berate him about Shadow, now he was wanting Riven to sell her out too? That was too much. ::. “I’m afraid there’s not much to say. I may cohabitate with her, but it’s not much other than that. She does what she does, I do what I do. I didn’t see her at all last night, and she wasn’t awake when I left the compound this evening.” .:: Falcon had taken a sip of his liquid, rolling his eyes as though he was completely ignoring anything Riven was saying. He thought Riven hadn’t noticed, but he had. Now he was even more annoyed. Falcon set his glass down on the table, eyeing Riven suspiciously. ::. “Perhaps you could remedy this? I must speak with her about this contract, it is absolutely imperative that it be taken care of.” .:: Riven shook his head. How could Falcon not understand this? ::. “Begging your pardon, but first of all, I am her companion, not her master. I can no more make her see you than I can single-handedly kill my progenitor with my bare hands. Second, she is not incompetent. If you continue to corner her and make her feel like she is, I do not believe the ending will be happy for you.” .:: Falcon’s eyes raged irrationally. ::. “Is that a threat?” .:: Riven shrugged, finding this display rather pathetic. ::. “If Shadow wanted to threaten you, she would do it herself instead of sending me to do it.” .:: Falcon’s anger dimmed for a moment as he nursed that wound. Yes, he knew that much was true, since Shadow had threatened him quite recently, holding a razor-sharp dagger to his throat. He gulped at the memory. It wasn’t a pleasant moment in his mind, lingering still like an itch he couldn’t scratch. He’d tried to come on stronger, maintain that presence to remind her that HE was boss in this case, but it didn’t really seem to be working. That made him angry. No, it made him nervous, but he pretended that it made him angry so he wouldn’t look weak. ::. “I’m not asking for much, just an appointment to see her.” .:: Riven’s voice began to show a hint of annoyance. ::. “You made one with her, and she didn’t come. The best advice I can give you is to wait. When she is ready with information for you, you will know, but until then, you had best not try to control her.” .:: Falcon slammed his open palm down on the table. ::. “No! That is not acceptable!” .:: Anger began to rise within Riven. He rarely allowed himself to be affected by silly things like this, but Falcon was crossing the line. Of course, he wasn’t intelligent enough to realize when to stop, so he continued his ranting. ::. “I demand to be taken back to see her, TONIGHT!” .:: Riven snarled. His rage welled to the breaking point, and then the barrier snapped. His drink turned over and spilled as Riven leapt to his feet, clenching his hands on the edge of the table, and snarling irefully down into Falcon’s face. ::. “You dare impose yourself upon the sanctity of my haven? You dare to DEMAND that I take you there? Mr. Falcon, I recommend that you remove these delusions of grandeur and control out of your head, or I will simply remove the device that is thinking them up before you ever THINK about Shadow again.” .:: Falcon’s fingers had begun to clench about the glass, in a rage, but now he was just terrified. These two were a touchy couple. ::. “I apolo-“ “Save it!” .:: Riven’s words were bitten off. ::. “I have nothing more to say to you. Be attentive. When it’s time for your meeting with Shadow, you’ll know.” .:: Turning, Riven stormed out of the club, everyone parting like the Red Sea. Everyone gave Sewer Rats a wide berth. If it wasn’t because they were so hideous it was intimidating, it was because of that cloying, grave-like scent they had about them. Falcon watched him, his eyes wide. ::. .:: Julian Falcon had never been a strong man, even in life. What he had gained from the Embrace, he had hoped would make up for that. He had power beyond compare now, he was moving among circles of aristocrats and bluebloods, or so he thought. What he was really doing was just playing games. The red, red vitae of Riven’s beverage dripped steadily onto his black leather pants from the ledge of the table, but he didn’t notice it. Julian Falcon was riveted in fear. He’d just realized that he was as powerless and clueless as he always had been. What had he damned himself into? ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: Riven slipped into the haven, not at all looking forward to having this talk with Shadow. He was worried about her, though, as much as Kindred could worry about other Kindred, that is. It wasn’t so much that she’d blown off this meeting with Falcon. Falcon was a pawn. Who cared about Falcon? What he WAS worried about was the fact that she seemed to be shying away entirely from the agreement she’d made. She was already high profile, as far as vampires went. The last thing that she needed to do was make enemies by welching on a deal. ::. .:: She was reading on the divan when he entered. The book that was in her hands was Baudelaire’s poetry, Les Fleurs du Mal, French version, if he had to guess. She’d heard him come in, but she smirked slightly, instead of greeting him. ::. “Dans une terre grasse et pleine d’escargots je veux creuser moi-même une fosse profonde, où je puisse à loisir étaler mes vieux os et dormirdans l’oubli comme un requin dans l’onde… Lovely, hm? I always wonder how Baudelaire could describe these macabre things so accurately without knowing anything of them. Had I been in Paris at the time, I would have sired him. He had genius.” .:: Riven smiled sheepishly. ::. “I’m afraid I never read much literature when I had the chance, so now, by force of habit, I don’t do it at all. Much less French literature… I’m not too knowledgeable of the language.” .:: She looked up at him, her green eyes looking satisfied, yet… different. He couldn’t describe how. ::. “Oh, my apologies then. Shall I translate?” .:: He nodded. Shadow appeared to think for a moment. It wasn’t always easy to transfer Baudelaire, or any French verse, into English that made sense. ::. “Umm… roughly, it says… In a soil thick with snails and rich as grease I’ve longed to dig myself a good deep grave, there to stretch my old bones at ease and sleep in oblivion, like a shark in a wave.” .:: Her brow furrowed. ::. “I do wonder if he had any clue of what might be beyond that…” .:: Riven shook his head, as though appreciating Baudelaire’s depth. Truth be told, he really didn’t understand much about poetry. It just wasn’t his forte, and it never had been. There was an awkward pause. Shadow’s attention was immediately drawn to it. He looked irritated. Must have had a run-in with Falcon tonight, or so she surmised. Riven often went to Club Tenebrae in the early evenings, and Falcon always seemed to be lurking there, like a specter. Riven looked at her, serious thoughts written across his usually neutral eyes. ::. “Shadow I… spoke with Falcon tonight.” .:: She rolled her eyes. ::. “Great. My favorite asshole. What did he want this time?” .:: Riven slipped his hands behind his back, pacing as he spoke. ::. “I’m sure you could guess. He was concerned about the fact that the two of you had an appointment last night, and you didn’t come.” “Don’t you mean he was mewling and whining because I didn’t fill him in with exactly the same details I told him last time?” .:: Riven smiled slightly, though his demeanor did not change. ::. “You know him as well as I do… of course that’s what he was doing.” .:: He cleared his throat. ::. “As well, he demanded to be brought back here to see you.” .:: Shadow sat up quickly from her lounging position, ramrod-straight. ::. “What the fuck? Please tell me you didn’t bring him back here!” .:: Riven shook his head slowly. ::. “No. I wouldn’t have done that no matter how irate he got. I’m a Sewer Rat, you should know me better than that. My privacy is more important than his concern over whether or not you are doing his bidding… By the way, I told him it was a very bad idea to continue hounding you so as though you don’t know what you’re doing.” .:: She nodded. ::. “Good thing someone told him. I was about to inform him of that fact via a knife up his ass. So I guess it’s a good thing that I left last night. He should appreciate that.” .:: Riven paused, then sighed. Considering how odd it was for a Kindred to sigh, that got her attention. She arched an eyebrow at him, refusing to question him. Something was wrong, and she knew it, but she wasn’t going to stand there and dig it out of him if he didn’t want to talk. ::. “Shadow… I’m concerned about this.” “This what?” .:: Her voice was slightly tentative. She was really in no mood for a lecture tonight, and she hoped Riven wasn’t about to give her one, although she had a distinct feeling that he was. Riven pressed his hands together behind his back, cracking his knuckles with a cacophony that was most unsettling. ::. “This meaning… this job, what you’re doing right now. You seem to be losing interest, you seem to me to be becoming… unsettled, if you will.” “Riven, please be blunt. I’m in no mood to beat around the bush tonight.” .:: She sighed, setting her book of Baudelaire down on the table, crossing her arms over her chest. He almost lost his nerve; Shadow wasn’t easy to talk to when she was irritated. This would prove no different, he was certain. ::. “Liana… it is just… Falcon is of no consequence to me. I’m not concerned about your clan duty, either, because frankly, I don’t know what that is, and I don’t care to know. But when it comes to my personal concern, I’m very worried that you slipping away from your intent will come to very bad ends.” “What do you mean?” .:: In his head, he gathered the evidence. There was plenty of it, he just had to present it to her in a way that would make her see. ::. “First of all, you know this LaBatte fellow is an Assassin slayer, at the head of a group of ravening thugs who would love nothing more than to get their hands on you. He can move about in daylight too, when we are vulnerable. He is cunning, and he has power. Ignoring those things would be folly, and if you get yourself into this hit too quickly, it will likely end up the worse for you. On the other hand indeed, if you back out on Julian Falcon, he will stop at nothing to see his wants revenged. He is a spoiled brat, that much we both know, and making an enemy is the last thing you need to do right now. Plus, from what you have told me, Assassins are bound by their word. You gave your word.” .:: She narrowed her eyes at him. ::. “I know that very well, thank you—“ “Wait. I’m not quite finished yet.” .:: He paused. She nodded for him to go ahead, although she was already eager to jump in and silence his lecture with a hundred words. ::. “You owe that much to yourself… to keep your deal. It’s part of your nature, and to go against that would set you back a hundred steps, not just one or two. Can you imagine how Amir would feel if you broke a deal like that, after everything he taught you while he was still here?” .:: She exploded inside. Why had he brought Amir into it? What business did he have doing so? He didn’t even know Amir well enough to say what Amir would have felt about anything. And besides… the longer she kept following the Clan’s outmoded doctrines religiously, the longer she was trapped. Maybe Assamites weren’t supposed to break their promises, but she didn’t care. ::. “Amir is NONE of your concern, Riven, so I’ll thank you to hold your tongue about someone you only know through the account I’ve given you. On top of that… Assamites don’t break deals. That includes deals to themselves, and I promised myself that I was going to take care of myself. You know what that means? That means, now and again, stopping to make sure I’m still on course in WMW and PTC. You know, what I’ve built up for MYSELF? It’s my responsibility to make sure that I’m independent, that I only need myself to get by, and I’m sorry if that doesn’t conform to Julian Falcon’s rigid standards of when I should do work for him. If he knew best, he’d shut his goddamn mouth and appreciate what he gets when he gets it, which will be MY time, no matter how much he pisses and moans! Julian Falcon is not my ally, nor my friend. I owe him nothing, and if it wasn’t for the fact that I want my damn payment, I’d have killed him by now.” .:: That was one of the longest rants she’d ever directed at him, usually being a person of few words. They had that much in common. But what they didn’t have in common was perception. Riven thought he saw her correctly, but she was always shifting, always changing shape. He supposed that was what Assassins had to do; they had to adapt to stay alive. ::. .:: He wanted to say something else, but he found his own voice frozen as he watched her stand up. She snatched her book of Baudelaire from the table, and slipped angrily into her coat. She probably wasn’t angry at him, just angry at her situation, and she’d probably come to apologize later. He didn’t know what she planned on doing, but whatever it was, if he tried to interfere at this point, she’d never forgive him. It was best to just let her go. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: The streets embraced her, as they always embraced their own. She’d been born of the Parisian streets, in the middle of a glorious revolution, and she’d been young, quite young, when Robespierre’s Terror had taken that away from her. And thus, she’d been cast out in the streets. She lived at her cousin’s house, but she and her siblings, especially her twin, had always been wild. Shadow retained the lessons of the street well. That was probably what had caught Amir’s attention in the first place, even when she was playing at being a demure little nun. ::. .:: She stalked through alleyways, ducking into the darkness whenever she sensed someone coming. It wasn’t so much that she had something to hide from, or that she was running scared; just that she didn’t like people or Kindred all that much in the first place, and tonight was a bad time to encounter either of them. ::. .:: When she felt far enough away from Riven and his pointed, stoic, serious lecturing, she slid into an inky-black alleyway with a flickering light at the end of it. She stayed away from that; her eyes adjusted well enough to the dark anyway. She made herself at home in the alleyway. Not like she hadn’t ever crouched in an alleyway, grubby fingers clutching a book before. The only difference this time was that she wasn’t clad in rags. ::. .:: Out of the darkness, a voice rang, deep, mellifluous, smooth like cognac. ::. “Dans ce ciel bizarre et livide, tourmenté comme ton destin, quels pensers dans ton âme vide descendent? Responds, libertin. Insatiablement avide de l’obscur et de l’incertain, je ne geindrai pas comme Ovide chasse du paradis latin. Cieux déchirés comme des grèves, en vous se mire mon orgueil; vos vastes nuages en deuil sont les corbillards de mes rêves, et vos lueurs sont le reflet de l’Enfer où mon cœur se plait.” .:: She didn’t look up from her book. In fact, she had read that very poem just a few moments ago, and whoever this was had apparently been watching her long enough to discern that fact. There was a pause, and a puff of cigarette smoke from somewhere nearby. She could smell its pungent, onerous odor. ::. “I like a bit of Baudelaire myself, but I am more enchanted by the goddess reading him.” .:: The voice that assailed her now was Iberian in origin. That made it slightly strange that the French had been so well spoken, she thought to herself. She’d never met one person in her entire life who could speak French correctly if their first language was Spanish. She still didn’t look up, and there was a mock-disappointed sigh. ::. “Not even going to compliment on how flawless my pronunciation was? I worked on it just for you.” .:: Her retort was smooth. ::. “My, my, aren’t you a smooth talker? You must be a Ravnos with all that rampant flattery that means absolutely nothing.” .:: There was laughter. ::. “Gideon de Medici, already serving you in more ways than you can imagine, plus whatever you might request.” .:: She looked up just in time for him to capture her hand and press it to his smirking lips. She stood up, slipping her book into the pocket of her trenchcoat. ::. “Hm… you’re practiced at this, are you?” .:: He smirked a little wider. She seemed put off, but he noticed that she didn’t pull her hand away. His charm had never failed him, even on the toughest nut to crack. She might have looked that way, but he had a feeling that deep down inside, she was just waiting for someone like him to sweep her off her feet. ::. .:: She had a moment to look at him. His hair was long, straight, and chestnut brown, hanging down his back in a silken curtain. It looked as though he took time immemorial each night to stand before the mirror and groom it, although that was a silly idea. Kindred styling their hair. What would be the point? ::. .:: His eyes were clear blue, piercing to the hilt, and dancing with mischief. So it must have been with all Ravnos, she surmised, although she’d never met one face to face until this moment. He dropped her hand softly, pressing one of his hands on the brick wall behind her, leaning ever closer to her. ::. “So… why is a beautiful muse like yourself sitting in an alleyway alone reading Baudelaire?” .:: At this, she laughed. ::. “I, a muse? I think you might have been drinking some tainted vitae, sir. People could hardly call Kindred muses of any sort, especially other Kindred. We tend to lose our inspiration when we’re Embraced, no?” .:: Gideon de Medici looked at her with an air of passion. ::. “Perhaps it is so, but I find my tattered soul awakens at the sight of immortal beauty. I feel almost human again, looking at you. I feel as though I should drop to my knees and pray.” .:: With all the facetiousness he could muster, he dropped to his knees and whipped out a rosary from places unknown, beginning to count his beads as he prayed a Hail Mary. Shadow arched an eyebrow, staring at the spectacle for a moment, then shaking her head. She was immensely thankful for her ability to sneak, she thought to herself. She left the alleyway quietly. Gideon opened her eyes to find her gone, and frowned petulantly. ::. .:: Moments later, she found him beside her again, as though he’d appeared out of nowhere. He smiled at her, beguiling, charming… something that utterly annoyed her to the core. ::. “Back so soon, Gideon de Medici?” “I wouldn’t give up over simple flight, my sweet. You have not repelled me with words or fists yet, so that must mean you are playing games with my fragile heart. I shall not let you sneak away, fair Imogen!” .:: She rolled her eyes, but nevertheless, found herself at least slightly entertained by his presence. She could see why some people found the Ravnos quite entertaining… the educated ones, at least. For a moment, she arched an eyebrow at him. ::. “I suppose the next thing you’re going to tell me is that you were Catherine de Medici’s grand-nephew?” .:: He smirked. ::. “Nothing of the sort. We all take our trade names, don’t we? After all, I would assume you don’t go by your real name anymore… what name have you chosen to grace yourself with, Assassin?” .:: He gestured to the dagger hanging from her hip. He’d also guessed from her lithe build that she was something like an Assamite, something quick, feral and dangerous. The only other thing she could have been was a Ravnos, and he’d have been bored of her if she had been. ::. “Shadow.” “Shadow?!” .:: His voice burst from his throat, surprised. ::. “How can you refer to yourself as something so drab? You do not fade into darkness, you illuminate the earth!” .:: She rolled her eyes. ::. “Spare the pleasantry please. Is there a point to this conversation?” .:: He shrugged, taking another pull of his cigarette in a rather unnatural fashion. She didn’t suppose it mattered; after all, his lungs were probably already dust anyway, so what harm would smoking do? ::. “A point? There is rarely a point to anything, my dearest Shadow. The best that we can do is grasp at straws until we find something that seems right.” .:: She turned to look at him sharply. ::. “I don’t have time for riddles. If you have something to say to me, say it now, or I’ll be on my way. Don’t make this difficult.” .:: For some time, they walked on in silence. Gideon felt a chord of sympathy reverberate in his jaded soul for her, for some reason. He’d always had a weakness for beautiful women, Kindred and Kine alike. This one was interesting, though. This one didn’t seem so shallow, or so full of petty concerns. Yet, he also sensed that she was like a tempest in a teapot. She was contained in her shell, yet under the surface, angst teemed in a vicious whirlwind. He tossed his cigarette away. ::. “What troubles could possibly furrow the ivory perfection of your brow, hm?” .:: For a moment, Shadow considered biting his head off, perhaps literally. But there was really no sense in that. After all, he was harmless. If he’d wanted to fuck with her head, he would have done it by now. What harm was there in talking to him? He didn’t know the whole story, and didn’t have any prejudices toward or against her. Maybe he could give her some insight. ::. “To be honest, I find myself pulled in two different directions. On the one hand, I have this idea… that I’m supposed to follow what my elders taught me, and accept that I am an Assassin with a mission, always with a mission to do something or other, always under orders from someone else. Up until recently, I’ve been perfectly alright with that. But… the past few weeks, I’ve been getting this crazy idea in my head that the past, those teachings included, are holding me back. That in order to move ahead with what I’m doing, I have to forego what I was taught and step into something new entirely.” .:: Gideon chewed his lip thoughtfully. ::. “Where are these ideas coming from?” .:: She shrugged. ::. “As strange as it sounds, visions. I’ve been having these visions, some involving the people of my past, my Sire, clan people, clan places… and then there’s the stuff that seems to be so familiar to me, but are visions of places I’ve never been. There’s always a man there, an Eastern European man, whom I seem to feel some obligation to. But I’ve never met him in my life. He’s always advising me to go down a new path. It’s quite… strange.” .:: He looked at her almost incredulously. Only an Assassin could come up with just the word “strange” to describe a spiritual experience like that. His blue eyes caressed hers earnestly. ::. “Visions aren’t corporeal, no, but they often serve as warnings or guides… I’ve manipulated them since my Embrace, but if you are seeing them without any outside help from anyone else, you should heed what they tell you.” .:: She nodded. At least he felt the same way she did. ::. “I would, but… there’s something holding me back. I probably shouldn’t ramble too much about this, but I’m in the middle of a dangerous job right now. If I don’t complete it, I’ll be breaking a deal, but breaking with my past all the same, like the man from Prague tells me to in my visions.” “Breaking a deal, hm?” .:: She nodded her head only once in assent. ::. “The Sewer Rat that I live with advises me that I should keep my word to my contractor, because if I don’t, I’ll be ruining my devotion to the Clan, and also putting myself in great danger.” .:: Gideon laughed. ::. “What does a Nosferatu know about living life without secret deals brokered in the middle of sewers teeming with rats and shit? Your housemate is unimaginative.” .:: Gideon shrugged almost inconclusively, but it was sort of a gesture that had become built into his personality. It didn’t really mean anything in particular. ::. “There’s a problem with this whole business of guilt-tripping yourself for deal-breaking.” .:: She looked at him curiously. ::. “And that is?” .:: He smirked. ::. “The only deal that you cannot break is a deal with the devil himself, my dearest goddess. I don’t think you believe in the devil, do you?” .:: He didn’t wait for her to answer before he continued. ::. “No good Catholics actually believe what they were taught. Besides… Julian Falcon is not the devil, no matter how much he wishes to think so.” .:: Surprise mingled with alarm jolted through her like an electric-chair blast. ::. “How did you know it was Falcon?” .:: She turned to look at him, in utter surprise. Laughter echoed in the city streets, bouncing off the walls. But Gideon was gone. ::. .:: ::: ::. --Poetry quotes taken from Baudelaire’s “Le Mort Joyeux,” and “L’horreur Sympathique.”--
