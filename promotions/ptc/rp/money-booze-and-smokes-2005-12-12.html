Money Booze and Smokes

12 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-12 It began Saturday morning. It was unusually warm for November, but not enough to neglect a jacket. The sun was peaking over the mountainous horizon. A light breeze caused the maple leaves to fall like rain. Teresa was deep in a dreamless sleep. After a night spent at a redneck joint called Shamrock, there was nothing more that she wanted to do than sleep the day away. A faint knock and a turn of the doorknob would disturb this peaceful slumber. "Teresa, you have a phone call. It’s Darrel. He says it’s important." Mama Tomas stood in the doorway with a cordless phone in her hand. The aging woman was dressed in her floral nightgown and pink fuzzy slippers. Her graying hair was rolled in curlers. Teresa pushed herself up sleepily. If she missed anything about her childhood home besides her mother’s cooking, it’s her extra comfortable cedar bed. Unfortunately when on the road, non-stop, lugging a solid wood bed and mattress is impossible. The alarm clock read 8:00 a.m. Teresa wondered why Darrel of all people would be up at this hour. Mama Tomas handed Teresa the phone and left the bedroom to continue with her morning chores. "I hope you know it’s 8 o’clock in the morning. This better be important." On the other line, Darrel sounded anxious. She could tell by his breathing that he was smoking. He spoke between breaths and his tone almost pleading. "Dammit Teresa, I’m in some shit now. You gotta help me out or I’m a dead man." "What are you talking about Darrel? What have you gotten yourself into now?" Teresa climbed out of the bed. Her bare feet hit the wooden floor. She was dressed in her long red flannel night gown with short sleeves and a v-neck. While she held the phone on her shoulder with her cheek, Teresa grabbed a pair of Levi’s from the foot of the bed and slipped them on, then walked to the closet to look for a shirt. Darrel continued after he released the last puff of smoke. "You remember last night at the Shamrock? Dang, I hope you remember. I got into that card game with that group of Mexicans. Remember?" "Yeah, from what I gathered, you lost your ass. Don’t tell me. You don’t have the money to pay the debt and you’re running to me for it." "Well yeah, but there’s more. I’m serious Teresa. I need your help. Those guys, they were part of the Lopez group. If I don’t come up with five grand by tonight, I’m toast." "Geez Darrel, hold on a sec." Teresa set the phone on the bed, pulled the gown off over her head and put on a dark blue Indianapolis Colts t-shirt. She then picked up the phone and walked into the bathroom. "Ok, let me get this straight. You lost a card game to a few Latino mafia guys. You don’t have the money and you wanting me to bail you out." "Something like that. Please Teresa. I make more money under-the-table than I should. You know that, but it’s just not enough. I’m gonna be out back around noon. Meet me there." That was it. Teresa heard a ‘click’ and then the dial tone. He had said to meet her ‘out back’. That was simply his way of saying to meet her at his hidden still. The old moonshine-making machine was about 5 miles into the woods behind his grandpa’s abandoned home. The still was indeed his grandfather’s and in Darrel’s eyes, he’s just carrying on the family business. Once the finished alcohol product is sealed in single quart mason jars, they are sold to trusted buyers. Darrel always seemed to get himself into some sort of trouble. When they were kids, he would always get caught stealing cheap toys at the local Dollar Store. As teens, Teresa took the rap when his parents found a pack of cigarettes and a six pack of beer in his truck for the first time. Now as adults, he still gets into some sort of mischief raging from coming on to a pretty girl in a bar only to find a bad-ass boyfriend with a mean punch to filling up his Ford pick-up at the Shell and conveniently forgets his money at home. She’s read all about the Italian mobsters, watched the Hollywood-hyped movies even. But this time was different. From what she gathered, the Mexicans were deep into the drug business and as vicious as vipers. If that’s not enough, where will she find five grand? HSW doesn’t pay her THAT much. Loans are out of the question considering her credit rating. Darrel’s own credit history is even worse. There were more. Darrel wasn’t telling her everything. Teresa just had a hunch. She had no choice. She had to help her cousin somehow
