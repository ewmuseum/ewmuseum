Family Matters: Part Two

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 Usually, only a large weeping willow tree cast its shadow over the gravesite of James Michael Wilson, father of PTC superstar Seymour Almasy. But today, ten years after his death, two other figures stood on the nearby soil, voices raised, ire aroused, and language disrespecting the memory of the unknowing man sleeping peacefully below. Perhaps it was best that James had died a decade ago. Especially considering it was his wife and son now clashing on sacred ground. "Come on, Seymour," Jessica said, taunting him with the calm expression that she was forcing herself to assume. "It’s been ten years. Can’t we let bygones be bygones?" "No," came the firm reply from Almasy. "I can not." The snort she gave in response made Seymour’s blood boil. "Why not? Surely this isn’t all over me leaving you home alone! It’s not like you even cared, with the video games we got you…" "Video games aren’t a substitute for parenting!" he thundered, looking at the woman who theoretically cared for him. "No," she admitted, grinning unnervingly, "but they did hold up for a while, now didn’t they, Jason." "My name is SEYMOUR." Seymour sighed deeply, before looking at his mother. Really looking at her. She’d aged a bit since the last time he saw her. Somehow, though, the prison time she’d spent hadn’t cracked through her proud dedication to her face and figure. He didn’t like to be petty. But she had called him JASON. In light of that, he figured he could allow himself to be petty. "You don’t look your age," he sniped, allowing himself a tight smile. "The Botox injections and plastic surgery Dad’s money got you seem to be holding up well." "Beauty is timeless," she retorted. "Even if it has to be paid for." He almost chuckled at that. Almost. In his anger, though, he’d almost forgotten something. A few things, actually. Most importantly was the question of why the heck his mother was here anyway. Less importantly was his curiosity about his biological parent. Child neglect wasn’t going to put her away for ten years, so when had she gotten out? And what had she been doing since? "Heh…some things don’t change, Jason," Jessica said, looking at her son with a knowing expression. "You still pout when you’re thinking about something." Seymour’s hand shot up almost immediately shot up to cover his mouth. His mother only laughed in response, letting her hands fall to her sides. "Animosity aside, I know you’re wondering what dear old mom’s been up to all this time, aren’t you?" Even ten years later, it seemed, a mother’s intuition was still a powerful tool. "Go ahead and tell me," Seymour said, "since it seems you’re rather insistent on keeping me here to chatter with." Jessica brightened. Clearly, she’d been waiting for this opportunity. Taking a deep breath, and clearing her throat, she launched into her narrative. "They sent me to jail, Jason! For three years! Three years of my life spent in that terrible place. It was HORRIBLE!" If she was expecting any sympathy from her son, however, she was in for a rather large surprise. "They don’t make jails to be nice, you know. They’re supposed to punish criminals. Like you." "I am NOT a criminal," she insisted, glaring at her son through slitted eyes. "Is going out partying a crime?" "It is when you leave your CHILD home alone to do it!" Clearly, Almasy wasn’t buying his mom’s excuses. "Besides," he continued, anger pouring into every syllable, "while it may not be a legally punishable offense, most people don’t look too kindly on adultery, do they?" For a split-second, Jessica Wilson’s face betrayed signs of shock, before she reconstructed her façade. "Adultery," she asked, tsk-tsking her son with a wagging finger as she spoke. "What would give you the idea that I would ever cheat on your father?" Oh, how Seymour hated her. She actually had the audacity to lie at the very grave of her ex-husband, the man whom she had been unfaithful to for most of their marriage. Seymour winced, realizing that what he was about to say would come as a great shock to a poor, helpless deceased man who had always believed in the sanctity of his family. Sorry, dad. But it’s time you know the truth. "Mrs. Winters told me. And Mrs. Chang. And Mr. Braun, for that matter. I believe he was one of your, err, companions?" "How the Hell do you know about that," she hissed, like a reptile, clearly not pleased that her son knew one of her many secrets. "It was bound to filter down to me eventually. If you want to keep clandestine affairs a secret, it would help to NOT fuck every married man in town. I’m just glad Laura told me, so that I could understand what a whore my mo--" "That SLUT!" Seymour’s mother seethed, shaking her head violently. "I always knew she was trouble, ever since the first day you brought her home in kindergarten…" For a few seconds, Seymour was ready to concur with the slut comment. But to do so would be to actually AGREE with the bitch that was his mother. "If nothing else," Seymour said, "she cared about me more than you ever did." "Even considering the whole torrid sex with a random stranger bit," Mom replied, wickedly. "Never thought I’d be reading about my son in the supermarket tabloids I love so much…" Fucking figured. Damned tabloids had cost him his marriage to begin with, they might as well betray the heart wrenching moments of his life to anyone who wanted to pay a few dollars. "Look," Seymour said, clearly ticked off. "I came here to pay respects to my father. I have. Now, will you let me out of here before I end up desecrating his memory by thinking negatively on the day of his death?" "Not quite yet, Seymour," she said, smiling. "We’ve got the matter of your career to discuss…" The look in her eyes was one he knew quite well. A look of greed. A look she had quite often when Mr. Wilson gave her money to go shopping. "Forget it. You’re not sponging off me like you did my father." "Oh come on," she replied, flipping her hair back. "You saw how Rich Rollins responds to a hot mom. With me in your corner, you can’t lose!" Seymour, for his part, was unconvinced. "If I let my mother manage me," he proclaimed, "I’d have to kick my OWN ass." "Ha, very funny. I’ve raised a comedian. Or, rather, neglected one. Good news though, son. I’ve come to a decision. It’s time to give you some motherly advice." Before he could get off some snarky comment about her waiting so long to give him any advice at all, she had already begun speaking. "Jason, this is a dog eat dog world. You can’t get ahead just by being a nice guy. You’ve got to take shortcuts. You’ve got to stand up for yourself. But, then again, you already knew that, didn’t you?" A quirked eyebrow was his only response. From the look on his mother’s face, though, it was all she could do to not gleefully rub her hands together. "I’ve seen your career. I’ve seen your life. You’re in a line of work where you succeed by ruining the dreams of other people. Like that Back Alley Brawler fellow you beat last round. He was just a man trying to make a better life for his daughter, trying to give her the things he never had. And you stopped him." He opened his mouth to protest, but her rant continued unabated, the acidic words whipping through the cemetery like a stinging wind. "And your beloved ex-wife! You cheated on her, Jason! With that slut from LOW, whatever her name was!" "Alyssa Sanders," Seymour replied, weakly. "You’ve decapitated small woodland creatures," she continued. "Innocent bunny rabbits, killed at your hand! You’re ruining a man’s relationship as we speak, by dating Tessa Windsor! You’ve committed more crimes and ill deeds than I ever could. And why, Jason?" She grinned then, a wicked, sordid grin that made the Final Fantasy’s heart stop. "Because you know what this world is as well as anyone. Your father died tragically, and I abandoned you. For years, you’ve been claiming to be this purveyor of good, a champion of justice. And you know what? You’ve fooled everyone. Everyone…except me. Because I’m the only one who knows what you went through in your childhood. I heard you cry as the bullies in school made fun of you. I saw you and Laura talking about your futures. You’re not a hero, Jason. You’re a villain. A monster." Lies. They had to be. All of them. But she spoke with such conviction that he believed it. Damn near every word. "But…but--" "And now, you’re going to destroy another man’s dream. I know you. You’re not going to lose this week. You’re going to beat that poor retarded boy Clyde Walkins senseless. And you’re going to advance in GTT5. Just think about your actions, for once. Just think about what you’re doing. Think about everyone you’ve hurt. Can you live with ruining another innocent man? Can you, Seymour?" Slowly, Seymour slumped against the trunk of the willow tree, utterly crushed. He attempted to blubber out a response, but his mother simply turned on her high heel and left, head held high, seemingly proud of what she’d done to her only son. "Is she…could she be right?" Somehow, Seymour had never really thought of his career in terms of constantly ruining other men’s hopes. Sure, last round he had, but that was due to the unique situation of the Brawler. But he HAD done inexcusable things in his career. He HAD cheated on his wife. He WAS trying to ruin Chris Storm’s life. And he DID decapitate innocent woodland creatures. Was everything really all a lie? That thought was crushing. It literally undermined everything that he had ever done in a wrestling ring. Looking down at the gravestone, he nearly cried. All was for naught. "I’m…sorry, Dad. I’m sorry I never really became a hero. I’m sorry for everything." ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Making her way out of the cemetery, she walked to the parking lot, where a long black limousine awaited. The chauffeur stood waiting at the door, opening it with a bow. Mrs. Wilson stepped inside, taking her seat next to an immaculately dressed gentleman, who gave her a quick nod. "Did it go well," he asked, a clear look of interest on his face. "Of course it did," she replied. "Idiot’s blubbering by his father’s grave as we speak. Now, where’s my money?" The gentleman reached into his coat in response, pulling out an envelope. "Here you are," he said, formally. "As promised. $25,000, in $100 bills." "Thanks," came the chirpy reply. "Now I can go get my tits done! Plastic surgery’s not cheap, you know." As quickly as she had entered the limousine, she left, heading for her car parked on the other side of the lot. Alone in the limo, the man pulled out a cellular telephone, and punched a few numbers. Holding the phone to his ear, he waited for the person on the other end to pick up. "Hello? Yeah, this is Phil. Yep, yep, things went perfectly. According to her, Seymour’s crying right now. He’s going to be in no condition to compete against Walkins. Yes, I know that if I’m wrong, I’m going to be in serious trouble. Yes, yes, Walkins is a moron, but even HE can’t screw up the pitch we’re lobbing him. Don’t worry. Everything’s going according to plan. Take care, boss." Clicking the phone off, Phil wiped his brow. "This’ll work." He thought for a few moments, before amending that statement. "For my sake, it had better work." With a wave of his hand, Phil told the driver to move on. Soon, there was little evidence that the limousine had ever been there to begin with. Just as it was supposed to be. ------------------------------------------------------- 1/10/2006 Dear Journal, Somehow, everything matters more now. I live for tournaments, for the thrill of battle they bring, and the sudden death atmosphere. There are few second chances. Win and advance. Lose and go home. It’s that simple. But everything’s getting so messed up. First Laura. Now my mother. Something’s wrong in my life, I can’t deny it. It might be my own doing, but I get the sinking feeling that it’s not. Someone’s trying to ruin me. As ridiculous as it sounds, someone is trying to destroy me and everything I stand for. If this was any other time of year, I wouldn’t believe it myself. I’d call myself the crackpot everyone would believe me to be if I made these accusations in public, like everyone thought I was when I believed Archibald MacGregor to be a murderer. But GTTs always lead to the best and worst of humanity. They lead to love and lust, hatred and sorrow, destruction and suffering. Men have died in the course of this tournament. Women have had their hearts broken. Life loses all meaning and value. And for what, for a trophy? For a chance to be called the best? Why does this always happen? Why are we all seemingly fated to suffer unimaginable pain? Clyde Walkins isn’t. Best I can tell, he’s grinning like an idiot, on the hot streak of his life. He doesn’t have a damn thing wrong with him. I envy him. But if I want to get to the bottom of this mystery, I have to beat him. Sure, I could take the easy way out and lose, like my mother suggested. The pain probably WOULD stop. That’s the easy way out. I can’t take that way. Not if I want to live up to everything I’ve claimed to be all this time. There are 32 men left in GTT5. Soon, there will be but 16. Being one of those sixteen means that I will suffer even more, but in so suffering, I may gain more clues as to why it is happening. This tournament is my ultimate quest, the most important I have ever embarked on. Through victory, I can understand everything. I don’t know that for sure, but it’s the only idea I have. I’ve been asking myself if I’m going to change the world, or if I’m going to change myself. The answer to that question is clear. I’m going to change myself. No more bunny decapitations. No more trying to ruin other men’s lives. Except in the ring. Unfortunately, that’s unavoidable. Clyde Walkins, I’m sorry that you were drawn against me. I have no quarrel with you. But you stand in the way of me understanding my pain. I must defeat you. For Laura. For my mother. For my father. And for me. To whomever is trying to break me, I challenge them to do their worst. Because, in trying to destroy me today, my mother reminded me of one thing. I have survived the death of my father. I have survived the neglect of my mother. I have survived cages and cells. I have survived divorce. No matter how despicable I may find them, I can survive their shadows. -S.A.
