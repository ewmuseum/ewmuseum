Arrival And Revelation (Global Title)

20 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was good to be the Uber Judge. He woke with a blue sky above and clouds below. A smiling dimwitted piece of ass in a silly blue costume provided by her airline greeted him warmly with, “Good morning, Sir, did you sleep well?” as she reached for the silly little tiny pillow that fit perfectly behind his neck. “Yes,” said the Uber Judge, “it was good. The sleeping.” There was much to do, but for right now, he was on the plane. Though the rule about electronic devices was in place, the truth was that was a rule only for little people who flew in classes other than first. But the Uber Judge chose to obey it none the less, as it gave him a small slice of time to do absolutely nothing at all. And gloat. Times had been good for ole UJ. Prime Time Central’s Administrative Division had taken an interest in him. A fresh voice, a fresh point of view, grounded in the reality and circumstance that wrestlers come from, yet with the mind and eloquence of an executive. Candid communication regarding what plans worked well and why, and suddenly he was the center of the most prized information that PTC had ever known. But mostly, the Uber Judge liked talking about tournaments. Often. Frequently. “I just can’t wait to say hello to that Prime Time Central Vice President of Tournament Execution.” And then the Uber Judge laughed. He laughed in that special deep voiced rumbling that he enjoyed perfecting so much. It came up from the diaphragm and spilled out of him like a sonic fountain, temporarily causing the plane the shake. The pilot claimed it was sudden turbulence. The Uber Judge covered his mouth with one hand in a mockery of shame, his grin barely concealed. Why was he so happy? He was flying back to the States from Australia. Yes, Australia, where he had spent several days in the company of the Ultimate Head Honcho of PTC referred to by his many subordinates as, “The Man.” The Man was said with a necessary amount of awe. If you just said, “The Man” without the awe, no one would believe you were talking about, “The Man” (-awe-), but instead just Whitey or whatever. Yes, he’d spent several days with The Man and had the privilege of meeting his ex-porn-star of a wife. Much was discussed. Much was postulated on. Behind the plane the Uber Judge rode on was a wind of change. The Uber Judge was pleased. He had a presentation that afternoon before the Vice Presidents of PTC. Most would be appearing themselves, though many would be there by conference call. “The Man” himself had a policy of never going to meetings though. He considered them complete wastes of time when he had better and more fun things to do. The Uber Judge didn’t necessarily disagree, as meetings were actually mostly wastes of time, but this meeting would be special. He was in charge. Well, not really. But he’d be the primary speaker. And that was actually better than being in charge, because that meant when he was done speaking, he could leave, which, as everyone who’s experienced meetings knows, is the very best part of the meeting, the leaving that is. But what made the Uber Judge the happiest, was that his rival in the PTC organization, The Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournament Execution, would be quite unhappy regarding the Uber Judge’s continued accumulation of power. The Uber Judge giggled a little at the delight of it. He’d been able to pick the brain of The Man regarding this dastardly character. It turned out that the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournament Execution wasn’t actually a person. He didn’t actually have a name. He just kind of appeared one day, and started arranging the tournaments. Questions were raised of course. For example, “Who is this guy? What is he doing here? Are we paying him?” which were quickly quieted by, “Shut up! He’s doing a great job isn’t he- it- whatever? You don’t want to break the spell do you?” And then there were only rumors. It seemed as if `the spell’ which originally just referred to the fact that tournaments were suddenly and uncharacteristically going well, was transmutated into the idea that the VP of those Tournament Things (which is what The Man called him) was some kind of demon or creature summoned onto the earth. But The Man admitted to not really giving a shit. So the Uber Judge took that in stride, wondering if indeed the VP of those Tournament Things was secretly villainous and ate babies, because it’s nice to pretend that your rival is something unholy since it makes you seem kind of holy by contrast, which is a pretty good feeling. The Uber Judge indulged himself in a bit of fun by practicing a clever line. “Good Morning Vice President of those Tournament Things, did you have any nice babies for breakfast this morning? Do you slice them up and put them in your Cheerios like bananas? Or do you prefer crack open their heads and spoon their brains out like a grapefruit?” Oh, what fun. Meanwhile in the United States of America! The Vice President of those Tournament Things growled in frustration as he gazed into the waters of his birdbath, his preferred place to do auguries (his back yard). “Just because I'm not a real person, doesn’t mean I eat babies, jackass!” he growled. He turned away from the birdbath and went into the place that served as his home. No living furniture. An empty kitchen made way into an empty “living room” where a flat screen tv occupied a wall (to save space?) and his i-mac rested on a card table with a plain wooden stool in front. He sat there and crossed his arms, “Moron practices his lines. Once a wrestler, always a wrestler.” “Henchman!” he shouted, and his flat screen tv turned on, revealing a a stick figure drawn in red crayon on a white background whose only distinguishing feature was a mouth full of poorly drawn sharp teeth. Out the tv’s speakers came the figure’s voice, “Yes Master? How may I serve you?” “The usual,” replied the VP of those Tournament Things nonchalantly. “Aw fuck,” said the henchman, “I was honestly hoping to maybe perform a task in the physical realm for you. Get some oranges, make you a latte..” “I have no need for such nonsense, henchman, and I’ll repeat myself, I aquired you to exist in that tv and that’s where you’re going to stay.” The stick figure burst into sobs, “But Master! I want to run around and DO stuff!” “I am a thinker, a plotter, and organizer by nature, henchman, I do not do stuff. Other people do things. I tell them what to do. You are merely an assistant to mental clarity through dialogue.” “But Master, isn’t the crux of your problem with the Uber Judge the fact that he won’t stay confined to his role as a wrestler? That he fancies himself a thinker and plotter and planner too in defiance of his role?” “Precisely! Yet another reason for you to remain in the television. Now, more questions pertanent to the matter at hand.” The stick figure sighed and began to pace back and forth across the television screen in a thinking type pose. “Well,” said the Henchman, “first of all, the Uber Judge has come back from a visit with the Australian. That means that all the others will be swayed by his mere presence. Second, it’s possible that the Australian has indicated that the Uber Judge is to do certain things. Third, the Uber Judge doesn’t like you very much.” “Stop,” said the VP of those Tournment Things, “The Australian likes intellectuals, but not necessarily to work with. I'm sure they walked around the villa, and the Australian talked about beer, complained about the weather, and bragged about giving his wife "what for" in the bedroom. The meeting was only three days, and I suspect that the Australian merely wanted another person around to chat with, and the Uber Judge seemed as good as any. The Australian doesn’t change his plans or his mind. His wants are simple and the Uber Judge is surely merely using this coincidence to his advantage to try and bully Primetime Central’s Administration into giving him more power and importance.” “Perhaps he wishes to become the Australian’s heir?” asked the Henchman. “No no no. The Australian will never die. Kind of irritating really. But your question was ill-timed. The third point you made is that the Uber Judge doesn’t like me very much, which isn’t true at all. I am merely his rival, but the Uber Judge deep down in his soul is not a hater. He just wants to embarrass me, take me down a peg, like a prank or something. He respects and admires my efficiency, and he should, since I'm the best director of those Tournament Things that could possibly exist.” “That’s true,” said the red crayon henchman, “you are.” “No I fear that the Uber Judge has an idea in his head that’s far worse. Something much much worse. But I am too frightened to think of what it could be. Which means it’s something quiet and secret and probably important.” “And probably revealed later,” said the henchywenchy guy with a small measure of iritation. “But all in all, I cannot be too much afraid. Though I am prideful, I'm not so foolish as to let my pride undo me. Since I exist in this world because I am a wrestling tournament elemental-” “You’re a what?” asked the henchman. “I am a wrestling tournament elemental,” repeated the VP of those Tournament Things, “I exist because of wrestling tournaments. I am a personification of their order, the graph's beauty. The perfect match of individuals, each bye, each subsequently delivery of a loser to the secondary bracket. Those things. While the PTC isn’t the most clean and beautiful tournament, that incidentally and anecdotally exists in Solon, Ohio held by a mother named Daphne Finnegan and uses the States NCAA Greco Roman style just because she’s come to love the sport that her four sons are involved in. But the PTC tournament is the one drawn from the most fame, that has the most fans watching, involves the most wide range of competitors. It is the true seat of my power to exist on this plane, and that’s why I do this, to maintain it’s integrity so that I may continue to live in this world forever.” “To perpetrate your evil deeds?” asked the henchman desperately. “Well, no. I don’t do much that’s evil, apart from hassle the Uber Judge and I suspect he kinds of finds that engaging. But really, no evil to speak up. Just tournaments, and the big bang with the PTC every year.” “This sucks!” shouted the henchman, “I thought you were a demon or something cool and that we’d … you know … take over the world!” “Hah! What kind of moron do you take me for? I’ll leave world domination to morons like the Uber Judge.” “I don’t want to be your henchman anymore! I thought we were up to no good! You clearly suckered me into existence and I want a refund!” “Too bad, henchman, you were the only possessed flat screen television I could find on e-bay, and I'm not about to sell you.” “Aw, dammit,” said the henchman. “Now, be silent!” shouted the VP of those Tournament Thingies, who then turned off the television. “And now, exposition. I believe that my driver will be arriving to take me to the meeting where the Uber Judge will speak.”
