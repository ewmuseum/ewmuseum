The Downward Spiral | Act I: Old Habits Die Hard

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-15 One Month Ago… McKail Jacob McKail abruptly awoke to what felt like an earthquake. Lazily alarmed, he opened his weary eyes only to see Elle leaning over him with her hands dug into shoulder, shaking him furiously into consciousness. “What the fuck….?” He groaned, limply slapping her hands away. “Oh finally!” Elle angrily exclaimed. She dropped down onto the side of the bed, out of breath and her forehead moist with sweat. “I’ve been trying to wake you up for five minutes!” McKail rubbed sleep crust out of his eyes and yawned. “Why?” He grunted. “There a fire or somethin’?” “If it was a fire, I’d have let you burn,” she told him with a teasing grin. “It’s five after seven and you’ve got work.” Work? Fuck… In his blissful early morning haze, he’d forgotten all about his job and his responsibilities. He hadn’t had a real job in the better part of fifteen years and he hadn’t been up this early for a lot longer than that. Times like this, he realised why. Welcome back to reality, dickhead. Elle kissed him gently on the cheek. “I know you’re not used to this kind of life, but you have responsibilities now.” She smiled and it lit up the whole damned room. “So get your ass outta bed - breakfast’s ready in five.” She stood and grinned proudly. “Sheriff McKail.” Elle disappeared off to fix breakfast, whilst McKail wrenched his weary bones upright and glared at his uniform hanging up on the wardrobe door. Sheriff-fucking-McKail. Elle loved the very thought it and let him know it every chance she got. He, however, was still coming to terms with the whole situation. Two months back, McKail prevented the local bastard from attacking Elle in the street when the rest of the town were too scared do anything about it. Somehow it secured him the affections of a good woman and, rather strangely, earned him the respect of the entire town. So when Mayor Doolan had Elle nominate him for Sheriff, the people of Aspen Park voted him in unanimously. It didn’t seem to matter much that he had no experience as a lawman nor that he didn’t actually want the job. Nevertheless, Elle managed to talk him into embracing the role. She wanted this for him more than anything and since he’d been implicated in entirely fictional match-fixing allegations, his wrestling bookings had dried up - he needed the work. In the end, he realised he didn’t have much of a choice and accepted the job. It was almost inevitable that Primetime Central would come back into the picture to fuck up his life. A few days into his new job, they called him and offered to resuscitate his wrestling career with an invitation to compete in the GTT. McKail didn’t know what in the hell to do; he wanted to make Elle happy, but he also wanted to wrestle - it was what he was born to do and nobody could tell him different. Perhaps the meeting with the PTC representative will shed more light on the matter? He considered, hauling his carcass out of bed. And if does, then what in the hell am I gonna do? Fuck… Elle “Goddamn uniform,” Jacob grumbled, wandering into the kitchen. Jacob wasn’t happy and Elle knew it. It’ll just take him time to adjust, she comforted herself. He needs time to accept that his wrestling career is over and he needs to move on. Deep down, Elle knew she was right, but that didn’t stop her from feeling guilty. He’ll do great as Sheriff, she thought. But did I do the right thing nominating him? Was I being selfish? Elle knew she was, but she had the right to be…didn’t she? I just want Jacob here, with me, not travelling all over the country. I want a normal life and I want to start a family. Is that being selfish? Elle smiled and readjusted his tie for him. He still hadn’t gotten used to wearing a shirt and tie to work and Elle doubted he ever would. “Coffee’s done,” she told him. “How do you want your eggs?” McKail kissed her on the cheek and poured himself a generous mug of coffee. “No time for breakfast,” he said, almost out of the door already. “I gotta be someplace.” “No problem,” Elle replied. “See ya later, hon.” Am I being selfish? McKail As soon as McKail entered the diner, the smell of bacon hit him like a brick up side the head. Immediately, it made his mouth water and his stomach grumble, begging to be fed. Ripping his attention away from his gut, McKail spotted the PTC representative sitting in a booth across the diner; somewhat easy to pick out being the only person in the place wearing a suit. McKail headed over to the PTC rep and couldn’t help but study the man closely. He was strange-looking man; his hair was shiny, seemingly moulded to his skull using entirely too much styling product. His face was round to the point where he didn’t look like he had a chin, which was unfortunately emphasised somewhat by his pointed nose. He reminded McKail of a lizard and as he got within a few metres of the man, he realised he amazingly bad skin which only reinforced the notion. As McKail got within a few feet of the table, the PTC rep took note of him. He scrunched his face up a little confusion and entirely perplexed as McKail slipped into the booth, sitting opposite the lizard man. “Hi,” the lizard man greeted, extending his hand, “I’m David Kaye.” McKail didn’t shake his hand and lizard man didn’t seem all that troubled by such blatant disrespect. “We didn’t have to meet in Denver, you know. I could’ve come to Aspen Park - it‘s not all that far.” It’s far enough, McKail thought. Such a meeting in Aspen Park would’ve set tongues wagging and eventually, word would’ve gotten back to Elle. She had no clue about the meeting, much less the offer from PTC and he intended it to stay that way until he’d figured out what to do. At least I didn’t lie to her, he told himself, trying to justify his actions. That wasn’t going to cut it. But omission of the truth is just as bad, McKail. “What’s with the uniform anyway?” The lizard man asked. “Fancy dress?” Not funny, dickhead. “Let’s move this along,” McKail ordered. “I oughta be at the stationhouse already.” “Wait,” the lizard man chuckled, “you’re actually a real-life Sheriff? Jesus! I thought you’d just come from the set of some sort of porn film or something. I’ve read your file and given your past record, it seemed like the only conceivable option - I never actually thought you were a real Sheriff!” Still not funny. “Why are we here?” McKail asked, eager to get down to business. “I told you over the phone I hadn’t made my a decision yet. If you’ve come here for the hard-sell, then you’re gonna be disappointed because I‘m still not done thinkin’ on it.” David’s smile disappeared within the blink of an eye. “I’m not here for the hard-sell, Jacob. I’m here to inform you that the PTC has come to decision regarding your involvement in the tournament that may or may not influence your decision to enter,” the lizard man explained. He paused for a while, perhaps expecting McKail to throw a question his way. When he didn‘t, the lizard man continued, “The PTC board are uncomfortable with your past and-” “My past?” McKail interrupted, not entirely surprised but reasonably angry. “What exactly are they uncomfortable with?” “Well, there’s obviously those match-fixing allegations from last year,” he began. McKail opened his mouth to form an argument, but was promptly cut off, “Allegations which, they understand, have been dropped and do not have an issue with.” Don’t have an issue with? Then wha-- “The PTC board were more concerned with your history of alcohol abuse, which they would like you to rectify prior to the tournament.” McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. “Alcohol abuse? Rectify? What the hell are you talkin’ about?” “Jacob, the board want to help you with your problem,” the lizard man explained. “They aren’t saying you need to be one hundred percent clean come the opening round, they just want to see intent.” “I don‘t have a problem,” McKail lied. “That might very well be,” David replied sceptically, “but the board require proof. Perhaps check yourself into a clinic or something of that nature?” Proof? Clinic? McKail shook his head. What the fuck is this? An intervention? I don’t need this… Without a word he stood and slid out of the booth. “Where are you going?” The lizard man asked. “Breakfast’s on me.” McKail turned and headed for the exit without looking back even for an instant. “I’ve got someplace better to be.” Deputy White “Where the fuck is McKail?” Deputy Lance White grumbled to nobody in particular. Not that anybody was listening; he was standing in foyer of the Sheriff stationhouse and not one of his subordinates had even bothered to acknowledge his existence. They didn’t like him and he didn’t much care. What really bothered him was how they, and the rest of the town for that matter, sycophantically adored the new Sheriff. He doesn’t deserve the job, he doesn’t deserve their worship, Deputy White decided, and he certainly doesn’t deserve Elle. Deputy White had been gunning for the Sheriff job for the past four years and when Sheriff Marshall finally resigned a few months back, he thought he had it all tied up. After all, he was the most experienced officer this town had and was plainly the most qualified for the job. But that didn’t matter too much to the Mayor. He knew the town worshipped McKail and saw a grand opportunity to exploit that and secure himself another few years in office. He took it; putting Jacob-fucking-McKail up for the job was the best damned career move he ever made. Fucking politics, he silently cursed. McKail isn’t cut out for it and when he fucks everything up, I only hope I’m right there to watch it happen. But that wasn’t good enough. If he wanted McKail out of his hair so he could do the job he’d spent years of his life training for, then he’d have to do something about it - he wasn’t prepared to wait any longer. Maybe McKail ain’t coming back? Yeah, wishful thinking. More likely he’s late for work. The little prick has been a couple of minutes late all week, he thought, a plan slowly unfolding. Perhaps I can use that. Undermining the prick in front of the workforce won’t win me over with the masses, but it might fuck McKail‘s credibility up at the same time. “FREEMAN!” He yelled, all of a sudden. All eyes latched onto him, wondering what kind of wrath he was going to bestow upon the young officer. Good, Deputy White thought. Everyone’s paying attention. The young officer ran over to him like an obedient lapdog. “Yes, sir?” “Is Sheriff McKail around?” He asked, already knowing the answer. Officer Freeman shook his head. “I haven’t seen him, sir.” “Goddamn it!” Deputy White cursed. “Has he called in sick or somethin’?” Officer Freeman shrugged. “I’m not sure, sir. I’ll--” “He called in this morning,” Ally, the receptionist, interrupted. Fuck! White mentally cursed. “He said he had something personal to take care of this morning, but he should be back before lunch.” Fuck! Fuck! Fuck! Deputy White silently screamed, realising his devious plot had blown up in his face. “Very good, Ally. Tell him I need to see him when he gets back, would ya?” Ally nodded and Officer Freeman took the opportunity to get out of there, darting towards canteen as fast as he could. Deputy White stormed back into his office and slammed the door shut behind him. Motherfucker… McKail “Deputy White has been looking for you, Sheriff,” Ally half told, half warned as McKail entered his office. “Like I give a fuck,” McKail muttered, slamming his door shut so hard it rattled the pane glass section in the upper half of the wooden frame. Lying to Elle and driving all the way over to Denver only to have dickless prick tell him he had a problem was not his idea of a good day. Deep down, he knew he should be thanking the PTC booking committee for their judgment. After all, they’d made his decision not to try and resurrect his already lost wrestling all that more easier. Thing was, he didn’t feel in the least bit thankful. He felt angry and bitter and he knew exactly what it meant too; despite his misgivings, his feelings and his responsibilities, he wanted to wrestle and compete GTT more than anything. Perhaps now, he could put that part of his life to rest and move on with the life he’d cultivated with Elle but he doubted it. McKail slumped down into his chair and reached inside the top draw of his desk, pulling out a quarter empty bottle of Jack Daniels. He ripped the screw cap off and took a generous gulp. He realised that the under pressure police captain/commissioner/lawman who always had a bottle of liquor on hand was something of a drawn-out cliche, but that was something he could live with. KNOCK! KNOCK! McKail glared at the door and, not caring in the least who was on the other side of it, yelled, “Fuck off!” The door opened anyway. It was Ally and she looked concerned. McKail didn‘t even try to hide the booze. “You okay, Sheriff?” “Yeah,” he told her. “I just wanna be alone is all.” “I‘m sorry to disturb you, Sheriff,” she apologised, eying up the Jack Daniels, “but Deputy White’s been askin’ after you again.” McKail nodded. “Don’t worry about that prick,” he muttered, already regretting his words. “I’ll deal with him.” Ally nodded. “Um, sir?” McKail swallowed another mouthful of bourbon, not really caring what she thought of it. “Yeah?” “Can I hideout here?” She asked. “Deputy White kinda, um, creeps me out.” McKail nodded. “Sure.” She grinned at shut the door behind her. “Here,” McKail said, tossing the bottle of Jack Daniels at her, “take a drink.” She caught it like a pro and glared at it gingerly as if deciding whether she should or not. “It’ll calm your nerves.” She looked at him and smiled, then unscrewed the lid. She smelt it first and then took a tentative sip, screwing up her face in disgust shortly thereafter. McKail chuckled to himself, already feeling his cheeks warm. “Don’t worry,” he advised, reaching for the bottle, “you get used to it.” Deputy White “Prick’s got everybody lying for him now,” Deputy White mindlessly muttered, peeking out from the blinds within his office. He knew McKail had come back already, his pickup was in the lot and a fellow officer had already confirmed it. So why is Ally lying for him? He pondered. Because he asked her to, or is there more to it than that? He wondered, straining his eyes to try and make out detail. A bottle? Wait…is that…booze? Fuck me, you’ve done it now McKail. “Hello, what do we have here?” He muttered, watching on as Ally glared at the Sheriff’s office intermittently. She looked concerned. Prick’s in his office then, huh? Deputy White was about to go on out there and burst into McKail‘s office, when he saw Ally rise from her desk and knock on his office door. She opened just a fraction and appeared to engage in a conversation. Through the cracks between her body at the doorframe he could just about make out McKail sitting behind his desk, with something in his hand. What the hell is that? This was it. Deputy White had him. If he stormed into office now and caught him in the act, McKail’s career was over. Perfect, he smiled, wait… It got better. Ally slipped into McKail’s office, had a guilty glance around to see if anybody had seen her and then shut the door. What the fuck are you up to now, McKail? This was getting better and better with each passing minute. True, he could burst in there and catch him in the act, but that would only get him fired. Truth be told he wanted to tear McKail’s world down around his ears and he wanted Elle. Even if she heard what had happened by word of mouth, the likelihood was he’d somehow slime his way back into her life. That girl adored him and would fall for any kind of bullshit that would spew from his mouth. They’d grown up together and Deputy White had loved her for the better part of his life. He’d asked her out a few times, but she’d always said no. Maybe it‘s time I turn that all around. Maybe she’ll need comforting. Maybe I’ll be the one to comfort her. He picked up his phone and dialled Elle’s number. She picked up after two rings. “Elle? It’s Deputy White. I think there’s something wrong with Sheriff Mc--” He stopped himself. Have to act the part. “With Jacob. You should come right over.” Deputy White put the phone down and chuckled to himself. Finally, things are turning my way. Elle Elle rushed into the stationhouse panic-stricken and wide eyed. When Deputy White had called she rushed there as fast as she could, with a hundred and one worst-case scenario’s running through her head, each more grisly than the last. I hope he’s okay. Her eyes darted around the foyer looking for a familiar face. She found Deputy White. He approached her quickly and grabbed her shoulders sternly. “Calm down, Elle. It‘s going to be okay.” She ignored him. “What happened? Where is he?” Deputy White smiled at her warmly and shook his head. “It’s nothing bad. I shouldn’t have got you all worked up. I’m just worried about him is all.” Elle breathed a sigh of relief and wiped the tears away from her eyes. “Thank god,” she muttered, trying to compose herself. “Then what’s this about?” Deputy White looked down at his feet. “This is difficult. It’s not really any of my business, I was just worried about him. I think he has a few problems.” Problems? “What are you talking about, Lance?” “Well, since he came in this morning, Sheriff McKail has been holed up in his office,” Deputy White explained, with more sincerity she though he was capable of. “And when Ally went in there earlier, I saw a bottle of booze in his hands.” She felt her blood boil and anger rise from deep within her gut. This better not be what I think it is! “I tried to talk to him, but he says he won’t see anyone.” Elle had heard enough. She stormed over to Jacob’s office and flung the door open, catching both him and the receptionist by surprise. She was sitting on his lap and they were both obviously drunk. “Bastard!” She yelled, fire in her eyes. “Elle?” He asked. “What’s wrong? What the hell are you doing here?” Elle’s eyes were filled with tears instantaneously, as she angrily speed out of the office. Bastard! How could he do this to me? I thought he loved me! I thought he-- “Elle! Wait!” Jacob yelled, getting to his feet and chasing after her. He grabbed her arm and stopped her on the spot, but she refused look him in the eye. “Nothing was going on, I swear. I was just feeling a little down and I turned to the booze.” “You couldn’t come and talk to me about it?” She asked him, physically shaking with anger. “You had to go and fool around with that whore instead?” Picking herself up from the office floor, Ally took offence, “Hey!” But Elle ignored her and glared at Jacob when a response wasn’t forthcoming. Deep down, she prayed he had a good excuse. She prayed the next words that came out of his mouth would make her feel better and help her to forgive him, but she had no clue as to what those words could possibly be or if they even existed. “It was difficult,” he explained. It’s not going well so far. “I couldn’t talk to you about it because I knew it would’ve upset you.” He gently brushed her cheek with the back of his hand and it took all she had to pull away. “So I dealt with it the only way I knew.” “Booze and whores?” She asked, her anger still raging. “No, Elle I…” Elle ripped her arm away from his grasp. “I don’t care what you have to say to me Jacob. It’s not going to fix things between us, because there is no ‘us’ anymore.” She tried to maintain her composure; she didn’t want to shed another tear for this bastard. “I don’t ever want to see you again.” With that she raced out of the stationhouse and to her car where she sat and cried and cried until no more tears would come. McKail McKail put the letter in the envelope and took a final mouthful of what was left of the Jack Daniels. Sitting in his pickup, outside Elle’s place he half wondered whether he should try again. Knock on the door and try to explain everything to her rationally. But it was already 2am and he had no clue what to say. He didn’t deserve her. He knew that all along of course, but he’d allowed Elle to convince him otherwise. He saved her from assault by a complete bastard; what she felt for him wasn’t love, it was gratitude. No, he told himself, sternly. That’s bullshit. She loved you and you fucked it up. Stop making excuses. He was leaving; it would be too hard walking the streets of Aspen Park and seeing her beautiful face so full of hatred for him would‘ve killed him. McKail couldn’t handle it, so the only thing left he could do was to abide by the PTC ruling, get himself clean and get himself ready for the GTT. Maybe one day he’d come back and maybe one day she’d forgive him. But he doubted it.
