Trial Series: The Black Heir Scandal

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-14 Before the birth of the Intense personified Vangelus Olsig that you all see before you today, there was a man by the name of Dante' Olsig who was born into a long line of Olsig's all who positioned themselves inside of the wrestling industry. Bogota, Columbia served as the basis of my childhood. It was a town that adored professional wrestling. The citizens of Columbia adored professional wrestling in the same respect that Americans adore football. There was no other sport that mattered in Columbia besides Professional Wrestling. It was the epitome, so to speak. My father, Czar Olsig, was the proud owner of one of Columbia's greatest wrestling territories, Tri-State Wrestling, a company which was given to him by his father. In fact, my entire family was born into professional wrestling, which made my inheritance of Tri-State Wrestling after my father's untimely death that more relevant. In turn, however, it also made my failure in maintaining that legacy all the more painful. Let me tell you the story. **** Guilty! That moment was so surreal. I'd spent countless childhood years watching my father fight territorial moguls, business honchos, and some of the most ruthless promoters known to man, and he conquered them all. I always envisioned myself as a reflection of my father and thus figured that I could follow in his footsteps with perfect alignment. For a short while, I did just that, but on this date, I met my match, and it came in the form of a long black robe and the pound of a gavel. My fate? Eight years of prison time for the illegal distribution of performance enhancing drugs, otherwise known as steroids. Though ruthless at times, I truly had a heart, and it sunk the very moment I glanced over at my poor mother, holding my younger sister and brother in her arms as tears gently rolled down her pale cheeks. I'd let them down. I'd let my father down. I'd let the business that I'd been born into down. I knew I'd bail myself out eventually, but there was no way of getting around the 30 day suspension of my business license that the court had placed on me in their attempt to strictly enforce their "No Tolerance" policy. Jail was not a problem, but for 30 days I would not be able to operate my fathers promotion, Tri-State Wrestling…that was a problem. A close friend of the family by the name of Anderson Crow was set to take over the promotion in case of a guilty verdict, but at the last minute, just when things began to look foggy…he backed out. My mother nor my siblings knew enough about the business to take over, and outside of those four, there was no one else I trusted to place with my father's legacy. Of course There was the obvious option of giving the company a 30 day hiatus, but the verdict of the court had already forced me to cut wages down by as much as 40%. Wrestlers and employees who were once loyal to the brand had begun to seek employment elsewhere, and with the way revenue was swinging, I knew that taking such a risk would land me flat in bankruptcy. I only had one option. I had to sell Tri-State Wrestling. And with that one option came an even bigger problem. At this time, there were not many "big" promotions operating, and the few that were available were not interested in purchasing Tri-State Wrestling..except one. That one just so happened to be a company by the name of AAA, the very company that my father had been competing with for countless years. I vomited at the thought of selling my father's promotion to his most hated rival, AAA, and therefore held out on the deal as long as I could. But then hours began to pass, and hours turned into days, and days turned into weeks. Before long, I had come to the realization that I had no other alternative. So on August 3rd, I signed the dotted line. AAA now owned TSW. **** August 23rd came and with it the chance for me to renew my license. Many anticipated my return to the wrestling industry, contemplating my reaction to AAA's total domination of the business. Many expected me to recharge Tri-State Wrestling seeing as it had been a major part of my family's legacy for so many years, but they were left disappointed when I made clear my plans to swallow my pride and apply for a job at AAA. Many began to point the finger at me, telling me that my own selfishness and lack of desire to be in the wrestling industry had single-handedly killed my families long line of legacy, but I simply paid them no attention. They knew nothing of where I was going, simply because they knew nothing of where I'd been. Before these events, however, I'd received a phone call from the most unlikely of sources…a man I'd come to know very well. That man was a man by the name of Roosevelt Von Buron. Von Buron was the Columbia equivalent of Roosevelt Von Buron. He was a ruthless media mogul who owned one of the greatest wrestling umbrellas to ever brace Bogota. After a fallout with one of the companies under his umbrella, he was seeking a replacement. He'd already had the television slot lined up, the sponsors, and even had some of the crew workers on stand by…all he was missing was a promotion. He wanted that missing void to be filled by an Olsig-ran promotion…but I declined, informing of him of my plans to join AAA. He stated that the offer was still on the table if I happened to reconsider, and with that we parted ways. August 27th. I had spent a great deal of time preparing myself to enter the AAA enterprises, and when the day finally came, I felt I was ready. Upon reaching the headquarters, I quickly took notice of the roster posted on the front of the door. AAA had clearly taken over, because as I glanced over the roster I came to note that half of it was filled with the guys who were main eventing my Tri-State Wrestling before it closed. I was flabbergasted. They really had taken over the wrestling industry. I knew at that very moment that I was making the wise decision by not trying to compete with them. I shook my head in bewilderment as I marched down the corridor to reach the offices of AAA Chairman, Garcia Sanchez. I entered the office, greeted by a huge grin on Sanchez's face. I proceeded to take my seat as the meeting began. All of the sudden, Sanchez yanked out a pair of yellow polka dot trunks in which he took the liberty of throwing into my face. I immediately began to question him, asking him what was the meaning of it. He answered by stating "Those are your wrestling tights. You're going to be the newest addition to our jobber list. How does Miss Olsig Jr. sound for a name?". He then proceeded to gleefully laugh. At that very moment, I pushed myself to my feet, gave Sanchez a great big "Fuck you", and made my exit with fury in my eyes. Never in my life would I allow my families legacy to be completely trashed upon by a man like Sanchez, and it took that scenario of embarrassment for me to realize that. I'd reached the middle of the corridor, prepared to make my exit, when my attention was caught by a situation going on to the left side of me. Three men were engaging in a heated argument that almost looked as if it was about to turn physical. Upon taking a closer glance, I realized that one of the men was none other than legendary Columbian wrestler, Sir Morris Stratford. Morris had been wrestling since the 80's and was considered one of the best ever. As soon as I came to recognize him, Stratford stormed towards my way with a shade of anger in him that I'd never seen before. He immediately stopped in front of me and began to air his feelings. I remember it like yesterday. His exact words were: "Can you believe this, Junior? These idiots want me to cut my hair, wear an earring, and job to some of these Tri-State idiots just so they can 'get over'. I'm sick and tired of this company! I'm tired of Sanchez, I'm tired of the politics, I'm tired of this dump as a whole! I'm out of here!" …And with that, he snatched up his duffel bags and stormed out. It was then that I reached into my pockets and retrieved my cellular phone. My conversation went something like: "Hey Von Buron! Is that offer still on the table? Good…because I'm interested…" And as a smiled crossed my face, I delivered the good news… "…And I've got the perfect man to build our promotion around." **** Upon informing Stratford of the news, he quickly jumped on board. Some weeks later, we both met with Von Buron in order to discuss the promotion. I made my intentions clear…I wanted something totally different, yet totally the same. Something that would draw fans back into the world of Professional Wrestling. AAA had their own thing going on, but I wanted something more authentic. I wanted my promotion to be considered a true sport. I wanted to go back to the roots of professional wrestling where prestige of a belt was about something and rules were strictly enforced. I wanted the emphasis to be fully on competitive wrestling. I wanted a championship committee and even a ranking system. I wanted this promotion to features legends from all around the globe while also building future legends. I wanted a cast of stars, all of which were capable of producing main event-esque matches. I wanted the best that Columbia had to offer…and those best just so happened to be lucha libre competitors. Thus, Lucha Pro Wrestling was born. Von Buron and Stratford were both suckers for lucha libre wrestling, so they quickly adhered to the concept. I began to endure some deep brainstorming which led me back to two weeks before my trial verdict. One Monday afternoon in which I spoke with the former TSW World Champion (before it's closure) who went by the name of Super Dragon IV. His real name was Jorge Fernandez and he informed me that he was behind me all the way. He was not going to any other promotion, he was not going to sell out…he was going to stick with me until the end. He went on to say that he knew I had the heart to re-establish a promotion, and when I got the chance to do that, he and his family (which included the TSW tag champs, Los Dragones) would be right there. Jorge Fernandez was a perfect example of what I wanted this promotion to represent, so I immediately dialed him up and sure enough, he kept his word. All he wanted to know was the when and the where, and he was right there. So at that point, we had a roster of four; Morris Stratford, Jorge Fernandez, and Los Dragones. Stratford and Fernandez were going to lead LPW. I'd already come to that conclusion. But now it was time to figure out just how they'd go about doing so. That's when things got heated. It occurred in a meeting between myself, Fernandez, Stratford, and Von Buron. My plan was simple. I wanted Stratford and Fernandez to go out there and wrestle in a best of seven series for the Lucha Pro Wrestling Heavyweight Championship. Both men agreed, stating that they could easily put together seven great matches consistently. I had much faith that they could do just that. But then, however, it came down to deciding who would do the job. Stratford immediately spoke out, stating that if we were building the LPW around the image of the old-school wrestling, we couldn't have the very man who embodied wrestling at that time take the fall (referring to himself). He'd already jobbed to Fernandez once before in a memorial tournament, and in the back of my mind, that's the reason I truly believed he didn't want to take the fall this go-around. Fernandez countered that with an argument stating that even though we were building in the image of old-school wrestling, we were going to have a new swing to it in order to keep it up-to-date, which meant that we shouldn't cling to the past. Stratford took offense to this, stating that if it weren't for the past, guys like Fernandez and his brothers wouldn't have a future. Of course, Fernandez took offense to this and before long we had a full fledged war of words on our hand. I looked over at Von Buron whom was chugging at whatever bit of hair he had left just as hard as I was. We had to do something about this. A few days later, I received a call from a reliable source who informed me that there was a certain free agent on the market. A man whose style was too relaxed for AAA. A man that would fit perfectly into the LPW while also solving the issue we currently had on our hand. That man was none other than Josiah Arafat, a man who was widely recognized in both the states as well as in Columbia. He had wrestled in the states previously for Matthew Lanier's Core Wrestling where he won the covenant Star Search tournament and also held on to the Classic Championship for a record number of days. I was a bit worried, because I didn't want the promotion to be jam-packed with older stars, but this was just too good an offer to pass up. So the deal was sent forth and the contract was signed. Arafat was now a wrestler under the LPW. Our plan for him was simple…we'd interject him into the Best of Seven Series and instead turn it into a Best of Five Triple Threat series with Arafat taking the fall. That way, if Stratford lost, he could use the "I wasn't pinned" argument. Same with Fernandez. Everything was right in the world again…or so we thought. Rumors began to float around the small LPW locker room of Stratford's plans to intentionally injure Fernandez in the first match of the series so that he and his good friend Arafat could carry out the series solo. This reached the ears of the Los Dragones who didn't take kindly to it and immediately approached Stratford…and boy were they ready to fight. A huge situation erupted with Stratford ready to take on both members of the Los Dragones and officials having to break them up. This situation only seemed to be getting worse with each passing day, leaving me with only one choice… The series was canceled. **** As Von Buron and I scrambled to deal with the main event situation, our roster began to develop nicely. During AAA's buyout of TSW, they found themselves releasing a lot of talent that they deemed "unmarketable" in order to make room for the TSW stars. This gave the free agent market a major boost and assisted us in our assembling of our roster. We filled it with a lot of your guys as well as a couple of veterans. Some marquee Columbian names were signed which drew interest from a lot of fans. Things were shaping up pretty nicely. After nearly two months of recruiting, the roster was still quite small…but it was filled with stars whom Von Buron and I both considered true "Lucha" talent, in every sense of the word. AAA had began to place gridlocks on the talent, but we remained grateful for that which we did manage to snatch up. Next came the T.V. Deal and arena booking. I knew exactly where I wanted to hold the first edition of LPW. A building that nearly defined the Olsig wrestling legacy…Crown Square Coliseum. I was presented with a problem, however. LPW's weekly show was scheduled to air Wednesday nights on America One International Networks, and Crown Square Coliseum's only available booking was the Monday before. I had my heart set on having the first edition live, but even more so to have it hosted in Crown Square Coliseum. It cut me deeply, but I made the sacrifice of a live edition of LPW's Wednesday Assault in order to have it hosted at the world famous Crown Square Coliseum.. The middle of November was fast approaching and many aspects had been settled outside of one…the main event situation. Stratford vs Arafat had been done already…we needed something fresh. The plan was to build to a Arafat vs Fernandez encounter, but it was then that we received the official advertisement for the show. It announced the return of Vangelus Olsig to the wrestling industry alongside Roosevelt Von Buron and a host of Columbian favorites…but at the very top stood a large, decorative advertisement for the Triple Threat Best of Five Series. Can you say uh-oh? Simply changing it was not an option as it had already been released to the public. This was what tickets were being built off of, and for us to not deliver on our very first show would have killed us. So we gathered the likes of both Fernandez and Stratford into one room and informed them of the situation. I could feel the tension boiling between the two even as we stood there. After telling them of the issue, we broke the news… We had to put the Series back on… And they had to work together. ***** It took some doing, but we finally managed to persuade Fernandez and Stratford to put aside differences for the good of the company. So with that, the January 19th debut date had approached. We had a little situation on our hands here, however. AAA had sneakingly given away the results of our first telecast on their website as well as on their live weekly show (which occurred the day before our telecast aired). Upon hearing this, I knew we were headed on a downward spiral into the ground…a spiral in which I didn't want to be a part of. The only reason I even associated myself with this industry was to keep my families legacy in tact and also make a buck, and at this point I could care less about my fathers legacy. As far as making a buck? Yeah right…that wasn't happening anytime soon it seemed. Or would it? Greed weighed heavy on my heart as I deciphered a plan that would bring me the income that I truly wanted. You see, as explained earlier, Professional Wrestling was huge in Bogota, Columbia…so huge that you even had certain people gambling on the sport. Some would gamble on matches, some would gamble on tournaments…but the big dogs gambled on ratings. That's where I stepped in. Rumors began to circle around the industry that major wages were being placed on the week one ratings between my LPW and Sanchez's AAA. I decided to take advantage of that and found myself approaching a notorious gambler by the name of "Big Money" Mikey Shipper concerning a possible wager. I told him that I was certain of who would win the ratings…AAA. Why? Because my plan was to sabotage the first LPW event thus making it happen. He quickly jumped ship and the plan was put into motion. Many of the opposing gamblers were placing their money on LPW since it was traditional for the first show of any new fed to receive huge ratings, which made Shipper's betting on AAA seem fishy to some. He went around, placing hundreds of thousand of dollars on AAA, further confusing every opposing gambler out there. They, however, took it as him going insane and also as a chance for them to rack up big time. Meanwhile, I was dealing with my end of the bargain. People wanted to make money…it was that simple, therefore I approached both Fernandez and Arafat asking them both to partake in the "fix". They were a big skeptical at first, but upon hearing how much money was being put down, they quickly jumped shipped. I had two major pawns, and with that…the fix was on. I booked the show…and what was the first match? The first round of the best of five series. Arafat and Fernandez went out and put on the most botched performance I'd ever seen in my life which left Stratford completely bewildered and left the fans drowning the Coliseum with boos. After the match was over, we lost millions of homes, sending ratings down hard. When Thursday morning results came in, it was clear that our plan had worked. AAA scored a 5.1 while we scored…wait for it…a 2.7. We continued this atrocious booking for the next 3 weeks, placing together matches the fans didn't care about, putting guys who couldn't cut a decent promo to save their life in front of the camera, and of course keeping the Best of Five series botched. We were pulling in loads of cash, so much so to the point where it seemed "too" easy. Things backfired though as our own pawn ended up being our own demise. Believe it or not, Fernandez found religion after the third week of tapings. Upon this life changing experience, he found himself unable to hold on to such a secret as this and openly confessed to the fix. I panicked and did the first thing I could think of…I fired Fernandez and told the world that it was the reasoning for his "false" accusations. That wasn't all, however, because soon after, Fernandez convinced Arafat to confess as well. That was all she wrote. All four of us were placed on trial and expected to meet a guaranteed doom. One day before the trial, however, the signed confessions of both Arafat and Fernandez both came up missing. Rumor had it that Shipper's associates had stolen then, but nothing was ever proven. This dismissed any evidence that was to be used against us, and therefore we were acquitted of the crimes. Von Buron and Professional Wrestling Commissioner, Ronald Gray, were not so forgiving, however, as they both banned all four of us from Columbian professional wrestling. Death threats rose rapidly from the passionate wrestling fans whom we'd cheated as well as the wrestlers whom we'd cheated. I'd shamed my father's legacy because of my own greed. So I changed my look and my name… And I fleeted. **** I passed through the borderline and presented all of my legal documentation. I was granted admission, but before stepping through I took one last look of the world I was leaving behind. One that'd rejected me, leaving me to flee in order to maintain peace. I didn't know where I was going from here, but nonetheless I stepped through the borderline and entered the United States. Restoration had finally come. Fin. *Authors Note: The following series entitled "Trial Series" is a series in which I will be taking some of the most famous trials and applying them into some key aspects of my character's backstory. With this story, you see an influence of the Black Sox Scandal and the 1994 Steroid Distribution Trial in order to further expand and explain the story of Olsig's inheritance of his father's company and the failures that came along with it. Enjoy.
