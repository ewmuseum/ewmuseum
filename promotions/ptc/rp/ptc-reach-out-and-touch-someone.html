PTC Extreme Title Match Roleplaying

8 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Reach Out And Touch Someone: “I really fail to see why you feel the need to do this.” With a PTC Extreme Title match against Hoyt Williams coming up, and round two of GTT5 on the horizon, Seymour Almasy was a man with a lot to think about. Unfortunately, right now, he wasn’t able to get much thinking done. Why, you ask? Because that bastion of cool known as the wrestling fan was following Seymour Almasy. Every. Fucking. Where he went. Again, you may ask why. Well, the answer to this particular question is that the fan is a Hoyt Williams fan. We have a very special word we use to describe Hoyt Williams fans, but we’ll just call this one “Al”. Al: It’s like I told you before, Seymour. You have absolutely no chance of winning this match. A very, VERY exasperated Seymour Almasy ran a tense hand through his long, white-dyed hair. All he had wanted today was to take a nice walk down the snowy sidewalks of New York City, and prepare himself for the battles to come, but he had been intercepted by Al no more than a few yards outside his apartment complex. Seymour Almasy: Okay, okay, I’ll humor you, since it seems that you telling me that AT LEAST twenty times without me responding would indicate that you’re not going away. Why do I have no chance of beating Hoyt Williams for the PTC Extreme Title? Al: Because Hoyt’s the son of God, hoss. If you didn’t see that answer coming, you probably don’t know Hoyt Williams, Duke Williams, or Al very well. Fitting that criteria of not knowing any of them well is Seymour, who appears to be doing all he can to not strangle the Hoyt fan with the cowboy hat. Seymour Almasy: You know, if I sit down and think about it, I have far more reasons why I WILL win this match against Hoyt. For example… The Final Fantasy paused for dramatic effect. Clearly, he already had several answers in mind, but he wanted to make it appear as if he was coming up with a mountain of evidence that would wash away Al’s silly comment. Seymour Almasy: I’ve held both the PTC Global and PTC Tag Team Titles before. I’ve won over 120 matches in my illustrious career. Besides that, I got to pick the type of match! We’re having a Cloister of Trials match, not some sort of stupid match Hoyt would pick where we would sit around watching Veggie Tales and trying to hit each other with gold plated Bibles. For his part, though, Al was unmoved. Al: …Hoyt’s the son of God, hoss. Understandably, this was starting to get annoying. Okay, scratch that, this was ALREADY annoying and was starting to become VERY annoying. Seymour Almasy: How do you KNOW that! It’s not like it says that in the Bible! Al: Does so, hoss. Here, have a look. Of course the Hoyt Williams fan has a Bible. You’re surprised by this? He eagerly hands the good book over to a very dubious Seymour, who begins scanning the cover carefully. Seymour Almasy: Is this the King James Edition? Al: Better! It’s the Hoyt edition! … Well, if nothing else, that explains the portrait of Hoyt gazing back at Seymour from the cover. Opening the Bible carefully, he comes to the first page, where, written in 40 point font, is Hoyt 1:1. Seymour Almasy (reading): “And the Lord did sleep with a hottie with enormous breasts, and when His divine spunk did combine with Her egg, a child was produced. This child was namethed Hoyt, divine son of God and His champion for all time.” Al: See? Seymour Almasy: … Speechlesly, the Final Fantasy throws the Bible over his head. It lands in a snow bank, causing Al to go into an utter tizzy. Al: YOU VILE SINNER! Running at breakneck speed, Al dives into the snow bank, retrieving the Word of Hoyt. Seeing his chance to get away, Seymour simply continued to walk on, paying no heed to the silly man in the cowboy hat. At least, until the Bible came flying through the air, nailing Seymour in the back of the head. The heavy book’s impact caused Seymour to stagger forward several steps, before he spun in the direction the Bible had come from. Approaching quickly was Al, who stooped to retrieve the holy book/weapon, carefully rubbing the excess snow off of it. Seymour Almasy: Okay, what the Hell was that for? Al: Hoyt made me do it, hoss. Seymour Almasy: Come again? Seemingly, Al is growing just about as annoyed with Seymour as Seymour is with Al. Al: I told you already. Hoyt’s the son of God. That means he has powers. He can control things. Like everything that happens in this world. Seymour Almasy: Oh come on. That’s a load of crap and you know it. Al: Fine, don’t believe me, hoss. You’ll see. Shrugging, the Final Fantasy continues attempting to take a peaceful stroll. For the first time, he gets thirty whole seconds of quiet. As second after second of no interruption passes, he begins to ponder the task at hands. The Cloister of Trials was his own creation, a match designed to cater to his strengths and hopefully confuse Hoyt. Having just beaten Jonathon Winters in GTT5, everything seemed to be going Seymour’s wa--*thwack!* Al: You getting hit with that snowball was Hoyt’s will. Almasy reached up, wiping the snow from his eyes. As he did so, he could see the snowball-thrower dashing off with a pair of his friends, continuing their pitched snowball fight down the street. The thrower called a belated “Sorry!” over his shoulder at Seymour, just before taking one to the face himself. Seymour Almasy: No, it was the result of a ten year old kid with bad aim…although I know, they’re roughly of equivalent intelligence. Al: Why must you mock the son of God, sinner? Next thing that happens you’ll tell me you don’t like President Bush. Seymour Almasy: Well, actually… Al: Jesus Christ, son! I should have seen this coming! You’re from New York, right, hoss? Seymour Almasy: Yes, although I fail to see what that has to do with anything at all, really. Al: That means you read that piece of liberal propaganda called the New York Times! Seymour Almasy: Actually, I don’t really read newspapers, just Final Fantasy fan fiction. I’m in the middle of this really good one with Quistis and Rinoa, and they’re both naked with strawberries, and… Thankfully for Seymour, it appears that Al isn’t even listening to his sordid tale of reading about lesbian sex between two ficticious characters. Instead, he is continuing to ramble about “liberal media bias.” Al: Don’t even get me started on CNN. Only good place to get news in this country is Fox News. Scarborough’s the gospel, hoss. The former PTC Global Champion finally simply stops dead in his tracks. Both hands rise to his head, as he begins pulling at his hair. One painful yank later, and he decides to abandon this tactic. Seymour Almasy: Okay, okay, wait just one minute… A clearly disbelieving Seymour shakes his head. Yes, again. Seymour Almasy: I’m sorry, but how can you possibly be THIS much of a stereotypical conservative? Al: What do you mean? Seymour Almasy: Well, to start with, you’re an unabashed Bush supporter. You believe in God, and that he controls each and everything thing in the world. Al: Yes. Your point, hoss? Seymour Almasy: And you refuse to watch CNN or read the New York Times because you believe in this ridiculous theory of a liberal media bias? Al: I already said that, hoss. Seymour Almasy: And you actually believe that the horribly edited and doctored book that you made me read is really a Bible? Al: Absolutely, hoss. Seymour Almasy: Yevon. No wonder everyone hates religion nowadays. It was the sort of thing that annoyed Seymour the most. It was bad enough that people could, at times, be very easily persuaded by charismatic leaders, but it was downright criminal when those charismatic leaders misled people. Seymour Almasy: Religion is meant to help people cope by believing in a higher authority. We can’t prove whether or not it exists, so people simply choose to believe or not believe. Those that believe do so for reasons that can’t be explained. How dare you try and corrupt those people by telling them Hoyt’s the son of God? Al: Because there’s money in it. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: Greenbacks. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: Dinero. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: Okay, this is getting ridiculous. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: Tired. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: You’re ripping off Deville’s joke. Seymour Almasy: What? Al: Look at the dog. Seymour Almasy: Wh--oh, fuck this. HOYT IS NOT THE SON OF GOD! Therefore, that means I can beat him, take the Extreme Title, and win the PTC Triple Crown! Al: You’re STILL wrong. Seymour Almasy: Okay, NOW WHY? Al: Because at Super Series he hit you with his divine boot and you went down like the sinner that you are. One solution to this situation finally comes to Seymour. He begins to run. Quickly. Maybe sometime he’ll be able to prepare for Hoyt. If not, well… He’ll have to put his faith in God. Or maybe Aeris.
