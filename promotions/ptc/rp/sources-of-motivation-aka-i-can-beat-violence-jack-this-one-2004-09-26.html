Sources of Motivation AKA I Can Beat Violence Jack (THIS ONE PLEASE)

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 “You, Jamar Gordo, scared? I almost want to take my last name back from you.” Merely two days ago, Jamar Gordo enjoyed the bright lights of the city of Los Angeles, engaging in some peculiar extra-curricular activity leading to a startling announcement. That announcement, which he received beaten and bloodied in a random alleyway via cellular phone, was that he would be facing a supreme monster in the Global Title Tournament. He would be facing Violence Jack. Infamy preceded Violence Jack wherever he went and even Gordo himself was taken aback by the announcement that his second round match would be against the Cthulu zealot. He had remembered saying loudly, and with cocky zeal that he could beat Jack. But by no means did he believe it. He needed a reason to believe it. That reason to believe could only come from one person. That person was the man that allowed him to believe he could LIVE again some time ago, when fortune and stardom had not even figured into Jamar’s daily world. He was a parent to the Master of All Styles when those appointed rebuked the responsibility. He was the man that gave him the ability needed to face any opponent, no matter the intangibles. He was also one of the few Jamar had any real love for. Love so strong he actually had no shame in admitting it. That man was Vincent Gordo. Jamar had taken a jet just a day prior from the sunny West Coast of Los Angeles, California to the southern hospitality of Atlanta, Georgia to be in his father’s company. The moment Jamar stepped off the plane to be one with Atlanta again, he knew he was home. All the jet-setting and expensive tastes could do nothing to tarnish his love for his hometown. The aroma of fried catfish, the dry heat of the sun, and the eccentric yet “down home” feel to Atlanta may have been enough to turn some away of a higher stature, but they were sensations the God of Gucci embraced to experience once more. But sentimentality is not the focus of our story. Which brings us to “Gordo’s Seafood & Steak”, a treasured restaurant in the heart of Decatur, Georgia. Known for its incredible cuisine and namesake foods, Vincent Gordo’s pride and joy had stood since 1971 as the finest dining establishment in Atlanta not enough people knew about. Within, the after church rush had already started and patrons of all ages, creeds, and styles were partaking of the fine food. Not a single patron seemed displeased with their order, which ranged from steamed blue crab to garlic roasted lobster tail with a thick T-Bone as its tag team partner. More so than the customers, there were two pleased in particular who quietly enjoyed seats in the back of the restaurant. One was the finely aged Vincent Gordo, still brandishing a solid build for his age. He was seated with a man he never saw as adopted or a surrogate…but merely a son, Jamar Gordo. “I don’t know how you do it dad. A lot of local places have closed down since I came back. What’s keeping this relic alive,” affectionately asked Jamar, removing his RayBan shades as he eyed a menu lying about; the sight of food everywhere had made him quite the hungry soul. Vincent smirked, running his index finger through his finely shaved Zorro-esque moustache as he replied, “You know like I know, you can’t mass market food like this. I’m glad I have enough people around here who seem to know the same thing. Not bad for an immigrant from Brazil, eh?” “Certainly not,” concurred Jamar. It made him smile inside to see his mentor doing so well, Jamar certainly not one to argue with his success. “So what’s this about you being scared of Violence Jack? You’ve been quite the cocky one since you left here, so what makes this guy so different?” “This guy isn’t just confident. He’s what I like to call a believer in his own insanity. The stuff this guy talks, it’s crazy dad. Yet, he believes it so much that he’s a serious threat to those who don’t follow…namely, everyone. I’ve heard things. Terrible things,” warned Jamar, that cocky sense of security gone in exchange for sheer vulnerability. Vincent shook his head disapprovingly of what had been related to him, simply unwilling to accept what came from Jamar’s mouth. “He bleeds and has an ass does he not?” “Yea.” “Then he can do two things. Get beat until he comes up dry and get his ass kicked.” Vincent continued, “Things are going to come at you, sometimes much more powerful then you can ever imagine. What you’ve done is concede to defeat before anything can happen. Jack should be fearing YOU, not the other way around. Don’t fight to win Jamar…fight to survive.” Those words rang true for Jamar, letting the potency sink in. At that moment, the one-on-one talk was interrupted by an exceptionally satin soft voice. “I’ve got the numbers done Vince, I’ll get to the orders later. By the way, when is the next training session?” Angels were never meant to look this good. There she stood, a hand on Vincent’s shoulder as she awaited an answer, a sculpted beauty if Gordo had ever saw one. Her hair was short and flipped at the ends, this woman of obvious Brazilian heritage just like his mentor. A clipboard was held tightly to her generous bosom, the rest of her just as generous and lovely as his eyes trailed down. “Oh, Suli, how rude of me. Allow me to introduce you to my prized student, my son, Jamar,” kindly introduced Vincent which lead to Suli looking over. She held and outstretched hand to Jamar as he took it within his, her skin as soft as he had imagined as he smiled. “So, you’re the one I hear so much about. You are supposedly the measuring stick I have to beat Jamar.” Jamar laughed, flattered at Vincent’s talk of him even after being gone so long. “Guess the old man knows talent when he sees it. I hope my legacy isn’t making it too hard for you.” “It’s not. I actually look forward to becoming better then you.” “That would mean you’d have to beat me.” “And I will, then you can tell me I’m better yourself.” Cocky, athletic, intelligent, beautiful, and good with comebacks? Jamar was fascinated by her, his hand barely wanting to let go from the initial handshake. It would be a lie to say he wasn’t turned on by the competitive nature in her dragon-skin green eyes, Vincent chiming in to say, “Settle down you two. I knew putting the two of you together would make for some competition. Suli is the daughter of some family friends of mine, she’s going to be staying with me for awhile to study Erratica…will probably be my last student at that. Suli, is it possible you can tend to those orders? I have something to ask of Jamar.” Suli smiled politely then answered, ”No problem Mr. G. I’ll take care of the orders and I’ll be ready for training later. As for you Jamar, I hope to see you again.” “I look forward to it.” The two shared a smile as the intrigue only grew in Jamar’s mind. He didn’t know if he wanted to fight her, date her, nor which he wanted to come first. His focus on the lovely new student of Vincent was broken by Vincent himself as he said, “Come outside with me, I have to talk to you.” The festiveness in his father’s voice was gone. It was the first moment since Jamar stepped off the plane that he had gotten worried since being home. The serious intensity in Vincent’s voice only fell upon the ears of Jamar when training was about to begin and in the rare case of Vincent showing his anger. Either way, he knew the situation they were to speak of was grave. Outside Gordo’s Seafood & Steak….. “Jamar…I’m dying.” His world had stopped. The Global Title, Violence Jack, even Jamar himself became irrelevant. All he knew at that moment was that his mentor…screw that, his FATHER was dying. Thoughts were unable to be put into words for this situation. The thought of losing the man who saved his life was a jagged pill to swallow, a fate he was unwilling to accept. “Terminal cancer. I was diagnosed 6 months ago and they gave me another year or so to live,” Vincent informed Jamar, the two taking a seat at the park bench outside of the restaurant. He held his head in his hands, still trying to comprehend the news that had been dropped on him like a ton of bricks. Vincent placed a hand against the back of his son, trying to comfort him at this trying time for the Master of All Styles. “Is there anyth…,” Jamar was cut off abruptly, “No. Nothing. The cancer is too forgone and nothing is going to save me Jamar. All I can do is live comfortably and enjoy the time I’ve been given.” His head violently shook from side to side, expecting to wake up from this terrible dream at any moment, yet disappointed in finding he would never awaken. “Jamar….I took you in all those years ago because I knew you were a fighter. All it took was looking in those eyes of yours to tell. You’re every bit as talented as I hoped you would be. That’s why you need to do two things for me. We’ll call it my dying wish.” “Anything dad.” Jamar kept himself from crying, looking up to his mentor as the two leaned back against the park bench. They both looked to the regal sun above them, burning brightly as the two bathed in their rays. It was something they could both agree to admire for that moment, enjoying it together, this moment obviously having more merit then usual. Vincent’s prodigy attentively listened as he proceeded to deliver what would be his final requests. “I’ve been paying attention to the Global Title Tournament closely and I have to admit, I was worried when you drew Violence Jack. I never in a million years thought YOU would be though. Your opponent, he’s only human Jamar…yet even know, I can see that he is still given deity status by you. I fear your own motivation won’t be enough to defeat him. Am I right or wrong?” Jamar hesitantly nodded in agreement, Vincent following with a nod of his own as he continued, a smile overcoming his face as a flock of birds flew across the sun in a beautiful chorus line. “Then can you beat him for me? Can you defeat Violence Jack for the man that took you in and kept you safe. If I made it my dying wish, a wish I could see happen before this cancer begins to break me into further submission…would you fulfill it for me? I am asking you Jamar, if you can’t drive yourself to beat him, beat him for me.” He was asking for a hell of a lot. Jamar had no clue how he’d be able to make that request happen, seeing as how even if Vincent was well and asked, it’d still be daunting. He was ready to tell his mentor no, it was not possible. His face turned to look him in the eye and tell him just that, but he made the mistake of doing just that…looking him in the eyes. Vincent wanted this for Jamar more then even he wanted it. The same intense eyes that stared a hole through him when he came blasting through his restaurant with the police on his heels, now stared at him with expectancy. Was it possible that maybe Vincent believed in Jamar more then even Jamar did on a good day? Either way, Vincent didn’t see the “victim of Violence Jack” everyone else did when that bracket was announced, not by any means. Vincent saw the man that could trounce the erratic religious zealot. He saw the better warrior in Jamar and knew in his heart that would make him the better when they faced off. It was unspoken, but his father knew Jamar was better then Violence Jack and only asked for him to prove it. It was challenging, but quite simple if you really looked at it. All he had to do was prove what Vincent already knew about him. That he was better. Was that asking too much? “Is this what you want father?” “Only as much as what I’m going to ask you after this.” “Then I am going to DESTROY Jack. I will kill him until he dies from it. I PROMISE you, because it’s you.” The difference from every other time he said it and now? He meant it. Jack was not just an opponent anymore, but the cancer that was killing his father. Jamar could not cure what was happening to Vincent, but he could make him happy. If happiness meant decimating Violence Jack and moving to the next round, then so be it. Fuck Jack and his beliefs, this was his father’s will and he would make it so…no religion, scripture, incantation, omen, prophecy, or god(s) could stop what was promised. If it meant being damned to hell for what he was trying to do, then bring on the fire. “What else do you need of me?” Vincent smirked, taking in a breath before replying, “This one may be a little bit harder.” Harder then what he just asked? What did he want him to do now? Take over heaven? It was the right moment for whatever Vincent requested however, because Jamar was willing to do anything to see it was granted. “I don’t know how you are going to make it happen. I get the feeling however if I ask you, it will get done,” started Vincent as his eyes still wandered about the powder blue sky above him as he finished, ”Do you like Suli?” “What is this, 7th grade? I suppose you’re gonna tell me to send her the “do you like me” note with the “check yes, no, or maybe” boxes,” Jamar jokingly snapped back, drawing a hearty laugh from Vincent, which helped to lighten the tense mood at the moment. “No…just get her to fall in love with you and marry her.” Wow. He certainly made the second request a blockbuster. Love was much more complicated then fighting. When you fight, you impose your physical will on another until eventually, they either give into you or make YOU give in. With love, impose as much as you’d like…but that imposition would still have to be accepted and cherished by the receiver. “How can you ask this of me? I mean, she has to want to love me the same way I would. I’m not sure I’m even ready to settle down dad.” “She doesn’t know it, neither I or her parents can bear to tell her. Her parents are too poor to take care of her and they want only the best for her, which is the reason she is now with me. They want her to go to a nice college and live a full life, but they also want her married. I saw how you looked at her and truth be told, I wish you’d settle down too.” Jamar let what was being told to him sink in as he looked towards the window of the restaurant, Suli within view as she laughed with a customer, the movie star easily seeing how infectious her good nature was to others. She certainly seemed like enough of what Jamar wanted in a woman, but did she see the same thing in him? Would his advances be accepted if he went through with them? “Yes I want her father. But I’m afraid I can only grant one request at a time.” “Since when did you start pacing yourself?” ‘The day you asked me to marry a woman I barely know.” “Particulars, particulars. I understand however. Think on my second proposition then, Jamar. Right now, you focus on the first thing I asked of you then. Either way, I know good things will come to you Jamar, just believe in them. So, ready to train,” festively asked Vincent to his prodigy. “Peculiar activity for a man given a year to live,” snapped back Jamar as the two rose from the bench, sauntering slowly back into the restaurant. “Yes I’m dying, but don’t treat me like a dead man,” stated Vincent, putting an arm around Jamar as the two headed back inside. What should have been a depressing moment was turned into a moment of hope for both Vincent and Jamar. The Master of All Styles now fought with new vigor and purpose, that purpose to please his mentor before he retired to his mansion in the sky. As for Vincent, he fought to live to see Jamar make his will come true. The sun began to set over the horizon as the two headed inside, painting the streets of Decatur in a dim shade. Time was drawing closer to Jamar’s meeting with Violence Jack, but it was now Jack that should be afraid. He did not like the circumstances of how he gained his motivation, but one thing rang true in his mind today that didn’t merely a few days ago. He could beat Violence Jack. And for Vincent, he would. Forever to be continued.
