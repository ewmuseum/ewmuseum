Internal Passion: The Action

11 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-11 November 1st, 2006 8:45 A.M. EST Twelve days had passed since that wonderful day in John’s life. The emotions he thought would go away did and were replaced with new ones. Nothing could have brought him down off of his high that day. As the days leading up to his meeting with Bud Selig ticked away, he began to think more about things. What about his wrestling commitments? He was never the kind of guy to leave anyone or anything high and dry. He really didn’t know what he could do. The World Series had concluded, leaving him the off-season to figure things out. Or so he thought. He stepped inside the elevator at the Major League Baseball Offices and nervously waited for it to reach his destination. He wasn’t sure what he had to worry about, but his stomach was filled with uneasiness. He tried telling himself numerous times that he was already back in and there was nothing that could change that for the time being, but it seemed less believable the more he thought it. "Calm down, John." He muttered to himself. The doors opened and he stepped out, staring at the large door in front of him. He gulped as he placed his hand on the handle and entered. The young lady at the desk sat jabbering on the phone as he approached. John cleared his throat to get the brunette’s attention. "I have to go, baby. There’s somebody at the desk." She said. John rolled his eyes as the girl continued to make incoherent baby talk with whoever was on the other end of the conversation. After another minute or so she finally hung up the phone and giggled about her conversation. "Can I help you?" She asked. John nodded sarcastically. Obviously he needed help if he was standing there. He reached into the pocket of his leather jacket and pulled out a set of papers. He unfolded them and presented them to her. "I have a meeting with Bud Selig at nine A.M." He stated. She inspected the documents as she flipped through them. She confirmed the papers with her appointment schedule and nodded back to him. "Let me just let him know you’re here." She said. John nodded as she picked up the phone and punched a few buttons. After a few moments and a little bit of speaking on her part she hung up and nodded in his direction. "He’s ready for you." She says. He nods to say thank you and snatches his documentation back before walking towards the office. With each step his butterflies grew again. They intensified as he pushed the office door open and stepped in. "Good morning, John." Bud said. John didn’t respond right away. He shut the door oh so quietly and then turned to see the commissioner. Bud stood at his desk waiting for John to approach. When he finally did they exchanged a handshake and sat in their respective seats. "Morning, Sir." He responded. John cleared his throat and tried to find a comfortable position in the chair. It escaped him for a few moments, which brought a glare from the man. John immediately stopped and leaned back into the seat, settling down. "There’s nothing to be nervous about, Mr. Covel." Bud assured. John nodded and took a deep breathe before looking across the desk again. No eye contact was made. Bud was busy filing through different stacks of papers. This went on for few moments, allowing the air to fill with silence. John had to break it. It would have driven him crazy. "How was your weekend, sir?" He inquired. No reply. He just kept retrieving papers that were necessary. John cleared his throat and sighed again as he realized he’d have to deal with the silence. It allowed him time to really take in what went on behind the scenes in the big leagues. "In that conference room you were re-instated. What wasn’t explained was the terms and conditions." Bud stated. John nodded in acknowledgement. What kind of terms could there be? The season was over. There was nowhere for him to go to do anything. He brushed it from his mind and looked back to the commissioner for answers. "That’s why we’re here, sir." John said. Bud selected a document from his vast collection covering the Oak desk and inspected it momentarily before handing it to John. John inspected it for a few seconds, not quite knowing what it was. "That is the official statement that will be released at the conclusion of this meeting. Do you object to it?" Bud asked. John smirked a little bit. He couldn’t believe how the man sitting in front of him could go from a friend one minute to the business man the next. The thought was quickly shaken from his mind as he began reading the piece. "From the office of Major League Baseball Commissioner Bud Selig: There comes times that we must break out the history books and change them a little bit. Today is one of those days. Today, Major League Baseball announces the re-instatement of Jonathan Michael Covel. The league wishes him the best in his future endeavors in our great American pastime." He nodded and set the paper onto the desk. "It looks fine, sir." John responded. Selig took a pen from his pocket and pointed to a line with John’s name printed underneath it. The universal sign for an autograph clicked in his head and John scribbled his name along the "x." "One down, thirteen to go." Bud announced with a chuckle. John cracked a smile as well. This was the catch. Writer’s cramp. That was his requirement to get back into baseball. He joked with himself for a few seconds until another document was pulled out. "I am sure you’re anxious to find out the terms of your re-instatement, so I won’t keep it from you any longer." Bud said. John took the document and sighed. "- Subject will be required to travel to Puerto Rico to participate in Winter Ball. - Subject must make all games, practices and work-outs while participating. Exceptions will be at the discretion of Commissioner Selig. - Subject’s participation in Winter Ball must prove that his anger is under control. - Subject’s skills must be up to Major League expectations, determined by a board of scouts appointed by Commissioner Selig. - Subject is allowed to conduct official negotiations with any Major League team, but may NOT sign a deal of any kind until the end of Winter Ball." Puerto Rico? What the hell? Now John really got to thinking. What did this mean to his wrestling career? John had been out of the game for a few years but knew Winter Ball would be starting in a few weeks time. It obliterated his original plan. "Sir?" John said. Bud looked up from his paperwork and removed his glasses to look at John. "What is it, Mr. Covel?" He inquired. John knew what he wanted to say, but didn’t know how to word it. Each way he came up with made himself feel like an ungrateful bastard to argue the privilege that was given to him. After a few moments he decided to come off as that ungrateful bastard and blurted out the first thing that came to mind. "What about my wrestling commitments, sir?" John asked. Bud smirked and let a small chuckle escape from his lips. John wondered if the same thoughts ran through his elder’s mind. John knew he shouldn’t have said anything. Sure he knew that now. But it was too late. "Son that is your own problem. You should have considered that before you began your quest." Bud stated. John nodded and inspected the paper a bit more as Bud returned to sorting the documents. He really didn’t know what to do. He couldn’t just drop FUSE and GTT for baseball. Even though he yearned to be back out on the mound of limestone, toeing the rubber and looking for the signal, wrestling had found a place in his heart as well. "Sir, am I allowed to compete under these terms and conditions?" He asked. Brilliant. It had popped into his head and out of his mouth before he could even realize it. It might work though. He knew FUSE only held one show a week and GTT was even less demanding. It was going to work. "In wrestling, you mean?" Bud asked. John nodded. This was good. He had caught the man off guard. The funny thing was that he cared so much. In the time he had been wrestling and attempting to get back into baseball he had always said he would just drop the wrestling and go back to the diamond. Now something different filled him. "I should have included something about that in the terms and conditions. How about this?" He stated. Uh-oh. John hadn’t wanted to play "Let’s Make A Deal." But he quickly decided to be open minded. What did Bud have to offer? The possibilities ran through his head, but again he stopped himself and decided to get the real answer. "And what’s that, sir?" John asked. Bud sighed and made eye contact with John. It was like a death stare. He just couldn’t break its grasp. He tried to turn his head, but it was like he muscles weren’t working. He was forced to see and feel the glare burn into him. "I’ll allow you to finish up the commitments that already have at this time. However, you will not be allowed to break any of the terms and conditions already given to you. If you make anymore commitments from this point forward, you will have breached the terms and conditions and will be subject to punishment. How does that sound?" Bud said. "Alright" was all John could come up with in his mind. On the outside he smirked and nodded to agree with them. It was getting him back onto the field. That’s all that mattered. Bud called for the revision of the document to be drawn up and moved on. January 19th, 2004 7:13 P.M. CST Life was different for John. Exhausting. He had forgotten what it was like to work a "real" job. The long summer days on his uncle’s farm when he was sixteen were ancient history to him. He hadn’t fallen back into the groove of working a blue collar job in the past three months. The repetitiveness of stacking fifty pound boxes everyday was taking its toll on him. He often wondered how people could do it for fifty years. "Hey, Johnny." His mom said. John offered a lack-luster wave as he entered the front door and kicked off his shoes. The twelve hour shift that day had left him yearning to lie in his bed. He entered the kitchen in search of a bottle of water, but instead received a Fed-Ex envelope. "What’s this?" He asked. His mother shrugged and turned back to putting dishes away. John shrugged as well and pushed the envelope into the back of his mind. Water in hand, he spotted his bedroom door out of the corner of his eye and made it his destination. But her voice rang out again. "John, honey. Don’t you think you should move out soon? You’re almost twenty-four." She said. Are you fucking serious? Of course he didn’t say that, but what was going on? She knew he had only been back on the job for three months and hadn’t saved enough money to do anything. Sure he had the money received from his contracts, but they were spread out in the money market. "I need money to do that, mom. I’m working on it. I don’t want to be here anymore than you want me here." John responded. His comment was enough to make her turn and glare in his direction. He cringed and left for his room. He knew he had made a mistake, but it was too late at that point. He pulled the work shirt from his back and fell onto his bed. As he laid there he inspected the outside of the envelope carefully. He didn’t recognize the address and couldn’t gauge what was inside, so he decided to open it. He carefully pulled the cardboard tab from the edge of the envelope and spread it open. He tossed the empty cardboard to his floor as he began looking at the documents now in his hands. After only a few moments, John’s face lit up with emotion as he read the first few paragraphs. "No wonder the bastard didn’t press charges!" He exclaimed. John sprung from his lying position and exited his doorway, looking for his mother. She still stood at the cabinet setting glasses inside the cupboard when he arrived. She turned to look at him with a blank stare on her face. "What is it?" She inquired. He paused for a few seconds to double check the front page of documentation. Yeah. He read it right. It was a damn good thing he hadn’t moved out yet. The next few months’ rent might have been in question if he had. "I’m being sued for five hundred thousand dollars." He stated. November 7th, 2006 4:01 P.M. AST He hated flights. They were so cramped and in your face. Especially flying coach and not first class. He tried to sleep for a majority of the ride, but failed at each attempt. At first he hadn’t agreed with coming to Puerto Rico for Winter Ball, but the more he thought about it the better it became. The weather was beautiful. Not to mention he’d have an opportunity to hone his skills before re-appearing in the Major Leagues. "Where the fuck am I?" He muttered to himself. He looked around as he stepped away from baggage claim. People were scurrying every which way and John had no intention of mixing with them until he knew where to go. After a few moments he finally made a decision to head in one direction. The flow of pedestrian traffic was much like that of New York City, which he didn’t like. As soon as he could John bailed through an exit and stood on an outside sidewalk, hailing a taxi cab. "Can you take me to the Star Hotel?" He asked the driver. The man nodded as he helped place John’s items in the trunk and returned to the driver’s seat. John watched the man punch a few buttons on his meter and pull away. John sighed and peered out the window. "You look familiar, sir. Aren’t you a professional wrestler?" The man said in his best Spanglish. John shrugged and continued to watch the passing city of San Juan. John really didn’t want to start showing his "celebrity" status just yet. His trip was draining and all he wanted to do was relax in his hotel room. "Really, you do remind me of someone." He said again. John finally looked at the man and sighed. This guy wasn’t going to stop. He might as well just get it over with. "I’m an American baseball player turned professional wrestler turned baseball player." He replied. That was all the man needed. His face lit up as he looked into the rear view mirror and began trying to pronounce John’s name. His concentration on the road broke long enough for a near collision with a parked car, bringing a shake of the head from Covel. It was going to be a lot different down here. November 7th, 2006 5:15 P.M. AST Why didn’t more players come down to play Winter Ball? John couldn’t believe the size and class of the hotel room he would occupy for the next three months. It wasn’t a hotel room. It was more like a palace. The walls were decorated with a soft red color and paintings galore. John unzipped one of his suitcases and began unpacking. "They better have HBO here." He laughed. He laughed even more as he dug deeper into his bag. The quick packing job he had done than morning after waking forty-five minutes late was enough to give him a chuckle. He reached the bottom and shook his head as he retrieved the final items. "Force of habit." He said. He looked to the FUSE Wrestling LiveWire Championship he held in his right hand and the complete set of wrestling gear that had spilled onto the bed. No matter what he told himself, he was going to miss wrestling. He was on the top of his game there. Coming back to baseball thought, who really knew? Suddenly a knock came at the door. "What the hell?" John set the belt down and slowly walked to the door. Who could have known he was here? Must be house cleaning. It couldn’t be anyone else, he had just gotten there. He sighed and opened the door, surprised to see the man standing before him. "John Covel. How the fuck have you been, man?" The man asked. John was speechless. He backed up and allowed the man to step inside. What are the odds of meeting up with the guy who caught his minor league perfect game in 2003? Slim to none he thought before reacting. "Kyle Torres. What are you doing here?" John inquired. This whole "probation" thing was shaping up to be cooler by the minute. An awesome hotel, being back on the mound and one of his favorite teammates; what could be better? The two exchanged small talk for a moment or two as they paced around the room. "Hendry has me down here honing my skills for the season. He wants me to back-up Michael Barrett." Kyle replied. John let an impressed look emerge onto his face as he continued to speak with the friend he thought had fallen from the face of the earth. "What is there to do here?" John asked, Kyle smiled and nodded as he began rattling off the different clubs, bars and restaurants in the locale. The number of American franchises sort of disgusted Covel for a minute. At least it would be more like home, right? "And I’ve been going to something each night that is right up your alley." Kyle said. How did Kyle know where John’s alley was, let alone what was in it? They hadn’t spoken in three years. Kyle stood silently as John took in his surroundings again, waiting fro a further explanation. "What’s that?" He asked. Kyle smiled and turned, walking over to the bed. He picked up the title belt that lied there and strapped it around his waist before turning back to Covel and laughing. Kyle un strapped the belt and handed it back. "That right there. There’s a promotion that runs shows a block from here nightly." He finally said. Now THAT was weird. Some things may happen for a reason, but what’s up with this? It seemed like wrestling had become John’s cheap pair of sunglasses. No matter how he lost it, it kept showing back up. "No shit." John said. Kyle nodded and slapped the LiveWire Title once as John turned to set it back on the bed. I guess it wouldn’t hurt to go check it out. It was something to do. That was all. "The promoter found out you were coming down here to play ball. He wants you to work some shows." Kyle stated. Uh-oh. That was not good. Going to the events wasn’t a breach of contract. Wrestling there was. What was he supposed to do now? He couldn’t go to the show. The booker would expect him to compete. Unfortunately, Kyle had already pulled his cell phone from his pocket and dialed the man up. John made motions that he didn’t want to speak with the man, but the phone was soon shoved into grasp. "Hello?’ Kyle laughed as John stumbled backwards and plopped into a chair a few feet away. He listened intently for a few moments, occasionally interjecting. The man spoke fast and loud, challenging John’s attentiveness. "Tonight?" He blurted. Whoa. Not tonight. The first night he got there? There’s no way he would have even considered doing it if he had been there a week. He had worked too hard to give this all up. "Twenty-five hundred dollars a night?" John repeated. Good money. Who was going to find out? He was in Puerto Rico. It was time for John to start living a bit more than he had been. His feelings changed from second to second; back and forth. He wanted the money, but loved baseball. He needed that money. After all, he was barely getting paid anything to play ball. "I….." What was he going to do?
