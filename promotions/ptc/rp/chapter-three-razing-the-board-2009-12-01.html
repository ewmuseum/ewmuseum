Chapter Three: Razing The Board

1 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-01 It had not been a particularly good day for Benita Martinez so far. In fact, if she were to try and rate it between one and ten on the shittiness scales, she’d definitely award it a “sucks major ass”. It had started when Demian had barreled into her room at three o’clock in the morning, crying hysterically because of a nightmare he’d had. One that, it turned out, had been brought on by some movie that his idiot brother had allowed him to watch (and she would certainly be talking to him about that later). She’d held Demian close to her, sacrificing an hour of deeply needed sleep so that she could eventually lead him back to bed, comforted that there would be no monsters trying to grab him in his sleep. She didn’t normally mind monster duty but on a night when she’d only managed three hours of sleep to begin with… The parade of annoyances had continued with the seventy year old woman they’d treated around lunch. Said grandma had been rather set in her ways, one of them being that there was no way she was going to be treated by some “spic”. She’d tried desperately to keep her sense of professionalism, grateful for the presence of her partner, John. It had not been easy, however. Around the tenth “spic”, Benita found herself wishing that the heart attack the old woman had suffered had turned out to be fatal after all. When granny started introducing “wetback” into the conversation, Benita had to stifle the urge to dump the old woman out the fourth floor window, stretcher and all. The capper to this symphony of suck had been when some cholo asshole had decided that the scene of a drive-by shooting, while she was in the middle of tending to the victims, was the appropriate place to try and hit on her. She wasn’t sure what annoyed her most about that, actually: some wannabe gangsta whose balls hadn’t even dropped yet trying to act like a man or his puta having the nerve to get possessive over him. Dios, would she have loved an excuse to beat the bitch’s ass… “How you doin’, Benny?” Normally, Benita would get pissed off anyone tried to call her Benny. From John, however, she’d learned to accept it as a term of endearment, a mark of the friendship she’d managed to carve out with him during the years they’d worked together. Hey, it beat “you”, which is what he’d called her for the first two years. “Como mierda, Johnny, how do you think?” Benita sighed. “La unica cosa que yo quiero es que esa dia ser terminando.” In the driver’s seat of the ambulance, John Brentford nodded. He didn’t understand the words but he could hear the exhaustion and the frustration in his partner’s voice. It had been a particularly long day, even by their standards, and all he could think about were the remains of a particularly good takeout dinner that he planned on tucking into and the game on ESPN later that night. They were mere minutes away from the hospital, mere minutes away from being able to sign out for the day and go home. Pity that wasn’t going to happen. “All available units, call received about an abandoned child located in the lot adjoining the bus station at Central and 10th. Caller reports child suffering from numerous visible injuries. Nearest available unit, please respond.” John paused for a moment. “We’re almost at the hospital, Benny. We can…” “We can’t do shit, John,” Benita responded, “and you know it. We’re the nearest unit. Go ahead and call it in.” John nodded at Benny before grabbing the radio mike. “Dispatch, this is unit 27, we are within minutes of the scene, we will be responding. Over.” “Affirmative, 27.” They pulled up to the lot a few short moments later, bringing the ambulance to a hard stop against the pavement. Benita jumped out of the back as soon as the ambulance stopped, dashing across the lot to try and find the child that had been reported. If this turned out to be someone playing a stupid joke, she’d… …Madre de Dios. The boy was young, about the same age as her Demian. His clothes were torn and dirty, every available inch of skin seemed to be covered in bruises and…dios, was that a cigarette burn?! “Over here, Johnny!” In the distance, she could John running as fast as he could, the sound of his footsteps alternating with the sound of the stretcher’s metal wheels rolling against the pavement. Turning her attention back to the child, she found herself having to fight the urge to throw up. “Who did this to you, carino? “ Behind her, John huffed to a stop. “What’s up, Ben…holy shit. Someone’s been treating this kid like a fucking piñata.” Normally, Benita would have something to say about that remark. This wasn’t normally. “Help me get him onto the stretcher, Johnny. We need to get this kid help, fast.” The two paramedics carefully lifted the boy’s unconscious form onto the stretcher, making sure not cause him any additional pain. John then whipped out his cell phone, quickly dialing 911. “911, please state the nature of your emergency.” “Yeah, my name is John Bentford. I’m at the corner of Central and 10th. I’ve got a kid here that someone’s beaten the living shit out of. Get someone down here, now.” Quickly hanging up and putting the cell phone back in his pocket, John then followed along as Benita led the stretcher back to the ambulance. “Don’t you worry, baby. We’ll have you to the hospital in a minute, they’ll take good care of you. No one’s gonna hurt you ever again.” Despite John’s phone call to 911, it would be three hours before the police were made fully aware of the fate of Zachary Marshall. By that time, things would be decidedly worse. “Bentley!” Detective Jason Bentley’s ears pricked up at the sound of Sarah Stevens calling his name. Over the years that they’d worked together, he’d learned to recognize the nuances in her voice, the subtle meanings that could be attached to her shouting his name across the precinct. Her “I need coffee now!” voice was a hell of a lot different to her “You need to get over here now, boss, this is important!” voice. What he was being treated to now, Bentley noted with some urgency, was her “Defcon fucking Five, Boss!” voice. Bentley trotted over to Stevens’s desk, parking himself on the one available corner of her desk. Stevens was deep in conversation on the phone, rapidly jotting down whatever information she was getting on a small legal pad. She thanked whoever was on the other end of the line for their assistance and hung up, a look of deep concern on her face. This couldn’t be good. “What’s up, Stevens?” Stevens sighed, her face still bearing down on the information she’d jotted down. It never failed. No matter how many times she’d heard of things like this, no matter how many times she’d actually seen them for herself...she still ended up fighting the urge to throw up. The first time it had happened, Bentley had comforted her, told her to be glad that she was so disturbed. That the day this sort of thing became just another day at the office was the day she should hang up her badge for good. It still didn’t make her feel any better. “That was Cedars-Sinai. They had a John Doe come in a few hours ago who matched the description of the missing Marshall kid.” Bentley nodded. They’d received a number of reported sightings of Zach Marshall since the amber alert had been issued, all of which had come to nothing. That was standard in these kinds of investigations. He wasn’t certain this would come to anything either but it had to be looked into. “Okay. You and Moscowitz, head on over and check this out.” Stevens raised her head. “There’s more. They ran a SAFE kit on this kid.” Bentley nodded again. “SOP.” “It came back positive.” Shit, Bentley thought. Now he was desperately hoping it wasn’t the Marshall kid. Because if it was…g-d help him when the parents found out. “You and Moscowitz, get down there now. Seal the kid off, make sure nobody talks to his doctor except you two. Anybody else tries anything, you have my permission to boot their ass out the door. I wanna make sure this is for real before I tell the Marshalls anything.” Stevens nodded but did not move. “The fuck are you still sitting there for? MOVE! “ Stevens jumped up from her seat, the legal pad clattering to the floor in the process. She picked it up hurriedly before dashing over to Moscowitz’s desk, hurrying him along to the precinct’s parking lot. She had no further desire to be in the path of her boss’s wrath. Bentley made a mental note that he’d have to apologize to Stevens. Sometime, once this whole thing stopped blowing up in his face. Because if this really was the Marshall kid… …he really didn’t want to finish that thought. He’d been staring at the bottle in the window for the last half hour. Lance Marshall wouldn’t actually able to be that specific, mind you. He’d had no real destination in mind when he’d left his house earlier that day and gotten behind the wheel of his SUV. All that he’d be able to tell you for certain is that he’d wanted to be away. Away from his home, away from the fear, away from the memories, away from the constant fear that the police would call to tell him that Zach had been found injured or dead… That he’d ended up in front of this liquor had been unintentional, a perverse form of muscle memory. Routine and half-remembered habits guiding him towards this rather shabby little part of town, placing him within reach of a little liquid memory eraser. Something that will make you feel good, whispered a little voice in his head, something that will take everything away… He could see the bottle in the store window. In the back of his mind, he could see himself walking into the store, calmly putting down his American Express to pay for the bottle and downing it all in one swallow before even getting back to his car. In his more extreme fantasies, he just pictured himself smashing the window and taking the bottle, consequences be damned. In truth, he hadn’t moved one inch for the past half hour. He’d taken a spot on the pavement where the bottle of Jack Daniels was directly in his eye line. He could hear the voice in the back of his head begging him for a drink, telling him that he deserved it, that nothing would be wrong if he did it just this one time… …and still, he didn’t move. Not that he didn’t want to. Oh G-d, did he want to. There was what was happening with Zach. There was his wife’s continuing struggle with her illness which would hopefully be resolved with her upcoming surgery. And there was the GTT tournament…he’d almost regretted his decision to continue participating in the tournament. But, in a perverse sort of way, it had been helpful. A venue for him to let loose, to unleash the feelings of anger and pain that he kept locked up the rest of the time. There had been that match with McKail…and now McKail was back in SCCW, seemingly by coincidence. Who knew how that would play out? Next had been Jason Snow…normally, Lance would have been thrilled to knock out a tournament favorite, a man whom all the pundits had casually declared would destroy him. Instead, he was just glad to have Snow out of the way. One less problem to deal with. Now, there was Buck Fledger to take care of with Fledger coming off a win over von Doom. Lance almost felt sorry for Fledger. The man was in way over his head and he most likely knew it. Lance took no pleasure in what he knew was going to happen to Fledger, what had to happen…he was just in the way. And if he wouldn’t get out of the way, Lance would just have to go through him. Lance’s reverie was interrupted by the buzzing of his iPhone in his pocket. He took the phone out of his pocket, sliding the activation switch by force of habit. The phone was up to his ear before he was even fully aware of it. “Hello.” It’s that damned detective again. Another dead end, another false…what? What did he say? “Are you sure? Are you absolutely sure?” Thank you, father. Thank you for listening to my prayers. Thank you for bringing my son back to me. “We’ll be there in twenty minutes. Just make sure nothing more happens to my son.” Lance hurriedly hung up the phone, running back to the SUV, jumping in and starting it up in a blur of motion. He pulled into traffic with reckless abandon, nearly colliding with a car coming the other way. But he didn’t care. They’d found him. They found Zach. Thank G-d, they’d found Zach. The Voice smiled as he watched Lance and Alanna Marshall make their way hurriedly into the hospital. Everything was moving exactly according to plan. And the best part about it was that it had all been so incredibly easy. Actually, that wasn’t true. The best part had been that he’d been able to have his fun with the boy. The sounds of Zach Marshall’s screams still reverberated in the back of his mind, bringing a smile to his face. That had been pleasurable indeed. He would have to remember to thank his employers if he ever got the chance. But that part of the game was coming to a close. It was time to bring this round to an end. The Voice slipped his cell phone out of his pocket. A cheap, tacky, pay as you go model, it was perfect for his plan. He didn’t even need to make all that many calls. The right words in the right ears…pretty soon everyone would be surging towards the story like a shark towards chum. He smiled as he dialed the number. Like dominos falling, he chuckled. Just push the right one and they all come tumbling down… “Hello, KCAL, how may I direct your call?” “That boy, Zach Marshall, the one who was taken. I think they found him, I think he’s at Cedars-Sinai.” “Who is this?” CLICK Lance and Alanna honed in on the familiar form of Detective Bentley, waiting for them at the end of the corridor. He was standing beside two others, people Lance guessed were his subordinates. An athletic looking brunette and a jittery, thin man drinking a cup of coffee he most likely didn’t need. Lance turned his attention to Detective Bentley, who was looking for all the world like he’d rather be anywhere but here. “Where is he?” Lance barked. “Where is my son?” “Is he okay?” Alanna asked. “He’s okay,” answered Detective Bentley, “for now. Before you see him, though, there are some things we need to discuss. Doctor Cerelli should be back any minute and then we can…” “I don’t care where the fucking doctor is, you idiot! I want to see MY SON!” A stern, firm voice came from behind the detective. “Are these the parents?” “Yes, Doctor,” answered Detective Bentley. A slim, tidy redhead stepped out from behind Detective Bentley. “Mr. and Mrs. Marshall, hello. My name is Elizabeth Cerelli and I’m the doctor in charge of your son’s case. I can tell you that he is resting comfortably for now. There are other aspects of your son’s condition that I need to discuss with you but I would like to do so in private. If you’ll come with me?” Doctor Cerelli led the Marshalls through a glass door to a currently unoccupied conference room, the detectives staying behind. The Marshalls sat together at the end of the table closest to the door, Doctor Cerelli in the seat just next to them. “It’s my understanding that your son was abducted from his school a week ago. Is this correct?” The Marshalls nodded. “Whoever it was that had your son treated him very badly. We found bruises across his skin and what appears to be a cigarette burn in the small of his back.” Alanna gasped. Lance gripped her hand tightly in his. “Luckily, there appear to be no broken bones. There are, however…other issues that need to be addressed.” This was not good, Lance thought. The doctor was beating around the bush far too much. “Like what?” “When we were changing Zachary into hospital clothing, we noticed blood in his underwear. As a standard precaution, we ran a SAFE kit.” There were times, Lance decided at that moment, that being a devotee of cop shows was not a good thing. A SAFE kit was informally known as a rape kit. But they only ran those when they suspected… “There is no easy to way to say this. I’m afraid that the results of the SAFE kit indicate that your son has been sexually assaulted.” Alanna’s face crumpled, the tears pouring down her cheeks. She buried her face in Lance’s chest so that no one would be able to hear her screams. Lance just held his wife tightly to him and asked “Are you sure?” “Unfortunately, yes. Once we obtained our initial results, we also ran a blood test…again as a standard precaution. Thankfully, your son tested negative for any sexually transmitted diseases. There were, however, traces of a foreign agent in your son’s bloodstream. Specifically, flunitrazepam. The doses in your son’s bloodstream would have had approximately the same effect on your son as a standard dosage would for an adult.” Lance shook his head in confusion. “Flunitrazepam?” “I believe you would know it as Rohypnol.” Kidnapped. Drugged. Beaten. Violated. Right now, all Lance Marshall wanted was someone to kill. “When will we be able to take our son home, Doctor?” “I’d like to keep him under observation for a few days, make sure there are no lingering problems, allow the drugs to work their way out of his system. You’ll be allowed, of course, to visit him at any time. If nothing comes up, I say it should be no longer than 48 hours.” Lance nodded. Doctor Cerelli was just doing her job. “Thank you, Doctor.” The Marshalls rose to their feet, legs shaking with the effort. Lance took his wife’s head in his hands and kissed her forehead before whispering in her ear. “We’ll get through this.” Lance Marshall then exited the conference room, walking over to Detective Bentley and, with the utmost calm, wrapping his right hand around the detective’s throat and lifting him into the air. Stevens and Moscowitz, taken aback by the sudden turn of events, quickly reached for their sidearms. Bentley waved them back with a quick brush of the fingers, silenced them with a glance. “You,” growled Lance, gazing straight into the detective’s eyes, “will make it your life’s work to make sure my son is protected every minute he’s in this hospital. I do not want him left alone or unguarded for one minute. DO I MAKE MYSELF CLEAR?!” Detective Bentley just nodded, gasping in air gratefully as Lance dropped him to the floor and stormed his way towards the hospital entrance. Taking a quick moment to collect himself, the detective then bounded off after the distraught father. “Mister Marshall! Mister Marshall, wait…” Both men stopped short at the scene that greeted them at the entrance. A throng of people, armed with cameras and microphone, shoving their way towards Lance and the detective. All talking at once, all talking over one another. “Mister Marshall, can you give us a statement?” “Mister Marshall, how are you feeling right now?” “Mister Marshall, do you have anything to say?” Lance glared at the last reporter who spoke. “Do I have anything to say? You’re damn right I do. I want to say this to the bastard who attacked my son. If you’re out there watching, know this. I would love nothing more than to wrap my hands around your neck and squeeze until your head pops like a grape. So you better pray that the police find you before I do. And when they do, know that I’m gonna be there the moment they stick the needle into your arm. And I’m gonna smile so big and so wide ‘cause that will be one of the happiest days of my life.” “…And I’m gonna smile so big and so wide ‘cause that will be one of the happiest days of my life.” The Voice smiled at the image on the TV screen, at Lance Marshall snarling out his hate, at the TV announcer expressing her fake, pre-packaged sympathy for the poor parents. The big, tough hero, swaggering and posing for the masses. But in the end, what could he do? Nothing. It seemed that Marshall still hadn’t learned that lesson. Still hadn’t learned that, in the end, he would never win. But that was fine. There would be more opportunities to teach him. The Voice admired the ID badge on the table for him, the one bearing his image and the logo of Cedars-Sinai Medical Center. It was all so terribly easy to dress the part. There were plenty of places where you could buy the right costume. But the details…oh, the details are everything. The Voice stared at his image in the mirror in front of him, admiring the figure he made in his blue surgical scrubs and picture perfect hospital ID. His mother would be so proud of him, the rancid little bitch. He was finally going to be the doctor she’d always wanted. And The Voice chuckled. “Begin round two.”
