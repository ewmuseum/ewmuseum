Date: 2005-10-02 “We had a visitor.” The decrepit voice of Sirius Smythe floated through the air like an asthmatic butterfly. He was attempting to surprise The Ripper, but it had never worked, and this was no exception. “There may be more. The Ripper trusts that the first was disposed of easily.” “Yes. He was as weak as you predicted. How, precisely, will this fool be of use to his team?” “He is blessed with an unbelievable amount of luck, unlike the man he has been searching for.” “Poor Cereal Killer,” said the ancient lawyer. “Did he enjoy the mirrors?” “That might not be the right word.” “We got the expected reaction?” “As we always do, Sirius. As we always do…” ******** You wake up feeling the same as you do every time you wake up. Breathe in. Maybe it’ll go away. It never does. He’s taken it a step further this time though, and you know it. There must be some point to all of this, some leverage that he wants. Nothing that The Ripper does is done without a reason. He may seem capricious and cruel for the mere sake of it, but you know him better than almost anyone, and you know that underneath his façade there is a cold, rational, logical core. No effort is ever wasted. No plan is ever without a motive. He wants something, and you know it. The problem is that he probably knows you know it, and from there you can get into so many double bluffs and elements of reverse psychology. That’s actually not the problem though, is it? The problem is that once he gets you thinking like this, you know he’s winning, and The Ripper does so like to win. What makes it even more excruciating (as if this was needed), is that when he sets out to win something, The Ripper tends to succeed. So, you go into planning mode. There will always be a weak point in any plan, even if the architect does not know of it. In this case, he’s probably all too aware of it, but being aware of something and preventing it from coming to fruition are two entirely different things. The first point of the plan would have to be to identify this weak point. No. You slap yourself in the face, and see the myriad reflections do the exact same thing. The drugs are wearing off, but their euphoria, the feeling of being so capable, it can get you ahead of yourself, and that’s dangerous. To focus on the weak point of the plan, you must know what the plan itself will be, and thusly that becomes part one of your own counter-plan. Just thinking methodically has made this day better than the preceding, well, however many days you’ve been cooped up in this place. You look into the mirror. Despite looking like shit, you can still smile at yourself and see a glimmer of the man that you used to be. He’s not finished you off yet. You’re still alive. There’s still hope. ******** The table was an antique, as were most of the artefacts found within the Domicile of Darkness. On it rested the sheets of paper that had been brought in by Sirius Smythe, and that had remained untouched ever since. Whatever business that The Ripper had been attending had been elsewhere, and the pristine white layers that lay within a plain grey folder were just waiting for whatever fate would throw at them. Into this tableau came Cereal Killer, creeping as quietly as he could, bare, scarred feet treading lightly as he looked for something, looked for anything that he could use against his captor. The antique table was one of his first ports of call, being the centrepiece of the room that The Ripper commonly used as an office. Well, thought Frank, let’s see if the Office of Darkness is ready to give up its secrets. His search was less than methodical, the furtive nature that his addiction had left him with proving not to be ideal for such a task. The grey folder lay there, unmoving, its secrets hidden, until a brief flick through it revealed certain words that piqued Frank’s curiosity. He’d found the plan. ******** The mirrored room had been created as a prison, but on that particular day, it felt like something else entirely. Frank had read through the documents, the contracts that Sirius had drawn up, and he knew what he was up against. In the end, everything did indeed revolve around him. “Good evening, little killer.” Frank smiled. This was the first time that he had been visited in his room. “Good evening, Jack.” “It is indeed a good evening. Many works can be progressed on an evening such as this, but The Ripper suspects that you know about this already.” The emaciated left arm of the captive reached down and flicked through the papers. “I’m not signing it.” “That comes as no surprise.” “So, what will you do? Forge my signature? Force me to sign or you’ll kill me, is that it?” demanded Cereal Killer. The Ripper contemplated his answer for a second, his head turning slightly downwards as he paused, letting his black hair slightly cover his face. Even with the mirrors reflecting his dark presence around the room, it was impossible to tell what look might be upon it. “No. You will sign it eventually, of your own free will.” “You want to bargain with me?” asked Frank. “Is there anything that you desire? Or is your life here perfect?” “You want me to beg for freedom?” “You are free, little killer. You can leave this place at any time, of your own free will. You are not, as you assume, a prisoner here, other than being a prisoner of your own desires… a prisoner of your addiction.” “I didn’t ask for that!” “What does it matter how you became like this? All that matters is the now, and the future. You can leave at any time, but you know that you would be lost on your own. Between the withdrawal symptoms and the return of your voices, you’d be a wreck. The press would latch on to your failure, as everyone knows how much the public loves to sneer at a winner falling from grace. You’d last a week or two at the most, and then The Ripper would be reading your obituary.” “At least I would die free.” “The doors are always open here, little killer. Do not confuse the illusion of your so-called freedom to starve and die on the streets with some inalienable right that you should cherish. You are a prisoner of sorts wherever you may be. Once it was to the voices in your head, and then to the company that you had worked so hard for. Now it is to something else, but it is a relationship of your own making. It is a symbiosis that keeps you here. Both sides take something, but they have to give something away in return.” “Both sides?” said Frank, incredulous. “What exactly are you giving up?” “Do you think that your… raw materials… are free? Do you think that The Ripper has nothing better to do with his time than spend it looking after you, a fully grown adult turned into a snivelling little child? Do you think that The Ripper has chosen this with joy, or an eager outlook? There are two sides to every story, little killer, and sometimes more than that. Your own side is ill-informed.” “So, why do you want this in particular?” asked Frank, waving the sheets of paper in front of him. “Why do you want my spot in the match?” The Ripper looked up and smiled; a sight that unnerved Cereal Killer beyond words. “Closure.” ******** It had been a long time since PTC’s Survival tournament had been supposed to air. After a set of gruelling qualifying matches, most of the federations had been eliminated in one way or another. The remaining, the strongest of the strong, had been about to go hell for leather, doing all that they had in their power to lay claim to being the greatest group of wrestlers in the world. For some it was the glory, for others a desperate attempt to inflate their self worth, or to prove a point. For The Ripper, it was merely another chance to exert his authority and subjugate others to a point beneath him. Their heartbreak as they saw The Ripper towering above them would fuel him. The moment when their dreams shattered would be felt so keenly, it was, to Jack, beautiful. Things had not gone according to the plan. An alleged power failure brought the event to a halt, with the match not yet completed. The question as to who was the greatest remained unanswered, until now. If there was one thing that The Ripper hated, it was unfinished business. ******** “What’s in it for me?” asked Cereal Killer. “What would you like? There are many options for you, and many things that you might request.” “A cure?” “You already have the cure. You inject it into your arm on a daily basis.” “I don’t mean for the voices. I mean for the addiction, for what you’ve been giving me for the last few months. I need this to end, before I die.” “A predictable response.” “What the hell did you expect me to ask for? If you want me to sign this for real, then I want out of this addiction shit!” Frank knew that once Jack’s heart was set on something, he would see it through to the very end. Whilst filing incorrect or forged paperwork would be infinitely easier to do, there would be a certain code of honour that The Ripper would adhere to, a set of rules of his own making that could shift like sand dunes, yet be as inflexible as a block of concrete. Since he’d found out about The Ripper’s plans, Frank had always held some sort of hope that he could turn this around for the better. The Ripper fixed him in the eyes. “You always fail to see the bigger picture, don’t you, little killer? You cannot see past the next day, or the next fix to enter your bloodstream. You walk through this house that provides for everything you need, and you treat it as if you are being destroyed by The Ripper’s cruelty! Look at your arm and see the track marks. Nobody forced you to do that. You are the one that has come to The Ripper time after time to ask for more drugs, to beg, on your knees, desperate to keep away the reality of withdrawal. Perhaps there is a cure for your addiction, but it too will cost you something, and more than just a signature. Do you really want to take the red pill now, and confront everything head on? Do you wish for The Ripper to take away the bag that you secrete under the bed, hoping that nobody will see your guilty little stash of needles?” “I want… I just want this to change,” said Frank. “Change comes from within, little killer. Sometimes you need a catalyst, but sometimes it can come in an instant, a bolt of inspiration hitting you from out of the blue. Sometimes you have to see your responsibilities before taking a course of action. Sometimes you have to be selfless.” “Selfless?” “Yes,” said Jack. “There are three other members of your team for this Super Series match. You are supposed to partner the likes of Saij, should you be deemed fit to wrestle.” “So now it’s all about winning a wrestling match? I think I have more important issues than that at stake!” “They seem to think it is important. You would perhaps be surprised to see how important these things can be for people… or perhaps you would not. After all, you found yourself in this position because of wrestling, and your will to win. You did everything in your power because it meant so much to you… and now you belittle their efforts and want them to lose by having a weakling on their side? Not just a weakling, but one that would certainly fail any and every drugs test known to man. You would bring shame and disrepute to the whole federation… the one that has kept you alive these past few years. The Ripper would not be surprised to see them cut themselves away from the pathetic wretch of a man who did this to them.” The Ripper’s voice was growing in volume now, taking on a note of authority, of a man who was used to never being challenged, at least not successfully. “Why do you do this to me?” asked Frank. “Given the respective positions of yourself and The Ripper, perhaps a more pertinent question would be to ask why you do not do the same thing, or why you allow it to happen to you so passively? Do not answer though, for it is clear already as to your motives and actions. You care not for yourself, nor for others. You would welcome the company of Saij and his ilk, to have them brought down to your level, a bitter and petty envy that twists you from once being such a good, virtuous boy.” “SHUT UP!” “So throw a tantrum, little killer. See where it gets you, precisely. You do not know what is best for you, as this room so admirably demonstrates. Under the guidance of The Ripper you became the PTC Cruiserweight Champion, and under your own you became a worthless junkie.” “You did that!” yelled Frank. “You did it to yourself, and that’s why the guilt digs in to you every time you look at one of those needles.” “I found your plan! I don’t have to sign the documents giving you my place!” “You brag about it as if all of your movements were not planned for and ordained by The Ripper himself! You found nothing, little killer. You were merely shown whatever was deemed necessary to be shown. Any belief of your own that you have held a degree of control has been nothing but illusory. Yet again, the person you cheat the most is yourself.” “Leave me alone!” “Sign the forms.” “Cure me!” “You really wish it?” “Yes!” “Then it shall be done.” Frank looked through tears at the face of The Ripper. Whatever his machinations, his word would act as a bond, and he would do what he had promised. Frank signed the form, giving the place of Cereal Killer in the Super Series match over to Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy. “One question,” said Jack. “All this time, with access to these mirrors, and to all sorts of devices with sharp edges. Why is it that you have never attempted to end all of this, to take the easy way out by finishing your weary life?” “Hope.” One dark eyebrow was raised on the face of The Ripper. “You still have hope?” he asked, with a wry smile. “It is all I have.” The Ripper nodded. Without emotion, he reached under the bed and withdrew the bag containing all of Frank’s drug paraphernalia. Holding it in one enormous hand, Jack moved to leave the room. “Will you come back with the cure?” “Cure?” “You promised!” “There is only one cure, little killer… and you’re not going to enjoy it at all.” The Ripper withdrew, and inside the room Frank’s anguished scream spoke of a man whose hope was fast evaporating in the face of painful withdrawal. ******** Sirius Smythe busied himself with various legal wrangling through the day, settling claims at one instant and then sending officious letters defending Citrufresh at others. As he finished his work for the day, The Ripper stood behind him, a smile upon his face. “How is the patient doing?” asked Sirius. “He bit clean through one of the leather restraints. It cost him a tooth, but he was certainly determined.” “Are we to leave him to rot?” “No.” “No?” The aged lawyer seemed surprised. “The Ripper has further plans for him. When he comes out of this he will be hones, compressed and tightened to something stronger. His evolution is not yet complete.” “Ah, I see. So, are you looking forward to returning to the ring?” “Yes. This moment has been too long in the making, but that will just make the final outcome all the sweeter for the anticipation.” “It’s been a long time.” “It has, but now this chapter will draw to a close with another victory. The taste will be sweet, but it will not be the only thing to savour on that night.” “No?” “No. The Ripper is also looking forwards to seeing the faces of woof woof, Saij and Scotty Spalzcutoff when the ring entrance goes off, and they see just who their fourth team member really is.”
