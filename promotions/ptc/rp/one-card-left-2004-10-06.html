One Card Left...

6 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-06 “I’ve often seen these people, these squares at the table. Short-stacked and long odds against, all their outs gone, one last card in the deck that can help them. I used to wonder how they could let themselves get into such bad shape. And how the hell they thought they could get out…” The sound echoed out from the small television set in the small living area of the apartment. The channel was set to a small cable television station, it played mainly B-List movies, the ones that are classics, but that no one has ever heard of because they never made it mainstream. Tonight’s late-night feature was Rounders, starring Matt Damon and Edward Norton. That didn’t matter to Ichiro though, his eyes weren’t even open. However, his mind and his ears were open. He took in the words from the television set and felt his body began to wake up. He was slightly battered, slightly beat up. He took in the words and his brain began to process them, they seemed so important. The blood flowed faster now, the quote had taken such a meaning to Ichiro. He sat up. He wasn’t overly excited about his newfound favorite quote, but it did strike him as odd to hear it. He couldn’t believe that he had heard such a phrase that not only was a memorable, great line from a B-List movie, but one that held so much meaning in his own life. The last few days had not been great for the former PTC and GW Tag Team Champion. He had undertaken a lot of troubling news and a lot of troubling actions. Things just seemed to have gone from bad to worse for him as of late. The death of all of his remaining kin at the hands of Maverick Reno and the TUE. Those losses had devastated him for weeks on end. Then the death of his brother, partner and sole friend, Jushin only a month or so after his father’s death and a week after his uncle’s death. But, Ichiro was not one to dwell on the past. He did not hide from his problems, he faced them. He stood proud. At least, he used to. Now, he seemed a shell of his former self. A defeated and weak man, battling to make it. Barely holding on. No one knew how true this was. He felt defeated, he did feel like a shell of his former self. He felt like everything he once had, had been taken from him. He had been robbed of his life, a great life at that. He went from a fighting champion, a strong fighter, a proud man and a caring brother, to this what he was now, a man battling for a grip of reality, fighting for the one love he had left, struggling to maintain a life worth living. He heard Matt Damon’s words echo through his head one more time, as he sat, wide eyed and blankly staring at the television set that was now showing a commercial for Frosted Flakes. “And how the hell they though they could get out…” seemed to be the only thing that was now repeating in his head. In his mind he wondered this himself. He saw himself as one of the people Matt was talking about. Although never much of a gambling man himself; Ichiro never really was a man of indulgences, he did have some belief that some gambling theories could be related to real life. Truly this was one of them. Ichiro could easily put himself in the spot of the subjects of Damon’s words. “These squares at the table,” Ichiro definitely felt square. It seemed to him that nothing was going his way lately. He was often depressed and he was often battling physical and mental abuse. Many of this abuse came from his own mind, he made himself do it. His body shuddered as he looked down at the long scar on his forearm. He shuddered as he remembered the painful day a few days ago when he had cut himself. He had become a mope. A man without a will to live. A being seemingly doing nothing, but taking up good air from the rest of the people. However, he was quick to rebuttal that thought, he was doing something. He was doing the one thing that he loved, the one thing that linked everything he had ever wanted to do in life. Competition, power, respect and victory. Wrestling offered him the chance for all of these things, and to him, it seemed as though this was the only way to keep going. His competition in PTC’s Golden Turnbuckle Tournament had been lackluster so far. It was far from impressing anyone, especially himself. He knew that he could do better, but he lacked inspiration, he lacked discipline, he lacked the killer instinct he once had. His training was futile, his meditation constantly interrupted. His mind and his body were no longer able to focus the way they once did. He had narrowly defeated his round one opponent, Hank Cobb. Ichiro’s true strengths were not shown at all, he merely stumbled through the match. Luckily, escaping with the win and not being eliminated embarrassingly in the first round of the competition. His second round match had not been a far better showing. He defeated “Sinister” Steven Lee, but that did not matter to him. His technique was sloppy and he was weak. His round three opponent would not be nearly as forgiving as the previous two. Karina Wolfenden. A tournament favorite and a great fighter. Although small, she packed one hell of a punch, and competing against her would not be an easy task. Ichiro did not doubt that he could beat her, he did not doubt his ability. He knew however, that he would need to make great improvements to his current ability if he wanted to advance to the next round. Ichiro stood up from the couch. He dropped the small remote control onto the sofa and it landed softly as he began to walk towards the counters of his small apartment kitchen. He walked slowly, without very much enthusiasm or energy. He made his way to the small counter and looked at the shuffled mess of papers scattered across it. He looked down and began to organize the papers. They were important, and he knew he should keep track of them. Mainly, he knew that he needed to keep them hidden. He had gone to an anonymous clinic where luckily, no one seemed to recognize him. He had several tests done, despite his hatred for the doctor and such trivial tests. He knew that he was in dire need of some form of consultation or help. As he organized the papers, his head began to throb once again. He knew what it was, it was him again. His demon paying him another visit. However, he could not allow the demon control, he could not lose control again, the results as the doctor put it, might range anywhere from “nothing to disastrous.” His body began to shake slightly, he felt as if his body had the shivers, and that he was going numb, with pins and needles everywhere. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the small bottle. He quickly popped the lid off, resisting the shaking and numbness. He counted two small pills into the palm of his hand. He quickly tilted his head back, his long hair falling backwards as he did so. He quickly swallowed the two small pink capsules, and his body began to relax instantly. He breathed a sigh of relief and put the bottle back into his pocket after replacing the lid. “Short-stacked and long odds against…” That was how Ichiro felt. He had little going for him and he knew it. He was depressed about it, and could some days barely cope with the harsh realities of how he was living his life. Long offs against, it was true enough. Wolfenden was a huge favorite, a likely pick to win the entire GTT. But, not undefeatable, Ichiro knew this much. He would do everything in his power to defeat her, everything. He read the papers from the doctor’s evaluation as he thumbed through them, adjusting and ordering the papers. He had not read it in full detail yet. It was long and quite dry and boring. However, as it was a critical part of his own health and safety he needed to be aware of. He looked at the small clock on his microwave oven. It read 9:37 p.m. He still had plenty of time before he planned on starting his late night work out and meditation. He decided to read the doctor’s diagnosis now. “The patient shows signs of manic depression. He seems to be quite down on life. He feels very unworthy of life. He seems to have one sign of life. He said that his one hobby is what makes him happy. It seems as though the only thing keeping him happy and satisfied and off the verge of dangerous depression is this hobby. -Patient neglected to mention hobby to me. The patient’s depression however, is minor in comparison to his other medical problems. The main one I was questionable of at first. When patient first described the symptoms to me, I had a notion of MPD or possibly Schizophrenia however, after CAT scans and multiple other neural, brain and psychological tests, these fears seem to be true.” Ichiro dropped the sheet and stopped reading. “What the hell did he mean seem to be true?! I’m perfectly healthy. I’m not schizophrenic! I’m not nuts!” he thought to himself. He managed to shrug off the insult and continued reading. He needed to know what else was in this report. He needed to know what needed to be done with it before the wrong people got their hands on it. “The patient is showing definite psychological and physical characteristics of MPD. Multiple Personality Disorder. I cannot come up with any conclusive cure or plan of action, however, I have asked patient to contact his personal doctor, and encourage him strongly to do so for a second opinion and to plan a course of action. -NOTE TO PATIENT: I know that this may come as a shock to you,” Ichiro shook his head and felt slightly defeated. What the hell was going on, he was confused. He had glanced at the papers before, but he had never read into them this far. He needed to find out for sure, Ichiro knew that much. But he needed to continue. He raised the paper once again, and continued to read the hand-written scribble jotted at the end of the paper. “however, it is in my personal opinion that you are suffering from your depression not as an individual case. But instead, as a side-effect of a much more severe disease. A form of Multiple Personality Disorder. Now MPD is not something to be joked about. It is a disease, often caused by a chemical imbalance in the brain. It is often non-fatal, but can do a great deal of emotional and psychological damage to the being. Now, I cannot be sure which personality may be reading this, or even how many you may have. But, rest assured they will come up, and take control at different times. Some may be stronger than others, and the result will be one being in control of the host body more often than the other. Some personalities may be aware of the other’s, however some may not. There is no sure fire way to defeat this, but these pills will reduce the struggle within one’s mind. Be aware however, that when all of this culminates, all personalities will become aware of one another, and there will be an extremely violent battle for full control. You also spoke of hallucinations of demons. However, these are also a result of MPD. These are not demons you see yourself battling, they are your other personality(ies). Do not fear this, it is a natural reaction. I wish you would have left a name, so that I may contact you, however. If you have any questions I urge you to call your general practitioner or myself. There was a name and number scribbled underneath. But, Ichiro was in too much shock to do anything right now. He let go of the papers, allowing them to fall into a mess on the counter once again. It was an awkward feeling, being proven medically insane. He slumped to the ground and curled up against the counter. He began to sob softly, once again losing the composure of the strong, proud individual he used to be. “One last card in the deck that can help them…” This was true for Ichiro. There was only one thing left for him seemingly. Wrestling. The doctor had even noted it himself, that must mean something. It had to be important if the doctor would mention it, in his official analysis. He needed to continue wrestling. He needed the action of the competition, he needed the thrill of victory. He needed it, to keep himself going, and he wouldn’t let it slip away. Ichiro stopped sobbing. He stood up, and began to regain his composure. He brushed himself off, and read the small number scribbled underneath the doctor’s note. He pulled his small cell phone out of his pocket and began to dial quickly. “Yes, Dr. Jacobson please,” he said softly into the phone. “Sure, I can hold.” He began to tidy up the papers on the counter as he waited for the doctor to pick up the phone. He took the papers and quickly tossed them into his stainless steel sink. He pulled out a small lighter and cracked it open on the tap. He poured the butane all over the papers, before pulling a pack of matches out of a drawer in the counter. He struck the match and tossed it into the sink. A flame bursting and engulfing the papers as the phone finally began to ring through. He knew that these papers would certainly damage his chances of competing in GTT. There was no way they would let him compete with a mental illness. He couldn’t allow that to happen, he needed GTT. It was all he had left. And there was no way he was going to lose that, not to a minor detail, not to a doctor and definitely not to any other man… or woman. “Yes, doctor Jacobson. I just read your diagnosis and note, I think we need to set up an appointment.” Ichiro waited a few seconds before continuing, “as soon as possible.” ------------------------------------------------ Opening Quote is from – Rounders (Miramax Entertainment. 1999 Film) Line quoted from Matt Damon’s character, Mike McDermont.
