Date: 2009-12-23 We always called Miss Ivy our ‘Mama Bear,’ it’s something her husband Sean Stevens started. She gives us the evil eye when we do, and she asks us if we want her to maul us… but in the end, Miss Ivy takes care of every one of her kids, whether it’s her son Shannon or we twenty – somethings that spend our free time hanging out in a bar. I always wondered how far she’d be willing to go for any of us, and I hoped I’d never have to be in a position to ask. Tied to a city because your girlfriend can’t go more than a few weeks without a trip to the emergency room? You’ve got so much more potential than that, Randall, I just don’t want to see you wasting it.” "You know I like Rose,” said Robert, “and I think your father is exaggerating a little, but I think he’s got your best interest at heart.” I don’t know what he’s got at heart, I said, but it doesn’t matter. Dad, you’re out of line. She’s my best friend and I love her, and that’s just the way it is. “She’s attractive, sure,” said dad, “and I’m sure you two have your fun, but is it really worth it?” “That’s going a little far—“ started Robert. What then, I asked, What’s the point of life then, Dad, if according to you, someone’s complications make them unfit to be happy, or to make someone else happy? Should I just latch on to some chick with no personality because she can take me places? That’s not me, and it’s never been me. “There’s plenty of time to settle down,” said dad, “and if this one is still alive in ten years, I say good luck and God bless. But for right now, you should be out there raising havoc like your old man was when he was twenty three.” Dad, I said, this isn’t your decision. When I was younger, the most prevalent memory I had of this man was the fact that he always had to be right, and he always had to be the most important guy in the room. I told him that my relationship wasn’t his decision for two reasons. One, it isn’t, and two, because I knew it’d get a rise. “I know, son. If it was, I would’ve tossed her to the curb years ago.” I stood up, not really sure what I was going to do. Fortunately, Rosie picked that moment to reenter the room. “Don’t—“ “Hey—“ What are—“ Rosie— As if any of us could’ve stopped her. Rosie walked, even strolled over to my father, and she did what I have a feeling she’s been itching to do for weeks now. She balled up her fist, and knocked him, ass over teakettle, to the floor of the dining room. “Wow,” said Mom, “Nice shot.”
