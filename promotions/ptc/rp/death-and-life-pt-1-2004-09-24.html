Date: 2004-09-24 Death and Life Pt. 1 It’s amazing the twists and turns that life can throw at you sometimes. Though I guess that’s why life is more interesting that I’ll ever know. As much as I have cursed it and sometimes even wanted it to end, it was always there strong and willfully. Life is the defining factor in this universe. So it is only natural to perceive that the universe revolves not around a person, place, or thing or even an idea. It is simply that this place we call the universe revolves around life and therefore it also revolves around death. Life and death are two things of which I have pondered much in my existence. To be and to not exist are some of the things that fascinate my mind. I believe to be close to death is a gift and a curse at the same time. It is a gift for the fact that you have come close to going onto the great beyond whatever you may believe it to be. It is a curse because you begin to realize that our existence can be cut short in a second, which makes you ask yourself the question. What if… ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- One was down. Two was coming up. Would I stand tall as I hoped… Or would I fall and forever be gone… These were the questions running through my mind. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ The low dreary overcast of the Oklahoma afternoon was depressing to an almost unbearable state. It had not been like this in quite some time. Though on the rare occasion that it does end up like this my mood is easily reflected by it. For the past two days since I had returned from my GTT match it had been like this almost as if an omen of what was to come. Dark, dreary and rainy. My least favorite combination. I noticed some darker clouds beginning to move in from the southwest as the cool wind licked at my face and arms. It hadn’t been a smart thing of me to wear a sleeveless shirt out today but it didn’t matter. The bland landscape dulled my brain enough to not notice the shivers that were beginning to run down my body on what should have been a warm Oklahoma evening. The handle and door creaked in unison as I opened up the door to my small black ’98 Toyota truck. A low grumble of thunder rolled through the sky to my ears and forced a smile upon my face. A storm would break the suffocating dullness in one single crack of lightning. Still though, there was nothing certain that it would even come this way I reminded myself. So once again I fell into my weather induced depression of the day. I hopped into my truck and slammed the door shut before fumbling with my keys in my jeans pocket. I finally retrieved them and inserted them into the ignition before getting to hear the familiar rattling of my slowly dying engine. I let it run for a moment before depressing the brake and shifting it into reverse. I turned my head to look behind me in case one of the local neighborhood punks decided to try something stupid, but fortunately for today nothing of the such was to be found. I pulled out my driveway and begin the trek down the short street of my abode. I reached the stop sign and like all good drivers checked both ways before taking off into the not so busy country road. The Oklahoma country side was so beautiful at that time of year. To the point that some of the brilliant colors of the leaves and flowers still were to find their way into my eye sight as I sped past them at 55 mile per hour. I began to think of the last time it had been this dreary out. It took me a moment, but then the unfortunate truth came to me. It had been scarcely four months ago when my mentor and adoptive father, Richards, had killed a man right in front of me and then turned the gun upon himself. I grimaced to myself and tried to clear the thought from my mind. The pain was still to great bear to think about and I felt it was not quite time for me to have to come to terms with it all. In due time I had told myself. Unfortunately for me on that day, my time was almost due. It had been four months ago that everything had begun to unravel. Before then everything had been going perfect. I was on the rise in my career. I had a great friend and Richard was nowhere to be seen. That was the way I had wanted it then of course. Because although Richard had been like a father to me. He was also the man I hated and distrusted the most in the world. My life before Richard had been a living hell. Then it was Richard who had found me and brought me to what would become my home. It was he who helped me hone my skills to become what I was then. It was Richard who had acted like the father I had never had. It was also Richard that had used me to make himself into the fat and gluttonous pig he was. He was much like the average person. Greedy. Though for him it was ten times worse. For Richard it had been a way of life that I didn’t know about until it was all too late. The sky around me was beginning to darken, so with the flick of a turn switch my lights illuminated the pavement in front of me. ‘Another two miles’ I thought to myself as the darkened landscape rolled by me into oblivion. It would be good of me to stock up on the cheap junk food delights that can always be found at a gas station. Though they wouldn’t be exactly what one would refer to as healthy. They were cheap enough and cheap was the key word of the day or the month rather. Since the unemployment checks had stopped running. My small savings account was slowly but surely dwindling into nothingness. I was on my last chance now. My last chance to prove myself to a big state so that I might hopefully be able to find a new home in order to wrestle. Somewhere where I could count on a steady paycheck. Though if no one wanted me like I feared. Then who knows what I would do. That’s why I had to keep winning in the GTT. Because I needed to be noticed. That’s why I had to succeed no matter the cost or the consequences. I noticed another set of headlights appear down the road from me. Yet as it appeared I felt a strange foreboding in my heart as if the car was an omen of some strange sorts. As we neared each other I felt my anxiety increase and over what I asked myself. It’s just a simple car. What the hell is wrong with me I wondered. This made no sense to me what so ever. Why was I getting all aggravated about a car. ‘Whatever’ I thought to myself and pushed the feeling into the back of my mind for the moment. The car and I were within a football field’s length of each other and I couldn’t shake the nagging sense that something was wrong or was going to be wrong. As the car drew nearer I found myself trying to wipe my palms as they had become damp with sweat. Nervousness? Me? Thing just weren’t making sense right now. What was going on? I could make out the dim outline of the person in the opposing vehicle as we neared each other. The car sped past me and I by it. Quickly I turned my head back to look at the car as it sped away down the long country road. There seemed to be nothing out of the ordinary in what I could see. Yet, the feeling inside remained as if something for coming or wrong beyond measure. A loud horn going off made me turn my head back so fast towards the road that I felt a sharp pain shoot up my neck. I saw the outline of a car hurdling at me. It seemed that in the wake of the car which had just passed me and my fascination to find something strange with that I had inadvertently drifted into the left lane. Which unfortunately for me was a very bad place for me to be at that time and place for the car that had used their horn to get m attention was only a few yards away. I tried to swerve to miss it, but to no avail. It seemed almost as if slow motion had been set for this moment as the next few seconds seemed to take a lifetime. I saw the two vehicles begin to collide. Then the strangest thing that had ever happened to me began at the very second. It seemed like time had stopped. I saw the collision beginning to take place but I wasn’t registering it. They say that your life flashes before your eyes right before you die. Though I had never believed any of that stuff before. Memories began popping to my mind. Flash There was me with my mom, though I couldn’t see her face. Flash There was me with my dad, his face also absent from my memory, riding my first bicycle. Flash There was me on the airplane for the first time. Flash There was me in the jungle exploring. Flash There was me finding my parents dead. Flash There was Richard finding me in the jungle all alone after years of being alone. Flash Richard Betraying me. Flash Me showing up to Richards house four years later. Flash It was this last memory that seemed to me not to flash before my eyes but in fact had me reliving it. It was the memory I feared the most. It had caused me sleepless nights and sad moments than anything else. *The room had turned suddenly cold, or maybe it was me. "He", began Richard, "like you, is a failure". Then Richard did something I would never forget for the rest of my life. Richard turned his gun and pointed it directly at the young man. Then his muscles tightened and a short loud blast emitted from the gun and a hole appeared in the area of young man's heart. The young man looked down at his chest where he had just been shot and then dropped to the floor. "That is what I do to failures." Richard said as he turned his head back to face me. He then started rolling in his wheel chair over towards where I was standing. As he neared me he pointed the gun directly at me until it was pointing directly into my stomach. "The difference between you and him, however. Is that it is not your fault that you are a failure." Richard then looked up at me as a tear rolled out of his left eye. "I'm sorry boy." Richard said, and then with that said I heard a loud shot go off once again, and a sharp pain start to emit from my stomach. Was this what it felt like to be shot? If it was then it didn't feel as bad as I thought it would. It was then that I looked down and realized that the pain I had felt was the recoil of the gun as it shot strait into Richard's chest. I couldn't believe it. Richard had just shot himself right in front of me. My former mentor and the man who was once like a father to me had just shot and killed one man and then turned the gun upon him own self. I had no idea what I would do as I sat there with Richard's lifeless head in my hands as he began to slump down.* I found myself staring at my steering wheel. Eyes wide opening. Shocked at what I was just forced to relive in my mind. I began to look up and realized that everything was standing still. The two vehicles though in mid collision were perfectly still, for no apparent reason. I gaped at this as my mind reeled into chaos. I had no idea what was going on. Of course though. It was then that I realized that there was someone sitting next to me. Nothing could have prepared me for what I was about to see in the seat next to me. There he was once more. His white hair combed perfectly back and his suit as strait as ever. Though now there were no braces on his legs. No sign of a wheelchair to provide him transportation. It still made no sense what so ever. “You’re… Dead…” I stammered, thinking of nothing else to say. It was impossible that he was there. I was at his funeral. I saw him buried. I saw him die! “De..Dead…” Dead.
