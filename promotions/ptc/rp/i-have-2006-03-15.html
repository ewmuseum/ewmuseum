I have…

15 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-15 He awoke as if in a different world. The colours in front of his eyes were more vivid than before, casting strange and uncommon hues across his vision. Shanty rubbed his hands into his face, but nothing was changed. It was as if he was looking at the world through some sort of strange camera filter. "Why am I…" "I’m colorblind," said Paul Goodman, wiping a bead of sweat carefully away from Shanty’s eyes. "So I…" "Yes, you take on certain characteristics. It’s the way it goes. You want to share things? Well, you’d better be ready for what they bring with them." Shan’s mind was slowly coming back into focus. He knew where he was, who he was, and what he was doing. Gradually it all came back in to the right places, and with it came the disdain for Paul. "You used me." "Just as you are using me now," retorted the man who was once his friend. "Nice try, Paul, but it doesn’t work like that. Debts are not repaid in such a fashion." Paul said nothing, but retreated from the prone form of Shanty Jones. They were still in the same room, and very little had changed. Shanty lay on his back, nothing separating him from the worn down threads of the old carpet. Cigarette burns marked it here and there, but none of it was of interest to Shan, who had come back to a realisation of what he had to do. "Where can I find him?" "You don’t want to go after him, Shanty," replied Paul. The name of his target didn’t need to be vocalised. "You know I have to." "Look, just concentrate on the tournament for now. Can’t you go to him afterwards?" asked Paul. "Jah!" chipped in Curiosity. "Concentrate on Tsonda! He is more zan capable of defeating you." "That doesn’t matter. It never did." Shanty got to his feet and scanned the room more closely. Something felt missing, moreso than just the full acuity of his vision. The feeling soon passed, only to be replaced by an increasing desire to see the source of his anger for one final time. The Ripper had to be brought to justice, and to Judgement. *** A week and more passed, and Shanty remained unfulfilled. He had been unable to find out any further information on the man whose memory tormented him, and none of the men who had perhaps once been his friends were forthcoming with ways or means of reaching The Ripper. Shan sat on a bench in the local gym. PTC had kindly paid for it in return for the guarantee of a press conference with the underdog; the man who stood one short step away from becoming the least likely champion in GTT history. "Jah! Zis is vhere you have been hiding!" exclaimed Doctor Curiosity, as he arrived at the gymnasium. Shanty didn’t move a muscle, as much as he wanted to turn around and beat the hell out of the newcomer. His mind had been distracted ever since he had reached the final, but it wasn’t the circumstances of his victory or the opponent waiting for him that weighed heavy on him. It was the dark figure at the back of his consciousness, the owner of Jayenne Industries and the man who had created the factories. It was Jack "The Ripper" Nobbledy that was at the forefront of the mind of Shanty Jones. "Get up," encouraged the doctor, who long ago had decided that he was to be the manager of the wrestling prodigy, no matter whether he wanted it or not. "I’ll train in my own way," murmured Shanty in his soft Welsh accent. "You look like shit," remarked Curiosity. "I don’t tzink zat your training regime is vorking." Shan wasn’t one for mirrors, usually eschewing his reflection ever since the bomb blast in Iraq had left him with a decimated jawline. The nails that had torn through the air had done the same to several of his bones, and the flesh around them had been mauled, leaving him with scar tissue all around his chin and cheeks. A week or more of neglect had brought a good amount of stubble and hair up on his face, but it was patchy and ragged, clinging on in clumps where the skin was healthy enough for it to grow, broken up hideously by the lines etched on his face. The eyes probably didn’t help the image either. Normally so cold and controlled, they were sunken in and looked hollow, a testament to the lack of sleep that the wrestler had been getting. "You’ve not fought anyone in an age, and you vere lucky zat time," said the doctor. Shanty knew that this was true, but it didn’t bother him at all. He had sneaked past Masquerade with a desperation move, and then gone on to face a formidable opponent in Chandler Tsonda, only to be left waiting in the ring with no opponent, the referee slowly making the mandatory ten count that counted as a victory and a path to the final of the tournament. It wasn’t the first cheap win he had picked up and if his career was to stretch past GTT5 then it might not have been the last, but Shanty did not concern himself with such matters. The tournament was a means to an end, a way of keeping himself from falling back in to his old habits; brawling in bars and picking fights until the police showed up and he spent a night in the cells… or, more recently, a year there. "Eegor is upset about all of this, you understand, jah?" Shanty almost smiled. The doctor’s brother had been his mentor in prison. He had been a man of influence and power, carving a name for himself as Paul Goodman, an alpha male as far removed from his ‘Eegor’ persona as it was possible to be. They had grown close, and it was Paul’s idea that Shanty take up the position in GTT5. At the time Shanty had thought that it was because he was to be rehabilitated, given a lifeline from his criminal record and a chance to make a living. It had only been in the past couple of weeks that the truth had been revealed to him. Shanty was being experimented on; just as he had seen the anguished faces of the tortured in and around Basra, so he had become one of them, and he hadn’t even realised. Had he been too far into his own personal Hell? Were the techniques just more refined, done with more finesse? Shanty shook his head. Only one man seemed to hold the key to the answer, and he hadn’t made a habit of hanging around. "He vants you to understand zat he had no choice in ze matter. He really did tzink he vas helping you." A snort of air shot through Shanty’s nostrils as he involuntarily laughed at the notion. His life had become someone else’s. The needles had been inserted into his brain and something had been done to him, the same thing that had been done in the factories, the same thing that he bore witness to, the same thing that forced him to pass judgement on his peers and set off his bomb. But to what effect? He had been brought back by either medical science or Divine intervention, and sent home. Nobody was any the wiser about him being the one to plant the explosives, and he had, in a sense, gotten away with it. In another, perhaps more real sense, his journey had just started. "He’d really appreciate it if…" "I’d appreciate it if you would shut up for just a second," snapped Shanty, still not raising his voice. "I’m sure that Paul’s beating himself up over it now, and maybe it’s even genuine remorse and not just disappointment that I found out and turned the tables on him, but it doesn’t change anything about what he did." "He meant ze best for you." "I don’t mean to me!" Shanty got up and turned around to face Curiosity, who took a step back in surprise. "Sure, he fucked me over, but I’m still here and I’ve overcome what he did. That’s not the point, can’t you see that? What he did, his complicity with the factories… Chris, it makes him as guilty as those who were doing the work over there. He tortured people, even if he didn’t do it with his own hands, and for what? To survive in prison? It can be hard, but there are levels that most human beings will not sink down to." Curiosity looked back at Shanty, who seemed torn between anger and tears. "But…" "No. No excuses for him. I’ve killed men for less than what he has done, and I did so with a clear conscience. I’ve been absolved for my sins and brought back with a purpose in life. I laid to rest the men I killed, and their souls have made their way to their final resting places, but now this? I’ve had the worst offender closest to me all of the time, and I never knew. I counted him as a friend, but I can never do that again. Paul is dead to me." "Shanty…" "He has to be dead to me," said Jones, locking pupils with the doctor. "Otherwise I’ll have to see to it myself." *** Paul Goodman sat outside in the cold Spring sunshine, idly kicking his heels under the wooden park bench and regretting most of the decisions he had made in his life. Every time he had gotten something right, it turned out to have been horribly, horribly wrong. He fell in love with a girl, but she was his brother’s wife. He tried to protect the very same brother from her when she turned on him, and had ended up killing her. Even in prison he had become a success, a respected and even a feared man… but in the end it was only by exploiting others, even the ones who he had come to feel genuine warmth towards. He looked around at the steely blue sky and the light wisps of clouds that lazily floated on minimal air currents. The beams of light shone down and made the grass such a vivid green; it picked the highlights out of blond children’s hair and made their red coats seem as if they were alive, little beasts of their own, riding away on the backs of the unsuspecting children. All of nature’s creation was wondrous to behold, especially to a man who should have been spending the rest of his life in a penitentiary. Poor Rollins, though Paul Goodman. Such an anticlimax for him to end up facing the death penalty, ruining the ending of anyone’s personal fairy tale. "Vhat are you doing out here?" demanded a voice, and unless there was more than one man sporting a fake German accent, then it belonged to Doctor Curiosity. "I’m trying my best to enjoy life, Chris." "Vhat if you are seen! Vith me! Ve could both end up in jail!" Paul sighed, and his shoulders slumped a little further down. Just trying to see life for what it was always ended up placing someone in peril. He could live with being caught again, incarcerated and placed into solitary confinement where he couldn’t hurt anyone again. That would be fine, but he could not take his brother down with him. They would have to part ways forever. "I’m sorry Chris. I’m sorry for everything." "Don’t be so stupid, Eegor! Just get into ze car and ve vill drive avay from here." "No. I want to see Shanty one last time before I give myself up. I want to be forgiven." "Zat is not, perhaps, ze best idea in ze vorld," warned the doctor. "I don’t care. Just leave me alone to go and talk with him. I have to make things right. I have to at least try one last time." Paul spoke with a voice that betrayed no emotion. It was flat and atonal, but Curiosity knew that beneath it lurked depths and volcanoes. "I shall come vith you." "No! I don’t want you to be endangered." "Believe me brother, I have to come vith you, else you might nto even get a vord out before he attacks." The siblings shared an unspoken glance of agreement, and once more Paul found himself cast as Eegor, acceding to his master’s wishes. "Vhere is Frank?" asked the doctor, getting in to the driver’s side of his rental car. "I don’t know. He walked off. He had a smile on his face and he just walked off." "Do you tzink zat he is, you know… going to report on us?" "No. If Jack wanted to stop us, he would have done so already." *** The self imposed exile of Shanty Jones continued in his new hotel accommodation. A slightly higher class of room, or indeed rooms, had been afforded to him since progressing through the tournament, leaving the old one behind in a haze of confusion and the permanent smell of gun smoke. It had been something he had never learnt to eradicate after performing his necessary duties. The location of a condemnation was doomed forever to contain the stench of a soul’s destination. The plush bedroom was dominated by an antique four poster bed. Drapes hung down the sides of it and the ceiling was drawn in crushed velvet. Ostentation suffused the entire decorative styling, turning it into an opulent motif that went just that little bit too far, tried a little bit too hard to whisper ‘luxury’ in your ear as a seductress, and instead ended up screaming it like a cheap whore. All of it passed Shanty by as he wandered through the rooms. He was almost amused by the amount of space, the sheer number of rooms for which purposes had surely been invented. Shanty was convinced that if he walked for long enough he would chance upon a room or an alcove dedicated to the washing of small terriers. As he passed the entrance to the suite for the third time, trying to work out what was going on with the world, the handle turned almost without noise. Shan stopped in his tracks immediately, and before a figure came into view he knew what was happening. "So you’ve come back then?" he asked the figure behind the door. "Well of course. It’s the endgame now, isn’t it, Shanty?" said The Ripper as he glided into the room like a shadow. "You tell me," suggested the Welsh wrestler. "Oh, The Ripper need not tell you of it because you can feel it inside of you. Your very molecules buzz with anticipation because the time is night. There are scant days left before you face Rich Rollins in the middle of an arena and determine who is the true champion." "What?" said Shanty, incredulous as to the topic of conversation. "You reveal to me that you were the one behind the torture chambers and the factories in Iraq and in the jails, and you still care about a stupid wrestling match against a man who’s already been convicted of murder and is facing the death penalty?" The Ripper mulled this over for a few seconds as he circled around Shanty. "Yes." "Yes? Is that all you’ve got to say for…" "No. The Ripper is not the only one who cares about this, because you yourself know, deep down, that this is where everything has been leading." "Well Rollins has fucked that up good and proper." "Don’t deceive yourself, Shanty. You know full well that you will still face Rollins. It is Fate that has drawn the two of you together, and it will not be denied by such futile gestures as courts and judges, or prison walls." "It doesn’t matter. I don’t care if God Himself comes down from the Heavens and pardons Rollins. It doesn’t change what you did, what you’ve been doing for the past however many years. You’ve killed and tortured innocent people. You’ve burnt out their tongues and pierced their skulls with your contraptions and your devices. Give me one reason why I shouldn’t kill you where you stand?" The threat could have sounded ridiculous, coming from someone who stood the best part of a foot shorter than the mammoth man in black, but the voice of Jones was laden with enough gravitas to make it seem like a promise. "One reason? The first that springs to The Ripper’s mind would be that you simply cannot do it." Shanty spun around and looked at Jack with hatred glinting in his eyes. He took a step forwards, and then abruptly stopped. The pain coursed through him like a jolt of electricity, and it took Shan a couple of seconds to realise that he hadn’t been shot with anything, that The Ripper was merely standing there, looking him directly in the eye, and smiling. The pain increased, and became a living thing inside Shanty’s body. He cried out as his whole body spasmed and he dropped to the floor, shuddering. The memory of the way his nerves had become raw and alive fizzed through his neurons, keeping him down and reducing his control over his muscles. It had been as if his entire body had suddenly rejected itself, a pain almost beyond comprehension, but one that Shanty had felt before, albeit in a lesser form. When he had discovered that Paul had been running a factory inside the prison, there had been a confrontation, and Paul had utilised the same technique, whatever it was, on Shan. It had been almost unbearably painful, but Shanty had worked through it, stepped beyond the sensation emanating from every cell he could feel, and he had wrested control away from his oppressor. That had been one of the hardest things he had ever done, but that had been child’s play compared to what he faced in the form of The Ripper. "What… what have you done?" gasped Shanty. "You don’t think that those factories just tortured people, do you?" replied Jack. "Of course you don’t, but you’ve never been able to work out exactly what they were doing. Even when you’ve seen the fruits of our labour and what we can control, you’ve never really understood even the angle from which this has all been driven. You’re a man of faith, Shanty, not a man of science. You’ve done work for God, and some might say you’ve worked for the Devil too, but you’ve never worked with the very fabric of the reality that they have created. The Ripper has done this Shanty. There was more than one purpose to the creation of the factories, but you couldn’t see beyond your first impressions. Yes, there was torture there and yes, it looked terrible to see a needle second into a young boy’s brain… but the things we learned! There was never going to be another opportunity as good as that one. The Ripper had waited years for a chance to move his plan on, to go further than anyone ever has done before." Shanty blinked, looking on in disbelief at a man who sounded for all the world as if he was insane. Were it not for the lack of strength in his limbs, it could all be discounted as the ravings of a madman. "Do you see it yet, Shanty? You’ll never have understood the details of it, but can you see what has happened? We are as gods, turning the very fabric of reality in our hands." "Is that it?" asked Shan. "You’ve just got a God complex?" "Oh no, it’s much more than that," said Jack, and his face grew less animated for a second, moving back towards the placid, implacable force with which he usually moved. "Perhaps your guests can fill you in on the details of it?" "Guests?" "Oh really, you should have spotted them by now. Chris, Paul, you must come out now." The Ripper clicked his fingers, and the snapping noise was followed by one of the many doors opening, through which the sheepish figures of Doctor Curiosity and Paul Goodman walked. "No need for introductions," said The Ripper, beginning to revel in his role as ringmaster. "Though perhaps the good doctor could fill you in… could fill you both in on the precise nature of your problem." Shanty turned his head despite the pain, and looked up in disbelief at Curiosity. "You said you didn’t know anything about this!" accused Shan. "I don’t! Vhat are you talking about, large and mysterious, yet qvite scary man?" "It’s time for you to give your friends a lesson in the very building blocks of the Universe." "Ikea furniture?" "Do not be coy, doctor. You know about quantum entanglement, correct?" "I am ze greatest mind to set foot on ze planet since Einstein! Of course I know vhat it means." "Then you’ll be kind enough to explain how it all ties together, before Shanty there dies of exasperation. You have worked it out now, right?" The doctor’s expression became at once grim and full of vigour. "Jah. I know vhat is going on, but it vill take a lot of explaining…" "Well then," replied The Ripper. "Unlike the State said to Rich Rollins… we have all the time in the world."
