2006 The opening titles for “Family Reunion” were brief, they flashed up on the screen and were gone. The show wasn’t about the flashy titles. Nor was it about the celebrity host. It was about evoking the raw emotions of putting together somebody with somebody they had lost. Family members they have never been fortunate enough to meet. As soon as the titles faded from view, a shot of a beautiful, young, red haired woman came into focus. She stalked the white beaches with the breeze gently lapping her pale skin. The salted air flicked that long red hair like a beautiful flame, ambiently glowing in a setting sun. “This is April. April is eighteen years old. She grew up in the care of her grandfather and grandmother after a tragic accident stole her mother’s life, before she was born.” The voice-over work of the celebrity host was respectful and apathetic. Paying particular attention to the choice of words as the shot of the young woman, hugging herself to keep warmer within the sea breeze, remained to fill the screen. It changed to side-by-side shots of a small infant child, clearly to be tainted by the ginger gene, beside a newspaper clipping. The headline read “High school wrestling star involved in tragic road accident”. With a sub-heading of “Female passenger pronounced brain dead at hospital”. The voice of the young girl was brave as she spoke over the top of the headline. “I was er… I was never able to meet my mother. Apparently her and my father were involved in a car accident and I was very nearly never here.” The shot changed, a close up of the beautiful young woman, April, filled the screen. Her back drop a darkened room, so that she was the centre of our attention and there was nothing else in the world that mattered right at the moment except for the story this young lady would tell America. “When they got her to the hospital she was pronounced…” the young woman, drawn by the emotion of recalling tales passed down to her by family, bit her lip to stagnate the surge of tears. “…she was pronounced dead. In the brain.” She expelled a long breath, which she might have been holding onto for all eighteen years of her life. “She was brain dead.” She slowly nodded, as if the facts were settling upon her for the very first time. A solitary finger wiped the corner of her eye, not giving that first tear any opportunity to touch her snow white cheeks. The shot changed to home video footage of a lady, in her early fifties, clutching the ginger babe in her arms in the hospital. She held her up, displaying her to the camera man who had clearly no concept of cinematography, or the zoom function. The image jolted forward each time he stepped to get a closer shot of the baby. “Who have you got there, Margie?” asked the voice of the camera man. The woman beamed proudly. Her eyes were sullen in their sockets and looked as though they had spent a long while crying. The bags were deep, her face drawn and exhausted. The woman before the camera sighed, emotion bubbling into a crimson glow of her cheeks. Her voice was a mixture of pride and sorrow, all at once. She garbled over the lump in her throat. “Our g-g-gr… grrAAANNNNDD-DDAAAAUUUGGGHHHTTTEEERRRR!” Her wailing didn’t seem to startle the sleeping child as the camera man lowered his shot, putting the camera on the bed in a lucky position so that it might capture them, even if it were in the top left hand corner of it’s lens, holding each other, trying their best not to crush the infant in her grasp. “An automobile accident took April’s mother, Ellen Jameson, away from her before she was even born. As doctor’s tended the injured sixteen year-old Ellen they discovered that she was in the second trimester of her pregnancy. She was carrying a baby. And that baby was April.” The shot changed from the middle aged couple hugging to their older aged versions, sitting side-by-side before the camera. The man kept a stiff upper lip. His index finger nervously shoving his glasses up the bridge of his nose. The woman, Marge, was clearly not as brave despite her best attempts. And that crimson flush, captured on the home video, was prominent eighteen years on. “Jonah and I… we had to make a very difficult decision. We knew that our Ellie ALWAYS dreamed of becoming a mother, didn’t we, Jonah?” Jonah testified with another shove of his glasses up his nose. “That’s right, Marge. She always wanted to be a mum just like her mum.” “The doctors said… to Jonah and I… that we need to decide that if we want to keep this baby. That they could put Ellen on machines to keep the rest of her body alive-” her words were intercepted by the onslaught of emotion. Jonah reeled her into his chest, patting her back as she sobbed. Her sixty year-old shoulders heaving dramatically with each exhausting breath. Jonah continued the story. “So… that’s what we did. It’s what Ellie would’ve wanted. And we may have lost a daughter but we gained her legacy. April. We named her just like her daddy.” There was a moment of pause, as Jonah listened to the silent question of the interviewer asked of him. He shook his head. “No. We never blamed August for the death of our daughter. They were two kids in love and he worshipped the ground she walked on. He woulda done anything for our daughter and not ONCE…” Jonah’s right hand rose, with a defiant index finger, emotion drawn by the disrespect that question posed. “…NOT… ONCE… did we ever blame that poor young man for the loss of our daughter. Because on that day he lost someone he loved, too.” The screen succumbed to the still image of a young red haired man of seventeen at a county fair, holding up an over-sized teddy bear to Ellen. The love in their eyes was thick. It didn’t seem like the puppy-love gaze of two teenagers. It seemed a stronger bond. Like Antony and Cleopatra. Like Romeo and Juliet. “August Monday was an up and coming wrestler who had collegiate scholarship offers long before he was ever eligible for college.” The picture changed to August in his Olympic style wrestling outfit, with Portland High School Wrestling emblazoned across the chest. Ellen clutched his right wrist with her left hand, holding it up like he was the champion of the world. “Together, they wanted to take over the world. But that devastating night separated two people in love.” Jonah Jameson filled the screen again, a sullen look on his face as Marge dabbed tissues to her eyes. “August went on and wrestled. But he never went to college. He lost interest in school. He wound up in jail for a little, after a brawl in his father’s bar. Around about the time April was born, wasn’t it, Marge?” Marge nodded. “That’s right. I remember it in the papers the February before. We tried to visit him in prison but he wouldn’t see us.” “The boy had a lot of pride,” Jonah proudly told the camera. “He held us in to high a regard to let us to see him like that. And as soon as he was out… he was gone. He was wrestling. 1987 was the last we saw him. In person anyways. You only had to turn on the telly between ’99 and… well… NOW to see August.” “August Monday fulfilled his dream to become a professional wrestler. Winning championships and fans alike.” Footage of August Monday catching a wrestler and powerslamming them into the canvas was shown. “His name was synonymous with violence and pride. He became a household name in professional wrestling, famously known as the Raging Fear. But what the Raging Fear didn’t know was that he had a daughter.” The show cut to footage of a suburban Portland street with a “Family Reunions” reporter knocking at a door. As the door to the house slowly prised open, August Monday emerged and stood within it’s frame. He looked at the man before him, eyes clearly reading the logo on the breast of their vest. He looked over to the camera then back to the man. They spoke for a few moments before August Monday’s voice could be heard speaking over the scene. “Gees, I can’t believe it,” August said as the shot changed, to him in front of a greyed and darkened background. He found himself opening and closing his mouth like a fish trying to find words. Barely able to manage the “wow”s which escaped here and there. “A daughter. I’ve… I got a daughter?” A photo was placed on the table he sat at. His massive hand gently picked it up for inspection. He squinted at it for a moment, with hesitation oozing from his expression. He could manage a stifled laugh. Not that he found humour in the situation, it was for the shock of seeing his own flesh and blood for the first time. Inquisitively, his eyes mulled over the image of his daughter, at six months old. His free hand pointed at the picture and he looked up to the mystery interviewer, who was just off to the right of shot. “Ginger. Poor little thing. She’s a ginger, too.” He let out a chuckle. This time it was thickened with pride. His cheeks began to wash scarlet. August pushed the knuckle of his index finger into the corner of his eye, running it along it’s underside and clearing his throat as masculinely as he possibly could. But it was no use trying to hide the truth. The camera captured every emotion. He looked up. With a half laugh. Half choke. “Haha… I got a daughter.” His eyes dropped again, locked onto the image of flesh and blood. He slowly nodded, affirming his thoughts. “Well, fBLOOPk me.” Cut-to the beautiful eighteen year-old, hair the exact tint of red her father carries. April sighed heavily as if she were in the middle of a thought, a smile crossing her face like a memory struck her. “I remember seeing him wrestle on television back in 2000. When he won his first title belt in IWL. The Hardcore belt. He was so tough. He was so fearless. I just remember seeing him and thinking that’s my dad. My daddy is SO strong and brave. I always felt a connection. I’ve been and seen him live. Just never been able to get close enough or known how to say… you know… hey, I’m April. I’m your daughter.” She sighed, her mind searching to find the humour in the situation but residing within sorrow. Her pretty little face twisted up, lip curling in the famous Monday snarl… yet this snarl fought tears. Her fight was futile. Her hands lifted and slapped against her thighs and she shrugged. “I just wanna know my daddy… That’s all.” Jonah and Marge Jameson came back into focus, with Jonah beaming proudly. “We raised that girl just like he did Ellen. She’s the love of our life and every day we look at her we see our daughter lookin’ back. She’s Ellen all over, isn’t she, Margie?” Marge’s chubby features wobbled as she nodded in testament. “She certainly is and it’s been her dream to meet her daddy. We tried to get in contact with him but he’s a hard man to get in touch with. He keeps to himself. And that when we thought of this show. We suggested it to April. That maybe she might be able to meet her father.” It cut back to April, who was dabbing her eyes with tissues. She forced a smile. “I just hope he wants to meet me, too.” She sighed, with a heavy heart. The image transitioned beautifully into a shot of August, who seemed like he were listening intently to the interviewer. “Your daughter is eighteen now.” “Eighteen? Wow. Don’t I feel old.” August ran his hand down the length of his face, finally winding up in his beard and stroking it as his hand ran its course. “Ya got a picture of ’er?” “No,” the interviewer replied. August’s cheery body language dropped and he shrivelled within himself. Shoulder’s slouching and smile departing his eyes. He nodded solemnly. “But she would like to meet you. Would you like to meet her?” The rejoice returned to August and he immediately rose to his feet, the camera man awkwardly having to jerk the shot upward to capture the smiling monster. Monday turned, tugging his jacket off the back of the chair, pulling his arms in as he turned back to the camera. “Let’s go. Let’s meet my girl.” The scene shot to an image of August Monday, wearing a dark blue denim jacket and faded blue jeans. He removed the John Deere cap from his head and smoothed his red hair before putting the cap back in place. Obviously nervous, August swung his arms back and forward, clapping his hands in front when they met. His massive right hand ran over his mouth and chin and he gave a nervous whistle for no reason at all except that it might take his mind off the nerves. “I gotta admit. Pretty damn nervous. You don’t meet your daughter every day, right?” The behemoth placed both his hands behind his head. He closed his eyes and expelled a long breath of uneasiness before opening them again, sheepishly grinning at the camera. “C’mon, you guys are killin’ me. Where is she?” He doubled over, took a few quick breaths before straightening again. He was clearly getting nervy about the meeting, growing impatient for the wait. In the background of the shot, a red haired young lady came into the focus, wearing a long knee length cashmere trench coat. Hands buried in her pockets. She stopped in her tracks when she saw August with his back to her. Bouncing from foot to foot, August let out a nervous laugh and shoved his hands in the pockets of his jacket, plucking out a packet of Marlboro Reds and a Zippo. He took out a smoke, stuffed it in his mouth and lit it, using the pack as a wind break. “I’m sorry. I’m just a bundle of nerves.” He held out the cigarette like it were an exhibit. “These help.” While he lit the cigarette, the young woman, April, had began to step slowly closer. And as she did so, August seemed to have caught a stray gaze from one of the camera crew. He spun around, turning his back to the camera. Staring across the field at the young woman twenty yards away. The cigarette tumbled from his mouth. “April?” His feet wanted to move, but his body wasn’t willing. “APRIL?” He bellowed across the field and the petite, red haired girl nodded in the distance. “APRIL!” And with the final repetition of her name his body decided to join his itchy feet. He charged across the field, monstrous arms opening up as he drew closer. The girl opened her arms, rushing to meet her father and the pair collided in a emotional embrace. He spun her around and clutched her so close to him, as if he might never let her go. As the camera man caught up with the emotional scene the uncontrollable sobbing seizing the boom mic’s feed as August howled, his head buried into the nape of his daughter’s neck. April wailed while she clutched her daddy ever so tight. A smile stretched wide across her face and her tiny arms wrapped around his neck, clutching him ever so tightly like she might never let him go. August’s massive hands wrapped around her shoulders and he held her out, eyes dancing as they investigated the young woman before him in his vice-like grip. His beard was matted by the saliva he couldn’t contain in his wailing fit. He released one gripping hand and ran the forearm over him mouth and under his nose, removing the extremities that had betrayed him. Then his open hand mopped at the tears which had ran down his cheeks to join the snot and saliva in his beard. April beamed as she looked back proudly at her father, and he tugged her back in for another long embrace. Eighteen years lost between them, all resolved in the embrace of a father with his daughter. They were complete strangers yet the hug seemed so natural. There were no differences and no bitterness. There was unconditional love between two strangers. As estranged as a father and daughter could be. Between the strongest bond that could ever be known through all of human existence. The bond of a father and his little girl. An unstoppable force. Unfathomable and indestructible. They clutched one another like they were scared to let go, as if their hearts finally understood why that hole was always there. And once again, August Monday held April out in front of him. His Cheshire grin was almost stark in contrast to his public image (and disgusting beard). “You look just like yer mother, ya know that?” he told her. “Yeah. I know.” She smiled, hesitant to break eye contact. Proud for the compliment. “I never knew you were alive.” He sighed. “We’ve always got today though. Right?” April smiled, pushed her arms under the father’s and wrapped her arms around his chest, pressing her head to his heart and listening to it beat a few moments before she even contemplated answering. “That’s right.” She gently sobbed. With pride. Pure, contented pride. At long last she had her dad. “That’s right.” She repeated. “That’s right.” And “Family Reunions” slowly faded to credits now they finally had their daddy daughter day.
