Perfection: Part 2

26 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-26 “Welcome home, Brian.” The shadowed figure stepped out from the darkness and reached over to the side of the wall, calmly flipping on a light switch and illuminating the living room. Brian snapped his eyes closed, blinded by the quick change in lighting, and began to rub his eyes furiously. After a few seconds he began to blink rapidly, his eyes almost adjusted, and then stared towards the place the figure had been. He had vanished. Brian suddenly felt a cold hand on the back of his shoulder, and he instinctively shot forward off of his couch, panicking. In his foolish burst he tripped over the coffee table and spilled the Corona onto his carpet, falling to his knees. As he glanced at the alcohol pouring out of the bottle and onto the floor he couldn’t help but lick his lips and think, ‘What a waste…’ Brian’s focus wasn’t lost for very long, however, as he remembered the reason for his outburst in the first place. He quickly rolled over and looked behind the couch, the place the intruder had been last. Again, he was gone. ‘Brian was alone in his home with a damn maniac…’ He gulped, looking around carefully, trying to figure out where the intruder might have gone to hide. He had been away all day, so no doubt whoever was in the house probably had plenty of time to survey the entire home and find all the nooks and crannies that were available to hide in. For the first time in Brian’s life he was pissed off that he had enough money to buy such an expensive, large, open house. It might possibly turn out to be his downfall in the end, if that was what that maniac had in mind. ‘Get something to defend yourself…’ Brian snapped his fingers and stood up, grinning in spite of the situation. There were plenty of things that he kept in his bedroom that would be plenty helpful in a situation like this. Brian didn’t keep a gun in the house, but there were other things that could be almost as useful as a gun. He marched down the hallway, making sure to turn on all the lights that he could as he went. He came to the shut door of his bedroom and barged in, flipping on the light and taking a look around. ‘My God…’ Someone had had a little too much fun when Brian was away. The dressers in the bedroom had been pushed over and the clothes had been tossed out, many of them looking tattered and ripped. The expensive mirror that had hung above the dresser had been smashed to bits and glass was scattered all over the floor. His bed itself didn’t look out of place at first, but when he crept closer he saw that there were several bulges underneath the sheets. Being careful to not step on any glass, Brian stalked forward and pulled the covers back. ‘Yech…’ Brian immediately plugged his nose and scoured up his face in disgust. The intruder, whoever he was, had dumped the contents of Brian’s garbage in Brian’s bed, and the stench was horrible. He quickly covered the bed back up and backed away, accidentally cutting his uncovered foot on a piece of glass. His foot started to bleed rather quickly, and Brian sighed. This was getting worse and worse by the minute. Ignoring his foot, Brian knelt down under his bed and looked for the item in which he had come into his bedroom in the first place to retrieve. He didn’t know what kind of person he was up against, as he had only seen his silhouette in the darkness, but he didn’t figure that a man of any size or strength could stand against a baseball bat to the face. He forced his arm under the bed, reaching and feeling for the bat. ‘I know it’s here somewhere…’ Brian peered under the bed, beginning to frown. ‘Gone. Damn it!’ That bastard had thought of everything. He was beginning to run out of ideas for this guy, and it was starting to bug him a little bit. He should have had the advantage, as they were in his house, but this guy seemed to have his moves planned out. ‘You dumbass! Use the phone and call the police…’ Brian couldn’t believe he hadn’t thought of this first, but he supposed that a life of fighting one on one in a wrestling ring had numbed his sensibilities a little bit. He wasn’t really used to relying on anyone else to fight his battles for him, anyhow. Brian moved over to the side of the bed and picked up his portable phone, dialing ‘911’. He put the phone to his ear and waited… and waited… Nothing. No dial tone, of course. Brian sighed. He couldn’t believe he didn’t see that one coming. If this intruder had been smart enough to find his hidden baseball bat then of course he would have been smart enough to cut the phone line. Wait a minute, this wasn’t the 1980’s anymore. His cell phone! Reality quickly dawned on Brian, and he punched the wall in rage. He had given his cell phone to his wife before she had left for her mother’s, and that was his only one, unfortunately. Intruder – 2 Calvin – 0 Brian clenched his fist and maneuvered out of the jungle that had become his bedroom, slamming the door behind him as he went. He peered out into the open hallway and glared ahead, realizing finally that he was all alone in this battle, just like he’d been alone in countless wrestling matches over his twenty year career as a professional. The only problem with this situation, however, is that he knew nothing about his opponent and his opponent knew everything about him. Brian frowned, and quickly thought about leaving the house all together. He could easily get to a safe place to make a phone call or get some help and not risk a potentially horrible conflict. He finally decided that wasn’t an option. If this person meant business, as he obviously did, then he wasn’t going to wait around for the police to catch him. And then he would be back. Maybe not the next night, or even the next month, but he’d be back. The signs were too serious; this guy had done his research. The alcohol was proof enough of that. ‘Who was this guy?’ Brian didn’t have a clue. It had to be someone who knew about his alcoholism in the past, but Brian didn’t think that anyone else had known, besides his wife and his rehabilitation group. ‘Had his wife spilled the beans?’ He doubted it. His wife was one of the classiest women that he had ever met, and she was very unlikely to have told anyone, especially since she knew that Brian would be very upset by it. This pretty much left his rehabilitation group, but he didn’t think anyone in that group even knew he was a professional wrestler. Of course, there was always the possibility that someone had recognized him. Being a professional wrestler on primetime for about twenty years must gain you some recognition among even non-fans, he supposed. Even when Brian broke things down it was still impossible for him make an educated guess, so he figured he might as well quit trying. Whoever was in his home, well, they had the advantage of their identity, if nothing else. Brian slowly crept down the hallway back towards the living room. The last place the intruder had been was behind the couch, right before Brian had panicked. It would have been difficult for him to slip upstairs in the short few seconds Brian was on the ground, so the most logical place for him to be would have been in either the kitchen or the dining room. Of course, he probably wasn’t just sitting around in the open, so any one of the closets or pantries in those two rooms were fair game as well. Brian turned the corner into the living room and found it to be just the way it was when he left it. The overturned Corona bottle was in the same spot as it had been and the wet spot on the carpet was very obvious. ‘Rachel will kill me if that stains,’ he thought. Unless, of course, he got killed tonight. Brian shook off the thought. He had no idea what this guy’s motives were, but he didn’t suspect that an outright killing was the plan. If he had wanted that he would have done it while Brian was lying on the couch, exposed. This guy wanted to toy with him for a little bit, for sure. If his death was in the plans for later, well, so be it. Brian didn’t want to die, but he didn’t have any more regrets. But he sure wasn’t going to go out like a damn sissy ass. He continued into the kitchen, pausing to check out the area. It was different looking at his kitchen with such a sharp eye, because he saw things that he had never really paid attention to in normal circumstances. There were cobwebs just under the support beam, the pictures on the fridge were a little crooked, and the shine on the wood counter was beginning to rub off on the curves. Looking for an intruder in your house really opened your eyes in a way Brian had never really done before. Crash. Brian jerked towards entrance hall. He had heard a noise just across from the bathroom from within the laundry room. He grinned. ‘Gotchya.’ Brian began to tiptoe forward, not wanting to make a loud sound on the linoleum floor of his kitchen. As he crept closer he looked around for something to use as a weapon. Anything. He finally spied his weapon of choice on the top of the microwave, only about a foot or so away. ‘Perfect.’ He reached forward and carefully slid it into his hand, soundlessly. It was The Fountainhead, by Ayn Rand. All one-thousand plus pages in all their glory. Hardcover style, of course. Whoever said reading would never be useful in life? As Brian neared the door to the laundry room he cocked the book back, ready to unleash the fury of Ayn Rand’s objectivism onto the skull of the intruder. The door was slightly ajar, and the empty blackness from the crack in the door peered gleefully back in his direction. The darkness was practically taunting him to come closer, and Brian could feel the eyes of fate looking into his heart, into his soul. Through the silence he thought he could almost hear the breathing of the monster that dwelled inside, and, for the first time, he was almost afraid. ‘Now or never,’ he thought with a shudder. Brian took the last few steps forward and he kicked the door open with a heavy yell that echoed throughout the empty house. He swung the book down into the darkness but hit nothing but air. Brian was confused, as the room had very small area to hide in, so he was bound to have connected with something. Apparently the room was unoccupied, much to his dismay. But how? He had heard a noise from within, he was sure of that. This is when his wife’s cat, William, shot out from the floor of the pantry and zoomed between Brian’s legs and into the kitchen. ‘That damn cat,’ Brian thought angrily. He spied the coffee can that the Calvin’s kept small amounts of cat food in on the floor, and he sighed. So much for that one. He knew that he should have told his wife to get a dog instead of a cat. Like an attack dog of some sort, something that he could have put to some real use. Suddenly Brian heard the toilet in the bathroom across the hall flush, and he jumped a bit, dropping his book on the floor. Before he had any time to react the bathroom door was thrust open and the intruder walked out into the open for the first time. He held an opened newspaper up, as if he was reading it, to cover his face. “Some nice reading material, Calvin. Did you hear that Dan Rather was just asked to retire? Big news, big news, buddy.” The figure quickly folded the newspaper in half and rolled it up, revealing his face at last. Vince Cutter?!? Cutter reached back into the bathroom and flipped on the fan switch, and Brian quickly heard the familiar ‘whir’ of his overhead fan flip on. “Sorry about the smell, Calvin, but I had Taco Bell for lunch this afternoon and their cheesy enchiladas really don’t agree with me.” Vince rubbed his stomach for emphasis. “Good deal, though. The commercials were right, I really was full after just two. Not bad at all, eh?” Brian gaped open mouthed at his former training partner from Fido’s Wrestling School in Denver, Colorado. He hadn’t seen him for years, not since his wedding, and this was the last person he would have expected to be trespassing in his house. Vince glared at Brian, and he made a sweeping gesture. “Close your mouth, Calvin, you’re letting flies in. Aren’t you going to ask me to sit down? Maybe prepare a couple of drinks for us? I left a Corona on the table for you earlier, because I know how much you like them, but you didn’t seem to go for it.” He smirked at Brian. Brian stopped, his brain racing a million miles per minute. Maybe Vince wasn’t here on bad terms? Maybe he had a funny way of surprising old friends? ‘Like dumping trash into your bed and destroying your bedroom?’ Brian glanced at his old friend and realized from the look on Vince’s face that this wasn’t a happy reunion at all. He glanced around and spied the Ayn Rand on the novel that he had dropped, and he reached down to pick it up. ‘I need something to defend myself with…’ Before he could reach it, however, a gunshot rang out and Brian froze. He saw the hole through the center of the novel, and he couldn’t help but think that this probably wasn’t the first time someone had put a hole through some of Rand’s theories. He stood up and looked at Vince, and then at the smoking gun he was holding in his hand. The look in Vince’s eyes confirmed Brian’s worst fears. ‘I’m not making it out of here alive.’ “What do you want, Vince? What are you doing in my house?” Vince scoffed. “Oh, I see how it is, Calvin. Once you make it to the big time you’re too good for the rest of the world, huh? Someone of my status probably isn’t even allowed on the grounds of the Calvin mansion.” Brian tried to be reasonable. “That’s not true, Vince. I’ve never once let a little fame and money go to my head, you know that about me, man.” “I thought I knew you, Brian, I really did. My psychiatrist tells me everyday that you had nothing to do with my misfortunes, too, but she didn’t know what she was talking about, either.” Misfortunes? Brian replied, “Vince, we haven’t spoken for years. I haven’t seen you since -- ,” Vince interrupted angrily and forcefully, “Since your wedding? When you married my wife?!?” Vince clenched his fists, his face turning red. This statement was another big shock to Brian. His wife? Rachel? No way. He knew Vince had dated Rachel for awhile before she and Brian hooked up, but from what Brian understood it was Vince who finally broke it off. “Vince, I think you’re overreacting here.” Brian raised his arms, and he took a step closer towards him. Vince yelled, “GET BACK, CALVIN!” Brian hastily stepped back, as Vince continued, “Overreacting? You stole my girlfriend from me. Rachel told me that there was someone else in her life, and that she thought our relationship was ‘stale’. Stale? I loved that bitch, Calvin… You stole her.” Brian had to think this one over. This was news to him, for sure. Possibly he was delirious, or Rachel had told him a little white lie before they started dating. Regardless, he didn’t think that he was going to be able to convince Vince of anything at the moment. “The last few months have been hell for me, Calvin. I lost my job; I was told I wasn’t fit to be a wrestler. After twenty years of trying to make it somewhere, I was told I wasn’t fit. That’s BULLSHIT, Calvin. At wrestling school they told me I had the most talent of any of their other students. Including you, Calvin! But I’ve had to watch you on television for the last twenty years, while I’ve been struggling to make ends meet. I’ve been killing my body for twenty years, for nothing.” Brian didn’t know how to respond, and he kept his gaze fixed at the barrel of Vince’s gun. He was waiting for Vince to snap, waiting for him to pull the trigger. “So I tried to be reasonable. I was referred to see a psychiatrist who would see me for free, to help get me back on my feet. That was a waste of my time. All it did is add fuel to the fire, Calvin. When that didn’t work I started planning other things. I did some research on you. Figured out that you had a little alcohol problem a few years back, eh? I decided to pay you a little visit. I staked out your home, figured out when you’d be gone and when I could get inside. Then I hung around the whole day, did some rearranging in your bedroom, put the Corona on the table, and stayed to watch.” Brian shook his head, the madness of the story overwhelming him. “If you had taken the Corona right there, Calvin, I was going to leave, my job done. Your life would have been ruined, just like mine is now. Our score would have been settled! But you didn’t. I waited and waited, but you didn’t. So I had to resort to Plan B.” He cocked the hammer on the gun back. “I’m sorry it had to be this way Brian, but your life was supposed to be mine. And if I couldn’t have it, I don’t want you to have it either. I’ll give Rachel my regards.” Brian closed his eyes and waited. “What the hell?” Brian opened his eyes and saw his cat, William, rubbing Vince’s legs. Vince smiled, and he turned the gun towards the cat. “I thought about killing the cat earlier, but I didn’t want to make a mess. Seeing how as you refused my friendly drink, however, it appears I’ll have a mess on my hands anyway, so I can indeed have some fun.” Vince shifted his gaze to the cat again, and this is when Flyin’ Brian Calvin made his move. Taking a swift step forward, he kicked the gun out of the hands of Vince, and wrapped his arm around his neck, putting him in a headlock.. Caught off guard, Vince dropped to the ground, gasping. “Sorry about this, Vince, but I’ve got a little bit of unfinished business to take care of before I can exit this world. Besides, my wife would kill me if I let you kill her cat.” Brian tightened the hold, and soon Vince turned a shade of red, then blue, and then purple, and finally Brian let go. Vince fell to the floor, passed out. Brian stepped to his feet and brushed off his hands, grinning. He picked up the cat and petted its head softly. “Hmph,” he said, “Maybe cats aren’t so bad after all…” In the next hour or so Brian was quite busy. First, he securely tied up Vince Cutter and went to his neighbor’s house. After briefly describing the situation he used their telephone to call the police, who showed up ten minutes later to arrest Cutter. He explained his situation more in depth to the officers who arrived, and they promised Brian that he wouldn’t have any other troubles dealing with that wacko. He spent the remainder of the hour cleaning up his bedroom, thinking of what he was going to tell his wife when his phone service was reconnected. The last piece of business was cleaning up the spilled alcohol on the carpet in the living room and disposing of the Corona bottle. Brian stood over the bottle, a small trash bag in his hands. He stared at the empty bottle a good minute, expressionless. ‘To think that I almost drank that son of a bitch…’ And he picked up the bottle and threw it into the bag with a satisfying plop. Never again. ‘I’m not a perfect person…’
