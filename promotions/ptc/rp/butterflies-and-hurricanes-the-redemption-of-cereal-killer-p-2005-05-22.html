Butterflies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Six:

22 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-22 There is a branch of science called chaos theory. The most famous exposition of this theory is that a butterfly could flap its wings just once, and the tiny change it made to the atmosphere could eventually be the difference between a hurricane ravaging a foreign coastline or not appearing at all. Sometimes the tiny, immeasurable differences can be all important. ------------------------------------------------------ Times just seemed to get stranger and stranger for the wrestler known as Cereal Killer. To be wrestling again after a lengthy absence was one thing, but to have won three straight matches against top class opposition was something else. To make matter even stranger, the brackets had opened themselves up in a way unforeseen and he was there, standing at the gates of his Mecca. He was in the tournament final. “Some champagne to celebrate, honey?” asked Valerie Rose Dubonnet, emerging from the kitchen. CK smiled as he sat on the threadbare blue couch. Just as time had been getting stranger for him, it had also been getting harder to cope with. The return of his former partner and continual nemesis, The Ripper, had added to the anxiety he was already suffering from. The nervous tension from being potentially on the verge of greatness was being amplified by his fear of The Ripper’s return, and also the return of something he’d been trying to hide from ever since he left XUW. “Do you want me to get the bottle or not? I mean, if you’re going to be training and can’t drink then I’ll understand. We can just keep it until you win the next match, right?” Valerie’s voice was not the only one that CK could hear. Inside his mind the air was abuzz with schemes, characters, voices and confusion. Whilst living a quiet life with Valerie, he’d been able to keep them at bay, to ignore them as best he could, but with his return to the squared circle came an escalation in their insistency. They had plagued him through his career, forcing him to change gimmicks whenever he threatened to become popular or successful. CK thought of it as a self defence mechanism. It allowed him to continue on with his job, getting paid enough to live on and doing something he liked, without ever having to worry about genuine failure, because he was bringing it on himself intentionally. The problem came when wrestling and Valerie became mixed together. She had said she had thought it would help CK to become happier about himself; that if she entered him into a wrestling tournament then it might give them an even greater bond. Instead, it was starting to drive a wedge between them. “Honey?” asked Valerie, her face showing concern. “I’m sorry,” replied CK, rubbing his hands across his face. “I just can’t help myself sometimes. My mind, it gets… tangled up somehow, and I don’t know how to stop it.” Cereal Killer was worried. He did care about Valerie a great deal, but every time he thought of her, it just brought back to his mind one of the prophecies of The Ripper; that Valerie would leave him before his next match. “I’m sorry.” “It’s not your fault, Valerie.” “You say that, but whenever I see you now you look like you want to be somewhere else. Or, with some… oh no. Please, no, Frank.” Valerie’s hands went to cover her mouth, and her tear ducts started firing off saline into her eyes. “No! No, Valerie! I don’t want to be with anyone else,” said CK, standing up from the sofa and shaking his head. “Then why?” she demanded. “Look, I love you Valerie, I really do. I just… sometimes it’s hard with the wrestling and being with you as well. I don’t mean that in a negative way, it’s just I’m programmed. I feel like someone connected all the wrong wires up in my head… except the ones involving you.” Valerie blushed and smiled, her sobs arrested and almost turned to giggles with a few short words. “Look Valerie, if I lose this match then I’m out of a job anyway. XUW won’t pay me to sit around at home any more unless I win this thing.” “You’re going to throw the match?” asked Valerie, confused. “No, nothing of the sort. If I win instead, though, I get to keep my contract. I’ll still be paid, and they won’t even need me to appear at shows, just do some promotional work for them.” “So?” “So either way I can be done with wrestling after this one match! If you can just give me that long then I promise I’ll give you all my attention. We can settle down and be together, just the two of us. No more wrestling.” “You’d do that?” asked Valerie, tears coming to her eyes again, but for very different reasons. “For you, Valerie, I’d do anything. I want to be with you for the rest of my life.” He started towards her, and she held out her arms, ready for the embrace. It never came. “It’s so touching to hear you speak that way.” The hairs on the back of Frank’s neck stood on end, as if someone had just walked over his grave. “It’s just a shame that Valerie must leave you now. Forever.” The two turned as one to see the bulk of the colossal figure standing in the open doorway. CK knew without even having to look. The words and tone were unmistakeable. It was The Ripper. ------------------------------------------------------ Back when he was in his early teens, Frank thought he saw someone getting strangled. It was in the middle of a mall, and he was walking home, straight home, just like his parents had always told him to. He ignored the video game shops that were selling the latest creations by Nintendo and Sega, and he walked past the comic book store with the newest pristine issues of Batman and associated other characters. He was a good, if slightly erratic boy, and he didn’t want to make his parents more uncomfortable than they already were with him. Frank understood that his behaviour was not always normal, and the pills he took were a testament to that. Despite being neither too fat, too ugly, too stupid or even too intelligent to fit in, Frank had sought to distance himself from the usual crowds of teenagers. He was more at home with his own company, and the company of the people he created to fill the gaps. They weren’t real, but the world they could transport him into seemed better than real. That day he was thinking of who he might talk to in the evening; whether it would be the Cosmic Cowboy or the Intergalactic Indian, or someone he’d never met before. As he pondered on his future, a young voice rang out. It was a voice he recognised instantly, and he looked around to see the pretty mouth that it was emanating from. He knew the second that he heard his name spoken that it was Emily, a girl from the year below him in school; a girl that everyone said had a crush on him. Frank thought that she was fairly pretty, though her freckles had been threatening for some time to join up and turn her face a permanent, peculiar shade of brown and orange. The braces on her teeth glinted back the strip lights from the ceiling as she smiled at him, one hand idly toying with her burnished bronze ringlets. “What ya doin’?” asked Emily, her eyelashes fluttering. “I’ve got to get home. My Mom won’t like it if I’m not at home,” replied Frank, trying to smile at her as he paused, and then moved on again. “Wanna play?” she asked. “No… I… I can’t.” Frank turned away from her and looked down at his feet. She wouldn’t have understood the way he played, or the things he said. It would be for the best that she was left alone, thought Frank. He walked on, unable to see her face glow crimson with embarrassment and disappointment, and equally unable to see the tear slowly rolling down her cheek. The young boy walked on, rounded a corner, and nearly walked into a huge stack of boxes that fell down right in front of him. Sighing, and reflecting that had he not even paused for that instant to speak to Emily he would have been the other side of the boxes when they fell, Frank swung ninety degrees to his right and took an alternative route through the mall, headed for the exit that would lead to his bus stop. Delayed as he was, albeit by scant seconds, and coming from a different angle than he would normally have been, he saw something ahead of him that sent neurons scrambling through his brain in panic and fear. It seemed like a middle aged woman had fallen over, or been thrown to the ground. The wrinkles and creases in her face were scrunched up as her face contorted in agony. Frank only gathered a brief glimpse of that face though, because there was a man kneeling down by her, with his arms around her neck. Shocked as he was, Frank recalled more words that his mother had taught him. He dashed straight to the nearest payphone and hit the numbers that were imprinted on to his brain. 911. Before the police could even arrive, mall security had placed another call to the same number, but this one had merely requested an ambulance, and not anyone from law enforcement. The paramedics arrived as Frank stood, aghast, looking at the woman being laid out on a stretcher and placed in the back of the van. Unable to contain himself, he darted forward, pointing an accusing finger at the man he had seen strangling her. Frank could not believe that these people were letting him literally get away with murder! It was only as the words themselves escaped his breathless, chapped lips that he realised that he’d been seeing things the wrong way around. The man had only been taking her pulse, trying to save her life instead of end it. The humiliation, the trouble he got in to, and most importantly of all, the look on Emily’s face as he stood there crying at the realisation of his mistake; these things changed him. Frank became noticeably more nervous and more withdrawn. He stopped making eye contact with his peers, and his time spent with his ‘creations’ became longer, and more frequent. From one slight pause, one brief hesitation at the behest of a girl, was set in motion a chain of events that led Frank to become the man known as Cereal Killer. One cornerstone of his neuroses, the afflictions that ruined his wrestling career once and could threaten to do so again, would always be caused by that one little conversation. It’s all butterflies and hurricanes. ------------------------------------------------------ “No! Can’t you just leave me alone?” pleaded CK. “If you knew even a fraction of what The Ripper knows, then you would have realised by now why you find yourself in this predicament.” ”Who is this?” asked Valerie. The Ripper smiled and shook his head, which infuriated Cereal Killer. It was as if every mannerism, everything that The Ripper did was to upset him. “Do not play games with The Ripper.” The dead pan delivery sent a frisson of ice cold terror up CK’s spine. “I’m not playing games. I’m not playing anything. Why are you here? I just want to win the tournament and go home.” “You cannot do this.” “I can’t win?” “You cannot go home.” “Why not? I can be happy with Valerie! I can live with her and you can just leave me alone. I’ll be fine, I’ll be better than fine, why can’t you understand that?” begged Cereal Killer, tears coming unbidden to his eyes. The Ripper paused, as if weighing up every word that he would speak. “You are deluded in thinking you can find happiness in that way. You would be a making a mistake that The Ripper will not stand for. She is nothing.” He inclined his head in the vague direction of Valerie, who stood still, as if transfixed by terror. “Why do you say that? Tell me why!” screamed CK. “Look into her eyes.” Frank turned, and Valerie did likewise. He could see that she was crying too, that she felt what he did. She wanted them to be alone, just like he did. He raised an arm towards her, which she mirrored. Her eyes glinted with moisture, her pale blue irises reflecting his. “Do you see yet, the extent of your delusion?” The Ripper’s words were uncharacteristically soft, as Frank continued to gaze into Valerie’s eyes. They blinked in unison, his mind trying to shield itself from the thought that was catching up with it. “Do you see now? Do you understand why this must change?” Frank closed his eyes, squeezing moisture that rolled down his cheeks and fell, hitting the floor with a miniscule sound that still made him think of a death knell. “You have to let her go, because Valerie does not exist.”
