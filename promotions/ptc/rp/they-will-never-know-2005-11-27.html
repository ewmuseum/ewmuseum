They Will Never Know

27 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-27 I hear her coming more than two hundred yards away, but I don’t react and I don’t try to evade her; even though every single one of my instincts tell me to do just that. "Mr. Winters!" She calls after me, getting closer now. I ignore her and continue to walk; my standard procedure in dealing with such annoyances. Though, I must admit that policy seriously needs reviewing - and soon. They never take the hint; they always persist, desperate for my attention. It is inevitable that they do actually gain my attention, but it’s never the kind they expect, nor require. Her stilettos rap uniquely - and very distinctly amongst the surrounding ambient noise - against the concrete pavement and tells me that she’s almost caught up to me now. They always catch up. That’s the way it works; I never pick up speed and I continue at my own pace. Better to deal with this right away, rather than escape from their clutches today and have them come back to annoy me again at another time. She finally catches up with me - she seems a lot slower than all the others that have annoyed me in the past - and positions herself in my path; a much more effective way of forcing my attention. However, I’ve done this dance a hundred times before and I know precisely what to do. I glare the pretty blonde in the face, grin, then sidestep past her with considerable ease. "Mr. Winters!" She calls again, for some reason expecting my reaction to be different this time around. "Will you stop a minute and let me talk?" I ignore her and continue to walk down the busy New York City street. "Asshole!" The woman mutters under her breath, trying to conceal the curse from me. She still believes that there’s a small chance that I’ll talk to her and so it won’t help her chances to let me here her curse at me. She doesn’t account for my extra-sensitive hearing; they never do. She attempts to catch up with me again, faster this time, and jumps into my path - nearly knocking over an innocent bystander in the process - extremely animated, a very determined look in her eyes. Damn, I could almost respect that. I try to side step her again, but this time she is prepared for it and steps into my path once more. "They told me that you were difficult," she informs me, "but I didn’t realise you were this difficult." "Sorry to disappoint," I tell her. I try to side step her again, but she reacts just as quickly as before, blocking my path. "I’m Adie Laurie…" She starts. "Of course you are," I rather rudely cut her off. She rolls her eyes around her head and develops an even angrier expression, but she still doesn‘t take the hint. "I’m a journalist, I only want to interview you…" "I know," I interject. "That’s why I’m trying to avoid you." "Look, I’m with the Wrestling Observer, the premier online wrestling journal," the woman informs me, but I’m not listening anymore. I just looking past her, wondering just how long this is going to take. "This is high profile publicity you’re looking at here! You should be begging me for this interview." I let out a chuckle, despite myself. "Well, that isn’t going to happen." "I know," she replies, "I’ve actually been assigned to do this interview." "Drew the short straw?" I ask her. "Something like that…" She replies. I use her momentary self-reflection, to finally succeed in side-stepping her feeble blockade. She reacts quickly, trotting by the side of me, attempting to thrown herself into my path yet again. This time, I don’t allow her to; I keep my pace always a step ahead of her. "I only want to ask you a few questions about the GTT," she pleads, the futility of her situation slowly beginning to settle in. "And I don’t much care," I tell her. She stops in her tracks and stamps her right foot, simulating a tantrum performed by a child at the supermarket. The big ‘fuck off’ sign that I’ve been signalling to her ever since she attempted to open dialogue with me has finally registered. Though, only the most tenacious journalists last this long. "I’m gonna give you a bad write up for this!" She yells at me, a last ditch attempt at getting something from me. I grunt something inaudible under my breath in response, but don’t break a stride. People always ask me why I act the way I do, but as with the others, I simply ignore them just the same and go about my business. They will never know what I’ve been through to be this way. They will never understand just how vulnerable I feel, twenty-four hours a day, because of the amnesia. They will never know, because I will never tell them; I’ll never tell anybody. That’s how I will survive.
