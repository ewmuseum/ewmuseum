Jake Hix Rooting Myself Back In Reality The Meeting The smell of cleaner was almost overwhelming as he leaned back against the hard wood of the pew. The church had been built before the idea of padded seats had taken hold in the local Catholic community. Instead, as he looked around the darkened, deserted expanse of the chapel he saw sacrifice painted as much in the mood of the dramatic archways and hard wood rails for kneeling prayer as in the art depicting the crucifixion. Some old widow no doubt spent hours a week laboriously cleaning every nook and cranny. Her remaining life dedicated to making the embodiment of God on earth as beautiful as possible. If you wouldn’t do it for God… Jake Hix didn’t want to be here in the house of God at the moment. He prayed to the Lord every day, but church had never been a great comfort in his life. The message had been urgent though, and at Robert’s urging he had taken the meeting. It wasn’t often the Catholic Church would call him…well…honestly it had never happened, and ignoring a Bishop just seemed impolite. Ignoring all that, sitting inside the church forced him to reflect. Maybe it was the giant cross Jesus was nailed to, but the Catholics sense of guilt and inner-reflection was overwhelming. He was cast adrift once again; his own family had turned away from him. He couldn’t blame them though because he had betrayed their trust. A price had to be paid for that treachery, and silence was extraordinarily the most effective weapon at their disposal. Sid was still out there trying to destroy him, his brother now half man and half demon. Xavier wasn’t speaking to him, and Bella would likely try to rip his eyes out if she ever saw him again. The strife had put Pitt in the most awkward position of all, and he barely spoken to him anymore as he tried to occupy the precarious middle of the battlefield. Alexandria and Julia were polite, but they knew there was tension between the brothers. Truthfully, any distraction from all those problems was comforting. “Pondering your place in His plan?” The voice asked. Jake knew who he was, a transplant from back East who still retained quite a bit of the Massachusetts accent bastardized by the Texas twang influence. He sounded like Ted Kennedy on a bad twenty-year bender through the Lone Star State. That wasn’t so unbelievable actually, “Bishop Grahmann, I wasn’t expecting a call from you.” “The Lord calls upon the faithful in times of need.” The Bishop spoke as he walked forward, turning to look the man up and down. Jake Hix had many reputations, the public ones and the private ones. He was a monster cloaked in a black wool suit; the kind of man the church had need of from time to time. Jake stood up. He looked straight into the Bishop’s eyes as he spoke, ensuring there were no misunderstandings. “God already called me, you aren’t God. I’m not Catholic, so the whole Bishop thing isn’t quite as impressive to me as to your congregation. The last Bishop we had in Dallas should have been hung from the spire of this church.” “Such hostility.” Bishop Grahmann shook his head slowly, “I didn’t expect that.” “No hostility for the church or the request, but don’t presume to begin the conversation with the concealed iron guilt inside your little velvet robes.” Jake brushed the Bishop’s shoulder, “It cheapens you, it insults God and it really annoys me. So, what can I do for you Bishop?” Bishop Grahmann was unsure how to proceed. Jake had thrown him off with his response, and now he stammered a bit as he recovered. “I…there’s been…I have a personal matter that requires attention of a delicate nature.” “I don’t know if you’ve looked at me lately, but delicate isn’t a word most people would use in describing me.” Jake motioned with his hand, and they began slowly walking to the front of the cathedral. “By delicate, I mean it may relate to some issues you are already dealing with. You have a fairly large family, am I right Mr. Hix?” Jake didn’t give anything away, but the simple inquiry caused his belly to twist with his own regrets. “Yes.” “So do I, and one of my nieces have fallen into trouble.” Bishop Grahmann began explaining. “It’s an old story. As you can imagine, we are quite strict in our faith, and my brother was perhaps too strict. She rebelled, and got into all manner of trouble. We’ve tried rehabilitation through psychiatrists, doctors and even through healing her spirit but none have taken.” “I’m not a therapist.” “No, and I would not call you for such.” Bishop Grahmann stopped short. When Jake turned he took a furtive glance around before speaking, “I believe your brother could be involved.” “My brother?” Jake was now giving the Bishop his undivided attention. “Yes, there were certain items found at her apartment that worried me. We found…things…that some of the church’s contacts said are connected to the Dark Messiah possibly.” Jake’s mind was racing. If Sid had begun operating in Dallas, he might be preparing some kind of nasty surprise for Jake’s family. He might not be talking to them at the moment, but he didn’t want any harm to come to them. Jake’s voice shook slightly before he got it under control, “Do you have what you found?” “Here in the office.” “Show it to me.” Bishop Grahmann led Jake to the front of the chapel. They stepped past the cross after the Bishop crossed himself in front of the giant crucifix. He led him back into the private domain of the priests. They came to a locked door, which Bishop Grahmann quickly opened and locked behind them after they entered. Office might have been charitable as this was a small storage room, empty except for the folding table directly beneath the hanging yellow light. Jake stepped forward, seeing the few items Bishop Grahmann had collected. The first was pretty standard stuff, some posters and memorabilia that might have shocked the Bishop, but Jake recognized it from some local bands. He ignored that when the next two items caught his eye. The knife was simple enough, a double-edged blade with a symbol engraved into the handle. The same symbol was present on a small burlap sack next to the blade. The symbol was ancient and unknown to almost everyone, but Jake had seen it over and over again. He first found the symbol in a tome he had recovered in Japan, and then found it over and over again as he penetrated deeper into Jake’s world. “They found this?” Jake asked as he picked up the bag. “Yes.” “Tell me everything you can about your niece.” Jake gripped the bag tightly as he demanded answers he was sure the Bishop never thought he would have to give. The Clue Sometimes things were easier than you expected them to be in the beginning. Patricia “Patty” Grahmann had an overbearing father named Richard and a mother, Isabel, who seemed too frail and shy to even say no to anyone asking a favor. They were not that good at handling much of anything, let alone their daughter’s latest disappearance. The issue would normally not be handled until her return, but the items found had alerted her Uncle more than the parents knew. For their sake, he was simply a friend of the Bishop. They had been no help, but a discussion with her sister had revealed plenty. She liked to hang out at certain clubs, and Jake knew the owners. For his next trip, he visited her apartment in Dallas. When he walked in, the entire place was covered in empty beer bottles, trash and plenty of empty baggies and broken vials. He had seen this before, but he wasn’t looking for proof of her addiction. Jake was forced to take his time, looking through the entire apartment and trying to ignore the putrid smell of shit and trash rotting in the heated interior. They could have just left the heat off and caused less damage. Whatever she had done, she had become oblivious to all of this. He wondered where she had gone astray, but the truth was he didn’t care. A few phone calls later; he learned she had been seeing the same man for months, a Hector Ramirez. He was a low-life from what everyone told him, but he liked to spend money so the club’s tolerated him. “What’s he like Tony?” Jake asked the bouncer over the phone. “He likes to talk, but he’s no threat. We just growl at him a little and he hides off in the corner.” Jake laughed at that, “Every bitch has to know their place around the yard.” “Exactly. He spends his money to get the girls, and he doesn’t deal in the club. He’s hooked up with the Tenth Street boys. Some little band out of the Cliff.” Hector was a simple street dealer, but a man with enough money to be a benefit. Hell, the element of risk added to the club’s allure. None of that made much sense. Sid didn’t bother with people at that level, but if he were suddenly involved then it could mean lots of trouble for Jake. Sid’s disciples and followers were kept at bay through various supernatural means, but some street punk with an AK could come blow the families away before Jake knew it was happening. A move into such a realm signaled a desperate shift in strategy. He tried to put that out of his mind as he parked on the street in The Cliff. The Cliff was Oak Cliff, but the bangers wanted to set themselves apart and labeled it The Cliff because it sounded harder, more dangerous. These were streets and young boys who didn’t suffer intruders. Jake had spent some time here when he was younger, and that was when the violence was comparatively tame. He saw two young men eye him as he stepped out of the S.U.V. and activated the alarm. He kept his eyes locked on them as he walked past them. Jake heard the discussion behind him, but they wouldn’t take a flyer against him. He struck a threatening visage for anyone, and now looking at his back they would see the distinct bulge under his shirt where the pistol was holstered. Better to talk shit and live, than to engage and risk everything. The housing in the neighborhood was sixty to eighty years old. Even back then it was cheap and rundown, and the gang wars and habitual users hadn’t done the neighborhood any favors. The small houses were entire worlds unto themselves as the residents never wanted to involve themselves in any trouble anyone else might be having. Better to wait until trouble was over; check the house for anything of value to take and then report the crime…if any report was made at all. Stepping to the back of the house, the back door was wide open. The only thing standing between Jake and the interior was a screen door made of rusting wire mesh. He reached down to his boot and pulled out the knife he kept there. The entire blockade was quickly cut away, falling inside like a feather touching with almost no sound. He had to shake his head at that one as he put the knife back and stepped inside. There was nothing but silence inside the house. The smell was familiar, all rotten and nasty thanks to the same trash strewn about here as he had seen at Patty’s place. He pulled out the automatic pistol, and slowly checked around. The kitchen, dining room and living room were all unoccupied except for the occasional rodent or cockroach. He doubted there was anyone in the bathroom, but he double-checked it after he peered through the open door to see the second bedroom was empty. Jake settled on the master bedroom, and heard snoring from inside as he approached. Jake turned the doorknob slowly, and pushed the door open. Inside, he saw a woman he recognized as Patty Grahmann spread eagle on the bed, completely naked as she lay next to an equally naked Hector Ramirez. Both of them were little more than sacks of bones. Patty’s mom had proudly shown him her high school picture, and a girl with blossoming features and curves to attract attention were now wasted away to sagging skin and pale, sickly looking flesh. Patty had come a long way down the wrong road. Standing there, he was struck by the continuing snoring coming from both of them. They were oblivious to his presence, in some kind of drug induced comatose sleep. He saw some aluminum foil with burn marks on the nightstand…right next to a revolver. Jake silently stepped to the nightstand and took the revolver. The revolver went into his jeans, and then Jake decided to wake the happy couple up. He switched the pistol to his other hand, and slammed his right fist down into Hector’s stomach. Hector lurched up with a painful groan, waking Patty next to him. A stream of expletives in Spanish came streaming out as he knocked the nightstand over on his way to his feet. Jake waited, and then sent him back down onto the bed with a straight right to the nose. The satisfying crunch and spray of blood let him know he had struck perfectly. Patty slipped down onto the floor. “Stay down Hector.” Jake looked over at the girl with a smirk, “For God’s sake cover yourself Patty. What would your Uncle think?” Patty’s eyes started exploding with fire as she stood up and came around the bed. “Fuck him! Fuck him! Fuck my family! Fuck my Uncle! And Fuck you!” Jake switched the pistol back, and whipped it out after switching the safety on. The tip of the pistol slammed into Patty’s forehead, knocking her straight onto her back. Jake pointed the pistol down at her, seeing a tiny bit of blood and what would no doubt be a huge bruise later. “I’m not your Daddy or your Uncle, so think about that next time or I’ll have to spank you. Now the shut the fuck up and put some clothes on!” Hector was looking around for a weapon, but he stopped, frozen with panic as the unknown stranger now locked the pistol back on him. Jake reached back and slipped the pistol back into its holster. “Tell me about the symbol on the knife and the burlap sack Hector.” Despite the curse for Jake’s mother as he came up, Hector didn’t have a chance. He grabbed Jake, but then a quick shot to the throat sent him back into the wall. “I think that was your junk that just touched me, and I don’t swing that way Hector.” Jake came in fast and hard, slamming an elbow into Hector’s chest. All the force knocked the winds out of him, and could have broken bones if he had aimed for the ribs. As Hector tried to find a breath, Jake added a knee to his stomach. Jake grabbed Hector by the throat, but instead of continuing the choke he simply slammed the crown of his head into Hector’s face. The addict crumbled, this wasn’t a fight as much as a beating. “Stay with me Hector.” Jake lightly shoved his chest with the point of his boot. “Now, tell me about the knife and the bag.” “What?” Hector was still in a stupor, unsure whether he was awake or having a nightmare. Patty was quickly putting her clothes on. Jake didn’t want to put him out because he needed answer. He turned his hip into the kick. His boot slammed into Hector’s fragile thigh with a loud slap. Hector looked up at the behemoth standing over him like a demon, and he spoke. “What? WHAT DO YOU WANT!?!” “Tell me about the symbol.” “What symbol.” Hector tried to fight back anyway he could. He just needed time to think of something. Jake kicked him in the balls, a particularly nasty shot since he didn’t have so much as a pair of boxers covering him. “The symbol.” Hector was holding onto himself in agony, but he refused to speak. Jake kicked his thigh again. “Leave him alone!” Patty screamed. Jake ignored her as he kicked him again, “The symbol Hector!” “Baby Smurf’s Devil Stamp?” Hector asked through coughs with a whiny voice as he tried to control the pain. “What?” Jake didn’t hit him this time. “What’s the Devil Stamp?” “Baby Smurf down on Tenth Street, that’s what he stamps all his shit with. He marks the product, if you want some fly the man better have marked bag and marked blade or he’s not with Baby Smurf.” Hector was holding onto himself. “What do you mean stamp?” “All the crews mark the lines. You know what you get, it hasn’t been trashed if it had the Devil Mark.” Jake leaned down, slapping Hector across the face, “Are you fucking with me? What do you know about Sid?” “Who?” Jake slapped him again, “Tell me about Sid!” “I don’t know any Sid!” He kept slapping, and then the slaps turned to punches until he was pounding on Hector’s back as the man tried to escape the blows. The sound of Patty screaming finally broke through, and he stopped. Jake looked at her, terrified yet proud behind her emaciated frame that hung sadly to small in the clothes she had chosen. “Is he telling me the truth about the mark?” “Yeah you fucking asshole! Everyone knows that!” She backed away as Jake stepped to her. He ignored the screaming in his mind. He grabbed her by the arm, “You’re coming with me.” “Where?” “Back to your family.” Jake looked back; Hector was looking up at the man taking his girlfriend away. “Hector, this is the last time we see each other. I vanish, she vanishes and you never come looking.” Hector’s eyes locked onto the blue pools of hate and death. The man wasn’t threatening him or trying to intimidate him, he was simply stating truth. Hector knew if he did anything other than what he had just been told, he would be found in shallow grave if he was ever found. He put his face back down into the floor, not watching as Patty was taken away. Jake took Patty, she was past screaming and yelling. It wouldn’t do any good with this one. He wasn’t one of the therapists or interventionists; he was simply running an errand. She sat silently all the way home where she was forced into the “loving” arms of her family. He didn’t even stay for the reunion, leaving to pursue his own thoughts and doubts. As he drove around, he was faced with the sad truth. She was a junkie, running after the broken dreams she found in the pipe or burnt foil. She had nothing to do with his brother, and Sid had never been involved. Bishop Grahmann’s contacts recognized the symbol and rightfully called him in, but he ignored every other sign. However they got it didn’t matter, this was just an old story he got caught in. Patty had a history of drug use, and she had disappeared before. Hector was a low life piece of scum, and Sid would never be involved directly with someone like him. The apartment being a shit hole wasn’t because anything had happened, but because she lived like a damn dog. He had been living in the battle between good and evil with Sid for so long he jumped to the conclusion that was least likely. He needed to forget everything, just for a little while. He was sick of the demons and devils and angels and all of it. Jake pulled into the valet drive of his brother’s Pitt Stop Grille. He handed the vehicle off to the valet and walked inside. He got waives and nods from the staff as he walked into the bar. His jeans and black button up didn’t matter because the dress code never applied to the brothers. “Jake, what can I get for you?” The bartender asked. “Whiskey, double.” Jake thought about it for a moment. “Bring the bottle.” He had been moderating his intake, but now he realized just how out of control he had been as of late. This was not a time to be restrained. Halfway through the bottle the memories and pain were finally numbed enough. He retreated to the office, and finally slept without nightmares and worries. Something Real The next day’s sunlight streaming through the windows awoke him, and made him aware of the splitting pain and tension in his entire skull. The hangover was not going to be nice. He pushed himself up off the pool of drool on the desk, and saw the empty whiskey bottle and the half empty scotch bottle. No wonder… Jake remembered it all now, and he needed something real to distract him. Unfortunately, the reality was the nasty taste in his mouth as he smacked he tasted a mix of puke, liquor and nasty he could have done without. Ignoring the retched flavor, he pulled his cell phone out from his jeans pocket and dialed his manager, John “Hawk” Peters. His friend picked up after five rings. “Jake!” The loud sound actually caused him to shake in pain. “Take it down man, I have a serious headache.” “You sound in the bag.” Hawk commented. “Slowly climbing back out of it.” Jake grabbed the scotch bottle and took a shot, the hair of the dog his only hope for the headache and the horrid taste. “I need a favor.” “What is it?” Hawk would do what he could for his friend and client. “I need a match.” “They’re all booked this week man.” Hawk wondered why he was so depressed. “Look, just find me a fight or a match anywhere. I don’t care anymore; I just need to be in a ring. I just need…to hit someone.” Jake wanted to find that moment inside the ring when everything else disappeared, when every time he hit someone it was release, and every time he was hit it was atonement for what he had done. “Well, PTC is having the final Extreme Title Match ever. You could jump into that mix if you wanted.” “Extreme Title Match?” “Yeah, last one. All sorts of crazy things there my friend. Some type of unification after this, so it’s sure to attract lots of people to knock heads with Big Dawg.” Punishment would be rampant, and that might cleanse him in some ancient way. It was bullshit, but he needed to hand his hat on something…anything right then. “Fine, book me and send me the details.” He hung up, and another shot helped carry away some of the dark afterglow from the previous night.
