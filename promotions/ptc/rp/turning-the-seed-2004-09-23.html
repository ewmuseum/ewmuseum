Turning the Seed

23 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-23 A wise man once said, find something you enjoy doing, and keep doing it; whatever it is, if it makes you happy, it can only do good things for you. For some people, it means following a dream of being a writer, an adventurer, a doctor, a pilot, a baseball player, on down the line. For the Latch Key Kid, it means being a professional wrestler. Another wise man once said, if you dig what you do enough, it’ll never get you down. And wrestling never got the Latch Key Kid down. No, throughout it all, the good times and the bad, Latch kept on digging what he was doing, and he always loved wrestling. Losing, however, is a different story. Losing got him down a fair bit. But even losing isn’t all bad; not if you learn from your mistakes and work to correct them for next time. He preferred to think of losing as a bump in the road, a simple obstacle to either swerve and dodge, or drive straight over. He usually chose to drive straight over it. A first round loss in the GTT4 had Latch down plenty. It also had him doubting his worth as a singles wrestler. Here he was without the partner he had known for the past two years. His best friend, gone, thanks to those pesky brats in XUDF. Maybe Nicole shouldn’t have even been in the Lunchroom Patrol locker room, but that was beside the point. Doughboy was gone, at least for the moment, and there was nothing Latch could do about it. He had to push it towards the back of his mind, no matter how much it resisted. But Latch learned from his first round loss. He realized what he had done wrong and what it would take to correct that. And he set out to do just that. When he received word of the play-in that had been offered to him, he jumped at the chance. Another chance to show the world what he was made of. Another chance to prove that he really did belong, that he could cut it as a singles wrestler. He needed to prove this not just to the fans, but also to himself. And so, he kept on doing what he was digging. Wrestling. ------------------------ Frank had spent most of the morning cleaning up; wiping off tables, washing dishes, lining up barstools and chairs, sweeping. Satisfied with the look of the place, he sits down to take a break and enjoy a cup of coffee. He turns on the TV and catches word of Ivan, Doctor Curiosity’s monstrosity, coming back to life and heading towards the coast of Texas. Galveston Island, the Latch Key Kid’s hometown, appeared to be a direct target for a large amount of rain and wind. Latch would not be happy about this. Frank turns the channel to get the previous night’s sports scores. A few moments later, the door opens, and in walks the Latch Key Kid. Frank hops to his feet. “Good morning, Brad,” Frank says, pulling out an empty mug for Latch. “Hey, Frank.” “You’re in here early. What’s the occasion?” “Well, Frank, I always say it’s better to be early than late. And I wouldn’t want to be late for my second day on the job,” Latch says with a smile, accepting the latte from Frank and taking a sip of it. “Your…second day, Brad? But yesterday was just a temporary thing, since I was shorthanded, and needed you to fill in. I appreciate your help, though.” “You know I’m always willing to help out, Frank. That’s why - ” “But, Brad, it’s okay. I talked to Betsy last night. She’s going to be here in a little while.” “Actually, Frank,” Latch says with a sly grin, as he sets his mug on the counter and puts on an apron, “I called her this morning and told her to take the day off. I figured she could use another day to get over whatever it is that was bothering her.” “You called Betsy? And told her not to come in? But why’d you do that?” “Well, that’s what good teammates do, Frank. They take care of the other teammates, right?” Latch picks his mug up and walks behind the counter, leaving Frank standing on the other side. “That’s right. But, Brad, you really didn’t have to…I just, well, I feel bad now for leaving you here yesterday to work by yourself. Now that I see how into it you are, I can’t help but feel responsible for pushing you along like this. It wasn’t fair of me to do that.” Latch frowns, and puts his mug back on the counter. “Look, Frank, don’t be so hard on yourself. You’re a great boss, and I love working for you.” “That’s just it. You don’t work for me. I’m not even paying you.” “Okay, fair enough. Working for you was a bad way of putting it. I should’ve said working with you. And I don’t care about money, Frank. I look at this as a way to help you out and a way to have a little fun. Besides, it helped me out yesterday when I needed something to pick me up. Just my way of thanking you.” “But Betsy,” Frank protests, “needs the money, Brad. She wasn’t upset about this?” “Not when I told her that I’d give her what she usually makes here. See, I figured, if I’m going to take over her shift for the day, the least I can do is compensate her for it.” “I have to admit, I’m a bit confused by all this. You want to work for her, that’s fine with me. You want to pay her, that’s fine with me, too. But why? That’s what I don’t understand.” “It’s all about being part of the team, right, Frank? You call your employees your teammates, so I figured this would be a great way to meet a few people and maybe get to feel like I’m part of the team.” “This is because the Lunchroom Patrol fell apart, isn’t it? That’s why you’re doing this? But you know, you don’t really need to. Things are fine here without you. I don’t want you to feel like you’re wasting your time around here.” “Nonsense. I want to do what I can to help the team. I may not know much about working in a place like that, but I’m sure I can teach these kids something. I just want to help try to make the team a little stronger, Frank. After all, it’s important to have a strong team, and a strong team can accomplish great things. Believe me, after being part of the Lunchroom Patrol for so long, I know first-hand.” “You’re absolutely right, Brad. A strong team is important. And I appreciate you wanting to help out around here. I’m sure you probably can help the team grow a little. But it doesn’t seem like serving coffee and wrestling have much in common.” “Oh, they do, Frank. See, with each one, you have an opponent. In wrestling, it’s people like the Shadow, DarkSeid, Wolfson, and the other miscreants in XUW. Here at the coffee shop, the opponents are things like fatigue, studying for exams, and caffeine withdrawals. In wrestling, you hit your opponents with things like body slams, clotheslines, and piledrivers. Here, you hit your opponents with things like lattes, espressos, and triple cappuccinos. And, just like in wrestling, the more force you hit your opponents with, the better chance you have of winning.” “No offense, Brad, but that makes about as much sense as the jokes you usually tell.” “Think about it, Frank. And look around. How many kids do you see sleeping in these chairs? None. How many are studying, or reading, or playing chess? Everybody.” He pauses. “Well, except for those two in the back corner, and they don’t really…Hey!” Latch yells at them and throws a towel that hits the wall. “Sorry. As I was saying, you don’t see anybody being lazy in here. That means you’re doing your job, Frank. And so are Bill, Nancy and Betsy. Well, when she’s here, that is.” “And I guess that means you’re doing your job too, Brad. Interesting little twist you put to that. I don’t think I’ve ever heard anybody compare slinging coffee with wrestling, but hey, you made it work. Sort of. But there’s one more thing that I don’t really understand.” Frank points to a barstool. “If I may?” Latch nods and Frank sits down. “This all makes a bit of sense, oddly enough. But I thought you want to focus on your singles career for the moment. Without Doughboy around, you’re on your own. You should be working towards building yourself up individually, not necessarily focusing on the team aspect of things.” Latch smiles. “Frank, Frank, Frank. A team is only as strong as its weakest link, right? That is, in order to have a strong team, each individual needs to be strong. So, while Doughboy’s out of action, and I’m on my own, I will work on building myself up as a singles wrestler. I'm going to work on making myself stronger as an individual. And I’m going to do that by focusing on this tournament and what lies ahead in the near future in singles wrestling. So you see, it’s possible to focus on both a team aspect and an individual aspect at the same time. They’re directly related, Frank.” Frank scratches his chin and furrows his brow. “Okay, look. When Betsy, Nancy and Bill are here, they work well together, right?” Frank nods. “Now what happens when, say, Betsy calls in sick? Do Nancy and Bill still work well together?” Frank nods again. “Of course. Because then two people have to do the work of three.” “Right. The loss of one of your teammates causes the others to work that much harder. If Bill and Nancy weren’t strong individually, do you think they’d be able to make up for Betsy’s absence?” “I suppose not. I see where you’re going with this.” “It’s like I was saying earlier. Teamwork requires strong team members. So, if I concentrate on singles wrestling while Doughboy’s gone, then theoretically, our team should be that much stronger when he does come back. Even if we never wrestle another match as a team.” “And you think participating in this tournament will help you become stronger?” “Well, it sure can’t hurt anything. I don’t see why it wouldn’t help me. It’s been so long since I’ve wrestled a true singles match that I’ve gotten out of the swing of things. A tournament like this is just what I needed. New opponents, new experiences, a new beginning. Yes, I do think this tournament will help me become stronger.” “Well, I think it’s safe to say you’ve got the right attitude about all this.” As Latch turns his back to the counter to clean up in the area behind it, the door opens and Katherine walks in. Frank calls out to her as she turns to hang her coat up on a hook by the door. “Morning, Katherine! How’re you doing?” “Great, Frank. And yourself? Let me have a latte, if you would be so kind.” Frank nods to Latch and puts his finger up to his mouth, as Katherine still has not seen Latch behind the counter. She walks up to the counter as Latch still has his back to it, pouring the latte. She sits next to Frank on a barstool and turns towards him and begins talking. Latch sets the mug down and interrupts her. “Here’s your coffee, Katherine.” Katherine is still looking at Frank. “How’d you know my - ” She turns and sees Latch standing there, smiling. “What are you doing behind the counter? And with an apron on? Frank’s not making you work, is he?” “Nope, he’s not making me. He’s letting me.” “I don’t understand, I’m afraid.” “I wanted to work here today. Just like yesterday.” “Yesterday?” Frank jumps in. “You didn’t tell her?” “Well, I didn’t see her yesterday, and I wanted it to be a surprise. So, uhm, surprise!” “I still don’t understand.” “Well, yesterday, Frank wanted to test me out and let me see that I could do something I wasn’t used to. So he left me here by myself to work this place for a few hours. And I had so much fun with it, I decided to come back today.” “And why exactly are you doing this?” Latch sighs. “It’s a long story.” Katherine looks at her watch, and then takes a sip of coffee. “I’ve got time. I’m not going anywhere.” Latch sighs again. “Okay, it’s like this. You know how a team is only as strong as…” Latch’s voice fades out, and we see the clock moving ahead in fast forward motion as people enter and exit the coffee shop, as Latch pours coffee and cleans up, and chats back and forth with Katherine. Finally, the clock stops and all goes back to normal. “…and that is why I decided to come back today.” Katherine looks quizzically at Frank. “And you’re okay with this?” Frank shrugs. “I’ve never been one to turn down good, quality help. Especially when I don’t have to pay for it.” Katherine turns to Latch. “You’re okay with this, Brad?” “Of course. I wouldn’t be here if I weren’t okay with it. In fact, this has taught me a few things. The meaning of teamwork from a different perspective, the fact that I can do something different if I set my mind to it, and that I can accomplish something if I set my mind to it. Frank’s a great guy to work for. In fact, if he doesn’t think I’ve screwed things up too much, I just might come back here again sometime.” Katherine looks at Frank. “Frank?” “Uhm…well, sure. That would be great. I’m sure we’d love to have you back sometime. But shouldn’t you be worrying more about the tournament than about coming back to work for me?” “I guess you have a point, Frank. Maybe I should get going.” Latch looks at Katherine, who nods. He takes off the apron and puts it on the counter, and then shakes Frank’s hand. “Thanks again, Frank. I had fun, and I appreciate you giving me this chance.” He walks around the counter and takes Katherine’s hand. “What do you say we blow this joint?” She smiles. “Sounds good to me.” Latch and Katherine head for the door, and she walks out first. Latch turns around and looks at Frank. Frank gives him a slight nod and a thumbs up, and Latch turns and walks out of the coffee shop.
