A Second Chance

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-14 No one should ever be denied a second chance. We all have one live to live, one chance to achieve bliss. Happiness can come in many forms. To some happiness is the fulfillment of material needs. You study for years to earn a degree to get the career that will pay dividends in the form of a house, sports car, and maybe also a beautiful wife. To others happiness is the ultimate relationship you find yourself in, the kind where the term sacrifice is meaningless. It is the unconditional love between parent and child, husband and wife – the kind that transcends all known rules and boundaries. Abigail Moriarty believed she knew what her happiness was. That was before it was abruptly taken away from her in a doctor’s office. Here she was again in a familiar scene hoping that the outcome would be different this time. Walking to the center of the doctor’s office, Abigail found herself rubbing her thumb against her wedding ring. She had tortured herself keeping the knowledge of her husband’s affair with another woman a secret. It had ravaged her mind with ethical questions. But in the end Abigail had decided that she loved Wolfgang more than she hated him and that was enough to attempt a second chance at true happiness. The door opened behind her barely making any noise except for the turning of the bronze knob. The doctor walked in with her wavy, red hair covering the right side of her face, which was buried into a clipboard. She walked past Abigail and sat at her desk, oblivious to everything in the room. "Excuse me," Abigail called out, a little tense. "Oh, sweet spirits!" the doctor shrieked. Abigail approached her with a weak grin. This was unusual for her. "We need to talk, Karla." Karla pushed aside her clipboard and grinned at Abigail from ear to ear. "I believe the person you need to talk to is your husband. Or did you do that already?" Abigail shook her head. "Oh, you are a piece of work," Karla mocked her. "He’s out there putting his cock into another woman’s love hole, and you remain the quiet little nun you’ve been your entire life." "Fuck you," Abigail snapped back. It came out as a whimper. "He did." Abigail rolled her eyes, "I know. It was so nice of you to send me those video tapes." Karla shrugged as if she had done nothing wrong, "I only offered you a chance to regain your marriage. Is that why you’re here? Are you here to ask me to leave Wolfgang alone?" Abigail hesitated with her answer. If it were really that simple to make all of her agony go away, then she would merely ask. However, she had known Karla for far too long to believe that anything she said could be taken at face value. "I’m not here for that." "Good," Karla said, "it would have been too easy for you." "I’m not here for Wolfgang at all." "Oh?" Karla found her curiosity piqued. "Well, it does concern him," Abigail explained, "but not in the way you intend it to. I’m here because, ironically, you’re the only one who can help me." Karla eyed Abigail up and down molesting her body with her eyes. She licked her lips waiting for the plea. "What is it?" "I want to have a child." "Don’t we all?" "I’m serious." "Tell your husband that," Karla said, "or is he the problem? Is he too tired when he comes home late at night? Can’t get the job done?" "You know what the fucking problem is!" Abigail snapped. "You know I’ve been diagnosed as sterile!" Karla giggled, "And what do you want me to do about it?" "I want…I need you to help me," Abigail answered. "That is what you do here isn’t it? You give people a second chance." Karla nodded, "That is the name of my hospital, yes – A Second Chance. We are a revolutionary hospital filled with the most advanced techniques in medical care and technology. Some of the more traditional anchors in our community find us a bit intimidating." "I know," Abigail continued on, showing that she had done her research. "You have a lot of untested methods. A lot of the other hospitals want you to shut down until you’ve conformed to federal standards." "We’ve already conformed to federal fucking standards," Karla snarled, viciously smacking the clipboard off her desk. "Those simple fools only want me to shut down because I tell their patients they have alternatives when those buffoons would rather have them addicted to medication and pills to fatten their wallets." "Then you can help me?" "I have the possibilities, yes." "Then help me." Karla stood up from her desk and walked over to Abigail circling her, judging her. "You’re either very stupid, or very determined." "I believe I’m very determined." "Why?" Karla asked, peering at Abigail’s eyes like a curious little kitten. "I have to be to find true happiness." "That is what a child will bring to you?" Abigail nodded, "I’m prepared to give you anything you want." Karla laughed robustly, "Sweetie, you are in no position to offer that kind of reward. I’ve already tasted what is yours." It took all of Abigail’s strength not to slap Karla. She repeated, "You can have anything." Karla ran her silky smooth hand down Abigail’s cheeks and caressed her body from her face down to her thighs. She leaned in and sniffed her citrus shampooed hair longingly. She whispered into her ear, "I suppose I could always taste the other half of the pie." Abigail flinched and pushed Karla away from her. Karla laughed. "I can have anything?" Abigail hesitated, "Yes." Karla turned back to her desk, "Come back tomorrow. I’ll have my conditions then." Abigail nodded and turned back to the door. Her slow walk soon erupted into a breakneck pace, akin to a videotape being fast forwarded. With a flash of light Abigail was back in the office. She was not wearing bell bottoms and a blouse, but a skirt this time. Karla presented to her a small vial filled with a milky substance, "Do you know what this is?" "A miracle," Abigail replied, her eyes fixated on the vial. "It’s science," Karla corrected her. "Here is the second chance you’ve been waiting for." "I can’t believe it’s this simple." Abigail replied, joyously. "It wasn’t." "What do you mean?" "It’s taken years for me to perfect this, "Karla explained, "The side effects are still unknown. It will work in the same principle as in vitro. You’ve heard of that, right?" "Yes." "I can’t guarantee any side effects, but I can guarantee that you will have a child that will be yours." Abigail embraced Karla in a tight hug, thanking her over and over. "I don’t do this often and yours is the first case I’ve done in two years," Karla said. "I want you to know that this vial here is the only one of its kind." "I can’t thank you enough." Abigail repeated through her tears. "You can have anything you want." Karla smirked, "Yes…about my reward…" With another flicker of light, darkness subdued the scene, shrouding it in silence. At her desk, Karla extended her hand to the DVD drive of her laptop to receive the recorded video she was just enjoying. She put the disc gingerly into a case, rotating it between her fingers, "Yes…what about that reward? We’re running a little late, aren’t we?" "But no matter," she muttered to herself, as she locked the disc in the upper drawer of her desk. "Everyone pays their dues in the end, you were no different." Karla’s smirk turned to full sinister smile. In her hands, she held a vial with a milky substance. The label on it read, "Abigail Moriarty." "I think it’s time our beloved Wolfgang knew the truth." A Dinner Date With a few nights left before the third round of GTT6 (and a date with Kimbusa), Wolfgang Moriarty – alias, Sentinel – met with his daughter, Angela, to enjoy their first dinner together since relocating to the site of that copious event. The dinner table was decorated with a dark blue linen cloth and a glass vase in the middle filled with tulips and daises. Angela had taken it upon herself to set the table according to her mother’s favorites, or what she believed them to be. She added personal touch in the form of ivory dinner plates with painted flowery designs and gold trims. Wolfgang led the table in a prayer before settling down to enjoy the course that was before him. Working through most of the afternoon, Wolfgang had prepared mash potatoes, coleslaw, corn, fried chicken, and sweet potato. For desert, Angela had tried her first attempt at baking, successfully conjuring a vanilla cake with thick frosting. Silence covered the table like a blanket as neither of them was confident to spark any discussions. Ironically, both were entranced in deep thoughts for events that had transpired the past couple of weeks. For Wolfgang, the relocation to the GTT site had been anything short of pleasant. His initial purpose was to win GTT6 or at least have a favorable showing. He never once believed that the past would come back to haunt him as Karla slithered her way back into his life. Angela took a bite from the crispy fried chicken, savoring the delicious taste. She was enjoying the food, but her mind was elsewhere. She kept glancing over at her father wondering if he was ever going to explain what she had found in the black box. Angela had never told him that she had found the pictures in the hopes that he would tell her himself. As usual, Wolfgang had remained secretive and Angela could no longer keep quiet. She swallowed the last of the chewed meat in her mouth, "Tell me about mom." Wolfgang smiled. He wondered about the random outburst, but welcomed any chance to talk about Abigail, "She was a wonderful woman." Angela didn’t look up from the fried chicken leg she was enjoying, "I mean tell me something you’ve never told me before, something bad. Did she smoke? Did she ever get sick?" "I suppose she had her bad moments like anyone else," Wolfgang answered. "She got mad a few times, mostly at me. I’ve never seen her pick up a cigarette, but she did enjoy wine and champagne every now and then." "No…" Angela clarified herself, "I meant…was she ever…sick" The emphasis Angela put on the word sick rattled Wolfgang. "I don’t know what you mean." Angela finished biting off the last piece of meat on her chicken leg before picking up another one. She kept her head fixated on the food. It was not the food that kept her attention; she was seething with anger over the mysteries she had uncovered in her father’s little black box and sitting at the table enduring her father’s lies. "Did she have any diseases? Was there something she couldn’t do?" Wolfgang shook his head. He peered at Angela bewilderedly. "She was perfectly healthy." "Then why the hell did she leave us," Angela shouted, tossing her chicken leg down on her desk, making her mash potatoes fly across the dinner table. "Watch it," Wolfgang warned her. "I don’t know why she left us. I keep asking that same question every night." "Then maybe you need to ask yourself different questions," Angela cried out, her voice cracking. "What the hell are you talking about?" "Who was that lady that was with you the other night?" "I told you, I never met her before." Angela took her plate, still with its mouthwatering food, and tossed it across the room. The dinner plate shattered when it hit the wall. "Stop fucking lying to me!" she pleaded. Wolfgang was infuriated. He stood up and walked over to Angela. "I’m warning you to watch your tone!" Angela looked up at her father defiantly. "Where did you go the other night?" "I told you I had to go to work." "Stop lying!" she screamed again. "I’m not going to tell you to watch your tone with me again," Wolfgang warned her. "What the hell is going on with you?" "You don’t even know do you!" Angela cried, no longer fighting back her tears. "Know what?" "Who was that lady?" "No one!" "Did she have something to do with mom?" "No!" "Who’s Brian Arson?" Wolfgang scratched his head. "Honey, Brian Arson is…he’s nobody!" Angela stood up. "Why can’t you ever tell me the truth?" "I’m telling you the truth. You better calm down or I’ll…" "What?" Angela mocked, tearfully. "You’ll hit me?" "Angela…" "You had an affair with that woman didn’t you?!" "No." "She’s the reason mom left, isn’t she?" "No!" Angela shook her head, "I can’t believe you still lying to me. I know you know her. I know you did something with her. I’m begging you tell me the truth." "Angela," Wolfgang explained, "I swear to you nothing-" "I fucking hate you," Angela. "You cheated on mom with that woman, that’s why she left you. Isn’t it? You weren’t man enough to own up to your mistakes back then, and you aren’t man enough now-" Wolfgang backhanded Angela across her face. She fell back, smacking her head on the hard side of the sofa before falling to the ground in a slump. It took Wolfgang a moment to realize what he had just done. He stared at his hand still unconvinced that he had just slapped his daughter. On the ground, Angela slowly rose back to her feet and touched her lips. The smack was hard enough to cut her upper lip and blood dripped to the floor. "Angela…" "Get away from me," Angela pleaded, fear in her voice. "I don’t want you anywhere near me." As Wolfgang advanced, Angela stumbled backwards, running to her room. "Angela." Wolfgang called after her. But Angela ignored him as she slammed her bedroom door shut. Wolfgang stood at the door and wondered if he was too late in telling her the truth. It was obvious that Angela knew much more than he gave her credit for. How much she did know, he couldn’t say. "Angela, we need to talk." Wolfgang pressed his ear against the door, silently testing the lock on the door. It was locked. There was silence in the room. "I’m sorry for hitting you. You know I love you and that was a mistake. I know there’s a lot you want to know, and I’m sorry for not telling you earlier." Again Wolfgang listened and still no response came from the room. "I lied about not knowing the woman. I did know her for a long time; her name is Karla. But I swear to you that she had nothing to do with your mother. Let me in and I’ll tell you everything you need to know." He tested the doorknob and listened in, but silence still covered the room like a dense fog. It bothered Wolfgang. The silence was too much to bear. He expected to hear Angela crying in the very least. He banged on the door, rattling it from its hinges. Wolfgang was convinced that something had happened and kicked the door open. Walking into the room, Wolfgang looked at the open window and sighed at the obviousness of the truth: Angela had run away again. The Belladonnas The old abandoned church building was not just the site of Cassidy’s reach out program, the Seconda Vita. It was his home. In order to maintain the pledge he had set for the program, he needed to keep the site as modest and common as possible to avoid business leeches that wanted to partake in his efforts. Money wasn’t the reason he set up an organization to help underprivileged kids get a second chance in life. The whole purpose of Seconda Vita was to give them all a chance, just like he had received five years ago. Cassidy Jones walked around the perimeter of the background with a pitcher of water in his hands. He used the Seconda Vita to teach a lot of lessons, one of which was the concept of life. These were kids who were once borderline suicidal for whatever reasons – orphaned, incurable diseases, or tragic eyewitness accounts. Cassidy had set up a greenhouse for them in the backroom and given them the opportunity to take care of plants. The room became a nursery filled with angiosperms, ferns, mosses, and small fruit trees, which would be transported once they were big enough. He hummed to himself, keeping himself occupied during his late night chores. The rest of the children were still asleep on the top floor. There was only him in the room and the sound of plant life, until he heard the front door open. The door was locked and there was only one person that Cassidy knew had a key. What the hell is she doing here? Cassidy asked himself as he set the pitcher at the end of the table. He ran straight to the front of the building, making sure that he did not wake any of the other children. She stood in the living room, her green dress drenched from the rain. "Why don’t you ever buy an umbrella?" Cassidy asked. "And deny myself of nature’s beauty?" "You could get sick, Karla." Karla Segur smiled and put her hand on Cassidy’s face, "Are you concerned for my health, sugar?" Cassidy turned away nervously, "Please…not tonight." Karla advanced, "What’s the matter? You a little too tired to keep up?" "I don’t think this is right." "Benjamin Braddock didn’t complain." Cassidy didn’t say anything. "It’s just as well," Karla said. "Where is she?" "Who?" "Don’t be coy with me," Karla warned him. "Where’s Angela?" "She’s not here." "I thought you told me you had succeeded." "It’s taking longer than I thought." Karla fumed, "Longer? I’ve waited fifteen fucking years for this!" "Please calm down." Cassidy whispered. "Everyone is still sleeping." Karla eyed him up and down like a predator filled with disgust for its food, "You better deliver soon, or do I need to remind you of your parents?" Cassidy shook his head. His prayers were answered with a knock on the door. "Cassidy?" It was Angela. "You have to leave," Cassidy told Karla. "She can’t see you here." Karla smirked, "I don’t know. She and I will have a very interesting conversation." "Please," Cassidy begged, "she needs to be able to trust me." "Fine, but I’ll need a payment. You know what I want." Cassidy gulped, "Yes. The boy, Jimmy, he has good eyes." Karla leaned in and cupped Cassidy’s face with her hands. She leaned in and kissed him passionately, reducing his body to pudding. She whispered into his ear, "You better pray on your parents’ charred bodies you don’t fuck me over." Cassidy waited for Karla to leave through the back before opening the door for Angela. Like Karla before, Angela was drenched. Cassidy immediately noticed that the rain was not the only thing that had covered Angela. "What happened to you?" Angela wiped her tears, "It was my dad." Cassidy drew a handkerchief from his pocket and wiped the blood from Angela’s lip, "that’s fucking absurd." "I’m sorry." "For what?" Cassidy asked, confused. "He should be sorry for this. I’m going to call the police and have him arrested." Cassidy flipped his cell open, but Angela snatched it away from him. "Please, don’t." "Why? He has to be reported for this." "It’s not going to help me." "Then, what do you want?" Cassidy asked her. "I want to be here." "Here?" Angela looked around, "I want to be part of Seconda Vita." Cassidy smiled. Hidden deep inside that smile was a sinister plot. "You want to help me, or do you want to be a part of it?" "I want to be a part of it," Angela answered, somehow unconvinced that she was doing the right thing. "I need answers, Cassidy. I need the answers my father refuses to tell me." "I can’t guarantee you’ll find what you’re looking for," Cassidy explained. He then smiled toothily when an idea came to him. "Come with me." Cassidy led Angela to the nursery. Angela looked around the room with wide eyes, like a kid in chocolate factory. "This is incredibly beautiful." "I agree," Cassidy said, staring at her body. "Did you do all this?" Angela asked. "No," Cassidy answered, "I started this, but the children did the rest. Everyone has a plant or two they take care of." Angela walked over to the flowering plants. She felt euphoric smelling the wonderful scents of roses, lilacs, and tulips. "They all smell so great. The colors are just so vibrant!" Cassidy blushed a little bit, "it’s just proper care. Honestly, the kids deserve most of the credit. I can give you a spot to grow your flower." "This is amazing." "It is nature." "No," Angela clarified herself, "I mean Seconda Vita is amazing. It’s so organized." "What do you mean?" "When I was here with you the other day, I noticed that everyone was so unified. Wherever we went, everyone was doing the same activities and no one was complaining. It was like they all had one mind." Cassidy chuckled. If only she knew how close she was to the truth, he thought. "What is that?" Angela exclaimed gleefully, pointing behind Cassidy. Cassidy turned around to face the only section of the nursery that was isolated, enclosed in a glass perimeter. Inside the cell were exotic plants and ferns, which were much more glamorous then the ones on the outside. Angela pressed her hands against the glass wall, "What are these?" "These are very special plants," Cassidy said. He wasn’t far from the truth. "Why are they kept in there?" "They need special care," Cassidy explained. Angela pointed to a row of flowerless plants that were kept at a great distance from the rest, "What are those?" "They’re called belladonnas. They’re very special." "What do they do?" Angela asked. Cassidy smiled, "They make your dreams come true." Angela stared at the row of belladonnas wondered if it was true. She hoped it was. "There’s an empty pot with the belladonnas, did you lose one?" "Oh," Cassidy said, his attention drawn to the empty pot. "No, I sold that to a very special person." "I’m convinced I want to be here," Angela said. "You’re more than welcome to be a part of this." Angela embraced Cassidy in a tight hug and thanked him over and over again. Cassidy returned the hug and smiled knowing that he was about to succeed. He then looked up and saw that he was not the only smiling. Peering in through the window was Karla, her red hair matted down against her head, smiling. The Black Box Wolfgang sat on the edge of his bed. He buried his face in his hands, cursing himself for what had happened. He convinced himself earlier that Angela would be back. She had left everything she owned in the room and the black Land Rover was still in the parking lot. It would have been a good idea to run after her, but Wolfgang still needed time to think about what he was going to say. But something still troubled him. During their dinner, Angela had shown that she knew a lot more than what he thought. Somehow she knew that he was lying about never having met Karla before. That wasn’t the most unusual of questions, however. Wolfgang surmised that Karla probably already spoke with Angela. Oh dear God, Wolfgang thought. He hoped that wasn’t the case. He didn’t know what Karla wanted with Angela or what that she-devil would say to Angela. If she had spoken with Angela…no, Wolfgang didn’t want to think about it. There was still much more that needed to be explaining. Angela’s questioning of her mother’s health troubled Wolfgang. He ran his fingers through his black hair grasping small handfuls trying to figure out a solution. The solution came to him in the form of an unlocked closet. His eyes traced the opening of the door down to the black box. The box had been moved from its original state and a piece of paper sticking out from the lid indicated that it had been opened as well. Jesus fucking Christ, Wolfgang muttered. He knew then what had happened. Wolfgang put on his black, white, and red leather jacket and raced down the stairs. He yanked open the door and stumbled back surprising both himself and the guest standing at his door. "Are you ok?" "Chuck!" Wolfgang cried out, surprised to see the AIWF President. Charles Stuart walked into the house, handing Wolfgang a bottle of wine. "I’m glad to see we were able to accommodate you so nicely." Wolfgang nodded. "Yes, this is the best place we’ve stayed at so far. Thanks." "Oh, don’t thank me," Charles replied, inviting himself to have a seat. "All of this was provided by the GTT committee." Wolfgang stored away the wine in the cupboard in the study, "I’m doing fine." Chuck looked at him with wondering eyes. He knew Wolfgang long enough to know when he was lying. This was one of those times were there was both truth and lies to his words. "I know you’re doing fine, Wolfgang. As the Sentinel, you’ve been pretty amazing in the tournament." "I don’t have a victory," Wolfgang pointed out. "But you’re still in the third round," Chuck replied, "Victory or not, people have been impressed with you. I admit it’s much more than I hoped for." "What are you talking about?" "You know I trust your abilities more than anyone," Chuck explained, "But I’ve never tested you against this sort of quality. I expected you to have a strong showing and gain some respect. This is unexpected in a good way." "I’m facing Kimbusa next," Wolfgang told him, rather abruptly. "That’s why we’re here," Chuck replied, smiling. "We?" Wolfgang asked. "A couple of the guys from the AIWF decided to tag along and show you some love. They figured you would need all the support they can give you now that the tournament is grinding down to its toughest rounds." "I can beat Kimbusa." Chuck nodded, "I know. He’s a bit slow, but I think we can train you for him." Wolfgang shook his head. "I don’t need that." "What?" "I know I can beat him," Wolfgang explained. "It goes beyond training. I’m fighting for fulfillment. He doesn’t know what that is." Chuck laughed. "He’s fucking huge, I’m pretty sure he knows the meaning of being full." Wolfgang chuckled, "No, I mean he doesn’t know what he’s fighting for. He doesn’t have that kind of love." "Like you do?" Wolfgang nodded. "I know he lost his father a long time ago. I fear he’s forgotten what that feels like. It’ll cost him the match." "Aye, I know how that feels," Charles replied, recalling his father dying from a heart attack ten years ago. His eyes shifted from the living room to the empty dinner table. "I’m sorry am I interrupting something?" "What?" Charles pointed to the empty dinner table. "Were you having company?" "No, I was just enjoying dinner with my daughter." Charles smiled widely. He adored Angela, calling her the granddaughter of the AIWF. Of course, no one ever told Angela of her nickname. Angela did not know that her father was the Sentinel. She did not know of the Sentinel’s existence. The Sentinel had bought Angela to his shows every night until she was about four years old and beginning to understand the world around her. When the Sentinel realized that, he realized it was time to keep the charade going on a little longer. "Where is Angela?" Charles asked. "I haven’t seen her in a long time." "She isn’t here," Wolfgang answered, not wanting to explain what had just happened. "It’s a shame," Charles said, "She was such a beautiful baby. Have you told her yet?" Wolfgang shook his head. Charles jumped to his feet, "You haven’t? You’ve been here for weeks now!" "I don’t know what to tell her." "I’m warning you in the best possible way. The longer you wait to tell her the truth, more she is going to both not accept it and hate you." "You’re right." "Of course I’m…" Charles replied, before stopping to look over at the empty dinner table. He then noticed the shattered plate on the floor in front of the wall and the mess the table was in. "What just happened, Wolfgang?" "She ran away." "WHAT?!" "She found out about Karla," Wolfgang sighed. "I don’t know how much she knows, but she found my black box." "Why aren’t you out there looking for her?" "I don’t know what to tell her." "You don’t know?" Charles snarled at him. "How about telling her the fucking truth? If you don’t do this now, you’re going to lose her." "If I do this now, I’m going to lose GTT6." Charles stood aghast. He couldn’t believe what he just heard. "Jesus fucking Christ, Wolfgang, what is more important to you: the tournament, or your daughter?"
