GOP, The College Years! (Part 3)

18 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-18 "Wow baby, I didn't know you had it in you." Maggie Meltzer looked at her boyfriend with admiration in her eyes gushing like water out of a firehose. "I… I didn't either." Gordon Powell was still shellshocked. Something had come over him to hop up on that bake sale table and preach the conservative gospel into that megaphone. In the past, he would have overthought the situation, wondered about how badly the black kids in the crowd would have kicked his ass, worried about offending his roommate. Nothing like that mattered to him at that point in time though. He shrugged the hard looks from the NAACP off like snow off the shoulder of a skier entering the lodge. His roommate even had a newfound respect for him, but if he didn't, Gordon wasn't about to care. This was something new for him. Maggie came right up to Gordon's side, moved her arm right inside of Gordon's and held his hand. His back didn't feel as stiff anymore. He relaxed. First thing in the morning that Monday, Gordon went over to the Campus Republicans' office. He walked in and found Jamey Bossy sitting right at the table in the small communal area, almost as if he was waiting for a visitor. "Hey Jamey…" "Gordon, I thought you might stop by." "Oh really?" Jamey nodded. "After what you did the other day, I thought it was only a matter of time before you came and joined our little group." Gordon's eyes opened wide, and he put his hand on his chin. "Your instincts are pretty good." "I know, and that's why I already have a position ready for you." Gordon took a step back, reaction to the shock of what he was hearing. Was he really planning this all out? For how long? Was Maggie involved? Was Maggie just using him? Gordon felt a strong urge to turn tail and walk out. He started to think retreating into a life of science without any political interaction whatsoever would be a good idea. Still though, he fought that urge. He hadn't heard the whole story yet. "What's the matter? You look distressed." "Yeah… oh, I'm sorry. It's just…" "I know what this looks like, and trust me, this ain't no elaborate plan to get you in our grasps. Not all Republicans are deceitful bastards." Gordon's expression loosened up a little bit. "If you don't want in now, I'll understand…" "No… no, I do want in." "Good then, you're our new spokesman." "Spokesman?" "Yes, Gordon. You're going to be speaking for us at all kinds of events. I mean, you gotta mighty fine gift there." "Oh… thanks." A small smile started to form on Gordon's face. "What does the spokesman do?" Jamey laughed to himself a bit. "He does what you did yesterday, Gordon. You're gonna speak for us at events. In fact, our first event's gonna be tomorrow, at the state house." "We're protesting at the state house? For what?" "Execution." "An execution? I thought the Republican party was for the death penalty?" "Oh, we are, we are. I should be more clear. We're protesting the Democrats' protest against the execution. It's a counterprotest." Gordon furrowed his brow. "But why are we doing that to the Democrats if we didn't like them doing that to us?" Jamey chuckled again. "You've been reading the Bible again, haven't you? It's alright, I know you're probably skeptical, but turnabout's fair play, right?" "Yeah, I guess…" "Exactly. And y'wanna know something? Yesterday wasn't the first time Brent Cowart and his little group tried to sabotage us like that. Every time we go out and do something public, he brings his group out and tries to cockblock us. That's the real reason why we don't do nothing on campus outside of keep to ourselves most of the time." "What a douchebag." "Well, yeah, he is. That's why I knew I needed to have you on board. You're the one person who can shut that turkey up. It's about time that we started shutting him up." Gordon nodded. Jamey smiled, but it was fleeting. A look of seriousness tinged with sadness replaced the brief smile. "Gordon, can I level with you?" He nodded. "I've never been a good speaker. I didn't get my position here because I'm a firebrand or because I even represent our party well. I worked my ass off to get to this position. I wouldn't be the kind of man who'd be able to handle the rigors of a public election. For all intensive purposes, I shouldn't be in this position. That's really why Brent's been able to have his way with us. That's why we really don't run events like we should, because we can't stand up for ourselves. But you… Gordon, you're a natural. You got personality in spades. You're our salvation." Gordon was torn between a 12 foot high tidal wave of euphoria and pity for Jamey drawing and quartering him. He stood silent. "Y'know, you'd think that in this great state of Texas, we'd be a flourishing group. But all college campuses, whether it's in Austin or Berkeley… they're all full of liberals. Liberals tainted by super-liberal faculty to go against what they were brought up to believe. That's why we need you, Gordon. Don't let us down." "I surely won't, Jamey. You can count on me." Most freshman classes at UT had two components: a large lecture part where the students filed into the auditorium like cattle on the Los Brazos trail and were taught by a venerable full-time professor (in the case of Organic Chemistry, that man was Dr. Harley) and smaller, subdivided recitation sessions taught by teaching assistants. Gordon went straight to his O-chem recitation from his meeting with Jamey. He walked into the classroom with a smile on his face and pep in his step. Suddenly though, a voice screeched his good vibes to a halt like a train pulling its emergency brake. "Oh look, here comes the racist." The words came out of the mouth of one Joe Kemp. Joe was Gordon's O-chem TA; he was slightly overweight with curly hair and haggard looking facial hair. "How does it feel being a part of the system holding the black man down?" Feeling emboldened to respond, Gordon sniped back. "It feels great. Really works my triceps." "Don't patronize me. That was quite the stunt you pulled yesterday. I never saw anything as hateful as what you were spewing yesterday." Oh yeah, Joe was a member of the Campus Democrats too. "Look dude, the stunt I pulled yesterday has nothing to do with organic chemistry or this class. If you have beef with me, let's just settle this afterwards, alright?" "Fine." Joe scowled as he stared a hole right through Gordon as he made his way to the back to sit next to Maggie. "Hey sugar…" "Hey babe… guess what?" "What?" "I just got back from joining the Republicans…" "Aww, sweetie, that's great." "Yeah, they made me spokesman…" Maggies eyes lit up. "Aww sugar, I'm so proud…" Their reparte was rudely interrupted. "Powell, Meltzer! Keep it down back there. I'm trying to teach." They piped down and recitation went down as scheduled. After the one hour class was over. Gordon and Maggie walked out of the classroom together, but Gordon's eyes were fixed on Joe, beckoning him to say something to him now that class was over. Joe just sat at the desk in the front of the classroom and stared back. Gordon turned back around to Maggie. "He didn't want any of me." "'Course not, sugar. He's not used to people standing up to him. None of the Democrats have." "I know. Jamey told me." The Austin night was crisp, but clear. There was a soft breeze in the air, noticeable, but not strong enough to cause problems for the Democrats holding their placards in protest of the execution of Troy "The Butcher" Carson. Brent led the charge on the megaphone. "The death penalty is barbaric! Why should we stoop to the level of these killers? We'd be no better than them! Think about the moral health of our society! Think of the children who will learn from this example! Think…" "Think of the families of the victims whose lives were viciously and unnecessarily taken away by this sociopath." That wasn't Brent's voice on the megaphone. That voice belonged to Gordon Powell on a megaphone of his own. Brent looked over at the contingient of Republicans, led by their new spokesman. He lowered his megaphone and went over to the throng of collegiate conservatives. "What in the hell do you think you're doing, Jamey?" "The same thing you've been doing to us for the last three and a half years, Brent. You know what they say about turnabout." "Oh fuck off. We protest your lame attempts at siding with the man because you're wrong and should be stopped. This is legitimate protest against a barbaric practice." "Brent, you know, you never did learn to take a look at the other side of the coin, have you? There's always two sides to an argument, and right now, I'm asserting my side." "Since when did you grow balls?" Brent almost immediately looked over at the man holding the megaphone after he said those words. "Nevermind. You seriously got that freshman indoctrinated." "And you all those other sheep you've got over there. Now are you gonna get out of my face and stop muffing up my counterprotest, or are you going to narc on us like you said you were going to do on Saturday?" For the rest of the night, Brent and Gordon took turns shouting out their rallying cries, like dueling banjos of political diatribe via megaphone. In the Democrats' camp, Joe Kemp stood with a shapely looking blond with utter contempt at Gordon on his proverbial soapbox. "That punk… Marissa, I swear, I can't believe how brazen that Powell is." "Honey, relax. He's just a punk freshman." "Yeah, but he's ruining our protest…" Marissa could only put her arm around Joe to console him. At 12:01, AM, word got out that Governor Bush didn't sign any last-minute stays of execution, and Troy Carson was executed via lethal injection. An audible groan could be heard from the Democrats' camp, but the groan wasn't answered with silence. "Lord, Troy Carson is in Your hands now. May You judge him correctly, and may You also give solace and closure to the families that Carson has wronged in his troubled time on this earth. May all his victims rest in peace, and may You be just in Your dealings with him. We ask this through Christ, our Lord." The crowd of Republicans answered "Amen." The Democrats groaned even louder. Gordon, Maggie and his roommate Jerry O'Quinn all walked back to the dorm room that Gordon and Jerry shared together, despite the fact that Jerry was part of the Democrats. They dropped Maggie off at her dorm, and then began the walk back to their room. "You're such a narc, man." "Yeah, and you're a murderer sympathizer." Jerry chuckled. "Man, I wonder if any other roommates are like us?" "Who knows, man. Who knows… hey… you really don't resent me because I was on the megaphone, do you?" Jerry looked up at his roommate with an earnest look on his face. "Naw man. I told you before. I respect you even if you are a narc. All this… all this is just window-dressing man. Nothing says that Dems and Republicans can't be friends, y'know?" "Yeah." "Hey… I'm actually hungry though." "What, we didn't make you lose your appetite?" "Ha… nah. I'm gonna go run to the Tofu Spot. I imagine you don't wanna come, man." "Nah. I'll catch you back at the room." Jerry jogged off the course and Gordon just continued to walk on. He broke a crisp stride, making good time getting back to his dorm room. He was tired from a night of rousing a counter rabble to the Democrats. He thought of his bed and sleeping, his mind at peace. That peace was broken very abruptly though as someone grabbed Gordon by the shoulder and yanked him against the wall of one of the dorms. "You!" Gordon got a look of his assailant. It was Joe Kemp. "You've got a lot of fucking nerve, you know that?" "I…" "Shut the fuck up, freshman. You think you're so hot because you came and ruined our rally. Well you know what? I don't appreciate it. Brent doesn't appreciate it. No one appreciates it. You fucker, you're going to pay…" "What… what are you gonna do Joe, kick my ass? Huh?" "No… no, what I'm gonna do… if you ever, EVER show your face at one of our events with your Republican butt-buddies like that and ruin our vibe like that… well, you know that term paper on conductive polymers that's due, next week, the one that's worth half your grade? Well, I'll make sure that Dr. Harley never gets it and that you fail O-chem. You understand me?" "What, you don't like it when you get your own medicine, so you're gonna fuck me over?" "I SAID DO YOU HEAR ME?" Gordon gulped. "You know I'll just go to Harley and have your ass reported." "Yeah? Oh yeah? I'm Dr. Harley's best TA. He trusts me with his life, and he'll believe me over some punk freshman. Now, answer my fucking question… NOW!" Gordon sighed then gave a forced nod. Joe let him down from the wall. He stared hard at Gordon, then turned and walked away. The next day, Gordon sat in the Campus Republicans office, reading a book on economics by Milton Friedman. He had a shellshocked look in his glazed-over eyes. "Hey, you've been reading that same page for an hour, Gordon." He was startled from his catatonia and looked up to see Jamey looking in his direction. "Something wrong?" "Oh, no… just soaking in Friedman. Brilliant… brilliant economist." "Yeah, that pencil analogy… hey, I just wanted to tell you that you kicked ass last night. I'm really proud of you." "Oh… thanks." "Don't mention it. We got another one coming up in a few days. They're gonna be protesting Representative Tex Schroder's office again, this time because of comments he made about Affirmative Action being bogus. That should be right up your alley since that's what you made your name on." "Yeah… but I don't know if I can go." Jamey looked shocked. "Oh… well why not?" "I… well, I might have some other engagement back home. My mom… is… she's a little sick." "Oh, well, if you can't go, you can't go, but hopefully, your mom's feeling a little better. We need you out there." "I know… I'm… I'm sorry." Jamey turned away from Gordon and walked back into his office. Later in the day, Gordon sat in his dorm room, reading the same book by Friedman he was in the office. This time, his catatonia was disrupted by a knock on his door. "Come in." Maggie Meltzer quietly came into the room. "Hey sugar." "Hi babe." "I just got done talking to Jamey. He said you might not make it to Tex Schroder's office." "Yeah. Mom's sick." Maggie looked into Gordon's eyes and noticed they were vacant like an inner city lot. "Something's wrong, isn't it?" He didn't answer. He slumped his shoulders and leaned into his girlfriend. "Sugar… I know you got things going on in your life. But we need you." "I know… I… I don't know." Maggie reached her arm around Gordon's limp shoulders and got in right next to him. "Look, whether it's your mom being sick or something else that's bugging you, I'll always be here for you." Gordon looked up into Maggie's deep hazel eyes. "It's Joe. He threatened to rip up my term paper." "Oh… well can't you go to Dr. Harley?" Gordon shook his head. "He's got pull, babe. I can't fail this class." Maggie held him a little tighter. "Sugar… grades come and grades go. You can always take the class again. But, you can't ever shake a regret for not doing the right thing." Gordon looked away from Maggie. His head slumped down. "Listen baby… whatever you do, I'll love you and support you. But you gotta make the right call for yourself." Gordon meekly rose his head up. "I don't know what the right answer is." A tear formed in Maggie's eye. She looked deeply into Gordon's eyes and laid him down on the bed, where they laid quietly for the rest of the night. The sun shone bright over Tex Schroder's office that Saturday morning. The Democrats were out en masse again, armed with their placards and Dunkin' Donuts coffee to wake them up. Brent stood up with his megaphone and barked out war cries to his fellow Democrats. The crowd was so big that the Republican contingient could see them from five blocks away. Jamey gulped as if he thought he was making a huge mistake. "You think he's gonna show, Jamey?" one of his fellow seniors turned to him and asked. "I don't know, but I hope to God he does." Jamey led his troops to the battle scene. They arrived to the protest site, all of them with unsure looks on their faces and silent as mimes, so silent that the Democrats didn't even notice them for two full minutes. Finally, Brent caught a glimpse of the new, unwanted additions to his crowd. He got up off his makeshift stage and made his way over to where Jamey stood. "So, you're finally here. I didn't notice you because you guys rolled up so quiet." Jamey didn't say a word. "Where's your head cheerleader." Suddenly, a muffled buzz came up from the rear. The crowd parted and through the throng of Republicans stood Gordon Powell, with a megaphone in his hand. He lifted the device to his face and pointed it right in Brent's grill. "I'm right here." Brent dropped his megaphone and held his ears from the loudness that was just poured into them. The Republicans cheered and Brent looked back with a scowl on his face. "Oh man, you dick." "Takes one to know one!" Gordon cheerily replied. It was on. Much like the rally at the State House, their dueling megaphones provided a truly fair and balanced protest session. After everything was broken up, Gordon made his way over to the Dems camp. He was looking for someone in particular, but he couldn't find him. Finally, he spotted his roommate. "Jerry… Jerry… you seen Joe Kemp around?" "Dude, I did, but I think he left like halfway through." "Shit." Gordon became almost limp. "Dude, you like alright?" "Yeah." Gordon started to walk back to the Democrats before he caught something out of the corner of his eye. A shapely blond woman, standing by herself, looking obviously pissed off. She looked vaguely familiar. He perked up as if he just had the most brilliant idea in the world pop into his head. He rushed over to Maggie, who was standing waiting for him. "Hey babe, I got an idea…" "What? About what?" "Trust me… but remember what we were talking about last Thursday, y'know, after we did it during the break between classes?" "Yeah sugar, but what does that have to do with anything?" He looked behind him at the blonde girl. "Everything." Joe Kemp walked down the hallway to Gordon Powell's dorm room like he was the cock of the walk. In one hand, he held a styrofoam cup of coffee, but in the other, he held the tattered and torn chances of Gordon passing O-chem in the form of a shredded term paper about conductive polymers. His smile was as wide as the Hoover Dam as he walked up to Gordon's room. He placed his coffee down and rapped on the door. It cracked open and Gordon answered in his boxer shorts. "What the hell do you want?" "Gordon, you son of a bitch, you thought I was playing, didn't you?" Joe held up the shredded remains of his term paper. "I have no idea how it happened, but this can't be turned into Dr. Harley." The door cracked open even more. Maggie appeared in the doorway, wearing a sheet around her naked body, hiding her goods. "What's going on here, sugar?" "Oh, Meltzer, I was just here to tell your boyfriend that his chances of passing O-chem are slim to none." He proudly shoved the tattered and shredded remains into Maggie's face. "Yeah, eat that. You shouldn't have crossed me, Gordon. You should have just stayed in your place. What do you have to say about that?" Gordon rubbed his chin, then snapped his head up and stared Joe right in the face. "You know what I have to say? Well, last night, I found something out. Turns out my little Republican angel here has a little secret. She's a little curious, y'know, she has some lesbian tendencies. At first, I was a little taken aback. Surely, I didn't want her to leave me for another woman, but then, I got my own ideas. Joe, what's every man's dream?" "Umm…" "Exactly, getting to have sex with two girls at the same time. Now, why I am I telling him this, babe?" "Well sugar, I think it's because you had sex with two women last night." "I did, didn't I? I had sex with you, and I also had sex with someone else last night. Who was it?" "I didn't get her name, sugar. I think Joe should meet her for himself." Gordon flung the door wide open, and there, standing right behind Gordon and Maggie was Marissa, Joe's girlfriend, wearing nothing but Gordon's XL Hulk Hogan Hulkamania t-shirt. "Hi Joe." Joe's mouth dropped and his face turned beet red. He went to say something, but the only thing that came out of his mouth was a rushed squeak. He balled his hand into a fist and threw a punch at Gordon's face, but Gordon caught it and twisted his wrist down. "Joe, just listen to what she has to say." Marissa walked forward into Joe's face. "I told you not to do what you were going to do. Hating this kid… no, man…" She looked back at Gordon and smiled. "Was one thing. I could live with that. But intentionally trying to ruin his academic career? I told you that was low. Lower than waht I thought you could even stoop. I don't wanna be associated with you anymore Joe. It's over." Gordon let go of Joe's fist and the TA stepped back in shock. "No… no." Gordon smirked. "Yes… yes." Joe shook his head violently. "Oh man… oh man… it doesn't matter. I… I still ruined your career man. There's plenty of fish in the sea… ones who aren't whores!" "And there are plenty of majors at this school. I don't have to graduate in four years." Joe was beside himself. "Fuck you man. Fuck you… you asshole. Fuck all you! YOU HEAR ME!" Joe kicked his coffee all over the hallway and stormed off. He left the shredded remains of Gordon's report on the floor. Gordon bent over and picked up the shredded and now coffee-stained remains. "You know, it's a good thing I bought those floppy disks so I don't have to retype this." "Do you really think Dr. Harley will let you rehand it in?" Maggie asked. "I think so. I've got you and Marissa here to corroborate my story. If he doesn't believe me, well, I can always take the class again." Maggie smiled at him. "Y'know, I'm still a little hot sugar. You wanna go again?" "I do. You want Marissa to join in again?" "Yeah, why the hell not? But just this once. I'd like to keep you for myself after today." "Understood." Gordon smiled and closed the door behind him, once again, on top of the world.
