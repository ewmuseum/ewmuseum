More Than You Know

11 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-11 The sun shone down on Washington DC, home to the recently re-elected President of the United States, about half a million other people, but most importantly by far, the Prime Time Central GTT4 wrestling tournament. Streets thronged with cars, and people stood in lines that wrapped around ticket offices in various locations. Occasionally a sale would go through, and the line would slowly move forward, a snake that juddered lethargically, making the rush hour traffic look nimble by comparison. Wrestling fans and sports enthusiasts from all over the country, and beyond, had turned up to buy tickets to see the bracket finals of the biggest thing to hit the squared circle since Goliath took on David in a Hell in a Cell. Allegedly. In the ranks of the queue were people of all ages, and of all ethnic persuasions. Some were fans of ‘Exceptional’ Dave Hurst, and others of the new sensation Craig Goat, who had deposed the PTC Global Champion in a major upset. There were some od looking people who might have been fans of The Deville, or of Kasper Sky… and there were the younger children. For some reason, they universally seemed to be wearing some hastily knocked up Sebastian Moraco shirts, doubtless the produce of some back-street merchant who would laugh in the face of image rights and lawful practices. Within this line of the great and the good, of the people willing to pay good money to see the top wrestlers in the world try and smash lumps out of one another, stood a man. He was just over six feet in height, and didn’t seem to be carrying too much in the way of weight. He had a baseball cap pulled forwards, with small tufts of white hair creeping around the sides. He wore a nondescript hooded jersey, emblazoned with the logo of some unimportant sports team, and a pair of jeans that had seen better days. Poking out from the ends of the jeans were a pair of training shoes that had seen a few too many training sessions. The ends were worn so much that there was a hint of white socks coming from underneath whenever the wearer flexed his toes. There, amidst a group of people who were apparently the greatest wrestling fans in the world, GTT4 Bracket Finalist Doctor Curiosity stood, incognito. His glasses were absent, and he was trying to remain unrecognised. His heavy heart told him that he was succeeding. Even with his status as a favourite for the tournament, and with his opponent for this round an estimated sixty five million to one longshot, he was managing to stay inconspicuous. Had Sebastian Moraco been standing there, the doctor reflected, he would probably have been buried under a pile of children’s bodies before you could say “I’m going to lose to Doctor Curiosity”. But why was one of the wrestlers in GTT4 standing in line trying to get a ticket for the event itself? Why indeed… ----------------------------------------------------------- Two days previous to his efforts in disguising himself as an average wrestling fan, the doctor had been told by his evil, scheming wife that she now wanted a divorce. Curiosity, not having expected this, was thrown, and denied her the chance… for the time being. With the affairs in the hands of lawyers on both sides of the Atlantic Ocean, the status of the marriage always seemed to be in some sort of doubt. The last time he had checked, the good doctor only needed to add his signature to one document and post it, and the whole sorry affair would have been over, consigned to history. When Curiosity had discovered Collette’s subterfuge, he had thought of ending everything right there and then, but some spark, some desire to hit back at her in ways more meaningful that that held him back from the action. She had retaliated cleverly with a huge bluff, and now their future hung in the balance once more. The doctor let Collette go that night, asking for one more chance to prove himself, but really stalling for time. He didn’t want to call the potential bluff, feeling that she might have laid a trap for him. He had not spent enough time, enough of his precious time, studying her movements, and now he was paying for it. “Eegor, I do not know vhat she could be thinking,” said the doctor. “Maybe it’s a bluff?” he suggested. “True, but maybe not. Can I afford to take ze chance and call her on it? Maybe she knows zat I know zat she knows zat ve know zat…” “No! Stop that! I was confused enough before!” yelled Eegor, before realising that he was overstepping the boundaries of his role again. “Be qviet, you insignificant wretch!” snapped Curiosity. He was feeling annoyed with himself at being outmanoeuvred by someone he considered his intellectual inferior. Of course, one of the reasons he had married her in the first place was because she was able to match wits with him, but the arrogance of Doctor Curiosity appeared to know no bounds. This was, after all, a man well-known for denouncing the research of Stephen Hawking as ‘plagiarised bunkum’. The doctor stormed away from Eegor, and set his mind straight. He needed to be free of the demons that plagued him, or he would not be able to concentrate on winning the GTT. Collette had taken the fight to him. It was his turn to take the fight right back. ---------------------------------------------------------- The man in the line rubbed his eyes. It was strange after all the time in the lab coat, to be dressed as a regular person, but Doctor Curiosity had total control of his persona now, and slipping back into his life as Chris Goodman was not as hard as he thought it might have been. His affected accent was held in check as someone asked him if he wanted to buy a cheap Sebastian Moraco shirt. Obviously they were expecting to not be selling any of the shirts the following week, after the pretender’s glorious run was brought up short in a crushing defeat at the hands of the doctor. The bookies were never wrong, right? Not at those sorts of odds, surely… Curiosity shuffled forwards, trying to keep his disguise in place. He needn’t have even tried so hard. With no lab coat, no glasses, and his trademark wild hair hidden under a cap, he could have been any other man in the street, looking to score some tickets to one of the biggest events in recent years. Under the left arm of the doctor was a folder. It was fairly thick, with sheets of paper looking ready to slip from the clutches of the rings that bound them together at any moment. The wind that whipped past occasionally made the loosest strands flap wildly in a bid for freedom, but then the folder would be tucked even tighter under his arm, and the secrets within would be pulled further from the public eye. The documents within the folder were important to the doctor. Whilst he had memorised each and every one of them, there was nothing like being able to read them again, looking at the images and be reminded of the videos, the strategy, the research involved. “Move up, you schmuck!” A rough voice caused Doctor Curiosity to snap out of his daze, and shuffle forwards to close the gap in the line. It felt strange for the doctor to be in such a place without being recognised, and just as strange to be on his own, without Eegor or Collette to keep him company. Mind you, after what had happened with Collette, the doctor definitely decided it was better that he was on his own. If everything went to plan then Eegor would meet him in the arena, to help with the proceedings in the match, but Collette… the good doctor knew that Collette would not be there. She’d definitely not be there. ------------------------------------------------------- In what seemed to be a daily occurrence, Collette pressed the buzzer that would let the Goodman brothers that she had arrived at the Curious Laboratory. Such was her fatigue from completing the arduous journey out into the desert that she failed to notice that above the intercom system was a large wooden box, like a cross between a grandfather clock and a large coffin. Had the lady noticed it she might have given it some consideration, and perhaps thought about not pressing the button, but the drive had taken the energy out of her. The buzzer sounded, and from the ground beneath her a rope whipped up around her ankles. It ensnared her legs, and was drawn up towards the top of the wooden box, suspending her two feet off the ground, and upside down. “Haha!” yelled Doctor Curiosity, triumphantly. He sprang out from behind the box, and looked at Collette, who was using her hands to try and prevent her skirt from falling down and exposing he more than it already was. “Let me the fuck down, and do it now, before I kill you,” she said, with a surprisingly even tone. “No, I don not tzink I vill be doing zat, my darling little vife.” The drawbridge across the moat dropped down, and Eegor came scurrying across it, pushing a gurney, with large straps attached to it. “What the hell is that?” demanded Collette, her face growing red, and her anger rising. Eegor pushed the gurney until it lay underneath Collette. Doctor Curiosity tried to get near her to bring her over to the human-sized trolley, but she was flailing her arms any time he approached her. “To be honest, Collette, I am glad it has come to zis.” Curiosity smirked as he pulled out his Curious Tranquiliser gun, and adjusted the eighteen different useless settings on it. The wind-speed gauge swirled around, and the flashing lights blinked on and off for no real reason. After he felt he had postured sufficiently, Doctor Curiosity aimed the gun at Collette, and shot her right in the ass. ----------------------------------------------------------- “Two tickets to see ze wrestling, please.” “You a fan of that Doctor Curiosity guy?” asked the ticket seller. “Why… why do you ask that?” stammered the doc, tilting his head downwards, desperate not to be recognised. “Well, you just spoke a bit like him, that’s all.” “Oh.” “Also, you seem a little strange. You’re not autistic, are you? He gets those kinds of fans, the real nutcases.” “No, I’m not autistic, and if you ask me, you’re a bit freaking pushy for a ticket seller. Now give me two tickets for tonight, or I’ll report you to… to… to someone important!” The vendor sighed and produced two tickets, exchanging them for the requisite amount of cash. The doctor regretted having to spend money on something so stupid, but it was a necessary evil. Grabbing the two tickets in his grubby hands, he bade the ticket seller farewell, and walked along the street, towards his rendezvous with Eegor. The incident with the blowtorch and the original tickets was still fresh in Curiosity’s mind, but even the greatest minds sometimes suffer setbacks, and it is in how they deal with these things that they show their true greatness. At least, that was the doctor’s excuse for burning his original backstage passes. -------------------------------------------------------- Collette awoke to find everything had gone crazy. Her head seemed to be very hot, and her cheeks full, as if she was blushing, having been found out in the worst possible way. In fact she was just being hung upside down again, this time inside the Curious Laboratory. Her short hair hung down, still looking remarkably good for someone whose head had been involved in a flamethrower incident just days earlier. “Where the hell… what the hell… have I been drinking?” asked Collette, groggily. “Nein!” yelled Doctor Curiosity, who was standing a few paces away, a long piece of wood in his hand, the kind that teachers often use to point at places on a blackboard. “Ah, crap,” said his wife. “Okay, I’ve learnt my lesson. Now let me down, my head is spinning like mad, and that tranquiliser has made me feel sick.” Doctor Curiosity stood, impassive, and shook his head. His face betrayed no hint of humour. The colds tone walls reflected his personality inwards, making him seem like he too was hewn from solid rock. He was not in the mood for playing. “Seriously, Chris, get me the fuck down,” snapped Collette, her voice growing desperate. “Why are you doing this?” was the question from the doctor. His voice was even and the accent was conspicuous by its absence. “Doing what?” “Playing this game. Why are you now asking for a divorce? Is it a bluff? Some kind of plot? A trick to ruin my life even further? What is it, Collette? You can tell me, and you know I can tell if you’re lying.” “No you can’t!” “Wrong answer.” The doctor smiled, but it wasn’t at anything that was funny. He moved across the floor towards the main workbench in the laboratory. From it he picked up a blowtorch, and he cradled it in his hands, the little blue pilot light fixating his eyes for a few precious seconds, before he clicked the button on the side and watched the flame shoot forwards, still burning blue; hotter than a regular flame, and a true implement of torture. Curiosity’s left hand caressed the flushed skin around Collette’s neck and rested briefly on her upturned shoulder. He was clearly torn inside, but some things needed to be done. Some things were important to the doctor, and he would stop at nothing to make sure that his goals were not to be obscured. He held the mechanical device in front of her face. “Don’t lie to me, Collette.” Their eyes met, and Collette made an unspoken plea for clemency. Locked into a gaze with the doctor, a tear fell up her face, seeming briefly to disobey the law of gravity as it moved slowly up past her eyebrow and forehead, before falling down to Curiosity’s feet. “Collette. You have to tell me the truth. Maybe what I say won’t be as bad as you think. Maybe I’m more human than you remember me. I’ve seen a lot of things… I’ve been through changes, Collette.” His finger toyed with the button that would send a jet of flame across her pretty face. “You don’t want to go through those kind of changes, Collette. The kind that can really scar you.” ------------------------------------------------------- Eegor was waiting for the doctor at the same café where Curiosity had met Collette before his match against Alex Grey. This time the erstwhile wife of the doctor was nowhere to be seen, and both men were confident that she would not be troubling them at all before the match against Sebastian Moraco. She was out of the equation, another variable dealt with and eliminated. Curiosity spared a smile as he dropped the blue folder down on to the table, and passed Eegor his ticket for the event. He still cursed his carelessness in burning the passes and documentation that would allow him to enter the arena via his normal route, but there were ways and means around these things. Once the tickets had gained them access, the doctor could get changed in a rest room, and then make his way to the ring in whatever way he so desired. He had his folder with him, as he always liked to before a match, and despite the unorthodox methods of reaching the ring, it would be what happened within it that would matter. The brothers toasted with glasses of champagne, but did not drink from them. There was work ahead that would require a clear mind and no loss of focus. With no further words, both men stood up and gathered together their belongings. They walked down the road towards the arena, their purpose clear and the methods by which they would achieve their goal even clearer. Curiosity thought back to how he had left Collette, still chained to the wall in the Curious Laboratory. She was safely in the main lab, and she had not been seriously harmed… yet. She had to learn, she had to realise that this was not just a game to the doctor. He lived for this now, and he was not going to let her petty machinations get in the way of his dream. Eegor understood him, and had in fact helped to create the man that Curiosity had become. Beneath all the frippery and foolishness there was a man whom pressure had turned into the hardest diamond. With no need for further words, they eventually arrived at the arena, and slipped inside. ----------------------------------------------------------- With literally minutes to go before the match against Sebastian Moraco would begin, Doctor Curiosity was in a public rest room, occupying one of the cubicles. It was not an ideal location in which to get changed and prepare for the test ahead, but Curiosity had made his living out of adapting to the difficult changes he would need to go through to become a success. In the cubicle he sat, his lab coat, shirt and bow tie now all adorning his body. Even the cracked glasses were hanging lazily off his nose. On his lap rested the folder, open somewhere in the middle. One of the sheets of paper was in his hand, and Curiosity was studying it intently. Ever since the results of the previous round had been known, Doctor Curiosity’s time had been split. On the one hand, he’d seemed rushed or lazy. He would sleep in late and then devote time to his wife, ignoring the match on the horizon. He would act arrogantly, not just thinking he could win, but knowing that it was a certainty. Yet that was the outlook of Doctor Curiosity, not of Chris Goodman. Though he appeared foolish and arrogant, the real man underneath was nothing of the sort. He never slept in late, instead spending every morning going over moves, counter-moves, training and studying his opponent. The folder on his lap contained everything there was to know about Sebastian Moraco, from his regular training regime to his favourite moves, and possible motives for his recent attack on a man twice his age. Though there had been rumours about Moraco turning his back on the competition like a petulant little child, Chris Goodman knew that he would make it to the ring. He knew that Moraco’s pride would not let him skip town ahead of such a match. He knew that the desire to prove the world wrong, the same drive that had brought Curiosity to that point, would be too strong to leave so close to the Promised Land. Chris Goodman laughed to himself as he moved out of the cubicle and started to limber up. He stretched his legs out, twisting every which way, his inherent flexibility giving him the ability to squirm and wriggle his way out of almost any given submission attempt. He was not going to be some old man caught in a trap, tapping out with his last, weak, wheezing breath. No. Chris Goodman had done his research. He’d worked out the avenues of attack, and the sensible, measured approach to take. Despite appearances, despite the nature of the two competitors and how they would be expected to act, it was Sebastian Moraco that would enter this match as the one suffering from delusions of grandeur. Chris Goodman was the very antithesis of arrogance. Knowing that his wrestling skills and pure strength were not his greatest assets caused him to second guess himself at every turn. The only way he had come so far, and achieved so much was because of his preparation, and his willingness to do anything, anything at all to get to the top of the pile. The woman chained to the walls of his home, crying at the blowtorch that sat just in front of her face was testament to that. Doctor Curiosity might let his personal problems and marital disputes get the better of him, but for one reason. His life, his reason for being, the one thing he does well… was wrestling. Everyone who had come up against him had the same mindset; that he was overrated, and that he could be beaten. No, that he would be beaten. Every man and woman who shared a ring with him had gone in ‘knowing’ that they would win. From George English through to Muppet Man, encapsulating Alex Grey and Jade too; they had all ‘known’ that they would win. They had all ‘known’ that Curiosity was beatable. They had all guaranteed their own victories. They had all been defeated. Curiosity slung his sports bag over his shoulder, hearing the files dislodge pages from within its grasp. It mattered not now, for every move, every nuance that Moraco possessed was in the doctor’s mind. He would use that information, distil it down to its purest form, and use it to defeat Moraco. If there was trouble ahead, then Eegor was at ringside, and he knew how to change things around without being caught. He passed a couple of fans, who stared, wide-eyed at the overwhelming favourite for the match. Curiosity walked not with arrogance, not even a hint of it, but with confidence. It was his opponent who would be arrogant. Curiosity had planned the match and prepared for it in great detail, whilst his opponent, a rookie kid, could only sit back and guarantee his victory in his own little mind. The doctor laughed. Against all the odds, it would be blind arrogance that was Moraco’s downfall. It would be his unwavering belief that he would win, and the failure to focus on Curiosity’s domain; the ‘how’ of winning. Doctor Curiosity reached the doors to the backstage area, and was let through without a word. Everyone knew who he was, and everyone knew why he was there. The most determined man in GTT4 dropped his bag to the floor, and looked at the entrance to the auditorium. He breathed in an even fashion, ready for this even though his entire life had been distilled for just this moment. A last moment spent running his hand through his wild, white hair, and Doctor Curiosity took a step forwards. He had an appointment with destiny; he had an arrogant young kid to dismantle; he had a spotlight to steal. He was having the time of his life.
