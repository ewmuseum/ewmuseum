The Uber Secret Show

2 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 Inside the Uber Judge’s kitchen, the Under Miner impatiently looked on as the Uber Judge took his time rehydrating himself. “What’s this about? You said you had tricks you were going to wait on? What do you mean? I thought all our time was spent building the tree fort and making the Uber Judge Elite Victory Propulsion Squad’s charter?” “That’s what it looked like, didn’t it?” finally said the Uber Judge after wiping his mouth with the back of his hand. “You were making secret plans without me?” said the Under Miner, whose feelings were a little hurt. “I'm afraid it was necessary. Follow me.” Heading into the Uber Judge’s pristine bathroom, the Under Miner gasped in shock as the Uber Judge hit a carefully hidden button behind the toilet, which caused a hidden panel in the wall to open into the crawl space that housed the air conditioner. “Whoa whoa whoa whoa!” said the Under Miner, “First of all, I thought you said that trap doors were implausible!” “This isn’t a trap door. This was an automated opening into an already existing space.” “I'm not finished!” said the Under Miner, as he followed the Uber Judge into the crawl space, “Have you forgotten that I'm your landlord? This duplex is my building, and you’re supposed to at least ask when making modifications like this! Holy fuck is that a ladder?” “Yes,” said the Uber Judge who began descending on said ladder. “And is this ladder going down into my flat? You made modifications on where I live too?!?” “It was necessary, and you always ask me to look at the air conditioner on your level before you bother to make a service call.” It was true, the Under Miner had to admit; the design of the duplex meant that the crawlspaces for each level were directly over each other, so that this unauthorized modification to the house didn’t actually disturb the Under Miner’s living space. Since the Under Miner’s part of the building was half submerged in the ground, it was a little less shocking to see that in the crawl space, the Uber Judge had build a tunnel that descended at an angle into the back yard. “We’re under your vegetable garden,” said the Under Miner, “All this time I you were building a bomb shelter right under my nose. That is what you made right? A bomb shelter, right?” “No, just a place to think.” “To think?” said the Under Miner, who certainly believed that this was the perfect opportunity for an explanation type monologue. Instead the Uber Judge didn’t answer, since this is actually the perfect opportunity for some much needed description. The thinking bunker, which obviously didn’t have an Uber long and Uber silly name yet, was shaped in the exact dimensions of the Uber Judge’s vegetable garden, a thirty by twenty foot (sorry, I'm still metric dumb) rectangle. It was brightly lit, more so than how the Under Miner liked to keep his own living space (often prompting the Uber Judge to accuse the Under Miner of deliberately creating cave like conditions, which the Under Miner never bothered denying.) It held a highly priced ergonomic office chair, an I-mac, what the Uber Judge presumed to be an IP phone, a treadmill, a space saving flat screen television. On the ceiling were painted the following words. “A JUDGE OR JAILOR WHO HAS NO CRIME OF HIS OWN IS A MONSTER, ALTERNATELY PURLOINING THE FORGIVENESS THAT BELONGS TO THE INCREATE ALONE AND PRACTICIING A DEATHLY RIGOR THAT BELONGS TO NO ONE AND NOTHING.” - FROM GENE WOLFE’S BOOK OF THE NEW SUN The Under Miner looked up at the quote for a few moments after he had finished reading it. He let out a sigh and said, “You designed this bunker’s shape around fitting this quote on the ceiling. And you designed the vegetable garden around the shape of the bunker.” The Uber Judge sat down in the chair. The Under Miner discovered that now was actually the perfect time for a monologue, his. “But this is what it’s about. Your views on how you can’t adopt superiority when judging over people, since, as the quote says, that’s pretending to be the creator of everything, rather than an observer of everything. And to become a kind of robot, a shitting, pissing calculator quantifying the transgressions of law, with no human empathy is what refers to the deathly rigor…” “And you know, that makes sense. I think you’re right on the issue of not being superior to others when undertaking evaluation and judgment, but to always keep yourself an equal.” “But what is the real purpose of all this?” asked the Under Miner pointing around the secret room, “Paranoia? Man, I’d love to believe that you’re just a crazy paranoid guy, but you’re not. This is hidden for a reason, and …” “And?” prompted the Uber Judge. “And I’d like you to tell me what it is,” said the Under Miner, who had stumbled upon a suspicion that he dared not voice. “Well, what’s not written in the quote above our heads, a part I deliberately left out, is about the necessity of becoming a criminal, to become a peer to every person on any side of the law.” “But,” said the Under Miner, “That’s secular law. And we know how that functions, with it’s tunnel vision, and you, yourself, have said that secular laws reflect the consensus of the desires of the law makers, and bear little in resemblance to the laws of the creator and the laws of reality.” “Correct.” “And even religious law reflects the imposition of the will of the law makers over that of the creator itself, being no improvement over secular law by your theories, and being worse in that it again means that people try to make themselves like gods by trying to decide what the creator wants.” “Correct again.” “So,” said the Under Miner, “to be a proper criminal, and an Uber Judge, you need to find a worthy type of law to violate, and the true law of the creator … what would you do? What law would you break.” “Well,” said the Uber Judge quite happily, “That actually turned out to be pretty easy to figure out. And it accomplishes more than just breaking the law to break it.” “What are you going to do?” said the Under Miner, “Apart from making unlawful modifications to someone else’s property?” “I'm going to save the world,” said the Uber Judge, “By becoming a tyrant.” “World Domination?!?” uttered the Under Miner. “Absolute World Domination,” said the Uber Judge, “Violating others autonomy, taking great responsibility, and rescuing the people of the world from impending doom.” “The secret goal under the guise of being so damn silly,” remarked the Under Miner, “Who is on your side so far?” “Well,” said the Uber Judge, “I penciled you in, hope you don’t mind too much.” “No problem,” said the Under Miner, “I’d be disappointed if you didn’t count me in.” “And I’ve got Henry Rollins and Patrick Stewart. Bill Clinton said he was interested, but I don’t really trust him.” (The first post in this series appropriated some lyrics from `If I had a million dollars' by the Bare Naked Ladies, this second post appropriated some text by Gene Wolfe from The Citadel of the Autarch.)
