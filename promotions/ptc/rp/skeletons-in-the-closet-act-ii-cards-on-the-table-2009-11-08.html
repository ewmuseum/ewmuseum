Date: 2009-11-08 Prologue: The Keeper Saturday, October 24, 2009 9:14 PM Lifecare Hospital of North Texas-Dallas Dallas, Texas She looks so small lying there. It was always the first thing that leapt to Helena Maddox's mind when she came to visit; it was why she was hardly ever there. The room felt small, oppressive; the rhythmic beeping of the machines that kept her sister alive was the only sound to be heard. Helena meandered to the well-worn, forest green chair perched at bedside. When she sat, the hiss of air from the cushion was something she'd like to think would have made Gwen laugh when they were young. "I'm sorry," Detective Maddox whispered, taking her sister's small hand between her own. "I haven't been by in... in a long time." It didn't matter that the comatose blonde couldn't hear her; the words themselves made her feel better. It wasn't the way that it was supposed to have happened – when they were young, Gwen was the fiery one. Gwen was the one who questioned everything, who had the drive to succeed. When their father died, they'd gone in opposite directions. While Helena was content to bury her sorrow behind a haze of alcohol, Gwen had been determined to find out what happened. She needed to know why. And then nothing – no word, no postcards, not even a phone call. It had proven a sobering realization. Helena went to rehab, put herself on the proper path, joined the police force... but she'd done it all without her sister there. A little over a year ago, they found her in a high school of all places, suffering from head trauma and damage to her spine. The doctors didn't know if she'd ever wake up. "They say I should come by more often." She'd felt stupid saying it out loud the first time, but Gwen's doctors said it helped, that studies showed it could improve their condition. Maddox knew it was probably a lie, but it didn't matter – because the talking helped her. "They say that I have some kind of need to prove myself to you, that I didn't... didn't abandon you when Dad died. They're worried that I need you to wake up more for me, more to say I've done a good job." Her eyes muddied over with tears. "But I want you back because I miss you." Maddox sniffled, wiping at her nose with the back of her hand. "We found a girl's body back home. She was... she seemed young. Twenty-something, her hair dyed purple, but she reminded me of you. All those years, I kept expecting that I'd get that call. That they'd tell me you were gone somewhere, and I don't know what I would have done. I don't even know who this girl is, Gwenny. I don't... I feel like I should call somebody. There's got to be someone out there missing her." Maddox fell silent, dropping her head. Wisps of mahogany-brown hair fell in front of her eyes as she retied her sneaker, one of many nervous habits that she'd never been able to shake. She didn't know what to say. She'd feel stupid talking about her boring life – her dog's visit to the vet, the typical gossip of her quiet suburban neighborhood, sports success (or lack thereof; she remembered Gwen liked the Raiders). The singsong up-down of her cellphone's ringtone disturbed her reverie, and Maddox smiled shyly as she dug through her bag to find the thing. She had to resist the urge to apologize, even though her sister was the only other person in the room. Maddox fished the phone out, flipping it open as she stood. "This is Maddox," she said. Bernie Dixon was eating something on the other end of her phone. "Hey, Beanpole," he said. "I don't mean to disturb your vacation or nothin', but... I got news." "Yeah?" Maddox swallowed the lump in her throat. She tore her eyes away from her sister. "It'd better be important." "You think I'd call you if it weren't? C'mon, I know why you go to Dallas." She could just imagine his lopsided grin. "You want the good news or the bad?" Helena rubbed at the back of her neck. "Just get to it." "Well, I was watching some television last night – you know, clearing out my TiVo an' shit..." "Bernie, I don't have time for this—" "I know who your Jane Doe is." Maddox spun to face the window. For the first time, the noise from the machines was bothersome; she pressed her fingers into her ear. "Say that again?" "It's... okay, it's this wrestling show, right? Sin City Wrestling or some shit. They were havin' a show in Arizona, some big convention, lotta goobs dressed up as wrestlers, whatever. So I saw your vic on the show." "On a wrestling show? C'mon, Bern..." "I'm serious. She crawled out from under the ring, dressed as this other girl wrestler and whacked this other guy with a fuckin' crowbar an' shit. I thought it was weird, since your victim had a wig on an' shit, but... but it was her." "You're sure?" "Hundred and twelve percent. Double-checked on their website." "Bring it in – we'll have the boys transfer it to videotape." "Yeahyeah, I saved it for you. C'mon, Beanpole, what do I look like? I put a call in for you to their offices, too." "Good." She bent, swinging her purse onto her shoulder. "You know where they're going next?" "Uhh, site says they've got a show in San Antonio tomorrow." "I'll be there." She touched her fingers to her sister's hand as a farewell. "When they call back, you tell them I want to talk to people. I want to know how she got from Arizona to California, and I – " "Beanpole?" "What, Dixon?" There was some hesitancy in the voice of the man on the other phone. "That was the good news." Maddox stopped in the doorway, her head dipping down again. She snuck a glance back to the blonde in the bed, tucking her phone under her ear. "What happened?" "There was an... Christ, Beanpole, I don't know how to say this." "Then just say it." Impatient strides carried Helena down the hallway. "The, uh, the body's gone." "What?" If anything, Maddox's steps quickened, the slap of sneakers on the tile the only sound other than the bleeping of the machine. "Say that again?" Dixon's voice quieted; she imagined his eyebrows beetling over his eyes. "For real. Some guys came in with, like, masks and guns an' shit. Took the body right outta the morgue. Darryl got his leg banged up pretty bad, cracked in the head." "Jesus, Bernie." Maddox reached the bank of elevators and pounded two fingers against the "down" button. "How the fuck do you not lead with that?" The soft chime of the elevator announced its arrival, and Detective Maddox stepped inside. "Send it all to my BlackBerry. I want that whole place torn apart by the best people we've got, and a full report by the time I get to San Antonio." In the hubbub of the call, Helena never really said goodbye to her sister, but she also never saw the man in the long leather coat slip out of a silver Lexus as she left. Then, Alpha: The First Friday, January 11, 2002 10:17 PM Sands Showgirls Seattle, Washington "Okay, focus," the girl mumbled. "Eyes on the door. You don't have to do anything more than that." It was another dreary day in the Emerald City, the skies gone grey with a building thunderhead. The Sands club sat just down the street, a squat stronghold of neon lights, protected by a velvet rope fence, and it was from here that Lauren Fox watched through a pair of high-powered binoculars. Fox lied prone on the metal floor of a silver-white Seattle City Light van, her vantage point a well-concealed hole in the rear door, screened by the van's logo. A manila folder lied open at her side, a rather unflattering photo paper-clipped to the top. With her feet kicked up over her head, the nineteen-year-old girl looked like she was prepared for a slumber party. "How do we know he'll come?" she asked, fiddling with the picture. "I mean, this guy's the type of dude you'd recognize on the street, right? You really think he goes to strip clubs?" The answer came in her ear, a quiet, forceful whisper that always drew the gooseflesh along her forearms. "He will be here, Ms. Fox." Alexandra Pierce's tone brooked no argument. "His pattern is quite clear, and, after a stressful day, he will need to blow off steam." "You said that last night, too," Fox mumbled into the palm of her hand. "I just think you guys are going easy on the new girl." "A great deal of what we do is based upon our patience." Pierce's voice over their intercom was pedantic, still the tutor giving her student a lesson. "We could kick the door in and demand his obedience at gunpoint, but so could any thug, and we are not thugs." "Yeah, and like Kathi says, this way is fun, right?" Lauren would have sworn she wasn't actually grumbling, although it certainly sounded like it. "As you say." The phrase was Desade's way of saying they should shut up, as the lesson was over. "Keep your eyes open." She stuck her tongue out, even though the other woman couldn't see it. "Like I was actually planning on taking a – oh, hey, wait." She twisted one of the lenses, focusing on a knot of people at the double-doors. "I think..." Fox peeled the photo off the stack of papers, holding it up as she compared the two in the pale orange streetlight. "I think he's here." "Where?" Despite her cool exterior, Lauren detected a note of eagerness in her trainer's voice. "Front door." The red neon of above the club's entrance illuminated the man's pale, half-moon face. "Yeah, that's him. Same creepy hyena smile. You'd think a guy like that would learn to like politics and boobies differently." "Who says they're any different?" The other voice in Lauren's ear was everything Alexandra Pierce was not: smooth, rich, and unabashedly loving her work. "As far as I know," Kathryn Shaw said. "Politics is all about men looking for better women to fuck. It's kind of their thing." "It is also what you say about nearly every man in the world." Even over the headset, Alexandra's voice was as dry as the Sahara. "Are you in place?" "Aye-aye, cap'n." Shaw was the only one who dared mock Desade, and Lauren couldn't help but wonder how long the proud Director would allow that to continue. "What do I do?" Lauren asked. "Stay there and stay alert." It was the same bit of advice she'd been given a hundred times, and Fox couldn't help but pout a little when Alexandra continued. "Showtime." * * * * * Eddie Vinchetti was an altogether despicable man, but at least he was happy with his lot in life. Sure, he had to deal with idiots, scumbags, and politicians (often all at once), but his was a name known by people in power. Eddie was a man who got results, without the hassle of trivial bullshit like "committees," "budgetary hearings," and – most importantly – "voters". Vinchetti had made his way into politics from a particularly ill-advised desire to change the world, but found, as a lobbyist, he could own it. If that meant that a gentleman's club like the Sands gave him a private, secluded booth, then so be it. If it meant his bodyguard Raul got one of the club's freakiest girls one-on-one, even better. Eddie was still debating whether he'd demand – sorry, "ask for" – some time with their adorable little blond waitress, just as the cherry on top. "You are sure you do not vant to join in, darlink?" the girl writhing on Raul's lap purred. It was the third time she'd asked, and Eddie had to admit he'd considered it. Godawful Boris-and-Natasha accent aside, the girl (she'd given her name as "Poison") was a twisted kind of hot. Her crushed velvet dress was bunched up around her waist, black vinyl boots gleaming in the low, purple-tinged lighting. Poison had one breast exposed, and Eddie watched Raul's tongue work along the snake tattoo that curled across it, like an infant suckling for its dinner. She beckoned Vinchetti with a many-ringed hand, and, if not for more wholesome treasures, Eddie certainly would have gone. The object of Vinchetti's affection buzzed by the table again, beaming her butter-wouldn't-melt smile. "Everything going okay?" she asked. "Yes, ma'am." Eddie had stared hard enough at the girl's nametag to know it read, "Stacie". He gifted her with his most charming grin – the one his detractors called "oily." "Seem to be empty, though." "You're not partaking?" She leaned over, her upper arms framing her chest in a way that simply couldn't be accidental. "Is Miss Poison not to your liking?" "No, she's fine," he said. As if to prove him correct, the dark-haired girl unleashed a slow, guttural moan. "I just... don't like to share." "I can get you another girl..." "Actually, Stace... the only one I'm interested in here is you." The girl's hand went to the flaxen tail over her shoulder; it was a shy gesture. "Me?" she stammered. "But... but Mr. Vinchetti, I'm not a—uh, not a dancer." "C'mon... you may not be a stripper, but you are a whore, aren't you? I don't think you're wearing a bra under that shirt of yours, and your nipples seem awfully hard." Eddie snaked a hand around Stacie's wais, fingers snagging a belt loop. "C'mon..." He pulled her towards him, forcing her onto his lap. "Just one dance. You know you've always wanted to try." "Mr. Vinchetti." She shoved uselessly at his chest. "I think you're drunk." "I've only had one fuckin' beer," he protested, even though he did feel a little hazy. His smile stretched lazily. "And I think you're... you're, uh... fuck." Eddie blinked hard. "Wow. I guess that was some strong shit." "Actually..." Stacie's reluctance vanished. "That's kind of my fault. I may have slipped something into your drink." "You... uh, what?" Eddie tried to get up, but he'd been rendered uncoordinated by the substance she spiked his beer with; whatever it was made him feel like Gumby. "Get..." His ineffectual swats were easily fended off. "Get off!" It was like someone flipped a switch, like the cute blonde he'd dreamt of making scream his name had suddenly been hollowed out, all the insides replaced by something cold. "I thought perhaps we might have a chat first, Mr. Vinchetti." Her pink-painted lips seemed too girlish to say the words so crisply, so emotionless. Like a robot. "I can't..." He pushed away again, this time more out of some inexplicable fear. "I don't want to! Raul!" "I'm afraid your compatriot can no longer hear you, Edward." The woman (he couldn't even think of her as "Stacie" any longer) pressed her lips together in an unamused line. Eddie's head lolled to the side to look at his bodyguard, but Raul stared blankly up at the ceiling. His mouth was curled into a rictus grin, walking the line between agony and ecstasy, the lips an unhealthy shade of red. "It is not just a name, babe." The accent dissipated into an ear-tickling purr, but the stripper still did not develop the shame necessary to cover up. "What the hell do you—whoa." Vinchetti's gaze snapped back down to his lap. His attacker had a tiny blade pressed against his inseam; he'd never seen her draw it. "Who are you people?" he demanded. "That is, I believe, markedly less important than what it is we want, is it not?" She seemed to smile, but it was more like the hint of the expression – he had to search to find anything. "I am going to ask you a question, Edward. If you answer it correctly, you may live. If you do not, I can assure you, I will make you suffer before you go." Her knife poked him hard enough in the thigh that he was sure it drew blood. "What... what do you want?" Sweat beaded on his forehead. "I'll call for help!" "There is an artery in your thigh, Edward." The threat was surreal coming from such a small, apparently harmless figure. "If I punch my knife through the skin of your thigh right here, you bleed out before anyone realizes this is more than just a pretty girl on your lap." Eddie looked down, defeated. He slit his eyes. "What do you—" But in that moment, call it his desperation or her false sense of security, Pierce was unprepared as the man rose quickly out of his seat. He put his forearm to the point of her jaw as he stood, knocking Desade across the table and into her partner. Vinchetti stood, the drug in his system sending his steps cockeyed as he raced away from her and towards the back. "Stop him!" She'd flipped the switch again; "Stacie" was back in the girlish squeal. "He didn't pay his bill!" Vinchetti shoved aside the disc jockey that saw fit to get in his way, bumping open the back door and racing into the back alley. His sense of direction was gone, and he turned a corner to get his bearings, looking frantically over his shoulder. A clatter of a coin in a can drew his attention. "Hey, mistah," a bundle of rags croaked. "Got some change so's I can get somethin' to eat?" "What?" Eddie was gasping, his hands on his knees. "Do I fucking look like I'm going to—" The silencer quieted the shot, but Lauren didn't expect the gun to jump so much, so her first shot just winged the man, catching him in the arm and spinning him halfway around. She cursed softly, bracing her right hand with her left and breathing out slowly, like she'd been trained. The second time, when the pistol lurched, she was able to keep the recoil from spoiling her shot, and the bullet tore through the man's torso, imbedding itself in the ribcage. He wheezed out, the blood filling his lungs turning the sound into something like blowing bubbles. It was his eyes she'd remember, blank staring green things blazing out from his slackening features. She saw hate there, and accusation alongside, and couldn't look up until Pierce and Shaw arrived – despite her blocky stack heels, Kathryn managed to keep pace with Lauren's nimble tutor. "Jesus," Shaw said. "You weren't supposed to shoot him." "I know that!" The two women were stepsisters – Kathi's mom married Lauren's dad, but she always felt tiny in Shaw's presence. "Don't you think I know that? I was listening in and I heard it going badly and I figured I should back you guys up and I brought the gun, because Dyson always said to bring the gun, and I didn't want him to get away and—" "It is fine." Pierce pulled at the prosthesis that gave her Stacie's button nose. "He is not dead, however." Fox clambered to her feet, shrugging off the overlarge coat she'd worn as a makeshift disguise. She didn't want to look down at the body. "He will be soon enough – we need to go before someone calls the police." "They likely already have." Alexandra's brow curled. "If the paramedics can resuscitate him, we all have to answer for this." "Then we move him and—" "That would expose you more." Shaw's voice was rueful. "Right now, the only thing you have to worry about is ballistics. If you touch him, could get a stray hair caught." "Then what?" "Shoot him again." Pierce's voice was flat, but something about her removal of that blond wig made the voice fit better, fiery hair skimming over her shoulders. "Make certain." "What?" Lauren went wide-eyed. "You can't expect me to just shoot a guy who's basically already dead." "I can and I do." Pierce stepped to her protégée's side, grasping her by the wrist and forcing the gun in line with Vinchetti's head. "Pull the trigger." The man on the ground made a miserable, mewling sound, and Fox closed her eyes. "Don't make me...." "Pull the trigger, Lauren." Pierce squeezed her hand around her student's, hard enough that the grip caused her to wince. Fox's eyes shot open, and she glanced to her stepsister, looking for any kind of aid. Kathryn, however, had turned away, her gaze back to the club. "Can't you..." Lauren's lower lip quivered. "You made this mistake, you fix it." It was a half-hearted explanation at best. "This whole operation only works when you take responsibility for—" FWIP! The shot was silenced, but it echoed in Lauren Fox's memory for years to come. Alexandra released her grip, and the three moved quickly from the alleyway. Desade never told Lauren, "Good job", nor did she congratulate her – in fact, they never spoke of it again. But the girl they would one day call "Savant" never forgot that moment, not even on the day she'd end up floating in a hot tub. Scene 6: The Accused Sunday, October 25, 2009 9:54 PM The Convocation Center at UTSA San Antonio, Texas To call it an office was something of a misnomer. It was rare that any arena would surrender any space of significance to the SCCW crew – no sports coach wanted their office ruined by two brutes arguing in front of Cinemax cameras. The officials at the University of Texas at San Antonio consented to giving Dr. Nigel Kensington a small space adjacent to the tape room, away from anything that could actually be damaged. The room was cramped, its small size even tinier when shared with the massive lights that would be necessary to film there. Dr. Kensington would have this meeting occur nowhere else, however, so they made do with what space they had. Alexandra Pierce stood near the window, her back ramrod straight. One finger tugged down the blinds, her unreadable gaze aimed down at the street. "I have already told you, Ms. Maddox—" "Detective Maddox, please." Helena looked up from the legal pad in her lap. "Lord knows I worked hard enough to get here. I know you've said it already but I'm working off a new page, and I want to make sure I've got everything straight." She offered up a thin, friendly smile – or as close as she could manage. Pierce sighed, flicking her stormy gray eyes over her shoulder. The blinds snapped shut as her finger fell away, and it took a moment for a smile (one Helena was fairly certain was fake) to touch her lips. "Lauren and I have not been friends for some time, Detective," Alexandra said finally. "Our careers went in markedly different directions earlier this year." "This would be after she left your... your alliance?" "'Stable' is the industry term, yes." Alex turned fully, folding her arms. ("Too casual," Helena jotted onto her pad.) "She determined she would be better off seeking her own path." "Had to bug you a little, though, didn't it? I mean, from what I've learned.... she'd been in your little group for years, right?" "Since her start in the business, yes." "And then one day, she just... left. Aligned herself with your worst enemies. That must have really gotten under your skin. Seems almost like that's a betrayal." Alex's smile thinned – this one was more real than the first. "She made her choice, and I would never begrudge her that." "Please, Ms. Pierce... I may not be an expert on the wrestling industry, but I know a falling-out when I see one. So what happened?" Helena wrinkled her nose. "Was there a fight?" "We had words." The redhead's voice – already labeled by even her fans as "whisper-soft but diamond-hard" – cooled notably. "But I did not kill her." "Oh, I never said you did." Maddox raised her hands innocently, but Pierce's silent brow arch impelled her to continue. "Look, I know how it seems, but really, I'm just trying to understand the world Lauren lived in." Her pencil touched the paper again. "So this... this 'change in careers' as you called it... how closely did it coincide with the start of your affair with a co-worker?" That caused Alexandra to bristle; she pushed off from her "look-I'm-relaxed" lean against the wall, her arms swinging down at her sides. Helena's hand fell off the desk, touching the holster at her hip before she even realized it. For a brief moment, the two women simply locked eyes, and every instinct and bit of training Maddox had said the redhead wanted to hurt her – not hit her, not punch her, but hurt her. In the end, it was the smooth voice of the third man in the room that broke the tension. "Ladies." Dr. Nigel Kensington, president of Sin City Championship Wrestling, was the epitome of a scholarly Brit: cultured, careful, and curt. He pulled his thin-framed bifocals down from his salt-and-pepper hair, far more salt than pepper. He did not look up from his paperwork immediately. "I have allowed this interview only because you were quite, ah... 'adamant,' Detective Maddox. Should it become contentious, I will have to direct you to Ms. Pierce's attorney." When he did lift his head, his dark eyes locked in on Maddox's face. "I will instruct them to begin their questioning as to your jurisdiction and work their way from there." "It's a perfectly valid question," Helena protested. "If Lauren objected to her former employer having an affair with a married woman..." "They did not coincide at all." Alex bit off the words, a tinge of triumph to her smile. "As I am sure you will find out from here. I know you are searching for something damning to hang on me, Detective. Some kind of conspiracy. Perhaps you are displeased Amy left a loveless marriage to a pathetic man, or maybe you simply disapprove of my lifestyle. I do not know what it is you hold against me, nor do I particularly care. No matter how many times I say it, you will not believe me." Pierce stepped forward, and even though Helena's perch on Nigel's desk kept the tall detective on eye level, Maddox felt as if Desade was looming over her. "I did not kill Lauren Fox. I am simply a professional wrestler; I am not capable of whatever you think happened to Lauren, and I was in Tucson the very same night you say she died." "I've been doing my reading, Alexandra, and I have read about what kind of a woman you are from every account of this business of yours. If you did this—" "I did not." Pierce's smile tightened as she moved just her eyes to Kensington. "If there is nothing else, Nigel? I am expected to appear on camera despite these accusations, am I not?" Nigel Kensington and Alexandra Pierce had a contentious relationship, to say the least – it was no secret his management group, the Kent Fusiliers, tried several times to force Desade from her ironclad SCCW contract – but Kensington was a showman, and his broadcast took precedent. "Go," he instructed. "Not a word on camera until the lawyers have sorted out what we can say." Pierce nodded perfunctorily, turning on heel-and-toe back to the door. Her voice drifted back over her shoulder. "Detective." Helena Maddox did not watch Alex Pierce leave. She lowered her head, watching her hand, balled up into a fist, and willing it to open. Maddox was angry and she couldn't place it. Pierce was arrogant, yes, but smart and cunning and... and familiar. Helena couldn't place it, couldn't understand it, but she'd seen that woman before, despite never having seen a moment of a wrestling program. But she would. She had to. Scene 7: The Lie Monday, October 26, 2009 12:39 AM The Convocation Center at UTSA San Antonio, Texas The crowd was gone, the arena was dark, but the people who'd put on the show were still at it. The ring crew was dismantling the ring and taking down SCCW's SinScreen, but the performers were still putting themselves back together. Nights like this one – nights where they did what they loved before a loud, raucous crowd – they remind the wrestlers that despite it all, despite everything that went on in their oft-chaotic lives, they were still professional wrestlers and they still loved their jobs. Or at least these nights were supposed to make them feel that way. The evening hadn't ended well for Amy Campbell, who winced on a sore ankle as she shrugged her bag onto her shoulder. Getting laid out to end a show wasn't an altogether new sensation, but it was the circumstances of it that were galling – teaming with Lane Stevens of all people, seconds away from getting her hands on the man who stole her title, eating a superkick from Dusk while Alexandra Pierce hung above the ring in a cage. Amy found her teeth grinding as she shouldered open the back door. A wrestling match was supposed to solve her anger, it was supposed to relieve her stress. It was supposed to... "Hi." Alexandra Pierce leaned against Amy's rented Cadillac, her own bag on the roof. The Director's gaze was clouded over, but it had been all night. "You okay?" Campbell looked at her for a long, quiet moment before she shrugged her shoulders. "Yeah. I'm fine." She knew 'I'm fine' well - part of the little lies everybody told their partners. Regardless, she used her keychain to unlock the car, tossing her bag into the back seat and leaving that door opened for Alexandra's things. "You?" "I'm..." Alex set her bag inside on the back seat far gentler than Amy did. "I've been better. You don't mind if I catch a ride with you, do you? I sent the rest of them back to the hotel." "It's fine, yeah." Amy swung into the front seat, the key in the ignition before the door even closed. "Come on." She peered up through the passenger's side window. "I want this night over." Pierce nodded a little, rubbing at the back of her neck as she slipped into the seat, buckling her seatbelt. The silence resounded between them, hanging like a cloud over them. It was oppressive. They could have locked you up because of that little memory lapse, asked more questions... Alexandra Pierce was a creature of extremes, tightly wrapped beneath a controlled veneer. Her posture was perfect, her motions always smooth, calm – it was like she was in a high-pressure bottle.
