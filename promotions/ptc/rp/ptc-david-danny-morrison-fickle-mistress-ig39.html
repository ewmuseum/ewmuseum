IG 39 Roleplay Thread

12 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David Danny Morrison - Fickle Mistress Opportunity was a fickle mistress, given a feminine quality for it's gentle caress as well as it's fridge callousness. For David Danny Morrison to receive the shot of his lifetime, Richie 'Ripper' O'Connell had to have his taken away from him. Fate's beaming smile was not all inclusive, as Ripper laid on his back, left leg perfectly straight. His fingernails clawed at his eyes, the sobbing taking on an animal gruffness as his skin turned flush. “What the fuck I do to deserve THIS?!” The pain shooting through his leg flipped Rip's internal stopping mechanism for cursing (as small as it was) off, his hands now pounding the concrete underneath him in a infantile show of frustration. His plastic wrapped eyes of red glass looked towards David, who merely looked down upon him. Pity, nor disdain, filled that glance. It was just there in all it's neutral glory, and it ate Richie up to be looked over like that. David had his reasoning for it. “You did it on FUCKIN' purpose!” Richie's roar came from the depth of his guts, coming out like a growl. The veins and arteries underneath his face protruded, mapping out his rage as he sat up, moving his leg while he was not thinking. A shriek ripped through the air as Ripper laid back down, settling into the throbbing pain. Morrison's cigarette was not helping matters. “Get that fuckin' shit out of here. I don't want no fuckin' cancer.” David took a hit, then looked at the cream colored surroundings that were bathed in a fluorescent light. The tiles on the floor looked aged and stained a slightly darker hue, giving the weathered trappings an even more somber air. It reminded him of his middle school lunch room, and inexplicably he found himself thinking of the pint sized cartoons of chocolate milk he would have with the mystery meat and potato side. Back then, things were a lot simpler. “Can't even look me in the fuckin' eye. That's great Danny. Fuckin' enjoying this own ain't you?” His disconnected thoughts were shattered by the stake like accusation that pierced on through. His neutral glare became one of jilted defense, the skin on his forehead furrowing and his lips curving into a frown. “I didn't do anything to hurt you out there Rip. Maybe if you weren't-.” “Don't EVEN go there Davey!” “Know what?” Morrison pulled the black bandanna off his head, balling it up and throwing it at his opponent from fifty minutes before. It fell just a few feet short of hitting Richie. “Don't even TRY and put it on me. You were drinking on the way here and you ain't gonna take this shit from nobody. And I sure as hell ain't takin' it from some punk ass Yankee fuck who's gotten everything handed to him on a silver plater!” “You let me take that fall and now your gonna take my shot!” Silence permeated the room for a moment, passing on through as the pair just looked at each other, the seconds uncomfortably ticking by. “You fucked me Davey. You took my shot man.” David Danny Morrison threw his cigarette against the floor, the sparks of tobacco scattering upon collision. The accusatory stance Ripper was taking with him had run him ragged, and he was sick of having to absorb the brunt of Richie's incessant falsehoods. But what Richie was accusing him of, of taking his shot, was no lie. What had Morrison upset was the idea that Rip knew what was going on from here on out. “Fuck you Rip.” It was matter of fact. No exclamation. Just cold hearted bluntness that bludgeoned the Great Lakes Wrestling Association Champion. Richie O'Connell felt a deep sense of entitlement to his future, as if he owned it completely. To have someone else waltz on in and take it was almost too much to bare. He couldn't react. His sense just shut off, and he gave into the stillness that the desolation he felt gave. He looked up at the ceiling, his lips pressed together, tears trembling toward the floor over his cheeks. He could not dignify David with a response, instead grabbing at the little tin title that was supposed to represent his status in this all so small corner of the wrestling universe. As if he was making the statement towards David that the two of them knew the score. “You're gonna get killed Davey.” “Fuck you Rip.” The rubber sole of his canvas shoes snuffed out the smoldering Parliament, turning his back towards the injured and disdainful up and comer. His head was tilted downward, his limbs swaying as if they were unconnected. He got a few steps away from the sterile room, running his dirty fingertips through the strands of his sweat soaked mess of hair. The insides of his fingernails were still black from working just earlier in the day. David Danny Morrison's conscious was now bullet ridden, wondering if there was anything he actually could have done on the moonsault that Ripper had attempted that was obviously off the mark. It was his nature to always worry about those sorts of things after the fact, where as when he was in the ring it was more a practice of going with the flow. He didn't get too far. His eyes looked towards the booker, Chuck Flannery. He lacked his trademark smirk, but his large gut still protruded from his black cut off as if nothing had happened. Morrison knew it was really bad when Flannery's eyes directly after he had looked up traveled elsewhere. Anywhere other than looking at the southern boy who had just taken away his little cash cow. * * * * “Fifteen minutes guys. No more.” Tyler Watt's teeth were yellow and, at points, misshapen. At least they looked as if they were to Dave. His boss had a dated sense of style, but given his chosen vocation, dress was the last thing on his mind. His large padded gloves had a permanent black stain across the palms and fingertips, there outer portions covered in little rubber beads, which David never understood the purpose of. He wore a Poison band shirt with a pair of tight Wrangler jeans, a chain extending from his right pocket to his back. The most striking thing, though, was his facial features. He looked worn, his jowls dangling with a peppered gray stubble. His hairline was receding, but that did not prevent him from having his short stubble cut done up in a mohawk. He did it on a bet from his kids Dave remembered. Apparently, Tyler had found a saving grace in unloading trucks during the overnight shifts at Target to the extent that he was transferred to one in Chicago. Watts spoke in a more distinct drawl, drawing David to question him as to where he was raised. He never received an answer beyond something along the lines of 'somewhere I'd love to forget'. Morrison had tried to do away with his own dialect to the point where he was homogenized with the rest of his co-workers, which was all the more hilarious given his current location. The Windy City was said to be the city of big shoulders, and in the two years he had lived there he never understood how it was any different from any other inner city he had lived in, save for the bitter winters. It took a little getting used to, but Morrison had gradually allowed himself to stop letting the numbing northern cold tear away at his will. He was barely full time at Target, and while some of his co-workers were still wearing jeans on into October, he was bundled up with jeans and a long sleeved shirt underneath a cut off that was covered in black sot. He reached into his pocket as he walked from the receiving dock, head looking down at the tiles, pulling out his paper packet of Parliament cigarettes. His sullied fingertips pulled out a random cancer stick, and he quickly deposited it on his lips. His senses were dulled to the point where he couldn't really register what was going on around him, just that he wasn't all that close to anyone on the rest of the ever changing overnight stock crew. He usually kept to himself, and keeping his head down made it a lot easier to avoid idle chit chat. His overtaxed mind raced over just how many hours he had to sleep before he could get some sleep. It didn't take long for fifteen minutes to race on by in an effortless rhythm of aching moans, covered in the haze of cancerous smoke. The last two hours of the shift went by at a machine gun beat, his heels feeling torn up through his stiff work boots. He looked into the darkness that was the empty fifteen thousand piece truck, his mind fatigued. He made busy work for himself, going onto the floor and placing boxes of appliances onto their given displays. He had mastered the art of looking as if he was busy, but in reality just taking his time. He would walk around the store at times, making excuses to go to the front desk and get a new blade for his box cutter, or simply to get a drink of water. Before he even knew it, the shift was over, and it was a race to the time card. In a whirlwind, Morrison power walked out the doors that had caged him into his wage slaving. If he was lucky, he could make it back home within an hour, depending on accidents and morning traffic. It had totally passed his mind that it was an early Saturday morning, meaning he would have significantly less obstacles than on a weekday. The drive over the highway was also obscure in his head, his sense that he had done this countless times before deadening him to the feeling that this was all too automatic. Getting off on the Addison exit, he maneuvered his Chevy Cavalier through his surroundings until he found a small space outside his apartment building. Parallel parking was the only way he could get it in, the loud banging noise of bumper to bumper collisions filling the air as David tried to deposit his shoddy automobile into the narrow space. He opened his car door, locking it up as he made his exit. The wooden door inside the apartments was creaky and old, left propped open by a piece broomstick. He kicked it away, letting the door lock into place as he made his way into his humble abode. His keys jingled in his pockets as he walked up to the third floor, opening the door to his barren room. What caught his eye upon entering was his girlfriend, Carla Merlino, hunched over a small laptop screen. Her fingers tapped away at the keyboard, the sudden noise of the door opening causing her to look over. David smiled as he looked over towards her. “Still up?” “I'm five pages into this paper.” She relaxed, pushing her back into the small desk chair the couple had bought. She was decked out in her pajamas, her lower portion covered with a heavy throw blanket that was navy blue. Danny walked over towards her, putting his hand gently on her forehead. Their eyes met. “You should've gotten some sleep. Don't you have to work today?” “I always work. We both always work. And if it isn't this, it's school.” “Sucks. But not much else you can do.” “It will be nice to get out of Chicago, Davey. The cost of living is better down in Raleigh, and maybe you can even go back to school.” David scoffed at the notion as he walked away, standing a few short feet away from her. A cup of coffee was on the docket, and he took the one Carla had left out for him before the small window that had a withered bouquet of roses resting next to it. “Education ain't my thing Carla. Never was too good at it. I'm better with my hands.” “You're smarter than you give yourself credit for.” “If I was so damn smart, I wouldn't be slaving on overnight shifts trying to make sure we have some heat.” “It's not so bad. We have blankets.” It made him feel guilty, but he knew there would be times when the heating bill would be late. They'd done as well as they could under the given circumstances. They were survivors. “Carla, I got to be up at noon for the drive. You going to come with?” “Just let me get a page more into this.” David sat on the lime green couch they had gotten off a street corner, easing onto it. Before he knew it, he was fast asleep, his chest serving as a pillow underneath his girlfriend's head just a few moments later.. * * * * Chuck's apartment was always a mess of nudie magazines and pizza boxes. Expensive ones from the shop just down the road, which he got for free because his brother took pity on him and always sent him with a pie to soothe his overbearing guilt. Flannery was just another do nothing piece of trash who hitched his modest living on returns from his small independent wrestling promotion as well as his document shredding service. David never got past the dryness of the place, or the assorted smells that caught in his throat. “I'm gonna be honest with you David. You ain't gonna win. You're out there to look good and lay on your back.” Days before, Chuck was upbeat about the proposition of seeing one of his own talents take on the PTC world. But of course, what Flannery was promising was something more utopian. He'd stumbled upon young wrestling talent, and for whatever reason the majority of them stayed loyal to him. Where as Ripper was getting dates from the independents around the northern seaboard, everyone else was working one or two jobs on top of wrestling, just to make ends meet. And Chuck abused them, knowing that they could be his meal ticket outside the slums of the projects. Ring rentals cost money, and Chuck was always a penny pincher when it came to spending. It never stopped him for overcharging on merchandise. A box of PTC event DVD's were in the corner, the tape being opened up with a box cutter by the fat man who always reeked like burnt hair. “You've been doin' this a year. Nobody has heard of you. You taking a dive is for the good of Great Lakes. And it's for the good of Ripper, because you fucked him over in that match.” He'd just gotten off another late night shift, and he wasn't in the mood to be guilt tripped again about the whole incident. It wasn't his fault, but nobody was going to believe him. Part of David thought that the notion of getting him into the Infinite Gauntlet was a punishment, as the Illusionist was drawing a comfortable cult following. Morrison knew his chances were slim enough even if he had all the riches that Matthew Ward's accomplishments allotted him. But he was making due on barely over minimum wage at a full time job and a part time one washing dishes. Then there was the traveling. The lack of sleep. And Carla. Not in the negative, but in the totally positive. What he wouldn't give to spend more time with her. And she had always been supportive, which was interesting given how many horror stories he had heard of girlfriends and wives in the wrestling business acted as negative nags who whined about injuries and being alone. Carla could only smirk at the stories David brought back to her, saying that the men were using the female figure in their life in an predetermined negative notion. Feminist pedagogy be damned, Morrison had to agree. “You don't know?” David's eyes were staring off into space as the holder of his pathetic wrestling future stood up, his stained shirt barely containing his massive curves. His voice was that of a gravel baritone, scratchy like a broken record of pretentious self importance which just fit Chuck Flannery like a glove. “You seriously didn't know?” David was never really into paying attention to people who were in the process of tangents. “He was going places David. Going places soon. And that was the door for all you guys. But now-” he trailed off, turning his back towards Morrison and going into his fridge. Pulling out a beer, Flannery stared a hole right though Morrison. “And now I can't pull that shit for you guys until he's back and healthy.” David didn't shrug. He just stared off into space. “How much does it pay?” He wasn't going to get into the gritty with Chuck. He just wanted figures. “Bookerman, you know I got to take Carla to class. Why you stalling on me?” “It pays two grand you miserable little fuck!” The beer flew across the room, connecting with the wall and shattering upon contact. It reminded Morrison of the home he had ran away from, that outward showing of aggression. He was deadened to it, but it had a drawing allure that always kept him subconsciously captivated. “You see this place? EH? EH?! I'm TRYIN' to save you fuckin' kids!” Morrison just adjusted his bandanna, shaking his head. “Fuck this man. You want to blame me then stop with the fuckin' guilt tripping already! What reason do I have of hurting Ripper, eh? He puts money in all our pockets-” “BECAUSE YOU'RE FUCKIN' JEALOUS OF HIM! Just like Timmy and Joe Joe! You wanna see the guy fail because he comes for a better upbringin' than the rest of us! And thank God, cuz without him we ain't got nothing! Nobody would pay their hard earned cash to watch a bunch of nancy ass pansies hug each other if it wasn't for him!” Before Chuck could even lay into him anymore, David was out the door, starting his rusted out Cavalier. * * * * His undershirt was like a gray waffle that kept him warm against the gentle breeze coming off the Chicago River, the ends of his red bandanna twirling at each soft gust. Ever since Rollins had broken through, those within the area had taken to wearing something that resembled the Renegade. It was copyright infringement in the form of flattery. Morrison was lumped into the rest of those people, his six foot one frame and long brownish blond hair reminding some of a younger variation of the grizzled anarchist. He took it as a compliment, for he was still far too green to really make any sort of real impression beyond the very basic. At least, that was what he felt. But there was something inside him that clicked, that made people believe in him. He was the quietly reserved scrapper who never stopped fighting. Chuck never gave him a character to play, rather just let him go on his merry little way and try to find himself. David was having problems performing in front of a few dozen people. Something as big as the Gauntlet was much more daunting. But Ripper wasn't exactly ever so shy. In fact, he was arrogant to the point where it made everyone detest his very existence. So he was doing his job well. But beneath the egotism was a well manicured sense of destiny. When he had heard about the crushing blow of a ruptured MCL, he couldn't help but blame everyone else but himself. Richie had been blessed, to that point, with perfect health, getting away with playground bumps that would send others to the hospital with a broken clavicle or pelvis during grade school. That continued into wrestling, which was part of the reason why Richie was so well received by small time fans looking towards their own entertainment. He would fly through the sky with the greatest of ease, his limbs cutting through the air in a graceful precision that made him look more acrobat than brute. Thus, he was dubbed Ripper by the fans. His parents would pay for his surgery and his rehab, just as they had paid for his place on Central Lakeview. Any spending cash Richie had came from stealing, which was the reason why he was called Ripper by those who knew him. Car stereos and the like all helped him along his way. And with getting away with these things came a large scale sense of being bulletproof. “I'm sorry for comin' at you like that Davey. I was frustrated.” The sun was at the edge of the sky, an orange haze tickling its outskirts. David was too busy trying to get his lighter to work to really look Ripper in the eye, but he certainly heard what he was saying. “I've been in a rough spot since it happened man. I'm so close to getting paid man. Big time wrestling man. And I'm going to take Chuck and all you along with me. We're trading up from the poorhouse to the suburbs man.” Morrison's lighter was as empty as the promises Ripper was giving him. It was all a lie from a contrite man who was trying to draw sympathy. He looked over into Richie's bloodshot eyes, trying to play the role of a caring friend. “Then maybe you should've considered that when you drank a pint of Jack in my car on the way to the event. I've been taking shit from Chuck about how I let you get hurt because he told me that I was next to get a shot at this PTC thing.” He failed at being caring, instead letting his opinions come out. Ripper was silent for a moment, collecting his thoughts as the pair watched the waves crash into the shore, the sound of traffic playing a somber backdrop to their surroundings. “I know we should've told you guys. But Chuck didn't want people asking questions. I mean, he's having a rough enough go of it as it is.” No excuses. “I don't even care about any of that. What I care about is the fact that I am having to own up to something I'm not guilty of. And it looks all the worse now that I'm apparently taking your spot in this Infinite Gauntlet thing.” “How much they paying you?” “Chuck says two thousand bucks.” Ripper scoffed at it, which provoked a glare from Morrison. “The fuck that for Richie?” “I was going to make five thousand on that man. They're paying you to just lay the hell down man.” The nature of the discourse was only serving to piss him off. “I know that Ripper. Because the fact is, I'm being told by you and Chuck that I'm basically going to get killed out there. Which is a great way to put shit on my shoulders.” “Look man-” Ripper tried to put his arm across the shoulder of Morrison, but David pulled away violently. It was as if he was shell shocked by the whole damn thing. “-You have to just chill out.” “I didn't try to hurt you man. Why the hell don't you tell people that? You don't get it man. You'll never get it. You have the world at your fingertips and you screwed it up. Now everyone is pissed at me because they think I purposefully let you take that fall. And now, here I am. It takes me over two months to make two thousand dollars Richie. You know what that's like?” Ripper was about to respond, but Morrison was quick to cut him off. “No man. Your fuckin' mother buys you everything and you sit there bitching about it as you smoke pot and all that. And you dare scoff at me. Two thousand dollars is good money. I'd take that lying down on my back.” The small, insignificant people in the world who need the cash will do anything to get it. Even if it means that they sacrifice their pride and dignity. “I don't understand though.” “What don't you understand Davey?” “Why it's got to be you.” A huff came from Morrison's lips as he forcefully tossed the lighter into the river. His head craned over quickly, looking at Ripper through narrowed eyes. “I work hard. Why is it that I got to get sacrificed to keep things good for the rest of you? What's me not even trying going to do for any of us? Why is it you who has to take us by the hand to some form of bullshit prominence?” “I don't know Davey. I just wanted to apologize for blaming you. I was in the wrong man.” The reason he could be so accepting of the issue was because David Danny Morrison stood no chance in Hell of beating the Illusionist. He had not chance of seizing the opportunity presented to him like Ripper could because he was an unknown element who had all sorts of other issues on his plate. The very notion of purposefully injuring someone was enough to ensure that he was completely destined to fail, as it took the shine off his chance. But David didn't see it that way. He saw it as his one chance to do well for himself. Win, lose, or draw, it was better than not showing up at all. * * * * The heat of the battle was immense, and David was too busy fighting off his own exhaustion to really stay completely focused. The turnbuckle pole had clouded his mind, while the tumble on his back to the concrete off the ring apron shot a stabbing pain through his entire body. His shorts swayed slightly as he rose up, looking upward towards the GLCW Champion Richie 'Ripper' O'Connell jump onto the top turnbuckle in an effortless display of athleticism. He wobbled there for a moment before turning around, jumping in a flawless motion. Joe Joe Bunkley was next to David, the pair making up an on the fly tag team. David was acting as a neutral in Bunkley's war with Ripper, and for whatever reason that was grounds for putting him in a match with the champion. The pair were supposed to catch him, sell the move, and be done with it. Morrison's limbs went a little slack as all of Ripper's weight was about to come down on his shoulder. The momentary lapse was enough to cause Richie's boot to touch the ground, the pressure causing a hyper extension and a popping noise. The next few seconds went by slow motion agony, Richie trying to stand up but not being able to. Joe Joe looked over from his spot on the concrete, looking at the still standing David Danny Morrison. And no matter what excuses he would make to anyone, be it that he wasn't ready or that his movement away from the moonsault was unexpected, it still looked like he had hung Joe Joe and Ripper out to dry. Nobody cheered, and nobody booed. Everything was just still. For Richie, it was the end of his wrestling career. Nobody knew it at that moment, least of all David, who would one day see just what his momentary lapse would cost him. Even so, guilt flowed through him, only to be taken aside by a defiant sense of self defense.
