Just Like The Movies: Scene I

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 "The sun rises over the angry monkey. Deep within the swan, the pearl rotates." -Some fortune cookie Clyde got Act I He walks along the busy sidewalk, stepping past kids on skateboards and yuppies talking on cell phones. He walks past old women walking their little dogs, and old men hardly walking at all. In his creased grey suit and tan dress shoes, loose black tie over a stained white shirt. He walks through the front door. There’s no going back now. No one knows who he is. No one. Except him. Sitting with his back to me. Sitting in his expensive little suit. In his expensive little chair. He knows who I am. And I know him. Now, he doesn’t know anyone anymore. All he knows is the bullet in his brain. Is that high-class enough for you, Charlotte? Is that expensive enough for you?! Everyone around me is screaming. I have to get out of here. The front door. They’re all focused on him. No one’s noticed you yet. Run, you fool! The guard behind me is still in shock, and the front door is just within reach. I open it and I’m free. No one’s screaming out here. I’m free. I’m… …What the hell is that? Act II I like the lights, the trippy lights, and all the pornographic stores, I light this thing as they complain about the junkies and the whores. Inside the room of a sixteen-year-old, Dope’s "The Life" plays through the stereo speakers next to his bed, drowning out every other sound in the world. His parents bang on his door a few times, but he ignores them. Nothing else matters to him right now. His eyes close and he’s no longer in his room. He can still hear his parents yelling for him to turn the music down, and he can still hear the music, but it all seems so far away. Like a call through tin can phones. But as he lies there, something feels different. Wrong. Something’s definitely wrong. He opens his eyes and finds the banging has stopped. So has the music. In fact, all sound has ceased to exist. And then he sees it. Act III He sees her coming from a mile away. If she’s like most of the others, he’ll be done with her in a minute or two and she can go home. No harm, no foul. He doesn’t believe in hitting women. Unless they put up a struggle. His lips curl back into a smile, revealing teeth that haven’t been brushed in a decade. If women thought penetration was the worst part of the act, they thought differently after being in close proximity to this guy breathing heavily for a minute and a half. Two, tops. The rest of the street was relatively empty. Even if it wasn’t, most people would pretend not to notice anyway. Still, makes things easier if there’s not a scene… His breath catches in his chest as he waits…and then she’s his. Her first struggles are cut short by a hand gripping her throat, as he presses his face close to hers. Her eyes show terror, and she barely notices when he tears at her skirt. You see; she isn’t afraid of him. And soon, he’ll be afraid, too. Act IV Straight off their title win at PRIME’s Great American Nightmare, both members of The Forsaken have decided to celebrate their newfound gold, as well as that bonus they get for being champions. But don’t tell them they got a bonus, because I’m keeping it for myself. So, they’re celebrating as best they can on a budget of twelve dollars and ninety-nine cents, the PRIME Tag Titles now proudly on the mantle, Clyde’s abomination having been pushed onto the floor next to the fireplace. Still glowing, the text now reads Connection Established, but no one can see that. Because it’s upside-down, facing the wall, on the floor. Hidden behind some old cushions and what might be a rabbit. We’re not really sure. Anyway, since getting drunk off dented cans of beer bought from a dirty midget in an alley isn’t normally a man’s proudest hour, I’ll do everyone a favor and forego detailing the shots of beer followed with chasers of beer, the quasi-drunken game of Twister that ended abruptly when Clyde accidentally palmed James’ ass, and the forty minutes it took for Clyde to convince James it really was accidental. Fast-forwarding a few hours into the night, the celebration has dulled to a Neil Diamond concert, and both men are looking to hit the sack as they sit at the kitchen table, empty beer cans everywhere. And they both talk with slight drunken slurs, but I’m too lazy to spell it out for you. So, just use your imagination. Or else. Walkins: James, did I tell you about the letter I got today? Farwell: More panties? Walkins: No, not yet. I got this letter from someone called I. R. Sleecee, and he said he wanted me in a porno. James stops. He needs to think about this one for a second. Now, how many beers has he had? Eight? Nine? Who knows…it’s all a blur. His head aches, and he feels like he’ll throw up soon if he doesn’t go to sleep, but still he needs to know just what the hell’s going on, because getting drunk one night and missing something like this is just the chance Clyde needs to get molested. Farwell: Not on my watch, you won’t! Clyde sits there, stunned. Walkins: What? Farwell: You’re not going into porn, Clyde. Besides it being degrading to everyone except the people watching, and just plain wrong, what kind of experience do you have in that field, anyway? Walkins: Hey, I’ve had plenty of experience! Remember the time I was in that other porno, Clyde Does Dallas? Cut to a drunken Clyde somewhere on the streets of Dallas, staggering around during the middle of the night, a beer bottle in one hand and his crotch in the other. Stopping at a corner to take a leak, Clyde’s head lolls back as he makes a proclamation to the masses around him. Walkins: Ladies of Dallas, I’m going to do you! And then he’s promptly felled by a blast of pepper spray to the eyes. Walkins: AHHH! Back to The Forsaken. Farwell: I don’t think that quite counts, but thanks for playing. Feeling another onset of a migraine coming on, James opens a bottle on the table and pops a few pills into his mouth. Holding the palm of his hand to his head as he waits for the aspirin to take effect, James closes his eyes but continues talking. Farwell: You have a way of sobering someone up like I never thought possible. James slowly and painfully opens his eyes as the light from the kitchen pours in, cringing as he looks at a smiling Clyde. Farwell: Too bad the hangover comes with it. With James seeming to think the discussion about being in a porn is over, Clyde reaches into his pocket and produces the envelope containing the letter. Walkins: It says here they’ll pay me a thousand dollars a day to sleep with a bunch of women. James! A bunch! And they’ll pay me! Clyde is absolutely giddy at the prospect. Walkins: And all I have to do is sleep with them. I sleep all the time. Last week, I didn’t even get up Thursday. Just imagine how much money I could make if I slept more. James sighs, then pops another couple of pills. Clyde can’t honestly be that dumb, can he? Which begs another question in James’ mind: has the little moron ever gotten any? Does he even know what any is? Either way, he’s not learning it here. Not now. Not from James Farwell. Farwell: Clyde, I think you should go through with your demon hunting. After all, Devil May Clyde seems to be a pretty big hit. You killed something and won a title. How much better can it get than that? James smiles as Clyde ponders the prospect of running his own business, giggling as his mind plays out what could happen. But as Clyde thinks it over, James slowly reaches out and takes the letter from Clyde’s hands, burning it with a nearby lighter. And just as it burns away, Clyde snaps out of his daydream. Walkins: I’ll do it! Screw porn, I’m gonna hunt me a demon! Clyde throws a fist into the air and jumps to his feet, immediately falling flat on his face, unconscious. Farwell: Idiot. Putting his head on the table, James closes his eyes, and soon everything is dark again. Act V Walkins: James, look! Look at this! Forcing his eyes open, all James sees for the first few seconds is a blur of everything in the room mixed with everything else he’s ever seen. And then the headache hits him. Holding his head, James slowly turns to look at Clyde, scowling at the panic-stricken face on his tag team partner. Farwell: What is it, Clyde? Walkins: The abomination! Holding his science project at arm’s length for James to see, Clyde takes one hand off it and points at the screen, nearly dropping it in the process. As James struggles to see what he’s looking for, he spots it. The words have changed. It’s no longer looking for the signal. It’s found it. Walkins: And look at this. Putting the abomination down, Clyde produces a portable TV, flicking it on to a news station. Sitting behind the standard news desk, a reporter looks slightly shaken, but remains composed as he goes over the details of what happened. Reporter: Earlier today, we started receiving reports of strange objects hovering in the sky. They weren’t given much credibility at first, but in the past three hours alone, we’ve received reports of such sightings in Japan, France, Australia, and Central America. We’re afraid we don’t know much more as of yet, but I’m being told that we have the first documented sighting of one of these things caught on tape. Folks, we’re seeing this for the first time, just like you. Overtaking the image of the reporter waiting for the tape to play, a video of a group of kids flying model airplanes in a park is shown. The man behind the camera doesn’t notice it at first, but the blue sky in the background is quickly turning into a light shade of pink, darkening to a deep blood red in a matter of less than ten seconds. Confused shouts and people pointing at the sky accompany the blurry zoom on the discoloration, and then it disappears without warning. The shot cuts back to the reporter, who appears to be in awe at what he just saw. Reporter: L-ladies and gentlemen, as you can see from that video, these are no ordinary cloud formations. We do not know yet what has caused this, but we assure you, you will be the first to know when we do. Clicking off the television, Clyde sets it on the table and looks at James. Walkins: Coincidence? I think not. Farwell: Where the hell did you get a TV from? Walkins: I traded your Air Miles to some guy in a pawn shop for it. Farwell: I was planning to go to Tahiti next year! Walkins: Well, that’s neither here nor there. The important thing is, we need to figure out what’s going on. Now, I have several theories that I’d like to discuss with you, if you have the time… James doesn’t look impressed by Clyde’s sudden intelligence. Farwell: Cut the crap. Does your theory involve setting fire to whatever’s causing this? Clyde hangs his head, rubbing his toe into the ground. Walkins: Yes, sir… Farwell: Well, then that’s not as much a theory as it is a plan. But beyond that, you can’t just go setting fire to whatever you want. For all we know, this could be perfectly natural. Suddenly, a loud buzzing comes from the abomination, followed by a high-pitched unearthly voice. Abomination: Transmitting location…target acquired…EXECUTE! James and Clyde exchange nervous glances. Walkins: What the hell did it just execute? Farwell: I don’t know about you, but I don’t intend on finding out. Grabbing his coat on the way out, James rushes through the front door and into the driveway, looking into the sky as the same cloud formations start appearing all over the place. Just as James doesn’t think it can get any worse, Clyde runs out with the TV in his hands. Walkins: James, look at this! Holding it out for James to see, Clyde runs up to him and nearly shoves the television straight into his face. As James pushes him back a bit, he sees the same reporter on TV, now visibly frightened, stuttering through his words. Reporter: F-folks…a-as y-you’ve just s-seen…we’ve…we’ve been attacked. I-I don’t know h-how to say it any other way, but we’ve…been attacked. A few minutes ago, this shocking footage came in, and I just don’t have the words…I don’t have the goddamn words… As a replay of the video begins for the third time since it became public, James’ eyes widen as he watches a cloud formation start over a farmer’s field, but this time is different. Viewed from afar, the camera picks up a bolt of lightning coming from the middle of the formation, hitting the ground at the exact moment the formation disappears. The video cuts and then starts again, with the man filming now at the farm, the farmer standing in shock next to him and a hole in the ground where one of his cows used to be. Farwell: Wonderful. Just wonderful. This is even worse than that time I was one of those monsters in Onimusha 3. Cut to Genma-Farwell in that giant battle between the Oni Army and Genma forces near the end of the game, as James slowly sneaks up behind an Oni warrior. But just as he raises his sword, his head is lopped off and he’s sent to Genma Hell, amidst all the others who’ve died. Upon arrival, one of the Genma near him pipes up. Genma: Hey, how many of those Oni bastards did you get? I took down a good twenty or so before some asshole named Jacques blindsided me with a whip and I wound up down here. Farwell: Well…I…uh…didn’t actually kill any… Genma: Wow. That sucks. I mean, even Phil here killed three of ‘em. Beside the Genma, one of his brethren - who looks a little…‘off’ - starts slapping his hand against his chest and making strange noises. Genma: Now, now, Phil…no need to get excited. Phil: Genma! Genma! Then he starts hitting his head against the wall. Genma: Whoa. Calm down there, big guy. Phil: GENMA! GENMA! Phil starts hitting the wall harder with his head, now screaming at the top of his lungs. Genma: I SAID SHUT UP, YOU GODDAMN RETARD! Phil the retarded Genma finally settles, sucking on his thumb as Farwell’s eyes look at the ground in shame. Genma: So, as I was saying, Phil here killed three of ‘em. Now back to The Forsaken. Walkins: Don’t worry, James. With my theory tossed to the side like so much discarded Wookie, I’ve come up with a new plan to save us all. But it involves doing the one thing you never swore to do again. James’ pale face shows his fear. Not at whatever’s coming for them, but at what he has to do to stop it. What no man should ever have to do. Act VI Farwell: Clyde, I don’t know if I can do this… Walkins: C’mon, James. You do want to save the world, don’t you? Farwell: Not really, anymore… Walkins: Nonsense. Now, it’s time for the kickass intro scene every good superhero gets! Cue "I Never Told You What I Do For A Living" by My Chemical Romance, accompanied with swiping shots of black boots, red boots, black suit pants, blue and red tights, black gloves, webbed gloves, black tie, white shirt, black suit jacket, red and blue jumpsuit, black sunglasses, webbed mask. Secret Jamesy Man. Clyder-Man. Farwell: I feel like a fag. James looks down at himself, clearly unimpressed with the suit. And that’s not even mentioning the strange plastic look it has. As well as the paper sunglasses Clyde made for him. Walkins: Oh, pish-tosh. You look fine. Now, be honest…is this not the sexiest package you’ve ever seen? Hands on his hips, Clyde proudly leans back and lets the full view of the enormous bulge in his pants be shown. James just lifts an eyebrow in response, causing Clyde to finally hang his head in shame after several seconds. Walkins: Clyde’s a fraud. That’s not all him. An odd look on his face, as if he doesn’t know exactly what to do, James goes to put a reassuring hand on Clyde’s shoulder, but pulls away at the first chance he gets. That chance being the doorbell. Little does James know, however, that a shocking revelation is behind that very door. Little does he know, the person ringing the doorbell is none other than the sister he never knew he had. Wait…that was the shocking revelation for next time, and I ruined it. Damn it! Why, oh why, do I always do this?! I’m gonna lose my job, man…my boss is gonna freak out when he sees what I’ve…wait a minute. I’m the boss. That leaves me in a quandary of sorts. I screwed up the hook to keep people reading, but I can’t reprimand myself, or the only employee in this twisted factory will be fired. And then who’ll write hilarious Forsaken adventures for you to read each week? Not Angelo Deville, that’s who. And not his damn dog, either. Act VII Farwell: I have a sister? The woman standing on the doorstep looks quite a bit younger than James, but should at least be a year older due to the death of his parents as a child. Hmm…that’s odd. Oh well, too late to change it now. Woman: You do. When I was born, our parents were too poor to raise me, and gave me up to a family that- Cutting her voice off, a manly robotic tone sets in. Robo-Voice: Kept me in cryogenic stasis for years. Woman: And that’s how I learned ninjitsu. Farwell: I…see. Woman: By the way, my name’s Heather. Heather Knightjesus. James’ eyebrow lifts for about the thousandth time in the past week. Farwell: I…see? Just then, Clyder-Man walks up to Secret Jamesy Man and puts his arm around his shoulder. Walkins: What’s going on, James? Heather gives a confused look, slightly taken aback by two men in costumes seemingly holding each other. Heather: I can come back later, if you’re busy… James quickly pushes Clyde off him, anxious to get away from the little creep. Farwell: No, please. Come in. Heather: Thank you. I was sent by Grand Master D to aide you in your quest to stop the oncoming invasion. We have much to talk about. As she steps into the house, James shuts the door behind them and Clyde claps his hands in glee. Walkins: Yay! I’m always up for a threesome! Heather and James look at each other, each giving the same ‘weirded-out’ look in reaction to Clyde. Ignoring Clyde, Heather takes a seat beside James on the couch, but soon gets up and moves to a nearby rickety chair after sniffing a few times. Heather: As I was saying, my master sent me here to help you in your journey. So it has been foretold, and so it shall pass. Our end of days comes. James suddenly has a very serious aura about him, and looks his sister straight in the eye. Farwell: Who? Who are they? Staring James in the eyes, Heather takes a breath in, swallows, then slowly looks up to the ceiling. Farwell’s eyes follow, and they both sit there in silence. Until Clyde breaks the silence by taking the chandelier with him after attempting a web-swing from the staircase. To be continued…
