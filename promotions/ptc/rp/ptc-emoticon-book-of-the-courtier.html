Book of the Courtier - Intermezzo I (Courtier Courtroom)

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Emoticon was known to Global Wrestling as the Soul of the Poet. As a hopeless romantic. As a chivalrous man. Thus, it might have been surprising that he wasn’t dreaming about the medieval era. Oh no, he was dreaming about, well, you’ll see… “Next defendant!” An authoritative bang of a gavel followed the equally authoritative statement. A massive courtroom loomed before Emoticon, who stood in the doorway gazing up at the man clad in a black robe with a white powdered wig. “This is case #56975 on the docket, Infinite Gauntlet Round Five, Primetime Central vs. Emoticon,” the bailiff said, eyes focused on Emoticon. “The honorable Uber Judge presiding.” “Welcome to my courtroom, Emoticon!” boomed the justice, voice strong and confident. “You are charged with having the desire to reign victorious in a confrontation between yourself and many of your peers, in which the winner will be granted the opportunity, at some later date, to face myself in a battle for supremacy as Infinite Gauntlet Champion. How do you plead?” Emoticon blinked slightly at the Uber Judge with big, blue eyes. The robed Infinite Gauntlet Champion sighed. “This is a metaphorical dream. It is based on the fact that should you win Round Five, you will be facing me in round six for my championship.” “No, no,” Emoticon managed. “I figured out THAT much. But do you have to be so verbose? It’s kind of off-putting.” “Well, yes,” admitted the Judge, sheepishly. “I am the Uber Judge, a divine figure when it comes to grammar and word usage. I ask again, how do you plead?” “Errr..victoriously, I suppose?” The wig nearly came off of the Judge’s head from the shaking. “No, no, no, that won’t do!” he exclaimed. “This is a courtroom! Stick with the theme. Guilty or not guilty?” “But if I plead guilty to wanting to win,” Emoticon reasoned, “we’d be going to the sentencing phase of the trial. I’m assuming we want to have an actual trial, since this IS a dream, yes?” “Well, yes, I suppose so. Very well then. I shall change the course of the dream.” “You can do that?” Emoticon asked, more than a hint of awe in his voice. “Of course I can,” the Judge responded. “I have creative powers.” With a wave of his all-powerful hand, a group of men appeared in front of the judge’s podium. All were clad in suits, and Emoticon recognized them immediately. “That’s Rich Rollins,” he said, voice cracking as he looked at the GCW Hall of Famer. “And Jolt’s Ambassador Champion, Ken Kaze. And--” “Yes, yes, Emoticon, no need to state the bloody obvious,” the Uber Judge chided, but good-naturedly. “Essentially, here’s the new plan.” The man known to the wrestling world as The Renegade stepped forward. Nervously, Emoticon braced himself for battle. Much to his relief, Rollins walked right past him, settling into the prosecutor’s chair. “Mr. Emoticon, you will take your place on the witness stand,” the Judge commanded, for his word was gospel. “Each of your fellow competitors will cross-examine you in turn.” “All of them,” Emoticon asked, looking at the relatively long line of his fellow Infinite Gauntlet challengers. Uber Judge looked down on the competitors from up high, and pondered the question for a moment. “Well, maybe not ALL of them,” he admitted. “I doubt you’d be able to understand Ken Kaze anyway.” “HEY!” bellowed the odd competitor, “THAT’S NOT VERY NICE! HIIIIYYY-YAAHHH!!” With George the Trashcan in hand, Kaze charged for the Uber-Judge. He raised his mighty hammered steel weapon high, preparing to crash the trash receptacle on the bewigged head of the Primetime Central ex-employee. Of course, about two seconds before impact, the Uber Judge waved his hand, and Ken Kaze disappeared. “Your powers again?” Emoticon asked, in disbelief. Compared to the Uber Judge, being a chivalry obsessed romantic seemed quite normal indeed. “Of course,” came the nonchalant reply. “I am the Judge Over All Creation! Such things are nothing to me.” “And people say I’m egotistical,” said an unbelieving Emoticon. With no other option, he rose, walking the short distance to the witness stand. Chivalry’s Last Stand gazed warily at the oversized gavel the Uber Judge wielded, before taking his place in the chair. “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, so help you God?” As Emoticon looked up, the bailiff was there, carrying a Bible. He extended it out for Emoticon to swear upon, but Love Actually shot a dubious look to the master of ceremonies. “Can’t we skip the formalities? This IS a dream, after all.” “Objection overruled,” said the Judge, firmly. “I am a creature of formality and habit, after all.” Emoticon looked defeated. He hated unnecessary formality. Believing in love and emotion, rigidity and logic were concepts he had distaste for. “Fine then,” he said, placing his hand over the good book. “I do.” “Excellent then. Mr. Rollins, the witness is yours.” The GCW Hall of Famer and former UWF Television Champion rose to look Emoticon in the eyes. Rollins’ gaze was piercing and unflinching, making the Soul of the Poet more than a tad bit nervous. Speaking with the authority and weather-beaten voice of a veteran of the mat wars, Rollins had but one question for Emoticon. One simple and pointed question. “What in the world makes you think you can beat me,” Rich asked. Emoticon almost appreciated the man’s bluntness. “I’m a GCW Hall of Famer, and you’re a guy who wears white face paint.” Really, it was a fair question. Rollins was battle-tested and time-hardened. Emoticon had wrestled thrice. Rich Rollins was a strong, well-known competitor. Emoticon was not. But the more he thought about it, the question answered itself. “Because, Mr. Rollins,” the unique competitor responded. “I lack your name and your pedigree. Thus, I must fight harder for what I believe in, to obtain recognition. Because like yourself, I know adversity.” Emoticon returned the frosty stare Rollins gave him. As one who highly valued love, he could not imagine the pain in Rich’s heart; the pain that came from having one whom he loved more than anything in the world leave him. But at the same time, he knew of adversity, and he didn’t hide behind a name. Well, he DID…but not really at the same time. How about he just explains it? “For my entire life, I have been judged and persecuted for believing in love and chivalry. We live in a world where women are treated as objects, where fifty percent of marriages end in divorce, and where American government seeks to deny those who love one another the right to union. I compete in a career which has an especially large number of male chauvinist pigs. I face conflict on all fronts in a never ending battle.” Rollins kept a bored look on his face, as if wondering when the Hell Emoticon was going to get to the point. Noting this, Emoticon decided to pick up the pace. “What happened to you is beyond my comprehension, Rich. Having the love of your life leave you must have been catastrophic. Grieving is understandable, Renegade. But what you have done…to yourself, and to others, is not. You hide behind the persona of the Renegade.” “But you hide behind the face paint and the ma--” “Not quite.” Emoticon had anticipated the question, and was prepared with a response. “I may wear paint and a mask, but I am who I am, with it or without. The Renegade and Rich Rollins are two different entities. Emoticon and I are one and the same. You hide behind grief and create a tough persona. Let yourself experience emotion, allow it to permeate you and make you who you are. I will win because I am true to myself, and you are not.” “But--” “No further questions, Mister Rollins,” insisted the Uber Judge. “I haven’t fini--” “NO further questions, Mister Rollins.” The Uber Judge spoke with the authority of a man who had repelled Rollins’ challenge twice. With no other choices, Rollins sat back down. “No further questions, Your Honor.” The defeated Rollins took his place back in the line. “Anyone else wish to question Emoticon?” The Uber Judge looked down the line. James von Doom was busy plotting evil plans. Sebastian Moraco stood contemplating his future. The others remained silent. “All right then,” the Judge said. “I suppose we can allow you your closing statem--” “Hold on.” All eyes snapped to the back of the courtroom, where a man sat calmly with both of his feet on the back of a row of seating. “Clinton Sage,” was all that he said, rising to his feet and walking to the dais. “You are out of order, Mr. Sage,” came Uber Judge’s predictable response, but Clinton didn’t give a damn. He was in the match, and that was good enough for him. A confident smirk adorned his face as he approached Emoticon, going eye to eye with the masked competitor. “How’s it going to feel,” he asked, “when I splatter your blood all over the ring? When I crush your dreams and shatter your ambitions?” Emoticon glared at Sage, letting his anger seep through for the first time. “Confident, aren’t you?” he asked, mockingly. “Damn right I am,” Clinton responded. “Good. I prefer it that way.” Emoticon hopped over the railing of the witness stand to put himelf on an even keel with Sage. The two newcomers in the Infinite Gauntlet stared one another down, twitching with the desire to attack, to go at it in the courtroom. “Order in the court! Order in the court!” The Uber Judge uselessly banged the oversized gavel. Fear tore through his heart. This was a situation he had no control over, something his obsessive-compulsive mind could not take charge of. “QUIET!” both Emoticon and Sage screamed, at the same time, turning their ire to the Judge. “When I finish with him, you’re next!” Sage promised. “Eep!” went the Uber Judge, who fleed the podium, running from the courtroom dragging his robes behind him. That left Sage. And Emoticon. And Ken Kaze, who somehow phased back into the room. And James von Doom. And Moraco. And the others. And as the brawl spread throughout the dream courtroom, a few things were clear. The Uber Judge was on borrowed time. Every single person in Round Five believed in the bottom of their heart they could win. It would be good versus evil, man versus man. Emoticon, in his mind at least, was good. The rest? You be the Judge.
