WOTY 2005 Hype

26 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The phone woke me again at eight in the morning. "Mr. Shipley, I'm calling from Primetime Central - I'm delighted to inform you that your name has been put forward as a nominee for PTC Wrestler of the Year 2005!" On any normal day, my heart would have raced to my mouth and my eyes starred over as the words rang again and again in my head. Wrestler of the Year... Wrestler of the Year... and this just a week after finding that I'd gone from nobody to not just a fan favourite but THE fan favourite in AWC, picking up the People's Choice award at AWC's Quarter 2 Awards. An incredible achievement, having come into AWC as a shy, antisocial student and only gradually having won over fans by the somewhat risky method of being myself. On any normal day, I would have proud to pride's extreme, proud that I'd found my niche at last, proud that I did have a gift other than the social handicap otherwise known as mathematics. On any normal day, I would scarcely have believed my luck. But this, as it happened, was today, and today wasn't the best of times for receiving news of an accolade as woolly as it was conclusive, as irrelevant as it was important. Kate Barnard had gone missing. Tony Aliso had left a note. They could be anywhere by now. She could be seconds from death and I wouldn't know. And on top of all that I was expected to show up at AWC's Winter Warfare event in Cologne later today and put up a defence of my Frontier title; not any old defence, but an entertaining, sometimes shocking, sometimes spectacular defence; a defence worthy of a twenty-dollar pay-per-view. I had a flight I didn't want to catch. I didn't want to do or say or change anything, because it might jeopardise Kate's chances. I could be flying into danger. I could be flying away from any chance to help her. And now they wanted to tell me that I'd been nominated for Wrestler of the Year. And today, as it happened, was today, and I didn't care, and I didn't want to talk to anyone, and I didn't want to hold up the line in case the police tried to call again, and I didn't want to do anything except nothing. "Bye," I grunted, and docked the receiver, because today, as it happened, was today.
