Wrestling Purgatory

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 *DING DING DING!* Seymour Almasy had heard the bell ring to end wrestling matches...the exact number escaped him, but suffice it to say it was a Hell of a fucking lot. Sometimes he was pleased at the end of a match. Other times he was rather displeased. This time, though? He looked...crushed. He crawled to his hands and knees and moved about like a lost fawn, tentative at first, looking around for the official. "Hit the Ground" by 6-gig began to play for the capacity crowd, and well, Seymour knew that song wasn't his theme music. Almasy made his way over to the official, hopefully holding up two fingers, but no, he was met with a firm three in response. At that moment, the referee looked into the eyes of the stunned Seymour Almasy. Stunned wasn't the right word, really, the PTC official thought. There was something in the eyes of the Final Fantasy that he'd never seen before. The official had counted three more times than he could count in his years as a wrestling referee, but this was different. The look in Almasy's eyes wasn't the angered look of a guy who thought he'd been screwed by a fast three count. It wasn't the look of a guy who'd been caught unaware, rolled up for the one-two-three, and now was trying to process that fact. No, this was the look of a man for whom losing hadn't even occurred as a possibility to. Kaden Calix versus Seymour Almasy versus Jared Sykes was supposed to be exactly what it had turned out to be -- a war between three guys hungry to advance in the most prestigious tournament ever. Almasy had expected that. What he hadn't expected, it seemed, was the result. "I'm telling you, it was a three count! One, two, three! Just like that! Fair and square!" The official knew it had happened, but the skepticism, the doubt in Seymour's eyes almost made the referee question his own judgment for a split second. But it had happened. The three count had been registered, and it meant that Jared Sykes was advancing in GTT7, not Seymour Almasy. Seymour nodded, but even so...it still didn't look like he believed it. It was as if Almasy's logical mind knew the result, but something deep down inside didn't comprehend its deeper meaning. Perhaps more to the point, it didn't want to comprehend that deeper meaning. The crowd cheered for the efforts of the three warriors, but Almasy acknowledged them only with a simple wave of his hand. For a moment more, his eyes remained locked with those of the official. As if guided by something else, Seymour finally turned away, and slid out of the ring. His walk up the ramp was somehow unfocused and yet steady at the same time. His outward mannerisms betrayed disappointment and lethargy, but he clearly knew where he wanted to go. Behind the curtain was his destination, away from the eyes of the people who had seen him fight one of his best battles ever only to come up on the losing end of the stick. Jared Sykes had been the better man, simple as that. And Seymour knew that. He could accept that. What he couldn't accept were the ramifications of that three count. Disappearing behind the curtain, Seymour made a sharp right turn down a hallway. What had started as light sobs soon burst into full blown tears that paralyzed him, forced him to drop to his knees in the middle of the hallway and simply endure, simply cry until there were no more tears to come. For those cynics who walked by as Seymour sobbed, stepping around him and chuckling at the over emotional wrestler, Seymour had but a single question. What would you do if you had lost a chance to save the world from evil? ---------------------------------- "I think," Jonathan Calver said, as he spoke into his cell phone, "that we've got a big problem." On the other end of the call was Dr. Eric Evans, the man who had hypnotized Seymour Almasy into the belief that the match he'd just lost would be the end of a chance to save the world. "What's wrong," the doctor asked, a sense of dread filling him. To put it mildly, he had never done such an ambitious hypnosis before. Any number of things could have gone extremely wrong. "Seymour is extremely upset," the agent said. "I had to have him taken to the hospital for an evaluation. He's physically fine, but was in an emotional state that I feared he might do damage to himself." A pause followed, before Calver dropped the important piece of information. "He lost in GTT7 tonight. I thought that when he lost he would forget about the hypnosis, doctor. Yet, he was extremely upset because he felt he'd blown his chance to save the world." The initial reaction on Evans' end of the phone was panic. But panic didn't solve problems, the good hypnotist knew. And so it was the second reaction, a frantic rummaging through one of his filing cabinets, that produced a possible result. The doctor pulled out Seymour's case file, looking for one very important piece of information. "Give me a moment, Mr. Calver. I'm looking up the exact words I used to hypnotise your client." Before coming to the conclusion that he was a failure as a hypnotist, Eric Evans was going to make sure that there wasn't something that was being missed. The devil of life was so often in the details. Coming across the words, Evan read them aloud into the phone. "First, you will forget this conversation when your time in GTT7 has come to an end." Jonathan Calver remained quiet for several seconds. "And his time's come to an end. I don't see how this helps us!" "Is there any way you could be wrong about that?" The question caught the agent off guard. "What do you mean?" "Before we assume that I'm terrible at my job, Mr. Calver," Evans insisted, "lets look at the wording specifically. His time in GTT7 has to come to an end for the hypnosis to break. Has it come to an end for sure? Does losing mean that things are over? Is it...double elimination, or something? I don't know how the tournament works." "No, the tournament isn't double-elimination," Calver snapped back. "Seymour lost, that means he's out of GTT--" The silence that followed made Dr. Eric Evans more than slightly nervous. On his side of the phone, however, Jonathan Calver had come up with a possibility. One that he felt very, very silly for not having thought of earlier. "Playins," Calver gasped. "There are usually playins in these tournaments. Seymour had the chance to be one in GTT6, but couldn't make it in the long run. Almost every major PTC tournament has had playin spots go to deserving eliminated competitors." "Does Seymour have a playin spot," Eric asked, the question suddenly of the utmost importance. "I..don't know," Jonathan admitted, "they haven't been made official yet. He might." The doctor scratched his chin. "Has Mr. Almasy competed in lots of PTC tournaments?" Jonathan couldn't help but chuckle. "He has, yes. He's noted for being a tournament whore. He's competed in every major tournament in PTC since...somewhere around 2003 or 2004?" The hypnotist had begun to pace through his office. "So he knows how things work. That means he knows there's a possibility of getting a playin. If so...perhaps his mind has treated his time in GTT7 as only being truly over once he knows he hasn't gotten a playin spot?" "That's a possibility," the agent agreed. "Seymour loves tournaments. He takes being eliminated from them extremely hard. And he has been offered playins before...perhaps it's a coping mechanism." Calver frowned at that thought. "A coping mechanism that's making things worse." The doctor could appreciate that fact. "I...can't even imagine what it's like to think you've failed to save the world, when you believe you have the power to do so." Evans thought for a moment, and something popped to mind that he thought the other man might need reminding of. "You...could always break the hypnosis yourself, remember. You know the words." That Calver did. The words were "Game over! Don't tease the octopus, kids," which was a ludicrous quote from Ultros, an octopus boss in Final Fantasy VI. Silly though the words were, they did hold a rather serious meaning. "I've thought it about, doc. Thought about it more times than I want to admit in the past day. I hate seeing Seymour in emotional agony like this. But the more I think about it, the more I realize that I have to see this through. He'd want it that way." This entire ludicrous hypnosis plan had been Seymour's vision. Up until its potential derailing at the hands of Jared Sykes, it had seemed...perfect, somehow. Almasy was happier than he'd been in years, Calver believed. If he said the magic words, the agent knew, Seymour would be back to where he started: a situation desperate enough that he had come up with the idea to be hypnotised into a ludicrous belief for the sake of giving him something to hold onto, something to believe in. Besides, if Seymour got no playin, the entire plan would be over, anyway. Why not wait and see what would happen? "Thank you, Dr. Evans," Calver said, breaking the silence between the two men. "I'm here at the hospital with Seymour now. I'm going to go back in and check on how he's doing." Calver clicked the call off before the hypnotherapist could respond. Pushing the phone back into his pocket, the agent was suddenly aware of his environment again. He hated hospitals; hated the white everywhere, hated the sterile smell that permeated the halls. It was beyond him how anyone could sign up for a profession that had as a consequence considerable time spent in a hospital room. That was why, in spite of being a lifelong wrestling fan, Jonathan Calver wore a suit and tie and represented wrestlers instead of becoming one. Well, that and the fact that he lacked an athletic bone in his body. Rounding a corner, Jonathan counted two rooms before he came to the proper one. Stepping inside, he found Seymour propped up against his pillows, sitting up in bed. He was clutching his BlackBerry like a long lost lover. Gone were the tears and worries that had characterized the Final Fantasy for the past thirty-seven hours, replaced with a new resolve. Replaced with...hope. "I'm not finished just yet," Almasy said, the corners of his mouth turning up in a small smile. There was, Jonathan Calver knew, only one thing that could prompt such a reaction. Salvation had seemingly come for Seymour Almasy. The emphasis, however, was on the word "seemingly." For as Jonathan Calver read the message Primetime Central had sent arguably its best known superstar, the agent was more tempted than ever to say those magic, hypnosis breaking words. -------------- Backstage, before GTT7 Round Two: I'm sitting--no, standing in a locker room backstage, in a situation I've never faced in my entire life. I'm in my gear, ready to compete, but I don't know if my number's going to be called or not. The last time I was given a playin spot, it was a guarantee: a match against Killean Sirrajin that I didn't make it to. This time, though, my fate is dependent on everyone else. Thirty-two people comprise round two of GTT7. For my story to continue, I need one of them not to show up. Well, technically, I need two of them not to show up, if Impulse accepted his chance to be a playin as well. Belltime is a few minutes away, and here I am, trying to keep myself psyched up and ready for combat. My name could be called at any time, or it could never be called at all. I throw a couple of shadow punches. It's purely for the purposes of keeping loose; I don't think I've thrown a punch in an in-ring match for years. My body is tuned for something like this. I keep myself in great shape, but will it be enough? Will my years in this sport be enough? You can't prepare for thirty-two different opponents. It's an impossibility. I know some of the people in the tournament. I've watched Alexandra Pierce wrestle. I've seen Nova ply his craft. I know what Jonathan Rhine can do. And, of course, I know what Jared Sykes is capable of. Wouldn't that be something if the Blackberry didn't show up and I ended up against the guy who took me out of the tournament? Unlikely, but I can't say I'm not thinking about it. And then there's Tempest, who I know of, and I might have seen wrestle during GTT3 once. Maybe. Jonathan's looking at me, and I can't understand the look on his face for the life of me. It's almost like he's optimistic and terrified at the same time. "Are you okay," he asks me, as he paces back and forth. He's worse than I am in that regard. I get nervous sometimes, but nothing like my agent does. He's told me I'm going to give him a heart attack one day. He might be right. "As okay as I'm going to be," I shoot back. My nervous energy is being worked out with shadow boxing. I throw in a few kicks too, since, really, I'm going to actually USE those in a match if I get to compete tonight. The expression on his face isn't getting any better. Not that I'm surprised. He considers it his job to worry about all things Seymour Almasy. I want to tell him he's got nothing to worry about. The weight of the world's not on his shoulders. It's on mine. I've already failed once. Jared Sykes prevailed over me in a match that I thought I couldn't lose. What hero falls before the first roadblock in his path? It would be like an intrepid knight's journey ending thanks to some nameless troll under the first bridge he comes across. But that sort of thing happens in the real world. Great men are killed by those who do not understand their causes, or who simply refuse to acknowledge them as just. Noble warriors are stopped on their journey before it truly gets started. My life is a story, but it is not a fantasy tale, no matter how much I wish it was. I always speak with grandiose words. I've talked a good and flowery game my entire career. I've backed those words up on most occassions, but it's the times that I don't that I remember. I would trade every victory I have earned for one more chance to do something truly meaningful, to get one more crack at saving this planet from the evil that waits for it in GTT7. My opponent tonight, if I have one, may be one of those evil individuals who wishes to establish their dominance through this tournament. It may be, like Jared Sykes was, a just man who finds himself across the ring from me. A warrior who is tasked with saving the world must be an intrepid and brave enough competitor to defeat anyone in his path. Tonight, that is literally the task before me. Any of thirty-two opponents could be my task. I embrace that trial, should I have to face it. The alternative is something I do not wish to contemplate.
