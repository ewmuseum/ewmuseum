Title: Uno Prologue The oil painting was riveted to the smudged brick wall, slanted in its angle, compromising the truth that it had once cost nearly a thousand pounds. It hung there in mid-air, years on end, untouched, unwatched. Until one day, it would be remembered as a sign of despair, as a memory glanced upon in the form of tragic imagery. He stood flat on the broken tiles, staring unnervingly at the portrait of a woman whom he once believed he had forgotten, healed from the jagged edges of soulful pain by the soothing years of time. But sometimes, not even time could restore oneself from the self-destructing feeling of damnation. His creased fingers ran lightly against the smooth surface, attracting dust against it like the pull of an iron core magnet. The crystal blue in his eyes glazed, enveloping his troubled mind in the virtual world of once beautiful memories. He managed a smile, more of forlorn than that of delight. "Found what you came here to see?" Mrs. Hudson spoke from across the large living room; exasperation loomed in apparent fashion from her tone. She was the house agent for the now-vacant house, with the price plummeting in recent months, as the victim of a murder tangle involving the owner. It was a Sunday, and she was woken up from her deep sleep in the early dawn by a call from a stranger, who wished to view the house. By now, she was already sure that he didn’t come there just to offer a price. "She’s beautiful, isn’t she?" He turned his head, glancing his soulful blue eyes at her, fraying her nerves a slight touch. She ignored his question, brushing it aside with no clear concern. "Can we leave now?" she replied, "I have an afternoon meeting with a client." "She was my wife." Her eyes widened in shock, taken aback by what he just said. "What do you mean? What’s your name?" she spoke harshly, crossing her arms across her chest. He smiled, giving the painting a final glance. He turned in her direction slowly, smoothing over the creases on his leather jacket with his rough palms. "It’s apparent that I have no further use for you." She didn’t reply. She never did. She couldn’t. A sharp sound of the hammer striking the metal rang through the air, attracting the attention of a few passer-bys. It was followed in quick account by a loud thud. And then, everything else was crimson red. I The glass was stained a glassy grey, dimming the reflection that illuminated from it. She circled her index finger along the circumference of the round frame, creating a soft screeching noise too soft for the mortal ear to hear. She beckoned him to step forward, with a slight motion of her head as her light brown hair flopped over her forehead. She frowned a slight touch, creasing the fair skin that encompassed her frail body. A heavy load was lifted from her soul, but in turn, it was replaced by another. "Are you sure you got it done?" she enquired cautiously. A smile formed on his lips, as he sauntered forward unopposed, placing himself firmly in front of her. He slipped his left hand into the pocket of his dark colored jeans, as his right slowly relieved his black leather jacket of its unwanted creases. "Yes," Dylan replied shortly, shrugging his shoulders. "A life is like a glass, you know." She whispered, stroking the smooth surface slowly, unnervingly. "It’s unimportant, up till the point where it is finally in the main picture." With a swift movement, she pushed out her wrinkled index finger, tipping the glass over the table and onto the floor. It shattered into a million pieces, the piercing sound reverberating onto the walls. "And before it can make an impact in this world, it is gone." She smiled back at him, gazing intently into his ocean blue eyes. Dylan let out a short laugh, brushing off her words with a nonchalant shake of his head. The real estate agent that he killed two days before had been, in his opinion, one of the easiest kills in his life. After all, she was selling the house that he used to live in. The walls within which his wife was murdered four years ago. His heart flinched a beat when he recalled the eternal minutes that led to her death. "What is my next assignment?" He enquired, heaving a sigh which released him from the depths of his nightmares. And it wasn’t just a nightly issue, he was living in it. She smiled, again with the same innocent touch to her complexion. "This will be a simple one by your standards." "What’s her name?" Her eyes widened. "How do you it’s a ‘her’?" She questioned, puzzled. Dylan wasn’t able to answer her. He tried, but he quite simply couldn’t come up with a definite solution to her question. He just knew. "Her name is Amy Matthews. It must be done in exactly a week." She went on when she received no reply from him. "Where does she live?" "Detroit." That was fine by him, more than just fine. But somehow, he felt uneasy. There was something at the back of his mind. It felt wrong, just quite out of place. It couldn’t put a finger as to what it was. Detroit. That was the city in which he was born. Now he’s going back to the town where he grew up. And Amy Matthews, is a girl whose name sounded distinctly familiar. II He woke up, drenched in his own beads of sweat. His head was throbbing, a bad headache forming confidently, ripping his mind into strips of pain. Pulling himself out of his stained white sheets, he crawled in his bed, desperately trying to reach the cord that will pull the curtains apart. It almost blanked him out, blinding him with an intensity that his mind could not handle. The rays of the morning sun made him reach for his Sinflex which were scattered on his wooden side table. But it was the pills that caught his attention. It was the two hands that ticked by uncaringly, making his world flow by him without him being able to stop it. The ring from the telephone took him away from his gaze, gripping his head like a vice. He pinched his eyebrow, trying to wake him from his slumber. Steadying himself by clearing his throat, he slowly picked up the receiver. "Y-Yeah, Bryan here." He muttered, his acting skills failing him once again. "Who’s this?" He waited a second, and then another before he heard a familiar voice on the other end. "Where have you been these past two days?" the girl on the end shouted, enraged. "I’ve been looking all over for you." "Amy? What do you mean where I’ve been the past two days? I thought I just saw you yesterday." He replied, trying to sooth out his headache at the same time by taking a quick shot of vodka. "Are you out of your mind?! Today is Friday. I last saw you on Tuesday. What the hell happened to you?" He wasn’t sure. In his view, she was just plain crazy. "So what is it this time?" He ignored her questions. He wasn’t going to complement her weird state of mind today, not in his current state. "There has been a murder. A real estate agent this time." She replied. "Where was she murdered?" "How do you know it’s a ‘she’? How come you seem to be always able to guess all these weird things right?" He just knew. But he couldn’t tell her that, she would think that he’s out of his mind. "I read it up." "But you didn’t read up that she was from Chicago?" He took another shot. "Chicago. Sounds a nice enough place for a short visit." III Luther pulled on his boots, pushing his feet firmly into place. His lapses of memory had become worse lately. He pushed back his light blonde hair which was covering his crystal blue eyes, and clenched his fists into a big ball of flesh. "Are you ready?" David shouted from across the room. Luther simply nodded his head. He knew he wasn’t in the best shape at the moment, he couldn’t even recall what he did for the past few days. His condition had worsened, but he simply couldn’t accept it. David trudged across the room, standing in front of Luther, a concerned look on his face. "You haven’t trained for the past whole week. Are you sure you can do this?" he asked, placing a hand on Luther’s shoulder. "I’ll get it done." He replied, without a touch of concern in his voice. In fairness, he couldn’t care less. It didn’t matter if he died in the ring. His life was meaningless, and ending it might even be something he was looking forward to. "Chandler Tsonda is an opponent that you don’t want to be messing with in this kind of shape; you know that as much as I do." David went on, a sense of urgency in his tone. "Have I ever let you down?" But Luther knew that he had, on many occasions in the past. It wasn’t his fault, he kept telling himself. His troubled past led him down this road, a road he never did ask for. His wife was murdered a few years ago, his only son ran away soon after that. And now he had nothing left. "Let’s get back to work," David replied. The Chicago air never really suited him. Right now, the smell of it just caused him to flinch with disdain. "I will win this match, don’t worry about it." "And the tournament?" "We’ll just see what happens next." David just shook his head; his motivational skills seemed to let him down his time. This boy was in no mental condition to compete, but he couldn’t tell him that. In truth, Luther never let him down before when it comes to winning matches. He always rose to the occasion when it mattered the most. Hopefully this time, it won’t be any different. "Put the past behind you, Luther. It’s not going to do you any good." David whispered, trying to place some sense in Luther’s head. "The past will always stay, no matter what you say David. It always will." "Maybe this tournament can be your road to salvation, Luther." "Maybe." With that, Luther Ryxn rose to his feet. With a soft smile, he rubbed shoulders with David as he walked past him with careful steps that echoed through the walkways. David turned, gazing at him with a forlorn touch in his eyes. He slowly shook his head. "You deserve your salvation, my friend. And one day, redemption will beckon your way." He whispered, deep under his breath. Subconsciously Luther smiled from across the room, his ocean blue eyes glistening under the dim white light of the gym.
