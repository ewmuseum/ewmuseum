Tickets

25 October 2006

Date: 2006-10-25 It was Monday. Three days gone by since the night that Steve Watson’s ring dreams came crashing down around him. He still wasn’t quite sure exactly where everything went wrong. He had game planned it all so perfectly. To the last minute detail. Steve had come in as ready as he had ever been for a match in his life. And High Flyer had done exactly as Steve’s extensively prepared scouting reports had dictated he would. He had sniffed out the holes in Flyer’s game and was well set to take full advantage of them. All he had to do was wait for it. And wait he did. He laid back, played it conservative until the inevitable mistake occurred – then Steve did just his game plan had dictated and pounced the instant he saw it. It was just seconds later that he was the one get his shoulders counted to the mat. 1 - 2 - 3. Steve had wrestled the most technically proficient match of his life and – still – he lost. How was it possible? Maybe High Flyer was not quite so predictable as he thought. Or maybe it was he who had just a step ahead of Steve when Steve though he had been the one leading the dance. All he knew was that he blew the biggest chance he had ever been given to make the big time. And, if he ever got another shot, he would learn from this experience and check his ego before deciding he could fully and accurately predict the unpredictable. If he ever got another shot. Steve’s wife had shown heretofore unseen levels of compassion and sensitivity after the match; tending his wounds – both those of body and of ego – with great care and genuine concern during the course of the entire weekend. But, deep down, Steve knew that no small part of her was overjoyed that his silly "Quest for Glory" had come to a quick and timely end and that he could now get back to the business of being the loving husband and faithful provider that she had married. As Steve stepped out of the shower, he caught a glimpse of his face in the mirror. The mat burn on the cheek bone just below his left eye was healing up nicely. If he was lucky, no one at the office would question it. But he had his excuse all prepared, just in case. This was the other reason Steve swore by wearing a mask to the ring - for he could only imagine the tall tales he would have had to come up with if the thick fabric hadn’t been there to lessen the severity of the abrasion. All-in-all though, Steve felt he had walk out of the arena looking less battered and beat up than a lot of the other first night "losers". Heck, he probably looked better than more than a couple of winners. Small solace, though, for a night that ended a lot less gloriously than his dreams and expectations. Try as he might to convince himself that life goes on, the fist in the pit of Steve’s gut would not leave him as he dressed, grabbed the toast and juice Elise had left for him, and walked out the front door to his BMW 5 Series sedan situated at the curb. In fact, he was so lost in his own head that he almost missed the envelope tucked under the driver’s side windshield wiper. It was damp from the morning dew and had probable been there since at least last night. Steve opened the envelope as he slid into the leather seat of the Beemer and removed the contents. What he held in his hand perplexed him. It was a pair of Giants’ tickets. Company box from the looks of them. Was this someone’s idea of a bribe? Anyone that knew the first thing about Steve Watson knew that he was a lifelong Minnesota Vikings fan…. Wait. The date was wrong. September 10th, 2006 – 8:30 pm kickoff against the Colts. That was the season opener, wasn’t it? Right – The Manning Bowl. This was odd. Hard to imagine a pair of these going unused. But there they were laying right in Steve’s palm, complete and unaltered as the day they were printed. As he pulled his car out of the development, Steve pondered exactly how these two tickets might have found their way to his possession; and by whose prompting. It had to have something to do with his investigation of the expense report discrepancies. Steve’s intuition told him at least that much. But he had emailed his suspicions to only a handful of names in upper management – and none of them would need to go skulking around in the dark to pass along such dubious and cryptic information. Or did they? During his short drive to the turnpike, Steve took a stoplight moment to look at the tickets more closely. They certainly looked legit, down to the watermark. But his was a hot hot ticket – heck, half the guys Steve knew would have given an eye tooth to get their hands on these ever since the NFL schedule had been announced all the way back in the Spring. The demand at Heckert, Bagley, & Dannon had gotten so extreme that Old Man Bagley had felt the need to institute a lottery distribute the company’s allotment of seats in their South Tower box. Tim Forbes had gone to the game, hadn’t he? Maybe Steve should check in with Tim to see who else might have been at the game; or, perhaps more importantly, who wasn’t. Steve reached for his cell phone; but, before he could dial, he was interrupted by the phone’s familiar salsa ring tone. Steve glanced down at the number. It was not one that he immediately recognized. "Watson." The GTT6 office? This was not a call that Steve had been expecting. "What do you mean second round match? I lost last Friday." This had to be a mistake. "No. Nobody told me anything." Or maybe it wasn’t. "I see. What kind of test? Like drug tests? You mean like steroids or something?" Steve had zero respect for anyone that would poison their body to get ahead. "No, no. Of course you can’t go into detail." Steve pinched himself just to make sure he hadn’t fallen asleep dreaming at the wheel. "Do you even have to ask? Definitely, yes – count me in. I am nowhere near too proud to accept a second shot at a chance of a lifetime." Nope. It really was happening. "Great! Then "The New Shit" Bobino, it is. I’ll be there. Steve hung up the phone and pumped his fist in the air. Well that was certainly an unexpected surprise. It is amazing how one’s fortunes can change in an instant if you live your life right. But there was no time to celebrate prematurely right now, Steve all of a sudden had another match to prepare for. During the rest of his drive into the office, Steve forced his mind away from expense reports and mysteriously appearing football tickets {there’d be more than enough time for that once he was officially on the HB&D clock} and he pulled up from his memory everything he could about "The New Shit." Unfortunately it wasn’t as much as Steve had hoped. Although Bobino didn’t have the universally recognized name outside of PTC of someone like High Flyer or Dr. Curiosity, Steve knew that didn’t make him any less dangerous. Steve knew his foe currently wrestled in FUSE and had previously held belts in either XcW or XoW; or perhaps both. He knew he had been involved in trying to wrest the PTC Extreme Title away from Archibald MacGregor. But that was about it. If he wanted to be honest with himself, Steve had to admit that he really didn’t have the first clue as to what he was getting into this week. And it was his own damn fault for thinking that he would not need to scout beyond the Eeyore bracket until the later rounds. Looked like a change in NetFlix cue priority would be in order as soon as Steve could get online. Before Steve knew it, he was pulling into the garage of the Trio Building and parking in his designated space. He got out and headed to the elevator. Bobino would likely be a tough nut to crack, but Steve knew he could do it. But then again, he thought he could crack High Flyer too. "No, Steve", Watson told himself, "Don’t do this. Don’t give in to doubt. You can’t function on your maximal levels unless you have full confidence in your abilities. You can do this. You will do this. They would not have called you back if they didn’t think you were up to the task." Steve disembarked the elevator on the twelfth floor and walked through the familiar gold trimmed, glass door adorned with the HB&D pyramid logo and the partners’ names in large Roman print. He looked to where Peggy sat at her usual station behind the front desk – a position that she had held since back in the days when it was simply Heckert-Bagley Associates with a store front on North Eighteenth Street. "Peg, would you be so good as to have Tim Forbes swing by my office as soon as he gets in." "Oh, Steve, you didn’t hear," Peg’s voice hinted at her distress, "Tim is in Intensive Care down at Mercy General." "Oh my God," replied Steve, "Will he be okay?" "They’re not sure yet?" "When did…?" Steve started to ask, but stopped mid-question. "Last night," said Peg, "Some lunatic ran him right off of Route 432 as he was coming home from Champps after the game." "Wow. That’s horrible," said Steve, "Did they catch the guy" "Not yet;" replied Peg, "License plate was blacked out. And according to the witnesses the driver was apparently wearing some kind of mask." "A mask, huh?" "Yeah, a gold and black mask," said Peg. "You know the kind that those professional wrestlers sometimes wear." Steve knew exactly the kind, for he had one just like it in the glove box of the vehicle he had just parked downstairs. A sick feeling came over Steve as he asked Peggy to send a vase of flowers to Tim’s bed on his behalf and headed to his office where he was to remain undisturbed. Whatever he was into was serious now. Whoever he was chasing clearly did not want to be discovered. Even if it meant mowing down an innocent man to send Steve that message.
