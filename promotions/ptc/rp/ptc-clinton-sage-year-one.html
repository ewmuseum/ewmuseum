Clinton Sage: "Year One"

24 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * * * February 11th, 2007 “If you think this is over, you’d better think again.” Nathan Hollinger drove his forearm against the throat of Clinton Sage, trying to cut off the blood to his brain. Sage’s eyes were wide, not out of fear, but surprise. It was the first time these two men had locked eyes in well over a year, and considering Nathan had no idea how he got into the Sage mansion, the first thing he saw was Clinton Sage driving a hard left hook into the jaw of his wife, Isis. Sage’s eyes were twin black holes, the iris matching the dark pupil at their center. They seemed to rip all the humanity out of anything they set their sight on, and judging by the current situation, they had done just that. Sage muttered through the chokehold, “We’ve escaped death.” Nathan was baffled. The last thing he remembered was being in a hospital in Denver, after having the word “SAGE” magically etch itself into his skin. Quickly looking down, the wound was gone. So were the others: the shattered kneecap, the broken teeth, and the burned palms. Everything that had manifested itself onto his body was now wiped clean. “What do you mean?!?” Nathan screamed at Sage, pressing his arm harder into the neck of the asshole. An eerie calm washed over Clinton Sage as he began to pass into unconsciousness, “The same bullet killed us both.” And then he was out. Slumping underneath the pressing arm of Nathan Hollinger, Clinton Sage’s body went limp and the dead weight was nearly impossible for Nathan to hold. Still confused as to what was going on, or for the better part when this was going on, he turned away from his mortal enemy and surveyed the room. Two dead bodies, one in the doorway and one near the foot of the shattered glass cylinder. An unconscious and hefty man, wheezing from whatever injuries he may have sustained, was on the floor to the right of Isis. His wife, his poor wife. Her jaw was swollen and she was rolled onto her side. By her feet was a shiny black rock that glimmered in the lamplight. Dropping Sage’s unconscious frame to the floor, Nathan Hollinger quickly raced over to Isis and frantically tried to make sense of the situation. She was unresponsive, but had a pulse. That bastard had knocked her out cold. For the second time in his life, Nathan Hollinger woke up and didn’t know where he was, when he was, or what to do. The first time, he was surrounded by 155 dead bodies on an airplane that had come to rest on the Philadelphia Airport’s runway. Amnesia had wiped away any memories he had up until that fateful day in August of 1998, but right now he could remember everything from that point until now. He remembered being charged with those murders, serving the 6 years in prison until somehow Clinton Sage proved that he was innocent of those crimes. He remembered. He just didn’t know what the fuck was going on now Nathan turned and hurried back to Sage’s body, reached into the pocket of his pants and yanked out whatever car keys were in there. Stepping around the broken glass with his bare feet, Nathan scooped up Isis into his arms and made his way out of the mansion, stepping over the twice-shot corpse of what looked to be an FBI Agent. Get the fuck away from here, and get Isis some medical attention. Figure the rest out later. * * * * * Clinton Sage woke, slumped against the wall of his shrine to Nathan Hollinger/Spartan. He didn’t know how long it had been since Hollinger had choked him out against the wall, but he knew one thing for certain: The Hollinger’s were gone, and right now there was two dead FBI agents on his floor and one unconscious and corrupt policeman starting to come around as well. Poor Reggie Hanes, an innocent soul who thought he could better the life of his daughter by taking Clinton’s money. With the plan quickly firing in Clinton’s mind, it would be Hanes who saved Clinton himself, and not the future of his daughter. Racing to his feet and frantically running through his mansion, Clinton Sage stormed out through the front door, turned and noticed that his silver Corvette was missing, and then ran around the side towards the futuristic looking helicopter that the FBI agents had arrived in. He raced up to the door of the pilot, slamming it with his fists in a mock showing of fear. “Help! Help! Your buddies have been shot, Dead God open up!” Finally sliding open the cockpit door, the pilot looked down at Clinton Sage in disbelief, but it soon melted away into a grave realization when he noticed the splotches of blood on Sage’s clothes. The pilot immediately radioed for backup, and then once again turned his attention towards Clinton, “What happened, exactly?” “This local police officer, after the two FBI guys were questioning me, he stormed in and shot the old and wrinkly FBI agent, and then in the shuffle with Agent Hamlin, both men were shot and wounded. I think only the policeman is alive, though, it’s not looking good.” Once again, the pilot radioed for backup, this time sounding more urgent. From behind the visor, the pilot could be seen scanning the surrounding area. He had obviously seen the Corvette speed off down the driveway, “Who was in the Corvette?” Clinton Sage couldn’t figure out how to explain to the FBI Agent that it was Nathan Hollinger, a man who had died nearly three months earlier, “That was my butler, I sent him off racing for help before I noticed this…chopper…sitting here. Did you radio for help? I don’t know if they’ll make it!” Clinton was proud of how cheesy and scared he must appear. If the pilot could only know what truly happened and how he’s pinning the murders on Reggie Hanes, Clinton was sure that the pilot would give him a pat on the back and a nod of the head out of pure respect. After all, the plan was genius. After all, Clinton Sage had thought it up in about three seconds. And the best part was, is that Reggie Hanes did shoot one of the FBI Agents, so it wasn’t technically a lie. The pilot looked down at Clinton and reached to pull the door shut, “Step back from the chopper, sir.” Clinton did as told, and within a half-minute, the rotors were up to full speed once again (and deathly silent, which still amazed Clinton) and the chopper lifted off of the ground. In less than a minute, it was out of sight. Clinton figured that the FBI’s participation in this wasn’t supposed to be divulged, and he had to back out immediately. But there was no doubt in Clinton’s mind that there would be dozens of police officers and other FBI agents making their “first” appearance in a matter of minutes. Finally having a chance to rest, slow his thinking down, and dissect the last half hour of his life, Clinton Sage was left wondering exactly one thing: When Isis Hollinger held that fucking meteorite, she obviously wished for Nathan Hollinger to come back to life and save her. Now it was time to see if the injuries that Clinton received would still manifest themselves onto Nathan, just as they had done up until his “death” three months earlier. * * * * * September 11th, 1994 “Happy 20th birthday, sweetie.” Lauren Sage said as she placed the small chocolate cake before Clinton, who was sitting at the dinner table in their southeastern Pennsylvania home. “Thanks, mom.” While there weren’t 20 candles on the small cake, which would set the delicious little dessert afire, there were only two, each meant to represent a full decade of life that Clinton had lived. He leaned forward, brushed his long black hair away from his face, and blew the candles out in one simple puff. Lauren smiled at her son. She admired him, so full of vigor and life and laughing every bit of the way. She loved him with all her heart, and he knew it and reciprocated the love in turn. “I really wish your father was here to see this, Clinton.” Lauren’s voice trailed off at the mention of her late husband, Maxwell Sage, killed a year earlier in a hit-and-run, “…please tell me you’ll never drink and drive, Clinton. Please?” Sensing the worry in his mother, Clinton Sage stood from the table, towering over his own mother, and gave her a huge hug, “Don’t worry, mom.” They embraced for a few moments before he turned back towards the table and sat down next to his birthday cake. He took the plastic knife and cut the cake in half, placing half in front of his mother, and the other half in front of himself. “Mom, there’s something I need to tell you.” “As long as it’s not your affinity for alcohol, I’m sure I can deal with it.” Clinton clenched his fists for a moment before continuing. He hated to disappoint his mother, and over the last year, he had been secretly training to become a professional wrestler. It was only a secret more out of embarrassment than fearing repercussions of his mother’s approval, but nonetheless, he was still worried at how she would react. “No, no…it’s not alcohol. It’s just that after I’ve finished my classes at the University, I really want to pursue becoming a professional wrestler.” His mother didn’t flinch, scrunch her face, or do anything of the sort to show any disapproval. At once, the knot in Clinton’s stomach loosened, and he smiled. When she looked up, Lauren Sage could see the smile across her son’s face. “If that’s what you want to do, then who am I to stop you?” “You’re my mom, mom.” Clinton said, grinning at the ridiculous repetition of the word. The face of Lauren Sage went oddly blank for a moment, before she shook her head to clear the thought from it. Clinton noticed this, but didn’t pursue it immediately. Instead, he took a huge bite out of his birthday cake, especially enjoying the sweet icing. “So you don’t care that I don’t want to become a doctor? Or a professor? Or a magician or chiropractor?” “Well, the chiropractor would be pushing it…but I really do think you’d make a good magician. You certainly have a way with words to subtly manipulate people, your father always fell for it while you grew up.” “I miss him, mom.” “I do too.” Clinton couldn’t contain his excitement any longer, and stood up and placed a kiss on his mother’s forehead, “Thank you.” “Just do me one favor, Clint? If you do actually pursue this career of yours, with you keep your name? I know that some of those guys change their name to sound meaner or more honorable…but you’ve got such a perfect name, Sage” “I’d planned on it.” “Good, your father would be proud of you. I’m proud of you.” “I don’t recall you ever being this gung-ho about something I’ve done. When I bought that sport car last year, I thought you’d shoot yourself into space with how fueled by anger you were. And what was with that look you gave me a second ago?” Lauren Sage’s eyes became oddly distant once more. “There it is again, what’s with that? You feeling ok?” “I’m fine, dear. I just wish your father was here to help me with this. We had always agreed to be together when we told you.” Clinton Sage stood there, confused, “Told me? Told me what?” His mother’s eyes began to shimmer with tears, and she said a silent prayer before trying to gather the words. She knew they would dig deeper than any knife ever would, or any bullet or any punch another wrestler would ever throw at him. But they had kept it a secret for far too long. Out of stupidity. And now Maxwell was gone, leaving her to bear the brunt of this task. “Mom?” Clinton asked, the joy in his voice replaced by worry. She took a deep breath, stood, and placed her palms on the cheeks of her son, “Clinton…you’re my only son. My only son…but you’re not my son. Your father and I were unable to conceive for years before you, our little miracle, were brought to our doorstep.” His black eyes widened, and Clinton Sage didn’t know what to do. His body felt numb, and instinctively, he took a step back away from his mother. “Say something, honey. Your father and I have wanted to tell you for ages, but we were just afraid. Stupid and afraid. You two were always closer than we were, and I just wish he was here to help you understand.” “Understand?” “I’m so sorry, Clint. It doesn’t mean I don’t love you any less than I have for the last twenty years…but with you growing closer towards becoming a full fledged man with each passing year, especially now that you’ve found your potential career-choice, I needed to tell you. I love you, baby, I really, really love you.” Clinton Sage didn’t know what to say. He continued to backpedal away from his “mother” and bumped into the wall in the process. Looking down at her, he felt this wave of emotion wash over him, drowning out anything he had felt in the last day. He felt deceived. He felt lied to. Twenty years of love and adoration, all a lie. Twenty years of signing his name as “Clinton SAGE” and knowing that it wasn’t who he truly was. Lauren and Maxwell Sage couldn’t bear children, yet they pretended they could with him? Clinton Sage closed his eyes so tightly, he started to see flickers of white and spots of red. He needed to get out of the house before he exploded in anger. Turning away from the outreaching arms of his “mother”, Clinton Sage stormed out through the front door of their small home, never looking back. He could hear her sobs, he just wasn’t sure if he should care anymore. He had been honest his entire life, they were the ones who had lied. * * * * *
