A Shattered Soul - Part 1

4 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-04 A Shattered Soul - Part 1 He lay there in her bed in innocence, not realising what the repercussions of his decision to stay could have been. He was still young, after all, and sometimes the youth of today made mistakes. It was in their nature. Yet, as the twenty-four year old Tony Aliso adjusted his position and wrapped his arms around her warm body, he felt at peace. Like nothing in the world could or would go wrong at that moment. Bliss, a feeling not often coarsing through the veins of Aliso. Tonight was different, though. Tonight, Aliso was comfortable. Her perfume smelt like peaches, a scent Aliso had constantly been treated to for the past two hours as he lay there staring up at the ceiling, wide awake. He had been unable to get to sleep. The truth was he felt slightly ashamed, that perhaps his antics with the girl before she invited him in had given her the wrong impression of him. He knew deep down that he was no Mr. Lover-Lover. Hell, he knew he would probably never be. But he was nervous, as he usually was around women, and this nervousness had led to Aliso sharing the same bed as her somehow. Aliso threw his muscular legs over the side of the bed, sitting up. He got a slight shock from sitting up so fast and realised maybe it was a bad move. He stood up prematurely and almost fell over himself as he wearily approached the only bathroom in the one floor apartment, a notch down from Aliso's own property, to put it nicely. Aliso clicked the light-switch on and looked into his own reflection in the mirror. He almost jumped when he saw the state he was in - his hair everywhere, his eyes tired and displaying the effects of a night on the town and worst of all, a rough, unshaven face. “Damn,” he blurted out under his breath, making sure not to wake his woman. He fixed his appearance up as best he could and re-entered the bedroom. The wooden floors creaked under his feet as he leaned down cautiously and picked up his items of clothing he had removed several hours ago before he made love to the woman laying there before him. She looked so peaceful and he didn't dream of disturbing her in her state. Her right arm was tucked in at her abdomen while her left was propped up on the pillow where Aliso lay moments ago. He couldn't help but adore her. She was gorgeous and he knew it well. Still, he knew - even if he could have watched her sleep all night - it was time to go. Feelings of regret and guilt flooded into Aliso's mind all at once. He threw his clothes on and without further ado, left the apartment where the only sound was the steady sound of her breathing. *** The following morning felt like any ordinary morning only the feelings of regret and guilt already bugging Aliso like a small child looking for attention had become accentuated. Even he found it hard to fathom - how could he feel so attached to one woman after one night of making love? He had never in his life felt so bound, so attached, to anyone before. It was like he was chained to her, unable to escape her grasp. She was completely oblivious to it but for some reason, he thought she might know how he felt. At least, he hoped she did. He showered early and got on with his morning routine. It was unusual to be so pre-occupied while working out but today he was. He brushed back his gelled hair with his hand and shook his head as if to prevent an oncoming thought from entering his mind. Suddenly, it hit him like a closed fist: He had to call her. Find out exactly how she felt, if last night was really just a way to let herself go. It was his mission to find out. He walked out onto the patio of his extended garden which gave a view of the L.A. skyline that couldn't be beaten. The property had been a good investment. As he removed his cell phone from his pocket to call her, he hesitated. Maybe it was too soon? Had she even woken up yet? Finally, he went with the only thing he had relied on for guidance all his life - his gut instinct. After three long and painful rings in his ear, she picked up: “Uh, hi.” “Tony?,” she asked, groggily, “that you?” A palpable sense of shock took over his voice just as he was about to speak. For once in his life, he was nervous speaking to a woman. Since his early teens, Aliso had always been the rogue rebel with no fear, the guy who the rest of them looked up to. A role model, a man on a mission. As he clutched the phone in his sweaty right hand, he realised that maybe the Tony Aliso of old had taken a hike. “Hello? Anybody there or am I talking to the wall?,” she inquired, sounding a tad bit peeved with his prolonged silence. “I'm here,” he said awkwardly, “listen, I'm sorry I had to call you up at this hour, Becky. I just needed to talk to you about... about last night. I sort of -- ” She cut him off. “You know what, Tony? Don't worry one bit 'bout it. Last night was... fun. I've never had such a good time with a man before, you know that? You're definitely someone I want to meet again, Tony. Definitely.” He was slightly taken aback by her comments. She obviously thought highly of him, based on last night's ‘performance’, so to speak. He had always been a good lover, though. So had he had been told by many-a-women, at least. Now she wanted to meet up with him again. The second date was on the agenda. He pounced on the opportunity. “I had fun with you, too, Becky. Uh, you mentioned you might want to meet up with me again?,” he asked her, more confidence showing in his voice than moments before. “Yeah, maybe. Problem is I got some stuff to do over the next few days...,” she revealed. He detected a tone of uneasiness in her voice now. Perhaps she had other plans for their relationship? In the past, Aliso had fallen victim to a gold-digger, defined as a woman who only wants you for what you own and how much your wallet weights. Nothing more, nothing less. He didn't dig females who played that game and he sure as hell wasn't about to fall for it. He decided to take the safe route. “That's cool, Becky. Tell you what, I'm available over the next couple days. If you change your mind or find some free time, you give me a call, a'right?” He waited for her response patiently, knowing that this would be the move that could make or break the continuation of their relationship. “Sounds good,” she finally conceded, “I'll see you later, Tony.” “Okay, bye,” he finished, hanging up the phone before any other words could escape her mouth about the plans for the two. He had a strange feeling that his feelings of regret and guilt earlier on had just been his masculine instincts kicking in. The morning after the night before syndrome. Beating himself up like he had hadn't helped one bit. It was time to kick back and relax. He turned his head to the Los Angeles morning skyline, its beautiful outline stretching for miles. The red-orange sunlight bounced back off all the high rise buildings and to him, the view was worthy of the title of ‘picture perfect’. He tucked his Nokia cell phone back into the inside pocket of his suede jacket and stepped back inside to finish off his breakfast of scrambled egg and bacon. His calm was restored. *** Tony Aliso sat on his living room's leather sofa in absolute relaxation. Nothing and nobody could make him end this period of utter peace and calm. In the background, the soothing sound of classical music played, adding to the comfort. He stretched his arms forward and opened his eyes for a moment. He leaned over to change the track on his Sony 5-disc changer CD player when his phone chirped in his pocket. The interruption was an unwelcome one, to say the least. He grabbed the phone and saw the name on the display - ‘Becky’. He picked up after a moment of pondering. “Tony, hi. It's me, Becky,” she said, though obviously Aliso knew full well whose voice it was. “Becky, what's up? Have you changed your mind on the date, toots?” He added in the ‘toots’ not to insult Becky but rather because he was feeling so at peace that he could get away with anything right now. If he had the choice to backtrack, however, he would have. Not exactly the right way to refer to a woman you recently made love to. In fact, it came close to offensive in Aliso's opinion. “I have changed my mind, actually. I'm sorry about earlier; I sort of blew you off and that wasn't cool. I was thinking maybe, if you weren't busy, we could -- ” He cut her off intentionally, knowing in what direction she was headed. No longer was he cautious in his approach with Becky. Now, it was all about seeing her again. As he propped his feet up on the coffee table, looking like a man on top of the world, he couldn't help but grin. A grin that could have belonged to a billionaire. There was no doubt - Aliso was a content man. “Where'll I meet you, Becky? Oh wait, I've got the perfect spot. It's good for couples and it's not far from your place... so afterwards, you know...,” he trailed off, hoping her mood would mean more of what last night brought. She said nothing for a few seconds. A few seconds too long. “I guess... if you're a good boy,” she teased. Aliso jumped out of his comfort spot on the sofa and crept in the hallway, though he wasn't exactly sure himself why he was so spy-like in his movements. Maybe it was just his way of letting out the excitement of the eventful night ahead. He slapped on some Calvin Klein aftershave and checked his appearance, all the while holding the phone up to his ear. “Okay, sweetheart. I hear you loud and clear. I'll see you at Garrison's Restaurant; it's just off West Sunset Boulevard. How's nine o'clock for you?,” Aliso asked, calmly and coolly. Again, that strange silence for a moment. He wondered why she couldn't respond right away. Maybe she was having second thoughts about the rendez-vous? “Sure. That's good for me. I'll see you then, Tony,” she said and then hung up. “Bye Becky,” he said to himself as he tucked his cell back into his pocket. Aliso bounded up the stairs to get ready. He only had less than an hour, after all. For a self-proclaimed vain man such as Aliso, that wasn't much time. Aliso started searching through his wardrobe for something - anything - to wear. This, he predicted, was a night he would never forget. *** The follow-up date was always the clincher. If she stayed for hours chatting endlessly about the most insignificant of topics, you could safely say that she was your girl. On the flipside, if she eats, takes every chance she can to metaphorically slap your existence in the face and basically takes her leave straight after the meal, then that is one woman you do not want to be associated with. These were the rules of the follow-up date in Aliso's mind. When Aliso arrived at Garrison's ten minutes late after the abysmal traffic, he was quite shocked to find that Becky hadn't arrived yet. He parked his BMW up on the sidewalk, hoping that the cops would pay no heed to it, and got out. The night air felt cold on his tanned skin but Autumn had crept in quicker than expected. He double checked the area for her car - which he remembered was a '96 Ford Escort - but saw no sign of it anywhere. The streets were still buzzing, of course. Perhaps Aliso had just missed her car in his quick search. After all, he wasn't truly in his senses last night when he left her apartment. The car he saw outside the apartment could have been anyone's. He crossed the street and looked up. Garrison's Restaurant was located on the second floor of a five storey building which was originally used for storage of computer equipment. Back in 1995, the place had been transformed into Garrison's by the owner, Jimmy Randal. Aliso decided to take his chances and entered the building. He passed by a couple of suits who gave him an ugly look for no apparent reason. Then again, that was probably how they looked at everyone they passed by, so it didn't bother him in the slightest. All he desired now was a night with Becky. A night with an amazing woman. He trotted up the steps and opened the door to Garrison's. Garrison's was quieter tonight that usual. Saturday's were always busy no matter what. If there was a nuclear holocaust, you could still count on Garrison's being booked up on a Saturday. It was one of those guaranteed things in life, like dying. Aliso glanced around at the restaurant. Of the people who were there, about seventy-five percent were couples on a night out together. He envied them as they ate together and chatted, still puzzled as to why Becky was nowhere to be found. Somebody tapped Aliso on the back, surprising him. “Hey kid, your shoe's untied,” said the familiar, gruff voice of the owner of Garrison's Restaurant, Jimmy Randal. Aliso did a one-eighty turn and faced Randal. That trademark smile appeared on the owner's face as he gave Aliso a playful punch to the ribs. He enjoyed Aliso's company and had enjoyed it since he met him back when he was only fifteen. Aliso had no time for formalities now, though. He needed to find Becky. “Hey! Jimmy, you haven't seen a girl, have you?,” Aliso asked with an impatient tone in his voice. “You know,” he began, still grinning like a Cheshire cat, “I see a hell of a lot of women each day. You'll have to be more specific, kid.” Aliso glanced around one more time before nodding to Randal and stepping backwards to leave Garrison's. “Well, if you see an attractive girl, about mid-twenties... a brunette, give me a shout. It's important I find her, Jimmy,” Aliso informed Randal. Aliso didn't waste any more time, leaving Garrison's and running back down the stairs into the main lobby area. He remembered passing by the men who simply stared at him with deceit and hate moments before. They were suspicious. Still, it was probably nothing worth worrying about. He left the building through the automatic doors and looked up into the sky, as if he was looking for an answer from above. He nervously strolled back and forth along the sidewalk, contemplating what to do. Should he just give her the benefit of the doubt? Maybe she was just late and hadn't arrived yet? It was feasible, although unlikely. He felt frustrated as he rubbed his temples, massaging them gently. Then, the alleyway beside Garrison's caught his eye. The rainfall from earlier had left a series of puddles around the pavement. A few cigarette butts littered the alleyway, too, all of them freshly lit. It appeared that whoever had been smoking them was in no mood to finish them. Aliso stepped cautiously into the alleyway, sensing no danger. No danger whatsoever. Then he saw her. Innocent and alone, wrapped in a white sheet lying only a few feet from his position. Her skin, of the skin he could see through the dark, was sheet-white. Her hair sat covering her beautiful face, a face he knew he would never have the pleasure of touching again. He realised that she had been bleeding profusely from her abdominal region, but that the blood had dried. She was dead. Becky was dead. He knelt down almost involuntarily beside her and shook her to attempt to bring her back but to no avail. Her motionless, cold body lay there without any sign of life. He brushed her hair aside with his hand and stared into her eyes, now deathly still. He smacked the concrete harder than he ever hit anything or anybody in his life before. “No! No! Why'd this happen... why?,” he whimpered, at the verge of crying. A small sheet of paper slipped out of Becky's waistcoat onto the wet concrete. Aliso reached out with his tense, shaky hand and picked it up close enough to read in the half-light. Through his tears, he read it out loud: “Pull out from your shot at glory, Tony. Pull out from everything you love. Give up while you still can... Or this bitch won't be the only one who dies. End it, Tony. Kill your dreams... or else.” The letter made no sense to him now in his shock. It was handwritten but he didn't recognise it. He could have been reading a foreign language, for all he knew. He cradled Becky's head and felt himself become woozy and disoriented as several uniformed men rushed over to attend to him and the dead body of Becky. He heard no words from the men although he knew they were speaking to him. Aliso just nodded and accepted the help they gave him towards the cars, which he instantly recognised as L.A.P.D. squad cars. Aliso regained his wits, if only for a moment, and handed the letter to one of the taller officers. “Found that on the body,” Aliso began, out of breath, “I'm gonna find who did this to her... I promise you that...” Aliso was helped into the squad car by the same officer he gave the letter to. The officer perused over the letter himself and nodded, labelling it as important in his mind. Aliso watched helplessly from the backseat of the uncomfortable squad car as the L.A.P.D. officers zipped up the body bag. Becky was gone. But it was far from over for Aliso. Who wanted to kill his dreams? Why? What would they have to gain from it? At that time, he had no clue. All he knew was that he had to find who did this and make them pay the price. Aliso's felt like his heart had just been ripped out of his chest and fed to the proverbial dogs. As Aliso continued to stare blankly as the cops hauled the body bag in his general direction, he could only think of who had done this. Whoever had committed this crime wasn't only looking at a sentence. No. They were looking at a life of torment courtesy of a man out for revenge. To be continued...
