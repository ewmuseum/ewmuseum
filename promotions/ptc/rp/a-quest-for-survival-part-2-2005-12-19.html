A quest for survival (part 2)

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 Prologue Taking a gamble The office window was open just a few minutes before a brown haired man arrived. His hair was wet from the rain. His clothes were absolutely soaking wet. With this temperature, the water was almost ice as the temperature was just one degree Celsius. He handed in his yellow-coloured paper. The lady looked at him. "Are you sure, sir?" "Absolutely, ma'am," he answered. She took a few forms out of a map and gave them to the young customer in front of her. "You need to sign at the bottom of each paper," she said as she handed over a pen. "Sure," he replied and the papers were signed as promised. "What’s it for?" "Bet office policy," she said. He signed the papers like he was told. "So you have all you need now?" he asked. "Yes, the bet is taken," she declared after checking a few things. "Good," he sighed as he found it hard to hide his relief. The pressure almost was too much for him to take, but life went on and so did he. Chapter One Parts Unknown Alec was being driven to the location his father gave him. He had no clue what to expect. His father hadn't told him anything about his new teacher other than that he was more than capable of teaching him to control his emotions. This was considered a sign of distrust by Alec, but somewhere deep down he also realised he needed help in this match. Alec hadn't been wrestling against an opponent comparable to those active in GTT for a few months now. All his time was consumed by work, for his father required much of him. Alec never minded it because he knew he was the only heir to the company of his father. One day, Alec would be the king of his own world. At least he wanted himself to believe that. In his mind it wasn't wrong to dream, but it was wrong to lose sight of reality. To become successful, he discovered that you need to work together with skilled people. Therefore his relationship with his father could be considered good, even with recent events in mind. Alec didn't have divided loyalties like so many other people and, Alec figured, it made things so much easier. Naturally, he couldn't tell this from first hand experience, but Alec was a rabid reader and through the course of years he had been able to read a lot of books. Alec deduced and derived a lot from those books. Not that he had turned into a control freak or that he had become obsessed with treachery, but he had been able to develop a good common sense of what goes on an average mind. Most of the day-to-day problems people experienced weren't really one of his concerns. He had plenty of money and although he didn't have a relationship, he also didn't have the time to ponder about it. Finally, he arrived. The house was old, but nonetheless in a good condition. The garden wasn't paid all too much attention to, considering all the weed between the bricks. However, it wasn't a jungle by a long shot. Alec didn't pay attention to it. He walked straight to the door. He knocked on the door. No answer. Alec knocked again, this time around a bit harder. A loud crack could be heard. This made Alec wonder if the house was in such a good condition as he believed it was. Ah well, all eyes could be deceived, Alec thought. Again, he knocked. This time he knocked so hard that the door opened. He could not see what was in the hallway. Alec never held it for possible that someone could live in such darkness. He stepped inside, curiously awaiting where destiny would take him. Suddenly all the lights switched on. "Ah, our guest has arrived," a familiar voice hissed. This message frightened Alec as it sent chills down his spine. Footsteps echoed through the room. In a glimpse of luminosity, Alec decided to count the steps. The pace of the footsteps made Alec wonder how many people were approaching him. He was absolutely sure that there was more than just one single person coming his way. Alec was proven to be right. "Is he hiding or something?" another one asked. "That explains a lot," he added to his question. Alec turned around as the voices became very clear. At the same time, the two entered the hallway. Smiling, they closed in on him. Under the impression the two wore a mask, Alec shouted: "You're a bit late considering carnival has already occurred, you know." This appeared to be a mistake. "A mask?" he said completely serious. "Do you know who I am, Alec Lancer? If so, you're either very stupid to call my face a mask or you just want to die." His glooming eyes scanned Alec. They skimmed him entirely, but very quick and superficial. An uncomfortable feeling sneaked its way into Alec. "I don't have all day," he insisted. "DO you or DON'T you know who I am?" he asked Alec in a shouting manner. "No," was all that Alec had been able to put out. He was approached in an entirely different way than he had ever imagined to. He smiled in an unfriendly manner, exposing his teeth. "I already thought so," he told in a manner as if he confessed his darkest secret whereas in reality he told him an educated guess. "Before I tell you who I am, I ask you, and this time please give me an answer right away, do you know why you are here?" he asked, not removing anything from the mysterious aura hanging around him. "I am here to be trained," Alec fulfilled the man’s desire. "I am Clyde," he declared, plainly. "The man standing next to you is Dark Gimmick, my father. My wife and daughter Bethany is preparing for your first exercise, but you will be introduced to her later." "I can't wait to meet her," Alec said bravely in an attempt to sound friendly. Clyde revealed his right hand to Alec as he pulled it from underneath the sleeve. The flesh from his right hand was complete gone. Clyde laughed about Alec's instinct reaction to this. "You probably wonder how this is possible," Clyde said to answer Alec’s unspoken question. Alec started to recover from all those surreal surprises, of which none he liked, and regained some of his confidence about all the correctness of the knowledge he had gathered. "You've got that right," he replied. "You ask much from our patience," Dark Gimmick intervened in the conversation between his son and Alec, assuring his presence was noticed. "So stop with your perky comments." "As I said," Clyde continued his story, "you probably wonder how this is possible. I am no human, I am a demon." "I am no human either," Dark Gimmick interrupted again to the anger of his son. "Shut up," Clyde commanded his father. "I am telling our guest a story here before his first assignment. He must be prepared properly, otherwise it will be a wasted exercise. Since I am easily bored, it would be better if that did not happen." Waving his arms to add more tension to his story, he started to tell his message. "Once upon a time not so long ago, I had a match against this young man. I was bored by his long stories and failed to defeat him in a match as he took my title away from me." His father burst out in laughter. "It was the biggest disgrace of your career," he almost cried maniacally. "SHUT UP," he shouted. "Like any champion, I got my rematch. Of course, I had been able to win my match for I was the more calculated man. You see, this was not just a normal fight. It was a race against time too. You might have heard from it. The most infamous UWF match of the year… The steelwork foundry match." "The steelwork foundry match," Alec repeated fascinated. "Indeed," Clyde said unmoved although he had experienced the hell of it himself at great cost. Most people would still be at a total loss after having suffered so much. Not Clyde however. He was still able to compete. Alec figured if he could do the same. Clyde, the infamous demon, had been on top of PCW. He's known throughout the wrestling world as an unstoppable force. Everybody Alec had ever spoken feared him. His actions were so cruel and vile that they were almost impossible to comprehend with common sense. The incest he had with his own daughter was maybe the best example of this, but it also proved this was in his blood. Alec was intimidated by Clyde. His appearance alone was worthy of remembrance, but his legacy was almost surreal. The awe he had for Clyde slowly turned into greed when Alec realised what his father had been trying to do. His father wanted him to use Clyde as a stepping stone, merely a tool to quickly gain more experience. Alec didn't see any dangers, he only saw the advantages. Clyde's knowledge, Clyde's experience and Clyde's wrestling abilities were the perfect mix of skills to add to Alec's collection. "But tell me," Alec requested on this phoney nonchalant tone, "what’s my first assignment you spoke about just a few minutes ago?" "If I would tell you," Clyde responded seriously, "I would ruin the surprise." Alec was disappointed with this answer, but he would find out soon enough anyway. He decided it was best not to ask any further about this, for it could possibly arouse suspicion. There wasn't more left to ponder what to say next, because Clyde had something in store for him. "Follow me," Clyde instructed Alec. Alec looked behind him, and Dark Gimmick waved him goodbye. Naturally, this didn't comfort Alec at all and Dark Gimmick probably knew this. Mind play, Alec quickly realised. Not knowing what lies ahead, Alec's mind was filled with doubt. Clyde turned right into a basement. There he opened another door and Alec followed him into it, but he was unable to see what was in front of him. When he was inside, the hot air filled his lungs which made him cough and his eyes became wet. He couldn't see the environment, but he knew it was hot and probably toxic. After a few minutes, he had recovered and adjusted himself to the atmosphere. He opened his eyes again and couldn't believe what his eyes showed him. Chapter two Test The steelwork foundry. He never suspected to be here. Although he didn't know how he had gotten here, he still remembered what he had seen. Melted steel poured down from above into huge containers. Clyde ticked on his shoulder, urging Alec to turn around. "This is your first assignment," Clyde told him. "You will face the same problem as I have. Your task is simple. Defeat your opponent, and detract the key before the fire melts it. The key will give you five seconds to get out after you have removed it. I will not help you nor will I come to the rescue should you fail. You stand alone. Don't forget the key will be hot by the time you reach it." "Thanks," Alec said. And Clyde left the foundry, leaving Alec behind. Alec looked at the key, and he knew he didn't have much time. He had to climb a few stairs at least, but what worried him most was that his adversary was nowhere to be found. Maybe, Alec hoped, it was a test to see if he waited too long before he went up. This was a naïve assumption, which Alec should be able to see but hope is the mother of all thoughts. He started to climb the first stair. Proficiently, he started to climb the stairs at admirable pace. His condition proved to be alright, as could be expected from a professional wrestler. There were a few rungs missing, but he couldn't be stopped in the slightest from that. He climbed the second stair without any problems as well. Things were going good. Too good. Nonetheless, Alec climbed the third stair successfully and was now on the third stage. He was close now. The key he needed to escape was in his reach. It was still intact. Sure, it might be hot by now, but he couldn't suppress a feeling of joy before he had managed to finish this job. Suddenly, his eye caught a young girl. She had blond hair and this innocent look on her face. Her mouth was gagged and her boy tied to the iron pole. Alec could see in an instant she has been tied to this pole for at least a few hours now. He made the first step to the key. But could he leave the young girl, innocent and alone? There probably wouldn't come someone to this place until tomorrow and then it could possibly be too late. He did a step back. He couldn't leave her, now could he? She was ten years at most. Even though people considered him heartless, even he couldn't leave her behind. Alec was prepared to go far, but he wouldn't murder people. Alec shared this dislike with his brother, although for very different reasons. Alec considered it a sign of weakness. His brother considered it a sin. He started to unravel the ropes from the girl. He left the gag on her mouth, because it wasn't necessary to remove that immediately. The rope was competently tied around her. Alec had great trouble to untie it. While attempting various things, he didn't succeed at unravelling her. Then he realised it. This girl was tied to the pole by Clyde. His opponent wasn't this girl, as he expected at first, but he was his own opponent. And time of course. He stopped his attempts at unravelling the rope and immediately grabbed the key from above the fire. He burned his hand while doing so, but he was on time to save it. Barely, but he had successfully accomplished the task. The door opened and Alec ran through it. Chapter 3 Ready or not He was back in the hallway again, where Dark Gimmick was patiently waiting for him with a cup of tea and a cookie. Alec felt relieved for he experienced the last few minutes very intense. The adrenaline was flowing through his veins and his heart rate was twice as high as normal, but he could still feel his joy. "I don't know if I should congratulate you or murder you for leaving my granddaughter behind," Dark Gimmick proclaimed seriously. After giving it some thought, he added: "I can't say I blame you, because we would all have done the same." "I don't care about you or your family," Alec stated. "I am more concerned about my hand right now." Dark Gimmick retorted this statement quelled, "I didn’t expect this from you, Alec. So bitter. I hope you aren't disappointed. You can identify yourself with Clyde after all. A little, but still the fact remains you can." Alec put his hand up in the air. "My hand," he said, this time louder. "No," Dark Gimmick said, refusing to help. "I will not take away that scar. A scar will remind you of a lesson learned." "A cup of tea would do then," Alec sighed. He was very thirsty. His throat was very dry because of the hot foundry he had visited just a few moments ago. Dark Gimmick extended his arm out to Alec, so he could take his wanted cup of tea. Alec was amazed at the amount of steam damping from the tea. He was about to take over the cup of tea, before he pulled his arm back. "I don't need another scar," Alec told Dark Gimmick and implicated he had learned his lesson. "You're a quick learner," Clyde complimented Alec from behind. "I am sure your father will be satisfied with the help you have received." Alec raised an eyebrow. "Is that all?" he asked. "That's all," Clyde reacted briskly. "No more tasks?" he asked in disbelief. "It's all," Clyde said. "I've got one other question," Alec announced. "Why did you help me?" "You bore me," Clyde replied. "Your father can answer all those questions." "I think it's best you leave now," the little girl said. "Before I will get my revenge on you for leaving me there." She started to complain even more about it on a nagging tone. "I would have told you if you would have removed the gag. Besides, I am never going to do you a favour again," she complained as he looked up to Clyde. "Oh Bethany, now you remind me why I shouldn't have removed the gag," Clyde teased her a bit. Bethany slapped him in the face. Alec looked on to this in a state of utter amazement. He couldn't believe Clyde's daughter and wife was so young. Not until today, anyway. To prevent that any more events here would disturb him he left. He now knew what he had to do to survive. He needed to act relentless. It required him to ignore his emotions. In a quest for survival, only your own well being matters. Clyde didn't teach him anything. He only showed him the way. Alec Lancer had to walk the road of immortality himself. Epilogue Regret Tommy Lancer looked at another priest. His face didn't quite express the sadness he felt inside. "Forgive me father, for I have sinned," he confessed whole heartedly. "What have you done?" came the question, peacefully and calm. "I betrayed my own blood and flesh," he said remorseful. He paused for a moment. "I lied to my brother for my father." "You should never lie," the priest agreed. "But I can't imagine how that could affect you so much." "It was for money," Tommy replied, his heart broken. ~!------------!~ OOC: A special thanks to Josh for allowing me to use Clyde, Dark Gimmick and Bethany.
