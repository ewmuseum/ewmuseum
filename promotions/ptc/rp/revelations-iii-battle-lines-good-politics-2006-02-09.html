Revelations (III) - Battle Lines & Good Politics

9 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-09 All seems to be falling into place. Metcalf was a good man, well-meaning if not a visionary of the righteous path; his death is tragic but an unfortunate necessity. The day will come when he will be remembered as a saint for what he has forfeited in the name of the coming insurrection. The flock is getting antsy at this stage. I'm not an unreasonable man; I can identify with their fears and all of the restlessness I'm forced upon them. But as long as they're willing to weather the night, they will behold the glory of the dawn. I've kept them in the dark for so long. Surely a little longer can be forgiven. By the time I loose the shackles on my followers I feel the constraints of the past several weeks will be completely forgotten. Time may heal all wounds, but I consider power a far greater, and more efficient, remedy. I have the book, the chalice, the torch. And I have assembled the myriad of texts needed to carry out the ritual. I fully intend to sound the final trumpet of myth and ring in the new age of man, minus the plagues and pestilence that misled prophets once claimed would herald its advent. It won't be an age built on a foundation of dread and illusion. The Church and its many rival faiths have already proven the flaws on controlling the world through fear-inspired awe. What I bring into our world has no need to feed off the terror of a divided populace. God's mistake is his own, let Him wallow in the failure of His pagan collective. Just as I soon plan to shed my own mask, I will pull back the curtain on reality, exposing an ignorant humanity to what lies beyond the veil. It will be a time of great revelation, and even more incredible rebirth. Even so, a threat looms on the horizon. There remain enemies who would seek to spoil the renaissance and they cannot be allowed to persist. Their uprising will be soundly crushed. Not even ash will remain of their failed legion. Every empire needs to make examples ots insugents, no matter how benevolent the hierarchy may be. * * * Dawson peeked through the second floor window, tapping the headphones on his head to rattle their wiry innards and adjust the reception. Nestled in the crook of a tree atop a thick branch, he settled and listened in to the discussion. He hated revisiting the days of espionage, a skill he had honed to an art in his dangerous past life. It was the very thing that had caused his life to spiral out of control and bring peril to every facet of his social existence; Sage's death had come at the hands of vindictive agents of an underground culture gone mad, their plans foiled too many times by Dawson and his ilk. Old habits truly died hard. Chaney had recruited him for this reason, to revive the banker's 'side-project' talents and use them for his own gain. Not a doubt remained in his head about it anymore. He counted his blessings that he had opted to keep his eavesdropping equipment under lock-and-key for the duration of his recent and tumultuous past under Chaney. Through the static he picked up the first conversation. Re-aligning his receptors to eliminate interference, indistinct murmurs echoed in the foyer beyond the half-drawn curtains. "…Dawson and Beckett will act in our interest. Don't fret over their services aiding our enemy." Shadows of varying forms, one readily visible as the man who called himself Brutus, moved about uneasily through the brightly lit, high-ceilinged chamber. Maury could discern tones of muddled doubt uttered almost as a chorus from whatever shady delegation he met with. One passed by the narrow opening between the drapes clutching a brandy glass, a face little more inviting than Marin or Thatcher. He didn't seem softened by the blonde man's pledge. "How do you expect us to believe this? A simple visit and you swing their allegiance to our side? While brandishing a loaded gun no less? Nonsense! Surely you must be mad for holding faith in something so absurd, boy." A leathery hand tugged on the cord that reeled back the drapes, spilling light onto the open lawn beneath Dawson's position and finally shedding the scene of its veiled secrecy. He was beginning to feel like supporting cast in a Bond film between all the intrigue and covert alliances. An aged man further down the table leered at him, disbelieving. With a gaudy surplus of jewelry on his fingers and wrists, cigar jutting from a mouth crowned by a thick European mustache, Dawson could only liken him to a cross of Don Corleone and a Haitian voodoo doctor. Fashionable he was not. "Are you sure?" He rubbed absently at one of his thicker rings. "You weren't recognized? I've heard tale that Beckett is a mastermind in the ways of subterfuge. He might have seen through your charade all along." "Don't dispute my own talents," scoffed the one called Brutus. "No man can act so rationally with that much alcohol coursing his veins. He bit. His eyes told me." He tilted his eyes down as he plucked something unseen from his pants. "Not even Christian Beckett can manipulate what his own eyes can tell me. It was a part of my training. Besides…he was long removed from their ranks by the time I had eliminated my first target. Your sense of time seems pretty vague, Dumas." Maury cursed his limited view. More shuffling as those in Brutus' 'virtuous' commission repositioned themselves; none edged an inch closer to their double-dealing agent and Maury suspected him as being as much a pariah as his own guru back in Shields' mansion, even amongst his own. A man donning a tan coat with silver hair pulled back in a braid drummed his fingers on the table before inhaling a mouthful of chilled scotch. "You weave a fine web, friend. Perhaps he taught you a thing or two about politics after all. Hell, even we have speculated whether or not you're one of us." Smiling indignantly, Brutus seemed pleased. "I'm sure you do, Carlton. I'm sure you do." * * * I fear my presence has been discovered by some of those I had tried to avoid. The impudent dogs have monitored my activities since shortly after I began to execute my designs and seem to think I'm negligent to that fact. But then their arrogance always impeded their potency as leaders. They pay too much mind to frail and long-dead doctrines to be a real threat; beyond their quickness to solve problems through killing, I wonder how they have yet to ruin the empire they inherited. I had hoped they and the Church would wipe each other out by now. I regret that it took so long for me to flush them out from whatever putrid holes had hidden them this long. But the important thing is that they didn't succeed in their agenda. They want the tools I've accumulated and they want them in order to summon powerful demons into our world. They've undermined all that myself and my allies have worked toward. I find that most disconcerting, and obscenely foolhardy. All in their ranks will suffer the agony of a thousand deaths for the irreverence they seek to force on mankind. Now I merely wonder when their threats will translate into action. And if they have, in fact, learned anything of the art of war before they lead their soldiers against mine. I hope not; they don't deserve so gallant a demise. I much prefer to see them die like opportunistic, bottom-feeding rodents than any battlefield marytrdom they may endeavor toward. Whichever happens, they are deaths I take pleasure in visualizing. To savor any death that doesn't feed the cause is a sin, but one I can accept. Blessed by my name, the right arm of divine justice. Amen to the end of demons that may defile and destroy this creation. There will be ample time later to lament. Jigsaw's words surpass cinema. Yes, there will be blood. * * * "Why should we trust you? You've already thrust a knife in the back of the man you once called a teacher and friend. I believe I speak for this council when I say that you're as much a liability as an asset. We don't like wild cards…'Brutus'." He hissed the word mockingly, venomously. No one present appeared to have any adoration for the brash youth with eyes of cruelty-forged ice. The one calling himself Brutus flashed a scornful look of his own at the man in the tan jacket, the ringleader of this shadowy council. This was a business partnership, not a crew of Sunday beer-buddies. "I've provided you with all the intelligence you requested. I've sent some of your most powerful rivals to their graves,personally. I've aided in the funding of projects I considered a ludicrous waste of time, at your petition. I don't like you. But that wannabe savior is a greater threat to our existence right now. He has to be silenced lest we face all we've worked for being completely extinguished. Surely that alone is reason enough for you to extend to me your cooperation, if not your worthless friendship." Cigar smoke wafted through the room as the silver-haired host smiled wryly. There was an animosity between the pair that could be felt, but business came first in the face of the impending disaster that he suggested. That he guaranteed, however begrudgingly. "Can he be killed? How refined have you become you in the art of death, 'Brutus'?" "I've been tagged as the 'Angel of Death'." The blond deceiver appeared to be legitimately insulted by such a challenge. "If I can get close to him, his life is forefeit. Merely give me that passage, Carlton. Gas his watchdogs and then, with the kennel empty, I can end his ambitions forever. Watch those pups scatter to the winds without their chief to rally them." Dawson suppressed a sigh. He was shocked that he didn't remember the fiery youth from his days living that double-life he so regretted. Such egotism was rooted in more than simple arrogance or his treasonous friend would be dead by now. He was quite obviously an efficient killer. "I'll give you men. Some of my best. You lead them." "Your men are despicable and weak. Chaney will seen them dead long before they can face him. I go alone. The goons you employ are the worst kind of gutter trash and they'll only get me killed." He seemed unconcerned that his arrogance was so grating on the crooked fellowship. Carlton was flabbergasted. Rage flooded his senses at sight of the whelp standing before him, defiant, still so brazenly taciturn with his allies anxiously prepared to see him die. He was either noble beyond belief or a complete fool. The council chairman was willing to gamble on the former; the boy's relationship with Death could almost be considered a marriage, or at least a very productive business venture for the latter. He wasn't expendable. Yet. The silver-haired man's words were like ice again. An even-temper and a short memory were essential in his profession. "He doesn't deserve the book or any of the other components. His intentions have been perverted beyond any point of return. The crimes the man has committed against this organization are reprehensible and no apology would constitute his foregiveness. It sickens me that men will still follow him so passionately." "His words are gospel for many. The man has a rare charisma and those he can beguile into clinging to his words will follow him to the gates of Hell. Nonetheless, Beckett and Dawson believe Chaney to be little less than the devil in disguise. Fear makes men gullible. It was hardly a problematic to convince them of what they already believed. Dawson is ours, Beckett will be equally useful if he'll simply relinquish the bottle long enough to fulfill his obligations." Maury's reaction nearly sent the bulk of his equipment careening to the frost-covered ground below. He yearned at that moment for a sniper rifle instead of sound gear, bullets in place of audio cassettes; it had been years since he had called a gun his partner and he was feeling the itch again, listening to Brutus spout his treason. The web continued to be spun, but it wasn't clear who the spider was that was weaving its infernal net. "Proposing that Chaney brings paradise to Earth as he aspires to do, where does that leave us, huh? It leaves us as condemned, that's where. We'll be scapegoats for the order to punish. There will be no initates to continue your work, no territories to protect and expand. The light he seeks to empower will swallow the shadows we preserve, and everything that lurks in them. With the keepers becoming extinct, so will the gate to the other side." Dawson slumped down further, absorbing the man's every wicked word. Nothing could change. He had to play the consummate bipartisan, answering to both while never letting on what he had learned and trusting no one who didn't already hold his confidence. Any slip-up could spell a death sentence for all that depended on the caliber of his act. He couldn't fail again. He wouldn't. It was a greater burden than any man should be obligated to bear. * * * Security had been increased three-fold at Avery Shields' estate in the hours between dusk and full-fledged nightfall. A gibbous moon peered down on the vast property between shifting cloud patterns, the fields to the north blanketed in darkness one moment and lit by the heaven's diminished rays the next. The day's light snows glimmered like flecks of starlight on the dead and twisted weeds bristling through hardened soil, the view a chilled slice of Heaven for any sentimental poet. In contrast, a bonfire raged on the lawn just within the sprawling gates, built at the request of one Masquerade/Chaney, a challenge to what he knew huddled past those lights and waited for their guard to settle. Shields and his retainers had left the grounds earlier at his guest's urging, no arguments offered; their pact was firm if predominantly unspoken. Staring past the drapes of his chamber, he chuckled as he thought of Waterloo. Masquerade was better versed in the subtleties of war than any who planned his death. At least the challenge would keep his senses sharp, an element he'd need to complete his task as much as in the ring this weekend. "Marching to their own doom. It's unfortunate that so many must meet their end so prematurely." Thankfully, he knew the numbers culled this night would be very few. Assassins raiding in the night he presumed. He'd repelled their sort before. A rustling caught his attention, prompting him to round and face the doorway. There, clad in a polyester suit with green-and-black slashed necktie, Gregory Marin regarded him warily. His eyes were murky and suspicious as he slid across the carpet, casting a manilla envelope onto the sprawling bed. The mask secreted away the inquisitive look that passed over the face of his benefactor. "My apologies for the intrusion." He wasn't, it was ruse. Masquerade had come to know the mocking face of a liar. "Either way, myself and a couple of my my colleagues have outlined our plan to prevent this 'Shanty' from making progress. Perhaps you'd like to…" "I can defeat the man in the ring without help. I've been here before, I know the feel of a big-ticket match in a prestigious tournament. I don't need your aid in this matter, Gregory. Go about your business, you have other duties to attend to." Marin was taken aback at such a candid dismissal, but he was also fed up with the man's brooding style. It wasn't the time for being hermetic or self-enterprising. Too much was at stake in the coming days and it irked him that so much of it was beyond his control as a businessman. He kept his tone steady if a tad more hostile than he'd hoped. "We follow you because you promised us a world where we would reign as kings. If you succumb to a greater force now, our time and loyalties will be for naught. I have personally helped handle the matters of diplomacy that you claimed were beyond your reach as long as the mask remained. Thatcher has seen to intelligence gathering issues. Warrick and Klinger have gone into the remote parts of this country to retrieve the artifacts you charged them with recovering. Quite frankly, all that goes to shit if you lose this fucking bout." Masquerade detected growing impatience in Marin for the first time since his vows were spoken. While he was far from murderous, his dissatisfaction would have a breaking point eventally. Best not to see how far he could bend him. He glanced down at the envelope and scowled beneath the pallid mask. They had clearly plotted a way of sabotaging Jones' bid for victory in his tournament. The sheep were willing to renounce what little virtue they may have had left to benefit the shepherd. He couldn't stifle a laugh at the premise and cackled maniacally for a long moment, disregarding Marin's cold and almost baleful gaze. "I'm intrigued, Marin. Well then, let's give your proposition a once-through, shall we?" Hands donned in gloves streaked black and azure-blue plucked the bundled stratagem from the spread and thumbed through several paperclipped pages, a series of advised processes meticulously outlined in a report that could have been fashioned by a corporation. His retainers took underhanded to a new height. He had no desire to win this way. Curiosity would sit in Jones' corner come their reckoning, and defeating one of the past champion's headstrong charges would be another feather in the cap of a career envied by even those who loathed his existence. For Masquerade, Jones' eccentric patron was as much a target as the man listed on the card. He wouldn't taint such an opportunity by resorting to these tactics. A glimmer like that of a flashlight blinked to the north. It was zero hour. "Lock yourself in your chamber, Gregory," instructed the masked lord while pulling the gloves from his hands. "Bring Warrick to me but do not rouse the others from their slumber. Go join them in dreamland." He slid the plans into a drawer . Marin obliged, departing with a polite bow and closing the door as he exited Masquerade's grandiose chamber. Footsteps echoed down the corridor, a door closing. He stood at the ramparts of his castle, a warlord set to test the gods in combat if need be. Tearing the mask from his face and casting it onto the bed, he flashed a vivid smile that would disturb the ease of any man before breaking into a gleeful cackle that nearly shook the walls. The laughter that filled the manor was far from that of a savior. But in order to be a messiah, he would have to be a monster. Hopefully just this one final time. * * * There was long silence as the two figures paused in their long walks, one striding north and the other to the south. Their eyes locked, but neither turned to face the other. Much time had passed since they last conversed, and time didn't allow for that to end now. Both had a deadline to adhere to. "Kendall." A nod. "Brutus." A curt smile. No more needed to be exchanged between them. The aliases would suffice as long as the masquerade continued. Brutus gawked at the bulky parcel tucked under the other's arm. Gravely he nodded approval, painting the air with an obscure symbol while the other looked on with reticence. A curl of the lip expressed his thanks. "So that's it, huh?" breathed the youth called Brutus."Your ticket to Valhalla?" A nod. "It will craft the new world. Humanity will sound its thanks soon enough." Brutus trudged on, leaving Kendall silent and statuesque in his wake, book still cradled under one well-muscled arm. "Safe journey then. The road is straight ahead." The dark-haired man continued his trek through the field, aged and frozen brush crunching beneath his shoes like softened glass as a light snow began to fall. He ventured nary look back; as far as he was concerned, the passing had never occured. Just a brief waltz before the music died and the partners went their separate ways. A howling wind glorified their separation, rattling the trees and chilling him to the bone. The snowfall picked up intensity, carpeting his head and shoulders as winter's breath caressed his face from the west. More clouds, dark and ominous even in the night, began to shroud the skies as far as his vision stetched. A storm was coming. But never one that could postpone the final dance that loomed ahead. The masquerade was drawing to a close, and the concluding acts would shake the very heavens before the music stopped.
