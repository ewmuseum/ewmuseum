Final Fantasy The buzz is in the air. It's the 48th round of the Infinite Gauntlet, Primetime Central's never-ending series of matches. It'll probably go on for years as long as the interest is there. Four guys are competing. One of them's Jackson Rust. Sort of brooding guy. Dark hair. Elizabeth thinks he's hot. Not my type really though. Then there's James von Doom, the mad scientist of professional wrestling. Guy's been involved with more freaks than Dr. Frankenstein. If he wins, there's a chance that the crowd might riot in protest. Then there's the champion, Curt Cunningham. The less said about him the better, as far as I'm concerned. Nope, I'm not here for any of the three of them. It's the last one I'm here to see. Seymour Almasy. PTC legend wrestling what is probably his last ever match. "I will never understand your obsession with nerdy guys," one of my friends tells me, shaking her head disappointedly. "To be fair to her," another points out, "how many nerds do you know with a body like his? I understand the attraction. Rust's just totally hotter." "Sure, says the girl who spent most of her college years wearing black lipstick and listening to Mindless Self Indulgence," I retort. "Better than wanting to jump all over a guy whose idea of a hot Friday night is playing his PS2 until four in the morning." Ordinarily, I'd take this sort of thing lying down. But tonight, my hackles are up. "I'll pounce him when he gets out here. I'm pretty sure he won't be thinking about a PS2 with me draped all over him." "There is no way in Hell," Elizabeth tells me. "I'll bet you. $200 says that you don't jump that guardrail and make out with him in front of everyone in this arena. I know you." Ordinarily, she'd be right. Unfortunately for my dear friend, I know something she doesn't. The announcer kicks off the contest, running through the facts of the matter. I barely even listen to him. It's time for me to get ready. Who's going to come out first? Please be Seymour. Let me get this over with. And then, it comes. His music. From what I'd been told, he actually came out to real music now, this song by Muse that was in Guitar Hero III. I always was terrible at rhythm games, so damned if I can remember what it was. Real music, though, wasn't what I heard. It was, instead, the song. His song. The song he'd come out to all those years ago. I can probably sing it in my sleep. Go now, if you want it. An Otherworld awaits you. "Down, girl," I hear one of my friends say, and I bite back a chuckle. If they only knew. I'm not paying attention to them anyway. My eyes are focused on the entryway, waiting for him to arrive. There he comes now. His hair isn't quite as long as it used to be. Probably cut it somewhere along the line. Still that dyed white color he always insisted on. For someone who hated Sephiroth as much as Seymour did, Seymour certainly liked his hair color. Other than that, it's all the same. He's still got the sort of body that makes a girl's mind turn over ideas, still has a sort of boyish innocence in the face of world spanning atrocity. It's the look on his face that almost breaks my heart, though. It's like he's going through the motions, content to make one final appearance and then fade away to whatever he'd do in retirement. Probably World of Warcraft. Fortunately for him and his brain, it's not going to come to that. He drops to his knees in the aisle, pyrotechnics spraying everywhere. He can act enthusiastic all he wants, but he can't fool me. Not one bit. He never could. Seymour is blissfully unaware as he walks down the aisle, slapping hands with kids. Completely and totally unaware that he's about to get his life turned on its head for the umpeenth time. For once, at least, he won't be disappointed. Both of my hands brace themselves on the metal railing. My friends shoot disbelieving looks at one another, and the whispers begin. "She wouldn't actually, would she?" "Damn girl's lost her mind." Maybe I have. Maybe this IS an insane idea. But it's the only way. The security guard is looking the other way, probably at some girl showing her tits in the upper reaches of the arena. Perfect timing; the nerd is making his way around to my side of the ring. "It's now or never," I say, flipping my friends the thumbs up. "See you in a few." I'm up and over the guardrail before I or anyone else can talk me out of it. Two steps carry me completely into his personal space, and my arms fling themselves around his neck of their own accord. "What are you doing?" he sputters, before I quiet him in the only way I can. Truthfully, right now, I'd rather smack him than kiss him, but I'll take what I can get. Out of the corner of my eye, I see the once-distracted security guard, moving towards me with a couple of his friends. Realistically, I've got about thirty seconds before I get taken out of the arena. I have to say something to him, anything. Something that'll explain everything. My lips are a breath away from his ear, and I finally come up with something. Sixteen-thousand people in the arena watch as Jason Seymour Wilson stiffens, drawing himself to his full and admittedly slight height. One of the guards has a hand on my shoulder, but the Final Fantasy waves him off. "How," he asks, dumbly, trembling with the mere effort of standing. "How is this...you..." He falls silent, stealing glances at me as if trying to convince himself that I'm not an apparition. "When we get in private," I explain, "I'll tell you. Probably after I've smacked you, you git. This isn't quite the reaction I expected when I met you again." "I never thought I *WOULD* meet you again," he hisses, tears in his eyes and threatening to spill everywhere. "You were supposed to be..." He can't bring himself to finish the sentence. Poor thing. "I know, I know," I whisper back, soothingly, "but you have a match to win right now. After that, I'll give you the hows and whys of it all." "You say that like I'll be able to concentrate on the fucking match." Ah, some things never change. Most of me is different now. Jet black hair where brown used to be, but that can change. Couple of years older, slightly less curvy, but he gazes into my eyes and I feel just like I did when I was in high school, in his arms on the dance floor at the senior prom while everyone else chuckled at the two awkward kids who couldn't slow dance to save their lives. "You always used to be able to," I offer, pulling up one of the ringside chairs and taking a seat in it, to watch Seymour Almasy's final professional wrestling match. I cast a glance over my shoulder at my friends, who'll probably call shenanigans on me for making a bet with them that I was almost positive I could win. I know that I'm about an hour away from a rather difficult conversation. I also know that I'm about an hour away from having back the one man that, for all of his faults, ever loved me unconditionally. No, Jason's not perfect. He's more than a bit arrogant, he gets absorbed in his work too much, and he can't stand up for himself. But I love him anyway. I'm not exactly a catch myself. I'm impetuous, I had an accidental affair with his mortal enemy, and I faked my own death because I couldn't deal with the aftermath. But what the Hell, on August 10, 2008, it's finally good to be Laura Marie Winters again. I shouldn't speak for him also, but I'd imagine it's also not so bad to be Jason Seymour Wilson today either.
