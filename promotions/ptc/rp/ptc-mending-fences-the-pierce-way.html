Wrestler: Charlotte Ramone/Alexandra Pierce Title: Mending Fences the Pierce Way. ***** There once was a man from Nantucket. Just like last week, we're not here to talk about him. "Joe Bob!" We're here to talk about this jerk, hanging up his telephone as he reclined in a black leather office chair that was more like a throne. "Perfect" Raymond Pierce was getting up there in years, but his cocky grin hadn't faded a bit. He set both feet on the corner of a mahogany desk, his tasseled penny loafers tapping out a rhythm only he heard. The eldest Pierce, brother to the SCCW's "Spider in the Web", fished a cigar from his desk drawer, turning his gaze to the thin man in dark clothes opposite him. "You don't look so thrilled, Otis," Raymond said. The thin man pursed his lips, the expression not far from a grimace. "Just thinking, Mr. Pierce." The side door to Raymond's office slid open, and Joe Bob Smith's pudgy face emerged in the room. He cleared his throat gently. "You called, sir?" Ray flicked his gaze away from the scowling man to his longtime manservant. "Bring me my pants." "Sir?" Joe Bob's brows resembled nothing more than caterpillars climbing up his face. "I'm uncertain why you need pants when you're clearly, ah... clearly wearing them." "Do you know how many times I won the World Heavyweight Championship?" "Eleven, sir." "Thirteen if it was two!" The self-styled "Impeccable Warrior" jabbed a finger into the air. It was neither thirteen nor two, but both the men in the room served Ray long enough they knew it was pointless to argue the point. "Yes, sir. Sorry." "Don't worry." Raymond was as magnanimous as he was arrogant. "It's easy to get lost in the annals of my greatness. Spend this evening studying the history books in the library." Well, maybe he wasn't that magnanimous. Ray kicked his feet off the desk, chopping off the nub of his cigar with a cutter that was shaped like a guillotine. "Every time I won, I wore the same pants to the arena the night I humbled my opponent unmercifully. It became something of a tradition, like lesser sports stars eating chicken or, I don't know, injecting the heroin." "But, ah, Mr. Pierce." Joe Bob broached the subject carefully; it was one of few guaranteed to set the professed "Flawless One" off. "You are not an active wrestler any more. Not since..." But no explosion came, just a thin smile. "I know," Ray said sharply, leaping to his feet. "Not since I made that Rhine fellow squeal for his woman, but tonight, I am going to have my most important battle since I pinned Soul Shadow twice straight in a three-falls match." "Yes, sir." Joe Bob retreated from the room – he'd learned not to question. Somehow, this never stopped the "Essence of Perfection" from speaking. He turned his grin to the thin man. "That..." He nodded at the phone. "Was my sister. She says she has someone she wants me to meet." The thin man raised an eyebrow. "Are you sure that's a wise decision?" he asked, tapping his hand to a thin manila file folder on Pierce's desk. "Alexandra is... formidable." "She is my baby sister." Ray nearly stormed across the room as Joe Bob reemerged with a pair of dark slacks. "What could possibly go wrong? She sounded actually nervous about... whatever it is she wants to see me about. She likely said, but I wasn't paying attention after 'I need a favor, Ray.'" Pierce snatched the pants away, tossing the never-lit cigar into a wastebasket. "Make certain those files get to the estate," he ordered. "I'm taking the rest of the day off." "You take the 'rest' of every day off, sir," Joe Bob sighed as He Who Never Errs pushed into his private washroom. Smith's limpid gaze met Otis' sharp one across the room. The thin man nodded once at the silent question, turning on heel and toe before disappearing through the door. Smith was left alone in the room, pulling Ray's well-worn attaché case (which he was relatively sure never contained anything worthwhile) onto the desk. The last thing he packed was the file folder – it was the only thing he looked twice at. "RAMONE," it read. Oh, yes, this was going to end well. ***** There was a house in the suburbs of Tampa that had been owned by the same family since the late 1800s. It started out as a cottage and kept expanding and expanding. As a youth, Alexandra Pierce was the rooftop would reach the moon someday. Thirty-some years later in a rented sedan at the curb, she wasn't sure she was wrong. "You sure you're ready for this?" she asked, though the question was just as much for her as it was for her companion. Alexandra didn't dress up for things – not unless she was playing at being someone else. The white pantsuit she used to wear everywhere was more than a uniform; it was her very identity. She felt strangely naked in that car, with white slacks exchanged for burgundy, and a soft, egg white blouse replacing her blazer. Her wild red hair was tamed, held together by a simple black rubber band. The other redhead was a contrast, as usual, in jeans and a fitted DC Shoes hoodie. "I grew up with five brothers. After my family, I'm ready for anything." Pierce slipped out of the car, and her dark gaze met the smaller woman's mismatched one over the roof of the car. "I know. I'm just... unaccountably nervous. I once spent a week and a half wearing hardly a stitch of clothing, hoping my Arabic was strong enough I wouldn't get shot... and I'm on the verge of a panic attack over this." Alex crossed over onto the sidewalk, offering her hand to the other woman (let's not be coy, it was Amy Campbell). Her smile slid to the side, almost shy. "I don't even think I ever brought a boy home." "We're... gonna need to discuss that week and a half," Campbell mumbled. "Did you want to discuss the 'hope I didn't get shot' part? Or the 'hardly a stitch of clothing' part?" Pierce slid a grin to the side as she rang the bell. "Just try to relax. I don't imagine you're the type to stop sleeping with me just because your big brothers don't approve." Alex turned a glance to the redhead beside her, but any response was swallowed by Ray's custom doorbell. "GO-AH-WAY!" it chimed. Campbell stifled a snicker, more from the expression that flickered across Alex's face than the sound itself. The woman who answered the door was tall – taller in her boots. Her black and white uniform could barely be called that, falling just barely along her thighs and cut low across a bronzed chest. She smiled briefly, dark red lips parting. "You are... ahh, you are Alexandra?" Alex found her hand clasped with Campbell's – she wasn't sure which of them had made the motion. "I am. You must be new" "You are es-pected." She seemed to be carefully choosing her words. "Joor brother, he ees waiting in ze dining room." There was mumbling from Amy again. "You won't get a bell, not even a door knocker." She snickered. "No wonder you say your brother brags – he bought her a pair." "If we get through this, I'll buy you whatever you want," Alex said lightly as they fell in behind the woman. "Just remember what we talked about." The maid turned behind her as she walked, smile not flickering. "We, ah, we did not know who you would be bringing, so I hope your, ah, your companion will not object to steak." "I probably won't be eating," Campbell winked at the maid, who seemed to be appropriately put off by it. "Interferes with my strict training diet of gin and tequila. And just for my own info... does he pay you to wear that?" "Mister Pierce, he is quite generous, yes." She stepped aside, waving the two redheads into the dining room. "I'm sure we have another around here somewhere." Raymond Pierce smiled at them from the head of the table. "If you'd like, we could see if it fits you." Amy's hand tightened nearly painfully on Alex's, but the other woman stepped behind her, fingers playing on the small of Campbell's back. "Ray-Ray," Alex said, mock-scolding. "I expected more from you." The Impeccable Warrior dabbed at the corner of his mouth, rising smoothly to his feet as the redheads stepped to him. He extended a hand to the SCCW champion. "Perfect Raymond Pierce, thirteen-time champion of the world, original member of every federation's Hall of Fame, and certified as one of the top twelve hosts in all of Florida. It is a pleasure for you, I'm certain. Any friend of Alex's..." Raymond didn't complete the sentence, simply offering his grin. "Guess it's a good thing I'm not much like any friend of Alex's." The gentle touch on Amy's back felt more like a guilt-gun than anything, and it forced her to spare the elder Pierce a kind, fake smile. She gingerly reached forward as if actually accepting the handshake might result in HIV, the sleeve of her sweater pulling back to reveal some of the color inked into her wrist. "I'm, uh, just Amy, and I've held more titles than I care to remember at the moment. Alex has..." Campbell looked to the Director with one brow raised, seeking some kind of confirmation. "...told me a lot about you?" "Oh, I tend to doubt that, Amy. My sister does not speak kindly of her family. I think... I used to think she was on the drugs." He gestured to the table. "I had Martha grill some steaks, so please tell me you're not one of Alex's wrestling friends with some ridiculous anti-food diet." Alex slipped between them, wetting her lips as she tucked a lock of hair behind her ear. "Did Martha make her coleslaw? I haven't..." She tugged out a chair for the smaller woman. "Haven't had it in years. I've been kind of..." "'Horrible,'" Ray said with a grin. "The word you're looking for is horrible. You've been horrible." "I know." The Spider lowered her eyes as Amy slid into the seat. "I have been, and I'm sorry." Amy couldn't help but chime in. "She's said perfectly fine things about you. I'm a little more... family-oriented than most people she knows, I guess." She'd grinned again, but it turned to a smile (albeit forced) after another quick, affirming glance over to Alex where she rounded the table. "She didn't tell me you had such a great estate, though. I can't imagine how big this place really is..." Raymond picked up a knife and fork. "The house has been in the family since we moved to Florida a hundred and twenty years ago. Alex and her brother Andrew don't understand the history of this place. She's spent her entire wrestling career trying to bring down everything this family has tried to build." "I know I have, Raymond." Pierce's body language was quiet, her voice low. "I was angry after you and Andrew kicked me out of the house after Mother died, and I..." Alex's shrug was lost in her sigh. "I'd like to say I regretted it, but I don't." "I wouldn't believe you if you said you did." Raymond jammed a piece of the steak into his mouth. "Not even through the influence of your..." The Flawless One's eyes narrowed momentarily, just putting it together in his head. "Is this your girlfriend?" Alex looked up briefly, a smile curling her lips. "She is... she is everything to me." "Well," Ray turned his smile up a notch. "We can't have someone who's a trip to New England away from being part of the family have Alexandra's poor grasp of family history." Anyone who knew her, especially knowing her well in the past few months, would have known Amy was halfway to a heart attack at that implication. "I'm not... I mean, we're... it's..." She finally shut her eyes and resigned to what they were there for. "I meant that I'd love to see the place, hear some of its history." Nothing excited Raymond Pierce more than the possibility of discussing his own history. He folded his napkin, setting it beside his plate. "We can go now, if you'd like. Victoria will call us when your steak is ready. We'll start in the viewing room." Alex put her hand to her stomach. "Ugh, you know how I am about that room, Raymond." "Are... are you... still nauseous?" The words were somewhat stilted, Amy's brows lifted her gaze flicking from Alex to Ray. "A little." Ray stood, gesturing broadly to the hallway. "We don't need her to have a good time, do we, Amy?" Ray asked conspiratorially. "Not so long as you've got wine." "I think that can be arranged. We have an exceptional wine cellar." Amy nodded finally, pushing to her feet with one more look at Alex, whose hand was shaking slightly as she sipped from a glass of water. It was not without some reluctance that the Red Raver followed He Who Never Errs down the hallway. "This..." Pierce paused before a closed door. "Is the viewing room." He opened the door for Amy, and the smaller redhead blinked against the blinding light. Several replica title belts rested in their own display cases, but all eyes were drawn to the National Wrestling Council World Heavyweight Championship belt – the real one, not the knockoffs that surrounded it. It was (justifiably) the Impeccable Warrior's finest accomplishment, alone against a wall. The NWC World Tag Team Championships won by "Hurricane" Andrew Pierce and "Psycho" Sam Leever, the Outlanders, hung on the wall on either side, but the rest of the room was dedicated to Ray, with floor-to-ceiling prints of carefully chosen shots. (The fact that most of them included one of Raymond's most persistent nemeses, "The Sheriff" Damon Hayes, was surely a coincidence.) "Go on, take a moment." Ray drifted to the NWC title. "Each of these has a story about how badly the Elite vanquished the competition." "So it's a wrestling family? I mean, past this generation?" She feigned interest. "Your sister and I don't... uh, don't do a lot of talking." Ray waggled his brows. "I can only imagine what you spend your time doing." Amy turned towards one of the display cases, more to hide her expression than out of any genuine desire to look, and Ray continued. She figured he'd continue even if she weren't in the room. "I've been destroying people in wrestling since the late 1980s, unbeaten since 1993. Andrew followed in my footsteps, because that's what little brothers do, I suppose. But there is no one past this generation, unfortunately. I... I thought there was, but..." It was the first time his confidence wavered since they'd begun talking. "But I underestimated Alexandra's hatred for me." Raymond half-turned away. "One of her... her underlings was in this house, pretending to be my daughter. For months." His smile returned over his shoulder. "But now she is groveling before me, hoping I accept your relationship. The tide has turned." He cleared his throat. "That picture you're looking at is one of my favorites, when I beat 'Sexy' St—" "I was right!" It was another gloating voice from the doorway, and it drew Amy's eyes that way. The maid, Victoria, tossed Alexandra into the room by the upper arm. Notably, she'd lost the over-the-top accent. "Otis told you she wasn't here to mend fences. I caught this bitch snooping around upstairs." The younger Pierce met Campbell's gaze, shaking her head slightly. Raymond was a bit more demonstrative. "You came into my home, paraded your... your..." His finger jabbed at his sister. "Are you even truly dating? Is this all another one of your games, Alex?" For a moment, Amy was looking at Alex. "You never told me you tricked your own brother into thinking he had a d—whatever." She sighed. "We'll talk about that later. No, Ray, we're not 'truly' dating; I'm married, which you'd know if you paid attention, But I... I'm kinda in love with your sister, and I'd thank you to keep quiet about it. She wasn't 'parading' me, just introducing me, and I think you both need to start acting more like brother and sister, instead of rival spies." "I have half a mind to call the police." "It's what Otis would want," Victoria pointed out. "Now, let's not be hasty." Amy stepped forward, grabbing the Spider in the Web by the elbow. "Just... give me a minute to sort this out." "One." Raymond folded his arms. "Just because you are a striking woman, and I will rather enjoy watching you leave." Amy guided Alex into a relatively secluded section of the viewing room, folding her arms. "Did you even—" Alex shook her head gently, crossed the distance between them. She slipped one hand around the smaller redhead's waist and lowering her lips to touch gently. Campbell's brows nearly shot off her face, and her shock allowed Alex to turn her to face the wall. When their lip lock broke, Amy's eyes fluttered open. "Camera," the youngest Pierce whispered. One of her hands slipped into the pocket of Amy's jeans. "Expected. What now?" Campbell spoke against the Spider's shoulder. "I think your brother's jealous, so cut it out before he gets creepier about wanting on the person his sister's screwing." "I don't know, but..." Alex grinned against Amy's ear. "Fuck him, he deserves to feel small." "Your time is up," Raymond barked from the other side of the room. "You've never beaten me before, Alexandra, and you're not going to beat me now." The Flawless One and the woman playing at being his maid stepped up to the pair of them, his drink hanging loosely from his hand. "I have had enough of this in our father's house. You do not treat a thirteen-time champion like this!" "I'm sorry, Raymond." Surprisingly, it was Amy who apologized. "I didn't know that she planned on using this as some kind of... of scam. We'll... we'll just—" Maybe it was the close quarters, but when Amy stumbled into Raymond, his glass of brandy splashed across her sweatshirt. "Ah, I'm sorry." It was also surprising that Raymond apologized. He reached out with one hand, but found his wrist snatched by his sister. "You're not really going to touch her, are you?" There was a threat in Desade's tone. "Alex, no – you've done enough. It's alright." Amy stepped far closer to the younger Pierce than her brother. "I'm gonna get this cleaned off before it's all sticky and shit." "You won't delay your sister's departure." "Not gonna try," Amy muttered, but she was already stepping out of the viewing room, her hands jammed into her pockets. "This way." The maid – or whoever she was – guided the Director out to the stoop. Raymond followed, still steaming. "For ten years, I've dealt with your lies and manipulations, but this... this is unconscionable. Did you really think you would fool me?" "For a second there? Yeah, I kind of did." Hard gray eyes flicked inside. "Don't fault her. She didn't – she doesn't play this game." "I don't blame your married girlfriend, Alex." Ray's smile neared sneer territory. "I blame you. Nothing you ever say is true. You've never been successful without a mask on, without some kind of lie. Sascha challenged for the NWC title. Bloody Mary managed the last NWC tag champions. And I know you're still Ramone – at least for the matches. Alex Pierce – 'Desade' – hasn't done more than have a few noteworthy television moments." He stepped up to his sister, brows livid. "So maybe I'm used to it, but someday, you're going to do the same thing to that woman in there, and that's a shame, because... you really love her, don't you?" The streetlights painted long shadows across Alexandra Pierce's face, but one of those gray eyes shimmered before her head lowered. It was all the answer Ray needed. "I thought so." He half-turned away. "I would wish you the best, but you've been cruel to me in the past, and He Who Never Errs never forgets those who cross him." "I'm ready." Amy appeared in the doorway, her soaked hoodie held against her chest, a Machine Head tanktop bared. "Can we just go?" Campbell stormed past both Pierces and up the walk, not looking back. Alex winced as Amy slammed the door, dropping her head. "You really fucked this up, didn't you, Alex?" Raymond asked, trailing Desade up the path. "That..." Gray eyes tracked the small woman's progress. "That would seem to be the case. I am... sorry," she said awkwardly. "Someday, we will mend these fences, Raymond." "Just not today." He folded his arms. "Just not today." She circled around the car, slipping behind the wheel. Her brother watched as the sedan jerked away from the curb. It only made it down the street before Amy's silence was broken. "Stop the car." Alex didn't argue, the car jerking to a halt. "I thought you knew about Samantha. It was Lauren—" "That was an incredibly, incredibly low thing to do, but I know it's not who you are now." Amy began. "I'm just getting pretty fucking tired of people thinking I'm just another one of your cronies..." Alex began to protest but, as Amy heaved a heavy sigh, a smirk came with the air, and the champion's own hand reached for something pressed against her abdomen, held between the fabric of her t-shirt and the tight skin there. "Because honestly, none of your staff could've gone into that place and walked out with exactly what you needed." "You..." A smile bloomed onto Alex's face. "You got it?" Amy dropped the folder onto the console between them. "Of course I did. Did you think I was that big a klutz?" "You're amazing. More than that – you're fantastic. Jesus, you even fooled me." "I'm not fantastic, just a lot smarter than you think I am," she replied. "And I'm not doing these types of things regularly, understand?" "I understand." Alex's grin returned – the one that rode the border between nasty and infatuated. "I'd be lying if it didn't make me all sorts of tingly to see it. I think..." Pierce's hand fell to Amy's thigh. "I think we need to get to the hotel quickly." ***** Elsewhere. "I'm not blaming you," the man behind the desk said. "Who knew that Alex's whore would be as conniving as she was – oh, wait, everyone did. Go back to your desk job, Ray. The next time Alex calls you, try calling me first." "Hurricane" Andrew Pierce slammed the telephone down, blowing out a sigh. "Alex got all of the intel we had on Ramone." Otis King folded his arms, shrugging his shoulders. "Not surprised. Once she learned we'd been digging, it was just a matter of time." The third person in the room was a blonde, and her smile was immediate. "Oh, no," she said. "How will the 'Fauxmone' recover?" She paused, accepting a bottle of water from the former NWC Tag Team Champion. "Oh, wait, that's me." She adjusted her eyeglasses. "I'm devastated, I think. But I'll get back to you." Andrew Pierce smiled, lifting a glass of his own. They say it's better to be lucky. But when you're a Pierce, you're so good that luck doesn't matter. ***** Later that night, after Amy Campbell finally fell asleep, Alexandra Pierce crossed the hotel room, picking up her discarded clothing as she went. She glanced back over her shoulder, smiling slightly as she unfurled burgundy slacks, digging into one pocket. She perched against the dresser, blowing out a long sigh as she watched the other roll over in her sleep. Campbell never asked how the fake maid had caught Alex snooping upstairs; they'd discussed it when Alex got the call. The plan was a good one. It should have worked. Alexandra turned a small, black velvet jewel box over in her hands, her smile small and just for herself. It should have worked... and it had so far. She just wasn't sure she could go through with the rest.
