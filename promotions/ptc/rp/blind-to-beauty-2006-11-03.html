Blind to Beauty

3 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-03 And lo, the beast looked upon the face of beauty, and beauty stayed its hand. And from that day, it was as one dead. -Arab proverb A gleam of pale umber hung in Kimbusa’s eye. Even in the dim candlelight the PTC Global Title sparkled, casting its brilliant golden shine upon his face. But even the radiant glow of the belt could not penetrate the shadow masking his face; it was reflected only in a dim, sad brown on Kimbusa’s dark eyes, as if the belt itself were locked in a struggle with the beast, and was losing. Holding the belt gently in his hands and rotating it slowly, Kimbusa studied the belt with an idle curiosity. He hadn’t entered the tournament for it; no, championships to him had always seemed to be a poor measure of one’s prowess in combat. He had entered for himself; to avenge his loss and make one last attempt at finding balance within himself. And he’d done precisely that. The darkness in his eyes finally won out, extinguishing the light of the belt as a deep red glow welled up within them. The mere thought of what he’d done to Seymour Almasy was enough to arouse his passions again. He had done what he came to do, and at first he’d considered leaving for good, having given Caldera what he wanted. But the more he stared at the Global Title belt, the more he savored his victory, the more he began to realize how insatiable his lust was becoming. Kimbusa looked down upon the top of the overcast sky. A storm was raging beneath him. Snuffing out each of his four candles between his thumb and forefinger, Kimbusa closed his eyes as the smell of sulphur wafted upwards. He had come to enjoy the smell. He wasn’t sure why, but for some reason it reminded him of home. The victory had brought unexpected (but not entirely unwelcome) results. He had expected some feeling of satisfaction, but in fact he experienced the opposite. He had destroyed Almasy, humiliated him in what was to be his greatest moment, redeemed himself for posterity and won his financial freedom from Caldera. And yet, somehow, none of that seemed to matter to him. He’d given it little thought since the match; that brief notion of leaving for good quickly gave way to the desire for more. He’d always known he enjoyed hurting people, but for so long he’d regarded that vice as a deep flaw, something to be feared. But now he’d embraced it, and it was becoming an addiction. The rush. The feeling of power he got from standing above Seymour Almasy as he gasped in vain for breath, taking his title and his dignity in front of millions of people, was incredible. Liberated from his fear and his guilt, he was free to relish the high, to let it permeate his flesh and circulate through his veins. It was flowing freely within him now; a pure, unadulterated love of hatred. The cabin was quiet. He was beginning to enjoy life at thirty-thousand feet. He felt free for the first time since he could remember. When he was with the Yakuza it was different. There he had embraced his rage and turned it against his enemies, but that was a blind, indifferent anger. He was wild, undisciplined; having the power to strike down an unsuspecting or terrified victim was unsatisfying. He was incarcerated in his hate, bound by its chaos. But now he’d harnessed it. This was more calculated, focused. He could control it, harness it, and turn it against his opponents in the ring, as if he were damming all his fury until he stepped through the ropes, whereupon he would unleash the raging waters. The engines changed their pitch. Kimbusa felt his tremendous weight lurch forward in his seat. The plane was slowing; they were nearing his destination. Glancing out the window he watched as the clouds began to break. A brilliant white line poured in through the window, stinging Kimbusa’s eyes. The sheer radiance of Tokyo had always disturbed him. A five hundred pound man is difficult to hide in brightness, but a Shadow can disappear easily in the dark. As he glided down towards Narita airport, an odd sensation rose within him. He couldn’t quite put his finger on it, but to his shame he finally pegged it as apprehension. Not borne out of fear, of course, but borne out of the unknown. He was flying to Tokyo for a very specific purpose, and despite his newfound confidence (to use a gross understatement) in the ring, he wasn’t certain he was prepared for this task. He knew he could destroy anyone who stood against him in the wrestling ring. But how he would fare in this confrontation was in considerable doubt. He hadn’t seen her in years. He’d been too ashamed. Ashamed of his failure, of his inadequacy. She had always stood by him, but deep down he’d always known this transformation was inevitable. Though he loved her for her kindness, he knew some day it would pass. For to become what he’d always desired to be, he’d have to become the very sadistic, heartless monster he found himself to be today. He’d accomplished his goal, and it took her as a sacrifice to do it. He was both confused and emboldened by how successful the transformation had been, how complete and uniform it was. As the plane slowly rattled in for a rather bumpy landing, Kimbusa searched the deepest regions of his heart, and found no love for her left lingering there. Epiphany. The name struck him hard, though it might only have been the plane touching down. Either way, though he did not love her, he maintained a trifle of respect for her, she who, even in his godlike ascension to power, could still reside in his memory. The plane slowly began to taxi towards a specified runway where a Caldera Enterprises limousine would collect him. By virtue of his victory over Almasy Kimbusa had won his freedom from Caldera, but the manipulative son of a bitch still had his uses. He wasn’t certain what he was seeking in tracking her down. His purpose on this, what would be his last trip to Tokyo, was closure. Now that that damnable struggle within him was resolved and the stronger side had emerged victorious, he had the power (or, more accurately, the control) to settle all his business and return to America free of those last lingering reservations. As they crawled to a halt on the tarmac, Kimbusa heard the cabin depressurize and the door pop open. He stood and collected his belongings, pressing through the narrow cabin as best he could, past the captain standing wearily at the door, and out into the cool Japanese air. It had a distinctive smell to it, even in the sprawling metropolis of Tokyo. It was an all-too familiar smell, one that nearly caused him to wretch. For so long he’d savored the clean smell of Japan. Now, for some reason, it suffocated him. Perhaps it was the lifetime of memories that hung on the wisps of wind that blew through the streets of the city. Perhaps it was the clean, wholesome feeling. Whatever it was, Kimbusa pressed on with a newfound urgency, overcome by the desire to settle his business and leave with all haste. A driver stood dutifully by an open limousine door, his stare turned to the east where the storm clouds were ominously swarming in. The air was cold and the wind was gusting in erratic spurts, and tiny pinpricks of rain were falling intermittently. Still the driver held his ground, until he spied the 500 pound beast moving towards him. Kimbusa was darkly attired in a black trenchcoat and a dark fedora, a massive shadow indiscernible as human. Only a pair of dark, piercing eyes looking out from beneath the brim of his hat gave him away. The driver swallowed hard as Kimbusa silently slipped into the car, which sagged deeply under his weight. "Downtown," was all he said, his voice quiet but menacing, like that of a snake. The driver closed the door and adjusted his tie. He had his orders, too, but he hadn’t been made aware of who, precisely, he would be working with. Kimbusa listened as the driver’s door closed from behind a thick plate of tinted glass which separated the two. The engine was already running, and promptly the limo sped forward. If his information was correct, he’d be there quickly. Not much time left to think. But something inside him had been tugging at him the more he considered it. It was as if some voice within him was telling him not to think; that he already knew his purpose, though he did not yet possess the foresight to know it. Thinking is what got him in trouble to begin with; thought and rationale and logic only served to cloud matters that, in truth, were all too clear. He wielded unfathomable power, and all those who tread before him cowered from his path for fear of his wrath. There were no consequences for a man of his stature, nothing hindering him from acting without judgment and without hesitation. Thought was the only thing that could slow him. Heeding this notion, Kimbusa pushed Epiphany from his mind. He would act as he saw fit in the moment, for he was a creature of high emotional potency; morality did not apply. Whatever emotion was evoked by the sight of her would be responded to promptly and accurately. He simply had to trust in himself, and the rest would fall into place. The limo sped into downtown Tokyo, and Kimbusa cracked his window to peer out at the city. He had forgotten how bright it was; as the rain began to fall steadily the neon lights lining the streets danced on the pavement. Thousands of anonymous people, along with the occasional conspicuous tourist, trudged along in their menial and pointless courses through life. The bright lights of Tokyo were penetrating, exposing all they touched, and Kimbusa found that to be the hilarious contradiction of it all. Millions of people living their lives, perfectly anonymous, though they were on display for all to see. That was why Kimbusa had made his way to the Entertainment District in the first place. Driven away by the revealing neon glow of downtown, he had found a place where privacy was valued and preserved with great care. There, the dark murky air smothered the light; everyone there was free to be no one. But Kimbusa didn’t need to hide any longer. Patience, Kimbusa snapped to himself, rolling his window back up. That’s business for later. After some time, the limo finally arrived at the destination Kimbusa had eventually procured for the driver. The driver, who had donned a long black raincoat of his own, stepped out under an angry sky and opened the door for Kimbusa. Kimbusa emerged into the rain in front of a towering high-rise; her flat was on the second floor. Blowing by the driver without a word of instruction, Kimbusa headed for the nearest entry gate. The driver, confused, called after him. "I can’t stay parked here! Where should I-?" he cried, but realized the futility of trying to communicate with him. Grunting, the driver adjusted his collar and slipped back into the driver’s seat, pulling away in search of a place to wait. For his part, Kimbusa found himself already slipping into a machinelike efficiency. He moved without much thought, as if some internal power were guiding him. He found a gate, which was of course locked electronically, with a keypad on the wall beside it. Without hesitating, Kimbusa ignored the keypad and delivered a heavy kick to the door, which gave little resistance to the blow. He slid inside like a ghost, his weighty footsteps making no noise, his massive frame gliding through the halls without the slightest sign of his presence. Kimbusa found himself in a stairwell, and he climbed to the second floor. Though he had crushed all the worries of the past and driven them from his memory, he found he still maintained many of the instincts he had developed with the Yakuza. He moved close to the wall, his eyes darting about warily, silent as a stalking killer. He pushed through another door and found himself in a hall. Two apartments to the left, two to the right. Peering through the threshold, Kimbusa felt a surge of adrenaline rush into him. Whatever doubts he’d had before, they’d given way to pure thrill. Stepping confidently to his right, Kimbusa came before apartment 245. He stared at the door for a long moment, giving his swimming emotions time to settle. After several minutes, anger won out, and showed its face on the door. Kimbusa rapped it twice with a resounding fist, and he detected a shuffle inside. Finally the door swung open, and the startled face of a man stood behind it. The man was instantly covered in the shadow from the door; the tremendous frame drowned out all light from the hall, dwarfing the cowering figure before it. The man attempted a greeting, but the strength of his voice left him, and he managed only a modest squeak. Kimbusa gave him a long, infuriated stare. It was not jealousy he was experiencing, but rather an intense, furious, maddening aggravation at the minor kink in his plans. Pushing through the door, Kimbusa drove the trembling man back into the apartment. He left the door hanging open, allowing just a few rays of light to pass over his body. The apartment was dark, save a few lights on in the rooms beyond the foyer. From the bedroom came a female form, who startled looked up at the shadow in the door. The silhouette was unmistakable. Epiphany, tall but thin, could be described as the most perfect physical form Kimbusa had ever observed. She was pale and wan, and yet possessed a spirit that brightened a room. Her face was kind and gentle, soft and welcoming. Her body was curvaceous, but despite its perfection could hardly be the target of lust. She had a presence that was almost angelic, leading any who withheld her to see her with awe rather than desire. She dropped the hairbrush she was carrying and sank to her knees. She whispered some barely audible command to her boyfriend, who shamefully sank out of sight into the recesses of the apartment. Epiphany slowly rose onto her shaking legs and stepped forward, but the hulking form before her remained obscured in the shadow. Unable to speak, she went for the lightswitch, but a hard voice stopped her in her tracks. "Do not bother. You dare not look at me, anyway," Kimbusa said, suddenly finding himself feeling rather empty inside. After all the thought and all the consideration he’d given to how he’d handle seeing her again, he found only apathy. His mind trailed to more pressing matters; back to the ring. He discovered himself pining for the battle once more, to return to America, leave his past behind, and concern himself only with attaining more power. Epiphany finally found the courage to speak. "Kimbusa- I- I have so many things to-" But that same cold, harsh voice stopped her again. "I have not come here for that, either. I came here to resolve our relationship for good. I had to put this to rest for good. But now that I look upon you, I find that you no longer sway me. You once had a power over my heart, but no longer. Seeing you now, I see that there is nothing more for me to say. I care nothing for you. I cannot see your beauty any more; you appear to me only as another of the identical creatures down there on the streets. Cower no more, you shall never see me again." Kimbusa turned and headed for the door. Epiphany cried out after him, her head swimming in emotion. She felt fear, pain, love, all at once, and was only desperate for some familiarity in the faceless shape standing before her. "Wait! Please!" she called out, but Kimbusa did not wait. Epiphany looked up through teary eyes at Kimbusa as he passed through the door; she caught his glance one final time. She flinched, expecting some horrifying demons to be residing in his eye. But she saw that his eyes were empty; black and lifeless, as a quietly sadistic shark waiting for its prey. There was nothing there, nothing but unrelenting darkness filled only with a glint of cold calculation. And that was all the more frightening.
