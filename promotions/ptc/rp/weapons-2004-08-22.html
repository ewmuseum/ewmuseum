Weapons

22 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-22 When making an argument, one must establish any points that one should make clearly and successfully. For without an established point, the argument is lost. So, it can be argued that Joey Vendetti has made it clear exactly who he is and what he is about. Let's take a look at a few of the facts. FACT ONE- Joseph Marciano Vendetti is an extremist, in one form or fashion. As he thinks of the word "extremist" he think of someone whose mere words are powerful enough to break another man down and bring him to his knees even before he has reached the ring. Joey believes that the power of a man's words is nearly, or more powerful than a man's fists. This point could be argued, but Joey's views have been made clear, and have been proven with a real life situation in Joey Vendetti vs. Moraless. Joey also has the ability to break someone down, both with words and fists. FACT TWO- Joseph Marciano Vendetti doesn't mind being the new guy, in fact he enjoys it. He has only ever compete with one man in this match, DDT. And before that, he has only had one PTC match, against Moraless. Joey is the man that these people know the least about, he's the wild card in this Elimination Chamber match, though somehow he's been able to say, time and time again, that he will walk out of the Elimination Chamber victorious and holding the Extreme Title Division's title high above his head. With the new guy, these other competitors, other than DDT, will never know what to expect. And as far as DDT goes, he can just expect defeat. So normally there are three points to a successful argument. And a successful argument can sometimes be the difference in whether a man lives or dies. What is Joey's third point? And what will be the difference in this situation?... The scene opens outside as the sun is slowly setting behind the city skyline. As the camera pans backwards and down, we catch a glimpse of an abandoned warehouse. It's the average warehouse with metal siding and a tin roof, rusted with age and only the single giant door that leads to the inside on the front side of the place. The camera zooms down to the small graveled area just in front of the warehouse, where the now familiar white 2004 Pontiac Sunfire pulls in. It parks and switches off just before the door swings open and our very own Joey Vendetti steps out of the driver's seat. Joey is now dressed in a white tuxedo with a black shirt and a red tie, with black shoes of course, and is carrying a crowbar. He shuts the door to his car and takes a short walk towards the building as the steadily setting sun sets behind the skyline. It is evening, but not yet dark. Joey approaches the warehouse and once he reaches the door, the camera rests just behind him. Joey wedges the crowbar into the door and does his best to pry it open. After just a few moments, it finally cracks open and the door swings with a rusty creak that echoes through the dusk air. Joey marches forward and into the warehouse and the camera follows him in. Joey slams the door behind him and the scene goes completely black for a moment. Soon, the sound of a switch being thrown is heard and a bright set of lights switches on. As the camera comes into focus, we notice a large pile of rubble in the middle of the room, with Joey standing next to it in his tuxedo. Joey grins as he looks at the pile of rubble, which of course includes such things as tables, baseball bats, and the like. Soon, Joey slowly walks around the pile and smiles at the camera. Joey: Hello Primetime Central. How are you all doing today? I know I'm doing just fine. I'm glad that I have found the time to address you all one more time before the Primetime Central I-PPV, which will include my Extreme Title Match. I hope you're all listening, because what I've got to tell you is not only very important information, but also a warning for my opponents. Listen to me carefully, and take care not to miss anything. Joey clasps his hands behind his back and smiles. You know, I've heard it said that in this business, the word extreme is a synonym for tables, ladders, chairs, crowbars, lead pipes and things like that. Things that can cause damage and literally tear a man apart. So, I have taken the liberty of coming here today to this warehouse, or more like a storage building for XUW I suppose, it's where we keep most of our weapons after they've been broken beyond repair, and I've decided to search for something truly extreme. I've decided to find the real definition of extreme, and not the kind one finds in a pocket dictionary. Joey marches around for a moment grabs a broken pool stick from the pile. It's the bottom half of the pool stick, and the top is splintered... this weapon has been used. A pool stick. This pool stick was, at one time or another, intended for use in a billiard hall. If you think about it, it's sort of ironic that a thing used for precision is soon swung blindly in an attempt to down an opponent. To succeed in a game of pool, one must have strategy. As you scan the table with your eyes, you have to determine in which order you should try to sink your shots. That is, when you're not playing with strict rules that set you to shoot the balls in the order of their numbers. In this case, there is nothing extreme about a pool stick, there is nothing deadly about a pool stick... until it is made what Kyo Kushinaga would call "extreme". Someone took this pool stick and nailed his opponent probably square in the middle of the forehead. Someone broke this pool stick over another man's head, and that made this weapon extreme. So, if we consider it, a weapon isn't extreme.... until it is used. Joey tosses the pool stick over his shoulder and it lands on the other side of the pile. A moment later he turns around and draws out a broken pane of glass. Joey examines it and grins. A pane of glass. Now what in the world could be extreme about a pane of glass? I imagine that most of the men in the Elimination Chamber match for the Extreme Title Division Championship live in a home of some kind, a home with at least one window. As someone glances at a window, they don't see anything extreme, not even to the furthest stretch of the imagination. It is completely harmless. Joey runs a finger over one of the pointed edges, created by an impact... Or is it? I'll pose this question to ICE of 4WW. Since no one could correctly use a block of ice as a weapon, let me ask you something ICE. Why is it that this pane of glass is extreme? Hmm? Well it's because... Joey swings the glass into a ladder that's in the rubble pile and the glass shatters almost completely in Joey's hands. Joey drops the rest of the glass and grins. Think about that ICE. The glass isn't extreme, until someone uses it. So maybe the generic definition is wrong. Maybe it's not that the weapons are extreme... maybe it's how the people use them that's extreme. And think about this ICE... There are some weapons that won't be able to be bought inside the Elimination Chamber... but look at the chamber that you may or may not be standing it. It's made of metal... and glass. You haven't said a word to me ICE, keep it that way. Joey reaches into the pile and draws out next, a taser. The grin on Joey's face is completely sadistic as he looks down at the black taser in his hand. He looks up at the camera. DDT... Joey presses a button on the taser and the electricity arcs. He releases the button and his grin fades. This is your favorite weapon isn't it DDT? Shaman may claim to be a hardcore legend, but in XUW, no name is more synonomous with "hardcore" than the name DDT. This, a man who held a hardcore championship and defended it night in and night out like a true champion, bringing his body to the limit. Bringing his body to extremes. But more than anything else, DDT liked the taser. I don't know how many tapes I've watched where this little baby made all the difference in the world. But if I were to set this taser on the ground, it would be absolutely worthless, there is nothing extreme about it. The idea that an item can be extreme is not only ludicrous, it's idiotic. It's not the item itself, it's how the item is used. DDT could sneak a taser into the Elimination Chamber, sure.... but he won't. DDT has gone soft since his hardcore title days. DDT is done. DDT won't win. Joey Vendetti will. Joey pitches the taser into a wall not too far from him. The bang from the impact echoes through the warehouse, and when the echo stops for the most part, Joey continues as he pulls a broken bottle out of the pile. Joey's grin looks less sadistic now as it creeps back onto his face. It actually has a more comical look now as he speaks. A bottle. For as long as people have been able to get wasted, the bottle has been the number one weapon in a bar. Whether you choose to break it over the bar and use it as a knife, or whether you simply use it to club someone over the back of the head, it's a brutal weapon indeed, but not while you're drinking out of it. No, the bottle is physically harmless, at least for a few hours, while someone takes a drink out of it. And this bottle brings no one to mind more than "The Irish Bastard" Sean McClancy. Why? Because he's Irish, what do you expect? The Irish have been the kings of alcohol for as far back as any person of European descent can remember. I myself was born in Verona, Italy, where even there we caught wind of the Irish and their filthy drinking habits. There's nothing good about an Irishman. I don't care about anything that Sean McClancy has accomplished, because it doesn't take away from the fact that he's probably a filthy, drunken Irishman with no self-respect. Or course, I suppose if I were a former champion and I suppose if I were nothing but a has-been then I wouldn't have any self-respect either, and I'd get lost in one of these bottles. But as it is, I'm not Irish, I'm Italian, and I have self-respect. But still very little respect for Sean McClancy. Hope for yourself Sean McClancy, that I don't find a way to make this bottle extreme. Joey tosses the bottle aside and reaches into the pile yet again, this time pulling out a bow staff. The long metal staff is bent just slightly. Joey runs his hand over the curved part of the bow, where it has been bent, and looks into the camera. A bow staff, mostly used by the Japanese as a weapon. I imagine that my friend Kyo Kushinaga has one of these. There is nothing menacing at all about this bow staff when you look at it. It's simply a long, slender piece of metal. But when this is placed in the hands of its master, that's when it becomes extreme. Much like Kyo Kushinaga, this is not extreme at all when you look at it. It even seems to be bent and broken a little bit. It's sort of like Kyo's confusion about his past life, if he could grasp it, he might be unstoppable..... But then again, I don't believe in that past life bull crap, so as far as Kyo Kushinaga goes... Joey takes a mighty swing with the bow staff that clangs against a large piece of metal in the pile of rubble. Soon the bow staff is bent beyond repair and Joey tosses it away. Just after that, he pulls a chair out of the pile. It is bent and bloody and broken. This chair has been used many times. Joey picks it up and examines it carefully, then looks into the camera with the same grin that has crossed his face from the get-go. The steel chair. What weapon in the product of professional wrestling has been utilized more than the standard steel chair? The resounding clang from the chair and the thump from the body hitting the ground is the sound that people can identify with the word "extreme". No single solitary weapon has been used more in pro wrestling's long history than this, the steel chair. But how did someone come up with the idea to use the steel chair? He simply saw one at ringside and decided to fold it up and smack his opponent with it? I suppose that's how it happened, it's still odd though, that an item such as this could become a weapon, when it's meant for the most stoic thing in the world, sitting. Joey brings the chair down hard on the concrete floor of the warehouse and the clang sound made echoes for what seems like ages. He drops it on the ground and stares at the camera. But that chair is anything but stoic... isn't that right Shaman? You should be able to answer that question, being that you're a hardcore legend and all. Well let me just go ahead and tell you Shaman that I don't care what you've done in the WWA. I don't care how many years you've been wrestling, I don't even care how many silly little nicknames you want to give yourself. You can call yourself the Native American Badass, or "Kicks-some-ass" or whatever else you can come up with, but either way it's not going to matter when we step into that Elimination Chamber. It must be difficult to carry on with this at your age Shaman. You may not be very old in actual human years, but this business has worn you down, it has made you broken, I can see it in your face. But look at me Shaman. Joey pulls his tux jacket off and lays on it on the floor. He stands up and continues. I am the perfect picture of a physical specimen. I was built for this business, I was raised for this business. You're probably just some indian who couldn't put his ear to the ground and hear the buffalo coming his way. You're probably just some know-nothing idiot that happens to be in a federation whose first World Champion is now set in a block of ice. Your nicknames mean nothing to me, how many times you've used a chair means nothing to me. You will never be as extreme as I am Shaman... never. You are too old, you are too broken. You can never win. So heed my words, and just don't show up. As Kushinaga would say, your efforts will be in vain. Joey motions to all the weapons all over the room in the pile of rubble as he picks up his jacket and pulls it back on. All of these weapons all over the room can be made extreme. With the right man to use them, they can mean absolute destruction... unfortunately for my opponents, they won't be able to bring some of these into the elimination chamber. Sure, DDT could smuggle a taser in, someone could bring a set of brass knucks.... But it's not going to be enough. There will be no chairs. There will be no tables. There will just be six men and a cage. The cage can be made extreme, the glass of the chambers can be made extreme, but nothing will be more extreme than these words we've thrown, and than the fists we will throw later. As for trying to carry a weapon in, this is the warning part of my message. Someone could try and smuggle weapons into the cage. There's no disqualification, so it'd be perfectly legal... But I can promise you all this, if a weapon can be made extreme, then the weapon that I bring will be far more deadly than any taser, than any chair, than any table that you could ever imagine. "What are these weapons?" You ask? A sadistic grin creeps onto Joey's face as he raises his hands, with the backs turned to the camera, up into view. He smiles. It has been said that words hit harder than fists, so in essence, my words are weapons. And I just made my weapons extreme. Now that I've used my words. It's time to finish the job. See you then. Joey casually walks away from the large pile of rubble and over to the light switch. We catch one last glimpse of a grin from Joey just before he shuts off the lights.... POW! Fade away...
