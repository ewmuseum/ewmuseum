Perfection

23 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-23 ‘I’m not a perfect person,’ Brian thought. This much was obvious to himself, but Brian knew that a lot of others, even those that knew him rather intimately, didn’t realize just how true that statement really was. Sure, he had had his differences with his parents, but that was relatively minor. Nobody that knew about his conflict with his mom and dad thought badly about him because of it. And, for a reason he couldn’t explain, a lot of people actually did know about that particular problem. His wife (was to be expected), his co-workers, and friends, and even his fans knew all about how he hadn’t been home in twenty years (before last week, that is), and they commended him for being strong enough to stick to his guns as long as he did. Nope, his parents weren’t the issue. The people that knew Brian the best, outside of his wife, had no inclination of Brian’s real problem. When they thought of Flyin’ Brian Calvin they thought of a man with great morals, a man that stood up for what was right, and a man that had relentless passion for his work and his life. And Brian was all those things, he reckoned, and possibly more. But, as was probably true for a lot of people, there was more then meets the eye. Brian was an alcoholic. He had first started down that path a little over two years ago, just after he first was injured in the wrestling ring and the doctor’s told him he would never wrestle again. The first three months after his injury was hell for him and his family. He could barely walk because of the injury to his knee, and the despair of his situation quickly overcame him. Instead of listening to his ever-optimistic wife and his trainer, Brian became pessimistic and turned to alcohol to drown his sorrows. He hadn’t been a big drinker throughout his life until that point. In high school athletics usually came before parties on Brian’s priority list, so he didn’t have much of an inclination to drink. He hadn’t gone to college or felt the need to go to every college party that came up, as he felt it was a waste of his time. Brian’s drinking had been very minor and consisted mostly of having a few drinks with his co-workers after a wrestling match or going out to a bar with a few friends on a weekend. But for three tremulous months during period where Brian was virtually inactive he started to drink from the hour when he woke up to the hour when he finally went to bed. He was drunk all day and most of the night, lying on the couch or in his favorite armchair, just chugging them down. His kids were in school and they weren’t forced to witness the worst of it, but his wife was home, making every desperate attempt she could to help the man she loved so very much. Rachel tried everything to get Brian to stop drinking, but he wouldn’t come around. She cried, she pleaded, she moaned, and she yelled, but it was to no avail. Brian was either too drunk or too stubborn to understand that all she was trying to do was to help him, and he took her actions as a sign that she just didn’t understand what he was going through. This only caused him to drink even more. Seeing as Brian was practically crippled, Rachel had eventually tried to cut off Brian’s supply of alcohol in a more practical manor. She hid the keys and took his crutches, making it effectively impossible for Brian to get to the liquor store to satisfy his cravings. Once Brian sobered up he began to get very angry, and he didn’t take to Rachel’s actions very well. At all. He believed his wife just didn’t understand the pain that he was going through. He didn’t think he was ever going to wrestle ever again, and just didn’t see how she could even PRETEND to relate to that. This is when things got particularly bad for the couple, and they had their worst fight ever. Brian remembered it all too well. ‘DAMN IT RACHEL, I’M MAN ENOUGH TO MAKE MY OWN CHOICES! NOW GIVE ME THE KEYS!’ Brian roared. Rachel trembled back, ‘I’m sorry Brian, I can’t d-d-do that… Y-You need help!’ Brian attempted to push himself off of the couch in an attempt to reach his treacherous wife. ‘YOU KNOW NOTHING ABOUT WHAT’S GOING ON WITH ME, RACHEL! NOTHING, GODDAMN IT! DON’T YOU SEE? I NEED THIS!’ Rachel stumbled away, frightened. ‘D-Don’t move so fast B-Brian, you’ll hu-hurt your knee even wo-worse…’ Her lip began to quiver, and tears flowed freely down her face. Brian continued to try to stand, turning red. He was furious. ‘YOU BITCH! YOU DIRTY BITCH! WHERE ARE MY CRUTCHES?’ Brian had never shown this much rage before, and had definitely never spoken ill of his wife before. The whole scene was as much a shock to Brian as it was to Rachel, but he couldn’t really explain it. The craving for the drug was just so powerful… It was his security blanket. His answer. Whenever he thought about not wrestling anymore and not being able to support his family… It sent chills up his spine. ‘Brian…’ Rachel sobbed. ‘Please, stop… I love you, honey, but alcohol is destroying your life. OUR lives! You have to stop feeling sorry for yourself. You need to stop drinking…’ This is when Brian himself began to break out into sobs, and he covered his face with his hands. This wasn’t the kind of person he was… This wasn’t how he treated his beautiful wife… When things got tough, Brian didn’t lie down and let life deal him the blows. He got right back up and kept fighting, because that’s what he had been programmed to do. But alcohol… It had changed him. It had done things to him that he would have never allowed himself to do if he was sober. He knew that he had to stop. ‘Rachel, I’m sorry… I’m so sorry… I love you too, you’re right… You’re right…. I want to stop, I need to stop! I just need some help.’ Rachel moved closer to her husband, seeing that his blind rage had subsided, and leaned down to wrap an arm around him. ‘It’s okay, Brian. You’ll be okay… Our FAMILY will be okay.’ Brian’s sobs began to subside, and he nodded. ‘It’ll be okay…’ repeated Rachel, looking away with solemn expression on her face. It had taken a lot of time, but Brian finally broke the addiction that had been eating away at his mind and body like some kind of a ravenous cancer. Breaking free had required support from his wife and his kids, not to mention endless hours of rehabilitation, but eventually he found himself able to accept that drinking was not the answer to his problems, no matter how mountainous they seemed. Only his wife and his rehab group knew about Brian’s disease (‘Alcoholism IS a disease,’ he remembered his rehab doctor repeating) , and that was the way he wanted to keep it. The most important thing to help Brian recuperate from alcoholism was, oddly enough, recuperating from the injury that caused him to turn to alcohol in the first place: his knee injury. Once he managed to get out and about and began going through the long process of rehabilitating his knee the pessimism that he had stored up was quickly turned into focus. Focus that had never wavered through his two years of rehabilitation, through his tryout into the WWA, and through his first four critical matches that had resulted in a 3-1 overall record for Brian. Until now. The past few weeks had been awfully stressful in Brian’s life. He had an unbelievably difficult bracket to get through if he wanted to advance in the Golden Turnbuckle tournament, and conflicts at his work in WWA were making Brian’s return to the ring very stressful. Not to mention that he had just reunited with the parents whom he had given the cold shoulder to for twenty plus years. If there ever was a time to have a drink, Brian suspected now would have been his time… He stared ahead into the middle of his dark living room, unblinking. The television was casting the only light just in front of him, but he wasn’t paying attention at ALL. He was slouched back on the couch, every feature on his body perfectly frozen, like some kind of dumb animal caught in the headlights of a car. Brian had been this way for the past ten minutes, as his mind had flashed back to the three terrible months during his addiction, when everything about life seemed wrong and nothing seemed like it would ever get better. On the coffee table in front of him, in a house that had been unoccupied since Brian had left that morning, was an opened bottle of Corona. His favorite drink. Someone had even been kind enough to put a lime on a napkin besides it. Although Brian had gone straight home from the arena that evening, his family had driven to the airport to catch a late flight to Rachel’s mothers house for the next couple of days. The plan was that Brian was going to get his things packed up before he had to fly in for his match against Gonzalo Rey, and then he would meet up with his family at Rachel’s mom’s house the night after the second round in the tournament (win or loss). But someone, someone that Brian couldn’t even begin to comprehend the identity of, had paid Brian a visit and left him a little ‘gift’. A gift that he thought he had seen the last of. He was having a hard time just trying to prevent himself from leaning forward, snatching the bottle, and taking a long, refreshing swig. ‘But who? Who else besides his wife knew?’ “Welcome home, Brian.” To the hallway to the left of the television a shadowed figure stepped out from the darkness. ‘I’m not a perfect person…’ To be continued…
