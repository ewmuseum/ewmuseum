Date: 2006-12-17 *** *** *** October 21, 2006 *** *** *** Nicholas "My dear, sweet Arriana," Nicholas whispered the words in old Italian, a sign of his love and respect. "How I’ve missed you these years…" Behind his thick glasses, Nicholas Ramone’s eyes glazed over as always they did when he set them upon the black and white face of his late wife. The photo was old and unframed, kept in his breast pocket at all times, next to his heart - where she belonged. He rubbed her film-cheek with his thumb, light so not to damage it, and then brought it tenderly to his lips. He loved her still after all these years, twenty of them, and he prayed that he might meet her one day in heaven; men like Nicholas had only their prayers when it came to heaven, for logic had long since abandoned the thought. "If only you were with me now," old Italian again, spoken as soft as their most tender moments while she lived, should somehow she be listening. "There are troubling times ahead of me… ahead of all of us… never did I want this role - you know that. Especially not now, old as I am. I wanted to spend these last few years with you - with our grandchildren. But what can I do when I see the walls of my fathers and their fathers crumbling around me? I have no choice, my sweet Arriana. I have no choice." He lifted his glasses from his nose and wiped his eyes with the same thumb that had stroked his wife’s likeness a moment before. "If only you were with me now," he repeated, an old man now, long past the days he ever thought these responsibilities would fall to him. The cancer took her in her forty-second year, and her twenty-fifth of marriage. Those last days had been the hardest of Nicholas’ life, sitting bedside with her in her hospital room while the weight of her evaporated from beneath her skin. Somehow, the memory was as black and white as her photograph, and he thought perhaps it was because of her face toward the end - gaunt white, with the same oil black hair she had in her youth. As much as it pained him to look at her in those days, he told her she was beautiful in the old Italian words she had taught him in the early years of their marriage. The end was made doubly cruel as her mind started to go, and her memories seemed to be peeling away one at a time until all that remained were the old days. She would speak of vacations in Naples and Sicily as if she were still there, awed by the culture of their ancestors. She did not know that while Nicholas attended to "business" on those vacations, his youthful discretions would present themselves, and often, he would wind up in the arms of dark-haired beauties and olive-skinned angels. It was condoned by his peers - more than that, it was expected. To say she did not know was not entirely true; she knew, as all wives in his business knew. She did her suffering in silence, however, and now that she was gone, it was his turn to suffer with his guilt. She had been young when they married - not yet seventeen, but in those days, it wasn’t so uncommon. And her father was pleased. He was old Italian blood, and deep in the business; a distant relation to the boss in those days - the boss before Doug Hattoni. Nicholas had been twenty-six, and already on his way up. Arriana’s father knew that, and he was happy for their union. Old blood, Nicholas thought as he reminisced, a true man. Unlike most wives, Arriana had known the secrets of Nicholas’ business. He kept nothing from her - not the ugliness, nor the danger. Only when it came to other women did he hold his tongue, for in those matters, she knew more than she needed to already. There had been other women since her passing as well, and a few that he even had intentions with, but nothing ever came of it. Nothing more than meetings in the night and hollow whispers. Promises that he could not keep. There came a knock at the door, and Nicholas set his glasses back on his nose. "My dear, sweet Arriana," he whispered to the photo once more, in English this time. And then: "Per sempre con l’amore." Forever with love. He tucked the photo gently back into the breast pocket of his suit jacket and cleared his throat. "Come in." Palo Rugazi, Nicholas’ most trusted of friends, appeared in the doorway and entered. On the outside, the two men that Nicholas had standing guard kept their backs to the room. Always there were two men standing guard outside of Nicholas Ramone’s study, even in the hours he was elsewhere. He was a paranoid man, and in this new age of technology, there was no way of knowing where the feds had their bugs planted. So he left two guards outside of his study both day and night, and two outside at the main gate to his driveway. Always two, because a man’s loyalty often wavered when left alone. Nicholas moved to a small cabinet in the corner of his study and removed two glasses and an old bottle of wine. A choice bottle of wine. He did this because he knew that Palo Rugazi deserved his appreciation. He knew even before he asked. "Is it done?" Palo nodded grimly. Only a formality. "How many is that for you now?" Nicholas asked. "Too many," came the response. "For you as the rest of us," Nicholas sighed. It was a dirty situation, this business with Jimmy Delvecchio. He’d been talking to the feds for damn near a month, but only in the last few days had it been brought to Nicholas’ attention. After declaring the hit on Jason Snow to his most trusted men, Nicholas sent out the most trusted of his most trusted, Palo, to do the job on Jimmy. Jimmy and Palo had known each other for five years, and Nicholas wished there was another man he trusted with the job. A necessary evil, he thought with a sigh. The only other man fit for such nasty work was Dante Gasperoni, but where Palo was efficient, Dante was vengeful. Rat that he was, Nicholas saw no need for Jimmy to suffer. "I gotta ask you, Nicky," Palo looked up. "Are you sure?" Nicholas turned his head sharply to his friend. "I’m going to be the boss soon, Palo. I’m always sure." "But how did you know?" His voice was shaken. Even with all the blood on his hands, taking a man’s life was still something Palo Rugazi took no pleasure in. The man was true steel, and he honored tradition the way a proper man should, fulfilling his duty to the highest degree despite his emotions. He was the hammer in Nicholas’ hand. "An old man has his tricks," replied Nicholas with a friendly, evasive smile. And yet, in truth, Nicholas should have known long ago. He should have known from the start. He handed Palo a swirling red glass of wine, a payment worth more between friends than any dollar amount. The dollars would come too, of course, but that was simply a formality. Tonight was about respect. This was the old way. "You’ll be my underboss, you know," said Nicholas as he seated himself in an old chair next to a phonograph record player. He had long collected the old Italian greats - the classics. But he also had guilty pleasures: Tony Bennet and Patsy Cline. The latter, because her songs reminded him of Arriana. "I know," Palo nodded. Nicholas gestured him to take a seat. "You still have your concerns?" "Not so much as before," shrugged Palo. Even in the subtle, classic tone of Nicholas’ study, Palo Rugazi was like a rough brick. All that time in the can, thought Nicholas. "What’s gotta be done’s gotta be done." His hands shook as he brought the wineglass to his lips. By now, Nicholas knew that the police would already have found Jimmy Delvecchio’s body. The feds might have even known by now. Nicholas had instructed Palo not to make any effort to hide the body. He wanted them to know: Nicholas Ramone was always one step ahead. Always. "Are you sure about this, Nicky?" Palo raised his eyes. "This business with the Snow kid? With Johnny Silver? We’re ultimately talking about mutiny here. That’s a volatile fucking situation. It’s not too late to get out." "It was too late the day the old Don died and saddled us with that incompetent son of his," Nicholas spoke plainly now - more plainly than he ever had to Palo, who so strictly held to tradition. Bosses were to be respected, but Nicholas wanted Palo to think of him as boss now. "It’s a good plan," Palo had to admit. "But are you sure we shouldn’t just whack Johnny Silver?" "Johnny Silver’s getting lazy in his old age," shrugged Nicholas. "Everyone knows it. He runs the north end like a fucking McDonald’s kitchen - like one of those fucking asylums everyone talks about where the inmates run the place. The only reason why he’s anything in this game anymore is because of that fucking mick he keeps at his side. Take out Snow, and it’s the same as cutting the ugly prick’s arms and legs off. No one will follow him, and what fault is it of ours? The kid’s a mick - not one of us." "The kid’s valuable," Palo gave Nicholas a serious eye. "Very valuable. You said it yourself, he’s the only thing holding Johnny Silver up. They say he’s better than Dante Gasperoni used to be - doesn’t even need to carry a gun most times. The entire red district up north is fucking terrified of him." "I know," frowned Nicholas. "If only there wasn’t that ‘w’ on the end of his name, the kid would have been something special, huh? Maybe then it would be me following him, setting him on Justin Hattoni’s throne. Maybe I wouldn’t have to deal with all this shit… seventy fucking years old." Palo eyed him serious then, holding his wine low. "That’s another thing," he raised his concern. "How long can you keep this up, Nicholas? You don’t need this shit on your conscience no more." Nicholas thought about how to answer that for a long time, staring sideways so not to see Palo studying him. "How old are you now, Palo? Forty-five?" Nicholas didn’t wait for an answer. "Perhaps you’re old enough to remember a time when we got a taste of everything that went on outside of the law. Everytime a car got jacked, every card game - everytime someone stole a fucking cupcake from a God damn bakery, they mailed us the crumbs." Palo nodded. "And now? Look at this shit. You know why my house is bigger than Justin Hattoni’s? Than the fucking boss’ house? Because I stick to the old ways!" Nicholas thumbed his chest. "Nothing goes on in my territory without a couple of points coming back to me. These other fucks…" Nicholas waved his hand in disgust. "Now Doug Hattoni… his house was even bigger than mine. The man was made of steel. Like me, like you. I never had that skill with my fists like you do, but I’ve got it up here." Nicholas pointed to his temple. "And I’ve got it here." - to his stomach. Again, Palo nodded, this time with a soft sigh. "It’s the God damn television’s fault," Nicholas went on, giving in to the bitter old man inside him. "Fucking Al Capone in the thirties - that’s where all this shit started. Kids running around with guns in sports cars, calling themselves ‘gangsters.’ Fucking disgusting. Only got worse from there. Fucking Sopranos. Everyone thinks it’s all about some fat prick acting like a fucking buffoon. And those" -he said these next words with particular, shaken disdain- "God damn Godfather movies!" Nicholas sighed and gathered his composure. Despite his usual poise, he’d always been given to these little tantrums. Palo was used to it by now. "I’m an old man, Palo," admitted Nicholas, "but with what years I’ve got left in me, I plan to set this ship straight. That all starts with the death of Jason Snow." *** *** *** November 1, 2006 *** *** *** Jason "Why don’t you ever bring your coffee into the livingroom?" Emily asked as she entered the kitchen, moving toward the refrigerator. Because the pictures are in the livingroom, he did not reply. Instead he sipped his coffee and stared at the front page of the newspaper; toward the bottom, a headline read: "Growing Gangland Violence." He knew he wasn’t going to read that article. "Well?" His flask was tucked tightly into the front pocket of his jeans, cold on his thigh. Rum tasted like shit in coffee. She held him in her hard gaze for a moment longer, and then, finally, her face softened and she sat down opposite him at the kitchen table. She snatched the paper and opened it, absently looking over news stories that she didn’t care about. She looked a lot like Raven, he reflected for the millionth time. Her hair was lighter and longer, and her eyes weren’t quite so big, but every feature was similar - her nose, her mouth… More than her features, it was the gestures - the way she rolled her eyes when she thought something was immature; the way she tucked her lips tight when she was making a decision. And she had endless patience with Haley. "So when’s the crying girl coming back?" She asked, her eyes still down-turned. "The crying girl?" "Angela." Jason wasn’t quite sure how to answer that. Angela had stayed with them for three days in total, after being delivered to his door by Johnny Silver. It was for her protection, but he couldn’t tell Emily that. That would lead to a conversation about Johnny, and ultimately about what it really was that Jason did when he went out on "business." He wondered what her reaction would be… And then he thought of the way she rolled her eyes and the way she tucked her lips. He knew what Raven’s reaction would have been. "I think she’s back at her apartment now," he told her. "Oh?" Her eyes danced. "What was the excuse again? It was being fumigated?" Jason waited for her to continue, now his turn for suspicious looks. Emily had absolutely no ability to disguise her thoughts with her face. He knew there was more coming. "Come on, Jay," she finally went on, rolling her eyes the way Raven had rolled hers when she was alive. "You can tell me that you have a girlfriend. I was the one that was pushing you for it, remember?" "She’s not my girlfriend," he gave a half smile and a raised eyebrow. "Yeah, right," Emily laughed. "Some strange girl, who happens to be gorgeous, shows up out of the blue and stays for three days… She slept in your room, Jay." "I slept on the couch." With the pictures all around, he thought. She rolled her eyes again. "I’m young, but I’m not stupid." "Emily," Jason leaned in, just a little, and shook his head. "I’m not dating her." Emily took a moment to gauge him, her brow furrowing and her eyes narrowing; her lips tucked tightly under her teeth. "Well why not!?" she finally exploded, as much as a girl of her make could explode. "We’ve been over this Emily," he said as he slid the newspaper back toward himself. "Obviously we didn’t go over it well enough!" she chirped. "The girl was here, crying in your arms - she stayed here for three days! And she was nice too! I got to know her pretty well. She’d be perfect for you!" Her eyes danced again. "Believe me, I’m an expert on these things." She flicked her eyebrows playfully. "She just had a miscarriage, Emily," Jason told her the truth, or at least the small portion of it that he was comfortable telling. "Somebody did something pretty bad to her." He watched her face change. "Somebody…" her voice trailed off as Jason bit his lip. Over and over, the image of Anthony Dugaze and Angela Alissandro played in his mind. He was hitting her, she had confided in Jason in her days at his home; kicking her in her pregnant stomach while she cried and screamed on the floor, shrieking for someone to help her. Anthony Dugaze didn’t realize how lucky he was to be in a prison cell. "Still," Emily went on, softer now, "I thought you were going to think about, you know, trying again - starting again." "I thought about it," he shrugged as he opened the paper. Now it was his turn to absently look through, trying anything he could think of to avoid this conversation. He should have known that wasn’t going to happen. "And?" "I just can’t, Emily," he leveled his stare on her. "Alright?" "No," she shook her head. "It’s not alright." "It’s alright for me." "No, it’s not," she replied softly, and she was right. Intuition, he guessed. "You can talk to me, Jay," she told him warmly, and then she added something that caught him off-guard: "We’re family, right?" Family had always been a thing with Manderly girls, from Raven to Danielle, and now Emily. "Right," he said quietly, submitting. "What about the other one? From Haley’s school?" "Alyssa?" he gave a self-loathing smirk. "I kind of messed things up there." "How?" The scene flashed in his mind; her lips on his - passionate, hungry. Their bodies pressed close, and then, just as he had been losing himself in the moment, finally letting his guard down, a title wave of Raven had come crashing over him. Alyssa hadn’t handled that well. "Lets not get into that," was what he told Emily. "That’s just not happening." "Well have you tried?" Emily persisted. "Girls like that, you know. You should go see her." "That won’t do any good," he chuckled, still self-loathing. "Well, it can’t do any harm either," she shrugged. Emily had a practical way of looking at things that Jason thought was probably uncommon in girls her age. "She’ll probably be happy to see you." "I doubt that." She rolled her eyes. "Oh!" she exclaimed. "And you can bring flowers!" "I can’t bring flowers." "Sure you can," she went right on, "they have people to help you in the shops, so you don’t even really have to make any decisions." That’s how I met Raven, he thought. "Or I can go with you," she was still talking, probably fantasizing about the beginning of some great romance between Jason and Alyssa that just wasn’t possible. "There’s this place that I go by on my way home from school that I’ve been meaning to stop into. Do you have anything to do today? We should go right now, then you can go see what’s-her-name tonight. Sound good?" Her eyes were still dancing. He couldn’t reply for a moment. There were a million things on his mind that he wanted to say, or maybe there were a million feelings in his stomach that he didn’t know how to put into words. He opened his mouth a few times, but then reconsidered. He hated these moments, but he knew he had to say something. Emily Manderly, much like both of her sisters, was not a girl to let things go unsaid. "This isn’t going to be easy for me, Emily," was all that came out, and perhaps, it summed up those million thoughts in his stomach. Her face softened again, and where a moment ago it had been playful, now there was only empathy. She even reached across the table and touched his hand, for only a moment, and then she nodded. That had all been brought down in the chaos that unfolded after the old Don, Doug Hattoni, died. The white leader dropped within a couple of months, blinded mysteriously and then killed while he was being treated for the wound. While the Italians and the whites scrambled, the blacks took the place over. They called themselves the "Thugs" and were run by the massive, and deadly, J-Thug.
