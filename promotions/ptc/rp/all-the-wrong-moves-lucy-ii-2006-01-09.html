All the Wrong Moves: Lucy II

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-09 The Second Year of Lucy Phoenix, Arizona Wednesday, January 01, 2003 She had haunted his dreams the night before. There she was, walking by the lake, her figure illuminated only be the moonlight. Her hips swayed gently as she walked toward the area where Gwynn rested, watching her, awestruck. And she would smile, and he would smile back. Her hand would reach forward, inviting him. He would rise and go to her. But there the dream would end. He knew that their lips would never meet again, not on New Years Eve nor any other day. She was not his, just a drunk, a prisoner of scotch. Nonetheless, William felt the call of duty to pick up the phone and give it a try. Perhaps she would remember him, perhaps she wasn't so drunk. Maybe William had a shot after all. He clutched the phone in his hand, and dialed the numbers she had given him. He placed the phone down and put it on speaker, not wanting to hold the phone to his ear. He heard the ringing. And he heard it again. "Hello - " "Hello, this is William Gwynn, is Lucy there?" " - you have reached time and temperature in Phoenix, Arizona. It is currently 9:39 A.M. and seventy-eight degrees with a wind blowing out of the northwest at twelve mile an - " the phone flew. Gwynn's fist slammed into the receiver. Everything shattered, and Gwynn's head was in his hands. 2003 was not a better year after all. Interlude Phoenix, Arizona Sunday, January 01, 2005 " … and that's the day I gave up," William Gwynn says, "I gave up altogether. My hopes had risen to that point where, if they're shattered, they're gone. At least that's what I'd thought at the time, and I'm not sure I've ever really recovered from that setback in that particular department of my life. Despite my turmoils of the days past, I was forced to go to the office on the second and sit in my litle cubicle, working out little financial glitches and finding loopholes. The dollar bill was not my salvation. Igloo worked there, too, and he certainly will have told everyone of my misfortune. In an office full of nerds, you'd expect many single people, but the truth is that they all found their little nerdy wives, and tend to make fun of the ones who can't. And I was not in the mood to be teased, if I remember correctly … " A Day at the Office Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 The black dress shoes tapped against the tile in the lobby. It was just another day at the office, but it was a different genre of day for William Gwynn. That day, William Gwynn had no hope. He turned a corner, and pressed the up arrow beside the elevator. The door crept open, and William slipped inside. Several other people were coming up the hallway behind him, so he begrudgingly held the elevator for them, saying not a word as they entered. William pressed the button for the third floor, and the three other men all seemed to be going to the fifth floor. William did not know a single one of them, though he has seen some of them before. He tapped the button for the fifth floor, unintentionally drawing the attention of the three co-workers in the elevator. "Hey Mr. Gwynn," one said, "heard about your trouble Tuesday night." William was annoyed at the fact that the slimy man knew his name, and even more annoyed at the comment. "What did you hear?" William asked as politely as he could manage, though he knew the answer, and he already knew the informant. "Oh," he said, slightly confused, "Igloo just told us that you were hitting on a drunkard, that she was all over you, and that you STILL couldn't get her in the sack." He chuckled, the other two laughed with him, almost as echoes. "Is that so?" William grumbled under his breath. "I'm not sure that's how I would describe the situation," William growled, but they did not pay him any mind, still laughing. He exited the elevator on his floor, already in a grumpy mood. He already knew that this would be the worst day of his young life. He already knew that he was bound to snap. He already knew that he shouldn't be here. He should have called in sick, hung over, something, anything. The time for that, he knew, was long since past. He ignored the rest of his co-workers until he arrived in his cubicle. He immediately turned on his computer and leaned back in his chair. He realized that he needed a cup of coffee. He rose from his desk and walked toward the kitchen, in a slightly better mood than he was in just minutes before. Coffee Break Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 When he entered the kitchen, there were two people already in there. One was a pudgy female in work clothes far too tight for her. The other a nerdy male, in a checkered dress shirt and dress slacks too short for him. To his credit, he was wearing black socks. The sight was not pretty, but William knew that it could be worse. They could be making fun of him, too. Fortunately, they obviously were not in a position to be doing that. "Hey!" the nerdy guy whispered to the pudgy woman, "that's William Gwynn!" Gwynn shook his head to himself, muttering. For the most part, he pretended like he didn't hear it. "Oh!" the woman said in recognition, "you mean that guy that Igloo told us couldn't get any from a drunk woman?" "That's him!" the nerdy guy said, and then chortled. The woman snorted, in full pig form. She finally finished her little seizure of laughter and came to what was left of her senses. "I don't even think I would have gone out with him, even before I was married," she said. William smiled to himself, and bitterly responded. "That's reassuring," the woman was hurt, immediately. Tears filled her eyes, not adequately shielded from common vision by the rolls and creases beneath her eyes. "That was completely unprofessional," the nerdy guy said, getting in the face of William Gwynn, "you hurt that woman's feelings. You should be ash - " William Gwynn was not ashamed, and he shoved the nerd into the cabinets. William grabbed his coffee mug and exited the kitchen, simultaneously somewhat aggrivated and somewhat satisfied with himself. Back to Work Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 When William Gwynn arrived back in his little cubicle, he found several of the things he had been working on earlier in the week. He had scribbled notes in the margins, and even though they were designed to help him when he returned, he found them not only illegible, but also unintelligible. "Department of International Relations working in the red" was one of the few he understood, but even so he didn't know what he was supposed to do about it. William Gwynn can't just make the numbers work. He can't just apparate dollars. If he could, many of his problems would have passed long since. For one, he never would have moved to the United States of America. As much as the place is beautiful and all that, he preferred it nice and calm in Scotland. Up there you didn't have to worry about getting nuked or terrorists. What kind of idiot would attack the Scots? They might find William Gwynn and William Wallace have a little more than their first name in common. He had dazed off again, and this time about medieval warfare. That was inexcusable, even by his relatively low standards. He picked up the calculator laying on his dead, and suddenly wondered how it got there. It was not a calculator that he was familiar with. He frowned and went to work. He placed the paper in front of him, and checks his math. "Seventeen thousand," stated the accountant, "plus last year's income, three million, three hundred thousand, four hundred and sixty seven dollars and forty four cents. Subtract this year's pay, one million, two hundred and five thousand, nine hundred and twenty nine dollars and sixty two cents. Zero? What the … it can't possibly be zero. I haven't put the expenditures in yet … fine, I'll just ignore it. Minus … one million, six hundred and forty nine thousand, one hundred and thirteen dollars and ninety seven cents. Negative … no, that's not right. DAMN IT! What'd I do wrong? Let's try it again." He tries it again, and again, and again. He keeps getting negative numbers. "THE COMPANY MADE A PROFIT! IT WAS THE BEST YEAR EVER! DON'T MAKE ME REPORT A LOSS! MY BOSS WILL BE SO MAD!" And people laughed behind him. William went red. "What?" he snapped. "I'm having a crisis." "Can't get your calculator to work?" someone asked. William glared. "I hear he can't get something else to work either, if you know what I mean," someone declared. Another one chuckled. "Sounds like he's been having some trouble with his equipment lately." "Listen," William growled, "either tell me what the hell you're talking about or get away from my cubicle - " The men laughed again. "Eskimo-boy switched your calculator for a trick calculator, because he knew you were working on the year-end figures - " he said. "Igloo was hoping that you wouldn't notice the deficit and hand it in to the CEO." "Fine," William muttered. "Just get the hell out. I'll get a new calculator. You bastards just wasted half my morning. Year-end numbers aren't something to be joking around with, it's very important if you want a paycheck." "I'm pretty sure I'm going to get mine," one of the more arrogant ones said, "people check your work, you know. It's really a question of whether you keep your job, isn't it? Get your paycheck?" And he turned his back and left. William grumbled. That was someone higher up on the food chain than he was, but he didn't particularly care. William Gwynn would not have cared if his boss walked in and fired him at that very moment. William grabbed a calculator and went back to work. His frustration made it so that he could not get the numbers to come out right, even with a calculator that he was sure was not playing tricks on him. His brain had been ravaged. His heart had been stolen from him. William Gwynn's motivation had been swept away by the breeze of love, though it be absent in his young life. Hours Later The co-workers gathered around the cubicle of William Gwynn. They had gathered surprises for him. They had placed them all about his desk as he had gone to the restroom. They had all returned to their separate workstations, to wait for the noise that would most surely soon ensue. He returned minutes later, not even slightly refreshed. His head was in his hands. His hair was disheveled, obviously held and pulled at recently. His facial features spelled "stress". He entered his cubicle and found all of the presents. He thought it was some demonic Christmas. He decided not to open them, the dismay of his co-workers. Eventually, someone walked into the cubicle. "Aren't you going to open those?" she asked. "I don't think so," William replied, with a pencil firmly between his teeth, working full steam. "Oh, okay," she considered, "well, I'll open them for you. Let's start with this. Ooh. Look at this magazine. I wasn't aware you were into that kind of - wow. Well, that will get you through some of those cold, lonely, Phoenix, Arizona nights, won't it?" There is some hooting and hollering in the building. "And what's this? A gift card? For … Miss Mystique, is it? Isn't that just wonderful? Someone went out and got you a psychic to help you with all of your problems, William! And another one to … oh, a lingerie store. I'm sure that one will expire before you get to use it, or … well, I mean, some guys shop there for themselves. Maybe someone knows more about you than I do - " her voice trails off, and many of the men in the office catcall. "Ma'am," William interjected, "I'm trying to crunch the numbers. I'm going to have to ask you to leave this cubicle. This cubicle is not big enough for the both of us." William made a gesture inferring that his counterpart weighed far too much to fit in a cubicle. She seemed hurt and decided to leave, but left the presents behind. William swept them all into an empty area of his desk. He looked down at the gift card for the psychic. It offered a free gaze into the future from one of the highest-esteemed fortune tellers in the area. A head peeked around the corner, it was the head of Igloo. "Let's go get lunch," he said. William had a lot he wanted to talk to Igloo about. "Alright," William muttered, "but let's get out of this building today for a change." "Agreed," Igloo said. William tossed the card into his pants pocket and left with Igloo. Lunch Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 It turned out that William was wrong about Igloo. He wasn't the one that had narced out. It was the other co-worker that was with them that night. He was slimy. He was gruesome. He was the prime suspect. The only problem was that William did not think he was present at the time when William was with Lucy. William thought that only Igloo had seen them. He thought that Igloo was jealous, as perhaps he could justifiably have been. William had gotten the girl on this occasion, and he was just the slim accountant. How could the slim accountant have beaten out the man of Eskimo heritage for the pretty girl? Of course she was drunk, which eventually eased Igloo's jealousy. She couldn't have possibly have picked William while sober. William sighed as he heard the story of how it had spread. "I'm sorry," William apologized to Igloo, "I thought you had told everyone. Why do they make it out to be such a big deal?" Igloo shook his head. "You've always struggled," he responded, "I've never understood it. You could go out and get the girl, but you couldn't keep her. It's a combination of bad luck and nerves, I guess. And you've got everything else. You've got the car, the job, the clothes, the house, the money, the strength. The only thing you don't have is a lover, and they need something to poke a hole in you with, I suppose." William smiled in reminiscence. "She was perfect," he said. "I called that number, you know." "I figured you would," Igloo murmured, "and what happened?" "She gave me the number for time and temperature," William growled, and Igloo laughed. "Compadre," Igloo said, "she was a walking brewery. Her breath was pure ethanol. She probably pissed drinkable beer, of the poorer variety, mind you. I wouldn't worry about it. Your co-workers are douches, but even they will get over it in time. Are you feeling alright, William? You look like you've got a headache." "I'm fine," William responded, but an idea seized his thoughts. Convenience Migraine Phoenix, Arizona Thursday, January 02, 2003 William Gwynn went up to the office of his direct superviser. "Sir," he said, "I've got a migraine. I was wondering if it would be an issue if I went hoime for the day. I am really not feeling up to the task today." His boss looked him up and down, and, convinced, submitted. "Certainly, soldier," he said with a smile, "get well, would you? We could use a brain like yours buzzing around the office at full speed tomorrow. About time to be pumpin' out those numbers for me, hm?" And then it was that pang in the stomach. The guilt had gotten to him. He tried not to show it in his face. As he walked out of his boss' office, and later the office building, he thought about that gift card in his pocket. He thought about the answers that the young Miss Mystique might be able to give him. His mind was in the air, and, for a fleeting moment, William Gwynn had hope again. Fade.
