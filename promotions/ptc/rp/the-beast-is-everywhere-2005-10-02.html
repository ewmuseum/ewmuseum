The Beast Is Everywhere

2 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 1. The Mark of the Beast Paddy O’Shea stands in the shadow of his own home afraid. I suppose you would too considering the whirlwind his life is trapped in, under the constant manipulation of the man called Hate. This man, this Hate, who had come within a split second of executing his father, burned his caravan and week after week beat Paddy within an inch of his life. And now he has left a once defiant and proud Irishman to wallow in fear in his own domain. Come Super Series though, he will have a chance to end all of this. Even Hate admitted, no matter what the outcome, it would come to an end. Whether that means being able to walk away from it is another question. In the lead up to the match, Hate’s campaign of terror has been increased. Maybe Paddy’s just a paranoid wreck but every time he goes out, even to buy milk at a corner shop, he sees the signs. And they’re everywhere. Whether it’s a slogan painted on the wall reading ‘Cleanse the impure’ or constantly seeing red painted faces in large crowds of people, the signs are there. Hate is watching him and testing him. Best thing about all this is Paddy knows that yet can’t see the reality of it all leaving him the nervous wreck standing before us. Paddy steps out from the shadow overlooking a table. These last few months have certainly taken their toll on him. He’s aged incredibly quickly and the stress is apparent in every crease and blemish on his once carefree face. Yet it’s his eyes that underline his pain. Those mad eyes, dancing around in their sockets like tribesmen around a fire. Always looking, always planning. The wind outside that has been blowing at a consistently low speed suddenly whistles past the window of his lounge with unprecedented velocity causing Paddy to jump back in fear. Only the wind he tells himself yet even the voice in his head seems unsure. Now Paddy stares out of the window, mesmerised by the tranquillity of the moon, which stands proudly amidst the stars, like a king before his citizens. The concept seems epic yet so simple at the same time and how he longs for that simplicity. The windowpane, hazy from condensation, blurs the moonlight somewhat. Paddy raises a finger to the pane and slowly traces the moons outline, letting untainted light seep through. Standing in only a pair of grey slacks, damp from cold sweats, Paddy looks childlike, like the loner, bullied child of the past who looked for refuge that was never there. Just like now, he has now no refuge from that man. Paddy presses his nose up to the window with a sigh. Once again he yearned for simplicity and in particular that of youth. He stares out of the window yet can only see himself, reflected with the aid of the internal lights. The streetlights went out last night and as of yet, the city workers have been unable to find the problem (another sign for Paddy’s frantic mind). Then a thought strikes him suddenly, a thought outlandish yet in his desperation, rationale as well – Hate has never attacked him at his home and with the match tomorrow, its unlikely he will break that principle. Paddy can almost feel a smile of relief tweaking at the corners of his mouth until… BANG! A man slams against the window from the outside although the amount of blood that comes along with it could easily make it another large mammal. Paddy falls backwards with a silent scream and lands hard on the wooden floor, his head cracking off the ground. Numb to the pain however, Paddy slides away on his back frantically, his breathing now deep and hard. He looks up again to see the man sliding down the window slowly, his eyes staring at him in terror, both dried and fresh blood plastered all over his face. For Gods sake, he can see some of the dry black blood flaking off his brow. Suddenly he slides out of view, the window now stained with a significantly large trail. Paddys mind goes into a frenzy over what just happened and what he has to do now. A man is dying or is dead just outside his window and he seriously doubts he did it to himself. (Hate) There’s no proof of that. Okay the streetlights were put out but the fact something like that happens when he can’t see it coming may just be coincidence, right? It’s a possibility. Right? Paddys questions are suddenly answered for him for in the complete darkness outside, something comes so close to the pane that its clearly visible…a black gloved hand. Staring transfixed from his seated position on the floor, Paddy looks up and sees the hand first wave at him casually then beginning to draw something into the blood in the window. The careful, steady finger then pulls away from the window to reveal a face, red from the canvas it’s painted on. (Hate) Now that Paddys questions are now answered, the feeling in his legs suddenly comes back and he’s immediately on his feet, running, first up the stairs then into the bathroom, anywhere safer, anywhere away from that thing. The only thing is there no place safe from Hate. Infact, right now, the wrestling ring at Super Series seems appealing to him for at least there he can look into the face of the beast. One day to go… 2. Seeking Refuge “It’s been awhile hasn’ it old fella?” The scene is in the porch of a Roman Catholic Church where Paddy stands alone. Brought up by not the most committed Christians, Paddy unsurprisingly didn’t engage in religious practise very often. The odd time he was forced to a Sunday service, he went through a stage of intense boredom that often had him jokingly questioning the importance of life. But he did believe. He was just lazy. Hopefully the guy in the sky lets that one slide then when the time comes. But it has been at least five years since he was in a Church. Five years where he neglected his Lord so why should he help him now? “Because if ye do, aye swear to G-, well you aye mean, tha’ from no’ on things will be different? Jus’ give me five minutes t’ outline me case. Okay?” Paddy is staring up at a picture of the Almighty on the cross. The picture is a modern take on the crucifixion with vibrant colours in the sky illustrating the magnitude of the event. Paddy is staring at this picture with a clearly forced smile on his face he’s trying to joke about as if he was charming an actual person but his shaky complexion and tone of voice show just how fucked up he is internally. Paddy looks around the small porch now for the first time. The pale yellow walls are disrupted with various cracks, meandering throughout the structure; plates of paint missing leaving the plasters delicate pink colour exposed like a gaping wound for all to see. The once proud wooden floor has long since been worn, the originally varnished surface almost white from wear. Paddy grimaces at a large table of about six feet by four. With only three legs intact, it’s being held up by a stack of missals placed on a chair. A few old community newsletters lay neglected on this table. Infact most of the room seems to be in dire straits as Paddy circles, scanning it quickly. Mismatched candles unlit and in a small pile in the corner, a wilted plant that obviously has some significance to palm Sunday by the type of leaves it bears, and various plaques on the wall that try to outline the stages of the crucifixion yet a number of absent stages of the story seems make the story and entirely different one to the one he knows because Jesus doesn’t apparently seem to die or to even drop the cross. To top off a thoroughly disheartening and woeful set up is the fact that everything is coated with a light layer of dust giving the room a sandy feel. Indeed the grittiness of the scene can almost be felt. Paddy turns once again and looks at the picture of the crucifixion. Despite everything else’s poor state in this room, this picture seems perfect to Paddy. No chips, no scratches, no discolouring – the power of the picture now touches Paddy as beautiful. With a sigh, Paddy turns to his right and pushes his way through a set of mahogany double door. As they creak open, Paddy slowly sees that indeed the actual building of the church is very much in the same condition as the entranceway. A number of pews overturned, carpet tore and a number of boarded up windows. Paddy suddenly questions himself as to this place actually being open. A solitary lit candle on the alter assures him that it indeed is. The two blocks of pews are interrupted with an aisle that leads from where he’s standing right up to the altar. Paddy now slowly and reverently makes his way to the actual altar area, his eyes now fixed on the massive crucifix that highlights everything this Church believes in. “God…,” Paddy squeaks as he danders forward, eyes still locked onto the same spot. “Aye’m not perfect. Aye’m not even close. But aye have tried. Okay, aye haven’t even come t’ mass but aye’ve done other things like…like…well nothin’ off the top o’ me head but ye know better than me tha’ aye have tried.” Paddy’s voice trails off as he loses his composure. Approaching a number of steps, he falls onto his knees, the swiftness of the move being compensated by the spongy fabric under foot. A deep blue colour that like everything else here just doesn’t match. “God…aye’ve come t’ the end of me tether…fer a start aye can feel tha’ aye’m slippin’ away. Me mind tha’ is. It be slippin’ away an’ every time aye think aye might jus’ have got things back under control he does somethin’ else and aye have t’ start again.” Paddy drops his head to the ground, staring past the carpeted surface and into his mind. He’s needed someone to talk to for a long time about this but only now has he realised the only person worth listen to was here. Paddy looks at the palms of his hands now, those scarred and battle hardy hands. He once held such hands in high esteem, so did other people too. He could swing one hell of a punch and he would destroy men twice his size. Then Hate came along and broke him and everyone lost respect for him. Now to them he’s just some sympathy hungry Irishman thriving off the exposure Hate’s acts have created. Of only those same people knew what it was like to stare helplessly as their father was standing over a drop of sixty feet, ready to be dropped by the beast. Or how about those people experience what it’s like to watch their home with everything that they ever held close to their heart, blow up in a raging inferno. Or how about those people witness a man die on their doorstep as their only stronghold is ripped away. Well, Paddy thinks he died anyway. The reason being when Paddy managed to pull himself from his hiding place this morning, all signs of anything actually happening were gone. Maybe he imagined it and that doesn’t even seem very unlikely with his state of mind at the minute but would Hate go to such extremes just to fuck with Paddy’s head some more? You better believe he would. “Aye doubt anything aye say will have an impact on ye. Hell, aye wouldn’t listen t’ me after some o’ the things aye’ve done but when yer fightin’ the devil himself, well ye be the only one t’ turn t’. And aye need ye…please. Just…don’t let me die.” Paddy swallows a lump in his throat as he feels tears well up. However the prospect of him crying is ended when a hand clasps his shoulder tightly. Paddy spins around so quickly that the arm connected to the hand goes reeling around in a spin. Paddy is suddenly on his feet, fists raised. “Calm down son!” Now Paddy is staring into the face of a priest and he breaths a sigh of relief. He immediately drops his fists and apologises. “Sorry father, aye’m just a little jumpy.” “Anything you want to talk about son?” The priest asks kindly, his small smile obviously trying to cheer Paddy up. “It’s fine thanks Father. Aye just needed t’ speak with the big man.” The priest nods knowingly and places a hand on Paddys shoulder. “The Almighty can always help you son. Don’t be afraid to ask him.” Suddenly Paddy feels the grip on his shoulder tighten and he screams as the priest’s fingers dig into his shoulder. “Well almost always. But definitely not when you go up against the beast.” Paddy tries to pull away from the man, but he’s pulled back with sudden inhuman strength. Now Paddy is staring into the mans face. His teeth are bared and his eyes are bulging from his skull. A thin trickling of saliva is edging from a side of his mouth. “You will die pig. Isn’t that right master?” The priest head suddenly looks up at the massive crucifix behind the altar. Only thing is that the head on the Christ is now completely red and the image of Hate flashes in Paddys mind. Now the priest is screaming with laughter into Paddys face yet even now Paddy can’t pull away, his lack of strength obviously accountable to the situation. “Cleanse the impure!” Paddy breaks his paralysis with a jolt and pulls away from the man. He never looks back as he blindly runs away. It’s a long time however when the ringing laughter is gone. Even at home the memory of it haunts him. Yet the message was clear and underlines one key, key fact. Hate’s out to kill. And there will be no mercy. And only a miracle can save Paddy O’Shea now. END
