The Messenger

25 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-02-25 The lights in the bar grow brighter every time I look up. That’s largely thanks to the dying apple martini sitting in front of me (and the eight before that), one sip away from contented disappearance. It’s just a typical bar sign, but now in the fuzzy drunken vision I’m enduring, it’s like a commercial halo, welcoming in all those saintly dollars and holy cents. This, I think, is the way you celebrate a bracket championship. Wearing youthful pink doesn’t change the fact that the bartender is well past the years when she could’ve gotten away with it. Her prettiness is only in scars and whispers now, her face admitting an eternal dissatisfaction that is numb to the drinks she serves to halfwits and nobodies. It’s strange to see a bartender who doesn’t have perky tits and a façade to match, but at this point, the server of the potations doesn’t matter; only the effect does. You might wonder how a wrestler with growing national renown is able to escape into a bar in the waning hours of the night, but you’d probably be surprised at my penchant for finding places that are sullied enough where no looks up from their drink anyway. If someone recognized me, I’d probably get the "you won the bracket that Angelo Deville left" treatment. I didn’t realize just what Deville meant to these people, his…fans (?). Sure, I was borderline obsessing over beating him, but even beating him doesn’t put his soul to rest. He’s gone, but painfully not forgotten, like shards of a knife floating in my bloodstream; too dangerous to remove, equally as dangerous to leave sitting there. From my booth (I’m prima donna enough to refuse to sit at the bar itself), I glance up at the only other patrons. A man and a woman are sharing my overpowering ecstasy, but they’re not together. There are four isolated people, each with their own orbit around the clock and whatever has to be done tomorrow. With a twinge, I realize that I have to do mindless press stuff tomorrow morning. I’m a rotten interview when I haven’t gotten my sleep, but the devil on my shoulder shrieks about how no one will take me as anything more than the underdog anyway. "Ma…miss? Uno mas"! My cry for replenishment breaks the silence only for a moment and, as if aware that time has already rejected her pleas to delay its continuing march, scuttles around detachedly for my fountain of youth. It’s a bad sign that it takes me a couple seconds to recall Shanty Jones’ name. As has been my credo throughout the tournament, I’ll trust that he can learn my name: his doesn’t matter to me. At this rate, I may still be trashed when I’m supposed to step into the ring and really. It’s a wretched thought process to go through when this match could validate my run or tell all the people in magazines that I’m just a shoddy fill-in for Deville. There’s still no sign of the old bean. After I did my best conquistador impression and stormed his castle (which I was sure would draw the nasty old alligator of a man out of hiding), the front remained quiet. Perhaps he’s really gone, I ponder just long enough to make it to the moment where the waitress plops down my apple martini. The liquid, almost too green to be believed, sloshes onto both sides of the glass, only to find their way back down into the cavity below. I’ve got almost superhuman focus when I’m drunk. * * * * * In the encircling night, it seems much chillier and that’s a shame. My heart feels warmed, but my buzz is wearing off more quickly waiting for a cab. The logical next step to abating my heart’s mercurial pounding is a cigarette, but I can’t justify doing any more damage to my body just days before a possibly life-changing match. The dark is louder than I first notice. In a city, there are not moments of tranquility; only snapshots, freeze-frames when all the sounds mistakenly stop at the same time. People are still racing around, so busy getting a head start on the next day that they consistently ignore the one right in front of them. The lights are buzzing, grating my nerves, which have previously been turned to mush by the apple elixir. I start hoping my cabbie won’t want to carry on a heavy conversation. A snap sounds behind me, but what, at this time of night, could possibly appear that would surprise me? Instead of taking the time out to watch the rat or whatever it is, crawling attentively through lazy piles of refuse, I sit down on the curb. I decide that the cigarette is a good idea after all. The first spiral of smoke dances turbulently as a wind comes along. Mirroring last round’s efforts in the tournament, my baby coil comes slinking back into my face. A mere smoking novice, I cough ferociously, unable to arrest the powerful throes. A vice grip grabs my chest and shakes it violently, not even affording me time to sneak in one breath. In irony’s greatest feat yet, I’m choking to death on air full of carcinogens. Through the wooly air, I can see the smoke and its web has grown. Everything in front of my eyes is lucidly white, the air crawling with alabaster spirits as the curtain falls. * * * * * I don’t know if I’m dying (or maybe dead…although this doesn’t look like hell), but all I can remember is Shari. In the cascading hallways and alleys of my mind, memories play on repeat. She’s moving towards me, gliding on atoms of cumulus. I’m almost nervous that, like a butterfly, she will wither with my abhorrent, sour touch. I reach out and touch her arm. "You looked positively angelic up there, Miss…?" "Shari. Shari Mansel." Her voice is confident, but it falls back into ambient noise as soon as she’s done talking. Her face is golden, fake tanned as deep as possible for her porcelain doll complexion. Her hair cascades, shining and opaque at the same time. "I’m Chandler Tsonda." "I know who you are." I can’t find the end of the memory. Like the stars you see when you close your eyes, there’s just nothing there of any substance. Zipping to elsewhere in my mind’s eye, I locate her again. In the cocktail dress, there is not a flaw to be seen on her. I take her hand over the table, my gaze traversing through the flaming trail of the candles, deep into hers. In her eyes is a supernova, moving and twirling light at its behest. "Chandler, I love you." The words sting because I can’t, in good conscience, tell her the same. Of course I…maybe, I do. Even as the only woman who could ever see through my piercing vulgarity, she is not privy to everything. There are parts of me that make me cringe, parts of me that stay flooded with gall; infectious like creeping discontent. In my dreams, there has been a ring in my pocket when she says this. Tonight, there is just the last paycheck from PWC. It’s going under and how do I take her with me beneath that wave? Just don’t look into her eyes, I try to remind myself. Why am I thinking of Shari in this, my dying moment? Surely my meeting with her last week brought up this pickle. Maybe, as a ghost, I’m now destined to walk the earth and fix things with Shari. Romantic comedy, here I come. And there’s the light. It’s dimmer than I expected, but hell’s probably not lit too well. Good-bye forev… * * * * * … … "How was your sleep, Mr. Tsonda?" "Oh fuck, you’re not the devil." "No, but if you do not cooperate, you will find me equally lamentable." I can only assume, given the current situation, that that’s not an empty threat. I’m in a small space, no larger than an average dorm room. Like every horror movie you’ve ever seen, I’m sitting in a chair underneath a solitary light bulb. The set-up is almost laughable, except that there is a very not laughable man standing in front of me. His shoulders are broad like mountain ranges, dipping and turning from one end of the prominent collarbone to the other. In the deep dimness, light only licks at his face, revealing a cruel square jaw transfixed, a clinical stare finding me. I’m not tied up, surprisingly, but the reason is sitting in his hands. The knifepoint slides off his fingers smoothly. Clearly, there is a knowledge of the blade within those hands that I’d rather not become acquainted with. "I don’t need to tell you why you’re here, although I should tell you that you were quite enjoyable as I released the concentrated laughing gas." The sensation of all white had not been smoke from the cigarette, but the deception that only one’s own mind can conjure. "I hate to be a stickler here, but usually when a strange man takes me to an even stranger location, I do make a habit of wanting to know why I’m there. Call me old-fashioned." His face does not shift an iota, impenetrable to my biting (and most likely misplaced) wit. "Are you enjoying this tournament, Mr. Tsonda?" There is clear-cut sarcasm on his breath, like asking an inmate if he enjoys jail. I hate to play his game, but this conversation will get nowhere without my playing along. Now, I realize, it may seem strange to some that I’m not freaking out or just running for the exit. Let me tell you two things: 1) a human mind is groomed to recognize dicey situations before "odd" ones and 2) I have no doubt that the knife will enter my cerebral cortex if I don’t play smart here. It’s not as much frightening as it is inescapable fact. "This has something to do with the tournament?" I sputter. Of course it does, but I figure more flustered questions leads to more concrete answers from this unnamed assailant. No, I’m not trying to get answers to figure out a way to escape. I’m trying to give him what he wants so I can live for at least a couple more years. "Surely, you wouldn’t kidnap a man and risk going to jail for years over infantile sports entertainment?" Apparently, he’s been reading too many Rich Rollins RP’s. BA DUM CHA! [/idiocy] "Mr. Tsonda, during the last round you created quite a stir." Last round? Salaok doesn’t strike me as the kidnapping type, but if there’s one thing I’ve learned in rejoining the wrestling community, it’s that, like anything, there are those who would stake life and more on it. "Your antics were noted in several newspapers and all across the internet." "My ‘antics?’ You mean the Deville thing?" And before the words are out of my mouth, it all computes perfectly. Who else but the Deville had been hurt by my efforts enough to send a goon on the warpath? Who else even had the means to do so? "You…work for Deville?" I consider several possibilities, but none give me pause that the man standing in front of me could be Angelo Deville. As someone who knows reconstructive facial surgery, I’m certain that the differences are too emphatically natural. "Ahh, yes…the plot thickens. I represent certain interests of Mr. Deville’s. Interests that you seem hell-bent on tarnishing to no end, Mr. Tsonda." His address is provident, like a teacher unwillingly scolding a star pupil. "Do you comprehend what your efforts, no matter how ultimately meaningless, are bringing to the table for Mr. Deville?" "Deville’s alive?" "If you are dense enough to believe otherwise, you are a fool." "So what? I beat him and now he’s gone off for public relations detail. He’s figuring out the spin to put on getting his ass handed to him by a pretty boy Asian." Hey, in the face of danger, I can’t forget my mojo. "Where does a knife-wielding henchman fit into that?" "More than you might think," comes the response, the most terrifying thing I’ve heard yet. His voice reveals, unconditionally, that our dealings are no more than business to this man. "You have yet to ask the smart questions, though, so I will give you a chance, before I reveal to you what may be the final ultimatum of your life." "Who are you?" I gulp and hopefully, in the dark, he can’t see the increasingly visible fear. However, he clearly put himself at a vantage point to read my every facial expression. He chuckles unsettlingly, either at the question or my face. "You may call me Mr. Richardson, if we must use formalities." "So, Deville’s pissed off that I’m tarnishing his image while he’s picking his spots to come back?" "Incorrect." He’s robotic in his delivery now, toying with what I do and do not know. "Mr. Deville is moving on to other sectors of business and, unfortunately for you, he does not want anyone in this industry to forget why so many have held their tongues for so long." Oh yes, I get to be the final sacrificial lamb; one final reminder why you do not fuck, ever, with Angelo Deville. "Why not Rich Rollins? He had Deville in his sights from day one!" I find myself half-pleading now, almost expecting this conversation to end with Mr. Richardson’s one-liner and a quick slash to my throat. "He stole the trophy…why not him?" "Oh, but you know the answer to that, Mr. Tsonda. Rich Rollins is globally renowned. If one man can stand on equal footing with Deville, it is Rollins." His voice is playfully malevolent again; a cheery, knowing gloom. "You…you are the glassy-eyed newcomer with delusions of grandeur. You assumed that you would get Angelo Deville in his prime and, blasphemous as it was, you expected to win." Sure, there had been doubt when I prepared for Deville, but I realize that I had been relatively fearless. Only now is thing dawning on me as a negative thing. "So because I talked some smack, I need to be offed." I’m hoping this suggestion will bring some sort of suggestion as to whether I’m surviving to fight Shanty Jones or not. "Like I said, the timing is rather inconvenient for both you and me." He twists in his chair, transferring the knife to his left hand, playfully spinning it by the blade. He leans forward, inspecting me closely. Perhaps this is the end of all things. "There’s nothing I can do?" I realize the plight: there is no negotiating with the Angel of Death. He is objective, only instructed and then rewarded. That high-society accent will be the last thing I hear, muttering about the alpha male or something equally horrid on the ears. "Well…you could…no, there is no chance." "Wait, what?! There is something, isn’t there?" This may be a gleam of false hope, but in the dark, where the light of day is a wispy reflection. "Well…you could win the whole tournament." "I don’t understand." The tournament resounds with Deville. Perhaps, there is something about the complete victory over all comers that binds men on a transcendent stage. "If you were to win the tournament itself, you could theoretically claim to be the best PTC has to offer. In that case, Angelo Deville could stand the publicity." I dare not tell Mr. Richardson that this offer makes little sense to me. My life is damn important. "Fine. Let me go and I’ll win the thing." "Easier said than done." "So was beating Jimmy Bong, Salaok, and the countless others." I know how to win. It’s like riding a bicycle and my training wheels came off five matches ago. "I can do it." "Just know that if you renege on our deal…there will be consequences." The final word is accompanied by his grip finding the hilt of the knife. Very little interpretation needed; subtlety be damned. And like that, Mr. Richardson is up out of his chair, flattening his dress suit, curling the knife up into his sleeve, and disappearing out the door. No longer tied in place, not by ropes but the eyes of the knife, I collapse to the floor and vomit several times, sending an apple martini/bile smoothie onto the ragged floor.
