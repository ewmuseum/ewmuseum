A Short Story: It Slowly Unfolds

1 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-01 The Difficult Choice October19, 2009 New Mexico She wipes the crusty evidence of sleep from the corners of her eyes, everything still a gray haze as she attempts to adjust to her new surroundings. Her name is Adelina DeAngelis and she has been held prisoner in one way or another for quite some time now, but she doubted she could answer exactly how long it had been. “How are you feeling?” She startles slightly, realizing for the first time that the mattress she was laying on was not just tossed on the ground. This is a queen sized bed with beige sheets and a maroon comforter that she must have kicked off of herself while she slept. “I didn't mean to scare you.” The tranquil voice that took her by surprise belongs to the girl sitting with her legs dangling a few inches above the floor across from her. She takes quick note of the girl's tawny brown hair, pulled back into a high pony tail that makes it look like a horse's mane, and the way she props herself up by placing her palms on her knees. The woman is beautiful; her skin is a light shade of bronze that further accentuates the way the light dances in her almond shaped hazel eyes. She has a pink tank top with the word 'precious' scrawled across the chest in a bubble style cursive script, and a pair of khaki shorts that barely reach the top of her thighs. “Did they hurt you?” The girl's interest in Adelina's health seems genuine, but she still isn't sure if she can even trust her. Questions like “Who are you?”, “Where are we?”, and “Who did this?” fill her mind as she takes quick stock of the rest of the room. Apart from the heavy gauge steel door, it looks like a college dorm room. “Hey, you alright?” Adelina nods her head. “You look a little weird.” “Sorry, I'm just...” her voice trails off briefly when she notices the small camera in the top right hand corner of the room. “Is someone watching us?” “Yeah,” the girl replies, taking a quick glance toward the camera before wagging her fingers in a slight wave. “Probably just Josh.” “Josh?” “You'll meet him soon enough,” she turns back to Adelina. “So, what's your name?” “Adelina.” “I'm Jasmine.” Jasmine leans back and uses her hands to prop herself up, crossing her legs and swinging them back and forth. “How long have you been here?” She shrugs her shoulders nonchalantly, never lifting them off of the burgundy comforter that matches her own. “How long have I been here?” “A few hours.” Jasmine cocks her head to the left, not really resting it on her shoulder as she flashes a smile. “I pretended to be asleep when Vesuvius brought you in.” “Vesuvius?” “The big guy,” Jasmine says as she sits up, curling her arms at her sides and puffing out her cheeks before blowing the air out in a giggle. “I don't know his real name, but he reminds me of a mountain.” “Why are you so calm about all of this?” “I learned a long time ago that there's no point fighting it,” Jasmine bites on her bottom lip as she turns her head and lowers her gaze toward the ground. “It's easier that way.” “What did they do to you?” “Nothing, it's just...” she lifts her head and turns back toward Adelina; the moisture, from the tears welling up in the corner of her eyes, glistens under the florescent bulbs from the overhead lighting. “I didn't like what they asked me to do when they first brought me here.” “Did they...” the words got stuck in her throat, unsure if she really wants to know the answer to the question she was about to ask. “No,” Jasmine chuckles nervously, her eyes avoiding Adelina's as they wander aimlessly around the room. “It's nothing like that. It's just that...” The sound of keys jingling, as someone fumbles with them, attract their attention toward the door. “That's probably Josh,” Adelina allows a smile to waltz across her face, replacing the slight pout that decorated them a moment ago. She uses her fingers to rub away the tears that were starting to puddle up in the corners of her eyes. “What he's about to tell you is going to sound weird at first, but listen to him... He only wants to help.” “And how's my number one girl doing today?” A young man, with dirty blond hair that looks as if he ran his fingers through it when it was wet, stands in the open doorway. He folds his arms in front of his chest before leaning against the door's frame. “I'm good,” she replies with a curt smile. “That's nice to hear,” he nods his head when he replies. “Hey Jazz, do you think you can give me and your new roommate a little privacy?” “Of course,” Jasmine answers as if they are old friends. Adelina can hardly believe it, considering they both are being held against their will. “Thanks.” “I'll see you in a bit, okay.” Adelina tries her best to offer up a smile in return, but Jasmine never gets to see it. The large man with a bowling ball for a head, has his hand on the door in order to keep it open. Adelina notices the nod and slight smile that he flashes in Jasmine's direction when she passes. There has to be something that she is missing, because Jasmine doesn't seem to be as scared as she is. “Something wrong?” Adelina turns to face her abductor, the first time she's ever seen him. He looks to be in his mid to late twenties with eyes as blue as the sky, at least that is what he wants her to believe since she can see the ring around the edge that proves them to be a pair of contacts. The door clicks, and she takes note that they are the only two left in the room. “Adelina...” She realizes that she is just staring at him like a teenager with her first crush. He smiles at her, and she notices that she involuntarily does the same. “Are you okay?” “Why did you bring me here?” He shrugs his shoulders slightly, almost as if he doesn't really know the answer to her question, then takes a seat on the edge of Jasmine's bed. “Would you believe me if I said we brought you here to help you?” To help me? “Would you get mad if I ran over and kicked you in the balls?” He laughs heartily, slapping his knee as he shakes his head. “I'd say yes, but I'm pretty sure I deserve it after the last few weeks.” Is he trying to amuse me? Wait... How many weeks is a few? “I mean, we haven't tortured you or anything, but I'm pretty sure you think we want to hurt you.” “You've had me locked up for God knows how long. How should I be reacting right now?” “I felt the same way when I was in your shoes, but I realized that this opportunity was just too good to let pass through my fingers.” “Opportunity? I haven't seen my family in forever and you call it an opportunity.” “Well, you haven't heard the offer yet.” “Maybe I don't want to hear it.” She had been raised to be strong, to defend herself if ever put in a situation just like this one. She knows what she has to do, but what happens after that. Is Vesuvius waiting for her outside the door, waiting to wrap that ham of a hand around her neck if she gets through the door. Could she even get past Josh... “I know you're scared, but there really isn't anything to be scared about. We can give you everything you've ever wanted.” She notices the slight smile that hides in the corner of his lips. It isn't something you'd expect to see from soneone that wants to hurt you, but one you'd expect to see your best friend make when he tells you that he's had a crush on you since you were both in Kindergarten. “Give me fifteen minutes, and we'll give you the World.” The Uneasy Allegiance November 20, 2009 New York He wasn't sure how many of the people in the Mount Sinai parking lot were actually federal agents, but if he had to guess he would place the bet on it being close to ninety percent. The two men to his left, near the white Chrysler 300, were more than obvious with their dark sunglasses and black suits. It was the gentleman with a cast on his right leg - hunched over with his crutches the only means of support - that wasn't quite as obvious. Tony learned at an early age how to spot a cop, but it was the fact that the man used his right foot to prop himself up - instead of his left - that really gave him away. He entered through the revolving door behind an old friend of Cynthia's, Agent Bethany Harper, who had been sent by his father in law, Daniel Berkman, to meet him at the airport when his plane arrived. Daniel had been brief when they spoke on the phone. A bit too cryptic for Gamble's taste, but he agreed to the meeting nonetheless. The man had given him time to enjoy the birth of his daughter, but now that he had gone back to work; it was only a matter of time before he would have to honor the promise he made to answer a few questions about the afternoon he spent with Adelina DeAngelis. He was so focused on what went on that day, he almost missed the curt nod exchanged between Harper and the young male nurse behind the admissions desk. He was possibly the only one Tony wouldn't have pegged for an agent. Not only did being a male nurse draw attention to him, but he had Barney purple scrubs on to boot. "So what's with all the security?" Tony tried his best to keep up with her when she refused to answer, her days in the military evident by her perfect cadence. He knew something was amiss the moment Daniel said he would send someone to pick him up, but Agent Harper's silence only fueled his desire to learn more than what he had been told. It started in the car when they exited the highway and he realized they were not headed toward the local field office. "Someone die?" It was the last comment that stopped Harper dead in her tracks. She spun on her heel with her arm outstretched and her index finger extended in his direction. "Look, I don't like you. Let's just get that out in the open right now. I didn't like it when Cynthia fell for you, and I hated that she ruined a promising career to be with you." "You don't like me. I get it." He laughed. She didn't. "No, you see, it goes a lot further than that." She stepped toward Gamble and looked down at him. "Berkman told me what was in that folder he's been carrying around. He thinks someone's trying to frame you, but I don't believe it." "She's my goddaughter." "And her father was like a brother to you." The reply had Gamble at a loss of words. He had refused to know anything that had to do with DeAngelis' disappearance, that way if he was found it could not be traced back to him, but this woman acted as if she had first hand knowledge of what happened and could nail him to the wall if need be. So he said the first thing that popped into his mind. "You never had a fight with your brother?" "Sure, but I never actually made him disappear." "I don't know what you're insinuating, but I can assure you that I had nothing to do with that." She shook her head, clearly under the impression that nothing out of his mouth would be the truth. "He's probably sipping wine in Sicily right now, trying not to get caught up in trumped up charges from your little witch hunt." "Witch hunt?" If nothing else, Tony knew exactly what buttons to push when he wanted to make someone angry. Harper was definitely upset when she raised her voice. "The DeAngelis family has been leaving a trail of blood and death for the last thirty five years. Just because we can't tie anything to any of them doesn't change that." "So they're guilty because you say so, not because you can prove it." He chuckled heartily, her last comment had amused him greatly. "You know what you sound like?" he asked the question, but it was clearly rhetorical. "You sound like a kid that fell asleep under the sofa waiting for Santa on Christmas eve. You want so bad for it to be true, just to fulfill your own personal fantasies." The ding of the elevator a few feet away signaled the end of the round. Though, he may have been the only one that considered this the opening round of a never ending battle with this woman. "Agent Harper," another agent stepped out from the elevator and noticed her narrowed eyes and furrowed brow. "Everything okay?" She nodded, even though each inhale and exhale caused her nostrils to flare. He had gotten to her, it was plain as day and written all over her face. He loved it so much he completed his smile. "I'm just taking him up to see Berkman." "Oh, he was on the phone with Yungman back at Quantico." "Great," she sighed. "Come on." She motioned for Gamble to follow her into the elevator. He paused briefly, unsure if he really wanted to be alone with this woman in a confined space with no witnesses. She had a gun, after all. "You coming or what?" she asked, the frustration of having to deal with someone she despised clearly evident on her face. "Yeah," he rushed into the elevator just before the door started to close. He shifted nervously, he wasn't as cocky as he had let on. Daniel Berkman had his back to the door when Gamble and Harper entered, his cell phone pressed to his ear as he spoke to someone in hushed tones. His shirt looked as if he had slept in it, and his tie was loose around the neck so that the top two buttons of his shirt could be unfastened. He looked distraught, Tony wasn't accustomed to seeing this side of the man. He normally demanded everyone's attention when he was in a room, something he expected to find when the man was in his own environment. There were a few others in the room, two agents huddled in front of laptops as another stood in front of the vending machine with a dollar in his hand. One of the two men looked up from whatever held his attention on the notebook and nodded in harper's direction, then turned to Gamble and gave him the once over. His gaze started at Tony's feet and ended at the permanent scar that ran from his lips up toward his ear. The moment took very little time, but it was enough to help Tony realize Daniel may be the only one that believed he was innocent. At least he hoped Berkman still felt that way, he couldn't be too sure. Harper walked over to the two men seated in front of the coffee table. The one on the left, who remained fixated on whatever was in front of him while the other passed judgement on Tony, looked up when harper approached and pointed at something on the screen. The two had a brief conversation, during which Harper turned in Gamble's direction and shook her head. Whether there was something on the screen that pertained to him, or a comment had been made about the guy at the door, he wasn't sure; he just knew he didn't like the way she looked at him. "Tony," Daniel exclaimed from the other side of the room when he ended the conversation on the cell. Berkman approached him with arms outstretched , but only took Gamble by the hand and gave him a good shake with both hands. "Glad you could make it." "I don't think Harper would have given me much of a choice," he gave her a wink and a smile when Daniel turned in her direction. He could almost picture her lift her arm and extend her middle finger, because she could only smile and nod when Berkman smiled at her. "She's one of my top agents," Daniel made the comment with a smile on his face, almost as if he took full credit for how she turned out. "Almost as good as Cynthia." The slight smile that had started to form on Harper's lips quickly faded. Tony doubted it was the first time he had made such a comment, he probably made the comparison on a regular basis. It was the agent that had decided to purchase a bag of animal crackers that broke the awkward silence. "Hey, you're that Gamble guy from PRIME." Indeed he was, though who he was wasn't as important as why he was here. "Agent Palmer," Berkman waved his arm from the agent with a wide grin on his face toward Gamble. "This is my son in law. I take it you recognize him." "You bet I do," Agent Palmer approached with his free hand extended, a smile as wide as Tony's on his face. "Me and my friends are big fans... Well, not of you, but of PRIME." It was the Grin's turn to have his smile fade away. "I guess it's nice to meet you then," Tony shook the man's hand. He really wanted to swipe at the man's open bag of cookies and scatter them across the floor, but he restrained himself. "Oh man," Palmer apologized. "I didn't mean it like that. I just..." "He knows what you meant, Palmer," Berkman placed a hand on Tony's shoulder. "He's just messing with your head." He wasn't. He really wanted to smack the guy upside the head. It had more to do with the frustration that had built up within him for not knowing why he was there than the comment the agent made, but it would probably be fun to just do it if the opportunity presented itself. He'd have to be on his best behavior. "You need anything to drink?" Tony shook his head. "To be honest, Daniel, I really just want to know why I'm here." "Right," Daniel nodded his head, then turned toward the other agents. "Do you think we could have a moment?" None of them looked happy to be asked to leave the room, but it was Agent Harper that took great offense to the request. "I'm not too sure that is a good idea, sir." "I don't recall asking how you felt on the matter, Harper." "I know sir, but as much of a high profile suspect mister Gambillinni is, he is still a suspect. We have procedures to follow, and excuse me if I'm crossing the line; it just seems as if you're derailing this investigation by not following those procedures." "I told you she was good," he joked with Tony as he jabbed his thumb in her direction, but the fun and games ended rather quickly. "Your comments are duly noted. Now if you'll excuse us." He motioned for her to follow the other agents as they exit the room like they were told, but the anger could be seen in her eyes. She wasn't happy with the way things were being handled, and he was sure she would bring it up at a later time. "She doesn't like me very much." "She's just a bit suspicious." "No," Tony shook his head. "She told me she didn't like me." That garnered a bit of laughter from his father in law. "She and Cynthia were partners when they first joined the bureau," he commented on the situation. "They were pretty close until we put her on your case, then they just sort of drifted apart." "So she blames me?" "I don't know really," Daniel shrugged his shoulders. "I wouldn't put it past her though." "Yeah, well, I'm pretty sure i'm not going to lose any sleep over it." Tony clapped his hands together and rubbed them gently. "So what do we got?" "What we have is a bit of a problem. Have a seat." Daniel waved his arm toward the seats that were previously occupied by agents. Tony made his way over and plopped down into one of them, Berkman took the other. "What kind of problem?" "Someone tried to kill my campaign manager." "What? Why?" Tony wasn't sure how this had anything to do with Adelina's abduction, but surely Daniel wouldn't have brought him out if it didn't. His interest was certainly peaked. "I wasn't being completely honest with you when I told you that someone had sent the photos to my office." "I'm listening." He could hardly believe that Berkman had lied to him. He thought thay had already placed that part of their lives behind them. "Cassel was the one who brought them to me." Daniel looked away momentarily. "I didn't know who they were from. He told me they showed up at campaign headquarters adressed to me, but there was no return address. He was lying." Were these just more lies, or could he actually trust the man to be honest for a change. "He didn't tell me who they were from because I had just approved them as a major campaign contributor. He knew if I went after them all of this would come out anyway, and we'd lose the race altogether. He didn't have Adelina's best interest in mind when he held back information that could have helped us find her a long time ago, and I apologize for that." "Why are you apologizing for him?" "Because he did that for me." Tony stood up, the frustration in his eyes as his lips twisted into a bit of a sneer. Facial expressions were always a bit difficult with the permanent grin that made him a household name. "No," Tony shook his head as he paced in front of his father in law. "He did that for himself. This would have damaged more than just your career, and he knew that..." He stopped pacing and turned to face Berkman. "Who was this contributor?" Daniel looked up, his eyes a little moist. "Some company I hadn't really heard of," he grabbed the envelope in front of him and pulled out the pictures and a small pocket notebook. He flipped through a few pages before he found what he was looking for. "It sounded like he said the Parody Foundation, but he was heavily seda-" "Pierotti Foundation?" "Yeah," Daniel nodded. "I guess that could be it. You've heard of them?" "Somewhat," Tony responded. "They're actually supporters of a few acquantices of mine, so I've never met them personally. But I know who they are, and I think I know why they did this." "So what are we going to do about this?" "We?" "They tried to kill my friend," Daniel shot up from his seat, his eyes wide with rage. "I know, it's just..." Tony tapped his index finger to his lips. "I don't see a reason for them to try and kill him, you know." "He was the only person that could link them to the crime. Isn't that reason enough?" "But they wanted him to tell you. How would they have any control if you didn't know who was behind it." "I didn't think about that." "That's what has me confused," Tony shook his head. "It doesn't make any sense." "Right now, I don't really care." Berkman placed his hand on Gamble's shoulder. "They need to pay for what they did, regardless of why." "I understand that, but we can't just go running into this blindly. If nothing else, these people have a method to their madness. They plan everything out - even the unexpected bumps in the road - so this isn't something we can just jump into head first. We need to have a plan." "That's fine." "And what about your pals outside... What're you going to be telling them?" "Harper's the only one making a big deal about your connection to all of this, and that's easily taken care of." "Alright then," Tony pulled his cell phone from his pocket. "You handle them and I'll find out where we can find our new friends." "I want to be kept in the loop on all of this." "Once I have everything rolling I'll give you a call," he stopped scrolling through numbers and looked up. "But you know there's some stuff I can't tell you, right?" "Just do what you need to do." "Oh, you can count on that," Tony pointed his cell phone toward Berkman as he grabbed a hold of the door handle. "Keep your phone handy... I'll be calling you soon." Berkman watched his son in law walk out the door, and knew that his agents would come in and ask a hundred questions. He wanted to be certain he gave them answers that would satisfy their curiousity, but all he could think about was revenge. Someone had tried to kill his friend, and he wanted to make them pay for that. Even if it would cost him his job. "You're just going to let him walk out of here?" Agent Harper wasn't even through the door and she had already questioned Berkman's decision. The other agents filed into the waiting room and headed to their makeshift work areas. "He doesn't have anything to do with this," Berkman replied. "I need you to put aside whatever grudge you have against him and focus on this case. I want you to run down the doctor who prescribed Jake the sleeping pills, make sure that was what she prescribed him." "He knows more than wha -" "Drop it, Harper!" The rise in Berkman's voice as he slammed his hand on the wall took everyone by surprise. "Do what I asked or I'll have you investigating cow tippings in South Dakota by Monday. You got that?" "Yes sir," she said the words under her breath, her eyes focused on the small black stain on the faux tiled floor. "Anyone else think they can run this investigation better than me?" The low hum of the vending machine is the only sound in the room. "Now that we have that straight, do you think we can find out who the hell is behind this?" Cassel told him who was behind it, and why he had not told him before. It made sense at the time to send the bureau after these people, to use every resource available to bring the Pierotti Foundation to justice. Then Jake told him that they were ghosts, that they wouldn't find anything to use against them, and he realized that this had to be taken care of in a way the bureau could not. He knew he had his team headed in the wrong direction, but he felt he could justify it if things blew up in his face. At least he hoped he could. The Troubling Revelation October 20, 2009 New Mexico She hadn't been able to sleep at all, the conversation with Josh kept replaying over and over in her mind throughout the night. She kept seeing his lips curl into a nervous smile every time he said something about the life he has been able to live, the places he's been, and the sights he's seen. He made it sound so glamorous, as if everything would be handed to her on a silver platter. She thought it all sounded too good to be true, but he just had this way of saying things that made it all seem so believable. She almost woke up Jasmine when she laughed out loud about how the serpent had probably sounded the same to Eve back in the Garden of Eden. She isn't even sure why that thought had crossed her mind, but it amused her greatly. It wouldn't be long before he came back for her answer, promising to inform her of something he felt she needed to know if she made the right decision. She wasn't sure what it was, but she wasn't sure what the right decision was either. She had spent the entire night thinking about it, and still had yet to come up with an answer for him. Jasmine hadn't been much help before she fell asleep either. All she talked about was how cute Josh was, and how she felt they shared a connection. It was funny, because Adelina felt the same way. She cracked a joke about how he was probably wearing Axe body spray, but she knew it was just the way he presented himself. He spoke with confidence, as if there were no other truth but the words that passed through his lips. “So,” Jasmine rolls over and props her head up with the pillow. “You figure out what you're going to do?” “No.” “Why not?” “Because of you,” Adelina replies as she turns to face her roommate. “You decided to take Josh up on his offer and you're still here. I don't want to spend the rest of my life locked up like a stray dog.” “A stray dog,” she laughs, shaking her head as she sits up on her bed. “Look, I know what this looks like, but I don't have anywhere to go; there's no one out there waiting for me to come home. I could have moved out a long time ago, but I like it here. Josh and Vesuvius have become family to me, and if you decide to stay they'll be the same for you.” “But I have a family.” “Do you?” “What's that supposed to mean?” “Nothing,” she turns away. “Just heard Josh say something about your dad.” “What did he say about him?” “I already said too much,” Jasmine gets out of her bed and walks over to the door that leads to the bathroom. “Besides, it's probably better if you hear it from him.” Adelina watches Jasmine enter the bathroom, not pushing the situation even though she has more to ask her. She decides to just sit there quietly and make a decision, because the clinking of keys on the door means someone is ready for her answer. “Good morning,” Josh says as he enters the room and glances toward Jasmine's bed. “She's in the bathroom,” Adelina points toward the door. “Yes, well, we can't all wake up looking as beautiful as you.” She swears she can see the fork in his tongue when he speaks now. “Girls don't usually fall for that, do they?” “No,” he laughs. “But it gives them a good reason to laugh, and I like to make people happy.” “Is that so?” “Of course,” he folds his arms across his chest. “What can I do to make you happy, Adelina?” “Open the door and let me walk out.” “I would love to do that,” he smiles out the side of his mouth as he turns toward the door. “But how soon I do it has more to do with what your answer is.” “Tell me what you know about my father.” “Like I told you yesterday,” he turns back toward her and chuckles softly. “I will give you everything you want once you give me the answer that I want.” “It's that simple?” “Pretty much.” “Then my answer is yes.” His chuckle turns into a hearty laugh that begins in his gut. “Is that what you really want, or are you just telling me what you think I want to hear?” “I stayed up all night thinking about it, wondering what I would become if I decided to tell you yes...” She glances in the direction of the door, Jasmine's words whispering in her ear like an angel perched on her shoulder. “But I didn't realize what I wanted to do until you walked through that door.” “And what was it that you realized?” “That I was born for this life,” her eyes tear up a bit as she speaks. “My father tried his best to keep me from knowing what he was doing, because he thought I wouldn't figure out who we were. I knew a long time ago that my future was going to have its fill of lies and death.” He stares at her, fully focusing on each word that passes through her plump lips. She fascinates him more than he'll admit. “I made a promise to myself when he disappeared, that I'd find out who was responsible and I'd make them pay for taking him from me.” She looks up at him with a trail of tears that are just about ready to drip onto her sheets. “You asked me yesterday what it was that I wanted the most...” “I did.” “I want to make the person responsible for my father's disappearance feel the same amount of pain that I felt. I want them to know how it feels to lose someone they love.” “It's funny you would say that...” She wipes away a few tears from her chin, all of her attention on the man standing a few feet away from her bed. “The person responsible just happens to be a man you happen to know quite well,” his smile grows slightly. “I think you call him Uncle Tony.” Her eyes go wide, an audible sigh escaping her lungs before she can place her hand in front of her mouth.
