The Annals of Time – Part II: Present

27 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-27 The Annals of Time – Part II: Present “Time waits for no man.” - A Danish proverb. It’s strange really. I find it disturbing that I’ve spent most of the week recovering from the success I had gained for winning the previous match in the tournament. It’s a bad thing to dwell on the past when you have things to deal with in the present right? But then I realised, I’m not the only one who shouldn’t be dealing with the present rather than the past. Someone else seems to dwell on the past much more than they should be, my opponent, Kasper Sky. You don’t realise Kasper, if you want to make it any further in this tournament, you need to stop concentrating on you murdering your own father, your friend’s father and stop wondering if you killed your mother as well. Telling your friend you killed her father won’t get you anywhere. I’m not going to lie down and let you win, no matter how much you think I will. Time waits for no man, Kasper. Even immortals have deadlines, and it seems your time is running out. ------=====|=====----- Sweat drips from every last part of my body, from every nook and every crevice, my body soaks in the fruit of hours of effort. My head is a boiling sweltering mass, a bright red sphere of heat. Every limb frail and weary, shivering from the exertion it had just previously undertaken. Physically I was completely, absolutely exhausted. My eyes open, my chest rising and falling violently like waves in a raging storm from my exacerbation. The only vision in front of me a bright beaming light. It came from ceiling lights. I am lying on the mat of the local gym. On my back, staring right up to the lights. And as much as I am enervated, as much as I feel like I can’t move another muscle in my body through my exhaustion and aching body, one overwhelming thought crosses my mind. I feel irritated. I feel uneasy and uncomfortable. This is not where I want to be. The light above me looks disgusting. It looked like it’s looking back at me, laughing. My head turns to the side. Sitting there on the edge of the make-shift wrestling ring is my small plastic yellow duck. Ducky looks at me with disappointment and disgrace. The duck cannot talk, but if it could I know the first two words that would come out of its little beak. Get up. The situation is making my blood boil, and even though I’ve exercised to the very limits of what my body is capable of, I garner that last little granule of effort and roll over onto my front. Now my hands push against the mat beneath me, lifting the weight of the lower body onto my knees. Fighting the exacerbation with every action, my left knee lifts up and I place a foot on the mat. With one last push from my hands and an absolutely colossal effort, I lift myself straight up to my feet. My knees buckle, my legs wobble, I struggle to keep my balance and stay on my feet, but eventually I achieve equilibrium. I am up… and it feels wonderful. The moment you most feel alive is right after your life was nearly taken from you. As much as it wasn’t the real thing, as much as Kasper Sky and a referee counting a pin didn’t accompany the lights earlier, the emulation was enough to invigorate me to the whole situation. Wrestling is my life, and neither Kasper Sky nor an angry gambler with a bunch of heavies is going to take it from me. I’m going to win this damn match. ------=====|=====------ I drop the sports bag to the concrete and slump against the brick wall of the gym. In a few moments the taxi will arrive and take me back to my apartment for some heavy-duty telly watching. No doubt Ducky will want the documentary channel on. My mobile phone rings. I bet it’s Wendy wondering how things are going. She has a column to write, about the GTT, so she’ll probably want some inside information on how one of the semi-finalists is getting along. Oh… it’s not a phone call but one of those flash picture-messaging things. Aww, it’s a picture of Wendy, how sweet. I aught to give her a call and find out how she is… hey hang on a minute. I take a closer look at the picture that had been sent to me and realise something very strange. That photo wasn’t taken from inside the office, but from outside the window. You can visibly see down the bottom of the photo part of the window ledge. Why would Wendy have someone take her picture from outside the office? The phone rings again, it is another picture message. It shows Wendy talking to another colleague at the end of the office, this time the angle makes it clear that it is being taken from outside, the frames of all the windows shown, this time almost as if the person taking it wants to know it’s from outside the office. What’s going on here…? The phone rings once more. This time it is a phone call, I answer it swiftly and place the phone to my ear. “Hello? Who is this?” “Lovely isn’t she…? So sweet… so innocent…” It was Jasper Achilles, the crooked gambler who paid me a visit while I was in my hospital bed. “You better not hurt her, you piece of shit!” I yell at the murderous fuck that had killed his father three days previous. If he lays a finger on Wendy I swear to God I’m going to rip his fat flabby-cheeked head from his fucking shoulders. “Temper, temper, Mister Hurst. Let’s not send out any idle threats will we? After all, there are two men with Colt 1911’s waiting eagerly, with itchy trigger fingers, for me to just say the word…” “You murderous fuck!” “Now, now, Mister Hurst. You need to listen to the words that I say. If you haven’t got anything nice to say, shut the fuck up… otherwise my two lovely assistants may have to demonstrate how to terminate a young newspaper columnist…” I keep silent. After all, there is nothing else I can do, or Wendy could be killed. “I have been keeping tabs on you Mister Hurst, it seems you’ve just finished a rather hectic workout in the gym. Now why would someone who is about to lie down and lose a match want to work so hard in the gym? You aren’t thinking of ignoring my request, are you Mister Hurst…? Yes… that’s what I had gathered. Fortunately, I have my own ways of ensuring that my plan goes swimmingly, as you are probably coming to terms with right now. Now you have a simple decision to make; and hopefully, you’ll regretfully take the correct decision. You will do everything that I say… or Wendy and a couple of pistols will make the next day’s headlines. It’s your decision…” The screech of oil-needy brakes distracts me from the conversation. The taxi had arrived. The window of the taxi winds down, a stubble-bearing face appears and asks with a hoarse and croaky voice. “Hey, are you Hurst?” “Yeah.” I inform the cab driver, “Can you hold on a second?” “Ah, the taxi has arrived.” Jasper says as if he knew one was arriving all along. “I suggest you ask the man to go to Alexandria, the Alexandria depot on Fannon Street.” “Okay fine, just don’t hurt Wendy okay? I’ll do as you say.” “Yes, you will Mister Hurst. Oh and if you try to contact Wendy in any way, or make any attempt to contact the authorities you can say goodbye to your precious Wendy, understand?” “Okay fine…” “Good, I will call you when you arrive…” -----=====|=====----- And so it begins…the descent of Exceptional Dave Hurst the wrestler into Exceptional Dave Hurst the loser. There’s nothing I can do. I am caught between a rock and hard place. But as regretful as I am for having to make it, the decision is an easy one, as Jasper said earlier. Wendy’s well-being is much more important than a wrestling tournament. I know that it’s not just any wrestling tournament. It’s the GTT, the biggest prize in wrestling. What is the greatest achievement of my career and could be so much more. But it all ends here. There’s no way out of it. I’m just going to have to lie down, take the pin and walk away. A loser. Well Kasper, it looks like I’m going to lie down for you after all… TO BE CONTINUED...
