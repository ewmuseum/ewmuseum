Going In Alone

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 It’s been four years since we first met. And I can safely say I love her with all my heart. "Stephen…" "We need to talk." I put my trust in her, and I believe it was a good move. "I’ve…been unfaithful." It’s funny how we grow to trust other people. We give them the assurance that we believe in them, and we rely on their integrity. Through trust, we form a bond with that person. As time progresses, that bond grows stronger and the amount of trust we have in that person increases. But all bonds are possible to break. After all, not all bonds are eternal. If they were, then our lives would be near perfection. Perfection is such an overused term. It’s a common phrase thrown around by many, and it is a word with numerous meanings. Many of the competitors in the fifth Golden Turnbuckle Tournament believe they are perfect. Many of the tournament’s critics claim that the likes of Angelo Deville and Digital Mortality are perfect. Unfortunately, with regards to Digital Mortality, I agree with the critics. He’s on what appears to be an untouchable and unshakeable podium. He has formed a bond with the Prime Time Central audience, becoming one of the more well-known stars on the network. But all bonds are possible to break… "Why?" Stephen asks himself as he sits by himself on the porch of his mansion. The night sky is covered with clouds, indicating the possibility of rain. It’s odd, rain during the winter months; the odd change of weather is a microcosm to Stephen’s relationship. Things always seem to go the way they’re supposed to, yet it’s not uncommon for something to come along to break the circle. But it doesn’t matter to Stephen "SuperNova" Baker. Charlotte’s recent infidelity was the latest nail in his coffin. Stephen’s recent run of problems haven’t exactly been a secret to all. Problems with Headstrong Wrestling and Joey Matthews meant that he was forced to return to the independent circuit prior to the tournament. And his up-coming match with Digital Mortality in the fifth Golden Turnbuckle Tournament was acting as a good hammer. "I did nothing but love her." Stephen looks off into the distance, smiling to himself. "But love is a funny thing. We believe the person we love will be the one we spend the rest of our lives with, yet it rarely ever happens." As Stephen continues to stare off into the distance the door behind him slowly opens. A pair or feminine legs make their way in front of Stephen, blocking his view as a way of getting his attention. Stephen turns his head away in disgust. "Listen to me." A familiar female voice booms, the sorrowful tone doing nothing to break Stephen’s rage. "It’s not your fault. I was stupid…" Stephen jerks his head towards the woman, interrupting her. "I don’t care anymore," Stephen hisses. "I’m not turning this into a melodramatic situation. And besides, I have other affairs to worry about." Stephen smiles at himself, turning his head away from Charlotte as he does so. "Was that a shot at me?" A tear rolls down Charlotte’s dainty cheek, a result of Stephen’s somewhat immature comment. "I didn’t intend for it to be, my de…" Stephen stops himself mid-word, realising that formalities are no longer needed. "…Charlotte." "Fine, you can act like this if you please," Charlotte begins. "But the affair doesn’t change the fact that I love you. Yes, my infidelity isn’t the best way of showing this. But it didn’t mean anything, and it was never supposed to happen." It’s funny how unintended events still occur. If one did not intend to do ‘something’, especially something which they can easily avoid through willpower, then one would not do ‘something’. "Who was he?" Stephen asks, turning his head towards Charlotte and staring into her glistening emerald eyes. Her face turns pale as her eyes travel away. "You…" Charlotte pauses. "You won’t know him. He lives in Auburn Hills." "I happen to know quite a few people in Michigan." Seeming intent to find an answer to his question, Stephen follows with a provoking statement. "Try me." "I don’t…" Charlotte pauses again, clearing her throat before continuing to speak. "I don’t know if I should tell you." "So this person’s from Auburn Hills. I trained in Detroit. So it could be someone I’ve wrestled with?" Stephen compiles a brief list in his mind. "Chase? Fredrick Storm? Peter Zafaria?" Charlotte interrupts him, blurting out the name in a brief bout of rage. "Jason Kennedy!" A bolt of lightning lights the skies as Charlotte bows her head. "Who is…" Stephen pauses, thinking for a moment. "No." Stephen speaks, a shocked expression taking hold of his face. "Not from my past…" Charlotte innocently nods her head, a tear rolling down her cheek. Stephen begins to reminisce. Back in his teenage years, before becoming professional wrestling’s Supernova, Stephen was semi-professional boxer. The main reason for Stephen taking up boxing, as it is with many occupants of the slums in New York, was purely for self-defence. But as time progressed and Stephen improved his abilities in the ring, it became apparent that a living could be made from this violent sport. And, for a time, a living was made. But one event would unfold which would ultimately end Stephen’s boxing career and set him down the path of wrestling. It’s the start of the second round of a semi-professional boxing match, being staged at the small Commons’ Center on the outskirts of Auburn Hills. "Stephen ‘SuperNova’ Baker seems tied up here," an local television commentator says, glancing at his scorecard. "In the first round, newcomer Kennedy has knocked Baker out twice." In his corner, a somewhat dazed Stephen Baker is being briefed by his manager. His trainer is bust sorting out a small but odd cut, a cut which extends both above and below Baker’s right eye. "Your form’s a little sloppy. Smarten the fuck up!" The manager is quite concise. He turns to the trainer, who is still examining the cut. "What the fuck’s wrong?" "Well, this cut seems a little…" The trainer pauses, turning Stephen’s head towards the manager. He moves his finger from the top of the cut to the bottom, pointing out how straight it seems to be. "It’s a little odd. I mean, the cut on the top and the bottom are both aligned with each other, and they’re remarkably straight." "I see…" The manager pauses, scratching his stubble as he glances over at the opposition. Stephen’s opponent, Jason Kennedy, is fastening what appears to be a new right glove. "The dirty…" The trainer and manager exit the ring as the bell sounds for the second round. Stephen gets up, struggling to walk the short distance from his corner to his opponent. He ducks a haymaker punch, but leaves himself open for a strong right uppercut from Kennedy. Stephen’s head jerks up as Kennedy lands an overhead punch. Stephen is forced back to the ropes. As he bounces back, Kennedy lands the fatal haymaker punch which sends Stephen to the canvas. "Technical Knockout, Kennedy wins." The commentator says, marking his scorecard. As Kennedy raises his arms in victory, Stephen’s manager enters the ring. "You cheating sonovabitch!" The manager shouts, holding up a right glove. He rips the leather, revealing an iron bar embedded within the glove’s foam. "Well…" Kennedy’s trainer looks over. "Your kid can’t be the favourite for every bout." He winks, prompting Stephen’s manager to punch him square in the nose. Kennedy turns around, glancing at his fallen trainer. "Heh, you shouldn’t have done that." Kennedy lands a punch to the midsection of the manager, forcing him down to the canvas. Stephen, who has just been helped to his feet, witnesses the event as security try to separate the two parties. "The pandemonium sure has kicked off here folks, with brawls and allegations of cheating," the commentator reports. Stephen removes his gloves, then his mouthpiece. As security exit the ring with Stephen’s manager, he walks over to Kennedy. "Good fight," Stephen extends his left hand in sportsmanship. As Kennedy extends his hand, Stephen thrusts his right forearm into Kennedy’s face. Kennedy kneels down slightly, covering his nose. "You broke my nose…" As Kennedy bellows in pain, Stephen knees him in the midsection. He forces Kennedy into an upright position, moving his hands away from his face and head butting him. The small cut around Stephen’s eye opens up as Kennedy falls to the canvas. "This could see the end of Baker’s boxing career." The announcer states, as security rush into the ring to separate the two. A doctor enters the ring, looking over Kennedy’s bloody face, as Stephen stares at him with a sinister grin. "So, how did you meet this entity from my past?" Stephen yells at Charlotte, grabbing her arm and forcing him to kneel over. He points to his scar, which travels down the left hand side of his face. "How did you meet the man who scarred me for life?" "That was your fault." Charlotte protests against Stephen. "You head butted him because you were too hot-headed over losing." "But if it wasn’t for him and his cheating antics…" Stephen argues. "You still didn’t need to sink to his level and fight him after the match." Charlotte replies, her glaring eyes indicating she is angry. "After all, you can’t be the favourite in every bout." "I know." Stephen smiles to himself uneasily. "That’s not the first time someone’s said that to me." Not wishing to argue any further, Stephen stands up. He turns to walk inside, reaches the door and stops. "We’ll talk more about this tomorrow." Stephen pauses. "Maybe we can work something out then, when we’re a little less angry." Charlotte nods her head in agreement as Stephen closes the door. I know I can’t win everything. Stephen sits dormant, staring at the open fire in his fireplace. I know I can’t be expected to win everything. Although I’ve never faced Vargas before, this is far from the first time I’ve heard his name. I remember sitting in the stands of the Super-J Cup, watching as Digital Mortality took out Juvian Ramerez. He won that tournament, further cementing his status as one of the greatest competitors in this game. But at the same time, he’s not perfect. Nobody is perfect. After all, perfection is a term commonly thrown around by others. Although there is so much more on the line, and the problems which plague Mortality’s personal life will surely enhance his performance. The only thing I can do is give him the greatest fight of my career. I must remember not to lose my cool. With everything on the line in this match, failing to maintain my composure could spell the end of my stay in the tournament. Although the critics believe my time here is limited no matter what. It seems more or less like a bond is forming in the confinements of Prime Time Central. The higher-ranked stars always come out on top over the rookies. I’m going to do everything in my power to break that bond. With the love of a woman as good as Charlotte backing me up… Well, I suppose I’m on my own this time around. The door to the room slowly opens, prompting Stephen to turn his head. "You called me, Stephen?" An unfamiliar female voice gently asks. "I did." Stephen’s voice seems shifty. I suppose I’m on my own. Without any bonds.
