The Girl: (The Death of) Dreams

3 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2010-01-03 "It turns out that an eerie type of chaos can lurk just behind a facade of order - and yet, deep inside the chaos lurks an even eerier type of order" – Douglas Hostadter * * * Epilogue: The Lullaby The Epic Hotel – Miami, FL Since meeting Lane Stevens, Regan Guest had stayed in some pretty nice places. There had been the suites at the Bellagio, Caesars Palace, and the finest five star hotels wherever SCCW traveled. While this place might not have been as expensive as some of them, it was probably her favorite. Almost any window in the hotel provided a sweeping view of the surrounding skyline. It was nestled on the bank of the Miami River, and when the sun went down it was right in the thick of the nightlife that had made this city such a popular destination. And yeah, the in-room spa treatments didn’t hurt. But on this evening Regan was not interested in any of that. She was walking down what seemed to be an impossibly long hall on the bottom floor. Her head was on a swivel, looking over the architecture. Even though she was distracted, it was hard not to take in the vaulted ceilings and glass walls. She was very much under dressed, (white pajama bottoms with images of different horse breeds, a white tank top and a blue spring jacket) but it was three in the morning and there was not a lot of traffic in the hotel - just giggling drunk people and the occasional staff member. She had not slept well the last few nights, because of a re-occurring dream. Roaming these halls at this hour, it was as if she was hiding from sleep, and in a sense that was true. She wasn’t like Lane; she didn’t like to put pills in her body to regulate natural cycles or functions, although she was momentarily tempted to take some of his Aderol. Regan knew eventually she would go to sleep on this night, she just wasn’t ready yet. The front desk was in view now, about twenty yards away. The last time she had passed it there was a cute boy working there, but he had been replaced by an older gentlemen. This made her sigh, but she kept walking. Lane was out doing god knows what, with god knows who, and she wanted some interaction. As she got closer she noticed the man was reading the latest USA Today, the front headline dealt with a thwarted terrorist plot and it made her feel isolated from the rest of the world. Ever since she had taken up residence in the bizarre universe that is Sin City Championship Wrestling, everything else usually seemed muted and uninteresting. Her sandals on the marble floor of the lobby made the gentlemen look up from his paper. His hair was gray and his face was kind. He was dressed in a button up white dress shirt with the hotel’s emblem over his heart. There were flecks of dandruff on his glasses that she couldn’t stop looking at. His nametag said Thom. "Can I help you with something, Miss?" Thom asked, and smiled. In response she put her elbows on the desk and said: "How’s your night going?" He looked her over again, this time his face wrinkled with curiosity. "Can’t complain. Just catching up the world," he gestured to the paper, and set it aside. "You sure I can’t help you with something?" "Eh, that’s okay," she peered over the desk and saw that he had a cup of coffee, and a few framed photographs. How she looked, was like a bored kid. "You get conned into this shift or something?" "No ma’am, I rather like it. I get to feel useful without the obligation to do anything. A pretty good arrangement if you ask me." Regan gave him a courtesy laugh. She imagined something far sadder underneath. His wife had probably died in some kind of horrible car accident, and this was how he dealt with the pain, coming to this stupid job and keeping himself busy, trying desperately to avoid the fact that he would never find happiness ever aga…Jesus Christ, she thought, where did that come from? "Indeed," she said, suddenly feeling uncomfortable. "Well I’m going to turn in I think, nice to meet ya." "You too Miss, enjoy your stay." "Well you might as well learn my name," she smiled and paused, "because I might be roaming these halls tomorrow night too. I’m Regan." His brow furrowed. "You wouldn’t be Regan Guest, room 433 would you?" "That’s…me," she said, it was her turn to look curious. He spun around and opened up a drawer. Regan couldn’t see what his hands were doing, but it sounded like he was going through some documents. After about fifteen seconds he returned with an envelope that had her name on it. "A fella dropped this off for you before, was going to have it delivered in the morning, but seeing as you’re here…" he trailed off, and extended his hand, offering her the envelope. She just stared at it for a moment, frozen. There was a rotten feeling in her stomach. "Something wrong, Miss?" Thom asked. She flashed a thin smile and tried to snap out of it. "No, thanks a lot!" she finally said, and turned away without another word. Regan walked as fast as she could without breaking into a run. Thom shook his head with confusion, but eventually just sighed and picked up the sports section of his newspaper. Regan got on the first elevator she saw, and ripped the envelope open savagely. This could be nothing, that was entirely possible, but it didn’t feel like nothing. When she got it open, what she found was one piece of paper, folded up. In the process of unfolding it, she almost ripped it in half. What she found on the paper was this: Dearest Regan, You and Lane have performed quite well. So well, in fact, that your services are no longer needed. I am truly sorry for some of the things you were subjected to, but some times I must take unusual channels to make sure that the right job gets done in the right manner. Please give Lane my best; he especially was a most effective instrument. Now please, do get some rest. If you need any assistance, read below. Golden Slumber kiss your eyes Smiles await you when you rise Sleep, pretty baby Do not cry And I will sing to you, a lullaby Regards, Bernie Blackcap Next to his name there was a stamp. The image was that of a black cowboy hat. "Oh my god," she whispered. * * * Las Vegas, NV - Earlier. Darren Hayward was not having a good week. "Fuck!" Crack. "Fuuuuuuuuuuck!" When we last left him he was being held in some kind of storage shed, tied to a chair that was bolted to the ground. There was somebody torturing him with a ring bell hammer. As it turns out, not a lot has changed. Darren’s shirt was covered with his own blood and vomit. The clearest source of the bleeding was his nose, and there was a cut on his forehead from being repeatedly struck by Lane’s elbow. He had two broken fingers on each hand, and at this point he had resigned to the fact that his knees and ankles were essentially useless. "Do you know why this is happening?" Lane Stevens asked casually. The River Rat was sitting in a steel folding chair next to Darren. They were surrounded by unmarked boxes. Lane set the hammer aside for a moment. "Because you’re… a goddamn… crazy asshole," Darren said, lightly sobbing between frantic gasps for air. He had stopped attempting to kiss up to The River Rat, that strategy clearly had no effect. Stevens chuckled heartily. "Perhaps, but that’s not what I’m getting at. You see, I don’t mind you giving my girl a hard time. Hell, I’ve been asked to do some pretty fucked up shit to people for money, for someone else’s idea of revenge or justice…whatever. I understand. This cowboy loan shark guy, he probably forgave some of your debts I imagine, that’s cool. I don’t mind you roughing me up. It’s my job to get roughed up. No harm, no foul. But I’ll tell you what I can’t allow." The River Rat leaned in to Darren, as if to tell a great secret. "That look on your face when you were putting the boots to me, this sense of entitlement you had. You thought that you somehow deserved that moment, that somehow karma had finally compensated you for all of your misfortunes. You believed in right and wrong, black and white. And that look, brief as it was… was the biggest lie you could have perpetrated. And I can’t stands me a liar, Darren." Stevens leaned back, and Hayward looked up at him, his face clearly portraying his exhaustion at these exchanges. "Am I going to die here?" Darren asked, as his tears mixed with blood. Lane looked at his prisoner as if he were the crazy one. "Well I’m not a murderer," Lane said, and laughed again. "Then when are you going to let me go?" Lane looked at his watch, but could not ascertain the time because of the blood that was smeared on it. He wiped it off on his Aimz t-shirt and then had another look. "About two hours," The Hand’s Warlord announced; his voice chipper as ever. "Why then?" "Because my plane for Miami leaves in like three and a half, and I’m going to need to shower first. I got your fluids all over me, man. Ridiculous." As if to punctuate his point The River Rat glanced down at his shirt in disgust. "Why not let me go now?" Darren asked breathlessly. "Cause we got to come to an understanding, first. You mind if I grab a beer before we get into the nuts and bolts of this thing?" Darren put his head down, and almost laughed at the absurdity of the question. Finally he just shook his head. "Excellent!" Stevens said, and walked over to the mini-fridge on the other side of the room. He popped it open and grabbed a can of PBR. The sound of the beer being cracked open made Darren remember what it was like to have freedom, which mildly displeased him. "I’m not going to call it the best beer around, but shit…it won the blue ribbon for a reason, ya know? They don’t just hand those out." Stevens took his seat again. "So anyway, it goes without saying that you can’t talk about this, about me anyway. But I suppose if it really did go without saying…I wouldn’t be jabbering on like a school girl." The River Rat’s laughter was like a splinter in Darren’s brain. "And you might be asking yourself, what can this guy do to shut me up? I mean he’s hitting me with a hammer and shit, I hate this asshole! I’d like to see him go to jail! Boo Lane! Understandable thoughts, indeed." Lane was touching his shoulder, as if to sympathize with Hayward’s plight. "I won’t say a thing," Darren said, and coughed. "Oh, I know. If you do…yes, I would probably be punished severely. But then there is the matter of your family in Cleveland. See, I’m just a wrestler. I’m not in the mob or anything. Wrist locks, not glocks…that’s what I’ve always said. But let’s just say I’ve met people over the years, through my comings and goings in this business, that have less…savory ties. It would be unfortunate if your mother was, for instance, raped with a broken shovel while your sister was forced to watch." Stevens extended an open palm and shrugged as if to say, ‘right?’ "I mean hey, we’ve all rented one of those movies out of curiosity…but I don’t think it ends well for anyone." Lane started, "not even the shovel." "I won’t say anything! Just please…" Stevens considered that, and took a big swig of the beer. "I’m afraid you still don’t get it, but I don’t blame you I suppose. You think I’m this horrible creature, and you’re this tragic hero whose life has just taken a few wrong turns. I am expected to be the villain, so that is a part I play. I would be lying if I said I didn’t relish the role, but it is a role just the same, a performance. Think of it this way…how most humans gauge things is my comparison. But it doesn’t always make sense. Let’s take a sentence like, ‘all girls are weird’. Well, weirder than what? If they’re all weird, it sounds like their behavior is standard to me, therefore they are all normal. If all human beings are shitty, and believe you me, they are…then none of them are. We just are what we are, because that’s all we could be. You and me, we’re the same. We should go fishing together." "What?" was all Darren could muster. "Don’t worry about it. Hell, let’s get you cleaned up!" Lane chirped * * * Two hours later, a Ford Focus pulled into the back parking lot for the Town House bar and grill and stopped for a moment. It was empty, as it normally was this time of day. The passenger side door opened up, and bloody Darren Hayward emerged. With the beating his knees and ankles had taken, his steps were very short and awkward. "Dude," Miles Cavanaugh said from the driver seat. Lane Stevens was nowhere to be found. Darren looked back, with a shocked look on his face. Nothing on this day in particular had surprised him until that point. He was used to the River Rat’s cruelty. But the half ass apology that he thought was about to come out of this blonde asshole’s mouth, might have just sent him over the edge. He also couldn’t help but get the feeling that Miles was staring at one of his eyes, which had swollen shut at this point. "Uh, yeah," Cavanaugh offered, and sped off. * * * And two hours after that, a United Airlines flight from Las Vegas to Miami was in the air. In the first class section of the plane the seats were bigger, there was more leg room, and they were positioned two by two, thus eliminating the annoying middle seat. There are many people who you might find interesting on this plane, but today we are going to focus on four of them. Because of the popularity of the flight, these four were not seated together. In the back of first class, Regan Guest was dressed in sky blue tank top and jeans. She was reading a people magazine article about Jennifer Aniston and twirling strands of her long black hair with her index finger. Sitting next to her was The River Rat’s newest ally and head of security. With one contact all white, and one all black, the former PRIME Universal champion had a unique appearance. "Does it interest you to know what famous people are doing?" Cozen asked. This was not a condescending question, it was an honest one. "I don’t know, I guess I’m not really interested. It’s just here, so I’m reading it," Regan answered. "You are now famous too you know these wrestling shows are shown on Cinemax and also Jennifer Aniston is on that channel last night they had The Good Girl on which has the guy from Donnie Darko who most girls would have sex with," Cozen’s response, when not taking on the role of another person, tended to be a stream of consciousness that took some getting used to. Regan was not entirely used to it. "Ah, yes I’ve seen that movie," Lane’s ‘special lady friend’ answered, and flipped the page of the magazine. The faceless fighter was not deterred. A moment later, the subject was changed entirely. "Has it ever occurred to you that maybe Alexandra Pierce has disguised herself as me and perhaps will murder you and Lane in the bathroom with something as simple as a pen or perhaps a chemical soaked rag because if she wanted to she could do that and right now she is really pissed at Lane and you associate with him so…" Cozen punctuated this point by choking herself, and acting dead. Regan looked up from her magazine, a confused and disturbed look present on her face. "No, I guess that hadn’t occurred to me." Cozen’s eyes popped open, and she looked casual again. "Maybe it should, oh hey look Angelina Jolie has little foreign kids!" she pointed at the magazine. Towards the front of first class, a different two-some was carrying out an entirely different conversation. "Is it really necessary to carry that around…you know, in public?" "Yes." "Oh." Lane Stevens and Professional Wrestling Illustrated writer Jeff Coltz sat side by side. The former was dressed in a black "I (heart) illegal foreign objects" t-shirt and black track pants. The topic of their conversation rested on this lap: the custom "World Champion of Beating Impulse". The latter was dressed in a charcoal suit. At first glance you might call Jeff bored, god knows he wasn’t enjoying spending time with this man, but upon further inspection it was something worse than that. It was a loathing that he could not even put into words. Coltz had been given the cover story, and the job to follow around the most notoriously sadistic member of the Sin City Roster throughout his tournament experience. There were ‘bad guys’ who were better wrestlers, and even ones who had done worse things, but certainly no one who had enjoyed himself more. Jeff had given charged with the task of getting inside Lane’s head, of finding out what made him tick. These discoveries were not joyous things. "If you believe that no one is truly better than anyone else…then why do you feel the need to humiliate most of your opponents? That seems to be sending the message that they are less than you." "Why should my actions have any effect on their own self worth?" "But you showed the world pictures of Amy Campbell naked, you created a web of lies to destroy the reputations of John Covel and Smitty Duluth…this was obviously intended to bring people down a notch. This was not about their self worth as much as how the world perceived them." "Hrm," Lane Stevens remarked, and took a sip of his gin and tonic. "Did you do that mock trial stuff in high school?" The writer’s glare told Lane that he was going to hold out for an answer. The intensity of his expression made the wrestler smile. "I believe that all humans are born with different skill sets, different mental and physical abilities, different tolerances to pain both emotional and physical, they have different family situations, different chemical reactions in their body…different circumstances. And at the end of the day, they are the only person they could have ever been. They have their choices, but even those are only based on all of these previous factors, ran across other individual variables. I don’t hate Jonathan Rhine for being born a sappy two faced poser, but I will hurt him for it because that is what I like doing. I would hope that he would also give me the benefit of the doubt and not judge me, but that doesn’t seem likely. So people like me and him are locked in epic battle just because the fucking twat can’t just have a little humanity!" Just then a woman in her late twenties turned at stared at Stevens, the displeasure with his language written all over her face, but she said nothing. A few moments later her daughter peered over from the next seat. "Can I touch it?" the little girl said, reaching towards his belt. "No," Stevens answered. "Why?" the little girl asked, the mother already starting to turn her around. "Because you didn’t beat Impulse. Duh!" The Hand’s WMD looked baffled at how such a concept could escape her. And like that, both mother and daughter were facing forward again. "Speaking of that," Coltz started again. "Fucking brat thinks title belts are just given out." Jeff cleared his throat, and Stevens looked at him. "Speaking of that, you obviously could not have game planned to fight Impulse again. Is there anything you are going to do differently this time around?" "Yeah, right when the match starts I’m going to do a wrist lock, cut off his hand, wave to him with it…and shove it up his fucking ass. These questions suck." "Would you like to answer a few questions about your strained relationship with Alexandra Pierce, then?" Jeff asked, and scolded himself for feeling an immediate wave of satisfaction at the vanishing of Lane’s smile. The River Rat finished the rest of his beverage, and stared out the window. When he spoke again, it was with dry and without enthusiasm. "As far as Impulse goes, I’m going to work on the knees more early, try to slow the thing down right away." "I see." The interview would stop there for a while. * * * There was no one guarding the door to the basement of the Town House, which Darren found odd. But what he found in the basement itself was even odder. There was nothing. All of the newspaper articles on the wall were taken down. The mailboxes that he and the other members of this cell had picked up their assignments in were nowhere to be seen. There was no phone, no computer, and certainly no other people. What was it that they had been doing down here? In retrospect, he didn’t even know. He had been out of the loop, but it felt important, it felt so important. He slumped down in the corner, and began to sob again. This man was at one point on the fast track to a full ride to college, and a career in professional basketball (before his knee injury years ago) this man who had picked himself up again and went to college anyway, and gotten a degree in political science, this man who despite all of his debt, told his mother that he would find a way…this man had hit rock bottom again. He had no money, no pride, it was possible that his actions of late could get his family maimed and on top of that he could have sworn he just heard the Cowboy’s voice in his head remind him that he had no God. But as it turned out, Darren Hayward was not alone in this room after all. There was an envelope sitting in the middle of the floor with his name on it, and a lump inside of it. How could he have missed that? * * * Regan and Lane had just checked in at the EPIC hotel. Their room had a full kitchen, living room, and a bedroom with a queen size bed. He was standing on the balcony, looking over the pool and the surrounding skyline. It was late afternoon, and the sun was beginning to set. To most people it was a post card. To Lane it was glass and concrete, gas and clouds. "I think I’m going to take a nap," he said. "Want to lie down for a while? I hear they have a sushi place in the hotel open to like four in the morning we can hit up later." She was unpacking. The question made her freeze. "No, I’d rather not," she said. * * * What Darren found in the envelope, was a Beretta handgun. "Huh," he said aloud. * * * If you have a moment, we will step even further back in time. The doors to Grandpa’s Gun Shop closed, and two figures walked out. Even though they were around the same height, the shadow of the one to the left seemed to extend much longer, and the cowboy hat atop his head appeared monstrous on the Las Vegas back alley. Bernie Blackcap and Drudge walked side by side, the latter carried a small bag. "Why couldn’t you have just hired a hit man?" Drudge asked. "Some things have to happen in a certain way, my friend. I’d say more but I’d probably blow your whole mind, and I like you just the way you are. Good job, fella." The Cowboy threw his arm around Drudge, and smiled fiercely into the night. You couldn’t see it on his face unless you had a keen eye, but if you did, it was as plain as day - as bright as firecrackers on the Fourth of July, or the flames from witches burning at the stake. It saddened Mr. Blackcap to think about how no one would ever understand how great a victory this was, but that sadness was soon lifted from him as he imagined the gears of great chain reactions grinding. He could feel the world changing right before his very eyes. The presence of the Cowboy’s arm on his shoulder was so alarming to Drudge that he almost dropped his bag, and the Beretta handgun inside of it. But the presence of his arm did something else to Drudge too; it filled him with a happiness he could not explain. He hoped that the Vegas night concealed his tears. "My work here is done," The Cowboy suddenly offered, as if it just occurred to him. Days after the Cowboy left town, Drudge would sit in an alley and stare at his tattoo, wondering what had become of his life. The man he had been following around, the man he had left his wife and his family for, seemed to be nothing more than a crazy person upon further reflection, a lunatic who spoke in vague riddles and accomplished nothing more than purchasing a gun that they never even used. But even worse than that, Drudge couldn’t even remember his own real name. * * * Darren stared at the gun, and all he could think of was an art history class he had taken – one of his electives at Columbus State Community College he took before he got into the political science program. All he could think of were Leonardo Da Vinci’s last words. "La tristesse durera toujours." The sadness will last forever. * * * Prologue: The Dream. Regan sat up in the middle of the night, breathing heavily. She looked around the bedroom of Lane’s condo, frantically searching for something normal, something safe. What calmed her slightly down was his "D.A.R.E." poster, which was taped up on the closet. She normally found the irony of this poster rather unoriginal, but in the middle of the night after a bad dream, it pleased her. After another minute of panting, Lane was woken up. "What the fuck?" he asked. She did not answer right away. Her breathing slowly came back to normal. "Another dream?" "Yes." "Oh fuck me," Lane sighed. "This one was different." After a bit of silence, Stevens finally got the hint and sat up. He rubbed his eyes and put an arm around her, a move that felt alien to him. But this was the sort of thing that she wanted him to do, just as there were certain things that he wanted her to do. A lot of these had to do with her mouth on his penis, but there were other things too. He liked it when she laughed at his jokes, or stood up for him. There was certainly no one else that did that. All in all, it was a fair deal. "What happened?" "It was just like before, with all the people gathered for the blurry faced man to talk. Everyone seemed so happy. It was a…celebration, maybe some kind of political thing." "I thought you said it was different?" Lane asked, hoping for a quick resolution here. Before going on, Regan took a few moments to compose herself. She knew he wouldn’t give a shit about this, but he had to know. "It was. The blurry faced man was gone; he never showed up. And then I got really sad. Everyone did." "Yeah, life sucks," The River Rat remarked, and kissed her on the forehead. "Do you think that maybe we’ve just…done something horrible?" Lane’s mind never even wandered to the man tied up in the storage shed just out of town. He just said: "huh?" "Nothing," Regan said, and rolled over on her side. This dream would repeat itself for the next several days, yet all it would take was a good lullaby to make it fade.
