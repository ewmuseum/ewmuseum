Insecurity and the Past

30 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-30 The sun has just sunk below the western horizon; it’s last few rays reaching out to grasp the features of the landscape, as if to keep itself from sinking from view. Long shadows stretch out from the trees, offering the daylight star a final salute as it fades entirely from view. Under such lighting the McFarlane estate looks as though it’s been taken straight from an H.P. Lovecraft novel, with each nook and crevasse carefully concealing it’s inhabitants from the last rays of daylight. The three-story structure stands several kilometers outside of what inhabitants have begun to call the Toronto “Megacity”, well away from the noise and the pollution and the press of humanity. As though such distance is not enough, a six-foot wall of marble, once highly polished, but now having faded somewhat with the wear of the years surrounds the entire property. A two foot, black iron, fence tops the wall and rings the house with a series of sharp spear points that salute the sky. A large double gate completes the defenses, the top coming to a rounded semi-circle that houses the same lethal looking points as the rest of the fence. Those very same gates are currently swinging wide to accommodate a single car as it turns from the highway and into the courtyards sheltered exterior. The Corvette Coups smooth lines glint a midnight blue as the houses light come on, likely triggered by the same remote that opened the gate in the first place. Weaving between a series of decorative hedges and around a large fountain the car comes to a stop before a converted stable, the wide doors having been renovated further to accommodate the latest in modern travel conveniences. The door retracts into the ceiling and the car pulls into a spacious car port, the deep purr of its engine cutting out as the door telescopes back into place. The driver’s side door opens and a long, cargo clad, leg precedes its owner out of the car and into the carports twilight gloom. Dawn pulls herself to a standing position and rolls her neck smoothly to each side, relishing in the pop of joints still protesting the long trip. “Ugnh. I’m never going to get used to that drive.” The passenger side door swings slowly open and the towering figure that is Corvus steps out with a similar grunt, rolling each shoulder and making a dissatisfied noise at the stiffness he finds there. “You realize we’re going to have to do this again tomorrow right?” He says, the deep baritone of his voice rolling through the carport’s open air like a motorcycle engine. Dawn doesn’t respond immediately, instead staring out the carport door as it closes, her eyes drinking in the beauty of the inner courtyard, at the same time seeming haunted by a sight that should be familiar to her. Corvus drifts up behind her and places one large hand on her shoulder, startling her from her reverie. “What’s wrong?” He rumbles, “I thought you would be happy to be home.” She looks over her shoulder at him, managing a wan smile as she covers his hand with her right, stroking his hand with her thumb. “I AM happy to be home…” she says thoughtfully, “I was just realizing that this was the first time in a long while this house has really felt like home at all. I didn’t really realize it until I came home by myself.” Corvus squeezes her shoulder lightly and she smiles again, leaning against the comforting weight of his large frame. ‘Well, almost alone.” He wraps his arms around her and holds her to him as the door slides fully to the floor, closing out the intrusive outside and leaving them standing together in the dark. After a long moment Corvus clears his throat, “You okay Dawn?” She nods, and although the darkness hides her face, her voice is thick with emotion. “Yeah…let’s head inside, there’s a lot of house to show you.” She tugs on his hand and begins to pull him towards the door into the house. He chuckles and let’s himself be pulled along, heedless that at his size he could just as easily dig in his heels and go nowhere at all. She leads him into a mudroom, gesturing around as she removes her black boots. “Pretty standard really, I keep the washer and drier in here since the basement is full of stuff anyway.” Corvus raises an eyebrow at her and bends to remove his own boots, “You mean you do your own laundry? I would’ve figured you had someone to do it for you.” Dawn shakes her head, reaching over to flick on the lights as she does so. “No, it helps to keep me honest. Even when I was with Top Shelf I kept to my own little rituals you know? I need help keeping the place clean and keeping the hedges trimmed and shaped, but when it comes to my own laundry and the like I prefer to handle it myself.” He nods, stepping out of his boots and following her out into a narrow hallway that leads into an enormous foyer that stretches the entire 3 stories of the house, with a grand staircase leading up to each floor in turn. She turns to him and smiles, hands behind her back and a slightly nervous tone to her voice, “So…what do you think?” Corvus cranes his neck to stare around the foyer, keen eyes taking in every detail before he smiles slightly. “It’s lovely. And this is your family estate?” She nods, gesturing at a door on the second floor landing. “As far as I know it’s always been a part of the family.” “You grew up here?” She shakes her head, “No, we lived in Toronto for the most part, but we came out here for vacations and stuff like that. This is where my dad kept his wrestling awards too…I think he wanted to keep Caitlin and I out of that part of his life. I used to love hearing the stories though…” Corvus turns to face her, again raising one expressive eyebrow, “Your father wrestled?” Dawn laughs and heads towards the first of the carpeted stairs, beckoning for Corvus to follow her, “Yup, that’s how I decided to get into the business in the first place.” Corvus places one large hand in hers and allows himself to be tugged gently up the stairs behind her. She leads him to one of the dark wood doors on the landing that rings the second floor; pausing before the doorknob as if uncertain she really wants to go into that room. “You could show me another time if you’d rather…” Corvus rumbles from behind her. Dawn shakes her head resolutely and pushes open the door, leading Corvus into the large room beyond. The rooms only lighting is provided by a recessed fireplace in the far wall, it’s warm glow spreading throughout the room. A pair of comfortable looking armchairs sit to either side of the fireplace, but a think layer of dust over each shows that it’s been a very long time since anyone sat in them. The fire glints around the room, reflecting off the myriad of pictures and trophies hanging from the walls, showing that all available wall space has been taken up by these mementos, the only free space being occupied by an age worn liquor cabinet. Corvus walks to one of the sections of pictures, leaving Dawn leaning against the doorframe hugging her arms to her torso like she’s suddenly cold. He lifts one of the pictures off the wall, holding it up to inspect in the flickering light. “Your father I think I can recognize, but who’s the man with him?” Looking perplexed Dawn walks over to him and peers up at the picture, eyes widening in recognition. “That’s Brian Valentine, he was my dad’s best friend ever since they were in school. They partnered together all the time nearing the end of my Dad’s career…” Corvus looks down at Dawn and wraps a massive arm around her shoulders, pulling her close to him as he inspects the picture further. “What ended your father’s career?” Dawn reaches up let her fingers trail down the pictures smooth glass surface, “Cancer. He was diagnosed with it around the same time Uncle Brian was killed in a car accident…” Her voice catches in her throat and Corvus instinctively squeezes her close, replacing the picture on the wall. He holds her for a long moment before his eyes are caught by another picture, this one showing the same two men flexing behind a pair of young girls in pink and pale blue wrestling singlets, with long ribbons around their heads. Evidence of a small smile tugs at the corner of his mouth and he reaches out to point at the picture, “That’s not who I think it is…is it?” Dawn looks up and her cheeks flush red with embarrassment, eyes widening, “Oh my god…I didn’t think he kept any pictures of my early days.” She says, sounding mortified. Corvus chuckles as he gently strokes her hair, reaching out to pluck the picture off the wall. “You look very…young.” He says with a smile. “Oh stop!” Dawn squeaks, the blush creeping down her neck and up into her hairline, “That was a really long time ago!” “I gathered as much, I don’t think I’ve ever seen you smile as wide as you are here. Who’s the girl with you? Your tag team partner I would assume.” Dawn nods, taking the picture gently from his large hand, “Caitlin Valentine.” “Valentine…your Uncle Brian’s daughter?” “That’s right, we both had the same passion for our fathers line of work, so we decided we were going to try and make our own careers. That was right after our first night, an eight-man tag with us and our fathers against ‘The Duke’ and his men at arms…we won actually, it was the happiest night of my life…the only time I wrestled in the same ring as my father.” “What happened to her? Caitlin I mean, I’ve never heard you mention a Caitlin except for your sister.” “We…drifted apart after Uncle Brian died. She sort of pulled into herself and we just found less and less time to spend together. Last I heard she was wrestling in EWI…but I havn’t spoken to her for over a year now.” Corvus looks down at her for a long moment, “Was she the only reason you lost contact?” Dawn has the grace to look embarrassed, returning the picture to its place on the wall. “No…we were both really shaken after our fathers died…I guess I wasn’t any better about finding time to spend together then she was.” She looks at the picture for several long moments before turning back to Corvus her eyes brimming with unshed tears. “Could we continue the tour now? I…I’d rather not stay here any longer.” Corvus nods mutely and allows himself to be led out of the room, closing the door firmly behind him. Dawn then pulls him up the last flight of stairs, stopping at the landing to gaze out of the large picture window set into the front of the house. “This was my favorite spot in the whole house you know…” she says, “I’d sit up here for hours with my legs dangling over the edge just looking out at the trees and the birds and…and…” Corvus’ large arms envelop her and hold her close to him, her cheek resting comfortably against his broad chest. She sniffles a bit, burying her face into his chest, sobs wracking her shoulders as he gently runs his fingers through her dark hair. “What is it? Why are you suddenly like this?” It takes Dawn a long moment to find her voice, trying to force the shaking in her shoulders to subside long enough to speak. “I…I wonder sometimes…if I’m really cut out for things here. I mean, I’ve been in this business for nearly my entire life, but all of that has amounted to nothing…” “How can you say that? How can you’ve call all you accomplished nothing?” She looks up at him, tears burning hot trails down the sides of her face as her teeth clench in frustration, “All I’ve accomplished? Accomplished?! I haven’t won a singles match for half a year Corvus, and then to make up for it I fell in with that miserable soul sucker…” “Leave Marcus to me, he’ll get what’s coming to him I promise you.” “But that’s not the point! What the hell has happened to me Corvus? I couldn’t break away from him, even with Bascah helping me from the inside…I was helpless until you came along. It was you that saved me from the nothing I’d become…” “I told you not to talk like that Dawn, you’ve never been nothing. You just needed a hand up, something to remind you of how wonderful you really are.” “I just…watching Tessa’s promo made me really start thinking about how much I give to this business, and whether or not I’ve already gone past my time. I’m not nearly as young as most of the girls I’m up against now.” “I hardly think that matters. You’re 3 years their senior at maximum. So what if you don’t have a tongue stud or a bellybutton ring? If it was your style you’d do it…don’t let the trends tell you who you are Dawn.” She sighs, resting her forehead against his chest, tears dripping off the tip of her nose. “I don’t…I guess I just sometimes feel like I’ve missed the boat in a lot of ways, like I’ve been so wrapped up in myself that everything passed me by.” “Even if that’s true, you have a chance to recapture it now. And I’ll do everything I can to help you, you know I will. Right now you need to focus on the tremendous opportunity you’ve been given in PTC tournament, show them how you really shine.” Dawn just nods, holding herself close to Corvus’ Dark form in the shadows of the hallway.
