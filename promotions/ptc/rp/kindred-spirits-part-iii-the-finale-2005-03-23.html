Kindred Spirits? (Part III. The Finale.)

23 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-03-23 The last act of defiance. Emilio remembered the poster hanging above his head: A tiny mouse standing proudly before a swooping eagle, giving it the finger. And that is exactly how Emilio felt. A mere rodent in the shadow of a winged beast. XUW and PTC Global Champion, The Latch Key Kid, talons outstretched, and the vultures, Nick Worrell and Mal Somers, circling overhead, waiting for the scraps. And all the while, Emilio stood alone. But if you stand alone in a shadow long enough , as the sun shifts across the skyline, so does the shadow. And as the twilight of Seven of the Best is upon us, so is the dwindling figures of Emilio’s opponents. While from a morning angle their shadows appeared mighty and fierce, by midday they were mere ghosts of their former selves, and by evening, slain giants. At night, they were totally exposed. Cut down to size, and open for whatever Emilio threw at them. Ripe for the picking. And Emilio had every intention of reaping the rewards of his harvest. For seven months he had waited. His first month in PRIME , in fact, was a Cinderella story. There he was, an illegal immigrant, a barely known, quasi-established Japanese and Mexican underground wrestling guru. Yet, he took it to the best that the wrestling world had to offer. And won. A month after his debut, he was ranked number one in all of PTC. From there, the list of accomplishments grew, spreading like gasoline-fed wildfire: The shortest match in PRIME history -defeating Cimmerian in just over a minute. The longest match in PRIME history- lasting over an hour and forty minutes in the Dual Halo. The longest Alias Title reign- 125 days, and counting. But all of those measurable items, all those quantified marks of respect, none of them would ever -could ever- measure up to the intangible pride that came with holding the PTC Global Title. The top prize. And insignia that said, in part, that he was numero uno, the best that the pro-wrestling world had to offer. The accolade of his career. The fruit of his half a year of servitude in the most grueling, competitive atmosphere, short of the battlefield, known to man. He would be proud. So why was it, then, that God had to get in his way? - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Exposing her. The fraud. The liar. The bitch. She was the arrow through his heart, the fire in his belly. But all he wanted to do was expose her. To cut her down, to rub her out, to fool his own heart into believing that she was what she wasn’t: a fraud. A self-serving leech who saw her ticket to fame as a ride on the back of an up and coming star. Playing the heart of a professional wrestler with a busy schedule, a sloppy style, and an axe to grind against the world. A man all too worn out at the job to taste the domestic conspiracy in the living room after work as she sat quietly on his lap, wooing him into submission. But he couldn’t. It’s not that he didn’t want to. Oh my, he did. How he longed for the sweet taste of freedom, the independence of being single again...regardless of the leash that PRIME had him on. It’s just that, well, there was no game of the pin-the-tail-on-the-donkey where he could be spun fast enough, as many times as needed, so that when he was given the tack and led in the wrong direction...he wouldn’t end up, by the hand of divine intervention, finding her and pricking his own heart. Because he always did. Always. She was perfect. - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - The trip home was a mess. After switching planes in Seattle, they found another flight delayed from Chicago to Boston. Total, unmitigated, hell. Nonetheless, Emilio persevered. He got in his licks at the gym by day, and patched up things with Maya at night. Instead of living out his fantasy and indulging in PRIME’s generosity for a hotel, he decided to take it easy on the wallets of his fans and invite her back to the apartment instead. And it seemed to work out. For the first few days, that is. “Honey, stop that!” Emilio turned to shoot Maya a glare of annoyance. The neighbors on the floor above them had been fighting for hours. ”Damn these fucking apartment.” Rolling out of bed with a headache, late for a training session at ten in the morning, marital banter was the last thing he wanted to hear. Quite frankly, the amount Linda’s mother-in-law snored when she was over, was of no concern to Emilio, and he didn’t want to hear about Jack’s problems at work, or Linda’s period ,either. So there he was, still in his bath robe. He gripped a fresh cup of coffee in one hand, and a broomstick in the other, gently poking the ceiling. “I don’t think that’s gonna work,” started Maya, rolling her eyes. “Quiet, you,” Emilio playfully instructed. He gave the ceiling another poke. A chalky white dust fell back in his face, causing him to cough. A lose piece of sheetrock bounced off his head and fell at his feet. Maya laughed. Still, the argument ensued. Emilio angrily gripped the broom, gritting his teeth in determination. “And honey,” Maya said in an informative tone, yawning, “I’ve think you’ve watched too many American movies.” Indeed, he had. “Cultivating the cross-cultural mind,” in his view, meant a four-hour marathon of Happy Days, and an afternoon of John Wayne. And none of them got him any closer to the American mind-set. Apparently, poking at people’s floors only make them eventually call the exterminator, and nothing more. Sadly, he lowered the broom. With slumped shoulders he walked across the room, tossing the broom half-assed into the corner. “Son of a bitch!” He stumbled over a pair of his own shoes, almost spilling his coffee. Maya laughed. Emilio’s face contorted into an irked smile. He turned to chastise her, but ever so gently, the lines faded away, replaced by an indelible smile... There she was now, wrapping her arms around him, filling his soul with her presence. Without thinking, he reached down to embrace her...and dropped the coffee. She laughed. Emilio, with a gentle roll of his eyes, followed suit. She only came up to the top of his chest, but laying down side by side in bed, she seemed bigger than him. There was something about her, some untouchable aura of self-confidence, that made him bite his tongue whenever he wanted to ostracize her for impeding on his career, for stepping over the boundaries of his heart, or well, even mocking him when he spilt his coffee. “So,” Emilio asked, confidently, “I guess this means you’ll make another cup?” He put his hands authoritatively on his hips. Unfortunately, he couldn’t keep a straight face. “Yeah. For myself.” “Oh, c’mon,” Emilio laughed, “I’m giving up a day of training to be with you, and this how you repay me?” Maya scornfully shook her add. “And what, may I ask, did you “pay” me with in the first place? You’d be a lonely, miserable wreck if it wasn’t for me.” It was true. As much as he hated to be bounded by rules, she saved him. Her invigorating presence had filled his dwindling soul. If anything, she made him feel more complete. If anything, she made him feel more aware, confident...alive. She had pulled him from the gutter of self-pity and destruction. And so, why was he so afraid? Emilio had to turn away to prevent from cutting a smile as she tickled his cheeks with her hair, reaching up to plant him with a kiss. “Why is,” she asked, “that you never have a comeback for anything I say?” “Maybe I’m too in love,” Emilio immediately followed, tactfully. Score. Maya giggled. Emilio gave her a wink. He did a quick about-face and trudged his way into the kitchen. A moment later, he called back, “Hone, I’m gonna pick up some more creamer...I guess we’re all out.” “Okay, hone. But hurry back!” “It’s just down the street...” he laughed, “why don’t you come with?” Maya didn’t answer though... She called his name, and he turned to find her half-naked, standing in the doorway between the kitchen and living room. She sent him a heart-melting smile. He shuddered. Emilio couldn’t help but shake his head in amusement. There she was, in nothing but her panties- And he realized, he hadn’t felt this young in years. While the trials and tribulations of his small vacation in Japan -and the ensuing flight home- had been dreadful, it had all been worth it. He felt like a new man. He imagined himself as a snake. No, not in the slimey sense, but as a wild creature always on the hunt for satisfaction, bathing in the golden rays of the sun on hot black rocks. He saw this phenomena on the Discovery Channel last week. It intrigued him. And he felt as if, just last week, he shed his skin. His new skin, softer, smoother, was refreshing, but something that would take some time to adapt to. But it felt great to be loved. She was perfect. Whether or not she was a distraction from his upcoming endeavors was no longer of concern. He was, by proxy of his affection, forced into trusting her. Which is why it broke his heart when- “Honey, where’s my money?” Emilio reached up on top of the dusty refrigerator and came down with his wallet. He flipped through the layers of the black leather, but couldn’t find the wad of cash that he stored the other night. “Huh?” Maya called from the other room. Emilio began mumbling under his breath as she slowly made her way into the kitchen. Emilio turned quickly, exposing an empty slot in the wallet. “Right there,” he said, pointing emphatically, as if she wouldn’t believe him, “I left a $50 bill right there.” Maya rolled her eyes as her boyfriend’s cheeks flustered red. Emilio said, offended, “Honey, I swear to God...I took my check down to the bank on Monday. I had a fifty right there.” He showed her the empty slot again. “Yeah, I know, I can see,” she responded, quickly snatching the wallet. Ignoring his protest, she immediately began shuffling through the multiple layers of the wallet: A Mastercard, Visa, Massachusetts Driver’s license, ReVolution backstage pass, a small 3x5 of herself...a falsified ID card. Nothing. She sighed, tossing the wallet back to Emilio. “But...” “But nothing. You must have lost it,” Maya informed him as she brushed her way past, on the way to the fridge. “Anyway, we’ll go down later. I think there’s enough left for one cup.” An awry tingle shot though Emilio’s cerebral cortex and down his spine. Without thinking, he rushed over toward the fridge. Before she could open it, Emilio slammed it shut. Maya looked down at her feet, momentarily. Slowly, she picked up her head and met his seething gaze. “Well?” he asked. “Well what?” “Did you take it?” “Take it?” Maya’s eyes went wide. She cracked a slight smile and couldn’t help but chuckle. “Yes honey, I’ve been dating a millionaire for the last week just waiting for the moment to steal a measly fifty fucking dollars from him! ...Yeah, hone.” Emilio didn’t look impressed. He leaned up against the fridge, arms folded tightly across his chest. “Here’s your chance now,” a small voice called inside, ”blamce the bitch and split. This is what you’ve been waiting for, your chance to end it.” But a calmer, more collected voice called out, ”Emilio, you know what’s what. Don’t shove your fist into a gif horse’s mouth, man. In all these years, if you can’t tell a burning star from a leer jet in the night sky...” Emilio rubbed his eyes. “Okay, we’ll go later.” For a moment they both stood, frozen. Then, Maya grabbed the wallet and tossed it aside, letting it fall to the floor. Emilio grunted in amusement. Letting out an exasperating sigh, she jumped into his arms where he held her against his chest. “C’mon...” she said, slowly directing him back into the living room where they laid together on the couch. Above them, the neighbor’s continued their petty argument. “God damnit, Jack, make yourself useful !” At first, Emilio just sputtered. And then, Maya bit her tongue. But slowly, moment by moment, they’re cheeks reddened and they suddenly opened up into full-blown hysterics. They fell off the couch and rolled around with one another on the floor, laughing. - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Emilio had his work cut out for him. But he also had his carving knife. And Somers, Worrell...Latch Key Kid, they were lifeless pieces of driftwood, washed up on Emilio’s shore. In his strong, brown hands he would whittle their destinies at Seven of the Best. In front of thousands of roaring fans, and millions watching live around the world, he would fasten their destiny as he saw fit. He would cut and carve their bodies into a bloody sculpture that only an architect of this sport -his sport- could: A ringside, broken-neck, shattered-dreams monument dedicated to past names falling under the onslaught of a brighter future. His future. PrimeTime Central was always ready for Primetime. And now it’s PRIME’s time.
