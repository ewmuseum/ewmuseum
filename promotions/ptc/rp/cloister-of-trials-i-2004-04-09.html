Cloister of Trials I

9 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-09 Sweet victory. The first step in Seymour’s ascent is complete. He has triumphed over Matt Jackson and Lorenzo Guevera. The first battle has been won. But for Seymour, every battle could now be his last. His career, in the best case scenario, will end with him victorious in the finals of this tournament. But every round, a new opponent will attempt to end his dream. And in this round, the opponent will attempt to make his dream a nightmare. For Almasy shall face the corrupted priest, Pietro Geist. The Final Fantasy is no stranger to corrupt religious officials. And if the force possessing Geist believes that a hero such as Seymour’s afraid of a little hellfire and brimstone… He’s got another thing coming. ------ Church bells ring, calling out to all those who would worship on a brisk New York City evening. All manner of people, dressed in the best their closets have to offer, can be seen walking up the steps into the house of worship. To these men and women, the building is a Catholic Church, and the day is Good Friday, one of the holiest in the Catholic faith. However, to the man seated on the steps of the church, the house of worship is a Temple of Yevon. The man on the steps, of course, is Seymour Almasy. With Aerith standing at his side, the Final Fantasy watches the worshippers pour into the church, a saddened look on his face. Seymour Almasy: You know, Aerith, sometimes…sometimes I think they’re hopeless. Aerith Heartilly: Who, Seymour? Seymour gestures grandly behind him to the masses entering the church. Seymour Almasy: The religious. Yevon’s teachings have already been proven to be at the very least of questionable truth, and yet, so many continue to follow them. Why? Aerith Heartilly: Because, religion brings comfort. It brings hope for those who have none. Seymour Almasy: But only false hope. It is as a great philosopher says… Aerith looks at Seymour somewhat dubiously, mostly due to the thought of Seymour quoting a ‘great philosopher’. Aerith Heartilly: Which great philosopher? Maechan from Final Fantasy X? Seymour Almasy: Actually, I was thinking of Frederich Nietzsche. He argues that religion is but a temporary balm for the ills of society. It does not cure what makes society sick. Aerith is speechless for a few seconds, before managing to spit out a response. Aerith Heartilly: Since when have you heard of Nietzsche? Seymour Almasy: Since I found his book on the bookshelf one night waiting for you to get out of the bathroom. I swear, you take FOREVER to get ready… The sheer normalcy of the comment makes Aerith chuckle slightly, but Almasy continues right on as if he hadn’t even said it. Seymour Almasy: But yes, I’m not a fan of the false hope religion breeds. However, at the same time, I am not in favor of using one’s religious beliefs in order to corrupt them, to steal their spirit. And that is where myself and the demon that controls Pietro Geist differ in ideology. Almasy nods thoughtfully, running the situation over in his mind. For the first time, he is not facing a pure human being. He is facing a man that is possessed by a malevolent spirit. As such, anything and everything is possible. Seymour Almasy: I am not facing Pietro Geist, a priest. I am facing whatever the heck is residing inside of him. This means that the body will have superhuman powers. The entity inside of him seems to be capable of anything, including flame attacks, I shall have to be on my guard. Aerith Heartilly: But, how can you defeat such an opponent? Seymour Almasy: The easiest way would be to exorcise the spirit from Mr. Geist’s body. Unfortunately, I am not capable of that. So, it shall be down to simple hand to hand combat. Speaking through Geist, the demon said that he would refrain from making his opponents burst into flames. I am assuming that such an offer shall remain throughout the tournament. At least then I shall be able to pit my skills fairly against his. If, however, he proves false, he shall have to match his devilish flames against the pure power of my almighty FIRAGA~! Almasy concentrates hard, and using the time-honored wrestling trick of flash paper that he keeps in his armor, manages to produce an admittedly impressive ball of flame. Aerith simply shakes her head, as Almasy grins. Seymour Almasy: Hah! See that, evil spirit! I can throw fire around too! Your flames do not frighten me! Aerith has been noticeably fidgety for the last few minutes. Reluctantly, she begins to reveal the reason why. Aerith Heartilly: But I know something that will scare you. Seymour Almasy: Blast it! He has Blizzaga too, doesn’t he! DRAT! Since fire is my main attribute, his Blizzaga spells will do double damage on me. This isn’t good…not good at all-- Aerith Heartilly: He doesn’t have Blizzaga. It’s not even about the demon or Pietro Geist. It’s about my parents. The Final Fantasy immediately ceases speaking. Aerith’s parents have never approved of him, never approved of their daughter’s marriage to ‘some nutcase who thinks he’s a goddamned video game character.’ Aerith Heartilly: I told them you’re retiring. They want to meet you. The kneejerk reaction of Seymour was to consider running. This wasn’t going to be a pleasant experience at all. He would have to walk into a room with two people who disliked him, and two people that really had no REASON to dislike him. Seymour Almasy: Wonderful. Just wonderful. Aerith Heartilly: I know they’ve been kind of hostile in the past… Seymour Almasy: KIND of hostile? KIND OF? They threatened to disown you when they realized we were married! Aerith sighed, a long, deep sigh. Seymour Almasy: Heck, you still call yourself Aerith HEARTILLY! We’re married! Aren’t you supposed to take my name? Aerith Heartilly: Well, not all couples do that. Sometimes the woman keeps her name. Other times, they’ll keep their name and turn it into a middle name. If I did that, I’d be Aerith Heartilly Almasy… Seymour Almasy: I get the point… Aerith Heartilly: And I get yours. I know, I know…I do it because of my parents. I love them, I just wish they’d be a bit more understanding about all of this. Parents are supposed to be supportive. Seymour Almasy: Mine weren’t. The minds of both drift back to their pasts. For Seymour, it drifts back to a time when he was Brian Wilson, before massive exposure to video games warped his fragile mind and turned him into Seymour Almasy. Of course, he now believes he has always been Seymour. What reason would he have to believe otherwise? Seymour Almasy: My father died in that car accident, mom just went nuts afterwards and sent me to live with Grandpa Cid… Aerith’s memories of the event are somewhat clearer. She can remember the angry conversation she had with Sey--err, Jason’s mom. An idealistic teenage girl against a grown-up woman, for whom her own son had become an embarassment. Aerith couldn’t imagine then how a mother could be so cruel. She still couldn’t today. But she knew entirely well that Seymour, despite not knowing the full story, had a negative viewpoint of his mother from that incident. Aerith Heartilly: Don’t worry. My parents are nice people. They really are. They just…need time to get to know you. Seymour Almasy: But why do they have to be hostile, Aerith? It’s ridiculous. Every single time I walk in a room, I see the pointing fingers and hear the whispers. Hear the hurtful remarks. “Look, it’s the RPG freak!” It’s almost as if they don’t believe me. Almost as if they don’t believe I’m a hero, one out to save the world… Aerith Heartilly: Don’t worry about it, Seymour. They just don’t know any better. Her remark was said in the space of about two seconds. She had to diffuse Seymour’s worry. Eventually, she knew, Seymour would have to be told that he WAS living in a fantasy world. But, she wanted to postpone the bursting of that particular bubble for as long as possible. There was enough Seymour would have to get used to between now and retirement… Seymour Almasy: You’re probably right, Aerith. But your parents are just like them. So judgmental. No one’s willing to take me at face value, accept me for who I am. Except for you, Aerith. The growing emotion inside of Seymour takes him over, compels him to take hold of his wife, dip her backwards, and kiss her, slowly and deeply. The kiss goes on..and on…and on, until the sweetly romantic gesture is broken up by an elderly woman, who pokes him in the leg with her cane. Reluctantly, Almasy breaks the kiss, whirling to meet his assailant. Seymour Almasy: What was that for, ma’am? Elderly Woman: You two ought to be ashamed of such a carnal display in front of a house of God, especially on such a holy day! Indeed, it was Good Friday, a holiday Seymour Almasy did not know anything about. However, Aerith did. Being a rather non-practicing Catholic, she had a few choice words for the old woman. Aerith Heartilly: It is YOU who ought to be ashamed! Days such as this are days to cherish the lives we have! Elderly Woman: They are days to repent for our sins, not commit them! Aerith Heartilly: It is a sin to kiss the one I love? Elderly Woman: Heathen… The elderly woman pokes Seymour with her cane one more time, for good measure, before entering the church, and disappearing behind the thick wooden doors. Aerith Heartilly: Now I see what you mean. They’re so…unthinking. Seymour Almasy: Not all of them….but most. Nowhere in the teachings of Yevon does it say anything about public displays of affection, or wearing revealing clothing…neither are written as sins. It is people who wish to force their concept of morality on others that declare them as such, using religion to veil their purpose. No wonder the demon inside Pietro Geist can find so many hosts… Aerith Heartilly: What do you mean? Seymour Almasy: The demon cannot feed on the truly pious. Those whose faith is pure and true are untouchable by the forces of darkness. Darkness can only feed on those who use faith for other purposes. For their own gain. To foster their morality on others. Purposes such as that. In the case of the bartender, the demon purported to use the power of the divine to increase business at the bartender’s establishment. Such is not something a divine being would offer…but the bartender was deluded. He believed that his piety entitled him to the divine gift, and so he accepted it. Almasy nods solemnly, and continues. Seymour Almasy: The moral of such a story is that those who believe their faith entitled them to anything, or seek to use it for their own gain, ultimately are not faithful at all. Aerith Heartilly: Seymour…what if the demon attempts to possess you? You would be far more useful to the powers of darkness than a frail priest would… The Final Fantasy shivers at the thought. Twice, he had given in to the forces of darkness. The second time concluded with him in a hospital bed after Hecate, Chryste in disguise, had powerbombed him onto an explosive device. It was the event in his life that made him realize, more than ever, that evil could not triumph. Seymour Almasy: The demon can come for me, Aerith. But he will find no blackness in my heart. All he shall find is purity in my purpose. And once the demon realizes that he cannot take me over, he shall be angered. And he shall engage me in battle. A battle worthy of my final series of them. Finally, I shall have the chance to purge a true evil from this world… The wind begins to pick up, slowly at first, and then howling faster and faster. Almasy smiles and stands, extending his arms out, eyes turned skyward. Seymour Almasy: Come, demon! I can feel you here! Why should we wait? If you wish to defeat me…COME NOW! DO NOT HIDE! Almasy’s challenge seemingly goes unanswered, as the wind dies down. Of course, a wind gust, to the average person, is no reason to believe that a demon is coming, but then, Seymour’s never played with a full deck of Triple Triad cards. Seymour Almasy: Hmph. It must not have been the demon at all. Merely a gust of wind. Ah well…I am a patient man. I can wait for my chance to prove myself in battle. Somewhat sheepishly, the Final Fantasy returns to his seated position upon the steps. Aerith Heartilly: And I am sure you will. But for now, I think we should go introduce you to my parents. It is clear from Seymour’s facial expression and sagging shoulders that he would rather battle fifty demons of darkness than embark on the quest he is about to undertake. But the Final Fantasy knows that his post-Battle Arena quest is equally as important as his one in the Arena. With a final, weary sigh, he brings himself back to his feet, determined to get his next mission over with. Seymour Almasy: Alright, Aerith. Let’s go. Aerith Heartilly: I’ll be with you, Seymour. I’ll help make this as painless as possible. Seymour extends a hand, helping his lovely wife to her feet. Hand in hand, they begin to walk off. Seymour Almasy: After facing your parents, some pesky dark demon should be a piece of cake… To Be Continued.
