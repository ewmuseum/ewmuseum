Date: 2004-11-10 ETERNAL: THE END <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> The beginning… Those words flowed through his mind like poison being spilled slowly onto his soul, contaminating it in a deliberate but painful manner, forcing his mind to focus and delve itself into a past in which he had always strived to forget. His face cringed as he surged down memory lane, finding his way between the jagged stray pieces of his eternal memories. With his fingers crunched deep in his pockets, he stared beyond the horizon of darkness, his crystal blue eyes glazed deep in thought. Where did it begin…? That question had penetrated his mind constantly, flooding it with likelihoods that he had never once contemplated. Ever since that night when he deposited a bullet through his father’s chest, a thought had seeped into his head like a continuous irritant, a voice whispering lies that he did not want to heed. I did not kill her… He was certain of that. He did not kill his mother, but why did his father accuse him of such an act? And as determined as he was to demean those words as lies, a sudden rush of uncertainly had pierced through his soul, questioning his own past. As much as many others thought otherwise, including his sister Krystal and Athena, he was able to recall most of his past. He remembered everything; his art of pretence had been completed to perfection that no one would have thought of his words as lies. There was just one exception; he couldn’t recollect anything of his childhood. He remembered nothing of his mother’s death, except for the way she died, and even that he was beginning to doubt as other thoughts raped his mind of innocence. Up till now, he had always believed his mother had died because of old age. He could even examine the memory in which he had sat by his mother’s deathbed, with tears welling up in his eyes. What if that memory was just a lie…? He shook his head violently, raging to rid his mind of that thought. It couldn’t be possible, could it? It couldn’t have all been a lie. Why couldn’t it be…? He couldn’t answer that question. After walking away from Krystal and Athena the previous night with their eyes arrowing through him in apparent hate, his mind had only been constantly filled with thoughts of his mother. He did not care for the other sins that he had committed, and he couldn’t care less about the fathers and relatives of others that he had killed in his eternal lifetime. Guilt wasn’t weighing down on him now for killing Athena’s father, nor was his conscience disintegrating him on the murder of his own blood. The only person I had cared for was my mother… He knew that those words were the truth, because they came from the depths of his heart. And as evil as he was, he still couldn’t bear the thought of the possibility of him killing his mother. Was it even possible? Surely not. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> You speak as if it was already a foregone conclusion. Obviously you’re deceiving yourself if you think that my mind will distract me from defeating Shadow. As if you don’t know me well enough already. You know that I will not lose. It will be pure ignorance for you to even suggest that. Let me hold your hand and take you back down in the eternal tracks of time. Can you even name me of a time when I was defeated completely? I thought so. You couldn’t even think of one name, could you? Yes, you can continue hoping. But don’t hold your breath; I want to kill you before you die. I want to feel the satisfaction of standing over your corpse, drowning myself in your red crimson blood. If you think that the only way that your life will be spared will be by Shadow’s apparent victory, then I guess you are dead already. By placing your life in the hands of someone who can never defeat me, you have proved yourself worthy of nearing death. You know that it can’t be avoided. You know deep inside, that in the end it will be you and me. You don’t want that day to come? Then continue on hoping. Go on wishing that someone who stands as an obstacle to my glory will be able to stop me. And if I were you, I will be writing a letter right now. With writings in a deep red bold, I will sign it with a flourish. If I were you, I would have sent that letter to God by now, begging him for Shadow’s victory. And if you’re expecting God to come out and assure you of my defeat, I will hate to see you disappointed. For even God will sit this one out. And if you think that this is the best I can do, you obviously haven’t been watching. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> “Sir, can you help a poor soul out of the kindness of your heart?” The voice of a woman startled Kasper, evaporating his thoughts in an instant. It had a soft tone to it, stained by years of humility. He turned his head slightly, noticing that his reaction had reverberated to the old woman, who took a step back thinking that he would lash out at her. In a quick scan of an eye, he observed the rags that were plastered on her body, tattered by the weary winds and the harsh cold winter nights. He flinched, looking up at her face. And in a moment of sight, his strained face disappeared replaced by an expression of bewilderment. She had a pale complexion that was full of innocence; her humbleness was clearly seen through her deep brown eyes. Instantly, he was captivated by her, observing the level of her frailty that was way beyond her years. Kasper looked around, trying to figure out where this woman had come from. The park was deserted on that night, with the scattering benches devoid of mortals. A small gush of the night wind flew past his face, covering his pale complexion with his misty white hair. With a slight push of his hand, he pulled his hair back, peering at the woman who was standing in front of him. “What can I do for you?” he managed to blurt out the words at last, after a few seconds of silence separated them from her previous question. Her response was putting forward her right hand in front of Kasper, with a small black bag dangling from her index finger. He looked down, and saw that the bag was half full with coins, and finally understood what she meant. She was a beggar patrolling that park, hoping that strangers would actually open their hearts and contribute to what would become her source of food and life. And throughout her years, she had realized that sometimes, words could be used as a medium for refusal. Most of the time, it was painful to her ears, but her humility carried her on forward, with the motivation of hope always urging her forward in the search of a better life. “Please sir, my two kids haven’t eaten in three days.” She begged in a soft whisper, noticing the hesitation in Kasper’s eyes. She took a step closer, hoping to sway his emotions in her favour. He kept quiet as he produced a couple of coins from his pocket and deposited them into the ragged black bag, observing the worn lines dismembered from the long years from being held in those woman’s rough hands. Her face lit up as she heard the sound of the coins clicking into place, a soft reassuring sound in the silence of the night, the sound of her following day’s meal, barely enough to feed herself and her two children. But yet satisfaction filled her expression, glowing in the dimness of the light, a sign of gratitude to the heavens. “You are kind, dear sir.” She whispered, her eyes glazed with tears as those words slid out of her throat. “One day you will be repaid for your kindness.” Kasper grunted, reality setting down on his shoulders like a pile of bricks, impaling him to the depths of the ground. “My sins far outweigh my kindness, I’m sorry that you will not see the day that I’m repaid.” He muttered under his breath, just loud enough for the old woman to catch his words with her sharp sense of hearing. “Sometimes there’s a reason behind sins that even the most complex mind can’t comprehend,” she replied in a soft tone, wisdom of generations resonating through to Kasper’s ears. He was surprised, not expecting her to reply in such a fashion. And for a slight moment, he was quite at a lost for words. “Never mind, you’ll understand with time,” she continued, flashing a smile that weirdly calmed Kasper down, reassuring him of the reasoning behind his past actions. “Maybe.” Her response to that was another smile, taking a step back in the process. She looked down, and turned away, her footsteps creating a small stifled thud on the grass. And then she paused for a quick second, turning her head back in his direction. “Thank you.” Her whisper was soft and humble, and the tone was filled with innocence and purity. And as sudden as her appearance had been, her two words stunned him, flooding his mind with lost memories that he had never seen. Her words reminded him of someone, triggering a string of events that he had forced himself to forget. Two words had suddenly turned his world on him, blanketing him with unified pain and madness in a single strong moment that seemed as if it lasted for generations. Those were his mother’s last two words before she died, he remembered it now. And as he searched through the bank of memories flowing through his mind, he caught the day of what had been the day of her death. It was all true. <><><><><><><><><><><><><><><> TO BE CONTINUED
