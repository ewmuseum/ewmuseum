Don't Hand Me Over … (Part 2)

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 Don’t Hand Me Over… "Lashing Out At The World." ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Two hours ago, Paddy O’Shea lay in bed, nestled amidst a mass of blankets. And he was contented with life, because finally he was being dealt a good hand. A career, a nice caravan. Back in the olden days of yore, all those years ago, all he seemed to be doing was shooting and being shot at. there was no homes, there was only accommodations. There was no beauty in what happened. There was blood and guts in both sides…but they didn’t have a choice. For the freedom of the people, there was no choice. Paddy had remembered as a boy of the terrible stories of how the English had tore his country down with ferocity that wasn’t seen again until the time of Hitler. I’m being too extreme? Well, one third of Catholics, Irish and Old English, were slaughtered where they lay within ten years of Cromwell coming to Ireland. Is it that far away? But he didn’t fight then because history was history right? We can use the political system to get what we want! Er, that’s a no because all the election boundaries had been fixed so that the true Irish never would get their say. And only the owner of a household could vote…in 1960’s Ireland?! But still, despite the oppression, he didn’t fight… Not until he seen that 90% of Catholic men in the city of Derry was unemployed, while jobs went to East where the supporters of the British were housed. Not until the soldiers came with their blackened faces and shot innocent men in front of their eyes. Not until the Battle of the Bogside, when the police force battled with civilians with the aim of burning every house down very much in mind. I repeat, that was the police force. The same police force then went ahead and did it in Belfast at Bombay Street, just because the people that lived there were of a different religion. Sickening. And then, when there was no choice, either eat or be eaten, he picked up a gun and went out and fought. And civilians got caught in the crossfire and it haunted him, yes, believe it or not, Paddy O’Shea has feelings. The people had been brought into a conflict by the British government, the same government that made a stand against the disgusting rage of Communist Russia in Hungary, when they mutilated a whole country, condemning a government for ever doing such a thing. Um…hypocrites? Those people that died, those innocent men who were at the wrong place at the wrong time… And it hurt him, when he thought of the faces of those he had watched fall. They were few and far between, but they were lives. But, as I said, he had managed to leave it all behind him…as the IRA morphed and began to use bombs more frequently, Paddy had developed a very bad conscience over the whole affair. He was juggling what was right by his religion and what was right by his country. And despite the IRA having support all over Ireland, from top to bottom, and from abroad even officially from countries sometimes like in Palestine, he walked out. And everything he had ever did became a secret. The British had never caught him, they didn’t know he was in the IRA. He didn’t tell anyone about that chapter in his life either. As far as he was concerned, when he was in America, no one knew and that was good. He was often a muddle between pride and shame from his actions, but he was always silent, and would remain so until he was decided… But…then the arrest. Perhaps they did know him from the past so that when he stepped off the plane, they were waiting. It was unlikely but it was possible. It doesn’t really matter now, because the damage is already waiting to be done. America…may soon know. What exactly they arrested him for didn’t matter, they’ll think of something damning enough in time, there was a guarantee on that…but damnit, all he wanted was his wrestling. He thought of the fans. Every night he’d come out to the biggest cheers in the crowd, just like he fought for the people in Ireland, he did so again in the ring, and they loved him for it. But would they hate him now? Would the boos slowly creep in? Would they try to understand that what the media says isn’t always true? That he had been just like them two hundred years prior, when he said "we shall overcome" they had mouthed the words too? Only time would tell he supposed. Hell, if they managed to convict him, there would be no facing the crowds anyway. There would only be four gray walls housing him for twenty years. Now he sits in only a small depressing room, the walls both questioning and unrelenting. Soon they would come in, their confession in one hand, their batons in the other and they would take what they wanted by hook or by crook. "Mr O’Shea…" a voice with an American twang croaked to Paddy’s left. From his hard wooden seat on which he sat handcuffed, his head jerked around and took notice of the man standing in the doorway. His sandy hair combed neatly over, and a navy suit clashing with the red tie he wore, he stepped back in shock at what he saw in Paddy’s face. There was a lump the size of a golf ball already below his eye with a series of thin cuts, black from dried blood, adorning his face. Pale and on the verge of fainting, his head swayed back around to neutral while he searched to find the energy to close his gaping mouth. "Th-they said you fell…" the man said nervously as he slowly entered the room before taking the seat opposite Paddy O’Shea on the table. Paddy only smiled at him. There was no laughter there. "Well, we can deal with that later…I’m John Rains, a PTC lawyer. We heard about this incident and got over here as soon as possible to see if we could get you out for your GTT match. Good news is, your contract means its compulsory for you to wrestle, you will be allowed to do so under the watch of heavy guard, then extradited immediately for questioning." Questioning…that will be when the beatings start then will it. "Paddy…to be honest, I was expecting you were arrested for disorderly behavior…not murder." On the word of murder, Paddy snapped himself from his daze while his lips fumbled to ask who, what, why and how all at once. So that would be what they nailed him for then, was it? Rains, ran his hand through his hair as he thought about the answers. He had already made up his mind that O’Shea had in fact did it. In his eyes, this was what the Irish were born to do. There was no thought of motive, there was just killing. But a job was a job and he had to defend this bastard in front of him. So, let’s see what alibi he has concocted so he can make sure it doesn’t fall apart in court. "Paddy…I just want you to run over, when was the last time you seen your jacket, the one that was found near the crime scene." The jacket was key to all he had predicted. He was being proved right. "Well…." Paddy muttered. "When aye left from the house yesterday morning…" ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ …Paddy had stood still on the porch for a few seconds, just taking in the cold January air that was stimulating all sorts of reactions in his throat. There was freshness about Paddy this morning, there had been a change. Tony Mullen had woken something inside of him. He had died as a murderer, a drugged up lunatic. But he was sorry, deeply deeply sorry. Years before, Tony Mullen had been in the IRA with Paddy, he was like an older brother to Paddy. And he was an honorable man. While other voices in the organization talked of bombing hotels and bars, Tony had always reiterated, there must be no one in the crossfire. Despite what you thought of war (because that was a war), Tony had sought to keep things concerning armalites between those with armalites. If there truly was a way of the soldier, he lived by it. He was a soldier then, a murderer later, but he had asked for forgiveness and Paddy liked to think he got it. And before he was executed, Paddy seen something in Tony he had never seen in him before…he seen a man who had fought all his life for peace, content to let the dream slip away. His friends has died at the hands of those men he hated, his country remained bound in chains…but he let it go. There was an example there, Paddy was sure Tony wanted him to follow. That morning, Paddy did. There would be no more wars, no more guns…there would be him, his career and his family. And that morning, as he walked to the end of his driveway and began his venture down to the corner shop, he finally breathed the air of free man. That feeling of tranquility with oneself lasted all of one day. Paddy had oft times sat in his house just wondering about the deeds he had done in his tenure as a rebel. Some made him proud, how he had made his first stand. When soldiers beat his neighbors, he made a stand against them. Like vagabonds, they were drove out by a mob. Other memories made him feel deep remorse…firing on soldiers in broad daylight, and maiming innocent people…there had been very few instances of this…but still…he had hurt those that had stepped back from the firing line. Like all those periods of reflection, he wanted forgiveness now. He didn’t know where to get it, the Church would let him confess his sins but there was always a tinge of contempt from them. He supposed just hoping for it was better than not wanting it at all. He had seen some horrible things, but for the cause, he had done what he felt was right at the time. This cause, not cancered as so many believe it to be, but the same as any majority in the world who are being suppressed by a minority, demanding freedom in whatever way they could. Guerilla warfare had been Paddy’s last resort, but when your enemy stifles the politics, what else can you do? The thing was, all these questions and emotions didn’t need addressed anymore. He was a new man, one of peace with himself, traveling from place to place, sometimes in caravan, sometimes in a house. He didn’t need that whirlwind drama anymore, there was a sense of serenity in his country now… Wasn’t there? As Paddy approached the shop, his eyes were drawn to a man who stood outside. Dressed only in a short sleeved t-shirt in the cutting cold, he leant against the wall, shivering. Then Paddy looked down at himself. Not so many years ago Paddy couldn’t afford the clothes he wore, hell, he didn’t have the money for any new clothes. It was this feeling of compassion that drew him to peel off his jacket before he even reached the man. The man himself seemed on edge as Paddy approached, glancing up and down the road almost feverishly. SO on edge that when Paddy was a few metres from him, he flinched painfully around and raised his fists. "Whoa there buddy. Ye jus’ look a bit cold there. Here, ye can have this jacket," Paddy said kindly, a warm small on his face. Paddy held out the jacket to him, even as he now stood shuddering, he didn’t reconsider the offer. The man looked at him suspiciously for what seemed like an age, then took it gently from him. "Er…thanks…" the man muttered, taking the navy jacket and pulling it on. Then he looked away. Belfast man Paddy thought, picking up on his broad accent. Paddy didn’t stay around for any lasting laureates. He merely turned to his right and entered the shop leaving the man behind. The new you Paddy O’Shea. Give, give, give… … and watch them take, take, take… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ "Nice story…though if I were you, I wouldn’t put as much emphasis on the fact the man is looking suspicious. Nice story though, how long did it take you to make that one up?" Paddy looked across the table at the PTC lawyer. So casual and easygoing, but the cogs of his mind might as well have been on his face. He could see the ticking. He was formulating something in his head. And the same must have been true of Paddy, because now he felt his own face contorting into half confusion, half anger. "Aye said…aye didn’t make it up. It’s true." Paddy hissed, his eyes narrowed. Instead of the lawyer agreeing, he merely chuckled, and this nailed Paddy hard. "Don’t fucking laugh at me!" Rains looked at Paddy hard, his smile disintegrating. Not into fear as Paddy had expected, basing his prediction of the way he had timidly entered the room. Now Rains was angry. There was a silence there, save the heavy breathing of the two men, for perhaps only a moment. "You…you insolent little bastard. You fucking terrorist. How dare you speak to me like that! How dare you! I would never have worked with PTC if I had have known I was dealing with murderers, detonating bombs without care, how dare you!" Breath….now proceed. "You think I went through law school just so I could get killers off the hook? I was born in American, I watched my country bloom into a superpower. It didn’t grow on the back of scumbags like you, standing with your guns in hands, killing innocent people. I don’t need this shit, okay? So fuck you, I defend people accused in the wrong no-" "AHHHHH!" Rains didn’t really get a chance to see what followed. But he did get a clear morale from that little rant. Don’t tempt a caged tiger John…because that cage might not be locked… The wooden seat Paddy sat it was broke as Paddy sprung upwards, the bending of his arm applying pressure on the chair at the right spot. Handcuffed and full of rage, he sprang across the table at Rains and proceed to stomp, kick, bite and all of the above twice over. "AHHHHHHH-" Paddy was cut off by a blow to the back of the head by a baton, he managed to turn around and look into the eyes of a British soldier. Then the soldier swung again and everything went black/ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Terrorism (noun) Terrorism is the use of violence for political reasons. They’re afraid of it. Government’s of countries that is, terrified of the terrorists. And why? Because they plant bombs, fire their guns and have a different view to them. In other words, they endorse their abstract views with force. What’s that kettle? The pot called you black? Never! I won’t even mess around with the point, I’ll get right down to it. How dare you. How dare you. Who are you to call Paddy O’Shea a terrorist? Looking down the scope of your gun, who are you? All the deeds you have done, chuckling as you recall your first kill, who are you to call terrorist? What separates you from him? What’s that? There’s rules in war? You can’t use nail bombs, but you can use napalm. You can’t plant bombs in buildings but you sure as can bomb from the air. You have to wear a uniform, because only the financed have the privilege of fighting. You have to fight cleanly. Because war can actually be done so. Remember the War of Independence. Commonly found in sentences with such fine words as ‘great’ and ‘revered’, the birth of a messianic nation. Does our War of Independence fall under that category? When men ran out with pitchforks and dungarees, tell me, was that the rules of war you had in mind. Ah, you were over throwing a corrupt ruler…*cough* Ireland *cough* So quick you are to separate your cause from ours. So quick. But if you’d only stop to think then you’d understand. In Vietnam, there was no such thing as the Vietnamese. This was a cancer to you…and cancer must be cut out. Napalm form the air, spiraling from on high and my, how quickly it burned the flesh from children as they sat in their schools wondering, would they die today, would they be luckily enough to see tomorrow. What about every city you’ve ever invaded? Sorry, you don’t invade, you bomb the shit out of the place, ripping the lives from thousands of people in a few weeks. It took the IRA thirty years to reach that monstrous level. Didn’t you just do what the IRA did quicker? Ah, but we’re the butchers. Let me tell you a story. All seated? There was a woman who had been widowed for many years. All she had was her small cottage, her eight sons and her four green fields. Strangers came to take her land from her. They took it alright, and then they took all of her son’s lives. Out in the cold she lay, as she watched the strangers tear down anything she had ever held dear. And she cried, and the valleys were filled with her grief. But her sons had sons, as brave as were their fathers and they fought for her, giving their lives when need be, to get back what was hers. Yet…by your estimations. These sons…are the butchers? Let me tell you something, terrorists are the people you see in films, those men who were once in the marines but now take over an island filled with nuclear weapons. Give us a billion dollars or KA-BOOM. Please do not confuse freedom fighters with such a concept. They don’t fight so they can get richer, most surviving members of the IRA are working class men, living in terraced houses, with no prospects and no future because they’re part of an empire that stifles their every opportunity. There is no benefit, despite how the media like to portray it. In act, let’s clear up a few myths shall we? Did you know? The IRA were and are not drug dealers. Quite the contrary, in the absence of the protection of the law in Catholic communities, the IRA choked any supply of drugs getting in. They policed by the peoples law and the people supported them in their bleakest hours. The Republican force that have dealt with drugs, is the INLA, who formed so they could do such things. That is why the IRA separate themselves completely from that organization. Did you know? Contrary to belief, money from bank robberies do not line IRA member’s pockets. It s a non-profit force hence the reason why I stated these men are working class men, still driving taxis, picking up rubbish and generally cleaning societies arse. The funds going into arms and political campaigns. Did you know? The IRA didn’t loan shark. They were never a business and never tried to be. Did you know? War is never clean. Outsiders like to look at the situation from 1916-1922 in a period where Michael Collins and Eamon DeValera ruled the roost. They call this period romantic. Romantic like your wars? The Gulf War? The Iraqi War? The Vietnamese War? Join three wars and get the fourth free? War isn’t beautiful, the IRA under Michael Collins still killed British soldiers in their beds. The only difference now is that bombs are all the rage. Like those atomic ones of yours, eh? Did you know? The knee cappings are claimed to be "a cruel and unusual punishment". So is the executions the IRA carried out…doesn’t the American Constitution say something along those lines? Yet we’ll ignore that an deliver lethal injections, hm? Ah, I forgot, the Truman doctrine committed the US to the defense of the so-called ‘free world’. So why haven’t they cast their eye to Ireland? Because every President, one after the other knows…the histories of Ireland and American are the same…every action has a motive. Outsider looking in? Hell this is the same situation that happened to you, only it took us longer. Lessons for the day? When a man is being oppressed, he will fight the power. When he fights, he doesn’t fight as to lose, he does so to win, what every means necessary. People looking in, who have such a shameful history themselves, whether it’s over the issue of racial equality or anything else, shouldn’t pass judgment. And finally, a man is honored for his cause, sometimes mistakes are made, but remember, if the oppressor hadn’t been here in the first place, it would never have escalated to this. Afterall, why are we the ones that have to bow down? ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Arguing with yourself is a sign of losing your mind Paddy…Jesus Christ, this isn’t the end of Paddy in the IRA…this is merely the revolution of the revolution.
