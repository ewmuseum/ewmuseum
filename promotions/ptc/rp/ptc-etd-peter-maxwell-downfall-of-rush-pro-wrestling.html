22 March 2005 The Downfall of Rush Pro Wrestling - Part I Peter Maxwell Global Championship Wrestling. The name “Peter Maxwell” used to mean something in this business. I’m not entirely sure what happened along the way, but, I’m fairly certain that thanks to my plan to attempt to take RPW down from the inside has left me to be a modern day pariah in the wrestling buisness. I’m going to take you down the path that few have ever seen, and describe for each of you, exactly what it took to simply take RPW down. I really had the best intentions when I got involved with RPW. I wanted it to succeed. But, the enemy of man is truly greed. And that’s exactly what it turned into. My greed, and my penchant for fucking up just about anything I touch in the end, doomed RPW. A few months before it was set to open, I was contacted by Rush Mason to enter RPW, using the Jason O’Neil gimmick. O’Neil and I had grown to be friends, and quite honestly, he was sick of wrestling, and wanted to disassociate his name, after how he was treated in PWC. Who can blame him, really? Ron Starr forced an entire company to fake his death, and despite reservations from those involved in the angle, some point to that very moment that Angelo Deville threw the “body” over a bridge, that PWC was over where it stood. Regardless, I accepted, and in my talks with Mason, I knew he was an absolute buffoon. He knew nothing about how to make a company work, and even before opening, RPW was in the red. He’d already paid out his ass to make RPW just like one of Ron Starr’s companies. He’d invested in a roster full of “talent”, and I use that term very loosely. Most were mid level guys that most companies wouldn’t touch. He’d spent thousands on securing a location for the shows, and overpaid by at least 400%. The guy knew absolutely nothing about buisness, and quite frankly, was borderline worthless. So, enter Peter Maxwell, the benefactor of RPW. Sure, I know what you’re thinking. “Xavier Kannon was the benefactor. Not you.” You couldn’t be more wrong. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ It was November 5th, 2003. There were just days to go before the opening of Rush Pro Wrestling, and already, Rush Mason was worried. I remember sitting in his office, while he argued on the phone with one of his questionable pieces of talent, trying to negotiate for the fifth time, this wrestlers appearance fee. Originally, it was supposed to be less than one thousand dollars. By this phone call, Mason was willing to pay fifteen thousand, just to have this guy at his show. And trust me, he’s not the kind of person that would put an ass into a seat, if you catch my drift. He was horrible, and I’m absolutely sorry that Mason picked him to be the first contender for the RPW Championship. The fans of RPW never forgave us for that, and I don’t really think I can blame them for it. Mason hung up the phone, and put his face into his hands. “Fucking hell, Pete. I can’t keep doing this. This is torture.” “Well, when you’re willing to pay El Diablo fifteen thousand dollars, that’s horrible. You really believe in this guy that much?” He looked up and contorted his face. “He’s one of the best I can get.” I knew at that moment, Rush Mason wasn't the man to carry Rush Pro Wrestling to Glory. Two days later, I sat again in Mason’s office in Charlotte, North Carolina, negotiating my takeover of RPW. Mason wasn’t cut out for this. He knew it, I knew it. The popcorn vendors knew it. So, as it became, Mason would be the on camera face of RPW, for a month, while I helped run things behind the scenes. Within minutes, a few of the people I’d been friends with from the past had called and wanted to come in. Now, don’t get me wrong. Rush Mason is a hell of a guy, and a hell of a competitor. What he lacks in behind the scenes savy, he more than makes up for in the ring, and as a human being. He’s a great person I’m glad to call a friend. He had the business restraint of 50 Cent in a golden brothel. As soon as I took over, I gutted the roster, and brought in guys that would work cheaper, entertain better, and make RPW look good. The talent I had lined up, even before RPW opened read like a who’s who of the wrestling buisness. Trashcan Man, Angelo Deville, Nerezza, Aimz, and Jason Dread were just a small list of the people set to debut in RPW, at some point. I hired them at good prices, and with good intentions. But, sometimes, even the best intentions leave a bad taste in your mouth, which is exactly where I was driven with RPW. Perhaps RPW was destined to become the next NTICW. Maybe it was Rush Mason’s influence, or the fact that the horrible lease we had on the Halton Arena. Or maybe none of us really wanted RPW to see any sort of success. Whatever it was, most everyone was simply prolonging the inevitable. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ The first big event that took RPW by storm was the “Blood Incident” that got us kicked off Fox Sports Southeast. Let’s break it down, first, and then take a look at what exactly happened. If you’ll recall, there was a segment toward the end of one of our episodes of Adrenaline (God, I hated that fucking name for a show. If there was anything more shitty, it was that we were stuck with the show names, thanks to promotional material. Sunday shithole was a much better and fitting name) El Diablo beat up two of Gideon Poe’s bandmates, and ended up licking their blood up off the floor. Total setup. In reality, Poe and I had talked before hand, Diablo was becoming more of a liability in and out of the ring. Not only did he expect that the title was going to be handed to him, but, he also expected Poe to carry him through each match. He’s a horrible wrestler, and even worse person without the facepaint. Let’s face it. When your entire gimmick is that you’re a mexican film director, there’s not a hell of a lot we can do to get you over. So, Poe and I formulated a plan to not only rid ourselves of the Fox Sports contract, but also rid ourselves of Diablo. A few hours later, just before that episode of Adrenaline, Diablo, Poe and myself sat down, discussed what would happen that night. “D,” I said to him. “We want to do something a little over the top at the end of the show this week. I want you to meet Fred Simpson and Paul Pogoszelski. They’re going to be playing Gideon Poe’s bandmates this week.” “What do you need me to do?” Diablo asked, as a smile crept up onto my lips. The only redeeming quality this guy had was his ability to do whatever was asked of him, in hopes that it could lead to bigger and better things. “I want you, to unleash a level of violence against them, never before seen on TV. And by that, I mean, I want you to cut them.” Fred and Paul were stuntmen, and good at what they did. We had students from the UNCC drama and film departments make them up with simple bloodbags, and rigs to make them look like El Diablo did a number on them. And it worked like a charm. Not only did we get out of our Fox Sports contract, but we also got to make Diablo the public scapegoat for what happened. We fired him, without even calling him again, pretty much, paid him six months of severance pay, and got him deported back to Mexico so he couldn’t say anything. I’d like to say I was the only brainchild of this angle, but, in reality, I’d had help in planning it. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ I couldn’t believe when O’Neil called me about a group of people who wanted to buy a stake in RPW. At first, I was a bit leery, but decided it couldn’t do any harm. We made reservations for a restaurant in the Charlotte Area, and sat in a private room. O’Neil and I made some small talk, waiting for the guests of honor. When they arrived, even someone like myself was in complete and total awe. First in the door was Xavier Kannon, with his wife Eleanor on his arm. He wasn’t a tall man, but he exuded confidence as he stepped into the room and extended a hand toward me. We shook hands, and said our hellos as a second man entered the room. Angelo Fucking Deville. For months, there had been speculation that Deville had in fact been killed, thanks to a mob hit. I never realized that Angelo Deville wasn’t just a simple gimmick for television; he actually was Angelo Deville. He looked at every corner, and every wall, making sure his safety was intact. His smug grin was plastered on his face, as he held out a hand for a handshake. I took it quickly, and he pulled me into a friendly sort of embrace. The mood was light. We ate plentifully, and talked about people we’d met together in the business, matches we’d wrestled, bookers we hated, and so on. It wasn’t until Kannon asked me about RPW that the conversation took a turn to the unbelievable. On his fifth glass of wine, Kannon raised his glass, and looked me in the eyes. “Tell me about this Rush Pro Wrestling company you’ve been running, Peter.” I began the story of Mason turning it over to me, and the people I’d brought in. He seemed impressed, and tipped his glass in my direction. “Very nice. Reminds me of a sort of wrestling utopia, I suppose. A place where people of my kind could gain quite a bit of respect.” “Well, if it were possible for you to be respected more, Xavier, I think it could be just that sort of place.” I replied, both stroking his ego, and hoping that I could indeed sign a deal with all three men at the table that night. The next few hours surrounded themselves with questions about the Charlotte area. Restaurants, schools, people, shopping, places, events, that sort of thing. They listened as I spoke, and seemed genuinely interested in coming into RPW. When Deville finally spoke, after an uncharacteristic long silence, it was with a hint of reservation in his voice. “Understand, Pete, that if something were to happen, and we’re speaking purely hypothetically here, but, you’d have to understand that things would have to be run our way. I don’t mean to sound like a totalitarian asshole, but, that’s simply something that we’re accustomed to.” “I understand completely, and every effort would be made to ensure that’s exactly what happens. I take care of my wrestlers, and I take care of my friends,” was my reply. Deville and Kannon looked at each other and smiled. “Good,” replied Kannon. “Currently, you’re running an angle about unveiling the benefactor of RPW, aren’t you?” I nodded. “Well, suppose we say it’s me. That after OSW, and all that happened with Ellie, I needed a place to ensure the stars of tomorrow are brought into the business in a manner like I did. So, I bought the company from Mason, and tried to keep my name secret by appointing this…buffoon, Little Al to run the company. I knew it was only a matter of time before I had to come in and take the reins myself, and that was the entire idea.” Little Al, for those that don’t know is the son of Kremmen, former PCW wrestler. Originally, it was supposed to be a gimmick to get Kremmen into the RPW fold, but, when RPW joined up with PTC, Kremmen’s duties within PTC that he currently holds wouldn’t allow for that to happen. So, his connection to the former wrestler were never revealed on TV. Only here. Funny how that works, isn’t it? Less than a month later, Deville was in RPW as the Dread Pirate Roberts, Kannon was in charge of RPW, and everything seemed right with the world. RPW was riding a huge tide of popularity and was poised to explode onto the main spotlight as the single greatest wrestling company in the world, thanks to the way Kannon did business. Unfortunately, three months later, it would all blow up in our faces. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ “You’re going to do exactly what we say, and when we say it. If you ever want even a sniff of wrestling in the United States again, you’re going to do exactly what I tell you to do. We all know how you feel about foreginers, Roberts.” The words came out of Angelo’s mouth as they usually did. His biting remarks, and overly serious tone made it certain that he wasn’t kidding around. Resist, and you’ll be spooning Jimmy Hoffa. Aaron Roberts stood before him, a look of bewilderment in his eyes. Known to the world as Magnum Wolf, he nodded, and accepted Deville’s proposal, which wasn’t really a proposal at all. More like an ultimatum with no multiple choice. At The Kannon Effect, Wolf was going to defeat Karina Wolfenden for the #1 contendership to the world title, knowing full well that the stipulation of the match meant that they could never wrestle again. Kwolf was going to win the championship that very night, after eliminating a departing Gideon Poe for the title. Gabriel Brown, better known to the world as Invincible Gabe had been given the title, thanks to Poe’s bum knee, and told to run with it. But thanks to a certain “backstage chemistry” so to speak and you can read between the lines there, between Deville and Wolfenden, she was given the championship. Imagine that. She was suckling the golden teat, knowing exactly how to get the things she wanted, and they were given to her. She buttered up to Ellie, and made her way to a friendship with Kannon. Quick aside: One day, I was sitting in my office, alone, when I heard Ellie and Karina talking. I’d assumed that Kari was the kind of chick that liked other chicks, but, that’s neither here nor there. Regardless, the two were talking, and I heard Karina almost turn into Ellie. She said, and I swear to Christ this is true: “Ohmigawd, those pants were like, sooooo expensive.” Seriously. Karina turned valley girl. I couldn’t help but laugh, stand up and look out into the hallway. The Kwolf was mortified, and flipped me a bird as I gave her a thumbs up and a wink. She never really liked me much. I didn’t care. Stupid bitch. Back to the story. Magnum Wolf simply became a puppet for Deville. Hell, most of the roster did as well. Remember when Storm lost the Mid South belt to Tessa? Totally Deville’s idea. Man, was that guy ever in awe. Storm may have professed to be "all man", but, I suspect that given the chance, he would have become a the woman Deville always wanted. I remember that conversation like it was yesterday. Storm came into Deville’s office as I was sitting there, and sat down. “Chris, what can I do for you?” I asked him. He sat down, and explained that he really felt that we weren’t using him well, and that he thought he would be better served in a higher division of the company. Deville thought about it for all of three seconds and agreed. I was in utter disbelief. “You’re exactly right, Chris. I do think you need a better role with RPW. I was just talking to Peter about this, and frankly, we’ve got a hell of an idea for you.” And that was the beauty of Angelo Deville. He could convince you to kill your mother, and make it seem like it was going to be the greatest thing in the world. Yet, he wouldn’t think twice about it. The way he spelled out this angle, it almost brought tears to Storm’s eyes. He was sold on it, before Deville even asked him if he liked the idea. It was pure shit, and yet, the way Deville told it, it was going to be the best thing going in Rush Pro Wrestling history. Storm took it, hook, line and sinker. Not many people respected Storm in the locker room, but, the people like Deville and I did, for one powerful reason: He’d tapped Tessa Windsors ass. I’m not talking about wrestling. I’m talking he banged the hell out of that chick, every which way possible. She looked at the rest of us like we were scumbags, and hell, we were. But, there was always a glint in her eye, whenever she heard the name Chris Storm. Some girls are like that. You could give them filet mignon, and they’re just happy to take the top round in front of them. Same was true with Tessa. She could have ridden the Maxwell Express to the top of the world and back, but, she chose to stick by a guy that lived, ate, slept, and breathed a fake sport. I wonder if she had to fake it too… Nevermind that. I don’t want to think about it. Really. I don’t. Shit. Too late. I’m going to wash my brains with gasoline and matches. ---- 24 March 2005 PART II – The Fall of Rush Pro Wrestling. Peter Maxwell Global Championship Wrestling. After the Kannon Effect, a pay per view named after our benefactor in name only, Xavier Kannon, RPW was riding a tremendous crest of popularity. Other companies were looking to RPW for a joint partnership, and a bit of talent sharing. The best offer came from the Squared Circle, a company born not unlike RPW. A few people from another company got upset with that company’s handling of things, and broke away, to form their own. They had much better names, bigger stars, and was much better run than we were. The owner and operator, Harvard Avalon was a tremendous guy, and it makes me sad to this day to think about how he suffered his last few months on this earth. Harvard died of cancer a few months back, and to be honest, if it were anyone else I would have dealt with for the merger of sorts, between tSC and RPW, it wouldn’t have happened. Avalon was more than happy to establish a sort of talent sharing network between the two companies that would allow his roster to come to RPW for selected shows, and RPW talent to work on Tuesday Night Wrestling, to gain a little bit of exposure for our product. In reality, we weren’t all that different. Both companies put an emphasis on the wrestling product, rather than the extra stuff you see in the WWE, and I think it helped both of us well. So, the first bit of talent we shipped over, was Jason O’Neil. Sure, you can look up and down a tSC card for a mention of him, but unless you knew where to look, you wouldn’t find it. His entrance into tSC was a simple plan by Kannon and Deville to take tSC over from the inside. They learn the inner workings of the company, and eventually, they come over to tSC, and do the same thing they did in RPW. They take it over by force. O’Neil had a brilliant idea for his character. His tag team he entered with, Los Primerios, would lose a whole bunch of matches, and in doing so, would attempt to devalue the team. Eventually, tSC’s roster would send him back to RPW, where they would go on a tremendous run, win the tag belts in RPW, and show that tSC had no clue how to handle talent. It was a great plan, with only one small flaw. O’Neil liked tSC. He liked the atmosphere, and it reminded him of what wrestling was supposed to be about. So, rather than simply sit and wait for the plan to unfold, he severed all ties with RPW, and went over to their side. I guess it was a better move, seeing as RPW didn’t last much longer after The Kannon Effect. ~~~~~******~~~~~ Magnum Wolf’s ego was starting to go unchecked. He knew that he was going to be the RPW Champion, and by default, he grew into an absolute monster. He started asking things from Deville and Kannon, and quite honestly, Kannon couldn’t care less. He was finally making money again, which was strange, since RPW wasn’t making a dime. At least, to the untrained eye. Sure, you could blame the contracts we still had to pay, or the horrible lease we negotiated in the Mason days, but, come on. The arena held a touch over nine thousand, one hundred fans a night. At ten dollars a pop, we’re looking at making ninety thousand dollars a week, just in gate prices alone. Add to that the nearly thirty thousand buys of our weekly pay per view show at six ninety-five a pop, and you can see that RPW was making cash like there was no tomorrow. So why then were people like Chris Storm, Steven Silver and Shawn Stewart not being paid? They were stupid. It’s as simple as that. When you’re playing to a packed house every night, and yet, you’re barely making enough to make ends meet, while Ellie and Karina Wolfenden are driving expensive cars, and our champion at the time had enough money to make sure the belt strap matched her outfits, we’ve got a problem. “Simple, Pete. When I took over RPW, I said I wanted X amount from you. I decided that since we’re making money, X should grow to Y,” Kannon replied. “It’s simple logic. I’m more valuable to RPW than anyone else.” “That’s bullshit! You’re just a character on TV.” I said to Kannon. He didn’t like that accusation, and said so by putting a fist to the side of my head. “You told Deville and I that we had free reign of this company. You said that we had control. You said whatever we wanted to do, we could. We took this place that was running with El Diablo as a main eventer, and turned it into a wrestling utopia.” Another fist connected to the side of my head, and I saw stars. Kannon was a lot stronger than people gave him credit for. Those punches hurt, quite frankly, and I took one more before I was down for the count. Remember Adrenaline 30? I didn’t seem like myself, and was a bit woozy? Yeah, thank Kannon for that. Later on, Deville met up with me, and it was the first time I feared for my life. He didn’t say much of anything; he didn’t have to. It’s tough to talk when you’ve got a pipe, and you’re hitting someone with it, repeatedly. I caught the jist of what he was saying to me though. It wasn’t a friendly conversation. It was more along the lines of “You are no longer needed in Rush Pro Wrestling, and you can take this beating as your dismissal. You’ll stay on TV until we decide to despose of you, and then, you’ll be lucky to get a job ever again.” Remember what I said earlier about failure, and how it seems that no matter what I do, I’m going to fail? This was the moment that it happened. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ For the most part, RPW fell apart around this time. Heading into Eve of Destruction, our fourth big pay per view, the entire roster was walking around on eggshells. There were rumblings of a mass exodus directly before the show by quite a few people. Fed up with not being paid, they were going to walk out the door, never to return. And we’re not talking about guys like Carys and Gabe. We’re talking Cisco Milloy, Scott Taylor, Chris Storm, Tessa Windsor, Magnum Wolf and Gideon Poe. Two of them actually did walk out, never to return again in Poe and Milloy. For the rest, Deville magically ponied up the cash and paid each of them for the show, to make sure it went on. By now, relations with the RPW “Management” and the talent were absolutely strained. Gone were the luxuries of having food at events, or even hot water in the showers. Deville was skimming any and all cash from the top of RPW, and even had his guys break into the ticket offices, steal a shitload of tickets to scalp outside the arena, and quadruple prices. It was surreal. Just a stones throw from Washington, D.C., and Deville was playing the Devil’s Advocate. He already planned to blame the student assistants for the breakins, and even went as far as to cut in an entire fraternity in on the deal. They’d get beer money and whores for their parties in exchange for security detail for the shows. These guys were absolute buffoons. It wasn’t uncommon for fans to be simply walking around backstage while these drunken assholes were in the storage rooms drinking the Halton Arena out of beer. Most of us wrestlers took refuge in a small, out of the way storage room that was set aside for basketball equipment. It was terrible. There were twenty five of us, packed into this small room, forced to change, dress and shower underneath a hose. It was like PWC all over again. So, Eve of Destruction came and went, and to no one’s surprise, Magnum Wolf walked off with the World Title. The roster groaned, pissed that we had this absolute pile of shit holding our biggest belt. Shit, even Chris Storm, who was normally Deville’s biggest advocate had questions about the entire thing, knowing Wolf was the laziest member of the roster. “We’re doomed.” Became our rallying cry. We knew we needed to get out of RPW, but it was tough. We had to come up with a solution and quick, or else this thing was going to get too big for us to even escape from. Deville had been in negotiations with a promoter by the name of Ronald P. Emery, a shady fellow most people from the old WO collective knew as Ron Starr’s right hand man. Emery had the personallity of an ambulance chasing lawyer, without any sort of redeeming qualities. He would tell one person one story, and turn right around and tell a completely differing version to another. You’d think that he, and he alone was responsible for the entirety of WO’s success, forgetting for a moment that there was quite a bit of great talent there. No, Emery had decided that because he negotiated PCW’s touring contract and arena dates that he was infact the head honcho, and his over inflated ego did absolutely nothing for the RPW morale. Deville and Emery negotiated a travel schedule for RPW, for the first time ever. We were going to criss cross the nation in such a way that would have left Christopher Columbus dizzy. The first trip, set to leave in a month saw us going from Charlotte to Los Angeles, to Chicago, back to Charlotte, up to Augusta Maine, down to Miami, up to Seattle, down to Phoenix, back to Charlotte, and ending up in Dallas. In the matter of two weeks. That’s right. Nearly sixteen thousand miles of travel in less than thirteen days. And to top it off, Deville was going to pay each person two hundred dollars a week extra, to make their own travel arrangements. He explained that each person had the right to do so, and that he wasn’t about to guide their hands to making sure that they made it to each arena. “Let it be said that if you don’t show up, and if you can’t make that two hundred dollars work for you, then we’ll have no problem hiring another group of talent, and you can find yourself a new company to work for…once your non-compete clause is up, of course.” That was Deville’s way of hanging something over our heads. When he “took over” he forced the roster to sign a non-compete clause. They could participate in any other companies events, so long as they were part of the RPW roster. If they quit or got fired, they weren’t able to wrestle in the US or Canada for one year. The agreement was so poorly worded, that there was no doubt if it were challenged in court, it wouldn’t be binding, but, it was more an agreement with Deville himself. If he found out you were going to wrestle outside of RPW, and you’d quit, he’d simply pay you a visit, break a few bones, pop a few ligaments, and crush a few ribs. That way, once your rehab was over, you’d hit a year. Diabolical, isn’t he? We’d all been fed up, and fortunately, I had an ace in my sleeve that would make sure that RPW wouldn’t survive much longer. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ I’ve said in the past that I have a penchant for failure. More often than not, those failures are my own doing, and I provide myself an out for anything I get myself into. When the contracts with Mason were drawn up, before Deville and before Kannon’s involvement, I made sure, under no certain terms, that I was the only one authorized to make any sale of the company’s name, liscensing rights, and held all rights to all things RPW. I wanted to make sure that Deville would fail, and that Kannon could not simply sit around and collect a paycheck any longer. I wanted to bring someone into the mix that wouldn’t be pushed around by a guy like Deville. So, I talked to O’Neil and set myself up a meeting with Harvard Avalon. He’d just revealed on television that he was a sick man, and that he would no longer be continuing the day to day operations of tSC. It was a sad day for all of those that knew him, and in my dealings previously, to set up the partnership with them, I knew that he always had an eye for the company. We talked and a few hours later, documents were being drawn up. I sold RPW to tSC, Inc, for one hundred and fifty thousand dollars, which was used to pay off our lease, and the rest of our talent contracts. Deville was livid. He accused me of taking the company out from under him, and that I was going to pay. He attempted to find some sort of loophole, but couldn’t. He wanted the company to himself, and was ready to take it from Avalon. The two never met, as Harvard checked himself into a hospital just a few days after the sale. His wife, Rosemary came over to assume control, and as soon as she arrived, things changed. Deville was the first to greet her. “You stupid bitch. This is my compan-“ Deville started before Rosemary waved a hand in her face. “This was your company, Mister Deville. This is now my company, and as such, you are no longer a necesarry part of it.” “Are you firing me? I don’t take kindly to being removed from my company.” “Again, mister Deville, this is my company now, and you’ve been removed from your ‘Position of Power’ as you liked to call it. You are, however, more than welcome to wrestle at the normal wrestler’s salary, if you’d like.” Deville growled in her face, clearly not intimidated by her smugness matched only by his own. He’d played the smug game before, and knew it’s weaknesses. He knew how to exploit it. That’s what he did. Exploit weaknesses. “It’d be a shame for something to happen to your cancer stricken husband, you know…” Deville said, smiling. Rosemary looked him in the eye, first shocked, but then a smile crept across her lips as well. “We’ve prepared a generous severance package for you, Mister Deville. Hopefully, you’ll learn when to quit. Of course, if you’re not a fan of money, we could always pull that off the table, and just allow the government into the books, hm?” Deville’s smug grin went from a look of rage, to one of complacency. “No need for that. I understand when I’m not needed. Have your people send me the papers.” Deville took a final look at Rosemary, and then myself. He made a slashing motion across his throat, and then pointed to me. Yeah. Welcome to 1965, Deville. Today, we actually kill people rather than make stupid threatening taunts at them. ~~~~~*****~~~~~ As far as Kannon, he negotiated a contract with tSC just before the sale, wrestled there for about three weeks, and decided the game had passed him by. He still makes the occasional “I need a few dollars for gas money” appearances, but for the most part, he’s left the game entirely. He was in attendance at RPW’s final show, and he and I sat in a skybox together, watching as Magnum Wolf lost the RPW Championship to Chris Storm*. “It was a long ride, wasn’t it?” I said to Kannon, who simply nodded, stood, and walked away. A week later, RPW was closed, and some of the talent went to tSC. Others simply scattered around the globe, looking for better contracts, and better piece of mind. Karina Wolfenden ended up in tSC, but left shortly after, when the place wouldn’t cater to her every whim. Imagine that. The spoiled little rich girl couldn’t get her way, and she left in a huff, crying about her experience. Chris Storm moved onto the UWF, and retired. And then unretired. And then retired. And then retired again, just for good measure. Then, he unretired, retired, and unretired in the matter of an hour. He’s currently unretired, but plans to retire soon. Tessa Windsor and I finally hooked up, and I can proudly say that I did take her to the top of the world on the Maxwell Express. Well, if by hooked up, you mean said goodbye when she left the company without punching me in the face for saying she had a great pair of tits.
