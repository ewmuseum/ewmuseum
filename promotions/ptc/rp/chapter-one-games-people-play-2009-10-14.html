Chapter One: Games People Play

14 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-14 “Hush, little baby, don’t say a word, daddy’s gonna buy you a mockingbird…” The voice is harsh and scratchy, the sound of someone who’s spent their life gargling with whiskey and razor blades. It turns a familiar nursery rhyme into a tuneless dirge, the notes of the song staggering around as if drunk. The air is stale and heavy with cigarette smoke. In the corner of the table stands a pitted and chipped ceramic ashtray, overflowing with cigarette butts. “And if that little bird won’t peck, daddy’s gonna snap its fucking neck…” The voice giggles, amused at what it sees as its little joke. The noise is harsh and ugly, less a laugh than a screech. Spread over the plastic surface of the table the man is staring at is a series of photos, the pictures taken over a very long period of time. The voice has had to be very, very patient. But the voice understood this. Everything had to be arranged very, very carefully. So much depended on that. “You see, like every game, there are rules that have to be followed. You can’t play the game unless you know the rules.” The images on the photos are varied but three figures recur in all of them. The first is a man, standing tall and proud in the middle of a wrestling ring, his body a seeming wall of solid muscle. His eyes seem to burn and the look on his face is one of pride and defiance. “We start with the most important rule of all. The one without which nothing else makes sense.” The second is a woman, standing tall and athletic in a t-shirt and jeans. Her long, dark hair is frozen in mid-bounce as she walks along the sidewalk, her body language speaking of poise, power and self-assurance. “And that rule is this: No one is safe. Ever.” The third is a child, his face a mix of the man and the woman’s. He dangles from a set of monkey bars in what looks to be a playground. His clothes are slightly scuffed, marked with the stains typical of the young and the active. He seems to almost glow with energy and his smile is bright and wide. The voice lingers upon this last photograph. Yes, it thinks, this will do nicely to start with. It picks the photo up and brings it to his nose. “The itsy bitsy spider went up the water spout…” It takes a deep breath, the scent of chemicals and photographic fluid and…something else…filling its nostrils. The voice smiles as is does this, pleased at the thought of what is about to happen. “Down came the rain and washed the spider out…” It moved the photograph down its face, pausing the image at its mouth. Its tongue traced a slow line along the child’s form, as if savoring the taste. Once done, the voice smiled once again. “Out came the sun and dried up all the rain…” Yes, the voice thought, this would do very nicely indeed. “But the itsy bitsy spider never got up again…” It had taken the majority of SCCW’s security team, led by Alan Branch, to restrain Lance Marshall enough to allow the paramedics to do their job. Not that it had been easy, mind you. Lance had thrashed and bucked the entire way back to the trainer’s room, unleashing a rather creative barrage of obscenities as he did so. Branch actually allowed himself a small smile as he thought on how Lance actually did manage to meet one of the stereotypes he’d heard about the army: the ability to swear for ten minutes straight without repeating a single insult. Branch was broken out of his reverie by the paramedic standing next to him. She was a short, mildly attractive African-American woman who was filling up a syringe with something or other. She had mentioned her name, Branch remembered, but he couldn’t recall it. Something beginning with J. “I can take a guess as to what’s happened, but what can you tell me about what went down?” she barked. “He and another guy went through a war tonight. I’m damn surprised one of them isn’t dead,” Branch answered. “Plus, I think Lance got maced.” “Doubt it,” the paramedic answered. “Real Mace is hard as hell to come by these days. He probably got a blast of Oleoresin Capsicum.” “English, please?” “Pepper spray,” said the paramedic as she quickly injected Lance with whatever was in the syringe. “Don’t worry, baby, it’s nothing bad. Just a little something to take the edge off.” “LET ME GO!” “Boy’s got a temper on him,” cracked the paramedic, removing a small flashlight from her pocket. She shined it in both of Lance’s eyes, forcibly holding them open as she did so. “Yup, it’s looking like pepper spray. His eyes will probably be fine in about twenty minutes or so. The sedative I gave him will kick in long before that, though.” “Sedative?” “Just a little something to make him go night-night. Little trick you learn on this job, get the big ones nice and calm before you try anything. He’s squirming enough as it is, I don’t know what would happen now if I tried using the baby shampoo.” “Um,” said Alan Branch, a look of confusion crossing his face, “you’re going to have to explain that one.” “Little trick for dealing with pepper spray.” “I thought you were supposed to use water.” “Pepper spray doesn’t come out with water. Baby shampoo’s the best thing we’ve found, so far.” Lance’s breathing was now starting to slow and become more regular. While still resisting the efforts of the security team, he was doing so with considerably less energy. “Let’s get him onto the stretcher, boys,” the paramedic barked at the security team. As the team manhandled Lance into the stretcher, the paramedic gave Alan Branch one last look. “Be easy on him, okay?” Branch asked. “He’s been through a lot tonight.” “Hey, baby, long as he doesn’t start anything with me, we’re cool.” As he watched the ambulance drive away, Alan Branch noted that he had seen neither hide nor hair of Lane Stevens since the end of the match. For all he knew, Lane could be lying in an alley somewhere, bleeding to death. Nah, thought Alan. G-d wasn’t nearly that nice. The next thing Lance remembered was the feeling of air conditioning hitting his face as he slowly regained consciousness. The hospital room was lit only with the phosphorescent glow of the interior lights, the outside view long since gone dark. To his left was one of the doctors, a brusquely efficient man that Lance had dealt with several times before. To his right was Alanna. Lance was only just aware of her presence but, even in his fogged out state, he could tell she had been crying. “Welcome back, Mr. Marshall,” quipped the doctor. “We’re thinking of offering you our frequent customer card.” Lance felt that he might have actually laughed at that had every part of his body not been screaming in pain. “You’re a lucky man, Mister Marshall,” continued the doctor. “I’m sure I’ve told you this before but you have the constitution of a rhino. Your entire body is one large bruise and you have more sprains and tears than I would have imagined possible. But there is no permanent injury. We’ll keep an eye on you this evening and, barring any complications, we should be able to send you home tomorrow.” “Thank you, Doctor,” sniffled Alanna, barely keeping the tears at bay. “Yes,” croaked Lance. “Thanks.” “I’ll be by in the morning to check on you. In the meantime, get some rest.” Lance watched groggily as the doctor left the room, desperately trying to keep his vision clear. Whatever drugs they had given him, he was having a hell of a time staying awake. “I asked them not to give you percoset or oxycontin,” said Alanna. Good, thought Lance. After his previous substance issues, it was wise to avoid any form of temptation. There was a moment’s silence before Alanna began to cry again, tears filling her eyes. “I’m so sorry, baby. I didn’t mean for any of this to happen.” “It’s…okay.” No, it’s not. I can’t believe you gave into that son of a bitch. “It’s just that he threatened Zach,” Alanna said, “and after everything he’d done, I was so scared…” “It’s okay,” Lance rasped. “It’ll be fine. Just head on home, let Zach know that I’m okay and I’ll be home tomorrow.” “Are you sure?” “Yeah. I’m really tired and I wouldn’t be much company right now. I just want to…I just want to sleep right now.” “Okay. But I’ll be here as soon as I can tomorrow morning. I love you.” “Love you too.” On the ride home, neither Lance nor Alanna spoke a word to each other. Lance Marshall could feel the afternoon sun beating down upon his skin as he floated in the pool that dominated his backyard, could feel the heat roasting his flesh the way an oven would a chicken. It was his own fault for being in this position. The doctor had warned him not to do anything strenuous or stressful for at least the next few days. Puttering around the house was out, though, as that would drive him absolutely crazy. Plus, it would involve having to talk to his wife. And right now, that was the last thing he wanted to do. Logically, he knew it wasn’t her fault. He believed her when she said she had been threatened by Lane Stevens, that Stevens had threatened the safety of their child. After everything he had done to them already, falsifying Alanna’s medical records to make it appear as though her cancer had gone into remission…it was hardly a leap to imagine that Stevens would threaten their son. He just didn’t want to address that right now. Right now, all he wanted to do was let his cares and his worries float away with the water. Not worry about psychotic redheads or a sociopath who treated the act of trying to destroy someone’s family as one colossal, demented joke. He had warned Stevens from the very beginning. He had let Stevens now that he would not allow Stevens to threaten his family, that he would not stand for Stevens making the people he loved his targets. And still, Stevens had pushed and pushed and pushed…until Lance had snapped, doing his damndest to kill Stevens in the middle of the ring. He needed his wife and child more than anything else in the world, Lance knew this. They were the things he knew he could rely on, they were the lifeline he clung to to keep from drowning in the rage and hate he could feel bubbling up inside him. But he just felt so damn broken right now…and he couldn’t inflict that upon them. It wouldn’t be right. “Zachary Marshall! You come in here this instant!” Zach knew that tone. He’d heard his mom use it a lot only she tended to add his middle name Steven to it. That’s when Zach knew he was in trouble because Mom only used Steven when she was mad. So, hearing Mrs. Konover use it, Zach knew he would probably be in trouble by the end of the day. He didn’t mean to do anything wrong. He’d known Recess had just ended and he would be heading back into class in a few minutes. But he was also just a few minutes from crossing the monkey bars three times in a row without stopping, something Greg Pilkington had sworn was impossible. And Greg was gonna pay him five whole dollars! “Zachary!” “I’m almost done, Mrs. Konover. I’ll be inside in a few minutes.” “You have five minutes Zachary, no more. If I don’t see you inside after that, you’ll have to stay after school.” Zach sighed. Just two more bars to go. But he didn’t want to get into trouble… “Coming, Mrs. Knover,” Zach replied at his teacher’s retreating form. Zach would never remember clearly what happened next. He’d have odd flashes, certainly, vaguely recalled sensation. Someone’s hand wrapping around his head. The smell of something strange filling his nose. And then…nothing. Which was exactly what Mrs. Konover found when she went out to get Zach five minutes later. Alanna sighed as she stared out at her husband in the pool. She knew there were things they needed to discuss, that needed to be brought out into the open and dealt with. She also knew that Lance wasn’t ready to deal with those things just yet and that he would be…eventually. She just wished she had an idea of how long it was going to take this time. At least the appointment with her new oncologist had gone well. After what happened with her old one (and she remained grateful that he hadn’t decided to press charges), the information her new doctor had given her had come as a blessed relief. Despite his best efforts, Lane Stevens had not done much to derail her course of treatment. The chemo could still continue as scheduled and it looked like they would be able to schedule her in for surgery in a few weeks. Alanna laughed slightly as she walked across the room to answer the now ringing phone. If someone had told her that she’d ever reach a point where she’d be thinking of invasive surgery as a step up from her usual…her train of thought was quickly derailed by the rapid stream of words coming from the other end of the phone. She could just about recognize Zach’s teacher, Mrs. Konover, but none of what she was saying was making any sense. “Wait…Mrs. Konover, slow down, I can’t understand a words you’re saying. What’s happened?” “What?! Are you sure? “ “Well, have you called the police?!” “Yes, yes, of course, we’ll be down there right away.” Alanna hung up the phone, no longer caring what Mrs. Knover was saying. Oh my G-d, no, this can’t be happening, now now… “LANCE!” It was the panic in his wife’s voice that Lance Marshall noticed first. That unmistakable edge to her voice that let him know that, whatever had just happened, it was bad. It snapped him out of his reverie as he swam towards the edge of the pool with powerful strokes. Lifting himself out, he hurried over to his wife’s side. She was shaking violently, her eyes brimming over with tears. She was barely able to hold the phone in her hands and was looking at it as though it would bite her at any minute. “Baby, what is it? What’s wrong? What happened?” It took a few moments before Alanna Marshall was capable of speech. But what she said froze Lance Marshall to the bone. “It’s Zach. Someone’s taken him.” All Zach knew for sure was that he wasn’t on the floor. He’d slept on the floor before, in sleeping bags at friends houses. He knew what that felt like, hard and a little weird no matter how hard you tried to get comfortable. This didn’t feel like that. He couldn’t tell any more than that because the room around him was pitch black, so dark that he couldn’t even see his hand in front of his face. Zach didn’t know where he was or how long he’d been there but it felt like he’d been asleep forever. Did anyone even know he was gone? How would they know where to find him? “I’m not scared of you!” Just the darkness. The sound of his own breathing. And someone…singing. “Hush, little baby, don’t say a word…” Mommy. Daddy. Find me. Please. “I’m not scared…”
