"Only Time Will Tell"

7 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Jacob McKail GCW. 9:00am. Friday 5th May 2006. Long Island, New York. Some Cheap Motel. McKail opened his motel room door and tentatively took a step outside, absently raising his right hand to shield his eyes from the sun. It was deceptively warm outside, the cutting spring breeze lowering the temperature more than he expected it too and so allowed a shiver to momentarily wash over him. Ignoring this, he quickly closed the door behind him and made damned sure it was locked. The motel was located within a particularly bad neighbourhood and he’d heard raised voices both outside and in the next room along for the majority of the night. Didn’t bother him too much, like most nights he’d only been able to get a couple of hours sleep anyhow and then it’d consisted mostly of nightmares about his dead wife and the promises that he made to her on her deathbed. Don't ask. McKail took an instinctive cautionary glance around, then set off toward the roadside diner just a few hundred yards opposite his motel room to get breakfast. Walking out on to the motel car park, he quickly came to realise that his thin yellow t-shirt was no match for the afore mentioned sharp wind, so he crammed his hands deep within the pockets of his light blue jeans and continued regardless. Then, his attention was drawn by a man and woman sat in a car at the far end of the car park, exchanging words, animated, their hand gestures clearly aggressive. McKail shook his head and smiled, amusing himself by allowing a number of assumptions to enter his mind regarding what their conversation could be about, choosing the most entertaining scenario his imagination could conjure. He decided they were a couple and she’d just found out that he’d been cheating on her. By the looks of their car and clothes, McKail decided that they weren’t a wealthy couple, so perhaps they spent the previous night at the motel as some kind of cheap second honeymoon? Somewhere along the lines, she’d found out that he’d slept with another woman behind her back, perhaps a best friend or sister, and now she wasn’t about to let him off the hook for it. McKail chuckled to himself, slightly amused by his delusions, and pushed open the doors to the diner, taking the booth right next to the entrance. That was the thing about being a partial insomniac, your brain has a lot more time to think and to entertain itself the best it can. McKail hadn’t had a good night sleep for the better part of three weeks, ever since he left his apartment for good to go out on the road and pursue his dreams as a more successful pro wrestler as he promised his wife he would. So, his brain has had a lot of time to fill itself with other meaningless assumptions, merely to entertain itself and presumably to prevent McKail from going mad - the last thing he needed on his hands right now was damned Fight Club scenario. The plump waitress waddled over with a jug of coffee and took McKail’s order of bacon and eggs. While waiting for his order to arrive, he absently glanced out through the window overlooking the motel car park to see the same couple making their way over to the diner, still arguing and very definitely still gesturing wildly. “...how many times do I have to tell you?” The man muttered, entering the diner first. “I didn’t touch her!” “You can tell me as many time as you like,” his girlfriend or wife told him, sternly. “I won’t believe you.” McKail held back a smile, contented - and somewhat disturbed - that his mind was able to fabricate what appeared to be the correct circumstance from what little information it had to digest. McKail understood well what the boyfriend or husband was only just beginning to fathom a fundamental truth - no matter what you’ve done in your past, one day, it will come back to haunt you. McKail’s already had and he‘d been dealing with it for quite some time. *** 21:00pm. 23rd October 2005. Mobile, Alamba. An undisclosed, and perhaps forcibly forgotten, wrestling arena. The clown costume rubbed against his skin, irritating him beyond measure, but he endured the minor inconvenience and, more importantly, he endured the embarrassment of having to dress up in a damned clown outfit in the first place. He felt more than a little stupid, but ultimately this was his idea. His gimmick that he created in a pitiful attempt to get over with the crowd. The match ahead didn’t much matter to him, win or lose, he was just thinking about how the crowd will react to his new in a very long line of personas. Truth be told, he couldn’t do much in the ring in his current get up anyhow, his freakishly large clown shoes didn’t offer him much in terms of manoeuvrability nor agility. Waiting to step out onto the entrance platform and for his entrance music to hit , he absorbed the crowd’s reaction to his opponent as he slid under the bottom ropes and posed for the audiences overrated adulation. They loved him. McKail seriously had to consider whether they would love him just as much, but he already knew the answer to that. He was beginning to get nervous and sweat had already formulated primarily on his forehead, causing the white chalk-based paint that covered his face had begun to smudge and crack. He would know within a matter of seconds if his career would rise or fall, he would within a matter of seconds if he would succeed or fail. It all came down to the approval of the crowd. *** Every time that one snapshot in many is regurgitated in my head and I remember just how much I wanted the approval of that damned crowd, how much I wanted to succeed to within that undisclosed wrestling organisation without putting in the in-ring performance, I cringe, completely disgusted with myself. It may sound as though I’m drivelling on here, feeling sorry for myself verbally, but it isn‘t like that. I realise what’s done is done and I’m not about to apologise to anyone for whatever I’ve done in the past, it’s time look beyond all of that and concentrate more on my future. I’ve already put this process in motion, I’m already attempting to move on. It’s just a damned shame that my wife, Mary, had to die before I could truly realise it. She made me promise, mere moments before her death, to change my life around, not to compete all the other morons on the roster for the adulation of the crowd. She made me promise to pick up my in-ring game and take it to the next level, to gain success fighting with everything I have in order to succeed in this industry. Since that day, I have not looked back, only forward, to what I can - what I am going - to accomplish. Progressive steps have already been made and this new philosophy has already bore fruit for me. I joined Global Championship Wrestling last month and I’ve had two matches. The first one was essentially a newcomer battle royal - word from the locker room suggests that GCW had somewhat of a roster reshuffle just before I was hired and I wasn’t the only new guy to join the ranks. I won the battle royal, beating six other guys, with minimal effort. For the first time, my eyes were opened. For the first time I truly understood just what I could achieve if I continued to travel along this path. It might very well be more difficult than to simply to be nothing more than a damned jester, entertaining the crowd with the singular goal of getting more air time, but ultimately this road is - will be - more rewarding. Don’t ask me how I know, I just have this strange sensation, deep down, this to be true and my second match within the GCW truly vindicated this gut feeling. I was placed within a three way dance for the GCW Television title, against current champion Jayce Bradley and well-established GCW superstar Virgil Ambrosi. Presumably, it was my reward for demolishing all of my opponents within the newcomer battle royal. Either way, I entered the match confident of my abilities, confident despite being the clear underdog in the match. That’s precisely why I succeeded and that’s precisely why I am now the GCW Television champion. Now another opportunity has presented itself to me. The GCW admin staff sent me an e-mail regarding something called the Infinite Gauntlet, which already comprises a number of the top wrestling superstars from all over the world. The Primetime Community will be hosting such a - I want to use the word tournament, but that’s a poor choice of words here. Challenge. That's it. They will be hosting this challenge and have made it open for anybody within the wrestling industry to compete. Now that I’ve decided to compete within round eighteen of the tournament, I wonder just how far I can go. I wonder just how much I can accomplish. Only time will tell. *** 9:20am. Friday 5th May 2006. Long Island, New York. Roadside Diner. McKail’s empty plate and equally empty cup littered the table in front of him and now all he was waiting for now was the check. The waitress was next to useless. She’d only come back to fill up his coffee cup on two occasions and now she was taking her sweet time coming back with the bill. There won't much of a tip for her, McKail had already decided. Then, his attention was effectively grabbed by the sound of playful giggling and sympathetic apologising and for the longest time thought he somehow recognised the voices. McKail glanced over his shoulder and saw the same couple as earlier on as they were about to leave the diner. Now they looked happy. Now they looked content. It’s amazing just how much time and a few right words can change a person’s perspective on things. It seemed as the husband or boyfriend had now been forgive or vindicated for whatever act the girlfriend or wife had accused him of. How this miracle occurred was something else entirely, something that McKail didn’t particularly want to throw guesses at. He just knew that it had happened and that was good enough for him. Perhaps in the future, time will heal all of his past indiscretions, or at least help him to forget them. Mary had already supplied him with all of the right words that he needed to hear in order to overcome his cringe-worthy history. Only time will tell. End
