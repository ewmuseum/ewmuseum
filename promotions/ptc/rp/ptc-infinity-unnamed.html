Infinity

29 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The wick caught light as flames began to flicker to life along its length, clinging to the single filament, that was literally their sole lifeline, with small fingers of red, yellow and orange. A small wisp of smoke faded quickly into oblivion from the tip of the flames as the candle firmly took light, the solid wax below the fire starting to sag as the heat began to melt it. A small breeze, little more than a breath of air, caused the flames to leap and jump as if dancing to an unheard melody. Reaching out, as if hands straining to caress a lover’s skin, the flames embraced everything, surrounding and enfolding … smothering as we fell into the fire. Deep into the flames we travelled, the colours becoming all as we became immersed in primordial fire and the World around us vanished as the tiny finger of flame on top of the candle became our all. Light surrounded us, so bright that that it became the antithesis of itself and rather than enabling us to see it blinded our eyes. Light into darkness. Darkness out of light. The illumination of a million candles shone forth as we journeyed ever deeper, ever further, and then suddenly the flames released us and like a foetus newly born we were expelled from the warmth, the safety, that we had grown accustomed to. Darkness and cold surrounded us as we were thrust forth and left floating in a sea of despair, empty, inconsolable. Unlike birth though we didn’t find ourselves held high, blood coated and mewling, screaming our first breath. Instead our eyes took in the expanse of stars that surrounded us, uncountable in their multitude. The majesty of the Heaven’s surrounded us and we gazed in awe as the infinite Universe spread out before us. The corona of the Sun from which we were born fondled our face in a gentle farewell as we hurtled through time and space, unknowing, uncaring. Planets hurtled by in the blink of an eye as, like a comet on an endless journey, we sped throughout the cosmos leaving no trail. Millions and millions of stars marked our passing with no blink of their all seeing, all knowing eyes and still we continued as if there was … as if there could be … no end in sight. A feeling of peace descended as a small planet, dark and secluded, went passed, or we passed it. As much as home is meant to be where the heart is the simple vista of a familiar planet in the foreign and alien landscape of the endless darkness through which we flew filled us with a feeling of contentedness. Pluto zoomed past us, forever shrouded in darkness, Charon … it’s ever faithful companion … it’s only company. Neptune and Uranus followed by in rote and then for the briefest of moments we danced upon the rings of Saturn before spiralling down past Jupiter. Once more an unbearable lightness filled us as we caught glimpse of the Sun ... our mother and father combined … ahead and tears of longing and joy coursed down our faces. Mars and Earth, therefore, passed in a haze of rainbow coloured tears as Venus sent out waves of love to help us on our way. With a final farewell Mercury shone it’s beacon and guided us … as if we needed it … towards the Sun and we found ourselves slipping back into the familiar and comfortable heat of it’s corona as if we had never left. Full circle, we fell back into the light that was so bright as to blind us, the light that turned our World dark, and it was then that we knew that all we had to do was reach out, all we had to do was open our eyes and we would know … “… I said can I help you my son?” The priest reached out a hand towards the shoulder of the figure that stood staring at the cabinet on which lay dozens of candles. The weather outside, cold rain that was so atypical for the time of year, had kept all of his flock away. The soul may be willing but the body was definitely weak thought the priest as his hand hovered inches away from the figure … the man’s … back. All but this man that is he thought to himself again. I felt the fingers reaching out to touch me even though they were still inches away from my body and it was that closeness that brought me back to myself. I wrenched my gaze away from the small flame that had seemingly captivated my attention and spun around in place with grace that would have made Nureyev envious, grasping the wrist of the man who had … somehow … got so close to me without my awareness. Three things came to the forefront of my realisation at the same time. The sound of the man … old with a mop of thinning white hair on top of a thin face … gasping in pain as my hand closed, vice-like, around his wrist … the sensation of holding an arm that was thin, too thin, in my grasp and knowing that it would take no thought at all to break it … and the expression of fear that played across the priest’s face as he stared into my own. … or at least the mask that covered me, always. A wry chuckle escaped my mouth before I could stifle it and I released the priest’s arm from my grasp and held both of my arms outstretched as if to show that I was safe. The fact that a near six foot man, clothed from head to foot in black, whose face was covered with leather that moved as if it was a second skin was standing in a church in a pose that resembled a mockery of an angel with wings outspread never crossed my mind. It obviously crossed that of the priest though, who stumbled backwards as he crossed himself and mouthed unheard words, a babble of prayer. “You have nothing to fear from me old man, neither your body nor your soul” I said as gently as possible as I stepped towards him, holding my hands out to him in supplication. “Oh Lord deliver me from evil, protect your servant from the scion of Hell and deliver me …” the priest muttered, stumbling backwards until his leg caught on the edge of a pew. His balance lost he began to tumble backwards, his head falling towards the hard and unforgiving concrete of the floor below, as his prayer cut off. The seven-foot gap between us was unto nothing as I leapt towards the priest and caught his body before it could hit the floor. Holding him against my chest, child like, I stared down at him and smiled, my lips barely moving … only the edges turning up … underneath the three-quarter mask that covered my face. “They do say that God works in mysterious ways don’t they Father?” I quipped as I raised the man back to his feet, holding onto him with one hand as I felt his whole body tremble under my touch. “Are you ok?” I asked as gently as I could, seeing my leather covered face reflected in the wide pupils of the older man in front of me. “Wh … what are you?” he replied, his voice barely a croak that escaped through fear dried lips. “What … don’t you mean who?” I laughed. I think that it was the laughter that finally broke the fear and tension in the priest, and I had to move quickly again to help guide his body towards the pew as his knees buckled. Helping him to a seated position I took a step back and hunkered down, hoping that the space between us and the fact that I was no longer towering over him would calm his nerves. “Can I get you anything … water perhaps?” I asked solicitously. “You are mortal aren’t you … not a demon sent to take me?” he stammered. I laughed again and then cut it off quickly as the echoes of the church picked up the sound and made it reverberate throughout the stonewalls manically. The priest’s eyes widened in renewed fear as I tried to console him. “Yes I am … and no I am not. You don’t have to worry about me taking you anywhere, I am not after your soul” the words came out as softly as I could make them and still I saw the priest shiver at the sound. “Why are you here then, why are you wearing … what is that covering your face?” he asked as if still not sure that I wasn’t what he feared. Moving slowly, as if trying to approach a frightened rabbit, I approached the priest and took his hand in my own. Gently I pulled his hand towards my face and watched the realisation sink in as the texture of the leather mask reached his brain. He peered closer at the seams of the mask and his expression relaxed … slightly … as he finally realised that it was indeed not my face. “ ... I am sorry my son, I didn’t mean to offend you but your appearance took me by surprise.” He said quietly, contritely, his hand still resting on my face. “I am not your son, but apology accepted” I replied with another small smile. “We are all children in the eyes of God my child, and in His house all are worthy of being forgiven” he began, an intent look in his eyes, “… are you looking for forgiveness?” Reaching up I took his hand again in mine and pulled it from my face, once more realising just how easy it would be to snap the bones in his hands like dry twigs. Standing up I backed away from him. “Ah … but I am not His son either Father, and no I have nothing that I need forgiven for thank you.” I said resolutely, not realising that with my back to the flames I had silhouetted myself with dancing shadows, framing myself with fire. “We are all God’s children my child …” he started. “I don’t believe in your God Father, and I don’t need a sermon” I interjected. The priest got to his feet, still looking a little shaky but the topic of conversation seemed to have filled him with strength. Walking towards me he reached out both hands and I … for a reason that I couldn’t even fathom myself … I allowed him to take my own in a strong grip. “He doesn’t need your belief my child, he believes in you” the priest stated, faith and belief in his words evident in the passion with which he spoke them, “ … and if you don’t believe then why are you here in His house?” A grin, large and true, filled my face as I heard his question. Taking my hands away from his own I turned around and pointed towards the rows of candles that illuminated the church, candles that until a few moments earlier had been dark and cold. “Ah that is simple old man, I may not believe in Him or his ways but I know that these candles are for the lost souls that need protecting, or those that will pass over soon enough … isn’t that right?” I asked him. “Yes … yes that is right” he replied, evidently confused. “Well you see that is why I am here, I am trying to give some people a chance, I am trying to give some people a hint of protection that they would otherwise not have. For each and every man that will soon cross paths with me on the journey into the infinite unknown I have lit a candle” I stated quietly. “Protection … protection from what?” he asked, fear once more evident in his voice. Without turning around I stared back into the flames of the candles, feeling myself falling back into their loving embrace. The fire that consumes, the flames that devour, the heat that rages and the embers that never … ever … die. As I lost myself once more in their infinity I heard a voice speak as if from thousands of miles away. My own voice. “From me”
