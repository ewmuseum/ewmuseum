Rite of Passage Saga: I Hunger For Heresy

1 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-05-01 Ethan Knight stared into the maddening eyes of Great Cthulhu. The colossal god's mouth-tentacles stretched hungrily in his direction,a mass of writhing proboscides sourced of a malformed head. The youth's eyes, marked with a subdued awe, casually deviated from the head to survey the monstrosity's hulking, hunched over frame and the vestigial wings set upon the scaly back. A beast borne of nightmare, and one he called master. He and his ilk had been deemed as madness incarnate, and few could dispute such a fitting label. Master of sunken R'lyeh, immortal high priest of the Outer Gods, and prophecized destroyer of humanity. It was his favorite statue on the manor grounds. The moon bathed the contemplative apostle of the Sect of Black Wisdom in a ray of marginal light. A 'garden' of vines and ivy cloaked much of the inner courtyard's walls and walkways, its influence expanding almost daily and threatening to swallow the entire area in a sea of green. The foundations of the various statues and monuments constructed around the cloister were encircled in their coils, though strangely the growth always seemed to halt only inches above the ground. What light the moon failed to offer this warm Spring night was made up for by way of incandescent lamps positioned about the yard atop sleek black poles. A collection of shadowy figures, other disciples of the dark faith, ambled about the expansive grounds around Wyatt Manor as they readied for the evening rites. Most were relaxed, speaking in low tones and many even emitting twisted grins as they passed along the footpaths. To the average onlooker it was a sad situation. Evil had so warped the minds of Shanahan's faithful that even their smiles were tainted. And many were so indoctrinated they were virtually the walking dead, reduced to hideous caricatures of human beings. Ethan Knight, however, wasn't in light spirits. Though a part of him was proud of his recent deeds, another lamented what he had become. Jago Sensaki was dead, as was a part of Ethan Knight's soul. This, however, wasn't the only thing he mourned as he stood in that sprawling courtyard with eyes on the idol of his depraved god. His mind had swapped his guilt of Sensaki's death to memories of an estranged childhood. His thoughts were divided and sullen, wholly imbued in regret. It's been so long. Their deaths, my fall from grace. I guess it's too much of a stretch for me to adhere to 'conventional religion's' explanations. Always has been that way. How did things change so much? When did priorities change so drastically? And most of all, why was I forsaken? I mean, Mother loved God. If she wasn't sleeping or going out of her way to keep me happy, she was quoting the Bible. John 3:16, God loved us so much gave us his only begotten son, yada yada. If I had a dollar for every time she spouted that one off to me, Father Shanahan wouldn't need to look far for our funding. Dad.....yeah, well he wasn't much different. They were active in our church, took prominent roles in the development of the youth. So it was no surprise when they decided to go on that mission trip to Jamaica. I still wasn't sure about the extent of my faith, so they were understanding when I turned the trip down. They weren't Bible-thumpers in the 'strongarm' sense of some fanatical Baptists, just adamant in sharing what they believed and loved to believe. Always one of their greatest qualities. To say I was exasperated when I heard the news was an understatement. A week into their work, they stumbled into the rough part of Kingston. A half dozen 9mm rounds were what brought them face to face with their Holy Spirit. Police still don't know why, and most of all, I don't know why. That was ten years ago. Murdered doing their God's work. Great to see such an omnipotent entity is so watchful of his faithful. And come to think that after graduation, people asked why I was so negative and gloomy when faith was brought up. I've embraced my betrayal. Once a spiritual vagabond and pariah of Heaven. Now a harbinger of the greatest truths. I take refuge in the darkness which has long cradled me, and shall in time conquer this world. Whereas my denouncement from God is what fuels me, knowledge of what is to come in the end times is my guiding light. Those who embrace the vices of this world are the greatest culprits. A mindless neanderthal and a false prophet have already been routed, and the next one is.....well, a midget. Who rides an oversized female sex toy. And by all reports talks to cardboard cutouts I'm told. As much as I'd prefer to laugh it off as some sort of publicity stunt or rib, I've already seen the cruelty of fate. I saw it in RPW when Father Shanahan was defeated two weeks ago and watched a deranged little boy win World title gold. Surely that was yet another trial.....but what about my fate? Right now I'm sure I'll be suitably rewarded for my offerings upon the pulpit. With that in mind, I can only bear in mind what Father Shanahan said in regards to what is to come. This isn't late-night Comedy Central. This isn't the Laugh Factory stand-up club. This is reality, in all its hellish and dismal glory. If I can defeat a heretic god, I'll have no problem overcoming a jester with a fetish for perversity. His gaze floats to the waning moon above, thoughts serenaded by the song of the whippoorwills carreied on the wind. I must confess, there's still a part of me that wants to be the Cockjockey. Not for the perversity and the rampant foolishness, but for the memories I never had. Thirteen and deprived of my parents. I never had the happiness and care-free attitude of the others my age. I don't have the memories to look back on and smile. I never had the chance for light-heartedness or to live the life of a free-spirit. I never had that innocence. Fuck it. If I can't have it, neither will he. And neither will the populace of PrimeTime Central. I was bereaved of adolescence and the joys that go with it. Is that the same 'fair' and 'just' Jehovah that those worthless Christians babble about and worship mindlessly? Fuck them too. My parents were saints, the others are vermin. They should all be the ones who are dead. This may just be what I need. My vengeance shall not be denied. Demolishing an overblown rodeo clown-slash-porn fanatic and winning this competition may not be the ultimate retribution, but it's a step in the right direction. Damn Cockjockey and damn his journey to make that near-cripple Saij proud. This isn't about the dwarf and the gimp, this is about me and the God who betrayed me. It's come down to my defiance in the face of Heaven and the downfall of a treacherous Almighty. The Sect will one day triumph, and His days will end. And I can spit in His wretched face as they cast Him into the abyss where He belongs. His cries will echo for eternity in the unhallowed gulfs of everlasting night. Only when that day comes will my vengeance totally be fulfilled. But one step at a time. Power will be mine, but the Cockjockey must fall first. There is no room for remorse in this unholy war. Forsaken of the light and pledged to the darkness. Cursed are the virtuous, blessed are the damned. Footsteps on the cobbled path caused the cultist's hate-driven eyes to deviate from the macabre idol and swing to his rear flank. Even before turning his fingers were closed on the hilt of the curved blade he'd kept on his person since the killing. "Nice to see you're alert, my disciple," came a somber voice, its timbre alone causing Ethan to lessen his grip on the knife. "The Coalition's agents should be wary." Ethan offered only a meager grin in welcome of Bruce Shanahan. His mentor was donned in black pants and sandals, garb akin to that of a monk and a stark contrast to his infamously savage in-ring personality. Ethan could only stare at the plethora of scars that embellished the elder man's torso and arms, a fact not lost on Shanahan. Running a hand over one particularly thick and discolored marking below his right breast, the one known as Violence Jack grunted before gesturing to the impressive array of old wounds. His expression was stern, that of a mentor. "Badges of honor, Brother Knight. These are my rewards for the trials I've endured in life. This particular one.....it wasn't bestowed on me in the ring. It has a more, shall we say, 'colorful' history. I won't bore you with details, Cthulhu knows you have enough on your mind these days, but suffice to say it has more significance than anything inflicted on me by any barbedwire or shard of glass. This was my initiation into the world." Knight's eyes locked onto his mentor's. They were cold and passionless, devoid of any tangible ambition. In a sense, subconsciously perhaps, he envied his elder's impassive stance on worldly affairs. It was a benchmark worth striving for. "1983. It was the beginning of December, nighttime. First big snowfall of the season. I was standing with a few of my friends I had back then, watching Boston as it got blanketed by the snow. To make a long story short, we had 'problems' with some guys on the other side of the slums and they knew where we'd be that night. Car came around the corner, five guys jumped out with knives. Not guns, knives. They had some distorted sense of honor that no one around there understood, and we were young and never carried firearms in those days. So we were helpless." He grunted, trying to remain openly apathetic. And as he promised, he didn't delve into detail. "After they had already killed Maurice, I tried to stop one. For my trouble, I got this scar. They all got away, left all of us hospital-ridden. Except Maurice.....he got the lone body-bag. He was their primary target, we were just in the way. Cops didn't give a damn so they never got anything out of it. That night my eyes were truly opened. Not only to that......but.....well, one of us did walk away. And he did as soon as he saw the car coming. To this day, I have trouble forgiving such a thing. Faith or no faith." Ethan was admittedly perplexed until his superior looked directly into his eyes and frowned. "The one that turned and walked away, Brother Knight, was Randal Craven." Craven was currently priest of Saint Augustine's Church in Philadelphia. Not to mention vassal of the Vatican, overseer of the Blackhelm Coalition's region operations, and friend of one Jago Sensaki. None of these facts were lost on Ethan Knight. "Something told me then that Randal had changed," replied Bruce with a shake of the head. "He wasn't one of us anymore, his loyalties had fallen completely to that of a faceless overseer whose very existence was left to the faith in Randal's heart. But I never fully believed he should be left behind as a friend. Now I can accept it. We operate to further much different means." Bruce glanced at his maturing protege. "You're thinking of your past again, Brother Knight, are you not?" quipped the high priest, noticing his apostle's eyes latched onto the grotesque statue of Cthulhu set across the sprawling courtyard. "Unfortunately I......" began Knight almost apologetically. "Don't fret over it, Ethan," assured VJ, his piercing eyes now falling on the still-confused disciple of the dark. "Conquering one's humanity is a long and strenuous process. Memories, emotions, human disposition......they're all obstacles. Innocence is a phony ideal. Nothing is innocent about the human condition. A grand illusion, nothing more." "Is revenge an illusion as well, Father Shanahan?" "Even the Old Ones have smited those who have opposed them," the elder gruffly replied, his eyes falling on the very same marble effigy. "As such....revenge is no illusion. Regardless of your past, I have no qualms about the mission I've appointed you. I've had my involvements with such an ambition." A ghoulish smile crossed his lips. "Revenge is a dish best served cold," hissed Shanahan with a sadistic overtone to his words. "And it was bitterly cold the night I left that warehouse in Anaheim a year and a half ago with an entire police unit shredded in my wake. It was my vengeance, and my gift from those to whom I'd sworn a lifetime of servitude. One of those in the unit that night was in the group that killed Maurice. Moved out of Boston shortly after what happened. And trust me when I say he looked ten times worse than Maurice when my 'visitor' finished that night." There was no denying the evil permeating Shanahan's tone. He was on the verge of cackling at the memory of the bloodshed in Anaheim that night, watching with morbid glee as the thing he had summoned from the gulfs of nightmare dismembered all on the task force investigating the supposed warehouse break-in. Ethan still remembered the newspaper reading that night; he'd followed the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament while in the Mid-West and found it odd that such a massacre had occured in the same city on the same night that Violence Jack was in town. "I'd like for you to visit Sister Sterling," said Shanahan, his demeanor again stoic. "I believe she could help immensely with your progression in our order. She's reliable and a visionary in her area of expertise." Knight was surprised at the request to say the least, though he did well to suppress displaying it. Marion 'Hemlocke' Sterling was one of Shanahan's primary subordinates and charged with overseeing things from her abode on the outskirts of the Pine Barrens of New Jersey. Much like the primal wilderness upon whose fringe she lurked, Sterling herself was an enigma. While well-known as an educated, refined lady of near unparalleled beauty, the borderline-hermit was equally renowned for both her acute knowledge of dark things and her innate cruelty. A black widow in every sense of the word, she had married three suitors in the past and left all three unions with a substantially larger bank account. And husbands encased in boxes under six feet of earth, victims of terrible 'accidents' soon after marriage. Not bad for a woman of barely thirty. "I have a message for you to deliver personally to Marion," continued Bruce, snapping his disciple from his thoughts. "One I prefer she gets courtesy of one of my most trusted advisors. She's a rather 'musing' individual, far from the impulsive type. Not to mention a scholar of the black arts whom I hold in great intellectual regard. It may take a few days for her to formulate a response to my request. It's a rather hefty one. Either way, while you're there, I'm sure you would do well to absorb some of her knowledge. She has a taste for antiquity that few of her age can boast, so while you have the chance please 'take in the sights' in her home." Ethan pursed his lips before looking to his elder. "What of the tournament?" he asked, not sure the effect the trip would have on his tournament match-up. "Next week," muttered Bruce as a breeze swept through the courtyard. "You depart after the Cockjockey is dealt with. I don't forsee him offering any extravagent resistance. At least not on the level of a jackal posing as a god. You are one of the Favored now, Ethan. Have no worries. Don't lose focus, but don't lose sleep either. Now....run and prepare for the evening sermon. I prepared it with you specifically in mind." "Yes, m'lord," replied Knight. "I shall not fail you in the ring this week......" "I know that, Brother Knight. May Azathoth bless you." As Ethan wandered off to ready himself for the night's ceremonies, Shanahan looked down on the valley and woods surrounding the hill crowned by Wyatt Manor. His dark aura was further stressed by the evil expression that twisted his countenance. "Revel now. Your desolation is looming."
