For Better or Worse - The Pitch

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 I sat there, with my head in my hands and groaned….I thought they’d never find those tapes. I had made sure to try and burn them once I was done in the NCWA because quite simply that was one of the stupidest gimmicks I’d ever done for wrestling hands down. I never wanted those tapes to see the light of day once I finished that gimmick and had done my best to ensure that they never did. But here I was sitting in a room just after seeing three of my most shameful moments in my wrestling career. As I stared at the now blank television screen I only had one question for my tormentors. ‘You brought me here for this?’ I had turned to these very tormentors, sitting across the table. Many would picture them as hulking brutes, men who have tied me to a chair, forced to watch such a horrific time in my career that it was almost unbearable. Men who have cunning sinister minds that have conspired to lure me in, bring me to their evil lair and then subject me to horrible unfathomable evil…such evil of me in shocking pink! But what I sat across from… …were two nerds looking expectantly at me. And now that they’d heard such a strange response to what they’d just shown me (though why they’d be surprised I have no idea - after all Jessica was sniggering beside me the entire time) and were fidgeting in their seats. Looking at the two of them it was easy to see why. David Walsh, the skinnier of the two (and that was saying something) was on the left and wore a faded blue jumper over a light blue shirt, complementing the whole nerdy look with a comb over for his cropped brown hair. Thin, stylised glasses made the man look a little more aesthetically pleasing but you couldn’t doubt that he was not one of the ones blessed with looks. He had a scruffy goatee to finish the look and right now was chewing on a bit of his moustache, trying quickly to think of what to say. He would nervously twitch every now and then and when he spoke, it was uneven and unsure. He was never absolutely positive of his tone and what it was he was trying to say. Meanwhile his partner Derek Walsh, his brother no less, was squirming in his chair, trying desperately not to make eye contact with me once he saw how annoyed I was. Derek on the other hand was a bit more modern in his styling. He had spiky brown hair in the latest fashion and wore no glasses, though I suspected he wore contacts with the consistency with which he rubbed his eyes throughout this sham of a meeting so far. This became more frequent now. He had a simple t-shirt over his slim build and it read ‘I’m a legend…tap me!’ the meaning of which was completely lost to me. Other than that he had no distinguishing features, and even if he did, he was trying his best to keep them hidden to everyone right now. When it became his occasion to speak, (which was even less than his brother) he jabbered like an excited D&D player. Which he was. ‘You brought me here for this?’ I’d repeated it because the two of them looked dumbfounded, absolutely mystified by my reaction and it was clear I’d have to repeat it to find out what the bloody hell was going on. It was now that David began to ruffle through some paper quite rapidly. Derek on the other hand slumped backwards into his chair, trying to slide underneath the table that we were sat at. The room we were in was very plain, a simple box room of basic furniture. There was one long board room style table in here and the aforementioned television also. They sat towards the window which was shuttered with the blinds covering them. As such the room was very dark and had been lit by the overt pastel colours of the 80’s only moments ago but had now returned to a dim bluish light in general. Sitting beside me was Jessica O’Grady, daughter of my trainer, Dan. She had taken it upon herself to become my own personal agent for the ridiculous frenzy of interviews and the like involved with the GTT and had conducted herself quite well I thought, managing to keep veteran interviewers in check and was very commanding. It also helped she’d decided to wear a power business suit and high heels which made here a bit more intimidating. After that her nice cleavage and flowing blonde hair completed the look. She looked good, even if I did say so myself…more than once. Finally David looked to address me, even though you could tell he was very annoyed by his brother’s effective support from beneath the table. He shot a glare that was nothing but venom towards him and all Derek did was slink further underneath. ‘Well Mr. Murphy…we’ve shown you this…footage…because…well…we’ve seen…potential.’ He spat potential out like it was a bad word and seemed to recoil once it came out. With not another word he had moved back from the table, hoping beyond hope that there was enough room between himself and myself that he could make a good escape through the window. Of course, that was depending on whether he was quick enough to get out of his chair before I did. Strangely though his words didn’t set me off like I thought they would. Instead, I was curious. Potential for what? ‘Huh?’ That really wasn’t what I’d had in my head. Thankfully Jessica saved me on this one. ‘What my client is trying to say Mr. Walsh is what do you mean by potential? Why exactly have you brought him here? To waste his time? Because that would be a poor…’ ‘No, no, no!’ His hands were wide up in his face, making a peace offering as if he were a lamb to the slaughter. He consistently shook his head, never once stopping to even allow the slightest doubt that he was lying. He just kept on going, randomly inserting a no here and there to make the point even more exacerbated. ‘Ok…OK! I get the idea Mr. Walsh. Now what I’d like from you is to tell me for what reason you brought us here for?’ David Walsh slowly calmed down, helped ably by his brother Derek who had managed to extricate himself from the subterranean hole he seemed to have dug in the floor. He shoved his brother a little from side to side and was even about to splash a bit of water from the table on him before his brother snapped back to his senses. ‘No it’s ok Derek I’m fine now. You’ll have to help me on this one for a change. Can you do that for once?’ ‘What do you mean for once? I always help you! Whether it be with my +2 Greatsword of DOOM, or the Bracers of Infinite Wisdom my level 14 Paladin of Torm is right by your side!’ Derek looked indignant, like he was completely absolved of all guilt for whatever troubling occurrence David had just referred to. What they were talking about I hadn’t got the foggiest notion but whatever it was David clearly wasn’t impressed, the smarmy grin telling it all. ‘Pssh! Yeah right, you remember the last encounter with the Orogs? What happened to your fancy paladin then? I do recall a certain case of liver tail!’ Orogs? What? ‘Well you didn’t try and stop them casting paralyse now did you? You think about that? You could’ve tried to utilise your dispel magic but you didn’t care, you only wanted to get rid of Graham!’ Graham…what a name. ‘Ugh! Regardless, the last time that was important was that guy Johnny Noble! You never closed the deal then, and you know he was perfect for the proposal…’ Yeah but what had you proposed buddy boy? I’m sitting here like a goon waiting for you two to finish your arguments that were better off in a nursery. ‘Yeah if we wanted a brain-dead man with big muscles…’ Oh screw this. ‘Eh excuse my boys but are you going to sit there and bicker amongst yourself like you were twelve again or am I going to find out why you just played some of the most torturous moments from distinguished career?’ Both brothers stopped in their tracks, just realising now that they’d been carrying on in front of me. They quickly swivelled back to face me and laid their arms out on the table, like two sleazy businessmen waiting for the opportunity to sell a hunk of junk, putting on painted smiles. They’d totally forgotten we were there until I’d voiced my displeasure. I smiled right back at them. ‘So…are you going to tell me what you’ve got me here for or am I going to have to mosey on out of here?’ The two of them looked at each other. Screw this. ‘Well then, I’d like to say this was time well spent but…’ ‘A film! It’s a film we want you for…’ Well, at least that got me to stay in the seat. It was rather comfortable to be honest. But a film…this was interesting, I’d never been asked to do something like this before. Derek picked up on this, seeing my upraised eyebrow that revealed my piqued interest. ‘Oh yes! A film, of epic proportions! You see right now you’re seeing all these superhero films yeah? Well, we wanted to take it one further…why stop at superheroes? Sure some people relate to superheroes but what about an anti-hero that can speak to thousands, millions…someone who has seen it all and is the common man…a man who deals with life itself, not some far off distant planetary trouble…’ David picked up the blurb. ‘Picture the scene; a dirty, grimy New York city is crumbling, morals and laws are being degraded each and every second that passes; men struggle to make an honest day’s pay and bit by bit the surroundings are driving people further and further into the gutter. Enter the head of the crime syndicate in the city, one Anthony Vincenzo Garrelli…’ A light bulb went off in my head. That was awfully familiar to a certain man I had known… ‘He’s the head of the syndicate but suddenly one day…he’s found dead, lying cold outside his mansion and leaving an entire empire to no heir. So naturally what’s there to follow? Absolute chaos!’ Derek now came back in, jumping hyperactively on his chair to interject and provide his own finishing touches. ‘So enter the one man who can bring and restore order…’ He stopped then and I looked at him, his puzzlement on his face the whole time. ‘What’s wrong?’ A simple question but I wanted to know the answer to it because so far the pitch for this film had been going pretty damn well considering those involved. Actually it was a miracle they’d done this well. ‘We don’t have a name for the main character yet.’ And Derek slumped back into his chair, all energy lost in one frustrating moment. David let his head fall to his hand and his face was a picture of pain. While the culmination was a bit of an anti-climax, the story had something to it that left me thinking. This was something that was…all too familiar to me. It made me think of times that I thought had slowly begun to fade away, to disappear into the recesses of my mind and not be a part of me anymore. But here it was staring me in the face. And it had been written by somebody else…apparently. So I asked the most obvious question that lay before me. ‘Where did…the story come from?’ Both of the Walsh’s kind of looked at me blankly. ‘I’m sorry?’ It was Derek that had decided to answer me, David looking at me expectantly and hoping that this wasn’t going somewhere he would regret. ‘Where did you get the idea from? Pure imagination or…?’ ‘Oh! Oh it was pure imagination…yes, pure imagination.’ Something about the tone of Derek’s voice was off but at the time of this meeting I didn’t notice it. Certainly Jessica didn’t as she was more concerned with the details. ‘So gentlemen you can see you’ve intrigued my client, I want to know the specifics; I want to know where, how, how long, with who, who he’ll play and most importantly…how much?’ A sly grin crept across her face and you could see she was enjoying this. I even thought I spied a quick lick of her lips at the prospect. I was beginning to see her in a new, different light. A very feminine one. The two Walsh’s meanwhile were shifting uncomfortably about this very pertinent question and I was left to wonder as the two of them began to blabber on about the future and such with Jessica. That was better left up to her because I hadn’t a clue what would be going on anyway. My mind instead turned to other things, more pressing demands. For two to three weeks now I had been warming up for not only the beginning of a climb up AWC’s ladder but also the imminent arrival of the GTT5. In my time away a couple of the tournaments had come and gone and I had not been missed from them. Time was when I was in one of the provisional tournaments that existed; the Universal Round Robin Tournament. Ah memories, they were so bittersweet. I can remember the exhilaration of victory, pinning Skylar G. and Syko the Goblin…but also the depressing defeats to Angelo Deville and Ivan Stanislav before the tournament was culled before it could finish. In a sense I never got a chance to prove myself and rise above those defeats. So when I saw the GTT5 and who was going to be in it, I jumped at the chance. A rematch with Deville? Exactly what I wanted and something that could bring me back into the fold itself. Even if Deville didn’t make it past the first round I would strive to defeat whomever had clinically routed ‘The Deville.’ But that was provisioned on the result that I defeated my opponent, someone called Surreal. My mind had drawn blanks on first reading the name on the page and many readings later hadn’t helped. I was left mystified as to who he was and that could be a very dangerous factor. How do you judge somebody and prepare when you don’t even know? So I prepared the best way I knew how: I was just going to beat the snot out of him like an insolent little fool. I drifted back towards the conversation now, comfortably aware of the possibility that something had reached a culmination. It inevitably had not. ‘…but you must be aware Mr. Walsh that my client is a very busy man. If this were to go ahead the project would take a up a sizable amount of time and whilst that is certainly plausible this would substantially take time away from his first love.’ Before they could answer I had my own to give. ‘If you have a copy of the script give it to me now and I’ll consider it, I’ll have a read through and then we’ll see from there. How’s that sound?’ Both of them nodded with Derek taking the lead. ‘Sure! We don’t have a copy right now but we can deliver it to you? How’s that sound?’ ‘Fine. Let’s go Jessica.’ Without another word I left, leaving them in silence, only to utter a mumbling of thanks to Jessica as she left. We’ll see what this story is really about…and if somebody has been spying on me.
