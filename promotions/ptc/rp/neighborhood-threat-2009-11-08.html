Neighborhood Threat

Date: 2009-11-08 ***WARNING: EXPLICIT CONTENT*** Is there even anyone under eighteen left in this neck of the woods? [detail withheld] Olympia, Washington Two weeks prior to the announcement of GTT7, 5:30 PM “So we were wasted, right? I could barely see straight, but I knew I was fuckin’ her harder than she’d ever been fucked before.” A middle-aged man dressed in a Hazmat suit slams the driver’s side door to a white van. The logo across the sliding passenger door reads “Frank Bros. Sanitary Clean-up – Doin’ the Damn Thing So You Don’t Have to!” next to a picture of a rotund man holding a steaming bag away from his face in disgust. “You’re full of shit, Buddy,” the other man says as he walks around the front of the van itching his sides, dressed in an identical blue suit. “Fuckin’ Tyvek shit is itchy, man…why we switched from Teflon I’ll never know…” “We switched ‘cuz that new shit is cheaper, and I was in the middle of a story, Richie,” Buddy chides, “pay attention. You may learn something.” He slides open the side door and removes plastic bags along with two long black-handled claw stick…things (they clean up the roadsides with them, hereinafter referred to as “claws”). “So there I was, givin’ this bitch a fuckin’ WORKOUT, grabbin’ her by the hair, back-a’-the-head, about to put her skull through the headboard…when she fuckin’ blew chunks all over the pillows.” “Ouch,” Richie winces as they start walking up the long driveway to a formidable white house. “Yeah, fuckin’ gross, right? That’s what I thought for a minute, too,” Buddy replies with a solemn nod, handing his protégé several bags and one of the claws, “but then I figured, ‘Buddy Frank, you’re fifty-one years old, and your dick is buried in the ass of some Starbucks barista who could’ve been at one of your daughter’s sleepovers,’ so I took a moment, and I manned up.” “You…what?” Richie says, cocking an eyebrow. “You finished?” “I finished,” Buddy replies proudly, a broad grin spread over his face behind the plastic shield, “and I figured that would be that. End of story. But it was not. She called me the next day. Wanted to see me.” “Wow,” Richie nods, “that’s, um…that defies logic. Good for you, Buddy.” Buddy’s facial expression becomes solemn. “This story does not have a happy ending. I went over to see her, and we’re fuckin’ again, and I’m about to blow and then she says, ‘Stick your finger down my throat.’ And I stop, look at her like ‘What?’ And she looks back at me stone-cold and says, ‘Gag me. I wanna puke while I cum.’” Richie’s eyes widen and he doesn’t see the lawn gnome before he stumbles over it onto the pavement. “Oww! What the hell, man, people actually have these?” “Yeah, I got a few, they’re fun,” Buddy says without missing a beat, “…so now she’s gone through eight pairs of sheets in five days, I’ve got a permanent yellow streak on my right index finger looks like a nicotine stain, and I can’t break this thing off without endangering the free caffé macchiatos I’ve been slurping back on the way out to job sites. Fuck, kid, I’m geekin’ out on caffeine right now!” “I’m confused about what I’m supposed to take away from this story, Buddy,” Richie admits as they approach the front doors of the residence. “I forget,” Buddy shrugs, “something about not fuckin’ a girl who pukes in bed, I dunno. Let’s go to work, kid.” They reach the doors. Buddy crosses his fingers and tries the knob. “Please be locked, please be locked, please…YES!” He steps back, grinning boyishly at Richie. “My favorite part of the job.” He steps back, points his claw at the imaginary outfield a la Ruth, and swings it forward shattering the glass pane in the right front door. “Now we just feel around a sec…and we’re in.” He opens the door and steps inside. Richie follows, instantly curling his nose. “Man, even through the suit…that first wave of funk gets me every time. Who called this in?” “Old couple a few houses down,” Buddy answers, hitting a wall switch that causes two of the four bulbs in the front hallway overhead lamp to flicker on. “Police verified the place had been abandoned, almost guarantee you there are some open windows and a LOT of cat and dog shit in this place. That’s the smell that hops the fence and gets the neighbors to call every time. Wouldn’t be surprised if every stray in three blocks was shacking up here…like that movie Hotel for Dogs.” “Must’ve missed that one,” Richie replies with an eye roll. “Who owns this place? It’s fuckin’ nice.” “Some pro wrestler, on TV and shit, can you believe that?” Buddy says with a snort, stopping in the foyer to stare at the wall adornments. “Here, this guy.” He grabs a framed photo off the wall and hands it to Richie. Richie runs a finger along the frame’s edge. “Family portrait, nice. That his wife? Pretty hot.” “Yeah, I’d plow that bitch,” Buddy confesses. “In the man’s own home,” Richie shakes his head, “very classy. Good-looking kid…you wanna plow that, too? You like ‘em young.” “Don’t be a wiseass,” Buddy grumbles, snatching the frame from Richie’s hands. “Alright, enough with the man’s treasured memories. Be alert and starting doing the job I pay you to do. I ever tell you how my brother Jacoby Frank died?” “Only twice a day, Buddy,” Richie sighs. “He was laughin,’ clownin,’ hungover from whiskey and chasin’ skirt the night before,” Buddy moans. “Doesn’t sound like anyone *I* know,” Richie says dryly. “Just wasn’t checkin’ around, Richie,” Buddy keeps on, “just wasn’t checkin’ around, didn’t see the corroded paneling around this big fancy chandelier in the dining room of this place, and it came down on his big beautiful head. That’s how…” “‘Frank Bros. Sanitary Clean-Up really became Frank Brother Sanitary Clean-Up,’ I know, Buddy,” Richie groans. “A problem I was hoping to remedy by hiring your worthless ass,” Buddy huffs. He flicks another switch as they walk into the main living room, to no effect. An end table lamp works, however, and as soon as Buddy clicks it on, mass shuffling can be heard as several cats flee into dark corners of the room. Buddy points with his claw. “See there? Open windows. Strays. The overwhelming smell of shit.” “We’ve got mold going up the walls over here,” Richie observes, taking steps down into the recessed area of the room, “fuckin’ garbage everywhere. These would be nice couches, too…hey! Dude’s got a fuckin’ bong sitting here!” Richie holds up a green water bong in one hand. “Whooo, feelin’ groovy there, Buddy?” “Yeah, I’m sure you’d know what to do with that, freakin’ hippie,” Buddy mutters. His flashlight glints off a number of crushed beer cans which litter the floor. “Looks like some kids were in here.” “Why do you say that?” Richie asks. “Natty Light,” Buddy smirks, holding up one of the cans. “Plus it looks like they spray-painted something on the wall here…” He squints. “Looks like…‘Such a Con?’ Such a con? I don’t get it. Think they had some beef with this guy or something?” “That says ‘Suck a Cock,’ Buddy,” Richie replies, “and no, I don’t think they had any idea about who lived here. Just stupid kids. Should we case the kitchen stuff?” They walk over to the long bar which wraps around out from the wall to enclose the cooking area from the rest of the living room. Buddy removes a large flashlight from a leg pocket and shines it across the bar. It glints on row after row of empty bottles. “Geez. This guy liked to tie one on, from the looks of it.” “Knob Creek, Woodford Reserve, Maker’s Mark,” Richie lists, hefting one bottle after another, “I won’t argue with his tastes.” “Yeah, yeah,” Buddy says, shining his light around the walls and ceiling, “listen, I’m gonna finishing poking around here if you wanna go ahead and have a look upstairs. Let me know what we’re dealing with and then we call it a day, start in on the clean-up tomorrow.” “Alright,” Richie nods before turning away and venturing to the second floor via the wind-around stairs across the room. Buddy’s eyes follow his light as it probes the corners of the kitchen. The countertops are of a rich grey marble, but thick dark splotches of things long inedible coat their surfaces. Ornate, painted plates inlayed with gold ribbon are stacked haphazardly next to the full sink, and one or two lay shattered below on the black tile floor. “Fuckin’ asshole,” Buddy hisses, spitting on the ground, “I didn’t make the money this year to renovate my kitchen. My daughter has to transfer to a state school. And this guy…” He shakes his head, “…this guy abandons a palace. Fuckin’ asshole.” He turns, eyes falling on the refrigerator. “You,” he says, pointing a finger at the unit, “I’m not fuckin’ with you tonight, you nasty bitch. You and I can talk in the morning.” He runs a hand along the counter, and feels a postcard in his grasp. He holds it up. The scene is pastoral, and he flips it over to read the clean cursive writing across the back. “‘Remember what Clarence said…no man is a failure who has friends. Love, Lindsay.’” Buddy snorts. “Takes more than friends in this life, sweetheart.” His eyes wander around the room. “Looks like your friend realized that much.” His attention is diverted by the appearance of his employee at the base of the stairs. Richie’s face is white, his eyes wide. He jabs a finger in the direction of upstairs repeatedly. “Buddy!” he hisses, “c’mere!” “What?” Buddy asks, annoyed. “C’MERE, man!” Richie pleads, waving his employer on. Buddy walks over to him, and Richie begins cautiously creeping back up the stairs. Buddy jerks his sleeve. “Richie, what the hell is going on?” Richie whispers something inaudible and continues up the stairs. They reach the second floor, and Richie turns around, pointing slowly at an open door to one of the rooms. He mouths something, and Buddy shakes his head. “I can’t understand you, asshole. What’s going on?” “Dude’s in there, man!” Richie says, almost in disbelief. “What?” Buddy’s jaw drops, “holy shit. Go back to the car, get your phone, and call the police, Richie.” Richie nods, jogging back down the stairs. Buddy walks to the door slowly, peering into the mostly empty room. The only light is coming from the moon through an open window, and a man sits next to it on a stool, smoking a cigarette. The ashtray on the window sill is overflowing, and butts are strewn across the sill and the floor around him. Turning to face his guest, Nova takes a drag from his cigarette and runs a hand over a bare patch on the top of his head. His mouth opens slowly. “My hair…” He coughs into his hand. “My fuckin’ hair is falling out.” --------*~*~*-------- Thurston County Sheriff’s Office Olympia, Washington Several hours later… Lindsay Troy steps out of a battered Oldsmobile Cutlass, swinging the driver’s side door shut behind her. It bounces back open. “Rentals,” she mutters, “I asked for something from this decade.” Pulling open one of the doors to the Sheriff’s Office, the “Queen of the Ring” is greeted immediately by a mustachioed, portly fellow in suspenders and slacks. He extends a hand. “Ms. Troy, I presume? Lieutenant Bluff, thanks for coming.” “I gotta say, I’m pretty confused, Lieutenant Bluff,” Troy remarks, following him down the hall. “What is my friend doing in your police station?” “Well, Ms. Troy, in Olympia we have extremely tough nuisance laws,” Bluff explains, “after all, as the capital city of dare I say our nation’s most beautiful state, we must be ever-vigilant against improper land uses which contribute to the degradation of our community.” “Yeah, that does nothing for me,” Troy shakes her head. “Mr. Vega allowed his substantial property to deteriorate to the point of endangering the safety of children and community residents around him,” Bluff goes on, “animals were moving freely throughout the home, garbage compilation was reaching the point of emitting noxious fumes, and once word spread that the home had been for all intents and purposes abandoned, vandals broke several windows, increasing opportunities for access by neighborhood children. After complaints were filed, police investigated and concluded that the house had been abandoned, though apparently that was not the case.” “Has he contacted his lawyer?” Lindsay asks. Bluff nods. “Yep. His guy is all tied up trying to get out here – we appreciate how expeditiously you were able to make the trip.” “I’ve got two travel agents on speed dial,” Troy nods, “I, um…do a lot of jumping around.” “You were his only listed contact person besides a Mr. Sonny Silver, but we didn’t have any luck locating him,” Bluff explains. “Yeah, Sonny has a talent for evading the police,” Troy replies, “developed out of necessity, believe me.” “Honestly, though,” Bluff goes on, “the lawyer will be a formality at this point. We aren’t really looking at any charges here, Ms. Troy. Hell, we did the guy a favor taking him out of that place. He’s, uh…kind of out of sorts, seems like.” They approach a series of interview rooms. Outside the second door on the left, two men are whispering conspiratorially. “C’mon, Hank,” one pleads, “I’m ready for bad cop.” “You’re not ready for bad cop,” the other insists. “I am!” the first protests, “let me put some heat on ‘im!” “Bad cop ain’t like choosing which girl to fuck at the party, Nelson,” Hank chides. Nelson’s shoulders slump in defeat. “With great power comes great responsibility,” Hank quotes in sage-like tones usually reserved for the invocation of the Old Testament, “Spiderman said that. Bad cop is a weapon…wielded by the right hands, with great precision and delicacy, it is a law enforcement tool to cripple the most hardened perp.” “Will you assholes stop wasting tax dollars and go do some actual work?” Bluff barks, causing the men to scurry away. He points at the window in the door which shows Nova sitting in front of an empty table with a bottle of water, tapping his fingers against the tabletop. “He’s in there. You can go on in. Let him know he’s free to leave, he can call off the lawyer.” “Alright, thank you Lieutenant Bluff,” Lindsay smiles before opening the door and stepping inside. “If I don’t smoke a cigarette soon, I’m gonna die,” Nova says immediately. “I don’t think that’s how it works,” Troy smirks, her facial expression morphing a second later to matronly concern and irritation. “You wanna maybe explain some of this to me? Like how a group of hoods could vandalize your house while you were inside of it?” “I may have stepped out for a chicken salad sandwich from the gas station when that happened,” Nova shrugs. Troy walks around to the other side of the table and takes a seat, grabbing Nova’s hand. “Caes,” she says softly, calling him by her abbreviation of his birthname Caesar, “talk to me. What…the hell…is going on?” “I…um…,” he begins, his eyes wandering. There’s no follow-up. “Listen,” she says sternly, focusing him, “I know you’re twisted around right now. The pain of Ariel’s death, Lulu…it’s all grown more acute with time, I get that. I don’t know what it means to lose your daughter to an illness and then your wife…” “Ariel committed suicide, Lindz,” Nova corrects, his tone blunt and acerbic, “I didn’t lose track of her at the mall.” Troy goes on. “You feel like a joke when you come around PRIME. You were fired and embarrassed in NFW, and insulted by management in TEAM. I feel you. But you have to pull it together, man. You’re thirty years old.” “I’m going fucking bald,” Nova says mournfully. “Male pattern baldness, who cares?” Troy shrugs. “Don’t you know bald men are more virile?” “Pffft,” Nova snorts, “my personal life? Reminds me of the adage, ‘Youth wasted on the young.’” “You hit a dry spell,” Troy replies, “you’re not gonna meet anyone sitting upstairs in your big-ass house while the world rots around you. And you’re also not gonna exorcise your professional demons by sitting in your bedroom shuffling through photos of the ‘Good Ol’ Days’ while you smoke eight packs of cigarettes a day.” As if on cue, Nova turns and coughs loudly into his hand. “Shit, is there any Kleenex in here?” “No,” Troy says, pulling a flier out of her pocket and unfolding it onto the table. “Read this.” “What is it?” Nova asks. “Read it,” Troy rolls her eyes. Nova looks over the flier and cocks an eyebrow at his friend. “GTT7?” “It’s finally happening, for real,” Troy grins broadly, “and a loooot of the old crew are coming out of the woodwork…” “Right,” Nova wipes his nose with the back of his hand, “like who?” “Oh, I dunno,” Lindsay replies coyly, “like…Tchu.” Nova tries to deadpan it, but his eyes light up at the mention of his old professional rival from PRIME and he raises an eyebrow. “Yeah? Wardsy came back for this thing?” Troy nods. “Said he requested you in the first round, too.” “Now you’re fucking with me,” Nova replies, sitting back in his chair. “Yes, I am,” Lindsay admits. “What about you, Queenie?” he asks, “gonna try to put your stamp on the Big One?” Troy averts her eyes, stalling momentarily. “I’m not ready to come back yet.” “Huh,” Nova squares his jaw, “sounds like a lot of ‘Do as I say, not as I do’ around here.” “Yeah? Well when you become a recluse and cut yourself off from the world, your sense of hearing can get a little messed up,” Lindsay shoots back icily. Her features soften. “C’mon, Nova. I’m trying my damndest to rescue you here. I flew all the way out here to tell you to throw yourself into this thing and forget about whatever else is going on for a while…or…” “Or what?” Nova asks. “Or…I’m worried that one day I’m gonna get a phone call in the middle of the night that you’ve done something really fucking stupid,” Lindsay replies, her voice heavy in an uncharacteristic moment of coarse language. “Please, Nova…throw your name in the hat and try to pull yourself out of this…this whatever it is that you’re torturing yourself over.” Nova’s eyes wander. “I feel…disconnected, Lindz. Like I have no anchor. Like I’m drifting, and I can blink one second and the day is over. I can blink again, and I’m in a different position, in a different room, in a different time. I made wrestling my life, but I feel like it’s all passing me by, rendering me completely irrelevant. Does that sound like the mindset of someone who should be entering the biggest tournament in the industry?” Troy doesn’t miss a beat. “Considering we’re also dealing with the leader of a satanic church, several suspected murderers, and James von Doom…yeah, I think you’re still okay.” She gives his hand a squeeze. “Listen, think on it, alright?” Her nose curls. “God, you smell awful. As much as your bouquet counsels against it, why don’t you come with me. They told me you’re free to go. I’ve got a hotel room not far away for the night, just to get you out of that blight of a residence you’ve created, and we can think about all this in the morning, alright?” “Lindsay Troy, you minx,” Nova teases, “are you picking me up?” “You look like shit, Caes,” Troy says, “you need a fresh start.” “Just let me hit the can before we bounce,” Nova replies, sliding back his chair and standing up. He turns back after opening the door. “Thanks for coming out here, Lindz. You’re one of the only people I have left.” She smiles and nods, and Nova lets the door close behind him. He walks down the hall in the direction of arrows to the restrooms…and then veers sharply right towards an illuminated exit sign, his stride escalating to a jog. In the interview room, Troy is lost in her thoughts. Nova…Caesar Vega…her friend of several years, at times like an indefatigable little brother, at times almost something closer…and other times scary and foreign. She has tried in the last three years to be a presence, a rock for him in the wake of his losing the two most important women in his life. She remembers well how professional indignity mixed with personal anguish and guilt to form a motivational cocktail which propelled him to massive success…and which has almost ruined him in its wake. She wonders if the secret this time isn’t motivation without the anger, a will to win, achieve, validate without having to point a finger, at himself or anyone else. She wonders if that stoner kid who loves to laugh and take shots at work and try out pathetic pick-up lines on her is still in there somewhere. She wonders…how long it takes to use the damn bathroom. Knock, knock, knock. Troy raps a fist on the restroom, waving politely to Lieutenant Bluff as he passes. “Hey, Caes…you fall in? Everything working out alright in there?” No answer. Knock, knock, knock. “Nova, c’mon. I’m tired. I’m hungry. I’m missing Project Runway reruns for your crazy, nuisance-causing ass, now move it.” Nothing. “Caesar, for real? What are you…” She opens the door, and stares at the empty toilet for a moment before whirling around, her face white. “Oh, shit.” --------*~*~*-------- [detail withheld] Olympia, Washington A short while later… A dingy Oldsmobile Cutlass screeches its tires to a stop in the driveway of Nova’s property, and Lindsay Troy flings the car door open, her mouth agape. A warm orange glow blankets the neighborhood, and Troy’s first steps are slow, disbelieving, as she walks up towards the house which is now ablaze. “Oh, my God…” Flames dart out of the broken windows and lick up at the sides of the house. Smoke billows up into the clear evening air. Nova’s silhouette is visible up near the front doors, and Troy sprints over to him. “The police are maybe two minutes behind me,” she says breathlessly, “Oh, God, Nova, what the hell did you do?” Nova raises a joint to his lips and inhales sharply, holding for a moment before blowing the smoke out through his nose. He turns to the “Queen of the Ring,” eyes glassy. “You were right, Lindz,” he says quietly, “I needed a fresh start.” As blue and red lights appear at the end of the road, Troy takes Nova’s free hand in hers, and they watch the fire in silence.
