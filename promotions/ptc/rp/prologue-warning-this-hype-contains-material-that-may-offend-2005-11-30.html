Prologue. (Warning, this Hype contains material that may offend viewers of a religious bent)

30 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-30 They say that history is only written by the victor, that we can never truly know what happened so many years ago, because it will only ever be written by those who wish those events to be viewed in one light and one light only. I am Curator Johanson, and it is my dubious honour to be tasked with chronicling the events that made up "The Secret War". It is my fervent wish that I am able to cast some previously unshed light on that troubled time, and maybe, show some unsung heroes in the light they were meant to be shown. But before you can understand the Secret War, you have to understand that it didn’t start the way most would have you believe. The Secret War didn’t start over something as grand as a pledge to drive evil from the world, or a dark pact to cloud the sky and flood the world with darkness…it started, you see, with Adam’s first wife. No not Eve young one, I said Adam’s FIRST wife…Lillith. I can see the confusion on your face, and rest assured that it does not surprise me. Many of your generation are unaware that Eve was not the first woman some paint her to be, but that she was Adam’s second wife. When God first created man, the companion he created for Adam was crafted from clay and she was called Lillith. But Lillith was not content to be Adam’s wife; she was in fact not even content to be a servant of god. When Adam demanded she lay with him and be his forever she refused, refusing to bend knee to him or to be possessed by him. In surprise, and anger, Adam called out to God, demanding that he humble this woman and force her to take her rightful place beside Adam. There is a great deal of speculation as to what happened next, or exactly what it was that God said to Lillith on that day. Whatever it was that he said, it would not budge Lillith from her position…and she refused to bend knee to God himself or to his servant Adam. Left with little other recourse, God cast Lillith from heaven, forever turning his light from her and forcing her to live in the darkness of the world outside Eden. Those rare sources that teach this portion of history will often leave the issue there, leaving the reader with the impression (or fact in some cases) that Lillith perished. She did not. Although she was cast from Heaven by God, Lillith was not cursed as Adam and Eve were, her immortality remained assured, although she was no longer truly one of the children of light, or one of the angels. Her…unique abilities allowed her to survive where many would have perished, and she grew stronger as time passed. When Cain slew Abel, it was Lillith who first approached the wayward son, teaching him what little of her power he needed to survive. But Cain would surprise Lillith, for he drank the blood of his brother Abel, hoping to gain some of his favour. But when God saw what Cain had done he cursed him; to live forever without truly living at all. Yes it took many years of study, but we have confirmed that Cain was the first "Vampire" and in fact fathered their entire race…with Lillith as his lover. Lillith taught the damned man what little of her powers she could and shielded him from the sun, helping him to grow strong in spite of his curse. But Cain would eventually turn on his mentor and lover, spurning her to walk his own path. It is said that was the first and only time Lillith was harmed by a mortal man, and it was from her tears and her flesh that she gave birth to a second race…of demons. These children, or Succubi, were neither truly evil nor good, birthed as they were from the "woman" who embodied free will at it’s most extreme. When the Angels saw what she had done, they arrayed a host of great power before Lillith and her children. They told Lillith in great detail what they would do to her children, but that she could live if she would renounce what she had done and beg Gods forgiveness. She refused. The host set about to slaughtering Lillith’s children, forcing their "mother" to watch as her progeny were hunted like game animals. Again they asked Lillith to repent, saying that they would spare her in the end if she only would. This time Lillith spoke one word: The true name of God. The battle that followed was fierce and marked the first time that Angelic blood was spilled on mortal soil. In the end the Angels withdrew rather then risk further damage…and before the could regroup Lillith spread her followers far and wide, instructing them to blend into society as the Vampires had done. The Secret War started there. Not as a struggle between good an evil, but rather a fight for life itself on one side. Eventually the Fallen Angel and his minions would seek to involve themselves, but they would remain on their own side, for Lillith and her children would have none of him or his. Lillith’s children scattered throughout the world, and many sought out only other Succubi or Incubi for companionship. All would eventually be involved in the Secret War, but few would make the impact that one woman did. Let historians argue about whether or not Roshael was the traitor they paint her to be…she made a contribution to the secret war that many aren't even aware of. Her only daughter: Dawn.
