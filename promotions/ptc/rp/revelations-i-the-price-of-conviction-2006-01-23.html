Revelations: (I) The Price Of Conviction

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-23 January 9th, 2006 Night had fallen on Philadelphia, and with it a torrential downpour amidst unseasonly high temperatures. Rainwater cascaded down the drainage pipes of the church and spilled into the street, streaming down the sidewalk before running into sewer grates bordering the street. The crescent moon strained to offer light behind shifting black clouds, occasionally offering a peak of its nocturnal rays when the evening's capricious winds gave opportunity. Two figures emerged into the waning light just before the moon was hidden once again, their eyes directed toward the towering steeple crowning the old Catholic abbey. The hymns beloved by the centuries-old faith trickled through the open windows and ajar front entrance, a guide to wayward souls needing solace and a peek at salvation as they fought the tribulations of daily life. The streets were devoid of humanity and the church parking lot empty; a proper guess would lead to the conclusion that the choir was a recording placed on repeat and the spiritual stronghold hosted scant few visitors, if any at all. "Quaint." The hissed observation echoed with spite as Kendall fidgeted in his pockets, his masked associate staring absently at the church and the particularly the angels and saints depicted on the stained-glass windows and outside ornamentation. "Their kings in Italy never fail to make their mark come time to draw up new blueprints. Let's go, surely he's inside." While not one of the greatest monuments to the power in Rome, the aesthetic qualities of the sactuary's interior were not lost on a patron of art and pious history like Masquerade as they stepped through the doors. A shining example of Renaissance-era architecture and artwork, even this suburban replica of the great cathedrals of eastern Europe radiated the feeling of antiquity and lore long established by the faith. Images of harp-strumming cherubs and seraphim embellished the backdrops of the upper balconies while gilded banisters and rails lined the pews and dais. As expected, a lone man knelt before the image of a halo-capped Christ behind the altar. He was donned in the standard garb of the clergy, a silver crucifix clutched in one hand, and still seemed oblivious to his guests. A modest smile crossed his lips at the creak of a pew as Kendall and Masquerade sat, hands still clasped in his nightly prayer and eyes closed in continued reverence. "I would like to believe you've come to repent, -----. But, unfortunately, I'm certain this visit isn't for such a happy occasion. Can I neglect my gut this one time and attribute your coming as a mere social visit, perhaps?" "It's never that simple, hasn't been for years." Cold and impassive, subtly contemptuous. The masked man's words were icy and biting. "There are more pressing matters. I offer you my apologies for that, whether or not you believe my sincerity." "A pity." In reflections of recent events, he had known the answer already. "I had hoped the loving tug of God had finally overcome that maelstrom swirling in your heart. It has not become so blackened that you cannot return to the light." Barring the two visitors and the priest, no one else occupied the sanctuary. A long silence passed as Masquerade removed his mask; there was no need for secrets or subterfuge here. He leaned back as one finger absently traced a symbol on the pale ceramic face, perusing the statues and ceremonial pieces adorning the altar while basking in the melody of the repentant symphony emanating from the sound system. "The Vatican displaces you more often than any military family," observed Masquerade as he peered through an open window and into the shadow-vested avenue beyond. "I have to admit that this doesn't seem to be your style of spreading the gospel. Unless, of course, you've altered your approach on me." "Preaching to the converted isn’t suited to my style." The priest sighed, a tinge mournful. "I can't open minds the way I did elsewhere, where my example was a beacon in murder and robbery-filled darkness. They stuck me in the pampered end of the city where news of a domestic disturbance is on the lips of everyone within ten blocks. There’s no challenge advocating the faith in a suburban area where prayer is already part of local dogma. My predecessors were cherished citizens of this neighborhood and at times, as terrible as it may be, I almost resent that." The priest drew himself upright, spinning to face the stone-faced pair as they examined the atmosphere. The raven-haired man he knew little of, the other a long-time acquaintance. The former, his face marred by a look that was in some manner simultaneously serene and baleful, walked away to leave the others to their conversation, eyes sweeping over the excerpts of scripture framed near the podium. The one known as Masquerade, now naked of that which shrouded his identity, firmly took the hand of his long-time colleague and offered a smile, remaining silent as he shifted in his seat. The priest spoke first, noting the demented gleam in the other's eyes. The gleam of a righteous crusader. "You've been called many things in the time we've known each other. You've been called a thug, a prodigy, a savior, a demon…and to be frank, I think you've fit them all at one time or another." A modest laugh from the other, nonchalant and befitting his current identity. "I assume it goes back to beauty being in the eye of the beholder. One's intentions can be read the same way. What you call malicious, I call a necessary evil. What I deem frivolous and mindless nonsense, you label as a requisite for fulfilled faith." "I know you are following what you feel is the right course for our world," stated the preacher, his eyes falling on the image of the virgin Mary. "I simply can't understand how you have yet to realize that it is not." "The world is constantly evolving," explained the unveiled Masquerade with eyes lowered onto the pallid mask, "progressing past its primal origins. In the Dark Ages, man needed something to believe in. A singular truth, no matter how flimsy or potentially delusional, to latch onto to preserve the hope that light would again shine on the world." He paused, that frigid and calloused demeanor more powerful than any fiery outpouring of emotion would have been. "That was where your glorious cult came in, to shield the world and offer refuge from the devil that had halted advancement, brought disease to the people, killed their cattle, and whatever other manner of terrible disaster fell on the poor downtrodden people of sin-ridden Europe." Anothe lengthy pause in that brazen diatribe as he lifted a Bible from a slot in the rear of the pew in front of him, weighing his mask and the holy book in each hand like a scale as his eyes met that of the priest he had known for years. "But, sad as it may be, we both know well of the nature of humanity. People don't like change, when they're comfortable they'll go to any length to remain in that state, even if it means embracing our ignorance. Mankind fears what it knows not of, and what better smoke screen against the bleak reality of our world than tales of an afterlife teeming with green meadows, golden castles and healing waters? With such wonders to look forward to, why abandon it in favor of nastier truths that make us apprehensive about our place in the universe? Humanity won't do what is in its best interest out of fear and pride in its own self-professed domination." His opinions had always been brash and in regards to conventional organized religion they were outright venomous. Flashing back to the events of days prior, the clergyman dismissed the attack on his beliefs and marched ahead with what lay on his mind. Besides, he had no inclination to weather some sermon about the evils of Catholic Church or the beast mankind. "It will take a great sacrifice to stop what you have chosen to empower. A benevolent soul to disrupt the designs of the conjuration you've evoked. I'm not blind to your motives, -----." So he did already know, but this didn't surprise Masquerade in the least. His old friend was well-studied in the same things as he, if not as understanding in their purpose or for the same intent. He'd once heard them referred to as a fractured single consciousness, but then the bearer of this observation had a tendency to speak in rather cryptic tongues. "Ah yes, the offering of a noble life to impede its 'evil' impression on the world. You would offer such parlay for the innocent, old friend?" He knew the answer to the question already, putting forth the query only as a way to raise the subject now that it had been partially eluded to. His inquiry was met with a forlorn sigh. "I have no need. Father Rockwell has already done so, God rest his soul. I do pray for your own soul and your redemption, but I cannot in this way. I know as well as anyone about the torment you've endured but I implore you realize this isn't the path. This won't give you the absolution you're chasing." "Because I won't offer my life savings to the Vatican or drink wine while kneeling before the effigy of a centuries-dead martyr? Tell me, is that the way to saving my immortal soul?" To suggest hatred burned in the eyes of the masked man would be a tad extreme, but disapproval would be a much more proper indication of his mentality. To him, the man upon the cross was an inspirational fairy tale that did well to move Christian merchandise and promote television specials, but the degree of truth surrounding his deeds was skewed to the verge of atrocity. "I do no more to condemn humanity than your superiors in Rome. In fact, I give them more hope than the pessimist charlatans that pay your salary." The priest's face was young but told of wisdom beyond his years. He had always handled these affairs well, but he couldn't suppress a grimace in the face of this disparagement. "Very few yet know what caused a devoted follower of the Lord like Rockwell to end his own life, but those of us privvy to such information wish we could also be ignorant of it." He fidgeted with the crucifix while shaking his head, rubbing his finger across the intersection as if wiping away the blood that once tarnished its true life counterpart. "Never was it my intention to provoke a superstitious old man into taking his own life. Perhaps you should keep better tabs on your clergy, old friend, the cardinals would have a PR disaster on their hands if the Church had to bear the weight of an epidemic of priest suicides." The other narrowed his eyes in obvious agitation, exasperated at the mockery shown by his unmasked visitor. "Father Rockwell often staked claims of seeing the Devil made flesh. I saw that hideous thing you sent him, it was revealed to me soon afterward. You're just as diabolic as you ever were." "Oh?" He was a tad shocked at the accusation, but his tone veiled his surprise. "To you Bible-thumpers and fly-by-night demonologists perhaps my methods are a bit unsound. But to call them diabolic, old friend? Shame on you. If you'd pull yourself away from all the faux righteous brainwashing the Vatican drowns you in, perhaps you'd come to view things a tad more realistically. Perhaps living in a throwback to when the Church held true power has clouded your judgement." "Rockwell believed things even many in my position see as heresy." With a deep breath, the priest kept his composure in the face of the obvious provocation. "To say I agreed with his theories is a bit much, not to say I don't entertain possibilities. Knowing the things I do brings the necessity of entertaining more blasphemous notions than is healthy for a man of God." "God believes as well. I fail to see the overwhelming sin in acknowledging things that He does from His paper throne." The priest glanced at him before looking away, clasping his hands behind his back as he strided in the direction of the light-crested pulpit. The other knew much of his assignments and techniques and to argue would be futile. They had once been next to brothers, before his corruption; he could never know what the other had seen in the shadowy gulfs beyond Hell. Kendall, yet to offer a word, lifted a chalice from a table near the altar, turning it to peruse the runes and Latin lettering engraved. He appeared more interested in the art and building design than the verbal exchange happening around him. The clergyman wasn't the least bit sorrowful over the dark-haired figure's musings; he had enough on his hands considering the perverted theology of the other, and the conviction in which he was willing to make it law. "The Church has been stained with the blood of ages. Your doctrines are a perverse thesis designed to control the masses through fear, not reverence and awe. That is something worthy defying, regardless of what your Pope and his advisors proclaim." "And you intend on destroying it all, blasting the hopes of millions for sake of your own selfish whims? You're mad, -----. Utterly mad." He stopped, his grip tightening on the crucifix. "Will you not stop until the world crumbles around you? How deep have you stared into the abyss?" A grin. A mark of the wicked to many, angelic to those who believed in him. The priest merely frowned as Masquerade slipped the Bible back into its place. "To shatter the illusion is my goal. I'll awaken the world." * * * * January 23, 2006 "Claude Metcalf, I presume? We come on business." Maury Dawson offered a wry smile at the ungroomed and sullied face that peeked at him through the door, ajar but still locked via a thin chain. Behind him on the doorstep, garbed in a body-encompassing leather coat and plaid beret of sorts, Ellis Warrick made no attempt to hide his early disfavor for the man they'd been sent to meet in this isolated hamlet, the very armpit of West Virginia. "What do you seek, traveler?" "We're after a very rare volume. Myself and my associate have been informed that you have a rather extensive collection and may be of help to us. It's of the sort you specialize." The man on the other side muttered something unintelligible before removing the chain and opening the door, his face suddenly glowing with an expression of boundless welcome that Maury wasn't sure was legitimate or the mask of a proprietor waiting to make a profit. Judging by the decor he observed beyond the threshold, it could be either. "I have few visitors you must understand," iterated the bookseller as he led them down a hallway lit by only a dying lamp and what minor expanse of light spilled through the door of an adjacent room. "Most of my customers prefer correspondence by mail and know exactly what it is they seek. Doing business face-to-face isn't typical for one in my trade. Especially not for such a, shall we say, 'esoteric' variety." The chamber they were led to, the merchant's study, they were told, was a far cry from that of an adamant professional, more readily bringing about visions of some crazy recluse's pillaged quarters than any makeshift office or storeroom for his dusty stock. "This is it," he announced with a broad gesture to his inventory. "My life's accumulation, and one I'm willing to share with any who can pay." A nervous little man with an perpetually furrowed brow, Claude Metcalf regarded his visitors with timidness the moment they passed into the room; very few had likely laid eyes on this archive and the peculiar little man liked it that way. In a world driven by technology and an evolving need for promptness, Metcalf's archaic business methods spoke of a self-enterprising hermit who preferred his privacy and balked at crowds, selling his wares to those of similar ilk. Through his manner alone Dawson drew assumptions of a once sickly and reticent child who now peddled many of the dusty old tomes that kept him company in his youth. "It's obvious that my service has been recommended to you, gentlemen," uttered the odd little dealer. He attempted to maintain the posture of a seasoned businessman but failed miserably, his unkempt appearance detracting from any sense of professionalism he tried to display. "Very few know about my services and those that do are counted as rather solitary antiquarians and exiled scholars. My collection is viewed as a tad…'obscene' in the minds of many." Maury Dawson ran a finger over the spines of a shelf full of dusty old books, studying the faded titles as he wiped a layer of grime from his fingertip and nail. A thin smile crossed his lips as the seller took a seat in a creaky old chair. "Obscene how? As in pornographic? I hardly consider some of these ancient old books to be arousing. Or am I merely looking at the wrong section?" "Obscene in a different respect, Mr. Dawson. No religious outfit would be caught dead perusing anything in my catalogue, at least not any god-fearing denomination respected in normal society. No no, I appreciate my clientele, seeing as most of them are regulars, but that doesn't necessarily mean I'd care to befriend them. They keep me fed and sheltered and that is enough for me." Maury tipped the brim of his hat to expand his field of vision, glancing about the room at the myriad of long-forgotten tomes and manuscripts set about shelves and mislabeled brown boxes. He recognized none of the titles that were readily visible; many seemed to have been scrawled in German or French or some other such foreign or romantic language and those that were not simply appeared to be of such impeccable rarity he was not surprised to find them in such a remote location. "Blasphemous, the macabre at its very worst." Ellis's first words since entering the home were sudden and rang with uneasiness. He didn't relish this assignment in the least. "The works of godless exiles and madmen, Maury. If it's been denounced and banned by the Church, even unreasonably, it's probably here." Claude laughed before degenerating into a wheezing fit; it started out a jovial cackle before disintegrating into a hacking cough that Dawson inferred to be the result of years of excessive smoking. Ellis thumbed through the brittle pages of a leatherbound tome as the bookseller recovered, clearing his throat with a pound of the chest as he did so. "In a sense, I'm honored, Mr. Warrick. In my field, it says a lot for the dealer when customers think his collection to be so far-reaching. Alas, there are many books of which you speak that I cannot access. Some are held in private collections by exceedingly wealthy men, usually under lock-and-key, others lost in the distant corners of the world and assumed destroyed by crusading inquisitors and the ignorantly fearful. Then there are even others that I would never bring into this home; they are far too dangerous, not only in the vicious cut-throat types that may seek them, but the knowledge inscribed on their pages are of terrible subjects no man should ever discover, let alone put to paper for others to learn." Dawson's curiosity was peaked at this disclosure. With a weak smile he pulled an envelope from his pocket and thrusted it toward the queer little trader. "This is a description of what we seek. Our employer sent us to procure it." Metcalf's shift in attitude was sudden, unforeseen by the two expectant agents. He had perused the letter for only a moment before his shoulders slumped as if in defeat, knuckles whitening as he crumpled the note into a tight ball. His face had been drained, as white as the parchment the request was written on. "I cannot sell you that book. It's not for sale. How could one even know I had such a thing in my possession?"… Maury had feared such an altercation but was left with no options. He reached inside his jacket while a lump formed in his throat, his gaze changing from one of confuseion to steel in a moment. "Let me rephrase then. You have something in your possession, bookseller. Something I'm told doesn't belong to you." The gun that had appeared in Dawson's hand placed added emphasis to his words, his murderous glower moreso. "We've come for it." Dawson had ventured that the nervous little dealer would become even more skittish having the muzzle of a .38 caliber directed at him. In an odd twist, however, Metcalf seemed almost relieved as he noted the shimmering barrel of that well-oiled firearm, a bemused chuckle not withstanding as he fingered a book-shaped package wrapped in inconspicuous brown paper. Maybe it answered questions for him. "I'm regarded as a rather skilled negotiator, Claude." Dawson hoped the other wouldn't perceive the shake of the gun, or the quiver of his voice. He wasn't accustomed to drawing weapons in the face of disintegrating bargains, even during that rough period he still regretted. "This isn't in my nature. I implore you, sir. We have the money, we can pay for the book. I abhor violence, I assure you with every ounce of my word." Metcalf's eyes hastened to lock with those of Dawson. If he didn't know better, the gunwielder would have attributed the look to one of seething rage as opposed to fear. Betrayal, perhaps. "Neither of you want this for yourselves, though I expected such when I let you into my home. You're neonates, you know nothing of the power these words can unleash. Tell me…who sent you? What vile manner of man would want the secrets housed in this book?" Dawson could hear Ellis draw his own sidearm, a grunt accompanying the the click of the hammer being pulled back on his revolver. He knew little of the other man but didn't put it past him to pump a handful of rounds into the bookseller if he wouldn't willingly hand over what they'd come so far for; that lack of personal knowledge and the suspicion he had of his many new 'colleagues' was what made the ex-banker so wary of his accomplice. "We know little of him except that his intentions are true and good. To us, he goes by the name of Chaney." "Chaney?" A grubby hand passed over his grizzled visage in a manner so composed that Maury almost wondered if he knew what the gun was meant for. "So…that's what he calls himself now then, eh?" "Who?" The old bookdealer laughed mightily, and for a second Maury considered if the old codger truly was mad. "The Demon. Or so I've come to consider him. He was a regular for a pretty lengthy period of time there, then his business fizzled out. The books he used to buy from me…hideous volumes they were, I tell you. Manuscripts based around wild philosophies, rare old tomes penned by eccentrics and evil men who delved far too deeply into the arcane for their own sanity. Weird and cabalistic babble about what lurked in the dark corners of the Earth and the stars mostly, sometimes propaganda written about Christianity, Buddhism and Islam." Claude peered out the window at the sun vanishing behind the horizon, its dying rays doing little to penetrate the gloom that had overcome him. He was in shock, confounded as to how his secret was unhatched. "He seemed decent enough outside his tastes, but I sometimes wondered if that calm demeanor was just a veil for whatever evil intentions he harbored. No man of sound and well-meaning mind would delve into the forbidden and the infernal, not like what was in these books anyhow. And not that one you request." Metcalf was genuinely shaken by the script on that letter, a letter that neither Dawson nor Warrick had seen. "Perhaps the only reason I hold onto it as opposed to destroying it is because even among the destructive knowledge it holds, word says there might be keys to salvation obscured penned within. But I now wonder if I've embraced a false optimism all this time." Ellis Warrick sneered as he strode to the side of Dawson, pistol aimed at the torso of the stubborn bookdealer, his determination unflappable. "Enough bullshit. What's in the book, you little bastard? What scares you so much about a goddamn book anyway??" Maury already braced his ears for the shot to ring out. "Evocations, terrible sorcery, revelations about eons past and a desolate future. A requiem for and about things neither living nor dead. I implore you, gentlemen. Leave this place. You cannot allow this 'Chaney' to have this book. You would be dooming your souls." "But you said yourself that it has redeemable qualities!" protested Dawson, lowering his weapon to argue with the disturbed seller as his voice waned in volume. "Surely he wants it to read into those good things himself." How much hope was he vainly putting into their enigmatic benefactor? And after all the good had he already seen him perform… "It is only a theory, Mr. Dawson. So cryptic is the prose that those few who have braved its study say such things in hope that it may have some redemptive property. I'm skeptical but pray the same. Believe this, however…the horrors contained within far outweight any potential good it could ever do. Sinister forces went into creating what is chronicled across these pages and many say those sinister forces still watch over it, preventing its complete destruction. Perhaps I've also bought into that possibility enough that it pervades my inclination to burn the damn thing. Either way, you cannot have it." Dawson shook his head, turning to walk out as he holstered his weapon. "I won't argue with you, and I certainly have no plans to kill you. I bear enough guilt without adding more to my conscience. I refuse." "But I don't." Warrick gave his brief ultimatum and fired. The first blast caught the timid bookseller in the shoulder, the second just above the sternum before exiting out the back and splitting the leathery spine of another soiled hardback. He collapsed among a cyclone of papers, sliding to the ground with an anguished groan before life abruptly escaped him. Maury, his weapon clattering to the ground, stumbled against a shelf and sent out a cloud of dust billowing forth, settling on both himself and the corpse. A sick feeling welled up his stomach as he stared at the package still clutched in Metcalf's hand, the brown wrapping paper concealing its features. They had been told that the contents of his rarest of volumes was worth killing for, and Warrick had truly taken that to heart. He believed in Chaney as much as, if not moreso, than Dawson. He had been willing to kill one more time for it. Dawson absently flipped open the chamber of his own handgun, peering into the empty recesses as Ellis snatched up the parcel in its brown anonymity. He had never loaded the weapon, it was a deterrent only. Warrick had never had any such intention and no such remorse. Chaney was his Christ, and no sin could not be redeemed by their human savior if they followed his orders. Whereas Maury had begun to truly question who they followed, it dawned on him that Ellis Warrick, and likely some of the others, had no such doubt. He was all that stood between them and a life, and potential eternity, of barren desolation. He could feel the eyes of the bigger man, an ex-Marine as he recalled, bear down on him with uncertainty. His lack of conviction, his refusal to take a life at the order of another, bred mistrust in the burly killer at his side. "We all must sacrifice, Maury. Why not one more time? He'll save us, and he'll save the bookseller too. We'll be fine. Trust me." Maury Dawson felt emptiness. Closing his eyes, he wished he was back in that burning wreckage, with no Chaney to rescue him. He cursed those icy claws that pulled him free and that forked tongue forming its many lies. He didn't deserve redemption. * * * "Then you will not release them from this unholy campaign?" The clergyman adamantly pleaded with his visitor, however knowing how futile it was. He knew nothing of what Masquerade's conscripts were being ordered to do around the world, only that none if it was humanitarian. He certainly was ignorant of what was to happen in West Virginia only days following this encounter. "They're my willing disciples." He lay one hand on the robes of his friend, seeing the tears welled up in his eyes. He had always cared for the lives of those around him, a knight of God vanquishing demons so that the innocent would know peace. He respected him for such a fondness for his fellow man. "The day will come when you thank not only me for what I am doing, but them as well. Be faithful. For once, trust in what you don't know." "Then you condemn them to death, -----. Spiritual death beyond what you and I know on this Earth. Save them now, stop this. Don't let them continue down the path to darkness." Silence from the nameless puppet-master as he turned away from the father. His decision was made, and not one that would be altered by a follower of a God he had always held such lukewarm faith in. They didn't share the gospel anymore. "I will cleanse the human race of its impurities, with your boon or without it. Your prayers never rescued me and I doubt they will now work to stop me. I will help the world where your testaments have failed." "Is this for McFarlane? This newfound cruelty, your treacherous schemes? How much of what befell Rockwell was for your insidious cause, or for a tournament to swell your fool pride?" A pause, however momentary. "McFarlane has lost. The stars have foretold it. This is for what lays beyond, in the big picture she is an insignificant pest. For a man of the cloth, you're remarkably naive about what lies beyond tomorrow." He sighed a moment as the choral hymn blanketed the cathedral in a baritone hum. Looking away to the doors and into the dimly lit street where a wind had begun to whip up, he echoed the one question the other feared confronted with so much. "Will you tell them about me? Lead them to my abode yet again, to the gallows that so many claim I'm destined to appear come my inevitable end?" Another awkward silence. Conflict raged inside one of the entities as Kendall finally turned to look down on the shorter clergyman, the dutiful long-time friend of a man he regarded as his mentor. "We may no longer be allies, but I cannot betray you. Not even now." Masquerade could sense the internal struggle within the priest, faith clashing with duty. His heart was divided and it still pained him that they could not be cohesive anymore. Convicted to what they had shared for so many years, he had no fears for now. "How much longer do you plan to continue this charade, -----? The mask, the aliases, the deceit?" "As long as it still suits our ambitions. Goodbye, Randal." Donning the pallid mask once again, Masquerade and Kendall left the church in Philadelphia. The most perturbing aspect was that the other truly felt his goals were noble. That thought freed the tears that had long since begun to surface at the corners of his eyes. The priest fell to his knees one more time before the idol of Jesus Christ, still aware of the dark essence of his departed visitors. For his victims in the ring as well as out, the priest named as Randal prayed with every shred of conviction he had for them all. They would be forgiven, they would ascend to the throne of God, they would walk the brilliant pastures of Heaven arm-in-arm with the Lamb. Delivered from evil, washed from sin in the light. Amen. For now, the quintessential anti-hero continued on a collision course with destiny. Defying the very forces of creation itself, still he marched on with a veritable army at his back, carving a swath of destruction through all once implied to be his ultimate end. At this point, for Shanty Jones, Digital Mortality, and Mal Somers, it would take more than the faith of one weeping priest. It would have to. It certainly wasn't enough for Claude Metcalf, or Dawn McFarlane.
