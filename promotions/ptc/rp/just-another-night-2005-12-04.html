Just another night.

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 For a brief moment, it felt as if it was nothing more than another dream. Just another nightmare that he was going to awake from in a matter of moments, drenched in sweat and taking deep breaths to help calm himself down. But this was different. This was no nightmare. This, as much as he wished it were not, was reality slapping him square in the face. An awakening of sorts, from the choices that he had made over the last few months, choices that he never imagined he would have to make at such a young age. Yet, there was one of those choices sprawled out across the cool, dark asphalt of the deserted alleyway in which he stood. Frozen in fear, Jayce Bradley could not divert his gaze away from the thick, crimson liquid that slowly dripped from the tip of the polished stainless steel blade. "I want you to finish it." Even the cold, lifeless words that seemed to slither off the tip of a seemingly split tongue could not steal his attention away. It was as if his whole world had been paused, allowing Jayce to immerse himself in the sensation that coursed through his veins; stinging him like the venomous bite of a rattlesnake. It was a feeling that he had never felt before. It was a feeling that felt so out of place. A feeling that he yearned to never have again. Russell Braun could see the fear in his eyes. He had seen it so many times before, from other "recruits" of the family. They always felt that they were ready, eager to sacrifice themselves for the greater good. For the most part, much like this current situation, their eagerness to please seemed to cloud their judgment of the task they were asked to perform. Braun was a nihilist at heart. He despised everyone. Raised on a small farm between Pleasanton and Atascosa, Texas, Braun learned at an early age what it was to be prejudiced against the minorities that seemed to litter the surrounding cities. Like rabbits on a hot summer day, they seemed to be reproducing at an alarming rate. Living off welfare and other government assistance programs that were funded on the back of the white man. His burden if you will. With pure Aryan blood running through his veins, Braun recruited others like himself. Amassing a small army who’s thirst for the cleansing of the nation resembled his own. No less than fifteen to twenty men followed his every wish, praising him as a deity, and placing him above all else. So when he made his way toward Jayce with his arm extended, he expected Jayce to take it from his hand without question. "Take it," a firmness in his voice as he held the blade before him. Jayce did not respond; his eyes transfixed on the blade mere inches from his face. Neither of them paid any mind to the African American man that lay clutching his chest on the ground, a river of crimson forming around his limp frame; his life slowly fading with each strained breath. Quincy Tyler was a sophomore majoring in Political Science at the University of Incarnate Word, earning a partial scholarship for his three point four grade point average. Tyler was far from a genius, but he worked hard to get where he was. While his schoolwork was his main priority, he had to work two jobs to pay off the rest of his student loans, not to mention pay for his books, transportation, and off-campus residence. Yet, while he laid there choking on the blood that seeped into his lungs, he prayed for forgiveness. Not for himself, but for the two men that towered over him. "Father forgive them, for they know not what they do," he pleaded on their behalf. Knowing that no amount of prayer was going to change the path the Lord had already chosen for him. His hands soaked in the copper-scented fluid trickling through the wound in his chest, he heard one thing before his mind blacked out. "An eye for an eye…" The words echoed through Jayce’s mind, memories resurfacing from the deepest corners of his subconscious. He finally turned his attention to Braun, who stood there with his arm outstretched. "An eye for an eye…" he repeated to himself, as he replayed everything that had happened to him over the past few months. Six long months. The death of his brother, taken at an early age for a material item that someone could not live without. He remembered standing over his brother’s grave the day after the funeral, vowing to "make the bastard’s pay for what they did." "His brother killed yours," Braun whispered into his ear like the little red devil that stands upon your shoulder. "End his life. Take back what they took from you, and rid the World of another porch monkey that we need to support." The words pierced through his skull, driving him to take the blade within his grasp. He turned his attention to the black man, staring up at him with fear in his eyes, yet seemingly ready to meet his fate. The man was mumbling something under his breath, repeating a prayer over and over again. "Shut up," Jayce hissed. "Shut up now, before I shut you up." The anger was building up within him, a rage that he could barely keep bottled up within. In an instant, Jayce drove the bottom of his boot into the mouth of his wounded prey, a shower of ivory and crimson spraying out into the darkness. Jayce scraped the bottom of his boot against the asphalt, attempting to dislodge a tooth that had become embedded into the heel. Tyler continued to pray, infuriating Jayce even more. Braun stood back, smiling as he watched that same fire being lit with Jayce, that had been lit within him so many years ago by his father. Like a father taking his son on his first hunting trip, Braun was proud of the progress Jayce was making. It would not be long before Braun controlled him as he did all of the others, though he considered this one to be a bit special. Unlike the others, Jayce had a special talent. He had a natural ability that the others would never aspire to attain. A talent that could help spread his gospel across the United States, garnering even more followers with each cities tour. Braun had ‘accidentally’ bumped into Jayce after one of Jayce’s backyard wrestling brawls, seemingly in awe of the raw talent that Jayce possessed. At the time, Jayce paid him no mind, on his way to find the makeshift booker that had put the exhibition together so he could take his twenty dollars and be on his way. Already sweating profusely, Jayce was coming down with a case of the cold shakes when Braun had interrupted his progress. Nodding to the blonde haired man with ice blue eyes, Jayce pressed on; in dire need of his next fix. Braun noticed the profuse sweating and slight tremble that seemed to drive Jayce more than his own two feet, and knew the symptoms all too well. Crystal Meth. With a bit of lecturing, and the prospect of having a family that would care for him dangled in front of his eyes, Braun knew that Jayce would follow him like the others had. It would not take much before his ‘fatherly advice’ would soak into the young addict’s mind, positioning him as one of the Family’s deadliest weapons in the war for cleansing. As he stood there, watching Jayce make his way toward the African American teen collapsed on the ground before them -- knife in hand -- the culmination of his true objective was coming to life right before his eyes. A devilish grin spread across Braun’s features. As Jayce ran the sharp edge of the blade across Tyler’s neck, piercing the Adam’s Apple and causing him to choke to death on his own blood, Braun realized that remorse was something he was not going to have to worry about with Jayce. Proven further when Jayce stood and used a white handkerchief that was stuffed into his back pocket to wipe the un-pure blood from the blade of the dagger. Braun walked over and placed a hand on the shoulder of his young apprentice, like a father would do. "You did good, son," he hissed with the split tongue of a serpent, as Jayce cocked his head to his left to look into the eyes of his mentor. His Father. Without another word spoken between the two, they disappeared into the darkness of the alley. A darkness that haunts Jayce’s dreams on a nightly basis. "Jayce, my son…" The same nightmare that haunts him at this very moment. "…are you awake?" The soft voice of Russell Braun wakes him from his slumber, what little he gets. "It is nearly dark out. The time for cleansing is upon us." Through slightly blurred eyes, Jayce awakens to find his mentor staring down at him. His ice cold stare causing him to shiver a bit at the sight. "I’m ready," was the response Braun received from the sluggish form of his apprentice, leisurely rising to his feet from the lumpy old chair in which he dozed off for a bit. Craning his neck in a semi-circle to stretch and release the slight tension in his neck, Jayce could feel the inaudible pops as the bones in his neck cracked in relief. "Just let me get my boots on, and I’ll be ready to go." Another night. Another hunt. "What do you feel like having tonight," Braun asked as he stared down at his own reflection in the polished stainless steel blade of the twelve inch survival knife that he held in his hand. "Mexican… Chinese… A nigger maybe?" The word alone brought a smile to his aging features, the wrinkles of time creasing his Aryan white flesh. "Does it even matter," Jayce questioned from the other room, a slight grunt being heard as he pulled on one of his boots. "Not really." Braun nearly licked his lips as he slid the knife back into it’s sheath, snapping the button that locked it into place before stuffing it into a black duffel bag. The same duffel bag Jayce packs his wrestling gear in when he has a match. Noticing that something heavy was in the bag, Braun reaches in and pulls out a piece of black leather with a large golden plate fastened to it’s face. "You didn’t tell me you won a title," Holding it up by one end in front of him, as Jayce walked back into the room. "Didn’t think you’d care much, to be honest." "What do you mean I wouldn’t care," Braun questioned, his eyebrow raised in query of the remark that Jayce made. "This is great, son…" Son. Just the sound of his voice caused Jayce to cringe, but hearing Braun calling him son brought a bit of vomit into his throat. "I told you you’d be a natural at this," Braun continued. "Did you beat up that slant eyed chink that gave you trouble the other night?" "No, I beat Varsity." "The football chump with the nigger for a friend?" Jayce nodded in response, pulling a black Carhart chore coat over his midnight blue tee shirt. "Serves the bastard right for turning his back on the pure race. Did you give him that curb stomp you were working on?" With eagerness in his voice, Braun imagined the vicious beating that Jayce had unleashed on the sinner. "I’m still not using the curb stomp," Jayce replied as he buttoned up the coat. "You should’ve broke that sinner’s jaw, at least." Jayce pulled a black skull cap over his head as he made his way to where Braun stood, glancing at the GCW Cruiserweight title before taking it from Braun’s hands. "You shouldn’t have missed." "What’s done is done, son," There’s that word again. "We need to move on from that, and focus on what lies ahead. Focus on what still needs to be done to accomplish our goal." "Our goal…" Jayce scoffed. "We’re nothing more than a couple of murderers. You think that basing it on race changes that?" "What’s happening to you, son," Braun asked as he brushed his hand along Jayce’s shoulder. "Don’t let your mind be clouded with doubt, Jayce. We are no more murderers than Bush is, eliminating those that would slay us when our backs are turned. Cleansing the World is what the Father asks of us, who are we to doubt his Divine Word?" "Well you’re going to be cleansing on your own for a few weeks," Jayce replied, grabbing the keys to the car off the top of the counter. "I have to go wrestle in some tournament." Lifting the duffel bag onto his shoulder, Braun heads toward the door. "It’s quite alright, I was thinking we’d take a little break anyway. We don’t need anyone to catch on to our pattern, realizing that the death’s occur during the same time frame as your wrestling promotion being in town." "Yeah, we wouldn’t want that." "This tournament," Braun inquires as he removes the chain lock that secures the door. "Is it going to be hard?" Jayce shrugs his shoulders, as Braun twists the door knob. "Not really sure, never heard of the guy I’m facing before." "Must not be too good." "That’s probably what he’s saying about me." The two exit the house, headed for the late model Ford sedan that was parked in front of the hotel room. Dark blue, almost black, with nothing to draw extra attention to the vehicle. "The only thing I know," Jayce continued. "Is that he called himself a legend of the sport. He’s some sort of former champion or something." "Still doesn’t mean he’s any good," Braun responded with a sly grin. "He could be the best wrestler among a bunch of crappy ones in some no name wrestling organization for all you know." "And I take it being on the same roster as Seymour Almasy, Michael Stevens, Rich Rollins, and Lestatt Knight gives me a bit of credibility." Opening the trunk, Braun tosses the duffel bag in before stepping in himself. "No, but at least you don’t pride yourself on beating mediocre talent." "I guess you have a point. By the way," Jayce walks over, his hands on the trunk lid. "I feel like Mexican." The words of his protégé brought a smile to Braun’s lips, as he watched Jayce slam the trunk closed. Just another night. Like any other hunt.
