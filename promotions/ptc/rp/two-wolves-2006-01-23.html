Two wolves

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-23 One evening an old man told his grandson about a battle that goes on inside all people. He said, "My son, the battle is between two ‘wolves’ that live inside us all. One is evil; it is anger, envy, jealousy, sorrow, regret, greed, arrogance, self-pity, guilt, resentment, inferiority, lies, false pride, superiority, and ego. The other is good; it is joy, peace, love, hope, serenity, humility, kindness, benevolence, empathy, generosity, truth, compassion and faith." The grandson thought about it for a minute and then asked his grandfather the question that was plaguing his thoughts. "Which wolf wins, grandfather?" The old man simply replied, smiling, as he ruffled the young boy’s hair, "the one that you feed, Ayashe Hakan." - - - - - - - - - - - It all started with an envelope. Just one small, white rectangle and my World had started to fall to pieces. Everything that I had worked so hard for over the preceding year, everything that I had sacrificed and put myself through, was forgotten as quickly as the pain from the paper-cut as I peeled open the envelope and read the contents. It continued with a scream. I know, now that I think about it, that it came from this mouth, from this body; just as I know that it wasn’t me that voiced the despair that vented its impotency in such a vocal manner. When you play the panpipes who, or what, is responsible for the music that you hear? The player or the pipes themselves? The scream was the music and I was nothing more than the pipes. The shock of the moment, the realisation that the past had moved forward into the future to become my here and now, as I read the three small letters that would act as the first of the dominoes to fall. GTT. So inconsequential on their own, nothing more than letters from the alphabet. Without knowing what they actually stood for most people would be forgiven for raising a quizzical eyebrow as they stood with a blank expression. Even knowing that they stood for the single most important tournament in the wrestling industry wouldn’t lead most people to a catatonic parade down darkened City streets, finally awakening in the middle of a horde of shocked onlookers as yet another scream rents the silence of the night. I am not most people however and this time, of course, I will acknowledge that the scream was purely my own. When you have seen something that you thought you had buried, literally, come back to haunt you it can sometimes be difficult to remain as calm and collected as expected. It was easier to call what followed nightmares than it was to explain to Khaled exactly what was really happening. My one, my true, my only friend. Khaled wouldn’t have understood. How could he? How could I expect him to take in the fact that I wasn’t dreaming, I wasn’t having a nightmare of a twisted plane of reality that I half-recollected but that instead I was actually there? How could I tell him that the ‘strange woman’ as he called her, the thing that he assumed to be figment of my mind, a symptom of whatever it was that ailed me, sang out to my soul and rang chords in my memory that I didn’t even know existed. I knew her without knowing how, just as I knew one thing beyond a shadow of a doubt. She was real. Very, very real. How could I tell him that? I couldn’t, off course, so I didn’t. I let him continue to help me with my training for a tournament that I didn’t want to be part off but could not afford to lose. I allowed him to watch me as I slept, knowing that he thought that he was protecting me but in reality all he was doing was helping himself. His presence did nothing more than comfort me. It didn’t stop the fragments of memory from coming back to me; it didn’t stop the images from appearing before my eyes whenever I closed them, nor did it stop the emotions from suffocating me. Not my own, that I could have handled, but someone else’s. They say it never rains but it pours, don’t they? For me it was turning into a flood of Biblical proportions. Sleep-walking during the day, being pulled into a dream realm that I knew was real, talking to a woman that no-one else could see and that I didn’t know but knew that I did, and feeling someone else’s terror, someone else’s despair. Surely that would be enough for anyone, wouldn’t it? Anyone but me it seems, because as much as I hated the tournament that I was part of I hated losing all the more. The WWA powers that be, it seemed, had decided to enter me to fly their flag, so to speak, and didn’t bother with the pleasantries of actually informing me in advance. Part luck and part fortune meant that I progressed through the first three rounds relatively unscathed. What was going on in my personal life was put to one side, not easily but at least enough so that I could face the challenges thrown my way and overcome them. Andi the Magnificent, Cyrus Skar and finally the PCW legend, Darkstar, himself. In the ring, with no thoughts to phantom women or grey filled landscapes, I fought them all; and I triumphed. Victory, it seemed, wasn’t quite enough though. Somehow word of my ‘predicament’ had leaked back to the WWA and they, in their infinite wisdom, had decided that they did not want the liability of having a man with a dubious grasp on reality representing them any longer. To protect them, while claiming it was in my own best interests, of course, they gave me an ultimatum. Drop out of the GTT, drop the WWA World title, and take a vacation; a long vacation. That or get medical help. I couldn’t help but smile as I realised that I had been played at my own game. They knew that while they couldn’t control me, could not dictate to me, that faced with those two choices I was really left with none at all. I wouldn’t drop out of the tournament and I wouldn’t drop my title. That wasn’t the sort of person that I was. I may not know exactly what sort of person I was anymore, but I knew that I wouldn’t do that. So I decided to seek the medical help that they had forced on me as a condition of continuing in the tournament and competing for the WWA; but I did so on my terms. I knew that if any details of what was going on in my life was to slip out I would be finished. I could just imagine the reaction of the doctor if I was to mention daytime somnambulism, or dreamscapes that were more real to me than reality, or the woman whose face was as burned into my mind as the scratches that her nails had burned into my face. So I knew that whoever I saw would have to be someone that I could talk my way around, could twist to my way of seeing things; or not seeing at all. Finding the only registered psychologist in the city who was also blind was a stroke of luck, and I knew from the moment that I set eyes on her that she would never – literally – see me coming. I would have no problems getting through this medical test, just as I would have no problems continuing thereafter on my journey through the tournament itself. One on one or three on one, it wouldn’t matter. I had it made now, nothing could go wrong. That was the last thought on my mind as I stepped foot into the shrink’s office and came face to face with an improbability, an enigma; my jaw dropped open as I saw the woman from my dreams made real, and stuttered inarticulately as she smiled through her words. - - - - - - - - - - - "Cat got your tongue, Kutnahin?" Stumbling backwards I feel heavily into the seat behind me, all my vaunted agility lost in my desire to escape. Adrenaline pumped through my veins and I felt a rush of nausea wash over me as the vestigial fight or flight part of my brain kicked into overdrive. I didn’t know why this woman should affect me that way but she did, just as I knew that I didn’t have to choose between the two warring sides of my brain. I didn’t want to fight; I just wanted to run and never look back. "Ember, what is it, what is going on?" I barely glanced over at Carole Gordon, the psychologist that I had thought would be my saviour simply because she wouldn’t be able to see me. I barked out a peal of laughter that nearly rose into hysteria but thankfully I swallowed it down, tasting bile as I did so. I didn’t waste any time in replying to her, not sure that I would be able to had I wanted. "Ember" queried the black clothed woman, her eyes blinking slowly, "is that what you are calling yourself?" Her voiced filled me with a mixture of emotions and feelings. I felt a rush of electricity course through my body, my nerve endings tingling as a very physical reaction started. I felt blood coursing through my loins and myself swelling in readiness but revulsion filled me. Tears filled my eyes as I crouched over myself, hiding my embarrassment, not knowing why I should be feeling like this, reacting like this. "Well Kutnahin – or Ember if you prefer" the woman almost purred as a lascivious grin spread over her face, "it seems that I haven’t lost my effect anyway." "Ember," Carole almost cried out, her voice rising with a touch of anxiety or fear, "talk to me, what is happening?" I glanced over at her and saw that she was leaning forward, her head cocked to one side as if listening to the words flying around her. One hand was clenched, tightly, around the white cane, her knuckles matching that colour with strain. "Who are you" I managed to croak out, ignoring Carole’s cries as I focussed all of my attention on the woman I front of me, the woman who shouldn’t – couldn’t – possible be there, "what do you want?" "Now there is a very familiar question!" The woman burst out laughing, her voice raucous like nails pulled down a chalkboard and I felt my hackles rise and my spine shiver as a glint of recollection came to me. I knew this woman, I remembered this woman, but from where, from when? I couldn’t grasp the fleeting thread of memory as it rushed away from me and I stared up at the woman, tears of frustration flowing freely from my eyes. She glanced at me and then stared at me, catching me with her eyes. Fly like, in the web that her gaze wove, I was trapped and as I stared into the pools of darkness that were her eyes I saw a flash of yellow fire and then I was falling, falling, falling … - - - - - - - - - - - Stepping out of the water the young girl shivered as the moonlight washed over her. The night was as still as it could be and not even a solitary owl’s hoot broke the silence as she brushed her flowing hair away from her body and back over her head. The spray of water landed on the surfaced of the gently flowing river and sent ripples dancing, the reflected full moon being dissected into many moving parts. As her hair, darkened to a lustrous black, clung to her naked body and with her teeth chattering, she walked a few steps from the river’s edge and crouched down beside from large rocks, huddling close to a small fire that was there. Leaning in she gently blew on the flames, fanning them until they rose up a little more and caught light again. With a satisfied sigh she relished the warmth as it filled her bones, reaching out to grab the blanket that she had brought with her, wrapping it around her shoulders. A noise off to one side, past the rocks and deeper into the woodland that surrounded the secluded part of the river where the flow had slowed enough to form a deep pool, caught her attention and she snapped her head around, apprehension on her face. A sigh, part relief and part wonder, escaped her mouth as she realised that it wasn’t her father come looking for her after all, he was probably still fast asleep back at the camp secure in the knowledge that his warning to her to stay close had been respected. The lure of the river and the midnight swim, however, had simply been too much; and now, wonders of wonders, something more had been given to her on this special day. Her fifteenth summer had been reached and she was now, in the eyes of her people and even her father, finally a woman. The gods were obviously looking down on her, she thought, as a smile lit up her face. The small wolf cub paused at the edge of the trees, his dark eyes twinkling in the moonlight as he sniffed at the air. His tongue lolled out to one side of his mouth as he placed one paw, ever so slowly, out from the shadows and onto the moonlit grass in front of him. His tail was between his legs, his hackles raised slightly, but nonetheless he could see the water in front of him and could almost taste it. Scampering forward, his caution overcome by his thirst, the cub dipped his head into the river and lapped up the water. The young girl held her breath, not daring to breathe for fear of scaring off the beautiful creature in front of her. She didn’t know if it was simply that the cub was in the awkward stage between youth and adulthood, innocence and growth, like she herself, or because of the moonlight that played off the creature’s fur to make him sheen like molten silver and fill her with wonder, but she knew that this was a special moment, one that she would never be able to recapture, no matter how long she would live. Knowing that fact she made the decision and, as with the cub earlier, she threw caution to the wind. Moving as slowly and carefully as she could she crept towards the river; towards the cub. Without turning around its ears rose up, swivelling slightly as it picked up the almost inaudible sound of her feet as they moved across the grass. It didn’t stop drinking, however, its young inexperience telling it that it was safe to drink no matter what its senses were trying to warn it about. It had never encountered man before, and so wasn’t afraid of it. The girl reached out one hand tenderly, awe filling her face as she realised that she was only a hands-span away from one of her people’s totems made real. Wolves were rare in this area, and rarer still was it to get this close to one. A small gust of hair blew from behind her, trilling a loose piece of her hair around her face. The wolf wrenched its head up from the water; his nose finally catching the scent that his senses had warned him was close. Spinning around a low growl vibrated from his chest and the wolf cub stared into the hazel eyes of the girl in front of him, his muzzle scant millimetres from her out-reached hand. Time froze as the air, already still, become motionless. The gazes of the wolf cub and the girl met and for a second, the briefest of instants, a connection was made. The blood hit the young girls face before she even registered the fact that half of the wolf cubs head had vanished, simply exploded in a red mist. The sound, like a crack of thunder, followed a second later but the girl didn’t react to it, shocked into insensibility as the blood of the cub ran down her face and into her mouth. The smell, like eggs that had gone off, assaulted her nostrils but still she didn’t move. Her mind was stuck in a fugue state, caught in the moment between the wolf cub staring at her with eyes of dark trust and the next when it simply ceased to be. "Well now, what do we have here?" Without conscious thought the young girl slowly turned her face and watched as three men crossed the river. One of them, the tallest of the three who came across the river first, held a rifle in one hand. Her eyes followed the stream of smoke that rose up from the barrel without comprehension, her gaze unfocussed. It was he that spoke, his voice gruff, as he walked over and kicked the cub out of the way, kneeling in front of the girl with a smile on his face; no warmth or emotion in his piercing green eyes, just the façade of a smile. "I said, what do we have here girly?" he asked as he reached out a callused hand and ran it down the side of her face. The girl shivered, this time not because of the forgotten cold, at his touch and pulled away but was stopped short as the man took hold of her hair and held her in place. She whimpered and tried to pull away but the man was too strong. Holding the blanket tightly around her the girl felt tears come to her eyes, and blinked them away as she stared at the wolf cub that was being thrown between the other two roughly dressed men. "Why?" was all that she could whimper, her eyes locked to the near headless carcass. The man who held her followed her gaze and then looked back at her, licking his lips, as he looked her up and down. Staring deeply into her eyes he fixed his gaze on her own, and at that moment the girl’s connection to the wolf cub came back to her and she knew that its fate would soon be hers. "Why" he repeated, mockingly, "simple; because I can." "Who are you" The girl cried, starting to struggle as she felt the green eyed man lean in towards her, tasting the stale coffee on his breath as he drew closer. "What do you want?" The only answer that was forthcoming was a chilling laugh as the man threw his rifle towards one of the other men, who had stopped to watch the proceedings. Roughly grabbing the blanket the man threw her to the ground and a scream was choked off before it had a chance to live. "What are they doing, Raven?" The voice came from the rocks above the river, but none of the men seemed to notice. "Throw her in the river" he stated as simply as if he was talking about the weather, "let it take care of getting rid of her." The two men scampered over and grabbed the girl by the ankles, dragging her threw the flames. Her hair caught fire, flames running up the locks faster than could be imagined. With a scream of agony the girl started to struggle, her panic giving her strength that would otherwise have been impossible. "Oh, pretty colors!" the boy crowed with glee, clapping his hands as he watched the fire take over the girls head. "No!" The scream rang out through the night air and all motion stopped. The flames hung statue like on the girls head as the men froze in mid-motion, pantomime like. Even the river ceased its motion as Ember ran forward, throwing himself at the young girl to tackler her to the ground, to roll her on the earth, to put out the flames; to save her. At least that is what he attempted, but in reality as soon as the scream of despair and grief left his mouth it was too late; as soon as he launched himself at the girl it was already over. Everything faded from his view and he was left, sprawled, on the ground, grey dust filling his mouth. Tears rolled down his face, flowing out from beneath his mask, as he clambered to his knees and saw the dark clothed woman, Raven, standing in front of him. Her hands were on her hips and her head was cocked to one side as she stared at him with her black, colourless, eyes quizzically. "Why," he croaked out through a throat closed with grief, "why didn’t you help her?" the question tore at him, he didn’t know the girl but he felt that he should have helped her. He had been forced to watch the scene as it unfolded, watching as the boy and the woman, Raven – even though she stood by him at the same time – as they watched it too. His mind couldn’t comprehend how he was both watching Raven and standing beside her, but that didn’t matter. Helping the girl did. It was all that mattered. He flinched as Raven reached down and placed one hand on his shoulder, drawing him to his knees as she crouched down beside him. She ran one hand over his masked face, pulling at a tangle in his hair as she smiled at him. "Oh my darling boy," she trilled, carefree, "you are full of wonders. A better question would be to ask why you didn’t help her when you could, but tried to help her when you couldn’t." Leaning in she brushed her lips against Ember’s own, breathing in deeply as she did so. With a sigh she leant back and rocked on her heels, licking her lips slowly as a smile formed on her mouth. "Ah, I see" she murmured to herself, "that explains a lot." Staring directly at Ember, the black and bottomless eyes filled his vision and once more he felt himself falling forward. Reaching out he tried to stop himself but vertigo rushed through him and he knew that it was hopeless; but still he tried. Dropping to his knees and screamed out, trying to hold himself together and then stopped, listening to the laughter that rang out around him. - - - - - - - - - - - Opening his eyes, getting his bearings, he found himself back in the psychologist’s office, on his hands and knees in front of the chair that he had been sitting on. Raven was still perched on the table in front of him, her expression knowing as she stared at Ember, waiting, seemingly, for him to make the first move. As he pulled himself slowly to his feet she nodded and stepped towards him. "I don’t know what game you are playing" she whispered as she placed her head against his own, her tongue flicking out to lick his ear lobe, "but at least I know the players now." She patted Ember gently on the side of the face, staring into his eyes again before walking towards the closed doorway. Reaching out one hand she grasped the handle and then turned to face him once more. "You know," she stated, as she looked him up and down "you really don’t suit that!" Turning to face the door she took a step forward and then simply vanished. Ember felt another wave of nausea rush threw him as his mind struggled to process everything that he had gone through, everything that he had seen. People don’t just vanish he thought to himself, and then started to laugh. Then again people don’t see history play out in front of them either. "Ember?" The psychologist’s shaky voice brought him, almost, back to his senses and he bit of the hysteria that he felt rising within him before it could take hold. She was still sitting on the chair, still clutching the cane between her hands, and her face was filled with a mixture of fear and incomprehension. "Ember" she repeated shakily, "what just happened?" Getting to his feet Ember stumbled over, his legs like lead, to Carole and knelt down beside her, half falling against the chair. She reached out to steady him and gasped as her hands connected with his latex covered face. So much for trying to get an easy medical pass Ember thought wryly to himself, realising that the mask was now the least of his worries. He didn’t know how much Doctor Gordon had heard, or could have heard, but he knew that she knew too much. "Are you wearing a mask" she asked in confusion, "is that what she meant about it not suiting you?" Ember sighed softly, Carole’s question confirming that she had indeed heard Raven’s words. He didn’t know how it was possible, but he didn’t need to, he just knew that there was only one thing that he could possibly do now; tell her the truth. Tell her everything. "She wasn’t talking about the mask," he said as he settled back on his heels, holding his head in his hands, breathing hard as he forced the words out, "she was talking about the body I am wearing." ~tbc~
