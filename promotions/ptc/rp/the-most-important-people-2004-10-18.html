The Most Important People

18 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-18 The GTT Coliseum, normally at full capacity, was empty on this Monday evening. Just days before, it was filled with boisterous cheering and jeering from all of the seventy thousand plus attendants that had been amassed thus far for the GT Tournament. Three rounds into the Primetime Central's most prestigious tournament, and three rounds at full capacity. Round four begs to be no different with all six brackets in their semifinal phases. However, the atmosphere seems calm at this moment. Hardly any of the other fighters have been seen, and the technical crew that is usually so busy fixing the arena is gone. Silence and desertion took form in the Coliseum now. It was completely empty save for the lone man sitting halfway up the seats. Sebastian Moraco slouched down in the seat with his head buried in a Maxim magazine. A few feet away from him another figure made its way through the seats. The overzealous black suit gave him away: it was [name withheld]. Jeff, Sebastian's assistant coach in his collegiate wrestling team, made his way towards Sebastian, taking a seat next to him. "This is a pretty big arena, isn't it?" He asked "Yeah, guess so." Sebastian never looked up from the magazine nor gave Jeff a courtesy look. He just continued to bury his face in the magazine not wanting Jeff to bother him any longer. "I knew I'd find you here..." "What do you want?" "To congratulate you on a most impressive win." Jeff remarked. "Digital Mortality was considered to be one of the best around, and you managed to edge him by. I admit it looked bad at first, but you got the win in the end. It was a close match, damn close match." "Thanks." Sebastian replied nonchalantly. "He made some mistakes. I made some mistakes. I just capitalized on more of them, and I got the win. It's over now. I told you before I'm used to these kind of things." Jeff through a white envelope on top of Sebastian's magazine, interupting his docile reading. "You aren't used to this. At least I don't think you are." He added in. "Open it up, things don't get any easier from this point on." Sebastian closed his magazine and tore open the envelope. It was an official notice from the GTT Committee about Round Four pairings and the timeline for the matches. Halfway down the notice, Sebastian read aloud the matchup he was involved in. "Elia Smith vs Sebastian Moraco." Sebastian tossed the notice aside. "I already knew about this. It's going to be another match." "Another match?" Jeff asked dumbfoundedly. "You can't possibly just treat this as another match." "Why, because I'm fighting a woman?" "Some federations don't even allow women." "Like the WWA?" Sebastian retorted. "They deal with their own situations. You knew from the start that there were going to be some unusual pairings in this tournament. We have three females in the fourth round. Don't you think it's saying something? We're all the same here. We're fighters. There is no male, or female, or human, or demon. We're all the same." "I don't want you getting bad publicity for fighting a woman." Jeff scolded. Sebastian had enough. He didn't expect Jeff to have any decency in him, or to understand the situation. Yet, he hoped Jeff would come around and see that this isn't the 1800's anymore. Sebastian sighed loudly in disgust, picked up his magazine and stood up from his seat. "Don't talk to me anymore, Jeff." Sebastian demanded. "I'll handle this on my own." "Where are you going?" "I have to make a call." "Who could you be calling at this moment?" Jeff demanded to know. "You're getting this close to having your name on the United States team for the world championships. You got to be focused, Sebastian." Sebastian laughed in somewhat of a courtesty manner. "I am focused. Round four of this so called prestigious tournament is the product of focus, isn't it? The real problem here is that you aren't focused. Goodbye." The Most Important People Sebastian's room was just like all the other rooms that had been provided for the GTT competitors. At least for those who accepted. Nearly all of them were professional wrestlers with at least moderate success. Even the less wealthy ones had their own laboratories where they planned their schemes for the next round. When he first checked into his room, Sebastian thought that he was the only one who accepted the suite provided for him at the Four Seasons. It was with good reason too. The other fighters, he had learned, were all averaging well into the high hundred thousands a year in salary. That was just their normal pay and didn't count incentives, or bonuses like commercial deals and other endorsements. After all he was the only amateur wrestler in the entire tournament. The only one to begin with and still the only one left. Quite a feat accomplished for someone who had to learn what a 'headlock' was just days before his first match. Sebastian was always a fast learner. He was always a hard fighter, and fought with a lot of heart and determination. But the best quality he thought he had, was that he always fought with a clear mind. There was never any dilemma going on. Never was there any concerns about who he was fighting, or if he was doing the wrong things. People do things for a reason, and things happen for a reason, he always believed. This match was going to be no different. Elia Smith was enjoying success as much as Sebastian did. Even if their reasons were different, they still found themselves in the same situation. Sebastian opened up the mahogany curtains to take a gaze at the D. C. skyline. He pulled up a seat by the window and grabbed the phone. Moments later the phone was answered on the other side. "Hello?" Sebastian asked. "It's me, Sebastian." "..." "I'm doing fine, Mom." "...." "Yeah, I got some pictures. I took pictures the first day I got here." "..." "Oh, don't worry I'll get more. Hey, I'll tell you what, I'll repeat my first day here and take pictures of everything." "..." "It's going fine. I told you, Mom, it's just the school thing. We're training for the season." What Sebastian told his parents was that he was being sent to D. C. because of school and his collegiate team. Which, if you had been paying attention, was far from the truth. He was indeed sent to D. C. What he will do in D. C. will affect his collegiate team. However, it wasn't the reason for his departure to D. C.. Sebastian was in D. C.because of the Primetime Central's GT Tournament. It's most prestigious tournament, and some argue the most prestigious tournament in all networks. However, Sebastian didn't tell his parents that. Not there was anything wrong with it, but his parents had already expressed great disdain over a hypothetical question. They disliked fighting, worse they disliked glorified fighting. Their child was going to be a college graduate first, a worker second, and a violent fighter never. So, how could he break it to them? He couldn't. He only hoped that the publicity he was receiving would be limited. "Aaron didn't come, Mom. He's on the basketball team. They got their own training to do." "...." "The food's alright. They gave us all separate rooms so I eat what I want." "Yeah, Mom. I'll watch what I eat." "..." "The rooms are nice. Very spacy." "..." "Yeah, everything's fine, Mom. I just wanted to let you know I was doing well. I think this year they might make me captain. You know with Ryan being expelled and all." "..." "Oh, no I don't hate him. It's just...well he isn't on my Christmas card list." "..." "I will, Mom. Is Dad home, yet?" "..." "Still working? It's like almost ten. What's he doing? Putting in more overtime?" Sebastian was upset. He was used to his father working overtime, and this time his demeanor was less obvious. Sebastian's parents were never well off, and his ability to go an accredited universary was the result of countless scholarships he had been offered and a combination of salaries from his parents and himself. Of course, loans also played a big part in Sebastian's enrollment to NYU. However, before Sebastian managed to get a loan, his father worked overtime every workday. His justification was that he was doing for the family. To help support them. The negative side to that was that he spent less and less time with his wife and son. There were times when Sebastian wouldn't see his father for a week, and when he did it was always in uneasy silence. Sebastian hated it and wanted those sacrifices to stop. Yet, he knew he couldn't stop it. His father was a man providing for his family, and right now the best Sebastian could do was to finish college like his father wanted to. "Well, just tell him I called and I'm doing fine. I got a lot of pictures and I'll get some souvenirs, I promise." "..." "Goodbye, Mom. Love you." Sebastian hung it and turned on the TV. He flipped through the channels before finally deciding to leave it on the cartoon network. He needed something to cheer him up. Despite Jeff's constant nagging and spiel about this being their effort to get him back into the spolight, it felt more like he was alone. With each passing round it was becoming more clear to Sebastian that Jeff had an ulterior motive. He just didn't know what it was. Sebastian thought about going to bed early, but then decided to stay up. Not to watch cartoons, but to make another call to New York. There was no way he was going to go to bed without talking to his father. The man who made him what he is now. It is said that men are measured by the quality of their friends. But it's the father whom molds you into the man you are to be. Sebastian knew that and believed that. After hearing about Elia Smith's father passing away in the earlier rounds of the GTT, Sebastian knew he couldn't take anything for granted. He already was spending less time with his father, talking less, seeing him less, and he didn't want any part of it. He didn't want to be alienated from his father at any point, especially not in his dying days. Sebastian's father was in good health, but then again so were many others. When you see someone lose their father, you start to wonder if you're wasting valueable time. Sebastian knew that work was starting to alienate them and he sought to fix that. Many deep sympathies for Elia Smith. Her loss was a tremendous loss, but she opened the eyes of countless others. Win or lose, Sebastian would remember the most important thing about his round four match with Elia Smith: Her loss woke him up. Nothing Jeff could make up, or prize the committee would offer would overshadow Sebastian's chance to reconcile with the most important people in the world. His parents.
