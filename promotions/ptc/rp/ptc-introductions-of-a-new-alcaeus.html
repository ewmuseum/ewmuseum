Introductions of a new Alcaeus

26 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The sun rises this morning…and I am its silent witness. Has nothing changed these past centuries? A lonely figure, perched upon the ancient building. In the darkness, he almost looked like part of the fixture…a forgotten statue, the diamond in the eye of some age-old dream that Architects often have occasion to dream. But of course…this was no statue. No statue yet at least. His eyes wondered the lonely streets of French Quarter New Orleans from his temporary castle…scanning for something. Only his eyes seemed to move, the rest of him almost scarily still…not even the movement one takes for granted as air is deposited from and into the lungs. But then a clean white hand raised, pushing through the golden hair of this statuesque beast of observation. The movement stopped, when on a lonely street, not two blocks away, he found her. He found his Bellezza Giovanile. She strode through the dark streets with a purpose, but her life lacked meaning. The furrowed brows, the way her hand lay loose beside her, the other latched almost uncaring onto her small black bag. In her lazily chosen outfit, she still looked like a beauty…but the silent words she spoke told stories of a wasted life. The love that got away, the pain of lost ones, the fruitless marriage and the thankless job. Yes…she was perfect. On any other night, she may have escaped the gaze of this lifeless monster, but not tonight… Tonight, My beauty, you’re mine The figure was gone… ______________________________________________________________ Marie Des Jardins was late. She was always late. But what made it even worse, she didn’t care. It wasn’t as if she wasn’t used to what her boss was gonna say. That fat grease Monkey never gave her a break anyway, whether she was late or on time. Screw him, she thought. I’m gonna take my time. She casually took a cigarette from the bag at her side. She’d given up smoking some six months ago, but the last two weeks had been rough. She no longer cared what the doctors would say…or what the TV would say in its hypocritical melodrama about the significance of smoking on this or that state of health. The cigarette lit effortlessly, and the drag that flew down into her lungs may have saved her an evening of grief. It was cold though…so cold. Untypically so for New Orleans. After the terrible floods, it hadn’t been the same. Lazy bureaucrats she thought. They were all the same, these men in grey suits…taking from the city, and never giving anything back. Just like the clients at her club. Sometimes they would sit there…staring. It was her job, but she didn’t do it for free…sometimes she wondered why she did it at all, but at this point in her life, dignity was not of too much concern to her. She decided to pick up the pace; something about the cold and her memories spooked her ever so slightly. Mary des Jardins hat flew off of her head without warning. An unexpected gust of wind must have pushed the damned thing off of her head, can anything else go wrong tonight? Oh Michael…if only… her hat had landed in a puddle of dirty rain-water – typical. She leaned over, careful not to get her thin dress wet (she wore her stage clothes to work these days…it was so much easier. Besides, it was only 2 blocks from her house, nothing could go wrong en route). The small black hat was stuffed carelessly into her black bag, along with all her other worldly values. But something told Mary she was not alone… Turning abruptly back where she’d come, Mary was relieved to see that no one was there. How embarrassing, she thought. She let out a sigh…maybe it would have been good to have some company…even a lazy cutthroat to take her bag…anything was better than her dull, inspirationless life. She turned back to continue her journey, but was astonished to crash face first into what felt like a brick wall. She fell back suddenly, onto her ass. Oh God, my dress…now that bastard’s really gonna kill me! She looked up, ready to give a brick wall a good thrashing with her handbag for being so unreasonable, but standing there in front of her was what appeared to be a man. She rubbed her eyes with her fists, as if to reassure herself that she wasn’t seeing things…where could this man have come from? It was at least forty meters to the end of the alley, and she’d only bent down for a second. Time plays tricks on us all…she hadn’t thought that, but heard it in her mind as clear as her own thoughts. Yes, and I’m late she replied almost mechanically. The man, standing before her towered over her…some six feet five she would have guessed. His blonde mane flowed to his shoulders, where it spread like a waterfall onto a briskly turned out suit jacket. He wore a Burberry tie, finely stitched, and what looked like a fitted white shirt. And sunglasses…why sunglasses…maybe he was blind. No, my dear…not blind. Voices in her head now…this was some evening. The man offered her a hand gracefully, and she accepted, lifting herself to her feet on his strength. She quickly brushed herself off, feeling embarrassed to make an acquaintance in present circumstances. “Thank you. I’m sorry for bumping into you like that, I wasn’t looking where I was going.” The man gestured with a polite nod, and she returned a smile. She gestured a goodbye, and shuffled past him, she had to get to work. “Excuse me, Mademoiselle…can I spare a moment of your time?” His voice was charming. Not quite American, not quite French…she couldn’t pin it down. She turned back to him, and smiled. “What for? I really am late Monsieur.” He walked slowly to her, his hands clasped behind his back…a reassuring smile putting her at ease. “Oh, I assure you ma Cherie, you shan’t be late any more.” What on earth did he mean by that? Perhaps I’ll show you was the response in her mind…but from where? “Here” As he spoke the words, he laid a hand on her back, and one on her cheek. She fell effortlessly into his grasp, and let herself. She didn’t know why…but he put her at ease…she hadn’t felt this way since…since Michael. Yes, ma Cherie…think of Michael. His love can save you now She closed her eyes. Michael was there now. Holding her. He spoke sweet nothings into her ear, and she was safe again. They were together again…after the accident, but how? Oh, it didn’t matter. He was here now. Finally, the Virgin Mary had answered her prayers; or someone had answered her prayers if not her. She felt his lips entrenched onto hers, and she embraced her love with invitation. The kiss was just as she remembered it. Never too strong, but firm enough to make her flutter with anticipation. His lips trailed, and she looked up, thanking whichever of the deities had answered one of her midnight cries…her pleas for his return from whatever netherworld he had been sent to. His lips found her neck, and she swooned in the embrace of his kiss. A slight prick on her flesh…and then the pleasure of the kiss…and something else. She felt ecstatic. A pleasure she’d never felt before, not even in love with Michael before. Hungrily, she held his head in place, and could feel herself going weak at the knees. The two of them, it seemed, fell to the floor, to their knees…her still holding his head just above her breasts. Oh Michael…I’ve missed you so much… And I you, my little princess Her thoughts began to drift. To home, Rural France. Her Mother was there again, preparing supper for her and her Brother, Jean, her father out on the yacht with Francois and Jacques. Her Mother smiled at her, her Mother who she hadn’t seen in years…so many years…not since…yes, bring me into your arms Ma Mere. Michael…don’t stop. The reply again…Don’t worry Ma Cherie…we’re almost there. Memories of those good times, in Michael’s apartment in Florence. The wisps of the fire, bathing her cheek in soft heat. She fell forward, her body limp… She felt wetness on her face, she must have been lying on the floor, the wet concrete against her face. The memories were fading… No, Michael…come back! A hand held her neck gently, pushing against where Michael had been kissing. A soft hand on her forehead. And then a voice in her ear, whispering to her… Sleep…My little French Tulip. Sleep, and you can dream of Michael all the while She smiled, and let her mind drift once again…into sleep, and the dreams of the man she had loved so dearly, and lost…but now had found again. Her dreams were of fond farewells and new beginnings. She would awake in her own bed, weak and frightened…but still alive. Yes, still alive…they are always still alive he thought ______________________________________________________________ A fire roared in the side of the room. The flame sputtering a conversation of sparks and broken wood into the bright room. 18th Century French paintings adorned the walls, along with portraits of a figure, all painted at different times. The portraits were of him…of Anacletus Alcaeus…the child of the millennium. And there he sat, in a chair made of mahogany, eyes peering out of his large, perhaps too large for one such as he, window. He held a hat…a small black hat. It still had her scent on it. He always loved to keep something from his “victims”. This one had been ever so sweet. He mused, perhaps arrogantly, or perhaps out of necessity - to avoid the insanity that would inevitably come – that he had given her back more than he had taken. “Hope” His voice was firm, but not without the charismatic flare he was now accustomed to. “I always try to give them hope. My lost brethren…they need hope.” He knew it to be true. He had seen the self-destructive paths of mortals many a time. He knew he could always give them hope, but he could never dare to actually save them from their own demise…that would be too painful. To fall in love with your prey…too painful indeed. “And so it is. I am exiled to this eternal ballad – neither to die, nor truly live…to be unknown…but to always know those who possess that which I cannot. But it was not always so.” He stood up, closing the curtains over the large window, looking out over New Orleans. Something about the city put him at rest in a country where there was no rest to be had. America…the cloned trap of fools, and the capitalist giant of the west…so many dreams and hopes had fallen into its black hole of monotony and lies. But New Orleans had hope…and flare…and music. How he loved the early 20th century music. So lively…and so bold. To stand up against four hundred years of tradition, and penetrate culture, it had to be bold. “As I perhaps once was…bold, youthful…and perhaps a little arrogant as well. But that story is for another time. I suppose this situation necessitates an introduction.” He spoke into the camera that faced him. It gave him some sense of company, which he dare not have otherwise. Later, he would play this tape back, and commit what he felt necessary to paper. He would be alone no more… “I am Anacletus Alcaeus; born 388BC, died 366BC and now, what you see before you. I am, for want of a better word, a vampire. Cursed by a Persian Merchant so many years ago, into this life of eternity. I have come to realise, over the centuries, that I can no longer end my existence. It is both painful and impossible, as I have found out, much to my dismay some years ago. But it is not as bad as all that.” He sat into a red-lined chair beside his Mahogany table, folded his hands, and entrenched himself in his monologue. “For over two thousand years, I have been able to travel, learn, to embrace the culture of the centuries. I have seen the rise and fall of empires, and the immortality of individual greatness. So no, it is not as bad as all that. I am, myself, a silent witness to the joke of time. The joke, of course, is that humanity is non-existent in the grand scheme of things, but has propelled itself to such deity status. We are all guilty of such ignorant arrogance – myself included. But, inevitably, time will have its laugh at our expense. But for now, let me say that I revel to participate in this joke, and turn it, for myself at least – into pleasant reality. “I believe it is time for me to put down this veil of secrecy, shroud of mystery, and ingratiate myself in the happy mediocrity of mortal life. Hence, I am here. To spend any more time alone…non-existent…would be a crime against my soul, even if against my better judgement. Of course, in this time of wide screen television and fantastical cinema, who would believe an extravagant story such as mine? If I were preaching my truths on the streets, I would be an insane man…or in the wrestling ring, I can blanket it as a cheap gimmick. Whatever the case, I must exist on the lips, and in the writings of others once again. But let me not bore you with my history, or my motives – just let it be suffice to say, I am here…mortal, immortal, whichever you choose to believe, or to want to believe…and I am here to make some difference, even if only slight and pitiful, in the world of men.” Alcaeus picked up a remote, and switched the camera to its standby position. Yes, maybe that will do for an introduction ______________________________________________________________ “So, where does that leave us now?” Alcaeus’ rhetoric resonated through the room. He let out a sigh, to add emphasis to the break in his monologue. So, this was it. He was betraying himself to the world once again, and this time not only to a city, or a tribe…but to anyone with a television screen. “Well, I suppose that’s a relatively easy question. That leaves us in the here and now. As I sit here, reciting for all who would care to listen simple truths and complicated lies, I am perhaps satiating myself more than anything else. This drive to be known, to be revealed…it isn’t arrogance, but something akin to that. If I cannot destroy myself, maybe this world that mortals believe so arrogantly plays host to their existence, can. Somehow, some way…perhaps I hope to hit upon some exogenous truth within my endogenous turmoil.” The solid figure of Alcaeus seemed to be external to the world for a second, as if some revelation had just occurred to the star of this one-man epic. The only thing that betrayed his consciousness were the stern yet brilliant eyes that peered into the camera, as if into a mirror…as if examining himself through a microscope and locating the inevitability that he hoped never to find. “Yes, I suppose that’s it. This is my surrender. My surrender to a world of confusion and twisted benevolence…to the sheep in wolves clothing, and the ever-watchful eye of the Sun. To grant Apollo my soul, and in return find some quiet in the restless screaming of my heart…and perhaps to end it all, once and for all. But we shall see. My revelation to this world will be more than dust and air…it shall be an intangible mass of eloquence and style. I shall be remembered…but for what I do not know. This is my chance to find out my meaning, and my place in ‘God’s’ plan, if ever there was one. Yes, it is of destiny that I speak. Whether I shape my own, or follow a pre-designed pattern, I feel it is in the light out of the shadow of my existence that I shall arrive at it.” There seemed to be conviction in his words, until a change of mood came over Alcaeus. He smiled briefly, before shaking his head. “Bah. Sometimes even I don’t know what I say or mean. No, this is simply to cater for my arrogance, no matter how I coat it in such fine silken fictions. So, Alcaeus will become a spectacle, and the people will love me. Yes, that is it, isn’t it? Like Mary des Jardins’ love for Michael. I crave such things…don’t all? And so it shall be…” He seemed amused by his diagnosis of his motives for this personalised diary. Alcaeus’ hand creased through his hair, giving it a living image where one could have mistaken it for a stone helmet, painted in the most immaculate gold. “Simple gestures. We have them, and so do you.” And so, it’s settled ______________________________________________________________ “It was nearly twenty four hundred years ago now, that I became what I became. But every night, I still dream about the night I made my first mortal kill. It sickened me to my stomach that humanity and life could be so fragile. My first instinct was fear. Fear because I had killed so easily…that I, too, could be succumbed to the fate of such an inconsequential death. But anger soon followed. Anger at my maker…the merchant from Persia. Anger at Apollo, for betraying me into the service of some blood god, forced to seek the world for eternity to feed his craving. But the ultimate feeling…that which overcame all others…it was, of course, a sense of power. A sense that I was apart from this world. Apart from the fragile nature of humanity. Free, to roam the night and do, as I would please. And so, I pleaded with my new master…my maker…Darius; ‘we should conquer master’ I would say to him idly during our evenings in bed together. ‘We should take this world in its ignorance.’ Of course he would say no, perpetually…it was far too dangerous for the world to know of us. But the urge remained. “And so, it came to pass, that I and my master drifted, and one night he was gone. He left a note, and a Persian ruby ring on my pillow, deep in our underground refuge from the world. The note contained some simple truths about our nature, an apology for his absence, and a warning – a warning that sent a chill through my spine. It read, ever reveal yourself in all that you are; ever proclaim our existence; ever aim high above your station in this world – Apollo will not be able to save you. I knew what it meant. It was not a warning for my own salvation, but a threat. If I were ever to betray our dark secret, our kind would be sure to find me deceased. “But alas, it has been over five hundred years since I have been with another of our dead kind. I grow weary of this eternal loneliness, and so perhaps this is what I crave. To entice my brethren out into the open. To find solutions – a partner – a kindred spirit, even if it is a spirit sent to destroy me. In that, I believe I shall find solace.” Alcaeus smiled at his personal revelation. He had thought it before, but never spoken it to himself, let alone to the world that would inevitably hear. “My name is Anacletus Alcaeus – and I am a vampire. Tomorrow I shall bare silent witness to the rising sun of another day…but how many more? That is the question…the question I shall endeavour to answer, and find truth in all things.” ______________________________________________________________
