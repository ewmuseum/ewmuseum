Petitioning An Empty Heaven

8 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-08 "He won. You must be happy." "Oh?" "Your associate, Tully. Defeating this 'Tchu' in your stead a couple weeks ago and all." "I suppose." Doctor Rourke shifted his eyes down to the dossier on the table between he and his patient. Only a few weeks past had Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan's scheme begun to unfold, allowing himself to be captured by authorities and inserting his protege into place in the GTT tournament. As things stood, Rourke cared for his charge's wrestling background only so much as was relevent to his stay in this institution; his talents and training granted him the capacity to virtually shrug off any of the security staff and that was a frightening prospect if he decided to get violent. No, Rourke's concern was with the aspects of his plan regarding the bigger picture. As dangerous as he reportedly was in a wrestling ring, his mind was far more dangerous than any physical pain a man could bring on another. Dangerous even for a man housed in a home for the criminally insane. "But why do you bring this up, Daniel? Professional wrestling isn't your concern. You have no need to humor me. Let us get to the business you actually brought me here for." Rourke slowly let his eyelids come together, offering a thin smile at his patient's observation. After so many interviews and sessions together, he should have known the other would see through his attempts at small talk. But he had a plan B. "I'm actually quite curious," he replied in earnest. "Why wrestling? It just seems odd that a man of your intelligence would use this form of entertainment of all worldly forums to do what he does." Shanahan merely shrugged. "A means to an end. Something I do well and have long before my eyes were opened to the true ways of the universe. Besides, exposure on television every week bolsters our recruitment." Chair creaking, Rourke sat back and caressed his chin. "Surely you jest. It simply doesn't seem to be the most favorable of avenues to help your group grow." "On the contrary, doctor. Our ranks have increased considerably." "Kids. Confused, lost outcasts." Doctor Rourke sighed, fingering the pen in his hand nervously. "Those who choose to only go halfway join underground music or ridiculous little demi-cults. Their faith only goes as far as their thirst for rebellion, to be different. They practice faux dark arts and disgrace those who are convicted to something that isn't a fairy tale. They believe in nothing. Those we retain do. You speak as if we leech future world leaders from the prestigious gated communities of suburban America." "And those that don't fit the bill? Killed?" The man calling himself the Pariah Saint chuckled. "We're not barbarians, Doctor Rourke. They leave of their own accord. They merely vow to say nothing of what they have seen and learned, and rarely do they have any desire to regardless. I don't run some savage house of maniacs lusting for blood like something torn from a survival horror video game." "You don't threaten them? Or their families?" "Tell me, Daniel. Are you a man of God?" The question caught the doctor off guard. It was a question he had anticipated much earlier in their relationship, given the pious nature of the high priest seated before him, but the timing was obviously unexpected. He'd already long known his answer, realizing the futility of ignoring the inquiry when it finally was asked. "Not particularly, no." So many times he'd warned the staff not to allow Shanahan into their heads, not to get a peek at their beliefs or the workings of their psyches. Too many were untrained and unprepared for the onslaught that a man like Bruce Shanahan could unleash in the aftermath of such revelations. But he felt no threat now, not with so many tranquilizers coursing the man's veins. "An Atheist perhaps? Feel that the Christian God is some dream conjured by ages-dead Arabs suffering from the kind of schizophrenia you now feel afflicts myself? Allah is a ridiculous hoax undermining the progress of humanity? Buddha a silly idol for silly followers desiring an impossible state of mind?" The doctor's response came as a singular shake of the head. "I simply don't attend church or offer prayers for anything. I'm not a 'saved' man, according to the church I suppose. It doesn't suit my lifestyle." "Why?" The session had been effectively halted, held hostage by a man of dubious intention and virtue who sought truth at the end of every tunnel pertaining to faith. Doctor Rourke was used to dealing with patients who strayed off-track often and interrupted with their own questions and intrusive statements, but nothing like the man whose gaze now burrowed into him like a bullet shot from a rifle. Mad as he may be, his purposes somehow seemed more viable than any the doctor had known in the past. It was bizarre and Rourke had no explanation for why he felt so. "I've done well enough without requesting intervention from a deity of any kind. I assume I've lived well enough to be blessed and not ask questions, and I have yet to be struck down by lightning or see my family hurt or ill." Shanahan seemed surprised by this response. Rourke suspected that most of his followers suffered from lives where affliction was numerous and salvation all but absent. They had long since given up crying out to an impotent savior and turned to the dark side. "Religion is a way of giving hope. It sheds light on the unknown and helps the world cope with life through various philosophies. Do you see it the same, Bruce?"" "Wrong, doctor." So matter-of-factly, like a biology professor condemning Creationism. Rourke suspected there was a venom for God hidden in his gospel that he attempted to hide, but if so, he concealed it flawlessly. "Religion preys on weakness and insecurity. It uses speculation and the literal interpretation of abstract notions to build its foundation. This is true for all religions, in one way or another. However, the truth is different." "So none of that stuff about following your heart, Bruce?" "You are a man of science, Daniel. Logic dictates to your kind that what I offer is merely a product of my derangement, and as for following your heart….well, seeing as you've been searching in all the wrong places already, your heart appears to be failing you as a guide. No. What I offer must be seen. And to those that accept my offer…they believe. When you accept the gospel of the universe, you cast out the paltry ways of this world. No more empty prayers. And no more petitioning an empty heaven." The Sect leader paused, rubbing his hands together. Daniel Rourke had initially wondered how much of his diatribe was an act for the tape-recorder and his assailants coming daily from this so-called 'Blackhelm Coalition', but it had become obvious early that Bruce Shanahan truly believed in every facet of his macabre doctrine. "Logic collapses on the subatomic level. Geometry breaks down on a cosmic scale. The very things society are swayed into believing only hold true for so long, Daniel. Everything you know about law and life is an anomaly. A well-kept lie, in a sense. My job in this world is to pull back the veil to enlighten the deceived." Rourke removed his glasses, placing them beside the dossier before thumbing through to a leaflet poking out of the upper corner. "In a sense, is that why you're here?" Your work to 'pull back the veil'?" Shanahan grew silent but smiled at the mention of the incident that led to his 'capture'. An incarceration that he professed to be the first step in a plan that would be revealed over time. The chief psychologist still endeavored to find out whether the statement held a modicum of truth or existed as an excuse to shed any thought of his carelessness. "What happened in that clearing? What did those men see that they refuse to talk about?" "Innocent virgins, flayed alive and gutted like fish, hanging from trees. Children tied to oil-doused stakes, their screams rending the hills as they burned, their charred flesh mingling with the sweet aroma of incense derived from exotic spices and human remains. An abominable blood orgy in reverence to Shub-Niggurath." Rourke felt his stomach churn at the image, the remnants of his Egg Foo Young from lunch bubbling up and rising in his throat. Shanahan responded to his disgusted look with a smug grin. "I kid you, doctor. Your men in blue merely stumbled upon a harmless daily rite. Why they found a few prayers uttered over some of our arcane relics to be so disturbing I can only guess." Rourke sighed, leaning forward to momentarily hide his face from his interviewee. His mind raced, his instincts honed by years at this job demanding that he believe and disbelieve simultaneously. Jesus, nothing made sense since this man was committed. "It can't be that simple, Bruce. It makes no goddamn sense for long-time veterans of the police force to be spooked by some chants and men marching around in robes." "I suppose we'll never know." "Why not?" "You said it yourself. They won't talk about it." Obviously the one known worldwide as Violence Jack would reveal no more today. Rourke had no problem playing his games, and he had all the time in the world to play on. The man was a puzzle that he was determined to solve in time. * * * "Is it him? Worse yet….them?" Father Norry glanced, shuddering, at his associates with an almost pleading stare, hoping the evil they all feared hadn't arisen yet again and wanting that assurance. But he knew all signs pointed to the contrary. The wickedness they knew so well had returned to mock them. "I'm afraid we'll need to contact Father Craven in Philadelphia," sighed Father Robertson, his own parish only a few streets over. He could still hear the screams ringng in his ears and he feared he'd still be hearing them echo in many an unholy dream in years to come. "This has become something of a specialty for him. I don't know of any other groups so wanton of bloodshed and he can deal better with than our own chapter can." None had been prepared to face the horror that awaited them in the First Church of Christ only minutes ago. The unspeakable carnage inside this house of the Lord had rocked these men of the cloth to their spiritual foundations, and the evidence that had been not-so-subtly distributed amongst the butchery brought forth a chorus of teary blessings for the dead. An anonymous tip to Robertson earlier had alerted them of something terrible, and after a few well-placed phone calls to the more influential of their order, this matter was taken fully into the hands of the Church. No secular forces could offer humanity the security that they could; they all knew that the only useful weapon in this conflict would be faith. The third of their party, Father Harrington, clutched a bloodied necklace in one white-knuckled fist. A silver cross dangled from the chain. "This will not go unanswered," he said. "Their desecration has gone on long enough. Why did Craven not demand some form of inquisition from the start?!" Casting the necklace back to the floor, he jabbed a finger angrily at the profane effigy in the sanctuary. "So many would have never died had he seen the madman stopped from the very beginning!" Norry finally took the smudged note from the hand of Robertson, grinding his teeth as he read its contents. The most damning piece of evidence, and maybe the most horrifying at that… * * * "That wasn't sacrifice. That was depraved butchery." Lieutenant Marcus Stryker's anger was reserved, fueling his passion to find the truth amongst all the cryptic bullshit but not violent enough to stir the ire of the man he once called friend and deliverer. The massacre months ago in Providence weighed heavily on his mind ever since he arrived on the scene that day to the stench of death, and today he wanted to make Bruce Fucking Shanahan recognize that comeuppance was just around the corner. "Perhaps. But it also wasn't any of my agents. There's another player, Marcus. Find him, or her, and you'll have the answers you seek." The agent of the Blackhelm Coalition smiled, obviously not convinced. Little of the man's words held weight for him these days unless he spoke the most obvious. This was far from it. "The words left in the wake of the murder sounded like yours'. They carry your stink. What kind of dumb fuck do you take me for? I wasn't born yesterday." He shook his head vehemently, crossing his arms. "What you know isn't that simple. I can see it in your eyes, old friend. Give me a name." Palms pressed together, the Sect's high priest beat his fingertips together absently. His eyes lifted from the manilla folder laid before him and met the baleful gaze of his long-past ally. "Raven." "Raven?" Stryker was perplexed and did nothing to conceal it. Shanahan would see it anyhow. Hell, he'd probably view it as mockery and delve even deeper into his head as a form of reprisal. "Who the hell is 'Raven' and what does he have to do with that slaughter?" "She. And that's for you and your teams to find out, Marcus. I know only what little I was informed about, and I know little more than a moniker at this point. And thus I can only offer you the few fragments of information I'm privvy to." Stryker grinned, drumming his fingers on the dossier. "If I were you, I'd leave nothing out. As is, there's enough evidence to have you thrown in prison for the rest of your sorry life. Wouldn't the irony be beautiful if you were finally put away for something you did not do as opposed to the atrocities you've committed that we can't prove?" Stryker found himself amused, but only so long as that blank expression lined Bruce's face. Soon that wry smirk returned, twisted and sinister as ever. It never failed to send a chill down his spine. "Ask Jason Snow. He should know her well." Outraged, Stryker brought his hand crashing down on the table. Shanahan didn't even blink. "Bullshit!" We know about you and every person in that ridiculous industry who you call a peer. The woman is dead, Bruce!" "Everything in this universe is a matter of perspective, Lieutenant Stryker. Even death." Pursing his lips, Stryker glared at the sedated maniac. "Another of your funny little puzzles. Does this amuse you, you sick bastard?" "On the contrary, I find it most disadvantageous. I'm the one you and your Bible-warped thugs have on preliminary trial in a looney bin. Unfortunately I often forget that you don't share my view of reality. You still only see what you're told to see and that often slips my mind. I know no other way to adequately state it." "Then stop being cryptic and tell me what the hell you know. Being smug won't get you any brownie points with us or any else of our kind. So, you're saying she lives? The death certificate is forged or some kind of sick joke?" "Now you're putting words in my mouth. I never said that. But this isn't a simple matter of life and death. To truly see what's going on here, you need to think outside the box that the Vatican has you locked inside of." "This is absurd. Even for your deranged mind. We're done here." Disgusted, Stryker snatched the file from the bare table and beckoned for his silent companions to follow. "As far as I'm concerned, they can toss you in prison forever. I hope the sodomites leave you a trembling mess every day you spend in that hell when we finish you in the courts. Enjoy the Hilton they give you as long as you can live behind an insanity plea." Stryker didn't look back at the solemn look lining his ex-comrade's face. He stormed across the room in a fury, wrapping trembling fingers around the doorknob before the other's voice shattered the angry tension. "Seek the Raven, lieutenant. And when that is done, you may find answers about your Eyes of Fear. And the 'wish meteors' you so adamantly search for." He quickly released the doorknob. Stryker's fingers, quivering with rage only a moment before, actually fell from the cold brass and to his side, his other hand just barely maintaining a hold on the thick file. The pair that accompanied him paused, looking back at the grave expression that faintly humanized Bruce Shanahan's evil visage. With the violence in Stryker's manner broken, Shanahan cast his eyes beyond the barred window on the east wall and into the fading sunlight. "Fate has united many of us it seems. The works and discoveries of the Eyes of Fear, the relationships of Jason Snow…and other things that even I still move to piece together. Those in the professional wrestling industry and their kin. For now, I know not the purpose. I only know that what I do here is essential in the grand scheme of this world. Perhaps the balance of all things depends on it." Stryker shuddered. If indeed the madman was right, then perhaps they would need to trust him yet again as he schemed within these maddening walls. It was a thought he was hesitant to ever entertain again after all the heinous crimes that monster had committed, but sometimes necessity made for the strangest of bedfellows. Even a pact with the Devil. "I'll check it out. We'll…we'll speak again soon." He tried to hide his displeasure with the potential that Shanahan spoke truth, and this would actually unearth something with bearing on what the Coalition was after, but couldn't suppress a stammer as thought of those rocks from the stars pervaded his mind. Thoughts of the hell they could unleash, and maybe had already unleashed on this Earth… "I'm positive we shall again, Marcus." The voice was cold and callous. Worst of all, it was assured. "Your God has failed you again. And only I, and those who follow me, can offer you refuge from oblivion.. Door closing silently in their wake, Stryker and his men marched gravely and soberly down the halls. Perhaps the demonic mastermind was right again. Perhaps they need to partner with his legion within Wyatt Manor to get what they wanted. As they departed the block, a manner of defeat echoing in their footsteps, Shanahan arose and peered wickedly through the bars. His interrogators were out of sight, but he couldn't help but chuckle at what had begun to unfold. Even confined in this asylum, his plans continued to blossom. "It's time you stopped petitioning an empty heaven and faced truth. It's time you allowed your path to be guided by the damned, old friend…"
