Lost and Found: The Flash

8 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-11-08 Your mom was right. When you were only about fifteen and had just gotten back with your first girlfriend, you were shocked to find out that between things had changed between the breakup. Your friends, who were generally approving, had revolted against the idea of you and Christine. Your best friend Jack, in fact, told you he wouldn’t talk to you as long as "that cunt is attached to your hip." Deeply troubled by this, you went to your mom and told her everything, expecting her to comfort you and tell you that everything would work for the best. Instead, she told you all your friends were right. And then she gave you the best advice you have ever received. "Don’t talk to your friends about the negative aspects of your relationship. You’re in love with the person, so you can forgive them. Since your friends aren’t, they won’t." And it made perfect sense. From that moment on, you kept the sordid relationship details to yourself. Until Katie. Blame it on love, on weakness, blame it on whatever you prefer, but Katie has always been able to frustrate you to the point of seeking help. Every fight would send you to your family, your friends, and eventually to Sarah Riley, who took advantage of it. And the overall result has been that no one in your collective circle thinks Katie Malick is the one for you. And you’re not sure if you disagree. All of the fights, the issues, the dueling infidelity, have finally made you tired of defending Katie from all of your friends. You’re done with fighting the world, being the only person who sees value in your relationship, and maybe it’s time for you to realize there might not be any value. So as you take Kody out for a walk with your friend and manager, you try to ignore the fact that it’s been almost a week since you have been home. You try to ignore a lot of things, like whether it’s time to accept the world’s argument and move on. You try not to think about any of this. It goes about as well as you’d expect it to. "I’m not the kind of friend to tell you what to do, Jon, but kick that bitch out of your house. I’m serious." Shweta Kallemullah doesn’t like the way the weather has suddenly changed, as her turtleneck and grimace suggest. Tucking her black hair behind her ear as the wind begins to pick up, she looks over at Jon with a concerned look. "Forget I said that. I know that you…" Jon shrugs. "No, you’re more than welcome to state your opinion. Everyone’s got one on the subject. I bet even Kody does," he says, smirking as he bends down to scratch the American Eskimo behind the ears. Even though it’s only been a week, the dog has grown, both in stature and in affection for his new master. Kody moves his head around to lick Jon’s hands, then jumps up to put his paws on his knee. "So if you do listen to me, who gets the dog?" Shweta asks, smiling what she hopes is a disarming smile. Jon’s scowl tells her it isn’t. "Not something I really want to think about, thanks." He stands up and they continue walking, and Shweta shrugs her shoulders. "You told me I could state my opinion, so I did. I just don’t like seeing my wrestler unhappy." Jon nods slowly. "Right. Or you don’t like seeing your wrestler distracted from the great ultimate battle against Desade." "That too, but it all goes back to your happiness. You’re going to be most valuable to the cause if you’re happy and focused. And with a woman who seems to be more concerned with whose affair was more acceptable, I just don’t see how you can be happy." Shweta shrugs again, then looks at her friend. "You aren’t, are you?" Jon’s sigh breaks the silence, but he doesn’t add to it for several seconds. Looking down the slope of the hill they’re standing on, he stops and stares. "It’s a really nice view of the city, isn’t it?" "So the answer is no, you’re not." Smiling, he shakes his head. "You can see everything. Every house, every car, every person. But you’re separated; you don’t have to worry about social behavior or keeping up appearances. You can just stand here and look." Shweta’s smile indicates she is thinking of another dig at him, but she holds it in, standing next to him and taking in the view instead. "Which one is your house?" "Not sure. I think it’s near the middle of that circle of houses," he says, pointing. "But you know, we just moved in and I don’t know much about the house or the neighborhood. I just know it’s beautiful, and that’s all I need right now." There’s something in his words that wipes the smile from Shweta’s face, and Desade’s former employer puts a hand on Jon’s shoulder. "Jon, I know you’re unhappy. You have to break it off." Jon turns to her suddenly, and replacing the pensive frown is a confident, toothy smile. "Shweta, I appreciate your advice, but you don’t even know Katie Malick. Not like I do." "Thanks for doing this, Katie. Especially with Jon…well…" Foster Nackedy has been involved in the wrestling business for close to fifteen years, and that immersion in such a masculine environment has, for lack of a better word, squashed his emotional capacity. "Uh, you know…with whatever’s happening." Katie Malick smiles, waving a hand dismissively. "Not a problem, I love Connor, and I could use some company right now while Jon’s…out." Connor Nackedy, eight years outrageous, lets out a squeal as he runs past his father, plastic sword in hand. Foster watches his son with an embarrassed smile, then looks up at Katie. "How long is he going to be…out?" Katie sighs. "Not sure, really. I think…I think I might call him tonight. We need to talk about a lot of things. And don’t worry, I’ll make sure not to fight in front of Connor or anything." "Are you kidding?" Foster laughs, stuffing his hands in pockets. "After spending eight years with Magen and me? Connor is an experienced spectator in that sort of thing. If you’re not hitting each other with furniture, he’ll probably be confused, poor thing." Katie’s giggle is probably forced, but the former NWC star doesn’t notice. "Well, have fun on your date. Good luck getting back with her. I know it’s been hard for you recently, with you getting back into wrestling and working with Shweta and everything." Foster smiles. "I’m sure it’ll be fine. And Shweta and I weren’t anything, so it won’t be an issue." Katie raises her eyebrows. "You guys dated for eight months." "And as long as you don’t text Magen about any silly rumors like that, everything will go fine. Mark my words, when I come to pick Connor up tonight Magen will be right here in the doorway with me." "Let’s hope," Katie says, looking back to make sure Connor isn’t causing any damage in the kitchen. "Well, good luck again. See you later tonight." "See you. And Katie? Thanks so much. This means a lot to me. You’re a really…thanks." Foster nods and waves to Connor, then walks out of the doorway. Closing the door, Katie leans against the door and sighs. Connor looks up from his sword-swinging and asks, "What’s wrong, Aunt Katie?" Faking a smile, Katie walks over to Connor and puts her hands on he knees, bending to his level. "Nothing, Connor. I’m just thinking about your Uncle Jon." "Did you fight?" Connor asks bluntly. "I…well, why did you ask that?" Connor swings his sword wildly, then shrugs his shoulders. "I dunno. I just see Mommy making that face when her and Daddy fight." Smiling, Katie sits on the couch. "Your Uncle Jon and I haven’t been getting along all the time, no. That’s very observant of you." "What’s observant?" "It’s…" Katie furrows her brow, trying to think of how to simplify the word. "It’s looking at something and seeing the truth in it." The sentence makes her frown, and she sighs again. "Why did you sigh again?" Looking at her feet, Katie says, "I just realized I’m a little observant too. Let me…let me call your uncle, you play with your action figures in here." As Connor plops on the floor and grabs his backpack full of toys, Katie walks over to the phone and dials the number quickly. Crossing her arm over the one holding the receiver, she looks up as she waits for an answer. When the machine picks up, she shakes her head and waits a second before speaking. "Jon, I…I just want to talk. I want to see you tonight. I want to…I want to apologize. Call me back. Or just come home when you get this. Just…um…bye." Hanging up, Katie sits down in front of the computer and hangs her head against the monitor. "Your dog is so cute!" A blonde woman in shorts and a sports bra bends over, petting Kody, as Jon looks down at her nervously. "Yeah he is," says Shweta, slapping Jon on the shoulder. "And he takes such good care of him too. It’s really hard, you know, without anyone else to help him out. Since, you know, he’s totally single." Jon scowls at Shweta. "Yup, single and desirable, this one. I always try to give him my lovin’, but he’s just too much man for me. Just like the dog is too much dog for him." Shweta turns to Jon and grins. "He doesn’t seem like a handful," says the blonde in an unusually high-pitched voice. "No, he doesn’t! He seems like a sweetheart!" She looks up to Jon for confirmation of this assumption. Jon shuffles his feet. "He’s a good dog, yeah." Shweta pokes Jon in the side and whispers, "Make a move." "Eat shit," Jon whispers back through gritted teeth. "Well, I’ve got to finish my run, but it was really nice meeting you…and your adorable dog!" she says, her voice once again skyrocketing in pitch and volume as she gives Kody one last pet. "Maybe I’ll see you on the trail sometime." Standing up, she winks at Jon and jogs off. "You’re such a pussy," mutters Shweta, walking past Jon. "I thought most of that goody-two-shoes stuff was an act, but you’re actually a genuine certified vagina." "Stop it, Shway." Smiling, Shweta turns around. "I’m just trying to help you out, buddy. And you know where I come from; you know where I learned my tactics. This is just who I am." Jon throws his arms up. "Who you are? Does that mean you’re going to poison me again?" Leading Kody down the path with the leash, he sighs. "Is it possible to trust a single woman on earth?" Finally dropping the smile, Shweta reaches out and grabs Jon’s wrist. "Jon, look at me." When he does, he sees her face has changed into one of concern. "I’m sorry. I shouldn’t have said that. And please don’t judge my actions from when I worked with Desade. I’m on your side now. And part of my mission in being on this side is loosening you the fuck up." Jon scowls again, and she raises her arms in the air. "What? It’s true! You’re always so worried about Katie, and fighting Desade, that you forgot what it’s like to just have a fun time. What are you, twenty-five? You’d be barely out of college now but you spend every day like it’s the biggest chore imaginable. Jesus, Jon, I wasn’t trying to get you to sleep with that girl. I was trying to get you to live a little." Rhine’s eyebrows shoot downward as he narrows his eyes. "Live a little?" "Maybe more than a little. Listen, the whole wrestling world knows that your determination is one of your biggest strengths, and it’ll probably get you motivated to get past Alias and everyone else in this tournament. But it’s killing you, and it’s preventing you from looking at your life and just seeing the truth about your relationship." Jon stares at his manager for a long minute, trying to see through her motives. Finally, he nods. "You’re right about one thing. My focus is what gets me past my opponents. And right now, that’s what matters. So, I’m just gonna go…" Shweta takes a step back unconsciously. "You’re leaving?" "I need to do some training since I can’t do it at home, and I kinda want some time to think for awhile. Thanks for trying to get me out of whatever funk you think I’m in, but I’m gonna be fine. I can balance my tournament, my war with Desade, and my relationship." Shweta sighs. "I hope you can. See you later." He watches her go for a few minutes before stopping her. "Hey Shway?" "Yes?" "About what I said…I do trust you. Really." Smiling, she nods and walks down the hill, bracing herself against the wind. Jon watches her go and sighs, then looks down at Kody. "You ready to go for a little run, buddy? Let’s go back to the restroom so I can change." He opens his backpack as they walk back towards the top of the hill, moving his phone aside as he grabs his shorts and tank top. "That must be your dad," Katie says as the doorbell rings. As she walks towards the door, she adds, "And hopefully your mom too." But as she opens the door, she only sees Foster, and her face falls. She asks the unnecessary question. "Didn’t go well?" He sighs and enters the house, shaking his head. "Maybe it isn’t supposed to happen. I don’t know. Whatever." He grabs Connor and gives him a big hug, then picks him up and holds him at his side. "I just think what happened is the flash went away." Katie tilts her head slightly. "The flash?" "Yeah, you know, the one moment where it clicks that there isn’t anyone else? I mean, I got the flash back when me and Mags were kids. It was just a realization that this was going to be the girl, the one, the cliche, and it was going to stay that way forever. And even when we had our little fights, I could still see the flash, just sitting there, saying ‘this is why you’re with her.’ But tonight, we talked, we argued, and I tried – for the life of me I tried – but I couldn’t see the flash. It’s not there." Katie frowns. "I’m really sorry, Foster. I was pulling for the happy ending." He nods. "Me too. But there are still happy endings around, we just have to look for them." He stares at her until she looks down, ashamed. His face reddens as well, and he claps his hands suddenly. "Well, Connor and I better get going, huh champ?" Connor shakes his head. "No, I wanna stay with Aunt Katie!" "You can’t, Connor, you have to go with your dad," says Katie, although if she’s trying to hide her smile, she’s doing a poor job. "But we were having fun, and you said we were gonna make a surprise for Uncle Jon!" Foster looks from his son to Katie. "So Jon’s coming home tonight?" Katie shrugs. "I hope." "Me too," he says, putting a hand on her shoulder. "You know what? If you don’t mind, why don’t you take him for the night? He just seems so happy here." Katie opens her mouth quickly, his eyes widening as well. "Oh, no, I couldn’t, I wouldn’t want to keep him from going home." Foster smiles. "Trust me, he’ll be happier here tonight, and he doesn’t get to do anything like this. I even put a change of clothes in his backpack in case he got dirty, so it all works out. That is, if you’re okay with it." "Come on, Aunt Katie! Slumber party! And I’m a great cook, I watch them do it on TV!" Connor shouts. Katie’s nose wrinkles as she pretends to be deep in thought. "Well…" she begins, putting a finger to her chin. Foster smiles as he views his son’s reaction, which is one of anxiety. "I guess I could use a hand with the cake." "YAY!" Connor shouts, struggling to get out of his dad’s arms. When Foster finally does put him down, he runs into the kitchen, joyously shouting, "Iron chef! Iron chef!" Foster laughs. "Yeah, he watches a lot of…" "Let me guess," Katie says through a smile. "Iron chef?" "Iron chef! Iron chef! Iron chef!" Katie and Foster share a laugh. Then Katie looks at her boyfriend’s trainer, concerned. "Are you sure…?" "Yeah, I think it’s a good idea. Connor’s attached to you, and I think you might need it too." She nods, running a hand through her hair absentmindedly. "Thanks, Foster." "Thank you, you’re the one doing me a favor," Foster says through a smile. "No, thanks for being so…thanks for not taking sides, I guess." Foster shrugs, opening the door and standing in the doorway. "What good would that do? Then I wouldn’t have a babysitter." After Katie chuckles, he offers a warm smile. "You guys’ll work it out. You guys still have the flash somewhere, I’m sure of it. Bye, Katie." Katie closes the door and only has one moment to close her eyes before Connor shouts, "Come on, Aunt Katie, let’s bake the cake!" "Can I have a piece when it’s done?" Katie looks down at her little helper and forces a laugh. This is the third time he has asked. "Yes, Connor, you’ll get a slice when it’s done." Connor nods, then looks down for a second before looking up again. "Can I put the icing on?" Connor asks, practically hopping on one foot. Katie giggles, putting a hand to her mouth to stifle it. "Sure you can, but we have to actually bake it first. We haven’t even put it in the oven yet." "Well then can I put it in the oven?" "In a minute, Connor." She smiles at him, then continues mixing together the ingredients. As she measures out shortening into a teaspoon, her eyes narrow and her tongue snakes out of her mouth just a little. Her concentration is ruined when Connor asks another question. "When are you and Uncle Jon gonna have a baby?" "What?" Katie looks down for a moment and loses her focus, and the shortening spills onto the table. Cursing under her breath and walking to the far counter to grab a towel, she looks at Connor with an appraising eye as she comes back. "Why do you ask that?" Connor looks at his feet, his cheeks growing red. "Mommy said that babies are made when two people love each other very much. And I know you and Jon love each other. So when do I get a cousin?" Katie cleans up the spill quickly, but in the middle of cleaning she stops. "I…I don’t know, sweetie," she says, leaving the shortening half-cleaned. She stands upright and furrows her eyebrows, then puts a hand to her chest. Connor looks worried until she breaks out into a big smile, and then he looks more worried. "Are you okay, Aunt Katie?" She doesn’t answer; instead, she looks to the ceiling for a moment, her mind obviously racing. "Is this the flash?" she mumbles. "What’s that?" asks Connor, putting his little hand in hers. "N-nothing, sweetie. Look, Aunt Katie needs to write something down. Can you be a big boy and put the cake in the oven? Just take the new pan out of the grocery bag, take out all the paper, and dump the cake batter inside, then stick it in the oven. Can you do that for me?" "Sure!" the child says, nodding emphatically. As he rummages through the grocery bag, Katie slowly walks towards the drawer by the TV. "Pen and paper, pen and paper…screw it, I’ll just use the desktop." "Are you sure you’re okay?" "Fine," she calls as she stumbles to the desktop. "Dear Jon…" she says aloud as she begins typing. Her eyes dull and she loses her focus as she thinks of Connor, of her relationship, of the flash. She suddenly looks up and sees Connor staring back at her, and she jumps, clutching her chest. "You scared me!" she shouts. "Did you finish everything that quickly, Connor?" He giggles. "I’ve been done for a few minutes. I was trying to get your attention but you wouldn’t look at me." Blinking rapidly, Katie shakes her head. "Sorry, honey. So you poured the batter into the pan?" "Yep." "And you put the pan in the oven?" "Yep." "And you removed the paper from the new pan before you did all of that?" "Yep." Katie looks back at the screen, failing to notice Connor look down suddenly. "Okay, well let me just save this and then we can play for a little while we wait for the cake to be ready." Katie clicks a button and stares at the monitor with a half-smile before turning to Connor. "Are you ready to play?" "Okay." They both walk to the center of the living room and Katie sits, grabbing a Transformer toy and holding it up for Connor to take. "Was that for Uncle Jon?" Connor asks as he grabs the toy. Katie smiles. "Yes, it was. It was…it was a letter telling him that I remembered." "What did you remember?" "I remembered the flash." Kody is a young dog, enthusiastic and eager. This is why, although he is small and somewhat weak, he continues to run, because his master is too. Up the path of the hill, Jonathan Rhine huffs, his eyes focused ahead. Below him, Kody runs tirelessly, yipping periodically as he does so. The top of the hill is near, and Jon’s mind is blank as he closes in on it. All of the drama, all of the pressure, everything that swims in his brain like an overcrowded public pool in the summer is washed away, leaving nothing but a calm, empty vessel. It is where Jonathan is most at home, even when he can’t go home. Unfortunately, the top of the hill approaches, and as Jon reaches it his brain fills up once again and he bends over, panting. Kody barks, jumping up and resting his paws against Jon’s shin. Smiling, he rewards his dog with a scratch behind the ears. "You’re good at this," he gasps. Jon walks over to a log that has long ago been fashioned into a makeshift bench, and he plops down on it, letting loose a loud sigh of exhaustion. Kody jumps up onto the log and snuggles close to his master. "You like it up here, buddy?" Jon asks, petting Kody. "Yeah, it’s nice up here. Even nicer without Shweta giving unneeded advice." He looks over the horizon, where he can see the entire city. He scans the sea of uniform houses, his eyes poring over the different roofs. He chuckles as he sees smoke billowing above the horizon. "That’s why I don’t smoke. Bad for you." He laughs again, but it’s an empty laugh. Then, he looks at his dog. "No offense, but I’m starting to miss other people." He smiles sadly and grabs his bag, then begins rooting through it. "Missing one other person, at least. Maybe I should call…" He stops as he grabs the phone out of the bag and sees the missed call. He takes a moment to check the voicemail, then listens to the hurried voice of his girlfriend. Nodding, he puts it back in his bag. "I think we’ve been in the doghouse for long enough, don’t you?" Grabbing the leash, he stands up. "Yeah, I think we learned our lesson. Let’s go show Katie how much we love her. Yeah, let’s go." It sounds weird talking to your dog about your relationship, but it occurred to you as you started down the hill that it was probably the best way to get your frustrations out. Dealing with your relationship should be internal. Shweta, as well-intentioned as she may be, doesn’t have a say in what you do with Katie. Neither do any of your friends. At the end of the day, it’s your relationship. And how you deal with it is your business. So you work out, you focus on the tasks ahead, and you vocalize your feelings out loud to the only thing that isn’t going to criticize you for it. And now you’re ready to go home. Something feels different as you make the long trip back home, and you convince yourself that it is your attitude more than anything. Because your relationship hasn’t changed: it’s still volatile, rocky, and prone to huge fights down the line that would make your friends disapprove. But what has changed is you’ve stopped caring about your friends’ approval. Because as you looked out over the hill and saw the city in all of its beauty, you could only think of one person you wanted to share it with. And it wasn’t Shweta, and it wasn’t any of your disapproving friends. It was Katie. And nothing will change that.
