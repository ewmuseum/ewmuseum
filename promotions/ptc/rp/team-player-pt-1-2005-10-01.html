Team Player, pt. 1

1 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-01 You know how this works, right? This is where you put it. Right here at the top. It’s what your English teacher called an epitaph. No, not the thing on the tombstone. Christ… could you BE that dense? It’s the quote that’s supposed to embody the content that’s coming. Maybe a line from a movie. Or a verse or two from a song you like. No, I DON’T think you should use any lines from that new Psychostick song. Why? Because it’s about beer, that’s why! That has nothing to do with anything! I don’t care HOW many Coors Lights you’ve drank in your lifetime, it really doesn’t apply. Look, I’m wasting my breath. You’d know that if you went to English rather than wrestling school senior year, genius. See? Look at what you’re doing. The spacing of the lines? The short sentences? You’re padding for space, aren’t you? That’s the oldest damn trick in the book. And you’d KNOW that if you ever went to English. Dumbass. * * * * * Your name is Dave Gibson. And you are Old School. That’s not just a catchphrase, not just a gimmick. For you, it’s a way of life. Oh sure, you’re a fan of all the modern conveniences. The iPod, the cell phone, TiVo—how else can you follow the O.C. while toiling in the squared circle without the glory that is a season pass—and of course, the splendor that is the Internet. Even though certain redheaded partners in crime might have less affinity for good ole cyberspace. But you digress. You tend to do that a lot these days. No, you’re old school where it counts. In the ring. Don’t misunderstand. You can cut a promo with the best of them—and have, from time to time. Verbal emasculation, from a collegiate point of view, would be your minor. The major? Technical wrestling with a concentration in cheating. It is your medium, your art, your craft, your passion. And you studied and practiced it to the misfortune of everything else in your life. Other kids went to class and took their sweetheart to the prom. You went to the wrestling school—actually several, they said you didn’t have “the look” to make it in the business—and the only social interaction you had was with guys as green as yourself or doughy wrestlers well past their glory days trying to “pay your dues.” Your friends got to caress the firm breasts of their fellow 17-year-olds. You found your face squashed against manboobs in many a collar and elbow. But you didn’t just know how to do a proper armdrag. You know how to segue the armdrag into the hiptoss, follow it up with the hammerlock and spend the next five minutes deconstructing an opponent’s arm until it had the consistency and strength potential of a spaghetti noodle. You lived wrestling. Ate it, slept it, dreamed about it, thought about it every minute of every day. Other people had kids or hobbies. You had your videotapes. Your memories of the old days and your desire to recapture that spirit, translate it for the new generation. And your dreams of glory inside the ring. Then, systematically, you proceeded to drown those dreams in a torrent of alcohol. Beer, whiskey, heck a few times toward the end you settled for some stuff that barely qualified as Sterno. You told yourself that was just part of being old school. Didn’t Andre the Giant once drink 117 bottles of beer? The least you could do was drink 10 percent of that, right? It was a noble gesture to try once. It was foolhardy to try it multiple times. Countless drinks, a detour into painkillers, a poorly chosen attempt as a booker and an ill-fated piledriver later, you found yourself on the sidelines. But not for long. You decided to give back, to open a school that would give kids like you the chance you never had. Dave Gibson’s Wrestling School. You were old school, so it was only natural you HAD a school, right? You might have found it poetic. If you ever went to English class. And when you did go, you never paid attention. Dumbass. “Are you even listening?” Mwha…? Damn attractive redheads and their ability to interrupt a perfectly fine exposition… Just nod and maybe she’ll buy it. Yup! “Good. Now, remember when you met me?” You don’t even have to bullshit the nod this time, even though you know where the story is going from the “Cokecokecokecoke” introduction. Her name is Amy Campbell, longtime friend, part-time crush (all one-sided on your end) and fulltime ass-kicker in the collection of motley souls that is PRIME. She’s the reason you have a job in the wrestling business again. Consequently, you could probably finagle a way to blame her for all the new scars you’d added to your already impressive collection since returning to the ring, but that’d probably be seen as passive-aggressive bullshit by some ball-washing therapist. You preferred to file it away as unnecessary thought, instead. The unnecessary ones don’t keep you up at night if you file them that way. You’re sitting together in a coffee shop—even though she’s allergic to caffeine—before jaunting off to some team-building workshop organized by the bigwigs at PRIME. On one side, you sit—trying to ignore the constant throbbing in your left shin from the exuberant kicking from Aimz as she regales you with her recent tale of addiction and recovery from the umpteenth time. On the other, she sits, doing the kicking, and lord knows you’ve done enough practice as being the kick-ee. You’re surrounded by your demons. There’s no chairs but plenty of room. You know what hers have in mind; she lets you know often. You keep your demons’ agenda silent. You don’t tell her about the ache in your muscles and your bones from all the matches, or the constant thirst that only one thing can cure. The one thing you swore you’d never touch again. “Crazy, huh?” Oh, hell, she caught you genuflecting again. Quick! Say something insightful! “Urgh…” Christ, you’re useless. “But now I can beat Karina fucking Wolfenden! I can win shit consistently! This is all amazing, isn’t it?” As Amy suddenly becomes fascinated with the holes in her jeans, then starts pruning them with the ragged edges of her gnawed fingernails, you notice the collection of coffee cups scattered in front of her like so many good little plastique soldiers. No, they’re not scattered. More… stacked. Ordered, actually. In a triangular formation. Not only did you have a team workshop—whatever the hell that was—to look forward to, you’d have to deal with a caffeine-charged Aimz. Oh, joy. “Had enough coffee?” you ask. “Oh, the coffee? Nah, I hate coffee. It was capuc—wait, haven’t you been paying attention? You look like you need a nap.” “Compared to you right now? Anything would.” Even a coma patient… “We’re LATE!” she squeals, grabs you by the wrist and yanks you up and toward the door as an Andrew Jackson goes flying back in the direction of the table. You rummage into a pocket and give him an Alexander Hamilton for company. You’re not sure 20 bucks would cover the ton of coffee beans Amy Campbell just consumed, and math was never her strong point. As you’re about to learn—and not for the first time—driving isn’t either. Amy speeds frantically through lunch hour traffic like a tailback finding a seam through a sea of defensive players. You try to take your mind off the pending crash by reflecting again. Once, not too long ago actually, you thought you might be more than friends. OK, maybe you were deluding yourself. You probably were… and if your own brain didn’t raise enough suspicions, catching Aimz in flagrante with Killean Sirrajin certainly sealed the deal. For about six weeks, you stopped talking to your best friend. Part of you thought she got off easy. After all, your LAST best friend got his hand broken by a hammer you wielded. Then you piledrived his girlfriend. Aimz was getting off easy, and you both were going to move on with your lives. Then something happened. Your mother died. Emphysema, diabtes and congestive heart failure finally combined to bring her down. Amy came to the funeral. And brought her boyfriend. Once you saw your least favorite Supreme Machine lounging on the doorstep of the drab building where blue-haired ladies you met once but were never worth remembering paid respects to the simple seamstress who was your entire world, you lost it. You ran them both off. Later, you realized what an ass you were and apologized. Since then, Amy and Killean were on the outs, and the two of you were traveling together. But getting separate hotel rooms, of course. You could bury past attractions, but you weren’t stupid. Best to keep temptation at bay—and an adjoining door between rooms didn’t count as much of a barrier in your eyes. Amy keeps prattling on during the drive. Something about team strategy. Part of you wants to clue her in. Part of you wants to let her know that every team you’ve ever been in as turned straight to shit. The Throwbacks came apart when you betrayed Clay Vendetta, the best ally you’d had in your whole career. New School Wrestling—the underachieving gang of schlubs you abandoned Clay for—disintegrated in an avalanche of ego and laziness, and isn’t that ALWAYS a winning combination? Then there was Power Play, the stable you formed thinking to be the latest homage to those guys made famous by the four-finger salute. You saw them as a group with the potential to dominate. Instead, they betrayed you, led by the man you were trying to recruit to the group, Matt Register. Later teams went just as well. The management agreement with Shawn Stewart? Ruined by your own greed. The subsequent deal with the Flying Frenchie? Poisoned by inactivity. You were too worried about your wrestling school to bother too much about Pierre outside of a few endorsement deals. That Nike beret WAS pretty catchy… Then there was Team Zambo. If your team for the I4 competition could be seen as an ill-advised offspring comprised from genetic manipulation, your team with Zameer was, on the surface, the afterbirth. Azala Zameer and Dave Gibson fit together about as well as bologna and French toast, at least on the surface. But the Snack Daddy showed more loyalty than you remembered from anyone since Clay, and definitely more than you deserved. And the damnedest thing happened. You succeeded. You started winning matches, lots of them. You came within an eyelash of winning the PRIME tag titles on a pair of occasions, and would’ve made the third time the charm. But then, just like all your other teams, it went to shit. This time, Azala had his leg pulverized by Los Diablos, all because you weren’t looking out, you weren’t trying hard enough to watch your buddy’s back when he’d proven time and time again of being capable of doing that for you. Could Amy trust you to watch her back? Could Kyle Lamen? Could Alex Ross? Could PRIME? After all, you’re fighting for the entire federation, not just yourself. The glory of an entire promotion rides on your shoulders. Not the time to be fucking up. Not the time to choke in a big match. Pity that that’s all you know. Tires squeal. Amy lets out a little squeal of delight that matches. You’re here. * * * * * In an attempt to spare no expense, PRIME had made sure to incur none. The team-building workshop had been set up in a conference room on the ground floor of your hotel. You walked in to see five chairs set up in a semicircle, and a whiteboard propped up on an easel next to one of the chairs. “T E A M” was written down the left margin. Each letter had its own color. “HELLO, and welcome to our team building workshop!” You look up to see a tiny man advancing from a table set up across the room. You see a couple of jugs of some sort of beverage, a bowl of generic Oreos and a bowl of equally generic potato chips—but at least they had ridges. The guy in question? Anything but generic. Closely trimmed shock of red hair, apple cheeks and a smile that you bet refused to dim under the most steadfast of physical torture (and just looking at the guy, you were more than willing to see how much it would take before that smile did indeed turn upside down.) The little douchebag in question—you feel comfortable calling him a douchebag having met many in your time—was wearing a bright yellow T-shirt, with the overly cheery proclamation of “Building Strong Teams All Over The World!” Somehow you doubted the Spurs could attribute their success in the NBA ranks to this little douchebag. “Will Landon here!” Little Douchebag said. “I'm going to be your team builder today. You ready to have some fun?” NO! screamed your brain. Your mouth, as it usually does, came up with something more creative. "I didn't know the workshop was cancelled…" Gibson mumbled. "Hey, a sense of humor! I love that! So, where's everyone else?" A sound as the door opened over your shoulder. "Hi there!" Will called out. "Oh. HELL. No." You turn to see a flabbergasted petite redhead standing in the doorway, taking in the sight of their team builder. Like you, she seems impressed with the proclamation on his shirt. "Come on, let's build some spirit! There's punch!" Oh, sweet Jesus… did he HAVE to say that? Damn, here she comes… You move across the room as gracefully as possible and pull Amy away before she can get within arm’s reach of Little Douchebag, who probably prefers to go by Will. "Amy, no…" "What? He said there was punch!" "Not punching. Come on, we can pull through this crapfest." There, she seems to be calm. And at least Will didn’t think to put more coffee in here. Maybe by the time this is over, her caffeine buzz will have worn off and you can get her to her room for some shuteye before the ma— Where’s she going? ”Amy! AMY!” Too late. She’s gone. Part of you is livid at watching her run out the door. Part of you registers a cynical lack of surprise at getting abandoned again. The rest of you is jealous you didn’t think of it first. Amy had abandoned you, and before the team even had a chance to make its debut. You had to give her credit for showing initiative.
