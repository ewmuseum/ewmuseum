Book of the Courtier - Intermezzo III - BRRRRRIINNNGG!

16 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
You’re traveling to another dimension… A dimension not just of wrestling and promos, but of shilling as well. On the sign ahead, all that can be read are the words “PTC GTT5: Coming Soon”. You’ve just entered… THE HYPE ZONE. Haunting music begins to play, obviously, somewhere in Emoticon’s subconscious. The PTC Infinite Gauntlet Champion’s body rests peacefully. His mind, on the other hand, is quite obviously going a mile a minute. “Consider, if you will,” a discouraging voice says, as the scene changes to a musty old library. A pale figure sits in a high-backed red velvet chair, shaking his head, “the case of Emoticon, the new Infinite Gauntlet Champion…” … *BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRINNNNNGGGGGGGGGG!!!!* Emoticon sat up in his bed, a cold sweat covering his makeup-coated face. It had begun to smear slightly, but still kept his face covered from prying eyes. As if there were any around, anyway. He shook his head out of disgust. “Come on,” the Soul of the Poet mused, sighing. “I already did this whole ‘dream sequence related to my Infinite Gauntlet match’ thing, and it didn’t help me.” Considering that appearance had resulted in defeat, some might have been shocked that Emoticon was the IG champion. But if one takes the word of numerous wrestling color commentators, the wrestling world has been shocked so many times that it should be charred a crispy black. In the case of Primetime Central’s Infinite Gauntlet, however, a legitimate shock occurred two weeks ago. “The Renegade” Rich Rollins, a veteran and indomitable competitor, lost his IG crown to a virtual unknown by the name of Emoticon. He sat by and watched as Round Nine took place, with another relative unknown reigning victorious. But as the Gods of PTC seemed to enjoy doing to plucky champions, a monkey wrench the size of Guam was thrown in the path of his reign. Three opponents. All capable. Some veterans, some not. Some huge, some not. Regardless, the PR blitz went on as always. Primetime Central put lots into its hype machine. The IG Championship match would have to be hyped, hyped, and hyped some more. That meant appearances everywhere known to man. And that meant Emoticon was very much NOT enjoying life right now. Groaning mightily, he got out of bed, sliding his feet into the nice fluffy slippers he loved so. A quick glance at the clock confirmed what he was afraid of: it was the middle of the night. *BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRIIIIIIIIIIINNNNGG!!!* “The hell,” he muttered, glancing around groggily. “My alarm clock’s not supposed to go off for another four hours!” *BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRIIIIINNNNNNNNGGGG!!!!!* It was then, and with a great deal of horror, that he located the source of the insufferable ringing sound. His cell phone. For a few moments, he stood in complete disbelief that ANYONE would call him this late. That disbelief soon faded to concern as he realized that, obviously, it was an important call. Emoticon’s life had more twists and turns than a trashy romance novel. It was certainly within the realm of possibility that something dire had happened. He made an immediate end-around his bed to grab the phone, where it was charging, and click it on. “Hello, this is Emoticon! What’s wrong?” He listened for a few moments. No sooner had he heard the voice on the other end than he immediately hung up. Emoticon shook with anger, squeezing the phone so tightly it almost broke. “HOW COULD THAT MOTHERFUCKER DO THAT TO ME?!” The Soul of the Poet fell back to the bed, defeated. In one sense though, he was quite relieved. Nothing was wrong. No one he cared about was injured. There had been no fire, no robbery, and no shooting. What there HAD been, however, was a PTC employee calling him. AGAIN. At 3:08 in the friggin’ morning. Was the Infinite Gauntlet Championship really worth THIS? Slowly, but surely, he was coming to the conclusion that it was not. Not with posters and billboards plastering his visage everywhere. Not with microphones being stuck in his face every five seconds, trying to get him to come up with some ridiculous sound byte to threaten men he didn’t wish harm on. “This idea seemed so much better in the two minutes after I beat Rich Rollins. From there, it’s been going downhill ever since.” Perhaps Drake would want to be the Infinite Gauntlet Champion. After all, he refused to speak, plain and simple. It wouldn’t be nearly as bad if Emoticon had that excuse. He KNEW that the Back Alley Brawler would want it. The loud, brash New Yorker was on the verge of a huge comeback, and a championship around the burly Bronx native’s waist seemed to be the big man’s number one mission. Or perhaps the Lord of Dreams, Gavyn Drezail would want it. Hell, it was probably DarkStar’s FAULT he almost dreamed another cliché promo. Really, for PTC’s purposes, all would make better champions with Emoticon. At least they could probably handle the spotlight and everything that went with it. *BRRRIINNNNNNNNNGGGGGG!* “I swear to God, if that’s PTC again I’m going to break the phone.” Slowly, wearily, he crawled over to where the phone had slipped from his hand. He flipped it open, and, not recognizing the number, set about putting his plan in action. “LEAVE ME ALONE!” he screamed into to the phone. The voice on the other end managed “Hi, this is PTC Adver--” before Emoticon winged the cellular telephone across the room He knew what he had to do, really. There was only one solution to his problem. Almost immediately though, he frowned. He needed a telephone for part of his plan, and the most readily available one lay in pieces a few feet from him. Things were NOT going Emoticon’s way, it seemed. ------ “You’re WHAT?!” The tables, now, had been turned. Now, it was Emoticon who was calling PTC at an ungodly hour, with an unreasonable demand. “You heard me,” the Infinite Gauntlet champion said, voice steely-cool. “I’m not going to be able to make my title defense. I have developed a rather severe head cold.” “But you don’t SOUND sick!” the woman chirped, growing somewhat annoyed with him. “Who are you to tell me if I’m sick or not?” Emoticon demanded, angrily. “I wouldn’t accuse you of lying if you called in sick to work!” “It’s entirely different!” she snapped. “You’re the PTC Infinite Gauntlet Champion! We can’t postpone the match!” Chivalry’s Last Stand sighed. Getting out of this was proving more difficult than he’d imagined. “Then don’t,” he retorted, calmly. “Strip me of the title, make it a three way match. Do whatever you have to. I simply cannot compete. Listen, my cough is atrocious!” The only thing atrocious about Emoticon’s cough was how obviously fake it was, a fact the woman on the other end recognized clearly. “If you no show this match,” she told him, “you’re never wrestling another PTC match again. Now stop being ridiculous and get some sleep. You have a photoshoot tomorrow morning at 9 AM, SHARP.” *Click* went the phone. *Sigh* went the Emoticon. He was in too deep now, he supposed. PTC couldn’t just cut their losses on him. Time and money had been spent promoting him, just as it had been spent on the Uber Judge and the Renegade. Just as it would be spent on the man who beat him. Shaking his head, Emoticon decided to head back to the bedroom, and get some sleep. Sitting with his mouth agape in his living room holding his portable phone was doing him no good, after all. He was still going to have to go to his photo shoot, and still have to defend his title. “This is going to suck,” an utterly lacking confidence Mocon said, “like nothing has ever sucked before.” Gone was the confident veneer of the champion of chivalry, of Romance’s John Doe and Aphrodite’s Right Hand. It was replaced, instead, by whining, and quite a bit of it to boot. As the long, slow walk back to the nice comfy bed began, however, Emoticon decided that, really, he was being silly. The rarely seen logical side of the most Emotional man in wrestling kicked in, and he began a short dialogue with himself, which went something like this. Emoticon: Okay, so I have to show up, no matter what, right? Emoticon: Right. Emoticon: So, therefore, if I HAVE to do this match and publicity, I may as well have something decent to show for it, RIGHT? Emoticon: Right. I swear, you’re never wrong. Emoticon: You’re not so bad yourself, self. Gods, that was stupid, but nonetheless, lets press on, k? Kay. With that thought in mind, he decided that going to bed, in spite of the secretary’s well meant advice, would be counterproductive. Instead, it was time to find out just WHY he hated the responsibilities of being IG Champion so. As it turned out, he didn’t have to look much further than the dining room table to figure it out. On it was the newest copy of PTC Magazine, opened to page 34. Page 34 was significant, as it was the beginning of the Magazine’s monthly Infinite Gauntlet coverage. All of the matches were covered, including a preview of the next one. The preview was the FIRST thing Emoticon had allowed himself to be treated like an object for, the first in a line of seemingly dozens over the past two weeks. A picture of him covered half a page, with the Infinite Gauntlet Championship over his shoulder. His body was posed in what PTC deemed to be a threatening position, one arm extended as if to tell the other three competitors to come get them some. It was all wrong. ALL wrong. Sometimes, Emoticon wondered if he was cut out for this whole wrestling thing, after all. Emoticon was NOT a mean person. He didn’t like to threaten others unless absolutely necessary in the course of his greater mission to spread chivalry. Thus, the constant attempts to get him to say that he was going to kick someone’s ass were, in a word, silly. “Let the Brawler do that, if he can even pronounce the word ‘ass’,” Mocon said, demeaningly. Even as he did so, however, he recognized the fact that the Bronx Barbarian probably had more reason to fight than anyone, if the rumors are true. He, too, was fighting for love. The love of his daughter. Beneath the battle-hardened and scarred exterior of the three hundred pounder beat the heart of a father. Of a gladiator. Of a champion. The Brawler wasn’t concerning himself with petty things like how he looked in a magazine. He was probably still awake at this hour, looking at a picture of his little girl, and training that much harder. DarkStar and the mute probably had their own reasons to be preparing, too. Everyone else was treating the contest with the respect that it deserved. Up until a few minutes ago, Emoticon was trying to get out of the match, to run away from his responsibility and sit around feeling sorry for himself. There was admittedly little time left, but Emoticon would do what he could. In his living room sat a pile of unwatched tapes, featuring matches of the opposition. Wordlessly, he picked up a tape of the Back Alley Brawler’s shot at the Jolt Triple Crown Championship. He turned it over in his hands, marveling at it. Perhaps it would be his key to victory. Perhaps not. In the end, though, there was only one way to find out.
