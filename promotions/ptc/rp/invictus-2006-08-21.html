Invictus

21 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-21 So, this is it. But if it is "it," if this is waiting for the biggest opportunity of my life, then it’s also an awful lot like waiting for the hammer to fall. This is a bittersweet opportunity for me, when it comes down to the truth, because as long as I have waited for this opportunity, as long as I’ve fought to have it, there are and always have been people saying that there’s no way I can do it justice. Then, there have always been doubters. WMW saddled me with the title nobody wanted, and then claimed they couldn’t give me any other sort of recognition, no matter how hard I worked, unless I lost that title first. Then, they threw me the dregs of their company. It was a calculated decision, I’m sure. PTC confirmed for me that, in a fair world, talent and effort are recognized, and applauded. I walked out of GTT4 not as the victor, not as the queen of the event… but as a dark-horse surprise. Someone who now had eyes focused on her, where before there was nothing but scorn. Win the Extreme Title, take it back to WMW… and receive more of that same scorn. It was then that I knew… it was time to walk away. And Global Wrestling accepted me with open arms—perhaps now, as I look back on it, much more a consequence of their rivalry with WMW than it was their great desire for my talent under their roof. I realize that now, but did not then, because after the others came along with me, I became just a name, a name in a list of people who had "jumped ship" and gone to the better company, the clearer waters. TV Title… lost, in the end to Dawn McFarlane. Master of the Mat… lost to Dawn McFarlane. And with that, a shot at the World Title, that elusive ‘big achievement’ that I had been pursuing since I signed my first PTC contract. And in response, I received the International Title, their consolation prize for being good, but just not good enough. I held that until Showdown in Tokyo—another big chance that, in the end, went to Jake Hix. I walked away from Global Wrestling. I walked away because there were a lot of things happening behind closed doors that I didn’t believe should have been happening. It was not an act of hubris, but one of conscience, and I would still stand by that decision. That decision, after all, is what sent me through the doors of GCW, the home (at the time) of Seymour Almasy, of Rich Rollins… of PTC legends. Right where I needed to be, I told myself, in order to make myself into one. But there came another colossal failure, once again at the hands of Dawn McFarlane, who appears to be, throughout my career, the most conflicted figure of all—at once, an enemy, a friend, a lover, a bane, a roadblock. Someone whose aspects are impossible to reconcile. Again, I was given a shot at another title—this time the Cruiserweight Title. I was the last member of Dies Irae to bring a title into the fold… the last of us to make any sort of impact, although I was supposed to be leading the group to a coup d’état over those in GCW who had come to view resting on one’s laurels as the real epitome of the sport of wrestling. I failed. I’m not shy about saying that I failed, but if you look back through the annals of history, I can’t be. I’ve never been the type of person to fit into a rags-to-riches success story, because it appears I’d rather keep the rags. I don’t have anybody to blame for that but myself. I don’t believe that I lack talent, but that perhaps at times, my intensity makes me too single-minded, makes me stake my worth on one match, which, when it comes down to it, is too much of a roll of the dice to risk one’s self-esteem on. Perhaps it’s silly, but it took me a long time to learn that lesson. I stand alone. I have fought and struggled up an invisible mountain of my own creation, and from this point at the summit, I can see it all. All the triumphs, the embarrassments, the wasted time, the wasted effort, strewn like little bodies down the side of the slope. But when it comes down to it, this is different. This time, I’m getting the opportunity at not somebody’s world title, but an elusive prize. The crowning glory of PTC. Something that many people want, but few people ever get a chance to have, and even fewer people walk away with. How many people, I’m wondering, have thrown themselves at the top of this mountain, only for the rope to break? If I didn’t take into account the very real possibility that I might be one of those people, I wouldn’t be optimistic, I’d be stupid. In fact given my history, I’d have to say that the chances of a complete washout are pretty real, pretty close at hand. When I look into the mirror before that match, I know what I’ll see—I’ll see a woman who wants this, wants this chance and wants this victory, more than anything else. Behind her, though, behind the warrior, sword-in-hand, is the fuckup. The one who’s screwed up so many big chances that her legacy may one day be the one who was just on the cusp, but never made it big. The past is nothing, though, if not an opportunity to learn. What I’ve learned is that it’s all too easy to make the same mistake as so many others have made. It’s too easy to get the idea in your head that you’ve got to pay attention to the window dressings that make you look like a streamlined, no-faults-apparent legend. When you’re thinking about the gloss that goes on top of your achievements to make them shine even brighter, you can’t concentrate on what’s real. The truth is, everyone has those rough edges, but this is a situation where any time your rough edges show, someone will pounce upon them, and make each tiny mistake into an error of Armageddon-causing proportions. There are a lot of ears, a lot of eyes, standing by and observing silently. Some of those people hang around to watch you fail. There’s a sadist in every bunch, of course. There’s always someone who secretly triumphs in your defeat, someone who will let you know through their lack of sympathy that they really feel you deserved everything you got, and maybe more. Maybe you’re just not good enough, their tacit comments say. Maybe you haven’t reached That Level yet. Well, you know it’s hard to get to That Level with so many other people around, it’s just a crap shoot that you’ve got to play just right in order to win. But that idea is, for all practical intents and purposes, so much bullshit. We all know there are gradations. We know there are those who don’t even try. We know there are those who try, but don’t have the talent to match their aspirations. There are the very good, who consistently hover around the top of the heap, but may not ever quite hit a consistent stride. And then there are the legends. There are the gods among men who walk into a room and cause everyone’s breath to catch in their throats. The people who, for all intents and purposes, have cults trailing along behind them ready and willing to worship every word that comes out of the golden one’s mouth. Call me overconfident, or even stupid, if you will, but I always had aspirations to be the latter. Not many people make it to that point, but when the aspiration came to me, I sincerely believed that I had the talent to do so. Maybe I was wrong. "You’re just not good enough." "The odds are against you!" "Are you kidding? You’re going up against a legend!" "You’re too experimental, too out-there. That’s not what people want to see!" "People find you personally inaccessible." "You’re a joke." "People think you’re a tired cliché." Should I go on? There are myriad reasons leveled at me each time I fail. Each time I come home with my head bowed, these are the reasons that are supposed to placate me, to prevent me from trying too hard to get back where I was before the setback of a hard loss. But I am not satisfied. I am never satisfied with mediocrity, or being told that mediocrity is acceptable. These niceties, these explanations, are their lessons for me. They are lessons I am to learn by rote, and recite whenever I get aspirations for more. Remember last time, they say? You’ve already tried this route, and it will fail just as it did last time, and the time before that, and the time before that… ad nauseum. I’m not sure exactly when I figured out that they were lying, but at some point, I came to realize the act. And the act always centered around who I am, or rather, who I portray myself to be. Since, according to some people, there’s an incongruity. Since, according to some people, I sincerely believe that I am living in Anne Rice’s next Vampire Chronicles novel. The bottom line is reasoning like theirs is useful. Useful, that is, when you can’t think of anything else to say. It’s ammo that you can pick up and hurl at will when you’re approached by someone you can’t quite explain, and you feel backed into a corner. It’s something easy—because I say x can’t be y, x can’t be y, and that’s all there is to it. This is the last line of defense, and the first thing that people pick up on when they want to get into a battle of wits with me. In reality, what does it give you? ‘She thinks she’s a vampire!’ Yes, well, some people think they’re intelligent, and they’re not. Some people think they’re highly entertaining… and they’re not. The bottom line is identity will always be a grab-bag among sentient beings. Hell, the Roman Catholic Church identifies people as Catholic even if they convert to voodoo. What matters most is not what people point fingers and say about you, but what YOU know you are on the inside. The conclusion YOU reach when you look at all the assorted gems, buffed and broken alike, inside of you. That, my friends, is truth. So in all reality… I’m not asking that you don’t judge me. That’s really an impossible request, all things considered. It’s simply in our nature to look at an incomplete picture of someone, and decide how we feel about him or her based on that. There’s really no rhyme or reason to it, it’s just whatever facet you choose to look at. Of course, that’s never enough. People never just want to judge someone for themselves and be done with it. We, being social animals, seek power in numbers, and the real power is not physical. The power is in convincing another human being that you are correct. He agrees with you, and you thus join your power together; it doubles in influence. Influence has always been the REAL way things get done in this world, so this currying of favor is not a task to be taken lightly among those who cannot or will not blaze their own trails. Some things never change. The eternal problem for me has always been that once someone else agrees with you, your idea loses some of its potency, its bite. It is no longer your idea, but is co-opted by anyone who chooses to stand under its banner. The real leaders of this world have not been the ones who would stand complacently under banners. They have been those who can successfully direct this great herd of humanity from idea to idea until a plan comes to fruition. They have been those who can innovate, and produce something never before seen which adds to the contribution of humanity on this teeming planet. It is the trend-creators who have the power, but you must note, none of them ever remain enamoured of the same trend for long. Once the tipping point has been reached, it’s time to move on to something else, because the first idea has become quite stale, its novelty used-up, its potency exhausted. There has to come a point when people will realize the silliness of always using the same argument. It’s silly to argue that those of us who choose to ‘be’ something whose existence you do not recognize are liars or otherwise fakes. It is not you who define who or what I am. It is still more ridiculous to keep hurling the same argument over and over, despite the persistence of evidence which disproves it. I am not a tired cliché if I, and others like me, can continue to exist and be created despite your disapproval. The paradigm of the day has been set. Any argument with it is unacceptable, because it threatens the opening of the doors to new ideas. For purists, this is unthinkable—we’ve just managed to clear all the rabble out, they’ll say, and now you’re wanting to let them back in?! You will always judge me. It is a conclusion I have reached that was not more apparently obvious because it was, in part, people around me who obscured the point. If I were to come out now and say that I was not a vampire, that I lied, that it was all a fake publicity stunt, you would still find another reason to hate me. If not for the trappings you hate now, it would be my hair, my clothes, my wrestling style. You will always judge me, because if you did not, what would you do with yourselves? I serve a purpose for you, and that purpose is simply whatever you make it to be. I can be your demon, your hero, your example of everything that is wrong and ridiculous in this sport. Those definitions, engineered by whatever you see on the outside of me, are made without any consideration as to whether they ring true with a closer look. It’s easier to simplify than it is to stop and consider. There you have the sum of the world: reduce, reduce, reduce into component parts, into ridiculous caricatures, into jests and jokes and jack-offs. It’s easier that way. You can judge me, but my care for those judgments has passed. I am what you make of me, or I am not, according to your pleasure, or perhaps your pain. When the time for decision arrives, however, my success will come from the talents I possess, not from the malformed opinions of people who cannot think outside of generalities, people who get uncomfortable past a certain point of so-called normality. Normality is overrated. Choosing the expected, choosing the well-forged path is not only disappointing, it is the coward’s choice. To rise into the ranks of greatness, you must be willing to take a risk when the time is right. You must be willing to see beyond the enhanced chance of failure that such a risk will bring you. You must be willing to, without a second thought, slit the throat of conventionality, then use its lifeblood as the warpaint you smear on your cheeks before stepping into that ring, scented with sweat and battle. This is my creed. If you find it inadequate, I presume you will judge it accordingly. Do so, please. I will still hold my banner high. ----- ‘Proud to be seen as an off-key in thy foreseen tune.’ -Elvenking ----- The title of this RP comes from the William Ernest Henley poem of the same name. It is Latin for ‘unconquered.’
