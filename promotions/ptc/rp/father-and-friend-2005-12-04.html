Father and Friend

4 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-04 Everyone wants to make a difference. For me, it was always through different channels. I never wanted to be the guy up front, inspiring the masses to reach for things they never thought they could reach. I wanted to be the guy that inspired that guy. The one that gave him the courage to be the person he was, and he fed off me, and inspired a generation of people, and looked fabulous doing so. I never dreamed in a million years that I would have so many fans, simply because I am who I am. All those vignettes I did for GCW and PCW? Completely me. I got so tired of the crap where people act all seriously, trying to pretend like their dads and moms beat them, and so, they’re going to take it out on someone in the ring. I’m a funny guy, and so, being funny just comes naturally. So does acting like an idiot. Sitting at a sewing machine leaves a lot of time to just sit and think. And honestly, the entire time I was in PCW and GCW, I still made costumes for people. Tessa Windsor, Ellie Kannon-Hall and I would go shopping, look at the clothes, and I’d come home and make something even better for them. Xavier loved me for it too. I worked cheap, and it saved him a fortune on clothing bills. [detail withheld] Though I was 3 at the time, I’d never met the man who fucked my mother, and injected her with his loser serum. He hightailed it out as soon as he found out she was pregnant. And since I never knew another as my father, [name withheld] was my father. He even had a corny joke he’d tell me, everytime I’d make a mistake. God, I hated that joke. "Petey boy, come, come huh?" he’d say, in his thick dialect. He’d point to an imperceptibly crooked seam, and frown at me. "Whata the fuck do you think you’re doing with the seam here?" "It’s not so bad. The stitch moved a fraction of an inch." I’d reply. "It not so bad, because you sew bad, you sominabitch." "Sorry, pops. I’ll fix it." "That’sa my boy." Sure, I laughed. But, to be polite. He smiled broadly, put his hand on my chubby cheeks and kissed my forehead. Our lives together were all about one thing, and one thing alone. Somehow, some way, my father must have been born with a torture detector, that always let him know exactly when the wrong time to verbally abuse someone is. After long nights of work, we’d walk home, which was just down the block. He’d stroll ahead of me, turning around, and walking circles around me literally. "When are you going to lose weight, huh?" "When you let me sew on a treadmill. It’s hard to walk and sew, you know." "Bullsheet. I have done. I can do." And he would. "Forty-seven. I still in shape. You be 23, and round like a ball. Exercise." "Okay, pop." "See, that your problem. You take too much sheet from people." "You’re my dad. I’m not going to talk back to you." "Good. Because I would take the back of hand, and smack you across face." "I know, pop." "Good." Like me, his mouth got him in trouble a lot. My father didn’t know when to back down from a fight. And though I never saw him fight anyone, I remember nights as a kid, he’d stagger into the house, sit in his favorite chair and my mother would grab a T-Bone, and drape it over his eyes to stop the swelling of an errant punch that caught him in the face. "You should have seen the sominabitch, Peteyboy. He at least eight feet tall, with hands like hams. I took that sominabitch down, and beat him up." "Really?" "Really. You think my black eye is bad? I give him four! Two now, and two when those heal!" So, side by side, we sat. Sewing, talking, living. I grew to love and treasure the times we spent together. When I met Jason Dread, my father was awestruck. He’d been a fan of wrestling ever since he came over to the US, watching people like Lou Thesz, Dick "The Bruiser", and a host of others on his black and white TV every Saturday afternoon. He’d sit in that chair, and sew. Between the stitches, he’d watch wrestling, with me glued at his hip. I’d met Dread at a local event, where he had a problem with one of his pairs of tights. I came to fix them, shook his hand and walked out as his personal tailor. Two months later, at the offices of Caldera Enterprises, I was signed to be the exclusive costumer/seamstress of GCW, based on the work I did for Dread. Caldera was a somewhat warm individual, certainly different than his TV persona, and welcomed me and my father to the team. The two of us stayed up nights, and though he denies it, I suspect he had a bit to do with Steven Caldera putting me in the ring, via secret conversations. "You going to wrestle this week?" "I’m not a wrestler, pop. I’m the guy that makes their costumes. Totally different." "You could be a wrestling seamstress, couldn’t you?" "Yeah. Maybe if they beat me up every week. Nobody but you and I want to be seamstresses, pop." "Bullsheet. Everybody wants to be us. They just don’t admit. But, I know it to be true." "Pop, if I ever get in the ring, I’ll be a wrestling seamstress, okay?" "Good." "Good." "Still, you’d be bad in the ring." he’d laugh. "Yeah. I know. I’d be so bad." "Yeah. You’d be so bad, because you sew bad. Mind the stitches." [detail withheld]
