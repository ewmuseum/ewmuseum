[IG47] "Cunning" Curt Cunningham is: Lord of this World

19 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LISTEN, DEAREST READERS, TO THE SONG OF CUNNING APOCALYPSE! The overbearing literality (or lack thereof) of the phrase apocalypse bears down over the world at this advanced date. The year is 2012. The world now knows one cunning master, the Cunning One, “Cunning” Curt Cunningham! Nations have been laid to waste under his cunning boot in order to be rebuilt in his own image: grandiose and triple-archfiendish and altogether cunning! Yet he cannot consider the supreme niceties of slumber, not now, for his dire mind is intensely occupied. What could trouble the mind of someone so mightily cunning? Read on, ye connoisseurs of letters, and discover! The Grand Dark Minister of Outer Northwest Beijing was on the videowall. He was a skinny man, short, and he wore his black hair long so it could mantle about his shoulders. The ear-high collar of his cape guarded his stay-puffed cheeks sinisterly. Cunningham had not wanted to appoint him, but he did well with the 15 through 35s in Beijing and that was a core demographic. His name was Hu Dat and that was not funny in the slightest. “What is it?” our archpriest of cunning machinations inquirized. He reclined in a fiendishly high-backed chair, as black as the deepest reaches of space, as black as Cunningham's own cape and clothing, as black as the murderous top hat that rested on top of our chief fiend's own cunning knee. These calls had been coming every day. He knew exactly what the Grand Dark Minister was going to ejaculate. Cunningham's dark features darkened. “More attacks from the Infinite Hand, my liege,” said the exasperated secretary-general of magnificent night-ishness. “They break the very seams of society! Ice cream trucks overturned! Factory workers given vacation with pay! A couple city blocks in flames! And their power grows, your supremity, it grows!” The Grandmaster of Cunning did not sigh. He did not speak. He did not do anything but lift both his cunning hands and boomingly clap twice to turn off the gimongous screen and disconnect the Grand Dark Minister's connection. He had no easy bake answers for that black commissioner of greatness. This dilemma reached further than the GDM of ONWB could possibly fathom. It occupied the great leagues of the Cunning One's brainstems. He would walk five hundred miles. The squid-spray blackness of the massive hall that topped the Tower of Supreme Cunning fit Cunningham like a glove dozens of sizes too big for the hand. He set his top hat with cunning accuracy on top of his head and turned, rounding his chair. This was a ritual he had engaged himself in for months, ever since the despicable word of the Infinite Hand had first reached his cunning ears. He clapped those instruments of cunning fury, his hands, together thrice. The wall he was now facing opened up, splitting into two cyclopean sections and parting to reveal a lit wall behind them. Hung on this was a mighty array of golden and silverine belts. Championship belts. To be more precise, all of the championship belts. For Cunningham, in his cunning, realized that they were the true path to power. The great villains of history had never had championship belts until now. The Cunning One had gone on a campaign to win all of the championship belts and he had succeeded, succeeded past his wildest dreams. Except for one, dear readers. Except for one. He had thought the Infinite Gauntlet destroyed, tossed into the utter volcanic depths of Mount Puhtisi never to be witnessed by humanoid vision again. He had thought that he would never have to worry his cunning head about it. But, of course, he had been wrong, wrong beyond his wildest dreams. He looked upwards at the empty spot that should hold the aurean fist of the Infinite Gauntlet. He had assumed he could make a plaster cast to get that eyesore out, but now he needed the unsightly reminder to remind him of the Gauntlet's absence. The problem with championship belts, though, was that their power waned. He had outlawed the creation of championship belts when he ascended to almighty supremacy, but with this came the decline of professional wrestling, and with that decline came the loss of their championship might. He had been baffled; he had thought that with no more championship belts, he would be unchallenged, but instead he had lost all he had worked for. His mighty cunning had, for the first time, failed him. It hadn't mattered until now. He was still lord and cunning master of the entire world. But now there was the Infinite Hand, and whoever led them held the Infinite Gauntlet. Who would have known that fashioning a championship article into a golden glove would make its power truly eternal? But here was proof, proof that the Gauntlet was truly Infinite, proof that, above all else, Cunningham was infallible. He had had a chance to win the Gauntlet once, long ago, without realizing its power. He must possess it. But how? No one would agree to a match with the ruler of the world and it was much less likely that they would put up the Gauntlet. So how? How to possess it? He needed help; even his indisputably cunning mind was not too arrogant to realize that. And he knew the only people who could aid him. “Bertram!” he called. “Ignite the engine of my CunningCopter!” He heard no response from Bertram, but he didn't need to. Bertram was his loyal manservant, dark-skinned in a tweed jacket, perpetually hunched but inexplicably ultra-competent. Cunningham made his way to the roof of the Tower by way of a cunningly placed set of helixine stairs and Bertram was already waiting for him, tipping his hat from the pilot's seat of the CunningCopter, blades whipping pure shearing death through the palpable night troposphere. “How yuh doin', suh?” Bertram called in his thick cajuno-plantation accent. Our cunning archmastervillain climbed into the right-handed seat, brushing his cape about him with supreme impatience. “Fine, Bertram,” Cunningham retorted, biting off his words as if they were the fingers of his most hated enemies. “Packed yuh a lunch, suh.” “Fine, Bertram.” “Pastrami sandwedge wit'—” “TAKE OFF, BERTRAM.” Readers, I hope you will trust me when I say that the silence within the CunningCopter was as thick as your mother's rear end. Which is to say, quite thick indeed! Bertram, that lovable butlo-chauffeur, was as sheepish as Dolly, the cloned sheep. “Um... where we goin', suh?” At that, the Cunning One could only laugh his evil, impressively cunning laugh. Where were they going!? As if Bertram could not deduce it! But, of course, why should Cunningham assume he could? Bertram was not privy to every cunning thought inside his master's cunning cranium. “Where are we going, Bertram?” Cunningham emphasized and repeated. “Dat's what I axed yuh, suh.” “We—goddammit, Bertram. It was a rhetorical question.” Bertram grinned slyly. “We are going!” Cunningham restated with great and cunning bombast, “to the mysterious jungle-tundras of sub-Asia!” And yes, dear readers! The “DUN-DUN-DUN!” button was duly pressed indeed and there was a great DUN-DUN-DUNning to be witnessed! DA-NA-NA-NA CUNNINGHAAAAAAM!!! The traditional offering of pretty daughters was wrapping up in the tiny village of Bhut Kong Pyong and Cunningham was glad for it. Normally, he reveled in these offerings, which happened whenever he visited a city, but there were two things in the way today. Firstly, there was absolutely no time. Secondly, this village had a serious case of the uggos. He supposed, in his cunning boredom, that the large tree in the middle of the village was the actual Ugly Tree of legend and song and that every child in the village was forced to be dragged along it after birth. Seated in a bamboo throne on a pedestal above the villagers, Curt Cunningham was absent. He barely looked at the girls being paraded before him. He barely listened to Bertram's perfect translations into English from Lower Eastwestern Mongolian. His eyes were on the badly-kept roofs of the village houses. The heat was nearly unbearable. Normally, he wouldn't even come to a place like this, but Bhut Kong Pyong had the AT&T distinction of being the last village before one was deep into the secreted double-shire known as sub-Asia. Bertram could not land his helicopter directly in those jungle-tundras, for the wild Uzbekimese tiger-bulls would certainly get at it. And so he was here, having his vast stores of cunning bored out of him by these quaint villagers. He didn't bother to hide his boredom from them. The village chieftain or whatever was a ridiculous looking man who wore a beard of feathers in order to appease the Turtle God. Cunningham would have told him about the only god being the one seated before him, but the only thing the man ever appeared to say was “Turtle God!” Sometimes Cunningham thought Bertram was fooling him because the man seemed to say a smattering of syllables that was different each time, but with one thousand thirty eight and one half ways to say “Turtle God” in Lower Eastwestern Mongolian, he guessed that he could live with it. “They's all done,” Bertram announced, taking a step backwards. He crumpled his hat in his hands, looking nervously over the girls, wondering if one of them would be offered to him today. Cunningham, of course, had no inclinations towards this course of actionstance. He rose from his seat with a great impatience and stormed down the uneven steps, cutting a swath through the startled villagers. One of his various and ever-useful mottoes was “Goodbyes are for chumps, wussies, and super-chump wussies.” He took care not to bump into the town tree. He could see the jungle-tundra's edge before him, the frost-hugged trunks of deciduous jungletrees welcoming him to a world of mystery and unnameable terror. He swept his cape around his shoulders despite the mind-dehydrating drop-it-like-its-___ conditions. Bertram struggled to keep up, slamming his hat atop his head upside down and scuttling. Cunningham cunningly heard Bertram following him and swirled around like a hurricane of impossibly cunning fury. “Bertram!” he snapped. “Yassuh!?” “Bertram, do not try to follow me into sub-Asia. You won't survive.” “But suh—” “But nothing!” “But—” “Bertram!” “I just—” “Bertram!” “I—” “Cunningham!” If you are wondering, O readers, whether or not “Cunning” Curt Cunningham shouts his own name in orgasm, rest assured that he does! But the voice that uttered his fear-inducing name was not his own! In fact, it belonged to a woman whose descriptors could (and indeed did!) include “fat,” “ugly,” “old,” and “yo mama.” That is to say, the woman was yo fat, ugly, old mama. Snap! The woman waddled towards Cunningham, extending a pale white string of beads and what appeared to be the teeth of some water-submerged predatory creature. When Cunningham extended his hand, the woman nodded emphatically. “What in the Blue Fuck of Kali's Sixth Left Untesticle is this?” impostulated our tripleantihero. The woman began to talk with a rapidity reached only by Super Saiyans of at least level four, and also they have to have done the Fusion Dance beforehand. It was left to Bertram to transnegotiate the languages. “She sayin' that iss a mighty charm. Or maybe Turtle God. Iss a mighty Turtle God that will bring you the great glory—or Turtle God—of the heavens—or Turtle God.” Bertram fell quiet as the woman began to speak even faster, shaking the necklace at them. “Now she is seriously jes sayin' 'Turtle God' overn' over agin.” “Keep it!” Cunningham declared, ripping the necklace from the woman's hands and hurling it to the ground. “I need no charms!” He poked his cunning index digit inerringly into the very center of Bertram's chest. “Don't follow me, Bertram! I need you alive to shine my shoes and to remind me why Song of the South was racist!” Bertram's eyes were as big as unidentified flying objects, but he didn't follow when Cunningham turned to the great jungle-tundras ahead of him. The woman was yelling frantically after him and Cunningham, that legend in the field of applied cunning, could only assume that she was was merely repeating “Turtle God” over and over. His cloak, flapping dramatically about his darkly-clad ankles, disappeared as he did into the mists of sub-Asia. The maze of trees about him did not scare him. He had been amongst them before, many years ago. He had completed his rigorous hypertraining amongst them. Once, in his more confused years, he had been intimate with a few. He knew these trees, even if he did not know the way. He could not know the way; no one knew the paths of sub-Asia except for the masters of this land, hidden entities of great power that would only reveal themselves after the most dire tests had been passed. And yes, Curt Cunningham intended to pass them with bright and vibrant hues that soared amongst the clouds. The only question was when. The snows on the ground grew thicker and the air grew hotter, denser, digging into his minuscule pores to retrieve the sweat within and bring it out on his skin to freeze. The vines and shrubs on the ground threatened to drip him into the snowdrift. And the fog, the fog was nearly as dense as a child of West Virginian meth-addicts, dense enough that even our cunning anti-tagonist's cunning perception was not enough to discern a great bargain. The keening of arctico-tropical vampire parrots kept Cunningham alert. If he found himself amongst a murderflock of those hellbirds, he might indeed be doomed. But they would not be his test. He knew in his heart of hearts that they would not. His cloak was now of intermittent use as he struggled to keep himself aright in the torrent of alabaster heat-powder. The tiny slaps to his perfect, chocolatic face were mocking titters from the protoheavens. Shouting at them would do him no good. He must simply bear it and let his anger build deep in his bilious gallbladder, waiting for his eventual war on the great and nigh-untouchable lords of the firmament. For the first time, the Top-Hatted considered the price of failure. He might be left to wander these unforgiving snow-savannahs for all time, never dying, never ceasing to walk, circling about in an unsuppressed spiral of undying and spiraling wandering. Or he might simply fail his test and be cast out of the sweaty icescapes of sub-Asia forever. And then what would he do? Watch as his global empire crumbles at the Infinite Hand? Go into hiding? Kurt Cunningham would be a good pseudonym; they would never discover him then. No! He shook those chillfever-induced thoughts from his cunning trap of a mind. No, he would succeed! He could not fail! What non-mathematic test could best him? He stood against the gust of snowy heat with a newfound resolve and lifted his face to the sky. His top hat, that icon of utter evil, did not stir from his scalp. And then the earth rumbled under Cunningham's cunning boots. Bewildered, Cunningham twisted his supremely and cunningly fit body around just in time to see the ground opening up in a manic fissure and a huge white beast of a beast ripping its way out of the womb-cradle of the earth to taste the bitter tang of fresh air for the first time! And Cunningham, looking upon that ungodly creature with broadly-grown eyes, knew what it was. It was a creature of legend. It was the Giant Ice Binturong and its wail was madness. Massive arms lashed out at Cunningham one after another as the vast creature of proto-sentient insanity dragged itself up out of the deepest pits of sub-Asian crust. The Black-Shrouded Cunningmaster, with unmatchable nimblity, dodged each body-shattering blow, dancing backwards in order to keep himself upright. The creature was at least as immense as a stand-alone Tim Horton's and it smelt quite strongly of elderberries (or perhaps of well-aged youngerberries). Beady eyes settled on Cunningham as it advanced towards him, its bulk making him feel quite sure that his deathbed would be made of fluffy, comfortable snow. The earth sagged at the Massive Frost Binturong's approach, trees toppling over, threatening to smash Cunningham into the hard-packed heatundra if not for his acrobatic awesomnitude. His mind whirled as he tried to formulate a cunning escape. He had expected an Uzbekimese tiger-bull or at the very most, a Japbodinamese tree-dragon. A Huge Snow... no, the Huge Snow Binturong was something else entirely. It bore down on him with malice in its evil, animal face. There was no counter that Cunningham could see. “Suh!” Imagine, loyal readers, the striking of a completely and indisputably badass power chord on overdrive. And then imagine the totally wicked sweet bending of that power chord. That was the sound that accompanied Bertram's arrival as he leapt up onto a fallen tree trunk with a huge machine gun cradled in his arms. Cunningham ducked as the Giant Ice Binturong's paw swept through the air, catching nothing as the Cunning One cunningly rolled to a position of safety. “Catch!” Bertram i-jaculated, hurling the machine gun to his master as best he can. And with a skill at catching that is unsurpassed, Cunningham caught the weapon in his arms. He locked it. He loaded it. He aimed it. The Swollen Chill Binturong turned to face him, eyes flashing black. “I hope you like soup, Chicken Noodles,” Cunningham sneer-shouted in the deepest voice he could muster. He squeezed down the trigger and an ungodly barrage of bullets emerged from the flaring barrel of the gun, tearing the air into so many shreds and burying themselves like tiny little binturongs into the Giant Ice Binturong's stupid FACE! Gimme a fuck yeah! The top hat on Cunningham's head barely rocked as he ran through the ammo belt. Great geysers of blood riddled the Giant Ice Binturong's head and body and one of its eyes exploded in an explosion of gore and pus and pus-y gore-blood. When the firing stopped, reduced to a series of East African-styled clicks, Cunningham could feel unfamiliar fear clawing its way up his intestines, obviously eager to settle in one of his vital organs. The Great Cold Binturong still stood. Was it not over? Yes, dear readers, it was, for the legendary Giant Ice Binturong slowly slumped onto its front paws like a tired student. Or a dead student. Or, I suppose, a dead tired student (that is to say, a student who is “dead tired,” not a student who is both dead and tired). But the Giant Ice Binturong was simply dead. Malboroian smoke filtered up from the barrel of the gun. Cunningham's labored breath filled the jungle-tundraic air. Bertram used his hat to dust his thighs from the snow. Slowly, Cunningham let the gun sag in his grip. He was alive. He was alive! He wanted to celebrate, but his entire body was as numb as a sucky Linkin Park tune (Ha ha, which one is he talking about? All of them?). But the encore was not part of this mash-up. Until now. Like a binturong clawing its way up from the ground, a great maroonic crimsonian smoke erupted from the Great Ice Binturong's back, fanning out slowly until it was transformed into the floating, gas-supported torsos (and arms and heads) of three ghastly chieftains who fixed the Cunning One with eyes of brutal wickedness and untapped secrecy. These were the three that Cunningham sought. “WE ARE THEE MOST-SECRET SECRET CHIEFS OF THE MYSTERIES OF SUB-ASIA!” came their screeching howl from beyond the world. “WHO CALLS UPON OUR SECRET COUNSEL!?” “It is I!” Cunningham cries out in confident rivalry to their power. “Curt Cunningham! Once your student but now master of the whole world, including Hoboken and East Cairo!” “THEN SPEAK, YOU URINE-DAMPENED FORGOTTEN BROTHER FROM HAPPY DAYS!” they shouted as one. “YOU HAVE BESTED OUR RATHER EASILY BESTABLE TEST AND HAVE THUS EARNED OUR COUNSEL AND A SINGLE PITIFUL REQUEST!” “I must possess the Infinite Gauntlet!” Cunningham expostulates. “I must have its power! Show me how I can wrest it from the grip of those undoubtedly pimply rebels of the Infinite Hand!” “IT IS SIMPLE, YOU HORRENDOUS AND UNEDUCATED TWUNT! YOU MUST GO BACK IN TIME AND WIN IT!” Cunningham was flabberwildered. Go back in time? A solution he had not even thought of! How could such a spark of inspiration have eluded his masterful cunning? No matter; he had known enough to come to those who could tell him. He summoned his courage and raised not one, but both his fists to those secret chiefs in defiance. “Then send me back!” Cunningham cried. “This I request of you!” “NO!” those specter-captains boomed. “No!?” Cunningham repeated, frustrated and confused. “YOU IN THE PLURAL SENSE BESTED OUR RATHER EASILY BESTABLE TEST, BUT IT WAS HE WHO ACTUALLY, YOU KNOW, REALLY BESTED IT!” And who did they point at? Who did they favor with their ghoulish and semi-corporeal fingers? It was Bertram! Bertram, who looked as shocked as Cunningham to be singled out. “YES! HE WILL BE SENT BACK TO WIN THE INFINITE GAUNTLET! HE IS THE TRUE MASTER OF CUNNING!” Curt Cunningham shook his head in utter disbelief. Bertram!? Surpass him!? No! Bertram's cunning was nowhere near his own! Bertram was a fool of the lowest, most contemptible, most spit-upponable order! Those chiefs of the ghastly after-death raised their hands and pointed their fingers again. From their crooked, awful digits issued three rays of blinding scarletry aimed right at Bertram. There was only one thing to do. It consumed Cunningham. He leapt like he was trying to stop Hinckley's bullet. He felt a heat over his right ear and he squeezed his eyes shut. DA-NA-NA-NA CUNNINGHAAAAAAM!!! “Cunning” Curt Cunningham sat on his bed in his personal room at Cunning Enterprises. It was a motel he had managed to buy and partially turn into the center of his villainous realm. There was a helopad on top, a helopad he was quite proud of because he had built it with his bare hands. Well, Bertram had built it with his bare hands and Cunningham had watched about half of it. That was enough; after all, Cunningham had actual cunning plans to enact. Evil to orchestrate. He stood, stretching his arms and yawning. He was, to put it as mild as red bell peppers, fatigued. He scratched his stomach. It was time, he thought, for a meal. The Infinite Gauntlet weighed heavily on his mind, but he could come back for it whenever. He wasn't going to sacrifice too much, not when he had a hero to dispose of in Wrestle Coast: Cascadia. Not when he had a promotion to bring under his heel. But where to eat? Burger King? They did have the Steakhouse burger. Or maybe Quizno's? Much better than Subway, after all. His cunning mind tried to devise a suitably satisfying meal. Ah well. He would decide on the way. “Bertram!” he called. “Start the CunningCopter!” He approached the door, drawing himself up to his full, villainously cunning poise. Time to meet the day. A shadow swept into the room behind him. “Hasta en lo infierno, chimichanga*.” Translation: “See you in the Great Satan's impeccably decorated foyer, you tasty Mexican dinner dish.” Before Cunningham could turn, the gun was lowered and a single shot exploded in the room, the bullet slamming into Cunningham's temple and felling him instantly. Somewhere, John Woo released a dozen doves into the sky. Stepping forward, the shadow revealed itself... ... to be a mirror image of the man just killed! The shock, dear readers! The shock! Curt Cunningham—the living Curt Cunningham—smiled wickedly as he stood over his past self. Perfectly executed. And now he knew what he must do. The Infinite Gauntlet would be his at all costs. His wicked, maniacal, villainous, and absolutely cunning laugh filled the small room. fin
