Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-02 The scene opens on a house on a street somewhere. It's a nice house, with nice hardwood floors and a two car garage and a picket fence out front and a happy family inside eating dinner. There is a knock on the door. A man in his mid-forties, hair graying at the temples, answers it. Standing on the stoop is our protagonist, woof woof! 'Hello.' 'Hi.' 'Can I help you?' 'I'd like to see Cereal Killer.' '…' 'Cereal Killer. Is he in?' 'Um, I think you may have the wrong house, sir.' 'Okay, I heard that this might happen. Listen to me, Mr. "Ripper," I don't care HOW many crazy title belts you've won, you have NO right to keep that poor, pathetic wreck of a man locked up in there, refusing access to anyone who comes to see him. Someone should have put you in your place a long, long time ago, Mr. "I'm so big and imposing and I'll Ripsaw you to hell if you should ever disagree with me"' And with that woof throws open the storm door and punches the middle-aged father of three in the face as hard as he can knocking him, the middle-aged guy with the graying temples, out cold. 'Hey, wow, that was suprisingly easy. The Ripper must be losing a step in his old age. Huh, guess I should go find Cereal Killer now. Hey, why am I talking to myself outloud like this? I should stop. It probably looks kinda weird to those people standing over there on the sidewalk staring at me. I'm going to go inside this house now.' And so he does. As he enters the house's foyer he encounters the man's wife looking at him with a shocked look upon her suprisingly youthful face. Her voice trembles as she speaks. 'Wh…wh…who are you?' 'Don't play dumb with me, Sirius Smythe. You think that just because you've taken the form of an attractive housewife I won't recognize you? Ha! You misunderestimate me, old man. Now, I demand that you tell me where you're keeping Cereal Killer locked up!' 'I…you…my husband.' 'Oh stop with the act, old man. It'll take more than shape-shifting into a comely mother of three to fool me! Now, where's Cereal Killer!? Tell me, or I'll dispatch you in the same fashion that I just dispatched the Ripper! And if I made such quick work of him, imagine what I can do to a frail old man with such wonderful, pert breats as you!' '…' woof begins to lunge for he when suddenly he stops and looks around the house. A curious look comes across his face. He scratches his chin as he looks from side to side as if he's pondering something. After a bit of this he stops and looks at the woman standing in front of him. 'Excuse me. Could you, uhm--what address is this?' 'It's…uh…325 Appleridge.' woof reaches into his pocket and pulls out a scrap of paper. '325 Appleridge, you say?' 'yes' 'Oh damn. I do have the wrong address. I'm, um, I'm terribly sorry about this. I, um, yeah, sorry for knocking your husband unconscious. And sorry for accusing you of being an old man. I, uhm, I'll be leaving now…' As woof turns to leave the housewife suddenly steps towards him. 'Wait…' woof turns to see that the look of shock on her face has changed to one of pure, unadluterated lust.' 'Take me.' 'What?' 'Right here in the entry way! Oh, strange intruder, my life is so boring. I need some excitement! Do me, right here in the foyer!' And she jumps into his arms and their mouths lock together and he begins to pull her clothes off and suddenly the director yells, 'Cut!' (and the writer, like last time, suddenly and inexplicably switches to script-style) Director: All right, everybody, that's a wrap for today. We'll shoot the rest of this scene in the morning. Now, we're starting early tomorrow, so get a good night's sleep. woof and the actress playing the housewife are up and dressed. woof: Well, see ya tomorrow. Actress: Yeah. have a good night. woof: You too. Hey, you wanna have drinks together tomorrow night? Actress: Hey, sure woof: Tremendous woof heads off the set towards the exit door. As he goes he waves goodnight to the various members of the cast and crew he passes along the way. When he gets outside he pulls a cigarette out of his pocket, puts it in his mouth, moves it around with his lips, and then hands it to a nearby homeless person. 'You need this more than I do, friend.' he says in a soothing, comforting tone of voice. Suddenly, a reporter jumps out of some near-by bushes. Reporter: Hey, Jerome Macintyretosh from the Clutterville Times-Dispatch. Can I ask you a few questions, Mr. woof. woof: Don't you think it's a little late? Jerome Macintyretosh: Not really, no. woof: Oh, in that case, go ahead. JM: Okay. First off, don't you think it's a little weird to be starring in a porno film with a plot based around a professional wrestling storyline which you are also presently involved in? woof: No. What's weird about it? JM: Well, I don't know, it just seems kind of, well, weird. woof: You're a terrible interviewer. Do you know what happens to terrible interviewers? JM: Um, no woof: They fall down wells. JM: Wha-- Mr. Macintiretosh is unable to finish what he was saying as he trips and falls into a well that was in his path. woof continues walking. As he walks his whistles a tune. A tune for which he'd long ago forgotten the words but for which he will always remember the melody. It was the song his mother would sing to him at bedtime when he was a baby. She had such a lovely singing voice… Then his cell phone rings. 'Hello' 'Hello, woof, this is your agent.' 'What do you want?'
