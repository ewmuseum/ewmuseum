Christopher Camero Cable Pimp Joined: 18 Feb 2003 Posts: 58 Posted: Wed Apr 09, 2003 4:17 pm Post subject: Global Wrestling Title Match RP#2 It had been three long years since I last crept through the menacing vestibule of Union Corp. Yet, my trembling feet guided me fluently through the maze of tunnels. My mind reciting the directions to my lower extremities; “Pass the first three doors then turn left, sixteen paces north, a hard right, follow the yellow brick road to the first blue door.” Like clock work, my footsteps echoed as I inched my way towards the blue door. I checked my wrist watch, quarter past twelve. I was late. I rushed to open the door. Inside the stale air and raunchy surroundings of the hidden vestibule were lost. Behind the mangled blue door lay a marvellous fortitude of inter-connected offices, experimental workstations, and miracle laboratories. As I entered, the men and women scrambled, pausing only long enough to pay me a half-hearted gaze. The familiarity of the surrounding made me question my own memory, had it really been three years? “Young Hawthorne” I twitched and gave a surly grunt as the voice of Nelson Anderson beckoned for me from a far. Anderson was, and always had been Dr. Hawthorne’s right hand man. I had spent the latter part of my teen-age years living with and idolizing Dr. Hawthorne, he had taken an interest in me, and I in him. Which leads me too Mr. Nelson Anderson. He was always around, where ever Hawthorne went Anderson was in close pursuit, and when Hawthorne needed test subjects for the then experimental chrysalis, Anderson nominated yours truly. After a brief squabble Anderson convinced Hawthorne I was the ideal subject, and with that I became intertwined with Union Corp., and known to the other employs as young Christopher Hawthorne. It wasn’t until I entered the wrestling world that I changed my name back to Camero in an attempt to free myself from the burdens of chrysalis and Union Corp. “Young Hawthorne” This time Anderson was standing in close proximity, his weary eyes tracing my jagged frame. I smiled as his eyes met mine. “Mr. Anderson” I found myself uncommonly nervous as I outstretched my hand cordially welcoming his presence. “Too macho to give an old man a hug I see” It was evident his rasp voice mocked me, while he returned the smile. I was shocked to see how the last three years had aged Nelson Anderson. His skin had lost its elasticity, and his hair had slowly begun to fade from a jet black to a silver grey. Even his shoulders slouched forward, heeding the sign of old age. “So where’s Dr. Hawthorne” It wasn’t that I was in a hurry, or disliked conversing with Mr. Anderson; but I knew it had to be an emergency for Hawthorne to request my presence here at the heart of his operation. “He bids you his sincerest apologies Christopher as he was required to leave for London shortly before you arrived.” “London?” I questioned Anderson, pressing for further information. Such as a reason to why I had been summoned here at quarter past the witching hour. “It’s merely a formality I assure you. However, Dr. Hawthorne has instructed me to brief you on your latest assignment.” The words had no more then slipped from Anderson’s forked tongue when an internal alarm set my central nervous system a blaze. The word “assignment” and “your” had caused a look of bewilderment to creep across my youthful face. However, Anderson continued unfazed, “There has been a string of mysterious murders in the past month, with the latest happening early this evening. However, until tonight the murders did not appear connected.” “I’ve read about them, but how is, Union Corp., and Dr. Hawthorne involved?” “Were not!?!” The false assertiveness in Anderson’s voice appeared forced leading me to believe he didn’t trust the words he had spoken. “Anderson!” The tone of my voice signalled my disbelief and concern in the matter. I wanted the truth, not some re-formatted rendition designed to appease the unquestioning mind. “Dr. Hawthorne would have my head, if he knew I told you.” “…And I’ll have it if you don’t” Anderson winced at the thought before growing deathly silent. I took his silence at face value, he wanted to tell me. Yet, his devotion to the secret forced him to maul over his decision- to tell me the truth, or to stick to the script Hawthorne had laid out for him. Hesitantly Anderson’s now meek voice broke the deafening silence, “Five weeks ago, someone broke into Union Corp. they stole a new genetic altering drug. It’s similar to chrysalis it heightens the users motor senses, while increasing strength, speed, and intelligence. However, the drug was still in the experimental stages. It’s been known to cause aggressive outbreaks and uncontrollable rage.” “And what makes you think the individual or group who stole this new drug is responsible for the murders.” It took every ounce of strength I had to keep my voice from wavering. I had be a party to the effects of chrysalis, seen how the drug altered my own genetics and chemical balance. The idea of a new more powerful drug, in the hands of a killer, scared me. Anderson continued to talk, however, his words fell on deafened ears. I could see his lips moving, yet, all I could think of was a rage filled killer with super human strength, speed, intelligence, and god knows what else; victimizing the city. The fear instilled a sense of urgency within me, and I found myself foolishly willing to become intertwined with Union Corp. again. “So, where do I come in?” “Well, Christopher, you were once our best agent, and according to your records, you’ve continued to use chrysalis. Therefore, you may be the only one able to catch this killer. So, let me bring the car around.” “What for?” I questioned “There’s no time to waste, were going to get you down to the murder scene before the cops trample it.” --- We had been travelling nearly twenty minutes before the black Lincoln town-car rolled to a gentle stop. Anderson, looked across from the driver’s side and said, “This is your stop.” I hesitated slightly before exiting the car; it wasn’t like I was scared of what I find. No, it wasn’t like that at all. I was, however, scared of who I was about to become. It had taken me three years to get overall the horrors I saw while working for Union Corp., and now here I was more involved then ever. I questioned why, but no answer was clear. Anderson had approached me from the other side of the car. Together we sauntered off in the direction of the police tape, trampling over it as we passed into a dingy farmhouse. Once inside it didn’t take long for us to uncover the dead body. I studied the dull face; even the swelling of death could not hide the emaciated features. She might have been pretty once, but now her face was cold and waxen. A mole on her left cheek, soft mouse coloured hair. Late thirties, I thought, but I have never been good at guessing women’s ages. I leaned closer; there were small pinpricks of red in the skin around the eyes and a circular bruise on the lower edge of her jaw. “Who is handling the case for the boys in blue?” I knew full well that any case of this proportion was going to be handled by Harry Brown, the local forces finest. “Detective Brown” Anderson confirmed my presumption. “Did he get any identification on her? An address?” I asked “Our sources say everything that was found is still here.” They had set her effects upon the sill: a broken comb, a shard of mirror, and a wafer of sunlight soap. In the corner to our right was a hat, a shiny hat of glossy black straw with a cluster of red wax cherries on the brim, brand-new- must have cost at least twenty to thirty dollars. I picked it up and looked inside, but there was no label. I peeled the beige canvas down past her shoulders and looked at the black clotted pulp that had once been her neck. Two cuts, the first beginning below her ear and running perhaps three inches, the second just below the first and running ear to ear, deep enough to sever the windpipe and gullet, cutting back to the vertebrae. The hair beneath was matted with blood. “Oh, I’m going to lose my cookies, Chris you mind if I wait in the car?” “Not at all; Anderson.” I watched as Anderson re-traced his footsteps to the car. As he disappeared into the night I knelt down and looked at the scattering of clothes, memorizing their order upon the floor. Then, like an archaeologist, I began sorting through layers: Side-sprung boots and black mealy-smelling woollen stockings off to one side. They had cut the shirtwaist down the front and cut the bands of the petticoats, peeled the clothes in layers from her body. Blood on the bottom of the petticoat, not a great deal- at first I thought it her own menstrual flow- another coarser slip, darned but clean, a top underskirt, close ribbed brown stays, a cheap rusty coloured over-coat with silver buttons, and a black shawl. At the bottom of the pile was a frayed red scarf, soaked through and quite stiff with blood. Judging by the ragged edge, the cops had cut it off; I paused a moment and examined the knot. Then I went back through the pile again. This time I didn’t miss a shape or store tag. Amidst the petticoat lay a motel calling card: The Red Dragon. The Red Dragon was a run down hotel on Prince St. The rates are based on the hour system, and its typical occupants were hookers and whores. One goes there only when there is nothing else. The beds are striped naked and the bath is only cold water. You awaken at dawn by police sirens or gangster gun shots. I stood, turned back to the slab, and lifted the cloth to look at the body. And gasped. There were two horrid cuts: one deep and clean, crossing the intestines; the second jagged, running her reproductive organs up to her navel- as if the killer had set about to disembowel her like a deer. The police surgeon obviously hadn’t lifted her skirt. The killer had. “Pretty ugly, isn’t it?” The voice was not Anderson’s, and it startled me, forcing me to wheel around. “Oscar? What are you doing here?” My voice was full of disbelief. “I was just about to ask you the same thing Chris” “Lucky for me, I asked you first, Eh Oscar.” “Lucky for me, I’m the one wearing the gun.” Game, set, match. Oscar was right, he sported two dual desert eagles, while in hast I had neglected to retrieve my dual berretta. In some situations the truth is not an option, this was one of those times. I tried to change to subject, “How could the surgeon have missed those gashes under her skirt?” Oscar shrugged, “Maybe he bats for the other team, and is insulted by the sight of a woman.” Oscar’s comment signalled he did not intend to play cat and mouse about my reasons for being at the crime site. “What are you doing here Camero? You some sort pervert who gets off on this stuff?” As he spoke Oscar’s hand slinked towards the butt of his desert eagles. “Nah” “You some sort of private eye?” “Nah…” “A terrorist?” “Nah…” “A cop?” “Nah…” “Then just what the hell are you Camero?” I could tell Oscar insisted on the truth. So I gave it to him, too some degree. “I’m a problem solver.” Like I said it was the truth, to a certain degree. “Well Chris, I suggest you forget about this problem.” Urged a disgruntled Oscar “And why is that?” “Because this lady here is Veronica Stevenson, a former high ranking government official. A government official involved with the research on the National I.D. cards.” Oscar was clearly upset, as despair sliced through his typically assertive voice. “You think her death had something to do with your investigation?” The answer meant more to me then it did Oscar. If he did have proof or reason to believe that this murder was linked to the National I.D. cards, then it was very possible that whoever broke into Union Corp. was also involved in the National I.D. cards. “I’m not sure yet, and I won’t say until I’m totally sure.” _________________ [detail withheld]: so you want to be my bitch?
