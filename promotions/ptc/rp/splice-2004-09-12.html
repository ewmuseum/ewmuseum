Splice

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-12 It is Edinburgh. Replaced are the walls and castles and fortresses depicted in the film Braveheart, featuring Mel Gibson. Office buildings, mansions and modern pubs replace those. Edinburgh would be one of those towns that would not be expected to so easily sway into a modern era. The Scottish people are, if nothing else, stubborn and resistant to change, at least their ancestors were. Edinburgh is a prime example of how some things change and some things remain the same. The Scottish people, who were once a brutal and drunk society, are now a rebellious, drunk culture. That is easily seen on Vrabel Avenue, home to multiple pubs and a brewery. Walking down that street is as close as walking into the past of Scotland as anyone can get. Brake lights are the greeting of Stewart Campbell as he pulls toward the Vrabel Avenue intersection. The light is red and Campbell is already late. He pulls into the left turning lane and flips on the correct turn signal. He checks to the right and turns. The road is nearly abandoned. Whores and drunks stand on the side of the road, doing their daily business rituals. It’s mainly for that reason that Stewart Campbell’s parents forbid him from driving down that road although there are obvious others. But nowadays it is a given that teenagers, even in Scotland, will rebel. It is then that a scream pierces the silent, dusty night. It is not a scream that one could merely dismiss as someone taking fright of a snake or a rat. It was one that said quite bluntly “help me”, even without the enunciation. It so startles Stewart Campbell that he finds his foot pressing on the brake and skidding into a parking space. Without even thinking he takes out his keys and locks the vehicle. He races toward the sound that is now a regular, continuous shriek. Within seconds it is silenced, but Stewart is now close enough to know of the location of its source. He inches around the side of a drug store to see a man kneeling over a woman. The man holds her down by her mouth with his palm and rips off her shirt with his other hand. Several empty beer bottles lie near. Stewart Campbell sees this and sneaks back on the drug store side of the wall, attempting to figure out the best possible plan of attack. His legs say something different, charging onto the scene and spearing the man into the wall. Campbell lays in the sneakers and smears the man’s face into the ground with his hand. He lifts him up and tosses him into the wall and slams his head up against the wall several times. After the man falls and whimpers in pain Campbell moves over to the feminine figure lying on her back. All she can do is mouth to him “thank you”. He helps her to her feet and the light from a nearby streetlamp illuminates the both of them. Just then he notices why the man was after her. He had never seen anyone more attractive. Suddenly she moves away and sprints across the road. Campbell turns around to see two hovering yellow orbs. It moves toward him, catching the light. It is the man, and he smiles and his teeth are yellow. Campbell catches a glint as a brown object comes crashing down on his skull. He falls to the sidewalk. The girl runs back now, trying to help Stewart, but she is tossed aside. Police sirens are abroad and the man sprints away. Stewart Campbell and the girl are both unconscious in a dark alley on Vrabel Avenue with beer bottles abundant. The girl’s shirt is ripped off and there are various abrasions on her body. The man is gone. That is what the police see when they get there and the obvious conclusion is drawn. One Hour Later “We believe, Mr. and Mrs. Campbell, that your son was out at one of these pubs with a group of his friends. We think that he met this unnamed girl and she wouldn’t give him what he wanted, so he moved to take it from her physically in an alley not too far away,” a police officer says quite casually. “He attacked her and strangled her until she was nearly unconscious. He began to strip her when she found a beer bottle and cracked it over his head. He fell unconscious and she, being already injured and out of breath, passed out as well. That’s how we found them when we arrived.” The parents of Stewart Campbell are, quite frankly, appalled. Stewart lies on the hospital bed next to them, his eyes closed. He’s heard everything in the past several minutes, although he is still pretending to be out. “I’ll swear to you, officer,” Stewart’s mother begins, “that you are mistaken. You have drawn conclusions, and I’ve never seen a sign of any physical violence out of Stewart. Besides, he was at a movie and coming home – “ the police officer laughs. “What?” his father asks chafed. “That’s what you know or that’s what he told you?” the police officer grills with a smirk. The parents clinch their teeth and fists and say nothing more. “We’re going to need to take your son in for questioning. The girl is being questioned now, she came back pretty quickly … lucky for your son here.” He stands up as the door is opened and accepts a donut and some tea from another police officer, who has a cup and a donut of his own. The new police officer whispers in the old one’s ear. “Oh,” is all that the original police officer can offer. “Ma’am, we’ve just found out that your son is innocent,” the police officer says, “the woman testified that she was attacked by another man and your son came to save her. The man cracked him over the head with the bottle and ran because he heard our sirens.” The parents look at the police officers smugly. Stewart pops out of bed and looks at his parents, then back at the officers. “Don’t worry, boys, I forgive you,” he pats the cynical one on the shoulder and walks out the door, his parents follow, bewildered at their boy’s antics. They walk out of the hospital and get into the family car. They drive well away from the hospital before one says a word to another; the silence is nothing if not awkward. “What were you doing on Vrabel Avenue?” his father interrogates coldly. “I turned onto it when I heard the scream,” he says, “I didn’t realize what street it was.” “Have we not made it absolutely clear to you, son, that you are not to travel down Vrabel Avenue at any time? Especially after dusk,” his mother reinforces. “You did a nice thing, son, but you both could have died out there. Would it have been worth it then? I daresay you would have died were it not for the police sirens, her too. Villains don’t leave witnesses. That was the most impulsive thing you’ve ever done in your entire life. Don’t do it again.” Her words ring in Stewart Campbell’s brain, echoing off of his cortex and returning to their source. “Most”. He couldn’t believe her response. “Impulsive”. Didn’t he do a good thing? Didn’t he save a life?” “Thing.” The turn signal clicking interrupts his thoughts as his father turns right into his driveway, across an empty lane of traffic. The doors all open and the three family members exit the vehicle, grabbing what they took with them. Campbell finds his car parked alongside theirs, brought in an hour earlier by his parents after they found out what had happened. As Campbell entered the door, ascended up the flight of stairs and entered his bedroom he still could not believe what had just happened. He did do bad things. Not only did he turn onto Vrabel Avenue, which was explicitly forbidden by his parents, but also he did it at dusk and then lied about it. But overall was it not fate that arranged the meeting with the girl and the saving of her? Would it have happened otherwise or would she be laying dead, mangled and mutilated in that dark alley at this very moment until someone finally notices the undulating odor of death rising from the alley and says something about it. Campbell looks down at a reclining chair sitting in the corner of his room and thinks for a moment. Stewart Campbell is an addict in the year of 2012. Technology has grown and created a great gaming community. Campbell joined this community immediately, at first reaping little of what he sowed. Yet now he has become a true force to be reckoned with. He devotes all of his extraneous energy to this one game. It is called Ambition and it is a wrestling game. Campbell chose this game for his own admiration in the wrestling circuit of the early 2000’s. The game is of the massive multiplayer genre. Everyone creates a character of his or her own and they compete against each other. Stewart Campbell chose the year 2004 and created a character named “The Avatar” William Gwynn. The name itself would generate attention, for being an “avatar” already alludes to the outside world. In the strict community of online gaming this is looked down upon. Campbell submerges himself in this game every spare moment and he has created a dominating force. Sometimes he can’t help but wonder if the game is really real, if this is really just an alternate universe where these things actually happen. Wouldn’t it be glamorous? But it already is, because glory is coming to Stewart Campbell in every moment he is on that game. How many Core stars started out three and zero? That is William Gwynn’s record and he is beginning to strive for bigger and better things. Campbell found out that his character has a shot at the Number One Contendership for the Pride Championship. He has a shot at a shot with Munson. But he has to earn it against Rzarector first. And that is why he finds himself sitting in that recliner, with his head bent back and his fingers prompting the virtual reality senses. September 11th, 2004; Phoenix, Arizona “Fifty-nine years of age,” a voice whispers, “William Gwynn is getting a comeback joke. And the crazy bastard is regarding him as an unripe talent, not fully grown. If anything could get Gwynn angry … it might be that. I tell you what, a lot of people are predicting this tSc punk, but I think they’re betting on a long shot. William Gwynn is no Jason Hughes yet, I’ll give you that, but he may well be sooner than later. And fifty-nine is well past anyone’s prime. He may have cardiac arrest in the center of the ring.” “Even so he is an icon,” another replies, “and Foreman won the title on a comeback at a late age. He’s coming back again, you know. You have a point, of course, more comebacks fail than succeed. And if I were coming back I’d want a little bit easier of an opponent. Gwynn will capitalize on the ring-rust, that is given. He’s got Salidus Stone as his manager; of course he’ll have every disadvantage of Black memorized. I think that people betting on Joe Black just don’t know William Gwynn.” William Gwynn has heard this from the locker room of the small gym. The two voices originate from just outside the door. They don’t have a clue that Gwynn sits but a couple of feet next to the door with his eyes closed, getting prepared for another training session with Salidus Stone. Finally he rises to his feet and opens the locker room door. One of the men outside falls through the opening; Gwynn looks back but doesn’t say a word. He makes his way diagonally across the gymnasium to Salidus Stone’s office and knocks lightly. A gruff voice answers, “Come in.” Gwynn complies, opening the door halfway, and shutting the door behind him lightly. He stands next to the door. “Well,” the voice says, “have a seat.” As Gwynn sits down, he admires the various awards and medals in Stone’s office. While the gym may not be that special his office is. If anyone ever questioned Salidus Stone’s success, he could simply take him or her into his office. There are several pictures of other’s who went through his training school. Chris “Strike” Rush, former GCW Champion and President, is everywhere. Chris “The Dark Horse” Neptune, who was never defeated in WrestleGod Four is not lacking. William Gwynn can even identify pictures of himself. Caesar Gore, Attila, Amerigo DeFur and Clover, some of Stone’s lesser and more recent graduates can also be found. Salidus Stone himself sits playing solitaire on his lab top computer. The irony of it all is almost enough to make William Gwynn laugh. Finally Stone curses at his luck and swivels his chair around. “Curse it, Gwynn,” he says, “all I needed was a red eight and I had them all lined up.” “That’s a shame,” Gwynn replies. “Isn’t it?” Salidus replies. “Anyway, William, I’d like to congratulate you on your victory over Tony Aielli last night. It was a shocker for a lot of people. You’ve shocked a lot of people in your run in cW. No one expected you to get off to this kind of start, even Matthew Lanier. I think that may be why you’re getting these tougher matches now. Either Lanier wants you higher or he wants to put your little streak to an end. You’ll certainly be getting a shot at both. What he wants is irrelevant at this point it’s what you make it that counts. You’ve got two upcoming matches. In my mind they’re both equal. You’ll have Joe Black in GTT. Beating him will give you an impressive win. You also have Rzarector in cW on Pride 5. Training for one will help you on the other. Even so, we will need to go through the distinct wrestling philosophies of both, point out their disadvantages and exploit them.” William nods, “So, shall we go to the ring?” William and Salidus rise from their seats. Gwynn holds open the door for Stone and the two walk through. The main ring is empty and they move to it. Salidus looks out into the group of students at the gym currently. “Hey Doyle!” he shouts. “You available?” “Gotta get home to the girl, although I’d love to have a shot at Slick Willy,” Doyle answers with a laugh, “seems like nobody can get their hands on him in the ring. If you’re looking for a guy with my build though, Amerigo’s here.” “He is?” Salidus asks in wonder and amazement. “Would someone bring him here then? I’d be interested in seeing this match.” It’s a couple minutes before Amerigo DeFur stands in the ring across from William Gwynn. The two of them were both hotshot rookies. DeFur was injured while unbeaten in singles and tag-team competition. He’s been wrestling the indie circuits since, still recovering. DeFur is bitter, much like after Stone was injured. Stone found a home in training, though, with Chris Rush. DeFur has found no such outlet. He’s been striving to keep up with the bills of what he bought during the prime of his wrestling career for his son Walden and his wife. The business of wrestling is cold, and many have learned that the hard way. Some respond to it differently. Amerigo DeFur responded in a way that makes men fearful to go against him. DeFur’s scars from battles long ago have healed physically but he carries their burden mentally. The two lock up, it is DeFur with the early advantage with an arm wrench. Gwynn reverses it with a hiptoss. DeFur gets up and Gwynn whips him off the ropes and into a dropkick. He drops an elbow and some boots. DeFur gets up and the two meet each other with right hands. DeFur gets the advantage and throws Gwynn into the corner, laying in shot after shot. “Act on impulse,” Stone counsels. Gwynn tosses DeFur away and smacks him with a huge right. He goes to the top rope and drops DeFur with a hurricanranna. DeFur rises into a fireman’s carry. DeFur gets up again … SURREALISM! Into a cover! One! Two! Three! Stone raises Gwynn’s hand. DeFur rises groggily and DeFur and Gwynn shake hands. DeFur stares for a moment, obviously shocked at the sudden spurt of strength out of Gwynn. Gwynn leaves the ring and walks away. “Most.” “Act.” “Impulsive.” “On.” “Thing.” “Impulse.” (Fade.)
