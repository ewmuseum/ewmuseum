Butterlfies and Hurricanes: The Redemption of Cereal Killer - Part Eight: "Don't let yourself down, don't let yourself go"

25 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-05-25 The Domicile of Darkness. Back when Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy was terrorising the souls of XUW wrestlers, and winning title upon title upon title, the domicile had been his base. A strange mixture of manor house, castle and gothic cathedral, the dark stone walls and spires were set back into a rock face that bordered a set of mountains which made Mordor look inviting. Some said that the domicile was a place only of legend; that it backed on to mountains that led the way to Hell, the River Styx running behind it, the reason that The Ripper escaped death when it came for him. Others scoffed at these tales, these myths, and said it was merely a large house, built to intimidate and confuse. One thing that all have said, though, is that the location was never, ever divulged to anyone outside of The Ripper’s closest allies, and that the domicile seemed to be twenty minutes away from everywhere. “It looks bigger than I remember it,” said Cereal killer, stepping out of the back door of the vintage Rolls Royce car. Nobody knew where The Ripper got his money from. He was never ostentatious about it, other than the car he was driven in, and the size of his abode. He wore no jewellery, no watch, and his clothes were not picked from the latest brands to hit the shops. If there was one thing that characterised the whole milieu of The Ripper and the domicile, it was one of permanence. Everything seemed to look like it had been around forever. Indeed, it was hard to accurately guess the age of the former XUW World Champion. He looked as though he might be in his mid 30s, but it was if he was hewn from the living stone of the earth. The only man who might have had the key to the mysteries of his past was walking slowly towards Cereal Killer. Sirius Smythe had been a lawyer in England for longer than he, or anyone else, could remember. Specialising in criminal proceedings as a defence lawyer, he gathered quite a reputation before leaving for America when he reached pensionable age. Upon arrival on the other side of ‘the pond’, he immediately went missing for the best part of three decades, only returning to prominence as the close associate and lawyer of The Ripper. There was some unholy bond between them that nobody could fathom. They didn’t appear to be related in any way, and as far as anyone could tell, they had no shared history before Sirius brought The Ripper to the world of wrestling. They say God works in mysterious ways, but he had nothing on Smythe. “It has been a long time,” said Sirius, moving well for a man who had recently passed his centenary. “Not long enough,” mumbled CK, his unease growing with every step towards the heavy wooden double doors that served as an entrance to the domicile. His feet crunched the gravel of the driveway, and all three men came to a halt before the enormous portal. The Ripper smiled, and as if acting on a prompt, the doors slowly swung open, beautifully oiled hinges making no sound at all, save for the light noise of the displaced air. “The Ripper expects that you were hoping to never see this place again.” Cereal Killer just nodded. Even when he had been Jack’s tag team partner, he had never come close to understanding what his motives were, or how he lived. Every night he had spent in this building was nigh on sleepless, and when slumber would finally take him, the nightmares he suffered were beyond anything he had experienced before or since. “You could say that.” The three men walked into the domicile, moving slowly to allow Sirius to keep up with them. The ancient man turned him eyes to CK briefly, before settling them on The Ripper. Despite his age, the twin orbs sparkled with vitality, and with distaste. “How did this fool take the news then? Were there tears?” he asked. “All was as expected. Everything that he did was perfectly in line with the plan.” “Ummm, hello?” interjected the voice of Cereal Killer. “I am still here, with you. Can you at least have the decency to not talk about me in front of my back…” His voice trailed off as the other two men stopped walking, and turned to face him. “I’m the one wrestling in this… this tournament,” he said, his fragile voice betraying any attempt at bravado. “Shouldn’t you be trying to stay on my good side?” The Ripper and Smythe seemed to weigh this up for a second, before they both turned and walked onwards down the corridor. It was a wide passage, with aged wooden floorboards covered in the centre with an antique carpet. The walls were also made of wood, dark with varnish that soaked into it in a time long since past. Paintings hung at irregular intervals, mostly work in oils, and mostly of generals, field marshals and other military figures. On occasion they would pass a door, and all would know, without having to try the ornate brass handle, that the door was locked. Illumination was provided by the steady flames of gas burners set into the walls. There were enough so that few shadows found space to play against the walls; the only thing that Cereal Killer was thankful for in the entire place. “Will you show him it?” asked Smythe. “It must be done,” said The Ripper. “He must have some idea of what he is fighting for here. There is more at stake than just reputation, though fame and publicity must never be underestimated.” Smythe cast a glance over his shoulder at the figure of Cereal Killer, who was lagging behind, feeling disconsolate. “Did you have to pick one so feeble? Why not a colossus, a brute of a man? Those are the type that bend to this work the easiest.” “That is not the nature of this tournament, and for his size, this man is physically strong.” CK was surprised, but soon found himself glowing with pride. Praise from The Ripper was rare enough, and despite himself, he was pleased. “He is physically strong,” continued The Ripper, “but his mind is weak. He has no resolve, and the only thing that has kept him going is a series of delusions, each more intricate than the last.” “Will he even understand what you show him?” asked Sirius. “Even if he does not, it will alter him.” “What are you talking about?” said CK, a little louder than before. Casting a cursory glance at his former partner, The Ripper turned back to Sirius, “Leave us now, for The Ripper must show this one what he needs to see.” Sirius nodded, and moved away, opening the nearest door with a practised flick of his ancient wrist. The door swung closed behind him almost noiselessly, and once more The Ripper and Cereal Killer were alone. The larger man motioned towards the door opposite where Sirius had left, and inclined his head for good measure. “You… you want me to go through that door?” asked Cereal Killer. The Ripper nodded his head, strands of black hair dangling down in front of his impassive, gunmetal grey eyes. “This is the thing I won’t understand; that I won’t comprehend?” “Yes.” “You can’t try to intimidate me.” “The Ripper believes he does not really have to try.” Cereal Killer grimaced, “So does the door just open?” “It will open when you turn the handle, but be warned; beyond that portal is a sight which your eyes will not wish to see. It is the very essence of the darkness, the power that drives The Ripper. You will stare into infinity and realise your insignificance. Vertigo will hit you, and you will feel as if the entire world has stopped spinning, that the only thing moving now is your head, your mind, as you orbit, confused, dizzy, helpless. Do not open the door lightly.” “What choice do I have?” asked CK, but it was rhetorical, and he opened the door. Beyond, he saw darkness, a blackness so intense that there was… “It’s a black room. What are you trying to do? I can even see the light switch! Look, if I tu…” Cereal Killer’s words died on his lips as The Ripper withdrew the hypodermic syringe from his arm. The Ripper shook his head, ruefully, and the drugged wrestler fell softly on to the carpeted floor of what appeared to be a perfectly normal, if rather dark, sitting room. ------------------------------------------------------- Throughout all of his gimmicks, throughout all of his life, Cereal Killer had always thought of his illness as a blessing. It had held him back many times, but eventually it had a knack for getting him out of tough situations. Sometimes it was just the sheer inventiveness that allowed him to think of an excuse, or an alibi so convincing that it just had to be real. Living like that made Cereal Killer a brilliant liar, and at times he was the best liar of all; the kind that thinks they’re actually telling the truth. Fact and fiction got mixed up in his mind all the time, but he continued to stumble through life with his imaginary friends, his attention deficit hyperactive disorder, and his solitude. It was never loneliness, because he always had the constant buzz in his head. Even in the wrestling ring, they’d be shouting instructions to him all of the time. Sometimes it became confusing, and he could lose focus, lose the impetus that had taken him so far… but because he was rarely trying to win, rarely risking his ego for a potential gain, it never seemed to matter too much. “Where am I?” Sometimes he had wondered, especially in the past few weeks, which situation would make him better in the ring, would make him a more deadly competitor. “Why’s it so dark?” Would he better off with the voices, the alternate opinions and the buzz that he had lived his entire life around, or would it be beneficial to be alone, to be away from them, allowed to live on his own at last? “Why… why’s it so quiet? ------------------------------------------------------ The Ripper looked through the glass partition and smiled. Cereal Killer was starting to come around, and the moment of truth would soon arrive. It was often a labour intensive process, to shape someone as he was doing, but the rewards always seemed to justify his means. One mammoth hand reached down and twisted the brass handle on the wooden door, and he strode into the corridor, walking confidently but patiently towards the next one along, the door that would lead him back to Cereal Killer. It swung open, as if on hinges made from air, to reveal the Cruiserweight Tournament finalist lying down on his front, stripped to the waist. His head was resting on one of his elbows, and his other arm was curled underneath his stomach. Hanging from his mouth was a thin trail of spittle, bulbous at the bottom, descending towards the patterned red and black carpet. “What have you don… to me?” asked Cereal Killer, trying to roll over on to his back. “Do not try to move too much right now. You might be slightly weakened from the anaesthesia.” “My head… the sounds… I can’t…” CK groaned and finally managed to turn himself over, flat on his back, his eyes weakly attempting to focus on the solitary light bulb dangling from the ceiling. “What was in that syringe?” “Not a lot,” replied The Ripper. It’s what you were given after you passed out that had brought on some… some changes. It was an unusual mixture of substances, but Sirius insisted that he knew what he was doing. The Ripper supposes that you do not get to be that age without learning a few things, but then again, perhaps he’s senile, and he’s killing you.” Cereal Killer was returning to clarity, the woozy feeling starting to fall away like so much skin needing to be shed. With it, came a focus he had not experienced before. “What did you give me?” he demanded. “You have taken Thorazine, Etrafon, Haldol, and some others. The Ripper believes you were even given Ritalin. Some of the more important drugs were… not ones you might find on the open market.” “Why? What’s the… oh. Oh my God.” “You see the point? You see what the darkness can do for you?” asked The Ripper, a grim smile curving his face into a rictus of mockery. “The voices.” “You see the clarity you can be given? You understand how you could see the world, how you could feel everything?” Cereal Killer didn’t even hear the words, despite the lack of anything else to distract him. His perfect ears and mind ignored the words coming in as irrelevant. He was, perhaps, still in a state of shock, and in a state he never remembered being in before. “The voices are gone. The voices are all gone…”
