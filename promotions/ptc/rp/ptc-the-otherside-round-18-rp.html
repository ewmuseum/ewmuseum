"The Otherside" - Round 18 RP

7 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Oh…that’s no good.” As part of my usual morning routine, I stand in front of the mirror after my shower. This used to be 100% about making sure I looked in as good physical condition as I felt, but now it is 50/50 along with admiring myself. I should be ashamed to admit it, really, but somehow I find myself just going along with it. You’re right. I stumble backwards in a mixture of shock and fear. Whether it’s in my imagination I don’t know, but what I’m seeing is a red-eyed reflection of myself talking back in the mirror. “Wh…what the…” I’m not sure why I bother saying things such as that. For one, I heard exactly what he said and, two, I don’t think I believe in mirrors talking back in such a manner. At the end of the day, I suppose I just want to see if it is a momentary lapse of my mind. Whether it is or not, I don’t suppose the word stumbling helped much either. Oh, you heard me. You’ve changed, Daniel. I can’t believe it. It isn’t even the fact that I’m getting advice from a mirror, or the fact that with the red eyes the mirror-me looks so evil. It’s the fact that I can’t help but take offence at the words I can hear. “No I haven’t- I don’t care what you think…you’re a figment of my- Honestly, the hardest thing about listening to a mirror isn’t trying to convince yourself that what it says isn’t true; instead, the difficult part is trying to convince yourself it isn’t real, then hearing it come back with remarks that leave you dumbfounded, with nothing to say or do even for just a couple of minutes. Of course you haven’t, Daniel. That’s why you spend time admiring yourself in the mirror. That’s why you spend more time with that belt than anything else. I even try to block my ears, but then the words start to sound even louder. How is that possible? Even I don’t know, and I’m the person who’s saying it. Face it. You’re arrogant. You’ve let this victory path get in the way of who you are. Two-way mirrors are funny things in a way. And I don’t mean the ones that you can look through i.e. what they use at police stations, I mean mirrors that talk back to you. At first, you’re in complete denial because the only thing that pops into your head is the thought that you’re going delirious. Crazy, even. And, by funny, I don’t mean that you have to laugh the troubles away. They’re just peculiar, because what is talking back to you is your conscience. And everybody knows that you just can’t hide from your conscience. It sticks with you every day, tagging along like a desperate friend who just won’t let himself leave your side. So you have to listen. “N…no I haven’t…” At this point, I even take a step back which just goes to show I’m scared of the truth. Deep down I know that it is the truth, but right now I’m willing to ignore that fact and carry on the way I am. I’m caught up in the moment, you see. Deny it all you want, Daniel. But everybody knows denial is the first sign of any major problem. “I’m not in denial. I just know that isn’t the truth. Why admit to a lie?” I keep desperately trying to convince myself that it isn’t real, or at the very least that what it is saying is an utter lie. Part of me wants to just admit it and cure myself of this arrogance that is burrowing further inside my veins, but the other part just keeps saying ‘What’s the problem? You have the right to be arrogant, so why not flaunt what you’ve got?’ Of course it’s a lie. That’s why it’s easy to see you care more about that belt than anyone or anything- “SHUT UP!” Now I start to get angry, and I look towards the bed at my beloved Laura. A confident woman, but she can take anything to heart. I cover my mouth as soon as I realise just as loud as I am, but I’m still not sure why I bother doing this. The sound has already escaped from my mouth, so I’m not preventing anything. I scrunch up my face, desperately hoping that she isn’t going to stir while it looks as if I am simply arguing with a mirror. Thankfully, she just rolls on to her side and scrunches up her face as well. Don’t get angry, Daniel. Once you start using useless phrases like that, you can safely say that you’ve made progress in admitting and confronting your demons. I start to get angry and I don’t know why. In a horrible moment of panic, I grab a pro wrestling trophy sitting on my desk and raise my arm, blatantly indicating to my alter ego that I am going to run from my problems. Running away doesn’t solve anything. Just look at the belt hanging on the chair. You polish it daily with wax, for crying out loud. You take better care of that than- This time I simply want to scream until my chest bursts and my organs drop to the floor with a thump, but instead I just have to settle with a normal bout of shouting and throwing, this time waking Laura from her beauty sleep, as she calls it. “I SAID SHUT UP!” In a flash of panic, I throw the trophy sitting on the palm of my hand forward into the tall, golden mirror, smashing the glass into tiny shards that spread all over the floor. I drop to my knees, slowly realising what I have become. “I’ve become what I hated the most…” I don’t even notice her sitting on the edge of the bed behind me; I’m too busy wiping away the rare drops of tears that stream very slowly down my face before anybody can see I’m crying. Of course, now I’m too proud to let anybody see me in a state like this, so I run out of the room, out of the house and out of the street without even acknowledging the awakening of my beloved. --------- I lean backwards, placing my derriere slowly on to the drenched park bench as I groan with relief at resting. Running three miles straight wasn’t easy, even for a supposedly fit athlete such as myself who is supposed to have the stamina to call that run ‘too easy’. I tip my head so that I am looking at the sky, taking a deep breath of the cool, fresh air that is always present at the Crescent Park at 6:15am. I look around, noticing that the entire park is deserted, which I think is a shame considering how beautiful it is in when it isn’t crowded with people. But, then again, would it still be beautifully deserted if many people were to try and experience that beauty? I suppose that is just one of life’s cruel displays of arrogance. This is just one of those magical, beautiful secrets that I’m forced to keep to myself for fear of making the secret obsolete. I’d always thought the same way about wrestlers from different federations around the country competing against each other in inter-fed tournaments. Up until now, that was. Only there was a difference between those two. The first of the two listed life cruelties was natural; the latter was just man-made. But recently, namely since I have become a success, I’ve begun to see the beauty of it. Hence, my entry. Experiencing the coming together of athletes from around the globe. All wanting to prove their talent, all wanting to achieve everybody’s dream; becoming one of the best in the world. And, in the rare occasion that two amazingly skilled wrestlers collide, the vision has such a tough, unrivalled beauty that you can’t resist but cheer for. I believe this is why there are so many wrestling fans. I stand up after about ten seconds. Right now I just can’t sit still. I have some thinking to do and, whenever I have serious thinking to do, it is an unexplained necessity that I must be strolling when doing so. I take a few paces forward onto the delightfully straight, clean concrete path that goes in a circle around the park and simply follow it. I don’t do it for a particular reason; I just do it because I am simply one of those people who pace back and forth just because they feel like it. I can finally admit to myself that I have become something I never wanted to. I’ve become the sort of person I saw on television that I got into wrestling for. The type of person who wins a couple of matches, gets cocky and soon begins to get their nose further and further up their own- well, you get the idea. But now, thanks to a strange way of displaying my own conscience, I can finally give myself the psychiatric rehab I deserve, and it won’t cost me a penny. I can get less serious about this sport, and I can finally start enjoying it again instead of making it into just another job. I lean against a tree behind me, looking around the block as if to look for anything remotely interesting that I can concentrate on. I look slowly to my right and, out of the corner of my eye, I can see a couple walking into the park and, even though at first I feel sorry for them, I soon begin to feel sorry for myself. They are an ordinary couple; you can see it in their casual appearance. I figure that they must have ordinary, boring jobs. The sort of job that you wake up on a Monday morning and think, “Oh, God, do I have to go in today?” But I had a good job; a job that I actually look forward to in the mornings. But then I realise that, with this job, there is a certain aspect of expectation surrounding you that is unrivalled in any other job. If you keep losing, everybody is at your throats, not least yourself in the midst of them all. And, with that, winning became a necessity. It needed days of nothing but exercise and training, but that was with the job when I first signed up anyway. I don’t want that expectation. I want winning to be a bonus, not a necessity. “I’d better get going…” Ah, that’s better.
