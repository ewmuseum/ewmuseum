"Questions, questions, questions."

19 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-19 * * * * * If only Legion hadn’t been a wrestler before a murderer, the morning might have been vastly different. “You have exactly 5 seconds to tell me what the fuck is going on, and give me a great God damned reason why I shouldn’t beat the living piss outta you!” Aidan demanded, his brow arched in anger and voice booming. Legion, disguised as a UPS deliveryman, held the assassin’s rifle pressed against his hip. Hiding it would do no good, and if Aidan refused to accept his explanation, using it as a form of coercion seemed plausible, “Because your life is in danger, Aidan.” “How do you–– Why should I believe you?” scolded the 19 year old, his voice heavy with annoyance. “Because Gordon Hart, courtesy of Mr. Arlington, sent me to protect you.” Legion replied, a blatant lie, using a name Aidan would undoubtedly remember. The sun warmed the August afternoon, and Legion could see that Aidan was suddenly transported back in time to a distant memory. Aidan’s eyes suddenly seemed to look through Legion, rather than at him. He knew that Aidan was remembering what happened late last December, only a few days before the turn of the New Year. As a wrestler, Jonathan Sail had read about Aidan’s triumph over tragedy during the Universal Title Tournament. He wrestled his way to the finals as an upstart rookie, only to have his father murdered a few days before his monumental upset victory against the vicious wrestler, Clyde. As a hitman, Legion knew all too well that Gordon Hart had saved Aidan’s life – only a few seconds away from being slaughtered by the madman known as ‘Casca’ in the mercenary underworld. Legion had asked Gordon about the circumstances of that day, and was spared no detail. Aidan stood there, a look of confusion washing over his face. His emerald green eyes seemed distant and glassy, “I told nobody that name, ever. Why is this happening again?” “I don’t know, kid. But if we don’t hurry, neither you or I will be around to find out.” Sail replied, another lie that was slowly transforming into the truth. Sent on a mission to kill without question, Legion was now going to protect while seeking answers. * * * * * Itagaki Freeze, Iron Lung, and now JaZon King. I’m quickly approaching my 33rd birthday, which falls in late November, and I can honestly say that those three competitors helped complete my very first “winning streak”. While I started training shortly after meeting Danny Harmon in a bar nearly 10 years ago, I had trained for 24 months, starting my career in late 1996 in a small bingo hall on the outskirts of Philadelphia. Since that day, I had never won three matches in a row, a streak per say, until last week. Thank you, Itagaki Freeze, Mr. Lung, and JaZon. I continue to shed the skin of a man whom tried to run from what he truly was: a wrestler. And I’m not finished yet, by any means. Like a wild boar sharpening his tusks against the coarse trunk of a tree, I’m preparing for future kills. Ironically, I’m still running from the kills of my past. When I killed for a living, I never allowed myself to wrestle at 100% ability. It was an easy way to appease my desire to wrestle, but becoming successful would compromise my anonymity as a killer. So I lost matches, very often, and avoided the spotlight. But oddly – before Danny was murdered – I never wrestled at 100%, either. And I still can’t begin to figure out why. At the time I clearly had no possible idea that I would seek revenge on Danny’s killer, which ultimately led to my downward spiral into contract killing. Yet, for those 24 months of training, and 2 years of shoddy wrestling…there was absolutely no reason for me to hold back my abilities. Yet I did. I suppose I’ll eventually figure it out one day. But now my next opponent is a woman. If there’s one thing I’ve learned in my dual-life, it’s never underestimate a woman. Ever. They will beat you at cooking, cleaning, running, jumping, and any other act if the right desire is put into their eyes and their hearts. Most men look at women as objects of affection, and that is honestly one of our greatest weaknesses. Most men haven’t seen a woman drive a ice-pick up a man’s nose, like I have. It’s a nosebleed that never ends, believe me. They haven’t seen a woman comfortably field-strip a 9mm weapon, much like I have in years past. Inside the wrestling ring, women are starting to dominate a favorably male-driven sport. Underestimating women is what probably has led to Karina Wolfenden and Shadow getting so far in this tournament. Cocky wrestlers, often staring at cleavage rather than deciding which wrestling maneuvers would ensure victory. Worrying about hitting a woman and hurting her, when they should be thinking about rolling her up for the three count. As for Shadow, if she had wrestled against me many years ago, she probably would have honestly defeated me. But no more, times have changed. Sorry lady, I’m giving it 100% and moving on to the bracket finals. I know, somewhere high above the clouds, Danny Harmon is smiling. Hidden behind an endless pitcher of dark Guinness Stout, he’s grinning with delight at how great his beer tastes in heaven, and how great his last protégé’ is finally making his career. * * * * * The diner smelled of lunch, an aromatic mix of both apple pies and steaks. Sitting across from one another, Aidan and Legion sat in silence. Both had food before them, Aidan’s half eaten hamburger and fries still littering the plate, while Legion’s pickle and smattering of ketchup clearly defined his satisfaction with the food he was given. And his distaste for pickles. The man known as Jonathan Sail inside the wrestling ring stared at his should-be target. He watched as Aidan seemed lost in a perpetual state of thought. Sail admired the boy, but decided it wasn’t the proper time to mention that keen fact. For all Aidan knows, the UPS delivery man sitting across from him just sent his world into a dizzying, and dangerous out-of-control spiral. Sail studied the Aidan O’Brien as he sat. Standing straight up, Aidan’s height never breached more than an inch above six feet. A far cry from the seven foot, awe-inspiring height of his legendary father, Armageddon. But what he lacked in height, he made up for in width. Nearly as wide as he was tall, the boy must have weighed in around 250lbs of solid muscle. His neck was virtually non-existant, and atop it, Aidan had the charming face of innocence. A dominating jaw melted into a slightly boyish face, while two sea-foam green eyes pierced out from behind a sandy-blonde mane of hair, lengthy but not extremely long. Jonathan knew that when Aidan smiled he could win over anyone with it’s power. A natural quality that Jonathan also had. The ability to put people at ease without saying a word, and the natural power to comfort them when you did. ”What’s your name? I doubt it’s really ‘Seymour’ like it says on your nametag.” Aidan asked, quietly, a quick flick of the wrist towards Legion’s UPS disguise. His voice was almost child-like, as if the life had been sucked out of him. Quiet, frail, and innocent. For a moment, Legion sat there wondering whether or not to tell him his real name. But he needed the boy to trust him, so he finally responded with the truth. “I was born a Jonathan. Named after my grandfather, on my mothers side.” A flicker of a smile danced across Aidan’s face as he replied, “I was named after my grandfather as well. Except on my father’s side. And Jonathan is a much better name than Seymour.” Jonathan chuckled at Aidan’s comment as he sat there, his belly full of food. He was wondering exactly where to go next. What to do? How to save this boy from another painful experience? But more importantly, Jonathan sat there wondering exactly who and why Aidan was targeted. Again. The second time within a calendar year. Why was this boy so damned special? “Why have you stopped wrestling?” Sail asked, the wrestler taking prominence over the hitman. It took Aidan a minute to respond, although Sail believed he was simply lost in the confusing events of this morning, rather than stumped by the actual question, “Because I watched as my father – after suffering career ending injuries – was reduced to third-rate wrestling conventions in order to make money. So I decided after my Universal Title loss to Xavier Kannon that I would enroll into college. I missed the first semester, actually, so I’m technically a semester behind all of the other freshman…but I love it, the college life. I’ve proven myself inside the ring, now I’ll prove myself inside the classroom.” “Your major?” “Business Management,” Aidan replied as he picked up his hamburger and took another mouthful. Given the circumstances of the situation, Aidan cast manners aside and talked with the food in his mouth as he chewed, “I’ll wrestle once I graduate, but once that career is over I’ll open my own business. Or perhaps work backstage for a wrestling company, I don’t know. I just want to be able to support myself if an injury cuts my career short, much like it did to my father.” “That’s very admirable, kid.” Jonathan replied, an honest smile sweeping across his face. “Thanks, I guess.” A moment of silence passed between the hunter and the prey. Aidan stared off through the window of the diner – their booth was pressed up against the front of the building – out at the parking lot and it’s occupants. Around them, the hustle and bustle of a diner during the mid-day lunchtime rush was giving life to the otherwise quiet business. Waitresses danced around the floor, customers casually entered and left the small building through a door that rang a small bell each time it was opened. The air was still thick with an enticing scent, and if Jonathan listened close enough, he could hear the sizzle of the steaks cooking in the kitchen. “So what are we going to do?” Aidan asked, his emerald gaze once again on Jonathan. “I’m going to try and keep you alive, and figure out what exactly is going on.” “Why did you have the gun?” “In case others tried to involve themselves, or if you weren’t so cooperative, I would have used it as a method of persuasion.” ”Others? There are others out there?” Aidan asked, the frailness of his voice now long gone, replaced by equal parts curiosity and disbelief. “Not technically. The exclusivity on your contract was for 48 hours. I was––” Sail watched as Aidan’s eyes were dancing back and forth, in heavy thought. He was equally as bright as he was strong, and it only took him a matter of seconds to put it together. His body tensed slightly, “The contract you took.” Legion watched as Aidan’s face flushed with anger, “That’s right.” The admittance only enraged Aidan, as he shot up from the booth and quickly stormed towards the door of the diner. As Jonathan quickly scrambled through the contents of his wallet, in order to pay the bill, he heard the front door of the diner being slammed open as Aidan exited. As Jonathan was pulling out two 20 dollar bills to foot the bill, the alarm on his watch started to vibrate. It took only a fraction of a second for Jonathans heart to start racing, trying to beat its way out of his chest. He threw the money down on the table and jumped to his feet, running for the exit. He would need to stop Aidan, who hopefully hadn’t decided to run off, and get him hidden immediately. He reacted so quickly to the alarm, he hadn’t bothered to turn it off. The exclusivity was over. The 48 hours were up. As Legion burst through the front door of the diner, he quickly scanned the small parking lot for Aidan – or anything out of the ordinary. Like other mercenaries, trying to catch the bounty on Aidan’s head. It wasn’t what Jonathan saw that drew his attention, it was what he heard. With the watch continuing to vibrate around his wrist, Legion heard the distinct sound of a muzzled weapon being discharged. A quick, short snap. Then another. Snap! Where the hell was Aidan?” * * * * *
