"DEATH AND LIFE" By [name withheld] Despite what some humanitarian souls in our alleged ‘civilised’ world might say, sometimes to fight is not a choice that we can make, sometimes it’s a necessity we cannot avoid. It’s human nature, pure and simple; a primal genome handed down by our ancestors throughout our millennia of evolution, blood, pain and death. To fight is to live; it’s what drives us, it’s what unites us and it’s certainly the key to survival; through the rough and the smooth, through desolation and desperation and through life and death. However, no matter how resilient we might be, there will always come a time when there’s simply no fight left and the only choices we have left are, to quit and die or to find strength from deep down and begin the battle anew. Though do not be mistaken by this simple truth, at any given time neither of these unavoidable choices are by any means mutually exclusive. “THE SAME OLD STORY” Venue: The Oasis Motel. || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago Jacob McKail woke up tired and carrying a hangover. It was only to be expected; he chose to live his life as ghost, teetering on the edge of society looking in at the rest of the world he chose to ostracise himself from. He chose a life -- if it could even be called -- of complete inebriation and misery, desperate to forget his past; or more accurately, desperate to forget the loss of his wife, Mary. True enough, it had been over two years since her passing, but he still hadn’t gotten over her death and a small part of him didn’t even [want to. If that was not bad enough, now his wrestling career was all but over too. His life was finished, now he just awaited death to come and take it‘s endless bounty. “Finally awake, huh?” The voice is female. Sweet, but a whisper and with undertones of intimacy all rolled into one. “It’s five after two; I thought you were going to sleep all day!” This can’t be good, he thought with a barely audible groan. His eyes flickered open and saw the stranger sitting next to him, legs crossed and studying him intently. Looked like she’d been doing it for hours, too. No, this definitely can’t be good. She was a pretty brunette with dimples in her cheeks and a smile that sent a tingle down his spine. Goddamn it! This ain’t gonna be easy… McKail grumbled a response and turned away, too ashamed of himself to even look the girl in the eye. He spun his legs over the side of the bed and reached down for his idly discarded jeans. Damned things stunk stale beer and cigarettes from the night before, not that it bothered him much. “So, what do you want to do today?” The girl asked, arching closer toward him. He could feel her sweet breath on the back of his neck and the tenderness of her lips as they gently caressed his neck. This is wrong, he told himself. The situation itself wasn‘t all that troubling, it was more to do with the small part of him that didn’t want her to stop. Jacob McKail, you’re a real bastard, you know that? He pulled the dirtied jeans over his feet and stood, hoisting them up to his waist in one fluid motion, wrenching himself away from the stranger’s delicate touch. “Huh?” He mumbled, reaching for his cigarettes delicately perched at the edge of the bedside table. “I said,” she replied, standing up now to continue massaging his neck with her lips, “what do you want to do today?” McKail lit his cigarette, took a drag and exhaled lengthily. Shaking off the haze of sleep. What do I want to do today? Is she serious? What the fuck is this? He turned to face her. “‘Do’ today?” “Yeah,” she replied, “last night you said we’d hang out.” McKail shook his head. “I remember nothing.” She rolled her eyes and giggled a little. “Well you were pretty drunk last night! Bet you don’t even remember my name…” The sentence hung in the air for the longest time to the point where it was just plain awkward. She was testing him, he realised a little later, and he’d failed miserably. Might as well just come out and admit it. “Actually, no. I don’t.” She visibly deflated before his eyes and although McKail knew he should feel at least a little guilty, he didn’t. He’d closed himself off from the world the day his wife died and he had no intention of opening up to anyone again anytime soon; especially to a girl he didn’t even know. “Well that’s okay,” she told him, shaking off her sorrow and disappointment and filling her face with a too cheery smile. “We can spend the whole day together and you can get to know me a little better. It‘ll been fun.” She extended her hand out for him to shake. “My name‘s Jade.” McKail ignored it. “Sorry darlin’. Can’t. Got stuff to do.” “Don’t lie to me, Jacob.” She folded her arms like a teenager in a bad mood - hell, as far as he could tell, she was a teenager in a bad mood. “You told me all don’t have any place to be. I’m a big fan of yours, Jacob. I know you got fired from FUSE a couple of months back for substance abuse and after last night, boy, now I can see why!” Fuck! He silently cursed. She‘s a stalker now too? Just great… “But I got independent bookings down in Kentucky. I need to--” “Don’t.” Her voice was laced with anger and disappointment. She turned away, McKail thought to weep, but it’d been a long time since he’d been a victim to the temperament of a woman, so it could’ve been anything. “You haven’t got to be anywhere! You aren’t even wrestling anymore! You’ve given up!” She turned back to face him. There was no anger now, only pity. “Now you’re just a glorified bum!” McKail opened his mouth about to argue with the woman, but no words would form. Instead, McKail took a lengthy drag of his cigarette and glared out of the window, into the eye of the spring sunshine that he just couldn‘t get excited about. “SEARCH AND DESTORY” Venue: Doyle’s Pub || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago Sam Thomas stormed into the familiar surroundings of Doyle’s Pub with all grace of a partially sighted bull in a china shop. His broad shoulders were poorly hidden under his deceptively baggy New York Giants shirt and he visibly heaved, perfectly in tune with his heavy breathing. His eyes darted around the bar, in search of someone. After a time and plainly unsuccessful, he gritted his teeth and approached the bartender warily. “Where is she?” Sam questioned, straight to the point and devoid of pleasantries. The bartender developed a confused expression and then spread it right across his face. “Where’s who?” “You know who!” Sam sneered. “The whore that was in here last night! Half expected her to still be here, propping up the bar or some shit!” The bartender’s confused expression intensified. “Whore?” Sam sighed. “Petite brunette, conventionally pretty, wearing a white dress?” A wave of realisation washed over the bartender, but guardedly, he immediately suppressed it. “Who wants to know?” A grin begrudgingly formulated upon Sam’s lips. “Her husband.” The bartender shakes his head in a very definite mixture of anger and disappointment. “Goddamn it, McKail! “McKail?” Sam asked. “As in Jacob McKail? The pro-wrestler?” “Yeah,” the bartender confirmed. “Comes in here now and again when he’s not on the road.” “She’s always had a goddamn thing for him,” he admitted. “Where is he?” “Not sure,” the bartender told him. “But he doesn’t have a regular place up here, so my guess is he’s staying in the cheapest motel within stumbling distance of here. But hey, take it easy on him, okay? He’s had a rough couple of years…” “Well,” Sam began, storming out of the bar, “things are about to get a damn sight worse!” “EXAMINATION DAY” Venue: The Oasis Motel. || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago “I’m sorry, Jacob,” Jade told him, “I didn’t mean--” McKail slumped down on the bed. “Don’t worry ‘bout it darlin‘. You ain’t gotta be sorry ‘bout the truth.” “But it isn’t true, is it?” Jade questioned. “Surely you’re only taking a few weeks off? You’ll be back on the road and wrestling again soon though, right?” He didn’t answer her, he couldn’t. Ever since his less than graceful departure from FUSE, or SCCW as they were now known, he hadn’t even considered stepping foot back in the ring. He’d given up and subjugated himself to weeks of alcohol abuse instead. “No, Jacob, you can’t give up,” she told him, “you mustn’t. You have to fulfil the promise you made to your late wife! You promised her you would continue your wrestling career and dominate the sport!” McKail’s eyes widened. He stood from the bed and warily backed away from her. How the hell could she know such a thing? He began to panic and question his own sanity at the same time. Is she even real? Is she a delusion? What the fuck is going on here? How much did I drink last night? “How--?” “How do I know about your wife?” She asked. “Wrestling forums. It’s common knowledge amongst certain circles on the Net; you can find out anything about everybody on there.” McKail and technology didn’t mix, so for all he knew she was telling the truth. He hadn’t gone out of his way to conceal his past at all and it was conceivable that somebody looking hard enough could find anything about him. Perhaps it wasn’t her actually knowing about Mary and the promise that put him on edge, maybe it was more reminding of it; he’d nothing to maintain the promise since leaving FUSE and the fact that he’d already given up. “I think it’s romantic,” Jade told him, thankfully shaking him free from such notions, “and you shouldn’t quit. What you should do, is let someone else into to your life who can help you, someone understands what you’re going through.” She lurched forward, placing her arms around his neck and her tender lips squarely upon his. For reasons beyond him, McKail didn’t try to stop her. “Someone like me.” After a time, too long for his liking, McKail wrenched himself away from her grasp and shook his head, disappointed with himself. “No. It’s over. My wrestling career is over. My life is over. Just let me drink whiskey and die in a ditch someplace.” “No Jacob!” She screamed, grabbing a hold of him roughly by the shoulders. Tears were streaming down her cheeks now and she was having a hard time keeping it together. “Don’t talk like that! Don’t ever talk like that!” “Why do you even care?” McKail question, angry now. “I‘m nothing, I‘m nobody and you don‘t even know me. I haven‘t even done anything great for you to--” “You haven’t done nothing great?” Jade shook her head, with a mixture of disbelief and disappointment. “Since pursuing your promise, you’ve come so close to greatness. You won the FUSE Universal and Livewire titles, you won the GCW TV title, you won the Infinite Gauntlet twice and you’re the current champion! All of this in just two years?” Her eyes widen, ready to fire across her final and defying point. “I don’t need to know you to realise all of that‘s a fantastic achievement, Jacob.” McKail allowed a smirk. “Just how in the hell do you know so much about me?” Jade blushed. “I’m a super fan. Not in a stalking you night and day kind of way though…” McKail stubbed his cigarette out on the motel room cabinet, without regard for damage. “Sounds like you have too much time on your hands.” “No,” she told him. “Just a god-awful relationship is all. I need to escape somewhere else sometimes.” “And the best you could come up with was me?” McKail shook his head, still mentally grappling with the subject. She chuckled and looked at her feet. “You underestimate yourself.” “Whatever,” McKail told her. “Let’s get breakfast.” “DISGUST” Venue: Outside of the Oasis Motel || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago Sam Thomas was just about ready to knock on every damn motel room door, when his slut of a wife emerged from a room at the far left of the motel compound. She wore the same damned clothes he last saw her in the night before and she smiling, clinging onto the arm of her wrestling idol and number one poster boy. “Fucking whore…” He muttered under his breath, reaching to the back seat of his car to retrieve his handgun. The time had long gone for reasoning with the woman, now he had to show her how serious he was, he had to illustrate to her the error of her ways and if it’s at the expense of Jacob-fucking-McKail, then so be it. Sam made sure his six-shooter was loaded and told himself that he was doing this for her own good; she had her head in the stars and she needed to brought back down to Earth. He was her husband and she needed to respect that. He exited his car and followed them, making sure to stick to the walls and not to get too close too soon. “DEATH” Venue: Outside of the Oasis Motel || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago. “Don’t fucking move!” The voice yelled. It held a think Bronx accent, with clear underlying tones of anger. What the fuck now? McKail silently questioned, stopping on the spot, but whirling around to face the source of the voice. The man had impressively wide shoulders and something of beer gut, all neatly hidden under a loose New York Giants jersey, but most predominantly the fucking lunatic had a gun and it was pointing directly him. Fuck! McKail silently screamed, fighting away the crippling fear. “Sam?” Jade questioned, covering the sun from her eyes to get a better picture of the assailant. “Is that you? What the fuck?” McKail sighed. I don’t like where this is going…“You know him?” “Yes,“ Jade replied, her voice soft and delicate. “He’s my husband, Jacob.” Oh FUCK! “Your husband?” McKail repeated. The man Jade identified to be Sam chuckled a little. “You didn’t tell him you were married, hun? Somehow, that doesn’t surprise me! You dirty fucking whore!” “What the hell are you doing, Sam?” She questioned, tears rolling down her cheeks. “Don’t you think you’re going a little too far here?” “You know, the thought did cross my mind,” he told her, “but then I just thought about all the times you’ve fucked around behind my back and I think ‘no’, this is a perfectly reasonable response!” Oh just fucking great, McKail thought, a lunatic with a gun who thinks I‘ve fucked his wife! That’s all I fucking need! “You’re paranoid, Sam,” Jade responded. “I haven’t slept with anyone behind your back! It’s all in your head!” “It’s all in my head?” He asked. “I just saw you leaving your beloved Jacob McKail’s motel room with him in tow!” “Okay,” she began, her voice smooth and calming, “it’s looks bad, I’ll admit, but nothing happened. I wanted it to happen, but nothing did happen.” Sam rolled his eyes and McKail couldn’t blame him. Hell, he didn’t think she was telling the truth. “You expect me to believe that garbage? What about all the others, Jade?” Jade developed a confused expression. “What others?” “Johnny from local store,” he told her. “Don’t think I don’t know the two of you got something goin’ on too!” “Johnny is fifteen,” Jade responded, getting angry herself now, “and he was just helping me with the grocery’s. See what I mean? Paranoid…” Sam drew closer and grabbed Jade roughly by her forearm. “I’m not paranoid though, am I? I just caught the two of you in the act, no matter how you try to sugar coat it with your endless lies!” “Let go of me!” Jade screamed. “You’re hurting my arm!” McKail sighed. I’m gonna regret this… “Take it easy. You‘re hurting the girl.” “Take it is easy did I just hear you say?” He released his grip on his wife and stalked forward, his handgun still pointed at McKail squarely. “I think you oughta be more concerned about your own safety don’t you reckon?” “Fuck you…” “What did you say to me?” Sam questioned, pressing the barrel of the gun up against the underside of McKail’s chin. With a sadistic grin, he pulled back the hammer. “Care to repeat that?” “Please, Sam!” Jade yelled. “Please what, Jade?” Sam asked. “Spread your boyfriend’s brains all over the parking lot?” Jade fell to her knees, tears streaming now and her voice merely a whimper. “Don’t do this…!” “Why shouldn’t I?” He questioned. “Why shouldn’t I teach you a lesson for being such a whore?” Jade could barely get her words out now. “I wasn’t--we didn’t--” “You should tech her,” McKail interrupted. Sam pressed the gun firmer against his jawbone. “Who the fuck asked you?” “Kinda think it’s my call,” he replied, glancing down at the cold metal pressed against his skin. “Don’t you?” Sam stared at him for a time. “So what are you saying? You want to die? You want me to kill you?” “Why not?” McKail told him, chuckling manically. “I have no life; I’m nothing and I have no one. All that’s left for me is death and I’d rather that be sooner rather than later. Besides, if you do kill me, it’ll be one less dick off the streets and that alone is worth it.” “You shouldn’t try to bluff me, man,” Sam told him. McKail saw the uncertainty in his eyes and chuckled some more. “I’ll do it! I’ll blow your fucking head off!” “Then do it already!” McKail told him, grabbing Sam’s hand and pressing the gun even harder against his jawbone. “Pull the damn trigger. See if I care.” “You’re fucking crazy, man!” Sam screamed at him, desperately trying to wrench his hands free. “Don’t back out of this now, Sam!” McKail told him. “Come on. Show me how much of a man you really are!” “Hey man, I was never gonna pull the trigger!” Sam yelled, fear gripping him entirely. “I just wanted you scare you a little, honest! Please! I just wanna get out of here!” McKail released his grip and Sam stumbled backward, dropping the gun on the floor and leaving it there, before taking off back towards his car frantically. “Pussy.” Jade picked herself up from the parking lot and brushed away the dirt and gravel from the knee’s of her jeans. She wiped the tears away from her eyes the best she could without ruining her very basic makeup. “You were serious,” Jade asked him, pointedly maintaining her distance, “weren’t you?” “About what?” He grunted in response. “Dying,” she told him. “You don’t care whether you live or die do you?” “No,” he lied. He headed off, toward the diner and Jade did not follow him. “LIFE” Venue: Happy’s Diner || Place: The Bronx, New York || When: Two Weeks Ago. McKail blew the excess heat from his cup of coffee and glanced out of the diner window, admiring the beautiful day that had shaped itself all around him. He pushed away the plate of demolished scrambled eggs and for the first time in an age, felt contented. It was a little harsh, lying to Jade the way he did, but McKail couldn’t help himself never mind help her overcome that nut job of a husband she’d married. At least without clinging onto the notion of actually being a part of McKail’s life she might actually be able to salvage something from her marriage and with lie he told her, he made the decision hers and thus his conscience was crystal clear. “How you doin’, darlin’?” It was the diner waitress. She chewed gum and shot him a coy smile, whilst scooping up his finished plate. “Good,” he told her. He finished his coffee. “Can I get a refill?” It was funny, if the same question had been posed an hour ago, he would have an entirely different answer for her. He would’ve told her to fuck off or go to hell. An hour ago, he didn’t care about life; his or anyone else’s. McKail attempted to light the cigarette, but his hands still shook too much for the task to be accomplished. The waitress noticed, but didn’t say a word. She simply poured him another cup of coffee and went about her day. McKail decided he didn’t really need a cigarette after all. But now things were different. Thing of it was, it wasn’t until that fucking idiot put a gun to his head that he realised it. McKail realised he wasn’t ready to die yet, he wasn’t ready to be reunited with his late wife and how could he be? He hadn’t fulfilled his promise yet. He extracted his mobile phone and dialled all too familiar number. It was to the Primetime Central headquarters and he was going to confirm that he aimed to defend the title that he’d rightfully captured all of those months ago. Whether they liked it or not. And that was only the beginning… End
