Paper and Ink

23 April 2007

March 22, 2007 2:30pm March had always been a particularly unkind month, and a cruel breeze sliced inland off the caps of a quarrelsome sea to prove the point. It was a different type of cold up here. Jason pulled his black leather jacket close while the mist thrown up from the ocean clung frozen to the stubble of a lazy beard. It had been a long time since he’d come home, and now that he was here, he thought perhaps it was a mistake. Too late now, he told himself, turning away from the water toward his rented car, parked on the side of a wharf built of wood rotted green-grey with age. It squeaked and groaned beneath his feet, just as it had a decade ago when last he’d braved it. Backwater was a place of grim weather and grim men; a place where a man’s words still meant something, and insults would often provoke violence. It was a village of barely five hundred, mostly fishermen and their families, with a reputation both old and deserved. The police were non-existent in the area, except when, on occasion, a patrol car might wander through every other month or so. As such, the locals were left to police themselves. They were honorable men - honest men. After all these years, Jason still considered himself one of them, having been born and raised here. And yet, it had been a long time since he’d truly been an honest man. In his life, he had loved four people - his wife, her two sisters, and his daughter - and yet there was a side of his life that he kept from each of them in turn. I’ve hurt people, his conscience told him, its familiar grasp taking him cold by the throat. I’ve killed people. And then he remembered them, those he’d killed, one by one. From the old wharf, Jason drove through the village, drawing curious eyes from the locals. A few he recognized, though he was sure they didn’t recognize him - not after all these years. Perhaps they followed him on television, he supposed, but celebrity was not overly valued in a place such as this. He waved to a few of them, and they returned the gesture in good-hearted nature. Within minutes he reached his destination, though it was not at all the way he remembered it. He pulled into the driveway of the small house where he’d grown up, from the time he was born until he was eighteen years old. That was when he left home to marry Raven and work at a cement plant in a nearby town. It paid the bills and paid Raven’s tuition until he stumbled into professional wrestling the next year. The house had gone to hell in the time since he’d last seen it, though in truth, it had never been much to look at. It seemed ready to collapse, with wood weathered raw and nearly naked of paint. He stepped out of the car to get a closer look, yet he found it only worse when he did. It was a wonder the old place was still standing. No one had even taken the time to board it up, and a few of the windows were cracked now. It had been almost ten years since he’d stepped foot in this house, and yet he found himself, now, crossing the threshold. October 21, 1996 11:45pm “Then get the fuck out!” Grace Snow let out her patented shriek, both gutteral and high in pitch - Jason could hear everything from his room, lying still as stone on his bed, staring like a corpse at the ceiling. Already, at seventeen, the alcohol had started, despite Raven’s pleading and threats. She warned once that she might leave him, should it continue. He knew she wouldn’t - couldn’t. He did his drinking separate from her, on nights that he couldn’t get to her, to drown moments like these from playing in his head over and over again like the oil-cracked screen of a run-down movie theater. There came the unmistakable crack of flesh on flesh, and Jason couldn’t be sure if his mother had just struck Jake or if Jake had struck his mother. Not that it mattered. It used to matter. But over time, Jason had come to learn that these things always ended the same way, and part of him believed it was exactly the way his mother wanted it. Right on cue, he heard her begin to scream hysterically, and moments later, a shattering of glass. It was probably a lamp. Jake always went for the lamps. Bobby had been prone to throwing coffee mugs. Chuck had just punched walls. None of it phased him anymore. At least not on the outside. He remained motionless. Wooden. Stone. Dead. Eyes glued to the ceiling. And yet his stomach boiled acid - boiled volcanic. His fists were clenched, though he did not know they were. It would be so easy, he thought, to slip out into the living room, take Jake by his weasel neck, and begin to hurt him - begin to humiliate him. At seventeen, Jason was already well-schooled in violent arts, and weighing nearly two hundred pounds, he knew he could handle Jake as easily as a kid in the parking lot of the high school. He could pull the abusive prick’s heart right out through his ribs and smear it all over his pointed face. He could suck his soul through a straw. He could. And he wanted to - but that wasn’t what his mother wanted. And in the end, it wouldn’t matter, because she would replace Jake with a Ron or a John or an Al, but in truth, he’d just be another Jake. Another Bobby. Another Chuck. “Slut,” Jake’s voice boomed from the living room, just as Bobby’s had not six months ago. The word came crashing through the paper thin walls of the house - walls that Jason’s father had built back in 1980, when Jason had been little more than a baby. And it had been his father, whom Jason had known only five short years, who had started the trend that had become such a routine in his life. The shouting. The cursing. The violence. “I’m calling the police!” His mother screamed. She made this threat all the time. She never called the police. “Fucking do it! Do it, bitch! Fucking -” Jake’s voice was interrupted by more breaking glass and then another flesh on flesh crack. It was all becoming too much, and finally Jason broke his stone casing, shooting upright in bed. But as much as he wanted to move toward his door - to solve all of this nonsense with two fists and angry explosions in his head - it was his window that he opened. And then he crawled outside, pulling his jacket out along the way, and braved the warning chill of late October nights - only a prelude to the months to come. The noise was muffled from the outside, and then bled to silence as he walked on. It was still as loud as ever in his head, but for that, it didn’t matter how far from the house he moved. His mother’s wailing was always there. Not even time could stifle her screams. It was midnight now and would be nearly two by the time he got to Raven’s house. And then he’d tap on her window until finally she got up to let him in, all the while being careful not to wake her little sisters in the next rooms. He closed his eyes and imagined himself nestled in the warmth of her arms, the fabric of her pajamas soft on the skin of his face. And then, he knew, even as he walked alone in the cold with the first sprinkles of a coming rain popping on the top of his head, that he would forget. If only for the night. February 26, 2007 4:50pm It was a little thing - only made of paper and ink. Nothing more, except maybe the glue on the envelope. But the envelope didn’t matter. It was just the container. The ship that delivered the cargo. The casket that delivered the corpse. Jason Snow’s name was scribbled on the front in penmanship so childlike that he at first thought perhaps a wrestling fan had found his home address. The painted likeness of a young Queen Elizabeth II smiled thin-lipped on the stamp, wearing a diamond tiara crown. Not the Statue of Liberty, nor a long-dead president. Not Elvis. The Queen of the Commonwealth. Paper and ink. The letter lay in the center of the kitchen table, abandoned in a way, while Jason sat back in his chair. He stared at it for a long time, his face expressionless - his body numb. He needed a drink. More than a drink. He needed to feel soft fuzz of blue pajamas on his cheek. I shouldn’t feel this way, he told himself. Before that, his mind had been as blank, as expressionless, as his body. “What’s up?” Emily’s voice startled him as she came bouncing in from the living room. His eyes flickered upward to meet hers and his mouth opened, but nothing came out. This isn’t a big deal, he told himself, and yet still, he struggled to find a response to such an innocent, routine question. Emily’s eyes narrowed on him when she saw his state, and immediately they turned down to the coffee mug on the table next to... next to... Paper and ink. “What’s going on?” this question was not routine. It was sharp and suspicious, both peculiar qualities in such a high pitched voice. She snatched the mug off the table and sniffed it, as often she did - checking for booze. But Jason did his drinking out of the house now, where the prying eyes of his fifteen year-old sister-in-law would never find him. “Jason?” She set the mug down and then her eyes turned to the piece of paper on the table, its folds turning it into a triangled structure on the table. Three points. Three sides. “Jason?” she said again, in a voice that worry had infiltrated. “I have to make a phone call,” he replied, and he found himself walking toward the telephone without realizing he’d even gotten up. And then he was picking up the receiver. Dialing. “Jason?” her voice came a third time. He barely heard it now. He lifted his hand to hold over his eyes and then drew it down the length of his face - there was a headache coming on. As if the aches already present in his body weren’t enough. Two rings. “Darren Stevens’ office,” came the voice on the other end of the phone. “How may I help you?” “I need you to put me through to Darren,” Jason said after clearing his throat. “Mr. Stevens is not currently taking calls,” the secretary said politely. She sounded pretty. Jason had no doubt that Darren was fucking her on the side. He was always fucking somebody on the side - but such was the life of a high ranking PRIME executive. “He’ll take mine,” Jason said bluntly. “Put me through.” “I’m sorry, but he left specific instructions for me to-” “This is Jason Snow,” he snapped as politely as one could snap. “I can’t exactly call Chet Worth for obvious reasons, so I need to speak with Darren. Put me through.” “Jason Snow?” “Match my phone number if you want,” he told her, surprisingly calm, yet there was a trembling in his voice. “I just need to talk to Darren. It won’t take long.” A moment of silence passed and then she said: “I’ll put you through right away, Mr. Snow.” Two more rings. Three rings. “Jason?” Darren’s voice came through, representing his gameshow host smile and car salesman manner from a thousand miles away. Jason could picture him swinging around in his chair, his feet on his desk without shoes, happily doing whatever the hell it was he did in his everyday routine. “I haven’t seen you in a while, buddy - little surprised that you’re calling me now. What’s up?” There was that question again. “I’m going to need some time off,” Jason had never been a man to engage in small talk or beat around the bush. There came a silence, save for Darren’s breathing. He wasn’t happy, Jason knew, but that didn’t matter. “Time off?” he finally replied. “I’ve got to be honest with you, Jason - your presence in PRIME hasn’t exactly been what we’d bargained for since you returned to the wrestling scene. I mean, we understood when you were deep in GTT6 that we might not see you as much, but now that that’s over...” his voice broke off, but only for a moment, “we were kind of expecting you to get back in the thick of things.” “I will,” Jason replied, “but not now. I really need... I’ve got to take time off.” “Well can I ask what for?” “It’s not something I want to talk about.” “Well Jesus Christ, Snow!” Darren snapped - a mistake on his part. “What the fuck am I supposed to tell Chet!? For all the money we fucking pay you, what the fuck have you done for us!? You were fucking MIA all through GTT6, which we understood - but now that you’re finally available to give a hundred percent to PRIME, you say you want to take time off!? Have you forgotten about the PTC Championship match coming up? You’re representing us in that, you know! Perhaps that hasn’t occurred to you.” “I’m not asking, Darren.” “What the hell am I supposed to say to -” “I’ll see you in a month or two,” Jason interupted, and that ended the conversation. He closed his eyes and his mind was drawn back to the table - back to the letter sitting on it. You have no reason to care, he told himself, and then he looked down at the kitchen counter to find his hand trembling ever so slightly. This doesn’t affect you! And yet somehow it did. When he turned around, his eyes crashed into Emily’s - glacier blue into teddy bear brown. Eyes that resembled her sister’s. He craved her sister more than he’d ever craved a drink right now. More than he’d ever craved anything. To crawl into her arms in the darkness and rest his face against the soft make of her pajamas. To feel her fingers in his hair while she whispered the softest of words into his ear. But Raven was dead now, as she’d been dead for more than three years. “Jason...” Emily’s voice was soft like her sister’s and her lip trembled to warn the coming tears. She was always crying - a true Manderly woman. She held the letter in front of her, absently - paper and ink between thumb and forefinger. She glanced down at it and then back up once more. She didn’t know what to say. There was nothing to say. March 23, 2007 11:30am Seventy-eight. Paper and ink. Scribbled casually on a card, small enough to fit into the inside pocket of Jason’s leather jacket, was a three line address. Yesterday, as he was coming out of the house he’d grown up in, he was met by a man at the front of the driveway. Frank Carle, as it happened - Jason remembered him well from his youth. He’d even worked more than a summer on his fishing boat. Frank had been curious about who was coming out of the old, broken-down house - nothing more. And when he saw Jason, his face had lit instantly with recognition. “Looking for my mother,” Jason had replied after Frank asked him, with a jolly grin, what brought him back to Canada. And then his face turned to confused, because its not every day that a man admits he doesn’t know his mother’s whereabouts. “She moved off, oh, three, maybe four years back. Last I heard she was living up north, just outside on Montaheye.” He thought hard for a moment, with one eye permanently cemented into a squint, as it had been a decade ago, when most of his hair had still had its color and he’d been a large, strong man. “You ever been up that way?” “Not since I was a kid,” Jason had replied. Frank took the time to draw up a map and write detailed instructions on how to get to the town. He even invited Jason to dinner, but Jason had to decline, citing his need to get to Montaheye. He arrived late last night and found a small hotel not long after. It was near midnight by then, so he supposed it would be best to make his visit in the morning. He spent the night drinking rum and watching late night talk shows. He came across a recent episode of ReVolution, and then pulled out a copy of a recent PRO WRESTLING ILLUSTRATED that he’d been keeping in his jacket. There was a picture of him on the cover, and inside, a long-winded article about how GTT6 had cemented his place in wrestling history. The author was generous with his compliments, and a few passages were underlined: “Not yet twenty-eight, and despite a three year lay off for what he calls ‘personal reasons,’ Jason Snow’s list of accomplishments already dwarfs those of most legends.” “...perhaps the most talented man in the history of the industry...” And near the bottom: “Next up for the GTT6 Champion? An well-deserved shot at the PTC title. And if his opponents have learned anything from the recent history of their sport, they’re shaking in their wrestling boots.” He rolled onto his back and thought about the article for a long time - not so much about the article itself, but about the underlined passages. The word “PTC title” had been circled in blue ink. He thought maybe, if he held his thumb to the page long enough, it might leave a blue stain on his skin... maybe... but those were tired thoughts, and it wasn’t long before he fell asleep. There would still be the business of actually finding his mother. But Montaheye wasn’t a large town, and the next day, he wasn’t long finding a woman who knew Grace Snow, though she gave him an odd look when he said the name. She pulled out a pen and scribbled the address down on a small, square card. “So you’re a friend of Grace’s?” the woman asked. She was in her middle forties - fifty, perhaps - and Jason assumed by the leathery texture of her skin that she had spent a fair amount of time drinking. Probably with his mother. “Yeah,” he lied, staring down at the card. Paper and ink, just like the letter in his car. Just like the blue underlines in the wrestling magazine. If I hold my thumb down long enough... “From a long time ago.” And now he sat silent in the driver’s seat of his rent car, drinking from a flask and staring down the street at the number “78,” in fake gold plates, screwed to the wooden siding of a house. Black shingles and blue shutters. The first scribbles on the card were “78.” The house was nothing like the one he’d grown up in. It was about the same size as his own - the one he lived in now. Raven had never wanted a large house, despite what they could afford. She can’t hurt you, he told himself, and yet somehow he was paralyzed. Just get up. She’s your mother - she’ll be happy to see you. And if she wasn’t? What did it matter? He was Jason Snow. Jason Snow!! He had millions of fans. He had millions of dollars. It was silly to feel this way. It was pathetic. And yet, still he remained frozen. Wooden. Stone. Dead. You didn’t come all this way for nothing, you pathetic fuck! His mind screamed, and finally, he found himself reaching for the door handle - found himself stepping out of the car and onto the sidewalk. The house was up the street a way, and as slow as he walked, he got there quicker than he would have liked. From there it was up the driveway and then the walkway until he was standing in front of the door, staring at the grain of its make. He rapped it three times. He thought of the letter. Two folds. Three sides. Three points. Triangle. And then he waited.
