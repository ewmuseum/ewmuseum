Wilt Thou?: Part Two

19 December 2005

Date: 2005-12-19 Without too much effort, Seymour Almasy could think of ninety-four different places where he would rather be than where he was at this particular moment. Sitting on a curb in some godforsaken part of the Bronx, he couldn’t help but feel a little bit silly. He’d gotten knocked out by some bitter old man with an axe to grind, and then dragged out into a back alley. But none of that compared to what he considered the ultimate indignity. The desire to get out of this place had made him call his ex-wife, the one person in this world that he couldn’t let see him like this. He knew exactly what would happen when she arrived. "I already told her what happened," he said, resigning himself to his fate, "so she’s likely already in usual Aerith comfort mode. By the time she gets here, she’ll have picked up the comfort milkshakes, and I’ll be trapped in the car for over an hour and forced to tell her every last detail." Part of him appreciated that side of her, and for more than just a nice frothy milkshake. It was nice to have someone his own age who actually cared about him. He had Grandpa Cid, sure, but he was a father figure more than anything. And there were some things you simply didn’t tell a father figure. With Aerith, though, no subject was taboo. She knew him nearly as well as she knew herself, which was both maddening and intoxicating. Somehow, it was simultaneously the reason their marriage had blossomed and failed. He couldn’t keep secrets from her. About anything. Somehow, she knew what he would get her for the holidays, every year. She knew what he would say before he said it. But, all things considered, and considering the jumbled mess of emotions he had become lately, he NEEDED someone like that to help straighten him out. And thus, he sat on the curb, digging his heels into the asphalt, waiting for her to arrive. It had been a few months since he’d seen her, not since their last partnership… "Yevon," he whispered, "if she’s dressed like that again she may very well kill me." Seymour remembered the outfit fondly. A red corset with black stockings and a skirt that barely existed wasn’t difficult to forget. And inevitably, it had ended up on the floor of various hotels during their final tenure as manager and competitor in the UWF, and then as tag team partners in PTC. Sure, they were divorced. But they had needs, like anyone else. The team wasn’t called Unholy Matrimony just because the name was cool. Simply put, Aerith was more than a match for him, in every way. She was more intelligent than he was, he knew. She also possessed a heck of a lot more common sense than he ever would. Given enough time and training, she could become even better than Seymour in the ring, and he had the bruises to prove it. Oh, and she was seemingly insatiable. Seymour rather liked that part. All in all, Aerith, or Laura Winters, to call her by her proper name, was the one person on Earth who Seymour could truly confide in about everything. And as he saw the tell-tale black Accord slowly moving towards him, he knew that he was likely going to have lots of explaining to do. He took a deep breath, remaining seated as the driver’s side door opened and the click-clack of high heels on pavement reached his ears. It was all bloody fucking ridiculous. Seymour had, for thirty seconds, been trapped in Angelo Deville’s Soprano. He had been drilled in the heart with Rich Rollins’ Anarchist Elbow. And yet, at this moment, he would rather have endured the combined pain of both moves than face the woman to whom he practically owed his existence. Click-clack. Click-clack. Click-clack. And then, there she was, standing before him, every bit as beautiful as ever. Her hair was tousled, her outfit seemed to be a cobbled together combination of pajama pants and an old t-shirt, but it didn’t matter one jot. Not when she dashed over in her mismatched outfit and flung her arms about him, clinging to him tightly, lovingly. For the first time in a long time, Seymour Almasy felt content. Until, that is, she pulled back and slapped him across the face. Hard. Seymour cringed, head snapping down and to the side. Before he could wonder what his offense was, though, her arms were back around him, and she was speaking. "Don’t you ever scare me like this again!" she exclaimed, looking into his eyes as if trying to peer into his soul. "Do you have any idea how worried I’ve been about you? All the way here I thought about you out here, all alone, in the cold…" Oddly, he hadn’t even realized how cold it had been, somehow. At least, not until her body pressed tightly against his made him aware of the contrast between the sub-freezing temperatures of the New York day and the familiar warmth of someone who cared. "I’m sorry, Aerith," he replied, looking something like a chastised puppy. "I didn’t mean to worry you. I didn’t even know if you’d be able to come get me." She looked at him as if he had suddenly sprouted a second head. "Seymour, I would do anything for you. You know that, right?" The word reverberated through his mind, taunting and teasing him with its potential. ‘Anything’ was such a very open-ended word, but her tone of voice told him she meant what she said. "Yes, Aerith. And I would do anything for you." She smiled at that, knowingly. "Then, come get in the car with me. It’s freezing out here. I stopped on the way and got us milkshakes. Do you still like chocolate?" Almasy nodded, faintly pleased that, for once, he’d predicted her as well as she was able to predict him. "I do, yes. Do you still like strawberry?" "Always." She playfully smacked him in the chest, leaning close to ask her ex-husband a question. "Soooo, what’s Tessa Windsor like in bed?" This, Seymour decided, was going to be a very long car ride. --------------------------------------- "So, let me get this straight. You’ve gone HOW long without sex?" Seymour cringed around the straw of his milkshake, managing to take another sip. He supposed that there was a whole separate circle of Hell reserved for discussing current relationships with past girlfriends. The car continued its slow journey over the bridges and streets of New York City. Traffic meant that he’d already managed to spill out most of the story of his relationship with Tessa Windsor, how they’d met, how they’d conspired to ruin Chris Storm’s life, and what few juicy details there were to tell. "Months," Seymour said, trying not to sound too typically male. He thought he’d said it with a level enough tone to fool her, but as usual, Aerith knew him far, far too well. "Poor thing," she practically cooed, bringing the car to a stop at a traffic light. She looked at him then, and giggled. "Pity that Tessa doesn’t know what she’s missing out on…" Ah, yes, that was the other thing that made things difficult for Seymour when dealing with Aerith. She brought up their past. A lot, and rather enticingly at that. She was beautiful without trying, enticing without attempting to be. Which, of course, made her nearly irresistible when she wanted to be. He shivered involuntarily in a way that had nothing to do with cold as the car continued on its way. "She’s not ENTIRELY innocent," Seymour responded, feeling the need to defend the woman who was, you know, his GIRLFRIEND. "Of course she isn’t," was Aerith’s good-natured reply. "It’s not possible to be as innocent as what she portrayed herself as for so long. I think that kiss she gave you proved that quite clearly." "Oh God, you saw that?" He flushed red with embarrassment. Obviously, Seymour hadn’t expected her to see that. The Final Fantasy, conqueror of more opponents than he could remember, shifted uncomfortably in his seat. Out of the corner of her eye, she could see this, and grinned in response. "Why yes. I still watch the UWF, you know. Among… other promotions that you’ve happened to grace over the past year." Oh, fuck. Other promotions likely meant Global Wrestling. And that meant she’d heard that he was the masked chivalric known as Emoticon. How the Hell was he supposed to explain why he’d done that? What rationale could possibly explain deciding to wear black latex, white face-paint, and a ninja mask? "I do wish you would have told me you had a latex fetish earlier. We could have used that…" Seymour cringed. Why did she have to do this to him? It was clear that she was teasing him, but why? He’d already told her that he was taken at the moment. Was that part of it? Was she testing him? ‘Or,’ he told himself, a little wildly, ‘she wants you. In which case, you are NOT going to be a stubborn idiot. You are going to call Tessa up and break off that relationship as quickly and gently as you can, because Aerith wants you back. You’ve let her slip through your hands twice, you idiot. Do it a third time and you won’t have another chance.’ The thought of being with his Aerith again was exhilarating. Seymour was shocked that he still thought of her as ‘his’, but then again, Aerith seemed to have a similar sense of him. He was HER Seymour. She’d said so herself when she picked him up from the middle of the ‘hood. But then there was Tessa. In spite of his worries that she was using him, in spite of the fact that thus far, they hadn’t done more than kiss and cuddle, she was still a wonderful person. To the best of his knowledge, she cared for him. He knew that he cared for her. Why did everything have to be so complicated? "So," she said, easily switching subjects to something Seymour would likely be more comfortable with, "you ready for the Brawler?" Ah, yes, the mention of the Back Alley Brawler jolted Seymour from his conflicted emotions. Being on the man’s home turf had given Seymour a look into who Joe Balboa truly was. He wasn’t just an angry, embittered forty year old with a damn good right hand. He was an angry, embittered forty year old with a damn good right hand with a hell of a lot of support. Seymour had gone up against many underdogs in his tournament career, all of whom enjoyed a degree of support, but the Brawler was different. "I am," he began, "but I’d be lying if I told you I wasn’t nervous. Guy that size…and known for a right hand, of all things. I can squirm my way out of powerbombs and suplexes. There’s not a whole heck of a lot you can do to get out of the way of a big right to the face." "No, there’s not," Aerith agreed. "Just try to hit and move, hit and move." "That’s the plan, but he’s got more than brute force and power." After going to the gym, and listening to the impassioned, if delusional trainer, the Back Alley Brawler was more than just another opponent identifiable by a name, face, and finisher. He was a man with hopes. Dreams. Ambitions. Desires. The chance at the glory that had eluded him for most of his journeyman career. "I almost feel bad stepping into the ring with him, Aerith. He’s almost twenty years older than I am. His knee’s fucked six ways to Sunday, and this is probably his last shot at the big time. But…" The "but" was key. Seymour had a lot to prove as well. His GTT4 performance had been solid, but unspectacular, and his CW2 performance was, to him, abysmal. Losing in the second round would barely be an improvement over CW2. Wrestling, Seymour knew, was full of cases like this. "You know a guy that proud would be insulted if you gave anything less than your best, right?" Once again, Aerith was right. The Brawler wanted a win, but he wanted to earn it. Sure, his definition of "earning" a win might involve an ether soaked rag, but he wanted to step into that ring with another competitor and reign victorious, using all of his unique skills. "I know, Aerith. I’m not throwing this match. I wouldn’t even think it. I need it too much." It was classic, really. Two competitors. One spot in the third round of GTT5. A match that neither man could afford to lose. On that note, Seymour nodded off, leaving behind the annoying sounds of New York City traffic for the much more soothing bliss of unconsciousness. ------------------------------------------------- Months of sleeping alone had taken their toll on Seymour. Thus, it was to his utter astonishment that he woke to the sensation of a hand running softly through his hair, massaging his scalp gently, lovingly. "Wake up, Seymour. We’re here." Slowly, his eyes came into focus, and he remembered that he was in a car. Aerith’s car, to be precise. Once he realized that, the identity of the building in front of him became more obvious. It was their house. Well, her house now, technically. Seymour had given it to her in the divorce proceedings. "Mmm," Seymour managed, still trying to shake off residual drowziness, "it’s nice to see this place again." Reaching for the door handle, he pushed the car door open and stepped out onto the sandy beach of Long Island Sound. Even with the splendid beach and mighty ocean nearby, the one thing he could look at was the house. It was a simple structure, really, two stories with white siding, but it had been the first place he and Aerith had ever lived. "I’m sorry I haven’t invited you over," she explained, exiting the car herself and standing alongside her ex-husband. "But things have been hectic lately. For both of us." Now there was something that could take his attention away from the building. He couldn’t help but chuckle again at her attire. A t-shirt, pajama bottoms, and a pair of high heels could not be said to match in any way, shape, or form, but he loved the outfit nonetheless. "I’m sorry about the outfit," Aerith said, Seymour’s gaze raking over her making her suddenly shy. "I was so worried about you, I just threw on whatever I could find…" Oh, this was dangerous, and getting more so by the minute. Seymour knew that he had a girlfriend. A rather lovely one, at that, named Tessa Windsor. Equally importantly, she’d been fucked around with by Chris Storm for so long that she’d never truly had a good boyfriend, someone who really cared about her. In spite of the issues and difficulties that they were having, though, Seymour did. But goddamnit, he was only human. "Let’s go inside," she suggested, snapping Seymour’s tormented mind back to the present. "I can make us some coffee, and we can chat." That seemed like as good an idea as any. Chatting meant distance between them, more than there was now with her accidentally brushing up against him every few steps as they walked to the side door. Everything seemed fine, but Seymour glanced back over his shoulder, seeing something that he considered quite odd. There was another car in the driveway. He quirked his eyebrow for a moment, before coming to the conclusion that it was likely Aerith’s. "Yoohoo, Seymour! Door’s open!" He shook his head. Of course it was Aerith’s. Who else’s would it be? Seymour shrugged, stepping through the door into the familiar living room. "It’s just like I remembered it…how I’ve missed this place." Truthfully, he was surprised he missed it so much. The house was filled with all of the things his apartment never was. Love. Warmth. Happiness. The presence of a person he truly cared for, truly loved. That, of course, was the kicker. He loved Aerith. He would never stop loving Aerith, whether they were together or not. A portion of his heart would be for her, and only for her, no matter how many others there were. Then why did something feel so very wrong right now? Everything seemed normal. Aerith was in the kitchen a few feet away, making coffee, and--there it was. A noise in the house. One that didn’t come from Aerith. One that didn’t come from Seymour. It sounded suspiciously like a yawn. NOW the other car made sense. Someone else was in the house. Seymour’s senses snapped to awareness, listening in the silence to ascertain the direction of the sound. Joints bent and muscles tensed in preparation for action, as the Final Fantasy narrowed his eyes, focusing all he could on the tiny rustling sounds that followed the yawn. Where the Hell was this intruder? Suddenly, it hit him. Master bedroom. "Seymour, what’s gotten into you," Aerith asked, alarmed at his sudden change in posture. He didn’t answer at first, instead picking up a heavy potted plant to use as a weapon. When she shot him another, questioning look, he turned to her solemnly. "There’s someone else in this house, Aer." With that, Seymour headed up the stairs to the second floor. The master bedroom was only a few steps off to the right once he reached the top, and sure enough, the rustling noises increased in volume. Tightening his grip on his makeshift weapon, Seymour crept forward towards the door like a master hunter closing in on his prey. He was so focused that he didn’t hear Aerith walking up the steps behind him. Rarely, if ever, had she been so confused before. In the space of a few seconds, though, that changed. Seconds were all it took to realize WHY there was someone else in the house. And seconds were all it took for her to realize that Seymour could not enter that room. "Oh God…no…please, this can’t be happening. Please…" Her eyes glanced upwards to the heavens for guidance, for forgiveness, but not even God Almighty could stop Seymour from opening that bedroom door. Once he saw what was inside, he dropped the potted plant on the ground, sending soil spraying all over the white carpet. Unfortunately for Seymour, that was the least of the mess in this particular bedroom. Eyes widened pitifully as he saw pieces of lingerie strewn all over the floor. He recognized most of them immediately, a pink thong that was one of Aerith’s favorites here, a black lace teddy there. Most damning of all, however, was the red corset that lay in the king-size bed. Next to someone, who somehow was snoring quite peacefully even after the dropping of the plant. There was someone in Aerith’s bed. No…someone in the bed that he and Aerith had shared their entire time in this house. There had to be an explanation for this, he thought, trying to understand, trying to comprehend exactly what it meant if there wasn’t one. Any hopes he could have had, however, were dashed by the nightstand. Aerith kept pictures there. One of them was a candid shot of herself and Seymour, holding hands outside this very house. Strewn haphazardly over that picture of marital bliss was a condom. A used condom, to be precise, with spent fluids squelching out of the latex, obscuring Seymour’s image in milky-whiteness. Seymour’s mouth opened and closed, but no sounds came out. This was perverse. Beyond perverse. This was the bed that he had shared with Aerith. A bed that they had exchanged countless tender moments in. A bed that was now covered in spooge stains, in a room where the fragrance of sex hung heavy in the air. He wanted to rush forward and strangle whoever the Hell it was lying in his fucking bed, but at that moment, he came to a horrible realization. It took two to tango, as they say. The man lying in bed was a sorry son of a bitch, but he hadn’t been alone in this desecration. The other party was the…he couldn’t say it. Couldn’t think it. In disgust, he spun on his heel to leave the room, only to find Aerith, on her knees, sobbing in the middle of the hallway. She looked up at him then, saw the utterly hurt, utterly destroyed expression on his face, and said the only thing she could think of at that moment. "I’m…sorry, Seymour." Sorry? She was sorry? He couldn’t expect her never to fall in love again, or never to take a boyfriend. Such would be ridiculous, after all, he himself had moved on from her, if only in fact. But she had taken him back to her--THEIR house after a night of… "Why? WHY DID YOU TAKE ME BACK HERE IF YOU’D JUST DONE THIS?!" He was screaming now, but he couldn’t help it. The first tear fell from his left eye, rolling slowly down his cheek as he looked at his former wife with equal parts disgust and contempt. "I forgot! You called, and I heard the phone and I rolled out of bed and you were on the other line and I got so worried about you and--" "HOW DO YOU FORGET FUCKING SOMEONE, AERITH!" "I was at a club last night! And I met this guy, and--" Seymour was tired of excuses. Tired of being in this house. And last, but certainly not least, tired of Aerith. He couldn’t take anymore. Life had dealt him enough bullshit for one calendar year. He headed for the stairs, brushing by his ex-wife as he descended. Before he marched out the front door, he had one final barb to toss at the woman he no longer could love, in the form of the name that he never called her by. "I do hope he was a good fuck, Laura." The door opened once, and closed once, leaving in the house one blissfully unaware man, and one sobbing, hysterical woman who had picked the wrong night to go out clubbing. Finally, it seemed that Seymour and Aerith had run out of happy endings. --------------------------------------------------------- In truth, he didn’t know how far he’d walked. At least three miles. Maybe four. Whatever it was, it still wasn’t far enough away from the drunken whore that he had once called his wife. "How could I have even CONSIDERED risking what I have with Tessa for that…that tramp?" he asked himself, clearly still shaken by what he had seen. Even at its most wild, his marriage to Aerith had never produced such a debase scene, such a cornucopia of debauchery. Even as he stalked farther away from the house on Long Island Sound, though, he had to admit that on a small, superficial level, he was rather jealous of the man whose name he didn’t even know. Ah well. That would go away with time. After this round of the tournament, he would find Tessa and cuddle with her until the pain of this day was a distant memory. His feelings for Aerith were lust, pure and simple, and it had taken this long for him to realize it. His burgeoning relaitonship with Tessa was pure. Wholesome. Love. Well, except for it coming as a result of a mutual desire to destroy Chris Storm. He sighed, continuing his march. There was time to sort out exactly what Tessa and he were later. For now, he simply needed to walk, needed to heal. Out of the corner of his eye, though, something appeared that made him stop. An electronics store had television sets set up facing the street, and on one of the images was a news report. The camera was fixed on the top of a set of steps belonging to a seemingly important building. A good-sized crowd had gathered atop the stairs, and they all seemed to be surrounding a statue and a broad barbarian of a man. "What in the heck?" Slowly but surely, the camera began to cut in, closer and closer, until the image of the man’s face was clear. It was a proud face. A beaten face. A strong face. It was the face of the Back Alley Brawler. Surrounded by his people, by the Bronx, he looked unbeatable, ten years younger than he was. The Brawler appeared to be the conquering hero, the fan favorite. And, standing on an anonymous street somewhere on Long Island, a broken Seymour Almasy looked at him with envy. How had this happened? How could fortunes reverse so quickly and so utterly? There was, of course, one remedy to this. GTT5. Round Two. Looking at the erstwhile underdog, the Rocky to his Apollo Creed, his Clubber Lang, his Ivan Drago, Seymour knew that there was only one way to improve his standing. Only one way to achieve his dream, and that was to utterly crush Brawler’s. "I must break you," Almasy said, knowing it to be the horrible, dank truth. Seymour Almasy was not a gigantic, genetically-enhanced Russian, but the point stood all the same.
