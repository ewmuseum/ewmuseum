Aspiring for Approval I

26 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-26 Has there ever been anything in your life that you’ve wanted it so badly…that you honestly think that you would die for it? Did the thought cross your mind even for the smallest of moments, thinking that if the proceeding accomplishment were to be achieved, the price you were willing to pay for it was unequalled? Many men sure have felt this necessary in their lifetimes and the history of the world is littered with them. It isn’t needed to display these men for their actions have spoken louder than words. It is that final step, that final push into the endgame which will seal their achievements for the rest in time - just as their bodies will tumble into the darkness and signalling the result of their endeavours. It is with this appraisal of how some men can be driven and pushed to the limits of both their physical and mental well being that we come to focus on another passage on the road to oblivion for men like this. For all the desires and ambitions are great when they reach this pinnacle of effort, as they strive to touch and feel the realisations of their goal but for many observers in this spectacle, the end product may not seem so important. This is one of those times. A man toils the earth with his bare hands to reap what he has sown - but to fully enjoy his works he may have to pay the ultimate sacrifice. This is one of those times. *** Striving. Surging and hoping for the ultimate high, the pinnacle of this moment here at the darkest of rooms. Silently we danced in the moonlight, placing shadows of trickery and tomfoolery to hold the emptiness at bay. Our bodies entwined and entangled one another like the very land was coming to take us back to the beginnings and show us the nectar from which hope springs eternal. Reaching…searching…struggling and faltering until… ‘This isn’t right.’ It is merely a whisper. Watch as the illusion shatters into pieces. Return to the banality of Kingston in the state of New York, a quiet little town which isn’t known for much in the grand scheme of things. Its designs and lasting impressions on the world aren’t evident insofar as those observing with any passing glance so it stays forever in a lax state of slumber. We churn through the monotony of the dusty old town eventually focusing on a quaint but plain detached house in a nice suburb. No excitement, no hint at the possibility of some gorgeous event which will pull this town out of the sleep that is has eternally been. No, the attempt is lost in this quaint house, the attempt for a state of transcendence. ‘This isn’t right.’ A slump to the left side of the worn down double bed. There are no trumpets or heralds here. Just the sighs and heaves of a fallen giant. He breathes heavily, lamenting the disappointment. Beside him, the muse lays despondent. ‘What’s wrong?’ He speaks, hoping beyond hope that maybe something had happened outside of either of their control, restoring the vigour and vitality required for such an event to be tried again. In essence though there was no such comfort, the cold reply leaving nothing but a shattering of hope. ‘I don’t know…but it’s something to do with you.’ ‘Me?’ ‘Yes you Jack. You haven’t been the same lately. I just don’t…I don’t feel like you’re interested in me.’ Something that a man never wants to hear. Something that never sounds right no matter what way you look at it. It is only now that I, the Jack being referred to, regain some semblance of control in the proceedings. Up until now my mind had been scattered to the winds, left considering so many other things separate from the sex that had now been left damp and feeble. Lately my thoughts had been set up on a higher echelon than ever before, concerning the ideas that my time as a wrestler were creeping ever closer to an end. Focusing more and more on the chance that I could not go anymore was a very real horizon on the cycle of life that I was facing. It was something that I didn’t want to contemplate, but I was being forced to. My body wasn’t able to take the punishment the same way it used to even a year ago. Matches which I would have powered through in no time had now become a slog, a struggle to pull through which lately wasn’t even working either. Despondency had turned into depression and nothing was pulling me out of this slump. Nothing had had any effect on me. Until the announcement of the GTT again. Inexperience on the grand stage last year had led to a slip up but had gotten me hungry for success, driving me to greater heights in the federation that I was still competing in. Looking at it from hindsight…I saw the GTT as the opportunity, the hope, the chance to become great again. The chance to right all the wrongs in my wrestling career and forget the shame that I have placed upon myself recently. Wrestling is my life, there is no alternative or replacement for such a thrill, such a rush being able to perform and display something that I can do better than anything else in life. All athletes see this I feel and it is something that is so hard to take away from them. They don’t want it to end. They don’t want it to be gone from their lives, because for the better part of their early life…it IS their life. So how does somebody answer a lover, a friend, a confidant who has stuck through you in thick and thin, someone able to understand your motivations? ‘I…I know.’ It certainly isn’t that way. The look of…disappointment and regret peeled the layers of hope away from her face, tearing her down one vowel at a time. She covered her mouth only slightly by her porcelain hand, trying to hold in the words which may cut or bite. I don’t know why because I had already dealt the opening salvo. I needed to make reparations. ‘I don’t mean to sound so…callous in my meaning Jess. It’s just…the tournament.’ A sigh emanated from her lips, slow and exhausted as the strength to argue left her. She laid back on the bed and curled over. Her breathing was ragged, raspy and low. My first instinct was to lay my hand on her shoulder and try to comfort her, plead my case and toss out any number of useless excuses for why I had said this and why it was so in my mind right now. I didn’t though, I didn’t have the heart to be so cruel. In truth, all I could think of was wrestling again. Instead I kissed the back of her head and pushed myself off of the bed. I pulled on my trousers, a sweatshirt and left the bedroom, a fallen conqueror searching for his fire. Social problems were never my first port of call on the list of things I could do well. Either they sorted themselves out and everything returned to normal or I simply let it change whatever would be happening. This was one such occasion. I laced my wrestling boots and padded out of the house, exiting the merged dreamlike state and back to things more important and pressing. *** The chill of the evening bit deep as I pounded the pavement down the street to the nearby park which was my only form of release. It was a small, secluded and serene place home to nothing but nature and the sanctity of simpler times. In many ways it reminded me of my youth back in Ireland, the breeze hitting my face as I raced the spirits and the lifeblood of the earth to best myself. Nothing ever got in my way of improving and striving. Before wrestling there was hurling, an immense game of skill and technique with the speed of lightning all packed in the punch of a hard sliotar. At the time of my youth, nothing compared to the grace of a hurler surging into the air and catching that ball in the palm of his hand, dwarfing his opposition and commanding from the sky. The very air was his fighting ground and he used it to impose his will, thundering the sliotar without mercy for the thrill of the hunt. The game took me with the passion that I now thirst with for wrestling. The speed, stealth and brutality of the game lured me in with its core values of the foundations of man: strength, toughness and agility. It was a man’s game, and only men walked away from it, none of whom would be unscathed when all was said and done. I had aspirations to dominate this game, to impose my own will but I never quite got there. I never quite reached the pinnacle of my athleticism, and irrespective of how much I raced the spirits they would never let me best myself. It seems looking back on it now they were saving me for my true calling. The fire burns strongly in my veins for wrestling; a sport so great that the gods themselves of Olympus were said to have engaged in such a practice. Titans alike grappling to test their skill and power, their knack and vitality merely for the sake of it. There didn’t have to be a reason for it, it just occurred. So too did I catch on to the ferocity of it all. I relished the opportunity to get in and square up with whomever would choose to face me. That is the truth behind my nickname of bull, not because of my strength or toughness, but rather the impetuousness, the tenacity and relentlessness which backed my every assault towards those who would defy me. Even then, I knew that I would never walk away from this sport a happy man until I fulfilled all that was within my destiny. And so I still walk the path of this journey even now if it may seem that I am satisfied. I am not. I will never be sated by those whom I face. I will never be able to rest and retire simply because I cannot. It is not within my power to be finished with this sport of men and gods alike. I will not rest until I am placed among the pantheon, no matter the cost. All this from a park, all this sentiment from the lush grass that now surrounded me in this small idyll. With little in the way of light, this park was only illuminated by the pale moon that bore down from the heavens. It was having trouble doing this however though as a sweeping mist had descended in the park. It clouded the vision but I knew the layout like the palm of my hand, crossing every line like it was my very vein. I fed off the ground as it collided solidly with my booted feet. The midnight air agreed with my general perception. With no one around I was able to have my thoughts all in order. I could let them run free like the spirits that I raced and they would cross my path every now and then, absorbing responses and questions and then rushing off again to search for the answers I so desired. With the current mist about me, this all took on a deeper, druidic significance like the land of Ireland and enchanted the entire evening. It was at this point that I began to hear the pounding of footsteps coming from another direction to mine. It was directly opposing to me and it galloped closer and closer as I raced towards it. Within a moment something flowed past me, bursting through the mist and revealing itself only for a moment. I surged to try and see but it had escaped me. Unconsciously my feet twisted and in no time I was following what it was. My legs pumped harder as I tried to speed up with all my might, to try and catch up to this apparition and find out exactly why I wasn’t alone on this night. I could hear the footsteps but with each step of my own it seemed like I was losing footing as the form sped ahead of me. Then without warning the form completely stopped, bringing me to an abrupt halt, just missing him by a hair as I skidded on my heels. What stood directly beside me was a man of an average height, arms placed on hips and breathing the night air like nectar. He cocked his head and smirked slightly, taking a look my sweat ridden body. I returned his gaze, eyeing him up and down. His body was lean and explosive by the appearance and action he had just displayed. It was clear that he was in the top shape of his life but he wasn’t satisfied with it either. Sensing a similar nature the man looked ahead of us and began to walk. I followed with the same cadence, matching his feet one at a time. We both moved as one, mimicking the other, keeping on our toes for either of us to bolt. We moved up the pace to a jog and began to play head games with one another, the man swerving on occasion to put me off. There would be no response from me other than to lift the pace, forcing the young stud to take me on at the terms I saw fit to battle with him. Jogging became running and running became sprinting as we each strove down the winding paths of the park, doing several laps as we gathered speed on the way. There was no inch or quarter given by either of us, ready to give it our all, to push through the barriers of pain and exhaustion until one of us quit. As this game continued, my appreciation of the man continued to grow. Here was a man that was like me, obsessive in a goal to the point of no return. Here was a man that was doing whatever it took to achieve his goal, no matter the cost, just like me. Here was a man that was pushing himself to the limit, unsure of how much he could do it without breaking himself but doing it nonetheless. He was proud and strong, cocky and brash all at once. I could tell that the man was a good decade younger than myself and therefore had a bit more arrogance than myself. But not much. As we passed as tree for the fourth time in quick succession it became apparent that this would go on all night unless something was done to stop the entire charade. We would never find a superior until one of us died and this man’s arrogance was clouding his vision from seeing this. Something had to be done to break the stalemate, to break the equality and it was going to be made by me, not him and his arrogance. There was a simple step necessary. I watched us both tumble from a bizarre detached experience, taking in the necessary step I had taken and agreeing upon it subconsciously. Knowing that I would take a fall also I had prepared for the event, able to vault myself back from the ground and continue to run. The jolt of pain in my right leg was of no concern right there and then as I simply needed to prove a point and show the young stud who was the titan here. I looked back for the briefest of moments to see him slowly picking himself off of the ground, hitting the pavement in annoyance as I sped away. Nothing would get in my way. Nothing. *** The Pontiac Firebird slowly purred its way into the garage of our house and I flickered off the engine, sitting calmly in the driver’s seat. Since last night my leg hadn’t gotten any better and in a moment of fear I had gone to the doctor to see how it would be. It wasn’t exactly the words I was looking for. Rest and relaxation were the words of wisdom from the doctor who told me that I shouldn’t be trying to burn myself out like this. I scoffed at his insolence and told him I’d be fine and that I just needed some painkillers to get through this. It was an accepted practice by the wrestlers to gut through pain I reasoned and nothing more needed to be said about it. Unfortunately the doctor didn’t see it that way though and denied my request, giving me the lowest dosage of prescription medication he could give me, warning me that only rest would solve my problem. What did he know, he hadn’t been wrestling for the past fifteen years on the road, gutting out pain and performing in front of a crowd each and every night, sometimes in towns where the best they could offer was the fact they had two bars to the big cities that had everything you could ever want. I‘d seen it all but nothing had quenched my love for the wrestling despite the sacrifices. It was just another aspect which I had come to terms with. So now I sat in the Firebird and peeled off the packet of the drugs, the painkillers I needed. Suffice to say, the doctor wasn’t the only means of finding painkillers that I needed. I rushed through the packaging and found what was the necessary component to my continued boundary pushing and let it wash over me. The elixir leaked in through my blood and I sighed contently. As I sat there, everything that was important in life paled in significance to the relaxation and feeling I had right in my body now. For the first time in years I felt like I had no pains, no weariness, no problems. It gave me the greatest rush to know that for once I was serene and at one with my body, a body that had been taking its toll on the road for the past fifteen years. I knew it had cried out for rest many a time but it was now that it was finally receiving its reward. My muscles sagged not in turmoil but in the serenity of placation. I sank into the driver’s seat and drifted off for the smallest of moments, picturing what it would be like when I finally scaled the heights of the industry and finally achieved what it is that I wished to achieve. Victory was merely a stepping stone. *** A constant rapping on the car window eventually woke me up. As I slowly came to there was something very dull and monotonous to the surroundings. I was left…very blasé about the entire waking up experience and what I had encountered before this was blowing normal life out of the water. I never thought that I could really contemplate it…but I felt like taking those drugs again. There would be no such time for it now though, because right now Jessica was standing by the car, looking very pissed off. She stormed round the corner of the car and opened the passenger seat, climbing in. ‘What’s going on? You’ve been in the car for two hours now.’ ‘Sorry…the doctor gave me painkillers for the injury and I kind of spaced out when I took them once I got home. I don’t think I should drive right now, is that ok?’ ‘Fine.’ She got out of the car and walked round again whilst I struggled out of the driver’s seat. As she passed me, there was an awkward sense while we mashed our bodies together. It reminded us of the night we’d had and it seemed to rub off on her too. We both looked and then ignored each other, trading places and getting into the car once again. Probably not the most appropriate thing to remember for what we were going to do. And by that I mean our visit to the hospital for tests. Fertility tests. Ever since that night, Jess had gotten it into her head that something must be wrong between the two of us because it simply doesn’t take over a year to produce a baby. She figured something had to be seriously wrong with either of our reproductive systems for this to be happening. It of course had nothing to do with the fact that we were both career driven people in a career driven time. For the past year since I had entered the AWC both of our time had been taken up with the passion of wrestling. Whilst mine was obvious, Jess’ had been taken up because she had developed an ability to spot talent when she knew it. Of course she’d managed to spot Adam Dick first of all, but that was beside the point. What matters is that for the past year we hadn’t had time to have sex as much as we’d liked to. There was always something else to do. I didn’t see the point in going today but then again nothing was going to change her mind. We were going and that was that. We’d already started to go to the destination. I took a deep breath and prepared for what I’m sure was going to be a fruitless exploration. Of course we’d already endured the tests and mind numbing questions before that so today was sure to be a fun filled day of finding out that what Jessica thought was wrong wasn’t wrong at all, and instead we actually have to talk about problems unrelated to our fertility. Great, I’m sure she’ll be very impressed by that one. In no time at all we’d arrived outside the hospital and as I got out of the car I couldn’t help but feel that I was being watched or followed, something like that. Instincts like that never leave you no matter where you are or how much painkillers you’d taken and I couldn’t help but turn around fully, taking in the surrounding landscape to see if I could see anything out of the ordinary. It didn’t look like it but we’d have to wait and see. ‘You coming in or what?’ It was Jessica from the door to the hospital. Shrugging my shoulders I went to follow her, deciding it was better to get this over with as soon as possible, that way I could return to proper things, like preparing for the GTT. *** ‘So you’re trying to tell me the tests weren’t conclusive?’ The doctor fidgeted about in his chair as I looked him square in the face, thoroughly annoyed by the last line he’d just uttered out of his pathetic mouth. Apparently all the test we’d done (and there’d been enough of them) hadn’t told him anything that could solve our problems. In fact he wanted us to repeat the whole procedure as well provide a further, more extensive profiling. Hell, maybe they’d be asking me my dick size too while they’re at it. It’s not like they haven’t invaded my privacy enough as it already is. ‘Mr. Murphy I am sorry for this but we really want to be sure on a subject like this. We would prefer not to tell either one of you what would be the wrong conclusion as…well people’s reactions to it can be quite extreme.’ ‘In what way?’ ‘Well, let’s just say that the last man I told was infertile…he was dead within the week. It’s sad I know but it happens. So as a representative of the hospital and myself, I would prefer not to be put in a legal position.’ ‘Fine.’ ‘It’s ok Jack, Dr. Mieitner just wants to be sure.’ I looked over at Jessica who was somewhat disappointed but at least was hopeful that this round of testing would see to the problem. I smiled half-heartedly to her and closed my hand on hers but really I was irritated. Really I was pissed off that I had to do this kind of crap again. I was losing time on what was coming up fast. GTT was right around the corner and I needed to be in the gym preparing, not standing around in a hospital for a piss test. But I grinned and bore it. *** After two hours of relentless, unnecessary and utterly useless test we were finally ready to go. Jessica looked just as tired and worn out as I felt but deep down I knew that I was ready for a good sparring session in the gym. I needed to get ready. You see the GTT was the prize that I had been waiting for in the past year or so, ever since I had been knocked out unceremoniously last year. It was the ultimate goal and frankly, who wouldn’t want such a goal in their life? I had agonised over my performance from the previous year, marking myself off as arrogant, cocky and most of all inexperienced, something which I despised to describe myself as. It was true however and I wasn’t afraid to admit it. With it getting closer and closer to the date now, I could feel the noose tightening and this great sense that I might just be unprepared again. I could not face another year of agonising again and so now I constantly, constantly trained to the point where Jessica was getting on my nerves. She just didn’t understand the importance of this whole affair. Sure, she tried to but she didn’t and never could understand the full importance of it. She didn’t have the fire for wrestling like I did. If there was any way I could get this across to her I would try, but I couldn’t see anything that would work. The two of us left the hospital, full of thoughts and permutations as to what might happen over the next couple of days. The doctor said it would only be a couple of days this time to make sure that we weren’t left anxious or irritated by the whole experience. The doctor profusely apologised to us on the way out but I couldn’t care any less than right then when he was doing that. I just wanted him to get out of my face. The walk to the car was brief as it was only ten feet or so from the hospital door. As we exited however I could see something standing directly in front of the car, smiling like a maniac. As ever, the man was wild eyed and certainly doped up. It was Adam Dick. His smile couldn’t be missed and I grimaced in response. Jessica clutched my hand tightly as the madman stood waiting for us. He looked pleased by the response. Every now and then however he flinched, looking around him like there was somebody waiting for him. Really not in the mood for all this, I wanted to make this short and sweet. ‘What the hell are you doing here?’ ‘Colonoscopy, Murph! With those pesky haemorrhoids, you can never be too sure.’ ‘Alright. You going to get out of my way so I can go home, Dick?’ ‘That's no way to respond to an old friend. Can't you AT LEAST say you kinda missed me?’ He folded his arms, looking like he was going to plant his arm on my shoulder before he did this. He smiled and waited for the response that he knew would come. ‘No, not at all.’ He cocked his head and falsely acted disappointed. ‘NOT AT ALL'? Come on, Jack! We had so much fun back in the old days! Like when you beat me and the Suicide Girl wannabe Amy Campbell and took my Crown? Or when I began to lose matches on purpose so I could steal the sympathies of both your girlfriends, lure you into a false sense of security and take my Crown BACK? COME ON! FUN TIMES!’ In a sick kind of way I probably could have said yes to the question, largely due to the success that the run had put us both onto the map of wrestling. We were synonymous at the time, you couldn’t get away from the fact that it was because of us both that we were where we were today. We fed off each other like vultures, pecking for the last morsel of food. ‘We had a blast, and we made a lot of money. Didn't you enjoy any of it? It was certainly true that we hadn’t run out of animosity or fire to go again. Jessica knew this and clutched tighter, begging for caution and sadly I had to agree. There would be no fight today. ‘No, I was glad to see you go at the end of it all. Things have been better since you left.’ ‘BETTER? Pah! You've been in the slump from hell since I've gone on to bigger and better things… and you know it. When I was around, you were on the top of your game! You know why? Because I dragged you UP there like your mother fucking taskmaster! And now you have the nerve to be disrespectful to me after I offer a few kind words? What kind of a friend is that?!’ ‘Don’t call me friend.’ ‘Jack, that hurts, because I'm really the closest thing to a friend you'll ever know and you cannot deny it. Lucky for you, I'll be around to help you in your time of need once again.’ I tilted my head quizzically at this one. ‘You mean you haven't heard? They drew second round brackets for GTT yesterday and as fate would have it, you've got to make up for your Bye. Because once again, it's you and I. One of us will advance, one of us will go home. Don't expect it to be you moving on, Jack, but then again, I wouldn't want you to lose all hope. That's not what true friends do.’ ‘Nah, you’re lying.’ ‘I wish I were. Was hoping for some real competition, like that doorman who's never wrestled before, but I guess you'll have to do. Anyways, I must be off. Assholes to examine and all that stuff. Don't you think you've got something to be doing too, Jack? Like baby making?’ I took a swipe at him but he dodged and shimmied away, smelling Jess’ hair as he went. ‘So good to see you too, Jess. I have missed you. But it looks like we'll be seeing each other a lot sooner than we thought…’ No more words, he simply left. I watched him turn the corner and disappear again. In frustration I kicked the wheel of the car, feeling the blood flow as the stinging pain rushed through me. I didn’t need any more impetus to winning the damn thing now. If anyone deserved to be the stepping stone on the way to my crowning it was Adam Dick. ‘Jess, let’s go. I have work to do.’
