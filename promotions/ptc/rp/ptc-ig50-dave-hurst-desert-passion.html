Itachi Kobayoshi versus Dave Hurst

Desert Passion (IG50 Promo #1 of 5) Desert Passion Satin sheet, Desert heat, Lips meet -- with wet sensation, Caress my lover’s silk hips, Large moon in the desert night, Beam of light, Glistening sweat drips, Our night of passion begins. -----=====I=====----- That was the last night I ever saw Trish. We were from two different worlds: I was a wrestling superstar, touring the world with Old School Wrestling with a disposable income and a party life of girls and booze. She was a hard-working barmaid in a Las Vegas casino, with no ambition for greater things, who was content in living the rest of her life in the city that never sleeps. That night she captivated me. It was another night on the tiles. We were living it up in Las Vegas, one of the more glamourous locations on our world tour. And while the wrestlers headed on to more bars and clubs, I was captivated by her. When she walked to my end of the bar, I could see something special about this girl. Something about the way her hair swayed, the way her hips moved, the way her eyes narrowed seductively when she spoke. “Another, sir?” she asked in her thick southern accent, her eyes fixated on mine. “Sir…?” she asked again, louder this time, snapping me from my daze. “Uh… yeah… please…” is all I could muster. She chuckled playfully as she took the glass from mine and walked away. When she returned we chatted about our very different lives. We chatted about her life, her content with the world she lived in, how her family raised her in Georgia, and then moved to Las Vegas. How she had no thoughts of ever leaving. We chatted about my life, about my ambitions and my tour of the world wrestling - we had very different lives. She intrigued me. All the other women I ever knew were ambitious women. They were 20th century women with careers, finances, and professions. Even the lapdancers had dreams of running their own club some day, but this woman was different. While all these other women lived for the future, she lived in the present. The life she had now was the life she would always have, so she enjoyed every moment that she had - perhaps we had more in common than at first glance. She actually had an interest in me. Not my body, nor my looks or athleticism, not even my wealth or my fame, but me. I made her laugh, I held her hand - we walked from the bar after her shift was over. The place was bright, and loud, but it didn’t faze us. I took her to my suite for some drinks. We chatted, we laughed, I held her hand some more – our night of passion had begun. -----=====I=====----- The bus screeches as it stops outside the Palace Station hotel. It's a cheap hotel on the outskirts of Vegas, but it's all I can afford with the rest of my savings after paying the house payment and for the trip down here. I’m flat broke now. “Last stop, Palace Station” the conductor yells over the intercom. I pick my little plastic duck, Ducky, up from the seat and place him comfortably on my palm before lifting my heavy suitcase by the handle and dragging it through the aisle. I’ve never taken the little guy to Vegas, and his eyes light up when he sees our new home for the next few days. It’s a big step up from the Nordic loghouses of North Dakota, I must admit. I slide the keycard through the scanner and open the door to our room. Unpacking is the last thing on my mind right now. I sling the heavy suitcase onto the bed and walk to the window. Ducky and I scan our new environment, looking out the window to the busy night. It’s past midnight, but there are still cars heading down the streets. The bright lights of the Las Vegas strip visible from here, and equally as dazzling even from this distance. It’s immediately evident why they call this the city that never sleeps. It is here that I’ll train and compete this week. It has been two years since I’ve entered a squared circle and I have no idea how things will pan out. Questions flood my mind: Am I in good enough shape? Is wrestling like riding a bike? Have techniques evolved too much over the last two years? With the questions comes a powerful feeling of self-doubt - like a hammer to my chest. What have I gotten myself in for? How can I possibly expect to win a single match, let alone this tournament? I try my best to push these thoughts from my head. I can’t do this! I look down to my palm. My companion sits there with a concerned look on his face. I know that look - he’s worried about what I’m going through. He feels my doubts and wants to reassure me. I’m so glad that he’s stuck by me through these years. When I found this little guy I was a drunk and a womaniser. I never want to go back to those days. He’s so proud of me. I really hope I don’t let him down. Just don’t think about it…
