The Rules of Attraction

18 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-18 The rain washed down the side of the windows like blood from a severed artery, possibly caused by a misplaced Curious Mallet. It almost seemed to ooze from the clouds themselves, so thick was the water in the air. From time to time the wind would lash a squall at the side of the building; a handful of soft bullets that could not break through the defences of their target. In stark contrast to the rain, the sun hung low in the distance, and was only partially hidden by the clouds on the horizon. The red and purple hues that it cast across the visible areas of the sky reminded Eegor that everything was changeable; that the good weather could come and go, and that it could not always be controlled. The doctor’s assistant looked out at the rain with a heavy heart. A glance over to the deliriously happy Doctor Curiosity confirmed that events with the Curious Laboratory could very well go the way of the sun, hidden behind black clouds, forced from the vicinity to become just a memory of happier times. "Snap out of it Eegor, you look like someone just shot your dog!" yelled Curiosity, bounding across the stone floor of the lab. "They did, last month." "Yes, vell, time heals all wounds, you know vhat zey say." "They didn't heal poor Spot's wounds," replied Eegor, clearly even more distressed than before. "Ve don't need to get in to such morbid subjects of who killed vhich dog, or..." "You shot him. You blew his legs off with your Curious Shotgun." "Yes, but sometimes zese tzings need to be tested on animals, jah?" Eegor shook his head, but it didn't matter. Doctor Curiosity wouldn't ever admit to being wrong about the dog incident, and nor was it what was really bothering him. The thing that was causing Eegor such difficulty was the audio cassette he carried in his pocket. It contained within the magnetic tape a recording of a phone-call made by Collette, Doctor Curiosity's wife, about her genuine motives. For all the good doctor believed she wanted to salvage their marriage because of love, the real reason was that she was hankering after the potential revenue she could gain as the wife of a moderately successful wrestler. It should have been easy for Eegor to play the tape for his brother, the doctor, but at that moment he just couldn't bring himself to do it. It wasn't out of any desire to protect Collette, quite the opposite in fact since it would be hard for anyone to think less of a human being than Eegor thought of Collette Goodman, but just looking at his brother now... he seemed so happy. Eegor knew that it was false hope that gave his brother this optimism for life, but to break it right then could have been dangerous. Curiosity was trying to win the tournament for Collette, and that could help him. Still… still… the whole concept rankled with Eegor. He stood and looked over at his brother, doubt making his insides swirl. “Eegor, you look so miserable. Cheer up! All is vell vith ze vorld! I defeated Alex Grey to move on in ze competition, and next up I’m sure zat I have some other loser who I can beat into submission. Jah!” Eegor managed a half-smile. “Come on Eegor, vhat is ze name of my next opponent?” “Do you know the Muppet Man?” “The Muffin Man?” “The MUPPET Man!” “Oh yes, I know the Muppet Man; who lives in WWA?” “…” “…” “Why were you talking with an English accent there?” asked Eegor. “Vhat? Ummm… you started it! Jah!” Eegor shrugged, and looked around at the interior of the Curious Laboratory. None of the Bunsen burners were lit, and the usual bubbling green liquids were sat there, as inert as if they were just water with green food colouring in them. The rest of the scientific ephemera were out of use too. There was nothing going on in the laboratory, nothing being invented… no curious plots to overthrow the world. Eegor shook his head again. This was wrong; very wrong. “Vhat’s ze problem, Eegor? You look so upset! Are you just jealous of my love vith Collette again?” “No master. You’ve got a match with the Muppet Man in a week, and you’ve not been working out a fiendish way to win. There have been no new devices prepared, no new mallets with which to strike people, no new gadgets and gizmos designed to give you an unstoppable advantage inside the ring!” “Vhy vould I need such tzings, Eegor? I am fighting for ze love of a beautiful voman! Who needs gadgets and curious mallets vhen you’ve got a voman to help you and motivate you? I know you’re feeling upset because Collette and I are getting back together, jah, but do you know vat alvays makes me happier vhen I feel upset?” Eegor perked up, eager to hear the advice. “I like to hit someone inferior to me… JAH!” yelled the doctor as he swung his hand in an arc, smashing it in to the side of Eegor’s face. The assistant collapsed to the floor, holding his reddening cheek. That was not really the kind of advice he had been looking for. As his impatience with his brother mounted, the tape seemed to feel heavier in his pocket. Getting back to his feet and rubbing his chin, the tape began to be like an overweight blue whale pulling down at him. Still Eegor resisted throwing it back in his brother’s face. “Master, I just think that maybe you should reconsider. This man has defeated some well known names, and you’re the next on his hit-list.” The words made the hairs on the back of Doctor Curiosity’s neck stand on end. He fixed Eegor with a gaze that spoke of fear. “He’s… he’s another freaking hitman? Oh great, zis is just vhat I need! Some insane maniac with an obviously made up name coming at me vith an assault rifle or similar…” “No master, that’s not it, he’s just defeated Justin Locke, Scottywood and…” “Eegor!” shrieked Dr Curiosity. “Have you seen any of zose men since ze tournament?” “Well no,” replied Eegor, who had naturally been concentrating on those who had won their matches. “I’ve not seen Horazon since Muppet Man beat him either, but…” “But me no buts, foolish one! Ve have got a clear cut case of a murderer here, getting avay vith… vell… murder!” The good doctor had an idea in his mind, and at times like that Eegor had learned the hard way that it was best to stay out of his way. Also, somewhat miraculously, these ideas and inventions seemed to bring him great success in the ring. Dr Curiosity had earned his doctorate, and despite his foolish appearance and fake German accent, when motivated he could be capable of astonishing things. Just ask anyone to have faced him thus far in the tournament. “I have an idea, Eegor! I shall be needing some electrical tape, a bag of nails and a rubber chicken!” declared the doctor. Eegor smiled to himself as he scurried off to fetch the list of unusual items. If his brother was back to his fiendish best, then all might be salvageable… -------------------------------------------------- “Hi, yeah, it’s me.” “…” “I’ve no idea what the hell he was going on about this evening. I’m not sure, but he might have worked it out… or Paul might have worked it out. I don’t think I’m going to have any luck with him this time round.” “…” “No, there might be problems with Paul. Oh, not with Chris, or ‘ze good doctor’ if you will, he’s like putty in my hands. There are no problems there at all. None at all, beyond him being terminally stupid.” “…” “What? Fall back in love with him? Are you crazy? I was never in love with him anyway, it was just… just… just some sort of passing madness.” You could almost hear the hand being waved in front of her face in defence of the facts. Collette had indeed once been in love with Chris Goodman, the man who became the twisted Doctor Curiosity, but that seemed to be so long ago, almost a lifetime. “Look, you know I only want you, Derek. This is just for the money. I’m already married to this imbecile, but we’ve got the papers ready so we can be rid of him in an instant if we want to be. There’s no pre-nup, this is how things work. He gets to be rich and famous for winning a tournament, and we get to run off with half of his money.” Eegor sighed in the gaps on the tape. He was listening to the evidence again as Doctor Curiosity plotted and schemed in the main laboratory. He only hoped that the machinations of his brother could match those of his sister-in-law. “Of course I’m not going to sleep with him again! God, I’m going to need to bleach my brain to burn out that horrible mental image you just gave me… thanks for that.” In other circumstances, Eegor might have laughed, but faced with the painful decision of whether or not to show his brother the tape, it didn’t even come close. The recording played on, of Collette and her new lover. “You did just give me an idea though. Maybe I could use that as a reward; an incentive to beat Fozzy Bear, or whoever he’s facing next?” “…” “Mmmm, you’re right, then I might actually have to go through with it! No, much better, I’ll dangle the possibility of sleeping with me again in front of his face if he wins the entire tournament… and then if he manages to do it… we screw him… just not in the way he would like!” There was something in the way Collette laughed that set Eegor’s teeth on edge. It wasn’t just the hatred he felt for her, believing as he did that she was to blame for his most appalling of transgressions in the break-up of her marriage, but there was something harder and colder in her voice than ever before. It came out most when she laughed, and it ran a cold knife down his back, as if it was choosing which swathe of pink flesh to embed itself in. “So it’s decided. I’ll taunt Chris with the possibility of sex and reconciliation, and knowing him, he’ll do anything to win that prize. Then I sign the forms and we’re sitting pretty. Ha ha, yes honey, I do hear that Costa Rica is lovely at this time of year!” The recording clicked off as Eegor pressed the ‘stop’ button on his cheap tape player. His finger quivered in the air with loathing and distaste. There was no choice. He had to tell the doctor. ------------------------------------------------------------ The wrench tightened up the final bolt, and the work was done. Doctor Curiosity stepped back to admire his handiwork… and try to figure out exactly what it was that he’d built. There was a large metallic object bent into a “U” shape in the middle of it, that was for sure, and there were a few rubber chickens attached to what may or may not have been an old mop and bucket. There was also a lot of wiring, and a few batteries strewn around in what appeared to be a haphazard fashion. Doctor Curiosity stood back and rubbed his chin. His thoughts of a devious use for the contraption were being impinged upon by visions. They were pleasant visions, and some were decidedly more than pleasant, and bordered on the plain vulgar. A smile coursed across the face of Doctor Curiosity, and he giggled to himself, happier than he had been in weeks. The doctor shook his head and his smile grew even wider. No, it wasn’t happier than he had been in weeks, it was happier than he’d been in months… no, years! Into this arena of happiness walked Eegor, his face set and his heart hardened. He would do what he had to do. “Eegor, I vas vondering if you could give me a hand vith zis contraption here. I have been tryink to make it do something useful, but every time I seek to use it in a devious manner, ze thought of Collette takes me avay from ze mundane details, und I am lost!” Eegor’s face relaxed a little. “Well, master, I think you’re focusing too much on her and not enough on your progress to defeating the Muppet Man. If only you would concentrate…” “But how can I concentrate,” asked Doctor Curiosity, twirling towards his brother, “vhen ze entire vorld is singing to me?” “No! Chr… Master… you’re letting yourself be fooled again. I don’t… I don’t know how to say this, but Collette is stringing you along. She doesn’t really love you. She’s just hoping to make more from the divorce if you win the tournament.” The room fell silent, but for the strange buzzing noises coming from the new curious contraption. Curiosity looked like he was thinking hard about this revelation, but in reality, his mind was folding in on itself. It hardened itself, becoming more insular, resistant to any changes that the world might try to make to its perceptions. Eegor took a step forwards and placed a hand on his brother’s shoulder. “Look, I’m really sorry if you wanted to get back with her, but it’s really for the best. She’s always been manipulative, but I swear to you now, she’s worse than before. She’ll play you along for everything you could give her, and then once you’ve been bled dry, she’ll open up her harpy wings and fly off to her next victim.” He tightened his grip on Curiosity’s shoulder, in true brotherly fashion, recalling as he did the night when he had fallen in to Collette’s arms. He knew all too well what she was capable of. He continued on, softly. “You’ll do better without her. Just concentrate on beating Muppet Man and going onwards in the tournament. You can win this thing if you keep your mind on it, you really can.” Eegor looked up and turned his brother around to face him, expecting to see tears in his eyes. Instead, he saw defiance, he saw disbelief and anger. “Nein! Insolent fool, you come here vith zese lies and expect me to believe zem? Vhere is your proof, Eegor?” “I have…” “You have nothing! Do not even speak to me, you treacherous svine! You have vanted her back since ze day she left ze country, and now you’ve seen another chance to get back at me, to cheat on me… again! You vant to have her for yourself, and you von’t get zat chance! She is mine!” spat the doctor, thrusting one accusatory finger at Eegor. “I don’t want that,” replied the exasperated Eegor. “I couldn’t hate her more if I tried!” “You’ve never been me… you don’t know how much you could hate her if it was all you tried to do for years on end.” The words were spoken without an accent, and came out as barely more than a whisper. A shred of Chris Goodman had once more made its way to the surface. It steeled Eegor’s resolve more than ever. “Believe me; she wants nothing good for you. Everything she says is a lie.” In the blink of an eye, the façade was back. “Nein! Eegor, qvit vith zis nonsense! She is mine, and you can never have her back! Mine! Mine! Mine!” With a flourish, the doctor returned to his curious contraption. He lifted the wrench up and tightened the bolt next to the large red lever. “Master, I have proof.” Doctor Curiosity rolled his eyes, as if presented with a particularly recalcitrant or thick-skulled student. “Zere can be no proof for vhat does not exist, my foolish Eegor. Now, behold my latest invention!” Eegor pulled the tape from his pocket, just as Doctor Curiosity threw the lever. There was a huge groaning sound as if the device was intended to rend asunder the very fabric of space and time, but thankfully for the Goodmans, that was not the case. The lights on the machine flickered on and off… and then stayed on. Eegor felt some strange tug at him, as if some distant land was calling to him, calling him home. He felt it again, a yearning, a definite feeling of being drawn to a place… “Behold, my Curious Electromagnet… with added chickens!” Eegor suddenly realized that the tugging sensation was mostly focused on his belt buckle. The huge core of bent metal at the heart of the Curious Electromagnet was pulling him towards it at an ever increasing velocity. The chickens were mostly just sitting there, and occasionally spinning round on the end of the mop. Eegor was horizontal by the time he smashed in to the Curious Electromagnet. He was also, unfortunately, in the middle of twisting round, and the belt buckle was trying very hard to burrow through his stomach to make contact with the huge magnets. One deft turn though, and he was stuck fast, but in no imminent danger of being ripped in half by a clothing accessory. “Mmmmmnnnn hmmmnsnnsn nnnnnggghhhh!!!!” Doctor Curiosity couldn’t make much sense of the screams of Eegor. It was alien to the good doctor that Eegor would be still wearing anything with metal in it, for he himself had removed everything metallic from the environment. On second thoughts, the doctor mused, perhaps he should have warned Eegor what his experiment was. After all, he had only called Eegor in to ask him if could think of any possibly use this could have against Muppet Man. “Mmmmmmmmmgggghhh aaaaarrrrrggggghhhhh!!!” Ah yes, the screaming. The doctor walked over and changed the position of the lever. Quickly, the electrical current dropped, killing the created magnetic field. Eegor dropped to the floor clutching at his jaw, and looking in severe pain. “My fillings! You bastard! That thing tried to pull the fillings out of my damn teeth!” The doctor just shrugged, his mind shooting off in ten different directions at once; wondering if he could use that weapon in the ring, if Muppet Man would have any metal weaknesses, if Collette would let him sleep with her again… and so on. “Look, now stop messing around and let me prove it to you once and for all!” Eegor yelled as he walked to the far side of the room and pulled a battered tape recorder out of the cupboard. He held up the tape, entered it into the machine and pressed the play button. Silence. Static. Silence. Eegor’s insides turned over, as Doctor Curiosity walked to meet him. “Now vill your silly charade end?” He couldn’t believe it. His one chance was gone. His one chance to prove to his brother that Collette was out to get him, his one chance to break through the layers of pain that surrounded his brother and show him beyond doubt the truth of the matter. Curious Electromagnet, meet magnetic tape. Evidence, meet oblivion. The tape was lost.
