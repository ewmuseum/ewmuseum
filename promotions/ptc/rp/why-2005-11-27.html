Why?

27 November 2005

Date: 2005-11-27 Why? Such a tiny word, yet it can so often halt any man dead in his tracks. Bizarre how sometimes it is the simplest of questions which fails to produce a logical answer. Indeed it was this very word that played on his mind, over and over. Unrelenting in it’s pursuit of an answer he simply could not provide. The man I refer to is Chris B., known as the former professional wrestler; SurReal. Note: FORMER professional wrestler. What business had he in entering a tournament consisting of the greatest talent professional wrestling currently had to offer? There was only one kind of business in Chris B.’s life… unfinished. So often he was left asking what might have been. Certainly the potential had always been there. Once he stepped into the ring he had it all; a persona that triggered a reaction from crowds wherever he went, the ability to cope with the punishment his profession continually tolled on his body, but more so than the aforementioned factors, he was technically sound. In a business so often dominated by large powerful men, often inept of any ability to wrestle, he had been inspired as a youngster by submission holds and in-ring psychology. But more than anything else, he was inspired by those individuals who simply out wrestled their opponents. It was this love of the technical aspects of wrestling that had pushed him into the business; it fuelled his undying desire to master new holds. He in turn wanted to be someone who could inspire the next generation of wrestlers and fans just as he himself had been. So where did it all go wrong you ask? It’s a very good question. The problem with SurReal remained a mystery to the average wrestling fan. Here was a man who could quite clearly put on a fantastic display and surely wouldn’t look out of place in a main event. Unfortunately, there is more to the wrestling industry than simply wrestling. When the camera’s stopped rolling you could see what had held him back, where it had all went wrong… Attitude. The wrong attitude to be precise. You see, there is a certain type of person who aspires to all these fantastic ideas and dreams, and then tries to find the quickest possible route to achieve those dreams. Forsaking the right path that leads only to success, SurReal most certainly fell into this category. There is another type of person who needs to feel needed, to feel like they are appreciated. They crave attention and encouragement. Once again, this description fitted him perfectly. "Why are you doing this Boom?" The nurse turned in his direction, her eyes inquiring just who he was talking to, as his eyes met hers he smiled, of all his bad habits, talking to himself was the least of his worries. "What’s that you’ve got there Chris?" She motioned towards the application form he had in his hands. He questioned if telling her was the best of ideas. Lying in a hospital bed hardly seems the best preparation for a wrestling tournament. "Oh this old thing, it’s just an application form, something that’s been playing on my mind for a long time. It’s for a job I used to love, I think I owe it to myself to try and go back down that career route." "Aw that sounds like a grand idea, anything would be better than going back to that god-awful wrestling!" He smiled and quickly looked away, telling her the truth would have only caused unnecessary drama, this was a point in his life where making a scene simply didn’t appeal. As he had laid here in this lonely bed, one thing had dominated his thoughts… the past. He had it all worked out now, in his mind he had corrected all the mistakes. He had taken himself as far as he could go, his drinking had to stop, there is only so much punishment one can inflict on oneself before it all catches up. The drinking was only a mask, it hid from the world his drug problems, as he thought about it a chill ran through his body; cocaine, ecstasy, marijuana, heroin. His blood was thick with this shit. A tear formed in his eye, as it trickled down his face, he thought of all the things he had thrown away; his friends, his family and his career. This was the same train of thought that brought him here. Losing everything had forced the decision upon him, his worthless existence had to come to an end, he pushed his palms into his temples trying to drown the memories but it was pointless, over and over he saw it. The memory as clear as day, he recalled knocking over the bottle, it was empty, they were starting to take effect. His heart rate slowed and everything around him began to blur into one. He reached for his cell phone and with his last ounce of strength he punched in a text message. "I’m sorry. I will love you always", before he could send it he dropped the phone, it was too late, death was upon him. Shaking in his bed his tears are now flowing, he couldn’t decide if his tears were of self-pity or self-loathing. No, he recognised them now; it was anger that forced his tears. He couldn’t explain why he was still here, he had made the effort to ensure that wasn’t the case, yet here he was. And yet, perhaps it was now, at his lowest point, that he found his strength. He lifted the pen from his table and brushed his tears off the application form. Golden Turnbuckle Tournament Application Form This was it, no looking back…
