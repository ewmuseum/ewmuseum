Flight

1 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-01 Without a doubt the biggest challenge of his career. Not that he minded, after all, it was more fun pushing himself than settling comfortably into his position. New experiences are what drive him, sometimes blindly leading him to places he would rather not go, other times providing him with an unexpectedly pleasant surprise. Adjusting to new conditions, evolving as an old friend liked to say, was the true mark of a man and of a champion. After all, if you can’t change you simply die. And change was something he was good at. Changing his style, changing his gimmick, even changing his very beliefs in order to get ahead. This had been a feature of his life and career for as long as he could remember and it sure as hell wasn’t going to change now. As he sat in the grubby green chair, he contemplated his opponent, a man he had never witnessed or even heard of until a few weeks ago. First his name - Buddy Kingfisher. It gives out mixed signals, more than a hint of friendliness and compassion intermingled with an idea of superiority and cunningly concealed aggression. Whether it was his real name or not was irrelevant, it captured the man perfectly. His history next. Nothing normal about this, he had seemingly studiously avoided the regular routes to a professional wrestling career. His young life seemed headed for the gutter until fate intervened in the form of a CORE contract. A man who likes to have fun by all accounts, likes to entertain. Not surprising really when we consider that just a short time ago he was one of the number of fans that pay to see a good show each and every week, men spending their hard-earned gains on a mere few hours of gratification that the whole family can enjoy, rather than what they truly desire. An interesting character then this Kingfisher. Not a man who cares about appearances, not one who would have hordes of women following him as they do our subject, at least not for any good reason. On the surface he is a jovial chap, posing little threat, just in the business to have a good time and take advantage of the free catering. This is what Joe Dirt sees but for those who have a little insight, it is clear that he is the proverbial wolf in sheep’s clothing. His list of accolades tells us as much, his fat concealing surprising strength, his dazed expression hiding a deep intelligence. He is not someone to be underestimated. A threat then, to our subject’s progression in this year’s tournament. A difficult obstacle, though one that is by no means insurmountable. How to go ab- The crackle of the tannoy system disrupts the train of thought, announcing that the flight is ready for boarding. Long journey ahead, plenty of time to think about Mr Kingfisher. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ No matter how often he flew, he couldn’t help but be amused by the rush that ensued when boarding was announced. Flocks of people dashing to be the first to their berths, usually in steerage, sorry, economy class, eager to immerse themselves in stale air, soon to be punctuated by various odours and emissions, the majority not pleasant. Our subject observed this with an amused detachment, not looking forward to the flight but secure in the knowledge that his first class seat would at least separate him from the hordes. As he waited for the queue to thin out, he pondered the purpose and motives of those in front of him. Were they travelling for business or pleasure? Was that young couple really as enamoured with each other as they would have everyone believe? Did that old woman really need pre-boarding assistance? Were any of them headed eventually to GTT, as he was? It didn’t look like it but as with Kingfisher, appearances can be deceiving. And would some of these people reach their destination only to be disappointed? Discovering that the reality is as far divorced from their hopes as possible, that their optimism was misplaced? Undoubtedly this is the case, as it always is, the real question was, would they deal with it? ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Rather comfortable these seats, though whether the extra legroom was worth the extra cost was another matter. However, he had to admit that it was nice to have a fully reclining bed, not to mention real food on tap. Also, the choice of films was a lot better, a minor point but one that he appreciated after years of having to watch mindless Hollywood blockbusters. Yes, to be able to watch a film with a plot and some decent actors would definitely make the flight go quicker. Not right now though, the safety demonstration was about to begin. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* In some ways he did hope that an emergency situation would arise, just so that he could see the looks of panic and confusion on the faces of these ignorant sheep around him. Frequent flyers or not, it was just downright rude to be so dismissive of the presentation, continuing their idiotic conversations when there are more important things being said. He, of course, would be fine, but he couldn’t see how Mr 2XL in row 43 seats A and B could tie the lifejacket round his waist, never mind fit out the emergency exit. Film that and you’ve got an Oscar winner on your hands. It reminded him a lot of the GTT. There are some old faces, ones who know what to expect and will adjust accordingly. Then there are the others, those who think they know what to expect but in reality do not have the slightest inkling as to what they are going to encounter. Mr Kingfisher undoubtedly thinks he is of the first classification, master of his own destiny, determined not to slip up. The fact of the matter may prove to be quite different. Now they were airborne he fancied watching that film, where’s the call button? ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Speechless. He could be described as that, or possibly just shocked at how deeply the film had affected him. How he bought into the lives, thoughts and feelings of the protagonists was surprising given his usual apathy. But to see a person’s life, a person with everything to live for, crumble around them was immensely powerful, despite being a work of fiction. The disappointment was somewhat offset though, at least in his eyes, by the rise and eventual success of an underdog, someone who by all rights should have been trampled underfoot by those above him but who fought back, stood up and was counted. Yes, there was a lesson to be learned from that. That smell can only mean one thing. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Please close the curtain he silently begged. Surely there must be some way to isolate that stench, that bland, dull and lifeless scent of warm cardboard that these companies liked to pass off as food, from the first class cabin. How anyone could eat that swill was beyond him, how they could eagerly devour helping after helping on a long haul flight such as this was a mystery. Did they truly believe that each meal tasted different? Or did they just blind themselves to the fact that the constituents were the same for breakfast, lunch and dinner, albeit in different ratios? Bland isn’t something he knows much about, nor is dull. He is always stood out, never faded into the crowd, with good reason. He is far from average in every sense, his intelligence greater than 90% of the populace of this planet, his physical attributes setting him in an even smaller group. His thoughts and ideals have always been complex and his tastes exotic, far from the home grown Wal-Mart goodness preferred by his forthcoming opponent . A real contrast then, sugar and spice, chalk and cheese. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* This may be even worse. The food had been disgusting but at least it had been a pure stench, now he was having to endure a slightly diluted yet no less pungent pot pourri of farts, feet and food, not to mention the odd whiff of baby sick. Charming. Oh great, now we hit turbulence. If there really is a God he must have a mean streak in him. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* The pilot handled that pretty well, he had to admit. Apart from the crazy woman in club class who had her belt so tightly cinched that her legs probably lost feeling, the passengers had remained calm. He supposed that things like this were the true mark of a good pilot, the ability to react to unexpected or difficult situations. This guy had probably been expecting a nice, quiet and uneventful flight. Instead he got severe and extended turbulence. But he coped and adjusted and now he was back on course, the minor blip overcome easily. Pity not all of them were that good. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* Here already? Quick flight…or did he doze off? The latter is probably the reason, though the tailwind undoubtedly helped. As the masses struggle to get sorted, dragging oversized bags, possibly containing small elephants, form the overhead lockers, those in first class get let off first, the first breath of fresh air doing for some what the first cigarette will do for others. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~* The good feeling quickly disappears though. Standing waiting for baggage seemingly takes as long as getting to the destination, the situation made worse by the continual streams of small children rushing about, inconsiderate adults tattooing his ankles with bruises courtesy of misguided luggage carts. As our subject stands here, he considers where these people are now headed. For the majority, this signals a time of relaxation, a period in which to recharge the batteries before getting back to the daily grind. They will treasure these days, soaking up the sun, eating out every night, maybe seeing some distant relatives and reliving childhood memories. Not our man though, He will take a different path, the route that leads to GTT. For him, rest time is over, his work is just beginning. There may be 128 men and potentially many matches ahead of him but he is obeying the old adage. Focus on your next opponent, look no further. For him, GTT has shrunk down to one man - Buddy Kingfisher, everyone else are mere figures in the distance. For him, this is his final. ~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*
