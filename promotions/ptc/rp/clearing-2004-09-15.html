Clearing

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-15 “Hello, dad.” Brian shifted his weight uncomfortably from leg to leg, swaying nervously. He was standing on the stairs to the front door of his parent’s house, stairs that he must have ran up and down a hundred thousand times during his lifetime, but it seemed so unnatural. So wrong. Brian felt so out of place because, in reality, he hadn’t stepped foot in his parent’s house in over twenty years. He had kept contact with the people that had raised him, of course, and his parents occasionally flew to his home for holidays and other events, but he hadn’t actually been back home in what felt like a very long time. He knew that it was very important to patch things up with his parents, however, if he ever wanted to relax. And he definitely needed to focus on the difficult tasks he had approaching him, so being here was essentially inevitable. “Brian…” His father, nearing seventy years of age, looked quite obviously shocked to see him. His parents had never talked about it openly with their son, but Brian’s absence from their household had been weighing heavily on their minds throughout each agonizing year. They constantly pushed subtle hints in his direction, hoping to indicate to their son that they wanted him to pay them a visit. But no matter for what occasion, Brian always seemed to have an excuse ready. ‘I’m booked in a big series of matches the next few weeks, dad, I just can’t get up and leave…’ ‘Rachel isn’t up to traveling right now, mom. The kids are really giving her some headaches and I wouldn’t feel right asking her to plan this big trip…’ ‘Why don’t you come to my house for Christmas instead, dad. Rachel’s family already booked a flight to come down and we’d love to have both families together…’ The excuses kept piling up and piling up, eventually making quite a collection. Twenty years worth, to be precise. Brian had thought occasionally that he probably had enough to write a respectable book on the subject. ‘How to Avoid Mom and Dad: A Stubborn Son’s Collection of Excuses’ – by Brian Calvin. He chuckled to himself. “Hi, err, Dad. Do you mind if I come inside?” Brian twitched nervously, pulling his hands out of his pockets and running a sweaty palm through his hair. His father stepped back wordlessly, possibly not yet rendering what his brain was telling him. That often was commonplace with the elderly, though. Sometimes it took them just a hair longer to react to situations then normal. Brian knew all this already and as his father stepped back he took one forward, bringing his right foot over the hardwood floor of the entrance hall. At the exact moment that his heel touched the surface a shockwave shot painfully up his leg, taking over his body and cementing him in place. His face contorted with surprise as memories of his past came suddenly flooding back to him. In the exact spot where Brian was standing right now, he saw his father. But it wasn’t his father as he knew him today, it was his father of over twenty years ago, on the day Brian was kicked out of the house. A bit leaner and a lot more muscular, his father had been an intimidating presence. ‘Damn it Brian! What, you think you’re too good for school, now? Ready to just pack everything up and go to college early? Newsflash, Brian, Colorado State isn’t going to give a football scholarship to a high school dropout!’ Brian stammered back, ‘I- I, don’t want to play football in college, dad. I’m sick of the bullshit. I’m not going to play a sport where they care more about winning then the people that are actually playing in the games.’ His father shot back, ‘We don’t have the kind of money to send you to a college like Colorado State if you don’t get that scholarship, Brian. What do you mean you’re done with football?’ Brian had quit the football team after overhearing his coach instruct one of Brian’s teammates to take out another player because he was of a different skin color. He had given up a college scholarship to CSU for his principles. The Brian of his memory just shook his head at his father, tears swelling in his eyes. ‘I quit today. The coach was going to do something to a player on another team just because he was black. So I quit.’ His father took all this in, and then, after a minute, said, ‘Is this why you want to quit school, too?’ Brian nodded. ‘They hate me there. I can’t go back.’ His father said, after another long pause, ‘Fine then. Quit football. But if you decide that you’re not going to finish high school, you can get out of this house. Now. I won’t tolerate a dropout living under my roof.’ More tears began to run from Brian’s eyes, and his looked up at his dad with hate and fury. ‘Then I won’t be living under your roof anymore, old man.’ He stormed away, ignoring the yells from his dad, telling him to come back. He was done with listening to anymore shit. From anyone.’ That was one of the last times Brian had been to his parent’s house. The pain from the memories was simply too much for him to handle. He and his father had eventually reconciled about his choice to leave high school and losing his chance to play college football, but his dad had still never apologized for kicking him out of the house. Brian thought he could see the man struggling to say the forbidden words (I’m sorry) quite a few times, but it had never come. And so, Brian had stayed away. He took a few more steps into his old house, looking around at what had changed. The living room ahead had been re-carpeted and the old furniture he remembered was long gone and replaced, but it was still the same house that he had grown up in. That was for sure. “Valerie!” Brian’s dad called out to the kitchen. Brian could hear the approaching steps of his mother. “What is it, Keith, I’m almost finished baking the --” She froze when she saw her son. “Hi, mom.” Brian’s mother blinked twice and then smile, ecstatic to see him. His absence from the Calvin household had upset her even more then it had upset his father, at least on the outside. “Oh Brian, it’s so good to see you!” She rushed forward, grabbing her son in a giant hug around his waist. He had to grin at the emotion that his mom was exhibiting, as it was a rare sight for her to lose her cool and ‘let go’ like she was doing now. “What are you doing here? I can’t believe it, you didn’t even call or let us know, I would have cleaned up more, I --” She paused as Brian’s wife Rachel stepped into the hallway, poking her head into the living room. Rachel had a nervous expression on her face, and she looked even more uncomfortable then Brian was. It was understandable, as it had been largely because of her that Brian had stayed away for as long as he did. Being kicked out of the house was one thing, and that had hurt Brian greatly. But it had hurt even more when his mother said she didn’t approve of him dating the woman that he had eventually married. He remembered talking to his mother on the phone over thirteen or fourteen years ago, telling her that he was still dating his high school sweetheart, Rachel, and that everything was going extremely well. He could also remember her reaction. ‘Brian, you’re too good for her. Look at you! You were an all-American athlete, you were a great student, you --’ He interrupted, ‘Mom, I didn’t even graduate from high school! I’m not exactly going by ‘Doctor Calvin’ these days. I’m just a professional wrestler!’ ‘She’s no good, Brian, she comes from a terrible family. No adult in town respects the Clifford’s. They live in an apartment behind Wal-Mart, for crying out loud!’ ‘WHAT? Mom, I don’t care about her family! She’s a wonderful caring person and if you can’t respect my decision to date who I choose then --’ ‘I just know you could do better then trash like that, Brian.’ He slammed the phone down onto the receiver and marched out of the room, red with anger. His mother looked past Brian and stared at Rachel for a few moments, before breaking out into a smile. “Come over here, dear! It’s been so long…” Valerie extended an arm and pulled Rachel and Brian together in one big hug. Finally Brian’s father stepped forward, having regained his wits. He too had a smile on his face. “It’s good to have you home, son. But may I ask why you decided to choose now? I mean, it’s such a surprise.” Brian’s dad looked quite curious. Brian cleared his throat. “I have a couple of very important matches this week, actually, and I wanted to stop by before I flew out. I’m wrestling the All American Team in a tag match for WWA and then my first match in the PTC Golden Turnbuckle Tournament is also coming up very shortly. I’m wrestling someone by the name of Miles Jerni.” He paused. “Look, the reason is, well… I know there’s been some unspoken aggravation between us the past few years, and I really wanted to clear everything up with everyone before I went off to wrestle this week. All of this has really been hitting me hard the past few weeks as I’ve begun to reestablish myself in the WWA, and I’ve been reliving some very old, very bad memories. I’m just tired of dealing with it. I wanted to bring everyone I love together and end the tension, once and for all. What do you guys say?” Keith looked up at his son with a somber expression on his face. “I’m sorry, Brian. For… for everything. For kicking you out of the house, for not listening to you, and for not being man enough to say I’m sorry.” Valerie wiped a tear from her face, and she nodded. “I’m sorry too, Brian. For not trusting you enough to make your own decisions. And you obviously proved me wrong.” She smiled apologetically at Rachel. Brian said, “It’s okay, I accept. I’m just glad that we can put this behind us, and I can finally go to work with a clear conscience. Now, if it’s okay with you, I’d very much like it if I could spend the night here on our way to the arena. I’ve got the kids waiting in the car, and I’m sure they’d love to see Grandma and Grandpa.” He grinned. Valerie smiled, and said, “Of course you can. Bring the kids in, I’ve been dying to see them!” Brian grinned to himself and put his arm around his wife. Together they wordlessly made their way back to their car. Brian himself was mentally exhausted, but relieved. After a career rebirth in the WWA he hadn’t been able to focus the way he knew he should have been, as the memories of his past were constantly being brought up. He knew that finally reconciling things with his parents was the only way that he would be able to free himself, and he was right. His mind was finally clear, and he was finally able to focus on the one thing in his life that he truly loved. Wrestling. It was his desire, his dreams, his life… His infatuation.
