Happily Ever After…

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 ‘I have always known the storm would come, listen now my young one. This is not a story I told of midnight, moon and sun… Are you ready to walk the forbidden road, learn again what we tried to forget? Look me in the eyes o’ Childer—this ain’t your fairytale.’ .:: ::: ::. There has always been something about the air in this place. Prague will always smell of winter to me, no matter if it is sunny and warm outside or not. I will always see these red roofs, and be reminded of a time when every roof seemed to be painted in blood as the plague ravaged the city. The old walls have long since been torn down, but once, there were wooden gates, and town guards with halberds, keeping the rabble away from Judith Bridge, the only way to Castle Vysehrad and the monastery atop Petrin Hill, home to our allies the Cappadocians. All this is gone, you understand, but as I stare from the tops of Castle Vysehrad’s peaks, the sun fading into blood-red oblivion behind the clouds and mountains, I can still see it as though at any moment, all of these cars and electric lights could fade away. They’d be replaced by horse-sentries pacing cobbled streets, by the seers on street-corners and their clamoring screams of the coming of Christ. Memories. There comes a time when they’re about the only thing that seems real, a time after you’ve exhausted what you feel like is every other option open to you. I’ve tried living in the present, and all that did was piss off everyone around me. Living in the future is the futile equivalent to flushing yourself down the drain. All that’s left then, is the past—to dwell on memories. Some might say that it makes you pathetic, to think about things that you’ll never have again, but for me, it’s an incentive to pick my head up in the morning. What I remember most is my family. The family. It’s been a predilection since ancient times for us to have the visions that I have, visions that remind us of what is important. Little clairvoyant flashes that make the heart of a matter transparent. Though it’s impossible to control when they come, the ones I have never cease to remind me of family I’ve forgotten, faces I haven’t seen in so long that the names become disconnected from them. Of late, I’ve been reminded of a promise I made not too long ago—a promise I made to my dead grandmother. Perhaps that sounds strange to you, or it would sound strange if you knew that my grandmother died in the 14th century, and I made the promise as recently as a few months ago. Time, though, like many other things that we use to measure our existence, is much more fluid than we give it credit for. ‘Someone has to continue the bloodline,’ she told me. I wish it was as simple as that sounds. But these days, it’s hard enough to keep your head on your shoulders. Worrying, on top of that, about what would be best to keep the legacy of our family alive is something often best forgotten, when you’re living hand to mouth like I have been. I didn’t have to, of course. But, I came to realize something, after Emoticon tried to kill Virgil, after I watched Virgil and Dawn glare at each other from across the living room of the McFarlane Estate. After I went to the Czech Republic to find answers, and found a good friend in danger of losing his life when some psychotic cursed mother hen decided to try and kill him. And that’s when the present became too tangled to continue within its shackles. I left. Call it cowardice if you have to, but that’s not what it was. I left because I was the prime problem to some of them, the prime solution to others. Emoticon was right—I had to make a choice between them at some point, or they all would have ended up hating me. Perhaps they all do in any case, but this is my choice. Solitude. In choosing this, I made perhaps the irresponsible choice. I told no one, but merely disappeared into the night. Even with my age and experience, I am fallible, and I was promptly taken advantage of—abducted.. What happened after my imprisonment, not even I can say for sure. All I know is that I harrowed hell, and whether physically or only in my mind, the journey to its depths has scarred me. Perhaps irreparably, in that there, I saw a specter from my past that reminded me of old duties that had been happily forgotten during my time in Global Wrestling. I gave in to guilt. With the scent of the road fresh on my skin, and a waft of brimstone following behind, I’ve left again. The wanderlust lives in me still. The journey has led me here. Prague. Where the journey always seems to lead me, no matter what journey it is. I know only half of what I’m looking for, but this trip, adding companions would be quite futile, no matter their promises to aid me. It’s been nicer than I thought it would be. The silence, rather than being oppressing, as it would no doubt be for some people, has been enlightening. In it, I hear more than if the void were filled by empty words. In it, I hear the echoes of centuries long past, the echo of who I used to be, and perhaps that is who I must be again in order to survive the ordeal that I’ve bridled myself with. And in it, I hear the same old story, the story that every Vlaszy child has been told for generations. We heard it, and it became a part of us all. It is impossible to be one of us, and not remember what you have been taught all your life. It is impossible not to come back to it, whether decades or centuries later. This is, after all, what we are made of. This story, so old it seems like fiction, is woven into the very fabric of our being. .:: ::: ::. And this is it how it began… Listen, my child… A long story, yes, but an important one. One that all little děti must hear. Why? Because it is our story, little one. Our story, our past… Now listen. Once upon a time, before our family had the name that we have now, we lived in the great black mountains that they call Carpathian. And a wild tribe we were, men who hunted with their bare hands, and women-folk who made clothing out of the animal skins so that our family could survive the winter. And it was not only hunters that we were, but sorcerers too. Witches, my love. Mages. The mountains of Carpathia, you see, were no ordinary mountains. They were, and still are, I imagine, mountains that are enchanted with the spirits of the land. Very powerful spirits who, while we lived there, protected us from harm. But… as all good things come to an end, our tribe was forced to leave the Carpathians when the Lupines came marauding. The land that we now call Romania was becoming a home to them. They liked, you see, the great dark forests, where there was much to hunt, and much to kill. They held nothing sacred, though, and it was for that reason that our family left. We were frightened, frightened that our long family line, great-grandparents living with their great-grandchildren would be devastated if the beasts made any in-roads into our little villages. The family elders decided that we must leave. We took to the winding mountain paths, and left the home that had sheltered us and nurtured us for generations. Often, the strongest of the family carried the oldest and weakest on their backs, but if there can be one thing said, our family never does anything without doing it whole-heartedly. We did not look back. We couldn’t have looked back and survived. Though it was sad to leave our home behind, it was really the only thing to do. And the dirt paths carried us to a place better for us. It was not the beautiful forested mountains of our old home, but it would serve us just as well. This place was called Esztergom, a village that was small then, but from the size of it today, you would not believe that, even if I told you a hundred times. There we settled, and we were happy, little one. It was only once the family had a new village that we realized there was one thing missing—the gifts. Yes, my child, once we had left the Carpathians, it was as though the eldritch magical gifts that each and every one of us had treasured as a family birthright… were gone. They had simply vanished without a trace, a stream dammed up at its source. What were we to do, child? We couldn’t just produce these abilities out of nowhere—we didn’t know from whence they had come in any case. And life continued for us without them. It had to. Until He came. Before He came, a storm whipped up on the horizon. It seemed to come out of nowhere, its great bolts of lightning splitting the bell at the top of the church tower, on the hill above the town. On the heels of the storm came a ragged traveler, cloaked in a dirty brown robe. His face was that of an old man, or it seemed to be at least, only the skin was stretched smooth, as though wrapped tight over bones. Don’t be scared, child, He was not Death, but He may certainly have appeared to be to your great-great-great-great-great grandmother. He did not state his business to any of the town guards, entreat Him though they might. But what He did was come straight to us—to our family’s dwellings on the edge of town, which must have looked to Him like scarcely more than a hovel, Him who had the scent of castles and horses and plate mail on him, despite His ragged appearance. The words of what he said to the head of the family—Marius, your great-great-great-great-great-great-grandfather, dear—have been kept long secret. And the words themselves are not as important as what happened after them. For he promised us that he knew what it was our clan sought—and it was not the power that we desired, but our birthright. In losing the protection of the lands, we lost something of our heritage. He would give it back to us, if we would promise to serve Him in the capacity that He desired. Some say it was respect for our family heritage that drew Him to us, that He saw a strong line, and realized that His descendants would need such, that even HE would need such when the first of the promised had died off. Other more critical members of our family, little one, say that He needed us to put Himself in the position that He desired. What He really wanted, though, is also of little consequence, since He has passed on to whatever realm it is that awaited Him after this life. We still endure, our stories still endure. The people of Esztergom, perhaps, would have preferred that we be buried under endless sands of time. He was a devil, a portent of evil, because he had come after the storm that nearly set the church on fire. And perhaps He was a devil, but because He had come to speak to us, you can imagine that they thought even worse of our family. They gave us a name, then: Vlaszy. A name that meant ‘king-maker,’ in the sense that we had made a king out of a demon. But the name stuck, and it has been an accurate one in more ways than one. He would not let us be endangered. He told us that again, we must leave, we must follow Him where He traveled. And so our family, once again forced from their homes, packed up their things, the strong carrying the infirm, and leaving no trace behind. They followed him along the winding path of the Danube, and towards the city that we now call Vienna. That journey was long, and it is said that many of our tribe died along the way, because it was winter, and the weather was harsh. When they reached the gates of Vienna, the Ringstrasse was covered in snow, and they trudged in, exhausted, and empty-handed… to what we now call a ‘chantry.’ In these days, they belong to a group called the Usurpers, the enemies of our first master. But then, it was He who had the chantry, and he took our people in, housed them, fed them, clothed them. That very night, he performed a ceremony conferring once again the power of our native lands back upon us—we started to understand. He, who called himself a Fiend, understood because He himself was under sway of those powers, only He had not lost them upon leaving the land. Later, we came to understand more of Him. The humans called Him Damned, but they also believed that His kind was mere legend. And by that point, our family was too enmeshed to say no to His desires. He had given us our birthright, and made us into His kingmakers. We owed Him a boon, and a boon we gave Him—the boon of our back-breaking efforts to make Him into the powerful ruler of Vienna that He wished to be. Here, the old woman’s voice was broken by a straggling cough. She’d been talking all this time without taking any water, and evidently had just been reminded of her age. The wooden cup touched her dry old lips, and the water passed through into her parched throat, all before she could continue. The child’s eyes remained rapt on her figure, wide green and fringed with delicate raven’s wing lashes; innocent, and not entirely understanding the story just yet. Yes, but times were different then than they are now, my dear. After our training in the chantry, we found that there was a whole network of these Fiends. And after our first master died, we were contacted by another Fiend, who said that our former master had dreadfully misused us. It was thus that our boon was carried forward—the new master continued ensuring our birthright, and we served Him. The Vlaszy line has thrived, and from the original family, we now have many branches, of which you are part of one, my little one. ‘But why are we here?’ plainted the little voice. Grandma Jalena smiled, knowing that the child was referring to geography and not metaphysics. Yes, you know that we are not in Vienna anymore, that we are in this place called Zdice, which is quite far from Vienna, which you may never even see in your lifetime, little one. But this is where our master bade us come. And his name is Vaclav Ionescu, and he lives in a castle that is in a place called Prague that is almost a day’s ride from here. Here, the old woman smiled, nearly toothlessly, but fondly, at her granddaughter. It was eight years ago that you and your brother Laszlo were born to us. The master finds you very promising—he says he senses that the gifts are very strong within you, and it is impossible not to trust what he says, because his senses are far stronger than even mine. Old as I am, he is older still. He is not quite what we are, you see, but we must not fear him, for he would not harm us. And there will come a day, when you are fifteen, that he will ask a commitment from you. He will ask you to show that you still serve him, and then you will be under his special protection. The greatest students of his art may sometimes go to the castle in Prague to visit him, and we have always hoped that you and Laszlo would have that honor, Lia. You must try hard to earn it. ‘Will you be mad at us if we don’t get to go see the man in the castle?’ The old woman’s gnarled fingers patted the child on the head. No, no. My dear, it is an honor that is almost inborn, something that must be constantly worked for. Most of us never earn that honor. I have never seen the inside of his home. But you were born to us at a time when our family had begun to lose hope—that is why your name means ‘works for the people’ and your brother’s means ‘hope has come to us.’ We have always had very high hopes for such a miracle as the two of you. Our wishes were granted with your births, and our wishes are granted each day with your continuing health. Whether you earn master’s favor or not, you have shown us that we go on, we Vlaszy. Always remember that, my little one, even after I am gone, and you are gone… we go on. .:: ::: ::. It is a shame that my grandmother was such a liar. The Vlaszy are all dead. Every member of every branch across Europe—‘slain to a man’ is what the history books say. I slipped through the cracks because I had already been made into a vampire by the time they were killed. I had already been packed off to that old, secret chantry in Austria, in order that Master Ionescu, who knew he was going to die, could preserve the knowledge that he had painstakingly taught his best students. The guilt over not dying with them has always been my cross to bear. But it is these visions that I spoke of that continue reminding me that bearing the brunt of a guilt tempest is not even close to the end of my familial duties. "I’m sorry. It was selfish of me. Sometimes it’s hard to remember that I’m the last of the bloodline…" "The last? You’re not the last, Lia," Grandma Jalena said, shaking her head. "I—I’m not?" "No! Have you no sense? Your siblings… Laszlo and Anezka are still alive." "Where?!" "Don’t ask me WHERE, don’t be silly. Open your mind and think, Lia. You’ll find them." It is this which troubles me, troubles me and guides me doubly. Anezka and Laszlo too I met in the abyss of hell which is all too fresh in my memory. And yet visions of my grandmother plague me in dreams, reminding me over and over again of this conversation. ‘You promised,’ she says, with her accusing eyes. I promised. It is this promise that caused me, as Global Wrestling’s Showdown in Tokyo approached, to give the Talent Office a letter announcing my resignation from the company. Today, I walk the Prague streets a free woman. Free to keep my promise, unimpeded by obligations of company loyalty, title defenses… free to see where a new tournament may take me in this pleasant diversion of mine, without concerns of who is watching back in the home promotion. This waltz is one that I must do on my own—that is to say, a waltz in which I must lead. Surely it’s true that none of us can exist without partners for this precarious dance of existence, whether they cut in for only one whirling split-second and then disappear or remain to pose and pirouette with us into the dimness of old age. I will no doubt encounter new partners in the coming weeks, new co-paramours of this life, even if it is only within the confines of a GTT5 wrestling ring. There, too, we will embrace, and tango until one of us is forced to concede being bested. It is refreshingly simple. Move, counter-move. Clasp my hand, and I your shoulder, pivot, spin to the whims of what music we can make out through this darkness. In this we find truth, you yours and I mine. The first truth is that, simply, I compete wearing my own aegis. Whatever spoils I gain from my journey are mine alone, sustenance for the long road ahead of me, in discovering whether I truly am the solitary heiress, last of what I represent… or whether there is another. What truth for you, then? You may hide it behind paper and clay all you wish, but I am not threatened by confidence nor subterfuge. Questions of your identity, rampant speculation, and the fear you must think ensues… all dust. Grace-notes in our tune, as insignificant as a speck of dirt on the soul of the devil. The choice has already been made: I lead, you follow. Do try to keep up. .:: ::: ::. Lead-in Quote-"Ain’t Your Fairytale" by Sonata Arctica
