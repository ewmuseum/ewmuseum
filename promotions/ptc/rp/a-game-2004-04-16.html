A Game

16 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-16 (A Game) Sometimes being alone has its advantages. No interruptions, no other sound, nothing. That's the way I had always liked it. To the point most of my life had spent just like that, alone. If not more so than almost anyone knew. There was one other person who knew though. He was the only person in the world who I trusted. The only person who's company I didn't mind. That man was my friend, and trainer, Fred Vint. He seemed to understand me. He was the one who had helped me through the return of the nightmares the previous weeks, which had now finally abated for some strange daunting reason. I would not have been where I was if it had not been for him. He helped me through the worst parts of my career. He helped me through my back injury after I had just joined the federation that I loved, the FAE. He was there when Richard had committed suicide right in front of me. He was there now as I tried with all my might to represent my love and my life with all of my might in the Prime Time Central Cruiser Weight Tournament. He was my emotional support. He kept my spirits lifted in the down times. He helped me see my way through my first round match up against Larry the Legionnaire. A match that should not have been as close as it was by far. Now he was helping me prepare my match for my second round against The Fallen Fae. I had never heard of someone teaching me in such a way as this though. He was using a simple game to teach me, or say he said. Fred had told me that it would help me concentrate on things to come. Though I had been left wondering. How could a board game of war help me in a wrestling match? ******************************************** "It used to be the game of the nobles." began Fred in the first words he had said since we began our match up of this seemingly stupid board game. With its small wooden pieces stranded along the checkered board. This was a game that for fun, not training. "Yea, Chess was played by all of the nobles back in the late 1800s." As if I cared. As much as I liked being alone, Fred was still the one person I liked having a round. I still don't know why. He annoyed me like no other. He had a ton of superfluous information that he always was ready to pass on. As to why I never knew. Yet in addition to being annoying, it also brought a sense of joy to me. For someone to be near me and treat me like a person was something that had eluded me most of my life. "I still don't understand how this will help me in my match.", I said slowly, purposely drawing out to allow the sense of boredom to become relevant. Fred looked up from the board where he was choosing his next move and looked at me with his hazelnut eyes and smiled. "Does it matter?" he asked with a soft laugh. I rolled my eyes and he looked back down at the board to continue the game. "It's all a matter of perspective" he said as he moved his knight to capture my bishop freely. "How so?" I asked with wonder. I figured that this was going to get interesting as most conversations did with Fred. Though at the same time long and bore some. "Well," began Fred, "You look at it as a simple bored game. You give it no chance whatsoever to teach you other things." What else did it have to to teach. It was a BOARD game. There was no conditioning, no repetitions of moves. Nothing. "Because it is a simple bored" I said as I moved my queen in for the kill on his King. He went back down to studying the bored, realizing that he didn't have much time left in the game. "Well that's you opinion." Fred said as he was crunching the "numbers" in his mind, trying to figure out what to do next. I ran my fingers through my long blond hair and sighed. He would probably be right in the end, but that didn't matter anymore. So I decided to pursue this further. It probably would help me anyways. "So what's yours?" Fred laughed once again. He looked up from the game and sat up and into the back of his chair. He looked at me for a second before stretching his arms back and over his head and ruffling his own hair. "Here's my take," he said, "Chess helps to strengthen the mind. It helps you to think methodically. When you're in a match you have to realize that every choice has a counter choice." "Yea, so, I already know that." I would hope that I did anyways. I thought that I had learned at least that by now in my wrestling career. "Yes, but do you truly know it?" Fred asked with a bit of mockery in his tone. "What you mean?" "It's like this," Fred began one again," I've watched you wrestle many times. I've watched tapes of you wrestle even more. Whenever you make a move you don't think for farther down in the match. You always try and end the match as fast as you can. You're always searching for the move or attack that will end it fast. You don't know how to wear your opponent down." "Ouch, that kind of hurts you know." I said sarcastically. I should have figured that this was coming. He always like to critique me about my matches. I guess it was his right since he no longer had his wrestling career due to me injuring him. "This is stuff you have got to know. You got lucky in the first round of the tournament, but this round is going to be much tougher and you know it. If you somehow make it past this round, you'll have possibly Pietro Geist in the bracket finals or worse, Seymour Almasy. You and I know both know you're not ready for him." I hated it when he had a point. Yet, like always he did once again have a point. A point I couldn't argue against. "Well you're right, as always" I said as I looked down at the bored looking to see if I could checkmate him the next move. "Of course I'm always right. I was born that way" laughed Fred and then he looked down at the board as I was and looked it over a few second before moving his own queen next to my King. I looked at the move and immediately became angry. His queen was protected by his other bishop which I had completely forgotten about. "Checkmate" he said gingerly as ever. I muttered some obscenities under my breath trying to quell my anger. He was right though. He had always been right. I had thought only about the next move of the game and it cost me the game. It was good advice that I would have think more of and put it into practice.
