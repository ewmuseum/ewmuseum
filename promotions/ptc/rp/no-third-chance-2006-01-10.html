No Third Chance

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-10 The writhing skin of flies made the carcass seem alive. Constant devouring movement imitated the tide of breath, but patches of dully glistening reds and pinks peeking through the blackness spoiled the delusion the body might be a sleeping panther, as did the wide-eyed blindness of the slumped head. Naked of the scourge, the head was still adorned with the golden fur that had been stripped from the rest of the lioness’ flesh, still marred by the majestic beauty that called jealous forfeit to her reign. Once she had surveyed the land as hers, with determination and wonder, proud, honoured and honourable. Now, through the freeing sheen of death, she saw nothing. She couldn’t see the other suffered around her, identically skinned and besieged, the males of her pride beheaded for their manes. She couldn’t see the blackened halos of dirt that surrounded them, the expiring evidence of their spilled life. She couldn’t see her cub curled beside her snout, tiny paws forlornly covering his own. Occasionally the pup whimpered, a shrill, wrenching whine of utter despair. Pawing, nudging, and nuzzling his mother had long-since proven futile, succeeding only in rousing the flies for a quick repositioning. Directly attacking the hungry blanket displaced it in a cloud, but temporarily; the feeders were unrelenting in their dedication to the buffet. A paw hit the caked ground with a liquid thud, unveiling a large, drooping eye, a brown sea of defeat. For the briefest moment a tiny buoy bobbed within the tempest, a signal of hope that maybe his nose was wrong, but with the focusing of the eye crashed down the drowning reality. He rose slowly, picking up the stain, and brushed her nose with his, breath held in desperate anticipation of feeling hers. Instead he felt dry, listless rubber, tepid, heated solely by the sun overhead. A bleating sound escaped him, a mournful, beseeching cry. Throwing his head to the heavens he bellowed another, louder, then jerked back instantly and cocked it to the side, ears perking up as he tensed for a reaction. Slowly, they folded back down, dragging his face with them. His entire body slumped, muscles again fleeing, then suddenly stiffened halfway down. Stretching forward with great delicacy, he touched noses a final time, flipped a farewell lick, and backed away with his head still lowered, unable to tear his gaze from what remained of his ravaged mother, once silently distinguished, strong, and proud. Fifteen feet away he stopped, rocking, rising on hind legs, throwing back his mane-less head to roar. Youth deprived him of the strength to conjure thunder with his call, but anguish filled it with the ferocity to answer his noble heritage. It echoed across the plains a promise of redemption. It rumbled at the thievery of pride. There could be no restitution for those slaughtered before their time; nothing would bring them back; nothing would return the land to the honoured battlefield it used to be. The cub knew his home was tainted, but the gallantry of those fallen would be rekindled through him. He’d survived for a reason, and it was more than the hunters investing value in his growth. He was more than a glorious skin to be stolen and sold. His realm was gone, but he could rebuild it. His jungle had been raped and razed, but he was still king. So long as he lived he would be, and so long as he breathed his vintage line would be exalted. A yipping sound behind him washed him anew with dread, stiffening a strip of fur down his spine. The mohawk was a warning sign for him and attackers both, a beacon advertising that mortal anxiety had kicked in, that instinct now ruled the roost. Most animals in this situation fight or flee, but while the latter is often the wiser choice, for a lion it isn’t even an option. Lips twitching into a snarl led him in turning around just as the yip became a low, mournful howl. Against the horizon a hyena stretched, alone at first but joined by others as the haunting siren continued. They were still quite a way off, but the cub couldn’t kid himself into believing they hadn’t picked up the foul stench of death. For them it was the sweet scent of opportunity. Any middling doubt was scoured clean as the three hyenas gave a final bark and began loping across the plains toward the criminal banquet. The last lion watched with horror as the scavengers descended, tongues lolling in eager anticipation of desecrating what little remained of his family. Slanted yellow eyes and mouths stretched back in avaricious grimaces made their faces seem unnaturally long and sinister, perverted, like robbers grinning through pantyhose disguises. An angry roar made the hyenas jump and back off, looking around wildly as their own hackles rose, but once their eyes found the cub, they continued on as before, now skittering laughter. Sense told the cub to run, that their arrogance was warranted, that he lacked the power to affect change. It would have, at least, if he had any left. It and almost everything else had been burned out of him with the ignition of his rage, sparked to fill the void left by vanquished dignity. With a roar, like volcanic wind he charged, racing for certain death to defend what was already lost. ~><>…<><~ The early-morning trip to the library had almost been as futile as Hezekiah had prophesied. Walking out, Abraham knew little more of Angelo Deville than he had waking up. In fact, in some ways, he thought he might now know less. At first he’d assumed that Deville was just a simple if successful wrestler who’d one night run afoul of the wrong people. Now . . . If the man had a permanent address, no one knew about it. Even when Abraham called PRIME pretending to be Angelo’s brother, the Human Resources department explained that Deville had insisted on being paid in cash, and further, refused to disclose his living quarters beyond "somewhere in Canada." It wasn’t that he had no bank account; he had dozens of them, but they all listed incorporations as their address. Phoning a handful of those had treated him to blunt refusal, feigned ignorance, cutting laughter, and the information that Angelo had no brother, followed by a very direct threat on his legs. Not even the Department of Motor Vehicles provided any help, saying that even if they did know, that information was confidential. Something told him he should have known not to seek assistance there, but it was a fleeting suspicion. He thought to call the police, but for some reason, just couldn’t bring himself to. Where Deville lacked in tangibility, he polluted in cyberspace. Abraham didn’t get through a quarter of the websites that mentioned him, from conspiracy pieces to wrestling magazines to online newspapers. Not one listed an address or even a hometown or any way to find him, and many more than hinted you wouldn’t want to. Some went so far as to suggest that his professional gimmick was an extension of his life rather than a fabrication. That while others in the business traditionally exaggerated their own personality or invented something outrageous, he played himself, only a milder version. Abraham took that for people buying too deep into the suspense, but every claimant cited evidence beyond the ring. One of his opponents actually disappeared just before his first match in GTT3, never to be seen or heard from again. That was about the time all of the articles started popping up. They asserted he’d snuck into the tournament in the first place, stealing the only free spot in the third round, an unprecedented move. Some claimed it was more than happenstance, less than random. Others were venomous in their conviction he’d somehow rigged the whole thing. Angelo himself never publicly addressed the matter, even for denial. He maintained a stoic stance of not commenting. There was too much information to take in, but even if there hadn’t been, none of it made anything click. Nothing he read spawned an epiphany of self-awareness. The deluge of photos were all exact duplications of what he saw in the mirror, but the connection just wasn’t there. It looked like him, but if it was or not, he simply didn’t remember. Back at the Klassen home, he was preparing to leave anyway. He didn’t know where to, exactly, but he imagined some of the man’s coworkers would know where he lived. Many of the recent sites he’d skimmed chronicled a romance with one of the female wrestlers, an icy daredevil by the name of Karina Wolfenden. He’d start with her; she looked like a down-to-earth woman. He didn’t have much to pack: a few suits provided to him by the family for church. The coveralls and other work clothes were being left behind, and the suit he’d been found in, now clean and pressed, was laid out on the bed. Hoping he was looking at himself in rags like these for the last time, he shook his head and donned the suit, then turned to the full-length mirror across the room. Clean-shaven for the first time in months, he started when he caught his reflection, a queasy realization worming into his stomach that he really was Angelo Deville. Penetrating eyes narrowed with suspicion as he cocked his head ever-so-slightly to the side. His lips twitched ambiguously, teasing either restrained smile or snarl. The first step he took was tentative, but the speed steadily increased thereafter, until he was a foot from the mirror, staring directly into it. Maintaining eye contact, mesmerized, he gradually raised a hand to shoulder-level, then grabbed the air. Jawbones rippled as teeth clenched. A sharp tearing noise cut the silence as Deville pulled down and across, severing substance and crumpling it in his fist. A rectangular expanse of black gaped before him, depthless, impossible to reconcile with the room around it. With a lick of his lips, The Deville stepped into it. Through it. Through stories, intrigue, and pretence. Through time. He arrived on the other side in the present day, cocooned in darkness save a bright, source-less spotlight bathing him in a warming glow. Rejuvenation swelled him, standing him tall and proud. The quirk of the past became the smile. "Fuck you." The smile became the snarl. "No. Seriously." Fuck you. Each and every last one of you with the audacity to believe for one drunken, inbred second that I’d seriously pursue that fallaciously pretentious and silently retarded amnesia shtick. Anyone who even entertained the notion that somewhere, someday, under any circumstance, I’d genuinely put that tripe, pointless bullshit forward for the purposes of competition rather than mockery. Hell, anyone who thinks that’s an effective way to address a match. To all of you people, again: fuck you. Is that the flag we look up to and salute? Is that really what it takes to win? How anything so numbingly tedious and disconnected can be celebrated ties my brain in knots. Has mindless melodrama become the standard we champion? When did presentation, period, begin to overrule presentation of character? How did addressing an undistinguishing audience take precedence over addressing the situation? What the hell has become of us? Please, please tell me we haven’t devolved to a society of sensationalist hacks. Heading down to the ring now I half-expect my opponent is going to break down in tears mid-match, or grow huge and turn green, or transform into a bat and fly away. The other half of my expectation is that they’ll be dead (still), in jail, or fraternizing in cornfields drawing symbols in the fertile ground and chanting their love for the dark lord. Do you have any idea how tired I am of always looking across the ring into captivating blue eyes apparently plucked from a naïve Swedish virgin just as someone stole home for the first time? I swear, as I was growing up, people had eyes of different colours. Thunder boomed with every footfall, threats were more common than greetings and smalltalk, and a litany of people inexplicably thought Storm would be an uber-stage-name, but damnit, sometimes the eyes staring at me weren’t the most spectacular things I’d ever seen. People didn’t write by number or read by checklist. They wrote with vigour and excitement, they read with pleasure. It was a high-spirited exploration rather than a studious chore. They knew why they were here, and their motivation extended beyond having a good idea for a story, or indulging a battle of pointless imagery or thesauri. The thought of trying to explain that while we don’t have to write about wrestling, our pieces represent the actual battle – we wrestle through our writing – never occurred. It would be akin to a member of a knitting circle having to explain to her peers that they’re more than a club celebrating yarn. That thought now plagues my mind without relent. And believe me, if I have to pick up a knitting needle, it won’t be used for yarn. This isn’t about literary mastery; it’s about literary generalship. At least it used to be. Now I don’t know what we’re about, or why we’re here, or what ludicrously monotonous journey I’ll re-travel when I seek out my next opponent. I don’t know if I’ve declined or if the Universe has sunk around me. I don’t even know if I’m going to finish this. There’s only one thing I know, and I know it for certain. There’s a reason I picked the amnesia gimmick. If this is what we’ve become, I’d rather not remember. ~><>…<><~ It echoed eternally in his head, thudding like the drums of war. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! It wasn’t an accusation with which to argue, but an indisputable perception. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! He’d been hearing it since his first major victory, a hateful cacophony in his head. They’d recognized the quality in him right away, and even if he’d had an inkling to try to hide it, he was never one to shy from the spotlight. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! Accepting the burden gracefully, he did his best to live up to their expectations, to exceed every benchmark. They said that he was the best, unconquerable, and he submitted his life to the cause of making it so. The sheer weight of such responsibility was enough to make his legs quake, doubling him like Atlas, but with a grin he straightened, winking to show he was fine. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! They wanted someone with strength to lead them, but not to show them the way. They wanted someone to hate. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! In public they cheered themselves hoarse, lauding his brilliance, bowing to his supremacy. His eyes and ears were treated to nothing but the best, an unending appreciation of his sacrifice. In private they prayed for him to fall, revelling in the thought of vicariously overcoming their perfect creation. In their heads they chanted. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! Among them, he couldn’t help but chant as well. Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! Wondering all the while on whom they were passing judgement. The untouchable villain, or the sheep that needed him? Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! The insatiable public that craved and created a monster, or the man that ruined himself granting their wish? Asshole! Asshole! Asshole! He knew, and it resonated with the chant. Asshole! It would echo with the revelation that he tried to rig the Wrestler of the Year election. Asshole! It would echo when his back broke from the strain. Asshole! It would echo as he finally walked away.
