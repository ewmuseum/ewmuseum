Date: 2005-12-05 Being The Man That You Are 1 The 2nd of December was a typical yet strikingly beautiful winter day, in the small town of Bundoran. Frost had changed the scene and now it seemed to have that white tinge in all surroundings that let you know it was indeed winter. Blades of grass glistened, doused in morning dew; the tarmac sparkled, a deceptively captivating mantle of ice crowning the toothed surface. All around, an icy wind was cutting the air, sending a sweeping chill into ever corner of the urban jungle. The irony of the situation was that the amber orb of the sun was seen clearly overhead, trailblazing its way through its arc, its blinding light not intimidated by the sub zero scene below. Two perfect contrasts in an imperfect world. This tale starts with a kite. Where it came from or when it escaped its owner, is any man’s guess. That isn’t really important anyway. The plastic patch working of reds, blues and yellows made up the number, cheaply crafted and cheaply designed. But it worked. The way it surfed the waves of the air, showed there was no doubting that. The kite dived as the wind died down temporarily, managed to right itself, then dipped bravely once again before catching a strong breeze and soaring on high once more. It danced over the tops of trees, as it continued on its way down May’s Road in rural Ireland. Rain had fallen earlier yet the little kite had fought through it, David versus Goliath, even as it tapped against its surface urging it too fall. But it had persevered. And now to the present…and here’s your formal warning, the scene isn’t quite as pretty. It is seven O’clock as Patrick O’Shea awakes from his slumber. From underneath the mass of a duvet, a groan is heard as he feels the beginning of a hangover kicking in. He squirmed around in the bed as the blanket twisted and moulded into the shapes his body was creating. He was frantically trying to get back to sleep now before it was too late, too late and he was fully awake. Maybe if he sleeps even another five minute, this hangover might decide to cut him a little slack. But already, he knew he was fighting a losing battle, he could sense his hangover was feeling a little masochistic today. A hangover is a fucker but the Paddy O’Shea kind is a sneaky fucker. Hell, he’s become so associated to waking him up with one, it’s almost become like a friend to him. Not really the friend who always remembers your birthday, more the variety that swears on thirty six bibles he’s got your back, then magically slips into a manhole just when Bruiser One and his mate Bruiser Two, want to pummel your face into intriguing new designs. Okay, maybe it wasn’t a friend at all but at least it greeted him when he woke in the morning. At least they were an indicator that he was still alive. His hangover (singular – it’s the same one every morning, Paddy will stick a needle in his eye to that) tended to pussyfoot around too. He’d (because the Beer God was obviously a man) trick you by giving you a ‘bottom-of-a-bird-cage’-esque mouth, an unquenchable thirst in layman’s terms. You could suck a tap dry but you’d be sick before you’d be satisfied. This phenomenon is to be associated with the gentle hangover and is often bearable because of the knowledge that it sure as hell could have been a lot worse. At this stage though, Paddy’s hangover is only warming up. He’d draw Paddy in, comfort him and even chirp in his ear sometimes. Par exemple: "Hey buddy. Guess what? "PICKLED STOMACH!" SMACK! "LIFELESS ARMS!" Bastard. Only words to describe the Paddy hangover really. Foul, deceitful bastard. Even worse was, once you started to try and combat them, you were too preoccupied to sleep. Let it run its course and before long, you’d find yourself a vomit clad boy. So, what’s the phrase then? Fecked if you do, fecked if you don’t. Yes indeedy, that’s the one. So the point is, Paddy isn’t getting back to sleep. On your feet boy! A strong upsurge of air pulled it back up level with the window and Paddy stood in awe at the kite’s determination. For a fleeting second, he was jealous of it. He was jealous of its simplicity, its freedom. It didn’t have the pressures he felt, it didn’t have responsibility over others or the desire to make a living. It had one task, and that was to stay aloft. So simple…so free. Paddy watched the kite go out of sight then tried to push the feeling away, slightly embarrassed in spite of himself. "Prisoner."
