GTT7 — Roleplay archive

Poison Pawn

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-15 1 “Ladies and gentlemen, I want to introduce you to the lady who is going to shock a lot of people in GTT7. She's undefeated in competition in Southern Impact Championship Wrestling, and is bringing her not inconsiderable talents to a wider audience.” Suzanne Larsson sighed as she waited behind the cheap cardboard hoarding that was serving as a partition to the backstage area. Charlie was clearly in the mood to shill, and even if there were only a handful of journalists there he'd try to wring every last ounce of interest out of them. “She's so quick in the ring, so strong and so lithe that Syko Arsonist will wish he'd stayed retired. He'll be lucky to even get his hands on her! Now, i know some of you think that our federation is some no-talent backwater full of those who either never-were or never-will-be, but this is a real diamond in the rough. Suzanne Larsson is going to surprise a lot of people!” Charlie Larsson stood back and surveyed his audience. He'd given it all he could, but the local reporters didn't seem to care. To them he was just one more stuffed suit spouting forth on a match of only incidental value to the tournament. He ran his hands through his dark thinning hair and tried to at least spark some sort of interaction. “Any questions?” “I got one,” said a man in tracksuit bottoms and a faded t-shirt. “Is it true that your girl's never even faced a man in competition?” “Well,” said Charlie. “Suzanne has not officially fought a man before, but away from our events she regularly spars with them, and believe me when I say that she's more than a match for anything we can throw at her.” “You think that's good enough to set her up to face some of the best wrestlers in the industry?” “Sure I do!” said Charlie. “When you watch Suzanne Larsson wrestle, it's like watching a mythical creature at work. She seems to know exactly where you're moving before you do. She anticipates and twists and turns like lightning. She's a modern day Valkyrie, slaying all those before her. She's...” “I'll take it from here, Charlie.” With those words, Suzanne came out from behind the cheap props and ushered her twin brother away from the microphone. He graciously gave way, letting the assembled few get a good look at her. Suzanne stood around six feet tall, her broad shoulders and overtly muscular physique meaning that she'd only turn heads for people interested in a particular niche market of the porn industry. Her face, too, was more functional than aesthetic; flat, wide nostrils on a round face that could have been pretty if a few thousand dollars and a skilled scalpel were thrown at it. “Syko Arsonist is a man, or at least so I hear. Apparently he was a big shot back in the day, but it's time for him to shuffle his decrepit ass over and let the new breed of the more accomplished athlete take over.” “That's people like you, Jon Rhine, Jared Sykes and the rest?” asked the man. “No my dear,” she replied, with a smile. “That's just me.” 2 “So, how do you think it went?” asked Charlie, jogging to catch up with his sister. “As well as could be expected? As badly as could be expected? Who knows...” “Are you okay?” “Yeah, Charlie. Just not too enthused about giving press conferences to a handful of people who don't care. I thought this was going to be the big time.” Charlie reached up to put a hand on her shoulder. Despite being twins, they looked little alike. Whilst Suzanne reflected their Icelandic roots, Charlie seemed like more a product of the country in which the pair had grown up. His hair was dark brown and receding despite being in his twenties, and whilst he could be called handsome in a certain light he was very self conscious of being several inches shorter than his sister. “Hey, we'll get there. Everyone entered into this thing gets a chance to do a proper press conference. All the people who follow PRIME, SCCW, whoever; they'll get to see you.” “I guess.” “And if they don't pay attention to you before, then they sure will after you smash Arsonist into the ground.” “Yeah, I suppose so.” “I know so. Look at me, Suzanne. What's wrong? You seem distracted.” “No. It's nothing. It's just that...” “Go on.” “I don't like leaving Kyle behind. He gets angry enough at my job already.” “Look Suze, I wouldn't have let you marry him if I didn't think he was a good guy. He'll understand that this is the chance of a lifetime for you. You could end up rich, or famous!” Suzanne nodded, and tried her best to smile. It was hard being a woman in a federation like SICW. Unlike some more progressive, more forward thinking federations she wasn't allowed to wrestle against other men. Even so, she spent enough time with them, practicing, sparring, training, that it took a toll on her husband. His world of wrenches and windshields at the auto repair shop was a world away from her slams and suplexes. He tried not to get annoyed about it, but sometimes these things just bubbled over. “It'll be fine, Suze.” “Okay Charlie. I've gotta head back now though. I'll speak to you tomorrow.” “Okay Suze, tomorrow it is.” The twins crossed the parking lot and entered their separate vehicles. Suzanne looked across at Charlie's beaten up old 70s Chevy truck and waved him past. The bodywork wasn't so great, but underneath Kyle made sure that everything ran just perfectly. As his tail lights disappeared around the corner, Suzanne took stock. Her first match was in less than a week, and she'd so many things left to do beforehand. She had to sort out paperwork, book flights, give a press conference, do other promotional work and, oh yeah... Tell her husband. 3 Suzanne killed the engine and walked to the front door. She was surprisingly light on her feet for someone of her build, and as her keys hovered by the lock and she took a deep breath, she held herself like a dancer. “Good evening, Kyle,” she said, doing all she could to put levity into her voice. There was no response as the white frame of the door slowly clicked shut. Suzanne walked into the living room and saw Kyle's eyes fixed as if by an invisible tether to the game on television. “I'm back honey.” A noncommittal grunt and a wave of the hand were all the greetings she received, until walking back through to the kitchen when there was a cry of, “Can you get me another beer?”. Trying to keep the mood positive, Suzanne complied with the request, grabbing a bottle of Dos Equis from the fridge and walking back to him with it. “How's the game going?” Only a mumble escaped Kyle's lips as his eyes remained glued to the screen and his hand searched blindly behind him for the beer. “Honey, we've got to...” “There's a game on, gimme the damn beer!” “No, we need to talk.” “We can talk at half time.” “When's that?” “Ten minutes.” “I'll be in the kitchen.” Suzanne slammed the beer down on the table next to him and stormed out of the room. She arrived in the kitchen and just sat down, trying to calm her nerves and work out how to tell her husband about the tournament. Kyle was a good man, and most of the time he treated her well, but since the economy had faltered he'd become surly. He didn't like having to rely on an income from his wife, nor that it was one she got from, essentially, fighting. She just couldn't help it though, it was what she loved doing, and deep down he knew that as well as she did. The minutes ticked past, and after fifteen of them, Suzanne went back to speak with the love of her life. “Kyle, I thought you were coming in to talk to me.” “I was, I just need to see some of this recap.” “But then the next quarter will start.” “Yeah, that's how it works. Can this wait?” “No,” she said, forcefully. Finally Kyle looked around at her. His eyes weren't focused properly; the beer in his hand was definitely not the first, or even in the first half dozen. “Don't you talk to me like that, and don't you get in the way of the game.” Suzanne had walked around the couch and was standing between Kyle and the screen. “Kyle, we have to talk. I've got a... a huge opportunity ahead of me. I've been entered into a tournament. It's for PTC, and I've got to go away for a few days, possibly longer.” “You got stuff to do here. You can't go.” “I need to go. This could earn us money, Kyle. It could get me a job in one of the big leagues. I need this break. We need this.” “I'm your husband!” said Kyle, raising his voice for the first time. “Wasn't there some vow you took about obeying me?” “C'mon, be reasonable, honey. This is good for both of us.” “I want you here.” “I've already signed up. I'll be a laughing stock if I drop out!” “Then give up your stupid wrestling and get a proper job!” Suzanne growled in frustration, but bit her tongue from saying anything else. She tried mastering her temper, calming herself down and walking away. Maybe he'd see sense after the game had ended. She reached the doorway and was about to go through it when something sparked in her mind. Some misplaced desire for rebellion, or just to stand up for her own dreams. “I'm going whether you like it or not, and that's the end of it.” Kyle stood up with all the force he could muster. Glass collided with wall to explosive effect as his beer bottle sailed past Suzanne's head. She looked into his eyes, and all she saw was the sum of the anger, frustration and resentment that had been building up for months. When he spoke, it was softly, but in a tone she had never heard him use, and it made her afraid. “I thought I told you not to talk to me like that.” 4 “So champ, you all ready to get on the plane and kick some PTC ass?” Charlie smiled as he opened the car door for his sister, but his face quickly changed expression. “What the fuck happened to you?” “I fell,” said Suzanne, getting out of the car gingerly and moving towards Charlie's truck. “You fell?” “I was being clumsy. It's no big deal.” The SICW Women's Champion tried to brush past her brother, but he moved to block her path. “Charlie, get out of the way. Just drive to the damn airport.” “Suze, what the hell is going on?” “I fell.” “No, you don't just fall. I've never seen you off balance even when you've had a skin full of booze.” The two stood opposite one another, and in Charlie's concerned face you could see the familial resemblance. It was up to Suzanne to break the brief silence. “Shit, Charlie... it's Kyle.” She tried to prevent her body from shuddering, attempted to bottle down on the emotions and the confusion that was spiraling through her mind, but it was impossible. Tears came to her eyes and found their way down her cheeks. Sobs occasionally slipped from her mouth, followed by gasps for air. Charlie held her for a minute, maybe longer, before tilting her head back so he could look into her blackened eyes. “Kyle... Kyle did this?” “He was drunk, and we argued, and I... I'm sorry.” “You're sorry? What?” said Charlie. Suzanne hugged her brother even tighter, despite the bruises. “Oh shit Suze... you didn't kill him, did you?” “What?” Suzanne replied, backing off. “Of course not!” “It's just that you said... oh shit, sis. Why didn't you just stop him?” “I couldn't...” “Of course you could, I mean, you're...” “No, I'm capable, it's just...” she said, between huge gulps of oxygen. “I can't explain.” For once in his life Charlie Larsson was quiet, and just held his sister. Suzanne was grateful for the respite, because she couldn't explain what had happened when Kyle had slowly walked over to her and the thrown a straight right fist into her cheek. It was too much to process. She needed time and space. “Charlie, you can't tell anyone.” “Suzanne, what the fuck? I'm going to go there right now and...” “No!” There was a note of command in her voice. “Charlie, it's complicated.” “He hit you.” “Do you trust me?” “Of course.” “Then listen. We're going to get some make-up and cover this. We're going to get on the plane and fly to Washington. I'm going to heal up, do my promotional interviews and then wrestle the match of my life against Sky Arsonist. I'm going to win, and we're going to live the dream. Do you understand me?” “But...” he started. “No. Just this once, Charlie. Trust me this one time.” “I don't like it,” he said, but by then they both knew he wouldn't tell a soul. 5 Suzanne sat back in her seat on the Embraer 170 and tried to work out what had just happened to her life. Her headphones drowned out the noise of the engines, and also muffled her brother's snoring to some extent. Quite how he could sleep in the cramped cabin, and on such a short flight, was a mystery to her. Unfortunately for Suzanne, her mysteries were multiplying by the minute. She tried to replay the scene in her head over and over. The fist connected and she didn't feel pain. It was strange, like a mix of elation and relief. She went from being afraid to almost being excited in the blink of a black eye. If that had been it, though, she could have written it off as a strange perversion. She'd never been one for getting tied up or whipped, but people have been known to do stranger things. No, it wasn't that. It was the visions that came with it. Kyle's punch landed, and she could see it, as clear as day. She was in the wrestling ring, grappling with Syko Arsonist. He shifted his weight, but she countered and threw him down to the ground. The scene faded out and she just stood there, her head tilted to the side. Thinking that he was being mocked, Kyle launched himself at her and threw a second punch, and then a third. Each time a blow landed, Suzanne saw more. Arsonist was out of breath, panting, but so was she. They had traded many moves and brought the crowd to their feet. They were waiting for the final blow, the coup de grace that would send one of them through to the second round and leave the other a failure. They went back and forth, both trying to set up for their finishing move. The vision faded out, to be replaced by Kyle's snarling visage. His face was contorted as if he were a feral animal, and she could see nothing of humanity in his eyes. The pain had come now, washing away the excitement and replacing it with fear and rage, but Suzanne wanted to see more. She wanted to know how it would end, or how it could end. Steeling herself for another strike, she let Kyle spell his fury out across her face in burst blood vessels and swollen tissue. And she saw it all, saw what she needed to know. 6 Two days later and the best part of 3000 miles away, a bear of a man was walking through his family home. His hair was unruly and his white beard hung down over his barrel chest, but this was no St Nicholas. The man looked bitter, perhaps at how time had robbed him of some vitality and seen fit to give him wisdom only when he was incapable of using it. He had clearly seen many years, and his demeanor was not pleasant, yet he walked with purpose towards the small television left on by his sister's grandchildren. “Bloody children,” he cursed softly in Icelandic. The man, Gunnar Arnisson, glanced briefly at the screen, which was showing a carnival of sorts. Pathetic American garbage for the brain. Gunnar lamented the youth of his country. No longer did they yearn to be strong, to work the sea and the land, but instead they wanted to be just like the people on the television. Nobody read the stories and legends any more. Names like Grettir the Strong and Egil Skallagrimsson meant nothing to these people who would one day oversee the country. No, instead Iceland would turn to Pokemon and Power Rangers for salvation. They would turn to the decadence of the American ways of greed and profiteering. Even now, after the utter collapse of the Icelandic banking industry, they would set their sights on the Dow Jones, or even on the Hang Seng and the FTSE. The old times were passing, and even now people were trying to shy away from the patronymic naming conventions. Gunnar blamed a lot of this on the ambitions of the previous generation, of his own children. They had been the ones to try and make Iceland known around the world. They had invited the two global superpowers, America and Russia, to the table. And across such a table made of sixty four alternating squares of white and black, a battle of sorts was waged. To many, the 1972 World Chess Championship was a distillation of the Cold War. To others it was an event that put Iceland on the map. For Gunnar, it was the start of the decay, the loss of everything that his people should have held dear, and the greatest theft he had ever witnessed. He lifted the remote control to change the channel, as the costumed clowns, or wrestlers as they were apparently called, were causing him pain. “And now we're going to hear a few words from one of the little known entrants to GTT7, Suzanne Larsson!” The host's voice had floated past Gunnar's ears unheard, until the last two syllables. Even so, he only gave the screen a cursory glance, until he saw what was on it. His mouth open, his heart racing for the first time in years, he grabbed the phone from the nearby table and reached desperately for the keys. Such was his fervor that it took Gunnar four attempts to hit the correct buttons. Each ring lasted a lifetime, until the receiver was picked up. “Hello?” “Lars, switch to SkjarEinn!” “What? Is this you, Gunnar Arnisson?” “Lars, turn on your television. Now.” Despite it being against their usual social dynamic, Lars did as he was requested. “What am I looking for?” “You'll see.” “Is that...” “Yes.” “After all these years?” “Yes, Lars Halldorsson. We've found them. The final two.”
