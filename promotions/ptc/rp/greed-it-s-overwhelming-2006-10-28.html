Greed… It's Overwhelming.

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 Greed. It had overwhelmed him the moment he had seen the briefcase full of money. He ignored his love for the sport, ignored the opportunity that had been granted to him, and snatched the briefcase from Bradley Stevenson's desk in an instant. It wasn't too much to ask. Lay down and lose… get half a million dollars. After years of never letting anything stand in his way of what he loved, he had succumbed to the green monster. Even if only for a moment before he had regained his senses, and no one would ever know, Sammy Brown had embarrassed himself. He had proven that he could make mistakes, but anyone could make a mistake. It was what the mistake cost you that counted. So as Sammy stood there in the small phone booth waiting for the damn dog walker, of all people, to come to the phone, he couldn't help to think how much this one was going to cost. This was not going to be some slap on the wrist, according to Stevenson; it would cost someone a large amount of money. Someone who was not going to be too pleased with the outcome of Brown's match… the one he was supposed to lose. "This is Robbie." The voice of Robert Stinson drew his attention away from the horrid thoughts that had begun to etch themselves into his mind. "Hey Robbie, this is Sammy, Jiara mentioned to me that you were with Stevenson last night." "Yeah," came the nervous response, "but he left with his wife and I haven't seen them since." Brown nodded along as he stood there, listening to the younger man on the other end of the line. "That's fine, Robbie," Sammy had the pay phone receiver pressed against his ear, his other arm was pressed up against the glass in the small coffin. "Look, I don't know what is going on, and I don't want you to worry about anything… but I need you to call the police and let them know that something could be wrong." "Wrong–" Robbie's voice began to falter, as if something were stuck in his throat. "But I– Nah, he's just hung over or something. Isabel was pretty pissed off when I called her." He slammed his palm against the glass, a few of the people walking past jumped in surprise. "Listen, Robbie," His breathing increased, while his nose flared with each exhale. "There's something going on right now that you don't know about – something you don't want to know about – and I need you to do me a favor and call the cops before it's too late." A few more heads turned in his direction as he spoke in a tense tone, but in a crowded airport where not many people understood what he was saying anyway, he was pretty much ignored. "I know all of this sounds weird, considering I'm not even in the States, but you need to trust me on this and just do what I asked." He tried to lower his tone, to no avail. "I can't tell you why over the phone, I just know something bad happened… I can feel it." "But I have Mrs. Stevenson's car," Robbie explained. "What if they think I have something to do with it?" "Would you rather wait until they do find out something is wrong, then have to explain to them why you have her car and never let them know that something was wrong?" "No, I guess you're right," was the response Sammy heard. "I'll call them right now, Sammy. I just hope you're not jumping to conclusions." Sammy placed the receiver back on it's base, pausing slightly as he thought about the last things Bradley Stevenson had said to him when he told him he could not follow through. "You can't do that, Sammy. I already placed the bet, and the guy we're dealing with isn't going to be happy if he loses. They'll kill us, Sammy… They'll kill our families." He shuddered to think about it being true, that someone would be so heartless that they would resort to harming a man's family for something they had no part in. It happened though, and with Stevenson and his wife missing, the guilt not only settled within him… It brought a suitcase. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ He stirred slowly, in stages, he had no clue as to what was going on except for two things, first, he was lying face first on the floor and, second, he felt horrible. Slowly, gradually, the muddiness began to fuse into more manageable perceptions. The air that brushed against his skin was cool but moist, the buoyant and shifty breeze carried scents that he was unfamiliar with. The firm surface beneath him resembled that of a mattress; the dryness of his mouth and tingling of his joints lead him to believe that he had been here for quite some time. Wherever here was. It did not take long for the significance of that to permeate through the haze clouding his mind. Where was he? How long had he been here? Where was Isabel? It came to him in short quick bursts, as he attempted to ignore the many thoughts of what they may have done to her that tried to embed themselves in the back corner of his mind. He struggled to open his eyes, blinking, as sunlight drifted across his face. He managed to lift his head from the sweat drenched sheet, then opened his eyes as soon as he turned away from the rays that danced across his nose. It took great effort to lift his body up off the bed, his arms barely withstood the strain. Still, he had managed to get up to a seated position as he slowly began to regain control of his body movements. His bare feet hardly touched the ground as they draped over the edge, his body slumped over to get a better view as he wiggled his toes. He lifted his head, trying to gain a bit more knowledge of his environment, as he thought of a way to use it to his advantage. Unfortunately, he ran out of time. The door of the room creaked open, a large hand the size of a small ham was wrapped around the golden knob. "Don DeAngelis will see ya now." From what Bradley could tell, if his memory served him right, this was the man that had carried his wife away. He was dressed in yet another custom tailored suit, this one an off white – almost vanilla – tone, and the sleeves reached just over his wrists; which happened to dangle somewhere around the lower part of his thigh. Getting a better look of the man, he could see that his shoulders were almost as broad as his height, with arms that hung straight down at his side and legs as thick as tree trunks. It almost looked as if he had cornrows, the way the wrinkles of his skin seemed to start above his brow and travel the length of his head toward his neck. "Hurry up," the man grunted, his voice coarse and choppy to go along with his primitive physique. Bradley stood, his knees buckling as he still tried to regain his composure. He was uncertain if he was hung over from the bottle of vodka he had attempted to drown his problems away with, or if he had somehow been drugged. The last thing he remembered from the night before, was being shoved into the back of a long black Lincoln. His wife was silent, tiny spiders of tears trickling down from her eyes. Then everything went black. A hand slowly wrapped around his arm, fingers like sausages almost stopped the blood flow from continuing through it's routine journey. "I doubt you want me to drag you, so get to moving" He shoved Stevenson in the direction of the door, which he barely missed colliding with as he stumbled to his side. "Where's Isabel," Bradley asked, "Where's my wife?" He turned to face the brick wall that skulked behind him, but a sneer turned his attention back on what was in front of him. "You don't have to worry bout that," came the hollow reply. "She's being well taken care of." The reply did nothing to soothe his fear as he marched along, uncertain of the fate that awaited him. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ Another event, another flight. With everything going on, Sammy felt a bit selfish as he snapped his seatbelt shut. How could he be on his way to Portugal, when the lives of two people could be in danger. Could this really mean more to him than another man's life? No, that wasn't it. What it was, was something he had hoped for since the first day he had stepped onto the slightly giving canvas, the first time he had leaned back against the unforgiving steel braided wires that surrounded the ring. It was to finally have someone take notice of him, and it did not matter much to him that it had taken this long. Until now, he had not felt that fire that burned deep within his gut, driving him to damn near obsession of pulling this out. Of winning when no one had paid him a second glance when he walked through the doors. 'This guy's too old,' was what they probably said, snickering amongst themselves about how his pacemaker may stop in the ring and one of them could add it to their list of accomplishments. It was the way of the business, something that Sammy had never had a full grasp upon. He never felt that killer instinct, he was never a blue chipper that things came naturally to. He had to work hard to even enjoy the mediocre success that he had accomplished. He had never won any championships, but he made people look good in his near decisions that kept the fans teetering on the edge of their seats. That was what this business was to him. It was all about the entertainment, pleasing those that spend their hard earned wages to show up every other week to one of their hometown events. It wasn't like the big time productions, where people only worried about whether or not the bookers were taking notice of their success. In Sammy's life, there were no advertisements, other than a sheet of poster board stapled to a memo board in a local gym. Those that knew of the event knew of it because they were there the week prior and had enjoyed themselves enough to drive a few miles to see what would happen next. That was the thrill of the business, and up until now, Sammy had enjoyed it. This though, this tournament, it had sparked a fire within him that he could not explain. For the first time, in a long time, he actually believed in himself. He did not know much about Easton Hall, but he understood that in this environment: it wasn't about what your name was. He stood on equal footing with forty eight other competitors, even those that were favored to win the entire thing, because most of them had lost that hunger – that desire – that had lifted them into prominence many times before. They were expected to win, but the non-factor that no one spoke of always seemed to come out of nowhere. Angelo Deville and Doctor Curiosity were not favored to win when they did, and Sammy Brown knew he stood as much of a chance than they did. All he had to do was believe. "Would you like a pillow, sir?" Just when he was about to drift to sleep, the flight attendant decided to make her presence known. It was always like that, as if they all huddled together and watched as the passengers became comfortable, then disrupted their peace behind a mask of courtesy and pleasantness. "No thank you," he responded, a courteous smile slapped on his otherwise grimacing features. "Could I get you something to drink," she motioned toward a drink cart that she pushed along the aisle. "Perhaps coffee or some juice?" He waved his hand to dismiss her, "No thank you, I'll be fine." She smiled and started on her way when he called her back informally. "Oh, and would you mind if no one disturbed me. It's been a long night and I would really like to get some rest before we arrive in Portugal." "Why of course," she nodded politely, a painted on smile of bright red lipstick drawing attention to the tiny dimples at the corners of her lips. Somehow, he doubted it was going to happen. And as much as he wanted to get some rest, her interruption had broken his focus. The thought of Bradley Stevenson and his wife being in danger taunting him from somewhere in the back of his mind. It was going to be a long flight. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ He had been led to a large dining area, the far wall was glass that gave a breathtaking view of the city's skyline. Don DeAngelis was already seated at thehead of the long, intimidating, table. Bowls of fruit were set in the center of the table – bright green apples, deep burgundy cherries, and the scent of freshly sliced cantaloupe drew his attention away from the man who sat with a crooked smile waving for him to take a seat at his side. The gorilla that stalked him on his walk to the meeting, closed the door behind him as he left. Bradley would have to be a fool to consider threatening the don, especially with no knowledge of where his wife was. It was safe to leave them alone. Bradley took his seat to the left of the don, pushing the plate setting on his table a few inches away from him. "Where's my wife?" "Right now, Mr. Stevenson," his smile vanished, and a look that striked fear in most men made Bradley slink back into his chair. "I think you should be more worried about yourself, because your wife is perfectly safe at the moment." He pushed the plate back in front of Stevenson, then motioned toward the fruit. "Now please, eat something so I don't feel uncomfortable." So he won't feel uncomfortable, he thought as he reached over for an apple and a paring knife. He could slit his throat, stop any chance of of him calling out to his men. It was possible, but highly unlikely. He wouldn't get far, someone was probably standing outside the door. Not to mention the fact that he had no clue as to where his wife was, she would be dead as soon as he opened the door. "You want to kill me, don't you?" Stevenson turned his attention to the don, who stared at him intently… as he tried to get a feel for what was runing through the man's mind. "No," Stevenson replied, shaking his head as he began to slice a piece of the apple's skin to get to the meat within. "It's fine if you are," the don said with a chuckle. "Many men have had that thought cross their mind, sitting there where you are right now." "You're going to kill me, aren't you?" The don shook his head. "No, Mr. Stevenson. You see, you might be valuable to me still." Stevenson lifted his head away from the fruit, surprised by the response. "What I want you to do sir, is tell me everything you know about this Sammy Brown." "But I–" He was cut off by the hand of Don DeAngelis. "The man turned his back on half a million dollars, Mr. Stevenson." The grin once again returned to the man's face. "He wants to win, and that, my friend, could make us a lot more money than we could have ever hoped." Stevenson became drunk with greed once more, DeAngelis had said 'us'. "Now, please, indulge me." Stevenson began to tell him everything he knew about Brown, and the welfare of his wife was replaced with the thought of money falling from the sky. Greed… It truly did overwhelm.
