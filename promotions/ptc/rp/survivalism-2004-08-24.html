Survivalism.

24 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-24 [The scene opens on a sound stage somewhere within the friendly confines of Toronto, Ontario. Toronto is known as “Hollywood North”, on account of how many movies are filmed there. Today finds us on the set of a public service announcement. Against a dark backdrop, NGW American Champ Dallas Reeves stands proudly. His long blond hair is tied back beneath a tan cowboy hat, and his seemingly ever-present smile is on his lips. Slung over his left shoulder is his prize title belt, and it drapes down in front of the blue Toronto Maple Leafs jersey he has on. We are just catching the end of what seems to be a television advert, as a skeleton crew films.] Dallas Reeves: And that, my friends, is just about all you need to know about hockey…for today. Until next time, keep your stick on the ice. [From the sidelines, the floor director barks away.] Floor director: Okay…hold that pose…annnd…cut! Perfect. That’s a wrap for today, folks. [The studio lights come up as a buzzer goes, signifying the end of another busy and productive day. The floor director shakes Reeves’ hand as an assistant passes the wrestler/actor a purple towel and a small bottle of water. The grips and stagehands enter to begin disassembling the set, as the camera operators begin their clean up rituals as well. Reeves pauses to shake hands with some of the crew, joking with a few others. He hands his title off to a stylish looking gentleman in his 50s. This man has a bushy gray mustache above his lip, a charcoal suit with bolo tie, and a white cowboy hat. This man is “Alberta” Jack B. Carrington, Reeves’ manager and close family friend.] Reeves: Thanks, J.B. Great work everybody! [Reeves steps his athletic frame towards the camera that was filming the behind-the-scenes action. He makes eye contact with the lens and smiles whole-heartedly.] Reeves: Hi, I’m wrestler/actor Dallas Reeves. If you haven’t caught my heroic exploits in the NGW, such as holding the American Title longer than any other individual in the history of the title, perhaps you saw me make my North American film debut in this summer’s comedic spoof, “Braveheart 2: Brave Harder”, as MacDervish the Glasgow Kisser. Incidentally, the film will be available on DVD and VHS just in time for the festive shopping season, so pre-order yours today. [“Alberta” Jack Carrington nods in agreement, and follows Reeves through the studio complex. From the non-descript set of the PSA, Reeves makes his way out the door to the hallway, with various workers going about their business.] Reeves: Perhaps that was a shameless plug, but it’s part of my job. If I want to continue on making my way through the film industry, I have to do these types of things to ensure myself a bright future, what with international sales, um, rental receipts…help me out here, J.B. Carrington: Tha betteh yo’ film debut pehfo’ms, from a totally fiscal poin’ a view ah course, tha mo’ likely y’ are ta land mo’ betteh jobs. [Reeves smiles at the camera as Carrington speaks in his western drawl. Reeves speaks directly to the audience in a hushed tone.] Reeves: You’ll have to bear with ol’ “Alberta” Jack there…he may talk awfully weird, but he knows what he’s talking about. He’s a good man, just, not quite right in the mouth. As my manager/agent, he advises me on many different aspects of my career. If I want to survive, I should listen to him. And I do, so I do. [From the hallway, Reeves veers right and enters a doorway to another room. The scene cuts to that of a World War II battle re-enactment. Reeves, with Carrington still in tow, crosses a battle-ravaged meadow, passing several men dressed as Allies, each of whom looks confused as to the presence of the duo. A hand grenade is thrown towards them, followed by something warbled by an evil Nazi, as if there’s any other kind, but is caught by Reeves. In one fluid motion, Reeves tosses the grenade from whence it came. An explosion occurs, as well as jubilation from the Allies. Pieces of exploded Nazi rain down from the sky.] Reeves: Every day that I get out of bed…which is every day, by the way…every day is a constant battle for survival. If I slip up in the ring at any time, I could lose my American Title. If I slip up in auditions, I could lose my part. But the battle my teammates and I have in store for us is one where we can only gain. The PTC Survival match, against such dangerous forces as the XUW, the UWF and GW…a night where the NGW can finally shed it’s tarnished image from years gone by. But that, that’s a story for another day. C’mon J.B., these guys have this under control now. Besides, those Krauts make me want to wretch. Let’s find a place a little less…20th Century. This hits a little close to home. [The camera stops in the meadow, as the Allied forces begin to pursue the remnants of the left-over Nazi contingent across the field. It seems that Reeves has inadvertently saved the day. Reeves and Carrington walk off screen, and we are magically transported to a north African bazaar, the street abuzz with folks dressed in colourful flowing robes, with buskers hustling goods. Horses and camels whinny from their corrals, and Reeves and Carrington stroll non-chalantly down the middle of the road. Without warning, a gaggle of lecherous looking Arabs fan out in front, each cracking their knuckles, eyes fiercely darting towards the two white men, who easily stand out from the regular bazaar crowd. Reeves stops at a little shop to peruse the goods for sale as the five Arabs slowly advance on them. Reeves haggles with the retailer over a dusty lantern as Carrington notices the motley crew.] Reeves: Thirteen dinars for this lousy lamp? Retailer: You no like for thirteen? Is good lamp! Shines like stream of bat piss at night! It gold while everything around is black! Reeves: Well, I have tract lights in my kitchen at home… Retailer: Tract lighting? Fine, for you…a friendly face…ten dinars. No more, no less! Reeves: You got yourself a deal, kemosabe. [Reeves turns his head aside to whisper to Carrington, who is shakingly staring down the five men.] Reeves: I like this place. A little heavy on the stereotypes though. All we need now are a bunch of ruffians who don’t like white folk in these parts. Carrington: Uh, Dallas…ya might wanna tu’n yaself ‘round… [Reeves fishes in his pocket and pulls out a crisp ten dinar bill, and hands it to the shopkeeper, who smiles graciously. He hands over the lamp.] Reeves: I’ll take it, but I want you to know that you are robbing me. Robbing me blind. Ten dinars for a musty old lamp. Look at this dust all over it… [Reeves rubs the sleeve of his jersey over the lamp a few times, then tosses it over his shoulder. It lands at the feet of the would-be aggressors. As it lands, a puff of blue smoke oozes out. Miraculously enough, the hazy form of a genie, looking remarkably like the one from Disney’s “Aladdin”, appears, turning towards Reeves, who still has his attention to the shopkeeper. Carrington squints, then rubs his eyes in disbelief.] Reeves: There. Ten dinars for a piece of junk. I basically paid you so I could have the task of throwing it away. Honestly…sometimes, sometimes I just wish that all those who wished to cause me harm would be sent far away. C’mon, J.B., we’re outta here. [The genie blinks and nods its head. Another puff of blue smoke follows, this one engulfing the five angered Arabs. When the smoke quickly clears, the five are nowhere to be seen. The shopkeeper stares at the genie, with fear in his eye. All of this has occurred without Reeves noticing, so he and Carrington walk off screen, and the scene then cuts to that of a coliseum floor, circa Rome 50 A.D. Warriors dressed in furs, steel and leather get-ups circle each other with bizarre, and not to mention sharp, weapons much to the delight of the tens of thousands in attendance. Dallas Reeves stands in the middle of the coliseum floor, his arms outstretched. He wrestles for this emotion, the pure thrill and emotion that it can bring about in people. Carrington stands nervously at his side, his left hand on his white cowboy hat, surveying the situation.] Reeves: Now this is more like it! Can you feel it, J.B.? The people, here to cheer on their champions…to heap insults and curses on their enemies and those who would stand in their champions’ ways…to witness the best of the best in a no holds barred ultimate slaughter match where only the strongest survive! Carrington: “No holds ba’ed ultimate slaughteh match?” Reeves: Yeah, man! Dig this crazy scene! Carrington: Consideh it dug, Dallas. Reeves: I think I’ve begun to wander off track. What I meant to mean was…survival is about a lot more than merely surviving. Survival is surviving, thriving, and loving every minute of it. Carrington: Well nah, Dallas…actually, suhvivin’ is mereleh getting’ by. Thrivin’ and lovin’ are thangs people strive fo’ in life. Suhvivin’ helps us ta thrive an’ love ev’ry minute of it. [The gladiators begin to do battle, with sword meeting sword, battle-axe meeting flailing limb, and pikestaff meeting retina all around. The enthusiastic crowd loves the carnage, as bodies, and body parts, start hitting the dirt in a crimson spectacle. Reeves and Carrington saunter through this chaotic revelry, still untouched by the danger all around.] Reeves: I’m not going to get into a battle of semantics with you, J.B. Partly because half the time I can’t understand what you’re saying anyways. Honestly, why do you have an oddly Texan accent if you are from Alberta. [Carrington glares at Reeves, but Reeves smiles. By this time, there are only a few gladiators left standing, the rest dead, dying, or trying to recover any pieces lost in the melee. A mighty chorus of trumpets bursts out, signifying the release of the lions onto the field.] Reeves: And you keep taking me away from my train of thought. The point is…you can’t survive by just pure luck. You can’t survive by just wishing it so. If you plan on surviving…you have got to fight tooth and nail, using whatever you can, to ensure yourself a lengthy and prosperous future. Look at my friend Russell Crowe here. [Reeves pulls a gladiator aside, and sure enough, it’s Russell Crowe, dressed as he appears in the film “Gladiator”.] Russell Crowe: Hi kids, I’m Russell Crowe. You may remember me from such movies as “Gladia- [Before he can finish another syllable, a large tan lion leaps for his throat, and Russell Crowe goes down in a heap.] Reeves: Guess I won’t be waiting for that sequel. Carrington: De-ah lo’d… [The lion sticks its head up, licking the red at its chops. It looks at Reeves, who gingerly extends his hand and begins petting the wild beast on its head. The lion begins to purr loudly. Reeves turns to Carrington.] Reeves: One man’s ferocious man-eater, another man’s overgrown pussycat. Taming the elements, J.B. That’s another piece of what I like to call “Survivalism”. [The lion trots off for more death, while Reeves and Carrington continue on their merry way as well. Once they get off-screen, the scene cuts to the front door of a large, pretty wealthy looking abode in a ritzy section of Los Angeles. Reeves and Carrington are both looking dapper in their black tuxedoes, Carrington with his white cowboy hat on, Reeves allowing his curly blonde locks to hang down past his shoulders. Carrington adjusts Reeves’ bowtie for him.] Reeves: So here I am, J.B. I’ve done a hell of a lot of surviving throughout my rasslin’ years. I’ve also done my fair share of thriving, as well. [“Eye of the Tiger” by Survivor begins to play out of left field. The door opens up from within, and the camera zooms into an elegant party in progress. There are tuxedoes everywhere…inter-mixed with a delicious assortment of beautiful women in lovely dressing gowns, bikinis, and little French maid outfits with bunny ears and cotton tails. A long table filled with hors d’oeuvres stands in the centre of the room, complete with a giant ice sculpture.] Reeves: And this is the part, J.B., this is the part where I’m lovin’ every minute of it. Hey, Hef! [A smallish, older gentleman in a purple smoking jacket and slacks turns around, about twenty feet away from Reeves and Carrington. His eyes widen as he removes the pipe from his mouth. Sure enough, it’s Hugh Hefner, and he appears happy to see Mr. Reeves.] Hugh Hefner: Why, Dallas Reeves! So good of you to join us once again! [As Hef makes his way over, Carrington pulls Reeves aside.] Carrington: Waitaminute nah, Dallas…ya still ‘aven’t said what ya plan ta do in tha ring against those otheh fellas from tha PTC. Whatta you and yo’ fella NGDubbyehs plan on doin’ ta suhvive? Reeves: It’s easy, J.B. On my signal, we’re gonna unleash hell, and show the entire lot of Primetime Central what the NGW is all about. [Hef and Reeves’ hands meet in a friendly handshake, and Hef pats him on the back. The voices and sounds of the party trail off as “Eye of the Tiger” by Survivor gets louder. The camera fades to black, the words “’Hollywood North’ Dallas Reeves: NGW’s Finest” appearing in gold lettering. This promo has been released through Dudesy Productions. MMIV)]
