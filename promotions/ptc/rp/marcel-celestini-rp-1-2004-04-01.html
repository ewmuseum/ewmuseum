Marcel Celestini rp 1

1 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-01 There is a definite curve, you know, when you’re world begins to fall away at the seams. It’s just one of those things that you don’t want to think about until you are actually in the middle of it. Even then, when your world tears itself apart around you, you can do nothing but try and ignore it. It’s fine at the beginning, the odd small problem that really doesn’t matter. You spend all day reporting a minor crime and come away with nothing better than your average, run of the mill, middle page story. You think nothing of it. There’s your problem. That’s the beginning of the curve, you see, the start of the downfall. Soon you find bricks through your windows, bullets in the areas surrounding your head and your daily death threat lying on the floor on your hallway. Well that’s what happened to me anyway. I am Marcel Fabien Celestini, native of Lyon, France. I have been the blue-collar guy for my entire life, working off the debts I built up as a student in England. It’s a life, nothing special, but enough to get me through. Now I cannot leave the house without expecting to be shot at. That’s what you get for trying to live an honest life these days, you know. All the money is in crime these days, and a lot of it finds the pockets of the local plods too. Just enough for them to not take any of your reported incidents seriously. I may seem a little bitter, and I apologise, but that is what happens when you have been cooped up in the same little flat for the last couple of years. I try and work freelance now, write a few feature articles for the odd magazine. Reporting went down the drain, incidentally the same day all of this started. Strangely, I have always tried to avoid the toes of those who will come back to haunt me, but I still end up here. Why? Who knows? I’ve been there so many millions of times and trod the same details over and over, pawing at each one in a vague attempt to make sense of any of it. I suppose I ought to let you know what I am badgering on about. Its one of those things that you cant forget about, even during sleep. They wont let me forget them. Like I said, I was a reporter and rising quickly, some will say. I worked for some time gathering small details, which I would eventually turn, into some mid-magazine article. I never had much going for me at home, so I buried myself in work, another thing I regret so sharply. But anyway, I’d spent a hard weekend snooping and digging away at things that turned into nothing but yet another waste of my less than valuable time. It seemed like another normal day. The sun was bright in the sky; little in the way of cloud was there to block the rays. There was a slight breeze, easterly I think, which twitched at the branches of the tree’s lining the street. The Rue du Catagne was rarely so quiet at four fifteen on a Friday afternoon, and that struck me. Something was odd and I had no reason to suspect. Maybe it was journalistic intrigue that drew me onwards, past the coffee shop on my right, which I usually stopped at for a quick recap with a cappuccino. Breaking routine was hard for me and I remember the feeling well as I passed by and turned down Chasten Boulevard, a small side alley which cut a fair few minutes from my journey. Here it was that I began to reflect a little on the day’s events. The shade created by the steep brick walls on either side of me seemed calming, almost therapeutic as I wandered on quietly. I had spent most of the day in a field, coating the knees of my old Lee jeans in a sticky clay like mud as I knelt to look at the findings. A harvester had turned up a few old bones and it had been my duty to report on it. There was nothing too much to say. They looked like a few more of the missing dead of the Great War that had been shoved in a shallow grave without time for a proper burial. There was nothing to suggest otherwise, anyway, so I just left it at that. I had a nice piece in mind and I had begun to write it mentally as I headed back towards my flat. The details may seem a little stark or fresh, but when you consider two years of having nothing to reflect on but the events that lead to how you became stuck in an apartment with the threat of being shot at when leaving over your head then you find you have a lot of time to think. But anyway, this was where things started to happen. I emerged from the alley and moved out onto another bright, tree-lined street. I forget the name. I had wasted a day and my mind had begun to dwell on the waste of time. It was very depressing and I could have done with going out for a drink. That’s when I saw Jean. Jean was a good friend of mine; he worked in a pub as a barman a few streets from my flat. He was in a hurry and his chubby figure waddled away at some speed. “Jean!” I shouted, obviously in French, to my counterpart who had not been fortunate to undertake the university education in England that I had. “Why the rush, big man?” He didn’t turn, or even acknowledge me in any way. I noted this down in my mental book of things to bring up at a later date and carried on with the walk home. Jean had been the only person I had seen on my walk that night, and it had confused me little at the time and greatly after two years of further analysis. I watched him waddle off until I turned in the opposite direction to him and we went our own separate ways. Kefffut. Something struck me in the shoulder; rather a bullet from a silenced gun had grazed the skin and torn my shirt. A stream of crimson ran down my arm and I panicked. Its not surprising and I would have still done the same thing now if the situation had arisen again. I sprinted at a rate of speed that truly backed up the theories of adrenaline. My direction was unclear, as the nausea created by the shock had began to settle across my body. I must have staggered for some time. I remember my Dictaphone crashing from my breast pocket and shattering on impact with the cold, hard concrete. I wasn’t thinking, I know that for sure, and even if I was I’m sure the same old pejorative would have been circulating my head like a buzzard. My thought had slowly begun to return and I quickly dived over a four-foot hedge onto a soft bed of uncut grass. It was no cover, I had seen enough thrillers to know that I needed to be as far away as possible, but I needed somewhere to rest my pulsating heart. The pain in my shoulder was biting, and it tore into my neck and chest. The warm flow of a sweat, sticky blood coated my thin white shirt, sticking it tightly to the wound. For some moronic reason I tried to un-attach the shirt from the coagulating blood to no affect but another stabbing, wrenching pain. I gasped loudly, too loudly, and I heard the echoing footsteps belting their way down the deserted suburban streets. My wits, influenced by a thousand nights in with a six pack of beer and a rented thriller film, urged me to move towards the house who’s garden I had planted myself in. there was a small gap between the house and a single standing garage that was shaded by a small corrugated iron roof. From what I could see at the time there was no back yard, only a drop to a lower alley. The garden sloped up steeply, blowing any idea of cover I could have had. I dragged myself, cursing silently, to my feet and sped up the small driveway and ducked as nimbly into the shade as I could. The faster I was running, the less my shoulder seemed to hurt. The pumping of my legs and the beating of my heart seemed a distraction and I carried on running until I stood on a nine-foot wall overlooking a concrete flagstone paved alley. My mind stopped working. The way I saw it at the time, I could have chosen one of two options. The first was to lower myself down. The only problem to this was my one working arm. There was no chance I could have held my weight, however small, on one arm, correction, my left arm. A right handed person who was never strong as it was could not hold himself on a left arm whilst stifling a yell as his stronger limb slowly screamed itself to death. I had to jump. There were four bags of litter that had been piled below me, just to my right. I could have impaled myself or slashed myself to death on the contents, it could have been anything, but I couldn’t go back. I wrapped my arm across my chest and held it tightly to myself, throwing my legs out horizontally and jumping slightly to the right. It seemed like an eternity until my body hit down with a sickening thud that set a neighbourhood dog off with a torrent of screaming barks. My legs had cracked down onto the concrete whilst my body landed relatively softly on the bags of household rubbish. My blood had splattered itself down the flagstones and I lied still, tending to my new leg wounds whilst listening intently. Then the footsteps came closer. I couldn’t stand; I knew that. My ankle had been shattered by the fall and I was in too much pain to crawl, let alone stand. I quickly threw a couple of the bags over me and curled my body into as much as a foetal position as I could muster. The wall provided enough natural shade to hide me if there wasn’t someone looking too hard, but there was a man with a gun standing practically above me. The footsteps stopped, there was an audible tut, and then they began to retreat. Not how I wanted to end the day, really.
