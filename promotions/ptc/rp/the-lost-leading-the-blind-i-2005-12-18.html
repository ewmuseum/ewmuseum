The Lost Leading the Blind: I

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 Dear Mr. Christian Beckett, I can help you. Few know the afflictions wrought by plumbing the forbidden, and the payment often exacted upon the feeble mind of man. Some call it madness, some label it enlightenment. Often, it is both; to stumble upon such alarming knowledge is always a double-edged sword. As such, you have two choices, and I will outline both of them after I offer you an explanation. I have petitioned the commission overseeing your confinement and offered to keep you under my care, for a rather substantial 'fee' paid to the board members. They have readily agreed to my request and as such you shall be released immediately into my care. I will have to report to them on occasion to assure them of your stability, but it's a small price to pay to me. Besides, men of such diluted morals will ask so few questions when they check their bank accounts. I'm sure you're asking yourself why a man you've never met is intervening in your affairs, offering you asylum from the medical officials seeking to keep your mind sedated and words silenced. To answer your question, it's simply because you've been wronged. I, too, can attest to the anguish of being forgotten and dismissed by society. I discovered your case file and thought you would be a good man worth helping, an unwilling martyr bound and gagged by the powerful out of fear of the things he would say if given the chance. Fortunately for those like us, those same men of power will turn a blind eye for the right price and extinguish their convictions. They will betray their own alliances and sacrifice their partners to the wolves out of greed. If you chose to go public with the things you know, Malcolm Beckett, the men who held you in confinement will now disavow ever holding you in their 'care'. I have already seen to that. You are free again, granted you still exercise a monicum of caution. I would like to tell you I do this sheerly as a humanitarian who feels remorse for his fellow man, but I do ask a few simple favors of you duirng this period while you're under my 'supervision'. Things will be explained in due time, for I fear I must bring this to a close, but I ask that you read this through to the end before making your decision. Shortly after you receive this letter, you will find yourself released from your cell. An escort will show you to a car waiting beyond the gates. Follow and we shall meet soon. At that time will I give you these simple tasks to perform in my stead, errands I cannot personally see to as I have many obligations to fulfill in a short time. You also have the option of ignoring my charity and remaining within those walls. There you will have safety and the necessary care to live, but at the same time it may be a long time before you once again find yourself able to walk the Earth without an armed guard eyeing your every step. To help me is to risk many things and I will not condemn you for rejecting my offer for the shelter of your current situation, mundane as it may be. This is not the Mafia, but it is also not the Salvation Army. We work for a good cause but there may be decisions you may not be comfortable making. If you opt to decline, merely tell the attendants so as they unlock your door. If so, this exchange never happened. Think hard and think quickly. There is no going back regardless of your decision. I hope to see you soon, Mr. Beckett. I believe your expertise will aid me immensely and my own dealings will reward you as well. I'm certain it will be a mutually beneficial enterprise. We both stand so much to gain. Sincerely, B. Chaney For the first time in weeks, Chris Beckett found himself a moment of clarity. Hope flooded into his mind and a smile crept across his face; the relieved grin of a genuinely happy man, not that of an unbalanced inmate of a mental institution. He found himself perusing the script several times, finding it somewhat abnormal that such a thing in this day and age was scribed in the flowing longhand of a man that, judging by the care of the writing, apparently preferred such a method over word processors and typewriters. In light of the gray walls he'd been staring and often screaming at for weeks now, a tinge of hesitance crept into his thoughts. His faceless benefactor had gone to great length to inform him of the risks involved in joining his camp. Not that Beckett had never expected the cost of such aid being a faith-spreading mission to the inner city or a teary-eyed testament to elementary schools about his misfortunes; there had obviously been substantial bribes spread out here, enough to cause dissension among men that Chris could destroy anytime via his information alone, and repayment for such a payoff would hold an element of peril. Footsteps echoed down the hall, the steps of several heavy men. They weren't orderlies; none of them working here were big enough to be so loud and none were baritone enough in their voices as the ones approaching. Decision-time had arrived much swifter than he had anticipated. Yes, Christian Beckett had taken more than his share of risks in life and he'd paid for more than his share too. Anything had to be better than lurking around this hell any longer, force-fed sedatives and tranquilizers a half-dozen times daily. Even if it meant a few oddball projects to help out some eccentric old man. He was certain this would entail little more than a few deliveries and some laughable espionage tactics. The door opened and he didn't even hesitate as he stepped into the corridor, the screams of the demented and delirious accompanying him on his walk to freedom. Whatever awaited him beyond the grounds of the institution, it would be worth the shrieks of the insane that had haunted his nightmares for so long. * * * * Maury Dawson wrinkled his nose; cigar smoke was putrid and he hated the things, but it appeared this Beckett chap had been longing for one throughout his entire stay in the looney bin. Now he sat across from the latest of Chaney's rescued accomplices and watched as he puffed on that damn Cuban, the first thing he'd requested when he'd been escorted onto the grounds of Avery's expansive estate. There had been very little discourse between the two; Beckett had made several attempts at small talk, running the gamut from NFL football to obscure politics and policies that Dawson knew nothing of. It wasn't so much the man's attitude or manner that raised Maury's ire, just some indefinable x-factor. "Six months huh?" said Dawson unexpectedly, lifting his eyes from his work. It was more resounding of a statement than an inquiry but it forced a nod out of Beckett. Maury wanted to know all he could about this other man and didn't feel like going through political affiliations and team loyalties to get to the root of his curiosity. "So he told you at least that much, eh?" boomed the other, a hint of laughter in his deep tone. "Curse me if I didn't almost enjoy the company at times. Though in fairness, maybe those bastards held some fondness for me. Or pity, I dunno. Occasionally I think they switched those tranquilizers with hallucinogens. I tell ya what, Dawson, I think the only thing that kept me sane in there were the conversations I had with giant blue iguanas or spectres of Marilyn Monroe lying in the corner of my cell. How's that for some fuckin' irony, huh? In spite of himself, Dawson couldn't stifle a laugh at that one. There was something about this Beckett, some nagging familiarity even, that repelled him, but the image of the man wrapped in a straitjacket chatting with an imaginary neon reptile just was too rich to hold back. The two shared a short chuckle, smoke still wafting from that chewed-up stogie in Beckett's mouth, before Maury dropped his pen and turned to gaze out the window. Outside delivering a handful of messages, his duties consisted entirely of paperwork so far. He knew there would be more, he only wished he knew when and what. "One thing is for sure. This Chaney fellow certainly has an interesting way of recruiting assistants, doesn't he? Is it really just us?" It wasn't the most fluid of topic changes but at least the man was trying. Maury wasn't sure himself, not as mysterious as his employer was. He only could shrug in reply, glancing at the clock with a sigh. "For now I suppose. As far as I've been told." He'd been told nothing not pertaining to his tasks or Chaney's promises and assurances. He'd attempted to dig up some information from Shields' servants but had come up empty on all queries; Chaney told nothing to anyone, and it seemed even Avery Shields knew just enough to tolerate his presence. The man's remark about them being 'kindred spirits' still echoed in his head. There was a connection somewhere… "This guy has money, Dawson," muttered the other, lowering the cigar a moment as he exhaled. "Believe you me, we're a small strand of a bigger web. This guy has a network and something is up. Trust me…I know these people." A momentary gaze is all that Beckett's comment illicited from Maury before the ex-banker returned to his paperwork. "I know them too. I have a rather interesting past of my own. Some affairs I'm not entirely proud of. And ones I prefer not to speak of, honestly." The half-smile vanished from Beckett's wrinkled visage, a grim scowl replacing what had been a pseudo-jovial disposition. "Let's say I know more than my fair share of how to get information using a computer. And certain parties have been a tad pissed off regarding my skills at doing so." The plot was thickening. Maury stood up and walked to the window, perusing the snow-covered south garden like had seen Chaney do so many times. The masked man held an odd reverence for the garden it seemed, claiming it put his mind at ease. The same trick didn't work now for him though, not as uneasy as he was feeling. His gut feelings were usually worth minding, and this one was nothing short of a dire scream to get out now. Beckett arched an eyebrow, speech slightly slurred as he spoke around the cigar jutting from his mouth. "Mr. Dawson…don't you find it more than a tad bit odd for a professional wrestler to be going to these lengths for the sole purpose of winning some tournament? "I'd be lying if I claimed I didn't feel likewise." Dawson sighed. " But the man saved me from death and promised me new life. I feel like if I don't repay him, I may as well have died in that wreckage. It was over for me…" "We were both lost then, Dawson. But we have to face facts. We don't know anything about this guy. Both of us may have to consider the idea that we stand more to lose by standing at his side. Death may have been preferable to what he offers. There's just…something so wrong here." Maury Dawson hoped his new associate's suspicions were the result of too many bad movies. But somewhere inside, he couldn't live with denial anymore. He'd been so eager to find some semblance of redemption that he blinded himself to the potential truth. He hadn't seen the serpent's coils wrapping around his own throat. He and Chris Beckett were going to compare some notes. Then, as soon as possible, he would do likewise with Chaney. He would dance on strings for no man. * * * * The roar of the crowd had long since died away, the stands empty albeit a rustling wrapper or overturned soda cup strewn about. The ring was almost disassembled and the riggings were nearly packed up, the offices as empty as the locker room now. Hours ago the arena was one of several worldwide that had played host to triumph and tragedy alike, forever a foundation to some blossoming careers and the tomb of others. Masquerade sat alone, slumped against a dingy locker in the corner of the facility with a dim bulb offering meager luminance. Showered and donned in his travel clothes again, the enigmatic man listened as droplets continued to drip from the showers and splash onto the floor, his head nodding in unison to the sound. Far removed from the weekly cries of battle in the ring, silence was a more common companion to him these days. Woefully and somehow fittingly, it was also all that he found in response to his prayers as of late. Money was no longer a problem nor an objective. Material things no longer mattered in the scheme of what he aimed for, and thus determined the rationale for this operation. For his spiritual health, success was the only viable resolution. He was a forsaken man. Roaming about the Earth godless and soulless, an outcast abandoned for his flaws and the mistakes he had made. This would be his saving grace, and he knew he needed help to do it. For now, stage one was a success. "Admirable work out there. Some would call it an upset. The oddsmakers sure did." "I suppose you're going to tell me you would have had the broad down in moments?" The comment was answered with only a snicker from the figure walking out of the shadows, lips pursed together just short of a mocking grin. "I know you too well. You're about as harsh on in-ring technique as anyone I've ever met, and I've been in the Japanese dojos, my friend. If only you saved some of that tenacity for negotiations and summits, we'd have them all afraid to so much as swipe their credit cards without your approval." The other was a dark man, tall with eyes like a hawk and a coy smirk seemingly engraved on his face. With short dark hair slicked back and garbed in a black, leather coat, he seemed the unsavory sort that any sweet belle would have to reconsider taking home to meet mother; it was really uncertain whether he would simply impress her, woo her, or slit her throat, but it likely be one of the above if not all. He stalked around the shadows, drumming is fingertips on the flimsy locker doors before leaning back against the wall. "I've been thinking a lot. These other men…are they really the type of help you want? A sorrowful, past-dwelling banker and some extricated computer hacker who could still be murdered anytime for the things he knows?" "I did my homework." The response was simple, impassive. They were the words of a man who knew he was in control, his leadership unquestioned regardless of the relaxed small talk. His mask, the pallid and nonexpressive parody of a face veiling his own, further supported his position in the hierarchy here. "Do you doubt my decisions, my brother?" "You know full well I don't, my friend. But if I'm going to learn from you then I may as well understand the reasoning for choosing the ones you do." "If you knew the things I do about Maury Dawson and Christian Beckett, you'd stop taking these things at face value, 'Kendall'. We'll speak at greater length when I don't feel like I've been run down by a tank." The other smirked more at the alias than the generic jest. They'd all but abandoned using their real names recently for sake of dodging potential slip-ups, even in personal situations like this. Too many things could go wrong with even a minor lapse in the illusion they'd designed. Masquerade was a product of several minds, not a lone hermit of a social outlaw. "We need to get back into favor, my friend. We've failed too many times. And if our enemies discovered us, we would be hounded until the day we were caught and executed. The men I've unearthed have precisely the credentials we need for the operation, to give us our anonymity to make progress and regain our power. They can give us what we need for our due ascension, and in return they will have their salvation as well." "You really want to help them then?" "I still think they can find deliverance. I've given them a taste of it, any normal man would want more. With my help they won't be blind forever, not as long as they're receptive. But for me to lead them to the river to drink, I need them to direct me back to the right path." The other uttered an audible sigh, nodding with reluctant agreement. However, his mind was on other matters. There would be plenty of other times for philosophical discussions. "The reason I came…as much as I love your symbolic diatribes, old man…is that I figure you may as well know the identity of your next opponent. I imagine you haven't heard a wretched thing lounging around this locker room for the past few hours." Masquerade's body was exhausted, bruises already forming on his chest and arms while a mild throbbing had materialized in his right knee. He'd have at least a week to mend whatever wounds Shadow managed to inflict at the least. At least 'Kendall' kept track of the competitive side of things while he kept Maury's brain occupied with outside paperwork before his most important assignments were put into motion. "Kaden Calix." Not a well-known personality, but then no one had put faith into the faceless Masquerade toppling the preliminary favorite Shadow. And he knew as well as anyone that an unknown was potentially more dangerous than any of the more dreaded heavyweights dotting the tournament landscape. He'd been on both sides of the fence himself. "We have work to do, friend. I refuse to play games with this one. Mercy simply won't be tolerated at this stage." He was never a man to court mercy for his enemies anyhow. But this time, his very survival may depend on it. That alone made him more dangerous than ever before.
