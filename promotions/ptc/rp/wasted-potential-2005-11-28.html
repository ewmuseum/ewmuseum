Wasted Potential

28 November 2005

Date: 2005-11-28 "Wasted Potential" By: Dan Murphy Four years ago I heard rumblings from a pair of west coast independents about a bona fide star in the making. Bay Area Wrestling as well as Pacific Championship Wrestling were spinning some tales about a pair of cruiserweights they had. Keith Scott Zimmerman was just starting out but had a distinct buzz, but the star of the west coast independents was a kid name Alastair Nagatsuka. Zimmerman has gone on to make a huge name for himself in organizations like Action!, the Squared Circle, and the Fans Wrestling Organization while Alastair is rumored to be a masked man in a small Japanese promotion. But another person caught my eye, someone who was not being hyped up or made to look like a megastar about to explode onto the scene. A young and eager former amateur wrestling star that goes by the name Brandon Youngblood. Seeing him work was something to behold. He wasn’t flashy, but he was technically strong. He knew the concepts of what it took to be a wrestler, and was studying as much as he could. I had a good feeling that Youngblood would be somebody in professional wrestling. I can not believe how naïve I really was. It’s one thing to get lost as someone goes to work in the squared circle, but it is another to let that completely jade your opinions about someone. When it comes to putting together a match, Brandon Youngblood might just be the best in the world. However, in almost every single other area, Youngblood has taken his enormous potential and flushed it down the toilet. Brandon has a term attached to him: cancer. And with his track record, it is impossible to deny the validity of such a claim. He was involved with the wrestler walkout of Action!, even though he had worked for the company less than a month. It is an amazing show of disloyalty, since it was Action! owner Reed Young who had used his vast amount of wealth to procure him when he was rather young and green. He allowed him to take a deliberate approach in his training and learning about professional wrestling, allowing his wrestling acumen to form. For two years, Reed Young had paid him six figures on a developmental deal, only for Brandon to cut and run on him. Brandon owns a home in the Mojave Desert, following in the footsteps of his idol Mike Randalls, in large part because he was one of the most vocal during the trial proceedings that led to Action!’s demise. Reed was not a smart man, but he cared for the wrestling product. He might have been controlling, but he paid his stars well and completely abandoned the philosophy of house shows. To consider many of the owners in professional wrestling, at least Reed Young’s willingness puts him ahead of the curb. That did not stop Brandon from stabbing him in the back and getting a hefty settlement. The sad story of Harvard Avalon passing away took the wrestling world by storm. It was perhaps a good thing it did for Brandon. Harvard’s struggle with cancer was one of the first things printed on the dirt sheets, and it allowed Youngblood’s departure from tSC to get put under the radar. Youngblood was fired from tSC for repeated violations of the company’s substance abuse policy. Brandon is a known recovering alcoholic, but the feeling is that Brandon was doing more than just drinking. The call came from above, from Harvard himself. Harvard had taken many chances on wrestlers, but the fact remained that Youngblood was the worst of the worst when it came to tSC. Whether it be the fall through of a reported deal with New Frontier Wrestling’s Western Conference, or the fact that he walked out on Jolt after being the company’s Triple Crown Champion, it all seemed like a distant memory when his PRIME run began. Brandon had apparently cleaned up his lifestyle and had dedicated his life to wrestling going into this year, and had a very successful year. For the first time in his career, it looked like the expectations he had laid the foundation for in those west coast independents was finally coming to fruition. Brandon Youngblood’s run in PRIME showed that he could be a megastar. He’s had classics with basically every big name the company has had this past year. He was one of the company’s top heels, and he was on the cusp of fulfilling his promise with a run as PRIME Universal Champion. The bottom quickly fell out on his PRIME run. The reason? Too high maintenance. Hard to work with. Huge ego. His current status as a fWo wrestler seems to be very lame duck. On television Brandon has been vocal in his displeasure of Disney and has not remotely fulfilled the company’s intentions for him. They were expecting a Chris Benoit or a Kurt Angle. What they got was a malcontent who never seems to get it right, no matter where it is. No one will touch him, but with the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament on the horizon, Brandon has a chance to seduce us all with his beautiful in ring ability. Hell, even a first round loss to Kenny Mercury will look good, as the match will assuredly be of typical Youngblood quality, especially with someone the caliber of Mercury on the other side. But do not be fooled. Brandon Youngblood is Terrell Owens with wrestling boots. And any company that signs him are fools.
