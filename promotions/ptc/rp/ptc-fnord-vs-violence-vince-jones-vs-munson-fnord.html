13 June 2004 Sometimes, it’s not enough to be ourselves. Sometimes, our lives feel so drab and mundane that the daily grind seems to literally choke the vitality out of our existences. For most people, there are two remedies for this sensation: a weeklong vacation… or a nice, long daydream. But, as most of the wrestlers in the whole of PTC have proven, there’s a third alternative: to literally become someone else. Why settle for simply dreaming about a level of greatness that seems so out of reach when you can convince the world that you ARE exactly what you always wanted to be: someone with riches and power beyond imagining. Perhaps with an exotic lineage. Or a military rank. Or a thug that calls himself Mr. Violence. There’s no limit to what you can be—IF you can convince people that you are who you say you are. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Let me tell you the story of… George Psalmanazar, The Formosan Cannibal The man who later called himself George Psalmanazar was born in 1679, in the south of France, where he learned Latin from the Franciscans. “This little cannibal bit me again!” When he turned sixteen, his destitute mother sent him to Germany to seek support from his father. “It’s 500 miles, so you’d better take the family shoes.” But when he finally found his father, the old man was tapped ou. “You want money? Go ask your mother!” The boy was forced to support himself by begging from priests in the flawless Latin he’d learned from the Franciscans. “Clever lad!” “Caveat Emptor!” He traveled to Holland where he joined the Dutch army as ‘George Psalmanazar.” After his discharge, he decided to disguise himself as a Japanese prince. “It’s so crazy, it just might work!” On the way, he invented a fake religion, a calendar, and his own version of the Japanese language. “Sushi!” By the time George got to Holland, he’d perfected his pose as a good-mannered pagan who worshipped both the sun and the moon. “Thank you, Lord of the Night, for leading me to this small and smelly shrine to your greatness!” This behavior soon brought him to the attention of the local pastor, who tried to convert George to Christianity. “Bless the God of the Light…” “That heathen will go blind if he keeps staring into the sun!” Finally the pastor succeeded with his reluctant convert, although he was never quite sure why. His associate, the Reverend Alexander Innes, has some clue. Grateful for the encouragement, George decided to give Innes something. He introduced him to the ‘Japanese’ alphabet (which he had recently made up.) “Understand--?” “?” To test George’s alphabet, the Reverend Innes asked him to translate a Latin passage by Cicero. Then, a few minutes later, he asked him to translate the same passage again. “These two versions are dramatically different—proving that you’re a fraud!” It was at this point that Reverend Innes suggested to George that he drop the Japanese prince routine and become someone even less known—a Formosan, a native of what is now Taiwan. “But… I’m not even sure what a Formosan native looks like!” “Exactly—nobody is! You’ll be able to make up whatever you have to, and no one will know!” In April of 1704, Innes brought George to England to introduce him to society. “George, this is the Bishop of London.” “I have so many questions! Perhaps we can discuss them over dinner--?” All that remained was to discover whether the world was ready for George… “You’re sure we can’t *choke* COOK that steak just a tiny bit for you, Mr. Psalmanazar?” One of the things that made George so convincing was his erudition—plus the fact that he was fluent in at least four languages. “Okay, one more story…” George told his rapt audience about Formosa, where gold was as common as dirt and people stayed home at midday to keep from being blinded by the glare given off by their own possessions. He told them about elephants so large that fifty people could ride on one animal’s back simultaneously. “—Now leaving for East Formosa and points beyond!” He told them about the plethora of wildlife on the island, including tame seahorses the size of a man. “Yip!” And about the harsh, swift punishments delivered when rules were broken, such as having one’s tongue bored through with a hot iron. “You have brought this on yourself—for reckless use of seahorses!” Natives were also hung upside down for three or four days, with dogs tied to their bodies. “Ha ha! It tickles!” “Better get bigger dogs…” And Formosan husbands who caught their wives in adultery were permitted to behead and eat them. “Just a little kiss, sweetheart!” “Harry! Not in front of your wife!” Worst of all was the custom of burning the hearts of 18,000 healthy Formosan boys each year, in the name of a local deity. “The Heart Drive is certainly going well this year!” Late in 1704, George published a book revealing these and other far-fetched truths about his supposed homeland. “Ha!” And in London, people couldn’t seem to get enough of it. “’Ere he comes, everybody! It’s the bloody Formosan ‘imself!” The success of his book even led to a teaching position at Oxford, where George demonstrated what a Formosan wears to keep cool in hot weather. “And it also makes a wholesome breakfast!” “EEUUUUUUU!” To prove that he was a tireless scholar, George started to leave candles burning in his windows until all hours. The Reverend Innes was also burning the candle at both ends, due to George’s great success. “Here’s to things Formoshan—and also to things fermented! *Hic*” When questions arose about the whiteness of his skin, George explained that he was a member of the Formosan upper class, and therefore lived in a cool, subterranean apartment. In 1709, a Jesuit priest named Father Fountenay—who had spent many years in China—challenged George to a debate, but he never counted on the phony Formosan’s formidable wit. “You don’t know nothing about Formosa. And your mother dresses you funny!” Sir Isaac Newton spoke out at great length against George, but he was only wasting his time. “If English society could only appreciate the gravity of this situation--!” Sir Edmund Halley likewise drove himself to distraction in his campaign to expose George as a fraud. “What do you people need—a comet to hit you??” It wasn’t until 1728, when George suffered a serious illness that the farce finally came to an end. “Dinner?” “I can’t take it anymore. No raw meat… EVER again! *Broooww-urp*” George confessed his deception and led a modest and pious existence. He even composed an annotated Book of Psalms. “I’ll dedicate this one to that poor sinner—The Reverend Innes!” Late in life, George was even asked to write the section on Formosa in the ‘Geography of the World.’ Innes, meanwhile, hadn’t changed. “What’s George thinking of? Nobody’s going to care. This stuff is much too accurate!” But the real labor of his life was confession, entitled ‘Memoirs of ****, Commonly Known by the Name of George Psalmanazar,’ which consumed the last decades of his life. “Mea culpa, mea culpa, mea culpa, mea culpa.” Although he completed the book in 1752, George insisted that is should only be issued posthumously. George had his reasons. “…To undeceive the world in a posthumous work, which would be less liable to suspicion, as the author would be far out of the influence of any sinister motives that might induce him to deviate from the truth.” And it may have been his friend, Dr. Samuel Johnson, who spoke most succinctly about George’s repentance. “George Psalmanazar was the BEST man I ever knew!” But did anyone really know who he was? George Psalmanazar never revealed his true name, and the paper trail stops dead in the place where he invented that distinctive pseudonym—Sluys, Holland, which was razed to the ground by bombs during World War II, along with any trace of the true record! ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- im·pos·tor or im·pos·ter ( m-p s t r) n. But one thing about the picture really got my attention—a part when these two little kids actually got ripped apart and eaten by a giant bug! I clapped and admired the filmmaker for not being a weenie. Now, I wasn’t applauding the simple fact that the kids got eaten (amusing as it certainly was)—No, I was happy that the director did not shy away from what turned out to be a wonderfully effective use of the nastiness. Remember when Steven Spielberg used to use that effect in movies like JAWS? Now he seems to just throw kids in to catch a bigger audience, one that knows he won’t be so awful as to have ANYTHING bad happen to them cute little children. Then… I began thinking of making a movie whose sole purpose is to test the tolerance of that moronic American audience. Something to make people get sick. Think of the power a filmmaker would wield if they could actually drive a person to illness! During the twenty-minute credit sequence, the audience will be treated to a high-pitched audio medley of screaming crack-babies and mating howler monkeys. To calm their jangled nerves, the film begins with some typical Hollywood garbage. In the classic tradition, ethnicity means a deathmark, and a reason to motivate the noble white guy. A female character is introduced, and, of course, the males in the audience will sit patiently through PLOT, to see if she gets naked. Just when the audience begins to get comfortable, an actual story with depth begins, making them have to pay attention. They get angry. THEN… a scene begins with the arrival of a giant monster approaching a little kid. Special effects attract the eyes of the restless crowd. The audience smiles, wanting something awful to happen to the kid. Jokes are made, and giggling sets in. And the creature DOES do something awful! It snatches the kid into its jaws. The audience howls with laughter, having had their ugly expectations fulfilled. The laughter continues for a bit, as the scene progresses. Three minutes pass, and the child, still alive and screaming, is still being chewed on, lazily, like a gobstopper. After four minutes of this, the audience begins feeling uncomfortable. Some still giggle, but nervously. After 5 or 6 minutes of the constant screaming and chewing noises, people begin looking around the theatre, more than a little uncomfortable with the scene on the screen. They wonder if there is anybody in the projection booth. As people try to leave the theatre, they find locked doors. The soundtrack rises to a deafening level, chewing and screaming, and chewing. TEN minutes have passed, and the kid still ain’t dead… And then… We cut to a tranquil scene, with our hero, and the female character destined for nudity. You know a film is getting bad when you really start wishing for unnecessary nudity. The men in the audience forget the trauma of the screaming chew-kid, and drool. Of course, the women are not excluded, as our hero is a handsome one. Hot stuff, indeed. Upon arriving at our heroin’s domicile, it is apparent that battle is not the only things our leads will do together. Though they have only known each other for less than a day, ‘clever’ writing will find some way to make them fall madly in love with each other. At least enough to have sex. Groins sensing the impending exposure of ‘purdy stuff’, goons in the audience murmur lecherously. Then… and interruption in the onscreen foreplay. Our female lead becomes ill all over herself. Vomiting ensues for several minutes. Once again uncertain as to what the hell is going on, it becomes apparent that this is no ordinary love scene. The ‘LOVE’ scene that ensues is the most grotesque perversion of obscenity most of the audience will ever see. The soundtrack of this scene is the overamplified sounds of someone stirring a large, saucy bowl of spaghetti and Mac’N’Cheese. Close-ups of veins are nice too. I’m not exactly sure what is going on here, but you can rest assured it’s pretty fucking sick. And we cut to a MUSICAL NUMBER!! After all the nightmare, a cute, happy song is in order. A cute, nightmarishly repetitive song. Some of the children begin to come out of their fright induced comas, and dance., the parents tolerate the idiocy, glad the sex is over. But soon, they see that nothing has changed as far as the intent of the movie is concerned. The volume escalates to a head exploding… um… volume, and then some heads actually begin to explode. I dunno… I am very tired. I want this to be over with. I need food. Then SILENCE… beautiful, sanity restoring silence. The people thank whatever god they pray to for the end of the nightmare of noise, of screaming, of hideous boobies. The silence goes on for another minute or so, and the audience is too stunned to think of escape. And then… [It’s too brutal to describe.] The doors unlock after two hours of this awful movie. Of course, it loses money as it is closed down only days after opening. Paid for out of my own pocket, the film renders me penniless. Reading the reviews of the most unbearable film in recent history, I laugh maniacally. I really do. Then I pass out due to malnutrition. But I realize my efforts to sicken the populace were all for nothing, as the late night cinema circuit begins showing my movie every Saturday, drawing a cult audience who dresses up like idiots in support of my trash. I kill myself. Well, I would kill myself if this actually happened. But it didn’t… well, not yet. I have yet to make a film.
