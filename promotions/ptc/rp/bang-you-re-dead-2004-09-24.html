Bang ... you're dead

24 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-24 Knocking on the locker room door with the knuckles of his right hand, Rick James twisted the handle and pushing the door open leant his head around it looking inside. Not seeing anyone immediately visible he rearranged the leather bag that rested from his shoulder as he walked inside, placing the bag on the floor as he pushed the door closed again with his foot. “Jay are you here man, it is me, it’s Rick?” he called out. Looking around the room, which was as bare and non-descript as any locker-room in any sports arena around the Country … a small couch against one wall with a table beside it the only furniture visible, a sink and kitchenette with a microwave and kettle against another wall that also contained another door … or at least any locker-room set aside for a prestigious former World Champion in a prestigious wrestling tournament that is, Rick heard the faint sound of running water coming from behind the sealed door. Walking over to it Rick reached out to the handle and gently pushed the door open, recoiling slightly as a billowing mass of steam rushed out to engulf him in it’s embrace. Wiping a thin layer of condensation from his face, Rick peered into the room … the en-suite bathroom … and peered through the steam. The reason for the sound of running water, intensified now that the door is open, becomes evident as Rick sees a clouded and silhouetted figure inside a shower cubicle, and from the small detail that can be seen it looks like someone is vigorously washing their hair. “Hey Jay, is that you?” Rick called out, leaning slightly into the bathroom that could more realistically be called a steam-room. The silhouetted figure turns around inside the cubicle, still not really visible. “Rick, is that you?” came the reply in the very familiar voice of Jay Phoenix, who continued, “haven’t you ever heard of knocking?” “Either you took a knock to the head tonight against Desolation, or you are getting old my friend … I not only knocked I even called out as I came in” replied Rick with a grin. “Less of the wisecracks Rick or have you forgotten that you are only a year younger than me?” quipped Phoenix. “Yeah but I have heard that in dog years and wrestlers every year counts … old man!” grinned Rick as he leant against the door jam, arms folded, “You coming out of that shower some time tonight, or do your old bones need to soak some more?” The door to the cubicle opened slightly as the soaked and sodden head, long auburn hair now water darkened and stuck to him, peered out around it. His face was grinning as he stared directly at his friend. “By the way Rick … I am sorry” he said with a grin. “What for?” came the confused reply from Rick. “This!” shouted Phoenix as he suddenly aimed a jet of water at Rick from inside the shower, the spray hitting the surprised man square in the face before he quickly leapt back into the main locker-room with a startled yelp. “Not bad for an old man, ey Rick?” came the laughing voice of Phoenix from the other room, “and just so that you know I intend to spend at least another fifteen minutes in here!” Running his hand through his wet hair, Rick pushed his blond fringe back over his head and out of his eyes. Walking over to the couch he reached into a large black bag and pulled out a white towel. Rubbing it vigorously over his head Rick walked over to the small kitchenette area of the locker-room and reached out to one of the taps at the sink. “Fifteen minutes … somehow I don’t think so” he grinned to himself as he twisted the tap and turned it on, looking over his shoulder towards the door to the bathroom. As the hot water started to fill the sink it was cut off to the shower in the next room, which suddenly became very evident to Phoenix. A loud shriek, followed by the sound of the shower cubicle being slammed open, was heard as Rick started laughing loudly to himself. With a towel being held around his dripping waist, and his hair looking like something out of the Creature from the Black Lagoon movie, Phoenix stumbled into the locker-room, glaring at Rick. “What the Hell was that for Rick, you nearly killed me!” he almost shouted as he pulled the towel tighter around his waist, looping one section into the other to hold it in place as he used both hands to pull his hair back over his head into one long dripping line down his back. Standing shivering slightly he glared at his friend. Looking Phoenix up and down, noticing the shivering and the look of ire on his face, Rick responded the only way that he possibly could. He laughed. With tears streaming down his face laughter erupted from his mouth in peal after peal. Wiping away the tears from his cheeks Rick looked back at Phoenix; defined muscles stood out on all parts of his visible body … traps that were tight and raised, pectorals that were as firm as rock, biceps that even with arms at rest stood out and abs that were closer to an eight pack than a six … proclaiming him to be at the peak of fitness. “You look pretty healthy for a near corpse Jay” Rick giggled, still unable to stop the laughter, “though I suppose that I could fix that if you want!” Pointing his fingers, index and second directed ahead with ring and little fingers curled inwards in an imitation of a child’s play gun, at Phoenix, Rick pretended to cock an imaginary hammer. “Bang … you’re dead!” he said softly as a smile played across his face. An answering smile came to Phoenix’s face as his eyes widened in remembrance. “Bang … you’re dead!” The young auburn haired boy, the hair tied back in a plait that fell across his shoulders, looked up from the heavy book that rested across his denim clad legs and, as he pushed the pair of purple-lensed sunglasses he wore down to the end of his nose, he stared at the boy opposite him. “You are meant to fall over now Jay”, crowed the smaller youth as he pointed his gun-shaped fingers at his seated friend. Staring down in triumph at his friend … who sat with his back against the bark of a large oak tree, his knees bent to form a rest for the leather bound book that he held onto tightly in one hand as he held a small plant in the other … the blond haired youth grinned, his blue eyes alight with mirth. “Excuse me Rick, but would you mind if I died later perhaps … I am a little busy right now”, sighed Jay as he continued to peer over the rim of his glasses. Suddenly sitting down opposite Jay, Rick folded his legs under him as he leant forward. Placing his head in his hands he sighed as he stared at the slightly older boy in front of him. “You are always too busy Jay … I sometimes think that you are more like one hundred years older than me instead of just one year, do you know that?” Rick stated. A small smile crept across Jay’s face as he listened to his young friend talk. For the last year they had been friends, as close as brothers in many ways in fact, and ever since the day that Jay had stepped in to save the young boy from a beating at the hands of others Rick had been like a shadow to him. For a solitary and serious boy like Jay, an only child who was admittedly much older than his years, the sudden adoption by proxy by Rick was a mixed blessing, and something that was not always easy to deal with. “You have to understand Rick that I have to study. My grandfather is as you know the Shaman for my People and he has al the knowledge that has been passed down through the generations … knowledge that he is passing on to me” explained Jay gently as he smiled at his friend. “Well your grandfather really IS a hundred years older than us Jay, so that explains how he has had time to learn all that stuff … surely one day isn’t going to make that much difference is it?” asked Rick plaintively. Seeing the look of hope on his young friends face Jay closes the book and puts it on the ground beside him, placing the plant on top of the cover. With a big sigh he got to his feet, brushing the dirt and twigs from his cut-off jeans as he smiled down at Rick. “Well I suppose I need to stretch my legs anyway Rick so what is it to be, what do you want to do?” Jay asked with a smile that turned into a grin as he saw the happy expression on Rick’s own face as he leapt to his feet with glee. “How about Global Thermo-Nuclear War, Dr Falken?” Jay asked in a robotic monotone voice, eliciting floods of giggles from Rick. “Knock it off Jay, and before you ask we are not playing tic-tac-toe either!” Rick replied then looked as if he was deep in thought, but the way that he peered out from under his eyelids belied that façade. Folding his arms, Jay leant back against the bark of the tree as he smiled at his friend. “You can drop the act Rick I can tell that you have already decided what you want to do, so why not just tell me?” Jay asked mildly. Rick looked up at him a small blush playing across his face as he used his foot to play with the dirt on the ground. Taking a deep breath he stared into the eyes of Jay. “I don’t want you to think that I am stupid Jay!” he almost wailed. “I don’t think that you are stupid Rick, I never have … annoying frequently, irritating often perhaps, but never stupid my friend” Jay joked at his expense, “so just tell me what you want to play and we will play it” “OK, but just remember that you promised” Rick crowed in victory, “so I say that we should play Cowboys and Indians!” An incredulous look played across Jay’s face, his mouth dropping open and making gaping movements … no words coming out … as he stared in shock at his young friend. Shaking his head from side to side as if to clear it he pointed at Rick. “Cowboys and … you must be jok … you CANNOT be serio … I mean c’mon that is just stupid!” Jay finally managed to splutter. “I told you that you would think that it was stupid Jay!” Rick pointed out, his face crestfallen in dismay at Jay’s reaction. Realising that he had hurt his friend’s feelings, Jay shrugged his shoulders in resignation. Walking forward he placed a hand on Rick’s shoulders and smiled down at the smaller boy, the emotion not quite reaching his eyes, but he made the effort to look happy nonetheless. “Well it is just a little weird playing that game considering my heritage you know Rick, but if that is what you want then that is what we will do” Phoenix stated gently. “I am not exactly a cowboy myself you know Jay, though my dad does say that he is related to John Wayne!” laughed Rick in relief. “…that makes all the difference then I suppose Rick” Jay muttered under his breath before taking a step backwards and placing both hands on his hips and addressing Rick “So how do you want to play this then … how about we make it interesting and I will play the cowboy first?” A grin plays across Rick’s face as he considers this and then with a vigorous nod of his head he agrees. “Sure thing Jay I mean I have never been an Indian before but I have lived on the reservation for over a year now so I should be able to manage” he said enthusiastically. Shaking his head in resignation again, Phoenix took a couple of steps away from Rick before turning back to face him with a false smile on his face. Taking a couple of swaggering steps forwards, his thumbs in his belt loop, Jay spat down on the ground at his feet. “Well there Chief got any thing to trade with me?” Jay said in an obviously fake Texan accent, which elicited a grin from Rick. “Erm … sure thing cowboy, let me just get them for you.” Rick replied trying … but failing … to keep a straight face as he turned his back and pretended to look around for something to trade. “Bang … you’re dead!” Rick spun around in shock to find Jay pointing his fake gun at him a large smile on his face. Holding his fingers up in front of his mouth he blew away imaginary smoke before holstering the gun again and staring at his friends confused face. “What are you doing Jay … you just shot me!” queried Rick in consternation “that isn’t part of the game I thought that we were going to trade … whey did you do that?” “Simple Rick, I am of the True People, what has become known as Native Americans. My people lived the game that you call cowboys and Indians and do you know what we learned … do you know why I ‘shot’ you?” he asked with a small smile? Rick stood there thinking for a few seconds but obviously couldn’t figure out an answer and so shook his head negatively, and waited expectantly for Jay to tell him. “You can never trust a cowboy” Jay said with another smile, and then turned around to walk back over to the tree and his waiting book. As he went to sit down though Rick ran up to him and grabbed him by the arm. Turning around to face his friend, Jay saw a large smile on Rick’s face. “One more go then Jay and this time I will be the cowboy!” he demanded. “So I suppose you want me to stretch my acting ability and play the ‘indian’ then Rick?” Jay asked with a grin. “Exactly!” came the answer from Rick, who turned his back and took a few steps away from Jay before turning around with a mimicked false smile on his face. With his thumbs in his belt he strutted back towards his smiling friend … Jay’s eyes glittering with controlled emotion … before spitting on the ground himself. “Well there Chief got any thing … “ Rick started before he was interrupted by Jay who suddenly brought his hand up in the fake gun position once more, a large grin on his face. “Bang … you’re dead!” Once more Rick’s face was incredulous and he stopped dead in his tracks as he stared in disbelief at Jay and his sparkling eyes. “What the Hell was that about Jay?” he said with anger simmering just below the surface “why did you shoot me like that, I didn’t get a chance to do anything!” “It is like I said Rick, something that my ancestors figured out a long time ago” Jay said as he walked over and placed an arm companionably around his friend’s shoulders “you can NEVER trust a cowboy!” Seeing the look of understanding dawning on his friend’s face Jay started to laugh with him before leading him over to the tree and pulling him down to a seated position beside him. Reaching over he brought the leather bound book back to his knees and opened it up again, where a picture of the small green plant could be seen. Handing the real plant to Rick, Jay indicated the book he was holding. “How about leaving the cowboys behind and joining the other side Rick … let’s forget the games for today and instead I will teach you some of the knowledge that my grandfather is teaching me. Maybe we can turn you into a Shaman instead of a cowboy?” Jay laughed. Rick’s eyes gleamed as he looked at the book before staring down at the plant in his hands. Looking up again into Jay’s eyes he slowly nodded his head. Suddenly a look of concern crossed his face though. “How long will it take to learn it all though Jay, do I have to live to be as old as your grandfather?” he asked worriedly. “That would be a start Rick because as he told me himself the day that you stop learning, the day that you realise that there is nothing left to find out … well that is the day that you die” he replied with a small smile, before pointing at the plant in Rick’s hand “ … but anyway, that is called saw palmetto and it’s berries are eaten to keep the body healthy …” Coming back to his sense as his memory faded back into the recesses of his mind, Phoenix smiled to himself all anger at the cold shower courtesy of Rick now forgotten. Walking over to his friend he grabbed the towel from his hands and started to dry of his hair with it as he sat down on the couch, looking up through straggled and damp auburn locks. “It has been a long time since you wanted to play cowboys and Indians Rick, and I had hoped that I had convinced you that playing Indians was much more fulfilling … I mean given another seventy or eighty years and you could have made a half decent shaman’s apprentice yourself!” Phoenix quipped at his friend, who sat down on the arm of the couch himself as he lifted the small leather bag he had brought with him. “Very funny Jay, but it looks like it won’t be me that is playing cowboys and Indians this time … but I DO think that Argyle or someone else in the PTC has a weird sense of humor you know!” Rick replied with an answering grin. Noting the confusion on Phoenix’s face he continued. “I found out about thirty minutes ago who your next round opponent is going to be Jay … it is that weird Texan cowboy, Tex "One Gun" Chattanooga” he finished off as he reached into the leather bag. Phoenix’s face looks incredulous at this news as he tried to digest it. Allowing the towel to flop around his shoulders he runs his fingers through his hair as a make-shift comb. Leaning back into the couch Phoenix looks lost in thought. “Well I have to say that I am impressed as I didn’t think that Tex would be able to get past Fnord, so color me surprised” he mused to himself “… I will have to rethink things then and not take this guy too lightly, I mean if he can do it once he can do it twice” A grin appears on Rick’s face as he pulls a folder out of his bag, followed by two videocassettes. Throwing these onto Phoenix’s stomach Rick stands up and walks towards the door, looking back over his shoulder at his friend’s confused face. “Like you told me Jay as long as you keep on learning you keep on living … which is why I have collated as much information on this Tex guy as I could find. Everything that I found is in that file … and you even have some of his matches, including the one against Fnord, on those tapes.” Rick stated with an almost smug grin and reaching out to open the door that led out of the locker-room he glanced back at Phoenix. “So it looks like you have a busy night ahead of you Jay if you are going to learn all you can about your opponent, but I can already tell you one thing that I know about Tex … something that I learned from a great man” Rick supplied with a grin. “What is that Rick?” Phoenix asked in confusion. As Rick exited and pulled the door closed behind him, his voice … filled with laughter … filtered back. “Simple Jay my friend … you can never trust a cowboy!”
