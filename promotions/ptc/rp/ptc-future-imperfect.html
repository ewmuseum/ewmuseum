[IG69] Future Imperfect.

23 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The not-so-distant future. "I failed once." It's a ragged whisper over the darkness, consumed with a sneer. Feminine only by genetics, the voice's whisper would curl your toes – and not in a good a way. Fade up. We are, as the song goes, along a lonesome highway, east of Omaha. Perfectly manicured cornfields hug the two-lane road, only intermittently painted with white stripes to delineate each side of the road. The moon hangs above; it's fat and pregnant, sparse clouds marring the clear sky. The camera pans down each side of the road, first to the left, then across the cornstalks to the right. "I lost everything up everything I had, everything I'd ever earned, every bit of respect and honor I'd scraped together for myself. And for what?" There. One snow-white headlight looms in the distance, speeding towards the camera with reckless abandon, too far away to make out more than the generic shape. "For love?" Scorn drips from the words, the placid night torn away in favor of a new scene, just a glimpse, barely in focus. Two bodies writhe together, ivory flesh melding together. It's indistinct, unclear. A fading memory, quickly claimed by the black. "For lust? For the hope of something that I knew could never be?" The shape races from one orange-yellow pool of light to the next; it's close enough now that some features become clear. The headlight belongs to the front of a motorcycle, more beast than bike, the growl of its engine presaging its arrival, all chrome pieces and a frightening exhaust. "Hope is dead. Love is for fools. But a fool I was, and I tried to grasp it in both hands, ephemeral like the morning mists. I gave up who I was and I tried to be someone else. I never saw the disaster until it had already claimed me." Flash. Another ill-lit, fuzzy scene. A door slams, shaking the wall it was attached to, hard enough to send a picture frame tumbling to the hardwood floor. Something hard, heavy, and crystalline flies from off-frame, exploding against the door. Flash. And we're back to the lonely highway in Nebraska as the motorcycle, its rider dressed all in black leather, face covered by the tinted visor of a riding helmet. The bike speeds past the camera without pause or acknowledgement. "I know what made me. I know what broke me. The pieces... don't fit together like they used to. I've tried to force them, shoving as hard as I could. But I think the edges of my puzzle are frayed and forever damaged. I can still feel the sting." The motorcyclist bears on, towards the low-hanging moon. "I will never feel it again." Fade to shadow, but only for a moment. "I failed once." The second speaker is also female, but this one is more noticeably so. It's the kind of voice that you'd hear in your dreams or on late-night commercials for sex lines, half a purr and half a coo. When the darkness parts, we're low, on the ground. The carpet is red, stylized curls of gold and green through the edges. A hallway, perhaps a hotel. The hall is lined with doorways, lit with dim, moody lighting that spills from sconces in the wall. "I tried to deny what I was. Tried to wrap myself up in the warmth of his embrace. I tried to be what I'd seen in the movies. I wanted my life to be a romantic comedy. I wanted to be swept off my feet." As before, the camera swings 180° as a woman turns the corner and begins down the hallway – or at least it had better be a woman, given the height of those scarlet heels. Gold winks along the strap, the cap of the stiletto gleams silver. "For a time, I like to think I was." Flash. It's raining, the sky turning the scene a murky, miserable gray. A building looms in the background, casting its shadow over the pair of them, held in each other's arms. The woman is underdressed for the weather, silky fabric quickly clinging. She shivers against him. Rivulets of the storm slide off the brim of the man's hat. "But life is not a dream, and leopards don't change their spots. Especially not when they wear them so well." Flash. She approaches, red toenails sparkling, toned calves leading bare thighs, to the hem of a dress that the camera's angle can't quite see – but that's just how high it is. Behind the camera, the elevator bell chimes; if anything, the woman slows her pace, one foot placed just before the other. "I lost sight of what was important. Lost sight of my goals, and all because I wanted to be lost in a story that could never come to pass." Flash. The murmurs become shouts, penlights flashing over the prone figure on a stretcher. Her neck has been stabilized, EMTs pushing her through a very different corridor. They're shouting about her condition, hurrying her towards an ambulance. Rain splatters the roof of the van as she's loaded aboard, too, but this is far less romantic. "I should have expected it. I should have known better. In the end, he only did what I have done. Turnabout isn't fair play, but karma is a bitch." Flash. Back in the hallway as the woman passes the camera position, followed in the other direction. A man pulls a wheeled suitcase, freshly-shined loafers gleaming in the low light. He stops as she nears, releases the handle of his suitcase. She presses herself against him, her left leg curling around his thigh. "I know what I am, and I am comfortable in my skin. I will never allow myself to be in that position again. Now I'm in control of my story." The man's left hand drops into frame, fingers reaching out to jam his keycard into the lock of one of the doors. A simple gold band glints on his ring finger, but he doesn't seem to know it's there. Fade back to shadow, again, only for a moment. "I failed once." The third speaker is also a woman, her voice a little flatter, a little more tired. This is the voice of a woman who has seen too much and cares little to relive it. "I thought I was doing the right thing. I thought I could make them see the light. In a world of masks, I wanted to shine the light of truth." The shadow parts as a yellow school bus comes to a halt outside a building in the bad part of town. The hydraulics of the door hiss as it opens, and a girl hops down the steps. Even though we watch from behind, it's clear she is the stereotypical schoolgirl: red-and-green plaid skirt, canvas sneakers, tall socks, yellow pigtails hanging from the back of her head and over her shoulders. "I could have stopped it all, could have nipped it in the bud. But I underestimated the power of the lies people tell themselves." Flash. We're on a rooftop somewhere, watching down the barrel of a rifle. A small hand grips the rifle, a black fingernail just barely tickling the trigger. Something – the scratch of a foot nearby – catches the shooter's attention, and a hard-soled boot pivots on the shingles, fist lashing out to greet the man who would stop her. "I'm good at underestimating people. Just as good as people are at underestimating me." Flash. Watch from the roof of the car as the girl pulls an aluminum foil balloon out of the bus; Winnie the Pooh is briefly visible on the face of the thing. She's skipping slightly as she approaches the rundown building. "I wanted to be the hero. I wanted to be the one who taught the lesson for a change. But I got my ass kicked. Again and again, I got my ass kicked. Eventually – call it survival instinct – I learned to shut up and wait. Things fall apart. The center cannot hold. "It wasn't an easy lesson to learn." Flash. A slight form is strapped to a chair, an IV jabbed into its arm. She's screamed herself hoarse, but the door still swings shut, plunging her into black. "When the house of cards fell down, I had a lot to make up for. I learned that the hard way." Flash. Another chair, another location; this one is a dentist chair, and she's strapped down with wide, brown straps, a lot of shining buckles. Another shadow approaches. We get a glimpse of a wide, green eye widening further as a razor is pressed to the tender flesh beneath it. Blood comes to the surface immediately, a livid red line. "I paid the price, and now the rest of the world will pay it back to me." Flash. The flaxen-haired schoolgirl pauses nervously in front of a dilapidated house. She is greeted by a tall man in jeans and a red headband. His gesture is obvious: she is unwelcome. She stumbles back in fright as the man lifts the hem of his shirt, just a glimpse of a silver firearm tucked into the waistband of his pants. "I am now a scarred thing, a monster with the face of man. My own missteps have put me in this position, and I cannot look at myself in the mirror anymore – not without a lie to hide me. I do not know who I am anymore." The string of the balloon escapes from the girl's hand as it goes to her mouth in panic, the foil shining in the dying light of the dusk. The camera follows its path towards the sky. A gunshot rings out, the bullet tearing through the thin material of the balloon. Winnie the Pooh drifts lazily down to the ground. The girl is behind the man, whose gun is out and smoking. The girl grips the handle of a garrote, wrenching the man to the ground with her. His struggles are futile. "I just know I will never fail her again." Down to shadow once more. "I failed once." The fourth woman is far more amused then the first three; the upturn of her grin audible in her voice. She seems like she's telling the punchline to a joke, like she's waiting for you to laugh. "My failure's probably bigger than yours, because I caused all this... ickiness. Got jealous, got angry, had a knife handy. Figured I'd take care of the problem, cut out the cancer." When the darkness parts, it leaves us in a bar. Not a rundown roadside bar or a rough-and-tumble biker bar, but one of those ultra-modern clubs that fits into no city as well as Las Vegas. This place is called the Red Room at Pure, and the room swathed in reds, from the lighting to the upholstery to the bar itself. It's a VIP lounge, and one that fully embraces its name, down to the skintight red that the servers wear. We follow a redhead, tray in hand loaded down with a variety of drinks, across the room. "Suffice it to say, I ended up making it a whole lot worse." Flash. The parking lot is nearly empty as she waits, hunched behind the front wheel of a pickup truck. She twirls the switchblade, snapping it open and then closed. The repetitive motion is supposed to relax her, but it doesn't seem to do more. Other figures approach; the knife-wielder bends low, almost obscenely, and we watch the shadow as she peers under the chassis of the truck. Sneakers lead the pack. The woman's lips pull back, teeth briefly visible as she shifts again, foot scraping against the asphalt. In a moment, she'll run out and pounce on the offender. She'll feel her knife tear through soft flesh. She'll feel the rush of warm blood flow over her fingers. But she'll never get the chance to finish the job. "I was stupid – probably am, still. Hard to tell, hard to see the forest for the trees. Hard to stop seeing the red behind my eyes. I failed, and in failing, I introduced the fire that nearly burned down my house." Flash. The table in the corner at the red room isn't like it is at other bars; it's a series of couches nestled away from the world, a small group of well-dressed men laughing together. The redhead's curling, Celtic inspired lower back tattoo becomes visible as she bends at the waist to set the colorful (and doubtless strong) beverages before the lot of them. Her appearance isn't enough to distract them from their conversation. "If you get your head handed to you enough, you might learn to deal with the concussion, but when you get your heart torn out over and over and over again, sometimes you welcome the pain." Flash. In the darkness, a woman with red hair is shoved headfirst into a wall, the point of her attacker's elbow colliding with the base of her long braid. She reaches out for something, anything that she can use to defend herself. She comes up only with a bronze bookend. It's heavy in her hand as she launches her fist over her shoulder. The attacker is struck, slumps. The redhead does not grin. She seems sad, the impromptu weapon spilling from her fingers to clatter against the floor. Shimmering red lips mouth just one word as the flash comes again: "Sorry." Flash. We're back at the Red Room, and the number of empty drink glasses has spiked increasingly. The redhead delivers another round. "I know what I did. I know what happened when I kicked over the first domino. They say that the flapping of a butterfly's wings in Brazil can cause a tornado in Texas. I like to think I was the wind the butterfly caused, but, in truth, I wasn't even the butterfly. "Though I will admit I ripped off its wings once." Flash. Again, we're starting at the ground level, though there is no lush carpet, just a hard, oily concrete. A woman sits on a chair in the background, legs crossed, white pantlegs notably smudged. In the foreground, tattooed arms are stretched out, a woman lying facefirst on the ground. Her hands are gripped by someone standing above her, tall boot planted between her shoulderblades. The woman on the chair moves – raises her hand negligibly, maybe – and the tattooed girl is lifted up by her arms, held bent-up over the oil-stained concrete. The force of the boot's stomp downward drives her face down into the unforgiving ground. The camera pans up past the victim, up to the teeth shining in the boot-wearer's grin. "Everything's back together now, all shiny-happy-new, and sometimes – just sometimes – I remember what I did then. "And I laugh. At least I can still do that." Flash. One of the men at the table blinks suddenly, moving slowly, like he's trapped underwater. He tries to stand up, but staggers, bringing the chair down with him. A second joins him, flopping against the table. The impact sends his wineglass tumbling to the ground. Fade to shadows, even briefer this time. "We all failed once." It's the original speaker, the beast of the motorcycle stopping at a valet station. The key is underhanded to an attendant; we follow the leather-clad rider as she (there can be no mistaking that that derriere belongs to a woman) cuts ahead of the line at Pure, the bouncer holding the door open for her. "Some of us more than others." The second speaker carries us into a dark room, limbs tangled, tongues dancing. They're in a booth somewhere, and they don't care. His fingers slip under the hem of her skirt. Hers are at his belt. His wallet comes free into her hand, but he never notices, never has a chance to notice. "And all of us have climbed back up from the hell we put ourselves in." The third speaker still sounds exhausted, depressed as a tiny fist bangs against a door; it's an apartment, and it's numbered 3A, though the "A" has come loose and now hangs upside-down. "Now the band is back together, and, even if the machine isn't as well-oiled as it used to be, it still functions." As the men at the corner table at Red Room spasm and lose consciousness, a dark-haired server rushes forward to provide aid. A hand in the woman's hair halts the girl's movement, a razor springing to life in the redheaded waitress' hand. It draws a second smile across the brunette's chin, a mouth that drools red. "And we will not fall again." The rest of the voiceover comes from the first speaker, haggard whisper finding its steel. Hard boots bang out a rhythm as she steps through the main room at the nightclub. Her helmet comes off, held against her hip. The back of the helmet features just one marking: a blood-red cross moline imprinted on its back. "Though the road may be hard, and the world cruel..." A woman answers the door to 3A, all limpid blue eyes with dark circles beneath them. An infant rests on her shoulder, eyes closed, pacifier in danger of slipping from his mouth. Her brows climb; we're again watching behind the schoolgirl, whose black-nailed thumb taps a link on its touchscreen. "...it is not as hard or cruel as we." The woman with the motorcycle helmet pulls open the door to the Red Room just as a woman clutches at her throat and crumples to the ground. Quick, sharp strides carry her across the room, but she goes past the gathered throng towards a secluded booth. "We have learned our lesson, molded in a forge fired by our own missteps. And we will teach that lesson to all of you. No matter the price we must pay, nor the depths to which we must stoop to get there." The motorcyclist draws aside a curtain that shields the booth from the rest of the floor, and a man looks up, cheeks flushed, breath unsteady. He says something – there is no sound to give us the words, but it seems to be a demand. He misses the smile that curls across the burgundy lips of his paramour, her hand at his belt and then lower. "Whatever prize you seek, we will take from you. Whatever hope you hold onto, we will crush beneath our bootheels." The man looks past the motorcyclist, his eyes widening. He scrabbles backwards, but his lover's grip is around something just as precious to him. "I have never cared about titles, or wins and losses. Plaudits have never been my goal. They are simply items for me to take away from you. And now I have returned. We have returned, together. Divided in our fall. United in our rise. Bonded by vengeance." The schoolgirl thrusts her cellphone at the blonde in 3A. The tiny screen shows a tawdry scene indeed, the camera lens fogging up. Her blue-gray gaze hardens. She nods once. That's all that it takes. "We will take it all, not for us to have, but for you to not have. The championships. The congratulations of our colleagues. We will revel in robbing you of everything you have ever wanted, so that one day, you will look at me and you will say, 'You have succeeded where I failed, even though I hate you for it.'" The helmet has been set atop the table. The motorcyclist's gloved hands screw a silencer onto a 9mm pistol. The man begs, eyes wide, mouth moving with a hundred different ways to beseech her. A thousand. The camera travels upwards, along a well-muscled back. Past a tiny symbol at the back of her neck – a lowercase Q, perhaps the Greek Alpha. Short, shorn bloody red hair melds oddly with the lights that suffuse this room. Sculpted cheeks draw inwards with a breath. Thin, bloodless lips pronounce sentence. "But this is not the story of our success, of our taking our nightmare and making it each of yours." The woman's left hand raises, sleeve pulling back just briefly, just enough to reveal the long, angry vertical scar up her wrist that disappears beneath the cloth. The gun's report is muted as we watch over her shoulder, face emotionless, without reaction – not even a flinch. "This is the story of how we got here. Because sometimes, the pain of our failure is all we have left to hold onto." The man's body jerks spasmodically, tumbling off the bench to the floor beneath the table. It is an ignominious, forgotten way to go. The serving girl steps briskly behind them, closing her razor with a smooth motion. The man's paramour daintily wipes at her hand with a red napkin, sliding to her feet on those treacherously tall heels. She probably says something witty. It goes unheard as the camera swings around to get a good look at the three of them. Kathryn Shaw and Drusilla Devonshire – lover and knife-wielder respectively – stand a stride behind the motorcyclist. Alexandra Pierce's stony expression is marred with tears she never seems willing to shed. Somewhere nearby, the schoolgirl turns away, her damage done in the form of a video, wrenching off a plaited wig. A forest of tiny lavender braids springing to life around her scalp as Lauren Fox disappears into the stairwell. "And because sometimes, you begin with the epilogue." Back at Pure, Devonshire hooks her thumb into the belt loop of Pierce's dark pants, an affectionate gesture that the Spider does not return. She unscrews the silencer of her pistol, her stony gray gaze locked onto the camera's viewpoint. We spiral into the gaze, into those pained eyes, into the woman's head. And into the past, shot from above, as the woman they call Desade awakens in a start, her arms around another redhead. Fear shoots through her, doubt follows like a jackal on its heels. She does not sleep the rest of the night, her own whispers haunting her. Flicker-fade down, the final words printed on the screen, written in shadowy red. DESADE. GTT7. Heads, I win. Tails, you fall.
