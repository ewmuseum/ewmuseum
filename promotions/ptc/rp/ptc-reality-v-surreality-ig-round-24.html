Reality V. Surreality (IG Round 24)

24 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“I don't believe in a God that I need to worship... I don't believe that I need to get down on my knees... I don't believe a voice from above can help me... I only believe in what I can see and the things that I can achieve...” - Lyrics from “Two Sides” by Clawfinger It's said that when you pay for your ticket, and go to the arena you have the right to speak your mind at a show. If you love something, scream; if you hate it, make sure to let out a long, loud jeer. Wrestling promoters have played off the passion wrestling fans show when they spend their hard earned money since the beginning of this industry. Every single wrestler knows that the most important thing when you're out there is to get a reaction. For years now, Bobino seemed to have found the secret to getting a reaction from a crowd. The funny part is, he never even tried. For years now, there have been a large section of the wrestling fan base that hated Bobino, not for what he did, or because he was the bad guy, but simply for who he was, and what he believed. To a large majority of the world, actually, Bobino insulting the most important part of a person's life, religion. Higher powers, gods, life givers whatever they were called, Bobino saw them as foolish, and without any merit. He wasn't the type to actively argue with the crowd, he was more of the type to completely disregard the fact that they existed. On this night, a group aimed to make themselves impossible to ignore. “ARE YOU MOTHERFUCKERS READY?!?! This Is The New Shit!!” The words echoed through the small arena as Bobino parted to black curtain and stepped in front of the capacity crowd. He had a self-center grin tattooed across his face as he stood in front of the people, looking oblivious to their jeers. Marilyn Manson's “This Is The New Shit” played to the crowd, as he walked down the isle, having garbage thrown at him at one point. Bobino chuckled to himself, knowing he hit a chord with somebody. He turned to smirk in the face of whomever threw the trash, and continued to move down to ringside. Bobino wasn't actually schedule to compete today, he was going to sit at ringside and join the commentary team for a bit of play-by-play. He made himself comfortable next to Hunter Jones and Scott Ambrose, FUSE's color commentary team. As Bobino continued with his usual banter with the announcers, it became apparent that a small grouping of people behind the announcers table was not please with the way “The Master Of Darwinism” had been handling himself. Not a problem, he's the heel, he's supposed to get under your skin. These people, however, had no qualms with the “Character” of Bobino. As Bobino and Hunter Jones made jokes back and forth, trying to ignore the increasingly louder distraction behind them, Bobino felt a hand grasp his shoulder. Ok, so it wasn't just one hand, it was about four or five. Three of the protesters behind the table decided to get up-close and personal with their own version of evil. Now, some people would've reacted violently here, but Bobino was smart enough to realize they weren't trying to hurt him, they were merely trying to get their point across. As the security team came over to restraint the zealots, they were dragged away, repeating different things. The tallest of the three men continued to say the name of the church they represented. “ His Father's Holy House! His Father's Holy House! His Father's Holy House!” he screamed as he was being taken away. The smaller of the two men that made the cross was in denial that he was being taken away, refusing to go without a struggle. That's not what caught Bobino's eyes, ears, and thoughts. The last of the three ex-audience members was a woman. She appeared mostly normal, for a religious zealot who protested at live wrestling events. He eyes were locked dead with Bobino's, and barely louder than a whisper, she simply repeated his name and told him. “We can help you. We can save you. May God have mercy on your soul.” Now, he wouldn't think anything of this, except she didn't call him his in-ring name. She looked him dead in the eyes and demanded attention from Robert Terryo. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The over-sized doors eclipsed him almost as if he were nothing to this giant structure. The rain poured around the building, making this one of the few times Bobino didn't look out of place in a trench coat. He looked around, the lights weren't on, the rain caused everything to look even more pale in the small bit of moonlight that shone down on him. He looked again at the sign in the front. “His Fathers' Holy House” it read. This was the place. He stood for a moment, in awe, not of the structure or pure size of the building, but of the building itself. He stood, wondering why that woman was able to pull at his inner drive so fiercely. Inevitably he stood there, wondering exactly what he planned on doing here. He took one last look, as he slowly opened the doors, and escaped the harshness of nature. Bobino wasn't ignorant. He knew what the inside of a church looked like. He had seen the pictures and descriptions of the pews and the stained glass windows. Problem was, nowhere in the picture and nowhere in the description, did it actually prepare you for seeing the dim moonlight splinter through the color-glass for the first time. Bobino immediately felt more out of place than ever. He contemplated turning around, and going back from where he came. No harm had been done yet, nobody knew he had been there. He struggled with that decision for a few moments, until it was finally made for him. He turned and saw an all too familiar face staring back at him. The soft eyes, the innocent (or as he would describe it, normal) tone to her face. She smiled, realizing that her message had gotten through, loud and clear. For a moment, he felt uneasy. This was all playing out too much like some bad horror movie for him. He tensed up, not wanting to be caught off guard by a hidden attacker. It's amazing what the soft touch of another being can do to calm you down. Her hand rested on his shoulder, as she offered him a seat in one of the churches pews. She realized how horrid the weather was outside, and offered him a cup of coffee to warm him up. Unfortunately for her, Bobino had never been one to accept charity. The ensuing conversation was more than awkward. Bobino honestly had no idea what he had hoped to achieve by showing up there, and Mary, the girl who drew him there, had no intentions of shying away from his or her beliefs. “Why do you insist on insulting God and his people?” she asked. If this question had come from anyone else, he'd think he was being undermined, or belittled, but the look in her eyes showed genuine concern and honesty. “I don't insult anything. I simply believe that life, and everything in life came from somewhere. I look at scientific proof that link human beings to other primates, and can honestly believe in the evolution of mankind. It's not insulting. If anything, it's a compliment to human kind, and how we've made strives to survive.” His answer flew past Mary's understanding, and simply caused her to question further. “Why can't you accept the truths given to us in the Bible? Why do you second guess God? And Why do you insist on doing these things on television, where the impressionable youth can hear it?” Her questions were getting more and more defensive, and this was feeling less and less comfortable. “Mary, maybe a better question is how can you sit back and take what's been spoon fed to you from your elders? How can you not require proof to believe something. How can you live your life by the guidelines of a book that has as much of a factual background as a Brother's Grimm Fairy Tale?” His voice was raising. He didn't like his belief in Darwinism being questioned and belittled. He also didn't like how she tried to manipulate him into being friendly. She looked at him, speechless. Her eyes started to swell, and it was apparent that her breaths were shortening. “Sister Ethel was right. She told us you were an evil man. She told us that you spoke the lies of the devil. We didn't want to believe it. We thought we could see goodness in your heart. We thought that maybe, just maybe, you were lost. I hoped that if I brought you here, in God's own house, and explained to you the error in your thinking, you'd understand, and let that goodness shine through. Leave this place, and may the God in his infinite wisdom have mercy upon your lost soul.” Her emotions got the best of her as she began to weep. She stood and started to walk away, leaving Bobino to show himself out. He watched her run up the stairs, as he shook his head and left the building. Bobino woke up the next morning, wondering if he might've dreamt the entire fiasco. Everything just seemed so surreal; there's no way it could've been real. He continued with his morning routine as normal, making his breakfast and watching SportsCenter. He tried to put the entire memory behind him as he watched the replays from the prior nights New England Patriots game. Bobino moved on with his day, and his week, and eventually his career. No mention of the religious fan attack was ever made, but then again, when fans got where they weren't supposed to, that was usually the case. Bobino never questioned management or security about arrests or the whatnot. The whole scene was mostly forgotten about. Except on those nights, when Bobino would fall asleep with the religious channel on the TV. His dreams of glory, women, and happiness were haunted. In his sleep, he could still see those innocent eyes, and that concerned voice, asking why he did what he did, and why he thought how he thought. He would always brush those dreams off as a recurring nightmare though, because how else are the strong supposed to survive?
