"When The Words Refuse to Coalesce" [Jacob McKail/FUSE]

13 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Last Night La Doche Restaurant Chicago, Illinois Caroline glided into the bar with all the grace of a world-lass Ballet dancer and Jacob McKail found himself hopelessly unable to wrench his eyes away from her undeniably curvaceous figure. The black dress she wore clung to her majestically, sufficiently highlighting all the things that sent a grown man wild and it took all McKail had just to control himself. She drew nearer, her pristine beauty finally coming into focus and he found himself hurtling back through time to his very first date with his late wife Mary, Caroline’s older sister, some ten years earlier. It was her face, it was the look in her eyes, it was the way she talked, the way she walked, the way she… No! He silently screamed, stopping himself from falling any deeper. This is NOT your wife! This is Mary’s baby sister! She’s family, you sick fuck… Caroline followed the Maitre D to the table, whereupon he gentlemanly pulled out the chair for her sit. McKail idly wondered whether he should’ve been the one to make such a gesture, but instead got caught up staring at her sultry red lipstick. Does that mean, what I think it means? He silently questioned, raising a speculatively eyebrow. No, it couldn’t, it shouldn‘t… “Hello, Jacob,” she greeted with a smile. She established eye contact with him and the unmistakable glint in her eyes, betrayed her otherwise innocent face and told McKail more than he ever wanted to know. “Uhm hello Caroline.” She noted his recoil and sighed. “Why did you ever agree to meet me?” Good question. McKail had no answer for her, at least nothing that was remotely truthful. “I’m the new FUSE Universal champion,” he said. “I think that warrants some kind of celebration.” A twinge of guilt shot up from his stomach and burrowed its way into his chest. It was the same feeling he always got whenever he was dishonest with Mary and that worried him a lot more than anything else. *** Two Weeks Ago Van Andel Arena Grand Rapids, Michigan People came and people went, but it was all the same to McKail. Time had stopped for him and he found that he didn’t much care. He was caught up in the moment and he never wanted it to end. Sat on the locker room bench and leaning forward, he had his head lowered staring at it in wonder and awe. He had been for a long time now. The reality had yet to sink in, but it was beginning to. The shiny metallic surface covered his face with a strange golden tint and the reflection from the FUSE Universal title allowed McKail to look himself right in the eye. It’d been a long time since he’d been able to do that, yet he still found the prospect somewhat unnerving. It’d been a year since Mary died; three hundred and ninety days, fourteen hours and twelve minutes to be painstakingly exact and still he was ashamed of himself. On her deathbed, he promised her the world, he promised her that he would live his life, that he would achieve his goals and capture his dreams. Mary couldn’t understand that she was his life, she was his goal and she was his dream. Nevertheless, he suppressed all of the pain, all of the anger, all of the grief and went about his vow nonetheless, never allowing the bitter kiss of failure to even enter into the equation. It was a recipe for disaster, that he now understood, and the consequent downward spiral he found himself trapped in only served to make things worse. His wrestling career was at a dead-end and his promise to his late wife wasn’t worth the air it was delicately whispered upon. Then came the PTC GTT tournament. Then came reaching the bracket finals. Then came the astounding victory of the already established superstar, Clinton Sage. Then came the PTC Infinite Gauntlet victories. And finally, then came the FUSE Universal Championship belt. A tear extracted itself from his eye and dripped down, pointedly, upon the gold that served illuminate the immediate area. He’d done it. He’d reached the top of the food chain. He’d carved himself out a place in the FUSE history books. He’d established himself as a major player in the wrestling industry. But was it really enough? In achieving all of this, he lost his dignity, he lost his sense of being and most of all he lost any chance of actually having a life. He lived on the road, in seclusion and very often inebriated to the point of universal numbness. Was this really what Mary had in mind for him? A life of solitude, pushing away of those people who threatened to care for him? He already knew the answer. McKail scooped up the Universal title, and fastened it around his waist, before heaving up his kit bag with his hand. Edging towards the exit, he shot the elderly janitor a brief nod in appreciation and opened the locker room door, whereupon he was met by the majority of the FUSE technical staff and even a few of the more friendly workers waiting for him. With a furrowed brow, he glared at the faces of those closest to him in turn, before asking the question. “What the hell is going on here?” A number of scenario’s immediately shot through McKail’s head. Is this it? Is this the attack I‘ve been waiting for? Is this Sage’s revenge? He can’t beat me in the ring, so he’s got to beat me the only way he knows how -- behind closed doors and in greater numbers? The man closest to him smiled and began to clap emphatically. Soon enough, all of the others in attendance joined his motion and McKail contorted his face further in confusion. None of this made any sense. He wasn’t exactly the friendliest person on the roster, hell most of the time he was downright ignorant, so why all of this? The claps turned into cheers and the crowd encroached him, patting him on the back and shaking his hand. Bewildered, McKail pushed his way through the throng of obvious admirers and headed toward the parking lot. However, the more he waded, the quicker it struck him just how many people were actually there. He couldn’t see the end and suddenly he became claustrophobic. “What is all this about?” He ventured, hoping to acquire some kind of answer. “You beat him!” A whimpering voice from the crowd offered. “You finally beat him!” Another repeated. “Yeah, fuck Clinton Sage!” A third prompted. “Fuck him in the ass!” “McKail! McKail! McKail! McKail! McKail! McKail! McKail!” A smile snaked across McKail’s face and this time, he didn’t even try to hide it. He was beginning to feel again, he was beginning to dismantle the long-standing barriers between him and the rest of the world. Now this is what Mary wanted him to be and the man she wanted him to become. Perhaps that’s why he accepted Caroline’s offer of companionship when she called him. *** Last Night La Doche Restaurant Chicago, Illinois “You’ve changed,” Caroline told him, taking a sip of her dry white wine and casting her eyes back over the italicised French menu. McKail glared up from his own menu and glared at her questioningly. “Changed?” “Yes,” she responded with a smile. She touched his hand. “I like it.” McKail glanced at the hand resting on top of his own and then into her eyes. She removed the hand and placed back around the stem of her wine glass. “How do you mean ‘changed’?” “I still watch the show, Jacob,” she told him. McKail’s eyes widened. “Hey, if this is about that Klu Klux Klan thing, then that wasn’t me, I--” McKail paused, feeling the fierce glare of an African-American couple sitting on the next table over burn a hole in the side of his head. “It was planted there, by that self-righteous fuckwit, Hoyt Williams!” The couple shook their heads dismissively and went about their meal, leaving McKail to wonder whether they actually believed him or not. After all, on last week’s edition of Killzone, Hoyt did manage to persuade David Bowie that he was a racist. “No,” Caroline said, with a brief chuckle. “That’s not what I mean.” McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. “Oh?” “I mean, the way you interact with other people backstage,” she explained, “You are actually trying to be nice, aren’t you? You‘re trying to fit in…” McKail shifted uncomfortably in his seat and looked away. “I don’t know what you mean.” Caroline chuckles. “But you seem to be having a hard time admitting it…” McKail remained silent, his eyes darting around nervously, looking anywhere except at her. Caroline took both of his hands gently and began to rub them tenderly with her thumbs. “Jacob,” she began, “look at me.” Reluctantly, he did. “You don’t have to hide anything from me. I know this must be difficult for you, but I can help you through it, if you’d let me.” She leaned closer and kissed McKail briefly on the lips. “Will you let me?” You want this, you dumb fuck, he silently screamed, but you can’t have it! You know how wrong this is. You know you can never be the man she or Mary want you to be. So, stop pretending. Stop leading her on you bastard… McKail silently removed his hands and stood, wrenching his eyes away from her in shame. He fished in his pocket and extracted a handful of scrunched up dollar bills. “Jacob, it’s okay,” Caroline responded, also standing. “I know this isn’t going to happen overnight. I know this will take time…” Don’t do this to yourself, man, he thought, as he threw the dollar bills down on the table. You can’t keep putting yourself through this! She’s your wife’s sister for god’s sake! Isn‘t this illegal? “I can’t,” he told her. He wanted to tell her everything, he wanted to just share all of his fears and hopes right then and there, but the words refused to coalesce. “I just can’t…” He left and Caroline knew him well enough not to follow.
