What Love Can Do, Part 2

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 The incident went about so quickly that it became more of a blurred vision than a photographic memory. The tattered and torn man pulled himself free of Sebastian’s grasp and leapt onto the path of the oncoming white 1996 Tahoe. Sebastian and Aaron looked in horror and helplessness as he landed on the cement with the SUV running over him in a sickening thud. However, their horror could not match the horror that the driver felt when she ran over this helpless man. She screeched a few yards away from the accident and at twenty years of age she had killed someone. Because one man could not deal with the loss of Love, he made others miserable and ended his own life. Once again, Love proved to be our most hindering emotion. On the Road Again Sebastian Moraco, the best amateur wrestler in the state of New York, slid down on his seat onboard Greyhound and lowered his New York Yankees ball cap just over his eyes. Underneath his cap, he wore black Sony earphones listening to his Spanish music as he took a rest on his trip to D.C. He insisted on taking the bus alone, and refused to take his car or to drive with Aaron and Stacy. Not that he was mad at them, or refused to drive interstate without a license, but he felt like he needed to do this alone. This was his match to win. This was his work to do. His friend had always been there for him, whether he wanted them or not. This time though, their presence would only be hindering. Sebastian scoffed looking uncomfortable in his seat. He still could not get the gruesome image of the man being flattened on the street. He couldn’t forget the stench of the corpse as the blood spewed forth from underneath and the screeching screams of onlookers who were too terrified to do anything else. Just moments earlier he had talked to the man. He had tried to console. Tried to tell him that his life wasn’t worth giving up for losing a loved one, no matter how precious they were. Sebastian was at a wreck when his dreams were washed up in bribes, corruption, and urinalysis testing. Yet, he did not once look at suicide for an answer. Obviously his lost was a lot different than a man losing his girlfriend, or worse his fiancée expecting their first unborn son. Such losses, Sebastian had been fortunate to not deal with. He knew that people experience losses worse than him everyday of their lives. It’s a sickening world we live in, but we all have to make the best of it, and the best of ourselves. No matter what we lose, we still have ourselves. But, if we lose ourselves, then we are nothing. Moraco hated the notion of suicide and euthanasia. He never understood why anyone would willingly end his or her lives regardless of the situation. To have such limited love and worth of yourself was worse than any death, raping, or beating you could have. Throw yourself to the Devil, and you might as well kill yourself. What benefits would be reaped from killing yourself? You end the pain, your own suffering, and you relieve yourself of the harsh memories you had to endure. You end all your miseries at the cost of putting them on someone else. Did your late fiancée really wish for you to kill yourself? Do you think your unborn son would want to watch you from heaven and see that the man he would have been proud to call father kill himself in a moment of selfishness and weakness. Suicide is misleading. People are led to believe that it ends the pain and misery. It only makes it stronger. It only gives it more room to expand. Sure, you end your life and you put to shame all the love that your friends and family had for you. You waste the chance that your maker gave you, and you spit on the graves of the loved ones you lost. The loved ones who would have wanted you to move on, because they loved you. They would have wanted you to succeed as they watched over you from Heaven. Time is everything, and in time you would have been with them. If happiness cannot be found on Earth, it can be found in Heaven. However, you waste that chance and you make others around you endure your pain now. The man Sebastian and Aaron had dealt with earlier in the week did the same. Even if they only knew him for a brief moment before his heinous act, they could still feel the sickening churns inside their stomachs. However, it was not they that the man’s suicide affected most. It was the young female driver who could not break in time that was affected the most. She was only twenty years of age, never been in a major accident or had to deal with loss, and yet she had the image of a man she knew nothing about branded in her mind. She had become distraught, and everyone knew she would never be the same again. The man thought he would be ending his own pain and misery. But, in reality, he only made it worse. He put the pain and misery on others, especially one girl who now had to deal with the fact and imagery of running s helpless man over. Sebastian took his earphones off and sat up straight. The ride was a bit bumpy to get comfortable with, and he couldn’t sleep with the thought of the man’s death still lingering in the air, either. In his hand, he still carried the letter that the tournament committee gave him announcing his official re-entry into the tournament. He failed once before, and he knew that if he wanted the exposure and courting of the top trainers in the nation, that he could not fail again. He had already defeated a worthy opponent, P. Vetra, a man who hailed from PRIME, a place known for their outstanding talents. Now his eyes were set on the next name down the list on the piece of paper. Sebastian traced his eyes down pass all the formal rules and regulations onto the announcement of his next opponent. “Digital Mortality.” He read to himself. Hailing from the UWF, Digital Mortality was one of the so-called favorites to get far in the tournament. He had suffered a setback in the first run, had been unable to make the tournament. A hurricane in his hometown was the culprit, and the committee knew it could not deal with the loss of a crowd-getter this early in the tournament. Thus, the committee gave him a second chance as well, and like Moraco, Digital Mortality wasted none of it. Digital Mortality was going to be a hard opponent, Sebastian thought. The information [name withheld] gave him only kept getting worse and worse. Digital Mortality was practically running the show in the UWF, and now had his eyes set on winning the GTT. Moraco had been in similar situations before. He had been paired up against opponents who had no business losing to him, but somehow they did. Digital Mortality definitely had the experience, and some say talent, over him. When the betting spreads came out, Digital Mortality was heavily favored. “How the hell can I beat this guy?” Sebastian wondered. “He’s one of the best remaining in the tournament and I do not have half the experience this guy has.” Sebastian merely shook his head and put his invitation away neatly folded in his dark blue jeans pocket. Jeff had told him that Digital Mortality was an impatient and short-tempered man. He did not give anyone a chance to explain themselves if anything went wrong. With all the talent and experience that he had over Sebastian, it looked like his short-temper would be his only weakness. The only question remained was how to handle it. Digital Mortality didn’t seem like the guy who would grow weak at the thought of loss. The man Sebastian met earlier was weak and didn’t live his life. He gave it away. Digital Mortality was nothing like that guy, Sebastian thought. There was no way Digital would succumb to suicide, or the mere thought of it. Even if he had himself lost a potential wife and an unborn son in a car accident, he would never resort to suicide. That would be wrong, and that would be wasting your time. “No, Digital Mortality is not that weak.” Sebastian though. “Digital Mortality would never resort to suicide as an answer.” Washington D. C. Sebastian Moraco arrived in Washington D.C., earlier in the day. D. C. was the city designated as the host of the tournament by the GTT committee. There were many tourists and visitors walking about the city when Sebastian arrived. Unlike them, however, he wasn’t there to get a picture of the Lincoln Memorial or the Washington Monument. He wanted no tours, and no one could guess why he and dozens others were here. D. C. was the site of the third round, and beyond, of the GT tournament. Soon, Sebastian hoped, D. C. would be the site of his third round victory. Having already checked in his belongings into the private suite provided for him by the committee, Sebastian decided to check out the training facilities the tournament had reserved for its competitors. The brochures and pamphlets he had received from Jeff, his official sponsor for the tournament, quoted the facilities as being topnotch. It was definitely going to be worthwhile to check out the gym and get a light workout in before his match with Antonia Vargas, better known as Digital Mortality. The fight was going to be soon and Sebastian was determined to win. He wanted to win this match to prove his abilities, but he could not do so if he took things too lightly. Once inside, he took awe at the size of the gym and the many attractions it held. He took a quick gaze around trying to see if any of the other fighters were present, but none of them were. It made sense, he thought. Most of them were more famous than celebrities. You had mad scientists, girls with flamboyant hair, Mafioso who returned from the dead, former hit men, and even drug dealers. Worst then that, a few of them were on the “ugly” side of the law, and being in crowds disgusted them. It made sense to Sebastian that none of the other fighters would be seen here, or anywhere else for that matter. One can only imagine the screams of joy the crowds would have as they tried to get an autograph or a picture taken with someone like Karina Wolfenden, or Seymour Almasy, even if he was lacking in height. Then again, the image of screaming girls paralyzed in fear as they watched a tall man with blonde locks and baby blue eyes smirk at them. Yeah, some people were never meant to be seen in public. “I’m glad you could make it.” A hand touched Sebastian on the shoulder. Sebastian turned around to see his assistant coach, [name withheld], standing next to him. “Can you just feel the competitiveness in this city? To think in a few days, that the third round is going to be here is just mouth watering.” “Yeah…” Sebastian said nonchalantly. He wanted to win as much as anyone else, maybe more. Yet, he still did not share the same kind of enthusiasm that Jeff seemed to endure. It was odd though, Sebastian was the one who was fighting, and it was for him that the benefits would go to. As far as Sebastian knew, he was the one who was going to reap the rewards. Thus, Jeff’s enigmatic enthusiasm seemed to be perplexing. “I do hope that you aren’t taking this match lightly, Moraco.” Jeff asked in stern manner. “Digital Mortality won’t just not show up for the match. He will be there with the intention of winning and no less.” “I know…” Sebastian responded totally not wanting to be lectured about Digital Mortality. “I’m serious, Sebastian.” Jeff continued. “You have to be on your toes for this one. You realize that no one is giving you a chance in any of the fights you’ve been in? Well, you win this one and I can almost guarantee you a few minds will be changed and a few eyes reopened.” Sebastian sighed in obvious disgust. “Yeah, I know how great he is. You don’t need to keep reminding me about it. He’s far more developed a fighter than I am. He has the talent and experience, and as far as I can tell seems to want to win this as much as I do. That doesn’t mean he will.” “Yeah…” “Yeah, nothing.” Sebastian continued. “I’ve dealt with challengers who were supposed to be a lot better than I am, and I’ve beaten them all. There are differences here that are evident in the environment we are in now, but the end result remains the same. He’s just another formidable opponent I have to deal with. I like my chances. You aren’t helping me by these continued lectures of how great he is.” “But…” Sebastian raised his index finger to his lips motioning for Jeff to be silent. “But nothing. As my coach, you’re supposed to help me find weaknesses and ways to win. Not reprimand me for doing things my ways and leaving you out of them. You’re not supposed to make him look unbeatable and question my abilities. I’m well aware of how good he is and don’t think I will underestimate him. But I’m good too, as you know, and I want to win this more than he does. I have my own methods and they do not and will not involve you. This is my tournament. Remember that? This is my match to win. I’m fighting for my second chance at the Olympics. Not you. You’re not here to regain your glory. You aren’t here trying to fight off 192 of the best fighters in the world, just so you can get some exposure. You aren’t here trying to prove the critics wrong at every round. This is my tournament. This is my match!” Sebastian Moraco left him without properly dismissing himself. [name withheld] stood in the same spot mesmerized for a moment. Not that he felt embarrassed for what happened, he was in awe of it all. He had wanted Sebastian to explode like that before and was hoping it would be soon. “He’s finally beginning to understand.” Jeff smiled. It took a misfortune in the first round and getting to the third round against a formidable opponent in Digital Mortality to open Sebastian’s eyes. Now he had regained the fire in his eyes that he lost a few months ago. The last time Sebastian had that desire in his eyes, he won the New York State Championships. A feat that is by means a comparison to the GTT. Nonetheless, he now intends to do the same here and win the GTT. After all…this is HIS tournament.
