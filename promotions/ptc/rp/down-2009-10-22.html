"Down"

22 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-22 “Motherfucker! I said stop!” A booming voice echoed like a series of waves down the long corridor of an alley, growing rapidly in power every time it reverberated from stone to stone. His footsteps mirrored his breaths, everything in time, smooth, efficient. The hunter chased its quarry, young, black, through the urban jungle, past knocked over trash cans and through the refuse that profanely littered the streets. For his part, Chris Mathias played the gazelle, dodging back and forth and hopping over obstacles that would slow the larger predator. He could feel his heart pulsing from within, rhythmically snapping like a snare drum. As he rounded a corner, a solid stone wall stood in front of him, a monolithic deterrent. The police officer cleared the corner rapidly and confidently drew his weapon, leveling it at the young man’s chest. The bullet begged to send its message home. Even so, the officer was trained not to heed his instincts, no matter what. The officer twitched his firearm and Chris stepped up to the wall splaying his arms and legs wide. “You know what this is, don’t you shit-bird? Point four-zero inches of copper jacketed lead backed by one hundred grains of powder.” He paused and ran his hands across the deep pocketed pants of Chris. “Let me tell you a secret son, out here you aint the law, I am.” The noise of metal ratcheting together echoed in the alley as Chris’s hands were cuffed. The young man’s head dropped slowly, deflated of any ego, and he is turned forcefully back toward the waiting mouth of the squad car. “Hear that boy?” the officer leaned into his ear. God his breath stank. The young man had to wonder what the officer was playing at. It was too quiet in the alley. Perhaps the uproar created by the chase drove the other animals away. “That’s the sound of nobody giving a fuck that a piece of shit like you is going down.” Darkness washed over Chris Mathias, and it burned at him like gunpowder lit on an open wound. Five years is an eternity when you are eighteen. His head bumped the top of the door jamb in the squad, and brilliant bolts of red and blue light streaked all around them. The car door slammed away the last breath of his home, and he felt the handcuffs as they gripped tighter than even his momma could. It wasn’t supposed to be him. It should have been another guy, not Chris. He was supposed to be down. 4 A.M. EST. – April 20th 2009, Five years later. Look up in the sky Look up in the sky You thought I was down You thought I was gone That I went around Thought I left you alone But look up in the sky Just look up in the sky See that I'm everywhere Shining down on you. Rain dropped against the window just above Christian Mathias’s bed. The incessant buzzing of his cellular phone along with the smooth intensity of Lupe Fiasco’s “Shining Down” called his name early on a Tuesday morning. He reached up absently to grasp it, looking at the picture displayed on the small touch screen. Chanté was calling. Sweet memories of a girl that used to hang around his block brought a thin smile to Christian’s face. She was the type of girl men round-a-ways drove up and down the street for, yelling catcalls at her and her friends when they were walking on the pained sidewalks. The button on the screen clicked under the weight of his finger. “New shit that each nigga on the street needs if they wanna be taken seriously,” his old friend Malik would have called it. “Hey, what’s up girl?” “How come you up so early Chris?” she said softly. “Christian,” He corrected her gently. “Well, I was mostly sitting up listening to the rain and watching the lightning, hoping you would call me.” He lied to her, but it wasn’t really a lie. “Nah, you was sleepin’,” Her playful voice lilted a bit as she poked at him carefully. “You was thinkin’ of some other girl.” Christian laughed a little, his voice carrying through his small loft apartment. “Hey baby, let me dream some more then,” he teased Chanté playfully, “and I’ll get her name for you. Then you can drop by and show that girl what a woman like you can do to a man like me.” She smiled. Even though Christian couldn’t see it, he always knew because Chanté always took a few extra seconds to say anything after he made her smile. “Okay,” she teased the air. “How bout you pick me up tomorrow night then? Eight?” Damn, she really cut to the point. He breathed out slowly, laying flat on his back, mapping the roadway of cracks in the ceiling tiles. “What about Malik? You sure he aint gonna get mad?” “Nah, if he wanted to keep me around,” Chanté paused slightly, “He knows he gotta treat a woman better than that.” The hurt in her voice was clear. She had spent a long time running from the men that hurt her. Every one of them that she got close to eventually dropped her into a pit. The roads converged at one spot, and even though it was rust colored from occasional water droplets invading his apartment, it was still the place that they came together. He traced a line in the air with his finger, pointing directly to that union before finally pursing his lips and whistling a low tune. He closed his eyes and imagined Chanté in the bed with him. “Yeah. Tomorrow.” Nine A.M. EST. The agreement for Christian’s early release from prison (following a conviction for possession with intent to sell) included community service. He had found a mentor in Paul LaVelle, an older man from the neighborhood that had a very similar past. Paul never bothered Christian for playing his music a little too loud at night, and let him sleep in the loft room above the local youth center on North Monroe Street. Christian had only been there for a few months, and LaVelle knew it was critical to keep him entertained. Freshly released men tend to want for lives that should have left them behind long ago. Christian helped where it was necessary. He was actually pretty adept as an athlete. Had he been from upstate a bit more and had he not grown up with his mind diseased by urban life, Paul often thought that he could have been All-State. Some of the local youth that were too young to be in gangs, but old enough to feel the influence of them, liked to come in and take lessons in different forms of self defense from Paul, who was an accomplished Judo practitioner. Christian occasionally helped him teach a class, and learned more than a few defensive maneuvers. Paul and Christian were of a different mold though. Christian was taller than Paul, and liked to wear his jeans low, his shoes bore the Nike “swoosh”, and sometimes he would trim messages into his hair. Paul was an old school G, and loved to tell everyone how lucky they were that they didn’t have to grow up in the era of N.W.A. and Public Enemy. “Rappers told the message of the street back then,” he used to tell Christian when he visited him in prison. “But us dumb fucks didn’t know that being from the streets and being hard wasn’t what it was all about. We missed the point they were trying to make until Tupac and Biggie got plucked. Then shit got real around here.” “Used to be a homey on every corner looking at you like you owe him something.” Paul liked to live in the past. “Now it isn’t any different. Cosby had it right when he said that young black men just don’t get it. All the G’s have been replaced by ignorant mother fuckers that can’t seem to do anything without shooting at one another.” That was about as poetic as Paul liked to get in the morning. Fuck, that was about as poetic as Paul ever got. But he was from a different time, when at least you had pride in your colors and it wasn’t just a bunch of mush-mouthed fuckheads in do-rags playing Hogan’s Alley outside the walls of the youth center. Christian was still buzzing a little from the call earlier. He hadn’t bothered to sleep. Instead he spent the next few hours listening to music on his Iphone and watching the outside world tumble and roar as the storm passed by. Paul asked him to go outside and wash some graffiti off the walls of the building, so he filled a bucket with water and soap and a scrub pad before walking out to see what Picasso had left for him this morning. He read the name “Drag” in giant opaque red, yellow, and orange lettering. Sometimes Christian liked to imagine “Drag,” a man who had enough free time to become an artist with nothing but a mottled blend of mostly warm colors. The rain had not deterred the graffiti artist. Instead they chose to tag the building on a fairly dry portion that hid under an awning. The bucket fell to the pavement with a jostle and a metallic clank noise, and then slowly the handle squealed as it fell to a rest on the side of the container. Christian grabbed a spongeful of soap and water and began to play art critic on “Drag’s” bubble letters. After ten minutes of hard scrubbing, almost half the mural was cleaned off the wall. Christian hummed a tune in his head, sometimes spitting short bursts of rhymes as though he were trying to tie his tongue around a beat. His attention was drawn by a vehicle as it rounded a corner. Christian turned his head up and looked down the block, spotting a heart-of-The-Devil black Chrysler 300C coming his way. The sponge dropped into the bucket and caused a bit of water to splash over the edge. Christian wrung his hands dry on a towel, and then slung it over his shoulder casually. To his left, the windows shook under the beat of subwoofers as though they were frightened of the approaching car. Double parking, the demonic Chrysler eased to a stop, and then rolled forward a few inches after being shut off. A door slowly opened, and out stepped a specter that Christian had long since left in another life. “Hey, hey, is that my nigga?” The man lowered his two toned sunglasses and then raised them back up, casting a perfect smile that reflected in multiple shades of precious metal back at Christian. “Shré́, this is the kid I been tellin’ you bout. This mutha fucka is one down-ass nigga.” A man that looked to be well on his way to seven feet tall stepped out of the drivers side door and cast a glance over to the man standing on the passenger side of the car. “This kid? You sure M.T.?” Shré’s glare was definitely not fucking around. Christian shook his head and reached back down for the sponge, trying to ignore the intrusion. “Yo, C-Note!” the passenger, M.T., shouted at Christian as he began to scrub at the wall again. “Hey. C-NOTE.” He was getting agitated. “Yo Chris, you aint got no time for me now?” Christian bit his upper lip slightly and turned back to the vehicle. Malik Tarver, aka M.T., was leaning on the hood of his car wearing a G-Unit knee length tee shirt, pants that defied gravity, several different flash-bulb studded chains, and hair that was formed into rows of braids and pulled tight to his head. “Hey Malik. Is that your car?” “What? Muh-Fucker, you know it is,” Malik smiled again. “Aint nobody call me by that name anymore. Niggas ‘round here call me M.T. now.” His leering gaze sent a small chill through Christian as he picked up the bucket and slowly walked closer to the car. “Yeah, well people around here don’t call me C-Note anymore,” he chipped in. “I just go by Christian.” M.T. stood up and nodded. His head moved back and forth only slightly, a gesture of respect, but also one of contemplation in what to say next. “Well, old habit’s aint easy to kill man.” “Amen” Christian nodded. “Hey man, what you doin’ tonight?” M.T. asked. Fuck. Malik was one of Christian’s oldest friends, but he never started a conversation with those words without some form of compensation coming his way. Still, Christian knew he wasn’t the type of person that would accept no for an answer, so he indulged the ambitions of his childhood neighbor. “Tonight? Nothing man. I don’t really do too much since I got out.” “Okay, so you comin’ with me then?” He looked over to the large man standing on the opposite side of the car. Shré́ wore a Ray Lewis jersey that barely concealed the fact that he had a .45 ACP tucked into his belt line. “My dawg Shré wants to hit up that Larry Flynt club down on East B-More. That aint but a couple blocks away.” “Christian,” he corrected. “I don’t know man. I gave that life up so I could live on the outside.” “Yeah, scraping some bullshit off some nigga’s wall.” M.T. walked over to Christian slowly and tapped against his friends shoulder as though he knew a secret. “That punk ass “Drag” aint even a real ass nigga anyhow. Just some punk who live down by yo’ old house named Clarence.” He held a fist out, and Christian looked down with a sullen longing to try his old life on for size. Just for one night. “Alright man, but I aint interested in C-Note anymore.” He bumped fists with M.T., and his best friend from days gone by slowly backed away. The smell of 50 Cent’s new cologne, “Power,” hung in the air like a harbinger. “Come on Shré, lets get up out this motha-fucka and go find some bitches for my man.” Malik paused and looked over his shoulder at the youth center. Paul LaVelle was watching through windows that had seen more than one storm pass by. He shot a wan smile at Christian’s mentor before closing the door on his whip and Shré drove it away, shaking the earth like a steamroller with their music. 10 P.M. EST. Bodies filled the club, and ground at one another like a mortar and pestle. Some of the girls working the floor were dancing for their customers, at a price of fifty dollars a song. Christian stepped through a large red double door, pulled out a piece of identification for the bouncer, and then took a moment to allow his eyes to adjust to the depravity. He smiled at a short girl as she walked by and gave him a once over with emerald green eyes that she hid behind long fake lashes. “Hey Chris!” M.T.’s voice cut through even the dancing flute melodies within Flo Rida’s “Low.” Christian bumped his head up and down with the beat, his eyes snapping left and right. On stage, a pair of girls dressed like police officers took turns arresting the intentions of the men sitting at the bar by committing uniform infractions. “Christian,” he corrected M.T. under his breath. He was getting sick of correcting people, though it was to be expected. He walked to the table with a pace that mirrored his nerves and peered at the men sitting before him. Shré flanked M.T. on the right while a new man sat on his left. This new man was the opposite of Shré. He was lean, very lithe looking, and his eyes hid a sort of danger behind them that the girls on stage were trying to sell wholesale. The new man kicked a chair out for Christian, and he sat down, trying not to be too intimidated with his surroundings. M.T. was the first to speak, as Christian assumed he would be. “Awright, wusup my nigga?” It was always niggas this and hos that. “You know Shré already.” He bumped the large man on the shoulder. Shré unfolded his thick arms and nodded. Christian returned the nod to the large man in the plain black shirt. “But you ain’t never met my boy Rabbit over here,” M.T. motioned to the smaller man, and Rabbit lowered his gaze a little, barely showing recognition of his introduction. “This muh-fucker make some of the best dope this side of the fuckin’ bay.” It didn’t please Christian to meet this man. Still, he had to be cordial. After all, he was in a world that had changed as much in the five years that he was gone as the girls that spun on the brass poles just behind him on the stage. “So what’s this all about M.T.?” Christian looked back to his friend. “Well nigga…what it is, is simple. You been gone a long time homey.” M.T. had a very real touch of emotion in his voice. “Things aint the same anymore bro. You remember how we used to hang at yo’ momma’s crib listenin’ to Ice Cube and shit on the boom-box and dreamin’ bout blowin’ up?” “Yeah, I remember.” Malik paused for a moment, taking a glass of Crown Royal in ice from a waitress wearing a sequined burgundy halter top. He sipped on it, slowly turning one of the hollowed ice cubes in his mouth before spitting it out. “Well I got shit now, and you my boy. I wanna give you a homecomin’ gift man, a way to help you get up out that shit hole you livin’ in and join the niggas on this side of the table.” “What kind of gift are we talking about?” The skeptical remark did not slip past M.T. unnoticed. “Nothin’ bro, just a little product.” He motioned to his right and Rabbit patted his jacket carefully. “Top grade shit. This stuff is as good as printin’ money round here. You can get whateva you want man, cars, guns, hoes. Fuck, my man Rabbit got two bitches to suck his dick in the BK bathroom last week for a couple teardrops.” “I see,” Christian replied and started to get up from the table. M.T. stood up as well, his G-Unit shirt falling down, revealing a pistol handle shaped lump just above his waistband. “Just like that nigga? You gonna embarrass me in front of my boys?” He reached for Christian’s arm before the young man could turn away. Christian pulled his arm away and adjusted the Sean Jean shirt he bought after their meeting in the morning. “Listen, I don’t mean to disrespect you M.T.,” Christian pulled the wallet out of his pants and dropped a fifty on the table. “Have a lap-dance on my dime and let’s just move on okay?” “But nigga, you don’t understand.” M.T.’s voice was thick, searing the words. “It’s a gift. And in old days, you know the shit we used to look up to, those niggas never disrespec’ a man by not takin’ his gift.” It became clear to Christian that Malik was acting hard for the sake of acting hard. Before the prison stint, they were inseparable. Still, something had to be done to assuage the situation because Christian was sure that the man standing before him had made a lot of changes in his life as well. For all he knew, Malik really no longer understood what it was like to scrape to get by, and having a little bread could be a very dangerous thing in the hood. “Okay, how about this,” Christian knew that the time to bargain had come. “You can give me a gift, but not the one you wanted to, and we can call it even.” “What you got in mind homey?” So far, so good. “Just let me be man. I can’t explain it to you, but being in prison made me a different man. I don’t really want those things that we used to talk about anymore.” He paused a bit, watching Shré and Rabbit for reactions. “We can be cordial man, you can come by the center and we’ll chill. But I ain’t about the game anymore M.T.” To his credit, M.T. didn’t break right away. It was pretty clear from his eyes that he never intended to hurt him. Still, something about the way that Rabbit looked through Christian gave him a shiver that the Baltimore night air couldn’t. All around them men were engrossed with the carnal pleasures of the world, and Rabbit couldn’t be bothered to even take his hand off of the small lump beneath the Orioles jacket that he had half buttoned up. “Aw-right. For you only, but…” he trailed that last word. “But I’m gonna warn you; I stay out yo’ business, and you stay out mine.” They bump fists again, this time Christian wraps one of his callused hands on Malik’s grip. “Are we cool?” “Yeah nigga, we cool. Now sit down Chris, you know I can’t be takin’ your money when it’s your party homey. Let’s get that sweet-assed honey you was staring at on the way in to come on up over here.” “It’s still Christian, M.T.” he reminded his old friend. “Old habits nigga, old habits.” 2 A.M. EST. Oct 21st 2009 The corner of the Baltimore ghetto at two in the morning isn’t the place one wants to be if they are trying to paint a picture of American life. The buildings were even more decayed than when he had left, some of them having lost any battle they ever fought. A small white house on his left was surrounded by a patch of dirt so bare that when the rain began ten minutes later, it had nothing left to protect it. Small puffs of dry terra escaped from the ground with every droplet. Around that yard, an overgrown chain link fence was beset by creeping nettles and rust. Even the road had stopped caring. Potholes were more prevalent than the faded yellow lines that once marked the lanes. So this was M.T.’s turf? This is what everyone died for around here? It looked to Christian as though the corner of Ramsay and Furrow was a bigger shit box now than it was when he was pinched. All around him, trash cans filled with tired black liners populated the streets where he and Malik used to dream. Light filtered through broken or stained windows from time to time. On one house, where the red clay brick was fading and crumbling at the corners, much like the old woman that sat behind a veiled curtain, the rays escaped and illuminated a series of brown pipes that ran along the side of the building. Christian wondered if Chanté was out there in a window waiting for him to call. In the distance, a gun shot several times. Probably small caliber.
