Don't Choose Drugs...

31 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-31 .:The collective murmurs from around the auditorium are to be expected. Playing to a group of disenchanted school children is something no teach relishes, let alone a non-teacher, but I stood there, ready and willing, about to try to make a difference to these kids and their lives. “Now, as part of our “Just Say No To Drugs” week, we have a very special guest...” .:The words trailed off into silence in my head. I am used to playing in front of crowd of thousands who will cheer at my every move, my every motion, but now, I am terrified. “A man with great personal experience of drugs and the damage they can do...” .:That is true, even though I wish it were not. I can feel the moisture beading on my hands as they get clammy from sweat, oh how I wish I was not here. “So, would you please remain silent for a man who you will soon be seeing on television every week, Mr Kirk Urban.” .:I stepped out onto the grandiose stage in front of a huge curtain not sure what to expect, but determined to do the best I could. “Thank you for that introduction.” .:I could feel their eyes burrowing into me, analysing every detail of my outfit, my style, stripping me to the bone with the clear cut precision of teenagers. “Now, before I begin, I figure it would be a good idea to introduce myself properly.” .:My English accent was going down a treat, I could tell that, to a group of Chicago school children, having a “Brit” standing here lecturing them must be lower than die on the “things to do before I’m thirty” list. “My name is, indeed, Kirk Urban, and as your principal said, I will soon be on your television, albeit every two weeks, I think your principal should do some research.” .: Ok, appeal to their sense of humour, should have made me feel more at ease, yet didn’t, oh dear. “I may regret asking this, but can anyone guess what show I will be on?” .:No-one says a word, the spacious auditorium echoed with the sound of gum chewing and incoherent sighing. “Anyone at all? Will no-one take at least a guess?” .:As I asked that question, I wished I hadn’t. From my time in school, I knew that question what the only opportunity they needed to answer me with their own humour, and true to form, a male voice from the back of the hall resounded. Why is it always the men who answer first? I will never know. “You gonna be on Playboy then?” .:Thinking on my feet, I answered back the only way I knew how. “Why, would that turn you on?” .:A few titters were heard from people, and the voice at the back was silent. “Since no-one will take an INTELLIGENT guess, I will have to tell you. I am a professional wrestler, and as such, will be with the World Wrestling Alliance very soon, I am assuming that some of you have heard of them? A show of hands if you have.” .:As I thought, nearly three quarters of the room raised their hands. The others I bet were just too macho or too shy, equally a curse to a child. “That’s a good number. Well, I am not here to talk about wrestling, mores the pity, no; I am here to talk about drugs and the harm they can do. I have first hand experience of drugs, so don’t think I am just some hypocritical bleeding heart type who just wants to help you, I’m not one of them, I just know drugs are a terrible thing, and hopefully I can tell some of you why.” .:I could see their faces now, screwing their faces up at the man in the suit, the voice of the man telling them what to do, I knew I had to get the message over somehow. “I want an honest show of hands, from the jocks at the back to the nerds at the front; I want to see an honest show of hands. Who thinks I do not have the right to be standing here lecturing you?” .:I saw two separate groups of hands go up at first. One was a group of jocks, no doubt containing Playboy Guy from earlier, and the other group was a group of what appeared to be clones, girls who looked identical to the leader of their clique. Slowly, once they got the courage, nearly everyone raised their hands, showing exactly what I had expected. “Who does the asshole in the suit think he is? What does he think he is doing? Are you all asking that question?” .:I almost expected to see people nodding their heads, but for a few small exceptions, I did not. I felt it was necessary to show them the wild card I had brought with me. I removed my jacket and laid it on the chair I had been provided with. “I bet some of you think you are better than the drugs you take, and don’t lie to me; I know some of you are on drugs. I bet some of you think that drugs are something you control, that doesn’t control you.” .:I pulled my shirt off to show my tattooed torso. “How many of you have done something stupid on drugs?” .:I turned around, showing them my back, and I openly heard a gasp or two. “How many of you have been so off your face that you thought it was alright to set yourself on fire for a laugh, and have to spend so much time in hospital getting the burnt flesh fixed.” .:I turned around, and could see I had some, at least more than before, paying attention. “Back when I was your age, I was at school in England. It was ok, pretty much like this one, except mine had one difference; they were blind to the taking of drugs. The head of the school was a blinkered old witch, and as long as there was no trouble, we got away with anything. That was where I got hooked. I was taking everything you take, and more. My parents didn’t know. .:I saw a glimmer in one or two faces, obviously hiding drugs from the parents was a big deal here. “My teachers didn’t know. Hell, even I didn’t know I was that bleeding wasted till I was 17 and set myself alight because I was mashed and so off my head I lost all sense of reality. How many of you have been like that? No need to answer, for obvious reasons, but how many of you have been so off your head that you would have done anything had the opportunity arisen.” .:Murmurs, I had them talking, excellent. “I did not come up here and expect to change all your lives with an inspirational speech, I didn’t. However, I did come up here with one sole agenda, to open your eyes. You see drugs education is not about preaching the sin’s of drugs, nor about preaching how drugs are bad. The way I see it, drugs are something that take over your life whether you want them to or not.” .:They certainly did to me. “Under your seats you will all find, if you haven’t found already, a small flyer.” .:I heard much rooting, and also saw a paper aeroplane, but I ignored that. I took one of the flyers from my pocket and hold it up. “Now, as you can see here, this flyer is advertising a match, one that will take place very soon, and can you see who it is between?” .:Stupid questions, for all those kids out there were smart, if unfocused. “You can see me, Kirk Urban, taking on a guy named Smokey Wead. Now, as you can guess from his name, Monsieur Wead is an advocate of bud, weed, dope, draw, hash, and reefer, all of the above. I am assuming that some of you have tried the demon weed, since it’s quite popular, and I would say that you all know someone who either does it or has done it. Weed is popular as hell in England...” .:Just in England? The stuff is popular everywhere. “And over here it is equally as rife. However, for all its positive effects, the calming qualities, the natural high, it is a drug, pure and simple, a drug that takes over your life. Mr Wead may advocate the taking of this drug, but that doesn’t make it right.” .:I was losing them again, but thankfully I had a big finish lined up. “Mr Wead has a date with me, as part of this tournament I am entered in. Nothing fancy, just the first round, but to me, I have to beat Smokey Wead for more than one reason. One is to advance, obviously, whilst the other is to show you, and all the people out there, those drugs are an evil, they are abhorrent. I do not expect you all to understand me and my motivation for the match. Many of you, I am sure, will go home and smoke, snort or pop whatever you smoke, snort or pop. I cannot stop you from doing that, only you can.” .:Why do drugs hit kids so hard, they have life hard enough as it is without chemicals running their lives. “Only you can stop yourselves from being a slave to that which has ruined the lives of so many people, of people your age. You must have all read the tragedy stories in the paper about the teen deaths due to drugs, so why do kids your age keep doing them?” .:I started to put my shirt back on, hiding the hideous scarring on my back, a mark of a stupid time in my life. “You all have your lives to lead without the need for chemicals, and yet some of you still do. I will never understand the logic, but all I can say is this. Drugs made me set my body on fire; they took my will and perverted it. I just pray that the next time I see one of your faces; it’s in the crowd at a WWA event, not on the cover of a newspaper having died in hospital from an OD or worse, killed in a battle over an amount of powder the area of a postage stamp.” .:Hard to think that people die for something so small, so insignificant. ”Drugs will kill you, sooner or later, drugs will kill you unless you kick the habit. For now, just think about what I said and focus. If you are taking drugs and need help, the number to call is printed on the back of the flyer you already have. Please, if you need help, seek it out, before the help finds you dead. Thank you.” .:I turned and walked off stage and behind the curtain, and was replaced by the principal. “Thank you, Kirk Urban, for that...” .:Again, his words faded into silence. As I walked into the area behind the curtain, I thought to myself, is advancing in the tournament the only real reason I had for beating Smokey Wead? Or need I add fighting a demon to that list. Do I really need to prove my willpower over the drugs after all these years of being clean? As I asked myself this, I was interrupted by a noise behind me. “Mr... Mr Urban?” .:The voice was so faint I could hardly hear it, and as I turned around, I saw the reason. I was confronted with the smallest girl in the year, who looked terrified she was speaking to an adult. “Is there something I can do?” .:The girl looked like she was hiding something, scared to say. “Is it something about drugs?” .:The girl nodded. She was holding a copy of the flyer, and on the back I could just make out, written beneath the number for the helpline ... “Sarah” “Who is Sarah?” .:The girl looked at me, then at the flyer and swallowed hard. “She is my sister.” .:I looked down at her. “You are looking for advice on whether to call on her behalf?” .:She nodded, and I could see the worry on her face about her sister. “Ring the number. The greatest crime one person can commit against another is to do nothing when they need help. Ring the number and let them help you, and her. Trust in yourself to do the right thing.” .:The girl nodded and ran away; probably to join her friends before they became suspicious, such is the burden of peer pressure. Then it hit me, the reason I had to beat Wead. I stood there for a while before repeating to myself. “Trust in yourself to do the right thing. Beating Wead is the right thing, so I must trust in myself to do it.” .:I turned and walked to the exit, ignoring the sycophantic words from the principal. “Trust in yourself ... to do the right thing ... how true.” .:The exit doors slammed shut as I was through them, the principal locked inside. I didn’t like him, too weak, such a Wead.
