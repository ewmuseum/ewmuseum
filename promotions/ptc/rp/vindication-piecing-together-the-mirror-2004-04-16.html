Vindication: Piecing Together the Mirror

16 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-16 (Continued from Shard #2) *CRASH!* The clear glass flower vase shattered upon the blue wall, making a dent and exposing some of the white primer underneath. Jimmy Diablo stood to his full height of 5’10” after ducking the projectile. He raised his arms in a ready stance, in the event that more objects flew toward his head. “What are you, psycho?” he said in the heat of the moment. The brown-haired Hispanic woman shouted back, “Yes!” The contortions of her face in anger didn’t do her beauty any justice. She looked better when she first opened the door for Jimmy Diablo. The weather was horrible, but she was still wearing a light green dress that hugged her in all of the right places. She must have had somewhere to be later. His arms coming back down a little, “What?” was response that Jimmy uttered. The table that separated them seemed like an ocean. Jimmy felt so distanced from her; he couldn't possibly get close to her, if at least in the mental sense of it. It was a good thing her boyfriend wasn't home; he had a gun and a chronic twitch in his trigger finger. She had tears in her eyes. "After that damned match, I didn't have anyone. That damned Sarushima Rikyu brat would brag about being the one to cause the downfall of Jimmy Diablo. He'd call me and brag about it everyday, and the oxymoronic Mexican police didn't do a thing about it. Then when I try to move on with my son, I lose him, too!" The light from the bulb in the center of the kitchen shone a dull yellowish-orange upon the two. Jimmy's faded-black cotton sweats and white T-shirt didn't look any different in the light. The white cabinets were completely undisturbed, and the kettle on the stove was cooling after the woman made some hot chocolate for the two of them. There was a stark contrast of the harshness of the moment between the two, and the overall placidity of the kitchen. Jimmy almost choked on his words, "Jimmy died?" "No," she answered. "Kidnapped right out from under me. He was right there one second, and then the next..." there was a very emotional pause in her speech. She had to force herself to continue. "You have no idea what it's like to lose two Jimmy Flores's in the same year." The glass on the floor would remain there. Jimmy had taken off his shoes, so he shuffled to the table in front of him rather than to lift his feet. He wouldn't step on any errant shards of the once-decorative container. The covered wooden chair closest to him was not so much a seat as it was shelter to him. He had to sit down; he couldn't take this discussion standing up. Diablo couldn't find the words to tell her, but he knew how she felt. In more ways than one. "Jocelyn..." he started. "Don't take that sympathetic tone!" Ms. Rodriguez snapped. She was the widow of Jimmy Diablo, a former wrestler herself, withdrawn from the sport following the death of her husband. "It's your fault that everything happened." He recalled their history, commenting, "When last we spoke eight years ago, you were trying to convince me that it wasn't my fault that Jimmy died." Her surly, but no less emotionally-distressed response was, "Yeah, well it took me a few years to realize that you were making more sense at the time. I'm not the same person I was eight years ago." Arguably, no one could have been more affected by Jimmy Diablo's death than Jocelyn Rodriguez. He had meant the world to her. Though they met in much the same way that she had had one night stands with men before, there was something special about him. His personality, his cleverness, and his smile attracted her like no other. He was the first person that she could ever have truthfully told I love you. "Yes, I'm psycho," she finally continued after a slow, lengthy breath. "Losing Jimmy, and then Jimmy Jr.; I was alone just like that. I had no support, no one to turn to for help. Even you might've been able to help me get through it if you stayed. I had a nervous breakdown that lasted for two years, at least. And seeing you just brings it all back when I was just starting to get over it." An I'm sorry' would be of no comfort to her. Jimmy understood this and didn't bother to say it. At this point, he had nothing to say. He didn't know how to respond to her. He knew that it would be an emotional reunion, but not like this. He didn't think that she would still feel so strongly about Jimmy Diablo's death. Then again, he had to remind himself that she loved Jimmy Diablo just as much as he had loved Jimmy Diablo. They both remained in that state of not knowing how to continue until Jocelyn finally said, "What are you here for anyway? To clear your conscience? You have some disease and wanted to bury the hatchet before you die?" In her current emotional state, she would have preferred to hear that he was dying. Not because she felt sorry for him, but because it would give her a measure of closure. He killed Jimmy; he should die. Jimmy Diablo shook his head. "Nothing like that, no. I wanted to come... I wanted to ask your blessing." Jocelyn took the seat opposite Jimmy at the thin table. It was only 4' in diameter. Her apartment wasn't that large, the kitchen was simply a stove, cabinets above and surrounding, a sink, fridge, and a little free space in the corner. "My blessing? For what?" she asked. It was an odd request. Jimmy closed his eyes, then looked into hers. He had been staring at the floor or the table for the majority of the conversation. "I'm back in wrestling. A tournament. Big event. I wanted to win it in Jimmy's honor." "A noble thought," she said, "but empty since he's not alive to win it himself." Diablo continued. "Not quite... I registered as Jimmy Diablo. I'm using his identity to try to win the thing. That way he could in some way finally break that Diablo Curse and have the glory that he wa--" A hard slap to the face stopped him in mid-sentence. He certainly hadn't expected that. The bone-to-flesh impact was loud, and echoed somewhat off of the walls. Jocelyn's chair was overturned, tossed about the floor when she rose from her seat to deliver the hit. "Get out..." she commanded in a low intensity. She was at her angriest when she kept her voice low; she wasnt' one for commonly accessing the lower-volume range. Jimmy sputtered, "I'm sorry..." "FUCK YOU, AYASHI!" she screamed at the top of her lungs. "GET OUT!" The man known to PTC and PCW as Jimmy Diablo rose from his seat and left. He didn't want to upset her further. There was little further that she could go in terms of being upset, though. Diablo said not another word as he exited the kitchen and went out to the door, where his shoes and raincoat were waiting for him. He closed the door behind him, having accomplished no more on his visit than to open old wounds. ***|||~~~|||*** "I was better off just going back to the hotel," he muttered, stepping back out into the rain that had not let up since he entered the building. Taking his cell phone out of his pocket, he was just about to call Jimbo K. when the phone rang. He hit the button, recognizing the number. It wasn't programmed into memory, but he'd dialed it many times in the past. He knew it by heart. “What do you think you’re doing?” The voice on the other line came through in Spanish. It was the only language that Hijo del Diablo, the second wrestler and first failure in the Diablo line, could speak. His tone was one of agitation, frustration. He was quite obviously displeased with Jimmy Diablo. “What do you mean?” Jimmy replied, though he had a feeling that he knew why HdD was upset. “You know what I’m talking about. What gave you the idea that it would be alright to impersonate Jimmy Diablo?” Impersonate Jimmy Diablo. Were it not a private, untraceable call, one would wonder who this man passing himself off as Jimmy Diablo really was. He seemed to be someone who wasn’t the type to speak up in his defense, since he remained silent. HdD, who had to be in his eighties if not older, was talking down to… well, the wrestler could still be called Jimmy Diablo for the moment. “I hired you back then because I knew that you could get the job done. Jimmy had to be eliminated and you carried out your task well. You even took leave of wrestling after that so no one would ask any questions. It was good. So why go and mess up a good thing by pretending to be an injured Jimmy Diablo? Have you forgotten why I wanted him dead in the first place? The new Jimmy Diablo shook his head, not that HdD could see it. “No,” he answered. Little more of a response was necessary; HdD wouldn’t have listened. “Well, you just come back to your senses. Those years of loyal service don’t need to go down the drain now. Just lose or bow out of your current and we’ll go back to the way things were. Understand?” Hijo del Diablo was asking this new Jimmy Diablo to give up his promise to the original Jimmy Diablo. It had wrenched at him for years that he had to sell out his best friend. He wasn’t going to allow it to happen again. But he couldn’t anger HdD; not before he had a plan to deal with the backlash. For now, he just gave a submissive, “Okay.” ”Good. The man on the other end of the line hung up his phone. Jimmy pressed End out of habit. He was left as he was an hour earlier, standing in the rain, contemplating what to do next. He was left with the same options as before: quit while he was ahead, or go through with things as his heart told him to do. "Dammit!" he exclaimed, stamping his foot into the puddle that had long since formed in front of the 70's construction-style apartment. This triumphant return to wrestling wasn't going very well for him, after all.
