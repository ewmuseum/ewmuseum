Sic Transit Gloria Mundi (Prologue)

19 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-19 "All sparks will burn out… All sparks will burn out… All sparks will burn out… In the end." - The Editors. A watched pot never boils. Well, more accurately, a watched pot never boils if the gas goes out under it. "Fuck," Angus McSweeney's broad Scottish accent curses under his breath, wary of the young children swarming around within a few feet of him. Shaking his left wrist, he tries to inject come kinetic life back into his watch, the constant checking of which had seemingly worn out. Unbuckling the brown leather strap, he has to peel it from his thick wrist, where the baking humidity had left a tan stain. A heartier shake fails to revive the watch, before its stuffed into one of his trousers' leg pockets. "A've been had," he dejectedly grumbles, accent grinding each word. "A've been proper had." Standing a whisker under 6'9, the colossal Scotsman had been drawing the attention of awe-struck passers-by for the 45 minutes or so that he'd been pacing back and forth. All around him, under the hazy August sun, lush parkland stretched out as far as I eye could see, and the imagination could envisage. Only the mesmerising heights of centurion trees interrupted the horizon, while the sounds of the city were repelled at the gates by the impenetrable serenity of it all. As balls were thrown, kicked, and pitched, energetic players slowed their dashes, almost bumping into one another as their brain had to think if the man was really that big, or if he was only a foot or so away from them. Their playful laughter was a notable contrast to the angry thrashing of death-metal that would befittingly accompany every stride of Angus McSweeney. Sleeve tattoos painted angry tapestries up each arm, while enough piercings to make walking past a magnet a dubious choice glowed warmly in the summer sunshine. A white vest clung damply to his close-to-bursting torso, merely taking a few steps back and forth in the muggy heat leaving his muscle-ballast body breaking out into a sweat… only thick, brushy black eyebrows keeping the salty sting from his sky-blue eyes. Bowing his head forward, gargantuan neck looking as if the movement would release a deafening muscle creak, he wipes his glistening face with his vest. "Sup?" a voice then bluntly states. The behemoth of a Scotsman heaves his 350-odd pounds around, to catch sight of a man casually slithering into the seat of the nearest picnic table. Decked out in a crisp linen suit, with white shirt underneath seemingly buttoned where he could be bothered, the man looks over the tops of his glasses' frames at the bulky Scotsman for all of two seconds. With his first impression having been noted, Angus looks a little on the back foot as the man scribbles down a few quick notes. "Well…" the man invites, prodding the bridge of his glasses back up his nose. "You don't look like you're here for the sunshine. So, tell me, what are you here for?" A broken bottle being thrust towards Angus McSweeney's face didn't even make him flinch, and odds of 4 vs. his lonesome on a rival's turf failed to even force a single bead of cold sweat out across his brow. But, towering over the unimposing man, casting a gargantuan shadow down across him, the Scot has to swallow down his nerves, along with the mere notion of asking why he was late. "Ah heard yah make things happen," Angus reveals, "and ah want yah t'make ahm happen fer me." "That's spiffy," the man casually remarks, half-giving his attention as a message is hurriedly punched into a Blackberry, before looking up, curiously. "And who are you?" "Ah…" the Scot fumbles. "Ah thought ah'd had me details passed t'ya?" "I know you as 2:30," the man coldly admits, finally having tucked away any distractions. "Not even I can sell that. So, spill. Pitch me." "Ah'm Angus McSweeney," he proudly proclaims, "tha Loch Ness Monstah… bruthah. Grew up barely ah'undred yards from tha banks." "So the deal is…" the man stalls, only truly needing a millisecond to mull it over. "The deal is that you're from Loch Ness, and you're a monster, right?" "Aye," comes the enthusiastic response. With a 'Hmmmmm', the man retreats back behind his eyes, bouncing ideas back and forth around his brain. Stray ends of ideas entwine and interweave, prospective shoots of plans are choked out before even their idea blossoms, and a vision rolls forward through the years. And then, as teeth scrape over his bottom lip, the man taps his pen down onto his pad. "Not interested," bruises the sky black and rains on the Scotsman's hopes and dreams. "Sorry." Slithering back out of the bench's seat, the man hops over the plank of wood to where the grass is apparently greener, having already screwed up the rough notes he'd made. Watching on, Angus' boulder-like fists clench tightly, bleaching the tips of his fingernails. "S'that it?" the mammoth Scot then exclaims, throwing tree-trunk arms up into the sticky air. "Ah was tol-" "You were told that I'd give you a yes or a no," the man cuts him off with, the gargantuan size of the angry Scot not intimidating him any. "You got a no. Go home, and all the best." "They're right about ya, bruthah," Angus declares, calling after the departing man. "Losin' ya touch, y'are. Dunno when there be gold right infront'ya." As the kids are saying nowadays: O RLY? "Spendin' all'ya time on Wolfenden," he expands upon the accusation with, "even though she be a lost cause, an e'ryone but ya can see't. But, hey, can't blame ya, bruthah. E'ryone has there comfort zone, y'hear? An, hey, it ain't like ya reputation done too bad off of the lass, huh? But how many others have ya cut-off or passed-up so ya can milk th'last bit ah glory outta her?" Hands dropping into pockets, the man drops his head to the side, not looking like he's entirely used to people calling him in such a fashion. Most just stare, starry-eyed, lavishing compliments upon him for the work he's done behind the scenes for a select few, loaning them his reputation in return for a dividend paid out from the kudos they earn. His name dropped to the right person is a golden ticket, a contract thrust forth without so much as a fake homage to negotiations, a instant ratings spike next-to guaranteed. He was Midas. But when a reputation reaches critical mass, the temptation to just cash in and live off it can come on strong and easy. "You got your answer," the man coldly seethes, eyes narrowing in spite. "When ah'yre gonna give us all summat new, bruthah?" Angus continues to press, pursuing the suited man he'd placed all his hopes and dreams in. "Tha year ain't 2004 anymore, y'hear? Ahm ya 2006, 2007, an beyond, bruthah. All y'gotta do is pull y'head outta tha sand and realise it's time ta give up on lost causes, if ya hear what'm sayin'?" Only stony silence greens the livid Scotsman as the world around them freezes, all enthralled by the impromptu free entertainment. "Ah think ya need me just s'much as ah need you," the Scot's passionate rant continues on with. "Ah'm what can prove y'aren't just livin' off that there reputation o'yours, that you're tha man who makes stars every time. But if ya don't wanna give the Wolfenden lass any competition that might show'er up, then that's your call, bruthah. Just don't begrudge me a wee smile when the lass finally fizzles out and takes ya with her." Refusing to get drawn into any debate, the man simply drops his head and walks away, feeling the goliath footsteps of the Scot following him for the first few paces. "So you just walk away, Mr W.… I'll do fine w'out ya, ah will."
