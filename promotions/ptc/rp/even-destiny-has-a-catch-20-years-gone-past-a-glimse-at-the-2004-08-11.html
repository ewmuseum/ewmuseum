Even Destiny Has A Catch (20 Years Gone Past... A Glimse At The Beginning)

11 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-11 “Man… I am too pretty.” And he was right; he was an extremely handsome young man. He cupped his hands, which were flawless and heavily creamed, and brought up a palm-full of warm water to his face. He poured the water onto that Adonis-like face of his and rubbed it in, he was having a long and tiring day. The man stood back up straight, flinging his long blonde hair over his shoulder. It tickled the back of his neck. He smirked into the mirror. “Far too pretty.” No really, he was and his mother couldn't have named him any better. His name was Kenneth. “Handsome.” Handsome he was, though he was too ignorant or just too uninterested to learn the meaning of his own name and things like that. He was much more interested in other more important things like the advancement of his cuticles, or harvesting those little black hairs in-between his eyebrows, or clipping his ear fuzz. He was only interested in the important things in life, because life was too short, and people should only bother with the really important things. Like flossing a dozen times a day, even after drinking water, and shaving his chest. He had this thing with hair. It's best to leave it at that. Really. He slipped on a black hair net, and carefully and oh-so-delicately tucked his precious locks under it. Kenneth scowled as he put on his greasy maroon visor. He hated it. It had, in yellow letters, “Burgerburg: Home of the Burg-burger”, written across the front. Creative, yes, everyone, even Kenneth, agreed on that… but he did not find it exactly fashionable. He gave one last look into the mirror, slightly tilting his chin upward and to the side, a small smile slowly cracked on his face. He gave a quick glance into his own beautifully-cold blue eyes. Kenneth cocked an eyebrow and walked on out of the Burgerburg restroom. “Jesus H., Kenny!” yelled Dave. Kenneth hated it when people called him “Kenny.” He was far more sophisticated than “ Kenny.” People can be so ignorant sometimes. “What took ya so long in there, hm? What did ya do, fall in?” A round roly-poly looking kid in a maroon Burgerburg uniform shouted with a mist of saliva. This mist spewed forth whenever he was excited, and he was excited, because he's a really very funny guy. Obviously. His face was squat and seemed to be all scrunched towards the middle, in which resided a small swinish snout. He had only one single jet black eyebrow that that practically went from ear to ear. Kenneth always noticed eyebrows first. They're an important factor in the judging of one's character. This boy also had a gap between his two front teeth so big that Kenneth had a hard time looking at him without feeling sorry for him. Between the gap and the brow, it was terrible. If only everyone could be as handsome as Kenneth, the world would have been a better place, surely. “Shut up, and don't talk to me.” Kenneth thought little of this Dave-kid. His bad looks and overall annoy-ability, that is the ability to annoy as opposed to the ability to be annoyed, was just too much for Kenneth. Dave. Dave was his name. Dave had only been working at the Burgerburg for a month, and he was only a junior in high school. Kenneth, on the other hand, had been working at the Burgerburg for six years, and was twenty-one years old, and much more important than some chubby boy. He even had two entirely separate eyebrows! Now, during those six years, Kenneth daydreamed of a better place. A place where everyone recognized him as he stepped out of limousines and cameras flashed at movie debuts and award ceremonies. This was the destiny of Kenneth and he was sure of it. There was no other reason for him to be on this earth than to be a star, because while he was still stuck among the commoners he made them look… well… ugly. Because, next to Kenneth, everyone else looked too short, or too fat, or too skinny, or too un-waxed. Kenneth was perfection and anything and anyone that fell short was garbage. So, the best thing that the garbage could do was to get rid of Kenneth by immediately idolizing him and putting him on a pedestal. A pedestal for the beautiful people, the important people. A pedestal so high that the short would feel not-so-short, and the fat felt trimmer, and the skinny felt fuller, and the un-waxed just didn't care. That's what they should do, and will do, because that is Kenneth's destiny and destiny shouldn't take over six years to fulfill! Really. So, after leaving Dave to tend his French fries alone, Kenneth took his post at the counter. He manned the cash register at the Burgerburg after he requested, 4 and a half years ago, to be moved there because he claimed that the grill caused his face to break out with acne. That was bad. Kenneth hated acne. Kenneth hated people with acne, like Dave. Dave had bad acne. He had a miniature Cassiopeia going across his forehead, not that Kenneth knew what that was. He seldom looked at the sky, night or day, because it wasn't relevant, nor important, to his quest for public affection. So, Kenneth manned the cash register. “Welcome to Burgerburg: Home of the Burg-burger…” Kenneth yawned at each customer. “Would you like fries with that?” Dave was picking his nose. “Do you want that in the large, mega , or ultimo size?” Kenneth liked the word “ultimo.” It was an important word. “Would you like that for here or to go? For here or to go? FOR… HERE... OR… TO…GO-O?” People couldn't hear for crap. Maybe if they cleaned their ears out once a year. Kenneth cleaned his every other hour. He had to prevent any threat of build-up. “Sorry, Sir, this coupon has expired.” What a jerk, trying to trick Kenneth into accepting an expired coupon, good thing Kenneth was also smarter than everyone. The long and tiring day dragged on… and on... and on… But, just when the day seemed to be dragging so slowly that time must have stopped or the earth fell out of orbit, or an electro-magnetic pulse from a Russian satellite fried all the clocks' circuits or something, a man entered the Burgerburg. He was a shorter man, with dark sun glass and a blue baseball cap covering his gray hair, which jutted out from under his hat at his sideburns. He was a middle-aged man, in his early fifties. He had a mustache; brown with streaks of silver. The man looked around the place and he seemed to be contemplating something. It was hard to tell on account of his dark sunglasses. Kenneth noticed that his cap was old and tattered, and that he must be going for the rustic look or something. Stores pre-tatter their clothes now-a-days, it's insane. But rest assured, this man's cap was not pre-tattered. He had probably gotten it at a baseball game his father had taken him to when he was a kid. It was a Houston Colt .45's game, but the Colt .45's don't exist anymore. The man waltzed over to Kenneth's counter, his head turning from side to side, inspecting the fast food restaurant. He took his sweet time too, and this annoyed Kenneth. But, finally, the man reached Kenneth's counter. He put his hands in the pockets of his leather jacket. They needed moisturizing, Kenneth thought. The hands. The man looked up at the menu, and then at Kenneth. “Welcome to Burgerburg: Home of the Burg-burger.” Kenneth always yawned when he said this. “How may I help you?” “Help me? Perhaps you can and perhaps you can't. The best thing you can do is to keep talking, son, and just stand there.” The man replied. Kenneth didn't like being told what to do, nor did he like being called “son.” “Ok, well, Pops , let me know when you make up your mind then.” Kenneth leaned over the counter, trying to see through the man's glasses, but to no avail. The man's tongue ran across his teeth. “Well… My mind sometimes has a hard time being made up. I think I know what I want, but I'm not sure. I have my doubts. What I want looks good, but I don't know if I want it. It might not be good.” “Then get something else.” Kenneth sneered. “Yes, maybe something else… But this first one seemed so perfect… I was almost certain I wanted it… then that I did not… yet, I'm reluctant to give up on it.” Kenneth was getting anxious. This man was a moron. Just order something and eat it, for Pete's sake. Two women got in line behind the man. “Do you mind turning to the side? I need a better view.” Asked the man. Jerk. Why doesn't he just move if he needs to see the menu. Kenneth hated it when people ordered him around, and this guy had already done it twice, his patience was wearing thin. A man got in line behind the two women. Kenneth hated lines… too many flaws on too many faces at once. The man was just standing there staring straight ahead like some stupid lawn gnome with sun glasses. Just order already! And now yet another ugly person in Kenneth's line. How wonderful. That was the last straw. “Look, Grandpa, how about I order for you, since you can't do it yourself. How about some BURGERBURG fries, huh? Would you like that?!” Kenneth was fuming, he can get upset sometimes. He had never reserved these outbursts when he was in public either, he liked attention. This guy was not only an idiot, he was old, had no fashion sense, and was probably very ugly under those glasses and cap. The man leaned back, as if Kenneth's own voice had physically pushed him back in its fury. Kenneth dramatically flung his arm to his right, pointing over to Dave, who sneezed as he filled a paper container with greasy French fries. “He picks his nose. I think you'll like that! You… nincompoop! ” Kenneth never cursed… he was home-schooled. The man leaned over the counter, completely forgetting about Kenneth. The corners of his mouth stretched upward. He had decided. “That is what I want.” The man nodded. “That's disgusting. You're disgusting.” Kenneth sniveled. “Perfect.” The man walked over to the part of the counter adjacent to Dave and his deep-frying snot-filled lair. Kenneth, though he had wished nothing more than for this man to leave the Burgerburg as quickly as possible and never return just a moment ago, now suddenly craved his attention. Kenneth wasn't done with him yet! How dare he just leave in the middle of a venting! And what was worse, he called Dave perfect, over himself. Dave , for crying out loud… “Hey, kid.” Dave's back was turned to the man and he was busy salting his fries with one hand and whiping his nose with the other. There were no tissues near, so his sleeve would have to do. He didn't hear the man. “Kid. Kid! Hey!” Dave turned around hesitantly, and looked at man's old baseball cap with his squinty eyes. They were squinty because of those big red cheeks that smashed his bottom eyelids almost to the up one. His eyes were really squinty, Dave's were. Dave questioned himself and pointed to his chest with his half greasy, half you-don't-want-to-know thumb. The man chuckled to himself and nodded. “Yes, you. Come here.” Dave took the whole three and a half steps over to the counter. The man extended his hand. Kenneth was thoroughly disgusted at this whole dilemma, but watched like hawk gazing down at two repulsive rats, too revoltingly ugly and moronic to ever be worthy of being eaten by such a majestic bird such as Kenneth. But they didn't even want to be eaten, that was the worst part… Kenneth ignored his line of costumers. “Peter Antonopoulos, from Moorland Pictures film company.” They shook hands. Kenneth's eyes widened. Moorland Pictures? They make movies! You just never know who's going to walk on into your Burgerburg! “You got a name or what?” They were still shaking hands. “Oh, yeah… sorry. David. David Simms.” Dave, embarrassed, smiled slightly. Simms? What a drab name, Kenneth thought. Nice try, Dave, really. He introduced himself as “David” to try to come off sophisticated like “Kenneth”. Too bad Simms gives you away… and being a stupid pig doesn't help either. “Nice to meet you David. Now, I've got a question for you.” Mr. Antonopoulos withdrew his hand from Dave's loose and oily grip and reached into the back pocket of his blue jeans. “Yes?” Dave inquired timidly. What could a guy from Moorland Pictures want with a slug like Dave? Kenneth couldn't make out any sense of this at all. “You see,” Mr. Antonopoulos continued, “We're looking for some area kids to be in a new movie, and I think that you'd be good for a part that needs filling. It's nothing big, but you'd get to be in a movie and you might even have a line or two. ” A line or two!? The movies!? Dave? What? What about Kenneth? What about him? What about his beautiful face and clean ears? What about his long golden hair, and muscular but not-too-muscular body? What about the hairless perfection!? What about it? What about his destiny? “You interested?” Dave smiled and nodded, “Yes, yes, Sir, very much so.” His eyes were especially squinty when he smiled. “Good, good. Well, now, here's my card.” Peter drew it from his back pocket and handed it to Dave. “Now, you give me a call at that number later tonight, when you get off work. Okay?” “Yes, of course.” Dave just repeatedly nodded. He was pretty dumbfounded by the whole thing. Really he was. While Kenneth just didn't understand this at all. Really he didn't. “Okay, David. I'll talk to you later. Hasta.” Mr. Antonopoulos turned and headed for the exit. Dave held the business card with both hands and smiled. He looked up from his treasure. “Good-bye, and thank you.” Hasta? Hasta? That must be Hollywood talk! Kenneth wanted to talk Hollywood talk, he dreamed of that elite lingo for years, and now maybe his only chance of getting language lessons was leaving the Burgerburg! Kenneth ripped off his hat and hairnet in one fluid motion and threw them to the floor. The ladies in line were hungry, really hungry, but Kenneth didn't care. He leapt over the counter and sprinted towards the door to cut off quite possibly the most important person in his life. His long hair bounced off his shoulders as he ran and sweat collected in beads with the face creams on his forehead. Mr. Antonopoulos opened the exit and took one step through it. Kenneth threw himself at the door, and nearly knocked Mr. Antonopoulos over. His face was strained and his palms were wet. He took his shot. “What about me?” Mr. Antonopoulos took his sun glasses off and stared Kenneth in the eyes. Cold blue met a worn wooden brown. They stood there for a few moments, Kenneth breathing heavily. Mr. Antonopoulos cleared his throat. “ Son, there are already enough ‘disgusting nincompoops' in Hollywood . There are too many of you nincompoops being disgusting and thus there are too many people turning themselves into nincompoops trying to be just as disgusting as you are. We just don't need anymore. I need people to play characters, not characters being their disgusting selves. Sorry.” And with that Mr. Antonopoulos side-stepped around Kenneth and went outside to the parking lot. Kenneth looked back Dave working at his deep fryer and the line of hungry people. Kenneth's eyes went to the restroom door. He went in. He stood before Himself. Kenneth looked at Himself in the Burgerburg bathroom mirror. He turned on the sink faucet and waited for the water to get warm. When it was, he cupped his hands and rubbed it into his face. It felt good to get the sweat off his forehead. It was a long and tiring day. Kenneth looked into the mirror, and stared into those cold blue eyes. He shook his head and smiled. “Too pretty…” Mr. Antonopoulos drove home to his wife in his Porsche. Dave's mother picked him up and he told her the good news. Kenneth road home in a bus full of stupid ugly people.
