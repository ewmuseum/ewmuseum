Numbers

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-14 Numbers. Numbers don’t lie. But they can deceive. Steve Watson sat at his desk staring at the flickering computer screen. Something wasn’t right. It just wasn’t right. But he couldn’t quite put his finger on it. Steve had reviewed the company expense figured on the twelfth of the month every month for the last six years. And every month for the past six years they had come out just about exactly where he had predicted they would. It had almost become a game. Steve would email himself out his quarterly expense "estimates" six months in advance and then review them when the time came. And they were always within 3.5% of where he had "budgeted" them. He took everything into account from at extra bit of "Office Supply" spending that always accompanied the "Back to School" Season in late August to folks self-assigned Christmas Bonuses to Chuck Dannon’s affinity for Belgian hookers. The figures always come out well within his assigned margin of error. Not today though. Today the figures were off my almost 12.4%. They looked more like what you’d expect in a March or perhaps even a June. But these were September’s figures. And September had always been the most consistent of months. Especially since anon isn’t scheduled to be back in Europe until early 2007. Steve hit the print button on his PCL print controller screen then arose from his desk and walked to his office window. It was at times like this that he wished his office had been situated on the other side of the floor - the side facing the park - so that he could gaze out serenely lost in thought and overlook the trees changing to their glorious red and gold autumnal foliage. Instead he had to be satisfied with the cold, hard, mirrored windows or the adjoining high rise and the sounds of traffic – both foot and automotive – passing by so far below. Soon that would change though. In time, he would get what was coming to him. In time, the partners would notice all his hard work, his talent and dedication, and they would reward him accordingly. In time. As he played over September’s numbers in his head once more, his mind began to wander to that nights other scheduled activity. The culmination of a whole different set of years of hard work and dedication. Something that the guys down at the Smitty’s had been trying to convince Steve could be his long awaited "Appointment with Destiny." Steve’s wife thought they were all just being overly dramatic about the whole thing. She thought it was silly for a bunch of grown men to get so excited about their "little hobby". She didn’t understand though. This absolutely was a Big Deal. It was a huge step up. A chance to prove that he could do more than just be a dominant force on the local Bingo Hall and High School Gymnasium scene. I mean it is one thing to make some weekend warrior/volunteer fireman tap out and quite another to stand across the ring from a man that many would consider a near legend in the business. "Hey Watsy. Why don’t climb on back in here from that ledge and join us for lunch?" Steve sun away from the window to see the figure standing in his doorway. It was Tim. Tim Forbes – the very man that Steve had beaten out for the Assistant CFO’s position back six years ago. Tim had always been far more than gracious about that outcome; but Steve knew that, even after all this time, it still had to sting. After all, Tim had joined the company a full eight years earlier than Steve had even graduated from college and it was common knowledge that he had long been considered Mr. Hainesworth’s heir apparent. "So a bunch of us are headed down to Benny’s for a quick bite and a brew. You want in?" "No thanks," said Steve, "Elise packed me a real nice chef salad today. And I have these expenses to finish. So I’ll probably just end up being a working lunch." "Suit yourself," replied Tim, "Although, for the love of Pete, I have no idea why a guy that looks like you would insist on eating rabbit food all the time. But, then again, maybe that’s exactly why you do look like that and I’m stuck looking like the Pillsbury Doughboy’s taller, slightly more handsome cousin." Steve could never tell Tim the real reason for his light faire today. He liked his long-time co-worker’s humorous nature and jovial camaraderie; but he could never quite bring himself to trust it. There was just too much history there. Too much chance that it would get back to the Partners. And Steve didn’t think the partners would take much shine to their number two numbers man moonlighting another gig on the side. Especially a gig of the vein in which Steve had been partaking. "Hey, eat a cheeseburger for me while your there then, pal. Oh, and could you swing by Peggy’s desk on the way out and ask here to call Iron Mountain. I am going to need all of the expense receipts for the last three months." "Can’t you just pull them off the network" said Tim, "After all; you’re the guy that approved IT spending that entire wad last year for that fancy Document Management System." "Nope. I think I am going to need to pour over the hard copy originals for this one." "Will do then, Chief." "Thanks, man," said Steve as Tim saluted and stepped out of the doorway. Steve turned and grabbed the printed spreadsheet from his HP LaserJet. There was definitely something here. There had to be. He just wasn’t seeing it. Yet. Steve knew he wasn’t quite as sharp as he should be today. He was distracted. But then again, who could blame him. I mean this was High Flyer he was schedule the face tonight. The High Flyer – reigning fWo World Champion (if there still was such a thing and a verifiable Idol to Millions). This was a real big time champion; not some paunchy gym teacher nurturing Ricky Steamboat pipe dreams. Watson knew that the folks running the GTT6 had to see him as the longest of long shots. He heard the talk. He read the chat boards. He saw the betting odds. Good God, had he ever even been a part of an event that even had betting odds. The world saw Steve Watson as a nobody who never even wrestled under his own name or showing his own face and "The High Flyer" Jack Harmen was a Superstar of the first degree. Heck, Steve felt lucky that he had even made the cut. But he was also the kind of guy that knew he could make his own luck. He always had, and he would do so again tonight. And it’s not like all the hype (or lack thereof) wasn’t something that Steve couldn’t work to his own advantage. After all, he knew almost everything there was to know about his famous foe while his foe had to know just about nothing about him. Heck, Steve had just watched Flyers entire 2003 series with Sammy Slugger on YouTube just last night. He knew that Harmen had a tendency to get ahead of himself at times. A propensity to take risks at times that they were really not necessary. He knew that High Flyer could make mistakes – and he just had to be there, ready to take advantage. Watson – on the other hand – would enter the ring as a total mystery. There was no way that High Flyer could have scouted him as well has he had scouted Flyer – not unless he had the additional ability to sneak into county fairs and church social halls fully unnoticed by scads of rabid rassling fans for the past three years. Steve could do this. He knew he could. RING! RING! It was the phone. Steve reached across his desk to grab it – until he caught the Caller ID. It was that kid from Icon again. Steve let it hit voicemail. Dammit man. You have to concentrate. This is your nine to five, Steve. This is your bread and butter – and you almost let a sales call catch you unawares. This is the job that you are great at, the job that allows you the luxury to pursue flights of fancy like GTT6 while still allowing you to live the settled life that you and the wife have become accustomed to. This is the job that requires your full concentration. At least until five p.m. Steve breathed deeply; filling his lungs with the buildings freshly filtered air. There will be enough time to worry about Arm Drags and Axe-Handle Smashes on the drive home. Right now his mind needs to stay on the business at hand. And that is the business of discovering a possible embezzler. Steve eased back into his desk chair and studied the spreadsheet again. Deeper this time. More intuitively. He had to locate the anomaly. That’s what they paid him for. That’s what made him rise so quickly. That’s what made him such a success. Accountants are not bred to think outside of the box. But that is exactly what Steve Watson was best at. September was off. It was wrong. And Steve would find out exactly why. Numbers do not lie. Outside the box September had always been the most consistent of months. So what made this September so different? Wait. Maybe that’s it. Maybe this September wasn’t different. Maybe it was the previous ones. Maybe it was all the other years that Steve had been gauging the figures that were different, and this September finally got it right. Steve grabbed for his phone and dialed extension 4868, "Peggy, it’s Steve. You call Iron Mountain yet?" … "No, that’s good. When you do, I am actually going to need the receipts and expense forms for the last eight years." …. "Yeah, all of them. And, depending on how those look, I might need to go back even further." …. "Great. Let me know how soon they think they can get them here." Steve hung up the phone and grabbed a rather large ledger from the bookshelf next to his desk and started to flip through it. He tapped his finger on the page as he found what he was looking for. Yeah, that’s it, Steve. Focus. His is your gift. This is what allows you to do so well on anything you set your mind to. It’s what allowed you to sail solo around the Cape of Good Hop. And it’s what will allow him to stun the rabid throngs tonight when you conquer… No. Tonight will have to wait for tonight. Steve grabbed the phone once more. Now what was the name of that new girl down in personnel? Suddenly there was a flash on Steve’s computer screen. He had Mail. And he didn’t like the looks of it either. Yahoo address that he didn’t recognize and in fact seemed picked exactly for that purpose. The subject line read the message simply enough "Stop It Now." Steve clicked it open on his Outlook. STOP WHAT YOU ARE DOING. IT DOES NOT CONCERN YOU. SOMETIMES IT IS BEST TO LEAVE WELL ENOUGH ALONE. Was this a threat? Steve didn’t take kindly to threats. Steve’s private fax machine beeped then kicked into action as the first page of a new document came across. Steve got up to look. The cover page read, "We do all have out little secrets, don’t we?" Steve’s jaw dropped as he looked on at what the fax machine continued to print. It was page after page of black and white photographs. Photographs of a masked wrestler in action in the center of a ring. Steve knew the pictures well. They were him.
