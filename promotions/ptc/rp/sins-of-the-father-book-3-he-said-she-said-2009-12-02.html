Sins of the Father Book 3: He Said, She Said

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 I woke up to what sounded like footsteps. Sunlight was streaming through my windows, which didn’t really tell me much of anything because the sun was up when we got home last night. This morning. Whenever. But I couldn’t get over the footsteps. Rosie was asleep in front of me, so it wasn’t her. She looked like she was sleeping soundly, and I didn’t want to disturb her; at the bar last night Valerie told me that she told her that she hasn’t been sleeping well lately. In hindsight, the fact that she was pretty much attached to a cup of coffee all day yesterday should’ve told me something. Regardless, she seemed to be peacefully playing the little spoon, with my left hand entwined in hers. I kissed her just behind her ear, but then I heard the footsteps again. Lou had told me he was staying at Sally’s last night, so that eliminated him. My brain took a few minutes to catch up: it’s a side effect of the whole process of touring. Gain an hour, lose an hour, catch a flight, miss a flight, drive two hundred miles one way and three hundred the other. Two nights ago I got back to New York. I think that’s why I took the match last night; it was a show I could take a cab there and back again. Pops crashed on the couch last night. I eased my way out of bed and grabbed a pair of shorts off the floor. My arms were still sore from the match last night, but that’s what you get, so I didn’t pay it any mind. As I stepped lightly toward and through the door, there was some low talking going on that abruptly stopped as soon as I closed the bedroom door again. “Hello?” said my father’s voice, quietly, from the kitchen. He emerged, phone in one hand and a coffee mug in the other, “Randall. I hope I didn’t wake you.” You did, I said, but it’s all well. I’m not doing much good sleeping through the day. I poured myself a cup of coffee and sat down at the kitchen table and looked up and back at the wall clock – it was one thirty in the afternoon. So what’s going on this afternoon, I asked. “Well, I’m meeting with a client tomorrow morning,” said dad, “so I’m pretty free this afternoon. I thought we could spend some time together. What do you do when you’re home?” I laughed and sat up, immediately regretting it. Post – match stiffness usually needs rest to ease it out, not an all- nighter. We don’t spend a whole lot of time at home just hanging out, I said, usually when I’m in New York it’s for just a day or two, so there’s bills, there’s laundry, there’s catching up on the goings – on of the neighborhood. Plus, Rosie works five nights a week and is sometimes at the bar fifty to sixty hours a week to keep things running smoothly, so that becomes the thing to do. Dad looked at me like I just broke the stereo again. “So you spend all your time in the bar?” he asked, “Don’t you think that’s a waste of your time?” No, I said, matter – of – factly, I don’t. I know you don’t really have anything to compare, but I’m actually getting a fair bit of traction in the wrestling business which means my face is recognized more often. I go to a bar, there’s always something else happening and I can get some time to myself, or me and my boys can decompress from the show or from the road. I continued. Why I like TC’s in particular, I said, is because of those same reasons, plus, as a regular, I never get bothered because of what I do. Miss Ivy understands the importance of a pub to a neighborhood’s sense of identity and community— “Okay, okay,” interrupted my father, “I didn’t mean any offense. I just don’t want to ever see your potential get lost at the bottom of a pitcher of beer is all.” Trust me, I said, you don’t have to worry about that. Let me wake Rosie up and we’ll go get some breakfast or something, cool? “Oh—“ said my dad, “Yeah, sure, no problem.” I didn’t really know what to make of his commentary. Is there a problem, I asked. “No, not at all,” he said, “I was just hoping to catch up with you, is all.” Gotcha, I said, but you need to also understand that Rosie is my girlfriend, and she’s my best friend. You want to catch up with me, you need to catch up with both of us. It looked like I might’ve upset him, but he’ll get over it. Nine years after the fact, the last thing I’m going to do is alter my life to make his easier, no matter how much I’m looking forward to spending some time with him. Rosie is a part of mine, so he has to deal with it. I really was sleeping the best I had in weeks when the hand touched my shoulder and shook. He was trying to do it gently, but of course, when has anyone ever been able to wake someone up gently? “Hey Rosie,” said RK, “You up?” I am now, I said, after taking a few seconds to untangle my brain from its wonderful state of sleep, why? “I thought we’d go get breakfast with my dad,” said RK. That’s right, we met up with his dad last night and he crashed at the apartment. And while I didn’t really look forward to the idea of getting up and playing nice… Sure, I said, just give me a few. “No worries,” said RK, “I was going to take a quick shower, but I can hold off if you want to go first.” I’m good, I said, I can wait until later. RK smiled and kissed me, and grabbed his towel off the back of the door before leaving the bedroom, making a left and a left, and closing the bathroom door. In case you’re curious, RK is what I call my boyfriend. Everyone else who knows him calls him Knox, except for his parents who both call him Randall, and my mom, who calls him Randy. She’s the only one who can get away with that. The rest of you most likely know him as Impulse. Me, I’m his girlfriend. Rosalyn Callasantos, at your service, though most people call me Rose or Cally. And at this particular moment, the prospect of getting out of a warm bed to have breakfast with RK and his deadbeet dad was less appealing than several hundred other things I could think of off the top of my head. But I know this is important to him, so I’ll play the dutiful girlfriend. I rolled over once to the edge of the bed and let my feet touch down on the floor, and picked up a pair of jeans from the back of RK’s computer chair and slid them on. Nothing like a dirtbag rockstar day to blissfully remind you that you don’t have to deal with a nine – to – five desk job. RK needs some throw rugs, or some carpet or something. He’s got finished wood floors that are freezing, even in the middle of summer. Fortunately, I steal his socks all the time. No sooner did I leave the bedroom, than the shower fizzed on. RK is ridiculously utilitarian, so I knew there was only five minutes or so until showtime. “Good afternoon. Rosalyn, was it?” asked Mr. Knox from the kitchen. Good afternoon sir, I replied, You can call me Rose. He walked toward me, nodding. “Please call me Max,” he said. Okay, Max, I replied. I shook his hand, noting the impeccably groomed nature of his hair and hands, and the smell of soap in the air. Obviously, Max had been up for a while and had had time to take his own shower, thus making me pretty self – conscious about my own haggard appearance. “Once Randall is finished we’re going to go get breakfast,” said Max, “Do you want us to bring you back anything?” I looked to my left, toward the bathroom door, then back to Mr. Knox. RK just woke me up, actually, because we were all going together. Did I miss something? “Oh,” said Max, “No, that was what we talked about, but I we could probably be there and back by the time you’re ready so it made more sense to just bring something back.” Ready, I asked. I’m ready right now. “Oh,” he said again. As if I didn’t feel awkward enough meeting my boyfriend’s father for the first time, right? I don’t embarrass, and I don’t really care what my outward appearance may look like from moment to moment because of my job – it’s afforded me strange hours and this current trip is comparable, I suppose, to getting up at four in the morning because you can’t sleep, pulling on whatever clothes you wore all day, and going to the corner diner for coffee and toast. The place RK likes is about five blocks down Dyckman, I said, and you don’t exactly need a reservation. You might even be a bit overdressed for it. Mr. Knox looked at himself and shrugged. “I always believed in the importance of good grooming,” he said. With that, the water shut off. I wasn’t sure why he was so intent on tricking slash shaming me into not going with them. Yeah, they hadn’t seen each other in years and I’m sure Mr. Knox wanted some time alone with RK, but that’s not my concern. What mattered to me was that RK asked me to go with them. Sorry, Max. You lose. “So, tell me,” said my dad, “Who exactly do you work for?” Pardon? I asked. “It’s not like the old days,” answered Rosie, “You’re not working for one promoter looking to put everyone else out of business. These days, the promoters all work together to keep the business going. RK works for one out of Baltimore and another out of Seattle, but he’s always going somewhere.” “Interesting,” said my dad, “I thought the wrestling business paid pretty well these days.” Oh, it does, I said, I just like to work. And when you consider how many people try to break into the business every year, you need to be able to separate yourself from the pack. We sat at a small, cramped table inside the best diner above 200th street, eating breakfast and drinking coffee. The conversation had been pleasant, but Dad kept steering things towards money, and I got the sense that Rosie wasn’t very comfortable around him. I’d have to ask her about it later, when we were alone. For now, there didn’t seem to be any point in calling either of them out. “I admire your work ethic,” said dad, “A lot of people wouldn’t look for an opportunity outside their own neighborhood. You get into a comfort zone and you stagnate.” He gestured toward Rosie. “I guess the kid’s gotta lot of his old man in ‘em, wouldn’t you say, dear?” “Yeah, I guess,” said Rosie, “What do you do again?” “I’m a salesman,” said my dad, “Office supplies. My territory is the Northeast, from Maine to Virginia. It’s rough going sometimes, but I enjoy being on the road, and I enjoy having a different place to sleep every night. Not to mention, someone different on the other side of the bed, right, son?” Dad, I said. “Excuse me,” said Rosie, as she got up and made her way through the crowd to the back of the diner. “Something I said?” asked dad. Dad, I repeated, neither one of us really want to hear about your nightly escapades. He looked equal parts shocked and offended. I can’t believe it didn’t dawn on him that I’m his kid and Rosie is my girlfriend. “I’m sorry, Randall,” he said, “You’re right of course, it’s just that we are all adults here, and I’m used to speaking frankly with my friends.” Dad looked in the direction Rose went. “She is an adult, right?” Dad, she’s twenty two, I said, and I’m only twenty three. Is a year and change really that much of a difference? “No, of course not,” he replied, “Listen, Randall… she seems like a nice girl, but I’m concerned.” Here it comes. What about, I asked, pretty sure of the answer. “Are you sure she isn’t holding you back?” he asked, “You’re out there, traveling, you’ve got some degree of fame and I’m sure you could have any woman you wanted. Is it really worth it to have an anchor tying you to your childhood neighborhood, whose life ambition appears to be to die there while serving beers to fat men in flannel shirts?” Don’t start, I said, You haven’t been around for eight years— “I know,” he interrupted, “and I’m sorry, and I know I don’t have any right to try and act like a father now. You look like you turned into a fine young man without me around, and don’t misunderstand – Rose seems like a sweet girl. I’m talking solely as an impartial observer.” I sighed. It’s true, he didn’t seem to have anything personal against Rosie. I could even almost believe that his needling and his backhanded statements were being made without his even realizing what he was saying. And I could appreciate that he was telling me he was looking out for my best interest. Still… Regardless, I said, she’s my best friend, dad. I love her, and I love the fact that she loves our neighborhood. I love this place, and wouldn’t have any plans to leave, even if I was single. For the record, she’s not just a bartender, she practically runs the entire bar. “I see,” said dad, “I stand corrected.” Good, I replied, I’d like you to apologize to her when she gets back to the table. I wasn’t crying. I’d like to make that clear. RK’s father was just so upsetting to listen to, not just with his words but with his attitude towards me, that I could feel my chest tighten and my breathing was getting just a bit labored. I will not have an asthma attack in front of him. I will not give him the satisfaction of seeing me that weak. So, a quick trip to the ladies for a pull on my inhaler and a repeated chorus of the above in my head as I stared at myself in the mirror, and I felt like I was ready to rejoin the world. I knew RK wasn’t buying what his dad was trying to push, but it’s still a little uncomfortable to listen to. Particularly when he is, to an extent, right. RK is destined for greatness, and I’d never forgive myself if I was the only reason he never re-rooted in a different area. Deep breath. The diner was just as crowded as ever, which isn’t surprising, since I was only gone for two, three minutes, tops. But there was a six top in the process of sitting down or getting up, and they were covering one of the two thin walkways. RK and his dad were still sitting at the table, having what appeared to be an animated conversation. Hey, I said, as I sat back down, sorry about that. “No problem,” said RK, “Dad?” “I’d like to apologize if I offended you,” said Mr. Knox, “There’s a time and place for certain comments and stories, and I really was talking in general terms only.” I didn’t believe him, and I know he’s only saying this because RK asked him to. Everything’s fine, I said, don’t worry about it. Now was not the time to talk to RK. Later, when we’re alone. “Good,” said RK, “are we ready?” “Let me finish my coffee,” said Mr. Knox. “No worries,” replied RK, “I’ll go pay – my treat.” Thanks, dear, I said, as I reached behind myself to grab my coat. “Rosalyn,” said Mr. Knox. Yes, Max, I asked, though I really didn’t want to know what he would try to say next. “I think you’re a very nice young woman, I appreciate the relationship you have with my son, and I can tell that you two think the world of each other, so please don’t think anything I’m saying is a personal slight.” But… I was waiting for it. “I just don’t think you’re right for him,” said Mr. Knox, “I think you’d do better with someone with a little less ambition and talent, someone who can stay home with you instead of jet – setting all over the world.” He gestured to my collection of hospital bracelets on my right wrist. “Someone who can take care of you when you get sick.” I pulled my jacket on and made sure the five bracelets were covered. Suddenly, my stupid little game of seeing how many I could collect before the end of the year seemed really selfish. I take care of myself just fine, I said, and I take care of RK just as much as he does me when we’re together. For that matter, do you really think you can show up after eight years and try to act like you’re interested in his life? He laughed, which I honestly wasn’t expecting, but can’t say surprised me. “I’m just trying to take an interest in my boy’s life,” he said, as he stood up, “don’t get hysterical on me.” With that, he stood up and walked past RK at the register, and out the door. I watched him light a cigarette through the window, and wondered if RK let him know about his strict no smoking policy at home. Even Lou, who lives there, goes out on the fire escape when he needs to light one. “Hey,” said RK, as we met at the register, “You ready?” Yeah, I said, and he put his arm around me and we walked out of the diner together. It was about a five to ten minute walk back to the apartment, and even though it smelled like snow out it was far too warm, so we walked at a leisurely pace. “Hold on a second,” said RK’s dad, as we passed a convenience store, “Need to get smokes.” I started to follow him inside, as everyone always does. I wondered about that, actually – when you’re in a group and one person is going into a store for something, why does everyone else feel the need to follow? “Wait,” said RK, breaking up my stream of consciousness, “hold on a second, Rosie.” I turned around and faced him. He looked really, really serious. “Something’s up between you and my dad,” he said, “What’s going on?” Nothing, I said. “Don’t feed me a line, kiddo,” insisted RK, “I can tell when something’s on your mind. And he might be my father, but he’s never acted like one. You, on the other hand…” And then he kissed me. I was in love with RK for probably three or four years before we first hooked up, and then it was touch and go when he was away at college. Even still, I still get a chill every time we kiss. It’s moments like these that make me realize how lucky I am that I’m with someone who gets me on so many levels, who can read my moods and always seems to instinctively know exactly how to reach me. Seriously, I said, as I smiled, He’s a little abrasive but there’s nothing wrong. I promise you, I’d tell you if there was. RK smiled, and I leaned into his arms. Why didn’t I tell him? Because even though my father died when I was nine years old, and as I was reminded once before, at the very least, I have positive memories with him. RK doesn’t really have that luxury. More to the point, he carries around the memory of being utterly unimportant for the past eight years. So I lied. Isn’t it worth it, for the sake of the ones we love?
