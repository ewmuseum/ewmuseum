Dr Faustiosity - Chapter 3: The Squared Circle

10 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-12-10 Doctor Curiosity sat with Merrick behind an overturned metal table. He’d been through a lot to get to the Grand Final of GTT4; in fact he’d been through a hell of a lot. He’d seen the wife he thought he’d divorced turn up and say that she loved him again; he’d had her turn out to be a manipulative psychotic bunny-boiler with designs on his money; he’d even seen her fall to her death at the hands of his own brother. This was without mentioning the Curious Mallets, Disney Devices and other assorted inventions of lunacy. Still, nothing had prepared him for hiding with a demon in a prison riot he had accidentally started whilst invisible. Some things you just can’t plan for. “Did I just see a disembodied head go past, or vas zat a boot?” asked the doctor. “It was a boot. Believe me when I say I know a disembodied head when I see one,” replied Merrick, who seemed to be quite enjoying himself. Doctor Curiosity flinched as another boot sailed over his head. “I vonder how Paul is getting on. I hope no harm has befallen him.” “He’s about ten metres past us, and he’s getting hit repeatedly in the stomach by a man smaller than him,” said Merrick. Curiosity’s eyes widened. This was all his fault, and he had to do something about it. Looking around nervously, he darted from behind the overturned table and scurried to where a fight appeared to be in progress. Of course, this being a prison riot, there were fights going on pretty much everywhere, as various law-breakers decided to settle scores with one another while there were no guards there to stop them. Curiosity had to peer invisibly over the shoulders of five or six fights before he found the right one. He didn’t even clearly see the people fighting, because as soon as he heard a yell and a grunt from his brother, he launched himself at the mass of bodies. Curiosity landed on the attacker and unloaded a stiff right forearm to the back of the head… before realising that the head looked familiar. Sitting behind the table still, Merrick smiled and spoke to himself. “Ah yes, I got it wrong. He’s repeatedly hitting someone who’s bigger than he is. How careless of me…” Ten metres down the hallway, Doctor Curiosity stood up and surveyed his handiwork. His brother, who had been doing pretty well in the fight until that point, was groaning and trying to get back to his feet. “Which one of you bastards did that? You were supposed to be watching my back!” yelled Eegor. The other men standing around looked sheepish, and Eegor rounded on one of them, shoving him back into the other nearest man. Curiosity stepped backwards and mumbled an apology, before sneaking back to the table, where Merrick awaited. Once there he turned and watched his brother in action. It looked like the strong survival instinct ran in the family. Eegor handled himself well in combat, having learnt a great deal in his time working with his brother. A strong college athlete and even a collegiate wrestler, Paul Goodman’s stronger attributes appeared to be earning him the respect of his fellow inmates. “Vell, Merrick, I tzink zat I have saved my brother enough for vone day,” said the doctor. “You saved him? I must have missed that one while I was looking the other way.” “Qviet, demon. Do you vant zis soul or not? You have proved nothing to me as yet. My patience is not infinite, hell-spawn. Get me out of here.” “To where?” asked the demon. “Take me to ze arena.” *SNAP* “Woah…” was all Doctor Curiosity could say, as the prison vanished in an instant, the demon whisking them to the arena in Washington DC where the final match of the tournament would take place. They appeared next to the seats that the audience would be perched on the edge of during the final. Down in the centre of the auditorium, the ring crew were working hard on making sure everything was in perfect condition. The match would not take place for a couple more days, but for an event of this magnitude there would be no room for error in the build-up. The only errors would be committed in the ring, by the participants… …or perhaps they’d make their errors before even stepping through the ropes. “So, Merrick, who vould you bet on to vin zis tournament?” asked the doctor. “Well, I would naturally choose you to win it, unless the other wrestlers go and get themselves demons.” “Zey can do zat too?” “Well, Dave Hurst took out Angelo Deville. I wouldn’t put anything past him,” replied Merrick. “Hang on now, vhy vould zey beat me if zey had a demon? Are you trying to say zat ze others vould conjure more powerful demons than you, Merrick?” accused Curiosity. “Now, let’s not jump to conclusions,” said Merrick, uncertainty almost creeping into his voice. “Demon, I command you to tell me ze truth now. Are you powerful enough for me to defeat Dave Hurst and Craig Goat?” “Yes.” “You’re not lying?” “No.” “Are you sure?” “Absolutely. As long as I’m with you, there’s nothing to worry about. Just don’t go offending me or getting too big for your boots, or else your chances go right down the pan,” said Merrick, his confidence renewed as he stood up and sauntered away from the doctor. “Vhy is zat? Vould you support zem in ze match to spite me, demon?” Merrick turned round, and for a second he cast off his civilised appearance. The nostrils flared and the reek of sulphur engulfed Doctor Curiosity. The lumps on the demon’s head seemed to grow to sharp points, and everything was the colour of blood. He held himself like this for a moment, and it was enough to terrify the doctor. “Do you really think that I care enough about you to do that, human? Do you really think that you are important enough to me? If I do not get what I want, then you will be left in disarray and troubles purely of your own doing. Just think to yourself what the other competitors are doing right now. They’ll be training hard, studying their opponents and preparing themselves for the biggest night of their careers. What are you doing? You’re sitting in the stands congratulating yourself on being so smart and so clever.” Curiosity gulped, but the demon was not finished. “You think you’re so smart, but if you make the wrong decision doctor, then you’re going to lose. You called me because you needed a miracle. Hell’s a strange place to be going for a miracle, but you got lucky. As payment for the power I give to you, there’s but one price. Just remember this, human; if I go home empty-handed, then so do you.” Doctor Curiosity shuddered. He had called the demon for precisely that reason. He genuinely believed that without Eegor he wouldn’t be able to overcome the likes of Craig Goat, and especially Exceptional Dave Hurst. There would be no assistance for him, no curious devices for him to design and Eegor to build, and perhaps worst of all there would be two opponents in the ring at once, making it considerably harder to cheat effectively. Yet something felt wrong about the claims of the demon. Doctor Curiosity found that he didn’t like his chances of a genuine victory being rubbished so easily. It was one thing for him to count himself out, but for another to do it so callously… it hurt his sense of pride. “You tzink I vould have trouble vith someone like Craig Goat?” he asked, in a low voice. The demon had left to walk elsewhere, but it turned around and wandered nonchalantly back over towards the doctor. “Yes, as a matter of fact I think you would lose. Let’s face it, you’re no Seymour Almasy, and you saw what happened to him.” “You tzink I vould lose to some little nerdy child who has been pretending to be a wrestler for too long? I vould have difficulty in despatching a pathetic wretch of a man who panders to ze every vish of some pathetic nurse? A nurse! Nurses tzink zey know everything! I’m no medical doctor, but I’ve been in a hospital before, and all zese vannabe doctors strut around is if zey ever studied! It makes my blood boil!” “Okay, so he’s inexperienced and you might be able to take advantage of that. If your tricks come off, you could beat him… perhaps. That’s not the man you’re afraid of though, is it? You’re scared of Dave Hurst. He’s been just like you for too long, and he’s done it without anyone else helping him out. You know he would beat you. You’ve not even prepared yourself to face him, it worries you too much. I’ll reiterate in case you missed me say it the last time. You are nothing on your own.” Curiosity was riled, and his stubborn nature bypassed his natural fear of the demon. “So if you’re here zen I vill defeat him?” “Of course,” replied the demon, calmness personified. “Zen vhy did Angelo Deville lose? Has he not been known to play vith ze dark arts?” The demon turned on him again. It wasn’t even intentional, but Merrick’s own mask slipped briefly, the usual terrible stench accompanying the blurring of his features and a glimpse of the grotesque. “This is not Harry fucking Potter, human. You know nothing of which you speak! Angelo Deville lost for one reason and one reason only. He turned his back on us. He turned his back on the way he had followed. He was tested and he failed. He called out for salvation, for hope in some other power to save him. He wanted to use faith against us, and you see where it got him? Do you know where he is now?” “N… no.” “Exactly. Deville’s loss was not of our doing. Remember that, human. He lost to Dave Hurst because he turned his back on us.” Curiosity was silenced. He walked down the stairs to the ringside seats in silence, as Merrick observed him. With a well practiced grace, the doctor hopped over the railings and was standing next to the ring technicians. The demon’s charms were still working, and none of them saw him, moving around his presence without comment or notice. The doctor rolled underneath the bottom rope and threw his stained lab coat to the ground. He moved around, surprisingly nimble as he danced on the balls of his feet. He reflected that he hadn’t found the time to train much recently. If it wasn’t one thing then it was another, preventing him from getting to the practice ring or the gym. He could have sworn that his belt had been one notch further in before, too. He sighed and patted his stomach ruefully. “Can ve get out of here, Merrick?” *SNAP* ------------------------------------------------------------- Time passed, and dawn’s light shone down upon the Mojave Desert. The van held ten men, all of whom had one thing on their mind. The eight in the back tested their weapons one final time, whether they were guns, knives or fists, and assured one another that this was the simplest job of their lives. The only one to feel any disquiet was the former amateur wrestler in the balaclava, Anthony. Something didn’t feel right about this whole situation, but he couldn’t put his finger on it. In the front, Frank and the driver of the van nodded in unison, and they began the drive to the house of their target. They drove to the Curious Laboratory. ------------------------------------------------------------- “You’re in no shape to wrestle, human.” It was less the words that woke Doctor Curiosity from his slumbers, and more Merrick tapping him on the head with an empty champagne bottle. The doctor mumbled something incoherent as he tried to sit up, looking around with bleary, bloodshot eyes. His wide pupils caught sight of Merrick, silhouetted in the window frame, and he turned his head away from both the bright light, and the mere sight of the demon. The previous night had been spent in a vain attempt to put the reality of the situation behind him. The most luxurious dishes had been served to him, along with enough expensive champagne to make a rap star weep. He had been pampered and fussed over, and all without having to pay for it. Demons, it would seem, were more priceless even than Mastercard. All of this had been done, and yet the reality was seeping in between the open curtains. Merrick still stood there, his face set in a mockery of concern over the doctor. “Damn… I’d hoped that this was a bad dream,” muttered Curiosity. “It’s very real, human. The match is almost upon us, and you look even more pathetic and wretched than you usually do. To think that you once harboured hopes of defeating both Dave Hurst and Craig Goat on your own. They’d make mincemeat of you now.” Curiosity groaned and turned on the television. He flicked around for a while until he found what he had been looking for. “If I’m so useless, demon, zen vhy do ze sports books have me as vell-fancied to vin zis match? I’m no outsider here, if anything I’m a favourite.” “Of course you are,” the demon laughed. “They don’t know how much of your success was down to your brother! You might have designed them, but he constructed most of your curious contraptions. Whenever you were in trouble in the ring, he’d be on hand to distract your opponent, and often would call down violence upon himself to preserve you. Without someone to take the fall for you, you’re doomed. They just don’t know it yet.” “Vell, I’ll show all of zem!” declared the doctor, trying to get up, but falling down clutching his head with a groan. “Got a hangover, have we? Well, it’s just a shame that my hangover cure charms aren’t working so well today. Isn’t life such a bitch?” mocked Merrick. “You’re no use at all!” yelled Curiosity. “Vhat have you done to make me think it vould even be vorth selling my soul to you? You’ve done nothing but hinder me at every turn!” “You want something good? You want a show of power?” taunted the demon. “Jah!” “Excellent. Now it’s about to get interesting.” “Vhat?” “There are people knocking on your front door. Let’s go.” *SNAP*
