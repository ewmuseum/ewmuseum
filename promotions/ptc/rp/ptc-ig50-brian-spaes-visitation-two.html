Itachi Kobayoshi versus Brian Spaes

4 October 2008

Visitation - Two Brian Spaes vs: Itachi Kobayoshi 917 words === The Moonside Diner. DUMBO, Brooklyn. Brian and Jack Spaes sat side-by-side over looking the counter space. The overhead lights were dim as bright day turned into the darkening dusk. The father and son hovered above their meal. Brian ate his fried egg burger selectively, slowly picking away at the sandwich and drinking a cup of coffee. Jack, however, ate quite differently; nearly shoving his chicken fingers and shoestring fries in his face. Ketchup and chocolate milkshake residue adorned his face. Brian put down his burger and looked at Jack. “What’s the rush?” *Animalistic chewing and swallowing noises* “Seriously Jack, you are actin’ like Denise starves ya.” Jack stopped and smiled. He rose above his plate as a cheetah would over their gazelle in the savannah. He had a bit of a mischievous and carnal look as he emerged from his kill. “Would you be too surprised if I said yes?” Brian chuckled and shook his head. Somehow, the fear of a nearly 12-year-old predator was not instilled on him. “I mean, I could say bad things about her but I’m not gonna—“ “- You’re the one who married Aunt D.” “That bein’ said, you are really scarfin’ down your dinner. Gonna get you a heart burn when you’re my age doin’ that.” Jack shrugged. The plights of a middle-aged man did not apply to the former Deputy of the Squared Circle. “It tastes really good so I want to eat as much of it as I can. That way I get to enjoy it.” Brian pause for a few seconds to determine his response. He knew he wasn’t that good at imparting knowledge or fatherly advice. Often he’d get flustered or use “country witticisms” that would go on deaf ears. However, Brian felt a pull, stronger than normal, to try to teach his son tonight. The courts did not react kindly to him re-entering wrestling. With all the dangers of the profession, and Brian’s addictive past, they were going to revoke visitation of Jack, and Brian’s two twin daughters, Kirsten and Hannah. He knew this would happen if he decided to lace up the boots again. He didn’t know why he felt a desire to “make good” with Jack. Perhaps it was because he understood the awkward years ahead. Alternatively, in a misguided way, he thought that the girls would be too young to realize their father’s absence, one that existed pretty much since their birth. After a judicious pause, Brian was ready to dispense advice. “Jack, this might sound crazy, but you should go with me on this. We go through life looking to find somethin’ great and good. Like your chicken tenders. We got bad things in life, like hurtin’ your knee or losin’ a baseball game. And it’s normal to want to shove your food down your throat. Life has made time precious to us, so we don’t wanna waste time cause we only got so much of it. But if we don’t slow down and enjoy our little small things, we’ll let them slip by. They get caught in our rush to get back to life. Sometimes we think little things aren’t important when they are actually the events that will stick out when we look back at it all and we try to figure out life and happiness. I lost so many of those moments due to many things. Jack, I made bad decisions and bad choices that made me forget or pass over the dinners and moments I would have cherished. I don’t want you to do that. I don’t want you to miss a small special flash of awesomeness because you were too concerned with the bigger picture. I want you to look back at your life without regrets. Because I wish I could do the same.” By this time, Jack was looking at his father intently. Regardless of this being a stereotypical “dad talk,” he was trying to soak in his father’s guidance. Chocolate milkshake slowly moved up the straw, as Jack was enthralled with the promo his dad was cutting on life. Brian didn’t think of this as a promo or a “call out” that happened countless times in the past. This wasn’t intentionally why he did this, but he desperately tried to vindicate the poor parenting and role-modeling job he did to this point. At this point, Brian realized why he wanted to see Jack tonight. He knew why he tried to now give competent advice. As someone whose childhood past him by, and his adolescence was spent training and learning on the road, Brian was envious and concerned about his son. Envious in that Jack would have an upbringing that’d let him mature and enjoy his young years. He was concerned because of all of the damage that Brian unknowingly inflicted upon his oldest child, the only one that would remember and know “Impulse” Brian James, the raging meathead and alcoholic. It would be impossible and foolish for Brian Spaes to attempt to make good on 11 years of a screwed up childhood. He couldn’t call a one-night do over. Brian would try to make this night special. He would hope that Jack could see the changes and the improvements in his father. Jack might finally see that his father cared. This night would be a night that Jack could look back upon with happiness during times of trouble. This final visitation would give him inspiration through out the rest of Jack and Brian’s life.
