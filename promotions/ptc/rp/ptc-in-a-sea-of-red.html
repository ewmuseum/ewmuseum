In a Sea of Red

14 September 2005

The Gilchrist family was the subject of unwelcome envy. They were treated as outcasts in that small, North Carolinian town. Dr. Colin Gilchrist had chosen that town for his newest research center. The unwelcoming isolation of the people contradicted the town’s claims of hospitality, but the location was still ideal nonetheless. It wasn’t a problem for the Gilchrist family, who bought a house on the outskirts of the town, away from any neighbors. At thirty-two years of age, Dr. Colin Gilchrist was one of the youngest and most successful scientists in modern-day medicine. His reputation was well known amongst the science community and it is that sense of awe that invited many of the small town people to fear him. They envied him. He was the epitome of the “dream” - a young, ruggedly handsome, successful business man with a stunning wife and two kids. The Gilchrist family had to go, or at least be made humble. Those Gilchrist were soon going to get what they deserved. That’s what the man thought to himself as he stayed crouched down in the bushes just outside their front door. He had surveyed the house several times before. He knew the names of the kids, the babysitter, what time the mother and father got home. He looked at his watch. Veronica Gilchrist was going to be home any minute now. He had an hour to do whatever he wanted with her before her husband Dr. Colin Gilchrist arrived from his work. On schedule, a black 2005 Jaguar pulled up in the driveway. He watched Veronica walk out of the car, carrying her purse. On any other day, he would love to see her, see her dressed in her short black skirt and white blouse. She was indeed a beauty and a well-respected pediatrician…in other cities. But today was different. He had to show the family that they were unwelcome here. The babysitter walked out of the house. One day, he was going to kill her. She was one of the few people that appreciated the Gilchrist family. She was going to get hers. He watched Veronica dismiss her for the night. He waited for her to look for her keys than made his move. Veronica was tired from another long day at work. She wanted to move out of the small town, but she knew how important this was for her husband’s research. She ruffled through her purse, drawing her keys. She opened the door and at a far reflection in the living room saw someone standing behind her. Veronica tried to shut the door behind her. She was not fast enough. The man rushed the door open, knocking Veronica down to the ground. She reached for her purse to try to draw out a weapon. He wasn’t going to let that happen and kicked the purse out of reach. Veronica struggled to get free, but her assailant mounted her and started to unzip his pants. She reached for her high heel behind him and smacked the man on the side of his head. She smacked him once more to free herself and then tried to run through the living room. In her frantic state she was clumsy and very vulnerable. It was all the man needed to grab her leg and pull her back over the floor in one ugly motion. He punched her in the face and ripped her silk blouse down the middle. He was now staring at her supple breast. But this was not for enjoyment, this was for power. He needed to show her just who was in control here. He mounted her and punched her again. Veronica lay there helplessly. She knew that she had to give in or she was going to be killed. She didn’t even scream. It’s not that she didn’t want to. There was no one around to help. There was no need to scream and wake the children up, if she did not know what kind of man this was. He hiked up her skirt and ripped her panties off and then removed his own pants. In one quick, vile motion he penetrated her. Veronica winced. The ravaging was gruesome, vile, horrifying. He had finally gotten what he wanted. There she was: Veronica Gilchrist, the powerful woman, lay humbled before him, servicing him. He ravaged her body over and over again. Veronica lay submissive to the attack. Yet, in a strange way she kind of liked it. No. She loved it. This was one of her sexual fantasies: to be taken dominantly and against her will. Veronica could no longer play the victim and kissed her assailant on the lips, “I love you, honey.” Dr. Colin Gilchrist returned the kiss, “I love you, too.” **** The basement of the warehouse seemed stuck in time. It was still covered in wooden floors, beams, and the walls appeared to be supporting moss. Only one light was available in the basement, and it hung by a thin wire in the middle of the room. The light gave the room a sort of orange hue and the floors creaked whenever someone walked overhead. Scattered throughout the basement were wooden chairs, tables stacked to the ceiling, and a pile of assorted wooden junk. John looked around. There were thirteen of them counting him. They were all of various ages, some in their teens, and many in their thirties. He watched them bicker among themselves. Sometimes they spoke of the matter at hand, sometimes they didn’t. He didn’t care. He was having second thoughts about the mission. As a superstitious man, John was aware of the omen of having thirteen men on the job. He reached into his pocket and rubbed his lucky rabbit’s foot keychain. It had never failed him before and it wasn’t going to fail him now. “Everyone just shut up,” John yelled. Everyone turned their attention to him. “Are we gonna do this shit?” someone asked. “Yeah…it’s about time we exposed that research center,” John replied. “They think they can come to our town and start experimenting with our environment, animals, and other furry little things, they got another thing coming!” A thunderous approval roared through the crowd. “What about the police,” someone asked, “don’t they got them blue shirts under their control?” “Yeah, what about the police?” someone else chimed in. “We can’t worry about the police,” John said. “Once we expose the frikkin’ doctor and what he’s been doing, it ain’t gonna matter a damn thing what the police say.” One of the men stood up, “he’s right y’all. The police ain’t gonna have a choice but to take that darn summabitch out of here. That bottom-feeding son of a bitch ain’t got shit to do here, but leave!” “Calm down, brudda James,” John said. “We all know what happened. And you believe me now son…no y’all believe me now…this doctor is going to be out of our town tonight.” James nodded and sat back down. “You know, my brother Frank got a job at that research center a few weeks ago…” “Yeah, we heard about that. What happened?” someone in the crowd asked. “Everything was fine until he came home one day like he had seen some ghost, or some shit. He wouldn’t talk to anyone. He was so afraid. I finally got him to talk and he told me of how he saw that they were experimenting on people in that darn factory. Frank told everyone he could. But no one believed him. They inspected the factory, but didn’t find anything. Then Frank went insane. The doctors said that it was some kind of mental illness, but that’s bullshit. We all know that they did something to Frank in there.” “Where’s Frank now?” “Dead. He committed suicide a week later.” “We gotta do something,” someone replied, “he done already gone too far. Our town was a peaceful town for twelve years before that doctor came here ruining all our lives.” John nodded. Everyone got up and exited the basement. John rubbed his lucky rabbit’s foot one more time. He held it firmly in grasp. “You don’t fail me now…you don’t fail me now.” **** Colin sat at the edge of their bed. His wife lay under the covers, reading her book in the lamplight. A beeping sound in his pants made her look up. She rolled her eyes. It was his beeper and most likely he had to go back to work again. Colin put the beeper away and sighed, “honey…” “Don’t start this shit again,” Veronica said, tossing her book over the nightstand. “Why do you have to go back to work tonight? I promise I’ll make it up to you.” “How?” Veronica asked, crossing her arms. “You going to buy me some jewelry and pretend it never happened?” “No. I promise I’ll stay home tomorrow and the entire weekend. It’s going to be an early Valentine’s Day vacation for you.” “Just…” “No, listen to me,” Colin said, taking her hand. “I love you a lot. I know that I haven’t been here a lot these days, and it’s killing me. I love you, baby.” “Go.” “Are you sure?” “Go.” Colin kissed his wife goodbye and dressed himself again. “Aren’t you going to say goodbye to your children?” Veronica asked. Colin nodded and stopped by their bedroom. He tiptoed inside the bedroom, trying not to make any noise. He looked down at his one year old daughter, Amelia, lying in her crib. Next to her, on his own bed, was their son, Ariel, three years old. Colin gently blew kisses to them and left the room, then the house. *** The Psychoanalysis Treatment Center was a huge campus located in the outskirts of the town square. The primary objective of the center was for research and experimentation, but also housed medical and psychological treatment wards. The main building was made of a grayish marble on the outside, a complete contrast to the colonial architecture of the town. Inside, Dr. Gilchrist’s longtime friend and partner in science, Demetri Vieri was head of operations. Demetri was an Italian-American, graduating from John Hopkins University in Maryland and very successful. He had been with Colin in nearly all of his research endeavors and shared a sort of respect and awe of his peer. Next to him, in the main research room, a small, pudgy man looked around nervously. He wore an old, brownish suit and sported a wig that looked like a dead raccoon. He took out a handkerchief from his breast pocket and wiped the sweat from his head. He was Ross Minster, the mayor of this town. “You…you sure he is going to be here,” Ross asked. “Why don’t you ask him yourself?” Ross turned around and saw Colin standing behind him. “Tell me, mayor, what the hell am I doing here?” Colin asked. “I…I know you done a lot for me and this community…” “But…” Demetri said, interrupting the mayor. “We do a lot of work for you and you still want more?” Ross shook his head. “No, it’s not like that at all. It’s just that…I can’t support what you guys do here anymore.” “Come with me, mayor,” Colin said, tugging the mayor’s arm. “We are going to the research lab. Now you haven’t been there before, have you?” “No…” “Good. Follow me.” Colin led the mayor towards the research laboratory. Inside, most of the equipment was turned off. The crew had gone for the night and in their absence the lab looked gargantuan. There were test tubes everywhere, caged animals, and everything you can picture in any forensics lab, or sci-fi movie. “What I do here is nothing less than for the betterment of mankind,” Colin explained. “Do I need to remind you that I am responsible for you being reelected this year?” “No. But I know that you also experiment on people here. I have got all kinds of people on my ass over this. I cannot keep covering for you and I am getting sick of it.” Colin grabbed the mayor’s collar and stared him in the eye. “Are you suddenly developing a backbone on me, Ross?” Ross stuttered, “I-I-I don’t need you anymore. You can do anything you want to me, but sooner or later someone is going to expose you.” “Looks like it isn’t someone but “someones”,” Demetri said. “What?” Colin asked. Demetri pointed to one of the security panels. On the screen, there were three pick up trucks filled with men carrying blunt objects. They were so rowdy and walked straight into the center unafraid of being caught. “Should I alert the police?” Demetri asked, reaching for the intercom. Colin shook his head. “You see?” Ross asked. “You cannot stay here anymore. Nothing, not being mayor of this town is worth this.” “You are right,” Colin grinned. “This is worth much more than you or this town. Remember that your little girl, Emily, was suffering from mental illness. I cured it. Why weren’t they alerting the police? The truth was that there was a much more exciting third option.
