Amy Silveira, GTT5 RP #1: My Return To Hell

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 I woke up, but that doesn't mean that I wanted to. Saturday morning had never been my favourite time of the week, usually because I'd have a glaring hangover or, on the rare occasion, I'd wake up next to some fairly average-looking lad in a hotel somewhere I'd never heard of. But that wasn't why I wasn't looking forward to waking up today. And it wasn't that Los Angeles had presented a bitter and cold day outside, either. The reason that I really didn't want to come back to life, open my tired eyes and try to force myself out of bed was the news I had heard the night before. I glanced over at the pink and furry clock a couple of metres away from the bed. Trying to make out the time as I forced my eyes open, I realised it was about half-past eleven. Not bad for me, especially on a Saturday. Of course, I hadn't woken up on my own accord - it had been quite a noisy day in the house today, and not only because of Catarina's sudden interest in listening to the Pussycat Dolls 24/7, either. I wish that it was just her pathetic music taste, though, because I knew that the noise downstairs was my father's 'brilliant' idea being put into place. Nevertheless, I just quietly groaned at the idea of an unwillingly busy weekend being ahead of me. Realising that my mobile had been flashing missed calls and messages since I woke up, I decided to make a move and get up. I swung my legs around and out of the bed, pausing as the chill of the cold house impacted their bareness. Ever since my father started saving up to buy additional crap to 'help' me, he'd decided to go all macho and refuse to put the heating on unless there was a good reason to waste money on it (I guess freezing to death isn't enough of a big deal to use the heating system, in his mind). As I reluctantly pushed the Winter sheets to the foot of the bed with my right arm, wanting to stay warmly wrapped up in them, my phone burst into life - Dogs Die In Hot Cars' "I Love You 'Cause I Have To". Especially set to only play when the devil himself was calling me, which meant it played much than my usual ringtone. And by that I mean, like, every ten minutes. I quickly shimmied out of the sheets, not wanting an in-person visit from the diabolic creature. I brushed down my compact, silk nightie to try and cover at least a quarter of my upper leg. The walk to the table a few metres away felt like a marathon when I'd barely woken up, but nevertheless I made it in time to take the call. Picking up the mobile, putting on a fake smile (hoping the smile would show in my voice) and sliding it open, I heard the voice of my hell raiser. "Hiya darling, it's getting late, you should really be getting up..", I heard on the other end of the phone. I didn't want to state the obvious and say that I can hardly be on the phone to him if I'm asleep, but still only half being awake, I just yawned at him. "Emm, Dad.. I'm already up", I eventually replied. "Oh, ok - Ok, sweetheart" he said, pausing for a breath and trying to sound considerate. "We've got a busy day today, but take your time, and come down when you're ready". I knew that by default meant to hurry up and run to him like the daddy's girl he wants me to be. "Ok, ok.. I'll be about, emm, half an hour", I answered meekly. I knew that he'd be up in fifteen minutes to 'help' me get ready, no matter what I said, anyway. "Ok darling, well, I've got to go and do some business.. Love you, bye". He hung up. Probably through being too scared to not get a 'love you' back, I expect. Now, don't get me wrong. I love my father, and all of my family, no matter what's happened. I guess I'm just that kind of loyal person, even though they probably don’t deserve my loyalty. Myself and my father, up until this Spring, had a perfect father-daughter relationship. I was kind of a daddy's girl, and even if he annoyed me sometimes by being too over-protective, I couldn't help to not want to be anything different. He'd do everything for me - if I needed money, he'd give me it; If I wanted to sleep at my friends house, he'd let me; If I really needed that expensive top from a designer shop in the town, he'd buy it without even hesitating to see if it was financially viable. And when you have a father like that, you have to wonder, what can go wrong? I'd always thought that life would always be great for me, and that it would never go wrong – but I was incorrect. I turned 18 in April, and it was in early June when things gone wrong in my life. My father sat me down to talk to him at about 8PM on a Thursday night. I'd just been out with my friends, having a look around some clothes shops in the central of Los Angeles, buying a pair of jeans and some hair products with his money. That didn’t matter, though, because hearing what he wanted to talk about, or rather tell me about, left a pain inside of me that hasn't even completely worn off to this day. He wanted me to become a professional wrestler. A wrestler - I'd never had any interest in wrestling, like, at all. Granted, my father had always loved watching PCW, NTICW and vintage CSWA matches, but he'd never brought up his wanting me to attempt a career in the ‘sport’ myself. The fact he wanted me to become a professional wrestler was bad enough - what was worse, however, was the way that he put it. He put it like he was fulfilling my dream, and showing how much he loves me. Yeah right. Since then, of course, I've had a few matches in a reasonably-sized promotion called Atlantic Wrestling Club. I've been battered and bruised in the ring, I've been insulted by people who don't even know me, and I've been jumped on in the street by a fat Asian man because he "seen me on the TV". My father withdrew me from AWC eventually. We didn't talk much about why he had at the time, because really I just hoped that he'd come to his senses. In my hope I'd forgotten that he doesn't seem to have any sense, and that he'd only withdrew me because I was "above their level". I suppose he's right in that I'm above having such a ridiculously brutal and low life ahead of me, but sadly he meant that I should be wrestling somewhere better. For the first time in my life, I'd actually begun to doubt whether my father really loves me, and maybe, if I really love him. And what's worse is that, when I doubt it, I don't flinch and say "don't be stupid" anymore. I'd actually begun to mutually dislike my own family. Nevertheless, I'd sort of begun to have a more normal life again, where I could do normal things like the normal person that I still am. I'd started going clubbing and shopping for recreation again, I'd started to have time to spend with my friends, and I'd even gone on a sudden holiday back home to Portugal one weekend, and met a few lads from the past. My everyday life had started to not revolve around my father's dreams of me headlining pay-per-views across the globe again, yet I still always knew that it'd only be a matter of time until his devilish goals returned to haunt me. Last night, while I was watching the TV and having a quiet night-in on my own, was when they returned and when the wrestling become the topic of conversation once more. Date: Friday 2nd December, 2005 Location: The Silveira Family Home, Living Room B "Look, the court won't accept the case. There's nowhere near enough evidence to even consider pressing the charges you're suggesting. We'd be suggesting he tried to raise a mutiny, and unless we got very lucky, we'd look..". Law and Order: Criminal Intent. The movies on weren't very interesting, and after a lot of channel-flicking, I'd settled on NBC's 10PM offering. Of course, I was getting tired, and the annoying inspector guy's voice was only aiding me in sending myself to sleep at a time when I'd normally be going out for a night touring the many clubs of Los Angeles. I had decided to miss out on the clubs for an evening, though, and had let my friends go it alone - I wanted a quiet night in, to curl up on our second living room's sofa and regain some energy after a few good days and nights out recently. Not to mention, on top of that, my hair was beginning to look a mess, and with there being a few guys I'm looking at right now.. Well, going out when I'm beginning to look like a gorilla would essentially be suicide to any potential future relationship with any of them. I'd already watched Rat Race, but after that had finished, boredom had kicked in. Now I was tempted to rush out onto the town, and to have a good night no matter how much I looked like I needed a banana to accompany me, but I knew that I wasn’t up to it this time. Instead I calmly opened another bottle of vodka, taking a sip out of it and trying to stay awake in the process. It was at this point that I heard the door creak open behind me. I turned my head back to look at the door, vodka in hand, and I noticed my father standing in the doorway, watching the TV as if he had a genuine interest in a television series he'd probably never even watched. He soon looked at me, as I glanced at him. He smiled slightly - A warm smile, but one that I had in the past learnt he only uses when he wants something. I smiled back with a kind of ‘cute’ grin, sort of not wanting to ask why he was standing in the doorway, just incase he'd had a personality transplant and just came in to be friendly with me. He smiled a little more back, before inviting himself into the room, closing the door and making his way over to me. At this point I stopped watching him, took a quick swig of the vodka, and turned to the TV as if I had a genuine interest in Law and Order. After a couple of seconds he tapped me on the shoulder from behind the sofa, making me spill a couple of drops of vodka onto my jeans. "Hi", he said calmly, as I turned back to look at him. Strange to greet me like that, I know, but I think he just wanted to try and trigger a conversation. After I returned the greeting to him, he took a seat next to me on the white leather sofa, almost squashing my feet by sitting down without asking me to move them. He sat watching the TV for a minute or two, not saying a word. By this point I could tell he wanted to talk about something, simply because in recent months he wouldn't have entered in the first place if he didn't want something from me. I prepared to ask what he wanted, but he beat me to it by killing the quiet. "Are you really interested in this?" he said, referring to the television. At this point some person was getting arrested for doing drugs by a woman with little-to-no hair. "Emm, not really, no" I replied, smiling and taking another sip of the vodka, handing him the television remote by default. From experience, any time he asked that question, he wanted to change the channel. This time, however, he didn’t change the channel. Instead he said thanks, before turning the volume down a bit. I looked at him, and he had sort of turned his body around a little as if ready to start a conversation with me. He picked a root beer up off the floor - one I hadn't even noticed was there - and flicked it open, looking me up and down. I was still pretending to watch Law and Order, but really, I was watching him in the corner of my eye. "Are those.. The jeans you wore when you were in that six-man tag match in AWC?" he said, smiling cleverly. I feared wrestling would make an appearance in the conversation. Although I'd personally forgotten that I'd worn the jeans in the ring, he seemingly hadn't, and was probably right. The jeans were a lightish blue, with a sort of rip pattern toward the top in them, and a small turn-up at the bottom. Along with them I was wearing a white leather belt, which I didn't need but personally thought looked good with them. My upper body was covered with a tightish top - white, with "I'll B Ur Angel" across it in silver sequin lettering, shimmering in the light of the television before me. I was also wearing a pair of white K-Swiss trainers, and to complete the "Look at me, I'm a human gorilla" look, had my hair in a sixth-grade style ponytail. I still had a few blonde highlights in my hair off when it had been dyed in October, but they'd mostly faded by now, and could barely be noticed without looking for them. I looked back to my father, replying "Er.. I dunno - Are they?". "I think so", he grinned. So he'd proven he was a know-it-all with my past ring attire, but he hadn't got to the point yet. He wasn't just making random conversation about my looks. "They look nice, though..", he added. "Thanks, daddy.. You’re not looking too bad either", I replied. Of course, the glare off the top of his balding head made it hard for him to look anything but bad. Ignoring that, he was looking pretty dressed up - A smart designer shirt, in white with thin silver stripes, along with plain blue jeans and a pair of smart, casual shoes. I'm sure it wasn't for me, though - he'd come and talk to me in his boxer shorts if he had to (ok, sick thought, but he's done it before. And I won't even get onto the time the fly wa.. ugh). "Thanks sweetheart.. Now I brought the jeans up for a reason". Well, I have to admit, I'd never of guessed. "I brought them up because I know how great the time you spent wrestling was, and I looked back at some recordings of it today.. You really were a superstar out there". He smiled as if I was going to get all excited, but I was now getting the point of his presence. I feared I was about to hear something along the lines of, "you're heading back to AWC". I kept my mouth shut for now, though, although it was admittedly hard to present a fake smile to him this time. "Yeah, I guess", I replied. He was waiting for a reply, and not wanting conflict, I guess I just agreed by instinct. I'm not scared of him, but from experience it's better to just agree with him. "Well, I've got some very exciting news for you.. You see, you've had such a lot of time sitting around doing nothing, and I'll be honest with you.. Finding a top federation that'll take you is hard", he said. "I'm sorry". He didn't need to apologize, of course - I'd rather nowhere would take me. Hell, they could take a one-legged ferret over me, for all I care. I'd rather the ferret get killed jumping off a huge steel post for the amusement of a fat man who spends his life sending 'fan mail' out to mail addresses he hopes belong to his favourite high-flying idiots. "I did find a solution, though", he said. Now I was getting really concerned again – I really didn’t want to have to wrestle ever again in my life, but I knew it was coming. "You might've heard of a tournament called GTT5 - I'm sure you have". Although he smiled, I realised what he was about to say. Even though I can't tell my Kasidy Drake from my Drake Coleman, I'd known before my wrestling ‘career’ begun what the annual GTT was. The GTT was where the best, most vicious wrestlers in the world battled to become known as the greatest in the world, and I'd heard by chance that only 128 of the finest in the world were in it. I'd remembered thinking at the time, "Good job my dad's not stupid enough to think of putting me in that thing". How unbelievably ironic. "Ok.." was my only response. "It took a lot of wheeling and dealing, and a bit of luck, but I managed to somehow get them to give you a slot!". He paused, expecting me to jump off the seat and hug him, or something. "Isn't that great?". My only thought was "Oh. My. God". My father's far from an idiot, but his idiocy in saying that it's "great" was almost laughable. Funny that the only emotions I felt now were pain, sadness and anger. He put his arm around me, shaking me from left-to-right a little, hoping for a positive response. He kept his arm around me, and discreetly switched the TV off. The room suddenly got plunged into relative darkness - Quite fitting, really. "Amélia.. Are you ok?". Amélia - my birth name, and a name dropped by everyone but him in what he calls "dramatic situations". Of course, it's not that I hated the name, but people in the United States had just not been able to pronounce it since I moved here when I was 13. So I sort of changed it - unofficially, of course, but no-one calls me Amélia regularly nowadays. "Yeah, yeah, I'm fine…. That's great daddy, really", I replied. Obviously I was lying - I wasn't fine, and it definitely wasn't great. Still, I wanted him to leave the room, but he had more to come. "Ok, good, I knew you'd be happy..", he said without the slightest hint of sarcasm. "The competition starts early next week, and you're facing a wrestler called Jason Warr. You can do it, sweetheart.. I've even bought us a wrestling ring so we can practice more at home". I didn’t know what to say to him, so I just decided to say quiet, downing the rest of the bottle of the vodka down my gullet quickly. Thankfully, at this point, my father was preparing to leave the room. He flicked the TV back on, lighting up the room – My face had turned to one of sorrow, but he ignored it. "Love you", he said, in his classic manner. I replied quietly with the same statement, and he left the room slowly. I looked back - He was actually smiling a little. I just couldn't understand the man who was once my guardian angel smiling over this, and that scared me. Back to Saturday 3rd December 2005, in Amy's bedroom I placed the mobile back on the desk, and lifted my hands to my face, rubbing the tiredness out of my eyes. I sighed, wanting to just go back to bed and forget I'd be spending today watching my father setting up his newly-delivered wrestling ring. No doubt my sister Catarina would be there to watch too, smiling in the corner, watching me suffer hell whilst she shook off her hangover from getting completely bladdered (and laid by some acne-covered lad, I bet) the night before. Of course, Catarina's age advantage over me had always meant she'd got away from being daddy's girl, whereas I had always been his little one. He'd always took more interest in me, though - Whether Catarina disliked that, I don't know, mainly because we'd never got along like sisters should. I can't really figure out why, but whereas she had always been a man-eater, I'd grow up to not want to get grouped with the dogs and the sluts. That’d been our premiere different through live. She'd always been more popular, too, which annoyed me. I'd never been unpopular, though, so it wasn't too much of a problem. Of course, she envied me in a way - whilst in AWC loads of boys had wanted to have me as their "chick" badly. That'd be the ideal situation for her – Plenty of people look at her anyway, because she goes out clubbing with practically no clothes on, but she'd love to have absolutely everyone stop and stare when she walks into a room. For me, it hadn't been ideal at all - I want people to like me for who I am, and not because they’ve seen me on the television a few times. Although I would never want to lead a life like hers, I’d take her place in the family any day. I looked in the mirror, noticing that my hair needs cutting badly. Brushing it back with my hands, I began rummaging through the dressing table drawers, taking out a few bottles and sprays for my use whilst getting ready for the day. Placing the various items on the desk, I made my way across to the wardrobe, opening it up and flicking through my many dresses, pair of jeans, tops and other items of clothing. I took out a pair of old light jeans and a plain black top, along with a few other odds and ends and a pair of aged trainers which had barely fit me for months - After all, why waste my new and expensive clothes on my father's ideas. I laid the items out on my bed, brushing down my nightie again, trying to at least cover myself. It hadn't helped that an already short night-piece had been shrunk by my mother when she decided to wash it with a load of my sister's junk. My sister found it funny, so I stole one of her nighties, until she found out and practically ripped it off me when I was leaving the bathroom. Looking down at the clothes lying on the bed made me feel weak. The state of the clothes really summed up my life - Plain, worn and tatty. I sat back down on the bed, leaning back on its backboard. I looked up at the ceiling, wanting to cry on the inside, wishing I could just crawl back under the light pink bedsheets and stay there until GTT5 was over. I just wanted my old life back, but I couldn't tell my father that - No matter how much I was beginning to dislike him and his ideas for my future, I still owed him a lot. And if I had to suffer this to pay him back, I'd suffer, no matter what the consequences are to me physically and mentally. Maybe he'd eventually become himself once again – This was hardly the Hugo Silveira that I’d known throughout my childhood. On Tuesday I'd be facing off against Jason Warr of Headstrong Wrestling. I'd never heard of him, and hopefully no-one would care about our match, so that I could lose gracefully; I was far from ready, despite expecting an unavoidable return to wrestling ever since I left AWC. GTT5 was going to be more than a return to wrestling, though - It was going to be a return to my hell.
