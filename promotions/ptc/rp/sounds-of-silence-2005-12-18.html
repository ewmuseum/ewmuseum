Date: 2005-12-18 The sound of spraying gravel snapped him from his reverie, jerking him from the bleak beyond. Numbly terror seized anew, initially stayed by doubt wrought of refusal to comprehend, like he’d awoken in a coffin as the first shovel-ful of dirt descended. His peripheral vision caught the retreating taillights, dying the fading tunnel red, an undelivered warning come late to mock in disaster’s wake. Vanity made him gasp for breath, but only heat filled his lungs. The wind picked up, taunting him with whistled freedom, rustling him as easily as the leaves autumn had yet to cast down. Cold crept in, first infiltrating fingers and toes, then spreading, turning passionate struggles lethargic. The thunderous pulsing in his temples began to diminish as the imprisoned blood coagulated. Thoughts frenzied with panic succumbed to the dull hum of indistinct static. He hadn’t expected his life to flash before his eyes, but on a distant level he couldn’t help but feel cheated by only overwhelming darkness. His legs kicked desperately a final time, seeking a purchase no fortune was vast enough to buy, then the night closed in around him. As the crickets chirped their inadvertent eulogy, The Deville swayed lifeless, hanged. ~><>…<><~ Breakfast was always a noble affair in the Klassen household, but on the Sabbath the spread would make the most pompous king gawk. Even having sixteen mouths to feed couldn’t account for the never-ending parade that graced the table on Sunday morning, their first exaggerated homage on this day of celebration and rest. Little discussion permeated the gluttony, with everyone too busy gorging to distract themselves with discourse. Dawn was just breaking, but already they’d been seated for half an hour, and the preparation had begun long before. Some of the men had polished off their fourth serving, and were leaning back in their chairs contentedly, surveying the rest of the family through happy, half-lidded eyes. The stocky man at the head of the table was among the satisfied, but his smug smile flipped when he noticed one of the plates untouched. He picked up his fork and tapped the air with it, pointing at the culprit refusing to indulge. "Something amiss with your bacon, Abraham?" Everyone stopped eating and followed his gaze, the men curiously, the women disapproving. The blonde youth shrugged uncomfortably and shook his head. "No, Hezekiah," he began sheepishly, then cleared his throat. "Just don’t have much of an appetite this morning." Hezekiah harrumphed, scowling. "How are we supposed to saddle you with a better half if you’re skinny as a rail? No woman wants a husband smaller than she is." It was a light comment, meant to make Abraham smile. He did, politely, then looked down at his plate and scratched at his beard. He wasn’t slight by any stretch of the imagination, with shoulders almost as broad as the other men at the table, but he was far less stocky, lithe like a bodybuilder. "Leave the boy be," the lady to Hezekiah’s right cut in sharply, poking him in the gut. "We don’t want a husband so wide we can’t hug him, either." The women chuckled as the men bristled indignantly. Hezekiah opened his mouth to retort, but Abraham spoke before it could escalate. "May I be excused?" Once again everyone eyed him dubiously. Hezekiah set his fork down with a sigh, then glowered. Abraham refused to look at him; when Hezekiah grimaced, he reminded Abraham of a bulldog, all frowning brow and sagging jowls. "You’re not thinking of trying to skip out on church again, are you?" Everyone looked scandalized at the very thought, but not from shock. Abraham had tried to skip out the week before. He returned a weak smile. "Of course not. I’m . . . not feeling well." Trying his best to look sickly, he added, "I just need some fresh air." Hezekiah grunted, but waved his dismissal. The rest watched quietly as Abraham rose, offered a muttered thanks, and fled the dining room. The silence stretched for a long, uncomfortable moment. "There’s something wrong with that boy," Hezekiah grunted at last, frowning. "Refusing breakfast. Trying to avoid church! I’ve never seen the like." "You’re too hard on him, father," piped in a young lady at the far end of the table. She immediately flushed, abashed over speaking to her father so, but forced herself to hold her ground. "It can’t be easy adjusting to our ways." "Mind your tongue, Mary," the first woman offered wearily, just as Hezekiah erupted with an annoyed "Adjusting! Bah!" ~><>…<><~ As always, John had found church an uplifting and rejuvenating break from the normal toils of life. The fall harvest had just passed, so their workload around the farm had been considerably lessened, but every day was still a full one, and every night’s rest most welcome. The sun had only set a few hours before, but he imagined he was the last one awake. He usually was, especially on Sundays, when the revelry left him excited rather than tired. Laying in his bed with his hands clasped behind his head, he let his mind wander, trying to find sleep that wouldn’t come. He wished Abraham would acclimate himself better. He and father had another falling out just before confession, after Abraham insisted he had nothing to repent for. John couldn’t fathom the thought. He had been told that not everyone lived as he and his family, but surely all people knew they sinned and the joy of being cleansed. Not for the first time, he wondered how he could help Abraham. He’d barely been with them a month, which wasn’t a long time, but he hadn’t made any progress. John wasn’t sure he was even trying. He worked hard, and he was polite, but he had no understanding of the important things. In the first week he forgave the trespasses, and he did still, but after they’d had him baptized he’d been certain the man would come around. A whispered greeting sliced through his thoughts, making him jump with a cry. "Shhhhhhhhhhhhutup!" came the hiss, then a hand clamped over his mouth. John peered up through wide eyes into the face of Abraham, leaning over him in the darkness. Unable to help but wonder how Abraham had snuck in under his notice, John tried to ask, but the words came out muffled. Angrily hushing him again, Abraham slowly withdrew his hand. "You scared me, Abraham!" John exclaimed, barely whispering. "I feared you were a burglar. I thought everyone was asleep." "They are, but they won’t remain so if you don’t quiet down. Christ, you’d think you’d never been covert before." John’s eyebrows rose in shock. "Don’t blaspheme, Abraham." Abraham sighed. "I’m sorry, John. You know I don’t mean it. But Je . . . eez . . . summon a bit of tact, will you? You’ll wake the whole house." "Well," John began, sitting up, "maybe we should. Is something wrong? Why aren’t you asleep?" "I can’t sleep." John nodded his sympathetic understanding. "Too excited from worship? Me too." Abraham stopped his eyes from rolling. "Yeah. Something like that." Before John could narrow his eyes at the sarcasm, he went on. "Let’s sneak out." John started, appalled. "What?" Abraham grimaced, sharply motioning for quiet again. "Sorry," John whispered, "but what do you mean? Why would we sneak out?" Abraham stared at him for a long moment. "Don’t you ever get bored of this? It’s so . . . tedious." "No." "Well I do. I am. Let’s do something." John was shaking his head. "Abraham, it must be close to ten o’clock. What could we possibly do at this hour?" Abraham grinned. "Let’s go drinking." "Drinking! I don’t know . . . Father might not notice if we take some of his wine, but—" It was Abraham’s turn to shake his head. "Forget that. Let’s go to a bar. I haven’t been to a bar since . . . well, I don’t know how long it’s been." John folded his arms. "I don’t think so. I don’t like the idea at all. It’ll take us an hour to walk to town, and Hezekiah certainly wouldn’t approve. Not that I approve either, mind you." "Why does he have to know? We’re grown men, John. Besides, we’ll be drinking in celebration of the Lord. How can you argue with that?" Abraham grinned, giving a nod as if it was settled. "It would be a sin not to go." Narrowing his eyes, John clucked his tongue. "I don’t think so, Abraham. Go back to bed; we’ll set up a get-together for next Saturday." Abraham heaved a sigh. "I’m going one way or another, John." His tone took on a regretful cast. "And you can’t let me go alone. What if something happened to me? You’ve heard how bars are." John glared. Abraham grinned. "It’ll be fun." Grudgingly, John went along with him, lamenting the whole walk to town about how he couldn’t believe he was, and there was going to be trouble, how he could sense it. Abraham brushed off his premonitions, insisting it would be a nice break, but John didn’t lighten up until they’d been seated inside for half an hour, a collection of empty mugs sharing the table with their elbows. John had asked where Abraham had gotten the money, but Abraham brushed it aside and changed the subject, not wanting to get into the fineries of borrowing. All that mattered was that he was paying, so John needed to loosen up and have a good time. The bar was relatively small, barely seating thirty people in a variety of booths and stand-alone tables. Half-full, most of the patrons were watching the many mounted televisions, which were blasting some wrestling program at full volume. Abraham was ignoring the screens, but John’s gaze kept flickering to them; having never owned a television, and only seen one twice in his life, he was fairly awed. "You’ve seriously never been to a bar before?" Abraham asked, quirking an eyebrow. John laughed. "No sir. We usually just drink in the barn. It’s a hoot, but things tend to get out of hand, so we don’t do it often. Better fighting than this," he smirked, indicating the screen above them. Abraham nodded, a grin stealing onto his face. "Kick a lot of ass, do you?" "Well . . ." John shrugged uncomfortably. "I give as good as I take. I’ve never been much for violence." He took another swig of his beer, smacked his lips appreciatively, and returned to the wrestling on the screen, now lost in an advertisement about some tournament. "How about you?" "Nah," Abraham answered off-handedly, "I don’t think so. I don’t remember much of anything, as you know, but . . ." Trailing off, Abraham licked his lips. "What?" John had gone deathly pale, slowly lowering his head from the screen to regard Abraham as if seeing him for the first time, his jaw slack. He blinked, trying to contain his bulging eyes. Abraham looked around nervously, then barked a laugh. "What, damn you? You look like you saw a gho—" Abraham cut off abruptly, doing a double-take of the room. The sole sound was that emanating from the television, and John wasn’t the only one staring at him bug-eyed. The whole bar had their attention turned to him, exclamations frozen on open lips. Then they began to point, rising in awe as recognition dawned.
