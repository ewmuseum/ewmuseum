I must escape!” As the dingy bar’s patrons looked on with apathy, the mysterious musician threw a smoke bomb onto the floor, snatched his accordion, and zipped out the door. Joe waved away the clinging smoke, trying to keep it away from his steak dinner with little success. He took a tentative bite. “Bleah!” Spitting the slice of steak back onto the plate, Joe discovered that the oily smoke did not enhance the flavor in the least. Snorting, Joe remarked, “At least I won’t choke on it.” Paying for his Coors and half eaten dinner, Joe Black left the bar, stepping out into the urban evening. He’d had to do quite a bit of walking to locate the dingy little tavern on the corner, and at the present moment, he couldn’t precisely recall where his hotel was. But that wasn’t such a big deal really. There wasn’t anything of interest waiting for him at the hotel room, with the possible exception of his bed, and he wasn’t feeling tired just yet. In his younger days, touring the south and southwest, he’d been an expert at picking up a piece of ass wherever he lingered. But as time progressed, most particularly over the past few years, his technique had to alter significantly. He could only get the attractive ones after they’d been rejected by their first choice. And eventually even the rejected wouldn’t give him the time of day. The situation continued to grow worse as he could only get a piece from the fat or the ugly. In his last desperate attempt at enjoyable coupling he’d actually asked the woman to wear the paper bag. He’d gotten slapped for that. Since then he hadn’t even tried. This left few options. “Hey baby, you want a date?” Joe turned to look, but the voice wasn’t addressing him. It was a whore inquiring with a passing car. For a moment, he strongly considered it. The deciding factor wasn’t the dignity issue of having to pay for sex. It was the fact that he didn’t have a car up here. Whores don’t like to walk in those cheap shoes. In fact the only whore that would accompany him on the long trek to the hotel would slap cuffs on him and whisper in her most unsexy voice, “I’m placing you under arrest for importuning.” He wasn’t even feeling all that horny, just lonely. A head to nod at him, pretending to listen while he told stories would be nice. Just someone to pay attention for a little while. That’s what he needed. The lonely walk back to the hotel didn’t last as long as Joe would’ve liked. Sure, his knees started to hurt again, but the pain provided something else to think about. And the act of traveling, the idea of moving from one place to another, was a lot better than arriving at the destination. If Joe had gotten lost, it might’ve led to some kind of adventure to take his mind off these miserable feelings. But no such luck. On the hotel bed, Joe rolled over, picking up his cell phone and dialing 411. He asked for a name, provided a state and a city. The city was a guess. After a few tries, the sympathetic operator came through with a number. “Hello, Wakeman residence.” “Delores?” “Yes? Who is this?” “This is Joe.” There was a pause. “Joe who?” she asked. But Joe knew that she knew who it was. But what could he do? “Joe Black.” “Oh.” “I’ve just been thinking of you a lot lately, so I thought I’d give you a call.” He could hear her turn off the television. Maybe the sound of her sitting down? “I see.” “So how’ve you been, Delores?” “Well, I’ve just retired. I’m considering going to school. How long has it been, Joe?” Joe’s brain sought to find the answer. 1983? 1984? “At least twenty years, heh. Isn’t that something.” “Yeah. That’s something. I don’t hear from you for … twenty years. Hard to believe I’d ever say that. That it’s been twenty years since anything happened. Why are you calling me?” Joe rolled over on the bed to look up at the hotel room ceiling, “Well, like I said, I’ve been thinking about you lately.” “I see. Remembering old times?” Joe smiled, “Yeah, a little bit. To be candid, feeling a bit lonely.” “Hmm. Did you know I had a son by you, Joe?” Sound faded away. In another part of the world, Joe could imagine that someone was speaking to him on his cell phone in a hotel room. He couldn’t hear her. The ceiling of his hotel room suddenly was key to the meaning of everything. In the pattern on the stucco, he could see a life that he was responsible for, another life, living for twenty years without him. Spelled out in very clear letters to his eye in the pattern, Joe could read, “I am not only not a good man, I am among the worst of bad men. I deserve to die.” (notes: the usual content nonsense. typing it over and over again is getting old. I don't think I abused anyone's creative property except that of the handler of George English.)
