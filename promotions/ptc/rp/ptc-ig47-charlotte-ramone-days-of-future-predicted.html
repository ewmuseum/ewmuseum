[IG47] - Charlotte Ramone: "Days of Future, Predicted."

20 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
June 17, 2012 There are no hover cars in the future. No One World Government, no apocalyptic World War III, no Rapture, and no universal health care. I'm sorry to say we're not even less addicted to crude oil. The future is the sum of all our regrets, it's the altar of all our sins, and it's the one thing I've always looked forward to but now the last place I want to be (and not just because the "no hover cars" thing is kind of a bummer). It's also fucking hot, but I think that's more due to the whole "South Florida in mid-June" thing than global warming or whatev. I'm writing this sitting on a bus at seven in the goddamn morning; if I had my druthers, I'd be as far away as I could get - maybe the Australian outback would be far enough away. But a promise is a promise, and I don't think I am callous (or callow) enough to break my word. "Dear Madison Sinclair or Smitty T. Duluth or whoever's in charge in Sin City whose name isn't Alexandra Pierce..." Yeah, that's where we begin another episode of the As-Yet-Untitled Charlotte Ramone Reality Show Project. Our Heroine (And Our Heroin) was perched on the edge of a black leather office chair, head bowed over a pad of lined white paper (college-ruled, because there's not really any other kind). The Fantastic Plastic face-shield laid on the desk beside her within easy reach, but the ill-lit room (just an orange glow from the foyer beyond) and the haphazard fall of pink hair around her head kept the camera from picking up anything but the tip of her tongue, snuck just past her teeth in concentration. The scene would be kind of boring, were it not for the magic of post-production adding the run-ragged voice of the new Infinite Gauntlet holder to the scene. (You can ask how this damaged the whole "reality show" concept later.) Charlie sounded kind of sad - shall we find out why? "It's with loads of regret (and Edie's say-so) that I hereby tender my resignation to SCCW, effective immediately. In Memphis, when Aimz dropped Kris on her head and Desade..." She trailed off as, onscreen, she bowed her head, scratching at the back of her neck with Martian green fingernails. "After Memphis, it's clear there are things more important than the wars we put on in pro-wrestling. People who make us what we are. Without Krissy, there's no Charlie. While I appresh all the opportunities I've gotten here, there's no way I'll risk her again. If this means Pierce and Campbell win, then I'm sorry but they were always gonna. I'm not Jon Rhine or Lance Marshall, and I can't keep doing this, not just for the sake of my own ego." Ramone set the pencil down, scooping a hand beneath the protective facemask she wore to the ring. Her hand lifts, fingers through the eyes, forcing it to look over her shoulder. "Hey, look, it's a puppet show." It wasn't Krissy Jamison's cheerful voice that came down the hall, but the poisonous sweetness of Sabrina Yates. "Does it say, 'Oh, by the by, I'm a lesbian' a lot?" Charlie dropped her arm, running her free hand through her bubblegum pink locks. "Bree?" There was probably squinting. "Bree, it's one-thirty in the damn morning. How did you even get in?" Sabrina lingered in the doorway, her smirk mostly hidden in the shadows. "Your sister let me in. I came by to see how she's doing." "Oh." The twist to Charlotte's lips spoke a thousand "damn you"'s about Edie Raymond-Ellis at once. "She's sleeping upstairs...doc says she needs her rest." The ultra-fashionable Sabrina stepped into the den (or maybe study), mahogany hair bound behind her in a loose knot that looked more casual than it probably was. "It's fine, I'll give her a look-in. I'm sorry I couldn't have been there for the party. Sounds like a hell of a shindig." "This is the lecture part? Because I've heard just about enough about it already." Charlotte Ramone and Sabrina Yates had virtually nothing in common; they didn't like the same music, they didn't eat at the same restaurants, and they didn't share the same sense of fashion. (That would have required Charlie to not think she was still twelve, gauging from the oversized Yosemite Sam t-shirt and Daisy Dukes.) But the two women shared a deep affection for Krissy Jamison - that's why they tolerated each other, and why they argued. "What the hell are you thinking, Charlotte?" Bree asked. "These people are lunatics." "That would be a 'yes' on the whole 'lecture thing." Charlie tossed the Fantastic Plastic onto the table. "I know what these people are, Sabrina." "Really? Because it sure doesn't seem like it. You don't antagonize people like that Pierce lady. Do you know how seriously Kristina would have been hurt if she'd followed through with that threat?" Charlie's hands came up into the shot. "Save it, Sabrina. I don't need you to rehash it, because I dream about it every night." "So what now? You and your psycho partner start some kind of arms race? Because you'll lose, and while I don't care if you can breathe in four years, let alone walk, I'm pretty sure Kristina would be devastated." "You know..." Charlie sighed - irritation and frustration seemingly welling up in an instant. "What the fuck is your problem? You've been around all the time since you popped back into her life a few weeks back, and all I ever hear from you is, 'Blahblahblah, Charlie fucking sucks at life.' It's like...that ship sailed four years ago, Sabrina. Now it's in my waters, and I ain't fixing to let it go." "You think you're riding so goddamn high, Charlotte. Edie bullshitted your boss into funding this bogus reality show thing, you're the hottest up-and-coming thing in pro-wrestling - even got one of those titles to show for it." Charlie tipped her head up to the brunette - even though the camera doesn't give the viewer a look at her face, the Pixie might as well be wearing a neon sign around her neck that informed any curious onlookers, "She's smirking quite a lot right now." "I do have a snazzy belt right now, yeah. It'd be like being the best model around...you know, without having to give all the blowjobs first." Sabrina's nostrils flared. "You can sit there and be smug all you want, Charlie." Sabrina rarely shortened anyone's name. "But don't just blame those two redheads for what happened to Kristina, because you put her there. And you'll keep putting her there every time you stick your nose in Alexandra Pierce's business." "Which is why I'm not going to do that. This is my resignation." The paper wrinkled as Charlie seized it in her hand. "I'm quitting wrestling." "No, you're not." Bree folded her arms, refusing the paper. "You wrote a letter, Charlotte! Your letter needs to be something more like, 'Dear Ms. Pierce: Please stop hitting my gal pal. Hugs and kisses, Chuck.'" Ramone leaned back in her snazzerific office chair, strapping on the facemask so her sister didn't kill her for not "staying true to the brand." "Yeah, get your jokes out of your system, Bree. I'm a quitter or whatever. I don't care, though. If you don't have anything constructive to offer, then shut the fuck up and go see Kris, OK?" Bree shook her head; a lock of deep brown hair sneaks free of her braid to fall in her gaze. "You want something constructive?" "Dying to hear it." "Me too." The third voice is the hallway is quieter than the brash Pixie and the measured former model. It was one of those shots that producers would kill for, though it was almost certainly not staged. Krissy Jamison's bedhead had flattened most of her honey-blond hair against her head, and she rubbed at her eyes. Her Maleficent nightshirt was more than a little oversized, but it wasn't clear what she was wearing underneath. "Kristina!" Bree said excitedly. "You're up." "Yeah, I heard all this yelling coming from somewhere nearby and I couldn't sleep through it. Either of you know where that might have come from?" "Were we loud, babe?" Charlie asked. "I'm sorry. We were just...talking." "Really? Because from what it sounded like upstairs, it was darn near yelling. So clue me in - were you talking about me?" "No," Bree said. "Yeah." Charlie's answer came at the same time. "So what was it about?" Krissy crept into the room, coming to stand between the two taller girls. "I mean, other than both about and not about me." "We were talking about..." Bree glanced over her shoulder for support from the pink-haired dynamo, but nothing was forthcoming. "We were talking about the future, Kristina." "The...future." Krissy Jamison was the very model of a modern major general...of doubt. Why Charlotte Ramone bought into the story is a question she'd probably be asking herself for quite some time. "You know, like, have you ever wondered what it'd be like in four, five years?" Krissy tugged at the hem of her nightshirt, wrapping the white cloth around her hands as she considered. "I guess. I mean...you're lucky if I think far enough in advance to know what I want for dinner tomorrow!" Her smile quirked - it was the kind of expression that'd melt Charlotte's heart. "Which is 'tacos,' by the way." "Done," Ramone said, going to her...we're gonna go with "friend". An arm snaked around Krissy's shoulders; the two women were forehead to forehead. This was, of course, not at all because Sabrina Yates was standing in the room. The mahogany-headed model nodded shortly. "I know this great place downtown - real, authentic stuff. The owner owes me a favor; I'll get us in tomorrow night?" Wide green eyes flashed under the Fantastic Plastic. "The three of us, I mean," Yates continued innocently. "Sure," Krissy offered. "If my Charlie is invited and this isn't one of your games." "Of course she is, silly." Sabrina Yates was one of those girls who never understood the meaning of "pouring it on too thick." "Any friend of Kristina's is a friend of mine." Krissy pulled Charlie's t-shirt out of her pants, glancing over her shoulder to her ex. "I'm sure you can wake up Edie and she'll show you out, right?" "I..." Bree turned away slowly, her chiseled façade in danger of crumbling. "Yeah." "Sweet," the blonde said, turning away. The microphones attached to the Sony 1080i High-Definition Digital Camcorder...OF DOOM~! (Charlie won the tilde and the exclamation point from Chris Storm too) barely caught the softest of sighs from Sabrina's lips. The report of Sabrina Yates' heels against the hardwood floors in Casa Ramone diminished down the hallway. Charlie's hands went to Krissy's hips, pulling the (Nickname Being Sought - Please Contact STD Entertainment!) closer. "That was kind of mean, you know," Charlie whispered. "Maybe I'm secretly evil." Krissy's cornflower blues locked onto the Fantastic Plastic. "Maybe that's why I love you." "You're going to want to shut off the camcorder now, Charlotte." "Why?" Charlotte's voice was barely a whisper. "Because I don't think the next hour or so will have any useable footage." "Oh." A long pause. "Oh!" The camera was turned off in short order. I'm the only one who gets off at that bus stop that Sunday morning in June. The wrought-iron gate stands closed in front of me, imposing brackish-gray. I creep close; I know she'll be here (she'd asked me to come, after all). As I approach, the gates swing inward with the ghastly growling sound of metal on metal. I slip through, feeling like nothing more than a character in a bad horror movie - the type who goes into the haunted house against their better judgment. "Hello?" I call into the cloudless afternoon. "I know you're here, so you can come out - slowly. I'm armed." (I'm not - guns have always given me a serious case of the Wiggins.) A brunette steps around the trunk of a tree, smoking a thin cigarette. "I honestly, honestly was beginning to think that you weren't going to show," she says in that flat, matter-of-fact tone that always made me feel like this was Opposite Day in school. "It's...been a long time." "Not long enough." My response is shorter than it probably should be. Sharper. Something about the other woman's appearance makes it so. "I can't believe you still dress like that." She gestures towards the light tan slacks and white, short-sleeved button-up blouse. "I thought it sent the particular message I needed to," she says, grinding the nub of the smoke under the toe of what can only be called "sensible" shoes. "To the rest of the world, or to me?" "Can't it do both? You know they still play the old show?" She smirks; the expression looks...strange on her face. Like a child's Halloween mask of what amusement is supposed to look like. "Come on, we're running late and you have to see everyone. And don't forget to smile - she loved that smile, you know." I try to dredge up the smile from somewhere; I try to be this person again. (I'm pretty sure I just managed to grimace, though.) I look over my shoulder; sure enough, the gates are closing behind me, the etching on the archway, long since pockmarked with rust, underscoring the message all too well. "Abandon Hope, All Ye Who Enter Here," it says. (Yeah, that's on the back of the archway. When Hector's grandfather opened the place, it was to provide a quiet place for his friends to have good food and some laughs. Or at least that's what he told his wife. Mr. Garza told his friends that he just wanted a place to watch lucha libre in peace - away from cries of "it's fake!" and "you're watching a soap opera, you know that?" Years later, Garza's Place was much fancier (Hector razed the old building when he inherited it from his father, Juan Carlos), but at its core, the it was still a place for good food and some laughs (it's just that those laughs were more titters from the chic restaurant goers, and that food was gourmet). They still watched the lucha libre on the weekends; it was just in the bar, however. The three women were gathered in a booth off to the side, nestled out of the way so the evening dinner crowd wouldn't bother them. The booths at Garza's were the only things Hector had saved - old, scarred wood with decades of hidden messages scratched into the surface. The camcorder (of DOOM) sat on a highchair at the head of the booth. Sabrina Yates sat on one side of the table, her posture perfect, ankles crossed, hands folded. Her signature mahogany hair was clipped behind her. In contrast, Charlotte Ramone sprawled on the other side of the table, right arm up over the back of the booth, while Krissy Jamison nearly laid in the booth against Ramone. "OK," Charlie began. The smile on Edie Raymond-Ellis' face was not a kind one, but it ws tinged with a good deal of sadness. My hand dropped to the gravestone itself, and I nodded as my fingers traced the words I'd read too many times. "Revenge," I said - whispered, really. "Charlotte Ann Raymond - Devoted Wife, Daughter, and Mother. Blazer of New Trails," the tombstone's epitaph read. "She Kicked Everyone's Ass But Death's."
