[IG70]Carved in Ice

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Troy doesn’t pace. Well, not normally. So that’s how I know this is harder on him than he’s willing to admit. I have thrown him into the very chaos I wanted to protect him from by concealing my return to wrestling, my entry in GTT7, and re-entry into a world of violence and clashing personalities and flashing lights and media attention that he has no experience with and no desire for. He didn’t sign on for this and he shouldn’t have to deal with it. When he met me two years ago, I was just a seamstress, working on costumes for a burlesque theater. No, that is not a contradiction in terms. He was building a stage extension and putting up lights. I made him laugh when I told him I was part of the support staff and indicated it with a big beaded push up bra. He didn’t know a thing about pro wrestling. Never heard of it, never followed it. Tempest? Never heard of her. He was perfect. I’ve wondered since returning if that choice, that thought made me a bad person. Now, watching him pace and worry, and worse yet pretend that he isn’t worried, that he still trusts me completely to know what I’m doing, it’s even harder to tell myself that I’m not. “It always looks worse than it is,” Tempest said, sounding stuffy and congested, her face and most notably her nose puffy and swollen. Around her eyes, circles of darkness were beginning to blossom like purple flowers. Small smudges of blood dotted her face here and there and were wiped away gently by an attendant trainer whose gloved hands worked carefully with gauze and spray bottle. “You make it look easy to reset that,” he said, standing back slightly and taking in the sight of her face as if measuring one more time. “But then I guess you’ve done it a few times.” Tempest picked up a small mirror from near a pile of bloody gauze next to her on the training table and looked at herself, blinking a few times and poking delicately at a few points high upon the bridge of her nose. “Six,” she said. “Six times. Well, nine if you want to count the little ones where it didn’t bleed. I’ve gotten to know my own nose pretty well.” She regarded the face in the mirror critically, assessing the damage and whether she had really fixed it entirely. It wouldn’t do to be off a millimeter. Besides the angry wound and it remnants, her makeup was a little caked but holding, as always. One uses just enough to be seen by the cameras and always the waterproof/sweatproof variety. It’s so hard to be pretty when fighting for your life. Her cobalt blue eyes gleamed forth, only slightly dimmed by the loss to Desade. Her lips, now void of gloss thanks to wiping off all the blood that had come from her broken nose seemed piqued, wanting color or at least a good drink. And all of this was framed by her jet black hair, splashed with hot pink, and matted back with sweat. Post match was always a mess. She wanted a shower, but knew it would have to be a careful one. The nose needed ice and lots of it to keep from giving her the raccoon look for too long. Ice appeared, or rather a soft chemical ice pack wrapped neatly in a thin sheath, in the hands of the trainer and he said, “Ok, there ya go. Get started on that for twenty minutes or so and let’s see if we can get the swelling under control. You feel ok otherwise?” “Oh yeah. Nothing else rattling around in my head any more than usual,” Tempest answered with a wink. The trainer chuckled and turned to leave, stopping to clap Troy Rivers, who jumped, on the shoulder. “Oh yeah, she’ll be fine.” He said and gave Tempest a thumbs-up before disappearing out the door with a quick click of his Nextel. “Storm 1 is code green.” Troy looked at the man, vaguely mystified, and then to Tempest, whose face was partially hidden behind the icepack. As the door closed behind him he ran his hand through his dark brown hair, pushing back those stray locks that never quite made it to the ponytail and took a few tentative steps toward the woman so familiar and yet now seemingly new to him. “Rachel?” The icepack slipped, lowered by fingers still taped here and there with hot pink athletic tape, and stained with her own blood. Tempest peered over the top at Troy, keeping the pack situated on her nose. “Yes?” she said, her voice a little muffled, but still clear. “You ok?” Troy let out a single humorless chuckle and shook his head with a rueful look. “ Am I ok? You’re the one wrenching your own nose back into place and bleeding all over the place.” He took a step closer, still seemingly unsure what was appropriate in this circumstance, but placed his hands on her shoulders, rubbing them gently. “And you’re the one who got beaten up and thrown around and stomped on. That’s um… tough to watch, you know.” Tempest lifted the icepack long enough to scowl playfully at her boyfriend and say, “Hey, just because I lost this one doesn’t mean I’m gonna stay down. I gave as good as I got. Did you see that plancha? Did you see that Rottweiler? No one’s going to forget that anytime soon, either Desade or anyone who saw it. I’ll be fine.” She replaced the pack with a little huff and Troy looked at her blinking for a moment before he spoke. “I… don’t know how to be proud of you for that,” he said quietly. Their eyes met over the icepack, the gulf of understanding between them extending in the silence for what seemed like a glacial age. And as slowly as that movement, words seemed to find their way again from Troy. “It’s not that I don’t want to be, or don’t care, or anything like that. I just really don’t know how. What you’re doing here is so alien to the Rachel I know, or thought I knew,” he said, peering down at her curiously. Tempest’s eyes misted slightly, but she took a deep breath, as if staving off tears. “I’m still the Rachel you know. I’m just this too. And I’m sorry I didn’t tell you. I thought I was helping, but… it was stupid. I thought I would just try it out again and see if I still had it, then I’d stop and you’d never know. It wouldn’t matter.” “But you just said…” Troy’s eyes narrowed, and the wheels were clearly turning quickly in his mind before he closed his eyes and lowered his head. “You’re not going to stop are you?” he asked quietly, stepping back. “Do you want me to?” Tempest asked, her eyes piercingly bright over the top of the icepack. “No… don’t put this on me,” He said, looking up abruptly and jamming his hands into the pockets of his jeans. “I never presumed to order you around before and I’m not going to now. I’m just struggling to understand why. Why after the last couple years of working and living together and having fun and... for chrissakes Rache, you helped build a community center for non-violent conflict resolution with me.” His hands were back out of his pockets and he was using them to articulate. Somewhere in Troy Rivers bloodline was a dose of Italian that had bestowed this habit if not the overall temperament. “You love yoga and lattes and you brush that old dog, Trouble for hours patiently. And now I watch you whip that redhead across a ring and try to take her head off with that flying… what did you call it?” “Well, I call it a Tempest-rana,” she said proudly, clearly smiling as her cheeks pushed up slightly against the icepack. “The rest of the world calls it twisting reverse hurricanrana, but that’s boring.” Troy looked at her sternly and said, “Whatever it was it was sick and no, I don’t mean that as a compliment.” Tempest looked taken aback, blinking rapidly and looking away. Troy resumed his pacing, but looked back at her and stopped. His shoulder slumped slightly and he said, “I’m sorry. Look, I’m not trying to insult your work out there. I’m sure it’s tough… well, obviously it’s tough. God I’m awful at this.” He laughed a single dry chuckle, but turned back toward Tempest, his expression more abashed than amused. “I’m just trying to figure out what went wrong.” Tempest stared at him across the room and the icepack slid further and she pulled it from her face and shook her head, saying, “Nothing went wrong. Troy, you didn’t do anything wrong. This is just something I have to do. Tempest is just as much who I am as Rachel, as all of me that you know.” Troy braved a step closer and said, “But that’s just it, the Rachel I know is compassionate, and high energy sure, but not violent. I mean, your dad that you love and miss so much, he was a doctor, right?” The words seemed to stun Tempest and she raised the icepack to her face again as if to hide behind it. When her voice finally came forth it was quiet and muffled as she said, “Yes. It was my mother who was the fighter. She fought in the pits of Hong Kong’s underworld. It wasn’t pretty or glamorous, or even safe. And she didn’t really have to once she met dad. But… I don’t think she knew how to stop.” Tempest lowered the pack slightly to look at Troy who had come closer as she talked. “She died there.” He leaned over and put his arms around her shoulders as he said, “Sound familiar? Look doesn’t that scare you?” She appeared to consider this for a moment before looking up at him and answering, “I think it would if only I could remember her. I fought in those pits too. But this is so much better. It’s safer and cleaner and you think they took care of me like this back then? No way…” Troy broke away from her, shaking his head. “Will, you stop and listen to me?!” Tempest snapped as she hopped off the table and strode in his direction. “Stop acting like this is somehow a reflection on you because it isn’t. You didn’t fail me. I wasn’t unhappy. It’s got nothing to do with that. This is…” She took a deep breath and started again, “Do you remember when that little boy fell into the tiger pit last year at the zoo?” Troy raised an eyebrow at her quizzically and nodded, ”Mmmhmm.” Tempest continued more quietly, “Well, everyone was shocked when the tiger jumped right into that moat after him and chased him down and attacked him. But they shouldn’t have been. Even if he was born in captivity and never saw the wild, he’s still a tiger. No matter how many years people have been handing him meat on a platter, he’s still a hunter. He did what he was born to do, and he simply doesn’t know how NOT to do it.” Tempest took hold of Troy’s hands, which he didn’t fend off, although his brow was knit in a way that suggested he was not reassured. She went on, “I was born to do this. I was made to fight. I don’t wake up every morning wondering what it is I’m supposed to be doing. And if I’m not doing it, I know something’s wrong, like if I don’t exercise or don’t eat what I need or drink enough water. Something essential isn’t there and I feel empty and hollow. No matter how great everything else is, that one thing being gone makes all the difference. I don’t know how not to do this, not for long anyway.” She blinked rapidly, as if tears were struggling through again and she would not have them. Troy shifted uncomfortably and said, “I’ve always said I could stand by you no matter what because I loved you. And I didn’t get mad that you lied to me, even though it hurt. I understood that maybe you thought you were doing the right thing at the time. But, you should have trusted me with this from the start.” “I know,” she said, “I should have…” Troy cut her off by placing his finger over her lips, and for a moment she looked confused, an expression that turned to fear upon seeing the look on Troy’s face. It wasn’t a look of anger, but of disappointment, and resignation. “Yes, you should have,” he said quietly. “Because if everything you said is true, you had to know. You had to know you wouldn’t stay away.” And with that, Troy Rivers turned and left the room. As the door slapped shut behind him, Tempest snatched up the icepack, just as tears rolled from her eyes and she pressed it hard against her face as the first sob shook her. She tried to hold it in and stop crying, stop the fresh flow of blood that was trickling from her nose again, stop everything before it got any worse.
