Vindication: Piecing Together the Mirror

30 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-04-30 Even after a few months, the Belmont, North Carolina apartment had seen virtually no improvements. The small two bedroom/two bathroom living space had been furnished only enough to be a place to sleep. The main room was nearly empty, save for the generic gray hard-plastic folding table atop which sat the Gateway FPD1730’s monitor, keyboard, stereo speakers, and lay-down tower. The kitchen still had the oven-stove, microwave, and fridge, with nothing else lying around. The bedrooms were just that: rooms that were empty save for two white-sheeted double-beds on them. Everything was bland; the sterile white of the rooms was harsh to the eye. One demanded more of a variety of color when taking in their surroundings. Baby-boomer Harold Dawkins sat at the computer, customized with two hard drives worth to have 60 gigs of memory, and the fastest modem available. All that computer expertise and he never seemed to get dressed; he was always sporting with a full white beard, walking around in his red pajamas and a robe looking like Patrick Stewart in A Christmas Carol. “Ayashi…” A deep sigh from Ayashi after he spoke. He was doing it. The two tournaments where running concurrently. PCW’s World Title tournament saw him in the quarterfinals. At the same time, he was in the Bracket 4 Finals of the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. For someone who had been billed as the extremely ring-rusted shell of a wrestling veteran, he had managed quite well at exceeding expectations. “I haven’t been called Ayashi in years. Over a decade.” That, of course, was due to the fact that he was wrestling as Jimmy Diablo. And he wasn't Jimmy Diablo. He simply knew Jimmy Diablo for many years. He’d been in the ring with him countless times. If anyone could pull off a Diablo impersonation, it was him. “She hadn't even called me that before." But he wasn’t here as Jimmy Diablo. He was here as Soga Ayashi. It was Yashi for short as he was growing up. He believed that his name sounded like a girl, so he didn't go by that name. "She always called me Rikyu." And he wasn't here for sanctuary. Not really. These were the only two people in America, the only two people with whom he was not associated, that knew his secret. They knew that he was living a lie; that Jimmy Diablo was dead, and that he had killed Diablo. He just needed someone to talk frankly with, and they had nothing to gain from disclosing his secret. "It was... just such a shock." *Blam!* I ducked behind the alley corner as the single bullet blasted forward from the chamber of the Browning semi-automatic handgun. *Blam! Blam!* The gunmen didn't really care that they were drawing attention to themselves. This part of town had its fair share of shootouts. Nobody bothered calling the police; they were in these people's pockets. All I could do was to evade them and somehow make it out of this alive. It took a lot to get me here. I never thought that I'd be making a return to Mexico, but here I was. It'd been a long time since I'd seen the city. The reunion wasn't what I'd hoped it to be, but I was getting used to disapointment in that way. The meeting with Jocelyn sucked. The meeting with Hijo del Diablo sucked. I wasn't good at this reconnecting thing. If I survived, I'd have to do better with it. The alleys were long, but it was dark enough that they couldn't see me. I ran as fast as I could while staying low enough to have most of the bullets whiz over my head. I could've just stayed put, hid behind a dumpster or something. It wasn't that dark, though; if they looked to the side, they would've spotted me and I'd have been dead. I had to keep moving and hope that I eluded them or that they ran out of bullets. I didn't care so much which happened first, but I wanted this whole episode to be overwith. Running around in the middle of the night in Mexico City in a place where no one would help me wasn't my idea of a fulfilling night. In fact, just running around was a pain for me, especially given the cool of night and the fact that my jeans didn't protect me from the chill in the slightest. I knew that there were two large and physically fit young men chasing behind me. They looked like twins; bigger, balder versions of Felix Trinidad. I only caught momentary glances of them when I was going into the restaurant to meet Hijo del Diablo. They'd escorted me out; the only reason that I managed to get this far in escaping them was that I ran as soon as they were taking me to their car. In the metropolitan section of town, they couldn't go shooting many bullets at me and nothing happen. They were Hijo del Diablo's only help; he couldn't lose them. I was resenting the fact that they'd given chase this far. Diablo must have told them not to come back unless I was dead and buried, or whatever they were going to do with me. Damn their dedication to the job... They're just like I was, way back when. Ayashi had thrown his tattered brown leather coat upon the floor. He didn't really do well for respecting people's houses. The yellow shirt and green pants that he wore hadn't been clean in a few days, but he laid upon Vangellus Oxios's bed as though he had just jumped out of the shower and put on fresh clothing. Adrienne Diamond made the bed; she wasn't too appreciative of that. "What did you expect?" the naturally-beautiful Diamond commented, adjusting her Hooters tank top. She never really understood why Oxios gave her that shirt, other than to remind her where she came up from. "You killed the woman's husband and steal his identity? That's not exactly good reunion material." She wasn't sitting on the bed with him. She didn't get that close to people right away. Soga Ayashi (aka Jimmy Diablo) was a stranger to her. She knew something about him thanks to the magic of thorough research, and Harold's lack of anything better to do, but she didn't know him. He was lucky enough to be alone in a room with her. "I didn't think she'd be all that happy to see me, I admit," Ayashi had his palms over his eyes. He wasn't crying, he was more tired than anything else. He'd just been up for so long. It had been 37 hours since he got any sleep, and the bags under his eyes showed that clearly. He took a cab to the Oxios residence and overpaid the fare by twenty dollars. Adrienne asked, "Then what did you want? And for that matter, why are you here?" Good questions. Ayashi's schemes had been a mystery to nearly all but himself. His motives were even more secret. Of the four people who knew that he was doing, one hated him for it because of her relationship to both Ayashi and Jimmy Diablo, another was disappointed because the man that he paid to eliminate Jimmy Diablo was impersonating him, and the last two didn't give a damn one way or the other. He came to the last two, because they were the last two people that he could turn to. He didn't want to involve Jimbo K. in what was going on. These two had found out the truth on their own, so he wouldn't be dragging them into anything. Ayashi stared up at the ceiling. He felt like a client on the psychologist's couch. "Here, there, it's the same. I was just... hoping for someone to see what I was doing; hoping for someone other than myself to think it's a noble exploit." "Yeah..." she sighed. Adrienne was not going to cut him any slack. Strangers did not deserve the use of tact, in her opinion. "But you have to understand three things. First, you're responsible for Jimmy Diablo's death. Second, this won't change the fact that he's dead. And third, maybe he'd have won something had he not had his neck broken eight years ago?" The defense mechanism kicked in, as Ayashi said, "Thanks, ass." "You were asking for it," she snapped back at him. The one question that she didn't ask was how he could pass for a Mexican, when Ayashi was of Hawaiian descent and raised in Japan as the son of a sumo wrestler. The easiest answer was the fact that his mother was a wrestler in Mexico; Ayashi, being named so, was so far-removed from his Japanese ancestry that it didn't matter in the slightest. "Look," she said, "you're just feeling guilty about what you did. And without actually doing anything in the way of punishing yourself, you're trying to make up for it to clear your conscience. Anyone who knows can see that. That's why you're not getting any sympathy from us; your noble act is just a selfish expression in the attempt to make amends in the way that is easiest for you to do without owning up to the fact that you can never atone for what you did. I know it, Harold knows it, this Jocelyn woman knows it; even you know it, you just don't want to admit it...." I know that I was wrong with what I did. I think about it all the time. Of course it ate me up inside knowing what I'd done. I never intended to kill Jimmy. I was just going to hurt his pride; an injury was probably going to happen, but I just needed to get him away from using the Diablo name. That's all Hijo del Diablo wanted, and it's all I wanted. I just wanted him not to have anymore problems. I guess I did get what I wanted, looking at it like that. But I also wanted Jimmy to... I really couldn't think like that. Not now. Not with men chasing me with the intent to kill. *Blam!* "Do they EVER run out of bullets?" I wondered. By then I had lost count, but they'd probably both used a full magazine by now. They came prepared. As I ducked into the building, I found myself bumping into lines of meat. Great, I was in a meat locker. They probably wanted to put me here anyway when they were done with me, and I just did all the set-up work. It made me uncomfortable staring at all the cattle ribs hanging from the ceiling. Not that the freezing temperatures made it any better. I was a little curious as to how a meat locker had a back door anyway, or what it was doing on the bottom floor of a building that I knew to be used residential purposes. Then again, what did I care when my life was in danger? Not knowing where to go, and thinking better of wandering around aimlessly for fear of the very real possibility of walking into the path of a bullet or getting shot in the ass while I tried to find my way out. Then again, it didn't look like there was anywhere to go if I wasn't heading for an exit. I didn't like moving around blind, and I didn't like being caught without the advantage. I'd been doing that in the ring as Jimmy Diablo. I'd been purposefully half-assing it in the past month, trying not to seem better than I really am. It did put my opponents into something of a 'mind at ease', but that probably won't work as well for me. I've made it to the quarterfinals of two tournaments and defeated a total of five people to do it. Even Propaganda lost to me, and he couldn't be make the cruiserweight requirements even if he asked Jesus for a miracle. ... God, that sounded so generic trash-talk. The cold, adrenaline, and fear were all getting to me. The two guys chasing after me were going to be barging in at any moment. I had a put distance between us by dropping the crippled old man routine while avoiding a lobotomy by bullet. My legs don't fail me when it's necessary. I quickly scanned the room. All there was to this room were the walls, the twenty-foot-high ceiling, and the cross-grid metal bars from which the hooks hung to the floor. There was nothing around to hide behind, except for the meat itself and that didn't do any good. The chains came down thirteen feet, and the meat was only another five feet, so it didn't reach down to the floor and I had to push it aside to move anywhere. They could just track the swinging beef to find me. Plus the meat didn't reach the floor, so if they were to actually do something intelligent like look underneath the meat to see my legs, I would be royally fucked. It was a gamble, but I came up with an escape plan, hoping that it would work for me. Stepping up to the first set of beef ribs only a few feet from the back door, I took off my shirt. Using it to protect my hands from the cold, I scrambled to get up the chain. It took every ounce of strength in my freezing body to cling to that chain and climb the thing. When the two tuxedoed henchmen ran in behind me, they saw the meat swinging from my climb, but they just assumed that I had moved into the rows and continued in after me with their guns held at ready. Thank God, it worked. Well, I shouldn't say that. God probably couldn't care less about me. After they had gotten about ten feet into the beef (it was a very large room), I slid down the chain as quietly as possible and jumped down to the floor. It would have been a clean getaway, had it not been for the stomping sound that my hard-soled shoes made when they hit the concrete floor. Wait... that wasn't so much the problem; it was the fact that I'd forgotten that the metallic freezer door made a large creaking sound. For some reason, at that point, I realized, "Oh, the butchers' shop in the front gets pickups back here." I don't know why I stopped to say that, but the sound of footfall coming my way made me pick up the pace and start running again. The thumping bass the techo/trance music. The stomping of feet against the floor. The collective breaths of young men and women enjoying themselves and appreciating every cathartic moment. The consumption of alcohol and drugs in large quantities. Such images would come to mind as some modern version of the speakeasy. Indeed, it seemed to be a variation of a party from F. Scott Fitzgerald's The Great Gatsby. For whatever the rave appeared to be, though, it was clear that the kids and adults were there to have a good time, whether that meant release or profit. Enjoyment was the theme of the night. High school teens and middle-aged men co-mingled in an unstructured dance of love. Love of money, love of persons, love of life. 'What happens here, stays here.' So the mantra of the night saw repetition with every wild act of debauchery. No one was doing anything that they'd want their parents or significant others to know about. Here, amidst the throngs of bodies, Sarushima Rikyu waited for his contact. He could be found here because it didn't matter who he was. Everyone was too drunk or drugged up to care that he was one of the most hated men in Mexico. To them, he was just another person to love. And it was here that he found the sixty-year-old Mexican man in the dark blue suit, still sporting black hair and looking decades younger than he really was. He knew exactly where he was going. He had been told to find the man in the center of the dancefloor wearing a pink shirt and Bermuda shorts. No one else in their right mind would come to the rave like that, so he was easy to find. "Excuse me," Colosio Zapata interrupted as Rikyu danced with two young girls from Oklahoma who had driven down for Spring Break. "Do you have a light?" The code-phrase spoken, Rikyu knew that his contact had arrived. Bidding a momentary goodbye to the women, he stepped away from the dance floor and away from the solitary building where the rave was taking place. Outside, they stepped into Rikyu's apple red 1979 Plymouth Reliant and took a drive down the empty road leading into the open desert. Zapata began, wiping his brow with the hanker-chief. He was constantly sweating, regardless fo the temperature or level of physical activity. "You've served us well these past few years. Almost as well as Soga Ayashi did in the past." Immediately, Rikyu coldly replied, "I never want to be associated with that name." "Yes, of course," Zapata nodded. He knew how much Rikyu wanted to surpass Ayashi. His words were calculated, so as to add fuel to the fire. "So your next assignment should be of special interest to you." "Oh?" Rikyu kept his eyes on the road, feigning interest. Colosio Zapata continued. "It seems as though Soga has taken his retirement as a free pass to do as he pleases, even against the wishes of our employer." "Do I ever ask for a backstory?" Rikyu shook his head. He never cared about any of his other targets; this one, despite being a personal pleasure for him to take on, was no different. "Where is he now?" Zapata grinned. "That's the best part. He's coming here. He'll be in Mexico City tomorrow; he has a meeting with Hijo Del Diablo. After it's over, you track him and eliminate him at your convenience. We trust you to get the job done, so there's no rush. But we want him finished before he returns to America in two days." Rikyu blinked and nodded, showing no emotion whatsoever. "Done." He'd waited for this chance for years. Finally, after so many years, he had the opportunity to surpass Soga Ayashi. Not only that; he had the opportunity to end Ayashi. He wouldn't waste it. {To be continued…}
