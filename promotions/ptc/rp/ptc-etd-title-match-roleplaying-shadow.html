Fatal Muse .:: ::: ::. The surroundings of Prague were overwhelming. Up until this point, Shadow had herself convinced that she’d never been here before, but she’d obviously been wrong. After the initial shock of remembrance, things had started to become clear. She needed no map to find her way around the ancient, narrow winding streets to Judith Bridge. It was like she’d been on autopilot to Vysehrad Castle ever since she’d seen its silhouette lurking in the distance. And now, the realization was at hand. She stood at the top of the steps, looking out over Prague. A lot had changed since she’d last been here. Considering the last time she had been here was well before electric power, though, she supposed that was understandable. And yet, there was a sense that something didn’t add up among all this—a sense that there was a link she was missing. Figuring out what it was? Easier said than done. She wasn’t sure what she was looking for, but as usual, her mind was swimming at the moment. So much to think about at the moment that finding some mystical key to her psyche was the last thing on her mind. She took in a deep breath of the cold air, though not for the purpose of her lungs—certainly, her lungs didn’t matter. It was simply the pleasure of being able to feel the cool air of her native surroundings flow through her again, like breathing in some sort of entity of the land. The feeling of ‘home’ had rejuvenated her. Wrestling had almost been synonymous with irritation lately, the way that she often got irritated with things in her life that were too static. But there was one thing right now, one thing that kept impressing itself upon her: the opportunity to compete for and possibly win the PTC Extreme Title. A momentous opportunity, at least that was what all the dirt sheets had been crying since the news had been leaked to the press. And due to her hectic schedule with WMW, it had taken long enough to set itself up. But here was her chance, looming on the horizon like it was about to swallow her sun—if she didn’t get herself together and rise to the occasion, she’d have to seriously reconsider whether or not she was worthy of the confidence that she’d built herself up with. Everyone knew her feelings about WMW. As essentially the only female competitor there, it made sense that she would have attracted attention, but yet it seemed like the upper echelon of the company was always out of reach. Someone was always there to put their boot on her fingers and send her careening back down the ladder to relative obscurity, the veritable ‘curtain-jerker champion’ as they saw her. PTC had been different—PTC had offered her a chance to get away from all that, and she’d been surprised what the freedom had granted her. The ability to step up to the plate, and go after exactly what she wanted. The only problem was, she’d gotten involved so deeply, so desperately in GTT4 that when she’d finally been booted out of it in the Flinders bracket finals by Kasper Sky, she’d hardly had any energy left to give to anything. Yet, WMW had kept her busy. Ace Superior Title bouts left and right—she couldn’t recall a time when she’d been booked lately that had been a non-title match. And yet, through the constant slew of opponents that were hurled at her head left and right, she hadn’t yet fallen. She was just one month short of breaking Noah Hanson’s record with the Ace Superior Title—and not only that, she’d be up against Fury, Jake, and maybe some others in a match to decide who would enter PTC’s next big event—Seven. And then the news of a title shot, amid all this. The news that the schedules of all three competitors had finally been worked out, and she was set to take on Vendetti and Harley. A sort of gritty determination had built up in her through a sort of slow burn ever since she’d gotten the letter in the mail. The doors may have closed on her in GTT4, and they seemed to remain forever closed in WMW, but somebody up there liked her. Of course, her own snorting scorn forbade her from believing in Fate of any sort; it was her own merits that had gotten her this shot. She’d kept a close eye on her opponents. She knew of them both, but reviewing the GTT4 tapes had been helpful in acquainting herself with them even better. What had made the difference, however, was seeing them this week. It was obvious that both of them had victory on the brain—no matter what the reason, they both seemed to her like the typical cocky bastards that pranced around a promotion’s hallowed halls thinking that they had been crucified on the cross of greatness and had risen three days later. She smirked slightly. She had news for them. It wasn’t going to be easy to deter her from this course, all things considered. She may have been simply ‘this Shadow character’ in the heads of her opponents, but the people who had actually stood toe to toe with her before knew that she was much more than that. After all, she wouldn’t have held this WMW title for so damn long if she’d been nothing. Thinking that she wasn’t worth the time to even investigate was a mistake on the part of the champion. Harley, she knew, would be a worry. Harley had just as much at stake coming into this match as she did. She was still largely considered a PTC no-name, looking to make her voice heard—well, without having to say anything at all, really. Harley was just as determined to take a bite out of the PTC pie, and this was certainly an honor worth fighting for. The champ puzzled her, and puzzled her immensely, she was willing to admit. She sensed, at least slightly, that he wasn’t quite as much of a showoff as he put forth on camera. Then again, everyone she’d seen thus far always put their worst side forth on camera. Maybe they thought they were making themselves look like conquering heroes, but they only looked like little Napoleons, marching toward their respective Waterloos. Weakness after weakness displayed themselves on the digital screen, and she had to admit it was almost overwhelming—too much to even make notes on. It was lucky for her that she had such a good memory. When she walked into that match against Vendetti and Harley, she’d have faithfully committed to her head everything that she believed she could use against them. For her, this match was far more about mental preparation than physical. Her mind was on fire, ready to go right now—in fact, it was hard to not pace back and forth, so great was her agitation. She couldn’t wait to get her hands on either of them, and it wasn’t because she wanted gold; wrestling had never been about that for her. Was it about honor? No, Shadow didn’t care about honor. Honor was a term tossed around by Ventrue and their high-society-minded friends. It meant nothing. She wasn’t interested in winning this match because if she did, she’d finally have all eyes on her. Nor was it really about proving herself. She’d been proving herself ever since she stepped in the door of WMW’s front office, and she’d proved herself enough in PTC that at least no one could say she didn’t go down without a fight. In fact, though, Joey Vendetti had touched on something very important. This match, it wasn’t about the title. Maybe it was for Harley, she couldn’t assume. But Vendetti himself had said he simply didn’t care, and to some extent, that was how she felt. This wasn’t about the title. What was it about, then? It was a question that had weighed on her mind considerably since Vendetti had put it into her head. It wasn’t about being Vendetti’s therapist, his punching bag on which he could vent all his frustrations about life. That was a dangerous conceit to allow oneself, if one was serious about a looming title defense. Even against opponents that she herself might have considered weak, Shadow never let her guard down. That opened the door for failure. What was Dave Harley doing here? The best she could figure, he was sitting around thinking about how wonderful he was, and how extreme his past had been. Was that enough for him to ‘conquer the PTC Extreme Division?’ That was a harder question for her to answer. She knew much better than to underestimate him, considering his in-ring prowess. Yet, she hadn’t been awed by it. Rarely was she awed by anyone. So her two opponents already had very serious weaknesses, at least as they had been revealed to her. Shadow knew, though, that the winner of this match wasn’t so easy to predict. It was still more like a crap shoot than anything else at this point, but it always helped when you could stack the deck a little in your favor. What did they know about her, after all, that wasn’t second-hand information? Shadow had always considered herself a very private person, even among friends. She could count on one hand the number of people who actually knew her well enough to match her in-ring strategy. It was always an advantage to be the wild-card in a situation, and who knew? Maybe everyone was expecting her to lay down and die. Maybe everyone was expecting her to be at a disadvantage because she was a woman. Opinions hardly mattered to this one, though. The air of Prague crackled around her as she paced the rooftop of Vysehrad, and her mind drifted back, sliding centuries through her fingertips to reach that long-ago place, where things had been much different. She remembered distinctly that you used to be able to see the tops of the buildings in the Old Town long ago, but now they were obscured by city lights. She remembered that long ago, the soothsayers used to stand at the gates to the mountains, prophesying about nonsense, but now there were only winos sleeping on benches. She remembered that before the time of guns, there had been only scantily armored town guards who could scarcely run down someone using their preternatural Celerity. The streets had run red with the blood of the innocent in those nights, and now the only reminder of that was the blood red rooftop of nearly every building in the city. Spires still clawed up at the gray sky, and the earth here still breathed with a fetid, gloomy, noxious scent that could only be intoxicating to those who had breathed it in long ago. And she had weathered all this change, though not without change herself. She had made it through trials that would perhaps have broken someone not quite so strong as she, though for the longest time, she had considered herself quite weak. Misconceptions could be made, of course, even when they concerned one’s own psyche. Yet even though she’d discovered her own fallacy, she kept it to herself. Best that they still look at her and consider her distracted and weak. The scores of years past had been good to her, she had to admit. And as they had passed, so had human frailty. Her heart, which had been the seat of the violent passion that got her where she was now, had turned into something cold—no longer was the burden of human emotion incumbent upon her. No longer was she susceptible to the little ills of conscience that would lay men low. And despite the fact that there were those crying for her soul, she knew that it too had been extinguished some time before—her own existence was proof enough of that, that neither God nor heaven existed, and if they had, she would have battled tooth and nail to destroy the ridiculous notion of a human utopia. As though they were deserving of it. A cruel look came into her eyes, the green eyes that people often found quite striking when they looked at her. It was through those eyes that most of her expression could be found, and in that case, she would have alarmed a man of even the strongest constitution at this moment. Memories coursed quickly over her. She remembered the nights when Vaclav had ruled this castle, and all who lived there had pursued their bloody arts without dissent or challenge. The Ventrue had changed all that, slipping in under cover of negotiation and taken beautiful Vysehrad from all the Fiends who would have rightly possessed it. Yet, as she stood on its roof now, it was clear that she had won the war. The Ventrue were long gone, had scrambled over to America to fight their petty little turf wars against the much more intelligent Sabbat, and they didn’t stand a chance. Meanwhile, she stood here, undefeated, once again reclaiming the sprawling fiefdom that was her home. It was without a doubt a principle of the little humans that every empire would one day fall, and from all that she had seen during her existence, it was true. Everything touched by Kine hands would one day turn to dust, just as they would turn to dust mouldering in their graves. It would have been a rather sobering consideration, had Joey Vendetti put his mind to it. She had to admit, as much as she was wary of him, she had doubts that he would think of things in the same way she did—but that was his loss. In any case, this was a war she wasn’t prepared to lose, because it was exactly the kind of war she had fought all her life. She held her hands up to the putrescent sky, glowing with a crimson cast from the low-hanging moon. Even in the middle of Prague, it was as though the atmosphere was embracing her, welcoming home a long-lost child. The empire was crumbling. She was simply the only one to notice it. Joey Vendetti’s admission that he had nothing left to lose only made him weak—meant that he had nothing left to fight for either. And that put Joey Vendetti right where she wanted him to be. As for Harley? She would take care of that by the simple fact that whatever he had in ‘extremity,’ she could top ten times over with bloodlust. As the reddish glow from the moon-drenched clouds seemed to encompass her hands like a halo, it all became clear. This was about blood. Pure and simple, it was about spilling blood—the way her palms appeared to be covered in it only reminded her of a dozen instances in her past when her hands had been wet with the warm essence of someone else’s life as it drained slowly away. It was a feeling she hadn’t wanted to get comfortable with. But slowly, she had realized that such was her lot. The whole of human history was drenched with the blood of the innocents, and she was only doing her part, after all. Feelings coursed forward—here she stood, on the top of her ancestral home, once again reclaiming for herself the blood ties that bound her to the mystical land here, land that was sickly from the pull of the spirits upon it. This land, this blood-soaked land—it was hers. And she intended to keep it. She smiled, a terrible, heavy smile. When it came down to it, there was a difference in fighting like a man who had lost everything, or a man who presumed that he already had everything, and fighting like a beast with an insatiable urge to spill the blood of everyone it came into contact with. The Hunger was getting ravenous. .:: ::: ::.
