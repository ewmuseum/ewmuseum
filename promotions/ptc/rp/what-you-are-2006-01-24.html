What You Are

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 "What makes you think you’re the good guy?" As he said that, it was almost like dying again, but I clung to hope. "Screw you, Shanty! Don’t try to turn this around on me. You were the murderer." "So those innocents that you let die, they don’t count?" "I was obeying orders… and so were you!" I screamed. "Well, I guess I stopped taking those orders, wouldn’t you say?" said Shanty, as calm as ever, rubbing one hand over the scar tissue that covered his jawbone, a badge that spoke of the bomb he had planted. It was true. In Iraq we had done things that were not good, not by any stretch of the imagination. Yet we were in a terrible place. What would you do when a car speeds towards you, the day after some of your friends have been killed in exactly the same circumstance? You fire at the car, and if you kill a family then they should have stopped at the warning shots. Only, that wasn’t what Shanty was talking about. "We were all in it together," I moaned. "The ones who died were lucky, weren’t they? The ones who didn’t die, the ones that were captured, they were the unlucky ones. In death you can find release, but what happened to the others…" said Shanty, his voice trailing off to the memories that I had been blocking for so long. Bereft of my body, clinging to the wreckage of my brother’s like a survivor from a shipwreck, I began to choke up with tears. Even so, I slipped the smaller knife into my hand as a show of readiness. "You think you know me, don’t you Steven?" I nodded my head. I had seen almost all there was to see about Shanty, and probably remembered more of it than he did. "Sadly for you, I only showed you what I wanted to." "What?" I asked, open mouthed. "Do you think you’re the first to come back and try to find me again? Steven, you’re the slowest of all. You’re the last one." "The others?" "All of them. Every last person who died in that blast has sought me out. They wanted to meet the man who killed them all, but had miraculously survived." "So, you killed them all?" I asked, dread creeping into my voice. With no options left, I started to lie. "I’m not scared of you, Shanty. I’ve already been dead, so what can you do to me? You were always strong, Shan, and quick… but you didn’t have the killer instinct." Shanty laughed, and I realised what I had just said. Everything seemed to be conspiring against me. "Kill you?" he asked, his murmured words sounding incredulous. "What good would that do?" Nothing was making sense. "I don’t intend to kill you, Steven. All I can do is offer you a choice. Anyone can be redeemed if they choose to be, even at this late stage." "Even you?" I laughed, my voice dripping with tears and sarcasm. "Oh, I found my redemption, Steven. Some of those who came for me found theirs too. Others… others took the wrong path. They took the wrong route, and I’m sure they regret it now. You’ve got to make a decision." "You’re trying to stall me until someone else arrives. You don’t want me to attack you." "You can’t attack me, Steven," said Shanty, laying his arms out as if to offer himself up… or as if to indicate the room around him. Shanty took a step backwards, and I followed him towards the wooden dresser. On top of it was the slate, along with a small piece of paper, perhaps a newspaper cutting, and a tiny golden bell. "Do you think you know me, Steven?" By now I was beyond speech. The knife in my hand felt like it was on fire, and I could hear buzzing everywhere. "Here’s the piece of the puzzle you were looking for." Shanty raised the paper into the air, and suddenly it flew into my mind, the memory knocking me back with the force of a sledgehammer, ice cold tendrils through my brain as neurons raced to keep up with it. Ten minutes delivered in the blink of an eye. I saw what happened when Shanty was seventeen. *** It seemed like an ordinary weekend, a lazy Sunday that was petering out to nothing but the promise of more classes on the Monday. Shanty Jones was walking down the road, debating with himself over which classes he should skip on the following day, when he heard a few cries behind him. At that time, Shan was on the road to being a dropout. His test scores and general interest in education were bottoming out at an all time low. It was only his love of rugby that was keeping him around. On Wednesday afternoons they would play, and at was in these moments that Shanty was at his happiest, hitting crunching tackles and being involved in minor brawls. A more formal match was usually to follow on the weekend, but this time around it had been cancelled due to a broken down bus. The school was not able to replace it in time, and so the weekend was free for the kids to do what they would. For Shanty and his friends this involved drinking a lot of strong cider, smoking cigarettes and trying to get laid. As often was the case, Shanty had managed both of the former the previous night, but precious little of the latter. It had all brought about a lot of frustration, and now there was someone behind him, screaming. Shanty turned to see two men running down the street towards him. The closest was a young white guy clutching a briefcase, trying to outpace the man following him, who looked to be of Middle Eastern origin, or from somewhere around there. Shan was not surprised, for in his town there weren’t many people who couldn’t trace their lineage back for countless generations in the same spot. People who weren’t white often got persecuted, and this time someone had stolen a briefcase. "Stop him! He’s stolen it!" cried the pursuer. Shanty looked at the white guy as he approached, and saw the fear of being caught on his face. It amused him to know that he could have the power to judge whether or not the thief was going to be caught. He liked the feeling of power, and how it set him apart from the others who were just standing by. Without thinking further, Shanty set his feet and as the criminal came past, he launched forwards, landing a picture perfect rugby tackle that left both men on the floor. Jones laughed as he stood up, looking down at the face of the criminal. It was weird, he thought, that someone could be so terrified of being caught. Was it a fear of jail that made him look so terrified, to look at Shanty with those accusing eyes? A noise like a muffled clap sounded, and all of a sudden there was blood and screaming. The pursuer arrived at the scene, and fired a silenced gun into his target six times, not stopping until the clip was empty. He looked up at Shanty Jones, the man who had enabled this, winked, and whispered, "Thank you." *** "What the fuck was that?" I asked. "A lesson," replied Shanty. "You knew that I became a born again Christian at that age, but you never knew why." "I still don’t. What’s assisting a murder got to do with anything?" "Nothing is ever as simple as you would believe, Steven. That is your downfall. Can you imagine what that would do to a teenager? I thought I was helping out, but all I was doing was actually judging people. I judged wrongly, and it taught me, it taught me the hard way that it is not for human beings to pass judgement on the lives of others. I didn’t see this right away, but my family and my real friends rallied around me. I had counselling, I saw all sorts of doctors, but it was in the church that I genuinely found solace. I realised that I was tested by God, and I was found wanting. I had made a mistake, Steven, and one that I would never make again." "So what? That was twelve years ago, Shan. You’ve changed a lot since then. Last I heard people like you protested against wars, and what we did during them." "I learned to go where the flow took me. I started trying again at school and got on with my education. I kept my head down and instead of frittering my life away I tried to make it something worthwhile. I became a success at University and then in the City, but I was missing something. It was the strangest calling, but I felt it, deep down. I had to join up, and then soon after we were at war. It was not my place to judge on the morality of it. That is what I had learned the hard way. Maybe what we were doing was God’s work, or maybe it was the opposite. I couldn’t know from sitting in a broking office in London. I had to find out for myself. I had to be there. I had to be involved." "You were involved all right," I spat, fuming that a murderer such as he could dare to speak of morality and goodness. "You were involved with the factories." "So I was, and I saw what was done there. From that moment I was struggling with myself. I was not supposed to judge, merely to observe, but the scenes I saw shocked me." "They shocked all of us," I countered. "Some of us just learned to live with it. War is…" "Don’t give me that shit about war is hell, or whatever you were going to say!" Shanty’s voice had become raised for the first time. "No matter what you were there for, you couldn’t see that kind of torture and condone it. It doesn’t matter if they were terrorists or innocents, nobody deserved that." "They should never have trusted you to see it. They should have learned their lessons from Abu Ghraib," I said. "The factories make Abu Ghraib look like a fucking holiday camp, Steven. They experimented on people who were supposed to be our friends." "They were terrorists! They were Al Qa’ida! Insurgents!" "No, Steven. Some of them were, but for every guilty man they took, there were five men in there who had committed no crime other than to be in the wrong place at the wrong time. It doesn’t matter though, because none of them should have been treated like that. The human rights violations alone could have seen…" "And our human rights weren’t violated too? Shanty… we were getting bombed every day. You saw your friends killed by car bombs and suicides. Are you that heartless?" "Heartless? I was the one who deplored how we helped more than anyone else. I could not stand there and let it happen, so I took matters into my own hands. I did what I had trained myself not to do for years. I made a judgement, and executed it." "You executed us, and you got away with it." "What makes you say that?" "Well, you’re here, alive, and I’m stuck in someone else’s body, come to make sure you join me in the realms of the dead. The time for talking is over Shanty. For all your claims of integrity, you’re no worse than the insurgents yourself. All you did different was someone make it out alive." "Actually," said Shanty, with a grin. "That’s not strictly true." *** He had been alone and lost in darkness after the bomb had gone off. The nails had torn through his jaw and sent splinters of bone throughout his face. Alive or dead, Shanty Jones would never look the same again. The blast wave hit him and the shock was sent through his body. He fell down and a metal table broke his fall, as well as his cranium. From there, Shan fell down and his momentum carried him under the table. His pulse weakened and his lungs began to fill with carbon monoxide and dioxide, a result of the fire that had started to burn in the police station. Slowly his vital signs started to fade, and fail, and Shanty Jones died along with Steven Atkins and the rest of the men in that room. He had felt the same as I did, only his awakening was much earlier. He could look down and see his body lying there before I was even aware of what had happened. Being prepared for death had given him that advantage. He was still dazed and unable to alter events, as fire slowly worked its way to reduce the building to rubble and a few remaining fragments of walls and furniture. He looked down at his body, kept intact under the table, and found that he could move back towards it. This, for him, was the ultimate vindication. He was truly anointed by God. One by one the others in the blast had come back to consciousness, to find that they were neither alive nor dead, instead kept in some state of Purgatory or Limbo, or whatever their religious beliefs entitled them to. One by one they hunted down Shanty, in various forms, but no matter who or what they came as, Shanty could recognise them instantly. Some he had to fight, and a series of minor charges relating to assault and battery eventually led to Shanty spending a year of his life in prison, where he made friends with Paul Goodman and waited patiently for me to arrive. I am the last remaining soul that has not been laid to rest, and I will not fail in my duty. *** "So it comes down to us, to this final battle," I said. "No." "Do not try to worm your way out of this now." "No. It comes down to us, and to your decision. You have but one choice, and I counsel you to use it wisely." "I am the angel of vengeance, Shanty. I can bring nothing else." "You stand there in the body of someone else and proclaim yourself of God? Steven, last time I checked, nobody had heard of angelic possession." There it was; the last piece that I had missed. For my sins, for the deaths that occurred at my hands of by my inaction, I was not coming back to life as an angel, but as a demon. In retrospect, it all fitted, far, far better than anything I could have put together myself. I was doomed, and in front of me was Shanty Jones, looking like he was about to burst into a smile. Shanty Jones, who murdered innocents. Shanty Jones, who killed me, and turned me in to this. My mind clouded over with red and the buzzing grew louder and more furious. I could still finish what I had started. "Steven, I beg of you to reconsider." His words were lost in the buzzing of a thousand flies, and I lashed forwards with the knife. It never reached its target, for Shanty took a step backwards and held the slate out in front of him. The marks on the centre were not a pentagram, but a Star of David, and around it were dozens of tiny etched crucifixes. I screamed and tried to move my arm forwards, to wreak death upon my tormentor, but I was held fast as Shanty picked up the book from the bed and read in candle light. "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me." The noise pierced me like needles and the sight of the crosses became too much to bear. I couldn’t move, couldn’t get to Shanty to kill him, to end the torment. I saw his right hand slowly move to take the golden bell, and he rang it out once. All noise stopped, and it was as if the world had turned in on itself. It was silence now that deafened me, but then I could hear a noise like a drain, and I was falling. I left the body behind and I was falling, falling, falling. All I could see was fire shooting around me, and it was just like when the bomb had gone off. Fire burned all around me, and it was with a heavy heart that I realised I was now at home. *** *** *** Shanty Jones dropped to his knees and wept openly, tears streaming down his face. Of all of the souls that had returned to him, only three had continued along the wrong route. Each of them almost destroyed Shanty inside, for it was not his desire to send them there. He had been brought back to life to act as the one thing he hated, a judge over the actions of the living. It was his penance for standing by as the factories continued their business in Iraq. Even his attempt to stop it had not been enough. He shook his head. If people were to know that there had been experiments with gen… "Can I come out now?" asked a timid voice. Shanty almost choked as his laughter was interrupted by a single peal of laughter. He had forgotten that Doctor Curiosity was still locked in the closet. Well, it didn’t matter any more, as it would all soon be over. There would be no more judgement, and nothing for him to care about. Shanty knew what the doctor had been through the previous year, and knew that perhaps now there would be a better bond between them, some deal of respect. It wouldn’t matter for the moment because of what was sure to happen, but it made Shan feel slightly better about the role he had been forced to play. "There you go," mumbled Shanty as he undid the latch that was trapping the doctor inside. "Now, I’ll be seeing you." Jones stood with his arms stretched out in front of his body, palms upwards, supplicating towards the skies that lay above the hotel. "Oh Lord, I have been your humble servant, and have done what was asked of me. My job here is now complete, and I ask to be reunited with you in Heaven above." Shanty stood there, motionless, awaiting his return to the Rapture. "I’m sorry, Shan," said Doctor Curiosity. "That’s just not how it works." "What?" asked Shanty, as he looked down at the doctor. "I have done my task allocated to me by God. I get to go home." "Not yet." The doctor was speaking with only the merest traces of his German accent. "How the hell would you know!" shouted Shanty, expelling more anger than he had when confronted by Steven Atkins. "Vell, mainly because this isn’t an episode of Quantum Leap. Zat’s not how the world works, my friend. That’s not how He works. You’ve heard of the mysterious vays, right?" Shanty nodded, not believing that he was hearing this. "He really does work in them. You’ve done one task, but that’s not all you get. There’s no leap home for you. Ve’re not going to find Al walking around with a coloured piece of plastic, talking to Ziggy about the probability of…" "Stop," said Jones. "It just doesn’t work like that," repeated the Doc. "Maybe you’ve got to make more amends, or complete more for the good of the world. Maybe you’re just allowed to have a life again, free of interference, or maybe you have loose ends to tie up that you don’t know about." "This can’t be happening," said Shanty. "It is happening. For starters, you’re going to have to explain to Mister Atkins over there why he’s several thousand miles away from home, and what just happened with his brother." Shanty looked down to the body of Artemis Atkins, slumped on the floor of the hotel room. He was breathing steadily, but it would be a while before he would wake up. "Then there’s your tournament. It’s important to a lot of people, Shanty. There are foes to vanquish there too. Cult leaders, murderers… violent, Godless people. Perhaps your new destiny is to see them all off? To become a champion of virtue?" Shanty slowly nodded. He was coming to terms with the fact that he was not to be going to Heaven… at least not just yet. As he turned to thank the doctor for his surprising insight, Curiosity flicked on the television. "Now let us vatch pornography and drink beer, jah!" "Not yet. We’ve got to take our friend here downstairs and feed him black coffee until he becomes coherent." "Details, details," said Curiosity, but he did help Shanty to take the body and prop it upright between them. They stepped out of the door and heard it click behind them, though the doctor couldn’t resist one more shot. "Go on… say ‘Oh Boy’ for me!" His accomplice smiled. Whatever lay ahead in GTT5 would hold no fear now for ‘The Angel in Waiting’ Shanty Jones, as he closed the door behind him and looked to the future. *** "On the news tonight we have a report in to what could be the strangest prison break in recent history. Prisoner Paul Goodman, jailed for life for the murder of the wife of his brother, professional wrestler Doctor Curiosity, seemingly disappeared without a trace from his maximum security prison, though no evidence of any escape has yet been found. Goodman is considered to be highly dangerous, and the police are advising anyone who comes in contact with him to try and avoid any type of conflict…" ___________________________________________________ Extract used from Psalm 23, King James Bible
