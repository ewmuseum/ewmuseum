The Life & Times of Ian Fetzer, Professional Fool

29 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-29 The Life and Times of Ian Fetzer, Professional Fool. It’s a normal Thursday night, March 25th 2004 to be exact, and the man known to all of XUW as ‘The Fool’ is sat on a small plastic chair, his head in his hands and an icepack taped to his right leg. The arena locker room that he sits in has been highly personalised. The hard wooden bench has been turned into a bed with the addition of a thin mattress, and a poster hangs on the wall, declaring The Fool’s love for former XUW Superstar ‘The Great Sombrero’. This is the locker room of Ian Fetzer, but it’s also his home. He looks over at the cracked television monitor resting on a small desk, and sighs as the replays show once more his latest in a long series of wrestling losses. In fact, since The Fool arrived in XUW around a year ago, he has yet to be involved in a victory of any kind. Twenty five televised matches have passed without his arm being raised a single time. He’s a jobber, and he knows it. The door opens, and Ian looks up to see his sometime tag team partner and general associate, X Factor. This is a man who was once a World Champion, and who once wore a mask and wrestled as ‘The Great Sombrero’, whose constant feuding and alliances with and against The Fool are the stuff of legends… if legends were written about opening matches and two minute extravaganzas that end in disappointment. Yet Ian Fetzer, this fool we see, was once a World Champion too. It wasn’t always like this… *************** A young boy in California grew up to be a man. Along the way he tried just about everything to get noticed, to be the centre of attention. Not being the most physically dominant, or the smartest guy in the world, he tried entertainment as a medium of drawing a crowd to himself. This boy learned magic tricks, he learned how to sing and dance, and he learned how to become an acrobat. This was all well and good… but neglecting his classes to perfect his back-flips left him in some trouble when high school finished. With poor grades to his name and no real direction in life, the young Ian Fetzer did what any self respecting fool would do… he joined the circus. The circus life suited Ian well. He didn’t have to worry about too many things, and he could be the centre of attention all he wanted, playing different roles as an acrobat, a trapeze artist and a clown. He toured with his fellow circus performers for over a year before a man came along and gave him the strangest offer of his life. He was offered a role as a professional wrestler. Seeing the chance to take himself on to a bigger stage, Ian jumped at the chance. He’d never considered himself to have the kind of build to be a competitive sportsman, but all the training he had indulged in left him with a toned physique, scarcely an ounce of fat on his six foot frame at all. Yet studying videos late one night in his caravan home, Ian Fetzer discovered the world of Luchadores. He saw that he could use all the skills he had built up over the years to become one of these high flying daredevils. It was like a match made in heaven. Alas, while the promoter that spotted him had an eye for talent, he had no idea at all of how to utilise it best. Instead of being allowed to roam freely, diving off turnbuckles and spinning through the air like a demented spinning top, Fetzer was told he had to play a typical bad ass character. This didn’t sit well with Ian, nor with the few fans that this federation had. The style was wrong, nobody was buying into it, and Ian was feeling like he had made a terrible mistake. The pay in the circus hadn’t been great, but he was one of the top attractions, not some opening act that nobody in the crowd paid any attention to. Fetzer was slumping, and everyone knew it. It was so obvious, that to mock him further the promoter set him up to lose in a match with the biggest joke they had at the time… a one-legged midget called Hopsy. The gimmick of the match was that the loser would have to dress up like a clown, and entertain the crowd instead of actually wrestling. It was meant to humiliate Fetzer, but instead it gave him release. There was no more ‘bad ass’ wrestler, no more façade to hide behind. The Fool was born. It happened slowly at first, the jester costume coming together from pieces of the clown he remembered being at a time that seemed so long ago. The crowd laughed at him to start with, but after he produced a dove from thin air, and then somersaulted around the ring, they started to cheer. It took a few weeks, but the move designed to make him an outcast had surprised everyone in making him not just a babyface, but a popular babyface. After a month of The Fool opening each show and entertaining people, he found himself back on the wrestling roster, and with his new gimmick stuck to him like glue. He was a hit. Suddenly everyone wanted to be his friend, and before long he was involved in the Main Event scene. There were posters of him selling like crazy, and he had the most popular t-shirts of anyone in the federation. They liked him so much that he became the first man in the organisation to get his own action figure made. It was a fairly crude design and it didn’t look too much like him, but for someone who thrived on attention, it was heaven. Until, in one fell swoop it was all taken from him. The promoter who was bad with talent was also bad with money, having failed to fill in his tax returns fully… or indeed at all. Up on more counts of fraud than he could, well, count, the promoter was forced to liquidate his assets to try and fight imprisonment. Everything associated with the fed was sold on, other than the talent, which was not his to sell. Fetzer was heartbroken, his new home taken from him just as he was starting to enjoy himself and become popular. It hit him hard, but then fate intervened in the form of someone telling him that there were more feds out there for him to participate in. Not being the smartest tool in the shed, Ian had thought his adventure was over, when it was only just beginning. So it was that fate, or perhaps aggressive marketing, brought him to a federation called IWZ. Carrying with him at all times a memento of how he got this far in the industry, ‘The Fool’ Ian Fetzer was an instant hit in this new federation. He carried his action figure with him to the ring, and started to talk to him too, giving him the extremely original name of ‘Mister Action Figure’. Nobody is quite sure at what point Ian Fetzer started to believe the action figure could talk back to him, but as long as he was picking up wins and shifting merchandise, nobody cared… and boy was he doing that. In fact, during his tenure in IWZ, Fetzer ran undefeated through the roster, all the way to winning the Internet Title, and then the World Title. After all the time, after all the pain and heartache, the lovable Fetzer had finally done it. He’d achieved something for himself he never thought possible. Then it all fell apart as fate intervened again… this time in the form of an eighteen wheeler. Racing across a road to rescue Mister Action Figure, The Fool was struck by the colossal vehicle, and sent flying into a wall, where he collapsed, a body of broken bones and torn muscles, his barely functioning organs just about held together by skin that was itself ripped and tattered. The Fool survived, but was unlikely to ever walk again, let alone wrestle. Thus began a long time of rehabilitation, but not in the normal sense of the word. You see, The Fool was taken from his hospital bed by a dark, shadowy figure, and nothing more was seen of him for a long time, until the man who has taken him died, and he was freed. Nobody knows what happened to Ian Fetzer in his time spent in the Domicile of Darkness, the home of XUW’s Jack “The Ripper” Nobbledy, but when he left it his body was completely restored to health. Sadly for Ian Fetzer, his mind was not. Almost all of his memories had been erased. All he could remember was that the action figure was his friend, and with that information he left the domicile, searching for answers, searching for the needle in the haystack that might unlock his past. Coming across a television advertisement for an XUW show, he attended, somehow knowing that wrestling was important to him, but not knowing why. In an unusual case of mistaken identity involved a vengeful personal assistant to the XUW Commissioner, Ian Fetzer was offered a spot on the roster. It only took one match before everyone realised this was not one of XUW’s brightest stars, Ric Austin, and in fact was a bumbling idiot who couldn’t wrestle to save his life. So began a strange year in the life of Ian Fetzer. A year in which he would wrestle, but never win. A year in which his past would be revealed to him, a past he would never remember. A year in which former XUW Champion X Factor would take him on as a personal assistant, thinking that having a gullible, innocent fool at his side would make it easier for him to steal things and make out with the lovely ladies. Which brings us nicely up to speed. *************** X Factor looks down at The Fool, and smiles as he shakes his head. Factor: Still nothing? The Fool laughs, and shakes his head in reply. This is a tradition now, after every match they do this, after every loss they see if it has come back, even a little bit… but it never does. Factor: Maybe one day, Ian. The Englishman, for that is what X Factor is, wanders over to a small water cooler in the room and pours himself a drink. He took on Fetzer as a pet project, hoping to use him as an aid for his favourite hobby, theft. Yet Fetzer has a strange effect on people, and instead Factor has found himself mellowing out over the past few months, until he’s at a stage where the fans actually like him, and cheer his name when he goes into the ring and puts on a match. When he promised The Fool that he would try to get him some of his own promotional merchandise, specifically some ‘Fool Foam Fingers’, he didn’t mean it at all, it was just a ruse to get Ian to join in with his nefarious schemes… but now he genuinely wants to help with that, at least to a certain extent. Fool: I need to remember how to wrestle. Factor’s eyebrows shoot up. Ian has wanted this ever since he knew he found out his past, but he’s never vocalised it in such blunt terms. Factor looks into his friend’s eyes, and sees within them a desire to succeed, and also a heart on the verge of breaking. To say it would be impossible might be the final straw. Once upon a time, X Factor wouldn’t have thought twice about crushing the dreams of a fool, but he’s been changed. Factor: Any ideas on how to do it? Fetzer looks disconsolate. Fool: Not really. I'm scheduled for a match in PTC, representing XUW, and I don't want to let them all down. Factor: I see. You could always smack yourself over the head with a hard, blunt object. Fool: You think would work? Factor: It does in the movies. The Fool shrugs and points X Factor to a metal folding chair, which is sat on his bed. Factor walks over and lifts the chair, up, as the action figure in The Fool’s hand starts to move around. You could almost call the movement a dance, if you had no sense of rhythm, and had only been told what dancing was supposed to look like… by a blind man. The movements are obviously being made by The Fool’s own hand, even though he himself does not believe that. Fool: Mister Action Figure seems to think that it’s a bad idea to hit me with the chair. Factor: Well, sometimes you’ve got to be cruel to be kind. X Factor smiles, a hint of the devil still left in him. The action figure dances even harder in The Fool’s hand. Fool: Mister Action Figure says that getting hit in… OWWWW! There’s a resounding clang on metal on skull, and The Fool falls off his own chair, to slump on the floor. Factor: Remember anything? Fetzer drags himself back up. Fool: No, because as Mister Action Figure was saying before you hit me with that chair, all the blows to the head I’ve been taking in matches haven’t exactly cleared my mind up at all, have they? Factor: Well maybe they just needed to take you by surprise? Fool: Hmmm… I don’t thi… CLANG! Factor: Remember anything yet? Fool: No, and stop hitting me with that chair, it hurts! Factor: Sorry, I was just trying to help. Fool: I know, but it’s not working. We’re going to have to think up a plan. Factor: Aha, planning is my strong point. Fool: Mine’s being able to cope with being hit over the head repeatedly with a steel chair. Factor: Sounds like fun… want another shot for luck? Fool: No, I think we’ll just have to either draw up a plan or go and ask for help from some other people. Factor: I’ll tell you what. How about you go and ask for help, and I’ll sit here and try and get a plan formed. Fool: That sounds like a plan! Enthusiasm regained, The Fool jumps to his feet, steadies himself after his recent beating takes effect, and then sprints off in search of someone who might be able to bring back his memories. X Factor sits down on the bed, and then turns over so he is lying down. Factor: Well, it looks like my work for the moment is done then. He smiles once more, and slowly falls asleep on his friend’s bed.
