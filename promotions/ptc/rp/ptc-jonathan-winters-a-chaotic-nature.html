[IG 28: Jonathan Winters]: A Chaotic Nature

18 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Vigilance. Now... Standing on the moonlight glazed rooftop, restricting himself to the shadows, Jonathan Winters glared across toward the adjacent building and grimaced as the massive neon Stakiri Corp logo, expertly crafted onto the side of the mammoth structure, glowered back at him with what he considered to be menacing intent. The building was owned by his former employer, Cornelius Stakiri, and in it held a lot of memories Winters would rather forget, but that didn’t seem likely. Not today at any rate. Based at the very heart of Manhattan, Stakiri ran a substantial underworld territory from the luxuries of his legitimate corporation building, where he found it second nature to mix his business and his illegal ventures to reach his dubious goals. Much to Winters amusement, such tactics had come back to haunt him recently and now Stakiri‘s sins had come back to haunt him. A rival organisation, going by the name OmniTech, were targeted by Stakiri over six months ago due to advancements in some new piece of tech both organisations were looking to release for public consumption. Stakiri wanted to get there first and he didn’t much care how. However, little did he know, OmniTech had underworld connections of their own and refused to bow down gracefully for the competition to just walk all over them. War was declared and Stakiri’s downfall set in motion. Despite himself, Winters found himself embroiled in all of Stakiri’s bullshit all over again and, what’s more, he was actually helping his former employer to survive. But he wasn’t doing it simply to help that slimy bastard survive for another day. Far from it, he was there for Aeryn; he was there to prevent her from making the mistake of her life. *** Pity. One Days Ago... Jonathan Winters stood, hands deeply entrenched deep within the confines of his knee-long, black business coat and glared at the chaos that unfolded all around him. Saturday morning in the local mall; it wasn’t an experience fit for the feint of heart. Children dragged their unwilling parents into a variety of overly expensive and transparently patronising business establishments, begging for whatever the latest craze on the school yard was that week. The parents were clearly too tired to put up any kind of resistance and simply went along with the ploy the best they could; anything for the easy life. Then there were the mallrat teens, who generated the most of the populace, hanging around with nothing better to do, annoying the establishment in their own way, contributing nothing to society. Winters shook his head and could not hope to hide his disgust. He pitied their dull, monotonous lives, but also secretly envied them. At least they had a choice in life. At least they had a childhood they could actually remember and somebody, somewhere who actually loved them. It was a hell of a lot more than he had on both counts. Hell, the earliest memory he had in life was two years previously, waking up in some fucking cornfield in Kansas, without a memory to his name. Soon after, he was inducted into Stakiri’s shady underworld dealings. He didn’t have a chance. The irony of it all, didn’t escape him. In fact, the chaos surrounding him was transparently a perfect metaphor for what he laughably called a life. Non-stop chaos from the outset, without much respite to speak of. Always rushing into one battle after the next, inside the ring and outside. Most of the time one clashed with the other and now was no exception to that rule. Recently, he’d experienced some success in Primetime Central, scooping up a win in the coveted Infinite Gauntlet championship. Now, as current holder of the title, his life outside of the wrestling ring had reached breaking point and it was about to get worse. Stakiri had sent word to Winters requesting a meeting and, reluctantly, he’d agreed to it. Winters needed something from his former employer, something only Stakiri could provide and he was certain this intention would be reciprocated. On high alert and his adrenaline pumping, the tug on his trouser visibly startled him. He glared downward, to find the gormless face of a young child staring back up at him. “Are you that wrestler guy?” The child questioned, his small notepad and pen already thrust skyward. Winters recalled the PRIME lawyers and the constant barrage of warnings they hurled at him on a daily basis regarding his conduct in public. Most of their petty rules had long since evaporated from his conscious thought, but he was certain punching little children constantly in the face was high on the list. “Yes,” he sneered. Perhaps if the kids mother was actually around, she would’ve taken the hint; she might’ve picked up on the fact that he didn’t want to be disturbed. She might’ve lead the child away and carefully explain that Mr. Winters was a very busy man and he didn’t want to be disturbed. But his mother was nowhere to be seen. Fucking typical! “Great!” he exclaimed. On his tip toes, the notepad and pen cast higher. “Can I get your autograph?” “No,” Winters responded, paying no attention to the child. His eyes still scanned the area, but still no sign. Come on Stakiri, he silently cursed, where the fuck are you? “But you gotta!” the kid whined. Winters frowned and eyed the kid up warily, his attention effectively diverted. “Why?” The kid shook the notepad and pen, as if adding credibility to his argument. “Because!” Winters shook his head. “Because, is not a valid argument.” The kid lowered his notepad and pen. “What does valid mean?” Winters sighed and didn’t bother to provide an answer, although he could tell it wasn’t the end of the argument. A confused expression had buried itself deep upon the child’s face and something was visibly running through his tiny little head. “Why won’t you sign my notepad?” the kid finally questioned. Winters shook his head. Where are his parents? “Because I’m a prick with better things to do.” “But you’re just stood here.” “Yes,” Winters told him, “I’m stood here being busy.” “But it’ll only take you five seconds!” The child’s whining had now begun to grate on his skin, perhaps because he knew he was right. It would only take him five seconds to scribble something illegible in the kids pad. So why didn’t he? Well, the answer was simple. He didn’t fucking want to and he was a prick with better things to do. “It’ll also only take me five seconds to twist your little head off your shoulders,” Winters said, with a sadistic smile. “And I know which I’d prefer.” The child’s bottom lip began to quiver, water filled his eyes and he scampered away, his dreams shattered. Tough world, kid, he silently commented, Tough world. Perhaps if Winters could remember his own childhood, then maybe he might’ve been able to sympathise with the boy’s plight, but he dismissed the idea as soon as it formed. He didn’t need any excuses for being a prick. It was simply his nature and there was nothing he could do about that now. Not that he even wanted to. Then, to the right of him, he noted a number of bodyguards all dressed in expensive suits, some Winters recognised, some he didn’t, filter into the place like a swarm of ants hijacking a picnic. Bystanders glared at the east entrance of the establishment with interest, expecting to catch a glimpse of some kind of celebrity amongst all of the muscle. Great, Stakiri, just fucking great, Winters thought, shaking his head. More attention, we don’t need. *** Shadows. Now... Through the darkened alleyways she moved, fearless of the shadows that deftly close in around her. In fact, it’s opposite; she embraced the shadows and looked to use them to her advantage. The area is clear to the naked eye, but from years of experience she knows that anything could be lurking around the next corner and nothing is a certainty. Even lugging the metal brief case, she quickly managed to scale a dormant trashcan, leaping with confidence at the bottom rung of the fire escape ladder. Her grip is undeniable, she’d done this plenty of times before, and with one hand pulled herself upward, enough to stabilise herself with a foot securely fastened to the step ladder. Quickly, she was on the platform, rushing ever upward in a clunky upward spiral, carefully not to allow the metal briefcase clang against the side of the fire escape. The rooftop was reached in a matter of minutes and instantly her eyes scan the area, firstly for safety and then for her designated position. The area is clear and the prime position almost calls out to her. However, before setting off to reach it, she allowed a few seconds to glare, in awe, at the dominating florescent logo from the target building; even from this distance the red afterglow covered her entire body and for some, this made her smile. She recognised a deeply embedded sense of nostalgia associated with the vision and, with piqued interest, desperately attempted to access these thoughts. However, upon doing so, a sharp burst of pain shot through her entire body and soon the need for the memories evaporated into the mild night air. She went about her task. *** Covenant. One Days Ago... “Please, Jonathan,” Stakiri began, shooting Winters with his trademark shark-like smile. “Take a seat.” Winters sneered an inaudible response, but reluctantly took the seat opposite regardless, ignoring all of the hired muscle who had taken it upon themselves to burn a hole in the side of his head with their eyes. The waitress desperately tried to approach the two, but deeply buried in a sea of muscle-bound clownshoes with suits, she couldn’t find a path inward. “You’re okay, darlin’,” Stakiri told her, stretching his neck to see her over his wall of faithful guards. “We don’t want anything.” The waitress half opened her mouth, presumably ready to spout the food court’s standardised policy, but the hired muscle quickly stood and whispered something in the woman’s ear. Her eyes widened and she fled for a her life, a sea tears clearly on the horizon. Winters glared at the suits, with a frown. “This really necessary?” Stakiri chuckled to himself. “You’re the one who requested this location.” Stakiri glared around at the decor with disgust. “God only knows why.” “You know why,” Winters told him, “but that’s irrelevant.” Stakiri’s eyes widened. “Irrelevant?” He chuckled to himself and slapped his knee. “Priceless!” “My question was, is all of this security really necessary?” Winters repeated. “You must’ve realised how much attention you received just walking into the place.” Winters glared at the gawping onlookers, just beyond the dividing glass windows. “And now?” Stakiri followed Winters gaze and grimaced at the drawn audience. Most of them had questioning expressions etched into their faces, still desperately trying to figure out who the substantial entourage was actually for. “You’re blaming me for that?” Stakiri questioned. “You’re the professional wrestler here. You’re the one who has your face plastered right across the television screen every week. They’re here for you!” Winters glared at him. “No one likes me and you know it.” Stakiri broke out a toothy grin and finally got around to answering the question, “As you’re well aware, Jonathan, I’m a wanted man. You can never be too sure these days.” Stakiri sighed and beckoned the nearest bodyguard over with a hand gesture regardless. He whispered something into his ear as he leant forward. Quickly, he took two of the other suits and positioned themselves in between the meeting place and the gormless bystanders. Stakiri turned his attention back towards Winters. “Satisfied?” “Hardly,” Winters responded. Stakiri rolled his eyes. “Good. Now let’s get down to business...” “Yes let’s,” Winters interrupted, taking control of the conversation. “What do you want, Stakiri?” “Want?” Stakiri asked, predominantly displaying an expression of false innocence. “I don’t want anything. I just thought it‘d be good if we cleared the air.” Winters rolled his eyes. “Cut the bullshit, Stakiri. You’re in the middle of a territory war!” The security guard closest to him stood and desperately attempted to appear menacing. Winters held back a chuckle and Stakiri quickly gestured for the suit to re-take his seat. “You wouldn’t risk coming here for a meet and greet if you didn’t want anything from me.” Stakiri lowered his eyes and allowed a smile. “But that’s acceptable.” Stakiri glared at him, eyebrow raised. “It is?” “Yes,” Winters told him. “Because I’ll be needing something from you.” Stakiri nodded. “I see.” He leaned closer. “What is it?” Winters grinned. “I want to know everything.” Stakiri frowned. “Everything?” “About Aeryn,” Winters explained. “I want to know who she is and why she’s doing this. Everything.” Stakiri nodded without hesitation. “Agreed.” Winters leant back in his chair. “And I want to know now.” *** Prevention. Now... Instinctively, the rifle was removed from its metal case and assembled in a matter of minutes. She gently rested the weapon upon the wall’s edge and lowered her head in order to glance outward, through the scope, adjusting the focus with an expert hand and unshakeable diligence. With suitable vision established, she then lowered her body and squirmed into a comfortable position. Her sight wobbled then completely disappeared. Bewildered, she quickly stood upright and glanced around. It was only when she saw him with the rifle in hand that she realised it’d been snatched away. How? She silently questioned, eye’s wild. I didn’t hear a thing! Pushing the shock aside, her eyes focused upon the perpetrator and she prepared to lunge. However, as the realisation took hold she stopped herself and questioned, Don’t I know him? *** Revelation. One Days Ago.. “This isn’t the place,” Stakiri told him, his eyes shifting from side to side. Winters smiled to himself. “Like hell it isn’t!” Stakiri leant back in his chair and allowed a knowing grin. “What are you getting at, Jonathan?” “Nothing,” Winters told him, “but if you think I’m going with you to a nice secluded place, you’re deluded.” Stakiri shook his head and attempted to appear shocked. “I don’t like what you’re getting at, Jonathan. You’re not my enemy and you never were. Don‘t you trust me anymore?” “Anymore?” Winters shook his head. “I never trusted you to begin with, Stakiri and you’re smart enough to know it. We do this here.” Stakiri sighed and reluctantly nodded in agreement. “They reprogrammed her.” Winters eyes widened. “What?” “OmniTech captured Aeryn and then reprogrammed her,” Stakiri casually explained, as though it were the most natural thing in the world. “OmniTech had predicted my intentions and had already scouted out my crew as a safety precaution,” Stakiri explained. “They found out about Aeryn on the first day and decided to use her against us.” “Use her against us?” Winters questioned. It seemed a reasonable explanation based on what he knew, but that didn’t explained how they accomplished such a goal. “I don’t know the specifics or the technical aspects of it,” Stakiri told him. He was lying, Winters could tell. “But they fucked with her head and her memories, for months. Probably the same technique the CIA use to train their assassins. The war started many months ago, we just didn‘t know it.” Winters shook his head. He was having a hard time following any of this. “What?” “You’ve seen The Manchurian Candidate, right?” Stakiri responded. Winters scrunched his face up, confused. “The film? You’re explaining this away on a Denzel Washington film?” “Precisely,” Stakiri explained. “But watch the Frank Sinatra version, it’s much better.” Winters glanced around and found a couple of the security guards nodding in agreement. “This doesn’t make sense.” Stakiri sighed. “Actually, it’s a perfect analogy. OmniTech has been around for years. They were the ones who actually developed the procedure for practical use.” “So what you’re telling me is,” Winters began, still trying collate everything in his head, “OmniTech muddled with Aeryn’s memories and manipulated her to turn against you?” “That’s precisely what I’m saying,” Stakiri told him. “And what’s more, I need you to stop her.” “Stop her?” “Yes,” Stakiri said. “Why do you think I called you down here today?” *** Recognition. Now... Winters glared at his watch for the third time in as many minutes. Damn it! He silently cursed. It was the second night in a row he’d been holed up in the shadow of the Stakiri Corp building and now, he was beginning to get pretty sick of it. In exchange for information regarding Aeryn, this was his reparation. Stakiri had enough resources left to siphon out a rough time period of an assassination attempt and obviously, he wanted it to be stopped. Then, just as he was beginning to seriously consider taking off early, he heard the noise. Remaining in the shadows, he silently positioned himself to obtain a better view of the fire escape and sure enough, he saw a lithe darkened figure, silently emerge onto the roof top and stalk towards the building’s edge. Even in the dark, Winters could see the figure was female and instantly, the thought struck him like a sledgehammer to the temple. Oh fuck no, he silently rambled. It can’t be... His sight of the woman was still obscured, but he could tell precisely who it was just by the way she moved. Stakiri, you slimy fuck! He watched the assassin, smoothly flick open her metal briefcase and soundly extract all the pieces Winters recognised need to make a rifle. It was an expensive fucker too; he’d seen enough of them in the past to decipher that much. He acted, silently rushing out of the shadows and making a stealthy approach. The weapon was assembled before him, a lot faster than he expected and all that the shooter needed now, was a good shot. He grabbed the rifle and yanked away from her surprised hands, He stood there, rifle in hand, staring at the woman he once loved, his fear confirmed. It was Aeryn. She glared back at him, wild eyed with barely a glint of recognition upon her face. Seconds flew by and they both remained still, neither sure of what precisely to do. Winters acted first, dropping the rifle over the edge of the building, into the alleyway that lay below. She watched her weapon fly from his hands and he was sure an attack was imminent. But nothing came. More time flew by and still nothing. “Aeryn,” Winters finally broke the silence, “it’s me, Jonathan. Do you remember...?” As soon as the question was processed in her mind, Winters saw something in her eyes. Recognition? Fear? Hope? Hate? “Winters,” she greeted, seemingly mulling the name over in her head. “Yes,” Winters confirmed, excitedly. She remembers! “I know what happened. Stakiri told me everything. You have to let me help you...” Without warning, she lunged forward and struck his throat, catching his off guard. Then she whirled around on the spot, with daunting speed, launching a wild foot at his chest. It connected and Winters tumbled backward, hitting the back of his head hard, upon the cold concrete surface. She stood over a him, a satisfied smile etched into her now frozen features. “They told me you’d put up a good fight.” She shook her head, disappointed and then walked away. Winters wanted to follow her, reason with her, make her remember...but he couldn’t move and soon enough, he lost consciousness.
