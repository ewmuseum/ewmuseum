IG 73 - Open Invitation - Where are they now? RP's here

25 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
His Terms of Surrender. The world is spinning. Not the 800 or so miles an hour that the earth usually rotates; this is a more localized phenomenon, a vertigo-inducing roller coaster ride of blurred imagery and streaking lights that eventually resolves itself into actual, usable footage. It's the grainy, bleached-out view of a cheap digital camcorder, with bad contrast that's almost completely washed out by the sunlight leaking through the dingy mauve curtains over our still-out-of-focus subject's left shoulder. What a subject he is, too – the silhouette is tall and wide and broad and massive and a lot of other adjectives that all add up to a beefy guy with short, shit-brown hair looking into the camera. The shot wobbles again slightly, the man snapping into focus as we zoom in unexpectedly onto thick, misshapen fingers like uneven, overstuffed sausages scratching at his craggy cheek. "Oi!" the man growls in a voice like he's chewing sandpaper, pockmarked flesh stretching taut with the word. "Willya jus' fuggin'—" Another moment and equilibrium is struck, presenting our subject from about chest up. He is the necessary link between man and our evolutionary predecessors, shoulders forward, head canted, and eyes narrowed in a way that suggests he's trying to figure the world out and hasn't quite reached a verdict yet. Longtime viewers will recognize the man immediately (many will have guessed his identity just by that mush-mouthed hodgepodge of an accent) but we've got to cater to the newbies in the audience, so let's cover the basics. As mentioned, our subject is a big boy, broad of shoulder and thick of neck, with a thick-lipped smile that he probably doesn't intend to be as intimidating as it ends up. He's not muscular in the traditional sense, not cut, but his arms are large in the fashion of someone who used to be an offensive lineman, back when both knees worked and that tiger tattooed on his right arm was the proper dimensions. The man is wearing an old, threadbare tanktop reading "Return to Purgatory"; it's moved from "seen better days" to "should have been chucked out years ago," and the only thing about him that seems in any way new is the nicotine patch affixed to his left arm that's a shade or two too light to avoid contrasting with his leathery skin. The man sits (well, slouches) uncomfortably in a discolored red leather recliner that's only slightly more than a beanbag chair, his tongue lolling in thought as he plucks at a loose thread on the armrest. A dry erase board covered in scribbles hangs on the wall over his shoulder. After an uncomfortably long moment, a sharp whip-crack of a whisper (little more than a "Hsst!") from the camera's operator attracts his attention. "Oh," he says belatedly, waving a hand distractedly through the air. "'Ey, PTC. Name's Roddy. Roddy Ashe, an' you may remember me from... well, prob'ly from absolutely fuck-all 'cept gettin' my ass kicked in, really. Used t'be I was one's the approximately forty-two hunnerd guys 'tween yer hero an' Allie Pierce. Got dumped on my skull lots – which it turns out sucks, in case you was wonderin'. But the pay was good an' I gotta hang out with some unbelievably hot chicks all the fuckin' time, so who'm I to complain, right?" There was a time that Roderick Ashe would busy himself with an ugly, chewed-up nub of a cigar, "No Smoking" signs bedamned, but that patch isn't just for show and that means he's got nothing to do with his hands, which rest sort of awkwardly in his lap. "So I bet yer wonderin'... what's that pug-ugly motherfucker's mug doin' on my screen an' why do I care? Wudn't he last seen doin' abso-goddamn-lutely nothin' of note 'fore he disappeared as good as a fat guy can? Yeah, maybe. You wanna make somethin' of it?" Ashe lurches forward, planting his forearms across his thighs. New canyons open in his rocky features, like a treasure map to disapproval. "I was never a real good, y'know, grappler. I got two bum knees an' a back what aches when the weather's gonna go bad, an' my whole damn career, I stood onna apron an' dropped fuckwads as far and as hard as I could manage. But back then, I had folks to back me up – Hawkers an' Allie an' Hunner an' the Dead Man's Hand. But you know what I got now?" He holds up two beefy, sledgehammer fists. "These. These two hands an' the most impressive track record of anybody you ain't heard'a. Four times – one, two, t'ree, four times – I wore the Enn-Dub tag belts. I still got the last ones from when the place closed in my footlocker. Trouble is, nunnya motherfuckers'll say, 'Shit, Roddy Ashe? He's the best tag guy that place ever did see.' They'll say Allie was there with her plannin'. They'll tell you that last time, I had Hunner By God Sabuani as my partner. They'll talk up pan-flashes like Erik the Wretched or the Sound an' the Puny or the wacky hijinks of Crash an' fuckin' Burn. They'll even chat up that pigfucker Loki 'fore me." He spits onto the tiled floor, staring down at the loogie. "When folks think'a Roddy Ashe, it ain't fer any'a that, though. They'll talk about the last couple years, before I... before I took a whatchacall – a sabbatical." The big man leans back, yellowed teeth bared in his grin. "Yeh," he snorts. "'Sabbatical'." * * * "This's fuggin' bullshit!" It all began last November – the night Alexandra Pierce kicked Lane Stevens in the face and the Dead Man's Hand crumbled. But the GTT7 Champion and the former SCCW Universal Champion were elsewhere at this point, and Roderick Ashe paced in a loading dock outside the arena. A cigar was clamped between his lips, his eyebrows furrowed like they were holding back the tide of anger in his eyes. "We known her longer'n any'a them fuckwads. I'm s'posed t'be the goddamn security, an' she ain't think to tell me she gonna kick Lane fuckin' Stevens inna head? What the fuck, man?" One of the many shadows congregating by the entrance split off with a distinctive step-step-tap gait. Elijah Hawke had been Roderick's partner for close to a decade, well before the accident that forced him to use that cane, and he was Ashe's opposite in too many ways to count. Built with too little material, all stretched out and taut and about as wide around as Ashe's forearm, the thin man was a little hunched over, bony knuckles of both hands protruding as he gripped the head of a walking stick that was more like a walking club. "I very much doubt the Director planned to strike down Lane this evening," Hawke said, his whispery voice another marked difference between them. "It is not personal." Ashe grunted. "Feels pretty fuggin' personal, Hawkers." "In every great empire, there is one moment where the rulers take a misstep that begins the avalanche which buries them." "Yer sayin' that every Beatles have a Yoko." Hawke allowed himself a paper-thin smile. "Yes." Ashe dropped his cigar at his feet, the fat toe of a size-sixteen boot grinding it out. "So now what? Lane's gonna go fuggin' apeshit. You know he is." "Kathryn has the route she's always wanted to be the one in control, as well. If Alexandra is serious—" "It's Allie. She don't know how t'be anythin' but." "Then Jadian will chafe under her orders as well. He is likely to do something unfortunate." The big man's bucket of a head jerked in a nod. "So what do we do? Allie's been there fer us forever, but that cooze has always been a bitch." "What do you want to do, Roderick?" Ashe blinked. "Y'ain't never asked me that before." "We have never been in a circumstance like this." Hawke might as well have been a statue to pensiveness, a mannequin dressed like a Sherlock Holmes villain; he was only missing the bowler hat. "We knew that this was a tenuous balance. We knew that Lane would push her over the edge eventually. We knew that Amy would be a problem." "We did, we did." "What we do next decides which side we fight for." Step-step-tap, Elijah approached, pale yellow-orange light making him look cold and pale in the cool winter air, more corpse than man. "I am old and tired, Roderick. We are neither of us the young men we once were. Jadian and Lane are not the only ones who could take this as an opportunity to buck free and step out on our own." "True, true." Ashe folded his arms across a broad chest. "An' Amy's a bitch. But Allie's been there. If it ain't for her, I'd probably be shakin' down assholes for some scumfuck loan shark." Hawke's left eyebrow curled, just a single ripple across the featureless surface of his face. "If not for Alexandra, you would be dead. Kosovo, remember?" "Oh, yeh." One bear's paw of a hand reached up to rub the stubble on his face. "I ain't sure I got it in me to go solo, man. My knees are busted up. Doc says I oughta have surgery." "Soon we will be two men with canes." "Fuck that, I'm gettin' my ass a wheelchair. It'll hover an' shit, like Professor X. Maybe put some pop-up guns inside." He bared his teeth in a wide smile, but it was brief, and it faltered away with his sigh. "We gotta choose tonight, don't we?" The smaller man lifted a hand from the bird of prey carved into his cane, making a negligible gesture. "Emotion will fade," he said. "Alexandra will circle the wagons, just like she was trained. Lane may have been surprised by the intensity, but others will not be as—" "Roderick? Roddy, are you out there?" The trilling question drifted through the propped-open door behind them. The voice was the kind of thing you'd only expect to hear on phone sex lines and in fevered dreams; it would inspire impure thoughts even if she wasn't trying, but Kathryn Shaw was always trying. Despite the promise the voice held, a sour look creased Ashe's craggy features, his nose wrinkling and lips twisting, as if he'd bitten into a lemon. "Speak of the devil-slut," he grunted. "I ain't so good at makin' decisions, man." He scrubbed thoughtfully at his short, short hair. "Allie is. You are. Don't make me make this choice. Tell me: what do we do here?" Hawke sighed, sharp and ragged like the last gasp of a dying man. "Stay here," he says simply. "Let me see if I can delay the choice." "Awright." Ashe nodded again, pulling another coal-gray cigar from the pocket of his camouflage cargo pants. "Fuckin' chicks," he grumbled, jabbing the cheap thing into his mouth. "Make a mess outta everythin'." He looked over his shoulder, just able to make out his slip of a partner, who managed to block the whole of the door from the woman on the other side. Even the shadows were willing to play Shaw's beguiling games, offering just a glimpse of one bent leg and the winking gleam of the bracelet on her left wrist. Roderick sighed with more than a touch of wistfulness, but his teeth soon showed again in a sneer, as he realized why he could see Shaw's wrist – her hand was on Elijah's arm, and he could bet she was smiling. Roderick never lit that cigar, hand falling away to rest at his side as he glared into the shadows. Hawke only looked back briefly, his pencil-thin brows arching, but Kathryn gently nudged the thin man's face back towards her, and, in that moment, the big man's choice was made for him. Ashe's wobbling strides had more to do with his bad knees than his bad feelings – or at least that was what he told himself. He made his way to his rental, looking back at the arena as he ran his hand along his hair again. That was the last time anyone saw Roderick Ashe in a wrestling ring, and the last time any of his friends saw him for more than a year. * * * Back to now, and back to the tiny little camera. "So, yeah... I left. I wasn't gonna fight Al, wasn't gonna fight for nobody who don't like me." He lifts one broad, bulky shoulder in a half-hearted shrug. "I ain't sayin' it was some brave shit, or that I'm real goddamn proud or nothin'. It just... was. Felt good at the time. To walk away from all that bullshit, because, hey..." Ashe leans forward, resting hairy forearms on his knees. "It'd gotten kinda outta hand, y'know? This guy don' trust that guy, that guy wants to kick the face offa the next guy, nobody wants to do nothin', they're redefinin' easy words like 'compromise'. An' the worst part? All these guys were fuckin' chicks, man, an' chicks're kinda unreasonable about these things." A hand waves through the air, dismissive. "That's neither here nor there. So I cut ties. None'a them knew where I went; I didn't follow where any of them got off to. I been gone awhile, an' maybe you're wonderin' what I got to doing for near-a-year. Ain't like I ever been any good at anything what didn't involve hittin' shit with my hands or yellin' real loud, right? Not like I'm gonna sit in a damn cubicle an' crunch no numbers or do tech support or nothin'. Can you imagine me flippin' burgers? Wouldja like some fuggin' fries with that?" The big man snorts softly, wiping at his nose. "I got by." He shrugs again. "Wasn't pretty work, but it was... it was relaxin'." There's a long pause here before he reluctantly adds: "An', okay, it was real goddamn boring. That's the thing about chicks. Love 'em or hate 'em, they ain't dull." * * * One Month Ago – Dallas, Texas The bar exploded with joy as the ball leapt off Jorge Cantú's bat that Saturday afternoon. The home run put the Texas Rangers up 4-3 in Oakland, putting them on course to win the American League's Western Division. Roderick Ashe didn't care so much about that. What he did care about was the mess these sons of bitches were making – a mess that he'd have to clean up before the college-aged hoochies dragged the slobbering rednecks out for an evening of shitty music and fruity drinks. Beer foamed and splattered on the ground, a bowl of pretzels flipped over, and when the final out is scored a little later, a particularly effusive fan will tip over his chair and the thing will break. Right then, he sat at the back of the bar, a pair of too-small-for-his-head glasses over his eyes, his beady little pig eyes and one finger following the text on a well-worn paperback ("Moonraker" by Ian Fleming, if it matters). The soft, imperious sound of a throat clearing was meant to attract his attention; it failed. The would-be interruption had to resort to actual words. "Excuse me," he said. "Man, can't you read the fuggin' sign?" Ashe didn't look up. "I'm on break. Go Rangers, hooray Spic-soundin' guy's name, go have a beer. They're t'ree-fiddy." "I was actually hoping for a moment of your time." Roderick sighed, dog-earing the corner of his book. He made a show of setting the paperback on the bar, kicking his feet down from their perch. In the past, this might have sent a nuisance running for the hills, but when Ashe looked up, the other man was still in place, his hands neatly folded on the bartop, his weatherman hair perfectly in place and his plastic used car salesman's smile unflinching. In short, he was just the kind of man Roderick would have liked to pitch into the middle of next week. "What?" Ashe grunted. "Roderick Ashe?" the man asked, his eyes sparkling and smile stretching, as if he already knew the answer. "Sorry, man." Roderick shook his head. "I ain't ever heard the name." "Ah, I see." The man leaned on the last word; his eyebrows came up and then down. "I didn't realize you were—" "I ain't nothin', man. My name's Jake, an' I serve beer to alcoholics." Ashe pulled himself up to the zenith of his six-foot-eight frame. "I also do some bouncin'. Mostly folk who disturb kindly patrons just here to enjoy themselves." The mannequin man stepped back, his hands lifted submissively. "I'm not here for a fight, Mr. As—ah, Jake." "Then why the fuck—" "I represent interests who would like to see you – er, a gentleman who looks something like you, rather – back in the game." "Sometimes the game passes you by." Ashe picked up a somewhat soiled white rag, bussing the bartop idly. "Sometimes the game won't let you go." "Can't help you, man." "I think you should reconsider." The man's smile stretched just a little more, as if his face were going to split open like a Pez dispenser. "If I tracked you down, I'm sure other people may be able to as well. People less likely to want to chat." He cast his eyes around the bar. "People who would be less likely to use discretion in their hunt." Ashe lifted just his eyes to the man, brows pushed together like two caterpillars in a duel. "I said I dunno what you're talkin' 'bout." "You did, yes." The man nodded again, reaching inside his blazer with just two fingers to retrieve a thick, high-quality business card from a pocket. "Well, if you run into Mr. Ashe, please pass him my business card?" "Yeah, sure." Roderick scrubbed the bartop again, harder this time, working at a coffee cup ring he knew wouldn't come out. "I can do that." The man – he never gave his name – touched the bar gently, shaking his head. He whistled as he left, Ashe's eyes following him the whole way. The first thing the big man did once he was sure the man was gone was tear that business card into four uneven pieces. A small microchip was buried in the paper; he made sure to smash that thing flat. The second thing Ashe did was quit his job. The third was to buy a camera. * * * Now. "An' so here I am." Ashe's wide face fills the screen, half his grin in place. "Lookin' for work wrestlin' an' shit. Again. Not 'cause I need the dough, not because I figure I can beat guys or be a champ no more – my best days're behind me, no doubt. But man's gotta do somethin' in order to keep from gettin' bored. I'm an old man, broke-down an' tired. But I figger you can find a spot for me somewhere. In some piss-ant fed, in one'a yer clusterfuck matches for some belt nobody gives two shits about. Somethin' like that, yeah? I got name value. I got a filthy fuckin' mouth an' a bad attitude." Roderick's paw reaches over the camera, clasping it in one hand as he pulls himself upright. "But you can find somethin' for me, maybe. Someplace where this videotape gets out there inna press. Create some buzz. I may not be able to beat guys, but I can make the kids look good, an' I can make the old fucks look like they can still go. Maybe sometime I can win a belt. Maybe not. That ain't important. What's important is that this tape gets out there. That folk know Roddy Ashe wants to wrestle again, just one more time." The zoom-in that follows is, in a word, uncomfortably close. You can count the hairs extending from Ashe's nostrils. "That's all I ask. Get the vid out. If you wanna make some cash too, great. If I gotta beat up some kid still wet behind the ears, I'll do it. If I gotta put an' old man in a grave, I'll do that, too." He tries to smile; it is not an entirely successful effort. There's a gap between his teeth, and there's something in it. "'Cause my name's Roddy Ashe, an' I approve this message." A moment of fumbling follows before he finds the off switch; the last thing we catch is that gravelly voice, muttering under his breath. "Fuckin' better work," he says. * * * In truth, being found almost a year later wouldn't have worried Mr. Ashe. He'd just disappear again – the training that Ashe and Elijah Hawke and Alexandra Pierce had gone through before they'd ever become professional wrestlers would have helped him out there. But all evidence to the contrary, Roderick Ashe wasn't stupid, and he wasn't telling the whole truth. The truth was, he heard what had happened to Alexandra Pierce. The truth was, he heard what happened with the Order of St. Julian, about the explosions, the attacks. He'd heard about the coup. So when some guy showed up, tracked him down in a bar in the Dallas suburbs, and gave him a marked business card... Ashe knew that the world he'd run from wanted him back. He didn't have any way to contact his old friends. As near as he could tell, Elijah had disappeared soon after Ashe did – he'd heard rumors, rumblings of a thin scarecrow of a man who existed on the fringes of the fringes they lived on. Those same rumors said that Alex had sold out, that she was a fed now, that she was working for the man to bring down the world she'd helped to build. None of the old dead drops worked. None of the meeting places were still being used. His calls reached dead numbers. His emails weren't returned – many of them bounced. But the one place that he knew he could reach them, the one way he knew he could get a message across was through wrestling. Nobody knew why wrestling mattered to Alex – why it mattered to any of them – but it did, and he had to hope that one of them would see his tape, would find the message it hid. If the world wanted him back, Roderick Ashe would have it be on his terms.
