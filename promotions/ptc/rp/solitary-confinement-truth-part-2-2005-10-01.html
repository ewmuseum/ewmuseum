Solitary Confinement (Truth part 2)

1 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-10-01 Excerpt from the journal of Lance Sullivan: …Honestly I really like Mike. He’s got his shit together in ways I can’t even imagine, plus he’s great for Sheena, even a bum like me can see that. I give the guy a really hard time, I probably shouldn’t, but I can’t help it. I look up to the guy, and I can’t think of another way to really show that, being a smart ass is the only thing I know. The thing is no matter how much I fuck up, I know he’s going to be there for me, it’s just the way he is. Sure he talks a big game about being an asshole, but anyone can see he’s got a heart of gold. And I don‘t say that just because he‘s my sister‘s in love with him, I know. The funniest thing is, he inspired me before he even knew who the hell I was… There weren’t very many people at the funeral. It seemed like there should have been more, everyone deserved a good send off. As it stood the only people Stevens recognized, were Sheena, and Alex. Cindy and her husband had been unable to attend thanks to prior obligations, however they’d sent their love. Sheena wouldn’t come near him. After she’d shown him Lance’s journal, the two had separated rather quickly, both pleading they needed time to think. What he’d read had sent Stevens into something of a tailspin, it was bad enough knowing you’d killed someone, to suddenly find out he admired you, that just made everything worse. He stood near the back, gazing out over the assembled crowd. Not for the first time he shook his head, there just should have been more people here damn it. He could count their number on two hands, if he didn’t include himself, or the priest, it’d only take one. Sheena, and Alex stood together, the big man had his arm draped over her shoulder, as she quietly cried. That hurt, he should have been the one up there soothing the ache in her heart, but that wouldn’t happen, not anymore. He was almost certain he’d never feel her touch again, that stung just a bit too. On the other side of Sheena, were two men. One an older fellow, balding, he’d forgone the traditional dark suit, in favor of jeans with a black sports coat. On his right directly beside Sheena younger man, long black hair tied in a pony tail. Stevens hadn’t seen his face, but there was something about his posture, something about the way he’d lean in and speak quietly to Sheena, Stevens just couldn’t place what it was. A familiarity maybe. The priest continued speaking, but Stevens couldn’t hear a word he said. All he could hear was Lance’s last sounds, screaming, pleading, crying for him to stop. At the time Stevens hadn’t been able to hear, a crimson vale over his eyes, the blood pounded too loudly in his ears. Yet now, he could hear it clearly as a bell, it had somehow managed to find it’s way into his brain and lodge itself there. Something told him that for the rest of his life, he’d never stop hearing that voice. Lance’s ghost would forever haunt him. It wasn’t just Lance’s voice either, the line about inspiration still bugged him. What the hell could Lance have possibly meant by that? He’d never met the kid before that day in the New York jail. At first he’d rationalized it, figuring maybe the kid had seen him on TV or something, that would’ve made sense. Of course he’d only recently started playing a character that anyone might term inspirational, so that didn’t really fit either. There must have been something else, something he was missing. Just then the younger man with the black hair, turned, glancing towards Stevens. The face struck him like a kick to the stomach. He felt weak in the knees and longed to sit, but their wasn’t a chair available. If it weren’t for the black hair, he’d swear he was looking at a ghost. The face wasn’t Lance Sullivan’s but it was exactly the same, and just like that, he remembered, clear as a bell, just like yesterday… ************************************************************************************* August 27th 2001 The bar was filled the stench of the great unwashed. This wasn’t exactly the type of place you went for the smell, you went for the scenery. The sign on the outside of the place had promised live nudes, which was a good thing, considering no one really wanted to see dead nudes., well at least he didn’t anyway. The drink in front of him was whiskey, not really good whiskey but it was the best you could expect in a place like this. Jack Daniels, extra ice even though he’d asked for it neat. The thing was in a place like this you drank more if the drinks were watered down, at least that was the logic behind giving someone a drink they didn’t order. The women looked alright, but they’d probably look a hell of a lot better if he could actually catch a buzz. It just wasn’t meant to be he supposed, but that was alright he felt good anyway. He’d finally broken it off with that clingy bitch Sheena. Sure it was fun for a while to have a bisexual nymphomaniac as your fuck buddy, but it got old really quick. Especially when you had to deal with her asinine ramblings, he could swear he’d met boxes of rocks with more brains. Thus far none of the dancers had tried to hustle him for a drink or a dance. He wouldn’t have objected really if they would’ve, but something seemed to be keeping them away. Maybe he should have chosen his attire a little better before heading into the place. When you go into a bar like this, money talks, and if you don’t look like you talk, you’re shit out of luck. It really didn’t matter that he could buy or sell half the people in the bar, he looked like some young punk kid, and that was enough for the dancers to give him a wide berth. Oh well, eventually one of them would get desperate and have a seat, then he’d make her the envy of the rest of these scheming bitches. The thought made him smile. Glancing around at the other patrons he silently gloated over his own superiority. Just twenty-one, all the money in the world, and a future looking brighter by the second. He’d retired from the ring, who the hell needed the hassle of getting your ass kicked night in, and night out anyway? He’d probably miss the action, but certainly he’d come up with something to entertain himself. Maybe actually take an active interest in running his company. Dad would probably appreciate that, but screw the old man anyway, filthy geezer never really did anything for him anyway. He’d probably just end up spending months on end at the bed, doing body shots off his fuck of the day. Flash the green, and the women open their legs without a second thought. Lifting the glass to his lips he toasted himself, even if no one else would. Life was good, and it’d just keep getting better. Now if he could only get another drink, and a stripper, he’d be set for a while. The waitress finally arrived, already bearing another drink, must’ve read his mind. As she set it down, she smiled provocatively at him. Well, he wanted a stripper, but a waitress would do in a pinch. Quirking his lips into a half smile he slid her a hundred. “Keep the change, and keep em coming.” Her eyes grew wide as she looked down at the money, and then up again at Stevens. She scanned him up and down, obviously trying to figure him out. Sliding the money from the table into her apron, she winked at him, smiling even broader than before. Her eyes no longer held just a hint of what could be forthcoming, they now held promise. “Sure thing sweetie, anything you want.” Anything, interesting proposition. While a thousand thoughts ran through his mind, there was only one thing in the world that would really put a smile on his face at the moment. The music was really starting to give him a headache, he couldn’t take much more of this hip-hop shit. “Tell that pussy DJ to throw on a Sex Pistols album, and find a friend for later tonight then.” She was clearly taken aback by the response. In her line of work she probably got all manner of lewd propositions, but obviously no one had put it so bluntly before. Her expression shifted first to disgust then back to the professional detachment she’d originally viewed him with. Obviously she wanted to be out of his presence as quickly as possible. “I’ll see what I can do.” Those last words mumbled as she hurried away. Frigid bitch, her loss. Smiling broadly at her retreating figure, Stevens lifted the drink, and threw it back. He’d lost his appetite for this place, it was time to move on to something bigger and better. He pushed away from the table, and stood, about to head towards the door. Two kids stood there, in an argument for the door man. They clearly weren’t old enough to be in a place like this, but it didn’t like that’d stopped them before. Both were clad all in black, matching hair flowed over their shoulders. They were slightly built, but both looked like they could hold their own in a fight. Stevens chuckled as he started walking towards them. Maybe he could have some fun here over all. Usually, he’d be the last person to stand up for a couple gothic rejects, but since he’d quit the ring he was feeling the itch for a good fight. The bouncers didn’t look too tough either, he could take out at least two or three himself. The other two guys, well if they could just handle one between them, it’d be a fair fight. As he came up behind the twins, he threw arms around each of their shoulders. Randomly he decided on a Cockney accent, just for giggles. “Oi mate! What’s your problem with me boyo’s here? They’re with me.” All three assembled looked stunned. However, the twins at least seemed smart enough to keep their traps shut, they probably figured if nothing else, Stevens would be able to get them into the bar. Poor patsies couldn’t have known that was the furthest thing from his mind. The doorman didn’t seemed pleased, maybe he’d seen the little incident with the waitress. Maybe he just didn’t like young punk kids trying to act like big shots in his bar. Either way he wasn’t happy, but he was trained well enough not to force a confrontation immediately. “This has nothing to do with you kid, just go back to your drink. These two were just leaving, WITHOUT their fake ids?” “Fakes? You’ve got it wrong mate, I’ve known Stinky Pete, and Skip here for years. Grew up with em. I said their with me, you gonna throw me out too?” He’d thrown out the bait, and he was banking on the bouncer biting. Stevens had always been great at goading someone into a fight, he considered it one of his best qualities, right behind his perfect body, and his ability to knock back four bottles of whiskey without puking. The doorman stepped out from behind the counter cracking his knuckles, and the smile on Michael’s face took on an almost animalistic quality. As for the kids it looked like they just wanted to get the hell out of there, but Stevens wasn’t ready to give up his back up yet, and he clamped down tight on their shoulders. He never noticed the bouncer coming up behind him. Maybe the drinks had actually taken their toll, slowed him down a bit. Either way he groaned in pain as the other bouncer pulled his arm off the twin to the right, and twisted it behind his back into a hammer lock. In the ring they don’t crank in this hard, nor do they normally send in a real knee shot into the kidney’s. That hurt a little more than it should have, and before he realized what was happening he was being propelled towards the door. His skull met it with a crack, pushing it open, and before he could say another word, or even think clearly he was rolling out onto the sidewalk. Furious, Stevens was back up to his feet. His knees and palms burned where they’d scrapped against the pavement, but that didn’t matter, he’d kill every single one of them. He took one step forward before realizing it was a fight that he couldn’t win. The two kids were being shoved out right behind him, by the five other bouncers he hadn’t noticed. Nine against three weren’t odds he was really to bet on, and the insult wasn’t quite enough to make him lose all reason. “FUCK YOU FUCKING MOTHERFUCKERS!” Short, articulate, to the point. He’d always been proud of his ability to continue to talk good trash in a tense moment. The bouncers didn’t seem impressed, as they laughed while allowing the door to swing shut. Just to add further insult to injury, he heard the open chords to “God Save The Queen” being blasting out the place. Spitting at the door, he rotated his shoulder trying to work the kinks out from when the bouncer had wrenched it almost out of it’s socket. Lighting himself up a cigarette he glanced up to see both of the twins staring at him in awe. They’d talked a big game to the bouncer at first, but when everything had gone down, they’d frozen and become useless. He turned to walk away, but he stopped after a step. He was always picking up strays, as he turned back he sighed. “I’m grabbing a cup of coffee, you two coming?” And just like that they followed him. Their names were Lance, and Jeff, and they were trying to get into the business. After everything had calmed down they recognized him, much to his chagrin. He hadn’t wanted to spend the night talking about wrestling but he had. They talked about some guy they’d met on the indie circuit, Buck something, a veteran he’d promised to train them. The twins wanted to know if it would be worth their time. He’d told them no the business was shit, and it wasn’t worth their time. When they parted ways he hoped they’d listen… ************************************************************************************* “STEVENS!” He hadn’t noticed that the priest had finished. Sheena and Alex were already out of sight, carrying Lance’s ashes away. Sheena had decided to cremate him, and spread his ashes in the Pacific. He’d rented them a boat for the day, it was the least he could do. Jeff stood before him, the older guy hanging back a few steps. The kid hadn’t changed much, same hair, same outfit, same face. His eyes were filled with hatred though, his lips twisted into a vicious scowl. When the spittle hit his face it was hot, then immediately became cold. He’d rather be punched in the face than spit on, it was the ultimate sign of disrespect. Normally it would’ve set him off, but right now, he felt like he deserved that and worse. Slowly lifting his hand he wiped it away, staring at Jeff. He longed to apologize, but the words wouldn’t come. He took a guess from the build of the older guy behind him, that wasn’t Jeff’s father. Maybe it was that veteran they said had agreed to train them. Come to think of it, he remembered hearing about a Jeff Sullivan wrestling in a tag team with some guy named Buck. Had to be it, everything fit. “Don’t you have anything to say for yourself you stupid piece of shit?” The kid still talked a good guy, although now it looked like he’d be more than willing to back it up. The last thing Stevens wanted right now was a fight, especially not here, especially not with that face. He wouldn’t take the bait, and nothing could make him. Still the kid deserved a response of some kind, but he wasn’t really sure what he could say. “No, no I don’t. Except sorry.” “It’s all your fucking fault, you’re fucking responsible for this.” His voice was deadly quiet, did he know? Had Sheena told him what really happened to Lance? He wouldn’t blame her, Lance was his brother too after all. There wasn’t anything more that he could say, nothing would make it better, nothing could ease the hurt this poor kid must be feeling. So he said the only thing he could think of, again. “I’m sorry.” “Don’t give me that shit, if it hadn’t been for your little ‘pep talk’ none of this would have ever happened. Lance woulda followed his dreams with me, and he’d still be alive. You told him the business wasn’t worth it, and he gave up. Ever since he tried to fill the hole with drugs, you fucking killed him you bastard.” He didn’t know, Sheena had kept the secret. That was a bit of a relief, but he still had to think of something to say to this kid. It wasn’t easy, his own guilt just kept getting worse. He hadn’t thought about the consequences of his actions much back in those days. He’d said what he wanted, and did what he wanted, when he wanted. There was no real future, just the present and a vague idea of good things to come. And now he’d realized he might’ve been able to avoid this entire mess if he hadn’t been looking for a fight. Apparently it’s true what they say, the past really can come back to haunt you. “Listen, I’m sorry about your brother. There isn’t anything more than I can say, except I was young then, and stupid, I’m sorry.” Jeff took a step forward, hands clinching into fists. He was ready to fight, and Stevens couldn’t blame him for it. He could only feel a shadow of the pain this kid must be feeling, and right in front of him was someone that is easy to blame all of it on. The saddest part was the kid didn’t even know how responsible Stevens was. The old guy was quick to action, grasping Jeff by the shoulders, and pulling him back. “Not here kid, not now. Look at his eyes, he knows, he’s suffering too.” The old man’s words just made him feel worse. And so did the weak smile he flashed Stevens. Jeff looked towards the man who was obviously his mentor, and his face softened. The anger was replaced almost immediately with grief. The older man squeezed his shoulders, and began leading him away, but not before flashing Stevens one more look and mouthing “I’m sorry.” They took a couple steps before Jeff turned back. His face as mask of pain, he looked on the verge of tears. “You’re responsible.” He couldn’t go to the spreading of the ashes, not now. He didn’t know if he’d ever see, or speak to any of them again, Jeff, Sheena, or Alex. There were too many bad memories, too much pain between them now. He was a guilty man, a monster. He wouldn’t be locked up, but his own mind would be prison enough, and he’d face his sentence alone. Turning he began walking away from the place. He couldn’t stay any longer. As he walked away, he heard Lance’s cries echoing in his ears, but now a new voiced joined it, accusing, “you’re responsible”. “More than you know kid, more than you know.” ~Fin~
