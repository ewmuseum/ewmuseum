Silence before the Storm

7 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-05-07 Silence Before The Storm RP#1 for Syko Arsonist The time had come. For many things, actually. Primetime Central had a dream match on its hands, two actually, but the first was more significant to the interfederational organization’s history. Seymour Almasy would fight Syko Arsonist. The last two men to win PTC events, the PIT and No Limits respectively, would engage in battle. It was a battle Seymour had been looking forward to for some time. But while Seymour’s mind was focused on the Bracket Two Champion, his wife was focused on destroying Seymour’s focus. She couldn’t keep her secret anymore. She needed to tell Seymour who he was, why he was. And so, with Seymour on the verge of one of the most important matches of his life, he is about to come face to face with reality. The reality that he has been sheltered from for most of his adult life. -------- For perhaps the first time in his entire life, Seymour is not in his home, nor is he in any exotic location. Nor, for that matter, is he on an elaborate and melodramatic quest. Instead, he is in a gym, training. Not with a sword, as he usually does, however, but training in the workout sense. Lifting barbells. Running on treadmills. Jumping rope. Getting himself into peak physical condition for the fight of his life. Sitting in a chair watching him is a somewhat awed Aerith, herself panting from an earlier stay on a rowing device. Aerith Heartilly: Wow, Seymour…I’ve never seen you this, well, dedicated before. Seymour nods to her, but does not speak, instead conserving his wind as he rattles off ten more jumps of the rope, finishing his set of 250, before finally collapsing into a chair next to Aerith, slowly regaining his breath enough to answer her. Seymour Almasy: This is a really, REALLY important contest. As such, I must prepare for it with all my being. He smiles, and Aerith smiles back. Due to the environment, Seymour is not clad in his usual elaborate armor. Rather, the Final Fantasy sports a black FFVII t-shirt, and a simple pair of sweatpants. Aerith couldn’t help think of how normal he looked. Despite the white hair and RPG obsession she doubted he’d ever give up, he’d be able to at least pass for a normal person…that is, once her secret was told, and Seymour got the counseling he’d most likely need. Aerith Heartilly: I understand, Seymour, but why no armor? I’ve never seen you out of it before. Seymour Almasy: Oh, come now Aerith. That’s simply not true. You see me out of my armor every single night… The words made Aerith giggle and Seymour blush furiously. The blushing only made Aerith giggle more. It boggled her mind sometimes that after nearly a year now of being sexually active with her husband, he still retained his aura of innocence. She liked to think of it as the teenage Brian Wilson’s influence on the mind of the adult Seymour. For now, though, she had to respond to Seymour, lest he continue blushing. Aerith Heartilly: Seymour! I MEANT in a competitive capacity. Seymour Almasy: Oh, of course…heh, you know, I really don’t know why I wear it. It’s not metal, and it’s not especially protective…but it’s sort of comfort at this point. A constant that I can count on. It’s strange, but I also find it indicative of my style. Aerith Heartilly: Your style? Seymour Almasy: Yes, Aerith. My style’s quite different from most others in the Battle Arena. I never seem to dominate contests. Look at me, Aerith. Aerith Heartilly: Yes? Seymour Almasy: I’m five foot seven, one hundred and eighty one pounds after a nice meal. You’re taller than I am. But for the most part, everyone I step into the arena with is guys larger than me. I defended the Global Title against Dave Harley and Angelus Sorrow. Two guys who weighed over three hundred pounds. There’s no way I could dominate those battles, they threw me around like rag dolls. Even against smaller people, people like Chris Storm, Roulette, Kenny Mercury…they are all much better trained than I am. And they prove it day in and day out. My success in the arena has depended on my ability to withstand the abuse, take everything they can dish out, all the while slipping in quick flurries, quick spurts of offense. And then, when their guard finally falls, I hit the one move, apply the one hold that leads me to victory. Aerith nods along with him, following his logic, but she is still confused. She still has one more question. Aerith Heartilly: Why though have you never tried to dominate? Seymour Almasy: I did try. Once. Aerith Heartilly: When was that, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: In the DWO against Hecate…Chryste. It was an even contest…but in trying to dominate, I made an error, and she defeated me as I did so many others. She capitalized once I faltered and beat me, because she was more experienced. If I had the opportunity to gain that experience, I would feel more comfortable, but it would kind of be silly at this point. Aerith Heartilly: Hmm, you know, in that sense, you sort of remind me of Karina. Seymour Almasy: Except Karina usually doesn’t have a problem taking control of a match… He shook his head. It was impossible for him to not worry about the Ethan Knight versus Karina Wolfenden match. If he managed to beat Syko, Knight or Wolfenden would be looming on the horizon… Seymour Almasy: …But for now I cannot worry about that. Syko Arsonist must be my sole focus. The jump rope in Seymour’s hand was cast to the floor of the gym, and he brushed a hand through his long hair, pushing strands out of his face. Seymour Almasy: You finished, Aerith? Aerith Heartilly: Finished with what? The sudden change of topic had caught Aerith off guard. Seymour looked at her, and she blushed despite herself at the intensity of his gaze. Seymour Almasy: Your workout, sweetie. Aerith Heartilly: Oh! Yes, yes I am… She had completely forgotten why she was here during the course of the discussion, and shook her head slightly at the lapse. Ah well, time to regain control… Aerith Heartilly: So let’s go home and clean up. Seymour Almasy: Mmm, fine by me! They rose, each knowing full well exactly what Aerith had in mind. They had specifically acquired memberships to this gym due to its proximity to their apartment, a mere block and a half away. Once they returned, Aerith would draw the water for their bath, and…bliss. The thought made Seymour practically squirm with anticipation. Innocent or not, he loved his wife and her nearly nymphomanical desires. Hand in hand, they strolled out of the gym, and began the short march home. Aerith Heartilly: Are you worried, Seymour? Seymour Almasy: No, I’m not. I’m entirely confident I can defeat Syko, and weather the storm of anything he can throw at me. And do you know why? Aerith Heartilly: Because you have heart and determination? Seymour Almasy: Well, I guess that, but it’s also because of my materia! The Final Fantasy’s free hand shoots into the pocket of his sweatpants, and comes out with a green marble. He raises it in the air, a proud smile on his face. Seymour Almasy: My Barrier materia allows me to cast Wall, a spell which puts Barrier and Mbarrier on me! I thus take half damage from all physical and magical attacks! This shall render Syko less able to cause damage to me. Aerith could only sadly shake her head. It was almost infuriating. Seymour regularly alternated between moments in which he seemed as normal as anyone, only to revert in the next moment to seemingly being in a different world. She couldn’t understand it, but at the same time, it made her pity him, and pity the fact that by the end of the day, his understanding of the world would be shattered. Aerith Heartilly: Seymour, are you sure your spells will work? Seymour Almasy: Positive! It was the purest form of the placebo effect. Seymour believed in his heart that the marble gave him magical powers, and that belief made him feel the effect. Aerith didn’t doubt that in the Battle Arena, he DID feel as if he had been magically bolstered. Aerith Heartilly: Okay, Seymour, as long as you’re sure… Seymour looked at her oddly for a second, but his expression quickly softened. After all, this was not the first time she had questioned his methods. She didn’t seem to have any materia of her own, but then, she didn’t fight in the Arena, so she had no use for them. Aerith smiled back at him, and calmly continued. Aerith Heartilly: Why do you like this match so much? When you found out Syko beat Carys you were practically jumping up and down in our living room. Seymour sighed, knowing that Aerith was pretty much right on the money, as usual. Seymour Almasy: It really didn’t matter who won, actually. Each opponent would have held great significance to me. Had Carys won… Aerith Heartilly: You would have got to face Chryste’s former partner, her apprentice of sorts. Seymour Almasy: Yes, that’s it. But with Syko, the motivation is far less personal and far more professional. He’s the last man to win a major event other than me…the No Limits Rumble. He knows what it is like to triumph over a large number of other competitors. Indeed, that makes our match most unique. For Arsonist and myself, this is old hat. The question remains though, which one of us is better at it? Which one of us has enough left to overcome the other? The walk was near its end, as Seymour and Aerith walked up the front steps to the apartment building, and calmly entered the elevator. Aerith pushed the button for the top floor, and the elevator quickly began to rise. Aerith Heartilly: You do, Seymour. I know in my heart that you do. Seymour Almasy: Aww, thank you, Aerith. No sooner than the two meet in an embrace do the doors of the elevator open. Reluctantly, they separate, and begin the short walk to their door. Seymour Almasy: Your confidence in me gives me strength. It is what keeps me going when all seems lost. Aerith Heartilly: And it is your faith in me, your love for me that keeps me going when I am sad. You make me so happy, Seymour… It was truer than Seymour ever could have imagined at the moment. Ever since she had been a pigtailed kindergartener, she had been attracted to him, at first on the level of friend, and then romantically. She never could have dreamed that she would end up married to him, just as she never dreamed everything else that happened. That Seymour had forgotten virtually his entire youth. That she would leave college in order to take care of him. It was all surreal, like a fairy tale…no wonder Seymour thought life was like a video game. His certainly had been. Seymour Almasy: As do you, Aerith. She reached into her pocket, and quickly pulled out the keys. Key slid into lock, and the door was opened. The familiar expanse of the apartment stood before Seymour and Aerith, but for the latter it was much less familiar than normal. Seymour saw it as he always did, the place where he lived with Aerith in happiness. For Aerith, though, it would soon be forever known as the place of confession, of realization, and potentially, the place where she tore her husband’s heart out. For now though, she had other intentions in mind. Aerith Heartilly: I’ll go start the water! She took off, practically running for the bathroom to get the water nice and hot for their bath. This left Seymour alone, and he collapsed to the living room couch, exhausted from a day of training. Seymour Almasy: Ick…this is going to be a very long week. So much more to do, so much more to prepare. So many more hours I must spend in preparation of this contest… The Final Fantasy’s body was already aching, already pleading with him to quit with the torture. He had put himself through a day of hell, violating virtually every rule of working out and working both upper and lower body in a torturously long session. He was amazed inwardly that he had managed to walk back, and do so as calmly as he did, with no complaining. Seymour Almasy: But this is the path that I have chosen. Victory over Arsonist will require dedication. It will require me pushing myself to limits I have not yet attained, to do things I had once thought to be impossible. Almasy shakes his head, as he realizes Aerith isn’t around, and that the only person hearing his noble words is himself. Seymour Almasy: That does it. I’m talking to myself…I really am obsessed with this. Obsessed with being atop Mount Gagazet, just one more time. For once I am there, my quest may end. The Final Fantasy series was full of metaphors to that effect. In Final Fantasy VII, the Northern Crater laid just beyond the Great Glacier. In Final Fantasy X, Mount Gagazet was the final obstacle before Zanarkand, the end of a commoner’s quest. For Seymour, Syko Arsonist was that final obstacle, the final level before the end boss. A final obstacle that would sternly test the Final Fantasy. Aerith Heartilly: Seymour! The bath is ready! The call snapped Seymour out of his deep thought, and allowed Almasy to fill his mind with much happier thoughts. He summoned a great deal of effort, and managed to coax his tired muscles to allow him to stand and slowly amble over to the bathroom. The door was wide open, and Aerith’s clothing sat in a pile on the tile floor. It didn’t take Seymour’s eyes long to move upwards and find Aerith lying in the oversized tub. Most of her body save for her head was rendered invisible by a large amount of bubbles, but Seymour knew full well what awaited him beneath the frothy bath. Aerith Heartilly: Light the candles and turn the lights out, won’t you sweetie? Seymour turned, and saw the candles set up throughout the room, the white wax standing in sharp contrast to the black marble tile of the bathroom. A set of matches rested on the sink, and Seymour used them to light the candles, and then turn off the bathroom light. Since there were no windows, the room instantly became dark. Aerith Heartilly: Ready for me, sweetie? The Final Fantasy could not see his wife, but judging by the cute giggle that followed from her, he surmised that she was probably smirking. Almasy’s clothes were rapidly discarded, and Seymour slowly stepped into the tub, and gently lay down next to his wife. Her arms wrapped around him, as his did around hers, and all thoughts of Syko Arsonist and the Cruiserweight Tournament were forgotten. Seymour Almasy: Mmmm…can we please stay like this forever? If only it were possible, Aerith would have been more than willing. Eating and sleeping were such unnecessary things. To be here, holding Seymour against her, with the warm water surrounding them both…such things were meant for dreams and romantic poetry, not reality. But here they were. Aerith Heartilly: As much as I’d love to, forever is not an option. Eternity is not in the grasp of people. But for as long as we can, I will stay here with you, Seymour. They cuddled even closer, Seymour’s hand gently running through his wife’s long, wet hair, as her hands did the same. Their mouths found each other soon after, gently exploring, gently reassuring one another of their love and support. It was wonderful, a perfect moment between two lovers. Even the dread and foreboding that Aerith had about having to confess the truth to Seymour had disappeared, instead replaced by a lighthearted feeling, mixed with more than a bit of both love and lust. She wanted to take him, climb on top of him in the water and make him hers once more, but she was able to resist her more carnal impulses. This was a moment too amazing to ruin. Seymour Almasy: I know, Aerith. Forever is not something we have on this world. We are born, we live, and we die. This is our life. Not my life, or your life…for our lives are intertwined. I’m yours, Aerith. Yours to do with as you see fit. At that, she couldn’t help but roll on top of him, teasingly brushing her hands over his neck, his chest, making him gasp out slightly. The usual feelings of love and power coursed through Aerith. She knew she could have Seymour if she wanted him. All she would have to do was ask, and he would submit as he always did…or perhaps take control if that was what she wanted. Aerith Heartilly: Mmmm… I love you. Seymour Almasy: I love you too, Aerith. Now, and forever. Everything was wonderful. Perfect. And yet, the nagging worries returned. She couldn’t keep it inside anymore. She had to tell Seymour the truth about his life, for both his own sake and hers as well. As much as Seymour needed to know…she needed to tell him. She felt as if she was lying to him by not telling him. It was an emotion that she could not bear. Aerith had promised herself long ago that she would always be honest with Seymour. And now, she had to fulfill that promise. Aerith Heartilly: Seymour… … But first, she needed some time alone. Aerith Heartilly: Would you mind going to our bedroom and drying off? I need a bit of time to myself. The emotional Aerith had done a good job of preventing her voice from cracking. Had the lighting been better, though, Seymour could have seen her quivering mouth, and the tear that had just begun to form in her right eye. As it stood though, Seymour could see neither, and he thus obeyed without protest. Seymour Almasy: Of course, Aerith. As Seymour stood, a towel on the rack next to the tub was quickly grabbed. Almasy wrapped it around his waist, and vacated the bathroom, closing the door behind him. Aerith was left to her thoughts, as Almasy dried himself off, and took a seat on the bed he shared with Aerith, towel once more wrapped around his waist. Seymour Almasy: This is going to be a battle for the ages… The moment Almasy left the healing arms of his wife, his own worries came back to him. Arsonist had been in the Primetime Invitational Tournament as Seymour had, but Syko had lost to Roulette amongst great intrigue. Roulette, otherwise known as Kayna Trielle, had then made it to the finals where Seymour defeated her. Seymour Almasy: I defeated Roulette. Roulette defeated Arsonist. If only this were mathematics, I would be proven superior to Arsonist by a simple property. But the property of transitivity did not hold true in wrestling, for competitors were much more complex than numbers. What made one competitor able to defeat another made him vulnerable to a third. Seymour Almasy: Such is not the way it works, of course. Syko Arsonist was nearly killed en route to defeating Roulette. He managed to survive a demented woman and her casino. Compared to a plot on one’s life, what is a battle in the Arena? Next to nothing. He has suffered greatly in his life, but he keeps coming. No matter how emotionally scarred, every single time he steps into the Arena, he gives it his all. That is the man who I am facing… He sighed audibly, and collapsed back to the bed, stretched out, and waited for his wife to emerge… ---- Meanwhile, back in the bathroom, Aerith still sat in the bathtub. Several of the candles had been extinguished, leaving four still lit. Aerith Heartilly: I have to do this. I have to. She gently blew out one of the remaining candles, and watched the flame flicker and die with a strange sense of fascination. Aerith Heartilly: I wish there was another option, but there simply is not. Seymour deserves to know. The second candle was put out in the same manner as the first. Two remained, as Aerith was beyond thought at this point. The decision had been made. It was just a matter of saying the words now. Aerith Heartilly: My silence has to stop. On the last syllable, the penultimate flame contorted under Aerith’s breath, before finally going out. Aerith shivered. It was her greatest fear. She could only hope that her words would not do the same to Seymour, wouldn’t extinguish the person that she loved. Aerith Heartilly: And it must stop now. She blew out the final candle, and all went black. (To be continued)
