I'll Just Wander My Own Road

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 I ain’t never comin’ home I’ll just wander my own road I can’t meet you here tomorrow Say goodbye, don’t follow misery so hollow You can’t shake me ‘round, now I get so lost and don’t know how- It hurts to care, I know now. … lost my friends, things I’ve done, and where I’ve been- Sleep in sweat, the mirrors cold See my face, it’s growin’ old Scared to death, no reason why Do whatever to get me by Think about the things I’ve said, read the page, it’s cold and dead Take me home Take me home Say goodbye, don’t follow. ~Alice in Chains, “Don’t Follow” .:: She woke up brushing hair out of her eyes. It had been days, days since she’d had any of those dreams, and she was starting to wonder what was wrong—why they had stopped. In the absence of them, there had been nothing, an empty husk, a blank canvas. Floating in a sea that had no variation, no color, no waves shifting its balance. Each evening, she struggled to the surface, gasping for air, sweat drenching her brow in a way that was all too human, all too mortal, all too frail. It had been no different this evening—a feeling of suddenly being flung out of stasis, realizing that she was drowning, and clambering towards the surface of her perception with jagged claws, desperate to escape the hellish nothingness that surrounded her. ::. .:: She sat up in the bed. It was Lakota’s bed, and despite the fact that Lakota was long dead, and Riven had graciously offered her this accommodation, she still felt out of place. Lakota’s scent, and his touch, were all over this room. She forced the covers to the side, groaning under her breath. Pain trickled slowly through every muscle in her body, searing unpleasantly. Her match with Fury in the first round of the Darwin’s Revenge tournament had been brutal, although she’d been prepared for that much. Still, she hated the way she felt the day after a match like that—like every muscle in her body was trying to climb through her skin and be set free from the cruel mistress who put them through such torture. She placed her feet on the rough concrete floor of Lakota’s room, and stumbled over to the mirror, her pale, haggard reflection catching even her off-guard. ::. “Merde, Shadow. Tu ressemble à l’enfer.” .:: The statement could not have been truer. She had forgotten to pull her long black hair out of its tail from the night before, and it was not only tangled with sleep and sweat, but also half-fallen out, stray hairs curling and snaring around the hair-tie that held it back. No doubt, it would be a mess to untangle it when she chose to do so. A black smudge of eyeliner jagged downwards from her right eye, smearing down her cheek—she must have rubbed it while she was asleep. Her milk-white skin wasn’t so milk-white right now—bruises had begun to show last night, and were know large, purplish marks on an otherwise perfectly porcelain complexion. Her head felt heavy—she tried as hard as she could to hold it up, as a stabbing pain ripped through the tense muscles of her neck, and she groaned under her breath. ::. .:: A bath, yes, a hot bath would clear this right up. She slipped on a long black tunic, not unlike a priest’s robe, and stumbled out of Lakota’s room into the main area of the compound. Riven was nowhere to be found, and she realized that, yet again, she had slept far later in the evening than she had meant to. It didn’t matter much—after all, now that there were no annoying Kindred “friendship games” to play, she had nothing to do, save sit around and muse on whatever happened to be going through her head at the time. Rubbing her temple, she slipped across the room into Riven’s personal room, and the bathtub that lay within, which he’d given her free reign to any time she liked. Once inside the sterile and utilitarian bathroom, she turned the knob; it stuck, and she jammed it hard with all her strength. As it let itself go, her hand slipped forcefully off, slamming against the wall and cracking her long thumbnail; a rather sickly stream of blood welled up underneath it and started to work its way out. She sucked on it for a moment, muttering under her breath, but was then distracted by the swirling convection of the hot water filling the tub below her. It rushed upwards in the constructs of the tub until it had reached a satisfactory level, and then she removed her robe, wrestled her hair free, and slipped in. Release, ah, release from all that had plagued her these past few weeks. For a few moments, she dropped beneath the seething surface, encompassed. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: Shadow had soaked in the bath nearly an hour, letting the waters cool gently around her until she started to get goosebumps, as her muscles gradually relaxed. Now, she sat back in Lakota’s room. Really, she should stop referring to it as his room, she thought, since he was dead and all. But it just seemed disrespectful somehow—maybe because she really didn’t know Riven all that well, maybe because she’d never met Lakota, but was sleeping in his room, and had moved all of her things into it. She didn’t know, but the feeling made her uneasy, like his vengeful wraith was going to come to her in the night—as though she believed in such things. ::. .:: In the corner of the room was a large trunk. Its top laid splayed open like a treasure chest, for the world to see. Inside was a large pile of journals of all different descriptions, some leather bound, some simple notebooks, and some looking as though they were so old, the guts could have fallen out of them, had the pages not already crumbled to dust. Some were simply tied around the cover with a suede cord, as though the sanctity from prying eyes would be kept out merely out of respect for such a warning. Many years were here, many, many years, and that much was obvious. Shadow coughed slightly, and cleared her throat, hunched over the large, heavy wooden table as she pored over a black journal that looked particularly old—perhaps it was another color when it was new. ::. “Je suis né en dix-sept cent quatre-vingt neuf… Ah… pardon. I forget sometimes that not everyone has a head for francais on their shoulders. I was born… well, some time ago. The truth of the matter is that the exact date does not matter, so long as you understand that I was born into a very different time than the one that I exist in now, a time when forever didn’t mean nearly as long as it does to me now, when I thought that vows were real, unbreakable, tangible promises… I was soon to realize that all vows are made to be broken, that tangible promises are as much a lie as something that you can’t even fathom with your mind. But I digress… the point is to let my own words speak for me. I’ll translate the best that I can…” .:: She cleared her throat again, a sort of nervous tension in her being, tangible just by looking at her. It flowed through the air in sparking, electric currents. She narrowed her eyes, looking closely at the tiny, nigh-unreadable black script that crawled across the pages, careening from line to line in a scrawl of French that wasn’t quite modern, but in a way that could not have been easily placed. It was her native language, so translating it into mongrel English often escaped her grip—at least translating exactly what she meant. ::. “It seems like I was born so long ago, but really, it is just an idle passage of time until now. I feel like I have been asleep to the truth, that I have not truly known who I am until now. It’s odd to find yourself in someone else—to look into his eyes and see the truth without having to question it. E., he knows what I mean when I say those kinds of things, no matter how he teases me about trying to be too profound for my delicate nature, I know that he does, I can feel it emanating from him—I know he finds truth here, between us, just as I do. That’s why we’re getting married. Not being officially married is the only thing keeping us from being wedded in every sense of the word, spiritually, by law, in front of God, or whomever watches these sorts of things. I know that they will bless us, for there could not be any two who love more truly, save the God and Goddess themselves.” .:: A bitter smirk wrote slowly across her features. Those words, they almost drove her to violence, but at the moment, she retained her calm. ::. “Ah, the purity of youth, n’est-ce pas? How quickly it is wiped away under certain circumstances that we ourselves cannot control. I read on to prove this to you… ‘It is not as I thought it would be, I will admit that, but yet I am told that you must learn to love through the hardships, the grief. We are suited for each other, perhaps even still the graceful consorts that I imagine us being for one another, but at the same time, there is a dissonance between us that I cannot immediately solve, that will not solve itself either.” .:: She paused. ::. “Regrettably, I inform you that it did not solve itself. E., he and I are never the type to get along for too long—but how could I have known that then? He disappeared from me only a few short weeks after I wrote that entry, leaving me in the midst of Paris, alone—‘He has gone. I do not know where he has gone, and I do not care. There is no God if he can allow such cruelty to be visited upon one of his happiest creatures, in her greatest time of need. E., he has left me, and I will likely never find him again. He left no clue, no trace—yet, as often as I repeat in these pages that he is gone, I still find it hard to believe. I still want to believe that he is coming back to me in the next moment. ‘The next time the tocsin sounds,’ I say, ‘he will come rushing through the door and into my arms again.’ But I cannot allow myself to believe that. I cannot.' “ .:: She closed the journal. That was enough—there was no need to continue through the rest of it, reading her strange laments after he had left her alone. She did not care about the laments—what she cared about was the fact that it was the first instance of what was to be a long history of abandonment after abandonment. She sighed for a moment, slipping the softened, almost rotted suede tie around the journal once again. ::. “What do others care for someone other than themselves? I learned this lesson through hardship and pain—that no one cares anything for anyone but himself, and that the only thing that matters is one person making sure that he has all he wants, all he desires. At least, that is what I thought after E. left me, but I was shortly to have my perception changed.” .:: There was another journal in her lap now—it had held up slightly better than the first. Hard red leather was its shell, a pattern stamped into it—a pattern of Persian lettering, but the average Joe wouldn’t have known that just from looking at it. She remembered that journal—Amir had given it to her shortly after they had met. ::. .:: A faint smile came over her face, but it just as soon faded out. She shook her head—back to business. The cord unwound, and she flipped through until she found the page that she was looking for. ::. “Calandre, I say to myself, do not allow yourself to become so absorbed in this young man! It is silly to be so enamoured of him, is it not? What are you chasing? Are you not just interested in him because he is so unlike E.? I cannot convince you to give him up, me, but you should at least be careful. The types cavorting about Paris now are the types that would have preyed on your mother’s and father’s blood when they were shot—do not let yourself get involved as though you are safe. Consider yourself in danger until you can find a way to assure yourself that you are not…” .:: She paused, flipping several pages through to the next relevant portion. ::. “He is so exciting, Amir—nothing like anyone you have ever known before, nothing like E., nothing like one of the people who wants to hold you in Paris, a captive lark for the rest of your days. Look at what he is offering you, Calandre! There is so much that you do not know about this world, and Amir would bind it up in a box and give it to you, but yet you are unsure of whether or not you should take it? I am starting to wonder about my own sanity, if I can willfully turn away from a gift such as what he is offering me. Freedom! A chance to get away from this wretched convent for the rest of my days, and no one will ever know where I’ve gone, no one will ever find us. An entirely new world—they say that nothing can go wrong in America. Amir laughed when I told him that, but I believe it—anything is better than Paris, oh, Paris dripping with its aeons of history and bloodshed, and dripping with your parents’ blood too. It has to be better to get out. I have to go find him, and tell him that I’ll come. Oh, waiting is over! Finally, I’ll be free, free of Paris, history, my parents, the convent, and most of all, free to forget about E. and finally move on to live a life that would make the gods envious of my joy. He will be my ambrosia, and I will be his nectar. Life will change—is not change what I have been looking for since E. left me? Damn him. Angela suffered for that lie between us, which even she saw at a young age—but I will no longer suffer for it. I can move on.” .:: Again, she paused to turn the pages. The next entry that she paused at was near the back of this particular journal. ::. “He is not what I thought he was! I told myself that I could not allow myself to become so attached that I would never see his faults, because we are all fallible creatures, and we all have faults—but this is something different. I am on this ship, passing from Paris to New Orleans, where we will live from now on—and I am terrified. Amir has told me something most terrible, and I do not know how to react. I do not know how he wants me to react. More than anything, I want him to see how much I care for him, but how can I care for someone who has such a secret lurking under their skin—someone who is such an inhuman monster?” .:: She smirked slightly, allowing a slight laugh to escape. It was all so humorous to her now, reading thoughts that were so juvenile, no matter how legitimate they had been when they were written. ::. “I am an alarmist, am I not? I find that the more I go through these old journals, the more I find that I would often get so alarmed about petty little things, and crow about them for page upon page, only to completely change my tune. Confused little lark—that is what Calandre means, you see, lark. Back then, it was so hard for me to keep a consistent opinion—I was too involved with the world around me, and its wonder, not knowing what I wanted out of it, and being bewildered that I should even be required to know such things. Amir planned to show me, though, what I wanted out of the world—or at least what he could perceive of such a question. That is why it amuses me that I was so frightened of what he revealed to me that night on the ship. It was nothing, compared to the atrocities that I have borne witness to since then—not to mention the atrocities that I have aided or perpetrated since then. But I was a young angel then, fearless, and disinclined to listen to anyone who told me that what I felt in my heart wasn’t right.” .:: Another journal found its way to the pile of journals that had already been read. Shadow slipped the next journal off of the pile in front of her, rimmed in crimson blood-red, gold etching across its cover. It was gaudy, or at least she thought it was now, but when she bought it in New Orleans, she had found it elegant—a symbol of that expansive American opportunity. Streets paved in gold, they’d said back in Paris. She hadn’t found streets paved in gold—but she had found something else that was more important. Truth. ::. “Confusion finds me writing this entry. I sit here, in my well-appointed room, well in view of the Mississippi River, thinking how ugly it is, compared even to the Seine. In any case, it is not the river that I think of, but myself. I wonder if I have made the right choice—Amir always has business, business, business to deal with in New Orleans, and I never see him. Yet I know that always, before I fall into hardship and selling myself like those girls in Jackson Square, he will reappear, laden with enough riches for my sustenance—monetary, material, and spiritual. I cannot say that I love him any less than I did when we left Paris, for that is not true. I have grown to have a dependency on him, an all-encompassing need, need, need for his presence, and even, for his very lifeblood, which at times, serves for an acceptable substitute. But tonight, he has promised me something different. Not in words, no, but he was different when I spoke with him, adamant that I should be prepared for this evening, and not be late. He chides me like a father sometimes, but I know it is only because he knows how lost in him I can become, and strives to remind me to care for myself as well. Retreating to this evening—I know, I simply KNOW within my soul, that he will give me the secret that he has shared with so few others. I pray for it, if praying does one any good in such situations. I prayed to St. Joan of Arc, just like Mere taught me… but what does a saint know of such things? A woman who lived her life by the Book and died by the Stake can know nothing of what is placed before me—I am on my own in this situation, by myself, standing before Amir, with the decision to make of whether or not I wish my life to change forever. Have I the courage to step into the fire?” .:: She smiled softly, winding the journal shut and placing it on the table. That period of her life had been full of discovery—after that night, there had been page after page after page of rambling, ecstatical entries about each new aspect of her life that she had discovered, each new treasure trove plumbed. Yet, things always had their wax and wane. ::. “For some time, I expressed my joy at Amir’s company, joy at his lessons, joy at the gift that he gave me—becoming a Rafiq, like him. I do not know what I did to deserve it, and Amir never told me any such reason, but to this eve, I am thankful that he chose to give it to me. And yet, things always seem to change—and they changed when Amir decided that he wished to take me back to Paris, to see the lovely, throbbing Vie Boheme that had sprouted up in the streets of the Quartier Latin… everywhere. I assented, and the unthinkable happened—the Sabbat, for whatever reason, took my Amir away from me. It is not something that I have discovered a reason for, to this day—yet even after he was taken from me, I still maintained myself. He would not have wanted me to roll over and die, or so I assumed—so I forced myself to continue on the beaten track he had laid out for me.” .:: After Amir had been murdered, she had stayed some time in Paris, collecting herself. The next journal that she opened was black, and the first page was a collection of Persian script—things Amir had once said to her, that stuck out to her. It was her in memoriam page for the journal, and most of the thoughts within it concerned losing him. ::. “Two weeks now, two weeks now since he has gone, and I am still alone. I do not know why I expected this to all of a sudden change, because it will not ever change. From this night forth, I must shoulder the load myself, and seek to finish the lessons that he began, though I confess, I know not how. Every eve, I wake with inconceivable pain hanging in my breast. I want to shout at God, to dig my talons into his face and tear it off for the way he always takes the things that mean the most to me, and makes mockeries of my devotion to them. But then I remember, there is no God. For if there were a God, he would certainly not allow beings to get so close to the very secrets that thump within his rotted, ancient chest—he would strike down beings like me. Yet he seems not to have the courage to lift his celestial hand against me. I shall soldier on—Amir would have wanted me to, and said so to me once or twice, though I quickly dismissed thoughts of him leaving me one day as folly. I should have listened, I should have listened—over and over again, I berate myself because I should have listened. But it doesn’t matter. I have to go on because it is what was set out before me, and Rafiq do not waver from their tasks. I will become a better Rafiq than even Amir thought possible—it is the destiny which I have set out for myself, and I will follow it. Yet, even as I say this, an ache grows in the pit of my stomach. There is too much that I do not know, too much that I cannot know without Amir at my side to show me. Fortitude when alone has never been my strong point—yet now it must be. Am I lying to myself, to think that I will actually be able to shoulder the load and move along on my own? My heart tells me that I am, with every unceasing, jerking, unnatural movement that it makes. I am not ready for this, I am not ready for this, I am not ready for this…” .:: She sighed. Sometimes, it was so hard, so unpleasant to remember these things, and yet they were the stuff of dreams, things that had been tormenting her since Amir had been murdered. Yet, things were never easy—never. By the time that she had reached the courage to struggle on alone, she ran up against another roadblock, this time, a knowledge much less pleasant. ::. “E. is not dead. As much as I would have thought that he would get his cowardly self killed by now, I have seen him with my own eyes, and know it to be true. He was at Lucien Forlani’s ridiculous masquerade ball. Creatures such as us, masquerading as Kine, masquerading as creatures. Aside from that, I did my task, I slipped in like a phantom and killed Forlani—they made it all too easy, you see—and then I ran across E. in the garden. He’d been watching me. And yet, he had no clue of who I was. It was as though something drew us together in a way that even I cannot explain—I am not pleased by such a development. London will not hold me long—I must leave before I see him again, and he promised to come by my hotel and visit this evening. I will be gone, long gone, by that time.” .:: Sometimes, it seemed to her like the universe abhorred her, for some unknown reason. There was no point in her pretending that she had ever had it easy, and thus she never did. Her words compelled one to believe that, directly enough. She sighed, looking up from her reading, massaging her temples—even after soaking in the bath, there was a nagging headache behind her eyes that simply refused to go away. ::. “Few journals survive from the next portion of my life. I know not if that is because I spent years in a depressed and blindly wandering fog and destroyed them upon later readings, or if I was so carelessly complacent during this period that I lost them. In any case, they would scarcely do you any good, because they only record weakness, and I am not made up of my weaknesses, I am made up of my strengths.” .:: She paused for a moment, picking up one of the last few journals that lay on the table. It looked to be a much more modern tome, though it was still leather-bound. It seemed to be much larger, much thicker than the rest, and encompassed several years of writings in its pages. ::. “I am tired of being alone. E. calls himself Corvus now, imagine that. There is no sense in my solitude, when someone so near my past is so near me physically. It would be no trouble to find his number, dial him up, and assure myself of his company for quite some time to come, I am certain. That is what I must do. I am not a solitary creature. I learn nothing when alone, save learning how to wallow in my own sorrow. I have become adept at that—it is now time to rejoin the world. I will call him. Tomorrow.” .:: She smirked, bitterly once again. It had been a momentous decision, calling Corvus after so long, and allowing him to know of her existence. In retrospect, it was a decision that she should have stayed far, far away from. This had only been a few years ago now—and ever since she had alerted Corvus to her presence, the two had spent their time alternately fighting, and deciding that they were allies, each split more painful than the last, and more pathetic than the first, considering that she could see it coming from a mile away each time they shook hands and agreed to be friends again. It was during this period that the dreams had intensified to the point where she was in danger of losing grip on her sanity—and largely, that was the reason why she had desired contact with him again. A rope. A buoy. A tether to lucidity to keep her from losing her mind when she was awake, because she certainly had no control over it when she was asleep. ::. “Corvus and I are an interesting case study, you see. When we are allies, we claim to have utmost loyalty to one another, yet there is always an undercurrent of doubt. Eventually, one of us turns on the other, and then the next time we meet, we’ll apologize, red-faced, and pretend that we’re sorry, only to do it again. A vicious cycle, if you care to use the modern terminology for it. In any case, so we met in WMW—the last chance, in my mind, for making good on the past, once and for all.” .:: She flipped several sections in the large journal that she had been reading from, scanning through the entries quickly, but finding none from the period that she desired. She picked up the next journal, another large tome, this one with its pages half-empty. This journal had started where the last one left off—her entry to WMW. The last few months had not been prolific ones for her writing, but there were poignant passages, nonetheless. ::. “I find myself trying to deny that I love Corvus. Why am I trying to do so? Perhaps because we have such a history of pretending that we care, and then abruptly turning that aside, to stab a knife right in the other’s heart. It is not even a knife in the back anymore, because as soon as the both of us start talking as friends again, we know it is coming. Denying that I am in love with him should be no shock. It is a frightening prospect, and a trap which I must not fall into. Must not, I tell myself, and yet I know that I will—because I know that he is holding it back too.” .:: A look of scorn came into her eyes. This touched on something recent, something unnerving—a bare wound for all to see. She didn’t know why she was putting herself through this, but she clenched her jaw, gritted her teeth, and flipped some more pages through her entries with resolve to finish what she had started. ::. “It is as I thought. We are in love with one another, and yet complacency grows… I can feel it, intangible, heavy, and sickening. It won’t be long.” .:: She growled under her breath. ::. “How right I was, n’est-ce pas? It was not long after I wrote those words that Corvus turned on me once again. Only this time, it was different. He is a man who thinks that he in infallible, and yet, he plays little Kine power games as though he is not above such things. And that is what mires him down—since then, I have learned that it was miring me down as well.” .:: She nodded very slightly. To some extent, Corvus leaving her for dead in the middle of that ring at Hardkore Hell had hurt, and she would not deny that just for the sake of making herself look invincible. Yet, this time, it had been different. This time, she had been prepared for it, and had taken the blow a little bit differently. She flipped near to the last entry in this journal, cleared her throat, and began to read once again. ::. “Perhaps this is what my dreams of Amir were telling me. Since his death, I have dreamed of him, but never so violently, and with such disturbing images, as this last time that I tried to fall in love with Corvus again. The past is nothing to me anymore—the past has been proven to be my weakness, and that has pounded against my skull numerous times as I write this entry. Yet, Corvus will not win, not yet. He thinks that I will continue the game; I will not. I will finally learn what Amir has wanted me to learn—Rafiq are solitary creatures, save when they are with other Rafiq. Munafiqun, especially wretched and worthless Degenerate Munafiqun are not the type of company for a worthy Rafiq to keep. Below-ground will be my new sanctity, here in Riven’s haven. Riven will not judge me, nor will he hamper my obligations, or my attempt to finally be who I am by synthesizing who I was into what I have become. I don’t know Riven well at all, but he is hospitable enough for a Sewer Rat—and we are both solitary enough that we will be quite suitable companions. I feel my soul breathing for the first time in aeons. I am finally free, free of those restraints that the past placed upon me. Corvus… even Amir… they are parts of me, that is true enough, but never again will they rule me.” .:: Quietly, she closed the last journal. She stood up, walking over to the entry of Lakota’s room. The heavy circular door was still open. She had heard Riven come in moments ago, and it was likely that he was sitting in the common room, enjoying some quiet time. She didn’t want to disturb him. Slowly, and with a creaking groan, she shoved the circular door of Lakota’s room shut, and walked over to the bed, sitting down on the end of it. Her sharp green eyes looked dulled at the moment as she stared down at the concrete floor of the room. ::. “For weeks I have been alone. I faced Corvus and Slayer, with Epitome as my partner, and was utterly disappointed in myself for allowing myself to lose that bout, although it was not me who got pinned in the end. Drawing my resources together, I prepared for the Darwin’s Revenge tournament against Fury… and since proved victorious. There is much before me. Much. And yet, still, even with so much before me, I chose to take the initiative, and place myself in harm’s way with GTT4.” .:: She paused, nodding. So many reasons why she had done it. ::. “There are many things that I could be doing towards getting myself together—but they would all be a moot point if they did not contain some sort of activity, some sort of way to prove, in physical and concrete, that Corvus has not beaten me, that he left me for dead, but I rose from my coffin and struck back. I would be the first to do so, even though he will not answer such allegations until I stand, staring him in the face, ready to face him and fell him like the oldest forest oak. It is for this reason that I stand before you. GTT4 is something that is outside my realm—but it is something that I am doing for myself. Hardly do I know the people that I see lining these brackets. Hardly do I know the man that I am up against… but knowledge of them does not translate to victory. For me, victory has always come from within. It is a fire in the soul, a preparedness, an assurance, and a need.” .:: Her brow furrowed. ::. “And whence comes this from someone who is rarely comfortable in her own skin? I do not know. I only know that GTT4 is a perfect breeding ground for me to find that assurance that I have been missing—to discover what it is about myself that makes me soldier on even when I am more than halfway beaten, and can hardly find the will to fight. Perhaps it is stubborn pride, perhaps blind idiocy, or perhaps some spiritual fire born within me at my Embrace. I do not know. I only know that my soul is restless in its shell, and I must find some way to comfort it. If the way to do so is to take some time away from all the constraints of WMW and face this tournament on my own, then that is the way it will happen. If not, I will turn my attention to another path. In any case, GTT4 looms on my horizon. I am nervous… disconcerted, more than anything else. But I can’t think of that right now.” .:: She winced, running a hand down to the base of her neck as she finished speaking. It was evident now, that this pain was not going to go away, no matter what she did to force its hand. In any case she supposed that, at least, she had said what she had to say in reference to this tournament that she was so wholeheartedly throwing herself into. She didn’t know what else she could have said, didn’t know what else she could have gleaned out of a mind that was already aching and tired with the effort to make sense out of her world today. She decided, simply, to give it up. With a sigh, she stood up, slipping over to the bed, and sliding under the covers once again. After a moment, she flicked out the light, preparing for another night of formless, nameless, faceless, terrifying tossing and turning before she got up to face whatever the Fates threw her way. ::.
