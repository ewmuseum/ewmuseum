Janus-Faced

21 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-21 .:: ::: ::. .:: The day lay dying. Passionate crimson threads stretched upward from the horizon, desperately trying to grasp something corporeal. Day did not wish to be snatched into oblivion; Day feared the darkness just as cockroaches feared the light. But while cockroaches skittered away for reasons of pure instinct, ever fleeing something they could not discern or ever understand, Day was perfectly cognizant of what was going on. Day was perfectly aware that what it struggled against was the coming of Night, the obliteration of all Day’s work in a few fell swoops. The flowers retreated into their shells, and the birds into their nests. The people sank into laudanum-fits of sleep, haunted by the nightmares and dreams they could never dare to realize in waking hours. But each sunset, Day struggled valiantly to retain its spot, clawing futilely at the casket, streaking the sky with its blood. Night won, every time. Day was put to rest for twelve long, creeping hours during which only the truly brave or wicked dared venture forth. ::. .:: Shadow didn’t remember what the sun felt like. She didn’t suppose that it truly mattered, for it was something that she didn’t need, something that did not affect her in the slightest. She was more than a mortal, sliming along the sewers. She was something preternatural, something above the laws that were made for man, and yet governed by the cage man was kept in. Her predicament was curious indeed, hanging somewhere in between an ending and a beginning, forever until eternity. It wasn’t often that she paused to place thought on such things. ::. .:: In another universe, she might have stopped to compare herself favorably to Jonathan Sail. In this universe, Shadow knew no such pity, equating herself to someone who had turned back on the course they had set for themselves. Or, that was how she saw it. It was easier to see it that way than to admit that Sail was more than he seemed. There were times, times indeed when she was able to give up the truths of the mortal world in order to revel in the eternal prejudices that had begun to build within her since her Embrace. Part of that was thinking that no mortal could ever fathom the secrets that she had fathomed in what seemed like such a short time. Of course it seemed like a short time, when one had eternity stretching before one. Really, all she was doing was making it easier on herself. It was easier to write Jonathan Sail off than to think about him as a sort of threat. She knew full well that he was, but why bother yourself with details, she thought? Once again, she seemed to be considered the underdog, but that was starting to be normal for her. For some reason, it seemed that the only person she had ever met who had full confidence in her was Amir, and the only time she ever saw him these days was when her brain decided to favor her with a hallucination that left her flushed, trembling, and entirely unaware of her location or what she had been doing for the entire duration of her chimaera. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: The Persian sun beat down warm on her skin. It seemed as though she should have been frightened, although she couldn’t remember why. She smiled. Ah, Persia. It was more beautiful than she had ever expected. And she had been so nervous about it! But Amir had promised that she would fit in here, and he had been right. She had never felt so alive in her entire life. ::. “Madame is here, Master.” .:: Nasim made a low bow, pushing open the door to the terrace. Amir stepped out onto it, a breeze ruffling through his silken black hair. His skin looked crisp and tanned in the sunlight, but his blue eyes, ever the anomaly, still bore through everything like a drill. He walked elegantly over to his Madame, smiling down at her with a gentle look. ::. “I expected to find you here, Liana. You are going to be darker than I if you don’t come inside now and again.” .:: She pushed her lip out, pouting at him playfully. ::. “Would you like me less then?” .:: He laughed, pulling her up to her feet, pulling her into his arms. ::. “I would dare say I’d like you more, but I don’t think that’s possible by any stretch of the word.” .:: He picked her up, spun her around, then set her gently down. His eyes took on a serious look, but his tone was still gentle. ::. “I am happy that you are happy here. I didn’t expect you would make such a stunning transition from Parisian to Assassin.” .:: She shrugged at him, in a way that Parisians seemed to develop out of habit. ::. “I am flexible, cher. It is something that has kept me alive all this time, and I only assume that it will continue to do so.” .:: She rubbed her hands over her arms, feeling the radiating warmth of her skin. For the first time since she had gotten here, she paid attention to it. Yes, he was right. It was much darker than it had been before. But that was acceptable, she supposed—after all, this was a new life. She was starting fresh, a new outlook on her duties. She looked back into the eyes of her beloved with a happy and innocent excitement that he was glad to see in her. ::. “Amir, has there been any word from PTC?” .:: She pronounced the letters sharply, with a French lilt. He was looking away from her on the balcony, looking out across the land that was his home, that he hoped to help her appreciate, although she was already doing a fine job of that herself. For a moment, he did not speak. When he turned back to her, there was a storm brewing in the twin azure seas with which he gazed at her. ::. “Yes, Liana, some days ago, but…” “Why didn’t you tell me?” .:: Concern flashed through her face. She didn’t want to miss something important, didn’t want to have to backtrack on her quest because she was days behind in preparing. She started towards the swinging doors back into the palatial estate in which he lived, going to rifle through the mail pile until she found what she was looking for. He sighed. ::. “Wait… Please.” .:: She turned to look at him, feeling confusion begin to swell. She crossed her arms over her chest, trying to stem the rising stubbornness that always made him frown so. ::. “What is it, Amir?” .:: He stepped forward to her, clasping her hands between his. His blue eyes burned down into her earnestly. ::. “I had hoped to take you all away from that, Lia. I… I don’t know. You are not suited for that sort of thing. I am more than proud of what you have accomplished with PTC, and WMW, and everything else that you have done back in that world, but… this is something new. I was hoping that something new would mean that we could start over too, you and I, without anything hanging over our heads from that old world.” .:: Her brow furrowed. ::. “Amir… It’s just an idle pastime. What could it hurt?” .:: It wasn’t an idle pastime. But, she thought that it might make him feel better if she said so. It was easier for him to digest it if he thought she felt that way. But she had a feeling he saw through that explanation and realized that she was getting at something else entirely. ::. “It’s not an idle pastime… I just worry that it could become something unhealthy, Lia. It is against the nature of an Assassin to do such things. If you were to devote all of your energies to the clan, I just… I think you would be happier.” .:: She sighed. There were plenty of things she could have said to him in response, but only one stuck in her head, because she was becoming angry. An irrevocable lava-flow was seething upwards, and it spurted forth when she spoke to him again. ::. “Is it so much that I would be happier? Or is it that you’d rather me devote all my time to bloody clan proceedings at Alamut and waste away inside a rotting fortress all day?” .:: Amir stepped away from her. He was becoming angry as well, she could sense it radiating off of him like a coal stove. ::. “Liana, do not play these games with me. You know I put your well-being first even before my own. I just think you are playing at childish fancies that are best left alone. You are talented, and you are wasting your talent by binding it up when you step into a PTC or WMW ring to fight petty wars against little Kine who do not even appreciate your talent.” .:: She bit her lip. Amir often seemed to misrepresent her every action as childish, but that wasn’t so. And he knew it, too! She’d explained to him legions of times the reasons that this wrestling career was important to her, but he never listened. ::. “Amir, if you can do nothing but call what I choose to occupy myself with childish, then I have nothing more to say to you. I will continue in GTT4 so long as I have the chance, and in WMW so long as I have the desire. Where are the PTC documents?” .:: Ire blazed in his eyes. ::. “If you want them, find them yourself. I will have no more part in your folly. As well, I have a meeting to attend tonight. Since you inevitably will be neglecting your duty and not coming with me, I would prefer not to be disturbed while I prepare for it.” .:: The anger growled within her once more. ::. “Fine. Continue to pretend that you do not know me, call me childish, as you wish. You live your life, I will live mine, Amir, but I am not a slave of Alamut, and you will not make me into one!” .:: With that, she stormed back into the estate, preparing to journey to the front hall and rifle through the correspondence that had piled up there for some time. They had been enjoying one another’s company, until now, that is. But it was all different, as she knew it inevitably would be. Just like old days… just like the way it felt to argue ceaselessly with him, in an idiotic point-counterpoint about things that were as trivial as what they chose to do with the next hundred years or so of their unlives. She didn’t care anymore. If Amir wanted to hate her for her choices, he could, and she would certainly be free to hate him for his. It seemed as though that was what they had come to. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: A growl of pain seethed through her, her body jerking forward. She whirled around to find the source of her pain to discover that she had been leaning on a light, happily beaming bright into the skin of her back. There’s your Persian sun, she thought to herself. She took in her surroundings, as though she had never seen them before. ::. .:: It was Club Tenebrae, the same as it always was, seething with Kine and Kindred alike. Some people seemed aware of the fact that this was just a breeding ground for ghoul-ship or meal-ship, whichever the case was. But some of the little Kine seemed to be perfectly aware what, exactly, they were standing in the midst of. The strange part was, it didn’t seem to bother anyone. Either too ignorant or too jaded, she supposed. ::. .:: She made a sour face. Why was she here again? Oh yes, Falcon had wanted an update. An update on that stupid bastard that she was supposed to be hunting down. Falcon was really starting to get on her nerves. He was goddamn bullheaded, and she knew that, but he was pushing her to go ahead with the LaBatte hit without more research. Dammit, she had told him, that wasn’t going to work, and if he had any sense, he would have known that. Obviously, however, Falcon had no sense. He had called her here to Club Tenebrae tonight, insistently. He expected her to have a full update on the status of the hit. There wasn’t much to tell him that was something different than the last time they had talked. LaBatte had seen her, so she had to conceal herself rather carefully. Falcon might have wanted him dead, and dead immediately, but he was going to have to learn some patience, and appreciate damn fine work when he had a damn fine Assassin on the job. ::. .:: For a moment, she thought on that too, her job, that is. She’d done a bit of searching, thanks to Amir’s research—a bit of searching on Jonathan Sail. The two of them weren’t so unalike, but Shadow very seriously doubted that the two of them would ever be the type to shake hands when this was all over. She wasn’t planning on it. GTT4 wasn’t about shaking hands, was it? No. It wasn’t, it never had been, and the people who were left had gotten as far as they had because they’d realized the very real cutthroat nature of the whole thing. ::. .:: But how long could they truly hold out? Men were men because they were fallible. Eventually, they’d give in to some ridiculous prick of conscience and lose everything; entire empires crumbled around men who set off down the darkest path possible and didn’t have the courage to finish the job. Sail might already be well down the path, but she could see the stingings of conscience within him, whipping him back and forth. She had no conscience anymore. Very few things concerned her, aside from the objectives she set out for herself, and if they concerned the ruin of a few beings, Kine, Kindred, or otherwise… what did she care? ::. .:: She didn’t. That was the coldest fact of all. That seemed to both create some of her biggest problems, and heal some of her biggest gaping wounds. She had fallen out with Corvus so many times because he wanted someone to worship him with the pathetic faithfulness of a little mortal wretch, and she hadn’t cared. Then again, she’d survived that so many times because deep down inside, she hadn’t cared. Was she just jaded? Or had she finally shed that skin? Questioning which it was seemed wasteful. ::. .:: She slipped her coat over her long, lithe figure and turned from the pounding music and the writhing crowd of Club Tenebrae. Damn Julian Falcon and everything he wanted to hear out of her mouth. Assassins kept their words, but they did not descend into ridiculous and unnecessary personal discourse with either victim or customer. Falcon wasn’t due for forty-five more minutes, and she was going home. Into the night she disappeared, the wind turning still cooler as it wrapped its sepulchral fingers around the folds of her coat. She didn’t feel it tearing at her ivory skin. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: The snow drove in never-ending wavelets, crashing up against the stone walls of the castle. It had withstood such a beating for years upon years, never yielding even an inch. The stone weathered at the edges, but it never broke; never did this harsh, cold clime win the battle. ::. .:: Her hands were cold, dug into the snow as they were. Still, just like the last time she had been here, so long ago, the mystic pull of the earth drew her in. Touching it made her feel closer, despite the fact that she’d spent most of her life being away from Prague. ::. .:: The Vltava was iced over this time of year. Still, she could hear streams trickling slowly beneath it, a sign of life amidst this city that seemed to be forever frozen in stasis, at least in the winter. But it wasn’t. She could feel the soil seething with life even beneath the dense piles of snow. She pulled her hands out of it, pausing to look at them. Elongated, razor-sharp claws extended well past the length of normal fingernails. Beneath them, hard black earth was caked. She flicked a few particles out from underneath her nails before she realized that ridding every single speck was a lost cause. ::. .:: Upon turning around, the sight of the castle struck her, as it always did. It seemed to reach up into black oblivion in this night. It never ended. It didn’t have the ability to end. It was just as fixed as the very landscape it rested on, inanimate but forever alive despite itself. She almost could have felt sorry for it, but it was in this living earth, seated on this intoxicated soil that drew her to it every time she was near. ::. “Too much contemplation will skew your focus, Lia.” .:: She knew it was Vaclav before she saw him. He approached her side rather quickly, and she slipped her arm through his, They walked through the yard and to the narrow set of stairs that wound up the outside of the castle. ::. .:: Up, up, up the two of them continued. Vaclav was always the perfect gentleman, and when she was with him, she felt like the Queen of the Carpathian Snow. At the top of the castle, everything was covered in a thick white dusting, crunchy underneath both of their booted steps. They proceeded across the roof, digging small soul-imprints into the snow, rounded at the toe and heel. He gazed down into the snow-mantled gardens for a long, long time before he turned back to her. ::. .:: His eyes positively dazzled her; they had ever since she’d met him. Given, she supposed she would have called them nothing special. They were a simple brown, but a brown that was deep, as black as the castle-stones, a brown that seethed intensely. Within was the spirit of genius, something that chilled her every time she looked at him. She truly was residing with and learning from the master of the Art. He rested a hand atop hers as both of them stared off into the darkness of the Eastern European night. ::. “I don’t want you to spend so much time thinking things over that you lose sight of what you intend to do, hm? It’s important that you remember the reasons why you’re here—you need a little strength, and you can get it here. But don’t get lost in the myth that you love so much.” .:: She smiled, albeit a bit fangedly. To anyone but another Kindred, it would have been hideous. ::. “I’m not trying to get lost, Vaclav. I just enjoy being here, I enjoy the fact that so much life stems from just sinking my hands into the soil, even buried under snow. I could stand out in the yard and do it for hours.” .:: He chuckled. ::. “You’d likely lose yourself in that reverie until dawn, and then I would have the great inconvenience of scooping you up in a dustpan and depositing you in a wastepaper basket. How rude you would be!” .:: He squeezed her hand, careful not to scrape her skin with his own claws. ::. “In all seriousness… I know this is important for you. I don’t want to see your hopes slip into oblivion because you get sidetracked by the past and its promises. You can’t fix those promises by living in the past, my dear. All you can do is remember the past, and move into the future hoping to one day reach catharsis in whatever way you may. I think you are reaching that point with GTT4. But you can’t lose sight of the goal.” .:: She nodded. Vaclav had always been so wise. Of course, she supposed, he had had centuries piled upon centuries to become that wise, so it was really no surprise that he could come up with something to aid her with whatever petty problem she might have brought to him. And yet, with all his wisdom, she had never been disinclined to listen. Vaclav did not see fit to flaunt his gift, but he used it when needed. ::. “What do you think I am reaching for?” .:: He paused for a moment, mulling over her question. He didn’t want to give her an incomplete answer. ::. “I think, Lia, that you are finally reaching the beginning phases of your Metamorphosis. It takes quite a while for some to do so… but you have reached your starting point. This week is important for you. It is a time for you to make those who would doubt you realize the truth. The most important of those, of course, is yourself. It is time that you quit doubting yourself, Lia. You will never make any steps towards the eventual realization of your Metamorphosis without that.” .:: He paused. She paused as well. It was a big chunk of information to digest. Vaclav always seemed to pull out something potent when she least expected it. ::. “I thought I’d made steps.” .:: He smiled at her. ::. “You have, my dear. You have. But you are not quite finished yet, because you don’t quite believe that you can make it as far as you want to. I can sense that much. You want to believe that you CAN make it to the bracket finals, that you CAN push past Jonathan Sail, a revered man in this tournament. Think of the possibilities, Lia. You have already defeated Tsunami Oni, a feat that many felt you could not manage. What says that you can’t defeat Sail as well?” .:: She nodded. ::. “I can see what you’re saying. It’s just hard to believe something like that is true when… well, when I feel like everyone is expecting me to fall flat on my face at any moment. It’s been that way since the beginning of GTT4, and people still seem to feel that way in WMW as well.” .:: Vaclav shook his head. ::. “Nevermind them. That’s your problem, Lia. You rely too much on what other people think of you, instead of the potential that you have inside you. If you stopped, paused a moment to actually think of that… then you’d see what I’m getting at.” “Consider the lilies?” .:: He made a rather distasteful face, at which she almost had to laugh. ::. “Please, do not quote the Bible. It has a tendency to make me quite ill. I don’t resort to reading a bunch of claptrap revisionist hogwash in order to get my wisdom, and I won’t resort to it in speaking with you either.” .:: She smiled. ::. “Alright, alright. I should have known you’d feel that way.” .:: A smile melted across his face as well, like springtime melting over his beloved Prague. ::. “My dear, do you honestly know any Kindred who will willingly quote the Bible? They’d have to be either newly Embraced or completely insane, the two of which at times go together. We cast that off soon after we discover what we are… if we allowed that… THING… to rule our lives and actions, we’d be nothing but cannon fodder for Gehenna. I’d prefer not to occupy that position, and I’m sure you can relate.” .:: She nodded. ::. “If I have to meet the Eldest, I’d rather shake its hand or kick its ass, not quote Bible verses at it in an attempt to make it go away out of the goodness of its heart.” “Likely none of those would be excellent choices. But that is not the matter at hand, and you shan’t change the subject so easily.” .:: He paused for another long moment, smiling slightly. He did adore Lia so, if only because she was so self-disparaging, but really did believe in herself, deep down, the entire time. He took both of her hands in his, looking her deeply in the eyes. ::. “Lia… now the time for joking is over, and you must listen to me. You are important to us. You are not just beginning your quest, no… you mean more to us than another disciple. You, my dearest, are a Vlaszy. You cannot give up on yourself, and you cannot give up on your existence. I have seen you dangerously close to doing both lately. Endure, Lia, endure. The rest of your line is gone—you are the only Vlaszy left. And for that reason, you must not stop fighting, even if you are surrounded on all sides.” .:: It sounded like such a grave charge, and yet she knew deep within her heart that it was true. Vaclav never lied to her. She blinked a cold flake of snow off of her eyelash before it melted there, unable to look away from him. There was something magnetic in his eyes, something true. She couldn’t hang on to those attitudes, believing everything that she heard around her, believing that she’d be put down by Jonathan Sail without a second thought just because people had heard his name before. She had to move past that, or she would just roll over and die. Vaclav would never forgive her for that. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: A hard, volt-like shock ran through her as she met the brute force of the wall in front of her. She stumbled backwards, holding her scraped forehead. In a rage, she kicked it, flung her fists at it—but still it was unmoving, like a fortress. It laughed at her. Everything laughed at her this evening. ::. .:: Her face was a black cloud as she continued down the street, jamming her hands into her pockets. What a mess she was—one part of her scrambling into the past for familiar shelter while the other tried to claw its way free, into undiscovered territory. ::. .:: To some extent, it had begun to bother her that these hallucinations, these specters of past and imagined lives haunted her so frequently. She was having conversations with Amir that she never could have had, considering he was dead—and she’d only seen Persia once. The rest was imagination. As for this Prague mess? She’d never even been to Prague in her entire life, so imagination or remembrance didn’t explain that away. Who the hell was Vaclav? Why the hell was she talking to Amir about present events when he was long dead, and wouldn’t have been interested in them anyway? She pushed the questions away. Questions never had straight answers. She hated questions with a passion. ::. .:: And what the hell was she doing anyway? Every time she thought of Amir, she fell into pangs over what could have been. He’d died in such an untimely way that it seemed that the two of them still had lifetimes stretching out before them that they could have lived together. But the chance was over. The longer she looked into the past, the more convinced she became that it held nothing for her. And what did it matter besides? All she got out of it was chidings from someone who thought he knew her well, but was too absorbed in himself to really know who she was. The past was holding her back, and it always had. ::. .:: But what of the present, or the future? It wasn’t like she wanted to drop everything and run to Prague, although some part of her evidently had the urge, despite the fact that she had never been, and had never showed any interest in Prague at any other point in her life. And what would be there once she got there? A bunch of strangely yellow buildings with strangely red and yellow and orange roofs. And a bunch of old castles. And a bunch of war-scarred land. None of that was hers, none of it belonged to the life she had lived in Paris, or the life she lived now, even such as it was. But still, there was a message in it. She’d found hope in it. What if this Vaclav character was right? What if she was on the verge of something new, and all she needed to do to get there was believe in what was in front of her, seize on the resources that she herself possessed, and make good of them? It all seemed so simple, deceptively simple. ::. .:: What did she know of simple in this case? She rested, right now, between taking the advice of the past that she had once loved—a past that wanted to hold her down. A past that wanted her to abandon everything that she had built for herself. But she couldn’t do that. She would have been lying to herself if she’d said she could or would EVER willingly do that. ::. .:: On the other hand, she was considering following the advice of a simple ghost, a man she’d never met before in her entire life, who was quite possibly a madman that her own fractured brain had created out of dust. So what was saner? Listening to Amir and willingly throwing her catharsis and her only escape down the drain? Or listening to a figment of her imagination that was telling her to pick up the reins and direct her own carriage, horses’ heads pointing towards eternity? She felt like she was trying to figure out which was better—being drawn and quartered, or being lovingly hugged by the arms of an iron maiden. ::. .:: A crossroads stood in front of her. One path was everything she had known in her life up until this point. The other path was the unknown. At this point, neither seemed to pleasant, but one thing kept picking at her brain, throughout the entire process. ::. .:: Jonathan Sail wanted to beat her. He wanted to beat her badly, because bracket finals were important to him. Because PTC was important to him. Maybe because he was one of the tournament favorites, and he didn’t want to disappoint. The reasons he wanted to defeat her weren’t important, it was just something that she could scent on him, as though she were a prized bloodhound. It set her senses baying at the moon, but until this point, she hadn’t known what to do about it. She wanted this. She wanted this more than anything, because it would singularly prove wrong everyone who had ever doubted her, for whatever reason. She wanted it for her. Not because she wanted to face anyone particular in the final round, and not because she had any particular goal in mind. She wasn’t targeting Deville, or Almasy, or Wolfenden, like most people seemed to be doing. She was just following the drive that pushed her from inside. She was doing what she had to do. Wasn’t that all she could do? She’d never found any answer to that question. If there was more to offer, more to give, she didn’t know what it was, or where within herself she could have found it. ::. .:: The door lay ahead. If she stepped through it, she’d be turning her back on her past, and jumping headfirst into uncertainty. Did that bother her enough to prevent her from having the courage to walk through? A week ago, she would have said yes, but now she didn’t know. She felt like her eyes had been opened, but they had been opened by illusion, mere vapour, the stuff dreams are made of. But truth, being elusive, often takes the same form. She steeled herself. Fear was weakness. Sentimentality was weakness. Doubt, weakness. Pity, weakness. Mercy, weakness. Hesitation was as well—the gate was open for her, and she stepped through. Night watched her with wicked eyes. Day could not save her now. Was she a creature of himself, or would she wither under duress? Her strength preserve her. It was the only way she would survive. ::. Because I could not stop for Death – He kindly stopped for me – The carriage held but just Ourselves – And Immortality. We slowly drove – He knew no haste And I had put away My labor and my leisure too, For His Civility – We passed the School, where Children strove At Recess – in the Ring – We passed the Fields of Gazing Grain – We passed the Setting Sun – Or rather – He passed Us – The Dews drew quivering and Chill – For only Gossamer, my Gown – My Tippet – only Tulle – We paused before a House that seemed A Swelling of the Ground – The Roof was scarcely visible – The Cornice – in the Ground – Since then – ‘tis Centuries – and yet Feels shorter than the Day I first surmised the Horses’ Heads Were toward Eternity – (E. Dickinson, ‘Because I could not stop for Death’)
