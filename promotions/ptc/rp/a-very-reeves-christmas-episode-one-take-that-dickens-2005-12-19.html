"A Very Reeves Christmas" -Episode One- "Take that, Dickens!"

19 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-19 The scene opens in the living room of a rustic townhouse. Decorated with 19th century furniture and charm, the room's centerpiece is a rather comfortable looking, and crackling, fire. The scene is lit with various lamps as well, and leaning his head upon the granite mantle is "Hollywood North" Dallas Reeves. He's clad in 19th century clothing; his shoulder-length blond ponytail touches the collar of his longer smoking jacket. He straightens up and steps away from the fireplace, sipping from his brandy snifter, looking very regal and proper, so very…un-Dallas Reeves. From his right steps a more modestly dressed Frankie Calrissian, Reeves' unofficial sidekick and protege. Frankie appears to be wearing the attire of a servant, and Dallas turns to acknowledge his arrival with a quick nod. He opens his mouth to speak. Dallas: Ah, Fra- The shrill sound of audio feedback shatters the scene, as a boom microphone plunks itself square in the middle of Reeves' forehead. Reeves shoves it out of his face, rips his purple velvet scarf from around his neck, and walks off the set. Dallas: Cut! C'mon, man! That's like the fourth time today! Unseen Director: That's five, everyone! Smoke 'em if ya got 'em! The film crew assemble on the set to adjust props, lights, and the like while Dallas addresses the boom mic operator, a younger man in college student clothes…brown cords and an indie band t-shirt…with big curly hair. Dallas: Dougie, if you want your class credit, you're gonna have to keep focused…otherwise I might just staple that boom mic to your chest. And flunk ya. Boom Mic Guy: Yes, Mr Reeves. Dallas continues walking off set, past script assistants and floor directors. The boom mic guy, now safely out of reach, flips Reeves off, behind his back. A small gaggle of visiting children approach Dallas, pens extended, paper rustling, eyes sparkling, all hoping to get themselves an autograph. Dallas pays them no mind, brushing them off as though they were a pack of frisky lepors. In the background, a banner is strewn across the wall, reading "A Very Reeves Christmas". Dallas: Ava!? Reeves freezes mid-step, looking around as he calls for his personal assistant, Ava. He calls for her again… Dallas: Ava?! …before he realizes she's right behind him, and had been so for the last seven steps or so. Ava's a youngish brunette in a business pantsuit, with bookish glasses. Though her hair is worn up, you can tell she's a knockout. Dallas: Ah, Ava…look, I'm not a primadonna or anything…but this is like the second time I've had to walk past star-hungry children. As soon as taping stops, they flock to me…and just because I'm the star of "A Very Reeves Christmas" doesn't mean the audience gets to be hobnobbing with me. Ava: Did you call that kid a hobgoblin? Ava wasn't hired for her personal assistance skills. She's not the brightest bulb on the tree. Dallas: They always…what? Hobgoblin? Ava: Hog gobbling? Dallas: Ho-…what? Look, just put up one of those doggie gates so the kids can't get near me. This is an expensive jacket. From the velvet-iest of sheep. Ava: Okay, Mr. Dallas. Dallas takes a bottle of imported water from Ava, and continues to make for his dressing room. Ava: Velveeta sheep? Ava shrugs her shoulders and saunters off, in her own cute-but-dumb way. Dallas finally makes it to his dressing room, and to the door that bears his name. He flips the sign on the door handle over to "do not disturb", and shuts the door behind him. Anxious to get a break, Reeves flips the lightswitch on, and is startled to find the form of a man seated on his sofa. This was a man that Reeves remembered at first glance, only, he was pretty sure that when last he knew the man, the visitor wasn't enamating this odd blue aura. Ghost: Evenin', Dallas! Dallas: What in the blue fuck?! Ghost: Do you really have to curse? Dallas: Holy fuckin' fuck! The rather pale, yet blue, visitor seemed to be an old acquaintance. In fact, the visitor seemed to be Eddie Olsen, Dallas' former manager/sidekick. Eddie had been an exceptional student at the Reeves' family wrestling school, as was therefore invited to tag along with Dallas in his earlier wrestling ventures. The only problem is, Eddie's been dead for several years. Dallas: Are you fuckin' kiddin' me?! Last time I heard, you were suffering from a bad case of "piano fell on your god damn head". Did they somehow come up with a cure for that!? Eddie stands up, and Dallas freaks out, leaping backwards, only to crash into the rather stationary wall. Eddie: Relax, Dallas. Dallas: Am I dead, too? (muttering to himself) That would explain the ratings… Eddie: Yes, I'm dead. The piano that fell on my head killed me. Dallas: Then what the fuck do you want with me!? Are you here to haunt me? I always treated you well, didn't I? Besides…how do I know it's really you? Eddie: Slow down. No, I'm not haunting you. If I was haunting anything, it'd be the girls locker room at my old high school in Winnipeg. Dallas: Yeah, it has to be you. Nobody wants to see a naked chick from Winnipeg. So why did you come…o great spirit? Dallas shrugs his shoulders at the camera, then speaks to it. Dallas: Like I know what to call a ghost. Eddie: You used to be about the wrestling, Dallas. About the thrill of the sport. Now look at you. You care more about your image than your wrestling career. You're borderline metrosexual- Dallas: Easy now. Eddie: Working in television and movies- Dallas: Canadian television and movies. Like regular television and movies, but with one-tenth the budget. That's how I got my own Christmas Special. Eddie: And "Hollywood North"? What is that even supposed to mean? Dallas: That's what they call Toronto…'cause a lot of low budget American films are done there. They dress Toronto up to look like New York, Chicago, LA… Eddie: What, do they bring their own garbage and dump it in the streets. Dallas: Well…yeah. Eddie: Oh. The two share an awkward moment. Well, not any more awkward than any other man-spirit conversation might have, but it's still kinda awkward. Eddie: So…uh…the reason I'm here- Dallas: Finally! Eddie: Is to instill upon you the true spirit of the season! You've lost your belief, Dallas! Now you must learn the hard way! Dallas: You want me to find the true meaning of Christmas? Eddie: No! The true meaning of…competing in Giant Wrestling Tournaments! Reeves face sinks low, and he lets out a loud, monotonous sound. Dallas: Whaaaaat? Eddie: Tonight, you will be visited by three of your former sidekicks and/or opponents. Take heed of their words, Dallas, for it is not too late for salvation! Dallas: This is all about GTT5? Eddie: Remember what I say…and know this! You totally squeaked through your first round, dude! It was luck! You'll need more than that to make a good showing this year! Now get the hell out of here! Dallas: This is my dressing room! You can't kick me out of my own dressing room! Eddie: Fine. I'll be going then. Eddie phases himself out through the door, walking right through a disgusted-looking Dallas in the process. Dallas: Grody! How did Bill Murray make this look good? Eddie disappears, and Dallas stands, spooked out. Dallas: It's just a dream… Eddie sticks his head back in through the door. Eddie: Three sidekicks and/or opponents! Dallas: I hope I don't get cancelled.
