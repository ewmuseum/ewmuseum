The Saga of the Uber Judge: Intermission

20 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Sage of the Uber Judge: Intermission On the night after the third victory, the Uber Judge had a dream. Unlike his waking life which seemed to be wrought with symbols, signs, and portents throughout, his dream was strikingly ordinary. Ordinary in the sense that it didn’t seem to operate on dream logic (much), but seemed to be an event indistinguishable from ordinary reality. Also, unlike most dreams being mysterious, upon waking the Uber Judge could feel with absolute certainty that this dream was merely a creation of his own mind. And yet just the same, `merely’ is perhaps a misleading term, considering that in spite of the state of the dream and its non-mysterious origin, it seemed to be significant enough to matter. So he remembered it. And this is what he remembered. == The Uber Judge sat across from a beautiful woman, who had light coffee colored skin, delightfully seductive cheekbones, and the most intelligent (the Uber Judge’s favorite favorable description of women he found attractive!) hazel eyes. The UJ wanted to know all about her. He wanted to ask if she was perhaps Creole in regard to her ancestry, where she went to college, her views on politics, anything to get her to talk about herself. But he did not. Her purpose was to ask; his purpose was to talk. Before they began, she smiled, and her smile was wide, but not too wide, showing immaculate white teeth (the UberJudge was pleased to discern that there was no way possible any smoker could have teeth that white). Her smile spoke of openness, of curiosity, and it invited him to that openness. She said, “Thank you for joining me, Uber Judge,” and her thank you, in a world of so many thank you’s being a mere formality, was an expression of authentic appreciation, and at this the Uber Judge’s fears were erased: he knew he could trust this woman’s integrity, and this mattered to him. “Thank you for having me, Sharon,” he replied. “You’ve accomplished a lot lately in the PTC,” she said. “You’ve managed to win three rounds of the Infinite Gauntlet series, you’ve joined the PTC federation XUW, you’ve won a shot at the PTC’s interpromotional Extreme Title, and you’ve won a garaunteed entry into GTT5 to take place this summer. I think we can easily conclude that in the few short weeks you’ve been a part of PTC, you’ve shown us quite a lot.” Her smile punctuated her remarks, reminding the Uber Judge that it actually is true that the shaping of the mouth while smiling does change the sound of the speaker’s voice. “Thank you,” said the Uber Judge. “But,” said Sharon, “now that we’ve seen a little bit of what you can do, I'm curious about the untold story. Who is the Uber Judge? And this isn’t a question of what your actual name is though, it’s more a question of what defines the Uber Judge as a competitor, what makes the Uber Judge the Uber Judge?” “Well,” said the Uber Judge, “I am the Judge Over All Creation.” “And is this a special gift, or a talent, or a power?” asked Sharon. “No, actually it’s not. In the respect of gifts or talents, I'm a perfectly ordinary individual like everyone else. Being the Judge, that’s about embracing the responsibility of being the Judge. I'm not special at all, but the choice to take the responsibility for Judgement, that can almost be said to be the element that can be qualified as special.” “So anyone,” continued Sharon, “who would take that responsibility, could be an Uber Judge, couldn’t they?” “Oh, absolutely,” said the Uber Judge. “Walk me through the process. The call to Uber Judgement, as it were. How would an ordinary person be an Uber Judge?” “Well, the first step, or the first requirement actually, is that the person has to actually care. If nothing matters to a person outside of what their own needs are, then they won’t even want to be an Uber Judge. So, if the person cares about the world around them, and by world, I don’t mean that they have to know every single intimate detail of every little thing, but more or less just what they actually see, then they’re pretty qualified.” “So it requires empathy?” she asked. “Yes, and yet, that’s just a part of it. Empathy means that you feel as you observe others feeling. The other part is also a willingness to notice which I tend to believe is almost as important as the empathy itself.” “Tell me about noticing.” “It’s the opposite of tunnel vision. When we think of noticing, we think about things accidentally coming to our attention as they enter our field of vision. People who have tunnel vision can care about what they see, and people who have tunnel vision can notice what crosses that limited scope, but this is different. To actually notice the way an Uber Judge needs to notice, there has to be a deliberate choice to widen your scope of vision.” Sharon laughed, “So do you have a special tool that allows you to measure the scope of vision, so that you know when it’s wide enough to Uber Judge?” The laugh tickled him, charming him just a little more, “Oh no. There’s no way to measure such a thing. The important thing is the desire to widen that scope, and the desire keep widening it.” “So caring and noticing an Uber Judge make,” summed up Sharon. “Those are the fundamentals, yes,” said the Uber Judge. “Now,” said Sharon, pausing for gravitas, “why wrestling?” “Well,” said the Uber Judge drawing a breath, “I am a wrestler. It’s become part of my life, the aspect of an entertainer, and the aspect of a competitor. It’s part of who I am. For a while I thought that the Uber Judge’s main responsibility was administrative, to sit in judgement over wrestling, but that was actually foolishness on my part.” “So the Uber Judge isn’t perfect?” asked Sharon, a comfortably teasing tone in her voice. “Oh absolutely not,” said the UJ with a smile, “far from it. I realized this only after my hand was more or less forced, and I had to face the facts. An important distinction of the Uber Judge is that he’s not actually seated above anyone else-” “Which is to say,” Sharon interrupted helpfully, “that he’s not exactly like the Judge in a court case.” “Right,” said UJ, “he’s more like a juror in that respect. He is an equal to everybody else, a peer. His decisions are rendered from that position, not the high seat.” “But we lost track a bit there, you were explaining the choice to be a wrestler.” “Right,” said UJ, “in the simplest terms, it’s what I am. It’s what I ought to be doing. It’s what I do best.” “I noticed that you distinguished that wrestling is a kind of calling, and yet the aspect of being the Uber Judge is a choice. Is it also a calling?” “No. At least not for me. As we said before, I'm not perfect, and the demands of being an Uber Judge are pretty high in terms of what they require. I continue to struggle to perform as best as I can.” “And how is wrestling different?” “Well,” said the Uber Judge with some shame, “in terms of activities and jobs and other callings, don’t there seem to be much better things a person could be doing with their time than wrestling?” Sharon laughed. “To be objective about it, shouldn’t an Uber Judge be an activist? Someone embroiled in politics? Someone who through caring and noticing, devotes one hundred percent to improving the state of the world? Probably. But at the core of my self, I don’t know if I could do those things, and if I could do them, I'm not so sure that I could do them well. But wrestling, being something that I know, something that I love dearly, has an irresistible call to me. And while it’s such a small thing, the aspect of entertainment does serve an important purpose. It’s trivial in a sense, in that the conflict and competition among wrestlers doesn’t do much to change the state of the world, but part of its charm and importance is because it’s trivial. Little things do matter. Engaging the imagination of our audience, providing that entertainment, is something that is genuinely good. And that feeling of goodness, that appreciation of that small good that wrestling provides to audience, I firmly believe is at the heart of every wrestler’s love for wrestling.” “I think you’re right,” Sharon said, “the little things do matter. Why do you think you’ve been successful so far?” “Because I'm the flavor of the month,” said the Uber Judge, and it wasn’t a joke. It in a schizoid sense, he continued speaking in a perfectly calm manner, even though he was horrified by what was coming out of his own mouth. “How so?” asked Sharon, a newscaster’s look of concern and interest summoned across her face. “I'm new to the fans of the PTC. The aspect of its impressive bureaucracy has remained a fascinating aspect for the fans. The fact that I appear to be a former part of that bureaucracy lends a sense of the mystique that surrounds the PTC itself. Now, I'm sure that mystique won’t ever fade for the PTC itself, but my association with that mystique can only last for so long. When I eventually stop being new to the fans of the PTC they will lose interest, and that loss of interest will be sudden and definitive. Simply, a time is going to come when people will see me, hear what I have to say, and go, `more of the usual from the Uber Judge, remember when he was over as the hot new thing?’ The shine will fade, and my abilities will be recognized for what they are: above average at best.” “That speaks for the idea of enjoying your spotlight while you can,” remarked Sharon. “Oh, if I wasn’t a complete moron I would be doing just that. In reality, I'm a textbook obsessive compulsive freak, and almost every waking moment of my life is spent fussing over one thing or another. My sidekick the Under Miner is definitely appreciating the pure joy of it, but that’s always been the fundamental difference between him and I. He enjoys and embraces life, while I go absolutely apeshit trying to control everything.” “So you have the tools and the circumstances to be perfectly happy in lucid moments like this dream, but you’re not using them properly. That’s too bad.” “Oh, it’s absolutely shameful. And now that I realize that I'm dreaming I do not want to wake up. This dream feels good, and let me tell you, it feels good just to be looking at you and talking to you.” “Of course,” replied Sharon, “I'm an aspect of your idealized view of a woman. I would hope you’d enjoy my presence.” “And that’s just the thing,” said the Uber Judge, “when I in your presence in reality, I think I’d fail to enjoy it, just because of how fucked up I am.” “Mmmmmm,” said Sharon, with an appreciative nod laden with gravitas, “well, I suggest we use the time we have wisely.” “And make sweet love the way I’ve always wanted to?” asked the Uber Judge hopefully. “Oh no,” said Sharon, “that’s just wish fulfillment. And you know how that can be. Instead, let’s use your lucidity to help you. Tell me your biggest concern right now, and I’ll help talk you through it.” “Oh that’s a great idea,” said the Uber Judge, “My biggest concern is obviously Sebastian Moraco.” “Why?” asked Sharon. “I'm afraid of him,” replied the Uber Judge. “Why?” “His youth, his fire, his rage. His desire, his determination to win. There are things that discipline and experience can accomplish, and there are things they cannot. There are people reason can reach, and there are people it cannot. Imagine a violent doberman, charging towards me, intent on only the kill, and all I have to use against it are my negotiation skills. The Doberman will not give pause to hear my reasons for being in its territory, in its way. It only knows that I'm the enemy, I am the stranger, that I am its opponent, and it cannot be deterred from the attack. I feel I am entering into a state like this with Sebastian.” “Ahhh,” said Sharon, “you feel what has worked for you against your other opponents, might not necessarily work against Sebastian. He’s driven, has all the charm and health of youth, and you feel powerless against this onslaught.” Sharon folded her hands and cocked her head to the side, “But I couldn’t help but notice that in the hypothetical scenario with the attack dog, that all you had was your negotiation skills, and that’s not necessarily true. Were a charging dog attacking you, you’d also have your hands and feet; you’re not completely helpless. While it’d be difficult and perhaps, bloody, and violent, you could defend yourself from the dog.” Sharon cocked her head to the other side, and continued, “More so, what is the biggest weapon of that charging dog? Not his claws and teeth, but the paralyzing fear it would bring with it. That fear immobilizes you, making you feel completely helpless when you’re actually not. If you disregard the fear, and see the attack accurately, you are empowered to respond accordingly.” “You’ve complained of your fear, and it threatens you far more than Sebastion Moraco does. Control it. Use the discipline you know you possess, and take control of that match, take control of that ring. Sebastion is driven, but the state of being driven contains momentum, and you are aware and savvy enough to use that momentum to your advantage.” Sharon smiled, and the beauty of it calmed the Uber Judge, “When the PTC loses interest in the Uber Judge, it will lose interest. That is beyond your control, so let it go. In the meantime, you need to use your strengths, your ability, and your talent, to achieve as much as you can while that interest is high, and perhaps you might successfully sustain that interest longer than you anticipate. Giving in to your fear of it is a choice that you can identify as foolish. Do not make it.” Sharon stood up, and crossed the distance to the Uber Judge in a few short steps, leaning close to his ear, where Uber judge could faintly smell vanilla and musk, he could both feel her breath and hear her whisper, “Live, breath, thrive.” == When he woke, the Uber Judge was enlightened. == For almost an hour. Then he returned to his normal obsessive compulsive self. == In spite of that, within him in his most secret of places, he embraced the concept that true courage is not defined by actions made in the absense of fear, but by actions taken in spite of it.
