Fanning the Flames

29 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-29 The last rays of waning daylight streamed through slanted window blinds, jutting into a silent chamber shrouded in relative darkness. Beads of sweat glistened on his brow, mingling with blood running free of a gash just under the hairline, but he'd decided to bear with it; a shower was out of the question here. Between his traitorous brothers and those parties supporting Mildred McMann, he could ill afford to lower his guard as long as he lingered in the unkempt bowels of this arena. He didn't particularly enjoy being hunted, especially not under the current circumstances, but it wasn't something he was unaccustomed to after openly spilling the blood of so many enemies. In an odd sense, there was always a hint of satisfaction. He supposed it was challenge that nullified the fear. McMann had been drugged beforehand; a rather subtle technique employed by agents of Jacob Diedrich had sent the fly spiraling unwittingly into the spider's web, that being the intricate scheme of one Ethan Knight. Although Mildred's initial performance in the bout had raised just cause in the murmurs of 'upset' stirring about the audience, he had ultimately faltered as the chemicals spread through his system and his speed and cunning failed to match the wicked cult prodigy. Surely individuals who gambled…..er, 'invested' in McMann's purported upset potential would be infuriated and seek a measure of retribution. Knight, though, had dealt extensively with their type on previous occasions, and nothing had befallen any of those vengeful, strongarm gangster-types but their own doom; between himself and the agents of the diabolic Sect of Black Wisdom, he doubted any outside the most powerful Mafia families could outmatch them in the cloak-and-dagger tactics prevalent (and favored) among their methods. "Even professional wrestling can't escape corruption," he sighed with a hint of amusement, drumming his fingers on the rickety bench. "This sport's not a farcry from our own seedy little world anymore really. A shame." "The profession has never been known for its foundation of integrity or brotherly love either," came the soft reply. A silhouette, lithe and slender, stirred against the blinds and peered between the window shades into the dying light of the outside. "Bear in mind that you're talking about a lifestyle where drugs and perversity were once the norm and not the exception." "Touche, my lady." Close friend and pseudo-mentor to the young Knight, Marion 'Hemlocke' Sterling was another of Bruce Shanahan's primary subordinates and charged with overseeing things from her abode on the outskirts of the Pine Barrens of New Jersey. Much like the primal wilderness she lurked on the fringe of, Sterling herself was an enigma. While well-known as an educated, refined lady of near unparalleled beauty, the borderline-hermit was equally renowned for both her acute knowledge of dark things and her innate cruelty. A black widow in every sense of the word, she had married three suitors in the past and left all three unions with a substantially larger bank account. And husbands encased in boxes under six feet of earth, victims of terrible 'accidents' soon after marriage. Not bad for a woman of barely thirty. And her current interest centered on the apprentice of the dark guru, the man she accompanied this evening in his wrestling obligations. One silken hand gently smoothed her dress as she surveyed the quickly-clouding exterior, her face an apathetic mask as she glanced at the fans milling about the outside and the stone-faced gorillas in suits stalking among them. Whether they were security or hired goons of McMann's supporters still couldn't be discerned, but they weren't about to take chances. Informants of both sides prowled the grounds even now, hers having indicated that Ethan's prior notions were spot-on. "Besides." She paused momentarily, turning away from the window as her arms crossed beneath her breasts. "You can't fool me into thinking that these things happen every weekend at shows across the country. These competitions aren't a common occurrence, and even then, organized crime tends to avoid wrestling tournaments. Do you really fear McMann's thugs so much, young lion?" She had adopted the nickname for Ethan as a reference to both his tenacity and the golden mane of hair he'd sported until recently. He'd grumbled vehemently about hopes it would grow back quickly after looking in the mirror. "I doubt they have any connection to the man himself," Ethan responded with a shake of the head. "But my instincts tell me that dear old dad will feel the repercussions far more than Mildred, if what we gathered is accurate. It's not like Pete Rose betting on his own performance, it's all outside with some input from those close. In either case, his run is over. Jacob Diedrich may even receive a 'Thank You' card for his efforts." Dirty towel in hand, he casually wiped away a mix of sweat and blood that had trickled down his forehead. Footsteps echoed in the hallway, moving away from them, and Ethan sighed with relief. The exhausted Sect representative appeared downcast and depleted as he sat with head hung and eyes closed, but if those same steps turned and approached then one could be sure he'd be on his feet like a cat, already deliberating how to best channel death. The last twelve months really had transformed the young man; it was a positive change if you wanted your man to be like something straight out of Splinter Cell, but the inverse otherwise. "I take it Almasy butchered that other swine. This….this…" He snapped his fingers, a gesture of his forgetfulness. When the initial announcement came, the other had been a comparable unknown that occupied but another meager moment of his time. A seasoned veteran with the pedigree of Almasy would surely dispose of him. "MacGregor. And no. It seems last year's champion wasn't up to last year's form. He's out of the competition." Ethan's only notable reaction was an indifferent grunt. He had particularly relished the fact that he may be meeting the 'virtuous' crusader Seymour Almasy, but though that possibility had been extinguished, it did nothing to weaken his resolve. In his eyes, one vermin was as good as another and they would inevitably all end up the same way. Whatever the means, the end would be the same. A grin crept across his face again at the same time that Lady Sterling's hand rested on his bare shoulder. "Between Almasy's early departure and the absence of both Wolfenden and Arsonist from the competition, you're the only one remaining from the late stages of last year. How do you fancy that, Brother Knight?" "In the whole, inconsequential. But I don't totally dismiss it as a potential omen of what it yet to come either…." It was the final, fleeting thought he could embrace in regards to the tournament for the time being. The first peal of thunder muted the sound of a muffled gunshot to all but the untrained ear and firm footsteps padding down the hall followed in its wake. He was involved in two drastically varying professions, and his activities in each had drawn a pair of matching bullseyes on his chest. He could only speculate as to which one had brought about this particular incident as the shuffling stopped outside the door, three men barging abruptly into the dimly-lit room without so much as a knock. Judging from their sidearms and sunglasses, he surmised that announcing their entrance wasn't something they did often to begin with. The smallest of the trio removed the shades and glowered at Ethan Knight, a glare so baleful one would think it was a personal vendetta between the two as opposed to something written off as ruthless business. "Ah, Mr. Knight?" His casual tone belied the aggressive stance of he and his kin. Knight's eyes remained cold as he nodded, addressing his presumed soon-to-be killer with detached indifference. He didn't even rise. "That I am. And what shall I call you? Agent Smith? Are you here to pull the plug on me, Agent Smith?" He felt a rise of reserved glee at the red flushing in the other's cheeks. Sterling's young lion could already tell that the other didn't have the talent he did, from the hesitant eyes down to his overly rigid stance. Two rookie attempts on his life in as many weeks, it was Nyarlathotep's own luck. "You can call me Brad," replied the other. "Typically I prefer Mr. Swanson, but seeing as you are both to die shortly, I'll grant you that reprieve." The satisfied look on his face spoke volumes for his confidence and self-indulgent wit. "Fair deal, no?" "I'd prefer addressing you as Bubbles myself, if it were up to me to choose." Knight's demeanor certainly didn't match that of a typical man ready to be executed, responding with his own bit of humor and throwing one foot up on the bench. "But if you insist, 'Brad', so be it. Who am I to rain on a man's parade?" He could have fun with this guy at the least; Brad Swanson hadn't drawn his ire as The One Once Called Carmen had. His elementary tactics seemed a thing to pity, besides. One of the others barely suppressed a chuckle, but an ill-meaning glimpse from Swanson quickly drew silence. Sterling, on the other hand, beamed broadly and openly, seemingly on the same page as her associate. Gliding across the room, she stopped between Ethan and his intended murderer, throwing back long amber hair to reveal her low neckline and the fullness it revealed. As anticipated by the renowned black widow, it did well to attract the eyes of Swanson and company. Once called a woman of such exquisite beauty that diamonds lacked elegance in her presence, she hadn't amassed her notoriety by being a prude, and her three dead husbands could attest to her deadly talents. The way Swanson and his thugs ogled Lady Sterling and neglected him, he may as well have faded out of existence for a long minute. Ethan only sighed, rolling his eyes. "So, exactly what is your business here, Brad? Outside the whole murdering deal, of course." He gradually elevated himself, just slightly taller than the petite Sterling and peering over her head directly at Swanson like a curious child. "What outraged soul sent you to off me today, hmm? Surely you have no qualms about answering the dying request of a man who should be pleading for his life?" Swansom seemed hesitant albeit his arrogance. With a shrug came the response he was looking for. "Seems fair enough, considering your knowledge will never reach anyone of value. Marquis Boller and Daniel Colby are my contractors. Apparently, you did something to really aggravate them both, eh?" The man really was an amateur; at least The One Once Called Carmen followed protocol to her grave. This time, Ethan couldn't curb a smile. "Obviously so, Agent Smith," chuckled the Sect negotiator/assassin, eyeing the dark suit and tie adorning each of his would-be killers. No sense of style at all, just that regurgitated Men-In-Black bullshit. "I'm not too endearing in my actions it appears, but then, death seems to come sooner rather than later for those in positions such as mine. But let's cut to the chase, what do you say? How do you intend to do this exactly?" One of Swanson's burly cohorts ambled across the room and glanced through the blinds. The impending rainstorm had sprung up and the parking lot was already soaked from the abrupt downpour, leaving the outside empty of life. Swanson and his boys seemed to think it was a blessing. They weren't alone, unlucky for them. The dark-suited man gestured to a snow-white car waiting beyond the entrance, wipers furiously clearing the windshield as quickly as it was covered again. The cliches were making Ethan's head hurt, and he could only guess from the blank look on her face that that his sultry accomplice shared his thoughts. "We accompany you out and put you in the car. We drive to a nice, secluded little grove just off the bay. Two bullets later, we toss you into a big hole and plant a tree over it. It seems a fitting end for one such as yourself, Mr. Knight. It's only a pity this gorgeous little peach has to share your fate." Sterling fluttered her eyelashes invitingly, but her true attitude was obvious through that transparent gesture. She was also contemplating how best to kill all three before they even reached their destination. A polite golf clap greeted Swanson's scheme. Radiating as much arrogance as ever, Ethan sardonically applauded his proclaimed killer, all through the appearance that the moron never appeared to even pick up on his sarcasm. Neither did his flunkies, all the while adjusting their stark-black ties like rejects out of Goodfellas. The fact that this was Boller and Colby's crack team of hired guns saddened and amused the young man at the same time. Maybe his own talents weren't as well-regarded as he'd been led to believe. The charade had been fun up to now, but it was dragging and time to move things along. "Well then, why delay the inevitable?" The brash young negotiator pointed to the door, grabbing up his duffel bag in his free hand. "If this is what fate has chosen, why fight it any longer?" Swanson only nodded, Ethan catching a whiff of cheap cologne as he moved closer to the second-rate hitman. Marion cast her companion a foxy smile before making her way to the door under a faux air of sullen defeat. Their captors continued to buy it hook, line and sinker. * * * * Knight paid little attention to the words of congratulations from arena staff and the dull, obligatory smiles intended for him as he navigated the corridors, explaining that the three men in dark suits were his 'entourage of bodyguards' when asked about their gruff expressions and rude conduct. To them, he was just a wrestler with an outside life veiled in obscurity, and that was where things would remain. Until this was resolved, wrestling was regarded a mere hobby, just as it tended to be these days. The side metal door slid open into an empty side lot, the rain continuing to drum down around them as umbrellas were hurriedly unfurled to avoid the downpour. Knight could only smile as water splashed onto Marion's blouse, further eliciting gawking stares from two of the three men as she was drenched from waist-up. Swanson retained a business-like swagger and avoided looking at the amber-haired beauty, but it was painfully obvious that the other two wouldn't require a lot of distraction to overtake when the time came. Three of the four sides around them presented high brick walls riddled with air vents and curtainless windows, only a couple dumpsters and empty pallets scattered on the outskirts, else he'd have just done it then and there. Witnesses, however, couldn't be afforded at this juncture; too much was at stake to be overhasty. At the lone open end of the lot hummed a long, white cadillac, back doors yawning open. A black-clad man slumped over the roof to each side, apparently in discussion as umbrellas screened them from the hard rainfall. "Lazy bastards," mumbled Swanson under his breath and without slowing his approach toward the car. "I told those worthless assholes to be right outside this door….." Silence swept over the previous-cocky hitman as he glanced at the two men outside the vehicle. From a distance they appeared to be leaning on the roof in conversation, but closer inspection revealed them both to be unconscious, splayed out over the white roof. Specks of blood dotted the surface, protected by the wavering unbrellas and unwashed away by the howling rain, but both still breathed; whoever had done the deed hadn't been aiming to kill, and likely for the same reason as Ethan and Lady Sterling. The same proved true of the driver, sprawled out on the other side of the vehicle, third victim of a yet-unseen attacker. No, it can't be. Thoughts raced through the head of the wiry young wrestler as he examined the scene, Swanson frantically cursing up a storm as he slammed a white-knuckled fist on the roof of the car. Surely he wouldn't have the gall to leave three guys laying in the middle of a parking lot like this. Certainly not alone….. "Fucking wonderful!" shouted Swanson, throwing his arms up in the air. "So who decided to up and KO my men? You happen to know anything about this, kid??" The disheveled hitman leveled his gaze on Ethan before glancing around the car, studying the scene of the crime for potential clues. The search would have proved unnecessary. What they were looking for found them first. The click of a gun being readied diverted their attention from the well-apparent carnage. Swanson was the first to face the sound, followed in turn by his two assistants and their assigned targets, and immediately found himself staring down the silver barrel of another gun. A pair of the big men lumbering amongst the fans earlier, angry scowls emblazoned on stubble-worn faces, now stood drenched and unshielded beneath the torrential rain as they sloshed toward the party of five. A dumpster just visible around the corner had been their refuge over the last couple minutes. "Hand 'em over." A forceful and stern command, its potency magnified by the .38 special in his hand. "We didn't kill your friends and we don't intend on killing you if it's not absolutely necessary. Just don't be a fuckin' moron and the only blood we'll spill today is these two." Surprise flashed across the face of Swanson as he expressed his displeasure in a rage-filled cry. "Now who the fuck are you?! What the hell is this??" Knight figured he could credit the man for not showing fear in light of having a pistol waved in front of his nose, but then he just attributed it to lack of brains. Crediting that buffoon just seemed more than he could take right now. "Associates of Gaetano Napoli." The words were delivered in thick Italian as if the man were a direct import from Sicily. Everything else about him fit the stereotype perfectly. "I think that's all ya fuckheads need to know so it's all ya will fucking know." It didn't take a genius to gather that Napoli was one of those 'investors' who had put stock in McMann overcoming Ethan only to see his venture ruined via some tainted pre-match cuisine. The stocky gun-wielder and his tall companion were all business, occupying the same general role as Swanson but on the payroll of Napoli. As if having to put up with a half-dozen wannabe murderers wasn't bad enough, now he had to endure this deluge of Hollywood cliches. Ethan could at first only shake his head and mumble under his breath as Swanson, irate at the Italian's interference in his assignment, hammered the husky other man with an array of insults and demands to 'just walk away'. Unfortunately it seemed Mr. Gaetano Napoli wouldn't approve of that, and thus neither would the stern Italian triggerman. Costa was the last name, grudgingly disclosed in an effort to shut Swanson's mouth, but that was all he was willing to give. Barring a bullet for Swanson if he didn't do as he was told. Knight cursed under his breath as he deliberated what was happening. This 'Costa' and his oversized associate were a wrench thrown in the gears; he had already decided how he was going to remedy this situation with Colby and Boller's contractors, but the introduction of two new players had changed the script completely. If they compromised into just killing the duo as a unit, he and Sterling would really be in hot water. "Mr. Napoli wants these chumps buried somewhere that not even the worms will think to look for 'em to chew on," rambled Costa, shooting awkward glances at both Knight and Sterling. "The kid cost him a rather large sum of money and the lady has the virtue of the Devil Himself, besides being his accomplice. They're comin' with me." "Over my dead body, you Venetian greaseball," declared Swanson, solidifying his assertion by jamming his own gun into the Italian's ribs. "I'll be damned if I lose my pay because your boss is sore over dropping a few bucks." It had taken only a moment for the situation to escalate into a full-scale stand-off. With the Italian and the cult-contracted killer levelling their weapons on one another, each man's associates did likewise, and in a flash a curved blade had found its way from her blouse and into Lady Sterling's fingers, the razored edge braced against the throat of Brad Swanson's second cohort. For a long moment, not a sound passed beyond the unceasing patter of the rain and a crack of distant thunder. Apprehension washed over the traitorous youth. This had never been a possibility accounted for in his mental blueprints. "I seem to have arrived at an inopportune time. My apologies." "What?!" Swanson let out a disgrunted snort at the new voice, not bothering to swivel around and face its source. Recognizing the firm, almost-fatherly tone, only Knight and Sterling dared tear their eyes from the multitude of trained killers. Less than a minute and already three new faces in the picture. In the confusion, no one had even noticed the man in the poncho silently duck out from behind the building and slide his own polished sidearm into the fray, directing the barrel between the eyes of Costa's burly accomplice. Knight's own trump card had finally emerged, albeit at an unexpected time, and it was a bittersweet sensation for both the young lion and the green-clad vixen. Regardless of the aid he reluctantly offered, hatred burned in the eyes of the fiery Blackhelm operative as he glimpsed back at Ethan. The Blackhelm Coalition, a secretive militant organization with financial and political ties to the Vatican itself, had been chartered with the mission of rooting out and destroying the same sinister forces that Ethan Knight and his brotherhood sought to fortify. These times made for odd bedfellows it appeared; Captain Marcus Stryker was one of the faction's more convicted zealots and efficient regional commanders, and yet his own Beretta was now drawn with the intention of saving Knight's life. The spinning of this web had become even more intricate. "Another wretched vigilante?" Swanson grumbled the words almost under his breath, never taking his eyes off of the unarmed Knight. Shifting his attention could be deadly right now. "Jesus, kid, how many people have you pissed off?" "More have become entangled in his plots than you could possibly know, hitman," Stryker echoed coldly, turning and shoving the gun barrel into the jaw of Swanson before eyeing the Italian. "Now release the boy and the lady. This would be utterly pointless bloodshed, my friend, the real enemy resides elsewhere." Costa's lip twisted into a smile as he shifted his own handgun in line with the Blackhelm agent's steady gaze. At this point, only Ethan Knight was still unarmed, and even he was in danger of taking a bullet from not inches away. "Good luck, soldier. Talkin' to this nutjob is like talkin' to a box of rocks." Stryker pursed his lips, eyeing up the opposition and noting the gun in his face as rain continued to pelt all the warring parties. "You have a knack for getting people to try and kill you, Ethan Knight. I should know. It's like some sort of an odd magnetism." He cracked a grin as if amused by his own dry wit. In light of the stability of the situation, or lack thereof, Ethan could find little humor in Stryker's jest. With knives to throats and barrels jammed into heads, it was like a still-shot from a John Woo film. With a .45 revolver pressed to his temple and eyes leveled on Costa's trembling trigger-finger, the Italian's own .38 special aimed at Stryker's skull, Ethan only hissed in irritation. His words were marked with a tired indifference. "This has been a hell of a past two weeks." The puddle around his feet exploded in a wild spray, the cunning young lion moving so quickly his best-positioned enemy couldn't react with so much as a trigger pull. A veritable wall of water showered all over those around him as he caught the arm of Brad Swanson, twisted up, and used the man as a temporary shield before disarming him. Taking a hint from the quick reaction, the battle-hardened Marcus Stryker used the element of surprise to drive an elbow into the throat of Swanson's nearest assistant, toppling him instantly, and fire off two silenced rounds at Sterling's aggressor in one fluid motion. The pair of slugs ripped into the kneecap and upper calf of Costa's brawny partner, sending the big man to the pavement with a pained grunt as Sterling evaded his flailing arms. Costa turned to fire on the swift-footed Knight, his gun-wielding arm meeting with the palm of a recovered Brad Swanson. The hitman buried a fist into the gut of the big Italian before pivoting and launching him onto the hood of the Cadillac with a judo-esque throw that even impressed the young lion, the windshield nearly smashing out on the driver side before Costa tumbled to the ground in a heap. Perhaps the man wasn't as inept as he'd been led to believe. An arm like a tree branch cut off his look of surprise, and the circulation to his brain, wrapping around his throat and locking him in a rear chokehold. It lasted only a split second, however, before the woman in green twirled the knife and dug a long slash in the forearm of Swanson's number-two man, severing both the hold and several tendons in one strike. A solitary shot rang out and the sound of a ricochet resounded across the side lot, smoke trailing from the gun in Swanson's grip as he wrestled with Marcus Stryker, the muzzle of the weapon aimed at the clouds as they struggled. A fierce haymaker courtesy of the young lion loosed the hitman's grip on the weapon, sending the firearm clattering to the ground just before another crushing right hook placed the owner beside it. Just like that, it was over. Though there was blood washing across the lot and bones had been undoubtedly broken in the scuffle, there wasn't a true casualty among them. There was relief evident in the deep sigh of the Blackhelm man, but it made no difference to either Knight or Sterling. They'd have killed sheerly for the rush if a level of self-control hadn't been necessary. The former whistled as he surveyed the scene alongside Marion, seemingly not appreciating the gravity of the situation. "I have to say, Captain Stryker, that between you walking out of thin air, Agent Smith here, and the goddamn cast of The Sopranos, I felt like I'd walked into a bad medley of prime-time TV shows." The Sect retainer scoffed as he regarded the tumultuous scene, groans rising up from semi-conscious men already clutching broken bones and dislocated joints, fumbling for their firearms. Oblivious to their pains, Ethan shrugged. "I won't bother to ask what took you so long. Now are we going to exchange pleasantries in the rain all night, or can we seek some shelter now? This car is looking pretty good now that Agent Smith and his gang won't need it while they take a nap." "Your party, cowboy." Stryker didn't wait for a response from the other. Regardless of his wording, he'd be damned if he waited for permission from a sworn enemy. "I'm glad to see you managed to curb your bloodlust for a while and not kill anyone. Does your esteemed leader know about this blasphemy?" The last words were delivered in a blend of malice and bitter sarcasm as Marion Sterling fingered the hilt of the knife she wielded in the skirmish. They obviously hadn't been directed at her, however. It took but a split second for the blade to be in the hands of the young lion, his speed even surprising the woman in green. Knight pivoted and launched the weapon overhand at the first shape he caught stirring, using the rear bumper for support in standing. The knife barrelled into the heaving chest of the rising Costa, piercing directly beneath the sternum with a thunk and dropping the Italian back to the pavement. He wouldn't move again this time, at least not until the morgue made their visit. Ethan looked back over at his ally, eyes lit with a sense of spiteful glee. He had wanted the last laugh before they made their getaway. "Sorry, had the craving. Shall we?" He slid back into place beside his sworn foe, not even offering a second glance at the dead man outside whose cold, empty eyes stared lifelessly at the gray heavens rolling overhead. His life had been as expendable as any other in the face of the assassin's challenged pride. "I'd think that between this and McMann, you'd be satisfied for tonight." Marcus was clearly repulsed at the actions of his pseudo-accomplice. Ethan only shrugged his indifference. Wrestling didn't offer the same thrill as slitting the throat of an insurgent to the cause, but then he could think of no other endeavor that offered that rush. Any who claimed otherwise, or even drew a parallel between the two like Stryker had, had never killed before. * * * * "You enjoyed the match then, Captain Stryker?" You could never mistake the query as anything other than an uncomfortable ice-breaker, cold and monotone as it was. The temperature outside the car had plummeted as it briefly traversed the state highway but the air inside the vehicle was only a tad warmer. If Marcus didn't know better, he'd think more than the pair's souls had been destroyed in service to the demons they worshipped. "I'd seen Bruce compete a handful of times when he was still among our ranks," said Marcus with equal (lack of) enthusiasm. "Different styles, I must admit. But then, we're not here to discuss your little wrestling tournament, are we, Ethan Knight?" There were no pleasantries or idle jokes amongst foes, and his vow to one day kill the young lion still burned in the back of Stryker's mind. Ethan felt no different in relation to the Blackhelm operative. There was little outside spite linking them, excluding the necessity of business. "No, captain, we're not. You and me, Marcus, we live wholly different lifestyles and bow before different altars. You claim to follow a pseudo-righteous path, ridding the world of those whom the corrupt Church selectively deem a threat to humanity, its empire and the immoral way of life everyone clings to so affectionately….." "And you break every commandment in the hopes of resurrecting hideous gods that Bruce and his collection of forbidden books have brainwashed you into thinking will bless you for your servitude when the End Times arrive." He cut off the other without so much of a second thought, venomously enough that his insolence would earn a dagger to the heart under normal circumstances. But such measures couldn't be afforded in the face of what was to come, not until all had come full-circle. "To each his own, captain." The younger man appeared distracted, eyes pointed forward and peering through the cracked glass. Marion remained silent, focused on navigating the road. "I'm still unclear as to your motivations, Ethan Knight." The still-young veteran was hesitant to address his 'partner' as anything but. 'Mister' was too dignified for the blonde devil, but much less would suggest fellowship outside the bounds of necessity. "The man has fed, clothed and taught you for the better part of the past couple years. Why the sudden need to see another seize his power, and him put in his grave?" "Petty vengeance has no place in our business, and little else drives the man anymore." He sounded truly scandalized by the whole ordeal, remembering the deaths of Maurice Thorne and Howard Ignalls particularly. "Answering to your own demons before you answer to the Old Ones is as great a transgression as anything the nonbelievers do and Father Shanahan has perpetrated too much for the sake of his own vindictive cravings. Their glory and ascension must not be superseded by fancies of the mortal flesh, not even if it results in 'His' fall." Stryker's eyes drifted, following the skyline silhouetted against the gray horizon. Streaks of orange protruded through the clouds to brighten the view ever so palely, the final remnants of dwindling twilight. He couldn't help but grasp the irony of the sight, smirking. "If this is going to work, please abstain from praising those infernal……'things' you pray to." His pause indicated that he had decided to substitute a different word than he had fancied using. Incurring the wrath of Knight and Sterling wouldn't help this plan move any more smoothly. "I ask for nothing more in this partnership." "Don't be overly hasty, Captain Stryker." Ethan addressed him with a wary chuckle, eyes never once meeting his new 'ally'. "I consider it less of a partnership and more of a brief truce borne of need. That said, I'll do as you ask. I'm not an unreasonable man, regardless of what you and your brethren may think." Marcus only bit his lip indignantly at the comment. He hated what Bruce Shanahan had become, but a part of him despised Ethan Knight even moreso. Here he was making back-alley alliances with those who had helped kill his partner, and friend, Jago Sensaki, and all for the purpose of getting closer to an old friend to stop his evil plans. The nightmare was as a vivid as the night it happened; he could still see the blood spilling from Sensaki's wounds, hear the anguished screams of the countless dying in that blasphemous pit of the doomed and the damned. Agreeing to this shaky treaty had been a test of his own allegiences, a constant reflection over what he thought Jago would have wanted. He was still at odds over whether his decision was the right one. "Things are properly arranged then?" "Everything is set, Marcus. What must be done, shall be. And you're comfortable with it?" A part of Stryker cringed as he recalled the past, not the least of which included Bruce Shanahan once saving his life from those denizens of the dark. The same darkness that had eventually consumed him. "Yes. You realize, of course, that my aid will be rather limited in these future endeavors….." "There's no need to elaborate further, captain," murmured Ethan, cutting off the other. "I'm already aware that the Blackhelm leadership doesn't approve of you intervening in our affairs, and for the moment they remain blissfully unaware of it. Right now, you're as much a rogue helping me as I am in helping to destroy your world. I'll keep your little secret in the dark." Marcus merely stroked at his beard as he peered into the mist forming outside. "I'd not be so quick as to group us as one yet, Ethan Knight. Helping you silence a madman bent on attaining immortality and murdering God doesn't even put me on the same echelon as anyone trying to revive abominable gods for a similar purpose. Don't blur your logic….'ally'." That word caused a shudder in both, perhaps moreso in the cultist. Never did he think he'd resort to having one of these Blackhelm lapdogs watch his back against his own people. Matters had become so damn complicated, though he knew part of the blame rested on his own shoulders. Ethan Knight and all he held shaky treaties with strived to bring into power the most dark and decadent of cosmic immortals, but Bruce Shanahan planned on joining the ranks of those same shadowy deities with the forbidden knowledge he had unearthed. Faced with stopping an immortal megalomaniac intent on personally burning down Heaven, Stryker felt that helping one in Ethan Knight's position was a small price to pay. Surely even the Coalition, and the Vatican, could overlook such a comparably minor sin in light of present affairs. The lesser of two evils. That's what it amounted to for Captain Marcus Stryker. Besides, he fully intended on killing the soulless little imp when the terms of their treaty were fulfilled, and he knew that the conniving bastard realized it. Regardless of alignment, they were too much alike for his personal comfort. "On the contrary, Christian swine," said the other matter-of-factly, "don't be so quick to overlook your own transgressions. The judgement offered by your Bible and your priests isn't the all-encompassing finale you may think. Who is to say exactly whether or not your loving father in Heaven will be so quick to absolve you of your sin." "I'm willing to believe any being who would send his lone son to die on a cross for me is capable of such forgiveness." Knight's laugh was quiet yet decidedly wicked. His childhood scars had freed him of such a faith that the captain and his superiors held onto. "What a well-conceived illusion. And you mindless altar boys have swallowed every bit of it…." The rest of the ride passed with nary another word. Regardless of his treasonous designs, not a doubt still persisted in the Blackhelm officer that Ethan Knight was a man built in the image of Bruce Shanahan; just as his deranged mentor, the boy endeavored to strengthen the Sect of Black Wisdom into an empire to rival the Church itself, and ultimately topple the spiritual dynasty en route to a state of 'religious totalitarianism', reveling across a desolate world cast into anarchy by the reigning Old Ones. He couldn't allow that to happen certainly, but comparably he was still a growing pup suckling from a rabid wolf. The day he matured, the Coalition would undoubtedly see his head mounted as a trophy. Stryker would be behind the trigger if he had any say in the matter. He was fairly sure the kid already knew that too, and he was correct. As they rode together in silence, Ethan Knight already fantasized about running the smug Blackhelm operative through with his favorite blade. This would be a tribulation in the truest sense of the word, and as he deliberated the future, the wily upstart called Archibald MacGregor was the furthest thing from his mind. Wrestling was a secondary thought. * * * * Everything we do has consequences. There's no escaping it. Every choice has its aftermath, every road in life bears its own fruit (or poison). If you hold true to most of the teachings of the world, following a life of honor, piety and virtue, you'll supposedly reap the treasures of everlasting fulfillment. Or if you're in the right part of the globe, rushing a band of Americans while wearing a girdle of plastique will deliver the same blessings. But then if you know anything of our doctrine, which I'm sure you don't, you know the doddering fools preaching such things are slowly drowning in their own deception. The consequence they'll know is their own ignorant damnation. Condemned alongside the fallen immortal they still call savior, but will come to call destroyer. Their dreams of an idyllic afterlife will come crashing down in one dying surge of rebellion, their final futile attempt to stop the siege of the gods they still call figments of madness this present day. You don't believe me? Pfft. Have it your way, friend. The Catholic Church banned the Necronomicon for a reason. For a society who cling so tightly to Biblical prophecy, they sure have an unnatural fear for contradictory reports of future history. Marcus Stryker intends on putting me in my grave the moment Shanahan is put in his; that I can be assured of given the hypocritical order of the Coalition and that corrupt Vatican. As an apostle of Father Shanahan, I've glimpsed into the vast Library of Celaeno and seen the true ambitions of the God they hold so dearly in their hearts, and knowing what I do I would never bow before such an insincere lord; hypocrisy and treachery are His domains just as much as they belong to Nyarlathotep and the hearts of mankind beat with that same vile fervor. At least the Old Ones don't deceive Their followers with hollow ideals of purity and innocence not even shared by Their evangelist. Pitiful sheep, yet I feel no sympathy for that level of weakness. Darwin's teachings far outshine those of that crucified sheepherder, and that's the way the universe is, like it or not, my friend. Ah, but I'm as bad as my mentor, wandering off-track. Father Shanahan always spoke highly of my ambition; my ascent to the position I hold among his top advisors was a swift one, and even now nothing will change. In the past I've dueled our enemies in the shadows with daggers, and in conference rooms with fluency of the mind and sharpness of the tongue, and the price for my acts shall not be an eternity drenched in fire and brimstone, but a standing among their most prized servitors. Surely fanning the flames of rebellion will have its own ramifications, but not in the way that God-fearing theologists would want to believe. Where Father Shanahan seeks progress through hate-stricken slaughter and ceremony that even our most open-minded kin won't tread near, I'll do so as They would wish. With praise and glory offered under Their hallowed names shall we bring doom upon the disbelievers, conquering the feeble-minded dogs that decree our destruction from their cathedrals and monasteries. When the cycle begins anew, it shall be I who stand at the helm. The world will cower before the Great Old Ones and beg at the foot of my throne as their mortal overseer. I, too, seek immortality. I'm simply not as brash in my privileges, and I do not need to be. I know what needs done. I'll see you in the abyss, MacGregor, and I won't be hard to find. I'll be the one ordering your suffering from atop my throne in the abyss, Favored of the Old Ones, as you stand chained among the nonbelievers. And when that day arrives, all the bourbon in the world won't ease your torment. I'll have you cursing your God over your mere existence. Yes, Archibald, you will come to know the darkest of consequences. Your tortured screams shall rend apart the burning heavens, and be the symphony that heralds my awakening. Pleasant dreams, Archie. Embrace the nightmares, they've been sent to prepare you…..
