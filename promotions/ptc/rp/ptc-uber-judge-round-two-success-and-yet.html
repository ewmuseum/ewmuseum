Round Two - The Sage of the Uber Judge : Success ... and yet

4 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Breath in, breath out. It’s real. Believe in it. It was with the most smug of satisfaction that the Uber Judge traveled to the PTC offices. It was with the most beneficiate of smiles that the Uber Judge rode the elevator up to the seventeenth floor. It was with the most polite and courteous knocks that the Uber Judge rapped on the door of the office of the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments. “Yes?” said the VPOPTCST, from his office. The Uber Judge sauntered into the room, pride oozing off of his robes like hastily-applied spray-on body scent. “Well, well, weeeeeeel,” said the Uber Judge. But before his intended monologue could take place, the VPOPTCST said, “My boy! My boy! Congratulations! You’re a smash hit!” standing up from his chair and offering a golf clap for the first ever Infinite Gauntlet Champion. “Yes, I did perform well in that match, didn’t I?” said the Uber Judge, unable to resist boasting. “Indeed, my good man, you did a fine job of it. Very impressive. A fantastic showing,” offered the VPOPTCST without a moment of hesitation. “And I proved something too, didn’t I?” asked the Uber Judge with a sly wink I his eye. The VPOPTCST didn’t bat an eyelash before responding, “Indeed, Uber Judge, you showed us that you’re in-ring skills far exceed that of your judging skills!” And at this, the Uber Judge said, “Huh?” and felt as if the bus he was riding on suddenly came to a halt, or that the record he was listening to was removed from the turntable with that violent scratching noise, or that the merry go round he was riding on BROKE DOWN and came to a sudden stop throwing him from that majestic brown horse. “Oh it’s true!” said the VPOPTCST without missing a step, “You’re a better wrestler than you ever were an administrator. While I didn’t watch your match, several of my underlings have assured me that you did a fantastic job, and that’s no small thing as those young men are quite hard to please. I think you’ve found your true calling.” “But-” muttered the Uber Judge. “But what?” kindly asked the VPOPTCST with a secret kind of viciousness, one that the Uber Judge did not notice, but we, the keen observers of these events, would know as a viciousness born of immoral delight. “But I came up here… to demand- er, well to ask for my old job back.” Silence. The VPOPTCST said in a very quiet voice, “And?” The Uber Judge gulped. He held the ridiculously oversized gavel in both hands, as he said with some small measure of awkwardness, “But I hoped… that… if I won, I could convince you to give me my old job back …. as a tournament administrator.” SILENCE. The Vice President of Prime Time Central Sponsored Tournaments stared at the Judge of All Creation for a brief window of time, the approximate amount of time for the proverbial other shoe to drop. “AH~! HAHAHAHAHAHAHAH~!” The face of the Uber Judge fell several inches, as the VPOPTCST held onto his belly as he laughed in his ergonomic office chair. He laughed. And he laughed. And he laughed far longer than one would think appropriate even under these circumstances. Finally, the VPOPTCST took a deep breath and said, “But that’s just absurd. You are a poor judge, and a simply fantastic wrestler. It’s so very clear to me that you are meant to be a wrestler in our tournaments, not an administrator.” “B-but,” said the Uber Judge, giving into stuttering, “but I thought that I might prove-” “Prove Shmoove, you nincompoop! You’ve proved that you’re good in that ring, and that our PTC fans love to hate you! They are in an uproar. We thought the Uber Judge would only have name recognition among the smarks, as they so call themselves, the foul creatures, but NO, there’s something that every PTC fan can hate about you, and that is simply splendid. You are a better draw than you ever were a judge!” The Uber Judge, who had seemed to shrink a few inches smaller, meekly said, “But I wanted to be a judge. I wanted to help shape the wrestling community. I wanted to open people’s eyes to the possibilities regarding what they might have missed. I wanted to help people see the things that they’d never seen before. I wanted to create a new standard of performance, so that everyone to come after would try harder, do more, and struggle to achieve an ever better level of ability. I don’t just want to succeed, I want my success to mean something, something that will matter in the business for years to come. That’s what my goal was.” The VPOPTCST slammed his phone down on the desk and said, “Oh, please excuse me, I had to take that call. Now what was it that you were saying?” The Uber Judge, emotional to the point of tears said, “I just said that I wanted-” “Oh right, right,” said the VPOPTCST, “you wanted your old job back. Yes, well, the answer is a definitive `no.’ And by `no’ I mean, `absolutely not, not in a hundred thousand years equals no’, are we clear? Good.” The Uber Judge and the Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments locked eyes. Again, silence reigned. After the pause, the VPOPTCST asked, “Do I need to activate the trap door again?” with just the barest hint of a threat in his voice. The Uber Judge shook his head no, and meekly left the office. = “Outrage,” said the Uber Judge with a sigh while he rode the elevator down the lobby. So weakened was the Uber Judge’s spirit that he could not declare his signature phrase with any enthusiasm. Weakly, he broke into his half hearted diatribe, “I possess merit. Through this merit I ought to have some sway over the state of the PTC, the PTC that I love and adore so very much, but I do not. I am successful, I am the first Infinite Gauntlet Champion, but what I truly want, has been denied me, seemingly by my very success. What does this mean?” “Am I not worthy to be Judge of All Creation? A few days ago I would’ve asserted that I am worthy with the greatest of confidence. Now? I am wounded and weak. My judgments are not valued. I would be prone to want to believe, in spite of the multitudinal persons who would defy my judgments, that I am correct and right in my decisions. But yet, but yet I am denied thoroughly. I thought I was fit to judge and administrate tournaments, but I am told that I am wrong. Is that Vice President of PTC Sponsored Tournaments more correct than I? Does he possess more knowledge than I? Does he know the way of the world better than I? “Again, my impulse cries out `NO!’ in the loudest of voices, and yet….” “and yet…” “It is his right and privilege to decide. And worse, he claims, with likely veracity, that his point of view is representative of the PTC fans, the fans who through my judgment I seek to serve. How could a poll lie? How could the fans be wrong, those who through their enjoyment are undeniably right in any choice they make?” The elevator door opened on the lobby, with the Uber Judge stepping out to make his way home. The tiniest sound of applause sounded. The Uber Judge looked up, to see that young man who worked the kiosk for the Infinite Gauntlet just the other day. Yes, this man was applauding. And making eye contact with The Uber Judge he said, “Bravo!” “Bravo!” “Bravo, Uber Judge, you had a fantastic match! Such a great job! Your execution of that Improbability Driver-” “Implausibility Driver,” correct the Uber Judge politely. “Yes, that’s what I said, the execution was fantastic. A splendid showing. I'm quite proud of you. There’s all sorts of possibilities open for you now.” The Uber Judge, not quite believing what he was hearing, waxed sardonic, and said, “Oh, how do you figure?” “Well,” said the enthusiastic and good spirited young man at the kiosk, “you’ve got plenty of options now. Being the first ever Infinite Gauntlet Champion is no small thing! Since you’re a free agent, you could negotiate joining any of the federations of Prime Time Central!” With great weakness the Uber Judge offered, “But what I really want is to-” “Be an administrator?!” said the good young man at the kiosk, his disbelief visible to all who’d care to look, “But that’s old news! Uber Judge, you’ve made your mark, as an administrator that is. But that time is passed. You had your influence in that regard, and it’s time for you to let it go! You’ve so much more to achieve as a competitor, and dare I say it, as a genuine member of a PTC fed!” “But,” protested the Uber Judge, “I adore the PTC, but as an outsider. I seek to look at it in awe, and remark on its immensity, its importance and significance, and I want to help it grow great and strong through that outsider point of view. I see a bold new future of Wrestling because of the PTC, and I so very want to contribute-” “And that’s the thing of it!” shouted the good young employee of the PTC, “you contribute to the PTC by participating in it! You’ve made the first step, you’ve become a competitor. How well can you contribute to the PTC by continuing to remain an outsider? You are the first ever Infinite Gauntlet Champion! Are you telling me that you don’t intend to join a PTC fed on the merit of that fantastic victory?” “Well,” said the Uber Judge, “I hadn’t thought of it in that regard.” “You fool!” shouted the very very good young man, “Who do you think you are? Seymour Almasy? Karina Wolfendem? Angelo Deville? The AWS Man, who is also known as Bill? Clyde? Duke Williams? That Doctor bloodymotherfucking Curiosity? Or any of PTC’s greats? You’ve no right to act like that! You are famous as a judge and an administrator because of the PTC! I dare say, Uber Judge, you owe anything you have to the PTC and by God you are obligated to give something back.” The Uber Judge, yet again, was struck silent. The man at the kiosk, satisfied that his point had been made, simply gave the Uber Judge a curt nod, and said in his most official and impersonal of voices, “Best of luck in the next round then, sir,” before going back to shuffling the few pieces of official looking paper behind his kiosk. The Uber Judge went back home. = Back at home, all was in order. And yet not. Though the mail was sorted into the correct piles, though all the glasses and dishes were either in the dishwasher or clean in their cupboards, though the newspaper had been folded perfectly on the coffee table in the leftmost bottom corner, though his boots were placed perfectly parallel on the section of carpet remnant next to his front door, though the bird feeder had been refilled… All was not well. The many birds, the delightful love affair of life, sound, and flight, had not returned to the yard. The house of the Uber Judge was clean, perhaps as a whistle was clean, but he could not take comfort from its cleanliness. Every space of furniture was dusted, every book in alphabetical order on his many bookshelves, first in fiction by author, then anthologies by editor, then nonfiction by author, followed by cookbooks, followed by graphic novels, followed by pure resource material (dictionaries, thesauruses, etymology guides). For all that the eye could see, everything was in perfect order. And. Yet. Not. There was a humming sound, perhaps a buzzing, a singular noise that existed within the spirit of the Uber Judge. Opening his mouth for the first time in a few hours, while seated in his couch (quite uncomfortably, oh the thorns of inner turmoil!) the Uber Judge said, “Change has come.” “In spite of my own ideas of worthiness,” said the Uber Judge, “I have been found unworthy.” “What will become of me?” he asked, and his plaintive cry was quite plaintive indeed. This was the sound of a man who is truly lost, giving voice to his desire for help. The Uber Judge fell to his knees on his living room floor. And… The Uber Judge prayed. = On his knees in his home, the Uber Judge gave voice to his prayer. “Oh that which has Created All, I have long sought to be your servant. I chose to seat myself as Judge over All Creation, not out of pride and desire for power, but through seeking to take great responsibility over all things. You’d blessed me with this mind, and in seeking to do your will, I have set to use this mind to the best of my ability, seeking to use the blessed gift of discernment that you had granted me to appropriately and accurately judge over all things that this poor mind can grasp.” Lowering his head, the Uber Judge said, “I claim no special or great powers of mind. I claim only to have willingly taken this responsibility, be my mind worthy or unworthy, and sought to do as I saw just, discerning all things to the best of my ability and keeping a record of all things meritorious.” “I had thought that I had served You well, that I had looked upon the beauty of All Creation and discerned which things showed the mark of Your Hand, and which things showed the taint of arrogance. For a time, I earned great regard as Your Servant, your upright and discerning Judge of All Creation. I was bestowed my moniker of Uber Judge, and from the German, Uber meaning Over, cemented the idea in my poor mind that I was Judge Over, and through being Judge Over (all things), I was serving Your Will.” Rocking back and forth on his knees, the Uber Judge continued, “But I have been set upon. My judgments have been denied. I have learned to know good and bad, right from wrong, art from pretension, and lies from truth, yet I am denied just the same. Is this a test, my Creator, my most perfect Artist, that which has lovingly crafted all of Creation? Am I to hold fast do what I perceive to be your will and pursue most right and correct Judgement?” = “No,” replied God, “You’re supposed to get a friggin’ clue.” The Uber Judge was taken aback and stumbled from his kneeling posture onto his posterior. “Was… that you?” “Of course it’s me,” said God, “who do you think it is? Some random disembodied voice in your head?” “Well, I’d thought that at first but-” “Sure sure,” said God, “You’ve done about enough talking, which I believe everyone would agree on.” God materialized next to the Uber Judge, and put a comforting arm around his shoulder, though the Uber Judge dared not look at him directly. “You see, UJ,” said God, “All Creation, though I did make it, and I don’t mind saying I did a pretty spiffy job of making it, doesn’t just belong to me.” “It doesn’t?” asked the Uber Judge. “Of course not, you nitwit,” said God, “it was originally mine of course, but I made a decision to share it all with every one of you. It’s mine, and it’s also yours, and everybody else’s.” “So the environment is our property to do with as we wish, and the Republicans were right?!?” asked the Uber Judge, who in spite of being in a state of shock, was coping quite well with the very presence of All Creation’s Creator. “Don’t be silly, of course they’re wrong. Not that the damn Liberals are right, but that’s beside the point. All Creation is for everybody, not just those who might think they are deserving. And wrestling matches are quite the case in point.” “They are?” asked the Uber Judge. “Of course they are. A wrestling match is good if the fans watching it enjoyed it, just like the fact that an Orchid is beautiful to anyone who takes the time to notice that it is. Do you understand what I'm saying, my boy?” “Oh!” said the Uber Judge, “of course…” and then very quietly the Uber Judge added, “not.” “Stop being that judgmental prick!” said God, “You don’t have to! You’ve influenced things enough as it is as judge slash administrator! It’s time to start showing people what you’ve got, instead of just telling them about it. Get out there, wrestle like a man possessed by the Adversary, making competing mean as much to you as judging did!” Swallowing very carefully the Uber Judge asked, “Can I still be the Uber Judge?” God shrugged. “It’s a good gimmick as any. Go with it. But I would like to see that Wrist Clutch Exploder.” “Really? Okay then! I call it… uhm … `Terminus Est’!” “Latin eh? I remember Latin, back when those damned priests would mumble that nonsense before Vatican II. I believe that your meaning in the coloquiel sense would be `This is the Line of Division’ which is very good judgey stuff.” At the reference to Vatican II, the Uber Judge could not help but ask, “So it’s Catholicism?” “Oh, Jesus Christ,” said God, “don’t be silly. I pay close attention to everybody that tries to do right by me. Who wouldn’t?” Even more desperate for an insider point of view, the Uber Judge asked, “And Jesus?” “Okay,” said God, “You need to shut the fuck up now. Just go out there, be you, honestly be you, and wrestle the best damn match you can. That’s it. That’s the message. No hidden meanings, not interpretations, no nonsense, just go out there and do what you do, and do it well. Got it?” SILENCE. God asked again with a little more OMNIPOTENCE added in, “GOT IT?” “Yes,” said the Uber Judge. “Good,” said God before vanishing. = The Uber Judge knelt in contemplation for a good while. At the good while’s passing, he rose, and approached his computer and composed a message for the Forums of Prime Time Central. To Whom It May Concern: One Free Agent, under the moniker of “The Uber Judge” seeks employment as an independent contractor (wrestler) in any of the Federations under the umbrella of the PTC. While this person's true identity must remain secret, he does have the honor of being the first PTC Infinite Gauntlet Champion, and would like to contribute further to the honor of Prime Time Central by joining one of its esteemed member federations. Thank you kindly for your time and consideration. -UJ With the message sent, the Uber Judge sighed in the first moment of pure contentment that he’d known in quite a long time. Outside the window of his office, he heard the distinctive voice of a certain crow from the higest branches of the his most prized elm tree declare, “Caw!” The Uber Judge reflected. And after reflecting, the Uber Judge said, “Good.”
