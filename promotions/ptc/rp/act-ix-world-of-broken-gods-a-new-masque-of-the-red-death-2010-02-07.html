Act IX: World Of Broken Gods; A New Masque of the Red Death

Date: 2010-02-07 Hope in reality is the worst of all evils because it prolongs the torments of man. --Friedrich Nietzsche Security at Gate 17 was light. It was purposefully designed that way; one of the passengers was a target of militants everywhere, a VIP slime ball, and not even the airport brass would have been distressed to see an assassin take him down amidst the chaos that had begun to overtake the city. Many claimed he was responsible for the horror unleashed on the world. Others lamented him as a relatively harmless lunatic turned scapegoat, his aberrations of faith making him a convenient whipping boy for a humanity going mad. Someone had to take the fall for a world that was on a one-way trip to Hell in a hand basket. The Big Apple had been rotten to the core for years. Efforts to reshape its image and clean all of the blood from the gutters had only revealed far more problems than it had swept away. With the blood soaked up, the world could see the skeletons piled in the sewers, forgotten and forbidden relics, a testament to the crimes and vices of a hell-flung metropolis. The grime had been a convenient mask for all the stink and all the garbage the town had tried to hide. And now that it was washed away, and the city labeled as Gotham had no more excuses to offer, no more lies to tell, and no more veils to conceal its sins, things were only more complicated. Elaborately, dangerously complicated. You can only live by the gun for so long before you end up dying of lead poisoning. The dance had come to New York City. The masquerade, Bruce ‘Violence Jack’ Shanahan’s glorious plan of misdirection and strange bedfellows, had expanded into the streets of a city already at war with itself. The planet was teetering on the edge of becoming an infernal wasteland, its future that of a landscape ravaged by global extinction that not even the cockroaches would endure. After two planes reduced the World Trade Center to a memory, the nation gave its full-fledged support to the grieving city. The pseudo-patriots of the United States waved their flags, donned their I Love New York shirts, and called for the blood of their nation’s cowardly adversaries. The embrace didn’t last forever. Mankind is a judgmental breed. Eventually the balance is always restored and the philanthropists’ flavor-of-the-month cause is exchanged for another. So it was with New York City. The roar of the engines died. Two men in suits, manilla envelopes in hand and stuffed with paperwork, spy the entourage of masked passengers departing the gate. Bernie Ross mutters assent into his cell and flips it shut with a practiced snap of the wrist. He nods to his partner and they move in, quickly dashing smiles over their hard looks. The PTC officials smiled and marched toward the men in the masks. Self-righteous executioners seeking to save the world through bureaucracy and obstruction. They were spurred on by money, reward, promises of extravagant bonuses if they managed to occupy the madman long enough to miss his match again, bringing history full-circle and possibly leasing more time for the human race to turn itself around. They were the types of men that Violence Jack professed resentment for. Corporate mercenaries who didn’t give a damn about much that didn’t entail their swelled bank accounts. “Mr. Shanahan?” inquired the first, peering into the eyeholes of the first mask. “I’m Bernie Ross and this is Peter Malkin, we’re co-organizers of the GTT7 tournament. We have some things we need to discuss before you depart for Madison Square Garden. Notably these contractual obligations, if you would kindly walk with us.” “I’m afraid that’s impossible, gentlemen.” The masks lowered, Malkin and Ross exchanging bewildered glances. Shanahan’s intuitions had resulted in this, a well-designed ruse. They were chosen from among the nameless. Only their role was necessary, as vital supporting players that allowed the dance to continue into its final and parting tune. They uncovered their faces, as did the myriad others emerging from the gate, and the gate beside. There was nothing threatening or malefic about them, these salt-of-the-earth folks that many called friendly neighbors. Not on the outside. The real evil resided in their minds, for they were the loyal disciples of the Bringer of the Black Gospel. The Sect of Black Wisdom had infiltrated the Big Apple. There was no overwhelming horror or acute sense of inevitable damnation on the parts of Malkin or Ross. Just disappointment and dashed hopes. It would be up to the men in the ring; with Shanahan already arrived, and no legal detours to seeing him finished at this stage, only Garbage Bag Johnny and Desade could stand in the fiendish high priest’s way now. This would be his final test. “You have failed. Father Shanahan will topple your would-be saviors, and he will do so unimpeded. You won’t repeat the crimes of GTT6. This world is ours.” ---------- “Shit man, fuck this. World’s goin’ crazy. If we gonna die, may’s well die happy, ya know?” Lo Mo snorted a line, handing off a rolled-up bill to Eli to take a turn. They didn’t know what had killed Air Fro. Hell, they didn’t even know Fro well. They only knew the guy’s skills on the basketball court were obscene, and he sported a fro like something straight from the 70’s. It wasn’t the most imaginative of nicknames but the kid seemed to like it. And keeping him happy was important. When he was in a good mood, Air Fro gave them discounts on the products he moved from his spot behind the courts. It had led to a lot of good memories, plenty of lost days, even some brushes with the law. But in this part of New York, the police had better things to do than dog some dropout kids who liked drugs. They wanted the distributors, the top dogs, the main players. Lo Mo, Eli and Pigeon were small-time, they were consumers. The police didn’t give a damn about them, it was their own money they threw away, their own brain cells they killed every day. In a city rife with turmoil, enforcement had better things to do with their time than waste hours on paperwork for what they felt amounted to long-term suicide cases. But the discounts were dried up forever. Fro was dead. Had Lo Mo and his boys been in better mental shape, unaffected by the bad mix of stuff they’d taken earlier in the day, they’d have gotten out of the area fast after one look at Air Fro. All they’d had to do was turn him over and observe his face. It used to effectively charm every girl for four blocks each way, but not anymore; now it looked like a raw cut of meat that a lion had chewed on and spit out. But the plastic bag in his back pocket had been more of an attention draw; the kid was dead, they weren‘t coroners or doctors, so why the hell was it their business? Sure they’d call an ambulance and all that, but other matters needed attendin’ to first. The pills had been green, made for one hell of a high initially. Started out normal enough, then things got really fucked up. Lo Mo was the first to notice, peering into the shadows of the alley. “What da fuck is that?” he muttered half to himself. The shit on Fro had to be some kind of LSD, a hallucinogen of some type. Lo Mo was seeing some weird shit jaunting around the alleys, four-legged misshapen things climbing the fire-escape stairs and colossal serpents coiled around the rails. It was a far-cry from his usual visions, the harmless disarrays of color and out-of-focus shapes. This was like walking into a bad dream. A vivid one, complete with reeking smell. He took several cautious steps in that direction, his legs wobbly, equilibrium totally off-kilter. Was he seeing things? Christ, they looked like monsters. He wasn’t used to taking shit that made him see monsters roaming around his streets… “Yo yo, you seein’ this? What the fuck is in these things?“ He turned around, incredulous, wanting to know if Eli and Pigeon saw the same kind of crazy shit. But they were gone. At least Eli was gone. Pigeon was only half-gone. The bottom half of him. A ragged top half was left over with his entrails spilled over the sidewalk beside their rumbling car. Lo Mo panicked. He saw the shapes moving, colors altering, the whole world spinning before his eyes as he tried to stagger to safety. He wavered, braced himself against the car before toppling over. It was poised behind a garbage dumpster, leering hungrily at him. It was nightmare come to life, a beast spawned amidst Hell’s most terrible abominations. It’s breath was fetid, awful and as hot as the infernal fires where it had been birthed. Lo Mo’s throat was paralyzed. He couldn’t scream. He didn’t get the time. It pounced on him, as did another crouched atop the car. It had been countless dark centuries since they’d fed, and their appetites were yet to be abated. Without any further sound, Lo Mo’s corpse was dragged into the sewer depths. Something else lurked there, a presence older than the progenitors of the first Man, and tonight it was awakening. Tonight they were awakening. September 11th of 2001 had been a nightmare for the city. What was about to fall upon it would tear it asunder.
