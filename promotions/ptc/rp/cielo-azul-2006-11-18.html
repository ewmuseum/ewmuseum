Cielo Azul

18 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-18 Part I: The Mustard Seed La Escuela De La Infante Santa Cielo Azul, Mexico 1989 Father Morales stood by the window of his office, looking down at the schoolyard, and the children who played there. Since taking the position as principal, his days have been filled with serenity and a sense of purpose that renewed him. Since turning his life to The Lord ten years before, that was all the more welcome. Time had been hard on him, but his God was very kind. Victor Morales was not a young man anymore, and he considered his youth squandered, but he was not beyond kicking around the football, or dusting off his old bicycle if it was required of him. He debated whether or not the Budgetary Committee paperwork could wait one more afternoon. After all, he was the Chairman, wasn’t he? Shouldn’t the chairman be entitled to a game of football on a pleasant day such as this? The issue settled, Father Morales walked to his closet, and slipped off his leather shoes, searching for his old cleats. They were there, just in the back of the closet a bit, he was sure of it. The old priest found them, and plopped down on the floor, pulling them on. However, a rise of cheering stopped him, and he shuffled to his feet, and raced back to the window. Damn. Damien Cruz was a young boy who lived with his aunt on the far edge of the town. He was unremarkable, and often spent more time playing with the insects, or pondering quiet childhood mysteries than he did playing with the other children. In the classroom, he showed flashes of intelligence and eagerness for learning, but also seemed to avoid welcoming too much attention to himself, and therefore held back where most of his teachers felt he could shine. At best, he performed along the high cusp of average, but nothing more. In his encounters with the boy, Father Morales often hoped to bring him out of his shell a bit, but was also cautious. He could never put his finger on why, but he found the boy intimidating. It wasn’t rational, and if asked to explain why; he couldn’t, but Father Morales often felt that Damien Cruz was a bee hive that might be best left to his own. He liked the boy, of course, but knew that some children are best left to their families’ devices. With a circle of students surrounding them, Damien Cruz was being beaten by the school bully. However, despite the torn uniform, the blood that trickled from his nose and stained his teeth, and the fact that he had not retaliated in any way, it was the bully who was crying. Ernesto Garcia was being cheered on as the schoolyard normalcy dictated that he should hold reign over the smaller children. But as the smaller boy continued to stand defiant, and as Ernesto began to weaken in his dominance, there was an uncomfortable silence that rolled in like a fog. Damien Cruz, by simply standing when others had cowered, was destabilizing what the others had come to accept as reality. By the time Father Morales made his way down there, it was over. Damien Cruz was sitting in the dirt, watching with a stoic curiosity as dark blood made mud of the dirt between his knees. Ernesto had run away, crying hard sobs as confusion and fear blurred his thoughts. The other children had scattered as the sight of Father Morales, and some laughed, others remained quiet, and a few cried to see blood and violence up close. The priest lifted Damien to his feet, and led him into the chapel, where he would clean him up. Teachers and older students brought the others back into the school to gather stories and to return the day back to normal as best as they could. Although they did, the change was evident, and there was a subtle shift in the culture of The School of the Holy Infant. The two walked in silence, and Father Morales struggled to find the words to break the silence. He was sure that he was to scold Damien for fighting, and was tempted to give the boy the typical lecture about how fighting solves nothing, and that what Damien did was wrong. But in so many ways, he knew that it was a lie. Damien did what Father Morales himself was not sure he could have at his age; or ever again. Damien was different from the others. It scared him to have such a responsibility, but Father Morales understood that it was his duty to cultivate the potential that Damien had shown. "It didn’t hurt so bad, Father Morales. I thought it would, but it didn’t hurt so bad." Father Morales gripped the boy’s hand tighter in recognition of the statement, but said nothing. Damien then wiped away a tear that had been resting in his eyes, and continued to walk with the priest. "Tell Ernesto I’m sorry. Tell him that I didn’t mean to make him afraid. I’m sorry, Father, I’m sorry I made him feel small like that." The priest looked at Damien, and could feel a chill grip him. "I don’t understand, Damien. What do you think you did to Ernesto?" Damien’s voice cracked, as if he were admitting a great wrong-doing. "He made me so mad. So I made him feel little. I made him feel afraid of me because I know that he makes other people afraid. I wanted him to feel afraid so that he would go away and not hurt the little kids anymore. I know that if I hit him, he would hit me back, and that he would feel bigger than me. So I just let him hit me. It didn’t hurt, so I let him hit me and see that he could not hurt me. He feels big when he hits us and we fall down and cry. But if I kept standing up, then he could not feel big. I’m sorry." Father Morales now understood. He understood in a way that made him want to sweep the boy up, and hug him. He understood in a way that made him want to protect the boy, and mold the boy, and redeem himself by teaching that boy everything that he had to teach him. Father Morales, for the first time since he woke up on a ventilator years before, had seen clearly that he had served a greater purpose. He was to teach all of his students, but this one was the student that would grow to be his greatest. In the years that would follow, he never said it to him, but Damien Cruz was the student that he loved the most, and oftentimes felt most proud of. And in moments of weakness, Damien was the one he envied. Father Morales led the boy into the chapel washroom, and helped the boy wash his face. He gave the boy a change of clothes, and then went to the small kitchen and prepared a small snack for the boy. When Damien came into the kitchen, he saw Father Morales sitting at the table, sipping a cup of coffee, and thumbing through a yellowed newspaper. Victor Morales told the boy to have a seat, and smiled at him. It was a wide, eager, joyful smile that Damien had never seen before. Father Morales smiled as he told him about Lucha Libre, and Damien listened intently. Part II: The Prelude El Diablo Loco Gymnasium Cielo Azul, Mexico 1995 Damien Cruz was hit with a cheap shot. Damien was floored by a full-on elbow in what was supposed to be half contact. Ramon apologized dramatically, but everyone, including Father Morales, knew that the hit was very deliberate. Damien sat up, and wiped away the blood that had begun to stream from his left eyebrow. "What the hell?" Damien called for a towel, and tried to wipe the blood away. "I thought you were going to duck. You just stood there and took the hit." Ramon leaned back on the turnbuckle, showing no remorse. "What are you trying to do in there? You could have hurt him." Father Morales stepped into the ring, and inspected Damien’s eyebrow, which had begun to bleed again. "I barely touched him." Ramon defended, growing defensive. "I’m alright, Father. I’m okay. I can finish." Father Morales smiled to himself, but wouldn’t allow it. Not yet. "No, Damien, I think we need to get some ice on that to stop the bleeding. I don’t think you need stitches, but it does look nasty. Jose, get these guys paired up and go through the takedown drills. I’m going to get Damien patched up." "Yeah, patch him up." "Shut up, Ramon." Jose chimed in, leading the others away. Ramon Bermudez had been training with Father Morales for over ten years. He was the eldest of his students, and among the least promising. When Father Morales began training him, Ramon was eager to learn as much as he could. The boy would often show hours earlier, and left hours later than what was expected of him. When he wasn’t in the weight room, he was on the mats practicing his ground work. Father Morales was excited to know that Lucha Libre had found fertile ground in the young man’s heart, and offered him as much time and dedication as the boy demanded. However, there was a bit of a shift. As the boy grew older, his passion waned. He put on a few extra pounds that slowed him down, he made fewer visits to the gym, and more often placed his priorities on his social life and chasing Anita Rios around. At first, Father Morales excused it, and resigned himself to the fact that Ramon was still growing up, and that Lucha Libre was not meant for everyone. He accepted that Ramon would probably not be a real talent unless he put his full effort into it; and if he didn’t, that would be fine. Ramon was kept out of trouble, and that was really the first role of Lucha Libre as far as Father Morales was concerned. Father Morales trained a handful of students at a time. As students came and went, the number was held at about five or six. Usually, students joined young, trained seriously until about fifteen or sixteen, and then became distracted or disenchanted by Lucha Libre. Father Morales had watched, with resignation, as student after student was pulled away from him be drugs, crime, or poverty. But the line of students continued, and Father Morales remained vigilant to his purpose. It was also for this reason that Father Morales allowed Ramon to stay in his school even after the fire had begun to flicker. Ramon was approaching seventeen, and by all accounts, should have been in the circuit by then. But he wasn’t. He wasn’t past his prime, of course, but Father Morales wondered if he’d ever have a prime to speak of. That seemed to change with the arrival of Damien Cruz. It wasn’t immediate. Damien was a skinny, quiet, almost awkward ten-year old. He wasn’t particularly strong, he wasn’t particularly fast, and he wasn’t particularly agile. But he had the potential to be all three, and Father Morales saw it brimming just beneath the surface. After a year of training, as Father Morales had expected, Damien quickly excelled, and became his most promising student. After five years of training, Damien was clearly among his best. Ramon noticed, and was not ready to be pushed aside so easily. "I want to finish, Father Morales." Damien was lying on the table, and Father Morales was cleaning out the wound with a cotton swab. The cut was deeper than he thought, and Father Morales considered a trip to the hospital. "No, I think I’m going to call Doctor Lopez. You just rest up. You’re done for the day." Damien quietly accepted Father Morales’ decision, but wasn’t happy about it. "You don’t have anything to prove, Damien. You’re a real solid fighter. In a couple of years, when you fill out a little more, you’ll be in that ring making me very proud." "I think I’m ready now." Father Morales laughed, and shook his head. "Damien, don’t be in such a rush. You’ll get there, eventually. That ring will be there when you’re ready. Believe me; I’m not going to hold you back." Father Morales held the wound together, and applied two stripes of tape. It was makeshift, and crude, but it did the job. "I know, Father Morales. I’m just anxious to do more. I know that you mean well, but I’m tired of holding back. Believe it or not, but when Ramon hit me, I felt a rush." "It’s called adrenaline. You’d feel the same rush if you stubbed your toe, Damien." Father Morales joked, but Damien seemed not to respond. Father Morales realized how deeply Damien was feeling what he was expressing. "When I felt my eye split open, it felt like something came alive in me. Almost like I was awakened. It sounds so stupid, and I feel like an idiot saying this, but I liked it. I felt like there was a reason why I had to be faster, and stronger, and more aware. I felt like being punched in the face is a lot more of a motivation than simply to complete a drill." The old luchadore felt silly. He realized that in his attempt to teach his students, he had drained something very important from the lessons. Granted, he taught them the movements, and the history of Lucha Libre, but had drained away the spirit. There was something very spiritual about Lucha Libre that resided in the idea of self evolution. And evolution came from blood, from pain, from the reality that faster, stronger, better came when slower, weaker, and lesser have real consequences. His students needed to be given a chance to grow, and training dummies and heavy bags can take them but so far. Father Morales pulled an extra piece of tape from the roll, and pressed it against Damien’s eyebrow hard. "Are you sure you want to go back out there?" Damien sat up, and took a mouthful of water. "Yeah." "Then tell Ramon to pad up. I think we’re going to have a little exhibition." Damien smiled. What happened next was a massacre. Part III: The Threshold Los Locos Lucha Libre Auditorium Cielo Azul, Mexico 1998 Damien Cruz stepped up onto the second turnbuckle, greeting the crowd for the last time. The Last Son of Lucha Libre took the L4 Grand Title from the ringside official, and held it high above his head. He then dropped back to the center of the ring, and called for the mic. "Brothers and Sisters, thank you for coming tonight, and every night for the past four years. Thank you for cheering with me, laughing with me, crying with me, and helping me grow from a skinny little kid into the Los Locos Lucha Libre Champion." The fans cheered loudly, and Damien bathed in their affection. "Now I have been here in my home town, doing what I love, and making sure that I make you guys proud of me. I want to thank you all. But I owe a great deal of gratitude to one man. A man who a few of you may know and many more of you never got to know. He is a man who was once in this ring as El Nino Oro. He then walked away from it all to be Father Victor Morales. And more importantly, he is the man who has brought the L4 together. He has a lot of help, of course, but it is through his hard work, his dedication, and his wisdom that a dirt-poor town like Cielo Azul has given birth to a nationally-respected operation like this. So, Brothers and Sisters, I would like to bring that man out tonight. " All attention turned to the entryway, where Father Morales reluctantly stepped through the curtain. Although much older, his broad shoulders and powerful stance remind a lot of those in attendance of the respect he once demanded. Father Morales stepped up and through the ropes with practiced familiarity, and embraced his student and protégée. "Father Morales, I wanted you to join me in this ring as I made this announcement." The priest knew the announcement because he had helped Damien to make the decision. "Padre, I stand here today as a result of your dedication, your support, and your guidance. After my aunt died, you allowed me to come to live with you. When I asked you to let me join your federation, you did so despite a great number of fears I knew you had. And most importantly, when I asked for your blessing a week ago, you not only offered it, but you helped me fulfill my dream when it began to slip away. So while there really are no words that I could say to truly express my gratitude…" Damien pulled the title from where it hung on his shoulder, and held it out to Father Morales, who did not expect the gesture. "I think this belongs to you." The fans cheered and rose in ovation, despite the question it instantly raised. Damien, in turn, answered it. "Now, Father Morales, this is a very difficult decision, but it has been one that I made with a great amount of thought, and guidance, and prayer, and debate. Brothers and Sisters, I have come to the end of my time here in Los Locos, and I am about to embark on the next step in my personal journey." The fans’ cheers quieted, as the weight of the man’s announcement settled in. They were losing their icon, and they knew it. Before Damien, the L4 was little more than a weekend sideshow. The talents, with any real potential, were quickly scouted and head hunted into other companies almost as quickly as they arrived. The arrival of Damien changed that. It came to be that challengers immigrated into the L4 as Damien’s fame and reputation grew. He was a prodigy, and many men were eager to see the man for themselves. Damien was happy to oblige them. He remained loyal to l$ even after other offers came in. He drew crowds and helped attract investors. Obviously, Father Morales knew how to market Damien in a way that also elevated the rest of the roster, but he was not naïve about how central Damien was to the L4. But he understood Damien’s need to grow beyond his small federation. There would be other talents to cultivate, and would be just fine. Father Morales took the mic from Damien, and public ally blessed Damien with a prayer that everyone in attendance joined in with. Damien Cruz, The Last Son of Lucha Libre was on a plane to Tokyo ten hours later, and would soon become The Latin Assassin.
