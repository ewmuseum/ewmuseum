Haunted

13 October 2009

Date: 2009-10-13 It was midnight when the doorbell rang. Syko answered the door in striped pajamas for a petite, white woman in a full black suit and black shoes. She was more mannequin than person standing under the moonlight - perfect skin, hair as black and slick as oil, and no emotion on her face. Do you have any idea what fucking time it is, Syko asked her. The woman pulled out a dark brown manila envelope from inside her jacket and handed it to Syko. Time to change your life, she said. Syko opened the envelope. What is this? An invitation to this year's GTT. Syko looked up at her. What if I'm not interested? It's merely an invitation. She smiled. But as a former Bracket Champion, we thought you might be interested. Syko thought back to the only GTT he had participated in. It was GTT3, though it was called Golden Turnbuckle Tournament then. It was Syko's first experience under the PTC umbrella - an experience he remembers with joy and regret. Syko fought through the first few rounds unscathed, but he was no match for the elite fighters. That became evident when he was eliminated by Inphino Blitz. Syko waved the envelope at her. If this is real, you would know I have not been in any GTT's since GTT3. Which is why it's the perfect time for a comeback, she said. This year's GTT promises to be the most competitive yet. Syko skimmed the list of already confirmed entrants. There were many names he did not recognize. Your first round opponent has already been picked, she said. The tournament directors believe you'll be most impressed with the quality of Suzanne Larsson. That is, if you accept of course. Syko laid down the papers on the love seat behind him. It's an interesting offer; I'll have to sleep on it. It's merely an invitation, she said. She looked around the small, humble apartment. Even only dimly lit by the moonlight, she could see the sorry, unkempt state it was in. She spotted a picture of Syko standing next to a beautiful woman with burgundy red hair and green eyes on the table next to the loveseat. They looked happy. She's beautiful, she said, pointing at the picture. Will she be cheering you on? Syko immediately laid down the picture frame face down on the table. That's none of your business. The woman turned and left the apartment. At the doorway, she stopped and turned around. It's perfectly fine to live your rest of your life in obscurity - you'll have no regrets. The words stung Syko like fishhooks digging their way into his flesh. # Syko was having doubts a week later. He was both out of shape and out of form - a bad combination going into GTT7. He took an offer from a small wrestling organization to fight their best wrestler. A light sparring session quickly turned into a lesson in reality. Syko was slower, weaker, and more susceptible to pain than he had been at his peak. When he laid on the canvas, waiting for the end, he heard the loud ovation from the crowd. He used to love that feeling. But now he hated it, because they did not cheer for him. Syko changed into dark blue jeans and a crisp white cotton t-shirt in the locker-room. He sat on the black bench, elbows on his knees and face buried in his hands. He was a sorry image surrounded by the bleakness of grey lockers, grey floors, and beige walls. Are you Syko Arsonist? Syko's trance was broken by a blonde, teenage girl walking into his locker-room. She stood inside the doorway with her hands in the pockets of her hooded, grey sweatshirt and dirty blonde hair in a ponytail. She looked like an undersized descendant of the Amazonians and her blue eyes had hints of running away and desperation to them. I'm not doing autographs. I don't want one. She said. Her voice was loud and confident. She said her name was Amy Brent and it was her brother who Syko had just wrestled. Well you're not supposed to be here. If I only went where I was supposed to go, I wouldn't get anywhere in life. What do you want, he asked. Amy took her hands out and walked towards Syko. My family is really big on wrestling. All my brothers and my father have been in the business. The problem is - They won't let a girl do it, Syko finished for her. Amy smiled and nodded. I was hoping you could train me. I'm only seventeen, but I'm in better shape than girls my age. I thought with your help I might make it big in PTC. Syko laughed and shook his head. PTC, he asked rather rhetorically. I don't think you understand exactly what that entails. Amy pleaded. Please, my family is well off. They won't support this, but I do have my own connections. I'll do anything. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] Syko laughed as he put his hand on the door handle. He looked back at Amy raging with anger - her fists clenched and her face turning a beet red. You fucking asshole, she cursed under her breath. Syko said, I'll give you some advice: Don't trust anyone. Syko left the locker-room and Amy was left with whatever remained of her dignity. # Syko was in the kitchen rinsing off a tall glass. He was still tense from the incident a few days earlier in the locker-room with Amy. He searched his Maplewood finish cabinets for a drink and found Orange Tang - his favorite. A skinny Chinese hooker with short black, fine hair like a horse's was putting on her shoes in the living room. She was one of Syko's regulars and this time she had let him fuck her ass for an extra hundred. She slung her brown D&G shoulder bag over her shoulder, counted the money, and walked towards the front door. See you next time, she said in broken English. Get the fuck out of here, Syko said. He added clumps of sugar to the Tang residue at the bottom of the glass. She did not say anything, did not even feel hurt. She was used to this. If you loved me Santiago, you'll leave GTT Syko turned angrily towards the hooker. What the fuck did you just say? The hooker was startled and wide-eyed. I no say nothing! You think that shit is funny? Get the fuck out of my house! The hooker was terrified and she hurriedly left the apartment, closing the door behind her. Syko slammed his fists on the natural stone countertop. I'm not losing my mind, he told him. I'm not losing my fucking mind. Syko searched his cabinets, this time for his medications. There were cough medicines, headache relievers, pain killers, and sleep medications. Yes, sleep medications. Syko took down a bottle of nonbenzodiazepine. He was only supposed to take them for a short period of time, but he desperately needed some rest. He was sure these little episodes were because he wasn't resting enough. He had been so obsessed with getting ready for GTT7, that he neglected his health. His mind was playing tricks on him because he was delirious. Syko opened the bottle and looked around his apartment. His apartment was a mountain range of trash - dirty dishes piled in the sink, dirty laundry scattered on the floor, and letters and junk mail stacked on the coffee table. I need a shower, he said, and walked towards the bathroom. Syko made sure the water was warm enough before entering the shower. He liked it to be really warm, but not quite scolding hot. The front door creaked opened. Syko had forgotten to lock the door when the hooker left hurriedly. A young intruder with blonde hair in a ponytail and a hood over her head crept in. It was Amy and this time her eyes showed anger and uneasiness. She looked around but saw no sign of Syko Arsonist. But she heard the water running. She was breathing heavily and her hands trembled in her pocket as she revealed a clear plastic zip lock bag with a white substance inside. It was the perfect opportunity for her to drug Syko, while he was helpless in the shower. Amy froze, because she realized she could not go through with it, and the fear of being caught strangled her. She wanted to run out of the apartment as fast as she could, to forget she ever came here. Then she saw the tall glass of orange juice. The opportunity was ripe and she needed to take advantage of it immediately. She poured a couple of nonbenzodiazepine pills on the countertop and began mashing them with a knife. If she made sure enough of the pills were ingested, they would only cause a minor overdose. The medical reports would then claim it as a suicide, and no foul play would be suspected. When she crushed enough pills, she spilled them into the tall glass. Then the water stopped. Amy quickly wiped off the powder with her sleeve and ran out the door, closing the door behind her as she had found it. Syko walked into the living in gray sweatpants and flippers. He had no shirt on and was drying his close-skin touch up short black hair with a head towel. He picked up the tall glass, added water, and took a sip. It was much too bitter. Syko saw the white conglomerate mass at the bottom of the glass and added lots more sugar. It was better but not much. Guess, I have to buy some more juice, he said. Syko took three pills of nonbenzodiazepine. He only needed to take one, but he figured an extra two wouldn't hurt. He swallowed the pills and gulped down the cold orange juice. Syko picked up a white t-shirt from the black leather couch; it looked clean enough. He sat down on the couch and turned on the television to watch ESPN. There was bound to be something about GTT7 there, and he needed more information on Suzanne Larsson. Who was she? Where did she come from? And would she like to go out after the match? After a while, Syko felt himself drifting in and out of sleep. A slight pain harassed him in his chest. Syko tried lying down, but the pain was getting worse and Syko was having trouble breathing. Then the rush came at him all at once. Syko never felt that kind of incapacitating pain in his chest before. His entire chest cavity felt like he was wrapped in the coils of an anaconda, and it was squeezing the life out of him. He struggled to breathe...then his left arm started to hurt. A heart attack? No, he was much too young for that. Syko fought to get off the couch with all his strength. He then started hacking, and stared at his own blood on the carpet. He needed help and he needed it fast. Syko staggered to the front door and fought to remain conscious. He stepped out of the first floor apartment to the bright, sunny day outside. Syko could barely stand upright as he wheezed for help. But everyone ignored him. Syko took out his Blackberry from his pants pockets and with trembling fingers dialed 911. He collapsed to the concrete floor, barely managing to hold on to the phone. He cursed everyone under his breath. With his last waking moment, Syko realized an inevitable truth: When you abandon the world, the world abandons you. Then he fell unconscious. # I want to live. The words came out slurred. Syko slowly blinked, his eyes trying to adjust to his new surroundings. Syko tried to move, but found he had no energy to even lift a finger. His eyes adjusted and all he could see was white. Pure white. He saw white walls, white ceiling, white sheets, people walking in front of him in white coats, and everything smelled so clean. And there was a woman standing over him. A beautiful woman. The most beautiful woman he had ever seen! She had shoulder length, wavy black hair, deep hazel eyes, and pouty lips. She was saying something to him, but Syko could barely hear her. Fuck, Syko muttered. I didn't want to die. Syko looked at the woman again. She was so beautiful. Syko then decided that being dead wasn't such a bad thing after all. It was better than the river of lava, flames, and eternal pain and suffering he was expecting. Why am I in Heaven, he asked the woman. The woman laughed. You're not dead, you're in a hospital. Oh, Syko could only reply. His vision and hearing had returned him and he finally understood his surroundings. The woman sat upright in her seat with a clipboard across her lap. Syko glanced at her name tag: Nicole Schleider. She asked, maybe you can tell me why you're here. I think I had a heart attack, Syko responded. Dr. Schleider shook her head. No, you tried to kill yourself. No, I didn't, Syko denied. Nicole flipped the medical reports back. She said, then explain to me why we had to pump out excessive amounts of nonbenzodiazepine from your system and give your heart a boost to get you going again. Syko shook his head, trying to remember what happened. No, he slowly replied. I only took two extra pills, I didn't think it was going to be such a bad idea. Nicole shook her head. She had dealt with these kinds of suicidal victims her entire career - they always were in denial. It wasn't two extra pills, it was like a dozen more. I didn't try to kill myself! His voice was barely as loud as he hoped it would be. Nicole crossed her arms. Are you saying that someone tried to kill you? That's not what I'm saying, Syko said. He then suddenly realized that the Orange Tang that day had tasted way too bitter. He cursed himself for not having realized it then, or just throwing it away and going to the store. But he could not figure out who would want to kill him. Was it the Chinese hooker? No, impossible. There was too much to think about, and he did not want to get this doctor involved. It was best to just let it go. Fine, Syko sighed. I admit it. I tried to kill myself and it didn't work out. Nicole sighed, but it was a sigh of relief. The first step to getting better is admitting you have a problem. What's your name, she asked. Before Syko answered, a short, pudgy white woman with curly hair, and way too much make up walked in with a tray of syringes and medicine bottles. She was Rosemary, registered nurse, and was Nicole's best friend at Presbyterian Hospital in New York. She stared at Syko's deep brown eyes and gave him a smile. Syko smiled back. Rosemary turned to whisper into Nicole's ear. I think we found the next Mr. Schleider. Will you get out of here, Nicole whispered to her loudly. Rosemary laughed and left the room. What was that, Syko asked. Don't mind her. Now what was your name again? Syko. Nicole sighed. Yes, we already established you are psycho. But what is your name. Just call me Syko. Fine, she said. Why do you want to kill yourself? Syko was shocked at how deeply the question affected him. He had only admitted to wanting to kill himself because he did not want the doctor tangled up in a more serious case. Now the question triggered a series of emotions and painful memories for him. Why did he continue to live without any love or happiness. Wouldn't it have been better if he had already ended his pain and suffering? Well, Syko began. We'll be together again, if you do it Syko looked to his side and saw a naked, white corpse laying next to him. She had burgundy red hair, green eyes, and gashes and burn marks across her entire body. When she spoke, her huge neck gash disgustingly broke open and it looked as she were being decapitated. You still love me, right Santiago? Your fucking dead, Syko yelled. You're fucking dead! Syko yelled at the top of his lungs, and flopped frantically, constraint to the hospital bed. Nicole shouted for help, and nurses rushed in to sedate Syko. As the nurses sedated Syko, Nicole stepped back and wondered what had happened. She had seen this before - hallucinations - but she had never seen anybody's eyes go as white as Syko's did when it happened. When Syko awoke the next day, he once again saw Nicole sitting by his side, monitoring his vital signs. This time he knew he was not dead, but there was something about Nicole's presence that seemed angelic to him. It was perhaps her striking beauty; it was perhaps how much care she showed her patients; it was perhaps the extremely bright ceiling lights highlighting her and making it seem she had a halo. Whatever it was, Syko was glad that the first image he saw whenever he woke up was of Nicole. It was an experience he would enjoy everyday if he could. What happened, he asked. Nicole turned and smiled. She was glad her patient had regained consciousness. Her smile quickly faded. You had a hallucination. Care to talk about it? No. Nicole nodded. I suppose you don't want to talk about the scars on your body, either? Syko looked down at chest and arms. Each scar had a story of its own: barbed wire matches, steel cage matches, ladder matches, last man standing matches, and of course, there were the many surgeries he had undergone. They come from work, he said. What are you, Nicole asked. A crash test dummy? Syko laughed. In a way he was one. A wrestler, Syko replied. That explains some of the scarring I've seen, Nicole said. Her eyes lit up when she came to a revelation. That's why you want me to call you psycho isn't it? Syko smiled. Well too bad, Nicole said. There was hint of playfulness in her voice. Your medical records came back, Mr. Santiago Rodrigo. Syko shook his head. I prefer Syko. Nicole nodded, seemingly understanding. When am I getting out of here? Not for a while, Nicole said. And you better not be thinking about going off to wrestle. You're in no shape for that. Why not, Syko asked. He seemed a bit hurt. Wrestling is... He paused to rethink his words. Wrestling is the only thing I have left. Nicole looked at him confused. Now that can't possibly be true. I'm sure you have a girlfriend, family, or friends somewhere. I'm sure you have some kind of hobby. Syko shook his head and looked off into the distance. You don't understand. This isn't like a fix, or an addiction I need to have. Can I ask you something? Go ahead. Are you happy with what you do: being a doctor. Yes, definitely. Did you know that you always wanted to be a doctor? Of course. And you're happy with the way things are in your life, with your choices? Yes, Nicole answered. There are some regrets, but I love what I do. It's my calling, not to sound cheesy. Well that's how I feel about wrestling, Syko turned towards Nicole. I grew up my entire life knowing only pain and suffering. Wrestling was the only outlet I had, the only thing that kept me from falling into the abyss. It is the only thing I have, because it is the only thing I've ever known. Nicole tried to search her words carefully. I'm very sorry to hear that. Syko shook his head. It's not your fault. I just need to wrestle, it is the only thing that keeps me going. Then I have to intervene as a doctor, Nicole said. As long as you’re in this hospital bed, under my supervision, you will not be wrestling. Let me ask you something, doctor. What if one day, you make a mistake and cost someone the life of a child, or a brother, or a parent. What if one day someone tells you that you made a mistake becoming a doctor? What if one day you realized that what you thought was your life's calling was merely a lie? How would you feel? Nicole thought about the question for a moment. I would feel devastated. I would feel like I wasted my entire life. That's how I feel, Syko said. There's something really important coming up that I need to be a part of. If I'm not a part of it, then I would never know if my entire life has been meaningless. And if that happens, then I would really try to kill myself. What's the point in living a life without meaning? Nicole didn't say anything. Instead she just nodded. As the days went on, Syko got increasingly better. He was now able to sit upright, and walk around the room for brief periods of time. He also regained some courage and flirted with Dr. Schlieder from time to time. But Dr. Schlieder immediately crushed those hopes, but Syko was neither deterred nor discouraged. Their conversations had become more cheerful since the day after the hallucinations. Syko had no more hallucinations either. They talked small talk mostly, exchanged jokes, talked about bits and pieces of their lives. Nicole talked about her college years, going to medical school and residency in New York. Syko talked about the many wrestling organizations he had performed in, the cities he went to, and the many nations he visited. They both seemed content to only talk about their work rather than their personal lives. Nicole was thirty, divorced, with no kids. Santiago, or Syko as he preferred, was thirty five, had no kids, and was married once. Syko did not press any questions about Nicole's divorce. Nicole, likewise, did not press what Syko meant by gone, though she assumed Syko was a widower. They shared only basic information, because it was nothing more than a doctor-patient relationship and nothing more. Then on the seventh day, Syko had left the hospital without any heed or warning. Nicole stood over the empty hospital bed, clipboard in hand, her arms crossed over her chest. She said nothing as her friend, Rosemary, walked in. What happened to the future Mr. Schleider? He left, Nicole answered. He left in the middle of the night. Damn, he really was the next Mr. Schleider. Nicole scoffed and gave Rosemary a cold stare. She then stormed out of the room. Rosemary called after her. I'm so sorry, Nicole! It was a bad joke! Nicole! # Syko was never good at following doctor’s orders. When he regained enough of his health, he left the hospital bed and went straight to his favorite gym. He regretted not being able to say goodbye to Dr. Nicole Schlieder. But what could he have said to make her understand? What would have happened in any case? The only future with Dr. Schlieder was in his fantasies and Syko was content with that. He would not get hurt, and most importantly, neither would she. Syko thought about the events that had transpired since the mysteriously pale woman had given him the invitation at exactly midnight. He bench pressed only two hundred pounds, a light work out, as he contemplated getting ready for GTT7. But there were so many questions swimming through his mind. Firstly, he still did not know anything about Suzanne Larsson. The woman had only said that he would be most impressed with her quality. There was also the idea of someone trying to kill him, but the more Syko thought about it, the more ridiculous it seemed. And finally, there were the hallucinations. That was definitely something Syko did not want to think about. Syko finished the third rep of sets and sat upright on the bench. Sweat dripped down his face as he took out a white towel from his black duffel bag and dried his face. Syko shook his mind off the many interruptions he was facing. There was only one thing that needed to be on his mind: GTT7. The mystery woman said that it was a good time for Syko to mount a comeback, and she was right. Syko had lived in obscurity for far too long, and GTT7 seemed like the ripe opportunity to cement his legacy in PTC. GTT7 was a few days away and Syko was doing everything he could to make sure he was ready for it. Syko was going to win GTT7... He took out a syringe from his duffel bag. It was labeled HGH. He looked around the gym - it was vacant. He pulled his sweats down halfway to his thigh and injected himself. ...by any means necessary.
