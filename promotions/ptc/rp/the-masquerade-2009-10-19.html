She wheeled back toward him with narrowed eyes and pointed with the conical spool punctuating her words as she said, “You did not.” Troy rocked back on his heels, rolling his eyes in mock innocence, “I did, buuut… Well, you know what I always say…” He held up an overloaded tool belt and laughed as she smacked palm to face and shook her head. “Tool belt for the win! Who knew it would come in handy for covering fashion disasters, huh?” He leaned down with that same vaguely vexing grin and said, “Come on, I would never embarrass you, Rache. Well… ok, not on purpose anyway.” Rache laughed a light musical laugh and said, “You might, but hey, I know you don’t mean it. I don’t think you’re actually capable of being embarrassed yourself.” She shook her head lightly, tossing a couple of locks of hair across her eyes, which only made her gaze look more mischievous as she gave him one of those looks, the looks that belie knowledge, but not a full understanding, as if that person might still be a puzzle that hasn’t been fully worked yet. “Yeah, maybe,” said the man, flicking his own dark bangs out of his eyes as he paced toward the long row of windows that lined the nearest wall, seemingly oblivious to his less-than-dressed state. “I guess I just don’t get upset over things easily. Not little things anyway,” he said and glanced back toward Rache and her machine as it whirred to life, running a seam over the split denim he’d so recently occupied. “Most things just seem to be easier to fix than to get too bent out of shape over. I don’t really think you’re all that different.” He tilted his head in her direction and that of her swiftly working hands. She looked up a moment and smiled before whipping the jeans out from beneath the machine’s presser foot, flipping them over, and rolling under an edge of fabric with a practiced grace before reinserting them into the machine. “You just like to make a show of things for some reason I’ve never figured out,” said Troy, leaning against the windows with his heavily tattooed arms folded across his chest. In the crisscrossing light that came through the windows on his side and that of the wall of windows that ran at a right angle to it, the artwork was thrown into sharp relief, mostly black and white and very geometric. Rache seemed to admire the play of shadow on ink for a moment before commenting. “Who Me?” she said. There was that mischief again in the tone of her voice and in the glee with which she hopped up and headed around the sizeable work table that held her machine. It was draped with a huge wad of deep crimson velvet that matched a stack of similar fabric on the floor nearby, apparently a large project. From the edge of that pile she scooped up a daintily feathered mask, black and blue with peacock feathers in the middle of the top forming a crest from which the rest of the feathers cascaded in the form of a crown. “Now what would make you say that?” She said, giggling as she placed the mask over her face. “Oh I dunno,” he said, shifting to admire her as she pirouetted by. “You mean besides the obvious? You working those into the designs for the Prospero I take it?” “The two center curtains, yes,” said Rache with a nod that made the feathers bob as if she were the original bird itself. “That’s their new tagline you know: Prospero – Playhouse of a Thousand Faces.” She swept her hands across her face ending in a gesture that framed it and the mask. “Nice. They should get you up there for opening night,” Troy said flicking the feathers at the edge of the mask gently. “What’s it? The Masquerade Reborn? I don’t care if you’re the seamstress, you’d be a natural for that kind of thing, at least for some small role.” Rache snickered and pulled the mask down slowly, peering over it with a perfectly timed theatrical gaze and said, “Ya think?” Troy laughed, “Yeah, I do.” “Well, speaking of shows, you’re making one for anyone who happens to be looking up at the moment, but hey, you’re the guy who doesn’t embarrass easy,” she said with an exasperated little huff as she fanned herself with the mask. Troy shrugged and said, “We’re eight stories up, my dear. I don’t think anyone can see up here unless maybe they’re craning their neck and using binoculars. And if they’re that much of a voyeur, more power to ‘em.” He peered at her questioningly. “You finish those things though? I am starting to feel the breeze.” She patted her thighs clothed in her own jeans, black with a purple undertone of contrasting thread, and said, “Funny, seems ok in here to me.” She winked and Troy rolled his eyes as he stepped closer to her and leaned down , his hazel eyes peering into her blue ones as he said, “Woman, you don’t re-pants me and I’ll just have to keep you close for warmth. It is October you know.” She appeared to consider this a moment and then shook her head. “That isn’t exactly a threat. But I’ll trade you. I see you got the mail.” “Hmm? Oh yeah.” Rache turned and released the jeans from the machine as he scooped up a small pile of letters from a table placed across the end of the workbench on which the sewing machine sat. He rummaged through them, mumbling, “Electric bill, Thai menu, Habitat for Humanity, GTT7… Rachel Tempest Tyrell. Tempest? Is that your middle name? Hey, that’s kinda sexy.” He turned to look at Rachel Tempest Tyrell with an inquisitive smile, just in time to receive a wadded up pair of pants to the face. Tempest. I didn’t look shocked this time or anything, not like the first time one of those letters arrived. I was really glad he wasn’t home when the first one came because words like “meltdown” and “freak out” come to mind in terms of my reaction. How did they find me? Here? Now? After all this time? I’m past wondering. Now I’m just wondering why I responded. Well, I didn’t at first. I threw the letter away. I pulled it out and started to shred it. But I couldn’t. Why couldn’t I? That freaked me out even more that I couldn’t, that I had to hang onto it and just not look at it for a couple of days, having stuffed it in the dog’s bed, puzzling over how in the hell they found me and why they would be trying to get me back into a tournament. They must really be scraping the bottom of the barrel. It’s not like it’s a nostalgia tour or something. That I would find more understandable. But they want me to come out of retirement? Don’t they know I have a life now? Well no, I guess not. That’s just it, they know nothing, or at least they shouldn’t. That’s how I wanted it. I left that world behind for a reason. I was done with it and I wanted it to be done with me. “Forget about me because I’m sure as hell gonna forget about you!” I said. So why didn’t I shred the letter? Why did I instead write back and sign on? This will be their last letter, the one with all the details: where, when. I already know the who - Darek DeAngleo, 6’10”, 292 pounds… what’s that in stone? It doesn’t matter, he’s a god dam Redwood. I hate that. Someone in the office always thinks it’s cute to throw me at the nearest tree and see if I go splat. It’s that David & Goliath thing, but it’s been done, DONE TO DEATH. All it means for me, win or lose, is a whole lot of Epsom salts. I should buy stock in the mines. Actually I should have bought stock in the mines during my previous career. I’d be a very rich woman by now. No, that matchup wasn’t fixed at all, pfft. Random name generator my left butt cheek… But DeAngleo’s an interesting guy, a veteran with a career that paralleled mine, just never crossed it for whatever odd reason. Different promotions, different directions. It seems like it’s worn on him. I have no good answers for that. I ran away. And then there’s Mask of William. I don’t mind triple threats. I’m quick, my major saving grace. But this guy is more like Mask of I-got-no-idea. So, I have nothing good to do in the way of preparing. As if there’s really any way to prepare. Strategy has never been my strong suit. Dealing with it is. What good does it do to prepare for the move that your opponent decides not to hit that day? All you have is what’s right in front of you right now. “As a matter of fact, it is, Mr. Rivers,” Tempest said in a chidingly matter-of-fact tone as she stepped forward and held her hand out for the letter. “Reeeaally, Ms. Tyrell? What’s GTT7?” Troy asked, almost distractedly. “Just a little side project,” Tempest said and reached for the letter. Troy held it just high enough to be out of reach and smiled playfully down at her from his towering height of 6’2”, a full foot taller than her, and said, “Whatsa matter, short stuff?” “Don’t make me go after your kneecaps!” she answered with a giggle. “You know I’m an ankle-biting, backstabbing, no-fair-fighting bitch.” Troy frowned slightly and sliding his arm around her waist said, “Well, that’s a little harsh. She pulled her head back for a moment to drop it over her shoulder and tilted her head quizzically and asked, “What about your pants?” “What pants?” said Troy and their laughter echoed in the space of the loft briefly until being silenced with another kiss. He’s got no idea. He has no idea the danger he holds in his arms, or what I could do if I really wanted to, if he wasn’t just “playing.” But that’s the beauty of it, he always is. Troy would never hurt a fly. This is a guy who rescues kittens and puppies he finds on job sites. He has no idea that one of the last men who was this close to me plunged through a window thirty stories to his death. That’s one of the reasons I liked him. When I met him about two years ago, it became clear to me fairly quickly that Troy was a doer, not a watcher. And he has no use for celebrities on much of any level. He doesn’t know who Jon & Kate are and when they pop up on CNN.com, he shakes his head and says, “That is so NOT news.” And he’s not too into sports either, but what little he likes is baseball. So I was certain he’d never been to a wrestling show, had always flipped right by it on TV, and had never even picked up a wrestling magazine. And he never would. He was perfect. Does that make me a bad person? So hearing him say that name is strangely electric and not just a little terrifying. It’s too close. Tempest. I haven’t been that name in years. And it’s a name that threatens the quiet, simple life I’ve built here. You see Troy is perfect in other ways. I came to love him for all that warmth and that no-nonsense outlook on life. He doesn’t need the world to know his name or thousands of people cheering him on to be satisfied with himself. And I admire that. I want to know what that’s like. I’m ready for that. Aren’t I? I’m enjoying the simple life. And don’t laugh that I can sew. I’m from Hong Kong. I did my time in a sweat shop like every other dirt poor street rat. And over here I did my time in a Chinese laundry. Typical as can be. You think a wrestler doesn’t have a day job? I had to start somewhere. In fact I got my first gig by being the one who could sneak the entire roster’s duds in the back door of the cleaners and repair them by day and then they’d “let” me appear by night. And no, it’s not menial; it’s relaxing to work on something that requires your undivided attention and careful precision, but doesn’t have the immediacy of a perfect dropkick. It’s satisfying to look at your work when it’s finished with a sense of finality instead of looking at your body, battered and bruised, feeling sore, and wondering If you really made the grade or not. Simple. It’s not fair to call him that. It sounds like an insult and really it’s meant to be the highest compliment I can pay him. Let’s say “uncomplicated” instead, uncomplicated by nagging doubts, a shady past, or an ego in constant need of shoring up. I wonder sometimes if I love him for what he is or for what I wish I could be. But right now, at this moment it doesn’t matter.
