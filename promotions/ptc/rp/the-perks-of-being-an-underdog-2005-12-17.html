The Perks of Being an Underdog

17 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-17 The New York Nightlife is a fantastic experience. But when one cannot go out, or chooses to stay indoors for the night, gazing over it through a large bay window is nearly as fulfilling. The Celebrations of his first round GTT5 Victory had since past, and now Jason Warr stood looking over the cityscape from his top floor studio apartment, deep in thought. His girlfriend Angela lay strewn on the couch, fast asleep. Some random action movie playing quitely in the background. The place was small, but sufficient for two career oriented individuals, often on the road. Some would say it was far too expensive, but everytime the thought crossed Warr's mind, he'd climb over to the bay window, and look out, instantly wiping all doubt from his mindset. He thought of many things, but his current fixation was GTT5. Amy Silveira, while talented, had no desire to wrestle him, and it showed. Her defeat had been sound, but it lacked substance, it lacked meaning. It was like fighting a wounded animal with a broken spirit. To Jason Warr, it was like getting a first round bye. It did nothing to prepare him for his next challenge. It had been five nights prior. He and Angela had gotten in from a meal, to find the mail dropped on the ground behind the door, as per usual. What wasn't usual, was the official mailing from Primetime Central, announcing he'd be facing Paddy O'Shea in the second round. Paddy O'Shea was a far more difficult challenge, and Warr had been questioning his readiness since the letter had arrived. For three nights, he'd found himself in front of this window, contemplating. He was the Underdog. Again. Rather than frown and continue his meaningful stare over the shining metropolis, a smile formed on his features. I'm The Underdog. Again. and this status suited him. He slinked his way over to the couch, laying next to his girlfriend, before slowly drifting off to sleep. Pleasant Dreams ====================== Jason Warr awoke from his daydream to the sound of chalk dancing against the blackboard. His eyes darted towards the clock on the wall, 2:14. One Minute until the end of class, and unlike most children his age, he wasn't looking forward to it. It was his first year in public school. His rich upbringing had afforded him the finest private academies, but that wasn't him. Even at a young age, Jason Warr highly resented the family in which had brought him to the world. He'd begged, pleaded, and finally was allowed to attend a public school. He'd never had any trouble with bullying in the past, but he supposed that was because of the strict regulations that his former places of education promoted. Now, for the past week he'd seen a new kid, sometimes two, become the victim of The Bully. He had a good three inches and 50 pounds on all the other students, and he was one mean fuck. Maybe mommy didn't hug him, who knows, but the each time the class was dismissed, everyone ran. Or at least, everyone who had to walk home. Warr was tired of running. The bell sounded, and Warr picked up his backpack, slung it over his shoulder, and walked to the door. He was fast, if he didn't want to be picked on, he wouldn't be picked on, but some of the other kids were helpless. As he power walked toward the gates, he saw it. The Bully had a couple of kids, and he was hitting them, giving him his much needed self esteem boost. Warr stopped and stared, feeling a tinge of guilt. As the other kids ran, they encouraged Warr to come to, but he was frozen. Eventually, he dropped his backpack. Walked forward. And as he approached the Bully, who had his back turned, he closed his eyes and swung. At this age, he was obviously not a professional wrestler. So he didn't know that his fist had sunk right into The Bully's left Kidney, with enough running force to down any adult. The Bully crumpled to the pavement, and Warr kicked him, in the head. Once. Twice. The Bully started bleeding from the nose, he spit out two teeth, and he teared up. Warr leaned in.. "If you ever fuck with me, or any of these other guys again, I'll put you in a coma". He had been the Underdog. And, rather than put him at a disadvantage, it had given him the boost he needed to right a wrong. The Bully showed up at school, for the remainder of the School Year. Each morning, he sat alone, and when class got out, he walked away, faster than anyone else. This feat gained Warr popularity, but he knew it wouldn't be the last time he'd be labeled 'The Underdog'. And he looked forward to the next chapter of his story. -- As his life progressed, Jason Warr faced many adversities. Most of them were your average, run of the mill hardships people have to deal with while growing up. Some, while insignificant to others, stood out prominently in his mind. The Bully story was one of them, and the Clubber followed quick suit. During his college years, Jason took a liking to hanging out in New York Area nightclubs. The whole scene fit his persona, and he found himself walking through the doors of a club nearly every night of the week. Whether he was there just to dance and drink, or to pick up the random girl. It was a cold night, as he stepped through the entrance. This place was new, only open about a month. He'd never been there before, but heard good things from some of his friends. It was a Monday night, but the place was busy. Warr was alone, due to his friends studying from exams, but it didn't put him off any. He never saw the need to study, he got fine marks just showing up. After wading through the mass of moving bodies, he found the bar. The first thing he noticed was the woman behind it. She was smokin' hot, long blonde locks falling past her shoulders, sparking blue eyes. He'd just come for a drink, but after catching a sight of the bartender, he thought a change of plans was needed. He'd gotten her attention, and five top shelf vodkas later, the two were emersed in blatant flirting. What Warr didn't know, was that the stunning Barkeep's ex was in the club that night. About ten seats down from him, grinding a fist into a palm. His name is irrelevant, but his friends called him Tiny. Why Tiny? Because he weighed over 400 Pounds, and was just shy of 6'8". Warr never found out of the moniker, so he would refer to the man as 'The Clubber' from that night on. Both he and her were buzzed by the time her shift ended. She took her purse, and the two proceeded for the door. At that moment, The Clubber stood up in quick persuit. As he was about to slam a bar stool over Warr's head, the Bartender screamed. Warr, though inhebriated, jumped out of the way just in time. The stool shattered against a table, causing it's inhabitants to scurry from the scene. Warr did what came natural, and starting throwing punches into The Clubber's stomach. In return, he received a headbutt to the face that caused him to tumble to the floor. Here was Jason Warr, yet again The Underdog. Faced with the decision to lay there and get the crap beaten out of him, he stared up at the Clubber, who was looming over him, pissed. As The Clubber reached down, Warr kicked up. He caught the Clubber in the back of the left knee, sending him to the floor of the club. Warr stood up, and considered running. That would be the smart move, right? But rather, he walked ten feet over to a billiard table, grabbed a cue, and smashed it over The Clubber's head. To a RESOUNDING applause from the other club goers. He'd been the Underdog, again. And, again, it had helped him. Who said being the Underdog was a bad thing anyway? -- I like being an Underdog. I like going into a match, with no one on my side. I like the rush of completely upsetting an opponent, it's like taking every drug invented by man simultaneously. It suits me. Why? Because, like The Bully and The Clubber, Paddy O'Shea isn't taking me seriously. He's probably embracing his roots, by chugging Guinness at the local pub, rather than studying, or working out. He might show up half drunk expecting an easy win. I hope he does. Because underestimation is like masturbation, my friends. In the end, you're just fucking yourself. -- He shook his head, waking up. The sun was shining through the bay window, causing him to stir. He'd meant to spend the night formulating a plan, something, that would help swing this second round match in his favor. And he had. His passing out on the the couch had given him all the edge he needed, his dreams feuling the fire to compete that burned inside him. He smiled, stretched, and lean back against the couch. He was ready for O'Shea. Walking into the second round, head held high, no nervousness whatsoever, is what some would call a Gift. But in truth? It's just a perk of being the Underdog. -Fin.-
