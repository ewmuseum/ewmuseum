Welcome to Camp Cocytus: Prison Bitch

15 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-03-15 In the summer of 1984 seventeen inmates were able to amass a number of riot control weapons from eight prison guards in the upper-west wing of Camp Cocytus, resulting in a fourteen-hour hostage bid in which the inmates threatened to systematically kill the captive guards until they were released from the facility. Using keys they had procured from their captive’s bodies, the inmates were able to infiltrate a supply room where they stole two crates of tear gas grenades and three handguns, quickly turning the situation into one of the most well-orchestrated prison breaks in modern history. The inmate’s terms were simple; they were to be allowed free passage out of the penitentiary with four of their eight hostages, at which point the other four were to be released at a location they would later specify. When Warden Gerald Hammond tried to reason with the renegade chain gang he was shot four times and hung from the catwalks overlooking the mess hall. It was then that his son Wesley, an officer serving under his father’s tutelage within the prison, took matters into his own hands. Leading a group of outraged riot police and fellow prison guards, Wesley Hammond stormed the west wing of Camp Cocytus and managed to kill seven of the eight rebel inmates. Three of the captives were executed during the scuffle. The sole surviving convict, Inmate #49780, was then taken into the mess hall and hung from the catwalks right next to the murdered Warden. Two weeks later Wesley Hammond was instated as the new Warden of Camp Cocytus. http://x11.putfile.com/3/7222173538.jpg ~<>~<>~<>~ 11:59am, Warden’s Office… He somehow managed to be both welcoming and withdrawn from behind a desk built of solid steel, observing the manacled Rich Rollins with glassy green eyes that had years of experience at tearing down the most unruly of convicts. Rollins stood in the plain blue prison uniform he had been issued earlier that morning, trying not to fidget with the shackles that bound his wrists and ankles. Grinning quietly to himself, Officer Luke Barks remained at Rollins’ elbow, the butt of his riot rifle intentionally pressed into the small of The Renegade’s back. Warden Hammond let the silence linger for what seemed to Rollins like the entire afternoon, but he figured the butt of Bark’s rifle had more to do with it than the Warden’s unnerving quiescence. Finally Hammond spoke. "I would say it was a pleasure to have you in my prison for the remainder of your short life Mr. Rollins, but that would be a lie." Barks snorted in amusement, but Hammond cut him off with a look before turning back to his newest prisoner. "I don’t deal with renegades, sir. Your time under my supervision will not be a trial of my generous patience, I trust." "Was that a question?" Rollins asked with morose sincerity. He received a painful nudge courtesy of his escort for the trouble. "No, it wasn’t." Hammond leaned back in his chair, locking his fingers in his lap as he crossed his legs with the kind of authoritative confidence that only twenty years as a federal Warden could grant. "I like to keep my inmates comfortable, Mr. Rollins. Especially your type; as a celebrated athlete, I don’t want you to feel threatened in my prison lest you threaten the delicate equilibrium of my prison, are we clear?" Rollins scowled darkly, flexing his wrists against his bindings in frustration. "I have no desire to kick the beehive, Warden." Hammond nodded politely at this, but Rollins had other things on his mind. "I want to ask you a favor. I need to ask you a favor." Hammond’s hard face suddenly cracked in amusement as he shared a short chuckle with Officer Barks. "A favor? And what could you possibly have that I would trade such favors for?" Rollins needed this. Here he was, caught in one of the most sophisticated maximum security prisons in North America, and it was one week away from the biggest match of his entire life. He had worked too hard and for too many years to see his dream get smashed like a stained glass window, especially here where he was at the mercy of the same kind of bureaucratic asshole he had fought against his entire life. Still he tried to smile, putting up a confident front that he most definitely didn’t feel. "My name is worth money out in the real world, Warden. A lot of money." Rollins could see he had to choose his words carefully, and from the way Hammond’s bushy eyebrows were raising he knew he was on the right track. "I’ve got a lot of royalties that are going to collect nothin’ but dust and interest for the next twenty years. I make five million dollars a year off of the book sales of my autobiography alone, and sales are only going up since my incarceration. I’ve got a lot to barter with, Warden." Hammond frowned. "What do you want?" Rollins stiffened. "I want you to host the GTT5 finals and let me finish my tournament against Shanty Jones." Rich Rollins had never felt so vulnerable in his entire life. Hammond had apparently not expected such a request; while he didn’t immediately disregard the offer outright, he didn’t exactly jump at Rollins’ copious approach. Absently scratching at the white wings that streaked his temples, Warden Hammond tossed a guarded look at Officer Barks who was likewise shifting uncomfortably behind his captive. "How much is your tournament worth to you, Inmate # 7902?" Hammond grinned at that, reminding The Renegade in no uncertain terms that Rollins belonged to him no matter how large a fortune he tried to bribe him with. "All of it," Rollins replied without a moments hesitation. "About twenty-seven million dollars if you want to get technical." The way Rollins figured it, why the hell not? If he was really going to spend the next few years in this hellhole just so they could throw the switch and fry his brain, what use did he have for worldly possessions? To fight Shanty was to complete a crusade he had embarked on years ago, and to win GTT5 would give him a sense of accomplishment that was worth more than twenty-seven million dollars he’d never get to see again anyway. He could die a complete man, an accomplished man, and the good Warden could turn an insane profit by just going along with it. "That’s not all Warden," Rollins pressed. "Think about the media coverage. Think about the publicity you’d get from hosting the finals of the biggest wrestling tournament the world has ever seen, right here inside your own Camp Cocytus. Hell, Shanty Jones has had his own experiences from the inside of a prison cell, so it could be like the Battle of the Jailhouse Titans. You think people in this very prison wouldn’t be willin’ to bet on a match like that? You could rake in a fifty percent house profit from prison bookies alone, and that’s on top of the fortune I’m givin’ you." "I don’t know, Wes." Officer Barks shoved Rollins forward a few steps out of spite, just to remind Rollins that he could. "What if this fuckin’ baby killer uses this as an opportunity to kill another man on national television? I wouldn’t put it past him." Hammond pursed his lips in consideration. "He’s got a damn good point, Rollins. How do I know you won’t go too far when you get the chance to face this Shanty Jones? If I allowed this match to take place and it ends up with a corpse in the ring… well, let’s just say I’d personally make it two corpses. I’d be deposed faster than a Ford F150 on the autobahn, and you’d never see a tournament trophy anyhow." Rollins regained his poise with a sour look in Barks’ direction before turning back to the Warden. "First of all, we need to get one thing straight. I didn’t kill any babies, and I didn’t kill that woman." He was simmering now, barely keeping a handle on the rage that was slowly bubbling up his throat. "I fuckin’ killed both those men, yeah, but it was only ‘cause they killed her. I’m only here because Seymour fucking Almasy wouldn’t say shit at my trial, but I can live with that only if I can have this one thing, Warden. I’m not killin’ nobody, especially not the man I need to beat in order to win this fuckin’ thing." "You need to calm down Mr. Rollins and remember who it is you’re talking to." Warden Hammond rose from his desk with deliberate care, shooting daggers at Rollins with eyes that hadn’t taken shit from an inmate in twenty years. "Don’t you ever raise your voice to me, son. I might consider your offer and I might not, but I’ll be damned if I give a murdering cocksucker like you so much as a pot to piss in when you speak to me like I’m one of the dumbshit sheep who watched you stomp your foot and slap your hands on television. I am not a wrestling fan, Mr. Rollins. Luke, get him the hell out of my office." "You’re a goddamn fool if you pass up the money I’m offering," Rollins snarled as Barks shoved him out the door. "You tell me no and all that cash gets seized by the state." "Wait just one second," Hammond called out before Barks was able to close his office door. "Bring him back here." Rollins stumbled over his shackles as Barks drove him back through the door like a piece of cattle headed to market. Standing there before the Warden, Rollins didn’t even try to hide the unrepressed anger in his eyes as Hammond grinned, stabbing a finger in Rollins’ direction. "I’ll take your money either way," he said. "The only question is whether or not I give you your match. You and everything you own belongs to me now #7902, and don’t you ever fucking forget it. Luke, stick him in general population." Rich Rollins’ veins burned with scorn as Barks hauled him back into the hall. He made a mental note that the next person to call him #7902 would never again digest solid food. ~<>~<>~<>~ 5:29pm, Mess Hall… "Stop looking like that." With a cardboard container of skim milk in one hand and a tray with something that resembled mashed potatoes with a side of horseshit in the other, Rich Rollins turned around to find a tiny old man standing directly behind him. "What did you say to me?" he asked, more than a little surprised. The old man’s face crinkled slightly as if he were offended to have to repeat himself. "I said stop looking like that." "Like what?" Rollins didn’t know whether to be offended or amused at this shriveled little beet of a man as he turned back around to find a seat in the crowded cafeteria, content to just ignore the fellow. But the fellow wouldn’t be ignored. "Like you think you could take on this whole prison without breaking a sweat on that sweet ‘ol bandanna-covered scalp of yours. There’s a lot of boys around here who don’t like you as a matter of principal, and all it’d take is that look right there to get a shiv stuck in your spleen." "Why the hell should I care?" Rollins spat as he took the first seat he could find that wasn’t packed elbow to elbow with convicted felons. "I’m gonna die in here, old timer. I’d almost prefer it came from somebody I could see rather than bein’ shoved in a hood and fried like a deep-dish pizza." The old man sat down directly across from Rollins, already stuffing his mouth with mashed potatoes. "I ain’t old," he said around a mouthful of mush, "and besides, old kids don’t die. They just adulterate." The comment made The Renegade cough down a gulp of his skim milk, completely caught off guard by the grizzled old convict’s brash sense of humor. He finally looked up at the man and grinned despite himself; with long tangled curls of brown hair down to his shoulders, the man could have been Rollins’ grandfather with all the skin that hung off of his cheeks. When he smiled his skin tightened like wax paper, which only made Rollins chuckle again. The inmates bustled in pseudo-organized lines between the aisles, brushing by without much respect for anybody’s personal space. Rollins let loose an agitated grunt as one man shoved by on his way to the mess window, feeling an elbow connect with the base of his neck. When he turned around all Rollins could see was a tall, muscular skinhead who was too busy joking with his bald buddies to pay The Renegade any attention. "Who’s the Ed Harris look-a-like?" Rollins asked his aging companion. "Somethin’ tells me he ain’t the most charming belle at the ball." The old man looked up, saw who Rollins was referring to, and winced. "That’s Leonard Harris, and while I don’t think it’s true, a lot of people think he could be Ed Harris’ brother. His friends call him Skinhead Ed because of it." "So what do his enemies call him?" The old man shook his head. "Nothing. He leads the resident supremacist mafia, they call themselves White Suburbia." He began twirling a strand of long brown hair with one finger, regarding Harris absently. "He’s one of the guys who don’t like you." Rollins rolled his eyes, trying to change the subject. "What’s with the curls, goldilocks? Shouldn’t you have been sportin’ a white sail, like, eighty years ago?" Trying to choke down a plastic spoonful of mystery meat, Rollins cracked a reclusive smile as he extended one hand. "By the way, the name’s Rich, but I’m sure you already knew that." The old man didn’t accept the offer. Instead he reached up around his ears with both hands and tugged, pulling the entire tangle of his long brown hair off of his head and placed it in Rollins’ waiting hand. "Name’s Fenton, and you’re my new cell mate," he said as Rollins jerked his hand back in surprise. "Don’t worry, it’s just a wig. I’ve been bald as a baby’s ass since ‘97 when they first diagnosed my cancer." Rollins regarded Fenton warily, not in a sympathetic way but more curious than anything else. Fenton just blinked, the now-obvious signs of chemotherapy having turned his wrinkled scalp into a polka-dotted treasure map without the benefit of a treasure. Trying not to slop the ends of the hair in his meal, he handed Fenton his hair back as he said, "They actually give you medical treatment in this shithole?" "They have to," Fenton said as he slid the wig back over his bruised skull, pulling the edges back behind his ears. "Some kind of humanitarian bullshit. You know since that whole cruel and unusual punishment thing, they can’t exactly let an old man get eaten away by a disease that can be treated. I get ferried to Fayette County Hospital down in Vandalia every third Tuesday, shackles and all. Hell of a deal," he finished before stuffing another spoonful of mashed mush between yellowed teeth. "Lucky you. I won’t even live to see my next birthday," Rollins mumbled. Fenton coughed into his hand, hacking and wheezing until a chunk of mystery meat landed in the middle of his gravy. "Neither will I," he said, grinning like a loon. ~<>~<>~<>~ 7:45pm, Courtyard… Rich Rollins had endured a lot of challenges in his life, but he quickly realized that it had all been preparing him for this one moment. The courtyard was big enough to hold a basketball court, a handful of rusted bleachers, a steel box where unruly inmates were thrown in for solitary, two volley ball nets and enough open dirt to toss a football around if the guards were feeling generous. It had enough space for half of the general population, which totaled around nineteen hundred inmates if what Rollins had been told was correct. They were herded out in groups of three hundred for no more than thirty minutes at a time, allowing them to socialize while armed security personnel kept watch from four strategic crow’s nests that sprouted from the four corners where one wing met another. But nobody had told him about the pipe. The entire prison sat right on top of a creek bed no wider than five yards from side to shore. It ran from the hills in the east all the way to the airport that Rollins knew was due west about four miles, and at its deepest was maybe deep enough to wet the saddle on a horse’s back if one were inclined to wade in a little. Where the creek ran through the courtyard was a different story; instead of a flushed out bowl for the inmates to kick around in, an enormous pipe had been constructed from wall to wall to keep anyone from getting any funny ideas about crawling under the facility on their way to free soil. The pipe ran through everything, including the basketball court, which made it difficult to play anything at all. Most of the inmates thought it was the architect’s attempt at a sick joke, a final "fuck you" courtesy of the free world just to make what open space they were given practically unusable. The creek that lay underneath the pipe was named, not surprisingly, Cocytus Creek. And while it made a game of shirts versus skins hard to organize, it made for a perfect surface to pick a fight with the new guy. "So you’re the bad motherfucker who killed that little girl?" The man who approached Rollins was a face he recognized; Skinhead Ed looked more than ready to break in the new fish. "They may not beat you around in the real world for that kind of shit, but you’re in White Suburbia now, bitch." Leaning over the slow curve of the pipe, Rollins offered the racist prick a defiant sneer. "It was a woman, and I didn’t kill her." Slowly pulling himself to his feet, The Renegade met his antagonist with a casual shrug that only barely hid his own dangerous intent. "You take one more step in my direction and I’ll fistfuck your face until my knuckles bleed, comprede?" Their voices carried on the wind, catching the ears of a few of Harris’ bald friends near the bleachers. Before Rollins had the chance to close the distance between them he could already see three more White Suburbia soldiers headed in their direction. Skinhead Ed flashed a snarling set of teeth that was missing a few members, cracking his knuckles in anticipation. "You know what they do to child molesters in prison, Renegade?" he asked, but Rollins knew it was rhetorical. "Let me guess," he said. "You swap stories on who got the better piece of underage ass, then flip each other over so you can decide who gets the tail and who gets the teeth?" The supremacist was not amused. "You got some mouth on you for a man who makes his living rolling around and getting sweaty with other men." He was right next to Rollins now, close enough for The Renegade to smell the man’s anger like a rancid pool of hate. Rollins refused to back away, ignoring the crowd that had started to gather around them. "I figured prison was the right place for me then. I take it you want to be my first boyfriend?" He could actually see the man’s internal restraint snap like a dry twig. Bellowing like an enraged elephant, Harris swung for Rollins’ head, having taken more than enough verbal abuse in front of his home boys. The crowd doubled in seconds as the inmates watched Rich Rollins get knocked across the jaw and shoved back into the steel supports of the courtyard pipe. A unanimous roar engulfed the courtyard as the raging neo-nazi reared back and kicked The Renegade in the stomach, lifting him off the ground and onto the pipe for a second time. Rollins fell to his knees, dry heaving from the straight shot to his gut but refusing to stay down for long. As the supremacist leader took a step back to wallow in the support of his bald buddies, his confident smirk quickly fell as he watched Rollins climb to his feet. "I’ll bet your daddy was black, wasn’t he?" Spitting a bloody glob into the dirt, The Renegade laughed in the man’s face. "Either that or your momma got fucked by one, ‘cause you hit like a ni-…" Harris was swinging wide before Rollins had the chance to berate him any further. It was just the kind of reckless swing Rollins was looking for. The blow was easy enough to duck out of, and Rollins came back with a stiff shot of his own into Harris’ nose. The supremacist jerked back briefly, but years of jailhouse brawling had more than strengthened his tolerance for pain. Rollins grabbed the man’s leg as Harris tried to plant a knee in his chest, grabbed the back of his neck with his other hand, and swung him head-first into the steel supports of the pipe. The crowd groaned in a collective symphony of sympathy as Skinhead Ed bounced harshly against the steel, knocking him on his back in the dirt. Rollins was already on top of him, pounding away on the bigot’s cheekbones until a few of the onlookers decided to step in before the rest of White Suburbia decided to pounce. Rollins shoved them all away. "This fucker had it comin’, and you all know it!" he roared as he continued to tenderize Harris’ face. It took a shotgun blast to halt Rollins’ furious assault. "Alright you two, knock it off before I blow it off," one of the prison guards yelled. The crowd - which was by now the entire courtyard - parted eagerly as Officer David Priess thrust his way to the heat of the brawl. "Rollins, I seriously suggest you step away from the skinhead before I’m forced to help his cause," Priess said as he leveled the shotgun at Rollins’ temple. "Slowly." It didn’t take long for Rollins to abide the officer’s request. Backing off of his nearly unconscious victim, Rich Rollins raised his hands in compliance. "I never hit a man who didn’t deserve it," he lied, cracking a cool grin. "Tell it to the Warden," Officer Priess replied, turning Rollins around and snapping a pair of cuffs around his wrists. "He wants to have a word with you." The crowd "ooohed" like a bunch of kindergarten kids at recess. "You done fucked up now, champ!" somebody hollered as The Renegade was hauled through the basketball court en route to the administration complex. "I done fucked your bald badass like the Warden’s wife," Rollins shot over his shoulder as he was pushed closer to the door. And he smiled. He was starting to get the hang of this. ~<>~<>~<>~ 7:57, Warden’s Office… If Warden Hammond was the boiling teakettle, Rich Rollins was the stove. Needless to say, the Warden was not a happy Cocytus camper. "Twenty-four hours, Rollins. Twenty four fucking hours, and already you’ve nearly managed to instigate a courtyard riot and almost got shot in the process!" His ears were turning red, but Rollins thought better than to point it out. "Do you hear me in there, or did your skinhead sweetheart bust your brains against that pipe too?" "I hear you just fine, Warden." Rollins indicated Hammond’s observation window with the scruff of his chin. "Maybe if you were payin’ attention to your precious prison you’d have noticed that I didn’t start that fight, but I damn sure ended it." Hammond’s mouth opened and closed as if he were about to say something but decided not to. Instead he came up to Rollins, met The Renegade at point-blank eye level, and kneed him swiftly in the crotch. As Rollins fell to the floor in a heap, Hammond caught his chin with the tip of a polished leather cowboy boot and looked down, making sure he had his prisoner’s rapt attention. "You forget yourself one time too many, Mr. Renegade. You really don’t get it, do you?" Squatting down so that Rollins’ groans were played directly into his ear, Hammond smiled an oily smile. "I want your PIN number, Rollins. And you’ll give it to me without incident, otherwise we’re going to have more friction than even you can handle." The Warden looked up at Officer Priess, who was observing the entire altercation with more than a few reservations at the way Hammond was treating a cuffed and unarmed man. "Priess," Hammond barked, "you can go now." Priess seemed incredibly uncomfortable, but after a stern look from his boss he quickly turned around and closed the door behind him. As soon as the officer was out of sight, Warden Hammond drew the tiny revolver that he had tucked into the back of his pants. Rollins was still coughing up his left testicle when Hammond pressed the revolver’s barrel under his chin. "If you can’t find the breath to speak I’ll be more than happy to get you a pencil and piece of paper to write it down," Hammond whispered dangerously. "I want your account numbers, every last one of them, and I want them now." Rollins turned his neck just enough to lock his own watery gaze with the Warden’s maddened stare, trying to work enough moisture into his mouth to speak. "W-what about… the match?" he croaked. The Warden’s lips took a dark turn. "I’m not negotiating with my prisoners. I’ve never negotiated with a prisoner, and I’m damn sure not going to start now. You’ll give me the information or you’ll get smeared all over my wood floor right here and now, Rollins. Take your pick." He was a dead man no matter how he looked at it. Damned if I do, Rollins thought bitterly, and dead if I don’t. It wasn’t a difficult decision. Three minutes later Rich Rollins was leaning back in a chair the Warden had provided, throwing down a pen on top of the paper he had just finished completing at gunpoint. Sliding his revolver into one of his desk drawers, Hammond finally smiled as he snatched up the piece of scratch paper that now contained everything that defined Rich Rollins as a financial entity; business accounts, PIN numbers, savings and checking information, everything. Every asset that Rich Rollins owned was now unlocked to the man who held all the keys, and the thought made The Renegade want to vomit. "I want my match," Rollins passionately declared. "You’re a rich man, Hammond. Now please, just give me my match." Hammond sat still for a good long while, watching Rollins sweat. The Renegade could hear his own heart beat out the steady rhythms of war through his ribcage, pounding in his earlobes and throbbing in his fingertips. All the while Warden Hammond watched, quite obviously enjoying his prisoner’s agony. "No," he finally decided, smiling broadly. "I don’t think you deserve the attention." Rollins’ lip twitched involuntarily. He pressed at his restraints in frustration, quietly fuming as Hammond sat there grinning like a Cheshire cat, infuriatingly calm. "Warden," Rollins said slowly, "if you don’t do this for me, you’ll never get the chance to execute me. You’ll walk into my cell tomorrow morning and find me hanging from the ceiling with a belt around my neck. I swear to Christ I’ll do it." "I don’t think so," the Warden replied. "I’m moving your execution date forward. Your tournament final would have been five days from now, correct?" Rollins nodded wearily, the movement itself hollow as a hooker’s promise. "Then we’ll do it in four." The words echoed in Rollins’ head as if they were spoken by a mirage, but the pain in his gut knew Hammond was deadly serious. "And you’ll spend those four days in solitary so that we don’t have any problems keeping you alive for the duration." It was too late for Rollins to feel threatened or intimidated. He had already crawled in the back of his own head, finally resigned to his fate. "You’re gonna fry like the Fourth of July, son. If you believe in God, I suggest you start making travel arrangements." But Rollins had already left the building. To be concluded…
