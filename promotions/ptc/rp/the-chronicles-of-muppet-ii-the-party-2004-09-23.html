The Chronicles of Muppet II - The Party

23 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-23 Everyone knows the key to a great party is not trying to make a big entrance. "Ready?" Well, almost everyone. "Ready." The organiser of this particular party, whoever he might be, certainly knew what he was doing, and had no lack of money to accomplish it. He had hired the local concert hall, removed all the chairs and stuff, and packed it full of people looking for a great night out, and on very short notice too. It certainly wasn't a highly exclusive party, I mean, the Muppet Man was there, was he not? However, even though his appearance had been hyped to death in only a few hours, he had not yet shown his face. Most people didn't care. They were just there for the beer and the music, but more importantly, they were all trying to get laid. That was on the forefront of everyone's mind, bar none. It was human nature at it's purest. Enter the Muppet Man and King Muppet. Behind the pounding wall of speakers they crouched, whispering to each other and giggling like school girls. By whispering, I mean half-shouting... the speakers really were very loud. The plan was to knock over the left side of the wall of speakers from behind (they had carefully placed themselves so no one would get in the way) leap on top of them with a megaphone, stir up the crowd, and really get the party going. Apparently, people had been complaining that the music was too loud anyway. In the Muppet Man's mind, the plan was perfect. They were going to be welcomed with open arms. "PUSH!" he screamed, and he and King simultaneously shoulder barged the back of the speakers, causing a massive distortion. They rammed it again, and slowly, it began to topple. "TIIIIIIMBEEEEER!" screamed Muppet, finding the whole thing incredibly funny. With an almighty crash, putting even Craven Moore's heroic leap from the outside of the ring to shame, the whole hall shook. I mean literally shook. A good few people lost their footing, and there were even a few screams. Luckily, the Muppets' faulty scheme failed to do any harm further than scaring the shit out of a thousand people, and a couple more undiscovered consequences. Grinning widely, they leaped up on top of the fallen sound system, Muppet whipping out the megaphone. Putting it to his mouth, he shouted something, but nothing came out. Looking a little irritated, he tried again. "Stupid piece of crap... broken..." There was a deathly silence as Muppet tried to fix the megaphone, King slipping off into the background, leaving his 'client' to face the crowd alone. Muppet felt his face reddening beneath his mask. Suddenly, a member of the crowd regained his senses, and looked over to what the sound system had fallen on... "Hey... he crushed the beer! GET HIM!" So much for undiscovered consequences. That was the thing about the Muppet Man. His plans, his ideas, his spur-of-the-moment schemes... they never worked. Not once in all the years he could remember had one of his 'ingenious' ideas actually worked. His surely unhealthy exposure to his 'manager' didn't help matters either. King was a perfectly intelligent man, but he lacked a little thing called common sense. Muppet didn't have it in bucketloads himself, either. This was all fine and good for a laugh now and then, but often, it had serious reprecussions. One of these reprecussions was particularly memorable to the Mupept Man. He had been losing faith in the world. He was turning his back on the world, and his partner. Without realising it, he was selling his soul, and throwing his life into the blender. He was never going to be the same again. ---- A solitary figure sat in the almost pitch black room. The only light was seeping almost hopelessly through the drawn curtains. The man was hunched over at a desk, poring over a book, almost obsessively. I'm sure there would have been little emotion, had his eyes been visible. The almost complete darkness did not hinder him, and he read on, occasionally stopping to take down some notes on a scrap piece of paper. On closer inspection, there were a good few pieces of scrap paper with notes on them... about 20, to specific. Next to the desk, there was a large pile of books, carelessly discarded. The Muppet Man of yesteryear was a man possessed. With what, no one knew. Not even his closest friend, almost brother, King Muppet, knew what he was truly up to. No one ever knew, or would know, what went on inside that darkened cell he called his brain. It would forever remain his and his alone, and would remain locked up inside there for as long as he lived. He was obsessed, compelled beyond anything to finish what he had started. He was so close... another day, and he would crack it. He had been telling himself this for 3 months now. Knock. Knock. Knock. The slow knock on the door startled him, almost awakening him. He turned to look at the door of his study, his private sanctuary, for a couple of seconds, debating whether or not to answer... or was he? It was impossible to tell how his mind was working. Slowly, though, he got to his feet and moved slowly over to the door. He turned the knob, and opened it an inch, so that a crack of light shot through into the room. "Andy... we have a match tomorrow. We need to prepare, this is a big opportunity, about as big as we're going to get. We can't fail here, Andy. We can't fuck this up." King Muppet's voice was low, almost hopeless. He had almost lost faith in his partner, given up hope. Something had taken over the Muppet Man, something had buried itself deep inside him, and was eating him up from the inside. "I... I'm sorry, Will. I won't be long... just give me a few minutes to gather my notes." His voice was shaky and unsure, but it satisfied King. "Great." Without another word, King Muppet slipped away, and Muppet shut the door, quickly darting back to his desk. He rummaged around with the paper, quickly filing it away in a draw, before opening another draw. He pulled out a cigar... but this was no ordinary cigar. It was unnaturally long, and freshly wrapped... ---- With the memory still fresh in his mind, the Muppet Man hardly had the heart to run away from the screaming mob. He didn't particularly want to be pounded into a bloody pulp either, though, so he really didn't have any choice. He was a very fast sprinter, which was more often than not a good thing, especially as the rabble chasing him were stumbling and tripping over each other. King Muppet was nowhere to be found. Bastard. Muppet quickly hopped off the speakers, sprinting towards the stage. This probably wasn't a smart move, but he didn't have much time to consider. Plus, there ws no way he was leaving now. Not of his own free will, that was. He dived up onto the stage, rolling to avoid a hand grasping for his foot. He was quickly back on his feet, glancing behind him. People were beginning to clamber up onto the stage, so he wasted no time in continuing his escape. The problem was, there was nowhere to go. He looked around hopelessly, seeing a door and darting over towards it. He fumbled with the handle, but it wouldn't open. Now really panicking, he felt himself unable think because of the pounding music from the other set of speakers. Suddenly, a voice piped up from below him. "The curtain! Climb the curtain!" It was King Muppet. Muppet swore under his breath. "You bastard! Get up here and help me!" "No time," King argued quickly, "Just climb!" It was the only option left. Grudgingly, he began to clamber up the curtain on one side of the stage. He was a good climber, and it wasn't long until he reached the top. He could make a stand there, more than two or three of them couldn't climb up at the same time. For now, he was safe, at least in his own mind. "Ha! Try and get me up here!" he shouted down to the crowd below, grinning. Rip. "Oh... shit..." Muppet fell a good 20 feet, screaming like a little girl, before landing right on top of the people. They didn't rip him limb from limb, as he thought they would, though. Instead, they began to pass him along. Was he... crowdsurfing? No, it turned out, as he reached the other side of the hall. One guy kicked open the emrgency exit, and they threw him to the concrete outside. He landed hard, and sat up, rubbing his back and cursing. "And don't come back until you get some booze!" Slam. There he was, outside, alone, his back hurting. Still incredibly peeved with King Muppet, he whipped out his mobile, keying in a number. It was time to make some phonecalls. ---- The Muppet Man leaned heavily against the concrete wall, bored as hell. He could hear the pounding music from outside, albeit very faintly, and it was driving him crazy. He tapped his foot to the beat, looking up at the dark night sky, and cursing himself silently for being so utterly stupid. How long were they going to keep him waiting? He had ordered a good deal of beer almost an hour ago, putting a considerable dent in his wallet, and they still hadn't arrived. What was really eating him up, though, was the fact that King Muppet was still inside, enjoying himself. Well, he thought, trying to cheer himself up, At least I have some time to myself. But he didn't want time to himself. He had got enough of that in the past 3 years. Plus, he really didn't want to have to think about his upcoming round 2 match in GTT4. He had defeated Justin Vivazi Locke in the first round, and he was ecstatic. There was nothing quite like performing in front of a packed crowd against a formidable opponent, and coming out on top, especially for an organisation as prestigious as PTC, in a tournament as big as GTT4. What he really wanted was a good long break, but he only had a week until his next match, this time against Scottywood from NGW. Plus, he was booked for WWA Meltdown, he had to take on Brian Calvin. "This is bullshit," he muttered, before slamming his head back against the wall for letting pessimism take control. What right did he have to complain? He had it great, really. He had a huge paycheck, the WWA US Title, a spot in round 2 of the GTT... so what the hell was wrong? All of this stuff was fine, but there was no finishing touch. There was no spark to ignite the fire inside the Muppet Man, nothing to get him really worked up... nothing for him to believe in. He had no beliefs, he had no religion. He had no motivation. Since almost re-inventing himself, going back to his roots, stepping into the light, he had been undefeated. He didn't want this to end. He desperately didn't want to fall into a period of depression again... it was his greatest fear. He so hated what he had been before that he was too scared to change, to adapt. When it came down to it, that had always been the problem, he was just too blind to see it. "Hurry up," he spat, trying to take his mind off the situation, only to find himself in another. Whatever he did, however hard he tried, he was never happy. He had won the US Title against overwhelming odds. He had come out on top in round 1 of the GTT. None of this mattered. If he couldn't find happiness in life, it was all meaningless. He was jolted back to reality by the sound of an overly large engine. A truck had pulled up, right in front of him. Finally... it was here. "Here's the shipment, got here as fast as we could," the driver said, getting out. "Oh, I'm so happy for you..." "Shut it. This isn't my problem." Muppet forced himself not to lash out, remaining silent. The driver handed him a cheque, which he hurriedly signed, not even looking at the quite substantial figure. He supposed he didn't want to know. "Thanks," he said quietly, after a period of silence. "Want to surprise them?" the driver asked, obviously having a sense of humour. Muppet almost jumped at the opportunity. "...No," he replied after a little delay. "No... not this time. I think I'll play it safe." And so the Muppet Man was in. He had managed to miss the whole early stage of the party; no one was dancing anymore. They were all relaxing, and trying to score. Muppet was relieved... he had never been a good dancer. Repressing several embarassing memories, he set off through the crowd, looking for the man who had got him into this mess in the first place. His manager, King Muppet. Come to think of it, he hadn't actually done ANY "managing" of any kind yet. Muppet was beginning to wish he had stayed solo. But this wasn't the time for wishing. Finally, Muppet noticed his friend's distinct long blonde hair. Moving closer, Muppet noticed that he was sitting at the bar (which had been very hastily erected. Stop laughing, you childish fools! Jesus...) with a drink in one hand, apparently deep in conversation with an old man. What an old man was doing at this party, Muppet had no clue, still he moved in, taking a seat directly behind King, who had yet to notice him. "And, so, obviously, I stabbed him with my bayonet," the old man said, beaming. "You stabbed him?" King said, alarmed. "Oh Lord no! It wasn't a real bayonet, just an umbrella. The expression 'stab with a bayonet' meant to poke someone with your umbrella! That was the style at the time, you see." "Yes, I see, Benjamin..." King said, quieter than before, trying hard not to laugh. "Please try not to interrupt, my good Nicholas... my memory isn't what it used to be. Yes, so, I stabbed the Johnson with my bayonet..." "The what...?" King said, trailing off, confused. "The Johnson! That was what we called paperboys back then, the Johnson. I don't know what happened to the old phrases... you young people, you have no taste!" "That's because of... uh... the head of the paperboys?" It was a stab in the dark. "Exactly." "And he was Johnson?" "Johnson? What... no! He was Dean! Johnson? Where's the logic in that?" Clearing his throat loudly, the Muppet Man tapped King Muppet on the shoulder. "We... need... to... talk," he said, slowly and clearly, through gritted teeth. King did an almost comic double take, his eyes widening as soon as he took in who was standing before. "Oh... hey... hi! So... how're you doing?" King said, putting on a huge, cheesy grin which fluttered before dying out completely. "Can the crap. You abandoned me up there! How could you do that to me?" Muppet said rather loudly, causing some people to crane their necks to have a look. "Excuse me, Jay, is this gentlemen bothering you?" the old man Benjamin asked. "No... no, Benjamin, it's fine," muttered King. "What?" said Benjamin, putting his hand to his ear. "It's fine," King said at a normal level. "What?" "IT'S FINE!" "Ok, ok... no need to shout, now, Ray," Benjamin said, trailing off into a gentle tut. King chuckled lightly, turning back to Muppet. "He's hopeless, y'kno..." he trailed off, seeing the expression on Muppet's face. "Ok. Fine. You're right, I'm sorry. What I did was wrong. I should have stuck with you til the end and blah blah blah..." "I was out there for over an hour!" Muppet said, his anger gradually building. "Well, bitching about it isn't going to help. What could you possibly gain from shouting your lungs out at me?" "Nothing. However, I could gain a great deal of enjoyment from kicking your ass..." "Come on, Andy... the night is young! There's plenty of opportunity for us to have a great time. And, you never know..." he ended, raising his eyebrows suggestively. He had never been good at hinting things. "...Fine," Muppet said, sighing. It was true... there was nothing he could gain from kicking his ass, especially here. Things could only get worse. "Heh. Sucker..." King noted very quietly, grinning broadly. "I'm standing right here!" "..." "..." "Note to self: Hold back revealing comments until coast is clear..." Muppet looked down, smiling at the floor. Maybe this night would turn out ok after all. "Up for a little wager?" King said, grinning slyly. He was quite the gambler. "Well..." Muppet trailed off, unsure. He had some bad experiences with King's 'wagers' from before. Memories of . "Ok, I guess." "Let's say..." King began, looking around. "Ah. I'll bet you that I can get that girl, over there..." he pointed out the girl, "I bet you I can get her number." "You're on," Muppet said, grinning. "How much?" "Oh, not much. Let's say... £1000?" "Ok, a... WHAT?" King suddenly coughed, a cough that sounded strangely like 'wuss!' Hardly an original tactic, but effective. Muppet sighed. "Fine... you're on." King Muppet winked, his smirk still intact (much to Muppet's annoyance), and he turned to walk over to the girl. Muppet leaned against the bar, a smirk beginning to appear on his face too, as King reached her, and began to talk to her. This was going to be a rejection of spectacular proportions. He turned, ordering a beer. He turned back, just about catching the girl (who really was very hot, if I hadn't mentioned that already) writing her number on the inside of King's arm, giggling, and then turning to walk away with him, arm in arm. The Muppet Man's grip slowly tightened on the glass, shaking, and causing a good deal of beer to spill. "Another $1000 down the drain, eh?" The old man, Benjamin, was back. As Muppet turned to speak to him, he realised that he didn't look nearly as old as he'd thought at first. Maybe he wasn't as insane, either. "What? Oh, yeah..." To be honest, Muppet had completely forgotten about the money. That was just sprinkling salt on the already gaping wound. He made a mental note to cut King's pay drastically... before remembering that King also handled the accounts. That clever bastard... "Well, you win some, you lose some. Hey... you're the Muppet Man, aren't you?" Benjamin noted, a hint of awe in his voice, as if just seeing Muppet for the first time. "Jeez, what gave it away?" "Well, obviously the mask, but also..." "It's a rhetorical question!" "Believe it or not, old chap, I'm a wrestling fan, and I've been following GTT4 pretty closely." "I believe anything, nowadays," Muppet said, still feeling rather dejected. His GTT hopes were practically going down the drain. "You're facing... hmm, who was it..." "Scottywood," Muppet informed him. "Ah yes, that's the one! Any tactical insights, any special gameplans?" "Well... float like a butterfly, sting like a bee." Muppet was fully aware of how pathetic that sounded, but it was the only thing that popped into his head, and probably his only chance, too. "You know, there's a funny story behind that phrase..." "Oh dear, look at the time. I really must be going..." "It's 10!" "...Must you ruin everything?" "It's my job, Andy." Muppet was surprised. This seemingly senile old man, who it turned out wasn't nearly as old as given on the first impression, had remembered his name. He didn't even remember telling him his name, come to think of it. Probably best to ignore that... Still, his confidence was beginning to slowly rebuild. What hadn't he overcome in the past? He had beaten Justin Vivazi Locke, which many people considered an upset. He had defeated Quietus... no... he had defeated the Devil's Own. He was on top of the world, and he wasn't going to fall. Not now. Not like this. Of course, NGW's famed Scottywood was going to be a monumental challenge. Of course, he was going to have to wrestle like he had never wrestled before. He didn't mind. The Chronicles of Muppet enter the third act. The opposition is formidale, but so am I. The opposition is focused, he is ready. So am I. It's been so long since I've had even a glimpse of success, a glimpse of true recognition. I've come so far, and I've taken the first step, and I'll be fucked if I give it up now. And so the show goes on... May it never end. "You know bees die when they sting someone?" "...Oh shit."
