Junkhead - Chapter 3: "We Will Pay Respect"

14 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-14 "Walk away me boys, walk away me boys and by morning we’ll be free. Wipe the golden tear from your mother dear and raise what’s left of the flag for me." – Flogging Molly The truth is that "The Jewserweight" Ilan Levin was Twizzy’s half brother. Davis Branxton had a tricky family tree that was ripe with divorce. Davis’ parents, Avram Branxton and Michelle, were married in the late sixties and had Davis soon after. Not long after that they were divorced. Each parent remarried in the early seventies. Davis’ father married a lovely woman named Sarah who lived in Lynbrook, Long Island, now known as the town that Raymond Barone lived in on the TV show "Everybody Loves Raymond." Avram and Sarah lived there the rest of their life and had one child, a daughter named Lilly. Lilly now is married and lives in Southside, Long Island with her husband and one-year-old. In May 2001 Sarah died of breast cancer at the age of sixty-four. Michelle, Davis’ mom, married a good man named Jacob Rothstein. Jacob had a daughter from a previous marriage, Jane, who married and divorced professional wrestler "100%" Jonathan Cads. Their son Jake is now eight. Jacob and Michelle had two children of their own as well, Becca and Ilan. Davis’ mom died in 1998 in a car accident. Being the only children who were boys Ilan and Davis were very close despite their large difference in age. Ilan denied it his entire career, but some say he followed in his big brother’s footsteps to become a wrestler. Before going to wrestling school he had graduated from Columbia to become an investment banker. He and three friends went to the school Courage Wrestling Skills over the summer in Georgia on a lark. Ilan and one friend graduated, but Ilan was the only one to even perform in a professional match. The funeral is a beautiful one. Ilan’s father spared no expense. Quite a few people from all over the wrestling business are on hand as well as a dozen of Ilan’s college friends and as much family as could be reached. Even two of the Israeli Rothsteins made it. Needless to say, Ilan was a very loved boy. And Davis was not going to miss his favorite brother’s funeral. He looks over the open casket solemnly. It is obvious that no one in the Rothstein family expected Twizzy to show up, but they didn’t say anything on the matter. This was not the time. Jacob, Jane, and Becca all greeted Davis heartily, but warily. For Jane and Becca it was often hard to speak to their step/half brother and Jacob never took himself to the position of father figure to Davis. They love him, but not in the same vein as Ilan or each other. Davis sees his half brother lying down, arms crossed over his chest and he whimpers. "I’m going to miss you, Ilan," he whispers. He smiles. It’s hard to do. "So many times Caldy asked me to get you to come to GCW. So many times. That would have been a lot of fun, wouldn’t it have? Goat and Triangles." He whimpers again. "Oh, it would have been great." His eyes close, he can’t look anymore. The water starts to well up and he can hear his crying start to buzz in his own head. "I love you," he says and walks away. Later on, once he’s pulled himself together and popped a Twistodium pill in the bathroom of the funeral home, Davis wants to mingle and share some memories about his brother. Not many faces are familiar besides relatives and neither party would enjoy that conversation. There is one face though that is very familiar. He sees the face of a true friend who Twizzy hasn’t seen in years. Someone he didn’t even think would be here, but it only stands to attest to his heart why he is. Ilan’s trainer, Twizzy’s mentor, "Courage" Ricky Victory. The man was beaming once he saw Davis. "How’re you doing, Twizzy my boy?" Ricky Victory, real name Ricky Vincent, asks. The two shake hands honorably. Ricky Victory was an all-star wrestler in the South winning numerous titles and even some tournaments. In the mid-nineties he opened up a wrestling school called Ricky Victory and Courage Wrestling Skills or CWS for short. The school gave birth to numerous well known stars such as his two boys Nicholas and Charlie, G.O.D., E.V.E., "100%" Jonathan Cads, "The Coolest" Johnny Crush, Quetzal, Yellow-Striped Tiger, and of course "The Jewserweight" Ilan Levin. Twizzy was in the very first class. Davis nods. "How’re you, Ricky?" He smiles lightly. The whole situation and the pills make Davis feel almost like he isn’t even here. "I’m doing really good, Twizz. My two boys are working really hard in the SWL. I admit I had to pull a few strings to get them to accept Charlie. If that boy can hone himself he’ll be a real show stopper. His brother though is doing real well. He’s such a real solid performer, Twizzy. S-O-L-I-D," he gushes. Davis’ smile gets bigger. Ricky never lost that in ring quirk he always had of spelling out certain words to emphasize their importance. "And the school is going really well. We’ve got some good people there. May take them awhile to develop their talent, but at least they’ve got it." "If anyone can develop some young kids, it’s you," Davis flatters. Ricky hits Davis in the arm. "Don’t do that. If I can only have one trait I’d like it to be modesty. Now tell me what you’ve been up to these days. Still smashing records and pounding faces in GCW? I hear that promotion is doing great right now!" The smile fades. Twizzy didn’t want to hurt Ricky and tell him that he’d pretty much left to pursue a new career in drug abuse. Everyone in the wrestling industry knew that Twizzy took Twistodium, but almost no one knew the extent of it. The only people that suspected anything were Steven Caldera, owner of GCW, and Davis’ father who had shunned him years ago because of it. "Actually," Davis began, "I entered myself in a tournament. GTT5-…" "Oh, I’ve heard of that," Ricky interrupts. "PTC, right?" Twizzy nods. "I already won my first match too. My next is against a guy named Red Rock. A skinny kid. He makes a lot of jokes." "Another comedian! Just like you!" Ricky punches Twizzy in the arm again. "’Goat’s Good!’ and all that, right? Hahaha!" His laugh echoes through Twizzy’s skull. He nods and smiles along though. Ricky pauses and his face turns from happiness to see and speak to Twizzy to sorrow for the reason they have gathered. "Hey, Twizzy, I’ve got an offer for you. Down at the school we’re having a tribute show for Ilan. A lot of the students and some alumni are going to be there. I know my boys and Yellow-Striped Tiger are wrestling. G.O.D., E.V.E., and Dr. Jenkins are all going to be there as well. If you’re not too busy we’d L-O-V-E you to be there. It’s this Sunday. I’ll pay for your ticket down to Georgia so you don’t have to worry about that. What do you say? Can you make it? I’ll put you in the main event with someone like Tiger or Crush or Cads. It’d mean a lot to us to honor Ilan’s memory. That’s what this business is about. H-O-N-O-R." Twizzy doesn’t have to think about it for a second. He still has his wrestling uniform. It’s one of the only material possessions he still owns. Everything else he has isn’t even his. Twizzy didn’t even own a suit to wear to the funeral; he had to borrow one from Jacob. An old gray wool number that looked awful and felt itchy. He didn’t have to think about putting on the tights and jumping into the ring for Ilan or for Ricky. But he did hesitate. He wanted to and knew he was going to, but he hesitated. Was it too dangerous? Had it been too long? A quick match with someone like G.O.D. or Jonathan Cads? He might hurt himself or worse yet someone else. "Of course! Of course I’ll be there!" Ricky smiles again, but this time lightly. "Good. Twizzy, good for you. I’ll see you there, boy." He pats Twizzy on the shoulder. "It’s good to see you, son. R-E-A-L good. I’m sorry for your loss, kid. For our loss. For wrestling’s loss." And then Ricky walks away to give his condolences to Jacob, Jane, and Becca. To honor Ilan’s memory. That’s what this was all about. Ilan’s memory. That’s why Twizzy will perform in Georgia. That’s why he’s back wrestling again. That’s why he called Steven Caldera when he heard that Ilan had died. That’s why he came to the funeral. That’s why he entered himself in GTT5 in Ilan’s stead. He isn’t replacing Ilan in the tournament. He’s avenging him. This is Ilan’s tournament, Twizzy is just doing the wrestling.
