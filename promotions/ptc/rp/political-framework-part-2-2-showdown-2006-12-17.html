Political Framework: Part 2 [2] - Showdown

17 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-12-17 Given his past, this wasn't the first time Cedric Riley had been held at gunpoint. Although it was an inconsistent nature, he was usually a fairly patient man – enough, even, to avoid panicking when a gun was pointed at his chest. It was a reaction that had grown only with experience; when he joined the CIA, the training process never actually involved a real-life encounter. Simulations were the bread to his butter in his quest to become an agent, but every quest leads to a bigger and potentially more problematic obstacle. With that being said, his first mission had gone remarkably smooth – a quick kill, if you will, that didn't involve much more than a quick seize and fire in the bedroom of a man's home. But there he was, surrounded by the inner walls of a luxurious yacht, with his opposition standing a close but safe distance away. Cedric had only seen the man once before – one of Victor Roberts handymen who he had referred to as 'Mike', but even that title couldn't be trusted. The man was of average height and build, with short, dirty-blond hair and thin eyebrows. He had a well-established shooting stance and held his weapon dead still between his hands, his finger poised on the trigger and ready to fire at any given moment. Cedric knew that the only way to escape such a predicament without getting the bullet was to seize the opportune moment. But it wasn't as cut and dry as that; it was complicated. Every move was crucial. Every thought that raced through each man's head contributed to what their reaction would be, and in Cedric's case, if he would come out alive. It wasn't that he was unaware of his disadvantage, he just knew that as slim as his chance of getting out alive were, there was still a chance. And that's all he needed. Because shooting someone isn't as easy as just pulling the trigger. You see it in the movies all the time, of course, where a ruthless bastard blasts a shotgun in a helpless person's face and thinks nothing of it. Those people existed in real life, but they were few and far between. The gunman in this scenario, Mike, had killed a person before during his time as a criminal. He was an associate of Victor Roberts, the organized crime mastermind, but there were other ways in which to send a message than putting a bullet in their head. He was in his mid-thirties, and feared that he was a hitman in training, something that he never wanted to become. He had a wife and a daughter. His way of making money to support them, while illegal, wasn't that of a killer. The potential victim in this scenario, Cedric Riley, had killed his fair share of people before. He knew exactly what was racing through Mike's head, because he had experienced it enough times himself. The most overwhelming feeling was that you were taking away another person's life completely; through familiarity that feeling had somewhat died inside of Cedric, but he still knew it. As much as anyone sensible would dread to know, that is certain. Fortunately for Cedric, he had a gun within the inner pocket of his jacket. But making the wrong move would spell disaster. They were seemingly frozen in time. The moment was still between them as their eyes locked and their limbs, while unmoved, were ready for action. Sounds from above them filtered through the deck but didn't deter their focus. The will to survive was predominant while emotions were put to the side – the motivation for shooting was far from emotion anyway. Mike didn't know who Cedric was, nor did he really care; it was his job to ensure that his boss's criminal secrets remained veiled. An untrustworthy stranger had come aboard this man's yacht and searched through his personal space, which would certainly yield unwanted results. Cedric needed to be dealt with – and Mike was the man to do it. "You're going to tell me who the hell you are and why the fuck you are down here right now or I'm going to plant a bullet in your head," the gunman, Mike, demanded forwardly. Cedric's eyes lingered to the gun. A smile flickered across his face as he made a conscious effort to seem unaffected by the threat, then replied calmly, "And why should I do that?" Mike jerked towards him. "Because I'll fucking shoot you if you don't!" "And what good will that do?" Cedric asked, tapping into Mike's conscience. "You said yourself that you wanted to know who I am and what I'm doing here." Cedric paused, then continued, "And if you shoot me now and leave me for dead, those questions won't be answered, will they?" "Don't be fucking smart with me," the gunman shot at him quickly. "I know these games that you're playing. I've been there before. I know what it's like to be hanging on to your last lifeline." "I'll have you know I'm well aware of my own situation," Cedric said smartly. "What are you, some sort of fucking cop?" the gunman spat towards his target, inching towards him again. "I'm more than what you are," Cedric shot back. "How does it feel to always handle someone else's dirty work? How does it feel to be pointed around like a little bitch, following orders to someone you don't even care about it?" "No – shut the fuck up!" he screamed angrily. "None of this fucking matters … one wrong move and you'll be dead." "Then why haven't you done it yet?" "Because we want answers, Johnathan." A sudden, yet very feminine voice came from across the room. Regina Saunders – 'Elizabeth Jones' to the participants. She strutted confidently across the room. Cedric looked confused, both as an agent and as 'Johnathan', and said, "Elizabeth … what are you doing here?" "I think the better question is, Mr. Carter, what are you doing here?" "I don't understand--" Cedric began, trying to figure out what was going on. "You can't understand that a powerful businesswoman like me would be in the ranks of a highly influential criminal? I always knew you were simple minded," she scoffed. "Typical cop." Regina stood next to the gunman now, and looked from Cedric to Mike. "Put the gun down, Mike, I can handle this." The gunman kept his woman steady and flashed his cautiously from Cedric to Regina and back. "No. Victor told me to kill him if necessary. I don't even know why the fuck you're down here." Regina rolled her eyes. "Victor was always too hot-headed and arrogant. This man here could be a valuable source of information, I'll have you know." She looked at Cedric. "And if you choose not to cooperate, we'll kill you." Mike submitted, lowering his gun. Wrong move. Cedric lurched forward and tackle the gunman, taking him to the ground. Cedric was on top of him now. He fed him three quick punches – blood trickled from Mike's lip and the corner of his eye. It didn't stop Cedric, as he had his hands around Mike's neck, effectively cutting off his air supply and causing an immediate panicked reaction. And the moment of stillness was when Cedric felt cold steel against the side of his head. Regina had her gun out and it was against her partner's temple. "Take your hands away from his neck and put them behind your head, and I'll consider not killing you." Cedric paused, his arms restraining the helpless fight that Mike's hands were trying to make. Regina leaned in and muttered in Cedric's ear, "Trust me." Cedric let go of the choke hold and put his hands behind his head, slowly standing up. "Now don't – fucking – move," she threatened, holding the gun against his head. Mike stood up quickly, rubbing his neck from the force exerted by his attacker. He faced up with his opposite, spitting out blood on the carpet and wiping the blood off his face. Mike looked at Cedric with sheer hate and a look of disgust on his face. The bloodied man reared back and head-butted Cedric right in the face. Lights out, AgentDash. * * * And just like Cedric Riley had been held at gunpoint before, he was not a stranger to being knocked out and captured. As a CIA agent, Cedric had gone through rigorous training, and one of the most prominent messages he received while there was to not get caught. Because after all, that would defeat the purpose of being a spy, right? Right: it came with the job description. Paradoxically, the ability to escape from captures and not give out critical information at the same time was in the same description; it was a rather multi-talented position, really. In wrestling, the worker takes on two different roles – his personality, and his gimmick. In the wrestling environment he kicks his real personality to the curb and takes on his character gimmick, which may or not be based on his actual self. As an agent, there is role conflict and confusion, because it is much easier for the two to interrupt each other. In the CIA, the job was essentially a lifestyle; while Cedric admittedly enjoyed that, he had to sacrifice the position due to other conflicting lifestyle decisions. That's not to say working for the FBI or CIA can be compared to wrestling, however. Because unlike in wrestling, the risk of death is more ostensible while working as an agent, which makes one wonder why Cedric opted to make the jump back. Well, first of all, it wasn't really a jump back, because he hadn't jumped in the first place. Above all, he missed the thrill. He missed the reliability others had that he was going to make something happen. He missed the power. He craved it. Needless to say, Cedric had found himself in another predicament – he awoke from his unconscious state, bound in ropes. It was very dark in his current position, which made him feel just as useless as being knocked from reality. He felt his hands tied behind his back and a piece of tape across his mouth. He instinctively tried moving around; it was hopeless anyway, but he realized that he was being contained in what felt like an enclosed space. And then there was a sliver of light as a door opened slightly. A female voice whispered, "Cedric?" Regina. Cedric reacted with a call for help that was muffled by the tape on his mouth. Regina reached for it and ripped it off. With his lips now free and mouth open, Cedric relished the idea of breathing through something other than his nose. Through the gasps of breaths he managed, "What the hell is going on, Regina?" "Quiet!" she hushed, trying to keep her voice low. "The party's dying down, and after everyone's gone, there going to shoot you, and throw you off the deck somewhere out in the ocean." "Explain to me what the hell just happened before I got knocked out first!" Cedric stressed, trying to keep his voice down. "There's no time. I had to do what I did to keep you alive … that bastard was going to kill you, and he's still planning on doing it," she informed while trying to untie the knotted ropes that bound Cedric's wrists. "The drugs … did you see them?" Cedric asked quickly. "Yes," she said optimistically. "I'm going to try and take care of that. First I have to explain some things to Victor." Cedric's hands were now free due to Regina's quick work. "Here's your gun back, just in case," she said as she held it out for him in the dark. He felt his way and grabbed it, putting it in his pocket. "Victor's man took it from you after he knocked you out. Thank god you didn't use it …" "There wasn't an opportunity," he muttered bitterly. "Okay," Regina said. "Anyway, you're in a closet in the same room that we were in. You need to find a way to go upstairs and jump off the boat before they decide to shoot you and dump you off in the ocean. Oh, and don't follow me right away." Cedric nodded. And she was gone. The newest FBI agent, meanwhile, sighed deeply at the danger of his next move. * * * "Mike … go start the motor. Elizabeth, you have some explaining to do." The party was almost dead now as Victor took Regina off to the side. The gunman, Mike, was nearby and the indications of his fight with Cedric were easily noticeable on his face. He stared at Regina, suspecting her motivation of the actions that transpired downstairs only hours before. "Go, Mike. I want to get this over with," Victor directed again, and his handyman followed his instructions. Another handyman stuck around, lingering off to the side, waiting for further instruction. "Elizabeth .. if that even is your name … I want you to give me a reason why I shouldn't throw you off the boat too," Victor said menacingly, cornering the woman on the rail of the boat. "This man was a cop! How are you involved with him?" "I didn't know, Victor," she said shortly. "I worked with him in Berkeley, and he returned to Los Angeles only recently." "That is not an excuse!" he spat at her, then smacked her across the face with a backhand. "Why should I believe you're not in on this too?" A scream of pain resulted from the backhand, but she turned back to him, her face expressionless. She was in his power. "I've helped you for months, Victor," Elizabeth explained. "I've covered for all your real-estate debts and managed all your illegal money for you … I could have turned you in by now!" "LIAR! You have not proven anything to me!" he yelled. The yacht was now moving out of the harbor, and Victor looked at his other handy-man who was waiting for further instruction. "Go get that fucking cop from the basement. We'll shoot him and throw him off." But Cedric was already on his way. He burst suddenly out from the stairway onto the deck and headed towards the railing, about to leap off into freedom as Regina had explained. "Shoot him!" Victor screamed at his handyman. Unfortunately, things hadn't quite gone as planned for Regina. She needed him now. "Cedric, don't!" she screamed at him. He stopped in his tracks and reared around as one of Victor's gunman came towards him, his gun out. Bullets were fired towards Cedric, but he was quick and able to leap out of the way. As he rolled he reached into his inner jacket pocket, where he had his gun stored from when Regina returned it to him He pulled up out of his role and was positioned on one knee with his other leg out – the agent crouch. Best of all, he had his gun ready, and shot two bullets into the handyman's head, killing him on the spot. Victor had turned towards him by now and had his own gun out, ready to do some damage. Regina reacted quickly and kicked it out of his hands, sending the gun skipping across the deck. Cedric approached Victor, gun at ready. "Put your hands up," he demanded. "You're under arrest." Victor smirked. "For what? Trafficking? Please, spare me," he mocked. "You forgot attempted murder of a federal agent," Cedric said back. And Mike was back, gun out, ready for a showdown. Cedric shot his ass too. … Some initiation.
