IG 73 - Open Invitation - Where are they now? RP's here

24 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
'Where Are They Now?' Episode III - Greg Davis Greg Davis sits inside his den with scotch in hand, the true drink of legends, and wearing a black and gold smoking jacket. No matter where you are, what you’re wearing, or what you look like, a double-malt can take away any worry in the world that may raise a hair. Greg knows this by the test of time and repeating the process for full analysis. “Dad,” the intercom cracks to life with the voice of Claire Davis, Greg’s daughter. “There’s somebody here to see you from the PTC Infinite Gauntlet Committee.” Greg parts his lips, allowing the amber contents of the glass slide down his throat, and lets out a deep breath as it finishes, smiling with satisfaction. His eyes shift to the black plastic box that speaks to him. He sets the glass down. “PTC Infinite Gauntlet Committee? This must be a prank,” Greg mutters, pulling himself out of his chair and stands over his desk. He presses down on the button. “What’s the name, Claire?” A few seconds pass as the curiosity grows inside Greg’s nearly empty head, due to years in the business of bashing brains and being bashed with heavy objects, on a nightly basis. His once golden hair is fading into a silvery mane, as is his thick beard. Still resembling the GWA North American Champion, Greg’s upper body is well built and his skin is still tan and gold. “A mister Mitch Price,” the box relays. “Wants to do an interview… a ‘Where Are They Now’ piece, he says.” Greg rolls his eyes, laughing a bit to himself, looking around his den and noticing the finest of fine pieces of art and trophies collected through over twelve years in the sports entertainment industry. His finger hits the button with a click. “Send him up, Claire,” Greg says to his daughter. “Remember, I’ve got to finish before seven. Denise’s parents are going to be coming over for dinner. If it looks like he’s staying any longer, I’ll call you in and we’ll get rid of him. Deal?” “Sure thing, Dad,” Claire’s tone was pleasant, giggling a little even at the funny practices of her father. After years of reporters, news pieces, photographers, and being the center of attention – he still treats reporters like distant cousins. Welcome, unless he finds something, anything, to do to get rid of them. Greg sits back into his throne, made of black felt and gold framing. The knobs on the top of the backrest are a ‘G’ and a ‘D’ intertwined together, the emblem of The Golden One during his wrestling days. He runs his fingers through his beard, stroking it in silence. It’s been years since anyone from the business has come my way, Greg thinks. The sound of Claire’s heels echo through the halls of Greg Davis’ south Miami mansion under the midday’s sun. Greg’s ear catches the clicks and clacks of the wooden floor, turning his head to the doorway to have a good look at his visitor. The stunning blond daughter of ‘the Ego’ steps into view, wearing a lovely sun dress that highlights her green eyes nicely. She gestures to her father, “Hi daddy. This is Mitchell Price.” Greg stands up, towering over his guest, and reaches forward with an outstretched hand. He takes Mitch’s weak hand in his meaty mitt and clutches, wrenching it up then down, like a real man shakes hands. Price winces a little but disguises it as a smile. Greg looks at his daughter, folding her arms over her chest. “Play nice,” she spins on her heel and makes her way out of earshot. Greg looks back at Mitch, who is laying out his laptop, digital tape recorder, and HD video camera. Greg takes another satisfying swig of scotch and picks up the digital tape recorder. He looks it over, Price watching him closely to make sure he doesn’t damage it. Back in the day when Greg was putting GWA on the map, he was being interviewed in locker rooms with shitty equipment and barely educated loudmouths. Now days, things were run by the nerds, the geeks, and the businessmen. The honor and glory inside the game had died long ago, like Greg’s career. “Care for a drink, Mitch?” Greg says, cutting out the bullshit. A big grin shines through Greg’s furry beard. “Got a full bar. You name it.” Price taps around his laptop and then stares blankly at the screen. He waits a few seconds then looks to GD’s smiling face, “Do you have any Ginger Ale?” “Sure do, bud,” Greg says, spring off his desk and heading toward his minibar. He picks up a glass, much like his, and pulls out a Ginger Ale. He cracks the tab and pours it over some ice. “Gin? Vodka? What’s your poison?” “No thanks, Mister Davis,” he takes the ginger Ale, plain. “I’m not a big… drinker, I guess.” First, the nerd comes in with his fancy gadgets. Then, he comes in sober. If he wants a fight, he’s heading in a good direction if he keeps disappointing the former fWo Cruiserweight Champion. Greg watches, confused, as the young man sits back in front of his reporting headquarters and throws down the rest of the glass of scotch. He refills it. “So, can I ask ya somethin’, Mitch?” Greg begins. “I’m wonderin’ exactly what the… Pee Tee See Infinite Gauntlet Committee wants with a retired, washed-up wrestler from ten years ago? I mean, I haven’t exactly had a very exciting retirement.” Mitch chuckles, “No. No, sir. You haven’t. We’re just shooting some interviews on former champions that can bring out some new fans, maybe even draw out some older fans, for our latest Infinite Gauntlet competition. We’re really hoping to pull a big crowd this time and, hopefully, you’re name will help us accomplish that.” Greg shrugs, slumping down into his GD throne. His eyes scan over Price’s reporting system. He types something up and then looks into the blue eyes of Greg Davis. He smiles, politely. “Are you ready, sir?” Mitch asks, folding his hands together. Greg nods, “Call me Greg. I’m ready when you are.” Mitch clicks his tape recorder on, turning on a little red light that catches Greg’s eye. He begins scanning over questions he wrote up on the flight to Miami. He reaches over and extends three small legs of a mini tripod that holds his camera. He clicks another button and another red light comes to life on the camera. Greg thinks of how easy these kids have it compared to the legends of their craft, the true reporters who made their job so easy. “PTC fans across the world,” Mitch’s voice is whiny and weak; “Welcome to the third installment of ‘Where Are They Now?’, a series brought to you by PTC.com documenting some of wrestling’s legends and finding out what they have to say on topics ranging from today’s superstars to yesterday’s memories and everything in between.” “After tracking down the infamous Jacob McKail and finding the mythical Sebastian Muraco, we’ve landed on the sand of South Beach to hear what one of the world’s greatest Cruiserweights has to say. The one, the only, the Golden One…” Price pauses for dramatic effect. “Greg Davis.” Greg smiles at the camera and shoots a gun finger at it. He reclines, rubbing his chin with one hand that is propped up by the armrest. Mitch types something into the laptop. “Greg,” Mitch looks off the screen briefly. “You’re a legend. Six Cruiserweight titles, GWA North American Champion, a Tag Team champion, owner of a wrestling talent agency, manager and trainer of wrestlers, and overall success. Aside from all your accolades, what does wrestling mean to you?” Greg smiles to the delight of hearing a question he can honestly answer after years of hearing bullshit after bullshit; “Wrestling is not just a business or a sport, or entertainment, for that matter. It’s a release. For you, for me, for all the fans watching this; to be able to let loose every Sunday, get rough, get tough, and get into a state of mind that can only be contained by arenas suitable for thousands of screaming and raging fans. What does it mean to me? It means I have a place to be myself and show the world a good time. That means the world to me.” Mitch’s eyes lift off his laptop again, this time holding a different look behind them. His ears are perked up, his eyes are open, and his head is up. He’s listening. “What is the time you remember most about that life? You’ve obviously moved on from that, so…” “Well, Mitch, you’d like to think so, wouldn’t you?” Greg’s eyes squint and he rubs through his beard a little bit deeper than casually, scratching perhaps. “I’ve spent over a decade in the ring. Now, one decade of wrestling is like dog years – one year equals seven. The amount of pain, stress, and punishment my 365 days sees is what you see in seven years. In my head, in my heart, and in my body I feel like I was at the top of the game for thirty years.” Greg takes another swig of his double scotch; “My all time best memory was when I won the fans Wrestling organization Cruiserweight Title, in Two Thousand One. It was October 31st, at Halloween Hell, in a Ladder Match. I wasn’t exactly a fan favorite at this point of my career; I had kidnapped Crusader’s girlfriend Clarisse to give myself the psychological edge. It was bold, shallow, but hell, it worked and I walked out the champ. Some call that my defining moment but, I am just glad to have had the opportunity.” Mitch nods, fingers pressing against each other, and he wonders if he should stick to the script or go with the moment. “We last saw you in action in Two thousand Three with fWo. How has wrestling changed since you’ve been involved?” “They certainly lost an entertainment value,” Greg snickers, thumbing to himself at being the ultimate entertainer. “But it’s all become a pony show when it comes to anything else. I’m tired of watching the constant monologues and has-been returns.” “When it all comes down to it,” Greg retorts, smiling a bit wider as he kicks his foot over his knee. “All the wins, the championships, and the money only add up to one, universal point… respect. You’ve come here today, with your new-age technology and ‘remember when…’ questions to ask me, a living legend of the ring, what it was like when wrestling was at its best. It’s obvious by the way you’ve come in here that you’re after my glory and not my story.” Mitch swats that idea out of the air, “No, sir, I’m not trying to-“ “So let me tell ya somethin’, Mitch, and all the fans who get a good look at this,” Greg scoots forward in his seat and grabs the camera, pointing it to many of his golden accolades displayed on the shelving around the room. “Where am I now?” Greg asks, displaying his GWA North American Title belt; “I’m living like a king in Miami Beach. Was it easy? God no. I spent three years in GWA and most of that time was fighting my boss for a chance to shine. He gave me my shot and it hasn’t seemed to be a bad choice.” Greg strolls over to his Tornado Wrestling display, showing a picture of a young Greg, full of life and joy, flying through the air. The Tw Cruiserweight Title rests above the picture. “Was it easy?” Greg snaps, “Hell no, it wasn’t easy. We didn’t treat our contracts like blind dates and cancel them when we felt pressured or overwhelmed. We fought through it. In the end, you had a job to do and that job was to put fans in the stands. Was it worth it?” Greg moves again, this time stopping in front of his prized possession, the fWo Cruiserweight Title. “When I look at this belt, I think of all the blood, sweat, and tears that I have lost in the past and how thankful I am for my job. For not being afraid of what people might say, not being scared to take a few bumps or bruises, and to have the guts to do what I love.” “Would you do it again?” Mitch interjects, now standing, and holding a small notepad in one hand and a pen in the other. Greg turns the camera, now on Mitch, and grins at the young nerdball; “In a heartbeat.”
