The Chronicles of Muppet I - Breaking the Vicious Cycle

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-16 "Oh... screw it." For the umpteeth time, the Muppet Man ripped the paper off his desk, balled it up in his fist, and lobbed it carelessly towards the waste paper bin in the corner. It missed, but he didn't care. He had been trying to write the introduction to a promo for a good hour, and he hadn't got anywhere. He threw open the draw, taking another piece of paper and laying it down on the desk a little more roughly than he had intended, so it was half crushed before he even started writing. He didn't notice. Twirling his pen absent mindedly in his hand, his mind began to wander from the task at hand, and he leaned back in his chair, slipping away from reality. He had a nagging tendency to do this from time to time. Like many other things, he deeply resented this, but really... what was he going to do about it? He had more pressing matters at hand. GTT4 loomed, a towering mountain just over the horizon. From where he was, though, he could see only the glittering peak, the utmost glory, the highest honour a wrestler could receive. It was so enticing, it drew him on, it beckoned him. The prospect of it was tantalising, intoxicating. Suddenly, nothing else mattered apart from climbing that mountain. He didn't even care how he would do it... that didn't matter. He had climbed mountains in the past. All that mattered was reaching the top. What he couldn't see, though, was the road to the top. As he closed in, as the days drifted past, the mountain drew closer, and he could see more and more of it. Suddenly, it was no longer intoxicating... it was frightening. The GTT tournament had a history like no other. From it's beginnings, shrouded in cheating and lies, to the almighty Angelo Deville proving once and for all that he was the most dominant force in wrestling, preceedings had been epic to say the least. Whether it was the second Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, or just GTT4, the Muppet Man didn't know or care. He had defeated Quietus, acquired the United States Championship, finally vanquished the demons that had haunted him for the whole of his career. He was free of the darkness. All that was left was a distant memory of times long past. But, it was a memory nonetheless. A memory that would continue to haunt him. He wasn't free... not yet. Your mind often lets you see what you want to see, and not what is actually there. In the forefront of the Muppet Man's mind, Justin Vivazi Locke was simply an obstacle. He was the first hurdle on the way to the top of the mountain, a mere rock that he could step over without as much as a second thought. In the deeper recesses of that mind, though, lurked the truth. The truth, constantly trying to pull itself out of this hole, but over the months, years, Muppet's brain had built strong, almost impenetrable defences. So strong, so overpowering was his denial. This was his fault, this was his failing. As long as he continued to deny, he would never get any further. He would never get past that rock, that obstacle, at the foot of the mountain. But... there were other forces, too. Regret and resentment were the only weapons that the truth had left to battle the ever-growing denial. For, as the Muppet Man had 'stepped into the light,' and defeated his enemy, the denial had grown, the sense of invulnerability, the ego had intensified. As they had grown, his deep regret, his deep resentment had grown with them. Now they were his only hope. Slam. In a moment, with a crushing impact, reality rushed back in, the dam holding it burst. In actuality, his chair had simply tipped backwards, but the effect was the same. Getting to his feet, Muppet cursed under his breath, lifting the chair back up. Before sitting back down, though, he began to stare out of the window. He did this a lot. Why? Well... why did anyone else do it? There wasn't really any reason, it was just something you did. A lot of things the Muppet Man did had no particular reasoning behind them. He did them because they felt right, and now he was desperately trying to change this way of thinking. A silent battle going on behind his emotionless eyes, it's medium something as simple as staring, unseeing, out of a window. Breaking away, he sat down once more, picking up his pen rather reluctantly. This was one of the things he deeply regretted... never being concentrated enough, or having the necessary will to get the job done. He resented not being able to finish what he started. In fact, he was able to finish it... but he didn't. This was what he really regretted, what he really resented. 'I'll do it later' was one of his mind's favourite lies. It was as if there was a snake inside of him, hissing venomous commands, too tempting to resist. Over time, it's venom got stronger, until he almost depended on it to exist. He had never had a strong will, whatever he displayed on the outside. He hated this. He hated falling into temporary lapses. He hated not being able to see anything through to the bitter end. He didn't just resent it... he hated it. Resentment wasn't a very strong word, but in his opinion, it had held him back all this time. Of course, that wasn't true. But, one thing he had learned above all else was that he wasn't always right. He knew when he made a mistake, though, obviously, he often only knew this after he had made it. Still, learning from your mistakes is a quality everyone must have to be successful. Muppet sure as hell wasn't going to slip up on such a fundamental rule. He was willing to accept new ways, he was willing to try new things, to try anything to achieve his goal. This was what seperated him from everyone else. It went like this. From nowhere, the Muppet Man would rise up, and claim a strong foothold. From there, his resolve would falter, his denial would grow. He would continue, days passing into weeks, all the time getting weaker and weaker, all the time his ego growing, even taking control. Finally, after a crushing defeat of epic proportions, he would be cast down once again. Slowly, he would find inspiration, and find his feet. Alone, he would struggle through it, and regain his foothold, only to be cast down once more. This was the vicious cycle that he had been trapped in for as long as he could remember. This was his vicious cycle... and now he had to break it. The only problem was, he didn't know that he had to break it. All he knew was that he had to beat Justin Vivazi Locke, and he had to advance further in the tournament, or face disgrace. The cycle would go on. This time, though, there was a fault in the cycle. A loophole that, whether he knew it or not, the Muppet Man was bringing upon himself. See, every time his ego was crushed, the regret and resentment that went with it were not. They continued to build, until now... now, they were strong. Coupled with his doubt, the very things he hated and despised about himself were slowly helping him to victory. He had to fight what he loved, and embrace what he hated. "Resentment is like taking poison and waiting for the other person to die." -Malachy McCourt This time, though, the poison was the cure. His whole life, he had repressed his resentment. Now, he had to embrace the motto of his old enemy, Quietus. 'Everything you regret.' There was another fault, another loophole, too. Before, he had always had to fight through it alone. Alone. The key factor here was that he was no longer completely alone. No, he had his former tag team partner, and current manager of sorts, King Muppet. Often, all you need to find the answer to a nagging question is another person to ask... "Hey... hey! Wake up, dammit!" Right on cue. The Muppet Man shot up, almost falling off his chair again. He looked around to see King Muppet leaning in the doorway, his arms folded, the usually goofy smirk clearly visible behind his extravagant mask. He really was a cocky bastard, and Muppet never understood this. 'What did he have to be cocky about?' Muppet thought. He was just like the next guy. "Nodding off on the job, are we?" he said, taking a quick, harmless prod at his partner/client. Muppet returned the comment with a scathing smile. "I don't get why I have to do this anyway." "You don't," King pointed out, shrugging. It was true. There was no requirement in the GTT contract that said 'each wrestler must promote the event.' Now that he thought about it, Muppet didn't even know why he was doing it. He couldn't think about it for long, though. "Hey, what is this tournament thingy you're in anyway?" "You're my manager, and you don't know that?" Muppet asked, amused. King just shrugged. "It's PTC's Golden Turnbuckle Tournament... at least, I think that's what it's called. Might just be GTT. Either way, it's pretty prestigious. This is a big opportunity." "The rings have golden turnbuckles? That is SO cool!" "No, that's just the..." "Can you imagine how much that costs? Still, it's awesome!" "But, it's only the..." "That would hurt! Imagine getting whipped into a golden turnbuckle..." "Would you SHUT UP for a minute?" King fell silent, mouthing some half-formed curse words. Muppet sank back into his chair, leaning his head backwards to look at his partner/manager upside down. 'Yeah,' Muppet thought, 'he is just like the next guy.' "So... think I stand a chance?" he asked. A harmless question, really. "Well... who've you drawn?" "Someone called Justin Virrazy Locks, or something..." "If I told you you're going to have a tough match on your hands, I'd be... lying. I don't have a clue who he is," King answered in his typical fashion. "You beat Quietus, though, didn't you? I mean, this guy can't be too much of a challenge, can he?" Muppet was silent. This was a question he had been debating in his mind since the draw was announced. He knew fully well his opponent's name... and didn't know why he'd pretended he didn't. He forced the slowly building grimace back down again. "I have to be ready for anything," Muppet said shortly. He had that slight touch of doubt in his voice, a touch that King Muppet picked up immediately. "You're ready," came the reply. Muppet stared unmoving at his colleague, who nodded to the left. Muppet turned his head to look. There it was... the symbol of everything that he had accomplished, and everything that he had put behind him. The WWA United States Championship. That was the symbol of everything his resentment had helped him gain, and he wasn't going to forget it anytime soon. As he stared at the gold, glinting in the thin rays of dying sunlight creeping through the window moving steadily into the night, he suddenly felt different. He felt almost... enlightened. "You're ready," King repeated, seeing the look on his partner's face. He couldn't stop a slight smile appearing on his face. When Muppet got like that... nothing was going to stop him. "We're talking mountains here, Will," came Muppet's strangely pessimistic reply. "Nearly two hundred other wrestlers, thinking exactly the same thing." "Let them think," King said, stopping rather abruptly. If there was one thing King Muppet had in abundance, it was belief. Together, he and the Muppet Man could overcome any obstacle. They had been through too much together not to. There was a slight silence, with both of them waiting for something. Finally, Muppet sat up, and slowly pulled himself to his feet. His muscles were aching... he hadn't realised how tired he was. It didn't matter. Slowly, he walked over to his prize. Lifting the US Title from it's custom-made frame, he stared deeply into the gold. His reflection stared back at him, gazing deep inside of him. Suddenly, all of the denial left. The task was mammoth, unthinkably great. For the first time, he realised exactly how hard this was going to be, yet he was no longer frightened. Denial only brought a hidden fear. Regret and resentment only brought self doubt. Finally and ultimately, he realised that he had to get rid of it all. Only then could he move on, and break the cycle. "Done," he said out loud, a smile blossoming on his face. King didn't question this, just retreated back to the door, but not turning around. "Hey Andy," he said, trailing off slightly as Muppet looked at him. Muppet smirked, nodding for him to continue. "Big party in a few days, loads of people... interested?" "Hell yeah," Muppet replied. "Great. You need to loosen up a bit. Everything's been so serious recently. You know exactly what you need... a girlfriend." He grinned, not knowing whether he was subtly mocking his partner or not. It didn't really matter. Muppet pondered for a second. This was a great opportunity to put all his denial and self-doubt behind him. No more resentment for the Muppet Man. It was time to take action. "I'm off," King said, "Need to get some things." "I'll come with you," Muppet decided out loud. "No point in continuing with this," he gestured towards the paper, "Anyway." "Right... come on, then." "Yeah, just give me a minute..." Without another word, King turned and walked out of the room, leaving Muppet on his own. Maybe King was just like any other guy, maybe he was just average, but he was a friend... and Muppet couldn't ask for anything more. He started to follow, but doubled back, turning and walking over to the table. Without a second thought, he picked up the pen and began to write, letting his thoughts flow down onto the paper. The cycle is broken, my endless trail of hopelessness is finally over. For Justin Vivazi Locke, the dream ends here. For me... it is only just beginning. No more denial. No more regret. No more resentment. The US Title was the first step, and now it's time for the second act in the Chronicles of Muppet. Now, if you'd kindly excuse me... I've got a mountain to climb.
