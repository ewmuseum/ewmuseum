[IG46 - J_A] A First Hand Account of an Ill-Fated Glimpse into the Past

19 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
To whomever said time flies when you get older... please go fuck yourself in the ass with a rusty crowbar. Four years have passed since I got back into this business. The official reason why I had to leave in the first place was a bad back and post-concussion syndrome. The real reason, at least the ones gleaned from dirtsheets and inferred from things read on the Internet, happened to be that I lost my smile. Not to be a liar, but there’s a little truth in both of those. In either case, a little timeout was in order. When I came back, I was refreshed, I was recharged, I had a clean slate in front of me. It was going to be a great year: 2004, the year of the Anglo Luchador. So much promise, and yet, here I am four years later, most of that promise unfulfilled. And it’s felt like an eternity. That’s why I cringe whenever I think back to those days, about how much I could have done with the start I had, whenever I hear people refer to 2004 like it was some glory day for them, why I said the things I said to Chris Storm in the last round of the Infinite Gauntlet. The past... it bugs me. So naturally, I turn my television on, and bam, staring me right in the face is the Classic Wrestling Channel on my Comcast Digital Cable. Yeah, I got the sports package, mainly because of this channel. It’s usually great. I can catch all the great action that I didn’t get to see because I didn’t get U-62 up North, or because I was on the road and creating my own story in the ring and couldn’t watch. Figures, with the state of mind that I’m in right now, it has to be The Year In Review: 2004, and with my luck, it’s this interview that I had with ESEN. If you haven’t seen it, it’s really cheesy. I’m all giddy and shit, and hell, to add to the hilarity factor of it all, I’m wearing a midnight green #81 Eagles jersey. Right there, that’s like a fifty out of ten on the Unintentional Hilarity Scale. But yeah, this interviewer, some stodgy guy they got rid of right after Miles bought the place out. He’s asking me about how I’m going to adapt to Empire and the FW circuit of feds. “I think it’ll be a rough transition at first,” I replied, all hopeful and crap. “But I think I can do it and really flourish. You know, I didn’t really show the world my Aye-game before I left.” Oh really, idiot? You really showed it to them after. And it was there where I had enough. Two-thousand-four me was just getting annoying. I just couldn’t take it anymore. Knowing everything that would happen: the dizzying highs of the Intercontinental Championship in my third match in EPW, returning to A1E and winning the Best of A1E Tournament AND the Pier Six Brawl AND the World Championship, going to CS Land and winning the Greensboro Title. The depressing lows: not being able to get further than a Number One Contender’s Match in EPDub, losing the A1E title after a mere month and then losing my mind after, dropping the Greensboro Title without even making the final Gold Rush ring, not being able to get anything started after being set back. I know a lot of that is my fault, but it doesn’t help with self-confidence issues, y’know? I guess that’s why I’m always looking for the rasa tabula. I don’t want my past to catch up with me. It worked in 2004; I didn’t unnecessarily piss people off off-camera and didn’t get all bitter when things weren’t going my way. Those situations didn’t come up at all though. It’s easy to avoid getting stung by a scorpion if you don’t venture into the desert. Yet here I am, four years later, staring another restart in the face; I’m adjusting to life in the A1E tag ranks with a new partner, EPW itself is going through a sort of restart, and well, here I am, fighting for the PTC Infinite Gauntlet. I know the mistakes I made, but sometimes the overwhelming inertia of the past makes you think things that may not be true. So now you see my predicament, why I’m sitting here in my den, on my leather couch, head down and left hand clutching a Yuengling Traditional Lager. My lovely fiancée, Lollipop, heads in from the kitchen, her probing mind warming up to see why her man looks so out of it. “Like, why so glum ‘n stuff, chum?” She almost predictably asks. “Ah, stuff.” That’s my stock answer for everything. I use it to try and get her to buzz off, but it never works. “Like, bullshit ‘n stuff.” I look up at her with puppy dog eyes. She’s still glowering at me like I was a germ warfare-protesting hippie and she were C. Montgomery Burns. “You really know how to cut me.” I lean my head back and take a nice long swig of swill before I continue my reply. “It’s... just about my career. Specifically those promos I cut for that Infinite Gauntlet thing.” “Like, really? You won that thing ‘n stuff. You should be happy about that.” “I know, and I am, but... there’s just something bugging me about the big picture.” Lollipop looks at the television. “Your TV is, like, fine.” “Har har.” She really knows how to make me laugh, even if I don’t show it. “But seriously, I feel like the same picture is being painted. I mean, look at it. I’ve got a clean slate everywhere I am. People are talking about me again.” “And like, that’s a bad thing?” “Well... yes and no. No, because hey, I’m back in the saddle. Yes, because... well, you know how the wheels came off and everything.” She stares daggers into me. Probably because I deserve it for being an emo douchebag. “You’re, like, being an emo douchebag.” Do I know her, or do I know her? “Seriously babe, like, if you can’t have a little confidence in yourself, then it’s your own fault that you’re going to lose and continue your pattern... ‘n stuff.” “But it’s so similar. It’s like history is repeating all over again.” “Babe, like, lemme let you in on something my father told me long ago. History only repeats itself if you refuse to learn from your previous mistakes. Like, if you keep going in with the same attitude, or if you think something’s gonna happen again so much, it probably will.” She sits down next to me and puts her arm round my shoulder. The Valley Girl exterior makes people think a lot of things about her that aren’t true, but if there’s anyone that embodies the old chestnut “Behind every great man is a greater woman,” it’s her. “You’re, like, only going to go as far as you let yourself go. You’re, like, really talented, and I’m not saying that just because we’re getting married ‘n stuff.” “Yeah... you’re right. Now, could you get me another beer?” I’m only playing with her. That still doesn’t stop her from smacking me across the chest. I’ve taken bullet-force chops from Joey Melton, and let me tell you, they pale in comparison to one of Lollipop’s smacks. So, she gets up from the couch and heads off into the study, a clear sign that I have to go refresh my bottle myself. Being out on the road seventy-five percent of the year and having no kids will leave your refrigerator door as vacant as Paris Hilton’s mass of gray tissue between her ears (I refuse to call it a brain). Looking into the stainless steel exterior, I’m halted from opening it to get another one of Pottsville’s best by looking into my own eyes on the metallic mirror. The reflection looks a little distorted, but it looks eerily similar to the face I saw on the TV just a few minutes ago. I step back, and that sense of dread, of inertia... of déjà vu. Then, it hits me. I didn’t have this crisis of faith before I started to get involved with Chris Storm and all those challengers to his title. What’s more, I didn’t have these thoughts in my mind when I was battling all those guys and gals in the ring, the Cunninghams and the Ramones and the von Dooms. I only started to dwell on this when the officials told me the theme of the build-up to the match was going to be all about 2004, that it was 2004. I look at my reflection in the fridge again, and I realize it’s distorted, much like my whole view on this situation is. I really don’t have a clean slate anywhere but in PTC, and even there, my name precedes me. I mean, I’ve done these events before. And Lolli’s right, I know where I went wrong in the past and I’m armed with the knowledge of how to do right instead. I realize a lot has changed. So now, I break my gaze from the reflection of my own face and open the refrigerator. I grab another beer, pop the top and plop back on the couch to check out what movies are On Demand. Just by the sheer fact that I don’t need to buy a DVD and have a player to watch whatever movie I want is a clear reminder that it ain’t 2004 anymore. And you know what? I couldn’t be happier.
