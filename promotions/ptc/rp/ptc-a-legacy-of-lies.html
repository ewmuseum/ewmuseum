A Legacy of Lies (Or, "One Chuck Too Many.") There once was a girl from Nantucket. But we're not here to talk about her; we're here about another bitch entirely. Her name was Charlotte Ramone – at least, so the wrestling industry thought – and that was part of the problem. Last summer, the so-called Punk Rock Pixie was without a doubt the hottest prospect in Sin City Championship Wrestling. Sure, she upset Chris Storm for the Rock Superstar's Infinite Gauntlet, but Charlie completed her rise at All-In, winning both the federation's LiveWire Title and the inaugural Heaven & Hell match on the same night. The match earned her an at-will bout for the SCCW Championship and the sky seemed the limit. She was witty, she was unrelenting against the federation's enemies, she was adorable, she was marketable, and she was by-God talented. Her rivalry with the Universal Champion, Amy Campbell, grew by leaps and bounds. The trouble is, she was also made of smoke and mirrors – the greatest lie the Devil Herself ever told. One fateful night in Ottawa, with one Spider's Kiss to retire Jonathan Rhine, Alexandra Pierce taught an entire federation a horrible lesson about trust and the depths to which she would sink. Just six weeks ago, however, the Lilac Lesbian returned. She claimed she'd been impersonated – lied to and manipulated by Pierce and her cronies just like so many before her. The returned Ramone took the fight to the Sin City's villains with renewed vigor, and Pierce, heartless Director of the Dead Man's Hand, appeared obsessed with reclaiming the Plexiglas facemask she once hid behind. But many (the almighty Alex Pierce included) have forgotten the most important lesson of life in Sin City. No one there is exactly as they appear. * * * * * Yesterday. "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned." It was a pilgrimage she made once a year, come rain or shine or gloom of night. No matter her responsibilities, no matter where she was in the world, every May, Alexandra Pierce boarded a plane, train, or automobile and wended her way to a lonely church in Tampa, Florida. "It has been... it's been too long since I was last here for confession. So much has happened. So much has gone wrong, and so much right. I think you'd be proud. I hope so." Usually, she made this trip alone, the solace straightening out her psyche. In all her years leading the cell professional wrestling fans call "The Dead Man's Hand," only once did their Director invite any of them along, and Lauren Fox had been afraid to leave the van. "I have done horrible, horrible things. I've hurt people. Destroyed lives. Ruined families." She closed her eyes, lips twisting. "You know, the usual. As ever, I am not regretful... not of that. I know I am not a good person, Father. There is no place in heaven for angels with tarnished halos, you always said. But for the first time, I feel doubt. Sorrow. Shame." If they knew that Amy Campbell fidgeted uncomfortably in the front seat of a rented sedan, her respect for the Spider in the Web losing the war to her own curiosity... well, it might have been the final nail in the coffin of her leadership of them. "There is a woman." Pierce closed her stony gray eyes. "I know you do not approve of certain things about me, but there is a woman I care for very much. I believe I mentioned her the last time I was here. I am... my heart is muddled by emotion. I cannot..." She sighed out, tapping her forefinger to her temple. "I cannot think straight when I am around her and I know... a part of me will always know that I am never destined to have her. I feel as though I am trapped in a Shakespeare play, forever destined to be the cackling villain. She can't love me, can she, Father? Not after all I have done." * * * * * Three Days Ago. Dr. Michael Greene's pocket vibrated against his thigh, skewing his putt to the left. The little turquoise golf ball rattled against the lip of the tunnel beneath the windmill, backspin sending it rocketing back towards him. Only a lightning-fast stab from his companion's stiletto-heeled boot prevented the ball from skittering past them. The man pulled the offending phone out, scowling at the caller ID as he squeezed the "ignore" button savagely. "Sorry," he said with a meager grin, jamming the phone back into his pants. "It's just—" "Just your Mystery Celebrity Client." Emily Beckett pronounced the capitalized words carefully, a smirk latching onto her pink lips as she sauntered past. "That's, what, the third call from her in the last ninety minutes?" Greene blew out a sigh. "Twice in the last twenty," he confirmed. He opened his mouth to speak again, but his jaw shut with a snap. He might tell you the pause was from doctor/patient privacy, and not the sight of the brunette's skirt swaying as she cocked her hip to the side, raising her putter to judge the straightness of her angle. He'd probably be lying. "Should you call her?" Emily wiggled to get into the right position for her shot. "I won't be mad, trust me." The head of her golf club smacked against the ball, and it whipped under the plastic windmill, rebounding off the far side of the hole and back to snake along the top of the hole. It wouldn't fall in, though, no matter how much body english she put into it. "It's after hours," he said. "I'm on my own time." "Really, Mike, I—" Her purse jangled this time, and she dug out an iPhone distractedly. "I guess I'm going to talk to my sister anyway." She shoved the man away playfully. "Call her. You can't pay off your miniature golf debt if you don't have your fat cat client." Dr. Greene swallowed his sigh, stepping away for some privacy. He looked back at the same time she did. "And no cheating – I'll remember how far behind you are!" she called. She put the phone to her ear as she sought out some solace. "Hang on a sec, Kathleen..." When Dr. Greene dialed, however, he went straight to voicemail. "Amy, it's Dr. Greene. Sorry I missed you..." But we're not here to talk about him, either. * * * * * "Hang on a sec, Kathleen..." "Did you just call me...? God, nevermind." The voice on the other end sighed. "You've got mail." "I... what?" Emily stepped into the ladies' room, all of the happy and most of the fun leaking out of her voice. "I have mail. At your house? Have you opened it?" "Oh, totally. I ran with scissors, cleaned a gun with the safety off and then took my poison-tipped letter-opener to the package you got. At my home." The dry, cold sigh that escaped her painted lips was relieved but ill-fitting, and it should have been – because her name wasn't Emily Beckett. "Good," Alexandra Pierce said. "Does it have a return label?" "Yeah, it's from you. In New Mexico. And by 'you', I mean the creepy ginger who walked in on us that one time." Amy Campbell groaned. "Where the hell are you?" "That..." Alexandra shoved open the door a crack, peering into the golf course, where Campbell's own psychiatrist waited for her. "That isn't important. I will be by shortly to pick it up." Pierce's use of contractions with Campbell was something wrestling fans might have been taken aback by. As was the smile that creased her lips as she turned towards back into the room. "If it's from Cambrian, it's probably not going to explode." "I can take it to you. And you telling me where you are isn't important means that it is important to me." Campbell sighed. "And what gave ANYONE the impression that it was okay to send mail to me?" "I didn't have a place when I moved to Oakland. I gave him your address, but it was only for emergencies." Pierce sighed, slipping into a bathroom stall. "I know Darcy will be unhappy and you probably want to get it out of the house, but you don't need to go through all that trouble. Really, you don't." "It's not about him being happy or unhappy – I can give him the same excuse you just gave me. It's about me being unhappy. I get mystery packages, don't know where you are half the time. I'm expected just to trust that because it's you and you think I'm cute." "Well, you are cute." The only answer Alex got was stone-cold silence. "Fine, fine. I... I'm playing miniature golf." "It's really not a good time to joke around, Alex." "And how often do I joke? I..." Alexandra trailed off, leaning her head back on the stall, staring dismally up at the ceiling. There were very few people the Spider hated to lie to. The SCCW Champion was one of them. She only hoped the misery sounded more like embarrassment. "I challenged Quinn to a game," she continued. "I thought it might be a fun mother-daughter excursion sometime. Only I discovered I'm... I'm not any good at miniature golf. And you know how much I hate to lose." "Yesterday, you slept with the Tiger Woods of putt-putt... and today you didn't think to invite me? I don't know if the scary, random mail or the rejection hurts more." "I hate to lose, remember? The last thing I want to do is lose to both the women in my life." She sighed again, but swallowed this one. "I'm at Golden Tee Golfland in Castro Valley. You want to grab what they're claiming are hot dogs and beat me unmercifully for a round? Bring the scary, random mail." "Better idea." Somehow, you could practically hear the woman's grin. "How about you leave and we'll meet at your condo so you can give me a few reasons not to be cranky about the mail and mini-golf betrayal?" Alex couldn't prevent her smile from blooming. It wasn't one of those dark, tiny, I-am-going-to-fuck-you-with-your-own-spinal-cord smiles that Desade was infamous for in the wrestling world, either, but a real smile, something more fitting on a love-struck teen than the so-called Spider in the Web. "Okay," Pierce said quietly. "Give me... maybe a half-hour?" "No later, 'cause I've been sitting in the car outside your place for a half hour already, and I'm sleeping over tonight. Darcy's out of town for a playoff game, I've got an appointment with the shrink ridiculously early tomorrow and I really don't wanna miss a minute of actual fun in the day." "You could have just said so. I'll see you soon." As Pierce tapped the red "End Call" button, all her warm feelings were sucked into the pit forming in her stomach as she realized the depths of her own depravity. She slipped out of the stall, staring balefully at reflection that wasn't her own. The woman in the mirror just smirked, kind of sadly. So this is what I am reduced to, she thought. Dressed as another woman, painted up like a whore, in heels and a too-short skirt, all to cozy up to a man whose only crime is wanting to help the woman I love work through her issues. Issues like her affair with me. Alex blew out a sigh, shaking herself out to force away the doldrums. "It's for her own good," Desade told Emily's reflection, dipping her little finger into a glass jar of lip conditioner. Spreading it daintily onto her lips, Pierce summoned up Emily's smile -- wide, curling, and sweet. She slapped her own cheeks lightly, just enough to draw a touch of color, and darted from the restroom as fast as her boots would allow. It's for her own good, she reminded herself, unleashing the most girlish, gleeful scream she could. "Oh my God! Ohmigod, ohmigod, ohmiGAWD!" Emily nearly squealed as she tottered up to Dr. Greene. "I... Mikey, you... you will not BELIEVE who that was!" "Your sister, I thought." She took both of his hands in hers, bounding from foot to foot like a giddy teenager. There was nothing of the cold, hateful Desade in that grin. "Yes, but it wasn't just her! I..." "Slow down," Greene cautioned, but he couldn't help but smile along. "You're going to hyperventilate." "You don't understand. My agent has been trying to reach me since yesterday. I have to be in L.A., like, tomorrow. I have an audition! For a speaking role! In a movie!" She could barely contain herself. "Oh, God. I have to go! I need to pack! I have to... have to memorize my lines!" She'd half-turned away, but when she turned back, it was like she realized where they were. Her lower lip jutted out in a tiny pout. "You're not, like, mad, are you? I know you had to make time for my silly Putt-Putt fascination." He squeezed her hands, smiling down at her. "I'd be mad if you didn't go. You can kick my ass in mini-golf anytime. Get out of here." "Aww, you're such a sweetie." Emily rocked up to her tiptoes, brushing her lips across Greene's cheekbone. "Your wife doesn't deserve you," she breathed in his ear. When she retreated, Michael's cheek burned and he looked away, crediting the tingle to embarrassment. Even as she turned, fingers lingering on his, Alexandra was putting the Emily part of her to rest. She slipped her sunglasses on over eyes she knew were going hard. One hand lifted, fingers curling in a wave that was more self-conscious than it should have been. "I'll see you later," she said around a smile she didn't feel. It was all she could manage, feeling faintly ridiculous in that get-up without the flighty would-be actress persona to back her up. When Pierce pushed through the gates and into the parking lot, her shoulders were back, her head up. The cadence of her heels on the asphalt was different than when they'd arrive – harder, crisper, snapping with each stride. She slid behind the wheel of her convertible, and the only sign of the woman she'd been was her clothing, and a stop at a McDonald's restaurant took care of that. Michael Greene was left on the eleventh hole of the Golden Tee Golfland course, unable to wipe the smile from his lips as he rubbed his cheek. The burning wasn't from shyness, but it would be some time before he learned that. * * * * * Thirty-six hours ago. It was later that night – after dinner (is it cliché if they have tacos?), a DVD ("The Departed" – it was Alex's night to pick movies), and... we'll call it an after-dinner mint that conversation turned back to the scuffed-up, white FedEx box propped up on the dining table. "So, uh," Aimz leaned forward, glancing hesitantly past Pierce to the table. "I still feel like it's going to explode. Or maybe has a hidden camera in it that's beaming us into space. In which case... today was not the day to wear my X-Men Underoos." Alex chuckled softly, coming to her feet. Her hand lingered on Amy's arm, and she briefly remembered using the same gesture when Emily Beckett left Michael Greene. "I, uh..." She turned her head away, tucking a stray strand of red hair behind her ear. "I have no idea." "Well, open it!" Amy bounded up behind her. "All your boxes are gone, so you obviously have..." Pierce produced a box-cutter, thumbing open the blade. Campbell's brow lifted as a quartet of quick swipes made short work of the wrapping. Amy had to restrain herself from snatching the wrapped bundle from inside the box, but she plucked a card free as the taller redhead eased out the gift. "'Alexi,'" Campbell read, but she paused, putting the card down. "Really? 'Alexi'? You really need to institute a policy about proper name-shortening." Desade nodded distractedly, slitting the clear tape and unfolding the bubble wrap. Campbell's lips twisted as she flipped back upon the card. "'Got you a spare. Sorry it took so long.' Then there is a list of... dates or something, maybe? What is it?" She peered over the Director's shoulder. Within the wrapping was a plastic mask with a series of straps, the front of the eye protection painted (if by "painted" you mean "splattered") in a rainbow of colors. "I hate that fucking thing," Amy growled. "The Plastic..." Pierce's brow knitted in consideration. "I used to hate this thing, too. Smelled like the underside of a rhino scrotum..." She flicked her gaze over her shoulder to Campbell. "Not that I have any experience with that. It... when I wore it, it blocked off a third of the right side of my vision. God, nearly couldn't breathe when I took Mayhem off the second tier of the Heaven & Hell." Alex lifted the thing tentatively, her pensive look only deepening. "Then would you mind explaining why we're staring at it right now?" "I don't..." Pierce turned, the mask dangling from her fingers. "I had asked him for a new one before Ottawa. Now that... now that Charlie is back, I didn't think about it again. You're saying his card has dates on it?" "First one is two days from now." Amy simply nodded, handing the card off. "Why would you ask for another? Why would he send it NOW?" "I wanted something that would fit better in case..." She looked up briefly, meeting Amy's gaze. "In case Jonathan Rhine wasn't in a position where Charlie would be useful." The admission – that she had a failsafe in case the Red Raver's Iron Man match with Jonathan Rhine (a match that Campbell had staked her own career on) – passed from Alex's lips with a quiet sigh. "Can Daniel find out if these dates correspond to anything the SCCW offices are aware of?" "I guess I could." Campbell sighed when she reached for the newest of her telecommunication conquests – the Blackberry Bold – on a nearby cushion, flicking her eyes back to Alex. "You can't just, y'know, call Cambrian and say 'Oh hey, why'd you send me this junk?', or is that too easy for you super-duper Order types?" "I'm trying not to." Alex looked up awkwardly. "If I call them every time I have a problem, it makes the idea I have retired... less than honest. It's bad enough that Kathryn and Lauren are here all the fucking time." "If your retirement wasn't in question, I doubt that package would've been on my step today." Desade sighed sharply. "I know. But this isn't Order business. This is personal. Someone is wearing a version of this mask. Someone is walking around backstage at SCCW events. Someone who isn't me. Someone has climbed up and taken what I worked five months in a sweaty piece of plastic to earn." Pierce closed her eyes briefly; Amy watched the rage physically melt off her face. "Do you know what that feels like?" In spite of the tension, Amy chuckled. "You haven't followed my career much, have you?" "Are you kidding?" Pierce smiled over her shoulder as she walked to the master bedroom. "Do you know how hard it was to get some of those AWC DVDs? Make the call, please?" The fact that the mighty Alexandra Pierce would stoop to asking, instead of telling, would have sickened a number of her associates. "I already know the answer, but I need to be sure before we go." "Go?" Campbell raised a brow. "I have a stop to make first. Kind of a personal thing. I'd like you to come along – after you make the call." "Fine. You owe me." Campbell grumbled, thumbing at the Blackberry. "The little bastard doesn't come cheap." If it was any consolation, it would have sickened many of Amy's associates to see the little redhead actually attending to the request. * * * * * Today. "I feel stupid." "I know you do." "Pretty sure I wasn't actually looking for you to agree with me." Amy Campbell had lost count of how many times she'd swaddled herself in a hoodie these last few months in an effort to hide a plethora of tattoos or her pierced nose or her neon red hair. But there she stood, arms folded on irritation as she leaned in what she hoped was a casual manner outside the door to the ladies' room. Amy didn't meet anyone's gaze as they passed through the hotel lobby, holding her breath when any of the kids in wrestling shirts peered in her direction. A Holiday Inn full of wrestling fans was not the place for someone as recognizable as the SCCW Universal Champion to fly under the radar. But, like many things these days, she did it for Alex, keeping a lookout as the Spider waited for her prey. The elevator chime sounded softly, a ragged peal of laughter from within the cabin balling the Red Raver's fist. Rather than using it for its intended purpose, Campbell mere rapped her knuckles against the door, twice. "Company," she grumbled. "Blue jeans and her t-shirt." "Kirsten?" The door between them muffled her voice. Amy turned away, tugging her hood lower as a pink-haired girl and a blonde walked past her arm-in-arm, a gaggle of fans drifting in their wake. "Who, Ames?" the faux-Ramone was saying. "She's... let's just say most of the rumors are true, y'know?" She winked broadly over her shoulder at the fans (mostly men), jabbing her tongue into her cheek to bulge it out. "Let me drop her on her head?" the hoodied redhead said. "Maybe kick her in the face? It's ridiculously freeing, I promise." Surprisingly, Campbell didn't actually follow through with her threat, glancing over her shoulder. "They're gone, by the way." "We tried it your way in Lawrence, remember? If it was just Kirsten and whoever is in the mask, it might work. But we don't know how much backup she's got, and I'm not going to play the game they want." Pierce pulled open the restroom door, swaggering out into the lobby. "I'm playing the game that I am good at." To say she was a spitting image of the Fauxmone was an understatement – and, truth be told, would have been a disappointment. It was the same frosted pink hair, the same wide blue eyes, the same ripped blue jeans (different rips, but you'd have to be paying attention), the same right-hand-red, left-hand-green nails. The only things that differentiated the two was the big, spangly bag that Pierce-Ramone slung over her shoulder and a Mickey Mouse watch clasped to her wrist. Even the half-smirk the woman in front of Amy wore matched the one she'd seen lingering on the other's lips. "Y'know..." Pierce-as-Ramone rasped, voice like Demi Moore with a throat full of razorblades. "I can never play her right without a drink. All the mannerisms make me feel friggin' stupid." "I know they do." Amy parroted back Desade's words, barely able to contain her smirk. Alex—Charlotte—swatted the smaller woman in the arm. "Nobody likes a smartass." "You've never had a problem with it." "You know, Ames..." She even said the nickname the same way. Whoever had claimed the Fantastic Plastic really did their homework. "Just shut the fuck up and let's get drunk." "I think that's the first time I've actually liked one of your hair-brained schemes since... well, since ever." Campbell offered Pierce (not Ramone; Amy still parsed them in her head) her arm. "Let's go be bad guys." * * * * * Yesterday. The stone was cool against her forehead, her eyes matching the dark marble. "I'm tired," she whispered. "Tired of being a monster. Tired of being Desade." Alexandra Pierce continued her confession, but she didn't come all this way to speak to a man of the cross. "I want to be something better. I want to be... I want to be a real girl – and I know how that sounds." The grave plot was befitting one of Tampa's wealthiest families, showy and ostentatious like her brother Raymond, but able to weather the worst storms like her brother Andrew. "Patrick Raymond Pierce," it said. "He was our glue." Alex stretched out on the ground, her shoulder leaning against the gravestone. She produced her own wallet (well, the one that actually held her identification), flipping to a picture of a dark-haired teen. "You have quite the granddaughter, you know," she whispered. "Quinn. She's... I am such a coward for hiding her for all these years—in Dallas. With Amy and her... with Amy. It doesn't matter if I had people watching her, doesn't matter if I saw it all on home video, I've been absent most of her life. What kind of mother does that?" A long sigh exploded from Alex's lips. "But I have nothing to give. No lessons worth teaching. The greatest lessons I could give would make her either a world-class poker player or just like me, and I don't... I don't really want for either. One day, when I am gone, I'll be in a plot right here, right beside you – well, maybe in a plot further away if Raymond is still alive. Will anyone care that I was gone? Will anyone ever visit my grave, telling sad stories to ratty grass and faded letters? Will I be missed, or will they be glad that I am gone?" The cobblestone-gray gaze of the Spider in the Web was dotted with growing puddles. "I miss you, Daddy," she whispered. "I miss our talks, I miss your port wine cheese crackers. I miss—" As lost as she got in these moments, she was still Alexandra Fucking Pierce, and the sound of a dry twig snapping underfoot still drew her attention, still flared the very same instincts she was decrying. The initial declaration came before Alex turned. She could have been so much quicker, but the fact that a twig had snapped at all was good enough indication that the approaching party didn't pose much threat to her skill-set. Still, Amy Campbell stood behind her with hands at her sides and a bowed head, speaking lowly – barely at all. "I promise I wasn't trying to sneak." Pierce looked up, rubbing the heel of her hand across her cheek – to clean it, of course. "It's okay," she mumbled. "I... ah, I haven't been here too long, have I? I... I tend to ramble. Keeps me sane." Amy's smile was somewhat meek. She looked more to the stone than the breathing Pierce. " 'I guide you in the way of wisdom and lead you along straight paths. When you walk, your steps will not be hampered; when you run, you will not stumble. Hold onto instruction, do not let it go; guard it well, for it is your life.' " Her eyes finally moved to Alex, "I get the feeling that might ring true to you. There's another, too... something about love being patient and kind. 'It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, and it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. Love never fails.' "Love keeps no record of wrongs," the little redhead repeated, "So you shouldn't waste the little time you have with him apologizing." "My father understood me. Before he died, we were... a family. I thought for years I would never find anyone else who would understand me. Lord knows I could never confess my sins to someone living." Her smile for Amy was wan. "But I wasn't apologizing to him for what I've done wrong. I was apologizing for not being anything worthwhile to maintain the family name. The Pierces' legacy is made of lies. I doubt anyone would miss me if I never showed up again." "I wou—" But Alex didn't let Amy get the declaration out. "Not really. You'd be sad for a while, but you'd end up back with him and I'd just be a mistake you made for a few months one winter." "I don't know about the Pierce family, barely know of your brothers, but this man's legacy is in you, and yours isn't over yet." Campbell kneeled in damp grass, little care for the knee of her jeans if it meant she could be between Desade and what made her. "And mine certainly isn't done, so don't presume to know how it goes yet. Everybody's legacy is riddled with mistakes – even the best of us falter – and yours won't be any different. It can be greater, though... you just need to allow it." "Every time I try, Kathryn or Lauren or one of the other mistakes I have made crops back up to plague me." Alex petulantly plucked at the grass at her side. "I want to be different, but I think the world actually conspires against me. I have nothing to give anyone, and I don't want to use blood money to buy something just to have my name on it." "Push through it and shed the skin." The smaller redhead grinned sweetly, but it turned sour after a moment of quiet. "I mean, look at who you're talking to. A liar, cheater, addict... and I don't even have a child to do right by. At least you're willing to try." "Trying only goes so far." Alex's eyes were still low. "There's nothing I can offer, no future but making people's lives miserable. I don't want that for Quinn. Or for you." "Hey, give me a little credit – I make myself miserable. And you need to quit acting to defeated – you've got at least another half century to offer the world, or find something to offer other than trying... though honestly, the trying's enough." "Really?" Pierce looked up slightly. "You know that the Infinite Gauntlet people called me? They had to know if I wore the Ramone mask when she beat Chris Storm. They wanted me to wear it again for their sixtieth match, just so they'd have the full list of winners. That is what I have given this world. Confusion and questions that don't have good answers." "I don't mean any offense, but we need to get you out of this graveyard. It's weighing on you." Amy sighed. "And I'm not gonna see you wallowing in your self-loathing, it's unfair to the both of us to let this go on. How about you say your goodbyes for now and we go – excuse my language, Mr. Pierce -- fuck with the fake Ramone a little more?" Alex extended a hand up to the SCCW champion, and for a moment – just a moment – the clouds in her gaze lifted as Campbell helped her to her feet. "That I can do," she said, a touch darkly. "Thank you for agreeing to my stupid plans. Dan's going to be pissed you're no-showing a signing for this, you know." Maybe it was proximity to the other redhead that lowered Desade's voice, rendering it a little more ragged. "It's... irresponsible." She brushed the hair from Amy's shoulder. "We should go or Dad's going to have to close his eyes." Amy nodded slowly, and neither woman would admit to being the one who reached out, but their hands were linked as they left. * * * * * Today. She was nervous. She didn't get nervous, not when she was in front of a crowd of thousands of people, not when she was wrestling, not when it was her show to put on. But it didn't matter that the crowd inside that American Legion lounge would barely hit a twentieth of the number of people at an SCCW event, she was still nervous. This was, Amy Campbell thought, why she'd never gone into acting. Amy looked over at Alexandra Pierce – to Charlotte Ramone – and she marveled at the differences. Her posture was different. Her smile was different. Ramone bounced eagerly from foot to foot as Amy waited for the voicemail to kick in. "Hey, it's me," Campbell said. "Remember what I was telling you I might need to do? I'm gonna have to so, if all goes well, you'll be reading about me acting like a drunken idiot by the end of the night. You already know why, and I promise I won't get too crazy. I'll probably call back later, but you might not wanna pick up since... yeah. Love you!" It didn't matter that a call like that would make her husband freak out – didn't even occur to her, really. She hung up the phone and jammed it into the a-little-too-tight-but-don't-tell-her-that pair of jeans, nodding to Charlie. "You ready?" the pink-haired girl asked. Amy grinned a little; maybe it was a little bit exciting. "Not sure I'll ever be ready, but... let's do this." "Remember, this ain't me, it's her." Ramone latched her green-nailed hand around the door handle. "And remember I'm the one with the Mickey Mouse watch." Ramone wrenched open the door, staggering through with a groan and a laugh. "Oh, good, they ain't started the show yet."
