"Progression"

9 October 2004

Date: 2004-10-09 * * * * * Would I have killed Aidan if I hadn’t recognized him? That’s the question I kept asking myself over and over again since that last week of August, when he came strolling over the hill and into my crosshairs. Had I not been a wrestler before entering the underworld of contract killing, I wouldn’t have known who he was. He would have been shot through the forehead, and dead before his body hit the ground. But I was Jonathan Sail first, and Legion second. Even though I only wrestled occasionally, I still avidly followed the sport in my spare time, usually reading magazines or internet articles on the bus trips across the country. Between the contracts, I escaped to the ring – if only living vicariously through the magazine articles. And that is why I didn’t shoot Aidan O’Brien, because I remember reading about how only a year earlier he had entered the Universal Title Tournament as a rookie, defying the wishes of his legendary father. I admired the kid, barely 19 at the time, and everything he went through during those months in Canada. He found strength in the toughest of times, which included the loss of his father. When I thought of Aidan, I often thought of my situation when Danny was killed – and how I failed at keeping my life on the right track through all the trauma I suffered. Aidan did, however, and I wasn’t going to be the one who ended his life. I was going to break my “Three Rules” for the first, and last, time. * * * * * The sunlight poured in through the massive window of his mansion, bathing Victor Arlington in the warmth of a late August afternoon as he stood with his hands cupped at the small of his back. He stood stoically, gazing across the low rolling hills and vineyards of California wine country, listening to a selection of Beethoven as the day progressed. On the far side of the room, an aged face poked into the room through the arched doorway, soon followed by the rest of his lithe body. Dressed head to toe in his butler outfit, Gordon Hart quietly tip-toed his way towards Victor, finally tapping him on the shoulder as the music died down between symphonies. ”I know you don’t like to be disturbed while listening to your music, sir, but I’ve just been informed that the contract on Aidan O’Brien is no longer exclusive to you or Mr. Sail. The 48 hours have expired.” Gordon informed his employer, somewhat tentatively. “I know, Gordon.” “You and I both know that Mr. Sail was never going to fulfill the contract on the O’Brien boy.” Arlington stood silent, perhaps waiting for the next symphony to start, but Gordon Hart knew all too well that the silence was just another way for Victor Arlington to agree with him. “Victor, Leon picked up the contract on Aidan,” Hart said, gently edging in closer towards his boss, “I have a feeling he will be sending the twins…” ”I know.” Victor said, his voice still flat and emotionless. “They might be deadly, but they are egotistical imbeciles compared to Jonathan.” “Should I contact Mr. Sail, and let him know of the situation? I expect he will take the boy to some secluded hideaway until he can purchase the contract from Leon, himself, or…” ”It’s why I put him on this, Gordon. He won’t kill the boy – he can’t.” Gordon shook his head from side to side, in disagreement, ”I wasn’t going in that direction, sir. I meant Sail will purchase the contract himself and never complete it, keeping the boy safe…or simply attempt to elude Leon’s entourage until either Aidan or Mr. Sail, himself, is killed.” “Gordon?” Victor Arlington asked, finally managing to pry his gaze away from the gorgeous summer day. His eyes looked vacant, almost lost. “Yes? Something the matter, sir?” “I started this,” Arlington paused, memories flashing across his eyes, “when I had you protect Aidan in lieu of the torment he was being put through during that wrestling tournament.” ”I’m well aware, sir.” ”The last six years with Jonathan have taught me many things, one of which is that the boy is in good hands.” Arlington said, a small grin gently playing across his bearded face. “You started a war, sir.” Gordon replied, referring to how Victor had ordered him to kill Longinus Miro – the assassin sent to torment Aidan O’Brien during the UTT – even though Longinus should have been untouchable by any hired mercenary. In the mercenary underworld, there are two kingpins, Victor Arlington and Leon “The Leg” Berkowitz. When Arlington had ordered Longinus Miro, Leon’s right hand man, to be murdered – it caused a rift in the underworld, lines were drawn in the sand. Now there are two distinct sects of mercenaries, one loyal to Arlington, the other loyal to Berkowitz. ”I know, Gordon. I know. Part of me wants to make sure Aidan and Jonathan live through this war, because neither of them deserve to be caught up in it. Or, at least the boy.” Gordon took a step forward, coming to a rest directly beside his boss, both men gazing out the window, “We saved the Aidan’s life once before, why don’t we save it again?” “Because, much like you have inquired as of late, it seems Jonathan is getting caught up in his old hobby once more. As much as Sail can hide that face of his, behind all the clever disguises, he can’t hide his desire. I was a fool to think that I could keep him under my supervision, away from the wrestling ring. It’s how I found him…a competitor drowning in the emotions of his mentor’s death. Gordon, this last contract is Jonathan’s ticket out. You and I both know, as good as he is, he’d rather be inside the wrestling ring.” “What about the boy?” Gordon asked, his graying eyebrows arching upwards. ”If Jonathan can keep him alive, which I am highly confident of, I imagine the wrestling world will be a dangerously competitive place with both of them.” ”You’re losing a good man, sir.” ”I know, but you can tell he wants out. I don’t think he ever wanted in.” ”As much as you hate to hear this, I think highly of your decision.” ”Thank you, Gordon. But I have only one more request for you, and then we’ll let Jonathan fight his own war…all while we prepare for ours.” ”And what would that be, sir?” Gordon asked, his wrinkled face once again turning to meet his employers. Gordon compensated the difference in height, nearly a foot between Arlington’s eyes and his own, by glancing upwards. Beethoven was once again starting to ring throughout the mansion. The beautiful chemistry of the instruments couldn’t hide the venomous spite in Victor Arlington’s voice: “Find out what asshole put another contract on Aidan, and get back to me. Immediately.” * * * * * Another round in the tournament, another victory. Looking back over the last decade, I can’t remember the last time I had two straight victories in such a short period of time. Usually I won a match, then purposely lost my next one, just so I would be considered average, at best. It’s easy to avoid the popularity that a winning streak brings, especially when you see-saw wins and losses. Until now. First it was Itagaki Freeze, and just the other day, Iron Lung became the latest conquest of mine while trying to win GTT4. Now I move onto JaZon King, and my first ever attempt at winning three matches in a row. Ultimately, all of these men will become barely a memory from my wrestling career. Even if I were to retire from active competition in two months, two years, or two decades…nobody ever remembers the “filler” matches that give a career some breadth. The diehard fans will remember the “highlight” matches – such as championship victories or tournament finals – but the general fan-base will simply remember whom I debuted against, and whom I retired against. Wins or losses, those matches are what you remember. The “book-end matches”, the first and last, are what everyone remembers. Hell, they are practically the only thing most wrestlers remember. Much like a novel, everyone remembers how the book started and how the book ended, while everything in the middle either builds off of the opening, or builds towards the conclusion. A wrestling career is a lot like that novel: It starts, it builds, and then it ends. All while the fans anticipate what’s next. I wish I could say that my various contracted kills were like that. I can remember exactly what the 33 individual faces looked like…each and every one of them. Except now, when I close my eyes, they are all a pale alabaster color, their eyes glazed over in a cloudy film. The ghosts I just can’t seem to exorcise from memory. I often find it ridiculous that I constantly draw parallels between my two careers…but for a third of my life they were all I ever knew – Wrestling and killing, killing and wrestling. I wonder what level of Hell I’ll occupy when I finally die. I’ve spent my life beating up grown men inside a squared circle, or taking someone’s life with a perfectly placed bullet. Certainly there’s a special level just for folks like me, who have had nothing but violence to define their life. Questions like these continually drive me to better myself, now that the assassin in me has been left behind. Perhaps I’ll be different. Perhaps those fans who decide to become Jonathan Sail fans will be different, because for all they know, I’ve tried to make it in the wrestling business for 10 years now. I just never could put “it” together when it mattered. So maybe I will end up remembering everyone I ever face, all the “filler” between the beginning and end. Maybe the fans will as well, because my second attempt at a wrestling career doesn’t have the sidetrack that my first attempt had. As I advance through GTT4, I can remember nearly every detail, with nothing fading. I hope they never fade, and eventually take the place of the nightmares I continue to drag with me day-to-day. GTT4 is my coming out party, only 10 years late. I just pray that I won’t slip up like I did a little over a month ago, which nearly cost Aidan and myself our lives. In fact, I’ll use those mistakes as a blueprint for advancement in this tournament. Live and learn, Jonathan, live and learn. Mistakes will always happen, it’s just how you react to them that has a direct impact on the outcome. In August and September, I survived a chase that spanned the length of the East Coast, a gunfight during Hurricane Frances, and a high-speed chase through the streets of Chicago – all while protecting the boy who returned me to the path Victor steered me from 6 years ago. I’ve lived and died once already, now it’s time to continue the resurrection of Jonathan Sail. * * * * * “There he is, mate.” A nasal voice declared, bathed heavily with an Austrailian accent. ”And there he is, crouched behind the delivery truck.” A nearly identical voice replied, the R’s virtually melting away into AH’s. ”How come Victah got exclusivity on this one? Wasn’t this the little bugger who got Longinus killed last year?” ”I believe that’s what makes our presence here so lovely, mate. That was almost a year ago.” “Quite the anniversary.” ”Quite.” “He’s got the rifle aimed on him, looks like he’ll take him out. Clever disguise, a UPS driver.” ”Reckon that’s the legendary Legion?” “I know it’s him, he may hide his face and body behind disguises…but he never changes the way he walks. You can pick him out by his gait, brother.” ”I should have thought of that, mate. Absolutely brilliant!” ”That’s why I’m smarter, and the better looking one.” ”But I was always mother’s favorite.” “Fair enough.” Both voices fall silent for nearly a minute. Four blue eyes watch as Aidan O’Brien continues to stroll towards the campus workout facility, and as Legion prepares for the hit. One pair falls on Aidan, while the other falls on Legion crouched with the rifle aimed at the Universal Title Tournament champion. “Here it comes, man. I can feel it. I bet you he goes for the head.” ”I say the heart, mate. It’s more poetic.” Another moment passes by. “Well, well, well…what do we have here?” one of the brothers exclaims. ”He put down the gun…” both voices replied, in perfect unison. “Shall we?” ”Not yet, contract’s not technically ours though. Still have a few hours, mate. Let’s follow them and see what the fuck is going on here.” ”That’s why I love you, mate. Always in it for the hunt.” ”Cheers to that, brother, cheers to that.” * * * * *
