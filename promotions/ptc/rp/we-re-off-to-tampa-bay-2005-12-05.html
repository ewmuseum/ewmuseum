We're off to Tampa Bay!

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 ~---*PROLOGUE: WHAT IS A FRIEND?*---~ What is a friend? A friend is someone you can rely on to be there for you when you need them. A friend is a companion, and will stick by your side through numerous situations. Or is a friend is someone you can rely on… A friend is someone… Or is it? For the past four years, there has been a friend in my life who has proved invaluable. Helping me through the highs and lows of my wrestling career, this friend has done everything a friend should. But if I could be honest, my "friend" wasn’t the best looking around. And a few girls did cringe when the met my "friend". Nonetheless, thanks to my career progressing, this friendship has come to an unfortunate end. Montiero kneels down and places a set of keys through the brass letterbox of an old and dingy door. "Farewell, my friend." Montiero seems reluctant to leave, staring hard at what has been his domain for the past four years. As a final goodwill gesture, he puckers his lips and attempts to kiss the door. His face is an inch away when something large crashes into the door from the other side, shaking it quite viciously. Montiero moves his head away as a series of squeaks follow. It appears my friend has a rat infestation. Montiero sighs. I’d…better get out of here before the landlord catches wind of this. The elevator doors open, the ‘ding’ sounding throughout the small hallway. Sensing it’s the landlord, Montiero flees to a nearby stairwell. The landlord does walk out, seemingly bemused by Montiero's sudden disappearance. He stands still and, sure enough, exactly eight seconds later, Montiero re-emerges with a glostly look on his face. "Keys in the letterbox?" The landlord enquires. "I've left my suitcase inside," Montiero kindly states, speaking over a sudden array of squeaking. ~---*WE'RE OFF TO TAMPA BAY~!*---~ So, we’ve taken the plunge. We’ve moved a good 3,000 miles across the states to Tampa Bay. I suppose the move to Tampa was long overdue; I’ve been wrestling across Florida for the past two years now. This Golden Turnbuckle Tournament simply acted as a push, since the money I’ll be getting for competing will be enough to buy a new home. Of course, I’m not relying on anything more than the money received for entering to help start this new home. As a matter of fact, it's more of a joint business venture than a new home. And I suppose it's for the better too. See, somebody upstairs thought it would be funny if I was placed against Bryan Arson in the first round… The December weather has taken a firm grasp of Tampa Bay, covering almost everything in sight with snow. Nonetheless, the sights of Florida have never looked better. Even while driving through in their not-so-vintage 1964 Buick Skylark, the pair took into the sights of the Sunshine State sans sunshine. They were far from what they knew, yet Montiero strangly felt at home in this new place. "This is the place." Rudolph steps out of the pair's 1964 Buick Skylark, pointing gleefully behind him as if he were a tour guide. Acting much like a confused tourist, a bemused Montiero shiftily exits the car. "Well…" Montiero glances at the house, smiling uneasily. Standing atop a snowy mound, Montiero and Polenski look at their new home. The house itself is considerably derelict, with the dodger blue paint peeling from the old wooden panels. Some of the wooden shutters have fallen to the ground, with two or three looking as if they are about to drop. The front lawn is considerably overgrown, with the path leading to the front porch invisible through the snow. And to round things off, there appears to be a large hole on the right-hand side of the roof. "It’s a fixer." Polenski quickly consoles. "And we’re not paying too much for it. So I guess we should have enough to make the essential repairs." Almost on cue, a hanging shutter finally takes the plunge from the top floor to the ground. Montiero just sighs. And he told me my taste in accommodation was crap. Montiero glances at Polenski, flashing him a million dollar smile before turning to unload his suitcase. Asshole. It’s less than a week away from the inaugural round of the fifth annual Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. And although the majority of entrants seem to have taken a serious approach to their campaigns in order to rally support, Montiero has been somewhat laid back. His laid back attitude has cost him too, as it appears Bryan Arson is the distinct favourite to advance to the second round. Nonetheless, his up-coming match still has yet to cross Montiero’s mind. His latest venture with Rudolph Polenski has occupied every last one of Montiero’s brain cells. Montiero had always wanted an outlet to express his creativity; that’s why he took up acting. The property development game is just what Montiero thought would help him release his creativity. Although judging from his ghostly facial expression, it seems more evident that a shovel, some lime and a wooded area would be a better form of release for Montiero. After all, any man who sang blindly to the tune of $35,000 dollars in a joint business venture would most defiantly consider ending the partnership in the most lethal way possible. Maybe I could propose we scout the area for gooseberries. We’ll drive out to the woods nearby and I’ll bring the shovel. Then when we find the gooseberries and he turns his back, WHAM~! "Stop plotting to kill me," Polenski says in an almost telepathic manner. Stupid telepathic Russian. The pair enter the main voyeur of the house. Glancing at the large wooden banister central to the room, Montiero makes a quick observation. Not sure his observation is accurate, Montiero questions his Russian agent. "Where’s the stairs?" Montiero gently asks as he places his suitcase on the floor. "I…" Polenski seems lost for words as he glances at the naked stairwell. "I don’t know…" "Well, how do we get to the second floor then?" Montiero enquires. Following a lack of response from Rudolph, he perches himself down on his suitcase. "Well, at least we have a roof over our heads." Polenski states, hoping to relieve some of the shock. "…with a huge gaping hole!" Montiero finishes. "Well…" Polenski starts. "I suppose I’d best venture off into this house. Since it doesn’t look like we’re getting upstairs anytime soon, I guess we need somewhere to sleep." "I’ll head to the Hilton and leave you to it!" Montiero jumps up, latching onto the handle of his suitcase and turning towards the door. "I have the car keys, moron." Polenski laughs out loud as Montiero’s face cringes. Boy, I sure could go for some gooseberries right now… The pair glance around the voyeur, skimming the numerous doors on the ground floor. Polenski randomly heads to the nearest door on the right-hand side, with Montiero following shortly afterwards. Upon entering the room, both Montiero and Polenski shriek. "RATS~!" Both men shout simultaneously. Sure enough, in the almost-bare room, a family of three rats squeak and scamper along the floor. As they become aware of their intruders, all three rats stop dead in their tracks. Polenski immediately heads for higher ground via an old chair to the right of the door, while Montiero dashes out of the room. He slams the door behind him, forcing Polenski to shake. "Open the door Christian…" Polenski says at a slow pace. "I want to get out of here!" Montiero says nothing. "Seriously Christian, I can’t reach the handle." Polenski’s becoming increasingly desperate to leave as the nearest rat begins to eye him up. "If you could just open the door slightly…" Montiero says nothing. "Please?" Polenski squeaks. All three rats rush towards Polenski, forcing him into a state of panic. As they reach the base of the chair, Montiero swings the door open. Holding an old broom handle, he looks towards the rats with a sinister grin… …and proceeds to prod Polenski, knocking him off the stool. Oh no… Montiero leaves the room, closing the door. Polenski screams wildly, rolling along the floor in agony as Montiero listens in. Those rats are going to kill him… Maybe, just MAYBE, I took things too far by prodding him off his chair. I mean, those rats could REALLY kill him. A loud thump follows, making Montiero jump. Well, it saves me the drive to the hardware store for that new shovel. And I can’t be bothered to travel into the woods on some farce "gooseberry" hunt. And how wide would I have to make that damn hole… Montiero is interrupted as the door shoots open. Polenski exits the lounge area, his suit in complete disarray and the bottom half of his scarlet tie missing. Montiero smiles innocently. "Guess we won’t be sleeping in there, huh." Montiero winks at Polenski, whose bright red face indicates Montiero is in serious trouble. "I’ll put it down to your warped sense of humour." Polenski breathes gently, trying to calm himself down. "Anyways, I guess the lounge is out of the question." Montiero intervenes. "Let’s try this one…" Montiero walks to the door parallel to the lounge. He takes a deep breath, sticks out his chest and opens the door. Peering his head around the corner, he stares into the room momentarily. Holy shit… Montiero quietly shuts the door, tiptoeing towards a puzzled Polenski. "What’s wrong with you?" Polenski booms. Montiero immediately signals for Polenski to be quiet. "More rats?" Polenski whispers. "Nope." Montiero replies. "Is there a dead animal in there?" Polenski randomly guesses. "Nope." "Is there a homeless guy seeking refuge in our house?" Polenski’s guess becomes more wild. "Yes." "Is there…" Polenski pauses. "What?" "In addition to no shutters, a hole in the roof, a rat problem and no stairwell, we’ve got a homeless guy sleeping in our office." Montiero states, making Polenski cringe. "Well, that’s just dandy." Polenski flails his arms in the air. "Could anything else go wrong is this damn house?" Almost on cue, another shutter falls from the house’s exterior. As Polenki dons that ‘I knew it was coming look’, a small creaking noise can be heard. The noise gradually becomes more intense before turning into a loud bang, which appears to have came from the top floor of the house. The whole house shakes, with dust flying everywhere. "I don’t want to know." Polenski crumples up his face and heads to the door. "Where are you going?" Montiero asks. "I’m going to the Hilton!" Polenski kindly replies. "Have you got the car keys?" Montiero asks in a similar, more sarcastic tone of voice. Polenski skims his pockets, realising the keys are now missing. "No. I don’t have the keys." Polenski replies. He sighs, making a run to the lounge door. He enters the room, yells and exits the room brandishing the keys. His deranged happiness soon turns to fear however. Standing in the office doorway, a half-awake bum glares hard at Polenski. Montiero is nowhere to be seen. "Howdy." Although he was kind, Polenski flees from the house through the main door. The bum yawns, turning back into the room and closing the door. ~---*BIG BANGIN' BREAKFAST*---~ Sitting in a café a few miles down the road from their new place, Polenski and Montiero have some time to reflect. It’s the day after they arrived in Tampa Bay, and the pair are eating breakfast before venturing back to their new home. Montiero pokes the fried egg of his "Big Bangin’ Breakfast" with the corner of his toast, staring out the window. "Don’t worry, Christian." Polenski smiles with assurance. "We’ll be able to fix that place up and sell it for a lot more than what we paid for it. It’s in a nice suburban area, and the other residents have kept with the times." "I’m not worried about the house, Polenski." "What is it?" Polenski asks. Montiero glances at him and pauses. "Nothing." Moving to a different area is always hard. Especially when you've lived in your previous dwellings long enough to class them as a companion. When I made the jump to Prime Time Central, I knew it would be hard to compete with the bigger stars. And they don't come much bigger than Bryan Arson. Or so I'm told. Based on what I know from my past, I really should've looked things up on Arson more thoroughly. But I’ve let my mind occupy itself with other things. I guess my mind really should’ve been on the ball for this one. Going into something blind-sided does have its’ advantages though. Sure, you discover elements which could work against you as they come. And sure, you won’t have a suitable strategy worked out for the match. But if I don’t know what to expect, I won’t fear it. And if I don’t have a plan, I won’t limit myself and stick to it. The less I know about Bryan Arson, the easier it will be for me to get in the ring and perform. The less I plan my actions, the more I will fight using my instinct. The only things I know of Arson are the so-called 'drug allegations' and his intentions for this thing. He seems overly-confident in himself to come out the victor over 'an unknown'. As far as I know, he looks down on me, billing me as some form of XUW rookie. He also seems intent to continue what seems to be an outwelcomed stay on the network, classing me as a pebble amongst boulders on the road to the finals. Well, Arson needs to learn that there is a world beyond Prime Time Central… …if not for his own sake. And I haven’t been one to fear drug barons anyways. As a matter of fact, resorting to the sale of drugs indicates something about Arson’s characteristics. Bryan Arson is a man who takes the easy way out. A hunch tells me I'm not far from the truth, but I can't rely on a hunch. With the amount I have riding on this one bout, I will not provide him with an easy way out. I won’t provide him with a way out at all. Because I know that if I do provide him with a way out of this match… I'll lose everything I've worked for. "I'm finished." Montiero begins to stretch as Polenski shovels the last scraps of food from his plate into his mouth. "You ready to go home?" "Yeah, let's sort this mess out." Polenski smiles. Lucky for me, I've got other friends by my side.
