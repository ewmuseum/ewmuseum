A Mother's Love, A Father's Sacrifice

17 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-17 ~<>~<>~<>~ Mercy Hospital, Chicago… Hush little baby, don’t say a word, Daddy’s gonna buy you a mockingbird The soft lullaby came off the lips of Frank Jacobs as little more than a passionate whisper, serving only to keep himself awake in the dreary confines of his daughter’s intensive care suite. That’s how the doctors had put it anyway, "suite." Their nonchalant euphemism to his only child’s death bed was a bitter pill to swallow, but every time Frank looked at the feeding tube that crawled down his Jessica’s throat, he figured there were worse things he could be swallowing. And if that mocking bird don’t sing, Daddy’s gonna give up everything There were words that a parent never wanted to hear coming from a doctor, words like "cervical quadriplegia" and "artificial life support." When the white coats start talking about quality of life over quantity of life, the inevitable outcome is fairly cut and dry. People start looking at you differently, like you’re a walking time bomb and they want to be anywhere else when your timer finally ticks out. He dared the first doctor to call his Jessica a vegetable. She was his baby girl, not a fucking dinner salad. And if your pretty eyes won’t blink, Daddy’ll find a coat of the finest mink He hummed in rhythmic tune with her heart monitor, wringing his thin hospital blanket into knots without ever averting his eyes from her pale and beautiful face. Long strands of auburn hair enshrined her picturesque innocence like an antique frame, painting her as an angel shrouded in sterile white hospital linens. Her chest rose in a synthetic parody of life; Frank knew all too well that her real future was trapped inside the machines that surrounded them, now more a part of her body than her own useless organs. And if that coat won’t warm your back, Daddy’s gonna have himself a heart attack She was a semester away from her psychology degree. Now she might as well have dropped out of high school for all the good it was going to do her. Her fiancé had taken one look at her, all shriveled up like a human turnip, and decided that he wasn’t ready to take care of a living corpse. Frank had wanted to strangle him for being so cruel, but in a sad way he couldn’t blame the boy. And if that heart attack comes true, Daddy’s gonna be laying there next to you Her shattered spine was beyond repair. The accident had severed the central nervous column at the base of her neck, pinching the nerves and snapping their connection to Jessica’s brain like a dry twig. "All we can do is pray," the doctors kept telling him. "We suggest you do the same." Oh, he was praying all right. He prayed that he’d find the drunk bastard who had ruined his daughter’s life. In the meantime, all he could do was watch in helpless denial as his baby Jessica slept in endless agony, crying himself through the sunrise. ~<>~<>~<>~ The abandoned Love Box warehouse, south Chicago… Most people couldn’t understand why Rich Rollins lived like an impoverished hermit when he could easily afford to furnish and maintain a hotel on Miami Beach with a summer home in Tahiti just for kicks. Having no family to take care of, the Renegade preferred to drink away the surplus of his fortune without having to deal with all the fine print bullshit of a consumer-driven society, catering to people that gave him what he wanted only because he murdered himself on a square canvas stage week after week. He enjoyed his frugality almost as much as he did his privacy, and he could afford bucketloads of both from the quiet sanctuary of his little warehouse in the slums of south Chicago. He had cleared out the main storage hanger earlier in the week, emptying a mountain of crates and boxes into the street without so much as glancing through them. Many of the old cases contained memorabilia collected throughout his career; promotional posters, tee-shirts and a hundred overstocked copies of his own autobiography were of little importance to the PTC Global Champion. They were trinkets of narcissism, nothing more than the shallow shadows of his past anchoring him to the forgotten glories of his storied career. Their nostalgic existence simply didn’t mean as much as it used to, not anymore. For better or worse, defeating Angelo Deville had changed the playing field for the rest of Rich Rollins’ natural life. He had long ago embraced his perennial underdog status, but now that he had taken out one of GTT’s most celebrated champions - the man whom the Renegade had watched in awe for years - he found himself smothered under the oppressive microscope of the wrestling world. This tournament was his tournament now, and the day he fell would be the day a new superstar was born. If that were truly the case, then Rich "The Renegade" Rollins was fully prepared to hand the wrestling world an abortion, one round at a time. Thus was his state of mind as Rich Rollins sat down in front of his computer, blinking away the remnants of a hazy dream. His strikingly stark excuse for a bedroom had formerly been the building’s main office, now cluttered from wall to cracked plaster wall with an assortment of loose paper and dirty laundry. Two mattresses sat stacked in the corner, stripped of their frame and covered in old blankets that were practically useless in the frigid winter months. That’s what the pretty blonde was for, anyway. His psychiatrist’s wife slept soundly through his early morning rumblings, hibernating inside his covers like a polar bear. Rollins figured that waking her up would have roughly the same effect, too; neither of them were what he considered to be morning people. Maybe I should work on that, he thought with mordant amusement, rubbing the crust from his eyes as the computer jumped to life. There was work to be done, after all. Pulling up his web browser, the Renegade began to research his next opponent the only way he knew how. He struck at his keyboard impatiently until the name "Virgil Ambrosi" filled out the search field. One keystroke later and Google was already on the job. A literal digital library bloomed across the screen within seconds, allowing Rollins access to a plethora of useless information. Links to book clubs and classmate finders assaulted his monitor, running off hundreds of pages of random sites containing either "Virgil" or "Ambrosi" somewhere in the text. Sifting through his options, Rollins almost laughed as the search engine asked him if he meant "ambrosia" or wanted an online guided tour of Dante’s Inferno, courtesy of SparkNotes.com. He’d already been through Hell. Unlike Dante, Rich Rollins’ guide had not been named Virgil and had instead been infinitely more irritating. Sighing in frustration, the Renegade was about to abandon his fruitless search when a name finally caught his eye. Virgil Ambrosi, Global Wrestling superstar, also known as "Gideon" … Gideon. The irony slapped Rollins like an angry chimpanzee. The kind that use men’s scrotums for chewing gum. Following the link, Rich Rollins delved into a bio page that ran down the history of his esteemed second round opponent. The man considered himself a vampire, a gimmick that was nothing new to Rollins. He’d already fought the likes of LeStatt Knight and won, but the thought of introducing the two put a twisted smile on his broad features all the same. Virgil Ambrosi was something of an enigma, enjoying the company of women the likes of Shadow and Dawn McFarlane, two former Global stars that Steven Caldera had eagerly sniped away from their rival federation. Rollins consumed the information rapidly, scrolling through a detailed biography and career accolades until his… "Honey, what are you talking about?" His sleepy mistress stirred in bed, exposing miles of slender leg from under a bundled mass of sheets. Infidelity never looked so smooth. "Nothing, Cynthia. Go back to bed." He must have been reading aloud. "Just doin’ a little scouting is all." She purred, stretching herself out like a lazy cat. "My renegade, the explorer." Rollins didn’t really know what the hell that meant, but he was only half listening anyway. Snatching up the folded bundle of leather that had been placed across his chair, the Renegade slipped his ankle-length black duster over his shoulders. Perching herself up on one elbow, Cynthia O’Doyle shot him a curious glance. "Where are you headed?" she asked. He made a beeline for the door, not interested in playing Twenty Questions at six o’clock in the morning. "Mexico. I’ll back in an hour." When the door slammed behind him, Cynthia followed his departure with groggy eyes. "Grouchy ass," she mumbled before collapsing back into slumberland. ~<>~<>~<>~ Rollins’ thick boots crunched a trail through snow so fine that he swore it was seeping through his soles. His walk was met by a foggy gray skyline, towered over by buildings still awaiting the rising sun. The world felt like a high altitude oxygen tank as he inhaled breath after thin and freezing breath, releasing the smoke inside his lungs as a dwindling Marlboro cigarette kept his fingers company. Hugging his coat tight against the bitter winter wind, the Renegade stopped before the open gate of his dreary destination. Resthaven Cemetery stretched out before him like an ominous abyss, welcoming his arrival in silent expectation. Flicking his cigarette into the snow, Rich Rollins willed himself through the high arched entrance without looking back. Rows of headstones peeped out through the fluffy inches of fallen powder, paving his solemn journey with the history of those who were sleeping in soundless eternity. The graves loomed like imposing monoliths of dead stone, and Rollins would have been lying had he not acknowledged the instantaneous unease that settled on his shoulders the moment he began strolling through their ever-watchful gaze. He had walked this path only once, nearly two years ago; he hadn’t attended his mother’s funeral, but he had paid his respects after coming to terms with his own conflicted emotions on her passing. They hadn’t spoken since he was seventeen. He had written numerous letters out of every city in America after his wrestling career had taken off, trying to reconcile their relationship after he had abandoned her and his older brother in pursuit of a better life. The only words of wisdom he ever got back were stamped on his own envelopes, mocking him in bold red ink: Return to Sender. In the end, liver cancer had brought their standoff to an abrupt halt. Every one of his letters had contained a blank check followed by a plea for her to really use it, to get the medical and emotional help that she needed after surrendering her life to liquor, but none of his "pity money" had ever been received. Margaret Amanda Rollins was as stubborn as they came, a trait that the Renegade had inherited more than any other. Even though he hadn’t visited his mother in quite some time, the simple headstone was nestled before him before he knew where he was. He shuddered slightly; whether it was from the bitter chill or his own regret, Rich Rollins would never be sure. Stuffing his hands as far into his pockets as possible, the Renegade stood for a long while, looking at his mother’s final resting place in silent contemplation. A multitude of confusing emotions swam in his brain, invoking memories of his not-so-happy childhood as if the proverbial flood gate had burst open his subconscious, leaving him alone to face the tide. Love and anger had long been one and the same for Rich Rollins, simply because they had both derived from the love of his mother and the anger he felt when he knew she never loved him back. A single tear rolled down his hot cheek, falling into the corner of his mouth like a salty exclamation point. Sucking in a ragged breath, he knelt down before his mother’s grave as he wiped away the snow that stuck to her name, clearing out the carved details that served as the bookends to a life that he had fought for for so long, only to watch it end without closure. Producing a large red magic marker from his coat pocket, the Renegade uncapped the tip and leaned into the smooth marble, scrawling a final Christmas message to the woman who had given him life. Angled just below the name Margaret Amanda Rollins, the message was simple: Return to Sender He set the marker beside a snow covered bouquet of dead roses, stood, and left her cold remains to freeze. ~<>~<>~<>~ Back at Mercy Hospital… The doctors tried to explain that their hands were tied. Frank Jacobs’ daughter was a fleshy body without a brain, and in so many words, was a waste of hospital resources. Her life was over. What the doctors couldn’t understand was that in accepting that fact, so was Frank’s. He stood by the end of Jessica’s bed, the listless look of a man beyond grief sitting just behind glossy pupils. A single nurse stood by the machines, hands folded, offering as much sympathy as she could muster after working an eighteen hour shift. Frank didn’t even notice. "I’ll turn the lights out, it’ll be easier when she starts losing oxygen," the nurse said quietly. Walking over to the wall, she switched off the overhead fluorescents before coming back to Jessica’s side, hovering over her frail form like the Angel of Death. The nurse gave Frank one more compassionate nod before cutting power to the equipment. "I’ll let you say your goodbyes," she whispered before making her exit, closing the door behind her. Frank knelt to his daughter’s side, scooping up her cool limp palm into his own loving paws. The heart monitor’s steady bleeping slowly died away. The intravenous fluid lines had all been pulled out, hanging lifeless to the floor. Jessica Jacobs was completely on her own, and it didn’t take long before her strained breathing began to come at longer and longer intervals. "Go with God, sweetheart." His voice was almost inaudible. "Y-you’ll be with m-mommy soon… I’m sure she can’t w-wait to see you. I swear to you…" but he couldn’t finish. Frank wept openly as his only child’s porcelain face began to turn a deep red, then slowly a darker purple as her body fought valiantly for air. Her chest wheezed up, then collapsed. Wheezed up… … then she was gone. By the time his sobbing began to subside, Frank Jacobs knew he would see his daughter again soon enough. His only reason on this earth was now very simple; he was going to bring the man who murdered his baby girl to justice. He was going to do what his lawyer had already failed to deliver. He was going to end Rich Rollins. ~Fin~
