IG 74: By Its Cover

22 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The mess all started the night I won the open invitational at IG 73. I was back in the trainers' room with a Ziploc bag full of ice on my right knee and a kid who smelled of antiseptics murmuring disconsolately as he examined my shoulder. I was sore and tired and thirsty and it had been thirty-seven days, six hours, and twelve minutes since my last cigar, but the cravings were still bone-deep. I don't say this so you'll feel sorry for me or any of that shit, just so you'll understand why I was in (another) fuck-awful mood, and why I wasn't paying a damn bit of attention to any of the cautionary warnings the medic was slathering on in a droll monotone. I couldn't tell if he was bored or reciting them from the memory of some medical text, but they all came down to "don't move your fucking arm", so who needs to hear the actual words? "How's our patient?" the man in the doorway asked, and I have to admit I hadn't even known he was there until he spoke. That meant he was either really good or I was really old. I just grunted, and the medic (his name was Tad or Thad or maybe Ted – I don't remember anymore) turned just his eyes to him in that way doctors have that manages to say both "'Our'? So you want to do this?" and "At least let me finish diagnosing him, you mope" at the same time. I've seen that look a lot in my day; wonder if he got it out of a book, too. Instead of voicing either of those opinions, though, the doc made a noncommittal sound, halfway between a cough and a murmur. "He's all right," he said finally. "His knee will be a little sore for a while, but it's his shoulder I'm worried about." "Oh?" Both of the man's brows arched genially as he approached. His smile did its level best to seem kindly, but played off his expensive, pinstriped suit to create an aura of sleaze. "Do go on." Tad/Thad/Ted glanced to me, asking for permission to continue. I shrugged both shoulders (one of them hurt – maybe he was onto something). "Don't matter to me, doc," I said, scratching my cheek. "He's the bigshot." The doc scratched at his forearm, but I doubted it was his skin that was irritating him. "I think it might be sprained, perhaps even separated—" The man in the suit wasn't nearly as concerned as he pretended, interrupting the diagnosis to focus that damnable expression on me. "I assure you," the bigshot said. "I am no such thing. Just an interested observer." "Innerested observers don't come in fancy fuggin' suits," I said. This would have been where I tapped cigar ash at the guy's feet. Have I mentioned I miss those things? My visitor stepped forward, extending a hand. "I'm being rude," he said. "Michael Prentiss, Primetime Central. It's my pleasure." I didn't shake. "Roderick Ashe. But I figure you knew that, what with the ring announcer shoutin' it real loud." "That I did." He gave me that smile again – somebody ought to tell him he's not nearly as charming as he thinks he is. "I was hoping I might get a moment of your time." Prentiss favored the doc with the same smile, and from the wrinkle of Tad/Thad/Ted's lips, he thought about as much of it as I did. "If the good doctor doesn't mind, of course." The kid frowned, again glancing my way. Again I shrugged. "Fine," the medic said. "I'm sure I'll have other people to patch up." He tapped me on the chest. "Don't do anything to make your shoulder worse," he warned. I wish I'd listened. * * * "I'm a what?" I'll admit it – I probably took that worse than it was intended. "A caricature?" Prentiss put his hands up, trying his genial little grin again. "We're just saying that perhaps your... your persona is a little bit harsh. The PTC has been assailed for years by accusations that we're not a 'proper' sports federation. The Infinite Gauntlet itself has a history of, well, to be frank... a history of freaks and weirdoes holding it. The current champion, Doctor Curiosity's, ah... companion, for example." I slid off the examination table, and to say I kind of towered over this guy is like saying I kind of like ribs. "So you want me to, what? Go to diction class? Wear a fuckin' suit? This ain't Mr. Universe, an' I ain't no caricature." "That's a double-negative, Roderick – does that mean you are or you aren't?" By now, I really wanted to punch him in that stupid grin. "I'm not asking that you change the fundamental nature of your character, Mr. Ashe. We're looking for a challenger for Matt Ward's Elite Championship, and your tough-guy image helps your case greatly, it's just that..." "That what?" Okay, now I was leading him on. If I was a hero, I might have just walked away. Maybe even listened. "Am I too big? Am I too brash?" Prentiss shuffled back a step. "You're trying too hard. I mean, I get that you've got this whole... thing going on. I'm told you used to smoke in the arenas?" "Quit a few weeks back. I'm on the patch." I rubbed at the back of my neck. "And I'm not sure I like where you're going." "It's no secret that this business is struggling, Mr. Ashe. SCCW and PRIME are both hurting for people. Before the IG match tonight, it had been some six months since we'd even been able to organize one of these things. If it's going to be successful enough to continue, we need a marketable champion, not a – " I thumped the man in the chest, just two fingers against the breastbone, but when you're my size, that's pretty much enough. "I'd watch your words here real careful, pal. I don't know who you think you are..." Prentiss stepped back, straightening his blazer with a small frown. "I've tried to be respectful, Mr. Ashe—" "You ain't been shit. I just wrestled a bunch'a guys, I'm back here with a bad knee an' a shoulder the doc don't want me to lift so high, an' the first thing you say – the very first fuggin' thing – is that you think I'm some kinda caricature? That you want me to 'be more real'? What the shit does that even mean?" "I'm trying to be helpful." He leveled a hard gaze on me, adjusting his lapels again. "I know you're used to other people telling you what to do, so I'm going to make this clear for you: you dial it down and you'll get somewhere. If you don't, you'll go back under whatever rock Alexandra Pierce found you." Okay, that was really lame of him. I know that. I've faced tougher guys than him. I've heard insults from chicks that were meaner (but then I've known some hardcore chicks). But maybe it was the adrenaline or maybe it was the fact that nobody from my old life even showed up to see me wrestle the match. Maybe it was even this guy's smile or his damn jacket or just because it was Thursday, but whatever the reason, I grabbed Mr. Michael Prentiss by his prissy lapels and propelled him into the wall. "You watch it, fella," I growled. "I ain't ever been interested in makin' a good impression on management, an' I don't need Allie Pierce to be anythin' worth a fuck." That last part might've been a lie. "Do you even want to make it to your match with Eegor?" I snorted, and I shouldn't have – little shits like this guy are always the type that rises to the top in the wrestling business. Or in business at all, really. "You kiddin'? I got a ration of Frankenstein jokes all lined up." "You might want to hold onto those. Or let me go." "You don't wanna tell me what to do." "If I tell you to go out there wearing a damn clown outfit, you will. It's my job to make sure that you – " I didn't let him finish his sentence. I've found that arguing with guys like this doesn't go so well for me. So I hit him. I took it easy on him, just a sharp, short cross to the face. It split his lip and it knocked him down, but more importantly, it sent a message. "Your job is to make sure that the Gauntlet's got somebody people'll wanna see," I said, jabbing a thick finger his way. "Maybe I'm 'dialed up' too much, but I guarantee you – I guaran-fuggin'-tee you – that I'll attract more than your freaks an' weirdoes." My shoulder hurt; I probably shouldn't have lifted the guy up quite so high. He scrabbled backwards, not getting up – he was probably afraid I'd knock him down harder the next time. "What do you think you're—" he spluttered. "Do you know who I am?" I shrugged again, and again I felt the twinge in my shoulder. "Another guy in a suit who don't understand a fucking thing about this business. Now I'm gonna walk out that door right there and go to my hotel. If you want, you can shout some real mean words after me. I promise I'll feel real bad about it later." He didn't say anything as I left, but I could feel his eyes on my back as I left. I didn't turn around, but maybe I should've. Maybe I should've stayed. Maybe I should've waited to see who he called next. Maybe I should've checked to see if there even was a Michael Prentiss at the offices of Primetime Central. If I'd have done those things, the next couple days would've been different. * * * Here's a truth about the exciting life of a pro-wrestler: you get pretty used to living in hotels. Sure, some of my colleagues can afford five-star accommodations or penthouse suites, but the rest of us get used to roadside inns and dive motels. The number one contender for a six-month dormant title doesn't get special treatment, so my trip to the Midwest brought with it another two-story derelict with drafty windows and a busted elevator. The room smelled like mildew and sadness, and I wasn't gonna help it with the six-pack of Guinness hanging from my limp left hand. But I was thirsty and tired (and all the other stuff I mentioned earlier, too). I still felt off my game, and I should've known something was up before I even opened the door. I certainly should've guessed when my foot crunched stepping into the room. But I didn't. So the carpet crinkled as I stepped in, and my first instinct was to bend down to figure out what the fuck was under my boot. I'm not dexterous (just ask any of the jackwads who recap SCCW shows on the 'net), so that involved a lot of shuffling backwards and shifting my weight and I had to put the beer down to manage it. It was a manila file folder, no different than you'd find in an office or, you know, an office store, I guess? Look, it was a folder, and I said, "What the fuck is this?" because I'm a dumbass and have clearly never watched a single spy movie ever. I didn't hear my attacker coming, which shouldn't be a surprise, since there are a lot more sneaky people in the world I left than in the world I entered. I sure felt them, however – the sharp sting of a taser between the shoulderblades is something you just don't miss. All my muscles spasmed at the same time, my eyesight went to spots, and I dropped my beer, stumbling to a knee. "Forgive me," my assailant said. It was a woman (which, if you're just tuning into this shebang, isn't as uncommon as you might think), soft-voiced but snide. She sounded young in that way that women do when they haven't learned that the world fucking sucks. Or, put succinctly, she sounded like a bitch. "I thought we should have this conversation on even terms." "You'd need a stepladder," I said hoarsely, because getting tasered hurts. She snorted a laugh, and then she did something unforgivable: she stole one of my beers. "So here's the thing..." the girl said, and I got the impression she took a seat somewhere nearby. "We were okay when you were out of it. We would've left you alone to bounce or play rent-a-cop or be a janitor. We even would have let you be a wrestler. But Alexandra is exiled. Do you know what 'exiled' means?" I grunted, pushing myself to my hands and knees. This was a mistake, as the cattle prod jabbed my back again. "No, that's wrong," she said patronizingly. "'Exiled' means no-touchy. It's bad enough that Drusilla has gone rogue. If we let the whole lot of you go back to her, then what's the point of the exile? The system will become out of whack. And then the world will fall into chaos." "You're..." My lungs were strained; maybe those years of smoking weren't such a good idea. "That ain't real sane, y'know." "Not really, no." It was a conspiratorial whisper, and it felt like I was in a stage play, or being filmed. You know that crawling sensation when you figure you're being watched? It was like that, and I started to think there was another set of eyes in the room. "But that was what the boss said to say, and we're all slaves to our master." I spat something on the floor; I hoped it was just saliva, but it was too dark to see. "Some more'n others," I grunted. Again, I pushed to my hands and knees, trying to see who else was lurking in the room. I thought I'd seen something when the woman kicked me in the ribs, sending me convulsing back to the moldering carpet. "That one..." My bravado degenerated into another fit of coughing, and breathlessness put the pitch of my words up almost an octave. "'S gonna cost ya." "Maaaybe." She trilled the words. "But seriously, Roddy. Drop this. Go and be a champion, win a belt, throw somebody off the top rope or... whatever it is you people do. But let this one go. Because the next time, I won't be so nice." I felt the sneer take my lips before I found breath enough to speak again. "Real easy givin' orders from the shadows like some overblown cliché." "Tt," she spat, a disdainful sound. "Like you're much better, trying to goad me into monologuing? Face it, hon..." She bent down close, tying her fingers up in my hair. I used to keep it short, in a military-style crewcut. Like so many other things, I'd let that go, so there was more to grip. "...this is one of those mysteries the hero only learns about at the end of the story." I felt the prick of the needle, but that was about it. Whatever tranquilizer the bitch pumped me full of worked fast and kicked like a horse. I woke up facedown inside the door, and I got to add "a splitting headache" to the list of my ills. Plus the bitch stole all my beer. * * * The next day, I set up my camera on a tiny table in the motel room with the drab, grayish-green walls as a backdrop. I sat on the edge of the bed, still wearing the same Black Ops t-shirt I'd left the arena in. A pad of scratch paper sat on the table, just out of frame. "Hey," I said, smile wide and teeth yellow. "Y'know, I heard on... on one'a them sites we wrestlers ain't supposed to know about – the 'dirt sheets,' or whatever – that this match is, like, two steps away from a dream match. There've been seven GTTs. Just seven, Eegor. That's it. One'a them was won by your boss an' one'a them was won by mine. See where I'm goin' here?" I scratched the back of my neck. "They're sayin' that if it was Doc verse Allie, it'd be one fer the ages. Two'a the best in all the damn world, face-up in the middle of the ring. But since it's just Eegor an' it's jus' me, they're sorta disappointed. What mighta been. What coulda been great's just about a belt that don't mean nothin' to nobody. We're gonna jump in the ring, you with your catch-as-catch-can grapplin' shit an' me with my 'drop-you-on-your-skull'... uh, skull-droppin' power style, an' we're gonna wrestle a match that I figure will be pretty okay. "But we could go out there an' play tiddlywinks an' they'd say the same thing." I leaned forward, my bad arm rested on the table. " 'Sure, that was a badass game'a tiddlywinks, but how awesome would a game between Doctor Curiosity an' Desade have been?' An' you know what I say?" I let the question sit there for what felt like a long damn time, fixing a pig-eyed stare on the lens. "I say, 'Fuck them.' Fuck bein' tied up in what we was, man. I ain't just Allie's meat shield, you ain't jus' Doc Curiosity's gopher. We're just guys, you an' I. An' maybe we've lived in that world where we're... we're 'caricatures', right? One-note goons. The important people come out and say their stuff, an' you an' me, we're just there to set up the jokes. "But all that's passed. You got yourself a belt – or a trophy or I don't know... a medal." I shrugged a little, only using one shoulder. "Whatever the fuck the Gauntlet is. More an' more, people don't just think of you as Curiosity's pal. You got yourself inna tourney to become Elite Champ. Hell, you beat Amy Campbell in it, an' take it from me, that ain't easy. You keep goin' like this an' Doc will be the undercard. ' 'Member that time when Eegor worked for Doctor Curiosity?' they'll ask. 'Those were some fucked-up times.'" Whenever I'm doing this, I end up fidgeting – I tap my fingers, I shift in my seat, I roll my neck. It's not nearly as easy to stare into a camera lens as you might think. "I want that. I want that shot. I want people to look at me an' not see the guy who got dropped on his head so Alex an' Amy could scurry into the stands an' disappear to their lesbian love nest. I don't wanna be the also-ran half of the Glorious Bastards, the one who ain't Hunter Sabuani, I wanna be the Glorious Bastard, the Man-Mountain, the monster nobody – no-fuckin'-body – wants to fuck with. "It sucks that only one of us gets to have our dream, man." I gave the camera my best serious face. "After this match, one of us is gonna be a half-drawn, half-remembered picture, always standing off to the side of somebody more famous." I shrugged again, this time using the wrong shoulder, and I lifted my hand away from tapping on the tabletop. I tried to mask the twinge, but I'm pretty sure that Eegor's gonna try to rip my arm out of its socket once he sees it. "I'm gonna damn well make sure that person ain't me. Yeah, I guess IG 74 might've been prettier if it was Desade vs. Doctor Curiosity, but I know I'm comin' outta Sin City loaded for bear, an' I still got one more good fight left in me. What say we see if we can make those fans in attendance forget who we used to work for, if only for a little bit, hmm?" My yellow teeth flashed again. "See you there, sidekick." It used to be that I could never find the stop button on these things. I'd have whole videos that ended with some wobbly, vertigo-inducing shot of whatever room I was in (or worse, the one time I just left it running till the battery died – got no end of complaining from the editors about that one). This new camera was way easier to use, though, and one button-press took us to darkness. I'd also become pretty good at uploading video. I just hoped this time, the message got heard. * * * This may surprise you, but I fucking hate Las Vegas. I know, right? Who the fuck could hate Vegas? Slot machines, loose women, high-stakes gambling, cheap-ass buffets, great shows... what's to hate, right? First off, it's hot, an' I'm a fat guy, okay? You don't wanna know the places I've been sweating. Mostly, though, it's the tourists. Tourists bring money, sure, but there're only so many hotels, an' the hotels compete to bring them to their place, which creates places like the Midway at the Excalibur, which is the kind of place where kids play dumb carnival games while their parents spend their college funds. The air smelled like cotton candy and abandonment, with rundown, dribbling water pistol balloon races and skeezy skee ball tracks providing the soundtrack. I hate places like this. Kids raced around, barely supervised; their hoots and hollers hurt my ears. Places like this were glorified playpens where irresponsible parents could legally abandon their children. Thankfully, the little ragamuffins steered clear of me as I walked in. Kids don't like me, which is fine because I generally find them pretty punch-worthy myself. It wasn't the most innocuous place in the world, but it did present certain advantages. I was the tallest guy here, and anybody who came for me would be just as out of place as I was. I peered across the room from my vantage point, but nobody was paying more attention than they should. I'd brought a glass from down in the casino, and I took a long sip as I considered where to wait. It needed to be someplace with a view of the escalators, but not someplace where there'd be too much of a crowd. That question got answered for me; I caught the glint of costume jewelry from nearby, a hand lifted in the air from a caricature artist nearby. "Monsieur!" the woman called. She was dark-haired and dressed like Disney's image of a gypsy in a white blouse and black vest, with enough bracelets and baubles to act as a makeshift disco ball. She gestured to the stool opposite her, picking up a marking pen. "Picture?" "Uhhh..." I looked doubtfully at her easel. After all the talk of caricatures and overblown images, the last thing I wanted was a real one. "Come, come." She tapped the stool insistently, rings clicking on the wood. "It will beautiful." I couldn't decide if her accent was French or Transylvanian, but I didn't have much choice; I was already the biggest guy down here, and letting the artist make a scene wouldn't help this stay on the down-low. So I sat down, fixing the gypsy with a hard stare. She was too short to be Alex or Dru, and didn't have the bazongas to be Kathi Shaw. Mind, that's just three chicks I know, so something was digging down under my skin, I just couldn't figure out what. "Just... don't do nothin' offensive or draw me with boobs or nothin', okay?" The little gypsy nodded, and I swear she had to hide a smile – maybe even a snicker. "I do a good drawing," she said. "You see. You see." So at this point, some of you might be wondering, "What the fuck is Roderick Ashe doing at a casino in Vegas, getting his picture drawn by some chick doing a bad gypsy impression? Doesn't he have better things to do, like rehab his shoulder or get a haircut or something productive?" And those people are right, because I didn't tell you something important. Remember that video? The one with the table and the video camera and the notepad and the tapping? Yeah, that one. If you knew what to look for, you'd have been paying less attention to the (admittedly kick-ass) words I was saying and more to what I was doing, you'd know that I was tapping the pad. And that on the pad, upside down, was a sword with "LV" scrawled atop it. Hey, I'm not saying it was brilliant or anything – I ain't the mastermind of around these parts – but I figured that if someone smart was looking for me, then they'd watch the video when it got uploaded. And I figured if they watched it, they'd figure out that the "sword in LV" would be the Excalibur. What I didn't figure was how fucking big Vegas casinos are. What I didn't think of was how to find one person in a hotel full of gamblers and tourists and children and, and, and... you get the picture. There's a reason I usually let other people do the planning. The casino itself was packed in that way that only a casino gets: people clogged up around tables and slot machines, rarely two next to each other unless there were other choices. The Midway was a little more crowded, but any adults would stand out something fierce. Which meant that the casino was cleaner, but the carnival games gave me a better chance to see them coming. I was relatively sure there'd be three types who'd figure out the code. Bitch and her employer would be looking for proof I wasn't being a good little puppy dog and coming to heel. But the guy who found me in the bar wasn't trying to drive me away, he was trying to recruit me for something. So if they were following close, they'd figure something was up. The third kind of people who'd figure it out would be Alex, and I wasn't sure which side she was on, though I held out some hope, obviously. Gypsy was talking occasionally as she doodled her picture, but it was the man in the ballcap and shades coming down the escalator I was most interested in. The cap was a Yankees one, the shades were Oakleys, and he had a thin, douchebag 'stache to go with it. Guy seemed shifty, his hands buried in the front pouch of a sweatshirt that didn't fit so well. Plus it was a Red Sox hoodie, and there are some things you don't mix. "You want I put you in the casino?" the artist asked. "Or maybe I draw you at the beach with your girlfriend?" "Nah..." I waved one hand. Douchebag wended his way around the room, never once looking my way. "Draw me wherever you want." The caricature artist nodded, marker moving on the paper. Again, I swear she hid a smile, and I was about to ask something when the guy dipped his hand into the pouch and I knew – I just knew – that he was drawing down. "Gun!" I shouted, jumping to my feet. The stool tipped over, thumping dully against the carpet. I tackled the artist – look, I'm not saying I'm always a bad guy, and it seemed like if he was gonna shoot me, then she'd be at risk, right? No gunshots came, though. No bullets whizzed by us. I heard some commotion, but I was still alive, which is always a plus. "Whaddafuck," I grumbled, pushing to my hands and knees. "Everybody—" The gypsy artist cinched her arms around my neck, lips at my ear. "Hey," she said, the horrible accent gone. "Meet us outside the casino." I leaned my head back, bushy brows climbing. "Uh," I said, as eloquent as I could manage. "Hi." Amy Campbell grinned beneath a mile of makeup and behind a pair of hazel contacts. "You're kiiiinda on my leg." "Uh," I said again, getting to my feet. A half-dozen guys, most of whom I hadn't even seen, had taken down the guy. I think one of them was a cocktail waitress, another a carnival barker... whoever they were, they were good. Gypsy-Amy got to her feet, making a show of righting her easel as she spat curses in French and proto-Russian and pseudo-Italian and even I think (I shit you not) Elfish. I got my big ass out of that room, making my way to the valet station. At some point, I picked up a tail, but I wasn't about break into a run. I'm also not fast (just ask the bloggers). I never valet park – call it force of habit. There's nothing worse than tracking a mark and then having to wait for some kid in a red vest to bring your car around. As I circled through the parking lot, I heard tires screech behind me, cutting off my pursuit. High-pitched screaming carried on the wind, two people playing the Berkowitz Bluff; it's a simple distraction, but an old couple yelling at each other works better than almost anything else. Bonus points if they're Jewish. A luxury sedan jerked to a halt in front of me, and the passenger window whipped down. Amy Campbell (one of Alex's ex's, former SCCW champion, all-around unfriendly type these days – or so I thought) sat in the passenger side, her gypsy-wig in her lap. She looked kinda like a raccoon. "Get in," Amy said. "Uhhh..." I make that sound when I'm thinking, I promise. "Somebody wanna tell me what the hell's going on?" The driver leaned her head down so I could see her, and Alexandra Pierce favored me with that teeny-tiny, I-know-something-you-don't-know smile that I knew so well. "Just get in," she said. I just got in. "So you two are... back bein' friendly with each other?" I slammed the door. "Hey!" Amy grumbled good-naturedly. "Easy." She lifted her eyes to the rearview, but the storm blew out of her eyes as she reached behind her. "Drew this for you." I unfolded the picture to find what could charitably be called a cubist drawing that resembled me only in name. "Uhhh..." Like I said: thinking. "Thanks?" My eyes met Allie's in the mirror as she shifted gears. "Good to see ya," I said. She was smiling. Amy soon joined her. And that's the story of how a six-foot-eight, three hundred and sixty pound guy slipped past two crews of pros, all with the help of two redheaded girls. Or, y'know, that's the part that I understand. Because when it comes to dealing with Allie F'in' Pierce, you never really know the whole score. And because I'm just a dumb, brainless thug, right?
