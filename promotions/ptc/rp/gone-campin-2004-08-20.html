Gone Campin

20 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-08-20 People learn history today for the simple reason that history repeats itself. The events and occurrences of this world all roll through in a never-ending cycle, the only thing that changes is the names and identities of those involved. As children, many used to play Cowboys and Indians. The result was always the same no matter where you were, the cowboys would win. Indians with their arrows and stones could never defeat the pistols of the Cowboys. Native American people, though strong in heart and spirit, simply didn’t have the manpower to defeat the mighty Cowboys. Today there is a group of people who celebrate the customs and traditions of Indians. They worship something called the Four Winds and appropriately they call themselves the Four Winds Wrestling, a professional wrestling promotion. Their roster is respected on a national level, perhaps even world wide. There is no doubt that Four Winds Wrestling knows how to produce superstars. Names like Patti Anderson and Fnord have becomes household names at the hands of the 4WW. They are one of the best wrestling promotions in the world no doubt, and not only that but one of the best in the Prime Time Central. Prime Time Central is the big leagues, it’s where only the best wrestling organizations on the face of the Earth get in the door. PTC is for the elite, and this September the Survival Event will take steps towards telling just who the most elite is. And 4WW, though impressive, and although they have assembled a very remarkable group of four to fight on their behalf, they are not the most elite. They are just the Indians. They will be outmatched by the Cowboys, just like history can tell us. 4WW’s last remaining opponents in the Survival Event (after RPW and NOW backed out for various reasons) are the men of Core Wrestling. Perhaps drawing a comparison to cW and Cowboys is a bit beyond the realm of reasonable though. It wouldn’t be illogical, Core is based out of Texas, the home of the Dallas Cowbys and owned by Texan Matthew Lanier, but for the most part Core doesn’t have much of a cowboy spirit to them. That is except for one man, on the Survival team, and that man is Munson. With a thunderous rev of the mangled old engine the 324 pound Rowdy Redneck comes swerving out of his dirt road driveway in his trusty old red pickup. Plumes of dirt buzz around in the wind like a pyrotechnics display as he makes contact with the road. He stick shifts into Drive and taps his earthy brown cowboy boot to the pedal. Above the boots are tight pair of blue jeans that fit snuggly around the monstrous legs and bulbous ass of the heavyweight. Above that is his typical garb, a white NASCAR t-shirt looking to not have been washed or ironed in thirty years inside a slick black leather jacket. And on his head is a good ole fashioned cowboy hat. With his chubby fingers he cranks on his in-car ‘stereo’ to a teeth-chattering full blast and whoops along with the words. Munson- “SWEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEET HOME ALABAMA! Duhnu, duhnu, DUHNU, GIRL I’M COMIN OME TO YOU! Duh, doopy doo, SWEEEEEEEEEEEEEET HOME ALABAMA!” Obviously Munson is somewhat of a redneck, and a man who enjoys his country music. He’s heard it all from his opponents over the twelve months he’s been wrestling professionally. He’s been called a pig humping hick, a horse humping hick, a cousin humping hick and all kinds of mean things like that. But what’s somewhat bizarre about this scene is Munson referring to Alabama as his ‘sweet home.’ Because indeed, Munson is not Alabaman. When he pulled out of his farmhouse just now and onto the dirt road it was his farmhouse in MONTANA. If there’s one word to describe Munson, it’s without a question; Montana. He loves Montana, born and bred a country boy in Billings, Montana Munson loves his home state more than most men love their wife. In the wrestling world he does indeed come down to the ring as “Munson” but he has most certainly earned the moniker of the “Montanian Madman.” It’s a name which he deemed himself of course, anyone else would have known that a person from Montana is a Montanan with no ‘i’ anywhere in there. But nonetheless, he is Montanian, and he most certainly is a Madman as he floors the gas pedal sending his truck into rocketing speeds near 45 miles an hour! Well perhaps that isn’t so fast, but for this broken down truck that might as well be running on square shaped tires it works just fine. You would think a man like Munson, given his occupation as a high class professional wrestler would be able to afford a few of the finer things in life but not really. Money has never been the most comfortable subject for Munson, mainly because, he’s never had a dime to his name. His father died while the Rowdy Redneck was still just a little tyke, and his mom hardly made near enough to support 7 children. After high school Munson never chased a college degree, he had a plan to start his own horse riding center called Henry’s Horses. (Henry refers to Munson’s real name outside the wrestling world, Henry Thurman.) Horses and farm animals in general were the only thing Munson really knew a thing about, but he damn sure knew a thing about them and he thought he could go big time off Henry’s Horses. Unfortunately he never got the funding he needed and was sentenced to what seemed like it would be a life of bottom of the barrel slave work. But given what Munson did, slave work might not sound to unappealing. The main word when talking about Munson’s various oddjobs during that time period of his life is shit. No not poop, not feces, not relief, yes it is a swear word but shit is the only way anyone could ever look at what Munson did. He cleaned toilets at large building for big time business execs. And believe it, they aren’t called BIG-TIME business execs for nothing and Munson learned that pretty quickly. He also did a job that human beings shouldn’t even be allowed to do where he cleaned up animal feces off sidewalks in a rich neighborhood for a few years. The pay was literally below minimum wage, and the heat outside during the summer in Montana was scorching. But the straw that broke the camel’s back on that job was the tools. Munson was given a scooper, and a bag, that was it. No masks to protect him from the smell, no rubber gloves, no nothing. And there was one other problem, the damn clamp on the scooper didn’t work, so to make a long story short; Munson’s tools were his right hand, and his left hand when he lost patient with the scooper. It was terrible. Things got a little better when Munson started doing low class construction. The pay still sucked and the work was still backbreaking, but Munson started developing some muscles. And it was around this time his saving grace came along, pro wrestling. A small promotion called Ultimate Wrestling Warfare out of Portland allowed Munson to join up with them for a few shows because of his towering size and outlandish personality. From there it all just snowballed, and six months into his career Munson found work in Core Wrestling. He’s worked there ever since. Given that he didn’t start wrestling until the age of 34, his body obviously takes a lot more beating than a lot of the younger guys in the business do. It’s doubtful he’ll be able to wrestle a full 5 or 6 more years, but he wouldn’t stop for anything at this point, whether his body wants to or not. He’s finally getting the money to flow in, at least enough to buy crappy red pickup trucks from the 80s. Pickup trucks take a lot of stereotype from people, in reality they’re very good modes of transportation. But more than that they can haul a nice big load around in the back which is exactly what Munson needs in this situation. He’s on his way to the PTC Survival Event where he’ll compete on behalf of Core Wrestling. But first he decided he’d head down to a camp site in southern Montana for a few days and just ease his mind. So in the back of his pickup, he not only has a tent, a sleeping bag, water bottles, three coolers packed to the brim with food (fat man’s gotta eat) and other camping supplies, but he also has his wrestling bag along for the trip. Inside that bag is just typical things, a black spandex, a black mask with spikes poking out and some wrestling boots. But beyond that there is also one other prized possession, and that is the Core Wrestling Pride Championship Belt. Munson is the first ever Pride champion, and hopes to be the greatest as well. There’s a lot of pressure being the Pride champion, holding any belt at all for that matter provides a great deal of stress obviously. It’s one of the reasons Munson wanted to do some camping before perhaps the biggest show he’s wrestled in his career yet. There’s so much weight on his back being a champion, he just needs some time to himself. But of course, being a champion is something that all of his Core teammates will be able to understand. When Team cW was first named, every single member on the team had a championship belt. Smiley, unfortunately lost his Classic Title on the last Core show so the illusion of gold from all sides no longer exists. But nonetheless Smiley is a worthy man, as one must be to hold the Classic Title, even if he only did have it for a week. Smiley is a somewhat odd and bizarre soul, but Munson would be the last to notice that. Next is another man who is no longer considered a champion in Core, Adolph Nilsson. However Adolph never lost his tag team title, the belts were simply done away with by Matt Lanier for reasons unbeknownst to everyone at the time. Adolph and his tag partner Bjorn are a great tag team, easily one of the best Core has ever seen of course. And to say that Adolph won’t be able to compete without his tag partner is foolish, as Adolph has certainly proven himself as a worthy singles competitor as well since the tag titles disappeared. Not only that, but Adolph knows when to tag, and he has experience functioning as a team, something he can hopefully teach the rest of Team cW who have very little experience in the tag division. Next up is Matt Epic, the Legacy Champion. He’s a man who Munson does have a slight bit of history with, but unquestionably a great wrestler. The Legacy Title is the top tier belt in Core Wrestling and the World today by many people’s standards. He defeated one of the biggest names in the sport today in Jason Hughes to win the belt, certainly that should mean something. Even if he did need help from Brock Rockhard. This is most certainly a team to be reckoned with in PTC Survival competition, assuming atleast that each wrestler brings their A-Game. Munson most certainly intends to… but first he’s got some camping to do.
