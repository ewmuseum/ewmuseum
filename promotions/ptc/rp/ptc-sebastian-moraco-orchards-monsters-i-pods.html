Orchards, Monsters, and I-Pods (IG Round 5)

27 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She feared for her life and the life of her son. She carried the small child in her arms as she stumbled trying to navigate through the apple orchard. Protruding twigs and branches stabbed at them at every opportunity as the woman frantically tried to find exodus out of the apple orchard maze. The sky was lit up in an orange hue, a slight mixture of yellow and orange as the sun began to set down behind the mountains. The woman continued to run through the maze, trampling every dead leaf, fallen apple, and twig that lay in her path, hoping that every corner was the exit. Her eyes were filled with despair, and her son closed his eyes so that he would not have to look upon the orchard they were tying to escape in fear of seeing the monstrosity that was hunting them. In the distance they both heard a wailing of some sort that grew closer and closer. It had chased them from their modest, wooden cottage on the other side of the orchard. Without warning it had struck and neither the woman nor the child could protest that they ever saw a more frightening figure. Its hands barely scraped her soft, yet soiled, skin, but they were colder than death. She had grabbed the child at the earliest opportunity and ran out of the cottage after tossing the firewood at the door and enclosing the monster in a cage of fire. Fire, however, only seemed to anger it more. It gave chase and led them into this maze of an orchard. The little boy began to whimper and his mother whispered into his ear. “Be brave, my son.” She said, trying to comfort him. “We’re almost out of here.” The boy calmed down a bit knowing that his mother would protect him. She was a Goddess to him. She would never let anyone, no matter how unearthly, do harm to him. He recalled all the times he was in trouble and how she was always there to save him. He was always scared, and she was always brave. This time, they were both scared, and thus both brave. The footsteps the monster approached closer and closer. The woman readjusted her son in arms, the fatigue starting to set in. She knew she had to find a way out of the orchard soon. Behind them, the leaves decayed absurdly rapidly, the twigs crumbled up, and the apples on the floor began to spread apart creating a path. It was almost as if the monster chasing them was so evil, that it made the immovable, movable, and the inanimate, animate. Yet, neither the woman nor the child could hear anymore of the wailing or the thunder-like footsteps behind them. Those piercing sounds seemed to faint away in this distance as they navigated further and further into the maze. The woman knew that they could not be completely safe until they were out of the orchard and across the river, back to the city. Nonetheless, her arms grew tired and the boy was beginning to slide down her body, her efforts becoming futile. After they rounded another corner, the woman set her son down, who began to weep again in fear. “Please, don’t,” she whispered. “Don’t cry now. We are almost out of here. Nothing is going to happen to you, ok?” The boy understood. “You hear that?” she asked, conjuring a smile that was both relief and hope. “It’s gone. There’s nothing there anymore.” The boy nodded. “Come here, baby,” she said to him and picked him again in her arms with renewed vigor. She continued further down the orchard, listening carefully to the sounds around her. Aside from the wind, and their own desperate breathing, there wasn’t another sound. She began to slow her pace down as she recognized the exit out of this orchard that was attempting to imprison them. “We’re out of here, son.” She whispered into her child’s ear. “The exit is over…” The monster lunged from the bushes and toppled the woman and her son down to the ground. There was no noise. No warning. The monster simply phased through the bushes and toppled them both. The woman grabbed a nearby stone and flung it at the monster. The stone simple incinerating in mid air as the monster let out a small growl. They were both trapped and the mother knew this. The child cuddled next to her, sobbing ferociously. She hugged her child tightly looking up at the monster that had stalked them so feverishly. It was humanoid in a way, but deprived of any features that were alive. Its skin appeared to be rotten and burnt. Its cold, dead eyes were so engraved in their sockets that it appeared as if he didn’t have any eyes. Its long fingers and razor-sharp claws hung at its side as it hunted them. With a small growl, it bared its teeth that seemed apt for ripping the flesh off a woman and her son. In a last ditch desperate effort the woman took a nearby branch and lunged at the monster hoping to protrude his skin. The monster grabbed her arm and threw her several feet across the orchard. The child wept louder calling for his mom. The monster turned to the child and picked up the frantic kid in one arm. It then turned to face the mother and smiled the evilest of all smiles. The woman was paralyzed with fear, unable to do anything. Without hesitation, the monster growled and gored the small child with a powerful punch, stabbing him in the abdomen with his claws. The sound of his claws tearing through the flesh was sickening, and the screams of the child were deafening as his legs frantically kicked, and slowly began to die, as his life faded from his body. The woman could not scream in terror. Finally, she cried out. “No!” she cried out, sobbing uncontrollably. Scene 2 “Sebastian!” Maria cried out springing up in her bed. Her heart was racing and she was in cold sweat. She looked over at the alarm clock. It read 3:15 am. It was just another nightmare. No, not just any nightmare, she thought as she laid herself back to sleep. It was the same dream she had since she was first pregnant with Sebastian. At the time, she had feared that she would lose Sebastian before he was even born. Sebastian was born, but not without complications and had to remain at the hospital for several more days. Since then, the nightmare seemed to occur more and more, with each time being more horrifying than the last. Maria lay on her side, unable to go back to sleep and stared blankly across the room. This time the nightmare was too horrifying. The monster had never succeeded in catching either of them. This time it caught her son and killed him. She was hopeless to save her son, and now she was scared. She clutched the pillow in her arms like a giant teddy bear and talked herself into going back to sleep. She closed her eyes once again and began to slowly drift back into sleep. Everything was going to be better in the morning. Scene 3 “No vas ala escuela hoy?” Maria asked her son, standing at the door dressed for work. Sebastian shook his head saying he was not going to school. ”No, not today. I got the day off.” He replied, continuing the conversation in Spanish. “Are you sure?” she asked. “Don’t make me call the school.” “Mom, there’s no need for that.” Sebastian replied, sitting up in his bed. “Besides, Polytechnic’s policy doesn’t allow for that. You can call them, but they won’t tell you anything without my permission.” “All right, fine.” Maria said. “Then go out, it’s a big city here. We didn’t come here so you can sleep in your bed on your day off.” “Maybe later, Mom.” Sebastian said, a bit tiredly. “I’m not feeling too well right now.” “Since you’re staying home, make sure you clean the house.” She directed him. “I’ll be back from work around six.” Sebastian nodded and grabbed his I-Pod from his nightstand. “What is that?” Maria asked him, pointing towards the I-Pod. “It’s an I-Pod,” Sebastian answered, adjusting his headphones. “I got it the other day.” “How much was it?” Maria asked, her curiosity keeping her at the door. “About three hundred dollars…” “Three hundred dollars?” Maria asked in shock. “Where did you get that money?” Sebastian took his headphones off. “I got some money from wrestling.” “Sebastian…” His mother replied. “I thought we talked about that?” “We did, but…” “But nothing,” Maria cried out. “You know how I feel about that? What else did you get?” Sebastian let out a half smile of guilt and pointed to his dresser and closet. Maria looked over and so all the new brand name clothing he had bought, as well as some surround-sound stereo system equipment. She sighed. “I want you to take this all back.” “Why?” “Because I don’t like where you got the money for this.” She cried out. “I have to go to work but you have to promise me you’ll take this stuff back.” “But…” “Just do it.” Sebastian sighed and put his headphones back on as his mother left the room and then the apartment. Sebastian fluffed his pillow and lay back on his head while Molotov’s “Matate Tete” played in his head. Normally, he would hum along with the lyrics as he always did when he listened to a song he liked. Not this time, though. Sebastian lay in his bed trying to find a good excuse to not give everything back. There was also the manner of him fighting again. Michael Sloan had actually been honest with Sebastian. He had delivered on his promise and Sebastian made some money without jeopardizing his time or health. Sebastian was well aware of the kind of man Michael was and was looking over his shoulder. A man like Michael Sloan was not a man you would trust easily. He had suspected that Mr. Sloan had other agendas, a suspicion he still harbored, but dismissed it when nothing solid materialized. He paid Sebastian the money he was owed and offered him another spot. Scene 4 (Minutes after Uber Judge Vs Sebastian Moraco) The crowd began to die down behind him as Sebastian walked through the backstage area of the arena. Just minutes earlier, he had lost another match, this time to the reigning Infinite Gauntlet Champion, Uber Judge. The Uber Judge was an enigma. He didn’t give off an aura of impressions; his moves in the ring seemed scattered. Nonetheless, he was very resourceful and found a way to beat Sebastian just as he had found a way to beat his previous challengers. “Not a bad fight,” Michael said, with a bit of gleam in his eye. “Not a bad fight at all. Of course, we could do well without that result.” Sebastian sighed. He was too tired for any complaints, excuses, or anything of that sort. His body ached from his long fight with the Uber Judge and he sought relaxation. “Losing isn’t everything. But it does suck.” “No need to get yourself down,” Michael said, putting one shoulder around him as him Sebastian was his son. “Remember I told you that this was an endless opportunity? Well, the Prime Time Central staff has informed me that they will be offering an open invitation royal rumble for the next event.” “And you want me to be in it?” “Oh don’t be so damned blunt,” Michael replied. “It is entirely up to you. But I do have to admit that you continued to impress them and they would like to see you there. Of course, it is up to you and I will be upping the stakes to fifty thousand dollars this time around.” Sebastian stopped. “Fifty thousand dollars?” Michael nodded. Money had such a powerful language. “I couldn’t beat one man,” Sebastian said, running his fingers through his hair. “How am I supposed to beat multiple men?” “That’s up to you,” Michael replied. “You nearly gave the Uber Judge a corollary. That was closer to beating him than any of his opponents have done before. Anyway, that’s going to be up to you to find a way.” “I’m not sure I can do that.” Sebastian said. Michael shook his head. “You can. There is more to this than the money, though.” “What?” Michael smiled. “You remember GTT4, don’t you?” Sebastian nodded. How could he forget? He had made it all the way to the bracket finals before succumbing to the juggernaut that was Doctor Curiosity. “Well, the winner of this match gets a match with the Uber Judge. Or in your case, a rematch. The winner of that match gets a confirmed spot in the next annual GTT tournament, GTT5, to be held in the Fall. That is going to be a huge deal. You know you want it.” “GTT5?” Sebastian asked, not believing it. “That’s pretty big. That’s like…jackpot big.” “It is.” Micheal said, agreeing with him. “Though you first have to win this event. So, do you think you can do it?” Sebastian nodded. “I’ll do it. I’ll beat all those fucking fighters and get my rematch with the Uber Judge. I’m going to show him that his victory was a fluke.” “I’ll be straightforward with you, Sebastian.” Michael said. “This time it won’t be such a sweet deal. You only get the money if you win. If you lose, I might be willing to part with some consolation prize. But why? Losing sucks.” “Losing sucks,” Sebastian said in unison. “Now come on,” Michael said. “Time for you to get out of here. What do you say?” Sebastian walked on ahead of Michael. “I say it’s time for me to go home. I’m fucking tired and all I want to do is sleep.” Scene 5 Maria slothfully walked into the apartment from another long, hot tiring day at the factory. Her hair was all fizzled up that it looked like a tangled spider web. Her skin wasn’t as smooth as it used to be and she kicked her shoes towards the corner entrance, keeping up her family customs. She set her purse on the table by the kitchen, which was actually part of the room, only separated by the discoloration of the ceramic tiles. She reached into the fridge and took out an Evian water bottle and began to gulp down the water like some kid drinking the juice out of the carton. She stopped from quenching her thirst and looked at the apartment. The floor was cleaned, waxed actually. The walls were wiped down. The sofas seemed to be wiped down as well. The windows were clean. Everything was put away in its place and every place had been wiped down. It looked like Sebastian had done what he was told, and did it to perfection. Maria Moraco walked into the bathroom. Sebastian had even done the bathroom. Before the bathroom was even unruly enough for the roaches. But now it was clean enough to be worth the high rent they were paying, barely. Maria walked back to the diner and sat down. She clicked on the television and turned it to Univision for the nightly news. With remote control in hand, a water bottle in the other, she looked at peace…if only that peace wasn’t derived from exhaustion. With a loud thud Sebastian dropped a bundle of money on the table before his mother. He then greeted his mom with a kiss on the cheek. “How was work?” “Fine, sweetie.” Maria answered. “Where did this money come from?” “That’s the money I got from wrestling the other day,” Sebastian replied, taking a seat. “Actually, it’s what’s left of it. I used some of it to buy some stuff.” Maria picked up the money. “How much money is this?” she asked. “A little over three thousand.” “Sebastian…” Maria began. “Look, mom.” Sebastian answered back, hoping to plead his case. “I know you don’t like me fighting. But I swear to you I won’t ever get hurt. Nothing is going to happen to me. You have to believe me that I did not want to do this.” “Then why did you?” “Because I didn’t want you to have to live this life all the time. Look at this place, mom.” Sebastian said, pointing out the apartment like some sort of game show host. “It’s so run down the rats refuse to live here. It’s so damn hot in this apartment without any AC. You come home tired from work every day, so much that you are about to die. I don’t want this kind of life for you.” Maria grabbed his hand. She knew he meant well. “I know that Sebastian,” she said. “Things haven’t been exactly better since I divorced your father. In fact, they’ve gotten worst. We are barely paying the rent. Sometimes I wonder if I made the right decision.” Sebastian quickly snapped his hand back. “Don’t ever say that!” Sebastian snapped back. “That man did not deserve to be by your side. He fucking hit me. He fucking hit you. How the hell can you even begin to give him a benefit of a doubt? He was a fucking waste of shit!” “Sebastian, calm down!” “No!” Sebastian said. “He fucking hurt you. I don’t want you ever giving him a second chance.” “I know…” “Look, mom…” Sebastian said, picking up the bundle of money. “Things don’t always have to be this way. I can make them better. I promise you, I’m going to get us out of this life.” “With wrestling?” “Yes.” Sebastian answered. “Not all the time, though. I promise I won’t drop out of class and go join some touring company and be away from home all the time. I promise I’ll still be in school, but I’ll be fighting every once in a while. It’s just so we can get a head start.” Maria sighed. She was about to give up. It was hard for her to deny her son when he meant well. “I don’t see why you just can’t get a good, honest job.” Maria said. “Because they don’t pay fifty thousand a night.” “What?” Maria asked. “It isn’t a guarantee,” Sebastian said, explaining the situation to her. “But if I go fight in the next event, against a handful of people, I get fifty thousand dollars.” “You sure you want to do that?” “Of course I am, mom.” Sebastian said. “That money can really help you out. That’s what this money is for. It’s for you. It’s for the rent. I don’t really want anything. I just want you to get out of this lifestyle.” “I do too, son.” “So…” Sebastian said. “Does this mean you’re ok with me wrestling?” “No.” Maria answered. “But it does mean that I am going to stop you, or deny you. I don’t want to see you fight and get hurt. But I know you mean well and this is what you want to do. I know your father pushed and pushed you to become an architect, and I was helpless to stop him and the others. But now we both have a chance to redeem that. If this is what you want, do it. I just want one thing from you.” “What is that?” “Try your best,” Maria replied, with a smile. “No son of mine is going to go and lose matches.” Sebastian laughed at the irony of her statement. If only she had known just how much he had already lost. It didn’t matter. Sebastian was not going to tell her of his close calls. As far as he was concerned, this was a new beginning. He didn’t have the weighted conscious of lying to his mother anymore to hold him down. Now he was free to do as he pleased. “Thanks, mom.” Sebastian answered giving his mother a hug. “You have no idea how much it means to me having you back me up.” “I’m going to the restaurant to get some take out, mom.” Sebastian said, heading for the door. “You look too tired to cook.” “Be careful.” Sebastian nodded and walked out the apartment. As he walked out of the building and headed down to the local Spanish restaurant, he began to feel a bit more content than he had felt before. The sun was beginning to settle down in the heart of Manhattan, and the sky turned a mixture of yellow and orange, a sort of orange hue. Sebastian turned back towards the skyscrapers that decorated the landscape behind him like mountains in an open field, and saw the sun start to set behind those city mountains. Sebastian breathed in the moment. “I’m going to win, now.” Sebastian muttered to himself, feeling more confident than ever before. “Uber Judge got a close win, but next time he won’t be so lucky. I’m going to beat all those challengers and get my shot at the Uber Judge again. The next time, I won’t lose.” Scene 6 Maria was lying down on the couch watching the nightly news when she heard a knock at the door. At first she dismissed it. But then the knock became more and more persistent. Finally, she got up from the couch and walked towards the door. “Did you forget your keys again?” Maria asked opening the door. “Hello, honey…” Miguel said. Sebastian’s father was standing on the other side. “It’s been too long since we’ve seen each other. Too long.”
