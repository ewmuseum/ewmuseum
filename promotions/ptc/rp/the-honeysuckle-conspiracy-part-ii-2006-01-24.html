The Honeysuckle Conspiracy: Part II

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 They scattered like so many grains of sand, tumbling in a soundless journey through time and space as Rich Rollins observed it all through a window of detached curiosity. His shoulders tickled. He reached up, running his hand along the contours of his own uneven scalp as if it belonged to somebody else. The muscles in his stomach suddenly tightened as his hand jerked away, his throat clenching to keep from screaming as he studied his fingertips in denial. Blood. He tried to look in every direction at once, finally recognizing these strange sensations for what they truly were. The hair was thick, sticking to the clammy surface of his skin like flypaper. His hair. Odd shapes invaded his privacy then, shadows of men surrounding him, suffocating him. Taunting him. Snipping at him with scissors the size of butcher knives, nicking his naked scalp without any heed to his miserable blubbering protests. Jonathon Winters cackled as he turned to Paddy O’Shea, using Rollins’ hair to fashion himself a mock moustache. Jayce Bradley wrapped a few remaining strands around his fist, yanking them from Rollins’ skull like wayward threads in an old woman’s crochet hoop. Unable to defend himself, the Renegade suddenly realized what Samson must have felt like as the Philistines rendered him powerless with the snip of a blade. His hair was his strength, and without that strength he was lost. "Aye don’t s’pose you’ll be a-needin’ this no more," said O’Shea as he lit a match against a corner of Rollins’ bandanna, dangling it in front of his nose until the growing flames began to lick at the Renegade’s lips. Jonathon Winters produced the familiar faceplate of Rollins’ Global title. "Or this for that matter," he sneered as he broke the belt over one knee. Jayce Bradley draped a necklace of decaying flowers around his neck; the honeysuckle smelled like rotting fish. "Why? Why?" Even though he knew this was a dream, Rich Rollins had no real way of waking up. When a fourth face appeared behind the tormenting trio he wished to Christ that somebody would slap him until he did. It was a face Rich Rollins could never forget. The man who had tried to put a bullet in his brain just before his round three bout against the chimpanzee materialized out of the nether, holding in his hand the 2005 PTC Wrestler of the Year statuette that Rollins had "accepted" on behalf of Angelo Deville. The other men parted as Frank Jacobs approached, his eyes dull and lifeless. "Scream for me," he whispered. Turning the statuette on its side Jacobs plunged the golden trophy into Rollins’ heart, snapping bone and piercing muscle with a single vengeful twist. ~<>~<>~<>~ Rollins woke himself with a shout that nearly gave the woman sleeping by his side a heart attack, jerking out of his dream and straight into the headboard. Loosing a string of curses more colorful than a Christmas tree he rolled out of bed, his relief at finding his hair unharmed cut short by a throbbing goose egg. "I take it you didn’t sleep well," his therapist’s wife offered with a yawn, her deadpan sarcasm apparently not impeded by the unwelcome wake-up call. "I was in the middle of being seduced by Tom Cruise, so thank you very much for that." Rollins placed his head in his hands as he sat on the edge of the bed. "Goddamnit." "It wasn’t that big a deal." Cynthia O’Doyle crawled over to meet him, draping a tender arm over his shoulder. "You still have a bigger co-…" "No, it’s not that." Rubbing the bridge of his nose between index and forefinger the Renegade released a heavy sigh. "It was just a nightmare, Cynthia. Go back to sleep." Go back to sleep. As if that were even possible. Still, Cynthia bit her lower lip as the distant tone in Rollins’ voice put a crease in her brow. "Something is wrong, isn’t it? You look more depressed than usual." "What the hell is that supposed to mean?" He had no intention of turning the situation into a three o’clock argument, but the comment struck a deep chord. "I’ve got every green thumb jackass in my profession wanting to beat my brains in, not to mention the other assholes who feel the need to shoot at me in the middle of a crowded arena. Excuse me if I don’t seem too fucking bright and chipper." "Oh Christ Rich, lighten up." Cynthia pulled herself away, burying her head underneath a pillow. "Wake me up when you grow a sense of humor." All was silent for about ten seconds as Rich Rollins sat there, alone and angry, staring at the woman who had more skill in crawling into his head than her head peeper of a husband. Finally he stood, glaring at the wall. "Fuck this." Pulling on a pair of jeans and an old Black Sabbath tee-shirt he made his way to the bedroom door. Just as he was about to leave a glint on the top of his dresser caught his eye. Watching him from its little perch, the 2005 PTC Wrestler of the Year statuette practically radiated a condescending sneer, reflecting his own inner resentment right back at him. He snatched the statuette before finally hitting the door, slamming it closed as an intentional exclamation point just to piss the woman off. ~<>~<>~<>~ Resthaven Cemetery, Chicago… The only thing more frigid than the wind at Frank Jacobs’ back was the heart that beat within his chest. A month full of lonely nights spent agonizing over the untimely death of his only daughter had long since begun to take shape across the bow of his tortured visage, forming an unkempt beard as gray as the winter weather along the nape of his neck and face. The hard lines under his eyes more than suggested that his legally appointed grief counseling sessions had been less than therapeutic; Frank had removed himself from that morbidly depressing pity party by the end of the first week. By the end of the second week he had completely given up on his own recovery. By the end of the third week he had completely given up on God. After all he was a Jacobs, the laughing stock of the Almighty and a modern day Job, the whipping boy to the whims of his Creator. Unfortunately he didn’t possess the same forgiveness of his predecessor. The headstones whispered their silent stories through the countless cracks that marred their aging marble as Frank strolled along the rows, pulling his coat tight against the wind. Not even the usual bustle of the city seemed able to permeate Resthaven’s hallowed perimeter. Only the sound of his shovel scraping the ground as he dragged it across the graves could be heard as the last of the Jacobs lineage cut a long and lugubrious line through the mounds of dead dirt. Caught in the snare of his own overwhelming misery, Frank Jacobs had finally resolved to make peace with his hatred the only way he knew how. His first stop was a modest headstone carved of gray granite, embossed with a white angel sitting in a weeping willow. Frank knelt, dropping his shovel as he reached into his coat to procure a small poster; displaying a larger-than-life Rich "The Renegade" Rollins, the GTT5 promo picture was personalized by the man who adorned it: To Jessica, the best fan a Renegade could ever have. Yours truly, -Rich "The Renegade" Rollins Frank had gotten it autographed during the previous round of the tournament, just before he tried to blow Rollins’ brains into next year with a .38 Special. After his daughter was taken off of life support, he figured the least he could have done was get her killer’s apology on paper before shooting him. She had died due to complications a week after the car accident, and he watched her suffer. He would make sure that Rollins felt that same agony and despair long before Frank sent him to his Maker. Placing the poster on his daughter’s grave, Frank Jacobs kissed the angel as he hefted the shovel once more. His journey through the dead was easy in the dark. Nobody was around to watch him work, not even a caretaker, even though Frank kept looking over his shoulder in case he was discovered. Men of midnight imagination rarely felt the need for heed or caution, but Frank’s paranoia kept him on his toes as he searched tirelessly for his quarry. There, the name he needed to see finally materialized on the marble of another generic headstone: Margaret Amanda Rollins 1954-2003 And just below that, written by hand in red marker: Return to Sender Rich Rollins’ mother. Fueled by heartache and an insatiable desire to send the Renegade a message he would never forget, Frank Jacobs began to dig. ~<>~<>~<>~ P.W.N. Pawn and Jewelry, south Chicago… It was the only pawn shop open at four o’clock in the morning, and the dismal display of broken radios and chipped musical instruments made it clear it was for a reason. A tiny bell signaled Rich Rollins’ entrance as he pushed through the door and a fat man with mutton chops the size of porterhouse steaks bid him welcome from behind bulletproof glass. "What can I do you for, stranger?" The place reeked of molded cheese, but Rollins wasn’t sure if it was the building or the pawnbroker. "I see y’got yourself an award of some kind, huh? Shit, is that real gold?" "Fuck if I know," said Rollins as he approached the enclosed counter. His contempt made it easy to hand the award that wasn’t his through a small window in the glass, letting the fat pawnbroker paw the statuette’s smooth exterior. The man fumbled with it for a minute before remarking, "It’s got the weight all right. ‘Bout seven pounds of gold in this frame. How much you lookin’ to get from it?" "I’m not selling it," Rollins replied. "I want you to melt it down for me, if that’s possible." The pawnbroker perked an eyebrow as he studied the Renegade with caustic speculation. "I don’t suppose you’re Mr. Deville?" Rollins laughed. "Not in this lifetime, pal. You interested?" The fat man mulled it over as he passed the statuette from hand to hand. Finally he nodded. "Yeah, I can melt it for you. You got a plan for what I should recast it as, or you want me to surprise you?" He flashed a yellow smile that made Rollins’ stomach churn. Leaning up against the glass, Rollins nodded. "I want two pairs of brass knuckles, preferably with an engraving." "You want your name on ‘em or something?" asked the pawnbroker. "No." Rollins shook his head as he looked up at a banner pulled across the wall behind the glass: Paul, Winston & Nathan’s (PWN) Pawn and Jewelry: We put the PWN in Pawn! It was a sign. Literally. The grin that spread across the Renegade’s face was contagious. "I want your store’s initials engraved on the knuckle. Reverse the letters too, like a cattle brand." "Can do." The man’s morbid obesity actually jiggled as the pawnbroker set the statuette on his side of the counter. "Have ‘em ready for you in about five business days. Pleasure doing business with you, stranger." The pleasure was all his, Rollins was sure. His ear-to-ear smile was smeared on the Renegade’s face all the way back to his ramshackle home of a warehouse. It was turning out to be a good day, after all. ~Fin~
