Prologue: A Journey Into Darkness

14 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-14 As the sun slowly descended below the Tokyo skyline, brilliant fluorescent lights began flickering to life all across the modern metropolis. The thin rays of sunlight still creeping through the towering skyscrapers cast long shadows, but dusk brought a golden brilliance to the more open places of the city. But deep in the claustrophobic recesses of the urban jungle laid forbidding alcoves of darkness, penetrated only by the occasional iridescent billboard or the headlights of passing traffic. Still early in the evening, the tourists were out en force, filling out the shoulder-to-shoulder mob ebbing and flowing through the more friendly sections of town. Usually Kurokame avoided the more populous sections of the city, despising the occasional run-in with a tourist who actually knew who he was. But today, he longed for the relative secrecy he would keep in the presence of those ignorant Americans and Europeans. Today, it was the locals he feared. For the past three years, Kurokame had lived comfortably in a luxury flat in a nice part of the city, complete with a spectacular skyline view and easy access to his training facilities. But his sponsors had driven him from his home in the wee hours of the previous night, having afforded him just enough time to collect what few personal belongings he had. Since then, Kurokame had been wandering the streets and back alleys of Tokyo, lost, disoriented, angry and desperate. The past twenty-four hours had been a blur. His only memory was spending his last few yen on a dark trenchcoat and fedora. He hadn't eaten, he hadn't slept; he had no place to go for shelter or friendship. His lone motivation of the day was anonymity, a difficult goal for a five-hundred pound social pariah. So it was without hesitation that he squandered his precious remaining cash on the coat and the hat, raised his collar and pulled the brim down over his face, and struck out into the city to wander like a sleepless phantom. Kurokame's head was throbbing. He wasn't feeling the after-effects of combat, but rather the blinding confusion of a series of events so confusing, so spectacularly unexpected, that he was finding them too fantastic to contemplate. He had spent part of the day wedged between a pair of dumpsters in a fetid back alley, hoping the seclusion and the smell might clear his head. But his mind hadn't stopped racing long enough for him to grasp the situation. Finally, as the sun began to set, Kurokame found himself feeling a bit more relaxed, desperately thankful for the shroud of darkness steadily falling over the city. Presently, Kurokame found himself wandering steadily away from the city center, down into the dicey depths of the outskirts. Turning a corner, Kurokame paused. Immediately he knew where he was. He was entering the "entertainment district" as some called it, but could have been more accurately described as the city's center for vice. For one seeking to drop a few thousand yen in an underground casino, or obtain a controlled substance, or even secure the services of a young lady for the evening, this was the place to do it. But, as with any such place, it was inherently dangerous. Though the police had done an admirable job of reducing the amount of violent street crime, these streets remained fully under the control of the Yakuza. After his moment of hesitation, Kurokame grew bolder. He first assured himself that, despite his humiliation the night before, he remained a fearsome presence, and surely had little to fear. Further, it occurred to him that the police might be looking for him, and the mean streets of Tokyo might have been the haven he had been searching for. Promptly lifting his right foot, Kurokame took his first step into the world of the Yakuza. At once, it was as if an enormous burden had been lifted from his massive shoulders. The air smelled of cheap booze and expensive women, and the streets were silent save the hum of the nondescript traffic and the subtle whisper of the street. Still peering timidly out from under the brim of his fedora, Kurokame's charcoal eyes scanned the sidewalks. Not three blocks back the city was teeming with life, and suddenly here it tapered off into another world. It was with a great sense of astonishment that Kurokame realized he wasn't being watched. Even in the presence of those that didn't recognize him, Kurokame had found it difficult to blend in. But here, those still out wandering the streets were content to maintain their privacy and pass through the night with a minimum of human contact. Here, the people passing by never made eye contact, keeping their attention focused squarely on their own two feet, or at the young woman contracted to keep them company for the evening. Here, the people minded their own business and went out of their way to avoid drawing attention to themselves. Raising his eyes skyward, Kurokame soaked in what might have been paradise. The buildings here were subtly lit, climbing into the night sky and fading into the darkness. As the sun descended, the rain clouds had rolled in, and a light drizzle began to fall. Kurokame began walking further down the street, passing by recessed doorways flanked by flashing neon Japanese characters. Overhead, a pair of incandescent legs danced through the air, as if suspended by some feat of magic. As the rain slowly began soaking the pavement, the entire street began to radiate with a sort of light Kurokame had never encountered before. It was a dull yet colorful light, a light that illuminated the street itself but did nothing to pierce the darkness in the air. It was as if the sidewalks were hidden beneath a permanent, impenetrable shroud, manufactured by its overseers to afford their clientele with safe and completely anonymous passage. Kurokame's felt himself approaching sensory overload as he shuffled down the street. The rain began to pick up, and Kurokame began scanning the buildings for somewhere to duck inside. The street was littered with strip joints and unsuspecting little clubs called "social clubs." His skin began to crawl as he weighed his options, having never really experienced such things before. Kurokame wasn't in the mood for the rowdiness or the debauchery of a skin show, nor the potential legal encounters he might find in one of the gambling halls. After another moment's hesitation, Kurokame grunted, trying to clear the uneasy feeling in his gut. Intellectually, he knew this was the perfect place to be. He had a feeling that he needed only to get his feet wet, and he'd find that he'd discovered the perfect sanctuary to escape from it all. Escape. That's exactly what he needed. The previous night's events had followed him all day, like a specter with no form, a shade with no color, always haunting his steps and forcing him to relive it over and over and over again. But he'd found a place where your identity was checked at the door, and you were free to become another person. Forcing himself to decide, Kurokame turns to his left and looks down at the subtly lit doorway before him. He almost hadn't seen it; it wasn't lit by some dazzlingly provocative billboard. Instead, it sat in a recessed section of a modest looking brick building, marked only by a single English word on its window: "Bar" Outside, an inconspicuous pair of bouncers, young Japanese boys with slicked back hair and leather jackets, were loitering about without much of a sense of purpose. It was perfect. Kurokame stepped away from the street, passing by a group of gregarious businessmen flanked by a gaggle of scantily clad hired ladies. As he reached for the handle on the door, the young boys gave him just enough of a glance to size him up. Kurokame snapped his collar back up to hide his face; he didn't have any reason to believe these young hoodlums would recognize him, or even be aware of what he'd done. But either way, he wasn't taking any chances. Pushing inside, he was shocked by what he saw. The place was packed. Immaculately dressed Japanese businessmen lined the bar, throwing down shots and comparing bankrolls. Beautiful yet suspiciously young Japanese girls weaved through the traffic, holding trays of cold beer and warm sake, faithfully enduring the drunken come-ons and slaps on the rear. A dull jazz beat bumped in the background, mixing with the cackles of the patrons to form a symphony of indecipherable racket. The smell of booze seemed less intoxicating than the smell of the women, who wore irresistible Japanese herbal perfumes that never failed to produce a few extra bucks at the end of the night. But most surprising at all to Kurokame was the lighting in the room. The entire bar, despite the jovial customers and nonstop commotion, was shrouded in that same sort of impenetrable shadow as the street. The bright spotlights overhead lit just enough to navigate the room, but afforded the patrons a modicum of privacy in an otherwise communal environment. Instantly, Kurokame scanned the room. For the past four years, he was accustomed to being the center of attention the moment he entered a room. His sponsors and their friends would stand and greet him at the door, always anxious for a photo op. Young women would tend to his every need, and awestruck fans would drop to their knees when he passed. For four years he had earned his fame as the only sumo in Japan to have never been thrown from his feet. He had been so dominant that he his title of yokozuna had gone unchallenged for six months. Unchallenged until last night… But no one rushed to his side here, there were no flashing cameras or pathetic fans. Actually managing a thin smile for the first time in twenty four hours, Kurokame glides to the bar and takes an open seat at the end, keeping his distance from the rest of the pack. Almost instantly, the bartender is on him. She stood out from the rest; a young white girl, probably American judging from her clothes. "What shall I serve you, sir?" Kurokame was barely able to hear in her broken Japanese. He winced. If you're not going to pronounce it right, don't bother. "I speak English very well," Kurokame muttered back. His deep, gravelly voice took her off guard, even as he kept his eyes trained on the bar, his fedora covering his features. "Give me the strongest drink you have." Nodding, the girl slowly turned and placed a small glass before him. She then expertly popped the top off a bottle and began to pour. She filled his glass halfway and turned, but Kurokame cut her off. "Leave the bottle." With a shrug she sets the bottle down beside him, and Kurokame lifted the glass to his lips. The first sip was perfect; it stung his palate and turned his stomach upon impact. Almost instantly, Kurokame felt a wave of euphoria envelop him. She couldn't speak Japanese worth a damn, but she certainly knew her alcohol. At five hundred pounds, it would take all night for Kurokame to get himself fully liquored up, but that first sip had gone a long way to soothing his nerves. Kurokame removed his hat and brushed back his long jet black hair. Looking up, he realized the young American girl was still standing in front of him, and for a moment their eyes met. She showed no fear or intimidation, but rather pity. His black, uncompromising stare still somehow conveyed the unimaginable pain he was suffering, and the young girl was trained to see such things. "You look like you've had a rough day," she says with an unflinching gaze. Kurokame wasn't taking the bait. Another quick swig and he downed the first drink, washing away his fears for the moment. Glancing around, he saw several drunken, failing businessmen, wedged between the successful ones, bearing their souls to their bartender. Kurokame scoffed at their weakness; no, he wouldn't become one of them. He may have lost his money, his honor and his life, but he was still Kurokame, and he still had his dignity. "You are American?" he asks, lowering his face to the bar again. "Yeah, or at least I used to be," she answers very matter-of-factly. "I've been over here for a few years now. Been tending bar here for about six months, I guess." After only a moment, Kurokame's chest tightened. He found her inane babbling to be infuriating, and but for his hair falling over his face, she might have seen his sneer. Kurokame had always disdained uneducated, ignorant Americans, and this one appeared to fit the profile perfectly. Her transparent attempt at sympathy gave rise to the impulse to strike her down, to put and abrupt and violent end to the conversation. But, cringing, Kurokame quelled the storm inside him, gritting his teeth as he humored her. "And what brings you to this part of the city?" Kurokame grumbled impatiently through clenched teeth. He shot an accusing glance at her from through his thin strands of dark hair cascading over his face, hoping his venomous stare would drive her away. But she answered without hesitation. "Oh, doll, everyone knows this is where the money's at. I do pretty well for myself," she stated proudly as she poured him another drink, this time filling it to the brim. Kurokame threw back the drink and drained it with one massive gulp. The young girl flinched at the sight, but responded to Kurokame's commanding grunt by filling his glass up again. "So come on, you gonna tell me what your deal is or what?" she asked pugnaciously. At once, a fire rose within him, his eyes burning red with fury. He felt his chest heave and his skin crawl, fueled by an unyielding wave of hellacious fury. Her trite attempt at conversation, her arrogance at thinking she could understand him, even her accent drove him mad. He felt himself overwhelmed by this blinding rage, consumed by his complete and utter contempt for this woman. Or maybe it was just the alcohol. Mashing his eyes closed, Kurokame tried to get a grip on himself. Why is this happening? was the only pithy thought he could manage. Had he totally lost power over his emotions? He felt as if he was de-evolving, reverting to some wild feral state, becoming less human and changing into some dreadful atrocity. He was spiraling out of control, and he only needed to sit down and think to see it happening. He had thrown his life away with one sweeping action, signed away his honor with a single burst of fury. The cool, collected Kurokame had died that night. Now he was being reborn as something else entirely. The girl was still staring at him, tenaciously hanging on as if desperate for the conversation. But the room had begun to spin around Kurokame, until the cacophony of boisterous laughter and subdued sobs drove him from his seat. He longed to wander the streets again, to move deeper into the mean streets until he found a place to match his own despair. Thrusting his hands into his trenchcoat pockets, Kurokame fished for his last wad of cash. Oh no. Kurokame’s sudden moment of realization must have given him away. The young American bartender subtly shifted her eyes to the door, where another pair of suited Yakuza began moving towards him. He’d spent his last bit of cash on the jacket, and he had almost drained the bottle. This was going to be bad. A sobering wave of panic straightened Kurokame’s sight, initiating his survival instincts. Get to the door, just get out of here. They weren’t going to shoot him over this, at least he didn’t think so. But he had desperately wanted to avoid drawing attention to himself, and this could very well lead to a confrontation with the police. Cornered, he began scanning the room for alternatives. They’re still fifty paces away, you have time. THINK. There. A side door. Don’t run, just walk away. Kurokame took one step back, before the cocking of a gun stopped him short. His blood ran cold. How far had he fallen in a day? He was once the pride of the nation, once one of the most honored athletes and artists in the land. Now, he stood among the scum of the city, moments away from being cut down by some wretched street predator. The gun pressed harder against his back, urging him forward. Keeping his arms at his side and moving slowly, Kurokame walked towards the two young Yakuza before him. The motioned subtly for him to follow, and with the gun still chilling his soul, they headed for the back of the bar. Wading through exuberant Japanese men and their escorts who paid no heed to the potentially violent situation brewing before their eyes, they finally arrived at a subtle door in the back wall, flanked by large mirrors on either side. One of the Yakuza men opened the door, and the gun ushered him inside. Reluctantly, Kurokame stepped into the room. The gun followed him in, but the Yakuza guards remained outside. The door slammed shut with an unnerving sense of finality, sealing them in. Kurokame glanced about apprehensively, waiting for his eyes to adjust to the dim light. Finally, a match flares to life, and a withered hand lowers it to a nearby candle. The smell of sulfur fills Kurokame’s nostrils, somehow igniting his senses and reawakening his sense of confidence. Don’t panic. You are still the yokozuna. The withered hand finishes lighting a series of candles resting on a small desk, and Kurokame catches a fleeting glimpse of a white goatee as the match is extinguished. The flames burn calmly, casting a mellow golden light over the desk as an unremarkable silhouette takes a seat behind the desk. "I am told you came to my establishment tonight with no money," comes the aged voice from the darkness, from a face still hidden in the shadows. It was a voice that sounded old and worn, spoken with precision from a weak old man, yet still commanding what could only be described as fear in the five hundred pound monster before him. Kurokame didn’t know what to say. "I… I was…" is all he could manage before abruptly being cut off. "You were foolish, my young friend," comes the sharp, cutting Japanese again. "Surely you know who operates this establishment." Kurokame knew where this was going. At once, he collected himself, feeling bolder now that he’d shaken the initial wave of anxiety. He hadn’t seen whoever was holding the gun to his back, but he was certain they could be dispatched easily. He knew little about guns, but it didn’t feel big enough to put him down in one shot. And the old man surely would be no challenge… "If you think you are leaving here, Kurokame-san, without word from me first, you are gravely mistaken," the voice stated in a more subdued, somber tone. This is it, Kurokame thought, He knows who I am. "May I ask you a question, Kurokame-san?" the grizzled voice stated in a manor that made it clear he wouldn’t be waiting for permission. "Why did you do what you did? Why have you disgraced yourself and your family with your… appalling display of contempt for your art?" Kurokame remained cold as ice, his jet black eyes unyielding. "I came here seeking that very answer," came his terse reply. The old man behind the desk was silent for a moment. He seemed to relax, but the gun remained firmly planted in Kurokame’s spine. A cuffed sleeve reached forward into the light, plucking a small wooden pipe from the desk. Another match erupted in the darkness, lighting the pipe and the man’s face for a heartbeat. But the man extinguished the flame, and a puff of aromatic tobacco wafted through the candlelight. "I have the answers you seek, if you would like me to tell you." Kurokame didn’t quite know how to react. He had anticipated a violent warning to leave and never come back; and in truth, Kurokame had feared losing his faceless paradise more than the physical repercussions. But this conversation had taken a rather unexpected turn. His statement meeting silence, the old man pressed on. "I excel in trafficking some of the most valuable commodities in this city. But none are as valuable as information, perhaps the most important thing to have in my business. And, when someone of your stature does something so noteworthy, it does not take long for me to obtain all the relevant facts. I also happen to have first-hand knowledge of the event itself, because I myself was in attendance." Forcing back a cringe, Kurokame inhaled deeply. He knew very little of the Yakuza, other than their strong ties to tradition. Sumo was a religion to some in Japan, and he feared what ramifications his heretical actions might have. "You are guilty of a unforgivable transgression," hissed the old voice, venomous and forceful. "You dishonored yourself with that vulgar act of violence, with your obvious inability to control your primal passions. Now you are banished from the civilized world, you are disgraced and your family humiliated. To speak your name will be blasphemy, your legacy will forever be tainted with the depravity of your actions. You are seeking escape, you are seeking acceptance. Who out there will take you in, who in the civilized world will sympathize with a five-hundred pound animal?" Every word brought Kurokame’s muscles to a higher state of readiness, until the old man’s voice peaked with a bristling intensity. Finally, Kurokame acted. Spinning to his left with speed that defied his size, Kurokame swatted the gun from a terrified young man’s hands. With three quick sumo palm-thrusts, he sent the young man to the ground in a heap. Spinning back to his feet in his ready stance, he found the old man to be sitting behind his desk, still puffing on his pipe, unconcerned. Suddenly realizing that he was gasping for breath, Kurokame did his best to calm his heart, even as he stayed at the ready for further violence. "You are the most perfect athlete in the world, Kurokame-san," came the voice with a disconcerting tone of kindness. Taken aback by the sudden change in tenor, Kurokame closed his eyes, trying to shake off the sudden feeling of weakness he had. But the old man’s voice continued, so suddenly warm and seductive that he found himself hypnotized by its benevolence. "You have surpassed your competition, you have evolved into something greater than they could imagine. You are no longer mortal, Kurokame. You have Samurai blood, you have the soul of a legendary warrior of old. Out there, they cannot begin to contemplate your greatness. I have watched you, my son, for so long, I have seen the pain you suffer. I have seen your soul desperate to break free from the restraints of their world and achieve the greatness you’ve always known you’re destined to achieve." Opening his eyes, the room suddenly seemed brighter. The old man’s face was in full view now, wrinkled slightly and covered in a graying beard. But the face was proud and strong, radiating wisdom and understanding. Kurokame opened his mouth to speak, but found himself unable to produce the words. The old man continued. "It was your destiny, Kurokame-san, to throw aside the shackles of their world. It was the same destiny that brings you now to me, here in my world. Stay with us. Here you will never pay for drinks, you will have all that you desire. Here, your Samurai spirit will be embraced, not rejected. I can teach you to feed on that which others find terrifying. Leave the sumo behind, leave your name behind, leave yourself behind. Be born anew, with the Yakuza your new family, and me your new father." The man stood, circling around his desk to Kurokame’s side. He rose only to Kurokame’s shoulders, yet seemed to tower over him. "There once was a great samurai, an ancestor of my family. This samurai’s name was Kimbusa, and he was known throughout the land as the finest swordsman to have ever lived. He cut down his lord’s enemies with ease, he slaughtered all who stood against him. There are many great tales of Kimbusa’s heroism and honor, but none greater than the tale of his… disappearance. One day, Kimbusa took his sword and his armor and cast them aside, leaving through the sea, never to be seen from again. His lords fell in his absence, and the people mourned the loss of Kimbusa’s great talents. But it was foretold that Kimbusa would return one day, to take up his sword and fight for his lord once more, to help him win back his kingdom." The old man stood erect, his voice no longer withered but booming with greatness. "Take up your sword, Kimbusa. Fight the great battle once more." Closing his eyes, Kurokame felt something change within him. All the doubt, all the fear, all the apprehension washed away, and suddenly he felt the greatness within him. He felt as if some part of him, dormant for all his life, had been called to life, and was taking hold of him. Slowly, Kimbusa opened his eyes for the first time. "What would you have me do, father?"
