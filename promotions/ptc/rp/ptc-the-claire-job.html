IG 64 -- The Claire Job

27 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Fuckin' Sox, man." "Fuckin' Sox." The two men sat moodily in the front seat of a 1989 Oldsmobile sedan, coasting slowly down the suburban side streets of Los Angeles. Bobby di Nonno and Alfonso Beretti were a study in contrasts. Alfonso drove (he always drove), his left hand atop the wheel, a heavy silver class ring gleaming in the setting sun. His salt and pepper hairline was receding, as if it retreated from his too-wide face. Thick brown sunglasses shielded his limpid blue eyes, protecting them from the growing furrows his perpetual eyebrows-up expression was carving into his forehead. Beretti was old school in both attire and demeanor, the first two buttons of his silk shirt hanging open to reveal a small cross necklace and a patch of wiry, slate gray chest hair. "I swear, I've give my left nut to get one shot at that Ortiz fucker," Alfonso grumbled. "Give me his fuckin' corked-up bat and I'll rack him a good one. Bam, right in the dick." "Fuck that." Di Nonno tended to punctuate his words with subtle hand motions. "You give me a baseball bat an' five minutes with Dave Ortiz an' I promise you that fat fuck won't hit no more homers." Bobby was a head taller than Alfonso, but lankier – like he was stretched out before cooking. He wore no jewelry, just simple clothes and a thick-lipped frown. In truth, the only thing the two men shared was a deep, abiding love for the New York Yankees that had somehow survived their exodus from the Big Apple. This, naturally, came with hatred beyond words for the Boston Red Sox, and they spent more time complaining about them than almost anything else. "I tellya, these guys..." Alfonso sighed, more to fill the silence than out of anything noteworthy to say. "Where's this girl's place at again?" "Around the corner, first one on the left." Bobby leaned back in his seat, pulling a beaten pack of cigarettes from his pocket. "You should see this girl, man." One cigarette came free of its mates, dangling from his lips as he fished for his lighter. "I think this might be the one." The Olds had quite the turning radius, and her brakes squeaked as it drifted to a halt. "You say that about every girl, man." Alfonso threw the car into park, reaching behind into the back seat. "Just don't tire yourself out so bad you can't come in tomorrow. I ain't doin' this alone." The elder man tossed a black duffel bag onto his younger compatriot's lap, but Bobby didn't even flinch at the weight, shrugging the thing onto his shoulder as he climbed from the car. "You're jealous because even your hand ain't putting out no more." "He's a comedian, this guy." Alfonso raised a hand. "Take care, man." The sedan pulled away from the curve, Beretti's thin smile fading as he shook his head. His home wasn't far from Bobby's girl's, and it was a peaceful drive that allowed him to take out his brain, at least for a little while. Or at least that was the plan. Alfonso knew he had a bad habit when he drove; the cops called it a "California stop," and his inability of his beloved Oldsmobile to actually halt at stop signs earned him more tickets over the years than any other offense. He thought – honestly thought – that he'd stopped at that corner, but maybe he was wrong. The clatter of the bicycle and the surprised yelp from its rider certainly seemed to indicate otherwise. "Jesus fuck!" he hollered, jerking the car into park. Gold chains clattered together as he clambered out of the car. "Are you all right?" The girl was young – he didn't figure she could be more than fifteen or sixteen from the look of her – but she was gutsy, pulling herself up to her feet. Alfonso looked her over (not like a perv, though he'd be hard-pressed to deny she was a striking girl), but there was just a little blood at her lips, and no visible signs of injury. The bike was a different story, warped in the middle from its impact with the car's fender, and it was that damage that held the girl's attention as she answered distractedly. "I think... I think I'm fine." She looked up, finding a small twist of a smile for him. "You should really watch where you're going, though." "Yeah, sorry about that. It's been a shit week at work." Beretti stepped closer, dress shoes crunching on the asphalt. "I mean, it's been a, uh, a bad week." "It's okay," the girl said. "Not like the kids at work don't say worse." She stood the bike up, and frowned as it wobbled slightly. "Dammit, I just bought this bike. My dad is going to kill me." "Here," Alfonso patted his pockets, frowning. "Let me get you some cash, get you a new bike. 'Cept I think my wallet's in the—" He'd just turned back to the car when the girl must have taken a wrong step, lurching at him. It was instinct alone that allowed him to catch her. "Ah, fuck, my knee," she hissed behind clenched teeth, clutching his arm. "You oughta go to the doctor." His frown deepened, and he felt vaguely like an overly stern father in saying it. The girl hobbled up to a standing position. "I'm... I'm good. And you don't have to give me anything. I was listening to my iPod too loudly, and I should've been paying attention." "Nah, man. I fu—er. I messed up your bike pretty bad." He guided the girl to the car she'd so recently hit. "Sit here for a minute an' rest." Beretti meandered to the car's door, pulling it open. He put half of his barrel-like body inside, thick fingers scooping the faux-leather billfold from within. When he stood up, spine popping with the effort, the girl seemed to be staring off into space. "How much did that thing cost you, anyway? Like sixty bucks?" "Uhhh." She gathered her lower lip between her teeth. "A hundred and twenty, actually." Alfonso whistled softly. "Damn, shit is expensive these days." "Tell me about—" The phone at the girl's hip chimed, a delicate up-down sound. "Crap, sorry. Just a sec." She cupped the phone against her ear, stepping away. Beretti tried hard not to watch her, but those shorts were short, and no fifteen-year-old girl should have legs that long. When she turned back, Alfonso dropped his eyes to the dull gray pavement. "That was, uh..." The girl awkwardly jammed her phone back into the front pocket of those shorts. "That was my dad. I'd... I have to go." She half-turned away, mounting the bike. "I can probably fit your bike in the trunk." Alfonso wasn't sure why he'd said it, and it just turned to a flush of embarrassment as the girl raised two delicate brows, doubt and not a little fear crossing her eyes. "Uhm." She glanced down the street. "Nevermind," Beretti quickly put in. "Yeah," she said. "I live just... just up the street." "Here." Alfonso jogged up to her, pressing three folded fifty-dollar bills into her small hand. "Sorry again for not paying attention." "Sure." The girl (Alfonso never asked her name) pedaled off, the bike wobbling horribly, but he couldn't bring himself to say anything as he went back to his car. "I tellya, these kids..." he mumbled as he turned the key, continuing his drive home in silence. It was less than ten minutes later that the Oldsmobile pulled into the garage of Alfonso Beretti's suburban home, and the man himself hummed a soft, jaunty tune (something he'd heard on the radio, he figured, though he couldn't say what it was) as he slid out of the car, circling around the car. Not for the first time, Alfonso wished for keyless entry; one of those newfangled remote controls would make digging through way-too-many keys to find the one that opened the trunk. When he finally opened it, the sight within made him stagger backwards, brows wrinkling like caterpillars. "Jeez fuck!" A blonde in a powder blue blouse and blue jeans was bound and gagged in the back of his car, eyes wide and wild, and mascara streaking her face. Her hands were bound in front of her, feet wrapped together in bungee cord. A length of duct tape covered her lips, and still she squirmed away from him. "I ain't gonna hurt you," he said, raising both hands to show they're empty. "We'll get you out of this, but you gotta be quiet, yeah?" The woman nodded, and Beretti leaned into the trunk, picking at the gunmetal gray tape to peel it away from her cheek. "This is gonna hurt. Like, a lotta hurt. But you can't scream, catch me?" The woman's bright green eyes flitted madly about the sparse garage before she nodded again, this time a little reluctantly. Her brow wrinkled in mute agony as Beretti yanked the tape off in one tug, tears springing from the corners of her eyes. "That's a good girl," Alfonso murmured, unknotting her arms. "Now sit up and tell me who you are and how you got in my trunk." The girl whimpered as she rolled her shoulder. "My, uh..." A small pink tongue wet chapped lips, her jaw clicking. "I'm Jennie. Jennie Walker. Don't—please don't hurt me. I'll do whatever you want. Just... let me go." Beretti's eyes narrowed, gaze sharpening. "I ain't asked for you to do anything but tell me how you came to be in my car. Tell me that and maybe – just maybe – I call a cab and let you go." The words fell from the girl's lips in a rush. "I was walking home from Miss Claire's studio, just minding my own business, and... and..." The sound she made in the back of her throat was pitiable. "And your buddy came up from behind. He... he wrapped his hand around my mouth and jabbed his gu—" Fresh tears stained her cheeks. "He told me if I didn't get in the car, there'd be a new picture for my portfolio." "You're sayin' Bobby did this?" Beretti's brows drooped in consideration. "Nobody told me anything about snatching girls." "Maybe..." It looked like it took some effort for Jennie to dredge up a smile. "Maybe it was all just some misunderstanding. You should just let me go, like you said, and it can stay that way. I swear I won't tell anyone anything. Just... just let me go, man. Please." Alfonso's lips curled downward, mirroring his brows and giving him an overall hangdog expression. "I should call him. Maybe even call Mr. B. I know things with that photographer girl have gotten bad, but this—" "You said you'd let me go! You said!" She scooted forward, trying to lurch up out of the trunk, a feat made more difficult by the fact that her feet were still bound. Instead, she tilted her head back and hollered at the top of her lungs. "Help! Somebody help—" The wet smack of Alfonso's hand cracking across her cheek echoed hollowly in the enclosed space of the garage. He stormed forward, eyes wide and livid. "You don't do that, understand me?" He snatched the girl by the hair, producing another, softer yelp. "I may not like the idea of a kidnapped girl in my house, but until I sort this shit out, you best shut the fuck up, catch?" Jennie nodded a little, and this time, when he sent her back into the trunk, all she did was whimper. "Now," he said through clenched teeth. "You gonna come inside quiet-like, or do I gotta carry you?" A gobbet of spittle struck Beretti's cheek in answer. "LET ME G—AKH!" The rag he shoved into her mouth muffled her shout, but at least he'd tried to lead with the cleanest bit of cloth. Walker kicked ineffectually at Alfonso when he snatched her up, throwing her over his shoulder like a sack of potatoes. She bucked like a bronco as he carried her into the house, clutching tightly enough to the doorframe that Beretti had to yank her free. He flung her onto the sofa with enough force that she rebounded, head ricocheting off the armrest. As he stepped closer, Alfonso jabbed a stubby finger toward the woman. "Now. You sit right there, real calm and peaceable, and hopefully – just hopefully – we'll get you out of here real soon. It was almost as if the fight had gone out of Jennie as she drew herself upright. She demurely laid both hands on her lap, offering no objection as the man retied them together. He fixed her with a stern glare as he retreated to retrieve his cordless phone from its cradle on the kitchen counter. Quick fingers dialed a number he knew by heart, his frown never flickering, as if it had been chiseled onto his features. "Shit," he groused, half-turning away as Bobby's voicemail kicked on. "Call me back, you asshole. I, uh... I got that girl with me that you, uhm... you found for me. We need to talk about this, man. Gimme a call." Beretti slammed the phone back into place with a long sigh, muttering unintelligibly as he stomped into the kitchenette. "I tellya," he said to no one in particularly. "These guys." This was not how he'd envisioned his night going – well, except for the beer he fished out of the fridge. It was about to get worse, whether he knew it or not, but the reasons for that are a story best left for another time. * * * * * Another time like... three days earlier. The Oaklawn Office Complex was completely nondescript from the outside, a gray slab among other gray slabs, all mirrored windows and pleasantly smiling security guards. It housed a publishing company, a temp agency, and it used to host a chiropractor's office up on the fourth floor. A few months ago, however, Dr. McNally's office went belly-up ("it's the economy," he told people). The very next weekend, a veritable horde of contractors and decorators descended on the office, gutting it completely. Everything was replaced, from the walls to the desks to the clocks to the carpet to the toilets. None of the offices surrounding Suite #47 knew what "The Elisabeth Foundation" actually did but, whatever it was, it clearly had a great deal of money to spend to do it. None of these modern, state-of-the-art comforts made the small redhead waiting in the lobby. Amy Campbell's leg bounced nervously as she glanced to the closed office door, pretending not to listen to the lanky woman seated next to her. "Stop it," the other woman muttered. "I'm having a hard enough time staying on my guard in here. I'm half-expecting the wall to open and, I don't know, ninjas to burst out." "Don't be stupid," Amy scoffed, "Alex wouldn't hire the kind of half-assed ninjas you'd be able to see coming." "Not. Funny." "Very, if you compare this goofy fear everybody has to the fact that I've watched several Disney movies with the woman." To that, Claire could only shiver. Campbell chuckled while she stood, stepping to the desk at the head of the room - just before that ominous doorway. She almost reached out to keep Amy at her side, but the redhead was already leaning on the mahogany. "Seriously, Trinity – can I just go in or what?" The woman behind the desk was always doing something; the SCCW Champion wondered if anyone in this building ever just surfed YouTube. The name aside, the receptionist didn't look anything like Carrie-Ann Moss (something that made Amy only hate her a little bit). She ran her fingers through her hair, lips twisting in thought. "Well, Quinny is in there with her mom, but... I don't think she'd mind seeing you." She glanced at the taller woman, eyebrows lifting. "And your friend, I suppose." Amy waved slightly, and Claire needed no further encouragement to trot up behind her friend. "Did... did that lady just imply we were... you know?" The small redhead shrugged it off as she pushed open the door. "You get used to weird things around..." The interior office wasn't any fancier than the rest of the suite; there was just plain beige carpet, and no real decorations – no posters on the wall, no pictures on the desk, not even a hat rack with a forgotten coat hanging on it. The only nod to comfort was the desk itself, hand-carved from teak in the late 19th century. Not that the woman in question (the name on the door said "Elisabeth Pierotti," but that wasn't her name) ever used the thing. Since moving from New Mexico to northern California in early 2009, only Trinity manned the Foundation's tiny office, and her steadfast care of the bureau was all that kept the dust away. Even on days like today, when the woman-of-the-hour was in, the desk rarely saw any use, but that was likely due to her guest. The scene that unfolded (and quickly turned Campbell's comforting smile into something of a frown) was a simple one – especially for a woman as complex as Alexandra Pierce. There were no masks, no guises, and no lies about her identity (Primetime Central's staff was still trying to sort out just who the Infinite Gauntlet's holder was). Just a woman and her daughter, sharing some mother-daughter bonding time. As the girl tried to pick her mother's pocket. "No, no," the woman so many called Desade said, retreating with a small smile. "Just two fingers. You bring a third in there and you'll lose control. You touch somewhere you don't expect to. Thumb, forefinger. You could use the middle for heavier things." Alexandra Pierce wasn't a particularly tall woman, with hard gray eyes and features that had been carved from marble. Her bloody-red ringlets were tied back, held in place by a plain black hair band, the only gift that Amy'd ever given her that she could wear openly. Her daughter adjusted thin, cat's-eye glasses, lips twisting into a frown. "But I'm not strong enough to pull anything with just the tips of my fingers." Quinn was just fifteen, but she was already just an inch or two shorter than her mother, build like a rail with a long, straight fall of blacker-than-black hair. "When I was your age, I used a rubber band to build the strength in my—" "Uh, Alex?" In that moment, Amy was thankful Claire was a good few steps behind – giving her that moment to slip into the office and prepare Pierce. "What... what're you doing?" That hadn't quite turned out as planned, though. "Whatever it is, I need you for a few minutes – I've got an appointment and everything. Later, maybe you can explain what kind of magic tricks you taught Quinn today." The frown didn't move when she continued. "I sure hope that's what I'm seeing." "Ames!" Quinn turned, surprise morphing quickly into glee. "I just came by to, uh..." Suddenly the girl realized the situation she faced couldn't even be spun by a teenager, and, as a boarder at Amy's residence even before her mother moved into town, the so-called "Devil's Daughter" had some rules even she needed to live by. "It's okay." Alex picked up her white blazer, draped across the back of a chair. "Go home and practice. I'll take the brunt of this." Campbell snatched the teen by the arm as she passed. "I think she meant practice your math and, y'know, spelling and stuff. Because that's what kids in school are supposed to do." "Oh, come on, Amy," Quinn frowned. "Don't be mad at her. I asked her to show me a trick she uses in her job." "To be fair," Alex said gently (and not altogether helpfully). "I could have taught her several tricks you would approve of less." A brow lifted. "You've a scheduled appointment?" Sometimes, even someone as schooled in deception as the professed "Spider in the Web" had to manage the awkward segue. "You know you don't have to do that to see me." "Are you two going to kiss now?" Quinn frowned. "Because that's what you tend to do when you're alone and I still think it's kind of gross." "Nah," Amy still held that stern look at Alex, "I'm here for your mom's problem-solving skills toda—" "I already told you, the keys are on top of the fridge!" Quinn sighed. "You're just too short to reach." Campbell shook her head. "I found those this morning, used one of the dining room chairs and... and it's not important!" She flailed. "I'm here on business." Alex's left brow quirked in thought, not quite rising. "Wait outside, would you, Q? I absolutely must hear this. We'll go out to grab some lunch once we've wrapped it up." "Well... all right." As was usual, her mother hadn't given her a choice. "Maybe I can make Trin, you know, talk instead of being a robot all the time. But promise me you won't invite any of your goons along? That Stevens guy gives me the heebie-jeebies, so it had better just be the three of us." "Might be four," Campbell noted. "But you've hung out with Claire before, so hopefully that doesn't make things awkward. And when you're out there, let her know she can stop cowering and come in now." Quinn nodded, and, whether intentional or not, mirrored her mother's right-hand-tucks-hair-behind-ear gesture, one of Desade's few tells. The moment she was gone, Amy's eyebrow lifted – the kind of expression that said, "Oh, we are so going to have a talk later, you'd better believe it, missy." The dark-haired woman that entered the office once the teen left smiled sort of ruefully at Pierce, wiping her hands on her pants. "Sorry, I, uh, needed a drink of water." "Alex, this is Claire Milleur." Amy did the introduction with practiced ease. "Claire, Alexandra Pierce." The two women shook hands gently, a certain tension falling into the room. "Claire, a pleasure. Amy has told me so much about you." "Likewise. I hope I'm not intruding." "No, that's quite all right. We overthrow Argentina next weekend." Alex gestured to the antique desk, scooping up her snow-white blazer, folded neatly across one of the high-backed chairs, swinging around to the business side. "Please, have a seat. I'd offer you something, but I haven't quite unpacked yet." The two women sat down across from Pierce, and they were a study in differences, with Campbell unfurled in the chair, frowning. "Sykes was right for once – you do keep these chairs as incredibly uncomfortable as possible, don't you? It's like they've had a cushion-ectomy." Milleur, for her part, curled in on herself, her expression carefully neutral, and it was she that Alex watched briefly, the tiniest curl in her eyebrow. Pierce rested one elbow on the armrest, hand curled around her chin. "What brings you two here?" "Amy's car," Claire muttered, earning her an elbow from the tiny woman who held a monopoly on Sin City's championship. "We were... well. We went to dinner the other night, she was being all pouty-frowny and I asked why. Turns out there's an issue that I'm pretty sure you can help her with, she's just too stubborn to ask for it." "Really." Again, the weight of Alex's stony gaze fell on her partner's friend. "It's nothing." Milleur couldn't help but shift in her seat a little; the Spider's gray eyes were so empty. "Just a dickhead. Nothing either of you need to worry about. Amy kinda threatened to tell you where I live if I didn't come up here, so..." Alex's eyes moved to the other woman – just the eyes, keeping her face pointed towards the guest. "Nonsense. If Amy says it is something I should consider, I would be a fool not to." "Yeah, she kinda hinted at that." Claire picked at fabric on the knee of her pants. "And just to clear something up that's been bugging me a little – I hope you don't find this awkward. I mean, I know a bit about what you do and..." She coughed briefly. "And I'm sure Loudmouth over here's told you some things about us becoming such good friends and, err, no hard feelings that I slept with her first?" Amy was the first to react, palm-to-face as she slouched in her chair. "Oh, wow. You actually just apologized for that." Almost entirely ignoring (or just fearing) the Director's reaction, Milleur glared at Campbell, sharply whispering. "What? I told you I didn't want to come here! If she's anything like I know you are, she'd want to snap my neck for having ever touched something belonging t—" "I don't belong to anyone." Amy stated. "Kinda do." "Don't." "Either way!" Claire huffed, finally turning to Alex. "I'm officially apologizing and letting you know I've got absolutely no leftover feelings or photos or..." "Seriously, stop. Not helping your cause here." "I'm just making sure she knows! No hard feelings, right? I don't have to look over my shoulder just because we were together all that t—OUCH, AMELIA FUCKING CAMPBELL." After a quick stomp, Amy crossed one DC sneaker over the other. "Stop?" Pierce's own reaction was, as always, much more muted. Her gaze briefly became a glare before it moved away, this time dragging her head with it. "You obviously consider whatever it is quite important. So I will not bog us down with the weight of history between the three of us, so I will simply ask again. Explain to me just what it is that brought you to my doorstep this evening." Amy shifted in her seat a little; the wordier Pierce became, she knew, the more irritated she was. "See, there's this guy who's buying up businesses in the area around Claire's studio. And when they say no, he sets these two goons on them. Mr. Li didn't want to sell his Laundromat, and he got carjacked. Which is weird, because I thought guys in Chinese laundry places were, like, kung fu masters." "And this man has come calling for Ms. Milleur?" Pierce returned her gaze to the other woman, brow arching a bit more noticeably. "He wants to buy the place, I guess," Claire leaned back in the chair slightly. "I told him I didn't want to sell, because I love that place, you know? It's not big, but it works for me." "And this man, he did not take that well?" Amy rolled her eyes. "Guys like this don't just go away, you know?" Campbell sighed, picking at the frayed knee of her jeans. "I've never asked you for anything. I don't... I don't like special treatment. But I was wondering—" "I'll do it." "You'll... wait, what?" "I'll do it." Alex repeated. "You don't even know what I was going to ask." Campbell's brows stilted. "I could've said 'I don't like special treatment, but I was wondering if you'd let me run off with Claire for a week of hot monkeylove in the Bahamas." "Would never happen." Claire assured. "Never. I'm over it." She made sure to meet Pierce's eyes. "So over it." "It doesn't matter what it was." Pierce's voice, whisper-soft yet diamond-hard, didn't waver. "I can only hope it wasn't that. You'd like me to look into this..." "Baldwin." Claire rolled her eyes. "Bryan Baldwin – with a 'Y'. He made sure to stress that." "You would like me to look into Mr. Baldwin. Make him leave Ms. Milleur alone." "This is stupid," Claire muttered. "I told you, the police said they'd look into it." Amy folded her arms. "The police would pussyfoot around until you had broken knees thanks to some wannabe-mafia corporate pricks. Let Alex help." "I didn't want this to escalate, Ames. I don't need people killed over my stupid little studio." Campbell cleared her throat, prompting Claire to continue with a little cough of her own. "Err, not that Amy's said anything about that sort of... activity. I just... you're a powerful woman, lots of money and stuff, and she and I are really close and..." She stuttered. "Look, Miss Pierce – you've probably heard some things about me not really approving of you—well, of this whole thing you two have going on... I mean, I can't pretend I do, and that's part of the reason I didn't want to bring it to you, but she seems to trust you to know the right thing to do. A lot." "It's a trust I have risked losing far more often than I care to remember," Alex said with a rueful smile. "In my, ah... line of work... there is an old saying. 'Avoid friends of the wife.' So forgive the awkwardness. I will do what I can, Ms. Milleur. I am not going to make any promises, of course. But I am relatively certain what limited resources I still have access to can likely solve your problems. Without killing anyone." "And without flirting, right? That would be awesome, too." Amy put in, flashing her miles-wide grin. "See, I knew you guys could work together." "I will need any names you may know of Mr. Baldwin's associates, and – please forgive my candor – but I'll need you not to question me if I need you to do something, so long as it is not unethical or illegal. Baldwin knows you, and I may have to make use of that." "So long as you remember that Ames is 'the wife' to somebody else, and keep in mind that she's my best friend – I—" "Claire!" Milleur raised apologetic palms. "Sorry. I'd say 'I don't want you to make me do anything you wouldn't make her do', but..." "But I think you should go to the waiting room or crank the AC in the car for a few minutes while I talk to Alex," said Amy. "Y'know, before your mouth gets me into trouble." Amy Campbell was one of those women you didn't say no to, and it was no surprise that Claire listened, muttering something dark under her breath. Pierce turned her attention away from the woman a half-beat after the door closed. "You don't have to say anything. I won't make you beholden to me or anyone else. It's a day's work, piece of cake. One asshole businessman. You know I've done worse." "You didn't even take the chance to give her a hard time about it. I... what made you agree so quickly?" she asked. "Because I am beholden to you, but for a million other reasons." Alex looked up, meeting Amy's mismatched gaze with a tenderness in her own eyes that few saw, and even them rarely. "I'm doing it because it's a small penance for all the wrong I have done, and because you asked me it of me. And because nothing else I am doing is as worthwhile." "You know you don't owe me anything, and I hated having to ask this of you if not only because... well, you might've noticed that she hates you. Well, not you – 'us', actually. A whole lot." Amy approached the Spider and quietly hoped that might take some of the severity from the other redhead's posture. "I appreciate everything more than you even know." When the taller redhead lowered her face, the smile on it was small and genuine – a commodity worth a fortune when coming from Alexandra Pierce. "I know a great deal, Aim." For a woman so fond of officious, proper naming conventions to shorten Amy's name even further was proof of her heart's say in the matter. "Come on, my daughter and your best friend are waiting to go to lunch. We should go before Quinn realizes how hungry she is." "If she does, I'm getting you to pick up the bill." "Oh, so you're looking to bankrupt me?" "Yep." Amy's grin latched onto her lips as Alex slid up to her feet and the two redheads joined the two dark-haired women for an afternoon of far-more-pizza-than-they-should-eat. For Campbell, it was remarkably relaxing, given the hectic circumstances of her life. But Alexandra Pierce was girding herself for war. * * * * * Two days later. There was quite a stark contrast between the Elisabeth Foundation's rather Spartan suburban Oakland offices and the downtown San Francisco headquarters of Bryan Baldwin's BBI, Ltd. The BBI complex was far more spacious, the entire wall that faced the street made from a single window, and the company's "Double-B" logo had been etched into a monument to the left of the tall doors. Elijah Hawke briefly rested his intricate, stone-headed cane against the sign obelisk as he held the door.
