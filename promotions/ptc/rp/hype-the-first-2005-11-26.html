Hype the First

26 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-11-26 Action figures. A world-renowned hobby shop in New York City had over 40,000 of them, of all makes and models. Among their most popular sellers were the obvious, the Star Wars figures and the Transformers. But when November 26th came around, the store was known primarily for one thing. Wrestling figures. And considering that this year’s post-Thanksgiving blitz coincided with the start of the world-renowned GTT5 tournament, PTC marketing was well prepared. The ten figures in the special GTT5 set went on the pegs at 7:00 AM, an hour before the store opened. By noontime, the section had been nearly decimated. Angelo Deville figures had nearly flown off the shelves. Hoyt Williams, with his PTC Extreme Title accessory, also seemed to be a favorite, especially among gullible parents looking for a "wholesome" toy for their child. Rich Rollins figures were eagerly snapped up, as fans of all ages debated the epic war to come in round one between Rollins and Deville. While still lagging behind the three heavy hitters, the Dawn McFarlane and Shadow figures were also selling well. Both seemed to be rather popular among men and women alike, especially a twenty something male in a Hooters’ t-shirt who bought ten of Shadow’s. That’s for knocking me out in round one, you bastard. At any rate, Jimmy Bong’s figure wasn’t doing quite as well as the two Global stars, clocking in at about the same amount of sales as Infinite Gauntlet champion Michael Stevens’ figure received. Kenny Mercury and Brandon Youngblood, while also not top, top sellers, were often bought in pairs, by fans eager to play out their round one matchup in advance in their own personal PTC Arena. That left Seymour Almasy’s figures, the row nearly undisturbed. Sure, a few people had bought the Final Fantasy’s toy likeness, but they had been quick to offer an explanation. Popular ones included "I’m a collector" and "Why don’t you have any more Angelo Deville figures? I guess I’ll take this one." Why were the Almasy figures undisturbed? Simple. PTC had moved on. He was a has-been. Done. He hadn’t even won a bracket in the past two tournaments. And to most of the net dirt sheets, that made him yesterday’s news. Besides, even if Seymour WAS sort of cool, there was also someone cooler AND newer. See Michael Stevens. See Brandon Youngblood. See Dawn, see Shadow, and see virtually everyone else in GTT5. It was at about that point that two young men entered the store, making a beeline for the back where the wrestling figures were kept. For the sake of today’s exercise, we’ll call them Steve and Derek. "I hope they still have some left," Steve exclaimed, virtually ignoring the elderly shop owner’s plea to slow down. His compatriot chased after him, a few steps behind. "Of course they will," Derek responded. "This place gets the most figures in town!" Around a corner they went, dodging Batman and Spiderman figures, and they arrived at the proper rack. Almost immediately, Steve stomped his foot on the ground and shook his head. "Awww, man, they’re out of Angelo Deville figures!" "Just get one of those two last Hoyt Williams ones," Derek replied. "Besides, he comes with the PTC Extreme Title. We don’t have that belt yet." Nodding in acceptance, Steve reached up, plucking his treasure from the pegs. "Now we can put the PTC Extreme Title on the line next time we do an event! You should pick someone to have him go against…hmm…who do they have left?" His friend scanned the pegs, looking at the figures carefully. There were many worthy choices, but he didn’t have a lot of money to spend today, and could only get one. Frowning as he realized this fact, he finally reached up and plucked one of the numerous Seymour Almasy action figures from its peg, prompting a snort of disgust. "Derek, why would you get another Seymour action figure? We already have one, and besides, there are far cooler people left. Like Youngblood! He’s the Only Diamond in Our Sport, and we can have him and Hoyt do a rematch from that PRIME PPV!" "You broke the Seymour figure!" Derek retorted, sounding somewhat annoyed himself. "Listen," came the reply from Steve, "it’s not my fault Doctor Curiosity dropped Ze Curious Math Textbook on him and tore his arm off in the process!" "It so is! You dropped the book on him! And besides, this figure’s much cooler than the other one!" Taking it from Derek with a grumble, the young man had to admit that his friend was right. Lovingly rendered with the image seen by millions who visited Primetime Central’s forums, it also came with Seymour’s Invincible, a sword. And if there’s one thing young men like as much as wrestling, it’s swords. "Fine, fine," Steve finally acquiesced, "but there’s no way he’s going to beat YOUR PERSONAL JESUS for the Extreme Title, in real life or not." "Maybe, maybe not," Derek said, taking back the likeness of his favorite wrestler. "But GTT5’s coming up. And in tournaments, there’s no one better." "Except Angelo Deville. If he faced Seymour, he’d SO kill him!" "I—you know what? Never mind." Derek simply sighed, rounding the corner to go pay for his figure. His friend followed soon after. Rounding the opposite corner was an obliviously unaware Seymour Almasy. Like a schoolboy, he gaped at the action figures, pulling figure after figure down from the pegs for his collection. And, just like the children before him, he pouted. "Damnit, they’re out of Angelo Deville figures!"
