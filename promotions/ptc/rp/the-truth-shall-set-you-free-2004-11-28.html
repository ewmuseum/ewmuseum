The Truth Shall Set You Free

28 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-28 Here, things are not always what they seem. What you’ve known as fact for so long, years perhaps can suddenly be revealed as being something else. What you have long since accepted as truth may, in fact, be false. What you have accepted as being false may be the truth. Things change, people change, perspectives change, worlds change, and lives change. But how do you know what’s true and what’s false? The simple answer is, you don’t; at least, not until it reveals itself as such. Until then, well, all you have to go on is what you know, what you’ve learned, what you’ve been taught, what you’ve been shown. In other words, all you have to go on is life. No, this is not the Twilight Zone, this is real life. This is the Latch Key Kid’s life. ------------------------ The lemonade stand was still there when the Latch Key Kid and Katherine returned from their earlier breakfast. The sign was still nailed to the top of the wooden stand, the supplies were still locked away in the trunk behind the stand; a cursory glance would reveal that nothing had changed. A cursory glance was all Latch and Katherine gave the place. The parking lot in front of Frank’s was still mostly empty, though a few cars had found their way in, and their owners were now inside the coffeehouse enjoying their morning latte, cappuccino, house blend, or whatever they preferred. “Well, time to get work,” Katherine said, as she unlocked the trunk and set up the cups and tiny sugar packets. Latch walked back to the car and retrieved a cooler full of fresh-made lemonade. He always said fresh was better, and the customers seemed to agree. He brought the cooler over to the stand and hoisted it up onto the counter. The business day had begun, and all they had to do now was settle in and wait. “I hope Frank will appreciate what I’ve decided to do,” Latch said, obviously nervous about how Frank would react. “I mean, like you said before, he has his own successful business, so it’s not like he really needs my money. But, every little bit counts, I’m sure, and if I can make at least a little difference in this, then I’ll be satisfied. I just hope it’s enough for Frank.” “I’m sure it will be,” Katherine replied. She watched Latch as he held a cup under the cooler’s spout, pressed a button in and filled his cup with lemonade. He always did like to have the first cup of daily lemonade out of the cooler, to make sure it’s up to par, he’d say. After all, if I don’t think it tastes good, I can’t give it to the customers. The cynic would say that he was worried about losing business if the product wasn’t of high standards. But Katherine knew better. He did it because he honestly cared about the customers, and figured that if they cared enough to stop and spend their hard-earned money, the least he could do was care enough to give them the best lemonade possible. “Frank’s a very caring man, Brad, one of the most generous people I’ve ever met. You operate along those same lines. You both have big hearts and genuinely care about other people. I don’t see how Frank could not appreciate your gesture. Besides, like you said, every little bit counts.” As he sipped his lemonade, he stared at Katherine, saw her as if he were seeing her for the very first time. The way the still-rising sun highlighted her blond hair still seemed as pure to him as a baby’s first breath. He marveled at the sparkle in her green eyes, the way they both complimented and contrasted with her blond hair at the same time, thought them to be the most beautiful eyes he had ever seen, the deepest he had ever stared into. He had often told her that the richest emeralds in the world wouldn’t be able to compete with her green eyes. Sweet Katherine, he thought to himself, I’m sorry for dragging you into all of this. You didn’t want any part of this in the beginning, and perhaps I should have left it at that. Now, my problems are your problems, and as such, Frank’s problems have become your problems as well. You don’t deserve this, and I don’t deserve you. I wish there were a way to take back – “What?” she asked him, with a slight giggle. Latch shook his head, stared down at the lemonade. “Huh?” “You looked lost, is all, in your own little world or something.” “Oh,” he said, and shrugged, “I was just thinking about this lemonade. Good stuff today. Well, it always is, but this tastes a little better today for some reason.” Her presence often had that effect on him, made everything better. Well, that, and sending him off into his own little world. He hated how he zoned out at times, but her beauty captivated him. He sometimes wished he were a poet rather than a professional wrestler, imagining that then, he would be able to share his thoughts of her with all the world in form of verse. He had never told her this, of course, for whatever reason; he could see her becoming embarrassed, he could see himself becoming embarrassed, he could see her telling him that he was perfect in what he was doing as a wrestler. That grated on him at times, as he often wondered if this was the line of work he should really be in. He feared hearing that he was perfect at it, as he often dreamed of being capable of much more. “Would you like to try some?” he finally asked her. She laughed, and punched his shoulder playfully. “I thought you’d never ask.” She turned and surveyed the parking lot as Latch poured a second cup of lemonade. He walked up behind her, and held it out in front of her. He placed the cup in her right hand, and kissed her on the side of the neck. “My prince,” she said, and took a sip of lemonade. “Mmm, you were right. If you ever quit your job as a wrestler, I think I know what you should go into.” “Lemonade sales? Are you nuts? Do you really think there’s money in that?” Latch asked. “Well, not on this scale,” she replied. “At least, not with a single stand. Now, you could branch out, franchise your stands, get the word out. You’d just have to make sure of one thing.” “What’s that?” he asked, afraid of the answer. “You’d have to keep your recipe a secret,” she said with a laugh. “My recipe? Katherine, it’s lemonade for goodness sake,” he said, matching her laugh with one of his own. “Come on, it looks like we’ve got our first customer of the day.” Indeed, a man was walking toward them, carrying a bag from the bookstore near the middle of the strip of stores. Latch poured a cup of lemonade, handed it to the man, took his money, and dropped it in the glass jar next to the cooler. “Thank you for your business, sir,” Latch said. “Well, thanks for the lemonade. I would’ve gone into the coffeehouse for a latte, but when I passed by there earlier, there seemed to be some rather unsavory types standing around the counter,” the man replied. Latch groaned, and stared at Katherine. He suddenly became very antsy. “Well, sir, we’re here every day, so if you ever feel like a fresh cup of lemonade, you know where to go.” He took the empty cup when the man held it out, and dropped it into the trashcan behind the stand. The man nodded and walked off. “They’re here already?” Katherine asked with a start. “But we didn’t even see them go inside.” “That can only mean one thing,” Latch said, his voice growing weary. “They were already here before we got here.” Katherine nodded, then help up her right index finger. “Unless,” she paused, “they came in a back door.” Latch shook his head. “Frank’s doesn’t even have a back door. At least, not that I’ve ever seen. And besides, those guys have always come in the front door, so why would they use the back door this time?” “They’ve always used the front door? Brad, how many times have you seen them come to, uhm, visit Frank here?” He started to count on his fingers, but didn’t have very far to count. “One,” he replied. He scratched his head and started into her green eyes again, and then his voice dropped. “Oh…no.” “Brad,” she said, softly and slowly, “maybe you better go have a look in the back alley.” He nodded, and walked off towards the small parking lot behind the strip of stores. He slowed as he approached the back corner of Frank’s, and peered around the corner. He drew back in a shock as he saw a car parked near the back of Frank’s. It was the same Jetta that Latch had seen the three men get out of the first, and as of yet only, time he saw them show up at Frank’s. And then, as he looked at the back wall, he noticed a door with a heavy deadbolt lock on it. He turned and jogged back to the lemonade stand. “Looks like we’ve got trouble,” Latch said, and took a deep sigh as Katherine embraced him with a comforting hug. ------------------------ Nothing in the life of Brad Jameson had ever been easy. From growing up without a father, to learning to fend for himself against the local school bullies, to eventually breaking into XUW and securing his spot among the best XUW’s tag team division had ever seen, he had always been forced to fight with all his inner strength. The times that he had slacked off, he usually wound up on the wrong end of the final result. His life had been full of finding out that things he believed in were not true after all. He remembered when he found out the truth about his father. His mother had told him and Courtney that their father was away, working on an oilrig platform off the coast of Louisiana. They were told he would be away for quite some time, and might be able to come home for Christmas. Christmas seemed so far away; ten months by the way Brad counted on the generic calendar on his wall. Christmas finally arrived, and their father was nowhere to be found. Brad sat on his bed, flipped back through the pages of his calendar, counted the red X marks he had made as each day ended, and hoped that his father might be home for next Christmas. He could make it, he knew, it was only a matter of red Xs. Their mother finally broke down later that night, and told them the truth. You deserve to know, she said, I’m sorry. Brad and Courtney went to bed crying, despite the presents their mother had worked so hard to buy for them. He remembered thinking he had finally gotten his big break in wrestling, with some West Texas league. He drove 13 hours to El Paso, only to find out that there were no cards scheduled at all. In fact, nobody had ever heard of West Texas Wrasslin’. He realized that he had been ripped off, the money he had given the promoter for advance promotion gone, his hopes of a career in wrestling extinguished. He called Courtney, pleaded with her to wire him some money so he could buy gas to return home. She reminded him that he never had to beg her for anything, and wired the money later that afternoon. He remembered the first time he and Doughboy won the XUW tag team championship. In defeating both the New Royal Legion and the Hardcore Icons, he was proud of the way they had won, through diligence and hard work. Only later, when he realized that they won because he was still experiencing a bout of mental madness and snapped, nailing everybody including Doughboy with a steel chair, did it finally sink in what had happened. Their first championship, after so many months of trying, and the irony was that they won it because he resorted to the same tactics he had seen from bullies his entire life. He remembered the first time he set foot in Frank’s Coffeehouse, dragged along by Doughboy to perform in some silly variety sketch show. Frank had been so kind to him from that very first night. He always seemed glad to see Latch, always took time to talk to him and Doughboy, offered his advice on life, wrestling, difficult decisions, whatever the case may have been. There was never any inkling that something was wrong behind closed doors, never any sign that Frank was being harassed by the goons who were probably back in his office right now. He knew what he had to do. He knew he had to make it right. ------------------------ “Are you crazy?” she yelled at him. “Do you realize what you’re saying?” “Yes,” Latch replied. “I mean, no, yes. No, I’m not crazy, and yes, I realize what I’m saying. But you don’t understand, Katherine. It’s easy enough to say that I should walk away and let somebody else handle this, but I’ve tried that before. I’ve left my life up to others before, and it usually hasn’t worked out in the right way. This is something I have to do myself.” “So you’re just going to march on in there and confront these guys?” Her voice was now betraying genuine terror; not so much along the lines of anger at him, but fear for his safety. She loved him, she knew that, and she hoped that he knew that, too, even though those words had never been spoken between them. She did not want him walking into that coffeehouse, into the back office, into another situation he shouldn’t be sticking his nose in. It was one thing for him to want to use his profits from the lemonade stand to help Frank; that, she could understand and accept, even admire. But to walk in there as a one-man vigilante crew, that was too much for her. Beyond thinking it was stupid for him to do so, she thought it was a dangerous thing for him to do. “I won’t let you do that, Brad,” she protested. He held up a hand. “Katherine, it’s really not your decision. I’ve made up my mind, and there’s nothing you can say to make me change it. I have to go in there to help Frank. I have to do what’s right, for once. I have to put someone else over myself for once in my life. It’s Frank, Katherine, not some stranger. Please, try to understand.” She shook her head. “I can’t, Brad. I can’t even begin to understand what you think you can accomplish as one person. What about the police? Hmmm? Have you even thought to call them?” He nodded. “I thought about it, but like I said, this is me and Frank now. And those three guys in his office. Keep an eye on the lemonade stand. I’ll be back in a bit. And if I’m not, well,” he paused, “then call the police.” With that, he turned and walked toward the front of Frank’s Coffeehouse. By this time, there were more cars in the parking lot and more patrons inside. She called out to him. “Brad, I love you.” He stopped, turned around, and smiled. “I love you, too.” Then he turned, and kept walking. A tear fell from her eye as she watched him walk inside. ------------------------ The bratty kid behind the counter called out to him, grabbed a mug and began to fill it with coffee. He set it on the counter, and motioned for Latch to sit down and relax. Latch sat on a barstool, but relaxing was out of the question. His nerves were wound tighter than the strings on a grand piano, and his hands shook as he picked up the mug. He took a few sips of coffee, and set the mug back on the counter. “Where’s Frank?” he asked. Billy started at him and shook his head. “Uh-uh. Not this time. I know why you’re in here, and I’m not going to let you do this.” “Do what?” Latch asked, his irritation with the pimply-faced coffee pusher becoming more evident. “Barge into his office and try to be a hero.” “A-ha!” Latch exclaimed. “He’s in his office.” Billy sneered and muttered something under his breath. “Unless I was only kidding and he’s not really in his office. But look, I really can’t let you go in there. Nobody’s allowed in, Frank’s orders. No matter what happens, he said, that door stays shut and nobody comes in until I come out.” Billy shook his head again. “Look, Billy, you know me, I know you, I know Frank. I’m here to help, that’s all. Frank’s in danger here, and I can help him. But I need your help. I need you to let me go into that office and take care of this. It’ll all be over soon, at least, it can be. Just, please, Billy…” Billy shook his head, his irritation at Latch becoming more evident as well. “I can’t do it, man. I wish I could, but I’ve got strict orders. This is my job we’re talking about here.” Latch snickered. “Your job. It’s about much more than your job, Billy. This is about Frank and this coffeehouse. You wouldn’t have a job if not for Frank, think about that. So, unless you want to-” Billy had walked around to the front of the counter, and stood in front of Latch. “I can’t let you do it, man, I’m sorry.” Latch sighed deeply and stood up. “Okay, fine,” he said, and started walking toward the door. He stopped suddenly, turned around and ran, pushing Billy out of the way, and rushed to the door to the manager’s office. The sign on the door directed visitors to knock before entering, but Latch of course did not knock. He tried the door handle, but it was locked. He pressed his left shoulder against the door, and then threw all his weight against the door. It flew open, and Latch tumbled into the office amid a few splinters that had broken off the door. “I’m here, Frank!” he said, as he stood up and closed the door, and then turned around and viewed the scene in front of him with shock. His mouth dropped open. Frank looked up at him from his seat on the other side of a round table. “Brad…I’m disappointed. I thought we had an understanding. You would keep your lemonade stand, and keep out of my business. What happened, Brad?” Frank’s voice grew deeper, his eyes grew darker, his facial muscles contorted. “What…are…you doing here, Brad?” Latch was frozen, staring not at Frank but at the table in front of Frank. Stacks of cash, several ledger books, lists of area businesses, a calculator. And, seated around the table, the three goons who had harassed him a few weeks ago. His mouth, still hanging opened, attempted to form words. “I…I…Fra-…nk…what’s…” Frank smiled, spread his hands over the table. “What’s this, you want to know?” Latch nodded. “Brad, this…is business. My business. These are Jason, Elmore and Percy.” The other three men seated at the table nodded politely as Frank introduced each of them. “And you, Brad,” Frank shook his head, “you’re in more trouble than you know right now.” “I…you…all this time…coffee…” “Ah, yes, the coffeehouse,” Frank grinned, “you didn’t think it was making me all that much money, did you?” He stared at Latch. “Oh, you did. You poor thing. Well, I do apologize for that. But you see, Brad, this is how things around here really operate. All of this, this coffeehouse, it’s just a front. Don’t you see? The money comes in, the money goes out, and it’s all in the name of coffee.” Latch scratched his head, and looked around the room again. “So you’re saying this is…all this is…fake? The coffeehouse, the employees, the customers?” Frank shook his head vehemently. “No, Brad, none of it is fake. Those people working out there, Billy, Nancy, whoever’s here today, they don’t know about any of this. They have a job to do and they know what that job is, and that’s all they concern themselves with. Those customers, they’re oblivious. You were too, when you were just a customer. They have no reason to suspect otherwise. It’s just business as usual as far as they’re concerned.” “But…I…help…lemonade…” Frank made a tsk sound with his tongue, did it again. “Hey, let me guess. You were going to take the money you made from the lemonade stand and give it to me to help me pay off these thugs who were harassing us, is that it?” Latch nodded. “Well,” Frank smirked, “I guess you can keep your money now.” Latch nodded, and started to speak again. Frank held up his hand and stopped him. “The way I see it, Brad, you have two options now. The first one is, you turn around, walk out that door right now, and forget about all this. Forget about what you’ve seen here; forget about what you’ve heard, forget about all of this. Forget this entire conversation. Well, except for what I’m telling you right now. And you walk out.” He paused. Latch blinked three quick times. “And the second option?” Frank scratched his chin. “I lied. There’s only one option.” “Why are you doing this, Frank? Why are you-” “I told you already, Brad. Money.” Latch shook his head, stared at Frank. “No, I mean, why are you letting me walk away? Why not something else?” “Good question,” Frank retorted. He pointed again to Jason, Elmore and Percy. “These three guys, I’m sure they’d love to see me do something else.” They nodded in unison. “And I was right. But I’m not. Know why, Brad? Because I like you. Despite you being such a, excuse the language, pain in my ass recently, I really do like you. You’re a good kid, you’ve got a good head on your shoulders, and you have a big heart. And, believe it or not, I do, too. So what I’m going to do is, I’m going to look into my heart, and I’m going to let you go. Just like that. Because we’re friends, Brad. And you’re going to do something for me, too, aren’t you?” Latch nodded, and Frank smiled. “Good. So you’re just going to forget about all this.” Latch nodded again, then opened his mouth. “What about…what about the…” Frank interrupted. “The lemonade stand?” Latch nodded. “Yes, well, I don’t really care what you do with that. Keep it there, close it down, it really doesn’t concern me anymore. One thing concerns me, Brad, and that’s you forgetting about this. Everything. Right?” Latch nodded. “Good. There’s a lesson here, Brad. You let your curiosity get the best of you. You can’t do that. You were focusing on your curiosity regarding my situation more than you were focusing on Doctor Curiosity. That’s a bad thing, Brad.” He noticed Brad’s change of expression. “That’s right. Believe it or not, I’ve been keeping up with your progress. You’re not out of the clear yet, but win this next one, and you’re in the final match. Imagine that, Brad. You, the Latch Key Kid, in the finals.” Latch nodded again, unable to speak, and Frank stood up and extended his right hand. Latch looked down at Frank’s hand, held out his own right hand, and then before Frank could shake it, Latch turned and used his outstretched hand to pull the door open. He walked out of the manager’s office, and headed straight for the entrance to the coffee shop. He walked right by Billy, who wished him a good day, and opened the front door and walked out. He walked over to the lemonade stand, as if in a daze, and saw Katherine handing a cup of lemonade and some change to a little girl. She saw him approaching, and after she had finished the transaction, she ran to Latch and embraced him with a bear hug. She stared up into his eyes, noticed the change in him, and then stepped back. “What is it? What happened?” she asked. “Don’t…ask,” Latch replied. “I don’t even know where to begin, and I couldn’t tell you if I did. Remember when you said some things are better left in the dark?” She nodded. “Well, you were right.” He noticed a little kid walking by on the street, a boy of about eight or nine years old, looking solemnly at the lemonade stand. Latch called out to him. “Hey, kid, come here!” The boy ran over to the stand. “Yes, mister?” “Would you like to buy some lemonade?” Latch asked. “I would, sir, but I don’t have enough. The sign says ten cents, and all I have is a nickel.” Latch looked at Katherine, then back at the kid. “Tell you what. Give me that nickel, and we’ll call it even.” The kid dug a nickel out of his pocket and handed it to Latch, who dropped it in the money jar on the counter. “All right, kid, it’s all yours.” Latch grabbed Katherine’s hand and began to walk towards the car. The kid called out. “Mister, I just wanted some lemonade.” Latch shrugged his shoulders. “Well, now you’re the proud new owner of a lemonade stand. Treat it well, and it’ll treat you well.” The kid looked toward the coffeehouse. “But what about that guy in there? He won’t care that I’m here?” Latch smiled. “Something tells me you don’t have to worry about him, and if he bothers you, just tell him the Latch Key Kid sold you his business.” Katherine still looked at Latch with an air of confusion, as he opened the car door and helped her get in. He walked around to the driver’s side, opened the door and got inside, and started the car. She finally spoke. “Where are we going, Brad?” Latch looked at her and smiled. “We’re going to Moonbeam’s to get a cup of coffee.” He hesitated. “And to talk.” The little boy watched as the car drove out of the parking lot, then walked behind the lemonade stand. He found a little stool on the ground that he stood on, making him several inches taller. He crossed his arms in front of his chest and smiled.
