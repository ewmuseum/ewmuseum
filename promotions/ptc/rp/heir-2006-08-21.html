"Heir"

21 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-08-21 * * * * * "You’ll wake up in a state of confusion, and more than likely be immediately dragged out to the battle lines with only two things certain: a fear of the Devil, Typherion, and the fear of what happens when you don’t do His bidding." * * * * * The mountains in Hell reached incredible heights, but the fortresses carved into their sides were equally magnificent. The Devil’s fondness of architecture gave an underlying beauty to a place where so much of the world’s ugliness came to reside. Typherion, as he liked to be called, looked out over his domain with calculating eyes. A thousand feet beneath him, stretching for miles, were his demon soldiers preparing for another attempt at conquering the realm of Heaven, which was erected and carved into the mountains parallel to his own, miles upon miles away. From his vantage point, standing at the very edge of a balcony jutting out from the mountainside, the realm of Heaven appeared only as a glimmering diamond in the far distance. Above Heaven, slowly floating skyward from behind the veil of mountains, was the moon. To Typherion’s back, the sun was beginning to retreat behind his own. Circling in the sky above the sea of demons, were his generals. Their horned heads and giant wingspans cast long shadows on the legions of souls beneath them, awaiting his command. He only had to let the thought run through his head, and all of those within his kingdom would begin their march for another barrage on Heaven. Even though he was hundreds of miles away, across the Great Battlefield where their war was waged, Typherion could see God, staring back. He could feel his penetrating gaze, wondering what his next action would be. Go. Suddenly, the winged Generals of Hell all roared at once, and a wave of movement swept through the ocean of soldiers. Their footsteps echoed up the sides of the mountain, and throughout the stone palace. Typherion turned from the Great Battlefield, and began his walk back to his quarters. He knew God would match his maneuver, and send the Angels to combat his demons. He could still feel the blue eyes watching him from miles and miles away, wondering how he could send so many of his own to their demise. Then, unexpectedly, a sudden presence in Hell caught Typherion’s attention. Almost immediately, one of his minion’s thoughts could be heard in his mind. "M’lord, the fifth MacGregor has arrived." Bring him to me. Typherion paused a moment, finally turning around once more and returning to the tip of the giant balcony. His mouth soon curled into a devilish grin when he realized he could no longer feel God’s invasive gaze. * * * * * Archibald Wentworth Matthews knelt by the bathtub and peered in through the ice. A few inches beneath the water lay Archibald MacGregor, his face a ghostly pale, his lips and eyes a sickening shade of purple. Dead. During his many years on Earth before being slaughtered in Vietnam, and his years in the afterlife since then, he couldn’t remember being this nervous. He knew his angelic powers would give the gift of life back to the deceased Archibald, of this there was no doubt. But there was just something about the whole scenario that felt wrong. Everything seemed off. The night was unnaturally quiet, without any wind rustling the trees or any bugs singing to one another. The house matched that silence with a silence of it’s own. The ice cubes floating above the corpse of Archibald MacGregor made no cracking noises as they melted and brushed against one another. If he had a heart, it would be thumping. Death no longer provided any sort of tension, so it was strange that he was actually somewhat scared by the silence. The clock on the wall read 10:13. Archibald had been dead for nearly five minutes. He would only be dead for five more minutes, based on their agreement. Then he would be yanked out of the freezing water, and Wentworth Matthews would place his palm against MacGregor’s heart. Life would return, as would his soul. Yanked away from Hell. From the floor below, Wentworth Matthews heard the front door rip open, followed by booming footsteps. Then came the equally commanding and disrespectful voice, "MacGregor! Where the hell are you, we need to talk!" * * * * * He woke up confused as to where he was, with his eyes hurting from the moment he opened them. He was moving, although not under his own power, overwhelmed by a sensation like he was being dragged by someone – or something.. He could feel the grating of his hands and feet on the stone floor. Lacking the strength to resist, he simply tried to get his bearings by looking at his surroundings. Hundreds of stone pillars reached from the floors to the enormous vaulted ceilings. On every fourth pillar, there were torches. Flames licking away at the air, darkening the stone columns they rested upon. Staring at the flames, completely in an unexpected trance, he was reminded of where he was. Hell. And the sudden and inescapable fear washed over him. The fear of what would happen of he didn’t obey. Finally finding the strength to lift his head, he glanced up at those who were dragging him. There were two, but neither appeared human. The thing to his right, looping what appeared to be a reptilian tail underneath his right armpit, had pointed scales from the tip of the tail to the base of his neck. Sitting atop broad shoulders, the head swiveled around and glanced down at him. Two yellow eyes, with narrow black irises, blinked sideways. The voice carried a natural gurgle, "He’s awaiting your presence. You’re going to be the final piece of the puzzle." "Heaven will finally succumb." The monster to his left said. Appearing more human than the walking reptile, this being appeared to be a hybrid of a man crossed with a spider. The muscular body was completely human, but when the face turned and glanced down at him, it had rows and rows of beady black eyes, and beneath them, two pinchers that opened up to a fanged mouth. Neither beast put fear in his heart. The only fear he could think of was disobeying Him. * * * * * "MacGregor! Where the hell are you!" Rear Admiral Daniel Davis shouted as he passed through the main hallway and into the living room. The house was silent, and it appeared that nobody was home. Making his way towards the steps, he grabbed the banister and took one step up and once again screamed for his future son-in-law’s attention, "Archibald! Look, I’m not here to argue. I just want to talk about our argument earlier." He waited a moment, listening. Nothing. Feeling somewhat ashamed at the confrontation earlier, Daniel Davis drove around the neighborhood for an hour before finally returning to his daughter’s house. He sat there, holding Aaron in his arms and marveled at his grandson. That’s when the shame hit him. He knew that Archibald was a loving father. A loving fiancé. He just had huge, glaring faults. Holding Aaron made him realize how much he had forgotten what it was like to hold an infant and realize just how much love you can have for a child, even after so little time with them. Aaron had only been in existence, including time in the womb, for 15 months. Yet he could tell how much Heather loved the child, and how much Archibald cared as well. So he wanted to apologize, and let Archibald know that passion can lead to the most wonderful feelings on the planet, and also the worst. But now, with the front door unlocked and the Hummer in the driveway, Daniel Davis couldn’t find him. Once again circling the lower level, Daniel Davis entered the kitchen through the living room and his trained eyes immediately saw something that sent a fit of rage through his heart. Sitting atop the counter was an open bottle of Vodka, and next to it, a glass filled to the rim. The shame was gone in an instant, incinerated by anger and disdain. Daniel Davis raced over to the bottle of vodka, picking it up in one swift motion and slamming it down onto the kitchen floor. With the shards of glass crunching beneath each step, he walked over to the refrigerator and grabbed the magic marker off of the Note-Board and began to write the meanest note he would ever write in his life. * * * * * Still confused and disoriented, he knew the vaulted ceilings and columns gave way to open air. Many steps followed, then a few corridors of equal architectural beauty. Moments later, he was being dragged out onto what appeared to be a marble balcony that reached out farther than anything else around it. As they dragged him out across the balcony, he could finally feel the sun on his back. And see the shadows of the two monsters that carried him, along with his own. His shadow appeared disfigured, but it was probably just the angle of the setting sun. At the balcony’s apex, his back turned away, was a man. A small man. Blonde hair, and dressed in a tailored suit, he never turned to greet them as the two beasts finally dropped him to the cold marble by his feet. It finally occurred to him, amid more confusion, that he couldn’t understand how it felt like his hands were dragging on the grating floor, and bumping against the stone steps, when the two monsters that brought him here were supporting his weight with them. The fear was unbearable. He refused to look up at the blonde haired man, even though he appeared to be rather tiny in size. Even with his own head resting on the marble, his eyes seemed to reach to the center of the suited man’s back. "You’re confused, I can tell." He wanted to lift his head up and look at the man, but he just couldn’t bring himself to. It seemed as if gravity kept him glued to the marble balcony. His head weighed more now than it did only moments ago in the columned room. The Devil spoke, finally turning to face his newest addition, "When you walked the Earth, you were an alcoholic and a horrible father. Upon entering my realm, Hell itself, and since your forefathers were equally as sinful, you’ve earned the rank of General." He could feel the fear just rushing through his body. But somewhere, there was something else. "Upon entering Hell, you lose who you used to be. That explains the confusion, because right now you have no idea who you are. In a way, this is your second birth, and this time instead of your parents choosing your name, you get to choose your own. The two demons who brought you here, their names are Scourge and Morrow." As Typherion continued his speech, the fear the Devil emanated remained oppressive. But inside, the weight of the gravity seemed to lessen, and soon his feet were planted on the marble, and he stood. "Scourge, during his years on Earth, was a rapist. Morrow, well he liked to talk about others behind their backs and one day, someone finally got wind of it and confronted him. He cut their throats and drank the blood that spilled from their wounds." The warmth of the sun was starting to cool as it hid itself halfway behind the peaks of the mountaintops. Looking down at his feet, he noticed there were no toes, and only talons. And in front of him, Typherion finally looked up and made eye contact for the first time. Electric blue, piercing and prodding, they scanned over him. He had to be nearly forty feet taller than the Devil. "You’re probably wondering why you’re so large, and why I’m a mere man. Well, upon entering Hell you get to choose any form you like. You’re in your natural state, while I’ve changed my form to that of something that every man on Earth, and demon in Hell, hates. The man they’ve always wanted to be during their lives. Even though they can’t remember it, they know it. So that brings us to the final question before I send you off to your duties, and hopefully the sacking of God’s palace…" Looking down at the Devil, the fear was quickly fading, being replaced by another emotion all together. "What name shall you be known by, as a General of Hell?" Defiance. The fear completely gone, he stood perfectly erect and let out a roar that nothing on Earth could match. Slamming one of his giant, talon-tipped feet down onto the marble balcony beside Typherion, he spread out what he thought was his arms. Giant wings, with five foot long black horns on the tips reached out fifty feet in each direction, blocking out the setting sun. Across the battlefield, and the marching troops, the shadow resembled that of a cross. The roar echoed all the way to the realm of Heaven and back again. Curling the giant wings around himself and Typherion in a fleshy cocoon, he knelt down and snarled in the face of the Devil, answering in the only name he could think of, "Archibald." The Devil stared back, without fear but still surprised. He began to morph into his natural form, which had rows and rows of pointed teeth and yellow-veined eyes. Archibald continued, both demons face to face within the winged isolation, "And the skin you took from Heather I’ll reclaim when I return." The Devil’s mouth never moved, but he could hear the words within his head, "Return? You’re here for good." Archibald smiled, and could feel a long tongue dart out over multiple rows of pointed teeth within his own mouth. He unfurled his wings and took flight as Typherion glared up at him with what could best be described as an unholy anger. Typherion’s thoughts, once again, penetrated his mind, "I won’t give up my throne so easily." On the Great Battlefield, both the demons and angels stared in a confused awe as the setting sun was once again blocked by this newly flying demon. Then, as quickly as he blocked out the sunlight, it poured through once again. He had vanished into thin air, leaving Typherion alone atop the Devil’s balcony. The sun finally settled behind the mountains, the new darkness casting deeper shadows over Heaven and Hell. * * * * * Quickly scribbling, the letters somewhat distorted by his anger, Daniel Davis wrote in black magic marker atop the Note-Board: "I was going to give you a chance. I had caved in and started to believe your story about love, but now I see what you TRULY love. I left the glass of vodka on the counter because I wanted you to have one last drink to numb yourself over the fact that you just lost your son. Forever." * * * * * Archibald MacGregor awoke with a shock and a palm on his chest. He looked up and saw the angel looking down in momentary confusion. Wentworth Matthews eyes seemed to be in a distant place, with his head cocked slightly to the side out of curiosity. He seemed to be listening to something, or someone only he could hear. "You’re right, I don’t remember anything. Just white light, and the cold." Archibald said, surprised to find that no fluid tried to purge itself from his lungs. Archibald Wentworth Matthews stared down at Archibald MacGregor in awe. His mouth went slack, and he stood up and slowly backed away towards the door. "What’s wrong? You look like you’ve seen a ghost!" Archibald said, chuckling at the obvious and cheesy joke, considering the situation, "What’s the matter, did I piss off God or something?" On the lower level of the house, the front door slammed shut so hard the window shattered. Archibald looked around, equally as confused at the source of the clatter as well as the state of his angel friend. "Who was that?" Wentworth Matthews said nothing, eyes wide. He pressed himself into the corner of the bathroom. A few beads of sweat began to form at his hairline. "Hey, what’s wrong? What happened?" Finally, breaking his self-imposed silence with a quivering voice, he started to talk to himself, "We were wrong. Everything was interpreted wrong." Archibald sat up, still confused, "What? Wrong about what?" "From this point on, everything changes." * * * * *
