Ron

Date: 2004-09-12 “You know, Ron, I’ve had just about enough of this crap.” Ron Black looked up from the menu at his older brother Joe. The elder Black sibling was scowling over his shoulder at the television in the corner of the bar, expressing his disgust with the wrestling program on the idiot box. Ron shrugged, “Then don’t pay attention to it. They cook up salmon really good here, you oughta try that.” “I’m fifty-nine years old, Ron. As an old fart, I’m qualified to complain for as long as I like about anything I like, as well as eat steak whenever I choose. Hey, sweetie?” Joe had flagged down a waitress, “My brother and I each need a Coors Light, I’m having the t-bone rare, he’s having the salmon crap that’s grilled like it’s real food or whatever the hell it is, and could you change the channel on that television there? Put on ESPN or something, they don’t have wrestling on that channel, right?” As the waitress retreated, Ron looked back at Joe, “How the hell can you live like a slob, eat poorly, work out only twice a week, and stay in that good shape?” Joe smirked back at his brother, “You forgot the alcohol.” Ron rolled his eyes, “How could I forget that? It’s bullshit and it’s unfair, Joseph, I’m on blood pressure medication, low cholesterol diet, all that crap.” Joe shrugged, smiling at the waitress dropped off their beer. The waitress produced a remote changed the channel over to the Texas A&M vs Utah. “Now this is more like it,” said Joe. Ron shook his head, “Okay, I’m changing the subject, but I’ll have you know that I’m not done complaining on the fact that you lead a charmed life with your health.” Joe nodded, taking a drink from his beer, “Fair enough. Be my guest.” “How come you’re not watching the wrestling anymore? You used to keep your eye on everything. You’d think what with the wrestling school, and your position in tSC, you’d be watching everything.” Joe snorted, “First of all, the school is in a great spot. We made Rana Venenosa, and he’s now the best in ring wrestler the fWo has ever seen. We’re booked solid with kids throwing down top dollar for a chance to be trained at our facility, not to mention the interest from existing superstars who want to improve on their skills. We’re financially set on that business enterprise indefinitely.” “As for tSC, I don’t really care. I’ve been marginalized. I’m just a road agent there; Harvard doesn’t even see me anymore. Rosemary is interested in doing things her way, and that’s that. I corral the talent and that’s about it, I don’t even get asked for suggestions anymore.” “Now with those aspects covered, wrestling itself is god damned painful to watch. We’ve been overdue for things to change for the past few years, but the only change coming down the pipe is things are steadily getting a little worse and worse. I’m frankly disgusted, even with tSC. The in ring performances there have slacked off. Initially they were looking at a change, a dramatic improvement in the quality of the presentation, but since then it’s slacked off. They’re phoning it in for television, and throwing in an ounce of extra effort for paid broadcasts. It’s a crock. Other promotions, I’ve seen all the same crap before done by better men, in a better time.” As Joe finished his speech, the waitress brought out Ron’s salmon and Joe’s t-bone. Both men entered into silence as they ate. Ron watched his older brother plow through his steak, not even enjoying it. “You’re really upset over it,” said Ron. Joe shook his head as he reached for his beer. “No,” said Ron, “you really are. You’re not even tasting your food before you’re swallowing it. You’re really pissed off at wrestling.” Joe’s mood darkened. He leaned over the table, and said, “I’m trying to enjoy myself at dinner with my little brother. You’re making that hard for me, Ronnie.” Ron held of his hands, backing off the subject. Both men resumed eating. After enjoying their food for bit, Ron finally spoke again, “So is that why you’re trying to relaunch your wrestling career?” Joe’s eyes opened wide, “Excuse me?” “Com’on Joe. I’m your little brother; you had a new pair of wrestling boots made and you billed it on the school’s account. What’s going on?” Joe deadpanned, “So that’s how you known what I’ve got you for xmas every year.” Ron couldn’t help but laugh, “Nice. But I’m not letting this one go, Joseph. What are you doing?” Joe wiped his mouth off with a napkin, stalling for time. He took a drink from his beer, emptying the bottle. His fingers drummed the table, “Well, first of all, I’m not relaunching my career at fifty-nine years of age.” “Your son says that the tSC guys still ask you to wrestle and you always say no,” said Ron. Joe took a deep breath, struggling not to let the forbidden subject cause him to lose his composure. Even so, the vein stood out in his forehead as he said, “I’m not going to talk about my son.” “Then level with me, Joe, tell me what’s going on.” “I’ve entered into a tournament,” said Joe. As he spotted a waitress he called out holding up his empty beer, “Could you bring me another, sweetie? Thanks.” “Tell me about the tournament,” said Ron. “GTT4, hosted by PTC.” Ron laughed out loud, “I’d heard that someone from tSC was represented in that tournament. I didn’t imagine it was you though.” Joe frowned at Ron, “Well it’s me, happy now?” “Who’s your first round opponent?” “It’s this kid called Avatar William Gwynn.” “Do you expect to win?” Joe glared at Ron, “Do I expect to beat Gwynn? Of course I do. I’m in fantastic shape and I’ve wrestle more matches than most of these guys have drawn breaths.” “That’s not quite what I meant act-” But Joe had cut Ron off. “You wouldn’t believe this kid. A freaking lunatic, like every other collection of freaking lunatics they try to feed into this friggin’ crap. Get somebody all crazy get somebody who’s unpredictable,” Joe stuffed another slice of steak into his mouth, Ron inwardly sighed, having to listen to another of Joe’s passionate rants on the state of the business today and the lives it’s claiming. Joe continued, “How many of these stupid kids become monsters because somebody told them it’s how to get over? How many kids actually become lunatics because they think it gives them an edge? Know how many of them live long? Know how many recklessly make enemies destroying their own bodies, living fast and furious and dying at age thirty? These stupid fucks are barely adults, and they come of age believing that extreme behavior pays off. And further the promotions that thrive on it through marketing the breaking of these bodies and the destroying of these lives are thriving on blood mon- guuurk!” Ron was quite surprised as Joe went silent. He watched his older brother blink in panic; Joe’s hands quickly went to his throat, trying to find a solution to this sudden problem. Ron realized two things. One. His older brother was choking on his steak. Two. He wasn’t sure how he felt about that. In the split seconds that followed this realization, Ron flashed back, thinking over his life with his older brother. == “I don’t want to do this Joey,” said Ron. It was the summer of 1961. Joe was sixteen years old and Ron was twelve. “Com’on Ronnie, I need you here. If Dad finds out that I don’t come home tonight, he’ll kill me. You’ve gotta cover for me.” “But Joey-” “Ronnie, how often have I protected you?” “From your friends! And you took your time getting them off me!” “Hey, I did the best I could, and it could’ve been worse if I hadn’t stopped ‘em.” Joe Black opened the bedroom window that he and his younger brother shared. As he climbed onto the dresser to make his exit, he said, “I’m counting on you, Ron. I have a date with Sandra and if I stood her up, I’d be in serious trouble. Tonight I’m going to become a man, Ron, and with her underwear for proof I’ll have two hundred dollars from the bet, of which you’ll get twenty-five.” Ron’s lip trembled as Joe looked back at him from the window, “You know what you have to do.” Joe jumped down out of sight. After a moment, Ron sighed and shut the window. He changed into his pajamas, turned off the lights, and slipped under the covers of his older brother’s bed. A tall figure opened the door. Randal Black, their father’s imposing figure back lit by the hallway light, “Boys? Hey. Where’s Ron at, Joe?” Ron winced, but played his part, “Hurh? Dad? I dunno, he was here when I went to bed.” The growl that came from throat of Ron’s father sent made Ron shiver, “Dammit Joe, Ron’s now starting to take after you. I hope you’re happy. I’ve had just about enough of this crap. When I find Ron, and he tastes my belt, I hope you feel a good solid measure of guilt over how much pain his behind is going to be in. You think on that, Joe.” His dad closed the door. Ron started crying, feeling the unfairness of the situation, that not only would he be beaten for Joe’s crime, but also that Joe would certainly not feel an ounce of the proscribed guilt his father had laid out for the wrong son. == “I’ve had just about enough of this crap, Joe.” It was 1973, and the Black Brothers had just finished another South-West tag title defense. Ron was applying ice to his back and left arm, as Joe looked on with disgust on his face. “What are you talking about?” asked Joe. “I’m taking all the bumps for our team. You’re taking all the dominant spots. I’m sick of it.” Joe sat down on the bench next to Ron, “Hey, you got the pinfall tonight!” “After you clobbered Rocky with the horseshoe for the finish.” “Ronnie, we just did the miraculous tonight. In front of a crowd of rednecks, we got them to boo the cowboys, white guys, and turned a pair of Negroes face. So everything had to be perfect tonight. And we did it. You were perfect tonight. That’s something to be proud of, Ron. Lighten up. I’ve about got this business figured out. We’re going to be making money hand over fist. You’ve just got to trust me on this.” The words from his brother washed over him, and though it didn’t wipe away the resentment he was feeling, it pushed those thoughts further away. Ron sighed, suddenly uncertain about how entitled he was to his outrage. “Do you trust me, Ron?” Joe Black asked, his eyes boring into Ron’s. Ron looked down, “Yeah, I trust you.” “Great!” said Joe, slapping Ron on his back, completely oblivious to the wince it brought out of his brother as Joe struck a sore spot, “Oh, by the way, I need the hotel room tonight. Got a nice piece of ass coming by. I’ll see you in the morning for breakfast.” With Joe leaving Ron to his ice and injuries, Ron sat there, seething, wondering where he’d be sleeping tonight. == It was 1985. Joe Black wasn’t on top of the business, Vince was. The pickup pulled up to the lot they had purchased. The town of St. Joe, Texas had always been small, with plenty of open wilderness at decent prices. From the driver’s seat, Joe said, “Well, here we are.” Joe’s ten year old son, Randall, asked, “Where are we? There’s nothing here.” Ron got out of the car, and looked at the dusty landscape of tall grass. He’d been afraid that his older brother had purchased a plot of pure desert, but fortunately Joe’d come through. Joe held the door open as Randall jumped down out of the pickup. “This,” he said, “is going to be a place where Ron and I are going to teach young men how to wrestle.” “You’re going to build here? You’re going to pour concrete slabs to build on right? I don’t like the ground here. There’s probably rocks under the soil, since this part’s never been a farm.” Ron and Joe shared an uneasy glance. Joe’s son had a way of freaking them out whenever he opened his mouth to say things that shouldn’t be coming out of the mouth of ten year olds. “Well, Randall,” said Ron, “you’re right, we’re probably going to pour con-” Turning to glare at him, Randall said, “I told you. From now on, I want to be called Rook.” The boy turned and ran through the tall grass away from the adults. They were a little relieved. “So,” said Ron turning an unfriendly look on his older brother, “this is where our dreams are going to die?” Joe frowned, “It isn’t my fault. We were cheated. That’s all it was, this is a good place to build from. We can change the business as it stands.” “Yeah?” asked Ron, growing more and more hostile, “You think so? What are we going to tell a guy that’s only five foot nine inches tall? Sorry kid, we can make you a phenomenal wrestler, but if you ain’t a huge son of a bitch, Vince won’t be able to see you.” “Look. The showmanship is going to pass. People are going to get tired of it as it repeats itself, and they’ll start wanting more. And the big hunks of meat aren’t going to be able to offer them more. Our kids will be able to offer everything they can hope to see. Look at Tijuana? How big are those guys?” “Oh like they’ll give us a foot in the door down there. And worse, what will they pay us? Pesos? This is the most idiotic idea you’ve ever had.” “This will work Ron, I promise you. Our prime is gone. But we can still matter. Time is on our side. As we teach more and more kids, we’ll make it work. The business will change, and it’ll change because the kids we train will set the standards for every other superstar out there.” “We’re only here,” said Ron, marching up to Joe to look him in the eye, “because Geraldine caught you fucking around on the road. The one in Missouri was the last straw, and keeping operations local made her happy.” Joe met his gaze and glared back, “I don’t think you’re really in a position to judge my marital life, faggot.” Those were fighting words, and Ron was a moment away from strangling his older brother, but he was interrupted by a small voice shouting, “PLANCHA~!” Randall had circled around them, climbing onto the roof of the pickup’s cab. He’d leapt off, crashing into both men, and it was all their reflexes to not drop the kid. As Ron put Randall on his two feet on the ground, Randall asked, “What’s a faggot?” Joe and Ron locked eyes. After a moment, Joe said, “A cigarette. Uncle Ron wanted a cigarette.” Pulling his pack of Marlboros from his shit pocket, Joe held the pack out to Ron. Ron took one, though he didn’t particularly care for smoking. As he lit up, he imagined putting the `fag’ out in his older brother’s eye. == It was 1988. Joe’s divorce with Geraldine was final. Ron felt for his older brother, and particularly his nephew Randall, but he couldn’t help but feel sympathetic to his ex-wife. Ron was directing the kids that they’d (mostly Ron alone though) trained, to load the van. With everything packed and ready, Ron went to look for Joe. Joe had always been lazy, but since the divorce he moved around like a zombie. Ron found him sitting outside in the sun, just staring blankly. “We’re ready,” said Ron. “I’m not going.” “What?” “I said I’m not going.” Ron smacked his palm against his forehead, “I don’t fucking believe this. This was your fucking idea. This was your dream. If we could get our guys trained out here and get them to do shows in Mexico in addition to our own tours they’d have an edge over all the other guys they were competing with. This is our chance. You wanted this. Now you don’t want to go?” Joe was unmoved by Ron’s outrage. As he stood there, Ron seriously contemplated putting a bullet in his brother’s head; after all, it would most likely do Joe a big favor. “I just got a letter from Geraldine. She informed me that I won’t be seeing my son again for a long, long time.” Ron was moved, just a little, but he couldn’t help but think that she was justified. Perhaps he wouldn’t need to shoot his brother. Perhaps he could just leave the gun out for him to find. “Dad, Uncle Ron.” Joe nearly jumped out of his shoes turning around to see that Randall was there, holding bags and covered in sweat. Incredulous, Ron asked, “How’d you get here?” “I walked from the bus station. Mom and I … well, we had a disagreement. So I wrote some letters to Judge Thornton, going into pretty great detail what I wanted and how that differed from what Mom wanted. But to make sure he believes I’m sincere, I’m having an outburst of defiance.” Joe blinked, “You’re what?” “An outburst of defiance, so he doesn’t think I’m just all talk. In precise terms, I’m going to Mexico with you guys,” Looking over his shoulder at the packed up van, “And it looks like I’m just in time. Anyway, before you both start objecting, I promise that I’ll be on my best behavior, and I’ll do whatever you ask me to with regard to helping out here.” Joe swallowed and said, “Aren’t you supposed to be at a chess tournament right now?” Randall shrugged, “Fuck chess. I want to be here.” Joe stared for a moment, before his cool resumed, “Well, you’re right. You’re going to be working pretty hard if you’re coming with us. Com’on, let’s get you changed out of that stuff, you’re going to stink up the van in those clothes.” As Ron watched his brother restored to life by the love his son, he felt relieved that his brother had a second chance, but uncertain on whether he actually deserved it or not. == In the present, Ron looked across the table at Joe, whose rant on the business today had been interrupted by a hunk of steak blocking his windpipe. The flashbacks came and went quickly, and Ron had a choice to make. Deep inside all those memories, was a loathing for his older brother, for the abuse, the exploitation of his trust. Rising from his chair, not really realizing that he was doing it, Ron came around the table and applied the Heimlich maneuver. Two tugs later and Joe spat out a hunk of steak that struck Ron’s plate and bounced off to land on Ron’s chair. Joe coughed, his face pale as he blinked his surprise away. The waitress arrived with his beer, and noticing his pallor, asked, “Are you okay, sir?” Joe nodded, “I’m fine,” adding, “Thanks sweetie,” as he reached up for his beverage. He took a drink and let out a sigh, “Well, took you long enough.” Ron picked up the half chewed piece of steak with his napkin and tossed it at his brother’s chest, “You’re welcome.” Both men were silent. After a moment, Joe said, “Hey, well thanks fo-” “Save it,” said Ron, “tell me one thing. Why are you in this tournament?” “Because I’ve just about had enough of the crap that I’m watching on television. I’m tired of seeing mediocrity. I’m tired of watching the end of a wrestling show and telling myself that not a damn thing of interest has happened for two hours. I’m tired of being bored, and I’m tired of everybody thinking that it’s all okay. I find the general state of this industry contemptible, and what’s worse about it, is that all the usual channels that I can work through haven’t produced any results. I work for one of the best new promotions, and I can’t influence the product the way I want to. We’re training the best new stars in the business, but they’re leaving our school and forgetting everything that they’ve been taught once they get signed.” “So,” said Ron, “you’re going to save the business again, aren’t you? You’re going to go in there and show them all how it’s done?” Joe looked at Ron earnestly, “I’m looking for a new way to make things better. I don’t know these people who are running this thing, most of their superstars don’t know me. I figure in terms of what I can see, what I can learn, and what I can teach, I’ve got at least a shot.” Ron met his gaze, “I see. Well, good luck with it, Joe. And if I might make a suggestion, chew your damn food before swallowing. You’re going out there by yourself. I’m not with you, none of your buddies or friends or allies are there. If you choke in the ring, you’re finished.” Ron tossed his napkin on the table and stood up. Joe watched him start to leave and called after him, “Ron!” “What?” “Do you … do you hate me?” Ron sighed, wondering how he could express his mixed feelings, “I find you to be one contemptible son of a bitch, and I resent you and a lot of the things that you’ve done that have screwed me over. But you’re my brother, and I love you.” Joe nodded once, and then quirked his eyebrow, “You didn’t answer the question though.” Ron smirked, saying, “I know,” and proceeded to walk out the door. ** All persons in this body of work are fictional.
