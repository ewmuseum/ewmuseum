The Chronicals of Alcaeus: After Mortality

23 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It is sometimes the naïve scholar who claims that war is fruitless, and advocates the altruistic path of ‘peace in our time’. It is sometimes the ambitious scholar who makes the audacious claim that peace is merely the ‘absence of war’. Through War, the world exists, and in its absence, the world grows from its lessons. Would society have evolved to its 21st century height had the Romans not have pursued their vicious conquests of Eurasia? At the same time, would it have sunk to such decadence? Had the development of the weapons of mass destruction this century has witnessed not taken place, would we live in this era of peace and prosperity throughout the west? Would we have television, radio, aviation or any other technological marvel mortals take for granted…without war? Where, indeed, would we stand if Sun Tsu hadn’t written his masterpiece so many centuries ago? Had the Greeks not developed their phalanx, had empires not been carved out of steel and blood…where, indeed. Most probably, we might still be that hair covered mammal, still figuring out what to do with a stick lying in the sand. And what if, perhaps, the deranged war-like creatures Neanderthals had driven our Homocapien ancestors to extinction through the vicious and bloody slaughter they were capable of… It is no coincidence that history is preoccupied by the achievements, legacies and destructive capacities of war; for it is the faculty of war that has sculpted the very civilisations that embrace the concept of peace, prosperity and growth. We are, all of us, children of the very steel and blood that drove the barbarians from the borders of Rome; that took the Macedonian armies to the farthest reaches of the mythological East; that stood up to Louis XIV as hegemon of Europe; that defeated the perpetrators of the holocaust, and her allies. Why, then, do I feel like a traitor to humanity? ______________________________________________________________ It was through war, that I found my mortal feet in a world of daemons, angels and giants; a world where immortality in one form or another was waiting around every corner…for the few brave souls to clutch at the loose sand and find gold between their fingers. And it was through war I became what I became. The history of my mortality, and subsequent journey to transcend its limits is embedded in the very gold-ridden sand I speak of: before Louis XIV; before Julius Caesar; before Alexander… In the Macedonian army I found true virtue; the ability to be a part of something that would leave its stamp on history. The troubles of my life vanished on the battlefield, where the steel sword sliced cleanly into the naked flesh of better men, and removed the heads of their lesser counterparts. On that field of battle, we were all equals…librarians, mathematicians, actors, musicians and peasant boys; we were all men; and then we were all dust. But on occasion, Apollo would smile down on his children: the gleam of the sun would catch the glint of gold in a field of sand…and it was in this glint, that Anacletus Alcaeus was truly born. ______________________________________________________________ In the years before my succumbing to darkness, I found my pride and sanity in the face of my enemies. When I joined the ranks of Perdiccus I’s Macedonian war machine, I didn’t know what to expect. From the first young man, probably no older than myself, that I killed in battle, to the brave and arrogant warrior who stood up to Cyrus, the Persian general on the field of battle after the peace of Kallas…I grew. It was in the eyes of my enemies that I found my courage, and my drive. A hero I was heralded to be, and I found my glint of gold. Men feared me, men loved me, but they all respected me. But it was on the fields of Persia herself, I found my weakness. My father disowned me due to my bloody conquests on the field of battle, and I had gone to Persia a disheartened man; nothing to live for, only the blood and pain of battle. And so it was, that in 443BC, I once again travelled East; not as Alcaeus the soldier, but Alcaeus the mercenary. Paid by a prestigious Athenian lord to hunt down and bring back the head of a travelling Persian merchant. I still remember his exact words when I found him. He had been adamant to take my blood, but I’d beaten him in battle; but this frail man, whose strength I’d never seen in any mortal man, spoke to me: “Spare me…heal me…and I will give you this gift of immortality. It is no curse…and true, it is frowned upon by the Gods. But join me, and they will have no jurisdiction over you.” His offer came to me in thoughts, as well as words. And so, Alcaeus the mercenary died, and Alcaeus the vampire was born. This, brief history of my rebirth, is perhaps necessary for where I am about to take you. For, where I must go and you must follow as my readers, is to a place where no mortal man could have gone. A place filled with dark dreams and savage desires; of mortal men crazed with fear and immortal beasts savaged with eternity. That I am a vampire does not matter to me, nor should it…for I was, and always had been a student of war; and when Palius, my new merchant master, left me…it was back into the only job I had ever been suited to that I returned. ______________________________________________________________ And so, we skip. I never again returned to Greece a hero. I never saw my Mother’s face again, filled with anguish over the departure of her son who had been groomed to be a musician. Never again would I see Lionidas…my father; the man who had first involuntarily given me the desire for battle, and the man who had left me in the ruins of my own regret in Athens. It was in Rome that I continued my story, and the chronicles of Alcaeus. In the very year that Censorship was established in the new Roman city, I crept behind her walls in the dead of night, and found for myself a place to set up my home. The expulsion of Tarquinius Superbus by Brutus, and the formation of the Republic, was still on the lips of every Roman I met in the streets of the embryonic city. Even though none of these young scholars and warriors had witnessed the ordeal, their fathers and grandfathers had…and it was through the inherited stories that the legend of Brutus was created. He was, I am sure, not as the people saw him; but who he was is unimportant. Who he was believed to be was what created Rome, I mean the true Rome. A sense of infallibility, of growth, new ideas and the progress of the arts of war and men, floated in the air. The people were infected with their created idols and legends, and were enthused with the same passion they had delegated to the names they spoke on scented air. Only fifteen years after Brutus had ‘saved’ the Republic, the first secession of the Plebs came to be in 494BC. This was, before my time as a citizen and stalker of Rome, followed by the Lex Publilia Voleronis: the Concilium of the Plabs and Tribunes was recognised. When I sneaked into the city, the law of the twelve tables had been passed, the second secession of the Plebs had come and gone, and the Valerian-Horation laws had defined the rights of tribunes and had established the number at ten. The Questors were elected by the Plebs, and the Comitia Tributa was established in 447BC; and in 444BC, the first of the Consuls was replaced by military tribunes. I built my home in this city of prospering ideas and cultural evolution, and even found for myself a way to satiate my craving for blood: every city had her exiles, and Rome was no exceptions. I would attend the meetings of the Plebs that would decide which vagrant was to leave the city walls and never return, and in doing so they would decide who I hunted that night. It pains me now that I could have been so indiscriminate in my early years, but in retrospect I had no desire for morality and conscience: I had divorced these virtues when my father had divorced me from his life. I also found a way to cheat Apollo. I discovered that with the right amount of preparation, and caution, I could create for myself a paste from the ashes near the pyres, the sand of the grounds just outside Rome and the new creation of Clay. It served a dual purpose, as I soon realised. Not only did it render my pale skin darker, and give it a tanned glaze so as to render me inconspicuous in public – but it also helped reduce the irritation from the Sun. It was still painful and uncomfortable walking out on a hot day in the Roman city, but it was bearable, and I had decided early on, it was worth the discomfort to allow myself to live during the days when mortals were most active. Despite my love for war, I spent my initial period in Rome watching the people go about their daily lives, and reading up on exploits in Greece. I yearned for home, but convinced myself that to return would be a pain unbearable, and thus endeavoured to stay away from my homeland. I set up for myself a quiet and inconspicuous trade as a trader. I would leave the Rome, and explore the Italian country for days at a time, which was made much easier by the new paste I had developed and my ability to run at accelerated speeds. I would find interesting objects on my travels, and return only to sell them to ambitious Roman citizens as ornaments, or as ancient artefacts of the tribal savages to the north; even if they were in fact only a piece of hardened sandstone, with scratches etched into it by the perpetual journey of time. It was not until the year 396 that I joined the Roman military, and that my chronicles can truly begin. ______________________________________________________________ In 396BC, military pay was introduced. The Roman army had, before then been a collection of mercenaries and slaves, who either fought for greed or fear. The few exploits the Roman military had had in the northern stretches of Italy had been mildly successful at best. A few village militias had been defeated, and outposts had been set up towards Patavium. The military was used as an enforcement wing of the Republic, quelling disturbances in the city, and making sure that conflicts throughout the Italian region never spilt over towards Rome. The ideal way to quell such conflicts was by subjugating the people, and that was what Rome did. First we were a city…then we were a cluster…and soon became a region. But my first real action in the new Roman military was as part of the new Hastati: foot soldiers, equipped with Greek shortswords and northern spears. The Hastati were a versatile group of ex-mercenaries and new recruits, usually who had seen war before. When I had explained I had been in the Macedonian and Greek armies, fought in Persia (though lying about the dates) they soon accepted me into the ranks. And so, I quickly became a field commander in the eighth Hastati unit of the fifth legion of the Republic of Rome. By 391BC, we’d heard of the troubles to the North. The villages we were either allied with, or had set up outposts in, came back with stories of barbarian savages surging southwards. Men returned having lost loved ones, with tales of burnt villages, women raped, and children slaughtered. The northern barbarians were like a plague that was journeying south, threatening the new civilization we had created. Soon, a legion of over-confident Romans was sent out to stop the tribal surge, but when they returned, it was not to the sound of trumpets of victory, or to the adoration of the new generation of peace loving Romans. They returned in drips and drabs, broken units, savaged men, carrying broken swords and the corpses of their comrades. And in 390BC, the Celts sacked Rome… We were not unprepared. The horde had been seen hurtling towards Rome, only thirty miles North of the City. They had learnt from villages and the doomed legion that a new prosperous city had been set up to the South, and were on their way. The Hastati were prepared, and we were lectured. Our commander, of the fifth legion, was a man named Trebonnus Julii. The name, at the time, meant nothing to me…but in retrospect, I realise now, that this was the man that was responsible for the Julii dynasty of Roman families, and ultimately the ancestor of Julius Caesar, perhaps the most famous Roman of them all…and a man who would exile me from the city I grew to love over three hundred years later. We were spread thin on the left flank of the army created to defend the Republic. The men were tired, frightened of the stories of their defeated comrades, and worn out by the struggle of training and rigours of city maintenance. We had never truly been trained to fight a full-scale war, especially not one where we were to be outnumbered seven to one by brutish beasts, who had seen action from their teenage years. The sound of screaming first came from the Right flank. I was totally unprepared for the events that transpired over the next few hours. The screams became louder, and louder, and murmurs went through the army of giants with huge axes tearing through our right flank. The men were shaken, and I immediately saw that this was not going to be a victorious day. Within half an hour of the first scream, from our lonely thin position on the left flank, we could see the rain of spears flying over our brethren. But then they hit our left as well… Blood spat onto my face before I realised that the unit to the left of ours had been outflanked and outnumbered by a huge ravenous war band of Celts, their faces painted blue, and their voices painted with the years of conflict and blood. I commanded our unit to turn to face the onslaught, but it was no use. We were soon flooded by more than eight thousand angry savages. They completely enveloped our left flank, and whether it was voluntary or involuntary, surrounded us. The next thing I knew, an axe flew past my ear, and knocked my right man to the ground, his arm severed from his body. My instincts took over, and a roar the likes I’d never heard came from my lungs. I could visibly see the enemy hesitate upon hearing the roar of this six foot nine man, who looked more like a Greek than a Roman, with the finest crop of blonde hair a man could wish for. In their hesitation, I recaptured my pride, my confidence…and the primal instincts took over. There was no unit to command in my mind, no flailing legion, no sacking of Rome…there was only a sea of blue faces waiting for the slaughter at the end of my steel sword. I thrust my spear into the chest of the man whose axe had crippled my brethren, and unsheathed my sword. My shield instinctively rammed into the chests of six oncoming barbarians, and their fall unbalanced the dozen or so behind them. My sword swung to the left, removing a startled brave of his ear, and gifting bleeding scars to two others. My shield followed, crashing into the knees of some three men, before my sword wrenched the life from their bones. I felt a blade pierce my back, and sent my sword back, into the gut of another victim of this fit of blood-ridden rage. I felt good…I felt really good. While the men around me fell and whimpered, screamed and whined, I laughed and yelled at the aggressors as they fell at my knees. The true nature of what I was took over. The scent of blood filled my lungs, and filled me further with the desire to hear the dying yells of mortal men. The men began to pile up, as the smoke from some fire to our East filled the air above the carnage. A clash of steel onto my blade forced my shield into the air, and through the throat of the man holding his sword against mine. The feeling of a spear through my side, gave way to the euphoria of my blade removing its owner of his arm. My shield shattered into a thousand pieces, as an axe crashed into my fist. I felt pain only briefly, as my preternatural blood quickly clotted the wound, and my skin hardened once again. I grabbed a man by his throat with the hand that had lost its shield, and drove my sword into the forehead of his neighbour. I crushed the man’s throat with my massive hand, and he fell like a sack of potatoes to the blood stained grass. The murderous yells I’d heard when they’d attacked gave way to the shrieking of men who now stood no closer than four feet from me, Alcaeus the warrior, Alcaeus the mercenary, Alcaeus the Roman…Alcaeus the Vampire. I suddenly realised why they were so terrified. A man had wedged his dagger into the back of my neck, and the skin around it had hardened so quickly, that the blade hadn’t made it to my spine, and was sticking out of the back of my head; and in my arms, the man who had driven it there was bent over, and my mouth sucked the blood from his neck. I dropped him quickly to the floor, realising what I was doing, and held my sword up to the sea of blue-painted Celts. The terror in their eyes fed my rage, and I rampaged towards them. My enemies almost parted as I tore through them; it was as if they were too frightened to attack me, and simply stepped back. Men fell as I raged through, the blood lust taking over. Brief feelings of pain as terrified warriors drove their blades into my chest, my abdomen, my arms, my legs; were outshone by the scent and satisfaction of naked flesh, tearing on my increasingly sharpened blade. My voice had risen beyond the capacity of a mortal man, and I could hear nothing whatsoever. My vision was blurred from my lust, and all that told me I was alive was the pressure on my arms as my sword flew into this or that man’s sternum. It was carnage… Until my blade stuck. It crashed into something hard, and wouldn’t budge, no matter how hard I pulled. My vision slowly unblurred, and my voice subsided…in front of me were no men, no blood, but a tree, and the beginning of a forest. My sword was wedged into the trunk of the tree, the blood dripping from its gleaming hilt and blade, my hands painted red, my uniform torn. I looked down, and discomfort struck me, as I realised two broken blades were stuck, one in my left breastplate of my chest, the other in my stomach. I felt ill. The scent of blood became sickening, and I let loose a flurry of vomit and blood onto the floor, falling to my knees. I remembered where I was again, and turned, leaning against the tree, back towards Rome. The City looked a giant distance, maybe a mile away, and smoke rose from its towers. Rome was burning… Before me though, stood the Celtic horde. Never before have I seen more frightened and subdued looks…they knew, and I knew that they knew, at that moment that I was no mortal man. Scars caressed my naked body, scars which should have been formed over weeks, but had been formed over minutes. I was thin, pale from lack of blood, the paste had worn off with the blades and shields that had been thrown against my skin. They stood back, as I got to my feet. I stumbled backwards, into the forest, as the city…my Rome…burned under the watchful eye of Apollo. I fled, as fast as I could, into the forest, and to the North. The rest is a blur, but I do remember that I felt scared… I was scared of myself. I should be dead, I thought…but I wasn’t. It was only then that the true nature of what I was hit me…and from that day, I never looked at the mirror the same way again. ______________________________________________________________ It is through war that we build for ourselves our realities: our futures, our civilizations, our customs. The men who fight for what they believe in, or what a nation stands for, become legends, embedded in the legacies of their victories. However, those same men are scarred for life. You could never look to them to occupy positions of communal mediocrity. They could not teach the next generation, or drive modern ambulances. They are broken, faceless victims of savagery. Their names are etched in stone, but their lives are lost on the winds of the moment, and supplanted into books written by the naïve and objective witnesses who were never there. It is these men who save nations, create democracies, champion civility…but ultimately destroy themselves. I stood that day, a victor on a battlefield of defeat. As I watched from a distance my city burn, and heard the screams of women, as countless barbarians ravaged my new home, I realised that those memories would haunt my dreams forever. I am Alcaeus the warrior, Alcaeus the mercenary, Alcaeus the Roman, Alcaeus the vampire…and Alcaeus the mourner. The mourner of the souls that were lost to the sands of time.
