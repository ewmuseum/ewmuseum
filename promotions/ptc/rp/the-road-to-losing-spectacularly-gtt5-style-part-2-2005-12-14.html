The Road to Losing … SPECTACULARLY!! GTT5 Style Part 2

14 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-14 Fatbadger looked sternly ahead. Both literally and figuratively. He sat and stared now, as he sat and looked to the future: the second round of GTT5. He hadn’t said it out loud. This was actually only the third time he’d been able to bring himself to think it, the first time he hasn’t passed out because of it. This was the second straight year he had entered this tournament, and though he talked like it, he never actually imagined getting this far. The second round. He’d never been here alone. He missed his friends, the Boomer Sooners, and wished that they could have seen his match. Fatbadger bit his bottom lip. Ragnarok was a brutal opponent, one of the most equal that Badger had ever faced. The end of the match came down to odd circumstances, not that those where anything he was unfamiliar with, seeing Badger barely come out on top. Ragnarok fought as if there were something driving him other than simple self desire to prove himself, it was more like he needed to. Fatty had claimed before the match a loss to Ragnarok in the first round would not have been spectacular enough, but he has since changed his mind. He hoped Ragnarok the best on his future goals, not really understanding that it was the destruction of people like him and his family. It made for an awkward handshake. Fatbadger looked at his hand and made a tight fist. It shook a bit as he clenched it. He needed to keep sight of his goal in this tournament … building on his status as one of PTC’s biggest jobbers, if not the best, but also to aspire to finding the most spectacular of losses that he’d ever faced. The bigger the stakes, the more spectacular the loss and the more spectacular the after-buzz. Perhaps it was the pressure, or better yet, the lack thereof afterwards. Like putting too much pressure on one of your legs. The longer is stays there, the more dull its senses. But once it’s gone, the tingle comes. Like a background noise at first, it’s hardly noticeable … but then it grows and takes over. The feeling consumes not only your leg, but your whole body, your whole attention, your whole mind. Most people couldn't stand it, trying their hardest to avoid it. Badger loved it. But he’d had enough simple tingles, they didn’t feel as good as they used to. He found that as the matches grew, the tingle began to grow with them, and each loss was that much more spectacular. He began to seek out bigger matches with bigger opponents and larger stakes. It had become like a vice to him, not like he didn’t have enough of them already. It was what led him to this tournament once again. Fatbadger gritted his teeth and squinted his eyes. His face wrinkled and contorted and flushed. Is this new opponent the key to getting that loss? A big enough name? Was there enough at stake? This was again, the farthest that Fatty had gone in a singles tournament in his long career. Is that enough? His opponent was known throughout PTC, having once held the Infinity Gauntlet Championship. Is that enough. Do those two ingredients mix together to form some sort of sweet tasting flavor that Badger would gladly sprinkle over his Funyuns? Did that analogy go to far? That fact that he was even questioning this at all proved well enough that this was not the time or the match. The questions brought the risk of the answers being wrong, the one risk he couldn't take. He would have to reach a point in this tournament where there could be no question. Then, and only then would he get some of that sweet, sweet spectacularness that only a major loss can bring. Meaning that his opponent became that much more important. Fatbadger ran his fingers through his hair, stopping halfway down and yanking harshly at it. DarkStar was a mysterious person indeed. Every time Badger caught a glimpse of him, he gasped for air, having to catch himself from falling, like getting hit by a strong sense of déjà vu. There was something familiar about him, but each time he thought he’d placed him, DarkStar would make eye contact. Those black pools of stars. Badger would fall into them and drift among them in space. When he’d finally snap out of it, DarkStar would be gone, like a daydream. Fatty was no stranger to zoning out … for hours on end sometimes, but this, this would leave him with a lingering chill on his spine. One that would keep him up at night, afraid he’d see him again when he closed his eyes. He could only imagine the effect of stepping in the ring with him could have. Seeing his eyes, hearing his voice, making contact. The thought gnawed at him like a nightmare. It became even worse when he realized he needed to beat him. Fatbadger’s eyes grew wide. He bit fiercely at the knuckle of his index finger. DarkStar had made his way through to the second round of the GTT by forfeit, but that doesn’t mean that his skill could be discounted. His opponent hadn’t even seen DarkStar’s eyes, imagine if he had. He probably took the easy way out. Unfortunately, Badger didn’t have the pleasure of that option. He wishes he did. The pressure suddenly seemed too much, the second round of GTT. Fatty wished for that pressure to be lifted, for the tingling to rush in, but he knew it was only going to get worse before it got better. He needed to move on. Fatbadger breathed a sigh of relief and stood up. He knew what he had to do. Nothing had changed. Badger came here to lose to the biggest opponent in the biggest match in the most spectacular fashion he’d ever known. This was not that match. This was not that opponent. He needed to keep going until he found them. Fatbadger pulled up his pants and flushed the toilet.
