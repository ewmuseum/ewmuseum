How do you solve a problem like... ummmm... Collette?

7 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-07 “You look, well, I don’t know what to say…” Collette’s words hung in the air on that light and balmy night. She stood in front of the Goodman brothers, Dr Curiosity and Eegor, laughing at them as they stared at her. Both men were shocked, looking as if they were auditioning for a silent theatrical reproduction of Edvard Munch’s “The Scream”. Greeting Dr Curiosity as she did, Collette’s words echoing those he spoke when he first laid eyes on her, it sucked all the confidence from the scientist. His ridiculous schemes shrunk in his mind and vanished, confronted by this confident businesswoman once more, the woman who had once been his wife. “I… what…” stammered Curiosity, not even coming close to forming a coherent sentence. Collette shook her head and turned round to look at Eegor. She quickly winked; a move than brought on a flinch as if the assistant had been slapped in the face. Collette knew that he regretted the decisions he had made, and his servitude to his younger brother proved that he was forever searching for redemption. “What are you doing here? How did you find me…. or get in here?” asked the doctor. “So many questions, darling? I just had to find you though, because I’ve got something to tell you… something very important to tell you.” Curiosity glanced over at his brother, and the two of them looked at the woman stood before them. “I’ve come to tell Paul over there,” she said, nodding at Eegor, “that he’s a father. When he left me I was pregnant, and I’ve had his child!” The shock of the announcement left both of the brothers reeling. Dr Curiosity looked away from the brother who betrayed him, and Eegor looked on at Collette with disbelief in his eyes. “Is it… can it be true?” asked Eegor. Collette smiled, and gently pressed one hand to her belly. Smiling, she sighed, before responding. “Of course it isn’t bloody well true. What do you think this is, some sort of soap opera? Quit your sniveling, Paul, and you Chris, and pay me some attention.” Both men turned to look at her again, like puppets on strings that only she controlled. Neither man knew what was true and what was false any more. “Look, the reason I came here was simple. You’ve not answered any of the calls that my solicitor has made to you, nor have you replied to any of the letters or demands we’ve been making from you. I thought I’d come down here to see if you were really dead, but sadly I’ve found that you’re alive and well. In which case, I’ll just request that you open some of your damn mail every now and then, before the police come here and haul you to court, okay?” Dr Curiosity’s head was spinning. This was too much to take, especially ahead of an important match against Jade. “I’m going now, boys, but if you don’t start playing the game, I’ll be back, and next time I won’t be so fucking polite!” The last few words were yelled, before Collette, the ex-wife of Doctor Curiosity, turned on her heel and stormed off, leaving two shell-shocked former lovers in her wake. ----------------------------------------------------- Three days later, and the mood in the Curious Laboratory was a subdued one. With his confidence in tatters and his long-standing fear of women cranked up to new heights, Dr Curiosity sat with his head in his hands. "Vhy is she here? Has she come back to ruin me? Is zat it? She needs to see me fail again, just as I have throughout zis accursed life?" Eegor stood silently, one arm strapped behind his back, giving him the air of an amputee. This was an awkward deformity to assume, but the doctor's brother would stop at nothing to prostrate himself before the sibling he wronged so badly in the past. Despite rapidly losing the feeling in his left limb, the assistant was still lucid enough to know that there were rhetorical questions being thrown out, and so he kept silent as the doctor got to his feet and began to pace. The doctor had, against the odds, progressed through to the next round of GTT4. His opponent, Jade, had not turned up for their match, giving Curiosity a bye through to make the third round. On the one hand, Curiosity was elated, as he got one step closer to his goal of winning the tournament, but on the other, his self-belief had taken yet another knock. The only way he could have convinced himself that he would be able to stand up to Collette was if he had managed to defeat Jade. As it was, without anyone to fight, the doctor just had his mind to keep him company, and it created more and more conflict in his own mind. Now, with the days coming in quickly towards his next match against Alex Grey, the doctor finds his thoughts always coming back to Collette. No matter how far he wished she was away from him, he was living in her shadow. Again. “If only zere vas a vay to get rid of her for good. Zen I vould not be so distracted, and I vould be able to concentrate on Alex Beige.” “Alex Grey, master.” “Vhatever,” replied Curiosity, with a dismissive wave of his hand. “I need a vay to get rid of her permanently.” “Well, you could start replying to the letters from her solicitor. I’m sure some of those documents are quite important, legally.” “Nein! Zen she vould have von! She shall not be ze victor, she shall never vin against me ever again. If zere is von tzing I promise, it is zat.” Eegor nodded, and Dr Curiosity walked back across the room. The laboratory seemed to be slowly falling in to disrepair. The blackboard was as dusty as ever, but also some of the scientific apparatus seemed to have seen better days. The liquids in the test tubes and beakers were no longer bubbling. The strange containers that normally could be counted on to billow smoke forth and create the exact right amount of scientific ambience were conspicuous by their absence. All in all, everything was slowly falling apart for the doctor. He walked over and looked out of one of the windows. The desert looked back at him, but it didn’t have any answers. Eegor coughed. No response from Curiosity. Eegor coughed again. “Jah, okay, you’re ill. Take the day off and go hang around somewhere that people might appreciate you,” replied the doctor, testily, his accent slipping momentarily. “No, I meant that I had an idea.” “Was it as stupid as usual?” Eegor though about it, before replying, “Yeah, but it would solve our problems with Collette.” “Hmmm… go on.” “Well, what if we were to find Collette, or get her to come here, and between us I’m sure we could… y’know…” Eegor mimed breaking her neck in his hands, which is no mean feat when you’ve got one of them tied behind your back. “Vhat?” asked Curiosity, his metaphorical mask slipping back into place once more. Eegor mimed shooting someone in the head. “Are you tzing vhat I’m tzinking, Eegor?” The assistant, smiled, nodded, and mimed being stabbed in the heart. “Jah! Ve shall kill Collette! She shall be as nothing before me again, and I’ll show her zat I vas the better one in ze relationship! She von’t go leaving me for… vell,” Curiosity cast a sideways glance at his brother. “She just von’t be able to make any decisions at all!” Doctor Curiosity punched the air in jubilation, before suddenly stopping, and shaking his head so furiously that his cracked glasses threatened to fly off his head. “No, Eegor… ve cannot.” “Why? We both hate her, and she’s…” “NO! Eegor, I don’t profess to be ze most vell known man in ze vorld, but I’m a professional wrestler, and my face is getting to be recognized in certain places.” “Then we kill her away from the public eye, in somewhere secluded, like this laboratory!” “Eegor, you aren’t listening to me,” replied Curiosity, with impatience creeping in to his voice. “Ve cannot kill her because I’m famous, and famous people can’t get avay vith murder.” Eegor coughed loudly, but it sounds a lot like ‘O.J. Simpson’. “Eegor, listen to me. If she gets killed, ze police vill be very interested in talking to me. I have a motive, I vill have ze means, and if she comes here zen I vill have had ze opportunity. Even if I don’t get put in to prison to rot for ze next hundred years, zere is no vay ze police vould let me wrestle. I vould forfeit my match against Alex Beige…” “…Grey…” “…and if I go out of ze tournament, it’s as if she vould have von anyvay.” The doctor resumed looking sullen, his brow wrinkling with the despair of having come so close to a great plan, only to have it taken away at the last minute. “Then we’ll have to do it where nobody will notice.” Curiosity turned, to see Eegor smiling. “Think about it, master. Where would another dead body make no difference?” “The Sudan?” “Great time to get political, Chris… I mean… master,” said Eegor, reverting to a more formal mode of address after getting a vicious glare from Dr Curiosity. “No, what I mean is that we need to do this in Washington DC.” Curiosity looked puzzled. Why would a murder there be less noticeable than in, say, New Jersey? “Don’t you see it? What’s happening in DC?” “Ze GTT is being held zere. Vhat are you trying to say? Surely killing her zere vould be even more obvious zan anything else? If you’re going to commit a murder, doing it in ze vicinity of a place vhere you’ll be wrestling on television might be a bad idea. Zat place is crawling vith camera crews.” Eegor laughed, before continuing, “Have you not been reading the newspapers?” “I have better tzings to do vith my time!” “Well you should have checked it out. The homicide divisions over there have their hands more than full. The increase in killings since the tournament started in DC has been astonishing. It’s almost as if half of the wrestlers in the tournament are just going around killing people. It’s insanity around those parts.” The Curious Doctor rubbed his chin, and repositioned his glasses further up his nose. “So… you mean zat ve could find some strange excuse to get my ex-vife to travel to Vashington DC, vhereupon ve vill ambush her and lead her to her death in a vay she is not expecting, and zat cannot be traced back to us? Zen after she is dead ve can use ze wrestling show as an alibi, and blame her death on the unusually high wrestler-related deaths in ze area, thus clearing me to wrestle and defeat Alex Beige?” “He’s Alex Grey… but yes, that’s the plan.” The Doctor rubbed his chin again. “It’s almost too easy…” It was all Eegor could do to not giggle like a giddy schoolgirl after her first kiss. There was nothing that his brother, the good doctor, liked better than a convoluted plan where almost everything could easily go wrong. He wasn’t sure if either of them would actually have the guts to kill Collette, and deep down he was genuinely worried about the prospect of spending too much time DC because of the genuine death toll, but he knew that seeing Curiosity motivated was better than seeing him wasting away before losing to Alex Bei… Grey. ---------------------------- The pile of correspondence and letters was piled high. We’re talking about eight feet high. Dr Curiosity looked at the innumerable slips of paper in multitudinous colours; some free gift offers, some junk mail assuring Curiosity that he had definitely won ten million dollars (subject to confirmation), and somewhere in the midst of it, the letters from Collette’s solicitors. The good doctor scratched his head. “Good luck finding ze right letters, Eegor. I’m going for a nap!” The assistant sighed and looked at the huge stack. This was going to take some time. ---------------------------------- Eight hours later, and Dr Curiosity woke to the dawn of a brand new day. The sun beat down through the blinds, a natural alarm clock that never failed to bring him to. Rubbing the sleep from his eyes, he stepped from his bed and wrapped a bath robe around himself. Before that he was dressed only in shorts, and it showed off his body beneath. Despite his slight frame, there was muscle definition there, hinting that somewhere amidst the tomfoolery of his scientist persona there was someone who knew at least partially what they were doing. Either that or he’d created a Curious Muscle Enhancement Device. Wrapping the belt cord tightly around his waist, the doctor walked out of the room to check up on the progress of his brother. A short walk through the House of Curiosity led him to the spare room, where the stack of unopened mail had been. The door was opened, and inside lay five exceedingly neat piles of letters, and one exhausted man. “Vell done Eegor! Now go and make me breakfast.” Many people have spoken of the infinite sadness of humanity, and about how life is a series of pointless set pieces before oblivion encapsulates the pitiful soul again. Philosophers have written tome after tome of words to express this hopelessness. The great thinkers of our time and of times past have filled libraries with their thoughts on the matter. Eegor nailed it all with a single groan. “But master, I’ve spent all night…” “Begone! I vant vaffles!” Another groan spoke of more fatigue than the sweat of a million basketballers. Nevertheless, the dutiful Eegor shuffled off in search of breakfast foodstuffs for his master. Curiosity looked down at the piles, especially at the one labeled “Collette and divorce”. Quickly rifling through the first few letters, Doctor Curiosity soon got very bored indeed. “Sign zis, ignore zat… vhich are ze important ones? Vhere are ze ones vhere I can organize a meeting vith her in Vashinton DC?” More letters were thrown over his shoulder and on to the floor, a sight that would have set his brother weeping bitter tears. Perhaps the order for breakfast was a blessing in disguise. “Decree Nisi? Vhat ze hell is von of zose?” The doctor’s ignorance in matters of marital law was flummoxing him. He threw more and more letters behind him, including ones that stated that though he had applied for a Decree Nisi, he needed to write and apply for a Decree Absolute, or… “Vhat ze hell is zis? EEGOR! GET HERE NOW!” A muffled yell about waffles came through the door. “Forget ze vaffles! Zis is important… more important!” The doctor held up a single piece of paper, dated very recently. He shivered as he held it in his hands and read the words on it. His mask dropped again, and with a cold heart he spoke the words aloud, the Germanic tone gone. “For the Decree Nisi to become a Decree Absolute and to finish the marriage, this form needs to be completed and returned. It must have been signed by both parties, it must have been signed in ‘utmost good faith’… it must have been this, it must have been that… it must have been love but it’s over now… cut to the chase, dammit!” The doctor scanned down to the bottom of the page. “If these conditions are not met, then the Decree Absolute cannot be issued, and the divorce cannot be final.” His heart dropped. Eegor entered the room, and Doctor Curiosity turned round to him, holding the paper in the air. He just couldn’t think of the words. “Oh fuck.” --------------------------------------------------- Dear Collette Ka… Goodman, Following the correspondence from your solicitors, dated the fourth of August last year, the twelfth of August last year, a few in October, and a couple from January 2004… then I think there was one in Spring sometime, and a whole bundle in the Summer. Anyway, I’m replying to them, about the Decree Absolute thing. I want it, and I want it as soon as I can get it. To discuss terms and get both our signatures on it as quickly as possible, please meet me in Washington DC the day after tomorrow (Saturday, in case your math is as bad as I remember it to be). There we can sort out the finalities of our god-forsaken marriage, and I can once more be a free man. You might even be able to do some of that ‘shopping’ you like so much, before I rid you from my life forever. Your loving husband, Chris Goodman P.S. No need to buy a return ticket.
