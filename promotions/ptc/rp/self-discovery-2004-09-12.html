Self Discovery

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-12 The small fragile fingers caressed the handle of Death; stroking the trigger; fascinated by the weapon that lay before it. Saliva dropped from the child’s mouth onto the side of the barrel and glistened in the light like a drop of dew on a blade of grass at dawn, somewhat magical and mysterious yet completely mundane. The infant seemed to be drooling over the tool its eyes brushed over, unaware that its spit was creating a damp patch next to the gun. Naively, the boy used all of his strength to pick the gun up and placed the hot end into his mouth; letting out a scream and recoiling backwards off the bed in pain. “Hey, who let the kid back in here?” the Police Officer’s voice contained a note of distress which was very evident considering his intonation. Almost shouting he ran towards the crying child and picked him up in his thick arms. The man soothed the child by whispering slightly into his ear and managed to settle him into a false sense of security that was sleep. “What’s up?” the second Officer walked through the door and apologized silently as he saw the child out cold on the bed. The first Officer rose from his seating position on the blood stained cover and walked over to his friend stood in the doorway. “The kid was playing with the shotgun when I saw him. He tried putting it in his mouth but the end burnt him and he jumped back in shock. It breaks my heart to see a life destroyed at such a young age. He’ll never get over seeing his father take that shotgun to his head.” His mouth trembled as he muttered these words through solemn lips. He had experience of the carnage that suicide could cause, though now he was starting to see it as a different way. He was starting to understand in some sick fascination of his mind. He respected the corpse lying near him for the coward he was, he’d taken the easy way out, but at least he was out. A tear slid down his cheeks leaving a salty trail, one of utter despair; glimmering in the dimly lit room, mocking life itself, spitting on the values most humans hold dearly. Disrespecting everything we are. “If you’ll excuse me I’ve got to go and write a report and before you ask; no the child’s presence in the room will not be included. I’ll let you off that one, but if you let him out of your sight again it’ll be you who loses his job, not me.” With that the Officer slowly walked out of the room after taking one last tearful look at the sleeping three year old. He didn’t deserve so many traumas so early in his life. In a way he felt guilty, he couldn’t understand why because there was no way he could have stopped the child’s father from shooting himself. There was no chance he could have saved his mother from dying when she gave birth to him. The poor kid was going to have no parents for the rest of his life and there wasn’t a damn thing he could do about it. He’d file to adopt him the next day, and then at least he could help in some way. He’d make sure that nothing would happen to that poor little life again. ---------- “Sir, he’s gone.” The second Officer’s voice was barely audible, he felt as though he’d let his superior down; but not only that, he felt as though he’d let himself down. “How can he have gone? A three year old child doesn’t just disappear? I thought you were watching the room?” it was clear that he felt that the last chance of him saving the child’s life was gone now. He’d have to accept that no matter what happened, he was going to have a difficult life, but when you’re brought into the life in the way he was, it’s only to be expected. “I just fell asleep for a few minutes and when I woke he was gone. I’m really sorry sir, I know how attached you felt to him.” The tone in the second Officer’s voice suggested real sympathy to the loss his friend had suffered. He understood on a low level the bond that he had structured with the dead man’s son. He felt like he could be the new father figure in his life; but once again fate had laughed in his face and snatched away any chance of him fathering a child. He didn’t reply to what his associate had said and walked out of the room with his hat in his hands and his head bowed. Tonight would be his last night working for the Police Force. He couldn’t take it anymore. Tonight would be his last night in this world. ---------- One of Cyrus’s first memories was when he’d been out with his father; they’d been out fishing together. Cyrus hated having to be so patient watching his dad’s float, waiting for it to bob under the water. He was too young to actually participate himself so he just sat by his father’s side for company. He enjoyed being out in the country air, with the perfectly clear blue skies and the birds chirping their happy songs in the trees nearby. Up until his father passed away Cyrus had spent a few brilliant days with him. Though the brilliant times he’d had were outweighed by the bad ones. The bad times began when his father was drunk, which unfortunately was nearly every day Cyrus resided with him. Now though the scenery changed with the man whom had changed himself from that small boy, so young, so naïve, so disrespectful to everything around him. Cyrus had to adapt and change to fit into his surroundings when he ran away; with no parents left and no relatives he knew of, he had to go it alone. He had to learn a lot of things he would later take for granted and have thrown back in his face. It was all about value, how much somebody valued your name. How much they were prepared to bet on you when you stepped into that fight, when you placed your body on the line just for some bastards’ entertainment. He could still smell the piss stained walls of the alleys in which he used to work, lit only by the lighters of a few ‘valued’ names throwing their money round as if it were worthless. He could hear their voices echoing in his mind, screaming coarse words of encouragement so they’d win their little bets, so they’d fill their pockets with more prosthetic pieces of paper in which they based their lives upon. Though he could not see their faces, never would he see their ugly, pulsating mouths from which the obscenities were launched. Hidden behind masks; too scared to let their identities be known to the public, not wanting their precious reputations to be tarnished by illegal activities such as backstreet fighting. Cyrus was never allowed to fear anything, the two brothers who helped him through his time on the streets taught him to be scared of nothing. They knew he would dominate anyone who dared stand up to him, he just had to believe in himself and believe in his ability, his physical build was abnormal and they knew he would help them into a better life. In a way they used him to their advantage manipulating him from his young naïve state and claiming to educate him in the world of fighting. They claimed to be the two who gave him the lessons in life, the two whom helped him survive without any family and any money, the two who helped him live. They took credit for the young man he became at the tender age of seventeen years old. A warrior amongst boys, he fought nearly ever day of his later teenage years and won nearly every fight he was in. He brutally assaulted each opponent before finally being called off by a worried man who feared for his enemies’ life. Cyrus had learnt not to think about his actions, not to contemplate the consequences, he couldn’t let himself feel regret or remorse. He had to keep punishing the fat-cats that wasted their money on prostitutes and drugs instead of investing it into children’s homes and charities. Plus he needed to live off something and he had no qualifications as he didn’t attend school, fighting was his only way of life. He’d swore he’d never go back to that life, once he’d gotten his feet firmly on the ground he’d promised himself that he’d never see another bloody fight again. He wouldn’t stain his clothes with another mans claret, nor would he invest money in seeing somebody else stain their clothes, and their soul. But for some reason; he felt compelled to go to one more fight, to kill the memories in his head and lock them away for the rest of his life. He needed to get rid of the past and leave it where it belonged, never to resurface into the present state of his mind. He couldn’t have anything creeping in to distract him at this time of his life. He knew he wasn’t getting any younger and he wanted to make a name for himself and show people he had talent and ability. Though, he wanted to show that he still had some morals and by doing so, he wanted to win something legal. He did not wish to participate in another street fight ever again, he just wanted to wrestle. So he’d found himself applying for the GTT tournament within PTC. This was maybe his last chance to win anything as he felt jaded with himself and the sport in which he competed. Maybe for the last time, Cyrus was going to put everything he had into the arena and hope that he came out on top. The one thing he needed most was concentration. It had evaded him in the past; with his personal life managing to infiltrate his career and physically dissect it, piece by piece. If he could remove his past from his memories he could focus on now and the task at hand, though removing his past would be tough and Cyrus doubted whether he had the mental strength to do it. As a shower of blood covered the young kid stood in the make-shift circle, Cyrus did not squirm like the rest of the crowd. His eyes stared fixating on the two opponents, one now lying on a crimson pillow on the cold, hard, concrete. The other stood, towering over his enemy urinating over his fallen foe. At this Cyrus turned away, it was not that he could not stomach the act of somebody relieving themselves, he just felt disgusted that this child had no respect for the man he had beaten. Feeling sick in his stomach Cyrus began to walk out of the now even fouler smelling alley and back out into civilization. A hand caught his shoulder as he reached the glimmer of a street lamp on the side of the pavement. He turned around with an expressionless look on his face, his eyes connected with that of the cocky young kid’s. His piercing blue pearls penetrated the adolescents mind and scared him; he quickly turned his head back to the now dispersing crowd. “Hey Boss! Did this f**k chuck any money in the kitty?” his voice screeched at the fat man hiding in the shadows towards the end of the alleyway. The man did not reply; he merely shook his head to acknowledge the boy and to give him his answer. The boy’s head whipped round; though his eyes avoided the face in front of him; as he spat onto the foot of Cyrus Skar. A smirk crept out across his face slimier than that of an eel as Cyrus’s hand connected with his right temple. Still the expression on Cyrus’s face did not change as he walked away from the unconscious shell that dropped to the ground. ---------- The birds sung no more, for they slept in their soft, warm nests oblivious to the rest of the sleeping world outside. Though, not all of the inhabitants in the surrounding area were asleep. A tall man dressed in a long, leather jacket sat on a solitary bench in the middle of the park in which he usually went to clear his mind. The night sky was filled with grey clouds taunting the occupants of the world beneath that they might burst at any second, drowning all underneath them. Cyrus did not fear a little water though, he could cope with that. Right now his mind was wandering over thoughts of the past, the very thoughts he’d tried to forget about. But the slow realization was creeping over him that he wouldn’t be able to leave that past behind. It was going to follow him around like a bad smell. It wasn’t going to leave him till the day he died. This realization crept over him slowly, suffocating him, choking him as it closed in over his body. His other personality being forced from within, making an appearance that Cyrus did not need. “It’s been a long time Cyrus. I take it you didn’t miss me?” the evil raspy voice echoed out into the night. It came from Cyrus’s mouth but it was evident it was not he who spoke when his lips moved. His other personality lay dormant most of the time, but it seemed to awaken at the worse possible moments in Cyrus’s life. Cyrus did not answer. He did not want to argue with his other personality right now. Thoughts of his father consumed his mind process; unfortunately his other personality knew this and brought them to the top of the conversation. “Who do you respect Cyrus? Wait, don’t answer that.” The voice had to stop for a second to make sure it didn’t laugh as it mocked Cyrus. “I know you’d say your father. But what did he ever do for you Cyrus? When he couldn’t be bothered looking after you he threw you into a cage, he used to drink constantly and beat you with his belt when you cried. Why do you block these bad memories out Cyrus? Is it because you want to have someone to respect? Or is it because you’re too afraid to accept reality?” “I respect my father because he had the guts to end his own life when he saw how pathetic he was. For that I admire him.” Cyrus stopped speaking to take a swig from the bottle within the brown paper bag. “Though he is the only person I respect, because nobody else I know deserves my respect.” Silence entered the night for a few minutes as Cyrus sat, head in hands, only raising it to drink more of that precious, expensive liquid that solved all of his problems. A few more moments passed and the voice spoke on, after giving deep consideration to what it should say. “And you expect to win in the state your in?” a cackle escaped Cyrus’s mouth whilst not under his commanding influence. “You don’t respect yourself, so why should anybody respect you? You tried to get that kid to learn some respect by hitting him. That means nothing because you are a failure yourself. If you do not believe what you preach Cyrus, you should not preach it.” These words hit hard, pushing a stake into the memories of Cyrus’s father and into the past he’d tried to forget. They seemed to ease his pain in a strange way and allowed him to start to see things more clearly. He was not a coward because he chose to continue with his life, he was stronger than his father would ever be. He raised his arm to take another drink from the bottle and then lowered it again. Why was he drinking again? To get out of the situation he’d placed himself in? No, it was not the answer to his problems. He had to search deeper for a long-term fix rather than the quick fix of a shot of whisky. The bottle smashed into the floor, the moons ugly face reflecting off the shards of glass that stared back at Cyrus’s bloodshot eyes. He’d made the right choice for the first time in his life and it felt good. As he rose to his feet he felt a new sense of urgency within himself, almost like he was re-born. He needed to respect himself and give himself a reason to do so. This was only the first step of a long winding road, but with time and effort he would achieve self perfection inside his mind. ----- His other personality had not tried to spur Cyrus on; it had only been playing with his value-system and toying with him as it liked to do for fun. Boredom took on many forms, but Cyrus’s other personality only ever felt like it needed revenge, for being suppressed for many years as background noise. For now it was only cheap jabs and wisecracks, but one day it would become the dominant personality; for now it just had to bide it’s time. It’d been close before to succeeding in its quest, failure came quickly last time it tried. The thought of failure again was not even entertained by Cyrus’s other personality now, only success…
