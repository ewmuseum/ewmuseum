Part 3B: Know When To

2 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-12-02 Just after midnight, the crowd at the Wildcard was picking up a little. Twenty minutes earlier, a large group of people had come in and taken over the main table, forcing the three men who had been sitting there to move. This suited one of them just fine. Wyatt Connors carefully carried three bottles as he rejoined his lackeys. He caught the tail end of their conversation as he approached. "So, yeah, that's what this is all about," Trevor Ratigan said, pointing at his freshly-cut hair. "I fucked up a little--no, I fucked up a lot--so if I'm ever gonna tap that ass again, I gotta kiss it for a while, y'know?" Deacon nodded. "I hear ya. Women. Can't live with 'em..." "...Pass the beer nuts," Ratigan finished. "I don't think that's how it goes." "Whatever. Point is, as soon as I'm getting laid regular, this shit is going to stop. Growing the hair back out, and I can stop wearing this lame-ass Eddie Bauer shit. You know, she tried to throw out my old flannel?" "The one that had fleas and smelled like a dog shat in it?" "I know! Can you believe it?" Deacon shook his head, and noticed Connors' return. "Hey, chief. I see you let someone else open your beer this time." Wyatt set the drinks down on the table. "Kept my eye on the bartender. If he'd put anything in there, I would have noticed." "Well, it's progress. Have a seat; T.J. was just telling me about how he's pussy-whipped now." Trevor responded with the height of rational discourse, the finger. "Least I'm getting some. Who was president the last time you got lucky? Eisenhower?" "Just about. Never did call your mom back, either." Deacon grinned, and laughed as Ratigan pulled back his hand, preparing to throw his bottle in mock anger. "Glad to see the two of you haven't matured while I was gone," Connors said as he sat down. "I might not have been able to find you." "What are you talking about?" Ratigan said, rocking slightly. He'd just started his eighth beer, and the effects were visible. "I am the very picture of maturity. Now watch my beer while I go drain the lizard." He stood up and weaved his way across the floor of the Wildcard. "What do you think?" Deacon asked. "Do we leave his car here for the cops to ticket, or dump it off in a part of town where they'll steal the tires twenty seconds after we leave?" Wyatt laughed. "I don't see any reason why we can't steal the tires ourselves." Something in his head told him to slow down, but he had no desire to listen. Whatever it was had already been wrong once tonight. "Nah, better not. He's broken up about his hair; he'd probably die if we fucked with his car." Dale took down about half his Coors Light in one drink--the fifth in the evening for him. "Still. Can't imagine what he'd have to fuck up that he'd actually be sorry for it." "I had to guess, I'd say it had to do with his father. It was just...what, six weeks ago that he got released from--" "Oh, shit. Forgot about that. Guess I thought he was already out." But Wyatt wasn't listening anymore. He wasn't speaking, either--he hadn't been interrupted with his last statement; he'd just frozen up. A small group of people had wandered to within earshot of their table. Three of the four were women. The red dress? No. The hair on her arms is too dark and coarse. Pierce wouldn't bother with that. Or would she... "Wyatt!" "Hmm?" "You're doing it again." "Was I?" Deacon pointed at Connors' left hand, which still had a white-knuckle death grip on the table. "Oh," Connors said, and let go. He knew Deacon was about to ask a question. It was going to be a difficult one, judging by the number of times Dale started to speak, but pulled up short. Before long, Deacon managed to get it out. "I gotta ask. What did she do that's got you fucked up so bad?" "You really want to know?" "Probably not. But I don't need you going all Falling Down and shit, either. If you have a nervous breakdown, who's going to make sure I get paid?" "Your concern is touching, Deacon." It could have been the beer--three bottles of Guinness was the most he'd had to drink in years. Maybe it was the shock of being so incredibly wrong about the girl he'd accused of being Alexandra Pierce. Whatever the reason, he just couldn't bear the weight off all the secrets he carried. He could part with one...but which one? The night he left Pierce's organization? His role in having Pierce committed to a mental institution? No...he wasn't ready to tell those stories just yet, and Deacon and Trevor weren't ready to hear them. He looked down at the table, and looked at the coaster underneath his beer. The ace of diamonds. And he knew. "Let me tell you about the first time I met Alex Pierce in person." # # # April 19, 2003. 11:58 pm. Just outside San Francisco, California. He'd received the message through his answering service that morning. "One-Eyed Jack's, midnight." As Wyatt Connors parked his rental car, he felt a surge of excitement. This is it, he thought. The big time. The establishment looked like it had seen better days, but that meant very little. The powerful could afford to be extravagant...but the very powerful could afford not to be. As he walked through the door, he found himself in a room that felt a lot larger than it looked. It had little to do with the decor, and a lot to do with the small group of people sitting at the center table. "Wyatt, darling!" came the sultry voice of the Sex Kitten, Kathryn Shaw. "You made it! We were beginning to wonder." She was wearing a tight dark blue dress with a plunging neckline and...well, suffice to say, she was dressed as Kathryn Shaw. She approached him swiftly yet gracefully, her hips swaying like palm fronds in the breeze. "Wouldn't miss it," he casually replied, hoping to get a look at the others in the room. Unfortunately, Shaw had come up too quickly, and blocked his view. She offered a hand, which he shook gently. "Come sit down, have a drink. We're just about to get started." She took his arm and walked him toward the table. The others stood up, and even without introductions, Wyatt knew who he was dealing with...either by face, or reputation. "Wyatt, it pleases me to introduce the rest of our merry band. You've met Lauren, of course..." "Yes," Connors answered. "Frisco, wasn't it?" Lauren Fox smiled, looking a bit sheepish. "The City," she corrected. "And of course, Hunter Sabuani and his wife, Madison..." Madison's pleasant smile was obviously fake. Hunter's was neither pleasant, nor a smile. "We know," he said, tersely. Someone's still a little sore from that title match, Connors thought. Good to know. "And this gorgeous specimen is Gideon Asher, formerly of the Black Ops." "Howaya." Gideon waved a meaty hand, not paying much attention to the newcomer. "I remember them," Connors said in an effort to be diplomatic. "One of the best tag teams ever." "THE best," Gideon corrected him. "Of course." "The boss isn't here just yet, but she'll be coming down shortly. In the meantime, the bar is well stocked, so help yourself. We'll get ready for the entertainment." Connors nodded, and headed toward the bar. He didn't especially want a drink, but he knew that not drinking would put them on edge. Wyatt fixed himself a scotch and soda, and walked back toward the table. By the time he arrived, Gideon was setting up some chips, and Hunter was shuffling a deck of cards. "The name of the game is poker," he said, his sense of showmanship emerging. "Five card draw, no wilds. And you'd better be able to cover your bets when the night is over." Connors sat down at the one empty chair, in between Shaw and Asher. "Good to know some people still play proper cards." Kathryn pouted her lips slightly, mocking disappointment. "Does this mean we're not playing strip poker?" Hunter started to answer, but Madison beat him to the punch. "Not a chance. For one, we don't want to send Lauren out of the room...and for another, I'd like my husband's eyes to stay in his sockets." "Besides," Hunter answered. "We don't want everyone to laugh at Asher when he has to drop his drawers. He's sensitive about that." "Fuck ya." The game began in earnest, with some light betting to go with the light banter. Connors made all the right responses, of course. He laughed at the jokes, responded to Kathryn's flirtations, and even traded a few barbs with Hunter and Gideon. Not that his reactions were genuine, of course. The way he interacted with the others was more or less automatic; he didn't really think the jokes were funny, nor was he really trying to get under the skin of the others. Most of his attention was focused on the other members of the group themselves. The way they acted in different situations, to different questions. He was listening...watching. Measuring. # # # 12:45 am. The game had gone reasonably well. Connors was still breaking even; Hunter was up, Lauren was down. At that point, the game started to turn to business. "So, Connors," Asher rumbled through a haze of cigar smoke. "These guys seem to be pretty high on you." "Well, I like to think I bring a little something special to the table." "Yeh? Like what? I mean, yer not big 'er tough. Sure, yer brainy, but smarts don't run in short supply around here." He pushed in a small stack of chips, prompting Madison and Lauren to drop out. Wyatt called on Gideon's bet, and discarded two cards. "I'm...good at reading people." Gideon drew a single card, and put a few more chips into the stack. "Y'mean like that profiler crap onna teevee? Bah. What good's 'at do?" Wyatt shrugged, and raised the bet. "If you know what a person is like, it becomes easier to guess what moves they'll make. If you can figure these things out fast, you can gain a greater advantage." "Well I fink yer fulla shit," Gideon barked, raising his bet again. Hunter thought or a moment, before pitching in his cards. "I don't know, Giddy," Kathryn said as she folded. "We do those things all the time." "'At's differnt. You guys get close, an' you take yer time. I don't see anyone lettin' him get close; how's he gonna figger anything out?" The Wise Guy smiled. "You'd agree that I don't know any of you very well, correct? Apart from the scuffle between Hunter and myself two years ago, this meeting is the most I've seen or heard from most of you. Agreed?" "Sure, but I don't see your--" Connors cut him off by looking sharply at Kathryn Shaw. "Sabrina--the original. Mask," he continued, this time shifting his eyes toward Lauren. Next, he looked at the Sabuanis. "The Godfather for Hunter; An Affair To Remember for Madison...but for the both of you, When Harry Met Sally." Asher opened his mouth to respond, but Wyatt didn't give him the chance. "And as for you? You tell everyone it's Sands of Iwo Jima, maybe Apocalypse Now...but really?" Connors' grin twisted into a smirk. "Weekend at Bernie's." Wyatt leaned back and took in the looks on their faces. Gideon was stunned, Kathryn was a little proud, and Madi...she was slightly horrified. "And that's just from forty minutes of conversation." Gideon's response was one word. "Fold." That was how Wyatt Connors won four thousand dollars with a pair of fours. # # # 1:19 am. Do it, Wyatt thought. One more time. The game had turned heavily in Connors' favor. Lauren was out. Gideon went bust after Connors taunted him into going all in with a hand that was better than Wyatt's...but not as good as Hunter's. He trapped Madison with a fake tell and cleaned her out. He stopped responding to Shaw's innuendo, causing her to fall behind. Wyatt had even managed to hide a card away for later use. You've done it twice. One more. Now, it was a big money hand between Sabuani and Connors. Hunter had made a heavy bet, and was waiting for Connors to make his choice. Wyatt had his eyes pointed mostly toward his cards, but he was really looking at Hunter's ring. He'd already turned it twice. Three times meant a bluff. There it is. "Call." Hunter scowled, and his mood grew worse when the cards were laid down--Connors' three of a kind beat Hunter's ace-high, a chance at a straight gone wrong. Everything seemed to be tilting in his favor. "Space for one more?" came a cold voice from behind him. Wyatt turned to see the speaker...it was exactly who he expected, and he still wasn't prepared. The blood red hair and pale skin were just like he'd seen in old footage...but a chill still went through his blood when he saw her face...when he said her name... "Alexandra Pierce."
