Reluctant Repentance

13 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-13 [Somewhere between the conscious and subconscious, Rich Rollins knew something was wrong the instant he opened his eyes. Or tried to, at least.] [Blackness.] [Vaguely aware of a firm pressure around his torso, Rollins strains for composure as his senses slowly begin to return to him, a jumbled haze of thoughts and images assaulting his mind as the surrounding darkness continues to swallow him whole. Confusion. Fear. Doubt. Despair. With the suddenness of a hot iron sizzling in a cool pond, the words assault his mind more so than the actual emotions, his psyche telling him he is feeling these things but at the same time shutting them out.] [No, not so much despair as a combination of the other three. Paralyzed, both literally and figuratively, The Renegade frantically tries to recall his last cognizant memory, piecing together a logical reason for his current predicament.] [After a few fruitless moments (which just as easily could have been hours, Rollins is unsure of even that), his head has as much definitive recollection as the swirling abyss of nothingness around him has definition.] [A blank slate.] … [Laughter.] [The rich baritone voice pierces the abyss and enters The Renegade’s thoughts, sounding at first like pieces of rough leather rubbing together. Maniacal. Sadistic. Ruthless. Malevolent. Unlike the first bout of random emotions, these feelings strike as true as the words stick in Rollins thoughts, his stomach knotting up out of sheer helplessness. Total, complete helplessness.] Am I… dead? [The words resonate in Rollins’ head, growing with fear not unlike that of a snowball growing into an avalanche as it rolls down the side of a mountain. His frantic confusion is infinite; without any semblance of sensation in his limbs, without sight or sound or explanation, Rich Rollins knows that he is completely and inexplicably alone.] [Without warning, a painfully bright light suddenly shatters the endless nightmare, literally slicing the darkness from top to bottom. Everything becomes a white, fuzzy haze; three points, each with the intensity of the sun itself, resolves from the unexpected intrusion of illumination. Rollins can both hear and feel his heart beating now, which finally gives him some reassurance that he isn’t dead. Or at least, not yet.] [That is, until he hears the zipper.] [The sheer bizarre nature of the situation stuns The Renegade. The light, he realizes, is blasting into his open eyes from a tripod fixture hovering above his head. Yet he only realizes this after he finally comes to terms with the reality of where he is.] [In a body bag.] Voice: … the lights, Thompson, you’re giving me a headache. [Unable to focus on the source of the male voice, Rollins can only allow his vision to adjust on their own as a single shape emerges from the blinding white inferno of the overhead fixture. The edges of a human form, featureless, leans over The Renegade’s body, momentarily blocking out the light. It’s then that the details become all too painfully clear.] [The speaker is a doctor, complete with medical-issue blue filtration mask and eyes that shine with Ivy League importance. The doctor leans in further, peering deep into Rollins’ doe-like, glazed-over stare. He smells like a moth-ball and a bottle of liquid sterilizer had a child and submerged it in formaldehyde, nearly choking Rollins’ lungs. If he could have taken a breath, that is.] [Another thought now occurs to Rich Rollins.] I’m not breathing. [Panic. Terror. Anguish. Rage.] [The Renegade tries to scream, tries to put the muscles in his jaw into motion just long enough to emit a strangled cry for help… but to no avail. The doctor jambs a gloved finger into his eye, spreading his eyelids wide.] Thompson: Time of death? [The second figure, somewhere outside of Rollins’ view.] Doctor: Approximately 3:27 in the AM. They wheeled him in upstairs, and he was already DOA. [The doctor’s lips move behind the sterilized moth-ball mask, jerking awkwardly at the bridge of his nose.] Thompson: Cause? Doctor: Multiple fractures to the skull, ribs and spinal column. Internal bleeding, I’d say. Thompson: Only one way to find out, I suppose. Doctor: Indeed. Are you ready for your final exam, Thompson? [The laughter returns.] [It was an odd sensation, listening to people talk about you in the third-person when you were right there, listening to them talk about you. But Rollins is anything but amused. His eyes burning from the constant exposure to the harsh white light, his entire soul cries out in a desperate, hysterical plea…] I’m alive, God damn you! I’m alive! [As the doctor moves aside, the second figure joins him at his elbow. Identical to one another behind their infernal medical death masks, both men exchange a knowing look before the senior doctor hands his student a scalpel. The edge of the precise blade glints off the overhead sunbath, shining like a star to The Renegade’s dilated vision.] [The thought of a serious lawsuit occurs to Rich Rollins, but instantly sounds both ridiculous and irrelevant at this point. Still, it takes his focus off the lowering blade and keeps him sane for a few moments more.] [The next few moments, as it would happen, become the absolute definition of eternity.] [Another voice beckons The Renegade then, somewhere deep from inside his own head. He knows it’s just a figment of his imagination, but at the same time wonders just where it emanated from. Now Richard Andrew Rollins had never been a God-fearing man, but the sound of this voice was unmistakable.] [It was the voice of a southern Baptist preacher.] [As the scalpel continues down its eternal journey toward The Renegade’s exposed abdomen, the preacher’s shapeless voice whispers into the depths of Rollins’ inner ear. It soothes the knots in his stomach, lulling his fears into a peaceful slumber, seducing away his frustration and anger into nonexistence. The voice drifts, beckoning Rich Rollins to drift right along with it.] Repent. [The thought was not his.] Forgive your aggressors. Forgive your God. But most importantly… forgive yourself. [This was too much information for Rollins’ mind to process. His consciousness lashes out, desperately trying to shut away the voice in his head, calm his racing heartbeat, ignore the cold razor that now presses against his skin.] [The scalpel pierces his abdomen, sinking into the sold folds of his internal flesh.] NOOOOOOOOOO… … … Renegade: …OOOOOOOOOO! [Pain. Unimaginable pain.] [The Renegade thrashes about, jerking upright from his sweaty bedding, his hand immediately going to his stomach. Heaving with great gasps of air, his fingers frantically play across the glistened surface of his abdomen, panicked at the slick feeling. With terror gripping his throat, Rich Rollins brings his hand to his face.] [Sweat. Just sweat.] [No blood.] [A nightmare.] [The Renegade sits there, his mind struggling to bring him up to speed, caught between his current surroundings and the horrible reality of the dream. His shoulders sink, his breath slowing down. His brain eases into the true reality; four plaster walls without a window, a slanted dresser in the corner that limps without the use of one leg and the piled box-spring mattresses that make up The Renegade’s bed are all that greet him.] [No doctor. No assistant. No scalpel.] [No preacher.] [His hands physically shaking, Rollins climbs out of bed and slips on a pair of blue jeans. Running a hand through his sweaty mop of shoulder-length brown hair, he pushes through the only door in the room and into the next. He emerges behind a darkened bar, glass shelves with mostly empty bottles of liquor to his right and a grimy bar top to his left. Swatting at the light switch, a few scattered fluorescent bulbs blink into existence, illuminating the dark recess of the hole that Rich Rollins calls home.] [He’d bought out the bar almost three years ago for practically pennies. The previous owner wanted nothing to do with it, considering the location. Placed in the middle of a high-crime neighborhood in South Chicago, Rollins was the not-so-proud owner of his own personal Hell, a bar that nobody visited in a neighborhood no decent citizen resided. Instead of customers, he had cockroaches. Instead of the obligatory Golden Tee golf-themed arcade machines, he had two beer-stained pool tables and no cues.] [He had nobody but himself, and that was just the way he liked it.] [Slipping around the bar, Rollins sinks into the nearest stool and rests his elbows on the countertop, his mind working overtime. With a deep breath he attempts to take a hold on reality, shoving aside the lingering knot in his stomach. It was the most intense dream he’d ever had, that much was certain.] But why? What could have caused such a vivid nightmare? [A simple question, and one he is unable to answer.] Could it be… because of her? [Luna Morbid. The Renegade’s first opponent in the illustrious fourth edition of the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. It was his attempt at regaining his edge, as it was the previous GTT that nearly put him out of the business.] [But after just over a decade in professional competition, this was the first woman Rich Rollins had ever been forced to compete against.] [A woman.] [Women were strong creatures, and The Renegade could think of a handful of dangerous vixens whose paths he’d crossed, but he’d never been put in a lose-lose situation like this. He’d never been forced to strike a woman. To brutalize and maim a member of the opposite sex in the spirit of personal glory and professional acclaim.] Well, there’s a first time for everything. [The voice was still in his head, though.] [The preacher.] [Rollins, the eternal sinner.] [Was it some sort of subconscious metaphor, the preacher in the dream? Was the dream itself forcing up feelings he wasn’t ready to deal with, or worse… was he intentionally avoiding them? Rollins didn’t have many dreams, which only compounded and complicated his feelings toward this one. What did he have to “repent?” Why should he?] [Too many rhetorical questions for his taste.] [The Renegade had accepted long ago that his choices in life, both good and ill, assured that there was no mythical place in Heaven waiting for him. He didn’t kid himself. But that didn’t bother him; indeed, it was what that statement implied that worried him.] [If there was no room in Heaven, would Hell even take him?] [Was it even worth repenting?] [Rubbing his eyes with the back of his knuckles, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins gives himself a good shake to clear his mind.] This goddamn tournament has my brain completely scrambled. And me without my skillet. [Decidedly stuffed with the abundant food for thought, Rich Rollins stumbles back behind the empty bar and into the confines of his bedroom, shutting the door on the rest of the outside world. Most people didn’t understand his reclusive nature; many believed he lived like a hermit so he could slam the proverbial front door on everyone else.] [What they failed to understand was that Rich Rollins was slamming the door on himself.] [Because in less than a week, his tortured psyche would be subjected to the uncensored beating of an innocent woman. A fellow “competitor.” A stepping stone toward immortality.] [Repentance, indeed.] [Fade to black.] ~Fin~
