In The Air Tonight

24 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-24 Morning greeted the Curious Weather Experiment with a colder air than it had done the previous day. The skies were overcast, and there was evidence that there might have been a storm on the way. The Curious Weather Experiment itself was indifferent to the change in climate, save for fewer lights illuminating the display panel of the computer that it was attached to. The weather experiment was an invention of sorts by Doctor Curiosity. He had often bemoaned the lack of dark and stormy nights that the location of his laboratory provided, and had set about studying a method by which he could alter this, reasoning that all the best diabolical plotting was done to a backdrop of thunder and lightning. The result was the Curious Weather Experiment. The first stage had been to monitor the weather patterns in the area, and correlate the data with world events, seasonal shifts in rainfall, and the prevailing mood within the Curious Laboratory at the time. As such, underneath a canopy that fluttered and flapped in the wind, lurked a computer and a video camera. The curious doctor had programmed some software to analyze the visuals being provided by the camera, and assess the weather, and the potentiality for a dark and stormy night. Thus far, it hadn't worked a damn, but Doctor Curiosity was not the kind of man to let abject failure stand in the way of an idealized thought process. Instead, he believed that the emotions he felt could perhaps be channeled into the atmosphere, and it could create a storm out of nothing. Whilst he did contend that Hurricane Ivan was caused by his devices, there had been no documentary evidence to back up his outrageous claims. Holding up a large wrench, the doctor proceeded with his usual method of trying to fix his useless appliances. "Take zat, you useless piece of junk!" he yelled, smashing the wrench down on the top of a metallic casing. The wrench left a dent in the metal, but there was no electrical storm to follow. "Hmmm... maybe ze pattern of ze dents could have an effect on ze veather?" the doctor mumbled to himself, hitting the casing over and over again with the wrench. The resultant outburst left Doctor Curiosity out of breath, but it was most cathartic indeed. The doctor was breathing heavily, and had worked up quite a sweat, leaving himself too preoccupied to notice Eegor approaching him from behind. The assistant tapped his master on the shoulder, causing him to jump with fright. "Could you perhaps announce yourself next time, Eegor? I tzought for a minute zat you vere Collette!" "It's the hair, isn't it? I should get it cut." "Oh, shut up Eegor. I need to fix zis machine, not worry myself about zat voman!" Eegor shook his head. Collette was still downstairs, and still in chains. The doctor had visited her earlier, and her mounting frustration caused her to increase her demands to eighty percent of his wealth. The doctor had acquiesced without argument. "If you're going to let her go, then let her go. I don't think you should do it, but just don't leave her around here. She'll only torment you," said Eegor. “True, Eegor. I guess all of zis nonsense vith ze veather machine is only serving to keep my mind off her.” “You’re still going to give her what she wants?” asked the assistant. “Jah,” said the doctor, with a sigh. “Still, I can’t vait to see her face vhen I tell her.” This finally made Eegor smile. Their one last card was going to be played very soon. “Zen after zat I can bring my mind to bear on my opponent in zis tournament. I cannot concentrate on ze Latch Key Kid vith her threats hanging over my head! I've done nothing about neutralizing zis kid." "Well, you tried to find his lemonade stand, master." "Jah, don't remind me about zat..." -------------------------------------------- The sign, crudely written in green crayon, read 'Lemonade – Twenty five sents'. It was stuck to a small wooden stall, crafted out of pieces of wood that the child's father had clearly not wanted. Bits of wardrobe mingled with what looked like pieces of driftwood, and even some cardboard to cover over holes. It was clear that the child sat behind the counter had put a lot of effort in to creating his own little lemonade stall. None of this was noticed by Doctor Curiosity. "I swear, Eegor, zere is a place called Frank's around here somevhere, and zis Latch Key Kid has set up a lemonade stand nearby. If ve trash his stand, zen he vill be too busy trying to fix it zat he vill forget about ze match!" Eegor scratched his head, unsure of the doctor's logic. "Jah! Jah! Ve have found it! Here he is!" yelled the doctor, pointing at the small child's lemonade stand. "I don't think I can see the other coffee shop." "Silence, Eegor! Here is a lemonade stand being run by a kid! It all makes sense now!" "No, the Latch Key Kid isn't actually a child, master, he's…" interjected Eegor, but Curiosity wasn't listening. Instead, he was wandering over to stand in front of the child. "So, Latch Key Kid… my nemesis… ve meet at last!" said the doctor, with an air of drama about him. The eight year old child looked up at him, and raised a cup of lemonade. "Hello Mister. Do you want some lemonade?" said the kid. "Lemonade you say?" questioned the doctor, his eyes narrowing. Was this some kind of trap? "Is zis… poisoned lemonade!" Doctor Curiosity yelled the final words, and batted the cup from the hands of the child. It landed on the sidewalk, the potentially poisoned lemonade spraying over Eegor's legs. "Why did you do that, Mister?" asked the child, politely. "Oh, you vould like me to drink your poisoned lemonade, vouldn't you?" "What's poisoned lemonade? I'm Tim. I don't know any poisoned lemonade. I just make mine with lemons," said the child, Tim. "Oh, ze nefarious Latch Key Tim plays all ze innocent now. Look, you're clearly trying to become an evil genius, much like ze good doctor. You must be evil, you can't even spell 'cents' can you? Your sign alone makes me vant to kick you in ze face!" "But… but…" stammered Tim. "No excuses, Latch Key Kid! I'm taking you out before ze match! Have at ye!" The doctor let out a weak battle cry and kicked the side of the lemonade stall, smashing a brittle piece of wood and causing the whole side to fall apart. The lemonade jug shattered on the sidewalk, drenching Eegor even further. Timmy was now in tears, wailing loudly… but Curiosity wasn't finished. He grabbed Tim by the throat and lifted him up into the air. "Prepare for a taste of vhat you vill feel in ze ring, Latch Key Tim Kid!" The doctor lifted Tim to his shoulders and spun round at high speed. He was setting up for a devastating head-drop move known as Ye Olde Curiosity Shoppe… when Tim's mother came out, alerted by her son's screams. Doctor Curiosity spotted her in an instant, and threw the child at Eegor. "It vas him!" screamed the doctor. "What the hell is going on?" asked the mother, in that screeching yell common to obese women around the globe. "What happened, Tim? Timmy, talk to me!" She would have run across to see her son, had she not been forced to waddle instead. "Eegor. I tzink it is time ve made ourselves scarce." For once, the doctor and his assistant were in agreement. They fled. ---------------------------------------------- "You know, Eegor. I vill admit to vondering vhy I was finding it so easy to beat ze hell out of ze Latch Key Kid," said Doctor Curiosity, back on the roof of the Curious Laboratory. "Master, I think that was because it was just a small child with a lemonade stand." "How vas I supposed to know? He looked like a kid, and had a lemonade stand! Vhat more evidence vould I need? Anyvay, it vas an honest mistake, but ze fact still remains. I can take out anyvon who runs a lemonade stand." "Especially if it's a small child." "Jah, especially if… no! Do not try to trick me Eegor, you trickster!" The two brothers were smiling as they walked towards the door that would lead them back inside, to Collette. In the distance, the clouds darkened, and drew closer… ---------------------------------------------- Collette sat with her back to the cold, stone wall. Her hair was ragged, and her clothes were torn from her struggles to escape. Three day old mascara still smudged her face, tracing two ragged black lines down where her tears had fallen, like make-up put on by a blind goth. These days had not been happy ones for Collette, but they were drawing to a close. With the money from her husband, she would be able to settle her debts once and for all. The promise of further payments in the future just served to sugar the pill. She could take being made to look like a wretch if it would get her out of her predicament. Desperate times often call for desperate measures. The lock on the door rattled again, and drew her again from her reverie. She flexed aching limbs and stood up, ready to meet her husband looking as much his equal as she could muster. It was time to demand to be released. Before long, her boyfriend, the source of many of her debts, would alert the police as to her disappearance and her location. She rolled her eyes. By rights, they should have already arrived. He was just probably passed out with a bottle in his hand again. She had to ask herself, why was it that she always ended up with the weird guys? The door swung open slowly, and in walked Doctor Curiosity and Eegor. Both were dressed in their usual attire, the doctor’s bow tie impeccably tied by hand, as ever. Curiosity’s lab coat swished along scant millimeters above the stone floor, and he moved with a flourish to a small wooden table on the far side of the room. He reached over and picked up the large set of iron keys that sat there, pausing only briefly to look out of the window at the sky. The weather seemed to be getting worse by the minute; enormous black clouds looming ominously in the air. Curiosity dismissed them from his mind and turned back to his wife. He smiled at her in a manner that set her nerves on fire. Something was amiss here; he didn’t seem like the beaten man who had promised her eighty percent of his wealth at all. She visibly stiffened, and Doctor Curiosity stopped his progress towards her. “Vhat’s wrong?” he asked. “You. You’re smiling. If there’s one thing I’ve learned it’s not to trust you when you’re smiling,” replied Collette. “Vhatever could you mean?” asked the doctor, his smile growing into a wide grin as he advanced towards her. “Stop! Get away from me. Something’s wrong!” she yelled, stepping back from him, gripped by sudden paranoia. Doctor Curiosity stopped, and his face fell somber once more. “I apologise my dear. I vas just putting a brave face on to ze matter. As much as I have enjoyed seeing you in zese chains, I vill comply vith your requests… nay, your demands. It gives me no pleasure to see you vin here, Collette.” She breathed deeply. He seemed to be telling the truth, but it was getting harder to read his intentions. He was moving away from her, not just in terms of their marriage, but in terms of how he held himself, and how he acted. It was only then, at that late stage, that Collette realized that in their years apart, Doctor Curiosity had become an entirely different person. It was only then she realized that she might be out of her depth. “Unlock me.” “Of course.” The doctor inserted the key in to the first shackle that held Collette to the wall. He turned the key without a word, and the strong chain fell to the floor. Seconds later the chain was joined by another, as both arms were freed. Collette rubbed her wrists, stimulating the circulation in them. Doctor Curiosity unlocked her left ankle, and then her right. She stepped away from the doctor; free at last. “Oh jah, zere vas von final tzing I had to tell you,” said Doctor Curiosity. Collette turned, her heart sinking. She’d seen enough episodes of ‘Columbo’ to know how this worked. “You are entitled to eighty percent of my vealth, correct?” “Yes… don’t you try and get out of that either. I need the money, Chris. Don’t even bloody think about backing out now!” ranted Collette, the desperation showing in her eyes. “I vould never do such a tzing. You see, Collette, zere’s just ze von problem.” The doctor smiled beatifically. “I have no money.” Collette blinked. She couldn’t possibly have heard that correctly. The famous wrestler, Doctor Curiosity, was penniless? The fWo Cruiserweight Champion? The Universal Title Semi-finalist? The man down to the final six of GTT4? “Wh… what?” “It’s true, Collette. I spent all ze money I had earned before wrestling in buying zis place. Zen after zat, vell, a place zis size doesn’t run itself.” “Not to mention the cost of maintaining the scientific apparatus and buying new components for his machines,” chipped in Eegor. “So, Collette, you’re entitled to eighty percent of precisely fuck all. In fact, I’m about fifty thousand dollars in debt, my dear. You’re velcome to some of zat if you so desire. If not, zen please just leave, and ve’ll call it quits. Jah?” The smile was back on Doctor Curiosity’s face, but it didn’t last for long. The problem was that neither he nor Eegor had anticipated just how badly Collette needed that money. Between the gambling, the alcohol and the drugs… especially the drugs, she had gotten herself in very deep indeed. She was in deep enough to know that if she were to return with nothing, after all this time, she’d be as good as dead. “No! No! No! You can’t… you can’t have frittered ALL of it away! You can’t have done!” she raged. “All of it and more,” replied the doctor, serenely. He turned to smile at his brother, Eegor, and in that moment, Collette’s instincts took over. With an almighty cry of primal rage, she hurled herself at Doctor Curiosity, bringing her knee up hard to land square between his legs. Curiosity saw her coming an instant too late, and it hit him where it hurts. She pushed him to the ground, and got on top of him, as something skittered out of his jacket and spun across the floor. She pounded at the doctor’s face with closed fists, but Doctor Curiosity had not won the entire Rockies Bracket by being a slouch in a fight. With hardly any effort at all, he had Collette’s arms in his hands, and he flicked her to one side, where she landed in a heap; all tears and desperate sobs. Curiosity had noticed a weight leave his jacket though, and patted himself down quickly. He’d picked up something, and it had gone now. It was the… Oh no. It was the gun. Collette looked up to see Eegor and the doctor looking at the patch of floor directly in front of her. Before they could move, she had snatched it up and held the gun in trembling hands. The deadly weapon pointed from one man to the other, as she glanced nervously around the room. “Collette, do not do something you’ll regret,” counseled Eegor. “Fuck you, Paul!” she yelled in reply, pointing the gun at him. “Put ze gun down, Collette,” said Curiosity, edging closer towards her. “Back the fuck off! I mean it! You must have money, you MUST HAVE SOMETHING!” “Collette, I have nothing to give you.” She was broken inside. Everything she had tried would be for nothing, and the man who had been there throughout, usually grinning, had won. She would leave with nothing, another body to be hunted down and disposed of by loan sharks and drug dealers. No, she thought. If she was going down, then he was going too. Fuck him. The only sound in the room was the staccato sound of rainfall; the storm had arrived and was starting to beat against the windows. She breathed deeply, raised the gun at Doctor Curiosity, and fired.
