Rebuilding the Team

8 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-08 How far will you go to get what you want? Everybody is a little different when it comes to this. Some people have no qualms with being underhanded, sneaky, sly, whatever you want to call it. Deceitful might be a good word, even. Those are the people you really have to look out for, the people you’d expect it from. At least, that’s the common perception. But what about the people you wouldn’t expect that from? The quiet ones, the polite ones, the rule-followers. Nobody pays attention to them when it comes to this, because nobody feels a need to. The good guys, they’d never resort to deceitful tactics to get something, people say. Oh, don’t worry about him, they’d say, he’d never do something like that. And then they turn their backs. And when backs get turned, the quiet people speak up. --------------------- “Oh, Frank!” comes the shout from the counter area. Frank is busy talking with a table of young poets, and excuses himself to walk over to the counter. He approaches, and sees the Latch Key Kid standing there, hanging up the telephone. “What’s up, Brad?” Frank asks. “That was the, uhm, hardware store on the phone. They said that new coffee maker you ordered is in.” “Huh. I didn’t even hear the phone ring.” “Well, that’s probably because of all the noise in here. Besides, those poets have a way of capturing someone’s attention.” Frank shrugs. “Ah well, I guess you’re right. But the hardware store? Are you sure? I don’t remember order…but that doesn’t surprise me. Too much going on. Who was it, do you remember?” Latch scratches his head. “Uhm…Jack…” Frank frowns. “…Joe…” Still frowning. “…John…” Frank smiles and nods. Latch speaks with more conviction now. “John! That’s it. Nice guy. At least, he sounded like it. Yeah, anyway, he said it’s in, and said you can go pick it up now.” Frank nods. “Thanks, Brad.” “So, I’ll see you later, I guess.” “You taking off, Brad?” Frank asks. “No. But you are, right? I mean, that coffee maker…it’s not going to last forever. You better get a move on.” “Oh, don’t worry about it. They’ll hold it for me. I appreciate your concern, though.” “Actually…uhm…John…he said you should go get it today. Apparently it’s been there for a while, and if you don’t get it soon, he said they’re going to have to ship it back to clear space in their storage room.” “But I just ordered it last -” Latch interjects. “Didn’t you just say you don’t remember ordering it?” “Yeah, I did.” “Well, maybe you ordered it so long ago that it slipped your mind. Maybe that’s it. But anyway, he was pretty insistent that I tell you it’s urgent that you go pick it up today.” “Hmm. You’re sure it was John? From Jackson Hill?” Latch snaps his fingers. “That’s it! Jackson Hill Hardware! Yes. He said his name was John. I guess it’s up to you, really, but he sounded like it was important.” “Okay. In that case, I better head over there. The bank’s on the way, so I can kill two birds with one stone. Thanks, Brad.” “You’re welcome, Frank.” Frank walks off as Latch smiles, and sits down on a stool in front of the counter. He continues to sip his latte while Frank gathers a few things for his trip. He watches as Frank says his goodbyes to some of the regular customers, and then heads out the door. Latch watches until Frank’s car leaves the parking lot, and then claps his hands and hops up off the stool. “Time to get to work,” he exclaims, as he walks behind the counter and puts on an apron. Just then, Nancy comes running up to him. “What do you think you’re doing, Brad?” Latch smiles. “I’m clocking in, Nancy. What do you think I’m doing?” She shrugs. “Well, I’m not really sure. After all, Frank told you that you shouldn’t be helping out around here anymore. So I don’t really know what you’re doing. Seems to me, though, that if he told you not to work here, then you probably shouldn’t be working here.” He shakes his head. “Didn’t you hear? He put me back on. Yep, right before he left to go get that new, uhm…coffee maker. He had to go pick it up at Jackson Hill Hardware. They called a few minutes ago, and -” “Who called?” Nancy asks, not believing him. “I don’t know. Some guy named John, I guess. Frank seemed to know who he is. Yeah, this guy said it was pretty urgent that Frank get over there to pick it up today. Not sure what that’s all about, but it’s not really my place to worry about that. Nope, all I’m supposed to worry about is keeping an eye on this place. Just like Frank asked me to.” “To be honest with you, I don’t believe you. I don’t know why he’d put you back on. He had a talk with the three of us yesterday, and it was pretty much a mutual decision. It just doesn’t make sense to me.” “Doesn’t make sense to me that he’d order a coffee maker from a hardware store, but he did. Apparently. Who am I to question these things, Nancy? And who are you to question them?” “I’m an employee here, that’s who I am. I guess I have to take you on your word, since Frank isn’t here to ask. But I’m going to be watching you, Brad. Just make sure to stay out of the way, and leave the work to the three of us. If you want to sit behind the counter and talk to customers, that’s fine. But try to keep the confusion down, would you?” Latch nods and waves his hand in a whimsical manner. “Whatever you say, Nance.” Nancy sighs and walks off, obviously not pleased with the preceding conversation. However, she realizes she has no choice but to let Latch stay on. --------------------- The Latch Key Kid has never been one to tell lies. In fact, he pretty much despises liars. However, given the circumstances, he has been able to justify it to himself just this once. Okay, so maybe this isn’t the only time he’s ever lied, but he’s only lied when it’s been necessary. And this time, it was more than necessary. I deceived Frank, he thinks. I played off our friendship and betrayed his trust. If he finds out what I’ve done, he’ll be genuinely upset with me. I may never be able to build our level of trust up to where it is now. There’s only one thing I can do. He opens the phonebook, looks up a number, and then picks up the phone and dials. “Hello? Jackson Hill Hardware? Yes, I’d like to place an order, please.” --------------------- “So he really told you that Frank asked him to come back on?” Billy asks. Nancy is standing next to him, watching Billy wipe off the table. She seems to be lost in thought, and doesn’t answer. “I said, he really told you that Frank asked him to come back on?” Nancy snaps her head up. “Huh? Oh…uhm…yeah, that’s what he said. I thought it sounded odd, but hey, what do I know. I mean, take a look around this place. It’s all odd. Nothing really makes sense here. When two of the most regular customers are professional wrestlers, who can manage to get their own table and sit undisturbed for hours, then yeah, I’d call that weird. And you know, Frank did bring him on to begin with, so maybe there is something to his claim. I don’t know, Billy. If you want to go over there and talk to him, be my guest. But me, I’m done. I’m just gonna wait for Frank to get back. It’ll be easier that way, know what I mean?” “I know exactly what you mean. About everything you said. To be honest with you, I don’t really care what he does, as long as he stays over by the counter and doesn’t interfere with us. Other than that, it’s all cool with me. Besides, how much trouble can he possibly cause?” Nancy shrugs. “Your guess is as good as mine. Oh well. I see a table that needs clearing. I’ll talk with you later, Billy.” Nancy walks off as Billy continues to wipe down the table and straighten out the chairs. She walks across the room and clears a few empty mugs from a table, then wipes the table down with a rag. She takes the mugs over to the counter where Latch is making change for a customer at the register. The customer takes the coins, drops them in the tip jar and walks out the door. “Your first tip?” “Nope. Third, actually. While you and Billy were chatting it up over there, I was busy taking care of our customers.” Latch smiles at her. “Here, let me have those mugs. I’ll was them in the sink.” “Whatever.” Nancy leaves the mugs on the counter and walks off, eventually pulling up a seat at a table of girls from school. Latch sets the mugs in the sink, and washes each one out with care, drying each thoroughly and setting it among the other mugs on the counter. When he finishes this, he grabs a towel and heads out to walk around the shop. He stops at each empty table and wipes it down, taking extra care to rub off any residue from food or coffee, and straightening the chairs at each table. He stops at one of the sofa and fluffs the pillows, then does the same at the other unoccupied sofa. Billy watches from across the room, and then sneaks a glance at Nancy. She shakes her head and nods towards Latch and sneers, and Billy matches her. Latch walks over to a table, clears the empty mugs off of it, and wipes it down. He takes the mugs back to the sink, and then washes them and tidies up behind the counter. As two new customers walk through the door, Latch greets them and points out the daily specials, asking if they’d like any snacks to start off with. “Naw, man, it’s cool. We’re just here to meet some friends.” He and his girl walk over to a table of a few poet type kids, and sit down. Nancy catches Billy’s gaze again, and Billy nods. He walks over to the counter, and sits down on a stool. Latch turns around and nods at him. “Hey, Billy. What’s going on?” “Brad, look. We need to talk. It’s about, well, the work you’re doing.” “It’s great, isn’t it? Having me back on the team?” “Uhm, yeah, it is. Listen, man, Nancy and I were thinking that maybe you could tone it down a bit. You know, your energy level and stuff. It’s just that, well -” “What about it? I’m not causing problems, am I?” “Well, it depends what you mean by problems, really,” Billy says. “All I’m saying is, you seem a bit too eager, too rushed, ya know? Almost like you’re working with a purpose.” “Well, that’s because I am. I’m working to help make this a better place. There’s so much more we can do here.” “Brad, we’re doing it already. This place doesn’t need to be better. Look around, man. People dig it here. They can relax, and have fun. You know what? I think that’s what you need to do. You just need to relax, dude. You know, slow down a bit, take it all in, have fun.” Latch frowns. “But I am having fun. Great fun. And relax? I can’t relax. Not now, at least. Once I relax, then that will mean I let up and quit, and then it’ll all be over. I can’t quit this job, not after Frank gave me a second chance.” “Ah, there’s your problem,” Billy says as he snaps his fingers. “You don’t know what it means to relax. See, it’s got nothing to do with letting up or quitting, dude. You can relax and still stay in the game. Take us, for instance, me and Nancy, and Betsy when she’s here. We take it easy around here, we have fun, and we still run this place well and take care of it. It’s possible, man. In fact, relaxing make things easier.” “But I won’t get as much work done.” “That’s just it, dude. You will get as much work done, it’ll just be at a slower pace. And you’ll be able to enjoy the job more. I was watching you earlier, man. You were running around this place, rushing around to tables like they were going somewhere. Nothing’s gonna leave, Brad. If you can just manage to relax, you’ll be able to see things clearer.” “Are you sure? I don’t know about that. I’m kind of hesitant to slow down. I don’t want to let up.” “I’m telling you, that’s not the same as quitting. There’s nothing wrong with easing up on things. I think that’s exactly what you need to do right now, anyway. Take this tournament you’re in, the GTT4, right?” Latch nods. “Okay. So relax with the tournament, too.” “No way, Billy,” Latch protests. “As soon as I relax with the tournament, I’ll lose a match, and be out of it again, and that’ll be the end. I can’t afford that.” “You only think you’ll lose, but you won’t. In fact, I’d even go so far as to say that you might even do better. If you relax, you’ll be looser, your mind will be clearer, and you’ll be able to react better. I do it all the time, man. It’s easy, and it’ll help you.” “You really think that relaxing is different than quitting? Letting up would be good for me?” “Yeah, I do. And Nancy over there,” Billy points to Nancy, who smiles and waves, “she thinks the same thing, man. And she’s into all this stuff like auras and things, I don’t really understand much of that, but she does. And she says your aura needs to relax. Dude, I’m telling you, if she thinks your aura needs to relax, then it probably does.” “But you just said you don’t know much about that stuff.” “I don’t. But Nancy does. That’s what I’m saying, Brad. Relax. Lighten up. Have fun. Don’t be in such a rush all the time. Things will be fine, everything will work out well, and in no way will it mean you quit or let up. Just, you know, go a little slower.” Latch starts to nod slowly. “Yeah. I guess I can try that. That might work.” “Try it, man. That’s all I’m asking. Just give it a shot, you might surprise yourself.” Latch nods faster now. “Yeah, okay. I’ll give it a try.” Latch looks out the window and sees Frank’s car pulling into the parking lot. He takes off the apron, picks up a mug of latte, and takes a seat on a barstool in front of the counter. The door opens and Frank walks in, carrying a large box in a bag under his arm. Nancy walks over to greet him. “Hey, Frank. What’cha got there?” Nancy asks. “A coffee maker,” Frank says with a bit of confusion. “I picked it up at Jackson Hill Hardware.” Nancy looks puzzled. “But I thought…you didn’t…” Frank shrugs. “Well, I guess I did and just forgot about it. They had it ready for me, anyway, knew right where it was. I must be losing it in my old age.” And then, Frank, Nancy and Billy are turn and stare at Latch, sitting on the stool, drinking his coffee. He shrugs. “Hey, don’t look at me. All I did was pass along the message.” Frank sighs and walks behind the counter. He sets the box on the floor and pours himself a cup of coffee. “So, how’d it go while I was away?” Before Nancy or Billy can reply, Latch answers. “Oh, you know. The usual.” Nancy and Billy look at Latch and smile, then look at each other. They nod in agreement and speak at the same time. “The usual.”
