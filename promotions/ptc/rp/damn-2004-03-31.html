Damn

31 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-31 1 Still the feeling haunted her. It just wouldn’t leave at all, no matter what the amount of sleeping pills she decided to take. She could feel eyes watching her every pained move; like a heat sensitive CCTV camera. Things had changed drastically over the last few weeks and it made a vital difference to every single part of her life that she doubted could ever be put back in place again. People considered her a fan favourite. Sweet Illusion had kicked the ever loving hell right out of her. The way things look now; bleak. There was nobody there to watch her back when she needed it the most, no family and no friends, or so she assumed and would continue to do so for some time to come; nobody that she wanted at least; just that oaf-like message from Jade that she’d taken five days to delete, after several attempts. Carys was perched on the plush velvet cushion that snuggled between her and the singular wrought iron chair in her over sized bedroom. Her chin balanced down on her hands that were in turn folded over raised knees. There was no one in the room, or for that matter in her life. ’What’s that?’ That annoying voice in her head questioned, for the first time that day. She tried to dismiss it, but the persistent noise that clonked in her left ear practically dragged her head from her hands. At first it would have been easy enough to dismiss as the gentle thud of rain splashing down on the veranda. But the second thud came clearer; its repetitive nature eliminated all doubt of the weather being to blame. Her feet went from the chair down to the wooden flooring prior to dragging herself from the haven of comfort she had built for herself. They plodded along, creating that similar dull sound she had heard from behind the window only clearer. It was enough to make her scared of her own shadow though and her pace picked up as she neared the draped curtains, hoping for some fluorescent light from the street to bring back her wall of comfort. The sight after the curtains being drawn back brought no such thing. She could had sworn that she saw a lifeless hanging shadow hovering from the lamp post, but after being scared to death by a creak in the floor behind her and looking back there was nothing. It’s coming for you. Is what the voice screeched inside her head. “Shut UP!” she mumbled after banging her fist lazily against the wall as though it were the living reincarnation of the voice. She sighed audibly, the loudness of it enough for the hairs on the back of her neck to ease off a little. Her hand went up to her head, testing her temperature just like her mother used to, hoping to high hell that this voice was just some side effect of the sleeping pills, or even that she was ill, physically ill was the only kind that she would accept. The telephone ringing in the other room was at first mistaken for something in her head, she’d hoped at least…oddly. After five rings and her ordering it to “be quiet,” again she sprinted into the front room and missed the phone by about half of a second. Carys moaned as she held her head again, she’d managed to work up a sweat in such a small amount of time. This wasn’t a good sign for her, she realised immediately, if she was in such a bad condition in preparation for her first match in the tournament. If she was scared of her own shadow she wasn’t so sure how she was going to cope when men were chasing after her trying to beat what little snot was left in her back out of her. Her worries of the upcoming Prime time Central tournament were interrupted when she realised that the TV was on. It wasn’t like she’d heard it when she came into the far end of the apartment and just had it not register with her; she’d almost jumped out of her skin, almost like it had just turned itself on. Once her nerves had stopped pounding inside her head and her yes had properly adjusted to use what little light was left in the room to the maximum. Her blue eyes flickered minimally by the flashing beacon of the answering machine. Her head thudded incredulously, had she not heard someone leave a message? Was she really that spaced out? Her hand grasped for something, sure that she was going insane and that any moment her legs would collapse from beneath her. “Oh crap,” she exclaimed silently as she remembered that it was a silent answering machine. She could have smiled a bright beacon of light into the room at that very moment in time, had a thought not dawned on her, well not so much a thought as that bastard voice creeping back over her. Jade it taunted what she had already managed to brush from her mind. Her hands clinched, an insane notion swept over her for the slightest of seconds, that being that if she hung herself from her bedpost the voice would stop bugging the hell right out of her. She backed away from the telephone like it held a single dose of the bubonic plague. Her footsteps were short and sure as she backed into the open kitchen; her head thudded on something wooden. “Crap!” she cursed, that was intentionally over loud, her back jolted with pain as she stumbled into the light switch and flicked it on. What she saw then freaked her out more than any amount of time with that goddamned voice could. Every single drawer, cupboard door and window was open in the kitchen; someone had been in there, her own house. She would have said something, but sadly she didn’t even have the energy to breathe. Her heart stopped mid pump, her lungs refused to take in any air whatsoever and her head felt like it was swimming in a pool of unimportant yet unimaginable colours. No, she was determined to keep some kind of grip on reality, her teeth clamped down on her bottom lip so hard that everything came back to her in a thunderous storm. A roused feeling of fear in her stomach almost had her down to the ground, on her hands and knees, emptying the contents of her stomach. Carys turned, her eyes struggled double fold in the lack of light and dizzying speed in which her head moved. All that movement had been elicited by the sound of the switch on the cinematic screened TV being pressed again. Her eyes and mouth lowered like she had been struck at the top of her head with her very own shovel, then had it dragged further and further down. “Remember me?” a voice croaked, a tight wire of plastic caught the small amounts of light as it ran around their neck. Their body was a tight silhouette against the screen’s light, which only flashed static on the screen. A different metallic edge carved its way through the night air as it made a beeline for her neck, all she could do was force a cry from somewhere beneath her intestines. She felt pain, an unforgiving one beat at her neck. 2 ‘CRUNCH’ The wooden shoebox that lay at the foot of Carys’ pointless four poster bed collapsed under the weight of her head as she fell over the guardrail. Needless to say everything but the pain in her neck was a dream. Something that she barely even remembered now, but her head throbbed. Her shaking hand went up to mop the liquid from her brow, her disdain increased when it came back covered in blood. Like waking suddenly from a bad dream wasn’t enough for the young wrestler, referred to as the ‘Seventeen sensation’ in her home federation. She shuffled along the ground, like a baby with her clammy super sized tee shirt hanging loosely around her delicates as she did so, the wooden floor hurting her hands little enough for her to ignore it as she approached what she needed. Her hand grasped the neck of the lamp shakily and traced it to the switch before clicking it into place. The pathetic mask that had spread across her face since the other dream strained into a smile a bloody one at that. The half empty – that was how she saw it, in a new pessimistic view - glass of orange juice was chugged down to its last drips. “Ahh,” she purred with satisfaction. Something that was short lived by her unwarranted paranoia, at least she believed, hoped perhaps, that it was unwarranted. She tried to change her mindset with a brief fought for what would happen in the Prime Time Central Cruiserweight Division Tournament, trying with all of her heart to worry about her more physical pains over the emotional. Fury, an ego, there was no doubt. Ego’s follow men round like the stink of testosterone left to sweat for a few days… “Gaw…” she couldn’t finish, too much thinking about the upcoming match was head trauma just waiting to happen, more head trauma. Her eyes averted away from the lamp and up to the squeaky ceiling, which forced an ear busting scream from her, one that hurt her lungs to start, but even had an after effect that felt like throwing up razor blades after drinking sulphuric acid. That figure from her dream suddenly made sense. It was Jade. 3 Like she had been immersed in freezing cold water for ten minutes then suddenly released her head sprang out from beneath her silk sheets, with a deep chesty sigh that could easily have been a cry. Another dream. It was not one she was going to put up with again on that night. Her feet dragged dozily across the bedspread as she wrapped the sheets around her body. A pop of the bones in her neck told her that finally this was no dream. That was something which both comforted her, and irritated her. The phone had cut into her sleep like a dagger separating flesh from bone. Prior to the rude awakening by the phone she had been snugly tucked under her sheets, which clung only in slight disarray that any sleep brings. In fact the only thing that was unusual about her sleep was that she was moist with sweat: from worry. There were equal points to blame for her un-rested and highly uncomfortable sleep. On one hand there were these increasingly dubious dreams of her one time mentor and friend, Jade. Then on the other, her injured one if you like, was the upcoming tournament, something that she had never had great success for. No matter the recent change of heart she still had the doubts clinging to her brain like the wet sheets she lay in; to be considered much greater than niggling uncertainties. Rain thudded down against the window, even in the early morning blue sky she could see the overcast clouds making their way into sight; meaning even more rain. There was a clash of thunder to follow the rain that sent a nasty sense of foreboding creeping up her spine in a shiver. The light that flickered to nothing on the bedside table is what snapped her out of her match worries and planted her right back in the middle of another Jade dilemma. She was coated in the darkness like I it had floated over from her dream. She had the overwhelming feeling that she was not alone in the room, that what little light there was remaining was casting shadows not from her person alone. A floorboard creaked, it occurred to her as very odd as there had not been a single board movement in the entire apartment since she moved; indicating that there was either a great deal of weight on the boards… …Or that something was coming from beneath. Her body felt tugged forward like a rope had been tied around her waist. Her clumsy hands traced along jagged ends of furniture as she made her way to the only existing light in the house. Her head fell to the side in a movement that made her look like a confused puppy. Her hand hung out in front of her as the light became clearer. It was emanating from the living room. Whatever it was drawing her dug up something deep down inside of her, pulling at something…maybe her pheromones. The sexual inexperience of the seventeen sensation only added to the aura of whatever it was in the front room. Hormones were no doubt to blame for this lack of sense, but she made no care for it anyway. The light dwindled to an afterthought as she made her way to the lounge. Her eyes chased the room looking for signs of life, and all the ice blue balls found was a flashing red beacon on her answering machine, without reservation she pressed the button, she needed something to do to stop sleep, after all. “Carys…hey baby.” she rolled her eyes, an involuntary reaction that happened every time Al referred to her as his ‘baby.’ “I want you to get into the Halton Arena and do a little promotion work for the crui…” the call was ended with a beep. “Message deleted.” The monotone voice of the answering machine replied to her pressing the grey button with haste. “Hi Carys, long time no speak,” a velvety smooth Italian accent made her ears feel like they were being massaged. “With Sweet Illusion all but gone now, I figure you’ll need a new hobby. Call me, oh and good luck.” her well wishing was greatly appreciated. What happened then would stick with her perhaps for the rest of her life. She span back on her heel and saw the single most shocking vision that could ever be mustered in life. So viciously ugly. Carys shrieked.
