Act VI: The Songs That Dead Men Sing, The Sights The Damned May See

3 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2010-01-03 December 26th, 2:33 P.M. EST Monstrous Sighting in Arabian Desert By Darren Kipling Associated Press Writer Cairo, Egypt (AP) -- Strange and even terrifying tales of unexplained phenomena have circled the globe in increasing frequency over the past several weeks. Newest to the list of occurrences is that of something viewed by hundreds in the deserts of the Middle East. Whatever the monstrosity was, witnesses claim it appeared to rise from the sands as if it was a part of the sprawling dunes that encompass much of that area of the world. Pandemonium erupted as reports came in of the sighted beast in not one place, but in several at once. If true, what could have stirred such a colossus to emerge from the desert reaches? An even greater question may be what caused multiple organisms to rear their heads to the world. Nothing of the sort has every been seen before, and scientists the world over are studying the few photographs of the animals to ascertain their origins. Reports indicate these to be massive serpentine animals, their upper bodies lined with tentacles that stretched out to feel across the sands as they lifted their heads to the skies overhead. Observation was short-lived as none of the beasts was visible among the sands for more than a few scant moments. After no more than a minute of making their appearances known to a world having never seen them before, thrashing about under the burning sun, the beasts crashed back into the arid sands of the desert, burrowing beneath the surface to wherever they came from… * * * * “I want to bring your world crashing down around you. That doesn’t leave much wiggle room for humanitarianism.” Even over a speaker phone, Shanahan’s charisma flourished. It was an unnatural presence; perhaps not even the image of Hitler in Nazi Germany could stir people to action the way the venomous words of Bruce Shanahan could. Maury Dawson had gathered any and all of his team together as he anticipated contact from the Bringer of the Black Gospel. With Wyatt Manor, his previously unassailable fortress, ravaged by explosives, his fearsome rage was tempered only by a calculating desire to ferret out the culprits. Dawson felt that, for once, maybe it was wise to give Shanahan what he pursued. If only to slow his inevitable rampage of violence. “Obviously not, Bruce. I don’t have any sympathy for you. But the fact remains that there’s still a rogue party out there somewhere, and whoever it is, they’re not playing nice with any of us. So enlighten me. What are your own suspicions?” On the other end, Shanahan exuded but a sigh for a moment. Anticipating the reply, Maury knew that Bruce was lamenting his mortal limitations. “The visions are hazy. The Old Ones have denied clarity as I’ve sought my transgressors. Perhaps, whoever it may be, has come across methods of reflecting my arcane scrying. Such failsafes exist, and it appears our friend has done his research.” “Allies seem to be difficult to come by right now, don’t they, Bruce? Even your lords seem to have lost faith in your crusade.” The maniac growled. “Clever. But if I discover that this was caused by any of those miserable curs under your command, Maury, the pain I will inflict on your allies will be grievous indeed. I will bring Blackhelm crashing down on itself. And that first attack on your holy seat will be a like a firecracker in comparison to its successor.” The warning was stark and simple. From a conniving monster like Shanahan, Dawson was sure he’d make good on it if push came to shove. “We’re monitoring all that we can,” he said, trying to circumvent talk of more fury-spurred attacks. “Hopefully your rogue stalker will contact us. It’s common. Hell, it’s probably inescapable.” “What do you suppose he’ll request of you?” asked the dark mastermind. “It’s difficult to say. Perhaps a fee for your death, an assassin of sorts. Or maybe he’ll up the ante by taking out something of ours like he did you, play a game of battle royale. He’s already played all of us against each other. He knew you’d be hiding out with Marion, and sent Mizakawa’s thugs storming in in the hopes of wiping all of us out. His next ploy is anyone’s guess.” “Hmmm. A fair assessment. That reminds me, I have a word of warning for you, Maury: Luther appears to be hellbent on a reckoning with Major Stryker. I don’t know why except that it appears to be some archaic sense of warrior honor. Perhaps that slant-eyed rat you stole from me could shed some light on the subject; Mizakawa claims to be studied in fruitless codes of that sort. Best that you somehow wrest some information from him before he’s dead.” Tatsuya had been sent to a secret location while the conflict was played out. Barring a massive intelligence leak, Maury was certain that Shanahan would never get his hands on him. But given the current temperament of his antagonist, he supposed it was best to avoid raising his ire further. “By the way, Maury…the mask. Have you donned it lately? Does it still fit as snugly as it did the last time we had the chance to share wine?” There were murmurs among those under Dawson’s supervision, some calling into question the manner of Shanahan’s ‘puzzling comments’ as if he spoke obscurely, in the abstract riddles they’d come to know. “I know you’re among friends right now. Do your subordinates know that you’re my loyal mole? How many are aware of our past as old chums, employee and employer in that first probing masquerade four years ago?” Bruce was using his window of opportunity to sew dissent among his pursuers. Fracturing them from within with distrust and suspicion. “Tell Gabriel I said hello. Adieu.” There was a chuckle and click denoting the madman’s disconnect. “How does he know what he does, Maury?” The question, and many variations of it, peppered him from all sides. Dawson struggled to believe his ears; his loyalty was under fire by his own team. Only Gabriel seemed immune to the poison secreted from the cult leader’s forked tongue, dismissing it with the outright certainty of a loyal blood-brother. “His stronghold has just been demolished,” the other said, trying to allay the suspicion that had broken the surface amidst the team. “Gutted like the Vatican was. His fury may be unbearable if we don‘t find who is behind it. Whoever is orchestrating all of this, he’s a friend to no one. And even if he does end up having more of a bone to pick with Bruce then any of us, he’s pushing him closer and closer to his trump card. God help us all if we discover what that may be. This isn‘t the time to be questioning Maury or his intentions. Shanahan‘s empire has been forged on lies and misdirection as much as it has blood and murder. Don‘t fall for his ruse.” “He spoke about Luther Ridley seeking a death duel with Stryker,” Gabriel pointed out. “Where is Marcus now?” “On Rourke’s trail. We stumbled on a lead we couldn’t pass up. He‘s in Virginia.” “Might it be a trap? Our ‘rogue party’ attempting to thin the herd?” Dawson sighed. “It may be. Marcus didn’t want to hear of it when I radioed him. He wants Rourke any way he can get his hands on him. The major thinks he may be a key to ending Bruce’s plans, and I tend to agree.” He didn’t mention he may have been more reluctant to see Stryker fall than anyone else in his command. The statement held merit but would drastically undermine their objectives. Gabriel‘s voice fell to a hushed whisper as the others returned to their duties, cohesion intact in light of their benefactor‘s well-timed words. “Between only us, away from the others, I have one question.” It didn’t take any psychic leanings to discern what it would be. He could only brace himself for the impact. “Are you a mole? Have you been feeding him elements of our operations?” Dawson was silent for a long moment. He tightened his lips and grit his teeth, reaching for the right words to answer the allegation. They were simple ones. “No. You have my word.” “Do you swear it upon your solemn oath as a ranking member of this great and holy organization?” For a long moment his only response was the echo of his footsteps that carried him past the upper circle zealot, and mysterious man he’d come to call a friend. Whether he simply found the query an insult, or perhaps something lay buried beneath the surface, was known to him and him alone. * * * * Marion Sterling’s home had been a pristine estate before Shanahan had come weeks earlier. Now an entire wing was undergoing repair, shattered windows newly replaced and walls patched up until long-term amendments were more feasible. The lady was lodged up elsewhere in a state of luxury she was more accustomed to, but the home was far from barren. Wyatt Manor was decimated, and it would take weeks of construction to pass for livable again, months to return to its former glory as a bastion of the Old Ones’ devoted. For now, Shanahan was on the run, and Sterling’s broken residence would prove a suitable base of operations. It would just require more layers of clothing than before. The nights were cold and it had yet to be sufficiently insulated for Lady Sterling‘s posh tastes. In a broader sense, perhaps it was a blessing in disguise; the manor’s resources were temporarily allocated across a vast territory, and to capture any one location would not cripple the entirety. It was as a web spun by their architects in the realms beyond, the schematics passed on to their prophet to strengthen his discipleship. The wind whistled through groves of naked, skeletal trees outside. The air was crisp in the house but the glowing embers of a fire did well to break the icy chill that otherwise consumed his mob of shivering apostles. They never once questioned his orders to patrol the grounds or maintain a steady surveillance across the estate; they were like dogs, loyal to their master until death, and at a command would tear the throat from any who encroached on his domain. The perfect instruments for the Old Ones. “Brother Knight.” The other paused. Resting in the shadows, the Angel of Death waited for the orders he was sure were forthcoming. “I believe Luther betrayed us.” As was customary for Ethan Knight, he showed no sign of emotion; rare was such a disclosure if not kneeling at their infernal mass or splitting open the throats of their enemies from the shadows. He only nodded gravely. “If such treason ever rears its head amidst the ranks of Seraph, or in Luther himself, stop it before it spreads.” His eyes fell solemnly on his lieutenant. “I may rue the possibility of losing such a powerful pet, but I will not be poisoned by the serpent I’ve fed.” It was a challenge the other would eagerly accept. Ethan smiled, a grim yet thankful assent by a man bored by fugitive life. “I will be as the mongoose to the cobra, my lord. Should his fangs come to bear, I will strike him down before his venom can do its work.” His pupil vanished, set to carry out his duty if deemed necessary. Shanahan continued to muse, eyes closing, his vision painted by a faint orange hue under the meager heat of the fire. With the dawn would come vengeance anew. Together with their allies they would paint the world red. Red like crimson. * * * * “I see the same as before. A cross, ashen robes cast over it. I see a flurry of rifle fire, blood-smeared evergreens, men in camouflage crashing against one another in brutal combat.” Blood coursed from his arm, though he’d taken care not to slice as deep this time. Kerry, a willing vessel that bridged the gap between worlds, was cursed, or blessed in the view of others, to serve with a morbid distinction as ‘The Bleeder’. With his suffering came a sort of remote intelligence, however cryptic as it may be. To spill more of his life onto the floor, and further challenge the longing clutches of Death, would invoke ever more knowledge, but Dawson couldn’t ask further of him. He could make due with the suffering oracle’s current messages. His martyrdom was well received. “I’m sorry to ask this of you, Kerry. I don’t wish to see you in pain. I just wish I could stymie it somehow… ” “That would compromise the visions,” the other said with a grimace. “If these many scars and sacrifices can redeem my brothers on Earth, they are justifiable. My tears aren’t the tears of a man grieving with agony; they’re an expression of my love and my hopes. No physical affliction can ever drown out my willingness to save this world from those who may blaspheme against Him.” Dawson’s hopes were to someday see Kerry ordained as a saint. But given the covert ways of their society, he knew it to be a hollow and ultimately fruitless ambition. There was a scream. It was the boy, and in that shriek was terror like the boy had never shown. “Something is wrong with Milo.” A chill swept down his spine like a spray of ice water and his gut tightened into a rock-hard knot. He feared the possibility of what the child had wrought, trying to shut out images of Milo suffering the same fate as his parents, torn apart by the claws of demons as he stared into that mirror. “Gabriel!” he hissed into his radio. “What’s happened?” “Come look. And be strong.” Kerry lowered himself onto a chair, finally clamping a bandage onto the gushing wound as Maury Dawson raced madly down the corridor. “Virg….Virginia. Angels falling to Earth…that dread name…ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn…” He looked down at the wounds that began to open on his body, old scars splitting of their accord. Blood dribbled from his arms, legs and chest before cascading to the floor in terrible scarlet sheets that flooded his cell. In his ears he could hear that abominable chant, rising in volume with every pump of his arteries and in rhythm with the thumping beat in his ears. A vision clouded his final moments. A silhouette, wings shredded and a scimitar in hand, hovered over a gore-strewn battlefield. It observed its former brothers, the tentacled star-spawn and sons of Man, as they died at each other’s hands, for it was simultaneously of both worlds and of none. Above, an object, a red moon, came into view. An eye opened, the moon’s own giant eye, and blinked once as it observed the carnage. The Earth below split as molten lava exploded forth, dousing the alien things as they raged their war. Inhuman screams and roars bathed a dying world as behemoths formed from the ashes of the dead, great illimitable things that soon spanned beyond the horizon in their girth, and set to forth to stalk the wastelands beyond the apocalyptic killing fields. Kerry fell to the floor, drowning in his own blood, and commenced dying.
