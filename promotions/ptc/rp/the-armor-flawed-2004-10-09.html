The Armor Flawed

9 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-09 .:: ::: ::. .:: Geffroi LaBatte was not paranoid, not as Alessandro Pazzi had been. His sprawling mansion lay on the edge of town, protected by a high brick wall, but not much else. There were no security cameras—she would have sensed the heat of their working, smelled the scent of heated little circuits. But there was none of that. ::. .:: She paced around the complex, finding an ideal place to scale the wall. It was isolated, on the side of the property, and a large, hanging tree loomed over it. She scaled the wall with ease and grace that only an Assassin could manage. ::. .:: A few moments of silence ensured that she was still alone in this area of LaBatte’s property. Now, she made her way towards the house, slinking towards it from shadow to shadow on the headily-scented lawn. It smelled as though it had just been cut today—LaBatte seemed spoiled enough to have a gardener, she thought with a roll of her eyes. But that was aside from the point. She was not here to make assumptions about LaBatte. She was here for information, real concrete information on how LaBatte lived, how he conducted his business behind the gate and walls that closed off his estate from the rest of the world. ::. .:: A few moments had her at the side door in the bottom floor of the house. With a credit card from her pocket—stolen, of course—she was able to slip the lock in no time. The door opened quietly, without so much as a creak, and she stepped into a darkened room. Her eyes adjusted quickly, detecting the silhouettes of bins and boxes around her. Using her keen scent, she picked up only refuse. There was nothing particularly important down here, and she could move on. ::. .:: Finding the floor plans of the LaBatte mansion had been difficult. She had searched and searched, but the only thing she’d been able to come up with was a preliminary plan designed by the architects at Cleveland’s Carlton Architecture. She wasn’t sure if the actual construction had gone to plan, but it wouldn’t hurt her to know the basic layout. From the outside, it looked similar, but then again, this room hadn’t been marked on the plans, and here it was. She’d have to be careful. ::. .:: A few careful, well-plotted series of movements had her on the entry floor of the mansion in no time at all, despite some close calls with servants milling about. They probably wouldn’t be so prevalent on this floor, especially considering that Mr. LaBatte was out, and probably wouldn’t be home until much, much later. Some sort of late night board meeting. Most likely with Vittorio and Cadenza Giovanni, if she had to guess. ::. .:: She slipped into the study, or at least the room that had been marked as the study on the floor plans, shutting the heavy wooden door behind her. For a moment, she was silent, slightly fearful. She had smelled a Kindred in this place. She didn’t know where the smell was coming from, but she knew that if one of her kind was here, she’d have to be even more wary than she planned on being. When the scent had passed, she took the time to look around. ::. .:: Shadow breathed a sigh of relief. This place was just like the floor plan—a proper study, laid out with a large desk, ornate wood-paneled walls, and a plush, comfortable chair. Several filing cabinets lined the wall, unobtrusive, and books lined a bookshelf as well. They were mostly books on finance, which she guessed a Giovanni ghoul would need—in any case, she guessed that they were pretty boring. She spent a few more moments glancing over the decorative touches in the study. Nothing too fascinating, other than LaBatte’s obvious taste for ostentation. He must have enjoyed making himself feel more important than he was. She rolled her eyes. ::. .:: Then, it was over to the desk. The desk was still littered with papers, as apparently, Mr. LaBatte had plans to resume his work once he came home from his meeting. A ghoul’s strength would give him the faculties necessary to work late into the night, she knew. She looked over the papers there. Most of them were simple financial duties. Apparently, Cadenza and Vittorio had planned for LaBatte to take over the duties of Alessandro Pazzi, now that Mr. Pazzi was quite indisposed. ::. .:: She opened a drawer. The first thing that she saw was a small slip of paper that held a set of seven numbers separated by dashes. She looked around her—most likely the numbers that would deactivate the combination lock on the safe that hung in the wall behind a tapestry. ‘Not too bright, this one,’ she concluded. Leaving the combination to the safe in his desk drawer was probably one of the dumbest things he could have possibly done, she reflected, but nevertheless, it was worth a look, she supposed, even if only to see the stockpiles of money that any worthwhile Giovanni or Giovanni ghoul would keep in his safe. ::. .:: She slipped over to the wall, lifted up the tapestry, and slipped underneath it. The plating of the safe faced her, a large combination lock creating a cancerous lump on the front of it. She was surprised something that large couldn’t be seen from across the room. With skilled hands, she twirled the lock, twisting in the memorized numbers quickly. There was a loud clicking noise, and she swung it open, peering inside the semi-cavernous structure. ::. .:: She’d expected to find mostly money or other objects in here, but there were a lot of papers as well. ‘Papers for what?’ she wondered. As her eyes scanned across the assortment, she noticed that one large notebook was crammed full of papers. In the clear pocket on the front was a picture of a large crest, and the words ‘Cleveland’s The Den.’ She rolled her eyes. Preliminary research on LaBatte had allowed her the information that he was the President of The Den—a hunting organization based out of Cleveland. She had to wonder what kind of nutcase this guy was if he was keeping the records of his hunting club in his safe. ::. .:: In any case, she didn’t have time to read them over. She looked over a couple of other stacks, these mostly financial records, careful to keep most of them undisturbed. A paper hanging on the back wall of the safe via a strip of gummy Scotch tape caught her attention. Messy writing scrawled across the front of it, and she leaned closer to scrutinize it, reading the message out loud quietly. ::. “Den Quarters… Turn lights off, avoid trip-lasers, place holder gem in mouth of statue, twist head towards west wall.” .:: Her brow furrowed. Why was The Den that much of a big deal? Why so much pomp and circumstance just to get into its Quarters? She carefully lifted a bag from the back of the safe, opening it. With a roll of her eyes, she discovered that it contained only stacks of greenbacks, as opposed to containing the holder gem that she was looking for. Oh well. Something else would have to do. She quickly memorized the instructions, then shut the safe as quietly as possible. She emerged from the tapestry, smoothing her hair back, and narrowing her eyes in determination. ::. .:: Nothing that she carried with her would approximate a gem, not like she needed it to. She walked over to the statue that was referenced, a gray and gruesome lion’s head with a setting in its mouth for a stone. A small stone, though. She sat down in one of the chairs across from the desk, crossing her legs. And that was when she saw it—a small pebble was wedged in between the treads of her left boot. Perhaps that would be sufficient. With her fingernails, she pried it out, going over the instructions in her head one more time before she slipped over to the light switch on the wall. ::. .:: With a flick, the room was plunged into darkness. She looked around, carefully noting the presence of trip-lasers emanating from pointers that were mounted somewhere in the wall. She ducked under the first, and stepped over a criss-cross pattern on the floor, then carefully held her coat up to step over one that was at about knee-level for her. This guy certainly took his precautions… but over the quarters of a hunting lodge? That question still stuck in her mind as she ducked over to the statue, slipping the pebble into its mouth, into the setting where Geffroi LaBatte’s elegant holder gem gleamed when Den meetings were in session. With a conscious remembrance of which direction was which, Shadow turned the head of the statue towards the west wall of the room. Now there was only waiting, to see if it would work. ::. .:: There was a grating noise, and then a portion of the west wall began to slide backwards. Once it had gone back about four feet, it slid to the side, conveniently out of the way. Flickering light was seen from inside this small tunnel. ::. .:: Shadow approached the west wall carefully, scenting out the tunnel and the Den Quarters before even venturing to put her head inside. Nothing. No one was here. She slipped into the tunnel, and started into the Quarters. ::. .:: The flickering was ingeniously produced by electronic torches on the walls. She paused to look at a set of them for a moment, then noted that there were pictures on the walls in between the torches. She examined one picture closely—a shot of Geffroi LaBatte preparing to take a stance behind a podium. He was dressed in flowing, regal blue robes detailed in gold. She rolled her eyes. He certainly had a penchant for ostentation. As she looked at the other portraits, though, she realized that the other Den members were dressed the same way. This was getting weirder and weirder. ::. .:: With caution, she proceeded down the hallway. Every step was a practiced, quiet tread until she came to the main room of the Quarters. This was apparently a mere auditorium, set up with several rows of chairs, and a stage with a podium in its center. Nothing too interesting here. What caught Shadow’s eyes, however, was a sign above the door in back of the stage. “Trophy Room” it read. That might be worth a look, considering that all this time, she’d never gotten an inkling of what, exactly, the Den members were hunting. ::. .:: With steps still as quiet as the approach of doom, she mounted the stage, and slipped to the back of it. Slowly, she pushed open, and it gave a slight creaking protest. A banner on the back wall caught her attention first. “We Will Give Them Their Gehenna!” it proclaimed. Her senses were immediately pushed upwards, on alert. ::. .:: She glanced around the rest of the room, her eyes adjusting very slowly to the dim light. Panic picked at her senses, horror shoving its way up from deep down inside her as the shadowy outlines from the walls became clear. On one wall was a mounted head, a mounted Kindred head, she realized. Its features were twisted into a vengeful snarl, fangs distended. The complexion and dark hair were all she needed to see—the head was from some being of undeniably Arabic descent. ::. .:: She looked to the other side of the room, her horror intensifying, revulsion looming up from the pit of her stomach. This was a more intensive figure—a bust. A looming Kindred with ravaging claws extended above its head, long black hair trailing down its back, and deep, nut-brown complexion. A mark of Haqim was etched on its forehead, and she couldn’t tell if it had been male or female—but it had been old. ::. .:: Over in the corner was something even worse. An entire preserved corpse. This time, the gaze was penetrating and penitent, the head swathed in a hijab. A woman. Her hands were clasped in prayer, a Koran clasped between them. Below her figure was a plaque: “Suffer no Child of Haqim to his unlife.” A scream surged within her entire being, chill bumps rising to the surface of her skin. ::. .:: She covered her mouth before she could let it out, backpedaling out of the trophy room as quickly as she could. She slipped out of the Quarters, darting back to the statue in the study to turn the head back to its proper position, and yank out the pebble that was substituting for the gem. She wished she’d never opened the Quarters, she wished… ::. .:: Panic seized her. ::. I have to get the hell out of here… Jesus Christ and Mary, if I stay here, he’ll hang me up on the wall. I have to get out, I have to get out, I have to get out… .:: That was the only phrase repeating in her head. She could hardly think about leaving things as they’d been before she came in. If she didn’t get out, it wouldn’t matter if things were left as they had been. She darted across the hall back to the set of stairs, and then out of the mansion through the entrance she had used to get in. ::. .:: Her throat was closing off—it wasn’t like she needed it to breathe, or anything, but it was unpleasant. At the same time, her stomach was heaving, and her brain was filled with utter panic. The madness broke upon her like a storm, and she bolted across the lawn, gasping for breath like a little Kine. She had no time to think about it, no time, no time, no time… ::. .:: Her lithe form was nearly at the edge of the fence when the iron gates began to open, their creaking mechanism interrupting the only other noise of the night, which was her frenzied breathing. Frenzy grew in her at every second, she had to get out before anyone saw her. ::. .:: A lithe, black-clad form vaulted over the fence and disappeared into the night just as the limousine of Jason LaBatte, now Geffroi LaBatte pulled through. He watched with interest. Whatever the creature was, it was using Celerity to escape, as he lost sight in the blink of an eye. ::. .:: LaBatte leaned forward, tapping on the glass of the limousine window to get his driver’s attention. The window slowly rolled down, as the barrier between the driver and LaBatte was removed. ::. “Klaus, we have no visitors planned, do we?” .:: The man shook his head vigorously. ::. “Nein, nein, no one.” .:: LaBatte smiled. Klaus had served him faithfully these years, even if Klaus was completely terrified of his master. LaBatte made a noise denoting his interest. ::. “It would seem our reputation has attracted someone’s interest, then.” “Should I call the Den?” .:: LaBatte smirked. ::. “Too well, Klaus, you know me too well. Do so immediately, and instruct Bedford to prepare the Quarters.” .:: Klaus picked up the phone in the front of the limousine. A tap on the roof of the car reminded him to raise the window between him and his employer before he called. In the back of the limousine, Geffroi LaBatte looked down at the gem set on the opulent golden ring on his finger with a twisted smile. This would be fun, he surmised. He could finally set things in motion to catch the Assassin who had killed Pazzi. He had no doubt that was who had paid him such a rude visit. And the Den had been clamoring for his blood all this time too. ::. .:: Geffroi LaBatte had not had a pleasing meeting this evening with the Giovannis. Vittorio was still angry that the Assassin had not been dealt with. Even Cadenza, who usually favored him, had been crisp and distant. But this could solve quite a few problems, and as usual, all it would take was one phone call. ::. .:: He chuckled under his breath. Yes, Klaus had served him well. He had served him well indeed. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: Shadow’s stomach heaved. She had already thrown up most of the blood that was in it by this point, but her body was still quivering, still spasming with the unnamable dread that the Den Quarters inspired her with. Those faces, all those poor Assassins cut down and strung up like prize lions in that bastard’s mansion. Pink-tinged tears filled her eyes. ::. .:: A voice rolled out of the darkness, smooth and mellifluous. ::. “Liana, do not tell me you are so easily intimidated.” .:: Something squeezed every muscle in her body. That voice… it was calm, tinged with a vague Arabic accent, but elegant and measured. There was only one person it could have been. She looked up into shockingly blue eyes, the same set of eyes she’d stared into the night she had been Embraced. ::. “Amir?!” .:: He nodded slightly, barely inclining his elegantly held head. He went on. ::. “I would have thought you’d handle yourself better. LaBatte’s been lucky so far. The Rafiq that he’s taken down have been stupid, cocky and stupid. And in some cases, unlucky. You are not any of those.” .:: She tried to grasp at what he was saying, but it was all sliding past. She could only say one thing, choking it out in a dry voice. ::. “How are you here?” .:: Amir began to walk down the street, and Shadow collected herself, pushed herself up from the ground and began to follow him down the street. He couldn’t walk away from her so easily. He sighed, not looking at her, tucking his arms elegantly behind his back as he walked. ::. “My dear, let us not question why I am here, only be thankful that I have been able to find you. It’s been a long time in coming. Please don’t ask me again.” .:: She nodded. Amir smiled at her. ::. “I want to talk about your project, pet. It is more interesting to me than talking about the past.” .:: She swallowed her fear, her shaking fear, and fell into step beside him. ::. “It is a contract for a Julian Falcon. For some reason, he is obsessed with the Giovanni in this area. He had me kill their financier, and killing this little Kine is the other part of the deal. I don’t know why he is so interested.” .:: Amir’s brow furrowed. ::. “You don’t know? Why have you not researched even what clan this Falcon is from?” “Munafiqun without a doubt.” .:: She paused. ::. “But it does not matter what clan he is. This is my last hit for him, because he owes me for the first one. He did not pay in full.” .:: Amir turned his head and looked at her sharply. ::. “And you suffered him to live?” .:: She smirked wryly. ::. “I want my payment, not a permanent job. Don’t think I’ve forgotten everything. Falcon knows what his chances are. If he refuses to pay what he owes me this time, then he loses what he holds so dear, and I become one step closer to Haqim.” .:: Amir laughed. ::. “Still following the old ways, I see. Good, good. I was worried that something had changed.” .:: He paused. ::. “In fact, I wasn’t sure how I’d find you. The word from Alamut was that since that hit on Forlani, you had fallen out of your practice. All I had to go on was the basic area you were in… but when I heard that Alessandro Pazzi had been murdered, and in what manner… I knew I was on the right track.” “So how did you know to look for me at LaBatte’s place?” .:: Amir shrugged, ever-so-casually. Sometimes, she wondered how he could act so nonchalant about things, especially regarding knowing exactly where she was, whenever he wanted to know. ::. ”It was the logical conclusion. I did a bit of research into the area Giovanni myself. If you killed Pazzi, and you were staying in the area to do more work, I just guessed that LaBatte would be next.” .:: His elegant, deep-toned brow furrowed into a perturbed expression. ::. “Of course, if I could have found you before you went to his mansion I would have warned you about his extracurricular exploits against the Rafiq… but I didn’t know where to find you. You’ve been hiding well. I see you don’t stay with Corvus anymore.” .:: She made a face. ::. “No. Mercifully, no. It became too much for either of us to bear one another’s company.” .:: Amir didn’t look surprised at all. ::. ”I find no shock in that revelation. You were begging to be freed from him when I met you in Paris. And now you are free, Liana. Don’t put yourself into his clutches again.” “Oh, I won’t. Believe me. Especially now that you’re back.” .:: Her eyes shone with excitement. The longer they had walked on, talking just like old times, the more she felt like she was finally whole, finally at home. Amir had never died, he was right here beside her! But the expression on his face was slightly saddened. ::. “I’m afraid I can’t stay. I only wanted to make sure that you were well. I have to leave.” “But… why?!” .:: After all this time wanting him to come to her out of nowhere, he was just going to leave. Shadow could feel herself being rent apart from the inside. ::. “I have responsibilities at Alamut, Liana. I’ve reconciled with the clan.” .:: That didn’t come as a shock to her. She knew that he would some day. Yet, now he was going to walk off and leave her, after all this time she’d thought he was dead. ::. “Why are you leaving me again, my Soroush?” .:: She reached out for his arm, to claw her hand into it, to entreat him not to leave her. Soroush meant happiness in Persian—she meant it. Amir had made her so happy when he’d been around. But Amir pulled his hand away. ::. “This is already difficult. I love you, and you know that I do, but the two of us are not good for one another. You have all that you need to make you an accomplished Assassin, and I have what I need at Alamut. We will meet again, I am sure of it, but until then, we must remain strangers.” .:: The moment he’d started talking, Shadow had known defeat. She’d dropped her head, and was staring fixedly at the concrete. When she looked up, he was gone. Something inside her had changed, intrinsically so. She stumbled, red flashed across her vision, and she slipped in her step, crashing into the wall. ::. “Hey, why don’t you watch where the hell you’re going?” .:: Shadow turned to glower at the little Kine who had just gotten so offended at her, but it wasn’t worth her time. She hardly had any time to get offended about it, however, because seconds later, a big nasty brute Kindred was in her face. ::. “Wot ‘re ya doin’ in a place like ‘is? We don’t appreciate ‘avin’ our territry staked out by no Assassins.” .:: Shadow narrowed her eyes. A filthy Brujah. Brujah Antittribu, if she had to guess from the number of holes pierced in his face. ::. “I didn’t realize it had your fucking name on it. But I suggest you leave your thoughts of laying a hand on me for it elsewhere, or you will find yourself one DEAD freak.” .:: She shoved past him. How, she didn’t know. It must have been the adrenaline coursing through her veins, allowing her to ignore his strength so. She had to get out of here. ::. .:: The realization slowly dawned on her. Amir hadn’t been here. She was hardly five feet from where she had emptied the contents of her stomach after escaping Geffroi LaBatte’s hellhole. She’d been standing here the whole time. ::. What the hell is wrong with me?... .:: Just that whole thing at LaBatte’s, she told herself. That’s all it was and nothing more. Julian Falcon would hear about this. What the hell did he think he was doing putting her in this kind of danger? She stalked off in the direction of Club Tenebrae. She knew the slimeball would be there. ::. .:: ::: ::. “I don’t know what you’re talking about!” .:: Even Falcon looked scared backed up against the wall. He hadn’t been prepared to confront the fierce Assamite temper. ::. “LIAR! You filthy Munafiqun! Dirty, traitorous liar! What are you trying to send me into? I could have been killed on the spot!” .:: She slid her dagger out of its place on the side of her leg, holding it to Falcon’s throat. ::. “But maybe that’s your role to play tonight, no?” .:: He had no clue if the little Assassin would follow through on her rage or not, but he was too scared to test it. He began to tremble. Julian Falcon was not ready to relinquish his unlife. He had found power here, power that he’d never known, and he didn’t want to see it go so soon. Tears welled up in his eyes. He was that scared. ::. “I knew nothing about it, I swear to you.” .:: Shadow removed the point of the dagger from his throat, snatched up one of his hands, and dragged the dagger’s point across his palm. ::. “Swear it with your blood.” .:: Falcon gasped, terrified of her. ::. “I swear!” .:: She dropped his hand, letting it fall back to his side, and began to laugh. With her tongue, she cleaned his blood off the tip of her dagger, and Falcon shuddered. He’d heard stories of Assasmites doing that—licking their blades before they went into battle. They had blood that was acidic enough to burn through skin. Falcon was still afraid. Shadow could smell it on him. But she turned from the alleyway outside Club Tenebrae. She was done thinking about Falcon, and ready to leave now. Once more, she turned back to him. ::. “If you are lying to me, I hope that you are prepared to cover your tracks well. If you are lying to me, I will know.” .:: Julian Falcon had never believed anything so wholeheartedly in his entire life. The little Assassin disappeared. ::. .:: ::: ::. .:: That had been hours ago. Or at least, she thought it had been hours ago. She didn’t know, she really didn’t know. Hours? Minutes? She looked at the clock. Hours. That satisfied that. ::. .:: Her entire body was still trembling, even hours later. A mass of nerves, tightening at the back of her neck. Her fingertips, they felt like they were on fire, and she could hardly keep them still, curling and uncurling her fingers just for the sake of motion, just for the sake of feeling like she was still in the real world. Her head was pounding, her stomach twisting in knots. She couldn’t sit down, but standing up made her back hurt. She was pacing. ::. .:: Riven had long since gone to bed. She couldn’t remember any time when he’d ever been asleep and she hadn’t, usually they kept such similar schedules. She hoped he wouldn’t be angry at her for keeping such contrary hours tonight. ::. Lia… hush. You’re going to drive yourself mad. Go lay down. .:: The rational side of her brain joined the salvo quickly. She walked mechanically into her room, shoving the door shut behind her. No harm in trying to sleep, she needed some sleep anyway, in order to get through what was going on in her life right now. She had to have some rest. ::. .:: She lay on her bed, her long black hair spilling over the pillow. The coolness of the bedclothes felt good to her almost fever-flushed skin, but she still had many things, so many things, racing through her mind. She took a deep breath. Sometimes, it was calming to do so. ::. .:: The images slowed slightly—no more racing, more just crawling now. She went over everything she had seen this evening in LaBatte’s mansion. Safe, documents, money, Den Quarters… The thought of the Quarters still made her shudder. It was disgusting, the way he had them strung up in there like trophies, that place, it was even called the “Trophy Room.” She tried to push past her indignation. Something was gnawing at her. ::. He saw… That was LaBatte coming in when I scaled the wall. .:: Her eyes flew open in the darkness. She hadn’t considered that. She’d worked as hard as she could to keep herself concealed, but she knew that LaBatte wasn’t that stupid. And even with Celerity, he would have seen her. ::. .:: Cold fear. What if he sent the Den after her? There was no sense worrying about that, though, no sense. If she worried about that, she’d never get this job taken care of, and that’s what she wanted, to get this job out of the way before it became something she couldn’t handle. She’d have to be more careful. More careful, more watchful, more meticulous planning. She’d almost have to go back to the beginning and start over. ::. .:: There. If that didn’t calm her down, she didn’t know what would. Nothing would work better than laying here and planning her next step in the LaBatte hit. It would be trickier than she thought, but it would give her something to think about, something that wouldn’t make her crazy like thinking about the contents of the Quarters, or thinking about her vision of Amir on the streets while she was on her way to let Falcon have it. She closed her eyes. ::. .:: Almost as soon as they closed, her vision split. No more a calm picture of the day’s happenings. Now it was though she was spiraling out of control, unable to take charge of her own direction. She felt like she was falling, falling from something very high. Oh, she knew she wouldn’t die when she hit, but it would shatter almost every bone in her body! She’d be left to rot in the sunlight, and--::. SMACK! .:: When she opened her eyes, though, it was not death that she saw, and it was not pain that she felt. It was something stunning. ::. .:: There was a castle stretching up to the night sky. Below that, a sharp precipice plunged down into a black trickling river. It was cold here, colder than any place she’d ever been, but yet she was used to this place, it was familiar to her. ::. .:: She was knuckle deep in the earth at the edge of the precipice, feeling some old and ancient power surge through her. These hands were her own, but were different somehow. They looked stronger… or perhaps deformed? She couldn’t tell. Nighttime snow fell on her eyelashes, melting and clouding her vision with precipitation. But that was acceptable. It made her smile. This place loved her, it was like a mother, holding her in cradling arms. ::. .:: A commotion was occurring near the front of the castle grounds. It did not alarm her much, since there were always comings and goings here, but this sounded a bit more fervent than the joyous sendoffs and returns often did. She tried to pay no attention. Tonight, she was trying to concentrate, he always said she should concentrate when she was at her lessons. ::. .:: The details of the night were mercifully obscured by the high snowclouds above the castle. If they hadn’t been, she would have been too struck by the wondrous beauty of it all, stuck in the details. A few footfalls crunched across the snow behind her. ::. “At your lessons, Childe?” .:: She dug her fingers deep into the cold earth, still feeling that powerful link pulling at her. ::. “Yes, at my lessons. I don’t want to get behind.” .:: She stood up. ::. “Why is there yelling in the courtyard?” .:: As she turned around, her Sire’s face looked troubled. Lines wrought his perfectly balanced features in twain. He put his hands on her black-clad shoulders, holding her at arms-length. When he looked at her like that, it meant that he wanted her to pay attention. ::. “Childe, I need you to listen carefully. You have to leave here for a little while.” “But why?” .:: No! This wasn’t what she wanted, she loved it here! She was studying here! Her Sire looked perturbed. ::. “Do not interrupt me, you know how it bothers me. I do not wish you to go either, but if you do not go, you may be in very grave danger.” “What about you?” .:: He dropped his hands from her shoulders. She was always thinking about him, and he appreciated it. But this time, he couldn’t give in. He knew that if she knew the truth, she’d want to stay, and she couldn’t stay. She had to carry on the line. ::. “I’ll be fine. I have to stay here. This is our haven, and I will defend it.” .:: He turned and began to walk away, hating that he was having to leave her in the dark about so much that was going on, but he had no choice. Her shoulders slumped forward as she was left standing stunned in the snow. He turned back to her. ::. “The wagon is waiting for you. Some of the others are already there.” “Who?” “Miloslav… Pavlov… Vasek… Krasna… Lojza… others.” .:: He’d just listed off some of his most promising students. Whatever he was sending them all away for, it was something serious. She wanted to stay. She didn’t want anything to happen to him. He looked back at her again. ::. “Lia, go! Your books are already in the hold of the wagon, you have everything you need to continue studying. If you miss the wagon, I shall make you go to Zdice with Ludmila.” .:: That got her moving. She didn’t want to go back home. She lifted up the trailing hem of her dress slightly, so that it would not trail and become wet in the snow. Quickly, she made her way into the courtyard, not wanting to make him any angrier. ::. .:: Lojza helped her into the wagon, and she sat down, stunned and silent. Around her were people she recognized from some of the other families, but none from hers. She was starting to be scared. The wagon rolled off, thudding across the cobblestoned path and into the streets of the city. ::. .:: All of them held some knowledge that they were going away. They didn’t know for how long, but most of them didn’t want to go. None of them had been as close to Master as she, but then she was the only one who had been his Childe, and not just his student. ::. .:: The wagon rumbled on. As she looked back towards the castle, she saw a glow rising on the horizon. People with torches. No! She could hear them from this far away, hear the rending of the gate as it was smashed apart by the battering ram. They were proceeding towards the door now, the dreadful tramp of boots echoing in her ears. She clapped her hands over her ears, trying to shut out the sound, but no one could shut out that sound. No one could stop what was happening to Master inside that castle, not even her. ::. .:: She screamed. ::. .:: And when she opened her eyes, all the lights in her tiny chamber were blazing. Riven stood over the bed, his mask hastily arranged over his face, situated slightly crooked. Without his coat, he looked meager, a lithe, skinny thing. ::. “What’s wrong, Shadow?” .:: She blinked, sitting up in her bed. Had she been asleep? No, she hadn’t been asleep, it was more like she had been living in the past. But it wasn’t her past. She couldn’t explain that to him. ::. “I’m fine, Riven, just a nightmare.” .:: He nodded. Kindred were often given to nightmares. Not that he didn’t understand, since he felt it was something perfectly natural, given their state of being. He looked down at her, brown eyes concerned, brown eyes that reminded her of that deep soil, buried under snow. ::. “You’re probably just stressed out about your matches.” .:: The soil. She could still feel it, cold in her hands, dug underneath her fingernails, and smearing brown across her ivory complexion. She shuddered. It was such cold soil. For a moment, she hardly realized that Riven was speaking to her, but then she looked back up at him. ::. “You’re probably right.” .:: Her tone of voice wasn’t hers. It came out easily enough, sounded convinced enough of what he was telling her, but it wasn’t hers. She couldn’t be convinced that it was really her sounding so put together when she was such a mess right now. He bowed slightly. ::. “I won’t keep you from your sleep. You have important work… Grest, Oni, this Falcon fellow’s job. Just… don’t work yourself to the bone, hm Shadow? I hate to see you pushing yourself to do things that not even Absimilliard could take on and accomplish by itself.” .:: That was all that he had to say to her, and he slipped out of the room. She appreciated him more each day, quiet, forgiving Riven who never asked questions, never pushed her, just worried about her more often than not, even though he tried not to show it. ::. .:: He left the room, and pulled the door shut behind him, but it was not enough to quiet her fears, even though she had pretended that it was. She wasn’t even sure if it was fear that was dancing around in her head right now so much as it was confusion. ::. .:: Perhaps she’d been confident, but tonight had changed all that. It seemed like things were pressing down on her all at once. She had been fine. Add the stress of a difficult assignment, in addition to two difficult matches, and it seemed that her mind was willing to crack with little to no warning at all. That was the last thing she needed right now, facing a title defense with Fred Grest, a difficult kill with Geffroi LaBatte, and perhaps her hardest GTT4 match yet, against Tsunami Oni. But it was something that she couldn’t control, and even if she’d wanted to do so, she wouldn’t have known how. ::. .:: She needed sleep, desperately. The daylight hours were beginning to wane now, and if she’d had no sleep by the time she faced off against Oni, she’d be a basketcase. But this was still digging on her. It wasn’t even LaBatte, or the apparent hallucination of Amir… it was this last… dream? Was that even what she should call it? It seemed too familiar to be a dream. But, she thought to herself, who is a half-neurotic Kindred to sit around and lay bets on what’s real and what’s not? ::. .:: Her brow furrowed in uneasiness as she pulled the bedclothes up to her chin again. Sleep was far off, she knew. She couldn’t remember how long it had been since she’d just slept, without either torturous dreams, or just a bleak, blank drowning feeling of nothingness. ::. .:: ::: ::.
