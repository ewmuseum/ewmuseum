"Internal Monologue" - Ariana Al-Doleh (IG 17)

24 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I’ve found that being a celebrity takes some getting used to. It seems like just yesterday that I made my wrestling debut, in the Infinite Gauntlet open-invitational. They told me that before the match aired, they would do a brief piece on me, explaining how I came from Iran, and why I wore the veil. I didn’t see it until later that night, when I saw the show air, but I must admit that they did a very good job of portraying me. I was afraid that I would be shown in a different light. Since September 11th, it is often not easy to be one of my faith, whether one lives here or in the Middle East. As for the match itself, I could have done better, I think. I could also have done worse. While I was not victorious, the feel of being in the ring was unlike anything I have ever experienced. Everything moves faster than I could have possible imagined, yet I am aware of everything. The screaming of the fans, my opponents, all of it. It is as if my senses become hyper-aware. What I was not aware of, though, was the fact that I would be noticed. When I woke up a few days later in the home that I share with my mother and father in Cleveland, Ohio, I was stunned to realize that I had received fanmail. Apparently, it had been sent to Primetime Central, and they had forwarded it to me. I never expected to be a heroine, of any sort. And yet, the letters I read cite me as inspirational. Letters from fellow Muslims. From fellow women. In a sport where neither group is well-represented, I am proud to represent both. It is breakfast now. I sit with my mother and father at the table, eating a piece of buttered toast as I pore over the newest batch of letters. I am unveiled here, as I am when I go to the accounting office to work. Perhaps someday, I can make a living on wrestling. Until then, though, I will work as hard as I can to make that a reality. Mixed among the letters today is one from Primetime Central. The PTC insignia is one I can recognize anywhere now, such is the role it has played in my life. I open the letter quickly, eagerly. Inside is a notification of the time and place of my next contest. My next opportunity. And upon reading it, it is all I can do to not cry out. I have the opportunity to be the Infinite Gauntlet Champion. This time, there will only be two other competitors in the ring with me, my fellow challenger Jason Spire, and the champion, Kyle Lamen of PRIME. My mother sees my excitement, and asks to read the letter. When she is finished, she passes it to my father. They both smile, happy for me. They have been nothing but supportive of my career, a fact which surprised me at first. After all, wrestling isn’t the safest career in the world. Nor is it the most “proper”. Many parents would tell their daughter ‘no’ if she ever asked. After all, many people see wrestling as demeaning to women. Perhaps it is, and perhaps it isn’t. It varies greatly depending on what you watch, and where you come from. I’ve come to realize, though, that in spite of what they were, the women of Primetime Central are all inspirational, in their own ways. Global Championship Wrestling’s top two champions are females, Dawn McFarlane and Vivica J. Valentine. While I do not approve of their methods, the fact of the matter is that they compete with their hearts and souls. PRIME has Karina Wolfenden, the enigmatic and temperamental star. She is known just as much for her random outbursts as for her prodigious talents. I respect them all. And yet, I want to be different from them. I want to be the woman all can cheer. The girl who comes to the ring to compete, with no ulterior motives. In a sport of grudges and vendettas and blood, I want to be the person the people can count on to always give it her all and compete fairly. Is it a naïve notion? Perhaps. But in a world that all too often crushes the naivete of the innocent, such notions are valuable. Innocence is a precious thing. Too often we use the word “naïve” as an insult. To say someone doesn’t know how the world works. It is the word they used when I asked why I had to wear the veil, a few years ago, in my homeland. A misuse of the term, really. I wasn’t naïve at all. I knew THEIR reasons for making me wear it, but what I wanted was a real reason. Something more substantive than “if you don’t wear your veil, men will be attracted to you and chaos will erupt.” To Westerners, the notion sounds ludicrous. Probably because to them, it is. On that note, I look over at the microwave clock. Time for me to get going. I rise from the table, and bid my parents goodbye. Smoothing my skirt over my legs, I head for the front door, and step out onto the street. I can’t drive yet. I was never able to back in Iran. It wasn’t so much that it was illegal for women to drive as it was unaccepted. Those women who did often had to put up with abuse from male motorists, and really, we put up with enough abuse as it was. I’ve begun to learn, though, on my mother’s Honda. It’s difficult, at first, but I can see how very useful it will be, so I will persist. For now, though, I don’t mind the thirty minute walk to the office. It gives me time to think, and time to people-watch. Never before have I seen so…much of people. Here, most do not veil. There are some who do, but they are the exception, rather than the rule. I see all sorts of attire. I see people’s faces. Across the street, on the other sidewalk, I see a young couple holding hands. I cannot help but be reminded of what I was told when I lived thousands of miles away. Is THIS the fitna that the extremists are forever worried about? People holding hands? Are they really so afraid of the exposing of my hair? Do they honestly believe that women walking around with their hair flowing behind them will cause the downfall of Islamic civilization? It’s silly to even think about. And what does it say about men? I find it hard to believe that all men are such lecherous creatures that the sight of a woman’s hair sends them into erotic convulsions. As a matter of fact, walking down the street here proves it. Male and female passersby nod to me as I walk by. I nod back, politely. It’s fun to walk the streets sometimes, knowing that even if they know who Ariana Al-Doleh is, they don’t know that I am her. The veil is part of my costume now, in spite of its more practical purpose of hiding my identity from those who would do me harm. It’s what I’m known for. Like the mask a luchador wears. There is so much to think about, though, beyond my religion, and the perversions of it that others hold as truth. This contest on the horizon is, obviously, the most important in my career. How do I go to battle against two gladiators, both of whom are more experienced than I am? The attention that I have been given is flattering, but I feel, somehow, as if it is unwarranted. After all, I didn’t win that Open Invitational. I suppose I feel somewhat like the auto racer Danica Patrick does. I have seen her interviews on television, and while she is gracious and thankful for the attention, inside, a fire still burns. Her accomplishments aren’t enough. She wants to win a race, not come in third or fourth. I could say the same. Third in the open invitational was enough to get my name out there. But it is results that matter. Third in this match is last. Perspectives change with every contest, and in this contest, victory brings me a reward that, mere weeks ago, I couldn’t have imagined. I know what this chance means. How many competitors receive a shot at a championship in their second match? And so, I have studied both men. For Jason Spire, this contest is a chance at redemption. He sought to become the Infinite Gauntlet Champion once before, but his effort was unsuccessful. Still, he has fought his way back to this point, to the precipice of success. Dangerous, he will be. Desire is a strong motivator. Being so close already will undoubtedly fuel him to new heights. For the champion… I must admit that the prospect of doing battle with Kyle Lamen troubles me. Apparently, he suffers greatly from an ailment that I know nothing about. Yet, he continues to fight. This championship that he holds must be a source of great pride for him. When watching PRIME to see Karina Wolfenden, I have seen him as well, fighting against impossible odds. Though opposed by the entire A-List, he does not back down an inch. No matter how much others may try to destroy him with nefarious tactics, he remains true to himself. That is an admirable quality. One that I hope to live up to. As I walk down the sidewalk, I imagine what it would be like to wrestle there, someday. To walk to the ring amidst the pyrotechnics and flash that PRIME is known for, to take on the best wrestlers in the world. Or to compete in GCW, and ply my wares against Shadow or Dawn. Or AWC. Or the UWF. Or any of the promotions in Primetime Central. The way to the contract that I so desire is in this contest. If I can win the Infinite Gauntlet Championship, I can accomplish my dream. If I fail… I will think positively, and avoid imagining such scenarios. Another step, and my internal clock goes off. It is time for prayer. I stop where I am, moving to the side of the sidewalk to allow other passersby to move on. Turning to face the holy city, I drop to my knees, reciting words in Arabic that are second nature at this point in life. The formal prayer can take a long time, or it can take a few minutes, depending where you live and what you believe. My personal prayer tends to be on the shorter side, at least as far as the Arabic is concerned. Afterwards, though, I make sure to insert several other statements. I pray for the continued safety of my mother and father. I pray that one day, the extremists of my religion will stop insisting that the West is a land of infidels. I do not pray for victory. I do not wish to demean the act of prayer to my God with such a wish. However, when I think of the plight of one of the men that I will be facing, I decide that a prayer for Kyle Lamen is appropriate. I pray for his health. For his safety. Some might ask me why, considering I will be facing him in a scant few days. To them, I would respond that there are some things more important than wrestling. After all, when you come from a land where your freedom is restricted, you come to realize that life, and the ability to live it, is the greatest gift of all.
