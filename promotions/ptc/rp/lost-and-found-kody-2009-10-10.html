Lost and Found: Kody

10 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2009-10-10 There are a lot of cliches that could be used to describe your relationship, or any relationship really. Your time with Katie has been tumultuous, susceptible to moments of exultation and frustration. It would be easy to describe it as a rollercoaster, or a pogo stick, or any other rudimentary image to portray an idea of an even mixture of positive and negative experiences. You prefer to think of it as Lost and Found. While you struggled to get to know one another early on in the courtship, you eventually found a common ground and stuck with it. By the time the two of you realized you had little in common, you were already a fixture in the FUSE main event scene, and there was not much either of you could do to change things. Even when you realized on a Monday that you had lost touch with the woman who was supposed to be your everything, on Tuesday you had to pose for a magazine spread that covered "FUSE’s First Couple." The phrase "America’s Sweethearts" was used no less than three times in the article, and it made you tentative and a little bit nauseous. You lost a little of your feelings for Katie when you had the affair as well. People always say that the human capacity for love is infinite, and finding another person does not necessarily take feeling away from your first love. You found out as you went to Sarah Riley for guidance and, later, for more than only guidance, that this was inaccurate in your case. Even when you weathered the storm, even when you professed your love for Katie and found her again, you had lost something in the process, and it never returned. And now, when the shoe is on the other foot and Katie appears completely unapologetic in the face of her own adultery, you realize that your losses are getting more prevalent, and the findings fewer and miles between. And as you prepare to enter your apartment for the first time in a week, you convince yourself to try harder, to search for what you lost. Because at the end of the day, love is worth finding, you tell yourself. The first thing Jonathan Rhine hears when he opens his apartment door is frantic yipping, and this immediately puts him on edge. "We don’t have a dog," he says to nobody in particular. As he cautiously walks through the foyer, the noises get louder. Dropping his gym bag and raising his fists in preparation of altercation, Rhine peeks around the wall and into the kitchen. "Hey babe!" Katie Malick says cheerfully, raising one hand to wave and then immediately putting her hand back in the original position, which is holding back a ferocious, tiny white ball of fluff. Jonathan looks from his girlfriend to the dog she is holding, raises his eyebrows, then looks back to Katie. "Um, what is this?" "This is our dog!" she shouts happily. "We don’t have a dog." "Then explain what I’m holding back from attacking you right now." Jon laughs. "Little, white, and makes more noise than any damage it could ever do? I’d guess Aimz." Katie scowls. "Don’t bring up the job after being home for all of two minutes. I don’t want to hear about…" After a second, Katie stops, then shakes her head. Forcing a smile, she says, "Just for that…" Katie releases her hold on the puppy, which launches itself at The New Life’s feet. "Jesus!" SCCW’s first Universal Champion shouts as he lifts one of his legs, jumping backwards. As the puppy is very young and very small, Jon’s prediction rings true. Yipping and yapping, the dog causes no harm, and he finally bends down and picks up the squirming pup. "So, we have a dog?" Katie smiles. "We have a dog." "We have a dog," Jon repeats, sighing. "What kind of dog is he…she…it?" "He’s an American Eskimo, and he’s a little over two months. His name is Kody, although if you want to change the name you can, since he’s yours." Jon nods, rubbing the dog’s belly, which causes him to emit a sound like a purr. "Kody’s fine. Why, though?" Katie shrugs. "Because I was thinking of names and Matchbox 20’s song ‘Kody’ came on the iPod. Seemed like a good enough name." "No, why did you buy me a dog?" "I don’t know," she says, standing up and walking over to him. "A few reasons. I know we’ve been sort of…we’ve had some problems lately. And I feel bad about all of it. I hate fighting, and I thought you could use something to cheer you up a little." The dog barks, and Jon laughs. "It might help. Thank you, babe." Playing with the dog’s fur, an uncharacteristically ecstatic smile emerges from the normally darkened face of Jonathan Rhine. "He’s adorable…hey, can you take a picture of us together?" Pleased to see her boyfriend so happy, Katie claps her hands together. "Of course! I think the camera is still connected to the laptop upstairs…" "Don’t worry about it, just use my phone," Jon says, removing it from his pocket. "That works, because then I can text it to Jessie. I already told her that I was planning on getting it for you, so she’ll love to see it." Katie raises the phone at eye level, preparing to capture Jon and Kody’s first moment together. The smile that Jon wears for the camera isn’t the uncomfortable one that he offers when fans pose with him, or the one that he gives when dealing with his boss. This smile is completely natural, real, and true. It is happiness that Jonathan Rhine has found hard to find in recent months. The flash causes the dog to whimper, and Jon smiles as he scratches it behind the ears. "Awww, how cute! Let me text it and then we can take him for a walk!" Katie begins ferociously tapping at the phone with a smile that is as natural as Jon’s. After she clicks the send button, she furrows her eyebrows suddenly, dropping the smile. Jon notices and his posture stiffens. "What’s wrong, did she reply already?" Katie’s response is slow and calm, yet the lack of emotion unsettles Jon almost as much as the words do. "No. It brought me to your inbox after I sent it. Jon?" she asks, the emotion slowly creeping back into her voice. "Why is Sarah Riley still texting you?" Jon, for his part, doesn’t show shock or guilt. The only indication is a slight slumping in his shoulders as he starts, "I…" "‘Hey, Jonny, you should call me up before your first GTT match. I can help you prepare mentally. Wink.’ She winked at you?" "Katie, I can…" "No you can’t, Jon," Katie says, her voice raising in both volume and pitch as she clutches his phone tightly against her chest. "You cannot explain how after months of fighting over my infidelity, you have texts from the woman you cheated on me with. You cannot explain how it was a good idea to talk to a woman who took away every confidence I ever had about you and this relationship, confidence that took over a year to restore. You cannot explain how this makes you look like anything other than a hypocritical, disgusting, cocksucking jerk. You cannot explain a goddamn thing." The New Life’s posture confirms this: sighing, he bows his head and looks down, absentmindedly still petting the dog. Kody has no grasp of the gravity of the situation and merely pants. Tossing the phone on the couch, Katie is once again devoid of the rage she just showed. "Walk him by yourself, and maybe find another place tonight. Looks like you have an offer on the table already." Jon makes no attempt to stop her as she walks into the bedroom and closes it gently. Jon waits a moment before putting Kody down and picking up the phone. Looking at the phone, he mutters, "Wink. Why do girls do that?" Then he looks down at Kody, who is staring at him expectantly. Jon’s enthusiasm for the dog seems to have dissipated, as he sighs. "What? You want to walk? Okay, let’s go." The talks with Sarah Riley have been innocent, but you wouldn’t dare tell Katie that. Because at the end of the day, they weren’t really innocent at all. A few weeks ago, you met up with your friend Jack, and he spent most of the time talking about one of his ex-girlfriends who he was maintaining a platonic relationship with. You warned him that it was a bad idea, because once you’ve had sex with a person and made that type of connection, it is nearly impossible to enter a friendship with them without being tempted to slip into that comfortable position you had before. And it with this same knowledge that you called Sarah, asking her for advice on your current situation with Katie. It is also with that same knowledge that you herd Kody in your car and drive towards her house now. As long as you still talk to Sarah Riley, you are reminded of what you had, and what you could have again. You know that you shouldn’t tempt this by seeing her, but you also know that you can’t go back with Katie tonight. You just hope you’ll be able to prove yourself wrong, and this will merely be therapy of the emotional kind. "Cute dog, but I don’t really allow pets in my house." Sarah Riley stands in the doorway, giving Kody a nervous glance. "I’m sorry, but I…um, he doesn’t have anywhere else to go." Sarah Riley stares at her former lover, her face softening at the view of the creases on his face. "More domestic problems? Okay then. Come on in, we can just give him the kitchen while we talk in my room." She grabs his wrist, but Jon twists free of it. "This isn’t that kind of meeting, Sarah. I just need to get my mind right." "Fair enough, I’ll make a pot of coffee. What’s the problem this time?" Jon walks in slowly, shuffling his feet. His eyes still on the floor, he sighs as he answers, "You are." Sarah turns suddenly. "Again? Over old issues?" "She saw your wink text." "That’s all?" Sarah asks, giggling unprofessionally. "She got upset over an emoticon in a text message? Sorry, Jon, I didn’t realize we were in middle school." Bending down, Jon pets Kody. "I understand it’s about you, but I’d appreciate if you could take off the ‘other woman’ hat and put on the ‘psychologist’ one. It’d help me think this through." "I’m afraid that’s impossible, Jon." Sarah pours the beans in the top of the coffee maker and pushes the button, then walks towards him slowly. "I can’t be impartial about your relationship because most of the tension revolves around me, and also because your girlfriend seems to have the emotional intelligence of a jellyfish." "Wow, that was a bitchy thing to say." Jon picks Kody up and pets him as he stares at his therapist sadly. "Maybe coming here was a mistake." Standing directly in front of him, Sarah looks at the dog nervously again. "I’m sorry, I’m just stressed out right now." "Is it because of Kody? You don’t have anything to worry about. I just met him today, but he’s a sweetheart. Give him a pet, he’s really great." Sarah stares at Kody for a long moment before timidly reaching out to pet him. After a few seconds, she nods. "He is really cute. Where did you get him?" This elicits another heavy sigh from The New Life. "Katie gave him to me." She nods, rubbing her nose slightly. "I see. Did she include a ‘Sorry I fucked another guy after hypocritically crucifying you for your affair’ card, or was that implied?" "Come on, Sarah! Insults are the last thing I need right now." "And you think I need this right now?" Sarah shouts, sniffling. "You think I want to see the only guy who I wanted to be with coming in here looking for advice? Not advice to be a better person, or heal his own problems, but to resurrect a relationship that is clearly not working out? You think I like to see the person that I loved ignore the obvious? Do you really think I want to contribute anything to you two reuniting?" Sniffling again, she moves to her couch and opens a drawer in the coffee table. Jon stares at Sarah for a second before, shaking his head. "Dammit. Sarah, I’m sorry." Clutching her fist, she turns away from him. "No, you’re right. I am being selfish, not providing the help you’re paying me for. It’s just that I…I have a hard time being objective in your case. It’s easy to impartially listen to my other patients talk about their issues, but I can’t hold back my emotions when it comes to you. I’m sorry…maybe you need to see another therapist." Jon watches her sniffle for a moment before grabbing a tissue and sitting down next to her. "Here." "Thank you." Visibly uncomfortable, Jon pats her shoulder gingerly, then is surprised when she suddenly turns to lean against him. He timidly runs his hands through her hair. "I can’t choose someone else, Sarah. You understand me better than any other therapist could." She nods, sniffles, then looks up at him. "You’re sweet." She sits up a little so that their faces are level, and she leans in. It could be the fight he just had, or the weakness that Sarah is showing, but in any case Jon is tired of fighting it. He sighs, gives in, and moves to kiss her, grabbing her hand… Which releases a small package that crashes to ground. "What is that?" he says, looking down at the package as she hurries to grab it. But as their professions might predict, Jon reaches the object much faster. As he grabs the blue box he looks at her, confused. "Claritin? Why were you holding…?" Then Jon looks from Sarah to Kody, who is laying down in the corner of the room. "You’re allergic." Sniffling, Sarah sighs. "That’s why I don’t have dogs in here." Jon shakes his head in disbelief. "So this…" he gestures to her. "All of this is just a performance?" Wiping her face with the tissue, she shrugs. "Maybe only seventy percent?" "Jesus Christ!" Jon shouts, standing up and slamming his hand on the coffee table. "Jon…" "No, you were right before. Maybe I do need to see someone else. Someone who isn’t going to prey on my good will, who isn’t going to take advantage of my fragile situation. Someone who will give me advice without making fun of my situation out of bitterness and spite." Jon walks to Kody and picks him up, then turns to Sarah. "You better take that Claritin. You look terrible." Without another word, Jonathan Rhine turns on his heel and exits the house, leaving Sarah Riley to sigh and slam her hand on the couch. You were right, but it doesn’t make you feel any better. In fact, finding out that it was better to cut Sarah off forever makes you feel even worse about Katie. You said that you should never involve yourself with someone after you had sex because it’s too easy to find yourself back in that role. And then you remember that after the Sarah Riley incident, you had broken up with Katie, and then later decided to be friends. It naturally escalated, and it’s been easy to think of it as a good thing. After all, you two have moved in together and, until this recent hiccup, have had few problems. But now you realize that maybe there was a reason that you weren’t together. Maybe there’s a reason you cheated. Maybe there’s a reason she cheated on you. Maybe there are some things you lose that you shouldn’t try to find again. So you grab your new companion, you get in your car, and you drive. You don’t know where you’re going, and at this moment you’re not sure you care. Because at this moment, you have no idea what you’re going to do, and you’re oddly comfortable about it. Right now, maybe it’s okay to be lost for a little while.
