Sebastian Moraco: Life After Death Quite often a man would call for Sebastian, wanting to kill him. Sometimes he would receive letters about why he should be dead with some imaginative ways to achieve that goal. Sebastian never knew who these people were, didn't care to find out who, but he understood how they felt. These men would always confuse or exaggerate details about that incident four years ago. They would say he used a pipe, when really there was no weapon present, or that he had arranged the incident, when really the two of them had never met before, or worse that he had no remorse for what he did, when really he was trying to seek redemption and finding none. Sebastian would sit at the kitchen table, doodling on a sticky pad, while he took these calls. He never said anything more than "yeah", "mhm", or agreed with the caller and then hung up. Sebastian was absolved of all criminal charges, infuriating the public. Instead, he was suspended for a year and then put on mandatory probation. He was not allowed to sign a contract with any major wrestling organization, though no one ever wanted him. Sebastian was only allowed to sign with local wrestling organizations or wrestling gyms during his probation. The only facility that considered him was a small wrestling gym out in Los Angeles. Sebastian was released and transferred to a gym in New York City at the end of his probation. Sebastian surveyed the people gathered at the waiting area for arrivals at JFK airport. He was glad they did not recognize him. Perhaps it was the sunglasses shielding his eyes, or perhaps the people had more important arrivals to wait for. Sebastian found a column to lean against. The trainer back in California told him the head trainer of the New York Gym was sent to personally pick him up. Sebastian was told to look for a very tough looking old man. 'If you see Clint Eastwood there, that's probably him'. A little over an hour before Sebastian arrives in New York. Brooklyn When Grady Adams decided to open the wrestling gym two years ago, he did it to cope with the loss of his son and daughter-in-law. They were killed in a drunk driving accident and the crash had left their bodies so mangled that Grady cried for a week after their deaths. He never had any ambition that the school would produce a wrestling superstar or make a profit, but after two years he decided that he could not continue to linger on like this. He could not continue to run the school simply as a means to cope with his children's death. It was time for him to move on, and to not do so was to dishonor his children. At sixty years of age, Grady still looked as tough as a drill sergeant. His skin was the color of tanned leather and his arms were like knotted ropes. He had been a very popular and successful wrestler in his younger days, and that popularity was enough for many aspiring young men to enroll in his school even if the school had yet to have any of its student contracted by a major wrestling organization. The school itself was actually an old public school in Brooklyn, abandoned after budget cuts forced the New York Board of Education to discontinue dozens of schools. Grady bought out the property, knowing that the building would need little work already having a cafeteria, gym, locker rooms, and twenty-five class rooms to serve as offices and storage rooms. In a way, the school resembled Grady himself. They were both still surprisingly sturdy and everyone remembered what they used to be. Despite his rugged no nonsense appearance, Grady was a kind and friendly man. It was perhaps for this reason that many of his students continued to enroll in his class despite seeing little progress. Grady maintained an open door policy with his students. For that reason, Cody did not feel obliged to knock on the door as he entered the office. Grady didn't have to look up from his desk to know who it was, Of all his students, the most persistent about getting a wrestling contract was Cody Michaels. And it was for good reason too. Cody was far and away the best at the school. He was also cocky, arrogant, unreceptive of criticism and refused to accept defeat. With his wheat colored tan, peacock blue eyes, and blonde hair, Cody looked like he belonged at a beach rather than a wrestling gym. What can I do you for this time, Grady said running his pen down the paper on his desk. I want you to sponsor me for the Infinite Gauntlet. Grady looked up. We been through this before son, the Infinite Gauntlet is...well you just aren't ready for it. And when am I going to be ready for it, Cody snapped. He leaned on the desk. I've been here two years, ever since this place opened up, and you never once told me I was ready for anything. Grady stood up from his mahogany chair. He did not appreciate Cody's threatening gesture, looming over his desk. I know you think you're ready, Cody, but the Infinite Gauntlet isn't the place to start. Maybe the next time there is- That is fucking bullshit, Cody interrupted. I'm the best fucking wrestler here. I've paid my dues. You're just a grumpy, washed up old man who's only thrill is keeping the students here grounded because you're too damn jealous to let anyone have any kind of success. Grady did not give Cody the pleasure of a reply. This was the biggest reason that Grady did not think Cody was ready for the world of PTC. Cody was physically talented, but he was an emotional wreck. Cody sulked out of the office, bumping Horatio as he left. What was that about, Horatio asked. He was a really big, fat penguin of a man - white shirt, black pants and blazer. He was the school's investor and most of the decisions ran through him because of the size of his wallet. Kid wants to be sponsored for the Infinite Gauntlet. I see, Horatio replied, feigning intrigue. He had no interest in wrestling. To him, this school was simply another business venture. He helped Grady buy the property cheap and using Grady's fame he could turn the property around and make a huge profit out of it. I have some important news for you. Grady clicked his teeth, standing next to Horatio at the doorway. What? You know that school out in California? Yeah, what of it? They're sending one of their students here, and they're paying us good money to take him in. They're paying us? That doesn't sound like a good thing to me, son. What's this kid about? Horatio laughed off Grady's doubts. Don't you worry about that. This kid just needs a new change of scenery and they think this school would be good for him. I do, too. And when is this kid getting here? That's what I came here to tell you. He's arriving at JFK within the hour, and I want you to meet him there. You know, to really make him feel welcomed. Present. Back at JFK Airport Grady looked around the Arrivals section, making sure he had the right flight information. According to the blue monitors the plane had landed a little over half an hour ago, meaning the new arrival should be coming out with his luggage already. He scanned the men looking for anyone who looked like he belonged in the wrestling ring. All he found were business men, retired couples, kids, lots and lots of kids, and the airport staff. There's gotta be something wrong with this kid, Grady thought to himself. People hardly ever transferred gyms within their own state, let alone cross country, and no one ever offered money either. Grady couldn't think of any circumstances for such an arrangement and decided it was best left alone. A tall man with sunglasses approached Grady. They told me to look for Clint Eastwood. You can see that's not me,son. The man removed his glasses, revealing an unfriendly stare. They didn't say you were smart, guess they left that part out for good reason. Excuse me? My name's Sebastian, he said. I'm sure you know who I am. Grady looked at Sebastian's coin colored eyes. He admitted there was a nagging feeling that he had seen this man somewhere before, but could not recall where. Instead, he said he did not know who he was. Sebastian Moraco, he replied as if the full name would trigger a memory in the old man. You must be the new student, Grady said. He made no effort to hide his disgust with his new wrestling student. Sebastian made no effort to hide that he did not care. So where's the welcoming committee? Where's the escorts,the red carpet, the burgundy. This is it? Why did they send you all the way to New York? That's none of your business, Sebastian said walking past Grady. Grady grabbed him by the shoulder. We were paid a lot of money to accept a student all the way from California. That stuff doesn't happen without there being a stipulation. What happened out there? Sebastian looked at the leathery hand on his shoulder then at Grady. They paid you to take me in? Grady nodded. Sebastian laughed. You get paid to take one of the best wrestlers in the world and you bitch about it? Do yourself a favor and enjoy this. You look like you could use a few more social security checks. I don't have to take this contract you know. Whatever. I know where the gym this. I was just waiting here because I figured you'd bring a girl or something. But now that I think about it, you'd probably bring some old lady in a wheelchair connected to a respirator. Grady didn't pretend to go after Sebastian. He shook his head in disgust. For as annoying, disrespectful, and arrogant as Sebastian was, Grady was not mad with him. He was angry with Horatio. He knew that Horatio knew exactly who the new student was and why he was sent to his school all the way from California. Horatio only cared about the money, no matter where it came from. Grady's Gym. Forty-five minutes later They recognized him as soon as he entered Grady's Wrestling Gym. He had grown his black hair short, recently getting a shape up, and he had gained a twenty pounds. But he still had those same penny-colored eyes and there was no mistaking him. They stopped their own personal routines and watched the newcomer. His copper colored eyes seemed as vacant and as remorseless as they had heard. They recalled what they had heard about this man about that incident four years ago. The witnesses told about how after the incident when a silence swept through the crowd after what they knew had happened, how he walked out of the arena with that empty stare in his eyes. Cody was the first to speak. What the hell are you doing here, you fucking spic. The rest of the wrestlers gathered behind Cody. Normally,they hated how arrogant and prejudice Cody was. Normally, they would be offended by the racist remark. But this time they gave him a pass. They stood behind Cody as if he had been their leader all along. I asked you a fucking question, Cody said. Sebastian. What? The name's Sebastian. I don't care what the fuck your name is. What the fuck are you doing here. Sebastian ignored him and walked past him towards the locker room area. Cody grabbed him by the arm and turned him around. His face was a few inches away from Sebastian's. No one will ever forget what you did. I'm not interested in kissing you, Sebastian replied. That angered Cody who swung at Sebastian. Soon the two of them were grappling on the floor with the gym cheering Cody on. What the hell is going on here, Horatio shouted. Someone break this up! But no one listened to him. No one at the gym had any respect for Horatio. The man had probably bought his way out of every fight in his life. The only time he had ever stood up for himself was probably at an all you can eat buffet line. There were only two people these wrestlers had any respect for. One of them was Grady and the other was Maribel. Break this up, Maribel said. She had long, wavy black hair, small nose, dark brown eyes, and olive skin. She did not tan well, but she was remarkably beautiful. She was Grady's granddaughter. She decided to help her grandfather run the wrestling gym though she had no interest in the business herself. The two of them needed each other after the death of her parents. The others at the gym had broken up the fight and were holding them apart. Cody seemed more eager to rejoin the fight as Sebastian merely stood and hardly needed to be retained at all. What is this about, Maribel asked. That guy has no business being here, Cody shouted. Horatio chimed in. He has every business being here. He's the new transfer student from California. Cody glared at Horatio. Do you have any fucking idea who he is? What he did? Of course Horatio knew. But he wasn't going to admit it, nor was he going to say the money he accepted was good enough reason for him to ignore what Sebastian had done. Instead he said that Grady had accepted this student and that's all that matters. He's right, Maribel said. If my grandfather has taken this guy in, then that's all that matters. Cody looked at Maribel. I'm sorry. Sebastian noticed the look in Cody's eyes as he looked at Maribel. He noticed the look in her eyes as well, the look of ignorance. She doesn't even know, does she? Know what, Maribel asked Sebastian didn't bother to explain that Cody had a crush on her and that she seemed oblivious to the fact. Cody didn't offer an explanation either and he was glad that Sebastian didn't expand on his statement. Grady walked into the gym and the rest of the wrestlers quickly returned to their workout routines, pretending as if they had been following the training schedule all along. Maribel greeted her grandfather with a hug and told him she missed him. Grady's eyes were fixated on Sebastian. Sir, you can't have this guy here, Cody said. I don't like him anymore than you do, Grady admitted. But he's here and you will make the most of it. You have no idea what the hell he did. No, I don't. But he's under my school now and everyone obeys my rules. I know you're mad that I didn't sponsor you for the Infinite Gauntlet, but that doesn't give you a right to make accusations. Sebastian let out a loud laugh as if he had just heard the funniest thing ever. The Infinite Gauntlet? Are you serious? You actually think you will do anything there? Cody used every ounce of strength he had to keep from rushing at Sebastian. He imagined Sebastian's face crumbling into a mangled, bloody mess as he landed punch after punch. But this was not going to be a reality with Grady and Maribel both present. Sebastian noticed the situation and decided to take advantage of it. Let's have a match here, Sebastian suggested. If you win, then the old man here can see you might stand a chance. When I win, then you'll know you aren't ready. Not a chance in hell, Grady quickly replied. Cody was about to beg for the opportunity, but Maribel did it for him. Let them spar, Grandpa. It could be fun. And Cody is the best wrestler we have here, this would be good for him. Alright, Grady said. He did not like it, but he could never say no to his own grandchild. Sebastian and Cody were inside the ring waiting for the signal to start. The wrestlers had gathered around the ring as if it were a lumberjack match. It was obvious who they were rooting for and even Maribel could not hide her bias. She stood with her grandfather behind the crowd. Grady gave the signal to start. Cody quickly went on the offensive using the crowd support as an enzyme for his adrenaline. He performed move after move keeping Sebastian on the defensive. Sebastian looked overwhelmed; he was unable to land a counter of any kind. The crowd cheered on and on sensing that Cody was going to give this new student a lesson he deserved. Who was this arrogant punk to come here and disrespect them and their gym? Even Maribel began to see that it was going to be a one-sided fight. Will you consider sponsoring Cody for the Infinite Gauntlet, Maribel asked. She was smiling with delight. No. Why? He's clearly dominating the new kid. He won't win. Maribel looked confused. The new guy hasn't even landed a single move. How can you say Cody is going to lose? Watch, Grady explained. Cody is fighting with his emotions. He's exerting too much energy. He's unfocused and undisciplined. Cody looks just as winded as Sebastian because he's letting him recuperate too much in this match. Sebastian has this match won. Maribel looked back at the ring. It sure looked like Cody was dominating the match. But her grandfather had been a very talented wrestler during his younger days and she trusted his opinion on things like this, but she could not see it in the ring. Then, as if on queue, Sebastian found an opening and the momentum switched. Whereas Cody was running on pure adrenaline, Sebastian seemed methodical. Maribel noticed that Sebastian's breathing wasn't labored at all, as if Cody's offensive onslaught had done nothing to him. I've seen you before, Grady thought to himself. He knew that he had seen Sebastian somewhere before, had heard of his name mentioned with great caution. He tried to recall the exact circumstances that allowed him to remember who Sebastian was. The answer was provided in the ring. The crowd noise had dwindled down to low whispers as everyone looked at what was going on in the ring. Sebastian was lifting Cody upside down, having both his arms and legs hooked. Then in one swift motion, Sebastian drove Cody headfirst into the mat. Maribel let out a small gasp, covering her eyes, and the wrestlers began to holler very much impressed with the move they had just witness. Everyone was cheering and talking amongst themselves about how fascinating it had been. Everyone that is, except for Grady. His face was pale as if he had just seen a ghost. The move was a package piledriver and Grady finally knew where he had seen Sebastian before. What was that, Maribel asked. She tugged at Grady's sleeve, but Grady seemed oblivious to her. He was staring at Sebastian making his way towards them. I'm sure you won't have a problem giving me your sponsorship for the Infinite Gauntlet, Sebastian said. Sure, Grady said. He could not take his eyes off those barren, cold brown eyes. In the ring, Cody was unconscious. A few wrestlers tried to revive him, but he was out cold. He was also very lucky, it could have been worse. Four Years Ago Once a year, the Extreme Wrestling Federation held an event called The Crucible. The concept was a night filled with open challenges. Any wrestler who was present at the arena was eligible and required to compete and answer to any challenge issued to them regardless of their affiliation. There were always many wrestlers from other organizations attending in the hopes of pitting their skills against wrestlers from other places. There were no rules against the number of challenges you can make, or against how many challenges you had to accept. The only rule was that if you were issued a challenge, you had to accept it. Sebastian used that rule to his advantage. There was always something at stake at this events - pride, glory, honor - but winning was still the biggest prize of all. Sebastian wanted to win his challenge and he sought the best way he knew how: to issue a challenge to an opponent he knew he could easily beat. That opponent was an aspiring young man named Cassidy McKenzie. Cassidy was relatively new to the business, but he was incredibly gifted. He also had a mother who was hospitalized that night for cancer and had just finished a grueling challenge he had won. The crowd jeered Sebastian fiercely and the judges implored him to consider his challenge. Everyone knew what Cassidy was going through and they knew that Sebastian would have no problems with him. But Sebastian refused to back down from his challenge and demanded that Cassidy accept the challenge. People would say that Cassidy was very brave, or that he was foolish, or that he allowed Sebastian to coerce him into it. Cassidy was merely a good kid who believe in the rules. He accepted the challenge because it was expected of him. The match went on as scripted. Cassidy was completely overwhelmed and never had a chance to mount a counter offense. The crowd jeered Sebastian loudly and the referee begged him to end the match quickly. Sebastian obliged, but he wanted to make sure no one will forget the ending. He was going to do something so incredible that people will always remember him, Be careful what you wish for. Sebastian set Cassidy up on the top turnbuckle and was about to perform a package piledriver from the top. Everything seemed to be going fine for Sebastian until he slipped and jumped back, driving Cassidy headfirst onto the canvas. But there was a problem. Sebastian had not managed to properly hook Cassidy into the move and Cassidy was driven down at an unforgiving angle. The crowd fell silent, and the referee stared at Cassidy on the canvas, his neck twisted in an impossible position. Sebastian looked at Cassidy. He saw the emptiness in his eyes. He had no idea what to do and just went for the pin. He won. Sebastian walked out of the ring saying nothing and looking ahead in a cold, empty stare. The crowd had begun rioting and chunks of debris pelted Sebastian as he walked down the aisle. Despite the loud noise, all Sebastian could hear was his own heartbeat. In the ring, the medics and training staff rush to Cassidy's aid. But they were too late. Everyone who was there will tell you exactly what they were doing the moment they saw the life rush out of Cassidy's eyes. They will tell you how they distinctly heard the crackling sound of his neck being broken as his head was driven down to the mat. They will tell you how Sebastian showed no remorse, how he left the arena without so much as an apology. People will always remember that night as the night one life ended. But really two lives ended that night. Cassidy's life ended quickly, well before his time. But Sebastian's life ended in another way. There was no amount of apologizing that could be done to erase what had happened. When he walked out of the arena with that empty stare, he did it because he knew that for the rest of his life he was going to be reminded of that night. He knew that there was nothing he could ever do to seek forgiveness or redemption. OOC: Just wanted to get back into the habit of writing, and hopefully entertain a few people.
