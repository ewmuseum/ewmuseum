17 December 2004 Wolfenwindensor RP#1 'Tis the Season : Karina Wolfenden. 17th December, 2004. "This is a story about... being free?" Karina Wolfenden's ears were long past acknowledging the ambient loops that teased and tickled her inner-ear. Nearly an hour and a half into the taxi journey, she'd been dead to the world before she'd even spent 30 minutes in the vehicle... paid for by her father in advance to make sure she got home for the Christmas period. Having not slept properly for over 48 hours, as she tried to make it back to Albany to answer her parents' calling, Karina first struggled to keep her eyelids apart. Then, when her lashes were joined in a relieving embrace, it didn't take long for the weary K-Wolf to drift off. Hidden somewhere inside an enveloping black hooded sweatshirt, only a few strands of Karina's blonde and pink hair could be seen in the murky shadow of the taxi's back seat. Although her body only seemed to move with the motion of the car, what could be seen of Karina's mouth appeared to be expelling silent words. "Where am I?" she timidly demanded to know. While she normally managed to let life's hassles bounce painlessly off of her, Karina wasn't looking forward to the upcoming 2 weeks. Summoned back to the near-suffocating family home, where stories of how her brother was actually DOING something with his life were bound to be piled onto her, the K-Wolf also had to deal with the prospect of her first ever Cage Match. "What is this place?" Wolfenden's thin, chapped lips appeared to mouth. It was a bout that went against all she'd grown to love about wrestling. It was restrictive, containing, locking her in when the whole of ringside was normally her territory. All over the top attacks down to the floor were ruled out... meaning a Negasonic Lupine Warhead would only plough into steel mesh and their solid, unforgiving fixtures. The only escape route was out of the Cage, and that would mean leaving her friend - Tessa Windsor - to the Wolves, as it were. In this case, the Primetime Central Tag Team Champions, the Shadow Riders. They got to pick the stip, and to steal the line, they chose... wisely. "Who's doing this?" she ever-so-slightly made audible, starting to fidget in her seat. Craving freedom more than anything else in the world, and needing to spread herself towards all four corners of the planet to do so, caging the um-tamed K-Wolf was a wise move. All she'd want to do was escape, not even able to see out into the crowd without cold, grey diamonds slicing across her view. Focus would always be drawn to the mesh, distracting moves that needed all her concentration to avoid drilling herself head-first into the canvas. And while her icy-calm exterior would always try to keep those worries locked away, far from a capitalising eye, Karina's inner-most thoughts couldn't ignore it. "You still asleep back there?" the driver called back to Kari, sensing some movement. "Huh, guess so." Under closed eyelids, and under the usually comforting blanket on slumber, Karina was very much alert. A sinister sense of threat lurked as an undercurrent, always there, dancing fleet-footedly around her consciousness. "What am I doing here?" Kari persisted in enquiring, but while her words were barely more than a breathless whisper, the echo almost floored her. With her feet lost in what looked like a blanket of thick, swirling cloud, the white that blanketed her surroundings was almost blinding. No matter how many times she blinked, trying to un-blur he vision and re-focus, she just couldn't lock her perception onto the environment that surrounded her. The very boundaries of her existence possessed stealth that her visionary radar failed to pick-up. "What am I doing here?" then eerily echoed back, causing Karina's hair to be blown back with its booming force. "What am I doing here? What am I doing here? What am I doing here?" "Cute," Karina disdainfully mumbled in return. Tentative paces seemed to glide Karina across the cloud, while curious emerald eyes scanned the bizarre, white universe she had inadvertently stumbled into. Each footstep didn't seem to make any contact whatsoever with the cloud under her sneakers, but was accompanied by a thunderous echo that almost shook her body apart from its core. Karina's unsteady breaths almost created a vacuum with every inhalation, while her elevated heartbeat acted as a tribal drum for all to hear. "It's official," the bemused Wolfenden sighed softy, nearly whipping the cloud underfoot into a tornado. "My brain is FUBAR'd..." No matter how far she walked, and no matter which direction she chose, Karina's didn't even register the slightest change in her position. Nothing got any closer, not that there was anything to get closer too, and as soon as her mind stopped casting optimistic illusions, Karina realised that not even the cloud passed under her as she strode. Stood, bathed in the whitest of lights that bleached her already pale skin, Karina decided the time for quiet and reserved ponderings was over. Frustrations had raised her pulse to the point where the beating of her heart almost knocked the K-Wolf from her usually sure footing... but she composed herself and drew a deep breath. The white before her eyes seemed to ripple as air was heaved into her lungs, a determined diaphragm ignoring the pummelling from her circulatory system. The pure, scorching oxygen felt like it would explode her lungs, but Karina held it in just long enough to release an impassioned roar. "ENOUGH!" As her voice erupted forth, the vast expanse of white brewed with swirling, dark grey. The echoes of her demand unleashed fierce tridents of lightning, the environment mirroring her mood... her emotions. Then, as naturally as the grey had swirled into being, it was bleached back into white, while the lightning subsided, only leaving a slightly singed odour in the light air. ”Interesting," Karina began to muse, before an almighty THUD nearly had her jumping out of her lightly freckled skin. Spinning rapidly on the spot, the K-Wolf's view of the heavenly white was obscured by ugly, pollutive steel. As her hair caught up, whipping across her face, Karina's eyes focussed closer, to observe a wall of dull grey mesh. Instinctively, Wolfenden sharply turned away, onto to see the same. To the left she turned, and the brutal looking blight on the landscape was already there taunting her. A snappy half turn revealed that she was boxed... or rather, caged in. "Heh," was all she could reservedly grunt. "Symbolism..." Looking up, almost blinded by the harsh white light, Karina narrowed her eyes and could just about make out the mesh stretching as far as her vision. It certainly wasn't something that even an adventure seeker like herself was going to climb. Strolling to the cage, Karina hesitantly extended a hand towards the mesh, leading with a timid index finger. "Oww!"" she then hissed as its touch burned her, instantly causing Kari to recoil her arm and lick her singed fingertip. As if her touch had triggered some jangling mechanism, a sense of special desperation began to creep to the surface inside Karina. Darting her gaze around, causing her flowing mane of blonde and pink to fly in all directions, Karina backed away from one cage wall, only to find herself closer to its opposite. With panic overcoming her, the K-Wolf just turned on the spot, eyes wide with rare anxiety as she felt herself become enveloped within the mesh. There was no escape, and her sense of freedom had be sliced away by the grainy edges of the rusting steel wire. "Here we are," the driver then announced, coming to a halt with a jolt to stir his dozing passenger. "Nice place." "Huh?" a slightly startled Karina blurted out as she blinked back into consciousness. "Oh, yeah, thanks..." Still half asleep, Karina dragged herself out of the car, the lower half of a scarlet dress being revealed under the overly baggy top. The dream seemed just out of the grasp of her memory, teasing her with vague ideas about what she'd imagined... making it feel like it was important somehow. With a look of almost frustrated thought on her pale, lightly freckled face, Karina reached back into the car to heave her bags out. "Well, you're all paid up," the driver reminded her, "so happy holidays!" "Yeah," was Karina's less than enthusiastic reply. "You too." Kari wished she'd have jotted the dream down on a post-it note and stuck it to the inside of her skull. Not even registering the beep of the car's horn as it departed, nor the gravel being displaced under foot, Karina trekked up the path towards the house with the nagging thought prominent in her mind. It was probably just something she'd placed too much importance on, a convenient distraction to take away from what awaited her... The Wolfenden Family Christmas. Not quite a Disney film, but drenched in saccharine showiness. "Don't show me up," were the words her father offered up as the guests started to arrive. "And I thought I asked you to dye your hair back?" Every Christmas it was the same. Called back to Albany, Karina just had to offer up false smiles as stranger after stranger to her arrived at the Wolfenden family home. People who were nothing to her would try and make polite conversation, while she and her father pretended they actually had things in common besides a surname. "Hey, it pays your rent for the next year," was her brother, Max's words of advice, reminding her that there were benefits to go with being part of the family. With her hair its usual blonde and pink, rather than being dyed back to a plain, mousy brown like previous years, Karina managed to stand out in the rather bland crowd. A scarlet, simplistic dress clung to her flat figure, while the sleeves covered the bruises and gashes that usually adorned her fall-breaking arms. But despite being surrounded by nearly 50 talkative guests, all Karina could do was aimlessly mill around, trying to remember the dream she'd had on the journey home. But the evening was starting to get on top of her. Karina was finding it hard to keep the polite smile prominent upon her thin lips. All the meaningless pleasantries being exchanged created an agitating hum, leaving her unable to think without fragments of throwaway conversation gate crashing her contemplation. Despite wearing only a thin dress, the air seemed chokingly hot, and so stale that her lungs couldn't seem to take the oxygen into her blood. Even the instantly forgettable background music grated something inside of her. Each amplified the other, leaving Karina with an overwhelming feeling of claustrophobia, in what was meant to be her family home. "Karina, hello!" a woman who may, or may not, have been her aunt tried to greet the K-Wolf with. "It's so good to see yo-" "Excuse me," Karina hurriedly dismissed herself with, having to swallow hard to stem the urge to vomit. "I need some air." Leaving her possible relative with a bemused look on her leathery, ageing skin, Karina made a bee-line for the patio doors, giant strides leaving the tail of the dress trailing in her wake. "Karina," her father called over, having caught a rapidly moving flash of red out of the corner of his eyes. "Where are you going?" The slalom course of mingling bodies seemed to last for miles, causing Karina to contort her body to avoid any collisions. Her sin began to feel clammy, and she sensed a sheen on her forehead, her body reacting to the heady heat she was feeling. "How've you been?" she caught as she walked past somebody else's conversation, their words echoing in her ear. "How are the kids?" Passing one of the drinks waiters, Karina swiped one of the champagne glasses from the tray, and although she normally despised it, the glass was rapidly consumed with guzzling gulps. Despite having been chilled on ice, all she felt were luke-warm bubbles, not quenching her arid mouth. A short, shrift shudder tried to shake off the aftertaste, while she felt a bead of sweat reach the summit of her eyebrow and trickle down into the socket. With each stride, her endless legs felt weaker beneath her, becoming light to counteract her heavy, clogged-up head. The bodies all around her seemed to be closing in, choosing to walk into her path rather than behind her. Each side-step nearly had her bumping into someone else, each spin blocked off by another body. Gasping for breath, Karina ran her hand through her hair, pulling it from where it had become matted down against her beaded forehead. "Excuse me, can I get through?" she repeated as a mantra. "Can I get past? Please?" Out of her natural environment, Karina couldn't seem to function. Their conversation deafened her, while their body heat left her panting. Not even pausing to apologise as she barged her way through the cage of chatting bodies, Karina was almost jogging for the doors, her hands already out stretched to push them open. "Hyuk," Karina spluttered, slapping a hand against her mouth. She could see from the peculiar looks she was receiving that others thought she was drunk. After all, while her brother was the career-hungry, ambitious Wolfenden child, she was always seen as the waster. The one her father had to gloss over, quickly turning to topic back to Max, or the next round of Golf they were to play. Like a fish out of water, her mouth gulped, trying to let their air in to refresh her, but it wasn't until she burst out of the doors that her lungs cooperated. "Hergh..." she gasped, heaving a deep breath in, doubling over so that her blonde and pink mane dangled below her face. It was at that moment, she finally remembered her dream. "Oh no..." Wolfenden then sighed, before the events played out in her slumber could be fully re-lived. "Uck!" Lunging towards one of the flower beds, Karina dropped to her knees and vomited into the damp soil. Scraping her hair back, and hoping it had dodged the barrage, Karina finally felt the chilly breeze start to sooth her clammy skin. A few coughs cleared the last of her vomit, before deep, refreshing breathes fought off the urge for a repeat performance. This was to be the holiday season for Karina Wolfenden. The one match that might inject fear into her Daredevil nature, and 9 days of family engagements while feeling like the Black Sheep. The sort of Black Sheep who'd throw up in your flower beds. "I hope Tessa is having a better time..." ---- 18 December 2004 Ten days until Christmas. It was something I need not be reminded of. Yet everywhere I went, there was some sort of reminder. On the road, Christmas lights paved the way to the holidays. At home, the chalky easel inside the children’s room, highlighted in multicolored the curt but certainly important message in a form that couldn’t be ignored. Though “ten days until Christmas” may not seem like moving words, my heart was melted each day as the numbers grew smaller. To them, it wasn’t about the gifts. It was about being able to actually spend more than a few hours with their mom. Heartwarming, yes. But it wouldn’t take any of the holiday stress off my shoulders. There was still plenty that needed to be done, and wants that soon turned into I needs. I Want It had easily become the phrase of the month with how often it had been used around my household. Well, followed by I need. There wasn’t a morning I woke up, that I didn’t hear it. And just as any other morning following Thanksgiving, until the twenty fifth of December, the tradition continued. “I remember what else I wanted!” The sun had yet to fully revel itself. The only way someone could find their way through the windowless hallway into the room was through the use of a flashlight. The children knew that. Which is why I regretted giving my only one to them. “It must be about five….again…” Latching onto the nearest pillow, wouldn’t save my eyes any. Rather it opened up the door to play. Instantly, the sleep deposits in my eyes were dissolved by the light that only drew closer, and any other symptoms of sleep deprivation were exiled, by the wrath of a six year old. “Isn’t it a little early?” I questioned, before letting out a giant but certainly not exaggerated yawn. “Maybe…” Crawling over to the empty spot beside my head, Evan made himself at home. “But I didn’t want to forget again.” At this early of an hour, the only one in this household prone to forgetting anything was me. But I kept that to myself as I attempted to shake the rest of the sleep off by yanking myself up into an upright sitting position, that would force the sleep out of my muscles. “So then, what is it?” “It was…” “TESSA GUESS WHAT!” Leaping onto the bed, my ten year old daughter quickly stole away the spotlight the only way she truly knew how to. By being the loudest of them all. “You know what you want for Christmas too?” I prayed, as the little girls eyes glittered in the spotlight produced by the rising of the sun, and the arrival of another morning. “Uh no.” Cutting in, in true preteen style, she voiced her total lack of excitement for the whole Christmas experience. Yeah she wanted presents. Yeah, she wanted to enjoy my time with me while I was home. But knowing very well that Santa didn’t exist, the whole deal seemed a bit cheesy. She was ten now, and totally grown up. At least in her mind. “What…” I started up, raising a suspicious eye after catching glimpse of a guilty grin. Then, nothing more needed to be said. “Oh no!” “Chris told us we could…” “NO!” “…spend Christmas with him.” She finished off, carelessly. To her, this was some sort of a game. I knew deep inside, she wanted nothing more than to be home with me for Christmas. I’d heard many times from the neighbors, my mother, Chris, and the rest of her extended family about how she’d cry herself to sleep, wishing for the chance to have an actual family, rather than just a whole bunch of people whose house she’d stay at from day to day. But in her mind, I didn’t deserve the chance to be a part of that actual family. Instead, I deserved the same kind of treatment I had given her by being on the road constantly. So much for family. Instead, I returned my attention to Evan, who only made matters worse, non intentionally. “Well, never mind then. You don’t need to know what I want.” He said, before dropping his head in shame, slowly crawling away and heading back towards his bedroom. “I still want to know.” Hurt, yeah there certainly was some of that evident in my voice. But at the same time, I had to stay strong. I understood where Christa was coming from. And Evan? He simply went along with her game because he had no one else to look up to. She was the only other one that could constantly be there for him. The only one I had to blame for that was myself. “But why?” His six year old mind wondered aloud. As much as he loved getting presents , the idea of buying for someone you wouldn’t be seeing on Christmas seemed foreign to him. “Because.” I spoke up way too soon considering I hadn‘t even thought of what to say after that. “Because why?” I could hear his young mind pondering as I paused for a moment to brush my hair out of my eyes. Well at least that’s what it looked like from the outside. On the inside, I still needed to formulate what to say next. Instantly, a seemingly flawless but certainly not genius idea came to mind. “We can have Christmas before you leave.” Wrong suggestion. “No we can’t.” Christa mouthed back immediately.
