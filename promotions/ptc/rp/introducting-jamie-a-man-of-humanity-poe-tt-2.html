Lacuna Debris/Sam Alotti Network Pimp Joined: 03 Feb 2003 Posts: 105 Location: Derby/Notts, England Posted: Fri Apr 11, 2003 8:34 pm Post subject: Introducting Jamie: A Man of Humanity (PoE TT #2) As he gawped at himself in the mirror, he became aware that being a human was not going to be all that easy. It hit him like a slap in the face from a lead glove. He was not the perfect form of a human he had expected. Sure, all his limbs were there (as far as he knew, anyway) but something wasn’t right. He could tell as soon as he saw his somewhat hideous reflection in the mirror that he was slightly lacking in something. Now, his small matter of experience in a human body was bugging him. His grip on the emotions was still loose; as he battled through the maze of passages of thought his brain had presented him with. He just couldn’t work out what was wrong with him, and why he felt so down. “Aha!” he boomed, still having lost none of his vocal power. “I’ve read about this phenomenon!” He crossed the room, gingerly moving his large feet across the thin red carpet of the little room and sat upon the bed, which depressed deeply as his weight contacted the bad part of the old mattress. And then something else hit him, yet this time not quite as hard. This feeling that he could not place, this emotion, was depression! Indeed, he had read about it not that long ago in a teenage magazine. When you felt down about something, or indeed yourself. This described his feelings perfectly! “This is not quite as enjoyable as I had once planned and imagined” he stated, his voice completely devoid of emotions but high in utter deadpan. He was a teenager: the lowest of the low, just another of the uneducated masses hanging around on street corners and getting drunk on cheap cider, not bright enough for a job and not motivated enough for education. But, he thought, he knew nearly everything! He had witnessed every act known to man since his creation, and this could be a great advantage! Almost as if planned by Fate, sitting up there in The Absence of Light, his emotional rise was broken up by a loud banging on the thin, white-painted wood of the door. Evil’s murky green eyes darted around for a place to hide as the banging got louder. He flung himself to his feet and grasped at the handle of his overcrowded wardrobe and opened it, just as the door opened. “You haven’t seen a large black shadow with three red gashes instead of a face by any chance, have you?” Evil stopped and glanced upwards at the figure, who was dressed smartly in a three-piece suit with her blonde hair done up into a very neat French plait. Her pale skin was decorated with freckles and a pair of very business woman-like spectacles over her brilliant blue eyes. “Janet?” question Evil, who was taken aback by quite how much like a secretary the woman looked. “That’s ‘Mother’ to you, now get dressed and get down stairs, you will be late for breakfast” she smiled as Evil realised he was dressed in his underwear and sprang two hands down to cover his groin with remarkable speed. _______ He sat across the economy rate wooden dining table in the overly cluttered mess known by most as the kitchen. In front him was a plate of what the humans called food, and either side of said ‘food’ were the implements for digesting it. He scratched at his mop of uncombed brown hair out of some natural habit he must have picked up some where in his unknown past. He was very confused. “So, what did you say we were to do with this ‘food’ again?” he asked Janet, once his secretary, now apparently his mother, who sat across the dining table, fiddling intensely with the folding top of the box of Cornflakes. “Read the memo from The Others. I think it explains it on there somewhere, if you can sift between the gibbering. I can’t believe those freaks actually write in their own laughter.” Janet added, not even thinking of taking her eyes from the simple fastening mechanism she was wrestling with. She took a hand off the box only to slide a series of yellow post-it notes across the table. “They were in the bottom of the chimney, I don’t think they have grasped the concept of mail yet” Evil took grasp of the large collection of soot covered notes and laid them out across the table, placing two across the bowl in front of him which sunk in the milk covering his cornflakes. He scanned the first few and looked up, his eyes flaring red. “This is so not fair! How come you get to keep your own name and I get a new one?” he shouted loudly at his mother, who was now placing the box back into the cupboard on the far wall, the top ripped into shreds by her sharp finger nails. “Because there aren’t many 13 year olds in the known world called ‘Evil’ I suppose. What’s your new name?” she slammed the cupboard door violently. “Oh, and your eyes are glowing red. That could be a problem.” “Jamie!” he shouted loudly “My stupid name is Jamie! Why cant I even get a name like ‘Lucifer’ or at least ‘Lucy’! This is just so unfair!” he stood to his feet and crushed the wooden chair in his vice like grip and smiled. “Whaddaya know, I’m freakishly strong.” “Well you are the living embodiment of evil, dear” offered his mother in way of explanation, and it seemed to work wonders for the flailing self-confidence of the new found teenager, who propped the remains of the chair in the corner and found himself a new one. The hastily written notes from The Others were hard to decipher as his new mind worked over time in trying to digest the information he was being presented with. Apparently, he had to go to school on the week days, where they would teach him all the things that he needed to know about the ways of the human’s. If he had any problems he was to contact The Others through the few powers that he still had left (although they didn’t bother to list any of them). He was also to try and keep his super human-ness a secret, so as not to make anyone jealous and/or frighten them to death. “My lord” huffed Janet as she approached the filthy cooker, which looked as if it had not been cleaned since day one. “They have even left notes in the oven! Who do they think we are? Idiots?” she opened the door to the oven, and sure enough around twenty more yellow post-it notes fell onto her shiny shoed feet. “They act upon what they see, Mother, so if they don’t know how to do something then they assume we wont. Then again, they haven’t studied humans at all, or indeed left that little room of theirs for a couple of centuries either. We are just more privileged, that’s all.” “Hmm, I suppose” she grunted as she heaved herself back over to the uncomfortable wooden chair. “Anything in those notes that could be of use?” Jamie scanned through a couple quickly and looked up, his eyes returning to their normal murky green colour. “Apparently I have a few of my abilities left, although they weren’t kind enough to list them for me.” “You mean you don’t know your own abilities? That’s plain stupid.” She replied, meaning no harm, although Jamie, who was taken aback quite enormously by the feeling, felt it like a kick in the stomach. “Fine, pick on the kid why don’t you? I used to know them, but that was a long time ago, well, it seems like it with all this manual exercise.” Janet shrugs. “I suppose you will just have to find out then wont you? I mean, how hard can it be?” “You would be surprised” Jamie began to ready himself for a long rant about the meaning of life, and how it was supposed to be lived, but nothing came out. He tried again, but was met by the same feeling of emptiness. “Janet?” he questioned. “Hey! That’s mother to you, what is it?” she replied, looking upon him with a feeling the she was unready for. She couldn’t even put a name to it. “I don’t know everything any more. I find that reasonably upsetting.” His voice was devoid of emotions as he tried to comprehend this new feeling. His vocabulary was up to its usual, 4000 year old standard, except he could feel everything else draining from his memory. “I mean, its like that soggy lump of matter in my head is disintegrating into tiny shards of knowledge or something.” Since being banished from the Absence of Light by his contemporaries, The Others, he had only felt his immortal life tumble down the steep slope of humanity. If only he hadn’t started to take an interest in the pesky human civilisation he was supposed to reign in blood over then this would not have happened, and maybe his secretary would not be his mother. It had started as a minor interest, just a few little human artefacts dotted around his area of infinity, but it had to grow. Interest was just like a seed, a seed that eventually blossomed into a full-blown tree, with all the trimmings attached. His obsession had seen him turn from the punisher of mankind’s sins into… a member of mankind’s sins. And Jamie wasn’t best pleased with this, oh no. “Now… about this food” he muttered as he examined a series of the post-it notes that were dotted across the grimy surface of the dining table, eventually settling his dirty coloured eyes on one particular note, the scrawled title barely distinguishable as reading ‘Food and the process of its digestion’. After reading through the note multiple times (a task that came across as relatively easy), he placed his right hand awkwardly on the spoon and place it into the bowl, roughly shovelling up a large chunk of cornflakes that had become hideously soggy after being left in a lake of milk for a hell of a long time. After a sullen few moments of contemplation, he lifted the spoon to his lips and forced it through, placing the spoon heavily down onto the plastic bowl, it clattering loudly He chewed awkwardly, closing his eyes as he did so. Janet had crossed the room and was watching him intently as she leant on the back of the cheap wooden chairs. A sweat began to break out on Jamie’s forehead as he attempted to swallow, failing miserably. He glances upwards and tried again, this time with success as the pang of flavour hit his dry mouth with a force unimaginable. He jumped to his feet and gagged loudly, opening his mouth wide enough for Janet to look in and turn away in disgust at the half chewed cornflakes inhabiting the top of his tongue. “That…” he gasped for breath. “That… so rules!” “Well hurry up hunny, or you will be late for School.” Janet added and turned away, leaving Jamie gawping at the back of his secretary turned mother. “School?” _________________ [detail withheld]: Oh mighty Simon ... what must I grovel about now?
