The Soldier Returns Home

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 File #045: "The Soldier Returns Home" Written by [name withheld] Karl was done with the war - he was finished. And as he lay on the floor of the dilapidated white row boat that had, for the past few weeks, been his home, he attempted ignorance of what horrible things lay on the horizon. They were going to be court-martialed, dishonorably discharged, or even worse – sent back. Karl had always had a hard time discerning Michael’s exact mood, but he knew in his heart at least that nothing was worth going back to Baghdad. A litany of horrible images from the past year passed through his memory all at once and he shuddered audibly suddenly feeling grateful for the splinters that pierced his bare back. Michael stopped rowing for a moment, and turned to him looking as blank as usual. "You alright?" he murmured. "And why don’t you put your jacket back on. It’s freezing." "It’s got the American flag on it," Karl said simply sitting up and brushing the wood chips from his back. "I don’t think I’ll be wearing anything red, white, or blue for a while after I get home." At this, Michael simply scoffed and turned back to the cold and watery horizon that lay ahead, "So now you’re going to be one of those Anti-American idiots?" "Yeah, I am," Karl growled, and to his dismay the boat rocked to the side a bit. "I don’t ever want to see that kind of shit again. I’m going to college. Fuck this shit." He settled himself back upon the floor of the boat grumpily allowing the splinters, which pierced his skin again even more painfully than before, to calm his nerves. Michael didn’t say anything for a long while until, approximately five minutes later, Karl thought he heard him mutter under his breath a blatantly uncaring "whatever," but it was indistinguishable from the constant swishing of the paddle through the salty water that taunted them and their horrible thirst. And being reminded of their horrible thirst reminded them of their horrible hunger. But their minds changed the subject quickly before it fully wrapped its self around the truth about their chances of getting home. But it was hard ignoring the obvious, and Karl knew that it was about time for him to learn over the side of their makeshift home and throw up another batch of empty bile, and he knew that if he thought about it – realized the potential for death in all of this, he’d surely go insane and die even more quickly. It was a few minutes later, however, that Michael turned back around suddenly startling Karl out of his hunger-induced half-consciousness with something that he rarely saw out of his old high school friend. A smile. "What is it, have you decided to eat me?" Karl asked and he got on his knees so as to see above the curvature of the boat. "What are you smirking about?" "Look," Michael mouthed and he pointed at some far off point in the fog. "Use these." He handed Karl his binoculars and Karl did as he was told. And he saw it. "Land," he groaned with an almost sexual desperation and he squeezed onto the rowing plank besides Michael who looked too pleased to be angry that he was nearly knocked overboard.. "Give me that other paddle. We’ll row together. Get there faster." And it was with a nearly feverish energy that the two of them began rowing forward not caring that as the minutes went on, arctic Atlantic Ocean water splattered onto them, stung their eyes, and was frozen in icicle shapes from their noses. Upon the tall plateau that they rowed ever-closer to, an impossibly long pole stretched up into the sky holding up what was, no doubt, an American flag, though neither of them could clearly see the warm red, white, and blue colors through the fog that came with the autumnal weather. This only propelled their thirst for home even further and all thoughts of the horror of Iraq and the illegitimacy of American politics flew from their minds as the most primal need they’d ever known rapt their entire existence for that short while. The need to be home. And as they finally drew near enough to the raised and rocky land to see the ridges of the terrain, ignoring the fact that they both knew that no such coastal line existed in the United States, they could not see the flag attached to the pole still. The pole was far too tall. They didn’t care however and, strictly on adrenaline, they clamored out of the boat letting the waves carry it back out to the foggy sea and headed up a makeshift stairway of stone and grass towards the top of the plateau with Michael in the lead. The scene was so euphoric and unbelievable that when Michael finally reached the top and saw what Karl could not yet see, turned and yelled out "run," Karl was deeply confused. "What do you mean run?" Karl laughed with a smirk sure that Michael’s unusual sense of humor had made a rare appearance when a massive hand reached out from behind an apparent wall of fog, grabbed Michael around the neck, and pulled him out of view. "Michael?" Before Karl could have a chance to react properly and decide if he should dive in after his friend, however, a complete figure emerged from the fog wall. The lack of sunlight prevented Karl from seeing it properly. All he knew is that it was a man, and that it was huge. The massive shadow dove for him, but Karl was ready, and he dove off of the stone stairs onto the rocky slope that dominated either side. Only able to maintain his balance on this slant for a moment, he dove back onto the stairs just as the shadowy figure, apparently not too bright, dove after him. They passed each other in midair and just as Karl landed on the stairs again, the figure, which Karl still had not seen clearly, hit the stone slope and began a painful and swift decent to the sea from which Karl had just come. What bothered him, however, was the fact that the shape, obviously human, had made no utterance as pains as the, no doubt, jagged rocks broke their fall on the way down. Too scared and confused to even scream, Karl simply looked from the fog wall and the summit of the stairway back to the similarly fog-covered sea below and was faced with a choice: find out what had happened to Michael, or take his chances trying to find their boat down below where Karl was sure somehow that the creature that had attacked him was not dead. But perhaps the arm that had grabbed Karl had been the creature’s, and now the creature was underwater or impaled on a sharp rock somewhere on the way. All at once, Karl was scared, angry, and upset at himself for having ever left the war in the first place. His mother hated him because she probably thought he was dead by now and his father, molded World War II medals and all, was turning in his grave as he spoke so as to turn his backside to him. He wanted to cry. He wanted his mother. Anything. And it was at that exact moment that another figure came out from behind the fog wall startling him. This figure, however, he could clearly define from the onset. It was a man – or had been – with ghostly white skin, piercing icy blue eyes, and long white hair accompanied by a black cloak. Though he was skeletally thin, Karl turned to turned to run anyway, but the man reached out and grabbed his shoulder and though it seemed done with an almost feminine motion, the grip was probably the firmest he had ever felt. "Don’t run," the man spoke in an odd voice that sounded like three voices at once. "You won’t get very far. I’ll protect you. Give me your hand." However Karl, who was both horrified and mystified by this man had no intention of giving him his hand; however, there was something almost fluid about the figure’s body and though it was almost anorexic, something told Karl that if he ran, he wouldn’t make it very far. He would be caught and obliterated – destroyed. "Give me your hand," the man repeated slowly and simply extending his arm. "Or you will die here once Guile makes his way back from that nasty tumble you caused him." What else could he do? "Kasidy Drake, by the way," he said at last once Karl’s hand became planted in his palm. "Kasidy Drake!?" Karl exclaimed as Kasidy gave a great pull and the both of them passed through the fog wall. "Holy hell!" Karl immediately forgot what he was about to say as the moody fog and the slow drizzle that had occupied his entire trip suddenly dissipated. Suddenly the sun was shining, and the ground was no longer rocky and unkind, but green and completely covered by grass, trees, and vines – the latter two all seemed to bear wonderful fruits. A small flock of bluebirds flew overhead chirping merrily as they passed. What’s more, there were strangely beautiful dressed in translucent white cloaks everywhere. Karl could see the bosom of each girl through the thin material of the body-veils, all perfectly shaped. It was as if someone had decided to clone an army of Angelina Jolies and Johnny Depp’s and let them roam free on the most beautiful untouched rainforest in South America. There was even a soothing waterfall going in the background and all of these beautiful and relatively nude people were speaking to one another quite merrily in some strange language that Karl, who had been all over the world with the military, had never heard. "Veex pekninv," one of the men said to one of the women. "Tugo u zeqojjox xuc" "Veex pekninv zikehok," the woman replied with a smile and a slight bow. "Qoh aj wekniyuho henivith, jituqq fo?" The man nodded to the woman eagerly and with a wink, picked some grapes off of the vine closest to him and carefully ate them as if he were rationing. "What the hell kind of language is that?" Karl demanded, his confusion driving him to anger. "Now look here, I got top marks in my languages class at the academy – and this gibberish doesn’t even have Latin behind it-" "Silence," Kasidy said lowly as some of the beautiful people turned to look at them – some were positively terrified at the sight of Kasidy and dove back into their homes. "You’re lucky these people aren’t snitches. Then your friend wouldn’t be the only one in need of saving." "Pic Mekinyo," one of the women spoke shyly, careful to keep her eyes locked to the grassy ground and Kasidy looked up, apparently understanding that he was being addressed. "Veex qayer in ceak puhyt in hato Global Title Tournament." Kasidy looked to her, his too-blue eyes looking the most human they had thus far. He looked… sympathetic towards her. "Thank you," Kasidy replied in a boyish way and then seeing her cock her head to the side corrected himself, "Hatuner ceuk." She blushed and ran off into some bushes her peers who had not dived into their homes giving her a nervous look. Kasidy’s face seemed to flicker between something human and something non for several seconds before he cleared his throat and marched on pulling Karl by his arm, "Come on." "Where are we going? Did that girl say Global Title Tournament" Karl demanded unable to fight Kasidy’s invisible strength. He looked upward at the flagpole that had been hidden behind the fog before they had reached the summit of the stairs but now was clearly visible amongst the sun and it was a flag unlike any he had ever seen – plainly white with a simple small cross in the top right corner. "Where are we?" Kasidy stopped walking suddenly and got in Karl’s face just as he was asking why all of the women were naked and good looking, "This," Kasidy growled impatiently gesturing around the beautiful isle, "is New Eden. It’s a small island nation that isn’t supposed to exist. These people are Edenians, and they speak Edenic," he turned and started to walk again without forcefully pulling Karl along. "And we’re going to go find your rather unattractive friend. Stick close – the Guard is always around." "Guard?" Karl asked cautiously following Kasidy who was striding ahead without looking back at him, his cloak trailing almost weightlessly behind him. "Like the Coast Guard?" "The Sovereign Guard is the military of New Eden. It’s comprised of little more than a hundred soldiers. Unarmed," Kasidy explained as they continued to walk and interrupted Karl as he began to say something. "They’re probably the most dangerous military on the face of this Earth. Don’t ask any more questions. I don’t know why I’m telling you anyway." As they continued their stride towards some destination unknown to Karl, he noticed for the first time that there were posters hung up between palm trees all along the grassy path featuring Kasidy in full color, arms folded over his chest and some weird script that he assumed was Edenic. The only English words on any of the posters was the same: "Primetime Central," and "Global Title Tournament V." Though much of the island was vacant, the few more Edenians Karl saw seemed to run at the sight of Kasidy – one was actually praying in front of one of the Global Title Tournament posters before he ran off. "I’m the Prince of this island," Kasidy said with a sigh after becoming bored with Karl’s dumfounded looks. "And also the Commander and Chief of the Sovereign Guard. Technically, I’m not supposed to be helping you… and yet, here we are." He seemed somewhat depressed about some part of that and looked at one of the intimidating posters of himself sighing deeply. "So why are you helping me?" Karl asked curiously. "And why are you on all of these posters. What’s this Global Title Tournament thinger business?" Kasidy smirked, "You ask a lot of questions. The Guard only deals with people who purposely trespass on our land and are considered a threat. You two – forgive me – don’t seem to be bright enough to find this place and attempt to do something considered dangerous. Unfortunately, I’m probably the only one on this isle that’s… so forgiving. The Global Title Tournament is a wrestling tournament sponsored by the PTC – a wrestling commission. I’ve been entered into it. It’s the only reason I’d be able to re-enter the United States." "Re-Enter?" "Surely you didn’t think I was born here. I was a professional wrestling for Core Wrestling not even a year ago. Smiley. Well, I was a doctor first. A biochemist for Maxwell Laboratories – you’ve probably heard of them since you’re not a wrestling fan-" ing fan-" "Oh yeah," Karl said feeling pleased that he wasn’t confused for once. "They’re the ones who manufacture my… uh…" and he trailed off blushing wildly. "Sexual stimulation drugs?" Kasidy finished. "Pen-X. They’re top-seller. Idiots could never get off of the novelties and get on the same page as me – I was trying to find a cure for cancer. That’s why I left." "Oh," Karl replied. "Er.. why are you telling me all of this?" For the first time, it was Kasidy’s turn to look slightly confused. He shrugged, "I don’t really know. That’s a good question. You ask so many, I suppose you were due." "And this Global thingamajig is a tournament you say? When’s your first match?" "Tomorrow," Kasidy sighed matter-of-factly. "You don’t seem too worried." "I don’t have to be," and then suddenly his eyes went wide. "Get down!" Karl immediately dropped to the ground behind a bush as Kasidy’s face suddenly turned from panicked to stoic once again and he walked Karl as if ignoring his presence, hands behind his back. "Veex pekninv Kasidy," remarkably British-sounding voice spoke and Karl looked through the bush to see a figure in the middle of a field dressed in a blindingly white suit. "You’re just in time. I was just about to do your job." "Oh?" Kasidy inquired as he strode into the field where Karl could see the faint outlines of other men including a bruised one that was, no doubt, the one that Karl should have killed. "Explain." "Ah yes well, Llevar found this eahjixok trying to trespass upon our land," the voice said sounding unusually light and conversational. "And though he pleads ignorance, there is no exception to our rules. Llevar was about to kill him himself, but I told him not to be selfish and to wait until you came along Kasidy since we all know how much you love to kill. It’s your Princely duty after all to protect the Edenians." "Uh-huh," Kasidy said, his voice quaking a bit. "Yes, but we’ve let wanderers go before Opmokek.Surely there is no reason to break a commandment today – just let him go back home like the others. They’ll all think that he’s out of his mind and cast him out onto the street. He’s no danger." Whatever the "Opmokek" was, he must have given some kind of strange look because Kasidy and the other members of the Sovereign Guard drew back a few steps. Including the one that had just survived an impossibly fall into several jagged rocks. "I appreciate your enthusiasm for the word of God Kasidy," the man in the white suit went on sounding dangerously polite. "And usually, I’d be prepared to agree with you – I hate to stain the garden’s grass with blood – especially only a day removed from Sunday, but things have changed. We discussed this. Certain plans have been set in order, and I have no desire to risk an outsider learning of and spreading our plans to the outside world. The Global Title Tournament only happens once a year – I failed to put my plan in full order last year, and I would prefer not to wait another three hundred and sixty-five days." "Yes Opmokek, but I don’t think he’s a threat-" "He’s wearing a uniform. U.S. Marines. That sounds like a threat to me Commander," the large black man in the crowd sneered putting as much sarcasm into the last word as possible. He seemed to be the only one other than the man in the white suite and Kasidy who could, or wanted to speak. "And Guile claims that there was another one. Shirtless. He threw him off of the stairway. You know what that means, don’t you?" "That Guile is an idiot Llevar?" Kasidy sighed as if he had something better to do. Apparently the two didn’t like one another. "And if you found him outside of the gate, why did you pull him in? That seems like more of a danger than-" "It means," the black man, apparently called Llevar, continued through gritted teeth. "That the United States has directly attacked New Eden! A marine attacked a Sovereign Guardsmen! Opmokek! Opmokek!" "They’ve done no such thing," the man in the white suit continued matter-of-factly and still sounding quite pleased in spite of the bickering. "The United States will never attack New Eden. He’s a stray solider. Specialist Michael Edwards. Didn’t have the gusto to fight a war that he signed up for in an attempt to get the government to pay for his college tuition, but he ended up here instead of the Jersey Shore like he had planned." "How do you know all of that?" Kasidy wondered aloud and quickly added "Opmokek." "I asked him." "Oh." "And now I’ll ask him one more question and we’ll end this silly dispute," the man in the white suit said, snapped his fingers, and had one of the non-verbal Guardsmen pick up something on the ground that Karl had not noticed before – Michael’s limp body. Karl stifled a horrified gasp, but then, to his slight relief, realized that he wasn’t dead yet. Just brutally beaten. "Michael? Specialist Edwards? Can you hear me?" Michael groaned painfully. "Good. Now Michael, I know you’ve spent your entire life following orders for the greater good – and so have your parents all the way back to your great grandfather who fought on the Union side of the Civil War for your race’s freedom. Now, before we kill you, we’ve got one last order to give you. Well, more of a question really. You see, we have somewhere very important to be in several hours and Prince Drake here – wave Kasidy – has to bloody someone so that we can go on about our plans and we can’t have you or your idiot United States military baboons ruining any of that. So tell me Michael… did you come to New Eden with someone else?" Michael groaned again painfully, and then Karl could hear the sound of Michael summoning his last bit of strength to spit in the white-suited man’s face and give a half-hearted "fuck you." Karl secretely cheered for his friend, but, rather than react aggressively, the man in the white suit simply took out a handkerchief and whipped the saliva off of his face and laughed. Laughed. And what came next exactly, Karl, probably luckily, did not know because the man replaced the handkerchief, reached for his face again, and must have done something horrible because the entire Sovereign Guard took at least five big steps back except for the one unlucky guard chaged with holding Michael up who turned to the side almost immediately. "Now let me ask you again Specialist Edwards. Did you come to New Eden alone?" "No," Michael replied automatically and Karl stopped breathing. "Who did you come here with?" "Lieutenant Karl West." "Do you know where he is?" "No…" "Very well," the man said politely and undid whatever he did to his face. "Kasidy? Sure you don’t want to do it." "I wish not to soil my hands only a day removed from Sabbath." The man shrugged indifferently, "Oh well. Llevar, it’s your lucky day. Riqq tip wek hato Sovereignty." "With pleasure my lord," Llevar laughed, and he stepped forward gripping Michael by by scruff of his collar facing him. "Any last words filthy eahjixok?" "Yeah," Michael breathed, the inflection back in his voice. "Fuck you you bastards-" But then Llevar’s head suddenly came forward and Karl clenched his eyes shut just in time; however, the world’s best earplugs probably could not silence the sickening crunch that followed. When he opened his eyes again, Michael was on the ground now completely motionless and unbreathing while Llevar wiped the blood off of his forehead. "Find the other one Kasidy," the man said with a yawn. "And if you don’t feel like killing that one either, I’m sure Guile would enjoy repaying him for causing him to fall off of the plateau." "Y-yes Opmokek," Kasidy replied with a respectful bow and he marched back towards the bushes muttering a half-heated, "Wek hato Sovereignty." "Don’t forget. We’re leaving in about an hour," the man said as the band of Guardsmen began to disperse. "So as usual, take your time. But I do not want him alive on my land when we return tomorrow night. Understood?" "Yes my lord," Kasidy replied and he walked off not acknowledging the skeptical look from the man called Llevar whose face Karl could see briefly as he walked off. He looked down at Karl who was silently sobbing on the beautiful grass and amongst the chirping birds and blooming flowers. "Come on. Let’s go." "Since the second grade… I knew him since the second grade…" "Let’s go!" Kasidy hissed. "You need to disappear quickly." And with a great amount of effort, Karl got to his feet again and the two of them began to walk westward which was, fortunately, vacant and to a small makeshift dock. A boat – with an engine – lay in waiting and Karl clamored aboard not really having the strength for any more questions. "You’re dead," Kasidy informed him severely. "Or rather, you’re going to become an electronic ghost by the time you get home. Your credit cards, your birth certificate… all will be gone by the time you get to the shore. If you’re able to find anyone whom you can convince isn’t going crazy, they’ll think you’re crazy. A magical island with superhuman wherein the sun is always shining and beautiful women walk around in the nude all day… if you don’t want to be locked away in a hospital or become a homeless man, keep your mouth shut and forget everything that’s just happened." "What’s happening at the Global Title Tournament?" Karl wondered aloud. "You’re going to kill them aren’t you? The participants? You’re going to kill them." Kasidy did not answer and simply hurriedly began untying the boat from the dock and making sure that it had plenty of gas. "The second grade man! He sniffed the markers, and I ate the glue – hey, answer my question. What are you going to do at the Global Title Tournament? Who’s going to die? Why does this place even exist? Why won’t the United States ‘ever attack New Eden…’" Kasidy untied the final knot. "Why didn’t you kill me?" Karl demanded realizing at last that that was the question he should have asked all along. "Good-bye Karl," Kasidy said simply, pulled the ripcord and walked away. Somehow Karl still had enough of a mind left over to realize that he had to steer it. He hated professional wrestling and didn’t even know what a Global Title was but some people were in danger, and some of them were bound to be American. Discharge or no discharge, someone needed to be warned. And so, one friend shorter than he had been when he had shipped out in 2004, and no longer too concerned about being court-martialed, suddenly the soldier was on his way home.
