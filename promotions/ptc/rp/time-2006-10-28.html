Time

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-28 Steve sat at his desk and brooded. The world had gotten so much more complex in just the last week. Tim Forbes would recover from his injuries. Eventually. His doctors knew that much. But how much longer they would feel the need to keep him in the drug induced coma and how much of him would be left when he came out would be anyone’s guess. And even if the brain swelling did no lasting or permanent damage, Steve knew that Tim’s energies would required first to assist in the police hit-and-run/attempted murder investigation well before Steve would be allowed to ask him about football games and suspected expense abuses. The police had, in fact, been in the office this Tuesday, presumably to justify the money and manpower they were expending on the case while they waited for their primary witness to wake up. Several of them had arrived early in the day and began asking around if Tim Forbes had any possible enemies in the company that might want to do him in. Steve wasn’t sure if his name came up that morning; but, sure enough, around 2:00 pm he was called into the conference room to talk a squat, brown-suited, detective who went by Smythe or Smitts or something like that to waste another hour or two before he kicked off for the day. Detective Smythesmitts had a series of prepared queries about Steve and Tim’s relationship. And Steve did his best to answer truthfully on how a heated rivalry had grown into a grudging respect and finally to what was by all accounts a healthy friendship based upon mutual esteem and admiration - how Tim’s people skills were countered balanced often against his own skills with numbers - and how the partners had originally played them against one another until they realized they much more they could accomplish working on the same team rather than against it. He spoke truthfully of what he saw as Tim’s strengths as a person as well as his weaknesses. After all, the easiest story to remember is The Truth – and Steve had nothing to hide. Until the questions turn to the topic of Professional Wrestling, that is. "Jeez – doesn’t this guy have a bank robbery to stop or a donut to catch," thought Steve while fessing up to how he used to be a fan as a youth before drifting away from it to other interests about the same time he went away to college. But he did not feel the need to go into his recent "rediscovery" of the sport nor his expensive knowledge of the "Masked Adversary" archetype in its historic lexicon. "Can’t you buy one of those things at any arena souvenir booth? Heck, I bet we could buy a half dozen of those things on eBay right now if we wanted to fire up the PC and give it a shot," Steve had told the detective who had laughingly agreed as he moved on to other matters in the case. Just because Smittssmythe had dropped a fishing hook in a lucky part of the lake that didn’t mean that Steve was obligated to bite it and automatically insert himself as a prime suspect in the case when he knew he had absolutely nothing to do with it. Although "it" may very well have had everything to do with him and his investigation - an investigation that had been seriously decelerated by the events of the last few days. Steve knew that time was working against him right now. He knew that whoever he was chasing in his investigation knew a whole lot more about him and what he was doing than Steve knew about them. It was like playing it in a game of hide-and-seek against the invisible man while wearing a day-glo cap on your head and strapping a blaring fire siren around your neck. Because of the sense of urgency that was washing over him to catch the culprit ASAP, Steve had even considered calling the GTT6 office to thank them very much for the opportunity and regretfully drop out. Oddly, it was his wife Elise of all people that convinced him not to. "If fate insists on giving you a second chance to chase a dream, who are you to tell it to take a walk – no matter how ridiculous that dream is?" Steve felt a deepening of his appreciation for Elise for that. Perhaps the first such deepening they had had in a long time. Maybe she did understand what he was about, after all. He promised himself that he would dedicate his match against Bobino to her, provided he actually got around to getting a chance to prepare for it. It was still over a day away though. And, if Steve worked quickly to find the invisible man, he was sure he would still be able to cram like a Pre-Chem major at mid terms and still enter the ring on Saturday as strong and as ready as ever. And it was with this in mindset that Steve sat at his desk and poured over the data in front of him. Steve had tracked back and email that Old Man Bagley had sent out in August to congratulate the eighteen lucky, "lottery winning" employees who would get to join the partners and select important clients in the company box for the Manning Bowl. And these were the names he was currently cross-referencing against the network folder - that Chad in IT had been good enough to direct him to - full of digital photos of those that had actually attended the event. Tim Forbes – Check. Kevin Dulce – Check Ed Dannon – Check …and female guest – also check. Old Man Bagley – Check Ingrid Brewster – Check. Radford Laney – Check … … All in all, Steve was able to find visual evidence that all three partners and at least fourteen of the "ticket winning" HB&D associates were definitely in attendance at the game. Which left four possible inside sources for the unused tickets that he now had concealed carefully in his wallet – plus any number of potential "sources" amongst the client guests. Steve had a gut feeling it was inside though. Or at least he should remove the obvious and nearby from his list before going on to the esoteric. He grabbed his phone, "Peg, could you come in here when you get the chance?" As Steve awaited Peggy’s arrival, his mind began to wander to the coming weekends Round 2 match. Steve had managed to track down a "Best of the New Shit" DVD from a local Coastal Video store on the drive home the Wednesday night, but was quite dismayed to find that it was just the highlight clips of some Norwegian Ska Band and their abbreviated tour of Southeast Asia. Luckily Elise had already called to let him know that a few videos featuring the real Bobino had arrived in their mail earlier today. And he would have to make sure he got on them as soon as he got home. "You needed me, Steve," Peggy arrived before his thoughts could take him any further with GTT6. "Yeah. You won a couple of those Giants tickets for the season opener, didn’t you?" "I certainly did," said Peggy proudly. "How’d you enjoy the game?" asked Steve. "Other that the wrong team winning, I thought it was fine," replied Peggy. "So you were there… At the game?" "No, I watch it from home," Peggy was unsure what to make from this line of questioning, "I gave the tickets to my son Mike, if that’s really pertinent." "Trust me, Peg. It is," reassured Steve. "I am trying to figure something out. So what did he think of meeting all the bigwigs up in the box?" "He didn’t. Truth is he never made it to the company box. He ran into a couple friends and watched the game from the end zone." Peg paused before continuing, "Did he get in trouble at the game, Steve?" she asked "Is that what this is about?" "No no. He’s fine. Or at least he didn’t get into any trouble that I know of." Steve dangled his preposition, "This is something completely different…. Don’t worry about it," and he conclude with, "Thanks tough." Peggy shrugged as she returned to her station and Steve looked back to his list. He once again reached for the phone, only to have his own intercom line buzz. "Steve, it’s Frank. I need you to come down to my office." It was Mr. Hainesworth, the company CFO and Steve’s immediate superior. "Be right there," Steve replied into the speaker phone. He got up, closed out his computer to make sure no one could walk in and see what he had been working on and left his office. On his way to the "park side" of the building Steve ran in Lisa Somers from Marketing standing by the water cooler getting a drink. The whole office knew that Lisa had long had a "thing" for Steve, but he always managed to rebuff her politely if quickly. "So Steve, did you see Survivor last night?" "Can’t chat right now, Lees. Hainesworth needs me in his office," he stated as he blew past her. Frank Hainesworth was always someone who when he said "Jump" you didn’t need to ask "How high?" because he always wanted you jumping your highest. But lately, as the whole company knew he was angling to become partner the instant that Old Man Bagley final found the good sense to retire, he was become more and more unbearable and demanding with additional extra month the geezer decided to hang on. And it was with that in mind that Steve made it to Hainesworth’s office in near record time. "Steve," said Hainesworth, sensing his entry, "Tim Forbes was scheduled to head out to Denver for that conference with The Wasserman Group. And obviously that’s not going to happen, so I need you to go instead." "But Frank, I am really back up right now with this whole…" "Your flight leaves tonight at 7:00. Whatever you are doing can wait until you get back next Wednesday." Shit. Next Wednesday? How was that going to work? Steve wondered why he of all people had been selected to take Tim’s place. It’s not like any history with Wasserman whatsoever. But as he thought on it a second more, his suspicions grew as to the real reason we was going. "Good enough, sir," Steve relented, "7:00 it is then. I’ll just make sure to grab my laptop so I don’t fall too far behind on my regular duties while I am away." "You do that" retorted the CFO as he dismissed Steve with a gesture. Steve stewed as he headed back to his office. So now he knew Hainesworth was involved, at least at some level. And that just steeled his resolve to get to the bottom of all of this. But it left him with another – perhaps bigger – dilemma. How was he going to make it to Denver tonight, check into his hotel, meet with the Wasserman’s for the next four days and still get back to town for his match in the middle of it all without anyone noticing he was gone? "Guess someone was going to be spending the next few hours on the phone with the airlines," Steve thought to himself. "And I better remember to put in on my personal charge card. Because I hear that our fool of an Assistant CFO is really cracking down on expense abuses."
