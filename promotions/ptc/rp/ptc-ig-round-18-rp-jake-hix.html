Jake Hix RP "The Instrument" The blackness of the night enveloped him as he sat on the couch, staring out the cracked window frame to the small red neon flickering in and out. The symbol didn’t mean anything he knew of, but elongated S looked like it had been drawn backwards, with a flare jutting out at an awkward angle. For the moment, time and fate had directed him to this room to keep watch over the dilapidated door leading into an equally decrepit bar. So he continued sitting in the threadbare chair, a spring sticking into his back, looking for a clue he could jump on. The Texas heat had barely relented even with the cloud cover and rolling rains. Beneath him, the dumpsters from the Chinese food take-out and the Indian restaurant were constantly cooked again as they sat festering for the scurrying animals. Animals of the two and four-legged variety liked this alley. They wandered the soaked alleyways looking for the food to sustain them in their empty lives, the instincts to survive pushing them forward. The homeless men and women he watched were as feral as the rats, cats and dogs roaming free. As he stewed in the heat and the stink of putrefaction, he caught glimpses of men and women finding the doorway. No one had been able to give the name of the bar, it was just known by the neon sign. There were rumors, but there were always rumors swirling around the dark passages on the backside of the city. They spoke of being able to find all manner of dark delights in those four walls, of the damned and the blessed walking amongst the flesh. He didn’t have any illusions about the rumors. The wind carried the scent of death from the club along with many other pleasures and pains. For him, this was all about the quest for a location. He didn’t need anyone to hold his hand in that house, but there was no need to push into the club until the man he sought was there. They had no set meeting date or time, but he knew the face. The dark hair concealing the man’s face led to eyes devoid of any warmth or humanity. As a former lawyer, much of that devastation may have taken place long before the man’s association with the Dark messiah, Sid. Malum had hunted his family though the swamps of Louisiana following the devastation of Katrina, and he continued hounding them as part of his daily life. He enjoyed his position as Sid’s enforcer, but now he would come face-to-face with one of his victims like never before. Finally, the scraps of evidence had come together to reveal a single fact about his tormentor. A simple twist of fate had seen a man friendly to their cause try and help a young woman given over to the ravages of addiction. While the priest tried to save her, he caught the look from the man through the group of bodyguards. Remembering the picture and the sketch he had received in his email account, the priest sent word. Jake had flown from Las Vegas down to Dallas at once. Hiding the new discovery from his brothers had been a necessary step, because Xavier could not be trusted to act responsibly at the moment. When a loved one so close is taken, men are willing to sacrifice too much to buy their return. Jake had to make sure they never returned to the perilous precipice where they could all be hurled into chaos. His love for his sister and his brother’s future wife could not interfere with the larger campaign they had committed to over the last few months. As he reached for the bottle of water on the scratched tabletop of the nightstand, he noticed them moving down the alley. These men were not the vagabonds and dregs society had thrown away. They were dressed in black slacks and nice shirts covered by expensive jackets to keep the rain off their bodies. In the middle of the pack of five men he saw the lifeless eyes he had been longing for, the eyes of Malum. His men escorted him into the club, and then the alley went silent again except for the rain tapping all around. He stood up in the black bleakness of his room and walked over to the door. He paused at the mirror, seeing his own reflection staring back at him. In the black room, he caught only occasional glimpses from the lightning outside. He was a strong man, a powerful man who once held an almost regal confidence in his features. Time and trials had robbed him of so much, and now he saw the full, healthy face giving way to gauntness at the edges. The hallow eyes were set atop the darkening circles from so many sleepless nights spent wondering when his excursion into the realms between would finally come to an end. Shaking his head, he tried to shake away the doubts inside. He was moving forward to meet a man who had tried to kill him and his family. Doubt and fear had to be controlled tightly to face such a man. Who are you? Jake hesitated as he thought about the answer. Here he was no longer Jake Hix. He wasn’t even the Big Dawg anymore. Here he was a simple man chosen for a simple purpose. Humility provided the deepest reserves, the greatest strengths that could be found. In front of those who sought to destroy him he would find his humility, and with it the strength to overcome them. I am the instrument. Jake pulled the black trench coat off the hook on the back of the door. The coat went over his wide shoulders to protect him from the rain, as thought the tears from Heaven could injure his soul more than it was. He stepped out into the hallway, walking through the trash strewn about by the short-term residents who didn’t care how they treated the hotel. He skipped the elevator, not that it would have come when he pressed the call button having been broken for years, and proceeded down to the stairwell. Jake found his way through the lobby and it’s traps of holes in the floor and furniture pieces turned over by unruly crowds to the back exit. He stepped out into the night, boot heel splashing the pooled water and rats scurrying away form the intruder to their world. First he looked down to the left and to the right. From his perch above, he could see almost the entire alley, but sentries and guardians could have been concealed or delayed in their deployment. He waited, expecting the shadows to reveal at least one person on the lookout for someone like himself. No one was there. He was alone in the alley, and he pulled his trench coat tightly around his body to fight off the chill running up through his body. The weather was cool, but he knew the chill came from somewhere else. As he proceeded down the alley, each step only intensified the bitter, ugly feeling clawing at his insides. He chalked it up to nerves and ignored it as he crossed beneath the sign through the door. Inside, the club had a red hue on everything it touched. The scratched up wooden bar and the painted red walls were only part of the tone, as the owner had used several red lights to keep the blood red hue on the patrons and furniture. Jake looked around, not recognizing any of the guards he had seen earlier. Here he saw the addicts, the fashionable, the dealers and the night animals all mixing together in a relaxed atmosphere. The music in the background was down low, allowing conversation between the guests. The dance floor had some patrons tuned into their own chemical additives and the music to create a strangely soothing back and forth sway on the dance floor. Jake pulled himself away, and he had the creeping feeling moving up his spine. He had no tingling here, there were no alarms going off warning him of danger. At the corner of the bar, facing him, he saw Malum sipping at a green drink in a martini glass. He finished the beverage and motioned to the bartender before fishing out the cherry inside with his tongue. Jake couldn’t stop his own horrible imagination from seeing what the bastard could have done to Bella and Alexandria with that tongue. He had to force his fist to uncurl before walking over to the bar. The bartender set two more martinis in front of Malum as Jake approached. He lifted one and held it up to Jake, “I’m so glad you could join me Jake. Please, have a drink with me.” Jake stopped his footsteps. He looked around, expecting men to be moving toward him, but they weren’t. He had nothing but space all around him. “Are you confused Jake?” Malum set the drink down on the perpendicular side of the bar. Malum seemed to be enjoying himself, “Do you ever play chess Jake?” Hix took a seat on the barstool. He pushed the martini glass back toward the man every fiber in his being wanted to strangle, “Not in a long while.” “I was never a great player, but Sid is. He watched me when I played chess with him. He once told me something that helped me. He told me to quit watching the few moves he made and watch the entire game laid out before me.” Malum lifted his drink and took a sip. “They make an incredible apple martini. I know it makes me look a little effeminate, but I have nothing to worry about. You see Jake, while you’ve been watching my few moves here and there, we’ve been watching the entire game. I mean sure, you do something simple like not accepting anything your enemy offers, but just because you had a fortune cookie it doesn’t make you a leader.” “I don’t think I need to hear about strategy and philosophy from you.” Jake leaned in toward him. “You know what I want.” “Of course I do.” Malum smiled as he took another sip. “You want your young ladies back, but we aren’t ready to make the trade. We are…verifying a few things. We will be in touch soon enough. That is, we’ll be in touch with Xavier when the time comes.” Jake reached over grabbing Malum’s wrist. He squeezed down hard, knowing he could break the thin wrist easily enough if he needed to, “You and Sid will be dealing with me. I want them back, and tonight you are going to tell me where they are.” “Subtlety is not your strong suit.” Malum endured the pain racing through his arm, “I will let this affront pass without punishment, but anything else you do to me will be visited upon your women ten times over. The men Sid has employed know darkness that you could never imagine. I know Xavier could imagine it though, since he has some of Sid’s memories in his conscious mind. Tell me, how is he handling this?” Jake let go of his arm. He realized the cards were not his to play. “That’s better.” Malum finished his martini then took the one offered to Jake. “Now, think about where we are. Sid and I wanted you to know a few things. We knew you had made contact with your friends in the ministry, and I know you sent them my likeness. We were surprised it took that moronic priest two weeks to notice me going into this club. You would of course stalk me here to try and force the information out of me.” Malum let his head fall back with a deep laugh, “So wonderfully predictable in your moral violence. You would do things to me that would make His Son turn away in shame, but he would allow them. No, today there is nothing for you to do. We want you to know that we are watching.” “You’re watching me, huh?” Jake leaned in. “In the end, I’m sending Sid to a grave he’ll never escape from. He’s going down permanently, and once he’s taken care of I’m going to turn my attention to you and all your friends.” Malum reached into his back pocket. He pulled out his video iPod and began finding what he was looking for in his files. He showed Jake pictures on the 2.5-inch color display. Jake watched as pictures slipped by every two seconds. The pictures were of his extended family, including all his cousins, uncles, aunts and even the family of his friends who had been helping him. Jake stared at the screen for a full minute, and the pictures kept playing. “We have our attention turned to you and your family Jake. We have a close watch on you because we want you to remember that for all your violence, for all your bluster and strength you have nothing to hold over us.” Malum put the iPod back into his pocket. He now leaned forward to Jake, “You and Sid will meet, but until then you will leave us alone or he will expand his interest. No one can stop what is fated to occur, but consider this the armistice between us. You will not attack me or my associates, and we will allow your family to safe conduct, unless they get in our way.” Jake realized his fists were squeezed into tight balls, his nails biting into the flesh of his hand. He opened them, feeling the sudden burn of the pain. “So Sid is leaving my family out of this?” Now the smile on Malum’s face was even wider. He loved the deliciousness of this moment. Jake was still so far in the dark he didn’t even realize what was happening. “Sid doesn’t know I’m here.” “What?” “Jake, at the end of this either you will kill Sid, or you won’t.” Malum played with the ring on his right hand, a hidden signal to those Jake couldn’t see. “If you fail, I will reign on high with Sid, but what if you succeed? Even with Sid removed there will still be machinations for us on this earth. Even if we lose him, we will not lose all of our power here.” “So the deal I offer is simply this, you will not move against me or any of my lieutenants either now or later. If we find each other in pursuit of our service or your opposition to Sid, so be it. That is the game we all play, but our armistice is to save our loved ones and ourselves outside of that struggle. You and our family will leave us alone, and we will leave your extended family alone.” Two of the bodyguards appeared. One held open the trench coat for Malum, and the other laid down a fifty to cover the drinks. Malum slipped his arms into his coat. He came around the bar, leaning down right next to Jake’s ear. He wanted this to be private. “Of all the weaknesses you have, your love for others is the most easily exploitable. I have reduce you from being a monster…to being a whimpering puppy…with nothing but words and pictures.” Malum walked away from the bar, his bodyguards quickly behind. Sitting on his barstool, the monster was hunched over. Jake was faced with the ugly reality of the truth Malum had seen so easily. He could be paralyzed by the threat to his loved ones, and there was nothing left for him to do but acquiesce to the demands of the man he hated so much. Jake looked at himself in the mirror behind the bar. I am the instrument... But I am not the hand anymore...
