Cut to the Bone

18 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-18 Do you know why I chose you, Jin? My endearing personality, maybe? No. The fact I’m not Jewish? Of course not. Because I can sweet talk the socks off an Eskimo in a blizzard? Getting warmer. Not the Eskimo, I hope. I took his socks. Pay attention, Jin. Do not be ungrateful. My gift to you is worth more than you will ever know. You wouldn’t mind taking it back to exchange it for something a little less eternally damning, then? No. Why? Because you, like me, are a jinn, Jin. Of course. And me without my tonic. < - > < - > < - > I remember a time when a room filled with conversation would silence upon my arrival. Round tables full of round faces, each one dressed in expensive suits and self-congratulatory smiles, turning to me. Pride filled my heart then, in the good days. Now I have no idea what keeps my heart beating. God knows I’ve tried to put a stop to it. God knows, and maybe he doesn’t care. He doesn’t talk to me much anymore. Somebody else has taken up that role. He calls me Iblis, this voice in my head. He tells me I am more than myself, that I am the temptation of man. I remember the day my parents put me on a boat bound for America. When Saudi Arabia adopted Basic Law I was ten years old, wizened by war far beyond my years, and absolutely terrified of leaving home. A pocket full of currency and a head full of freedom are no substitute for good parenting, popular culture be damned. My parents believed America would end their war for oil and raise me as a good, upstanding Saudi American. All things considered, one out of two ain’t bad. I found sanctuary at a school for Islamic youths, studied the Qur’an, and grew up. What more is there to tell? You wouldn’t understand the details. You don’t want to hear about the mosque burnings, or the cathedral bonfires we lit in retaliation. You don’t need to be filled with more contempt for a race already condemned to die by the leaders of the free world. I remember all of these things because I felt the heat of my mosque burning on my face. You remember September 11th because it was on CNN. This isn’t a debate worth getting into. I’ve seen those memories, too. The ones where men and women chose to jump to their deaths because it was better than being roasted alive a hundred stories above the pavement. I remember the deaths of more men than you’ve ever met, simply because I’ve experienced them first-hand. I live them in my sleep because He wants me to remember. Do you know what the final jumper was thinking before she leaped out of the World Trade Center? Absolutely nothing. She only fell because a flaming piece of fuselage exploded near her head, throwing her over the edge. It felt like your precious Fourth of July had been celebrated on the back of my skull. Pardon me, her skull. I once tried to jump off the roof of my firm, just to see if the wind felt the same coming off a fifty-story plunge as it did an eighty-story swan dive. All I walked away from were four feet of cracked cement and a splitting headache. The breeze was breathtaking. Last night it was something new, something completely unexpected yet somehow sadistically relevant all at the same time. I dreamt of a man named Curtis Henning, a man who had apparently gained some respect on the professional wrestling circuit. If I had to define the one worthwhile benefit to experiencing death over and over again, it would be this: I learn things I never thought I would need to know. For example, I now know the difference between an aspirin and a hormone supplement the size of a horse tranquilizer. When my (excuse me again, when Mr. Perfect’s) left arm went numb and the heart attack was imminent, guess who grabbed the wrong bottle? I don’t want to remember anymore. I didn’t ask for this. Suicide isn’t the answer; of that much I’ve been certain since my first ill-conceived plan to shove my head in an oven. It was time for a different approach. "You can blow it inside me for an extra twenty," the hooker had said. She was a keeper, all right. "But I don’t do anal. Hey, you got any coke?" I told her I was a lawyer. What the fuck did she think? An emaciated hand wound down her cheap pink blouse, accentuating breasts that were already spilling out the top. "I accept multiple kinds of payment, mister. I’m easier than an ATM." Her smile was weary, but satisfying. The negotiations were over and that horrid pink blouse was on the ground before she had finished turning the key to her apartment. It was easily the most disgusting cesspool of paper and pornography I had ever seen; in short, she was the perfect candidate. I stepped on a used needle trying to carry her to bed, but let’s face it, do you really think I gave a shit? She cried during sex. Don’t ask me why; I sure didn’t. I already knew the woman was half a step from the edge, waiting for her white knight to swoop in and rescue her from the fall, desperate to be held, for once to be touched without being penetrated. When it was all over I did just that; we huddled together, warm and sticky and exhausted, pressed together in licentious harmony underneath sheets stained with our scent. I initiated the proposal when I told her about my childhood. I told her about the mosques, about the beatings, the racist propaganda, about the week I spent hiding in my basement after 9/11, about my home in Saudi Arabia and its monarchial bullshit, about all of it. I’d be lying if I said I didn’t cry just a little, too. She had her own stories, of course. Abusive father, alcoholic mother, reckless sex with boys (and some girls) before dropping out of high school; all the necessary ingredients to make one hell of a prostitute pie, served up piping hot and ready to be devoured right here on her musty bed sheets. It was only a matter of time before she joined me on the floor, arms outstretched, razor held in one shaking palm. We sat facing one another, cross-legged, holding the other’s left wrist right our right. It was such an easy thing. Why hadn’t I thought of this before? "Don’t be afraid," I told her, but at this point I knew it wasn’t me. It was Him. "You are not taking your own life, but instead taking mine. In return, I take yours. May Allah welcome our arrival to Jannah with arms outstretched." She nodded at me apathetically, her expression already dead. The mile of cocaine she inhaled across her greasy kitchen counter might have had something to do with it, but I just didn’t care anymore. Her right hand squeezed my left wrist, balancing the razor blade between thumb and index finger like a guitarist ready to shred her last riff, as it were. Because it was. She looked with wonder at the pink scars already emblazoned across my forearms, climbing out of her drug-induced fog and finally displaying some semblance of personality again. "You’ve done this before?" She tried to smile. It looked horrid. I nodded. It was all she needed. The hooker clenched her teeth and pressed as hard as she could as she drew the blade lengthwise across my forearm and wrist. I followed suit, cutting her slender skin with certainty, praying to a God that doesn’t listen for the chance that this time, this time, it would work. Her bleeding began immediately. I never bled at all. "What… what is this?" she asked. I held on to her gushing wrist as she gave a half-hearted attempt to jerk it away. "I cut you to the bone, Jin! Why aren’t you bleeding? Why?" She was growing frantic, but was still too stoned to give it much emphasis. I had no answer for her. She died bleeding in my arms, sobbing to take it back, begging to be made whole again. I wanted to die with her, to be taken by her, to end this hellish nightmare and be doomed to an oblivion where at the very least it’s somewhat accepted to be possessed by Iblis, perhaps even respected. I’m just not sure anymore. I’m sure you’re waiting for me to explain that this encounter was nothing more than another memory of a dead man; another dream sent from Him, testing me, molding me, tempting me. But it wasn’t. How I wish it was.
