The end of lurking

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-15 Sergeant Michael Waters of the New York Police Department glanced out of the driver’s side window of his cruiser with a nondescript expression on his face. What he saw was essentially the same thing that he saw every night at this time while patrolling this sometimes unruly - and what appeared to be inhospitable - neighborhood. A lot of nothing worthy of attention, and the standard hobo standing outside of the local twenty four hour convenience store, opening and closing it for the customers while politely asking for spare change. It was a strange anomaly; in the nice areas of town there seemed to be more incidents where a vagabond decided that he or she could be a bit more bold in their actions - hell, they had nothing to lose anyway, right? And the nicer, tame members of society were more susceptible to fear and panic from the attendance alone of one of these sorts. Those places recorded more trouble. A place like this? Nah. Here the homeless knew that despite whatever illness or filthy disease they may be carrying, no matter how scary they looked to the average Joe, they were just as prone to being stabbed or shot by one of the local toughies as anybody else. This place had a bad reputation, and that rep almost lingered in the air causing everybody who breathed it to be just a bit more cautious in where they walked alone, or what they said aloud... and especially when they took out anything of value that could be seen by anybody. That general fear was a good thing for Sergeant Waters, believe it or not. Nobody got out of line, and if the petty swindlers and thieves were afraid of either the police or some mystical belief that this was a bad neighborhood just because it was, and that was that, he didn’t care all too much either way. The place looked like hell, he thought, but at least the crime rate had dropped. His superiors were content enough with that. “So Sarge, should we go tell that guy to move along?” The naive youngster sitting next to Waters was a rookie by the name of Marshall Payne. This was his first time on the beat, this beat or any other. He was fresh out of the academy and eager to do something other than stare at people and things from his passenger side window, and this disheveled guy opening the door was lingering on private property, and that was not allowed dammit. Waters only chuckled as he kept driving at his slow patrolling speed. “No my lad, we’ll let the poor sod be as he is. He is starting no problems, and the store keep hasn’t made a complaint.” He could feel that his young apprentice wanted to say something more, maybe argue the point and quote some statement made at the academy, but he dropped it like a good rookie should. Waters smiled and gave the youngster a piece of sage-like advice that he might one day consider. “Payne, if we cracked down on every single person that broke the smallest of laws all the time, then we would be loaded with paperwork and our jails would be overrun. I know that sounds lazy of me to say, but I would rather be out here serving as a deterrent for serious crime instead of worrying about a poor bum who would just walk around the block and return to that spot anyway when the coast was clear. The world is not a perfect place, and we are not here to make it one. We just try to have it not be so bad.” That lecture ended and Marshall Payne nodded his head in compliance. His delusions about changing the ‘hood and making a difference once he was in the dark blue policeman’s uniform were going to be subdued and it was obvious to him that the status quo suited his superior just fine. Little did he know that Waters had graduated from the same academy as he did twenty three years prior, and had the same dreams and aspirations. “You’re right Sarge. The bum’s not doing any real harm” and he really wasn’t, the youth rationalized. Little did this officer know that he would get his excitement and chance to make a difference soon enough, with another who lurked in this neighborhood after hours. ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ... He awoke suddenly, and the first thing, the only thing to be felt was an icy chill. It was more than that though, for it was freezing cold in an arena that had only moments prior been of a cozy temperature - even warmer for him considering the match he had just been a part of which had his heart pumping blood faster because of the physical activity. Understanding the sudden cold was impossible, but a hint came to him as he lifted his head to take stock of his surroundings, but was tragically unable to do so. He was helplessly immobile. All at once the fear came to him. Paralysis? Death? He didn’t know nor could he even move his body to discover the truth, and that scared him. Shock took him then. He started gasping for air as though he was drained of oxygen, resulting in a serious case of dehydration. His eyes bugged out of his head but he could only see bright distorted lights dancing above him mockingly. He had to take control of this situation. He knew at least that much, because he had been here before. He remembered now the car accident from ten years ago. He recalled it as though it were a few hours prior and it was chillingly similar to what he was experiencing at that point. He wasn’t dead yet, he couldn’t be, and as that realization calmed him, he slowly began to feel more and more, and what was felt terrified him for only the second time in a brutal life span. He felt pain, numbingly beautiful pain. He was remarkably helpless still as his senses returned to him and his eyes could make out figures who surrounded and administered the proper medical aid. It was all coming back to him now; the XUW World Title match against DarkSeid and Fatbadger; the interference by Blake Wolfson concluding in him being set up for a chokeslam, which he didn’t entirely understand since Blake was an ally... or at least he thought so; the resultant chokeslam by DarkSeid enabling him to get pinned for the third or fourth time ever in his career - but a simple chokeslam could not have taken him to this current state of medical emergency, could it? No, there was something else... something more sinister, more deliberate, an intentional move aimed directly at taking him out for good. But what was it? And who was the force responsible for this action? Everything after that point was a blank in his immediate memory and he could not recall. But then he saw it through his still blurred vision. He could sense that he was being lifted off of the ground, pavement, yes it was pavement although it felt a tad bit softer around his head. Oh yes, he could see why now. There was a pillow of blood there where his head had been. He wondered how much blood he had lost but that thought faded as the figures placed him on another semi hard surface, a stretcher he thought to himself. With his head positioned and strapped down, out of the corner of his eye he could see another figure from afar, and that figure was laughing maniacally. He recognized that man, but not the look in the man’s eyes. He had never seen that look in this particular man’s eyes before, which was odd - come to think of it - considering that man was part of a group full of men who had that look in their eyes at one time or another. The eyes were mad, animalistic, much like a tiger’s eyes would be directly after killing his prey - the hormones still rushing through his senses, still hyper, overly tense, and alert. That was the look that he saw in the man’s, Blake Wolfson’s, his attacker’s, eyes. The Shadow shed a tear as he was rolled out of the arena in a stretcher, and that was the final time he had been seen by anybody in Xtreme Ultimo Wresting, the victim of betrayal yet again. Roughly six and a half months have passed since that fateful night in Boston, Massachusetts, the site of XUW’s Reborn Pay Per View. Several opportunities have arose since then for The Shadow to return and have his vengeance, but he has yet to do so. He has simply vanished from all sight, and as the days continued to pass the memory of what at one time had been the most powerful collection of raw talent and viciousness in XUW, the Ultra Violents, a stable that he had embodied as his own life philosophy, became nothing but a nasty memory of the failed experiment that it was. And as the legacy of the Ultra Violents faded into near obscurity, replaced by new threats and new enemies of XUW, memories of The Shadow and what he had at one time accomplished also faded. For the first few months, any time that an unknown person was about to be revealed, his name was suspected amongst others, but eventually his name simply stopped being mentioned on XUW TV. But The Shadow was not gone, not entirely. “I never suspected that I would be standing here, once again” the all too familiar voice said quietly to himself. The voice itself was an obvious alteration of anybody’s normal voice, caused by simply constraining the vocal chords and forcing air through them at the same time. The end resulting voice was similar to that of a man who had throat cancer or heavy congestion, but this man did not have any such thing. Sometimes when angry the voice was more of a high pitched squeal, and at other times it was low and threatening by nature. It was low as he stood on the street corner, facing the other side of the street. He was wearing an old pair of ripped up blue jeans and a dark green sweater with a hood that he had covering his head and most of his darkened face. “Six months ago, and that last night was too much, just too much...” His voice trailed off into an inaudible whisper. The streetlight above him projected his defiled and blackened silhouette into the street in front of him, darkness that pointed in the direction that his eyes were looking towards, and he had a brief laugh at that. He was being led here by his own darker side for the first time since April of this year, and he was still conflicted about just that. Did he really want to go back to this, this life that hurt him so much in the past, that haunted him for years and eventually ended with him helpless and afraid as EMTs attempted to revive him? He shook his head and leaned back against the light post. “But I miss it so much... I desire it... I need it. I don’t know how I lasted for so long without the craving” He mused to himself. “It has always been there, lurking, but now I can’t seem to control it any longer. I need a release!” His voice was intensified and his eyes closed. It took a deep breath and clenched fists to calm the nerves and once that happened, his eyes opened again to gaze across the street. It was time to make a decision. Go forward and reopen old damaged doors, or turn away back into the unknown - fade back into the obscurity where he had been content to lurk for the previous six months. He took two steps forward in the direction of his destination, paused, and then one step backward in a struggle with indecisiveness. They had moved onto the more boring part of their patrol route where no foul activity ever transpired. There were no stores to rob or apartment buildings to observe with a keen eye - looking for potential trouble. Here there were only a few scattered businesses that have long since closed down for the evening, and some industrial buildings. So it came as a surprise to Sergeant Waters that a hooded man would be standing at the street corner about a block ahead of him, lingering. He didn’t react or say anything of the matter though. He wanted the young rookie sitting next to him in the police cruiser to take notice first. This could be good for practical training. Waters slowed down to a crawl and approached with the proper caution. That got Marshall Payne’s attention. What got the officer’s attention even more came seconds later when the man took a few steps forward and then stopped suddenly and backed up. “Sarge, did you just see that?” “I sure did lad. Suspicious behavior, don’t you think?” “Without a doubt. Do you think he saw us and stopped doing what he was about to do?” “Maybe, maybe not. But you’re thinking like a cop and suspecting the worst. Very good.” Waters grinned sheepishly and Payne nodded in acknowledgment of the compliment. “So what do we do now?” “We’ll pass him once, slowly, and then circle around and allow him to see us again. That way he’ll know that we’re watching him so that he won’t try anything foolish, not on our watch at least.” Waters was sure that his young apprentice would have much rather hid in a dark corner and spied on the mark, hoping to catch him in the act of doing something foolish so he could get his first bust, but that was detective work, not work for uniformed officers driving a patrol. Poor kid, he thought as he chuckled. Payne glanced over curiously at his elder partner with a raised eyebrow, but then turned his attention to the man lurking at the corner, hoping for him to do something stupid. The police cruiser passed and he barely took notice. The inner struggle he was putting himself through had his full attention, and nothing else was of importance, even being watched closely by two cops driving by at no more than fifteen miles per hour. He took two more steps forward and was now off of the curb and in the street, his destination - the knowledge that he was almost to the point of reliving the sudden and overbearing rushes of excitement and nirvana that he used to experience - was that much closer to him. His main vice in life, his addiction to violence that at one time consumed his every motivation in existing, lay directly ahead of him. Another step was taken. He had held it off for so long, but he had gone too far now and couldn’t turn away, not with his destination in sight. His body tensed and he picked up speed while walking across the deserted street. “Why couldn’t I be stronger? Why?” He asked himself that question repeatedly as he stepped onto familiar ground on the other side of the street and approached a door. “But I am stronger now. I’m not doing this in XUW like I’ve wanted to. I’m not getting revenge as I’ve dreamed of doing. No, I’m just doing this to people whom I have never met. It’s better this way, right? I don’t know them. There’s nothing personal about it. No, no it’s still giving in! Why?” His voice was building up in anger, self loathing for what he was about to do once again. The door was directly in front of him now, an old wooden door that had a few small cracks in it from years of weather abuse. That made it vulnerable and quite easy to collapse as he angrily stormed through it and disappeared into the darkness inside. “Sarge, he’s gone. Wait, wait a minute! Do you see that?” Officer Payne pointed frantically to the broken door across the street that was dangling just barely from the hinges. That’s when the red and blue lights placed above the cruiser lit up. “I sure as hell do.” Waters lifted up car radio. “Unit 255 to base. Unit 255 to base. We have a suspected break-in at......” He looked out of the window nearest to the desk where he was standing over. “I really don’t need this...” The police lights had gotten his attention and upon seeing them he darted off back into the darkness and toward the back of the building, away from the police cruiser. What followed was standard police procedure. They parked near the broken door and exited their car, drawing their weapons promptly. Knowing that this break-in occurred in the past two minutes and thirty five seconds, it was entirely possible that the suspect was still inside the building, and that meant they had an opportunity to apprehend him in the act. The impulsive and inexperienced Payne moved in first, haphazardly kicking the door completely off the hinges and storming in like a cowboy, gun flaring. Waters was quick to follow, but like a smart cop he was more hesitant and aware of his surroundings, and had a flashlight turned on. That little bit of light just happened to catch an object near the back of this building dart to a rear exit. “Freeze! This is the police!” Payne’s yelled order and statement did not stop the man from fleeing. Payne ran after him as Waters tried to find a light switch. “Ah, there we are.” He flipped on the lights, revealing a site that XUW fans have seen in the past. The most noticeable aspect of this place was the insane amount of dust and cobwebs that have accumulated. Obviously nobody has taken as much as a step in here in a long time, Waters thought, well... not until tonight. “Well what do we have here?” He looked down at the ground and as clear as day he could see them, footprints in the dust. After first studying his surroundings he wondered just what there was of value to rob, and now he just might find the answer to that question as he started following the minute old footprints of the man that his partner was now chasing through the back alley. They led him into a side room and to a blotch on the floor. “Something had been removed from that spot, a shirt or a pair of pants maybe” the Sergeant thought to himself as he continued to follow the path which ended at a desk near a flashing window. “Well, it looks like we telegraphed our arrival a bit too much. Damn.” Moments later some footprints were heard and Officer Payne walked into the room. “He got away Sarge. I tripped and fell in the dark, giving him plenty of time to escape. Shit! I knew he looked suspicious!” Payne, upon not hearing a reply from his superior, walked up to him and looked over his shoulder curiously. “What is it Sarge?” “Nothing really, but I think we have a fingerprint here of our friend. There it is, see? In the dust it’s as clear as day.” Waters grinned. “And he obviously stole something too, because there is a line here on this desk about two feet long and an inch or so wide where there is no dust, meaning that there was something sitting there that was taken. We officially have an important case here, my lad. Don’t worry about falling. We’ll get to the bottom of this.” As the two policemen were preparing to make their case, another was preparing to delve back into a world in which he had loved and loathed at the same time for many years. He dusted off the black object in front of him and placed it over his head. It was a black mask, and old bloodstains were still visible on it from a destructive career. He then reached forward and lifted up the other object that had been removed from the building, a toy that he had quite the affinity for, whose uses were obvious. “I’m sorry, I’m sorry.” His hands shook as adrenaline flooded his system. He nearly dropped the old and stained lead pipe - the symbol of his craved violence - that he was holding. He was excited, angry, and extremely disappointed at himself all at the same time. Regret was in his distorted voice as he kept apologizing to himself for what he had just done and what was to follow, for reviving the violent cravings that had been lurking in peaceful hibernation for these many months, like a former smoker would be angry at himself for lighting one up after such a long period of abstinence - but he would also be happy, thrilled, and dizzy. This hooded - and now masked - man could not wait much longer for the world to once again spin around him, to get lost in the moment of a glorious violent act. “I’m sorry, I’m sorry....” He took solace in the knowledge that he would only indulge himself a few times - he promised himself - and that was all, only during his participation in Primetime Central’s fourth Golden Turnbuckle Tournament, perhaps only against Token Weed if the man were capable enough to withstand what so many others weren't. “Sarge, headquarters called. They just confirmed the information that they previously gave us, despite how odd it seems.” “Really?” “Yep. This building is owned and registered by a fellow who goes by the unusual name, The Shadow.” Months of lurking had ended. Ultra Violence was ready to resume.
