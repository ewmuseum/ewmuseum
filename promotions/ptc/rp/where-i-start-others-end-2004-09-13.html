Where I start, others end

13 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-13 Sympathy - A feeling or an expression of pity or sorrow for the distress of another; compassion or commiseration. - A relationship or an affinity between people or things in which whatever affects one correspondingly affects the other. Of all the people in the world, and in all of the many states human beings can be in, facing a man so obsessed, so angry and focused is both idiotic and futile. Joshua: “That’s the world’s definition of something I see as a weakness, Sympathy I have no sympathy for the likes of Façade. This man, this wrestler whose lack of presence of loyalty has him without a company to call his home. Am I meant to fear a man who’s so inept he cannot even find a company to work in? Is this his own doing, or is it merely down to his lack of skill. Men in this world, men like me, a warrior who seeks battle, seeks wars will always have a company to call home. Does this Façade have those same qualities? Does he have the mentality to walk into a competition, a competition in which you know none of your opponents? It takes a special type of person to succeed within this tournament and really, do you Façade have such qualities?” Joshua stood on his floor balcony the sun on his face and a chilled martini in his hand. The world beneath him as he watched the living sob stories walking around from sadness to disappointment. There every ending dream to be someone, have there 15 minutes of misguided fame front and centre in there minds. Joshua knew he only need ask any random one and he could hear how the world screwed each of them over in some small way. Countless high school and college players holding onto the false believe the reason they never made pro was a knee injure that was fixed years before. Beauty contest winners, hope, begging and screwing there way to one more addiction, one more casting party looking for there big break. Joshua: “Sad how everyone has a reason, a story that explains why they don’t have the million dollar deal. Why they aren’t making movies, or making hit records. There’s always a reason, always looking for that sympathy to make them feel better. A need be pitied, why do people have that within them, they purposely hold onto a weakness. People use to ask me for sympathy, some mistakenly still do. In Prime there's an endless line of people who feel in someway there defeats to me require a reason other than me being better then them. People in wrestling never stand up and just say, yes you were better, you beat me because I was not good enough. I personally have never done such a thing because I have never been beaten by an opponent who deserved such a thing. I have also never found the need to explain myself to the men I defeat, countless of them sort some reason, an explanation to why they could not beat me. They couldn’t just deal with the fact I was better, they sort a reason a need to feel better about themselves because their pride could not accept the truth. The sympathy they required was to force the truth away and allow there false sense of pride to remain.” Joshua slipped from his chilled glass, the martini tasted good as the sun fought to melt the ice. As Joshua watched the world happy in his every growing ego, the name of his opponent had him wondering. Would not knowing anything other than a name really not make a difference? Joshua began to think, he didn’t enough know if the man was tall or short, fat or thin, skilled or just out to fight. “How do I prepare then” he told himself, this was after all a stage the world over was watching. Prime was a huge company but this was a competition between all comers. Companies from all over the world, wrestlers from the darkest corners of the world would be taking part in this competition and Joshua knew only one other person. Joshua’s mind started to work through many different scenarios, he knew he was good, knew he could handle anyone given time to prepare, but knowing nothing about your opponent other then a name, that was a problem but one Joshua had already told himself he was able to overcome. A woman down on the street stopped below Joshua’s balcony, she was 30 something but likely said she was 27 to make herself feel better. Her little lie was some 22 year old guy’s nightmare once she came clean. She looked up at Joshua; he saw her looking up waiting for some kind of opening but Joshua simply leaned back taking her from his view. Why should he concern himself with some women who was either looking for help or looking for sex. Any women who would sleep with a total stranger within minutes of meeting them was a women Joshua wanted no part of, and anyone seeking help who Joshua never knew, would find themselves wanting. Joshua feelings steamed right back to his training, his fathers words about placing himself in certain position rained down on him repeatedly. His teachers were based around self benefit and glory for oneself. “Only help people when it benefits you, if there's nothing coming back to you then walk on by. People who need help and cannot provide anything in return are weak and you should never allow yourself to be used by them. You’re too good to be wade down by a lower class member of the wrestling community.” Joshua had taken those words and done just that, his father maybe a person he no longer talked to despite his closeness, but his teachers and lessons had made Joshua the most technical sound wrestler Prime had ever seen. Joshua could remember the preparing his father had him going through, getting ready for the Japanese circuit was the goal. Then the move to America happen and Joshua heard the words from his father that sowed the seeds for there rift. “You’re done wrestling, we cannot return to Japan and you cannot work in America.” That had been the start to a now bitter and almost none existent relationship, but Joshua got no, and ask for no sympathy when it happened. He didn’t cry to others about his problems, his pain. Joshua remembered his actions, standing up for himself and stepping from out of his family’s shadow. It was this attitude that had lead me out of the lower ranks and on the cusp of greatness. Joshua: “Why would anyone believe they could deny me? They should be thankful I have yet made someone an example, unless you count Zyfloi, he will be put into a league of his own at Primes King of Kings PPV. Façade however, you my son will be measured, and left wanting on the mat wondering why god hates you so much. The look is something I can’t really explain, you eyes will seem distance yet aware. You Façade will understand yet disbelieve the result. It’s the same for 1000’s of others in 1000s of contest the world over. You tell yourself, this one is easy and then along comes me to rip your dreams from you and leave you wanting. I suppose at a time like that it’s your friends you will turn to, you will seek there reassurance you’re still the man, still a great wrestler. You will want there Sympathy! And that makes you pathetic and weak. If you cannot make it on your own, then you should not be allowed to make it at all. Façade you are merely my first round opponent, were as I am you last opponent. This truth comes from my skills, my drive, my passion and my understanding with my talent. I see myself moving beyond you, beyond the next man and further and further into the tournament. People like you however, you plan for both victory and defeat and that’s the only slip I need to destroy you. I’ll see you in round one, then you can see me again in round two, and three and so on. I’m sure even you can afford a Tv to watch me after I beat you. Round one Façade, round one.”
