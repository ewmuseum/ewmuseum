Christopher Camero Cable Pimp Joined: 18 Feb 2003 Posts: 58 Posted: Wed Apr 09, 2003 4:16 pm Post subject: Global Wrestling Title Match RP#1 I had spent the night alone for the first time in quite some while. As it seems the events of April 4th, 2002 have left me dumb-struck. Salaok, Bubba McCoy, Silent Wind; they all lingered amidst my memory, all holding their own personal gratification in a dreamland not meant for them. It was on April 4th that my phone rang, paving the way for my return to Global Wrestling, a return made possible by the new Co-Owner Salaok. --- This morn, when I drew the dimity curtains, I saw a dull grey sky with the sun steeped in wet cloud. By noon the sky was opaque, the sun a green sheen struggling to break through. Amongst all it’s glory, the powerful sun, reminded me of my soul; struggling once again to be free, to break through the clouded shrewd of diplomatic lies. It was then that I realized the cherubic sunset of today signaled the tenth anniversary of my first encounter with one, Dr. A.J. Hawthorne. When he and I met, my life was filled with troublesome quarries. My father had just passed, my mother a professional call girl, and my wrists the appointed summit of anger. It was April 5th, 1993, when Dr. Hawthorne found me nestled deep within the underbelly of civilization, my tender hands wielding the sharp curvature of death. I was a young boy lost within static of a culturally depleted world. He reached out to me, made me his own, made me a part of his family. As I stood, semi-clad peering out the window onto the hushed reverberation of the evening glow, I realized I was late. The sun was setting, its prisms of light lessening as a darkness tolled across the horizon. I scratched my head in a perplexed manner, had I been standing here that long? Prior estimation had put the actual time somewhere near four o’clock, yet the blinking alarm clock next to my dresser draw read five. I would be hard pressed to make it to the Café by six, but as is the way in today’s society I fumbled through a cold shower, tossed on a pair of uneven pants, and buttoned up my shirt as I tore through the exit into the night air. I was well behind schedule, thus my elongated strides hastened as I mingled in and out of the bustling streets. It seemed peculiar in nature for this many patrons to be muddling about on a Sunday evening. Despite how their presence intrigued me; they were no concern of mine. I kept my head held high, shifting my eyes to and fro across the populous street watching as the sun set lower into the sky, while my trained feet scampered along the weathered concrete of the passageway. “Fire!” Someone shouted, and the on-coming pedestrians stopped where they tread; the store keepers popped into there doorways; the street grew black with people. For a minute I thought I saw tongues of flame rising high over the south end of Madison Avenue. Then I realized it was the sun. A red wash of colour spread itself over the buildings and streets, the people’s faces. My hand glowed red as I shielded my eyes. Then the bloody red sheet was lifted as the sun set for a second time. It grew very dark with great suddenness. The stars came out in the black sky. I had just bore witness to a celestial phenomenon. There was no fire, nor had there ever been. Yet, the on lookers gazed on in disbelief as the once burning sky, lay blanketed in darkness. I didn’t have their luxury. I was still late and therefore shifted my weight from beneath me and continued on my way to the Café. ---- When I arrived at the Café, Dr. Hawthorne had long since retired. Left in his place laid a letter that read, Dearest Christopher, It appears that for one reason or another you were unable to attend our soiree this evening, however, it is with a light heart that I bid you join me at Union Corp., later this evening at quarter past the witching hour. At which time we will further discuss future employment endeavors. Yours truly, Dr. A.J. Hawthorne Upon completing the letter, I neatly folded it, before sliding into my shirt pocket. Believe me when I say that if you have never met Dr. Hawthorne, you might be compelled to consider him a bit of a loon as he typically over-acts his British accent, and routinely makes the largest of fusses over the pettiest of ordeals. Nevertheless, he has always managed to maintain my utmost respect. Furthermore, his actions shield his true feelings, feelings of burning hatred for those who he deems unjust. As I turned to leave it occurred to me what Dr. Hawthorne meant by business and to fully understand you’d have to bare witness to the story of my troubled childhood, a story I’m not fond of telling, so excuse me if I don’t. I was standing, readying myself to leave when it occurred to me; I had not managed to consume my supper. I glanced at my watch, four hours until I went calling on Hawthorne, might as well take the opportunity to scoff a meal. --- I sat at the Café until the wee hours of the night, just sitting killing time, while contemplating my future, the business at hand with Salaok, Silent Wind, Global Wrestling, and now Bubba McCoy. To them I was a drug addicted ex-superstar from a federation they never dreamed of, but inside I am so much more. The drug I take, Chrysalis, is manufactured by the Hawthorne Bio-Chemical, one of many branches of Dr. A.J. Hawthorne’s propaganda fronts designed to maintain minimum suspicion around the billions of dollars he has amassed over the past two decades. In actuality, Hawthorne's fortune can be credited to Union Corp., a company he owns and operates beneath the surface of public and government knowledge. As I stood up to leave, I noticed my head swaying side to side as I considered the numerous possibilities related to A.J.’s latest request. Begrudgingly I pushed the thought from my mind, dropped seven dollars and thirty-eight cents on the counter-top and disappeared from the Café. --- At this hour of night, the streets were quiet, only the hustlers and nightshift workers were left in the South End. So I paced my stride in an attempt to appear carefree to their watchful eyes. As I walked, they watched, and the streetlamps silhouetted my rugged figure against the brick back drop of passing stores. I knew what Hawthorne was going to ask, but no matter how I played out the situation in my mind, I never had a suitable answer. I was going to do it again, I was going to take the drug, and I was going to kill. As I toddled within my own existence, my footsteps traced an indirect path, challenging the night’s shadows as I made my way towards the lower South End of the city. Hawthorne was no dope, Union Crop., was a multi-billion dollar agency, manufacturing bio-chemical weapons and state of the art warfare- all without public or government intelligence. They maintained their enigmatic culture by concealing their operation within a run-down warehouse, or so it seemed. To the unknowing eye the warehouse appears to be a shed behind old man Montgomery’s broken down car factory on Industrial Blvd. It’s not surprising I suppose that the Lower South Side, with so much crime, does not draw the slightest of attention from local authorities to its real on-goings. The authorities see it for what it is, or should I say what it appears to be, an abandoned neighbourhood to far gone to serve any real use to the community. The Lower South Side does without a newspaper, without a bookseller’s shop, without concentrated wealth of any kind. Its only active business remains a dingy tavern three blocks down. Entering the warehouse, I felt part of the melodrama. The door creaked loudly; I adjusted my eyes to the dimness, which slowly gave form to the outline of a large crate. I moved forward pursing my lips together, while releasing my breath in a tranquil whisper across the crates idle exterior, casting the dust billowing through the room. With my right hand I further removed the dust, revealing a handle a top the wooden crate. Bracing my feet against the lower corners of the box, I heaved open the door, revealing a stairwell that led deep into the under ground labyrinth of A.J.
