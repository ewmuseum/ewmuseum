Date: 2005-12-18 File #046: The Eulogy for No One (Part I) by [name withheld] Karl suddenly became aware of himself walking down a scarcely crowded Atlantic City street without any recollection as to how he’d gotten there. The last thing he could remember was sailing away from some dreamland far away, the name of which he could no longer recall. He had talked to a man there, but he couldn’t remember his name and the man had told him something – something that seemed so vaguely important now that it had escaped his memory. The few people he found on the streets during this, what was a frightfully cold Sunday morning, looked at him funny – some with compassion and others with disgust. By the time Karl had reached the corner he realized that he wasn’t wearing any shoes. Or a shirt. "My head," he grumbled in a voice that seemed unfamiliar and he sat down on the curb clutching his temples with rough palms no doubt covered in dirt. "What’s… what the hell…" Across the street, a little girl strayed from her mother who was distracted with the purchase of a hot dog from the stand. She was sweet-faced wearing an adorable yellow dress, stockings and Irish shoes and when Karl laid his eyes upon her, in his daze he could have sworn that she was some kind of angel. He reached out for her and she trotted across the street looking concerned and felt as if he were drowning in the deepest ocean and he was reaching for some kind of lifesaver. But then the mother realized that her daughter was missing, raced across the street and dropped her hot dog and scooped her up in one swift motion before she could be hit by a car. The car that had nearly crushed her beeped its horn noisily and uncaringly and the mother shot the driver a disgusted look before carrying her daughter the rest of the way across the street putting her down beside Karl who suddenly realized that the notion of an angel coming to rescue him was absurd. "Angela!" the tearing mother, who shared her daughter’s beauty, yelled. "What’s the matter with you! You know you’re not old enough to cross the street – and you could have been hit by that moron – and since when do you leave my side without my telling you to?" She trailed off into a string of somber nonsense now sobbing uncontrollably. Every now and then Karl caught phrases like "almost lost you" and "stupid hot dog stand" and the little girl, upset by seeing her mother break down in public like this started to cry too causing Karl to feel very guilty. Apparently the mother felt just as guilty because she fell to her knees and embraced her daughter begging her not to cry. "I’m sorry mommy!" Angela cried. "But the man across the street looked really sad and he looked like he needed help!" "What? What man…?" choked the mother as she stood up again and looked around finally becoming aware of Karl. "Oh!" Karl stood up quickly ignoring the pain that shot through his head for doing so and bowed slightly in apology, "I’m sorry ma’am. I didn’t mean to cause any trouble." However, the mother didn’t look entirely upset and looked Karl over with sympathy, "Oh gosh, you poor thing… you shouldn’t be walking around in this weather without a shirt or shoes… and those pants are just filled with holes-" She broke off as she took a closer look at the faded pants that even Karl had forgotten he was sporting. They were camouflage. Furthermore, he was still wearing his dog tags. "You’re a veteran aren’t you?" she demanded silently and Karl couldn’t mouth wordlessly long enough before she continued, "I’m Emile. I’m with the Silent Gun Fund – it’s an organization for homeless war veterans. She gets it from me – Angela, I mean. She must have noticed your tags…" Karl thought the situation over for a brief moment – the near-impossibility of just happening to stumble across this woman and her daughter, and how correcting her assumption by telling her that he was actually AWOL from the current Iraqi war wouldn’t help him much. He caught his reflection in the windshield of a car parked in front of him and saw that he had grown a coarse mane of hair which undoubtedly made him look much older than twenty-two. He looked back to Emile. "Usually I have a van from work, but today’s Sunday," she explained hastily while Angela looked up at him with a smile. "If you don’t mind clearing some of the junk out of the back seat of my Toyota, I can get you to a YMCA or something…" "That’d be very kind of you," Karl said with a nod. "Ms…?" "Just call me Emile," she told him. "The closest YMCA is a couple blocks… unless there’s somewhere else you’d like me to take you…?" And with that, Karl’s face suddenly brightened and he smiled. Karl stepped out of the barber shop, his hair now stylishly short and combed back and his face now beardless and youthful again. Moreover he was considerably warm in the new coat that Emile had bought him along with the new slacks, shoes, and shirt that she had also procured for him. Everything was going correctly again – he was back home in Philadelphia, he was clean, and the war was thousands of miles away just as he and Michael had originally planned. The fact that Michael wasn’t home to share the euphoria one can only experience by being home only made Karl feel more inclined to smile and strut down Broad Street – enough for the both of them. But still, Karl could not recall precisely how Michael had died and that troubled him quite a bit. He recalled having some horrible dream about and island… but it seemed far too ridiculous to have been real. Michael must have died during the voyage across the Atlantic – could have fallen overboard or anything. All that mattered was that he wasn’t in Iraq anymore and though Karl would have been far more content with his friend walking down the street alongside him, truthfully, he was better off dead than in that Middle Eastern hellhole. In a way he had gotten home. But as he sat on a bench pleasantly awaiting the arrival of the uptown bus, he realized that something would not let him forget the dream. The island with the beautiful nude women and the strange language they spoke. The strange man that had helped him named Kasidy. The man in the white suit… it had all seemed so real. "You’re dead. Keep your mouth shut and forget everything that’s just happened." Karl shook his head trying to rid himself of these thoughts, but only manage to become aware of the bus that rolled up before him and opened its doors – though he hadn’t noticed it coming. The bus driver looked down at him impatiently, and he stood up and approached the door. Before he could climb inside, however, he noticed an advertisement plastered on the side of the vehicle that suddenly made him feel very cold inside. PTC Presents: -The Fifth Annual Global Title Tournament- "Don’t Believe the Hype." "What’s happening at the Global Title Tournament!? You’re going to kill them aren’t you? The participants? You’re going to kill them!" "Hey asshole," the bus driver called causing Karl to come back to reality once again. "Are you coming in or not? I ain’t got all day." "Sorry," Karl mumbled noticeably disturbed and he cast one last look at the Global Title Tournament advertisement before climbing aboard the bus which closed its doors and drifted away. * * * "Let’s try the Fisherman’s Buster again," Calvin commanded in a voice so easy-going and pleasant that it didn’t sound like a command at all. "It needs to be a bit tighter Kasidy. We don’t want this Zenith character wriggling free in mid-air. He could land on his feet behind you leaving you completely vulnerable. So… let’s try it again." Llevar groaned in protest, but carried his obsidian toned body to the center of the ring again, "Opmokek, can we not give someone else a turn? This would be the twentieth-straight Fisherman’s Buster I’ve taken today and I-" "No Llevar, we cannot ‘give someone else a turn,’" Calvin replied politely. "Kasidy specifically requested you as a sparring partner. You’re the second most fit soldier we have – being able to wear you down will be much better training than if we used someone else." Kasidy, dressed in simple wrestling gear and with his long silver hair tied back in a ponytail, came forward with a knowing-smile as the both of them knew that this was not the reason that Kasidy had chosen him to take twenty Fisherman’s Busters. In contrast to Llevar, who was dripping with sweat, Kasidy didn’t seem to be tired at all. "Yes Opmokek," Llevar grumbled grudgingly and he allowed himself to be struck with the move – first the headlock, then the hook of the leg, and then the lift into the air – which looked impossible because Llevar was so hug and Kasidy was seemingly anorexic – and finally Kasidy dropped him on his head again with a particular viciousness. "Better," Calvin confirmed with lazy applause. "But it could use a little bit more work. Take a break Kasidy… we’ll pick this up again in a few hours. You too Llevar." Llevar, who was groaning like some kind of wild animal, thrust his arm upward from his downed position expecting Kasidy to help him up, however Kasidy promptly exited the ring and he had to wait for Guile – a tall muscular Guardsmen with spiked blonde hair – to help him back to his feet. Llevar shoved Guile off of him once he was vertical once again and stared tat the back of Kasidy has he left the gymnasium with loathing. "If it weren’t for Opmokek…" he growled silently. However, Kasidy Drake – who could hear Llevar’s incomplete threat even at great distance, did not care about how his bloodthirsty underling wished nothing more than to kill him – nor had he forgotten how to properly execute a Fisherman’s Buster. None of it mattered in his mind. Not until Tuesday night. In the meanwhile, he was content with being dreamy, and he walked into the hall of the manor from the gym not even noticing the scrambling Sovereign Guardsmen who hailed him and tossed a towel around his neck – though he hadn’t broken a sweat. His long fingers curled around the polished wooden rail of the main spiral stairway and he sauntered up it to highest floor and to his room whereupon his entrance into its darkness, he closed its door behind him. As he always did, he looked into the full-length mirror upon the back of the door and though he had not turned on the light and the sun had nearly set, he could see himself just as clearly as ever and was vaguely disgusted by the emaciated reflection that looked back at him equally dismayed by what it saw. "You know, it’s not really training if you don’t at least try to improve," came a familiar and undoubtedly feminine voice from behind him. Kasidy closed his eyes briefly as if tired. He was hallucinating again. Rather than fight it, he deemed it more comforting to play along with his twisted and disillusioned mind, and he turned to see the smiling face of the beautiful red-headed woman that had once agreed to be his wife. Before he looked the way he did now. "Hello Cassidy," he whispered and he crossed his massive bedroom and walked out of the far doorway onto the balcony knowing that she would follow. "The sun’s almost gone. If you look closely enough you can almost see the stars…" He leaned against the stone barrier and a tear silently fell from his left eye as he felt – or thought he felt – Cassidy’s hand place its self over his and grip him firmly and lovingly. "I saw your match with Mookie," she said lightly and conversationally as she looked off into the distance as well. "There was a point when I thought he was going to beat you. And then I realized that’s not possible. Because of what you are and all… you weren’t really trying, were you?" "Of course I wasn’t," Kasidy laughed as the single tear fell from his chin and splattered atop Cassidy’s delicate and creamy hand. "Of course I wasn’t…" "Because you realize what it would mean if you won this tournament," Cassidy continued. "You know what Calvin has planned. And deep down, you don’t want it to happen. But then, all of a sudden, you crushed Mookie with ease and I realized… you hadn’t even broken a sweat." Kasidy shifted his weight uncomfortably and pulled his hand from underneath Cassidy’s and stood upright. Firm. "And there was the American soldier you let go," Cassidy continued not seeming to notice. "And the other one whose blood you refused to put on your hands. I know that in your heart you don’t want any part to do with Calvin Riley, or New Eden… and yet here you are in Riley’s mansion two days away from doing it again. Placing Calvin one step closer to that horrible goal of his. I don’t understand you Kasidy. You’re torn between two horrible roads. You need to choose." Kasidy laughed again and turned back to the interior of the mansion without actually walking into it. Cassidy straightened up, but did not turn back towards him so that they faced two completely different directions with an almost defiant nature. "I keep wondering why I keep seeing you Cassidy," Kasidy said loudly. "When I know that you’re hooked up to about fifty different forms of life support in the basement of this mansion. And it’s not just because I feel bad about what happened to you, or even because I love you… you represent something. That other road, I guess. You represent me getting married, and me being loved. You represent me being happy and nonviolent and… all the things that would ensure my humanity. On the other hand, Calvin wants me to do the opposite – destroy everyone, rise above emotion…" Cassidy looked over her shoulder at her love, but Kasidy did not return the favor. "It’s not about killing everyone or saving everyone," he went on. "It’s about one. You don’t destroy the world – you destroy one person. You don’t save the world – you save one person. Following Calvin’s plan would ultimately lead to both of our destructions – yours and mine. Refusing to do as he asks would, perhaps, make us both free and alive." "You’re already alive Kasidy," Cassidy shot back with a sudden anger. "No… I’m about as close to death as you are my love. And I realize now… that the most difficult decision that a man has to make, when all of the distractions are gone is between life and death. And once you’re there, the righteous path isn’t quite as obvious as it should be." Suddenly there was a knock at the door and Kasidy could hear Calvin’s voice call to him that it was suppertime. He turned to say good-bye to Cassidy, but when he did, she was gone. He really was loosing his mind, wasn’t he? * * * By the time Karl had gotten off the bus at Ninth and Wolf Streets, it was half raining and half hailing. He swore silently as he rounded the final corner on the way to his girlfriend’s house. He had chosen to come to her first because she was closest. Secretly, he wanted to make sure she hadn’t cheated on him during his time overseas. Even more secretly, he figured she’d be so happy to see him that they’d probably end up having sex. Nine-twenty-seven, twenty-nine, thirty-one, thirty-three, thirty-five, thirty seven. He suddenly became very afraid as he rung the doorbell. What if she really had cheated on him? It was a common occurrence – high school sweethearts stay with their men when they hear they’re joining the armed forces after graduation, and by the time they get back, they’ve already begun banging another man. Had she even loved him before he’d left? He couldn’t remember – so many things had gotten twisted and imagined while he was in Iraq. For all he knew, he could have simply imagined that this woman had been his girlfriend at all- Suddenly the door creaked open and Julia’s face came into view from behind the screen door. She looked worn out like someone usually does after crying for a while, and she sniffed loudly before looking down at Karl, who was standing on the second step, and nearly screaming. "Karl!" she cried. "But… you’re dead!" "Of course I’m not!" Karl offered brightly. "I’m just AWOL!" Somehow, both of them thought that this was far better than him being dead, and she pulled him into the house by the collar. For the moment, all of this business of dreams and people named Kasidy would have to wait. And yes, they had sex.
