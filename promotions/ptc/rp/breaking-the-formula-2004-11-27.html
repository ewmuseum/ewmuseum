Breaking the Formula

27 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-11-27 CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK No bullets. If Collette's spirits could have sunk any lower, then they surely did; through bedrock, all the way down to her core. Feeling that she was doomed, she had decided to go down fighting, taking her husband and nemesis, Doctor Curiosity, with her. Unbidden, a tear crept into her eye and crawled slowly down her left cheek. Doctor Curiosity breathed out for the first time in what seemed like years. "Eegor, you could have told me it vas not loaded!" he snapped. "I thought you had loaded it. I thought you might have changed your mind!" Collette stared at them, wide-eyed and very, very scared. Doctor Curiosity's eyes began to widen just as much as the reality of the situation began to sink in. "You vere… you were going to actually kill me," he whispered. "Collette…" The seriousness of her betrayal finally hit him, harder than any suplex or DDT he had received in the course of the tournament. She had genuinely meant to kill him. Even in his wildest moment, when he had threatened her with torture or worse, he had never seriously intended to carry out his threats. He would never have stooped so low or done so much; it had all been intimidation and mind games. Deep down, Curiosity was sure that Eegor felt the same way. He had given the doctor the gun, but he had not loaded it. The Goodman brothers had tried every trick in their books, but Collette had gone one step further. She had pulled the trigger, believing that the gun was loaded. She stood now, terrified, as Eegor and Curiosity exchanged shocked looks. Fearing revenge, her mind racing in a mixture of terror and paranoia, she pointed the gun at the head of Eegor. Another clicking noise signalled that the gun was empty, but the distraction was all she needed to sprint to the door, fling it open, and run through. Neither Doctor Curiosity nor Eegor moved immediately, both knowing that she had taken the door through which they had entered; the door to the roof. "Ve had better go and get her," suggested the doctor. Eegor shook his head, unable to come to terms with her attempt to kill his brother. "Vell, I am going. Zis has to end." Doctor Curiosity steadied himself, and then opened the door, taking care as he walked up the stairs towards the roof. ------------------------------------------ Collette stumbled out in to the rain, which had begun lashing down only moment earlier. It washed away the tears from her face, but not the sorrow from her heart. She bit back sobs as she tried to think of something, anything that she could do. She had no idea of how to get out of the building, and even if she did know, where did she have to go? Wiping a loose strand of her hair from across her forehead, Collette looked out into the distance, and saw two cars approaching from afar. The pinpoints of their headlights were accompanied by red and white flashes. If sound had been able to travel that far, then she would have heard sirens. It was almost enough to make her laugh. The cavalry had arrived, but they were just that little bit too late. Collette continued to cry, looking out over the desert landscape, and hearing the thunder that echoed off the plains and mountains in the distance. The rain seemed out of place, an anachronism brought on by some force unknown to her. Cursing the dramatic nature of fate, she turned around to see her husband standing a few metres away. "Why, Collette? Why did you do it?" Collette bit her lip, and tried her best to look weak. In her state, it was not a hard undertaking. She just wanted someone there to give her shelter and tell her everything would be fine, even if life was as far from fine as it could be. All she could manage in way of response was a shrug, as she continued to sob. The doctor walked towards her, and she took a step back, closer to the edge. "No, Collette. I'm not here for zat kind of tzing," said the doctor, trying to placate her. "Then drop the act, Chris. Stop talking in your stupid accent and maybe I'll believe you once in a while," she spat back at him. Doctor Curiosity thought about it, and effortlessly dropped all his pretences. He wasn't German, and had indeed never even visited Germany, but that kind of thing didn't even feel like a secret any more. "Okay Collette. It's just you and me now. I... I want to understand why it is you did that." "I need... I need the money. Debts... I owe people money." "I owe people money, Collette," replied Chris Goodman. "Hell, I've got two mortgages out on this place already!" "I owe money to the wrong kind of people. We're not talking reasonable interest rates that you pay off over fifty years. We're talking ridiculous rates, and you sure as hell don't get years to pay it off in." The doctor nodded. He'd heard stories of those kind of people, though he never thought he would know someone who had dealings with that sort of murky criminal underworld. "Without that money I'm as good as dead. You've got to help me," pleaded his wife. "After that? After what you just did, you want me to miraculously find some money for you, and pay off your debts? What do you think I am, an idiot?" "No, but I thought... I thought..." The tears running down her face seemed distinguishable, even in the rain. Doctor Curiosity truly was just Chris Goodman for a few moments, a man who had suffered a lot of humiliation, but who had always wanted to be accepted, and to be loved. She looked down at the ground, shame joining the plethora of other emotions fighting for attention within her body. She went to look the doctor in the eye, but as she was raising her head up, he spotted the cars coming closer. His train of thought was derailed, violently. "What the hell... what are they doing here?" Collette turned and saw them. "Are zese your doing, voman?" yelled the doctor, back into his guise completely. "No, Chris, no they're... well..." she stammered. "What? Are you trying… have you been…" Curiosity was incredulous. All he could see was a woman in front of him who had an unlimited potential for deception. Was her current grief just a trap? The doctor looked around, and he smiled. Chris Goodman was not equipped to deal with this kind of thing. It had been the continual stress and inability to cope with the world that had made him create his alter-ego of Doctor Curiosity. It was he who would have to face such circumstances. “Even now, you try to cheat me, Collette. Not content vith just cheating on me in a carnal sense, you try to finish my career now. Ze von place vhere I have found some sort of self-vorth. Ze von place vhere I have become accepted, vhere people have tzought zat I fit in. Ze vorld of wrestling has embraced me, Collette, and yet you still come back, time after time, trying to end zis dream for me. I vill not let it happen Collette, I vill not let it happen.” Curiosity moved off to the side, pulling the lid from a large plastic crate. He looked down inside it and saw a huge metallic tube. He smiled again; it was time to take control for good. “Jah, behold ze Curious Laboratory Defence Mechanisms!” The doctor grabbed the tube and lifted it from the crate. As he held it over his shoulder, his wife recognised what it was; a missile launcher of sorts, the kind beloved by terrorists and insurgents around the world. Collette ducked as Curiosity pointed it at her… no, beyond her… and fired. The rocket flew out of the launcher, and careered towards the police cars. “Jah! Jah!” cried the doctor. Collette could only turn, her stomach churning as if her guts were slowly dissolving, the stabbing pain reminding her that this was very real indeed. Curiosity stood, triumphant, but only for a second. The rocket flew towards the cars that drove towards the laboratory, but then it started to move skywards, exploding in a shower of red and green sparks. “Vhat ze hell?” said the doctor, looking down at the launcher. It had a little tag attached. THANK YOU FOR PURCHASING THE WORLD’S GREATEST FIREWORK – THE ROCKET-O-MATIC 3001! THIS IS GUARANTEED TO GET ANY PARTY STARTED WITH A BANG, AS YOU CAN SHOOT DOWN THOSE INFIDELIC HELICOPTERS IN THE SKY! ONLY JOKING OF COURSE, THIS SHOULD NOT BE USED IN ANY WAY TO HARM PEOPLE… IT’S FAR TOO PRETTY AND SPARKLY FOR THAT! “Vell… it vas very pretty…” muttered Curiosity. Collette just shook her head. In the middle of the greatest crisis of her life, her husband was running around and shooting off fireworks. “Zat did not vork, but ve shall get zem vith ze sharks!” yelled Curiosity, moving over to stand by a large red lever. The police cars were nearby now… almost at the door of the laboratory. “Release ze sharks!” cried the doctor. He pulled the lever down hard, and in the side of the laboratory, two portcullises were raised. Nothing else appeared to happen though. “Ah, damn zis night! Ve took ze sharks out for a few days vhile ve vere re-paving ze bottom of ze moat.” Collette had had enough. She turned to the police cars on the outside, and tried in vain to make herself heard over the swirling wins and driving rain. “Help me!” she called. “He’s up here! He’s trying to kill me!” The words carried to Doctor Curiosity, but only barely. He shook his head. “I vould never have tried to kill you. I never vill try to kill you, Collette.” “Well, that’s just tough shit then, doc,” she said, mockingly, as she turned back to face him again. “These policemen are going to see me here, and they’re going to want to know I’m in such a state. I wonder what they’ll say when they see the marks on my wrists? I wonder who they’re going to believe about all of this. They just got here in time, didn’t they? I’m going to sue you, and put you in jail!” “I have no money Collette…” “I don’t care any more. I just need to see you fail!” Curiosity was rooted to the spot. He knew he couldn’t kill her; he just wasn’t capable of that kind of malice… yet he didn’t want to fail now, so near to success in GTT4. A spotlight flicked on down at the base of the laboratory, and the police shone it up at the ramparts on the roof. Collette was caught in it perfectly. Silhouetted in the dazzling light, she looked like some kind of angel. Doctor Curiosity reflected that she would be the angel of death for his career, and for his dreams. “He… will… not… fail…” The words, spoken with a relentless energy, came from the left of Collette. She turned round slowly, to see Eegor almost upon her. He had sneaked up behind the various apparatus of the Curious Weather Experiment, and stood scant centimetres from her face. She could smell the stench of his breath as he grabbed hold of her shoulders. She struggled, but he had her tightly. He would not release her. “NO!” cried Curiosity, dashing forwards, knowing Eegor’s intentions within a millisecond of seeing him. He lost his footing slightly on the wet stone, and could only look on in horror as Eegor moved the struggling women backwards, and pushed her up against the low wall that separated the building from the sky. Words flew from Collette’s mouth, but none of them had any meaning for Eegor. This woman had ruined his life, and was trying to ruin his brother’s too. He didn’t care about consequences any more. His life had been turned into some nightmare of servitude, and the blame, whilst partially his, also lay at the feet of this woman. The feet that were now being taken from the ground. The doctor and his wife both screamed, as Eegor lifted her over the edge. Her arms beat down upon Eegor, raining blows upon him, but he was not going to be stopped. There was a stoic inevitability about his actions, as unhurried and deliberate as they were. He moved like a force of nature, unstoppable as an avalanche hitting Collette, and thrusting her over the ramparts… and down. Curiosity had scrambled to his feet and dived to try and reach her, but his fingers missed hers by the smallest of margins. The doctor hit the edge of the roof and looking over the stone wall, catching sight of her eyes for one final time as she dropped away, before she slammed full force in to the newly laid concrete base of the moat. With nothing but the cruel, hard ground to break her fall, death was instantaneous. ------------------------------------------------- Doctor Curiosity sat in his locker room and reflected back on the events of the past week. The one thing that stuck in his mind the whole time was a question: How had it come to that? He was meant to be a wrestler. He was meant to be able to go out there and entertain, or annoy, the fans. He was meant to be well-versed in headlocks and acrobatic moves, not in dealing with the police and sorting out the aftermath of a murder. Curiosity shook his head slowly as he applied tape to his wrists. In the end it had been an open and shut case, as they say. The police had seen Eegor throw Collette from the roof of the Curious Laboratory, and the video camera from the Curious Weather Experiment had captured on it the doctor’s statement that he would not harm her, and his attempts to stop Eegor from carrying out his final act of atonement. Of course, he had been forced to answer thousands of questions, and give statement after statement to the police and the investigating officers, but with Eegor’s testimony of being the sole one at fault, and the evidence from the night itself, Curiosity’s name was cleared within days. He remembered what had happened right after the impact had robbed Collette of the rest of her life. His brother, no longer playing the role of Eegor, but just as Paul Goodman, had turned to him. “I’m sorry, but I couldn’t let her do this.” The doctor himself had been too stunned to speak. Paul started to weep uncontrollably, knowing the fate that would await him. He had committed murder, with witnesses, and unknown to him, a camera catching it all in perfect digital quality. He knew that his life, as it was, was forfeit in any meaningful way. Whether he would end up on Death Row or just in an ordinary prison was immaterial to him. He had just wanted the best for his brother. He had wanted his brother to have one shot at getting things right without Collette interfering and ruining it all. “I hope… I hope that you can forgive me,” he said, between sobs. “I didn’t know what else to do… I needed to give you… to show you… I don’t know. Forgive me, forgive me… forgive…” His voice had trailed off, and he walked to the door, before slumping down and awaiting the police to come and take him away. Doctor Curiosity did not know what to do. His life had been changed, altered forever by the events of the night. Whilst he would never truly grieve for the loss of Collette, he was not the sort of man to ever rejoice in the death of another. He could not ignore his brother though, and something had to be said before he was taken away. The doctor could not leave him in limbo for the rest of his life. Something had to be done. Something had to be said. The sound of the police approaching through the laboratory was apparent, and so Chris Goodman locked eyes with his brother for the final time in freedom, and spoke to him. “I forgive you, Paul. Your debt is repaid.” There had been, even as the police forced Paul to the floor and handcuffed him, a look of relief in his eyes. A look as if despite the circumstances of the occasion, he was the happiest he had been in several years. The doctor sighed, his mind back in the locker room, preparing for his match against the Latch Key Kid. This had been the least actual preparation he had been able to get for one of his matches in the tournament. Between seeing the police time and time again, and speaking to his lawyer, there had been little time to get to the gym, and almost as little time to prepare a dossier of his matches and likely moves. His research had also been hindered by the lack of tournament footage of the Kid in action. There seemed to be some gaping black hole around this man, and it was bothering Curiosity. He had seen tapes of him from the home fed, Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling, but they were always in tag team matches. Evidently, the Kid had learnt to stand on his own two feet; as nobody would have reached this stage of the tournament without being extremely talented. If only it was could be as easy as smashing the child’s lemonade stand had been, Doctor Curiosity laughed to himself as he started to limber up, stretching his arms and legs to avoid potential muscle strains. In the end, the police had not been called to the Curious Laboratory by Collette’s boyfriend. In fact, he had been found dead of a drug overdose while Collette had been stuck in the laboratory. No, it had been little Timmy’s mother who had phoned the police, telling them to arrest Eegor for attacking her son. Doctor Curiosity’s attempt to shift the blame had evidently worked, and Eegor had decided to accept the punishment for that also. Whatever he could do to protect his brother, he would do. Such sacrifices had been made to enable the doctor to participate in this match, to continue his dream of proving everybody wrong, of showing the world that had mocked him for so long that he was capable of more than they had all said. He had some little curious tricks up his sleeve (and some in his boots) to help augment his wrestling skills, and below the surface that everyone looked at, he had a determination that would not be matched. His brother had forfeit everything to place him in this position. He had gone above and beyond the call of any duty to allow Doctor Curiosity to live his dream. With the determination of the pair of them, Doctor Curiosity believed with all of his heart that there was nothing that could possibly stand in his way. He would not let his brother down.
