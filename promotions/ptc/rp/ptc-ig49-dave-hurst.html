"Exceptional" Dave Hurst. "Oh great..." In restless desperation I turn the ignition once more. The car whirrs with disobedience, before collapsing into a low beeping - The battery is dead. I breathe a heavy sigh. "Looks like another day off for you little mate." I glance over behind me to my small plastic duck, Ducky. He looks on disappointed. He really loves school, and it pains me each morning the flaming car freezes. One of these days I'll get around to having that bloody block heater installed. I reach over and unclip his seatbelt. He'd do it himself, but he's got no hands you see. I pick him up and place him on my palm. Slamming the door shut behind me, we head into the house. ----====|====---- North Dakota. I was told it'd be cheap to live, and a stress-free environment. Free from the chaos of the city, and free from being worked into submission - they were wrong. The farmers and the farm hands have all the work, and the only places left for people are the restaurants. I'm fed up of working tables just to put some bread on the table. I step outside, into the whistling gale and the chilling day. My fur-lined hood flapped, threatening to expose my face to the freezing breeze. I pull two blocks of wood over my shoulder before heading back inside. I push the first block into the wood furnace and marvel as the embers spring to life with an orange glow and a crackle - warmth is on its way. "Don't get too close," I warn Ducky. "You'll melt your skin." Ducky glances to the blank television screen in front of him, yearning for the discovery channel. It’s been three months since we had cable television, and yesterday the electricity was cut off. The lack of amenities doesn’t bother me. In fact, it’s in times of adversity when I’m truly alive, and when I truly appreciate the good fortune that comes my way. That being said, sooner or later I won’t have enough money to feed us both. Good fortune is something that I could do with right now. I just need something to get me started. I just need a little push to get me moving. In search of an answer I glance over to yesterday’s mail. I haven’t even looked at it yet in fear that it would be something I’d regret. On the table was an envelope that didn’t look like a bill. It didn’t look like junk mail either. I get up and walk over to it and pull it from the pile. The envelope was marked “PTC”. Could that be Primetime Central? Surely not…
