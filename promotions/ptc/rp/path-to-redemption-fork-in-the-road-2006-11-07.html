Path to Redemption: Fork in the Road

7 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-11-07 Never speculate about your opponent; always seek facts. Don't listen to rumors, seek information from those who know. Check out the truth of everything before moving, for if you have sought lies you have sought failure. -- excerpt from Sun Tzu's Life Strategies from the Art of War Path to Redemption: The Fork in the Road A deep sigh passed her lips as she drown out the voices around her in favor of another attempt at remembering anything. Golden pools found themselves closed behind quivering lids as she looked into the darkness of her fragmented mind. Nothing came to surface. It was as if she born only a few moments ago. Grimacing she lifted those lids and, once again, scanned her surroundings. Perhaps this very room would help to trigger a memory that would help her to piece the puzzle together. Looking to the left she noticed the formerly empty jury box now held twelve figures in it. Brows furrowed. She could have sworn it was empty not a moment ago. Shaking off the uneasy feeling that attached itself to her heart she continued to travel the room with her gaze. At the table parallel with her own sat a man that caused her skin to crawl. Something about the man had her on edge and, yet, she couldn't stopping staring at him. He was handsome in every aspect. Strong features that were just subtle enough to keep his features from being harsh. Raven hair that gleamed with a healthy gloss that would have made most women jealous. His eyes were almost hypnotic; brilliant blue that rivaled that of the Caribbean sea. He was the epitome of a perfect male specimen and she still felt the urge to scoot her chair further away. Shuddering, she forced her gaze away and looked to Cassie. Like the unknown man, Cassie was the personification of the perfect woman, only with her, Mya felt a warmness. She felt safe. Cass was her baby blanket, that one thing you always clung to when frightened knowing that it would shield you from all harm. The thought made Mya smile as she studied the golden curls on Cass's head. It was as if they held a shining light in them. Her head seemed to glow with a halo as if she were an angel. If she could help Mya with whatever this trial was about, Mya would forever think of her as just that -- an angel. Cass looked up and smiled gently as Mya quickly pulled her gaze away, embarrassed to have been caught staring. A soft palm covered the back of her hand and gave it a tiny squeeze drawing a small smile back to Mya's worried lips. It gave her a sense of confidence, the feeling that whatever she faced during this, she would come out okay in the end. " Mr. Lomax you may begin with your opening statement." Spider legs crawled over her skin bringing forth a shudder of revulsion at the mere mention of that name. Her gaze shot over to the extroadinary man as he stood up and gave a charming smile in her direction before he moved to the jury and began his opening speech. " Your honor, ladies and gentlemen of the jury this is a case about human morality, the morality of one Mya Yamomuko. This is a woman who appears to be intelligent, kind, even forgiving at first glance, but I tell you the only true part of that statement is she is intelligent. And how could she not be? 47 lives stolen by her very hand and not once was she caught. She cleverly disguised each and every murder in such a way that others often paid the price for her crimes. I ask you, does she care? The answer is no, she does not care. She slayed each and every one of them without conscience and without remorse, something that you will be able to see clearly as the evidence of this case is brought forth…" Mya's heart sank into the pit of her stomach as her mouth dropped open, gaping in shock by his words. Murderer? She was accused of being a cold hearted killer with no conscience? How could this be? Her terrified and stunned gaze swung over to Cassandra in question. Cass merely shook her head slightly as she caressed Mya's hand once again. Mya needed answers and Cass was not willing to give them, at least not yet. Frowning, she turned her confused and mortified gaze back to Dante as he continued with his opening statements against her. " I am asking you, ladies and gentlemen..no I am telling you to keep your minds focused as each point of evidence is brought forth. Do not let her tears or remorseful gaze sway you from what has to be done. Do not allow her to beguile you with false innocence. She is a cunning fox, a cold blooded killer and she deserves the worst fate can deal to her. Thank you." Mya bit down on the inside of her cheek to keep from screaming her innocence. How could she have killed so many and not remember a single one of them? Was murder not often described as an intimate affair, something akin to sex for the twisted soul? The sound of Cassie's chair scraping lightly along the hardwood floor brought her beseeching gaze up to the face of this woman whom would be her champion in this fight for her life. Cassandra offered her a small, reassuring smile before she moved to stand in front of the jury and state her own case in this macabre play. "Your honor, ladies and gentlemen of the jury, as Mr. Lomax said this is a case about human morality but it is also a case about trust and the betrayal of that trust. The picture my fellow counselor painted is grossly contorted and meant to paint her as something she is not. What he said may be true. She may have killed these people. But I ask you -- what reason would she have to do so? The answer is simple. Self Defense. These men tried to rape her, the intention to kill her is evident by the fact that her own brother was killed in that very same incident when he tried to rescue her from the grasp of these villians…" The instant the murder of her brother was mentioned Mya felt a wrenching in her soul. The room spiraled out of control as a queasiness settled deep within the pit of her stomach. Only a moment ago she could remember nothing and now the vision of her brother's face swam before her eyes. He had kind eyes and a warm smile. Swallowing the need to let out a small cry she lowered her lids, tightening them painfully against the onslaught of memory that proceeded to flood her vision in shattered pieces. A knife gleaming against the moonlight. Her screams of terror and rage. Distorted face of men she did not know laughing heinously as she was pushed from one to another in a morbid game of hot potato. All of this came at her with the force of a hurricane. She wanted it to stop. She needed it too! That ebony tressed head shook violently side to side as she fought of the unwanted attack of painful and confusing memories. Finally, they subsided and Cassie's voice came back into focus. " This was only the beginning of a series of events that surely would have led to the destruction of her life. At the age of nineteen she narrowly missed being murdered by a car bomb planted in the family vehicle only to be forced to watch as her own mother was killed by something possibly meant for them both. This woman has been pursued relentlessly and thrown into situations that forced her hand in order to ensure her own survival. Is this murder? No. It is self defense. Then there is the awful truth. Her own father was the culprit behind it all. This man was the one who planned it all, as the evidence will prove beyond a doubt." Cassie gave her speech a dramatic pause as she took a moment to look at the facial expression of each and every member of the jury. Anything from shock to disbelief could be seen. " I can see from the looks on your faces that you are as shocked as I was when I found out this dark truth. This girl…this innocent woman has suffered the worst betrayal any human being could suffer. She was betrayed by her own father. She had been lied too. Her brother, her mother -killed…and her father was the perpertrator behind everything. How much can one person take? Could any of you take that kind of betrayal without loosing some part of yourself?" Mya's chin quivered as she struggled against the tears rising at the edge of her eyes. Though her memories were coming in distorted pieces of a puzzle, she could still understand the pain Cassie spoke of. She felt the pain. The sensation of this pain washing over her was even more startling than the fragmented memories, after all, to feel the pain of what happened and not having a clear memory of it would be disturbing for anyone. Cassie took another careful look at the faces of the jurors. At this point she knew she had at least half of them sympathetic toward Mya's plight in life. Satisfied, she nodded and returned to her seat next to a very confused and mournful Mya. By that point Mya was beyond listening to the voices of those around. All they seemed to do was bring her pain and something else. Something she couldn't quite put her finger on. The only thing she knew was there was this burning sensation in her stomach that was building to a point it felt as if she would explode. Anger was foreign to the woman who's memory was practically that of a new born child. ~*~*~*~*~ What should have been hours felt more like seconds as Mya's mind once again focused on the events surrounding her. Lomax was looking over a paper in his hand with a decisive look on his face. After some brief mental deliberation he finally raised his head. " The prosecution would like to call Iashi Yuko to the stand." Something deep rooted within her soul suddenly flared to life at the mention of that name. It was an unfamiliar feeling that felt almost as natural as breathing. A true oxymoron. Slowly, that head turned, eyes seeking the vision of the man called forth. The instant her gaze met with his face, that inferno that had been born in her breast flared to new heights. Her saliva seemed to turn to acid. Healthy lungs tightened painfully making it near impossible to breath. She knew that face. The eyes embedded upon those features could never be forgotten on the subconscious level. They were the eyes of the man she hated with every fiber of her being. Shock stormed through out her form. Hate. That was the sensation she could not place earlier. This was beyond mere hate, however, this man was the only man among the five who attacked her all those years ago that still had the privilege of breathing. He had been given the last extent of her mercy. His face brought back everything. She remembered it all now…well all but the last few days of her life. She broke her stare at the startled sense of a hand softly placed against her arm. " Mya.." The whisper of her lawyer drew her angry gaze to the gentle eyes of Cass. " Calm yourself, you're trembling." Mya looked to her hands resting flat palmed on the table and drew a deep breath. She was quaking, just as Cassie had said, but it was not out of fear as it should have been. It was the unbridled fury coursing through her veins. " Mr. Iashi can you identify the defendant?" " Hai. Her name is Yamomuko Mya." " What is your relationship to Miss Yamomuko?" " I… I know her through past transgressions on my part." " Transgressions?" " Hai. I made a mistake when I was younger and participated in an attempted sexual assault on Miss Yamomuko when she was around 15." " I see. Do you hold any ill will toward the defendant now?" Yuko shook his negatively. " No. I paid my dues for my mistakes. She's done nothing to warrant any ill will from me." Lomax looks down at his papers before raising his gaze back to Yuko. " Nothing at all?" He paused to place a paper on the small ledge in front of Yuko. " Because according to this statement made by you only two months ago she kidnapped you, beat you and left you for dead." Yuko dropped his gaze. Color flooded his face, though she could not tell if it was from shame or anger. When he lifted his gaze again, it was not to Mr. Lomax but to Mya. " Hai. It's true. She did kidnap me. She even threatened to kill me, but I can not blame her for her actions. She believed that I was part of the car bombing that took her mother from her. People do--" " That is all Mr. Yuko. We have no need for your explanation just a straight answer to my questions." Yuko snapped his jaw shut and nodded as his gaze slipped to Mya once more. As Mya stared back, she saw something she never thought she would see. True remorse. It had a startling effect on her psyche. All the anger subsided replaced by a feeling of uneasy peace. For the first time since it all began, she found herself wanting to forgive someone for everything she had been through. The prosecutor continued his interrogation of Yuko for a few more minutes, carefully wording each question to garner a response that was colored in the light he wanted the jury to see her in. That of a cold blooded killer. As soon as Lomax was done Cassandra stood up for her own questioning of the witness. " Yuko earlier you were giving your feelings on Mya's actions in reaction to everything she had been through. Will you please continue with that now? For my own edification, of course." " Objection! Mr. Iashi is not a trained psychiatrist. His opinion as to why she did what she did should hold no bearing here." " Your honor, he was a victim of her violence and as such his opinion on why she did what she did holds a great bearing on this case." " Over ruled." " Mr. Iashi if you please?" Yuko glanced to Mya briefly as he cleared his throat, clearly uncomfortable with the situation. Mya felt the same. It was an awkward feeling to know that your life rested in the hands of a man you beat half to death. Swallowing the lump lodged deep within her throat she laced her fingers together, bowed her head and silently began to pray. If she had been looking up, she might have noticed the judges eyes resting intently on her. " I believe Mya did what anyone would have done given the circumstances. I was part of the cause of her breaking down. Most everyone who she attacked was part of the cause. I can not blame her for her what she did. I…" "…..I would have done the same." " Thank you Mr. Iashi." Judge O'Donnell allowed Yuko to step down. Yuko's steps were slow as he began the trek to the aisle. They stalled completely as he came to stand next to the defendant's table. A low clearing of the throat brought Mya's head up and her eyes to his face. For that instant they connected and without a word he told her how sorry he was for everything. For the first time ever, Mya smiled at him and nodded as a single tear of forgiveness ran down her cheek…. ~*~*~*~ Over the next two hours the prosecution and defense battled, each doing their best to convince the jury one way or the other. Apprehension clamped around her heart, slowly tightening the vice grip as each minute passed. She stole another glance at the jury hoping to see if they were leaning one way or another. The stony faces told her nothing. It was Lomax's turn to recall any witnesses and he called the name she did not want to hear. " I call to the stand…." Her own. " Yamomuko Mya. " Her lungs froze. Her skin turned to ice and those black pupils dilated with the fear that settled within the pit of her stomach. Slowly she rose and moved to the witness box. It was with a numbed mind she took the oath and sat down in the uncomfortable chair. " Please state your name for the records." " My name is Mya Yamomuko." " Miss Yamomuko, can you please tell me your where abouts yesterday evening." Mya opened her mouth to speak but nothing came out. She was drawing a blank slate. When she tried to recall what she was doing nothing came to mind. Frowning she looked to Mr. Lomax and slowly shook her head. " I…." " You don't recall?" He interrupted her before she could finish. Hesitantly, she nodded. She found this whole thing to be odd and baffling. She was on trial. Most trials took weeks, even months to commence after an arrest was made. Was she on some medication? Wouldn't she have been in jail? No, she could have been out on bail. Right? " Right." The prosecutor's voice interrupted her train of thought as if he had read her mind. He turned and moved to his desk retrieving something from his briefcase. He brought the object forward and slammed it down on the ridge in front of her. " Perhaps this will jog your memory. Do you recognize this?" Her gaze fell to the object in bewilderment only to widen. Panic raced through her veins as she mechanically reached for it. There, within her hand rested a 9mm Beretta pistol. Her stomach twisted violently as the urge to vacate the premises took over. She doubled over as she haphazardly put the gun back on the tiny shelf. She recognized it. She remembered. Everything was in place now. Not a single detail was misplaced. It took a moment to breathe through the need to vomit. Timidly, she straightened up and looked at Mr. Lomax through tear filled eyes. " I remember now." " What do you remember?" " I was in Bangkok, the warehouse district last night….with…my..f..f..father." " And the gun? Do you recognize it?" " Yes. It's the gun I used to shoot him with." Lomax smiled. As far as he was concerned he had won this case. " The prosecution rests." He turned and gave Cassie a smug grin only to receive an angry glare in return as she rose and approached a befuddled and extremely frightened Mya. " Mya..I mean…Miss Yamomuko, can you tell me why you shot your father?" Mya lifted her quivering chin and glanced around the courtroom before coming back to look at Cass once more. " I shot him because I found out it was him." " I don't understand. Can you elaborate?" " He set it all up. Everything. He paid Yuko and his friends to attack me.He had my brother killed. He had my mother killed. He tried to kill me. It was ALL HIM!" The rage she felt as she revealed the truth stained her words. Breathing became labored as she struggled to gain control once more. In the next instant she realized her mistake. She had basically admitted to everything in those few words. She showed her true colors. That amber gaze snapped to the jury in panic, then back to Cass. Oh god, what had she done!? " One final question. Do you regret any of it? Is there anything you wish you could go back and change?" Mya's brows drew together as confusion masked her face. Did she regret any of it? Yes. She regretted killing some of those who were pawns in the sick game her father had played. She regretted not seeing the truth sooner. She regretted…. ….not killing him sooner. Her gaze locked to Cass's. " No." Cass frowned as she looked to the judge. " She is lying your honor." " OBJECTION!" Cass whirled and stormed to Lomax slamming her hands on his table. " You can't object! You know she is lying!" " It doesn't matter! She said no! She has sealed her own fate!" " NO! She does not deserve to go to hell and you know it!" " She is MINE Cassandra. Her sins far out weigh her virtues!" " Do they? AN EYE FOR AN EYE LUCIFER Isn't that what you always say!?!" Mya sat in the chair staring at them both in utter confusion. What was happening? Hell? Sins? She didn't understand. " ENOUGH!" Mya jumped at the overbearing sound of Judge O'Donnell's voice. Her gaze shifted to him, the fear and uncertainty still ripe within her eyes. Judge O'Donnell stood and Mya felt that sudden urge to flee his sight. Something about him struck mortal fear in her heart. Cass turned to him and rushed to his podium. " Father, please! We all know she is lying. Her soul is salvageable! Don't let him take her!" Lomax rushed forward. " This is an outrage! I won this case fair and square. The bitch is mine!" " I SAID ENOUGH!" Both of them cringed and quieted down as the judge's eyes turned to the baffled gaze of Mya and spoke. " I have made my decision."
