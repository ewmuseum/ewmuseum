When The Adrenaline RUSH Subsides

22 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-22 The warmth of the low, afternoon sun that filtered through the window was the perfect compliment to the feeling of pride that Karina Wolfenden felt deep inside her. Feeling it on the back of her neck, uncovered by her long, blonde mane that was tidily restrained in a ponytail, Karina jolted her locker open, to be greeted with the visualisation of her RUSH Pro Wrestling career. In an almost cliché, high school way, Karina had taped photographs to the inside, overlapping one another to show the timeline. Instantly, Karina broke out into a smile, not even bothering to scan around before doing so in her usual self-conscious way. Standing out in the middle of the montage, the centrepiece, was a photo of herself and her two closest friends in both RPW, and wrestling as a whole... Gideon Daray Poe and Brianna Washington. In grainy black and white, making the nostalgic moment seem far older than its eleven or so months, the trio stood under a banner that boasted, "Tonight - The Debut of RUSH Pro Wrestlings Adrenaline." The first time Karina saw it, all she could think of was the missing apostrophe, but now... now it was destined for a frame in her apartment. All around it, similar photos helped refresh Karina's memory, making the captured moments seem and feel all the more vivid. The photo of an amorous teenager waving his "Marry Me Karina!" sign still brought more than a hint of a chuckle forth. While next to it, the picture of herself crouching down with the kids of the RPW ring crew reminded Karina of why she came back to the RUSH Pro family week after week. Sure, she didn't get along with too many wrestlers, or members of management. But there were good people she'd leave behind, reluctantly, in Charlotte. Bordering the more sentimental photos were the snapshots she'd captured from the wrestlers balcony. Finding something fascinating about isolating a frozen moment from an energetic bout, Karina went through film after film. High-flyers were shown defying gravity at the zenith of their moonsaults, while the technicians were frozen mid-Suplex, their backs bent and necks tensed. All her memories of RPW, now freed from the dusty, metallic beige confines. Unzipping the front pocket of her rucksack, Karina slid them each in turn into a plastic wallet, before dropping them into the compartment. A few CD's, scribbled notes, and a pair of sunglasses later, and the K-Wolf had cleared her locker out... removing herself once and for all from the Dale F. Halton Arena. For nearly half a minute, Karina's focussed and thoughtful emerald eyes stared into the shadowed interior of the empty locker. Immobile, insignificant, and permanently engulfed by the smell of the stale clothes stored around it, Karina's locker had been her first port of call at RPW shows over the last 40 or so weeks. A part of her weekly routine had been removed, making even the mundane of activities feel like they'd be missed. It looked as if she was the last to arrive for the clear-out too... in fitting with her usual tardiness. Every door had been freed of it's padlock, left ajar, mourning the loss of the nostalgia their removed contents fled with. As much as she would deny it to anyone who dared call her on it, Karina was trying her hardest to keep the emotions inside. RPW was the only time in her 'career' that she'd grown attached to a place, a fan base, a location. Natural poise and cold - borderline frosty - exterior held firm, and only the smallest of sniffs sured up the walls that held a tear back. Turning in a sudden motion, as if she had to tear herself away, Karina went to leave... only to stop, and swing her rucksack around in front of her. Rummaging through the contents of its front pouch, Karina narrowed her eyes in focus, before whipping out a carefully selected photo. Held between her thumb and index finger, both devoid of any nail-polish, Karina closed her eyes and smiled, making sure she could still visualise the moment captured in the picture. "Something to remember us by," she then warmly whispered, placing the black and white photo of RPW's first Champion - Gideon Poe - stood on the turnbuckles, looking out into the crowd with belt held proudly and victoriously aloft. She wanted to leave one memory for the Halton's next inhabitants. One memory that would sum up RUSH Pro Wrestling. Mission accomplished. After closing the locker, Kari untied her hair, letting it cascade down her neck to combat the scorch she was feeling from the magnified sun, then once again peeled herself away from the locker. With slow, regretful paces, Karina walked back along the hallway, leaving her left arm hung out to the side to strum along the open locker doors. Fighting off that nagging feeling that she'd forgotten something, one she always seemed to develop when deserting a flat or house, Karina retraced her steps through the Halton Arena's labyrinth-esque backstage, attentive emerald eyes doing their best to scan every sight into what she wished was a photographic memory. Each tile, each chip in the thin wall's plaster, every overly-bright fluorescent light above ... all absorbed into Karina's mind so she could rebuild the Arena in her fondest memories. "Karina," a familiar, yet only vaguely recognisable voice called. "Miss Wolfenden?" It was a voice she knew, but not one she'd heard before. The words carried familiarity, but their delivery couldn't be matched to a memory. Turning, slightly startled, she realised why. "Someone said you were here," the smiling, and slightly out of breath Jerry Quecard revealed, slowing down out of his jog and back to a normal walk. Although to many he was simply the RUSH Pro Wrestling ring announcer, Jerry was the first member of RPW's staff that Karina had met. In the early, fledgling days, everyone mucked in, from ring-crew to ring announcer, from referees to the head of security. Multi-tasking was rife, and it was needed to fulfil the pipe-dream that many thought RPW was. "Yeah," she answered, hesitantly as she tried to think of what her parting words should be. Things like emotional goodbyes never came naturally to Karina. "I had a few things to pick up, and wanted to take one last look around the place." Kari's words took on a rather curious tone as they tailed off, responding to the rather unnerving sight of Quecard rummaging in his trouser pocket. 'An innocent explanation right about now would be good... she thought to herself, head slightly cocked to the side. "I had one of these saved for you," Jerry then divulged, pulling a rather plain looking piece of card out... which suddenly gained all the more meaning when he flipped it over. "I'd saved these from the first show, as, well, y'know... a bit of a souvenir." Karina instantly recognised what was on the card. It was her. Scribbled down in black biro, and only legible to someone with as scruffy handwriting as her, was Karina's very first introduction in RPW. The words that called her down to the ring as she made her debut, preserved in cheap ink scrawled on tatty card. Priceless. "Wow," she exhaled in instinctive excitement, almost ripping the card from Quecard's hand in her eagerness. "This is... wow." Amazing how sentimentality adds all the important value to an object. "I've been handing a few out," Quecard explained, pleased to see how Karina too valued the card. "We didn't have many guys or gals left from the first show, so I've got a fair share to hold back. Oh, and I know you and Brianna are close, so can you pass this on to her?" Flicking through the deck, Jerry slid another out and passed it to the K-Wolf. "It's a shame she couldn't make it back before the end." "Yeah," Karina replied in almost automated fashion as she scanned her observing eyes over the second cue card. "But she'll need to be back to full fitness to wrestle this thing off me..." "Hey, play nice, okay"? Jerry teased with a faux stern expression, causing Karina to bow her head jokingly in return and fake a sullen nod. "Well, I have to head off. Y'know, commercials to film and all..." "Commercials?" Karina questioned back instantly, half-expecting a joke to follow the words up. "You wrestlers weren't the only stars, y'know!" Jerry proudly boasted, adopting a "strike a pose" stance, mostly in self-deprecation. "The voice of RPW is in demand. Well, the voice of RPW has a car dealership and a county fair to advertise. But who knows, RUSH Pro could have been my big break!" That seemed to make it a little easier for Karina. Knowing that even though their 'home' had been closed from under them, RPW's staff would also move on to greener pastures. Well, hopefully. "Good luck with it," Karina bid him farewell with, sincerity rife in her voice. "Don't forget us little people when you're raking in the royalties." It was odd, but pleasurable. For some reason, Karina always liked the way Quecard announced her name, usually having to boom a little louder over the aging P.A so he could be heard above RPW's fiercely loyal and deafeningly loud fans. It was something she'd miss, and although every sense of self-reservedness she had was battling her, Karina couldn't resist the chance to hear it one more time. "Hey, Jerry?" she timidly asked, turning around in the doorway and skipping her gaze around him in what could best be described as embarrassment. "This is going to sound lame. And I mean, like, REALLY lame... but... ah, no, forget it..." "You want me to announce you out of the building?" the seemingly omnipotent Quecard asked, making it sound like it wasn't the first time someone had made the odd request. "Well, yeah..." she admitted, her blushing obvious against her usually pale, freckled skin. "But tell anyone and I'll deny it until the day I die..." With a wink, Karina turned and faced the door, just waiting in anticipation and concentrating on the shiver that was crawling its way up her vertebrae, wanting to amplify the feeling. "And now exiting the Dale F. Halton Arena..." Quecard boomed in his familiar announcing voice. The voice that began and ended every RPW match ever sanctioned. "She will be missed by all... she is the K-Wolf... and she was RPW through-and-through... KARINA WOLFENDEN!!! Letting her startling eyes slowly open, Karina took in the sound for the last time, before raising a thumb in acknowledgement. "You tha man, Jerry!" Kari shouted back over her shoulder as she left the building, almost breaking out into self-conscious laughter to cope with the sudden feeling of forgivable embarrassment that overcame her. As Wolfenden emerged from the shadow of the Halton Arena's entrance, the low, swollen afternoon sun shone with all it's hazy glory straight into Karina's emerald eyes. The heat that caused her eyelids to lower and pale skin to glow helped burn the memory in deep, adding another sensation that would help with her sensory recall. Shaking her pink fringe down over her freckled face, Karina lowered her head away from the sun's full brunt, and made her way for the last time around the building to where her bike was parked. "Don't look back," she whispered to herself, already thinking she was a sentimental embarrassment for getting so attached. Scathing self-appraisal was something she often good at in such emotional moments. Looking down to where her Vans sneakers trod the cracked path, her eyes scanned the graffiti that had been daubed all over the concrete by RPW's expressive fans. Not happy to leave it at just bringing in banners and buying t-shirts, they'd left their messages on the paths surrounding the building... and after the 3rd show, the grounds staff hadn't bothered trying to clean it off. "Don't tell us you're leaving us without saying goodbye!" Just as she was slipping back into her apathetic, eyes-down state, Karina instantly perked up as she heard the warm and familiar words. Spinning on the spot, Karina braved the harsh glare to stare back up at the Halton Arena, where a pair of silhouettes slowly gained in clarity as they blocked out the low sun. To most of RPW's workers, as with the probable majority of all promotions out there, they were simply ring-crew guys. Not that important, right? Well, to some, maybe not. But to Karina, they were the first group she became close to in RUSH Pro... she favoured their company above most of the others she had to share the backstage area with. Greg Hanley and Dan Lewis... two very ordinary men. Neither had ever popped a crowd or cut a promo. Neither had their name put on a t-shirt, or sent the camera flashes wild with a high-risk spot. But what they did was build the structure from which RPW's stars hit their trademark moves. Leaving ringside before the fans arrived, and walking through the curtain to empty arenas, they got none of the glory... but all of the kudos from the K-Wolf. They tightened her ropes and erected her posts. Stretched her canvas and padded her turnbuckles. "You guys," she warmly greeted them with, again not sure how exactly to put what she wanted to say in to words. "I had no idea you were still around..." "Yeah," Dan acknowledged, showing that not even he expected to be back after RPW's closure. "Kannon found a buyer for the ring, so we're putting it up for him to inspect... then we take it down one last time." "What about after that?" Kari questioned, concern on her voice for what the two family men would be doing after their last job for RUSH Pro. "Halton has taken the whole crew on," Greg answered, easing the K-Wolf's worries. "They've been busier than ever with events and bookings, so we got first refusals on the new posts they made. Who knows, maybe another Fed'll move in and those "Ring Crew" shirts we had made won't have been a waste of money." "You guys... had t-shirts made?" the curious Karina enquired. "Yeah," Dan smiled proudly, reaching down into the hold-all that he'd dropped by his side. "I have a feeling it'll be a bit... well, a lot too big for ya, but here ya go." Holding her receptive hands out, Karina watched on in excited anticipation as Dan lay a black t-shirt, with 'Ring Crew' simply emblazoned across the chest in white, onto her welcoming palms. It was another item of very little value to anyone but her, but there wasn't a price offered that the K-Wolf would accept for it. "You sure?" she hesitantly asked, reluctant to pull it close to her. "Of course we’re sure!" Greg exclaimed, not seeing what the big deal was. "Hell, you autographed nearly every page of my lil' girl's program the first time you met her!" "And my boy still has that pair of glasses you gave him last month on his desk," Dan chipped in with. "He won't even wear 'em in case they get broken." "Wow," was once again all Karina could offer as she pulled the shirt close to her chest, touched by the sentiment of their gift. "I wish I could think of something to say except, well.... thanks!" "Just you make sure to do us 'ol RUSH Pro guys proud in the GTT," Greg jokingly ordered, wagging his finger in Kari’s beaming direction. "Ya hear?" "Sure thing," she chuckled back. "And I know I suck at goodbyes... so, goodbye... and take care. Tell the kids to stay safe too, all that 'don't try this at home' stuff, or something..." "And a fond farewell to you too, Miss Wolfenden," Dan replied, bowing to her as Greg mimicked his submissive motion. "Knock it off, guys..." the embarrassed Karina whinilly pleaded, playfully trying to pull Dan upright. "Hey," he argued, "gotta ride ya one last time." "Well, consider me ridden," the smiling Karina snapped back, taking a few paces away from the duo and raising her left hand in a parting wave. "Thanks for everything." "And you," the two men replied in unison, letting the young woman leave, knowing it was hard enough for her to walk away as it was, without them lassoing her with any further words. Another sniff kept back the tears, while rapidly blinking eyes did their best to stem their flow also. She never thought she'd miss RPW so much... but she began to realise that RPW was just a set of initials. RUSH Pro Wrestling was just a name, and the Dale F. Halton Arena was just a building. The people, the conversations, the brief but treasured moments, and the sentimental memories were what made RUSH Pro Wrestling. From the people who she shared a locker room with, friend or foe, to the guy who called them to the ring, to the unsung crew who put the ring together. She was no longer wrestling as RPW's Karina Wolfenden, she was wrestling for everyone who'd help make her stay in RUSH Pro so memorable. When they saw her name, she wanted them to know that she was a RUSH Pro'er, and that she wouldn't let its memory fade away as its recognition diminished. It wasn't a reason to win. No, nothing as proud and honourable. It was simply a reason to smile on occasion, perched atop a turnbuckle, remembering where she had the most fun of her 'career'. "Thanks for the memories..."
