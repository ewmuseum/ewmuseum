For Better or Worse - The Script I

12 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-12 A thud hit the desk of my office which was now littered with a cascading flow of papers, notes, memos and other assortments of sycophantic drivel that wasn’t very much to my liking. This particular thud was a whole other animal however. The mound of paper was held together by one giant clip and looked as if it would fall apart at the slightest movement. Despite this, the papers looked quite promising. ‘The script?’ ‘The script. Hope you enjoy reading all two hundred pages of it.’ ‘Two hundred…?’ But Jessica was gone, swaying her body from side to side elegantly but at the same time extremely provocatively. There was hint of enticement and even the tone of her voice had that je ne sais quoi that intrigued me. Now wasn’t the time to consider it however. I had an epic to read apparently. The week since the first round of the GTT, in which the majority of the matches were held under closed shop, in other words no attendance, I had managed to power through and despite the secrecy of the 1st Round (the results were meant to be kept schtum so that the 2nd Round would be in actuality the first main round) I had received an inundation of phone calls, faxes, emails and memos from around the world. All of a sudden…people knew who I was. And where the hell had I got a fax number from? Jessica had taken care of it all apparently. While I was slavering away with her father, training for the match, she had been doing her own toil, preparing a media blitz of coverage of my ‘inevitable’ victory. When it was all said and done, she had merely kept her word and now that was settled, the media were more than happy to deal with her. For the beginning she was just another voice in the crowd, flogging a horse that would probably be dead just like the other half of the field. But now she had potential…now she had a thoroughbred in the making. And by god she was milking it. She had arranged a frenzy of activity for the next couple of days, plotting to blanket spread my name all over the wrestling dirt sheets and get people excited in the name of Jack Murphy. ‘The Bull’ wasn’t a man to be trifled with and she had all the press releases prepared ahead of time, so that she was on top of everything and making sure that nothing was misrepresented. She was very particular about that. But it wasn’t just putting forth the good image, there were other, dirtier things that needed to be dealt with. Having seen the past career I once enjoyed (the sight of those vile promo segments made me retch at the thought) she was adamant that she track down every piece of footage that inexplicably linked me to that retarded gimmick. She was extricating the legend of the Bull so that I could be reborn as a cold hearted, destructive force and not the pansy Adonis body worshipping fitness instructor I once had the ignominy of being. I didn’t envy her task. She seemed to take it with aplomb, moving effortlessly from reporter to journalist, TV crew to sound man, dealing with each in turn as if she knew them from the beginning. A flirty laugh, an innocent grasp of an arm, a chinwag with the females, she knew exactly how to deal with people no matter what background or social status. And that as…. I bolted, stopping before I even thought the end of that sentence. It was good too because she had re-entered the room, talking on her mobile phone and speaking a different language to that of the plain English. I liked to call it the blurb. Basically it amounted to shortening as many words as possible without making it incomprehensible. Of course, the fun was in trying to listen in on a conversation without knowing this. ‘Yeah well the bus will be dealt with pront, but you’ve got to expl the ridic convo that took place on Monday mmkay? Right, love you too darling.’ The click of the phone closing and she let out a sigh of relief, slumping noisily into her chair that sat opposite from my own, behind another desk, which despite the amount of business that took place on it, was immaculate. ‘Seriously, what are people’s problems with speaking normally? Or is this technological innovation only stupefying people?’ She pointed to the phone and tossed it playfully in the air, her legs angled to peek out of the desk. A high heel dangled from her left toe, clinging on the precipice, about to fall at any moment. She twirled it provocatively in my direction, though her gaze was directed at a computer screen. All the same it seemed like it was on purpose… ‘You ready?’ ‘Hmm? For what?’ ‘For sex?’ What??? ‘I’m sorry?’ ‘For the session with the news reporters? You know it’s in an hour and you’ve got to be quite precise and succinct for them. They aren’t going to take any shit. Now I’ll be with you but they’re speaking to you, not me. You need any ideas for what you’re going to say?’ ‘No, I think I’ll be able to handle it.’ Jesus I was hallucinating with my words. How could I believe she’d say that? Sex indeed! I ran my fingers through my hair and blew out some hot air, pulling on my t-shirt as I went. It was time for some air. ‘I’m going outside, just let me know when we need to head ok?’ ‘Sure Jack…you ok?’ ‘Never been better Jess…never been better.’ I guess she’d noticed my red face. I didn’t until I hit the mirror in the bathroom. *** The flicker of flashing bulbs went off, though not the old style camera ones. These were the latest hi-tech, the ones that would give the best possible image to those had the right ability with them. And they didn’t come cheap. Well, I suppose to get the best you had to work with the best, no matter the cost. I think that was one of Jessica’s slogans. She had many of them but money was something in particular that drove a lot of them. Right now she was acting more like a boxing promoter, hyping her new blood, hawking the wares to anyone who’ll listen…for the right price of course. Her reasoning was that I wasn’t getting any younger and now was the time to try and build a reputation so you can live off some of those fat contracts that people would be foolish to offer to someone. Maybe foolish to you, but I’m worth every penny. And I had earned all this glitterati that now surrounded me like a circus. It was finally good to get some damn recognition and not be ignored by punks like Pearl Harber over at AWC. This type of exposure would only serve to irk him even more. If he was going to try and shove dead beat worthless wrestlers who couldn’t find the time to properly wrestle a match like Lavelle as the champion, then who’s to stop me finding other avenues to prove he’s a misguided fool? Exactly. Game, set and match. So right now currently I stood, flexing my muscles for some athletics magazine or other, about to give an interview about my current upcoming match with my next opponent. I found it funny that out of all the people they could have drawn me against, they found the one person with the exact same name as myself. ‘Could you turn to show off your back? Yeah that’s it.’ At the same time, this was a pretty pointless exercise. Why would I be doing this other than for the fact Jessica arranged for it? Maybe it was just me, but I couldn’t see how I was going to get exposure from doing some silly interview. My view was best spoken from my tool of choice. The ring. ‘Alright Johnny I think that’ll do. You think I can take Mr. Murphy now for that interview?’ It seemed the journalist had arrived. Surprisingly he wasn’t as fat or small as I thought he’d be. In fact he was pretty well built, about an inch or two off my height but damned if he couldn’t have been a bodybuilder in his spare time. He greeted me warmly with a firm handshake and motioned towards a seating area for us to sit down. ‘Mr. Murphy, it’s a pleasure to meet you.’ I immediately froze as the words slipped out of his mouth. The guy was licking up to me and I knew how to smell shit from a mile away. The tone was smarmy and grossly over-exaggerative. This guy was ass-kissing and trying to take the piss out of me. Oh boy, this was not the time to do this, definitely not the time. ‘So…are you ready to start?’ Boy was I ever. ‘Yeah sure.’ ‘Alright…’ He fumbled in his small slipcase he had with him and produced a small Dictaphone, switching it on and laying it beside his side on a small stool. ‘This is the week starting December 10th and I am Craig Jason speaking with Jack Murphy, promotional article. Okay, let’s start with the basics Jack…can I call you Jack?’ ‘I suppose so, though it’s a bit late now isn’t it?’ ‘True, true. Anyway, tell me about yourself, what is it that makes the mind of ‘The Bull’?’ ‘What do you mean?’ ‘What makes you stand out? I mean, there are hundreds of muscle-bound freaks who could replace you if you’re simply a vanilla man, so what makes you worth it above and beyond everyone else out there?’ ‘Muscle-bound freak? Take a look in the mirror before you go off with your buzz words. I’m no freak as you say but the existence of all that can be achieved with perseverance, hard work and a little luck. I’ll be honest I’m no spring chicken like many others in this industry that I work in but that doesn’t mean I have any less to contribute. I have earned my place, they haven’t proven themselves yet.’ ‘Then why enter the GTT? Surely if you’re already established there is no need looking for yet more publicity is there?’ ‘Of course there is a reason to enter; the thrill of competition, the rush of adrenaline when I enter that ring. I live to compete and progress as far that I can push my body. When that fire in my belly dies…then it’s time to quite while I’m ahead.’ ‘But you haven’t answered my question…you claim to be established, yet I don’t see your name in lights anywhere. If anything you could merely represent a last gasp effort to try and capture an insignificant glory that has eluded you all these years. What do you say to that?’ My responses had become exceedingly terse because his false attempt to get me on his side at the beginning had led straight to him throwing it all in my face, trying to make a mockery of my career. He didn’t give a damn who I was, he was more concerned with the insecurities of the business and showing it up. ‘What I say is that there are plenty of athletes that may not, for whatever reason, reach their potential until nearer the end of their careers. It is perfectly plausible that the person has the ability but things always stand in his way.’ ‘Well what has stood in yours? Injury? Not that I can see, in fact you’ve been pretty luck to avoid anything more than minor bruises.’ ‘That I would rather not go into, it’s a personal matter.’ ‘Personal? But Mr. Murphy, you’re in the limelight now…you can’t shy away if you feel like. People want to know…everything. Whether or not you like it, people are going to find out. I’m merely giving you the opportunity to shed those demons while you still have the chance to do it yourself. I mean, there’s no harm in admitting to things like the well documented falsity of the wrestling business…the politics, the drugs, the deaths…’ ‘Who are you interviewing now? You don’t care about gleaning any sort of worthy information about what is important to me. All you care about is enlightening the public to the next pathetic broken wrestler. I don’t see how this has any relevance to me. You want your story? Why don’t you go talk to the baseball players who are shooting up to try and live up to the heightened expectations you thrust upon them! You think it is purely on their shoulders that they do this? The media has its part to play too and your crucifixion of those who have done wrong is simply hypocritical and wrong.’ I had stood up by this point, measuring up to him and getting angrier by the second. ‘Go look in the mirror…maybe it’s time the media had some introspective writing because it can’t get away with mocking everyone around it without some turnabout. You can’t have your cake and eat it. This is done.’ No more words were spoken. I didn’t even care whether or not he would print that vitriolic bile that had reared right from my bowels but I was glad that I had said it. His attitude had done nothing to display any sort of redeeming quality to his profession and after I finished speaking he still had a smug grin on his face. I knew then that he’d still got what he’d wanted. In the end, no matter what way I had phrased myself, he still had enough ammunition. ‘Thank you…Jack.’ The urge to punch was unbearable. ‘Come on Jack, let’s go.’ All this time Jessica had stayed quiet in the corner but it was now she decided to speak. Now, of all the times when she could have saved me. I stormed past her, pulling her with me uncontrollably as the journalist stayed sitting in the corner, scribbling notes to himself, all the while with a grin that could have reached to the moon. As I left the room I heard one last bit of conversation. ‘This is gold Johnny…pure gold… *** No words were said on the journey back to Kingston. There was no attempt by Jessica to scold me or punish me for my rash and irritable remarks made to the journalist. She was merely dead silent the entire time, eyes straight ahead in the car, the hands at two and ten, concentrated on the road ahead. Nothing would move her glare. I didn’t care. I was stewing in my own world of rage and regret, compounded by disappointment. I had let her down…big time. *** When we pulled in the driveway, the only words I heard from her was mumbling for the car to shut off. Nothing more, nothing less. She then promptly left the car and entered the house with no glance towards me at all. If I only had seen one glance, that would’ve helped. But I got nothing, so I was left to the stewing. I trampled up the stairs to the office, hoping to find Jessica working hard on her computer, about to blabber out some indecipherable jargon that would explain how I would climb to the pinnacle of the sport and that today’s event was only a minor glitch on the path to stardom. The light was out and nobody was residing within. I opened the door, perhaps to see her still at the desk, just working by her wits and no light, trying to keep me away but there was nothing at all. The room seemed even more chaotic on my side than ever before. Not only was my desk in a mess, the very confines of its surroundings made it all the more telling. Books, magazines, newspapers, cut outs of times when I had excelled, when things had gone my way and when they hadn’t were strewn on the floor. I noticed now that I had a very particular way of tip toeing to my desk so as not to disturb my mound of crap. And then so incomparable was her space, uncluttered, clean, warm and welcoming. Mine was sign to stay away because I really didn’t need anymore hassle. Knowing what I had to do, I began to clear it all up, piece by piece. It was all going well too until I remembered the script lying at the top of the heap, waiting to be delved into. Grumbling, I picked it up and looked at the title, written in bold on the front page: A TALE OF UNFINISHED BUSINESS BY THE WALSH BROTHERS Hmmph, an artsy kind of title, I’ll give them that, trying to pull in the more esoteric film goers but at the same time appealing to the popcorn viewers. There was the promise of action, but drama, explosions, but suspense. Who was I kidding, they were nerds. This couldn’t work. My loathing at the media juggernaut popped into my head again and I tossed the script carelessly onto the floor, letting numerous pages slip onto the floor and lay cast about, lost to the pattern and order they once held. I cursed at my luck, then slowly began to pick up each one, knowing I would have to at least return the script to them. They could use some sort of feedback, even if it was a big flat no. Then one page caught my eye…just one at that single moment and all was changed. Scene 2: Int. - Basement - Night The small drip, drip of water is heard in the background as the camera pans across the desolate basement, devoid of all light bare one shining in the centre of the room. Sat underneath, on a plain wooden chair is Main Character. He looks battered but not broken quite yet. Antagonist is standing in front of him, looking to continue whatever punishment. Antagonist You know, this could take all day if I felt like it…but I simply don’t have the time, nor the inclination. I think this might be something that’ll change your mind however. Boys. (clicks fingers) A man from the shadows opens the door and calls to someone from outside. In steps another man, leading somebody else. As the scene develops we cut to the shot of our Main Character looking shocked and appalled. Main Character My son! End of scene. Notes: needs to be fleshed out and provide more drama. Perhaps more sound effects and evolvement of music? The page hit me like a hammer. This wasn’t just something that piqued my interest like the original pitch had…this wasn’t just something that I could consider envisioning myself doing. No, before it had been an idea that I could toy around with, an avenue that had opened itself with exposure and everything with it…but this…. ….this was me. And at once all the flashes of the journalist from that accursed interview came rushing back. ‘Mr. Murphy, you’re in the limelight now…you can’t shy away if you feel like. People want to know…everything….’ The words ringed out hollow in the room as I kneeled in the darkness, its enveloping shadow taking me in…
