An Outside Chance

22 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-22 Strange isn’t it? People handle insignificance in so many different ways. There are some people who like to hide in the shadows, use their enigma as an advantage over people. With the mystique comes uncertainty, the uncertainty breeding fear, the fear becoming a weakness and the insignificance becomes a weapon. And then some people decide to ignore the insignificance. No-selling their obscurity and pretending they are more than they really are. Boasting about their abilities, boasting about how they are greater than their opponent, no matter how respected and skilled that person may be. Other people take the insignificance and use it as motivation. So the unknown motivates aspiration, the aspiration pushing the person to perform at their utmost, surpassing their usual achievements and gaining something more; more than okay, more than good, more than great, they become… …Exceptional. “Excuse me this is an emergency!” I scream rushing aimlessly through the seemingly endless white corridors past door after door, each looking as similar as the last. The pain of the injuries now subsided, the cold now nothing more than a bit of a runny nose, I'm now fully functional, alive and kicking. If you haven’t figured it out, I’m in a flaming hospital, I have an emergency: if I don’t get my friend to a doctor soon he may just die. I’m not going to let my friend just lie down and die. Now where’s this flaming doctor! “Doc…!” I scream at the top of my lungs, seeing a man in a white coat walk out of one of the never-ending walls through one of the limitless doors. The man is completely shocked as I run straight over to him and lay my friend into his arms. “What is ze problem?” the doctor tells me (he must be German or something). “What do you mean, what is the problem?!” I scream at the guy who obviously isn’t as clever as his doctorate suggests. “My friend… He’s dying!” The doctor looks blankly back at me, the wrinkles of his forehead twisting and contorting into as the most curved shape imaginable. With his eyebrows arched out of all dimension, he pauses for a few seconds, holds my friend up to me and says… … “But this is a plant…” What does that have to do with the price of Bratwurst? Is this guy a bit shocked to see a plant or something? I thought hospitals were equal opportunity these days! They will take white people, black people, yellow people, why not green people… er… plants…? “So… is that a problem…?” I ask him, and for some reason he’s started to chuckle to himself, a beaming grin amassing all over his pasty pinhead. “Ha, ha…! Zat is the funniest sing I haff heard in a long time!” He says enthusiastically, well I’m glad I’ve brightened this bloke’s day, but I have a medical emergency here! “OI…!” I scream at the eccentric German bloke. “Are you going to have to operate or what?” Another outburst of laughter, this guy’s sides are splitting, he better operate on himself after he’s dealt with my plant. After a period of laughter that seemed to last an eternity the guy has obviously gotten bored with the pained expression on my face and decides to take the situation seriously, he better do something soon, I’m not sure how long my plant has left. “You cannot take a plant to ze hospital…” he informs me, “we only treat people… not plants… haff you thought about seeing… you know… a psychiatrist, or some-sing?” “Been there, done that, got the certificate matey.” I inform the good doctor, he now seems to be back-peddling slowly, almost as if he didn’t want me to notice, but I have. “Well… I haff to go… now… yes… I must go…” he back-peddles faster and faster before turning and running. I wonder what that bloke’s problem is. Anyway, obviously the hospital can’t help me with my plant trouble. Maybe I should go someplace else. -----=====|=====----- “So… what is your problem, Mister Hurst…?” a voice asks dryly, precisely, with no emotion at all. “It’s my plant. I think he’s going to die.” I say lying horizontal, staring straight up at the ceiling. The ceiling light shines straight down at my face partially blinding me and making the whole situation feeling like an interrogation, the atmosphere made ever the more uncomfortable by the distinct mixed smells of dust and freshly painted walls almost making me wretch. “This… ‘plant’… is this something in your dreams? Something you think you see in front of you?” “Eh…? You what, mate?” I say sitting up from the couch and looking at the old-suited guy. “This plant is real! He’s over there? Look!” “Of course he is, Mister Hurst. Now tell me, how often do you see this plant of yours?” This guy is obviously not too bright either, and he won’t even look to see the bloody plant! It’s right over there for Christ sakes! The doctor said I should see a psychiatrist, well fix the bloody plant already! “I see him when I come home, only today I’ve been carrying him around trying to get him fixed.” “Mmmhmm… okay,” the man says as he gets up from his chair, taking a clipboard from his desk and wandering over to me. “Do you have sexual fantasies for this plant, Mister Hurst…?” “WHAT?” I scream at the guy as I get to my feet, “You’re not a psychiatrist mate! You’re nothing but a bloody pervert! I don’t care if you want to fix my plant or not, I’m off!” Puffing and snorting in disgust I grab the plant, place it under my arm, open the door and walk out. Bloody perverted doctors, I can’t stand them. I storm from the front door of the psychiatrist’s and down the busy street. I tell you what it’s bloody nippy, brass flaming monkeys I can tell you. I need to find someone to fix my plant, pronto. The bloody hospital didn’t help me, neither did the psychiatrist. And before the psychiatrist a bloody pet owner tried to feed it to the tortoises the murderous fuck! In desperation and possibly sub-consciously looking for warmth as well I step through the first door that comes my way, a door with smoke bellowing out of it, clouds of thick white and grey surrounding my body like a bubble as I stepped through it, suppressing the need to breathe anything in so that I don’t choke on it. Then I realised where I was, I had stepped into the smokiest place on the face of the planet. You thought that stepping into a small hut of ganja smokers in Jamaica was smoky, this place beats even that! With the wall completely obscured, not just with smoke, but also with stacks and stacks of television sets. Most of them with blue screens and a bunch of incomprehensible numbers scattered across them, the others with pictures of Horse Racing or Greyhounds, it was a local bookmakers. Well while I’m here I might as well have a flutter. “Hey man! You’re EDH!” A random voice from nowhere screams to me, then out of the clouds of smoke coming into my view is something I never thought possible… a couch potato… only without a couch… he wore cotton tracker bottoms and a yellow t-shirt with a black smiley face on it, stained all over with an ensemble of different condiments and suspect fluids, this man was the just a short, large, round ball of rubbish (that’s garbage to you Americans). And of course he had a fag in his mouth (don’t even think about saying it American readers!). I must be the only bloke in here not smoking… Anyway, the guy was obviously really excited to see me. Maybe he’s a wrestling fan or something. “Er… alo,” I say to the guy, at least blurting out some kind of greeting for the man, “do you want an autograph matey?” “No, man!” the guy says still way too energetic for a person of his slobberation (yes that’s a made up word, I know). The guy already smells of what he’s eaten over the last month we don’t need to add fresh sweat to that as well do we? “I just put a bet on you! You better win this week!” the guy said with authority, the seriousness clear in his voice despite the goofy grin on the guy’s face. I don’t know what to say. Is that a good thing or a bad thing? “Well… thanks… I guess…” I say, still a bit taken aback by the guy’s appearance and smell. The smell does knock you back a bit I must say. It brings tears to your eyes, I’m telling you. “I didn’t get very good odds though, man. I think everyone takes it as a given that you’re going to win the match. You’re going to obliterate that other guy.” “His name is Odium,” I say informing the sweat-ball. “And he is taking this contest very seriously. He doesn’t see himself as an underdog in the match, he has full confidence in what he’s doing, which makes him dangerous matey.” “I get ya man, you shouldn’t underestimate him. What are you here for anyway?” “I was going to place a bet. I think I’m going to go for…” I quickly scan the horses for the next race coming up. Ooh! There’s one that’s 100/1! I’d get 100 bucks! “I’m going for number 5 I think,” I say to the guy. He looks back at me completely disappointed. He shakes his head in disagreement, looks up at the screen again and then back to me. “Havabita Hope.” “Yup.” “You know he’s lost 37 races in a row, right?” Oh. No I didn’t know that. This guy obviously knows his horses, he looks like the kind of guy that spends his life gambling, either that or watching telly of course. Well, I’m not going to look a monkey in front of this bloke! So what if he’s lost 37 in a row, he can still win! “Well he’s due one then!” I say to the potato matter-of-factly, he shakes his head and lets me go and take the bet. I walk over to the counter, smiling to the lady behind it as I place the plant on the side to grab a betting slip. I scribble the bet down on the slip and hand it to the lady, she looks at it, gives a slight chuckle and goes to put the bet through. Smiling back to me the plump woman returns, “That’ll be a dollar then,” she says the smile widening from her chubby cheeks as I rummage around my pocket and pull a dollar from it, placing it on her hand. The clang of the cash register rings through my ears as she places the dollar into it, slamming it shut with a similar clang once again ringing through my ears. “Is that your plant?” she says, seemingly interested in it for some reason. Well, more interested than that flaming psychiatrist anyway. “Yeah…” I reply sadly, I don’t know, I guess it’s doomed to failure, a premature death for the plant that was my responsibility. Damn I suck at being responsible! “It looks pretty… well… dead…” she says with the smile on her face that never seems to leave. “Yeah, I’ve tried to find someone who could sort it out for me. I’ve been everywhere but nobody seems to know what I should do.” “Well… you know what I’d do” the motherly woman says pointing at the plant, “is trim that branch over there. Then just get it in some good light, and water it regularly, but not too much so as to drown it, just enough to wet the soil.” Wow… she seems to have a great deal of information on this subject! How about that, I go to the most professional people I could find on the subject and they couldn’t even point me in the right direction. I go to someone who probably dropped out of school, has no qualifications, no career path ahead of her and she could tell me exactly what it is I have to do. “Thanks a lot! I’ll give it a try!” “I do a lot of gardening in my spare time, so I deal with a lot of plants. It’s something to keep me occupied, you know.” “Heh…” You know what, that last sentence has brought something very serious to my attention. I never really gave this plant much of a thought before this week. Hell I went to my match with Jay without watering it when I was supposed to and then neglected it for three days! There was a single, subconscious reason for me paying so much attention to this plant this week and now I think I have realised exactly what. It’s something to keep me occupied, or even preoccupied. That’s the thing, since my win over Jay Phoenix people have been raving about what an upset it was, but also about how I’m really impressing people now because of it. Loads of people don’t just think I’m going to win this next match in the tournament, they EXPECT me to win the next round of the tournament. That’s quite scary, living up to the hype of being an actual contender in the tournament. Can I actually do that? Was the win against Jay a one-off? Do I have enough left in me after that match to beat this guy? Odium isn’t in the bracket semi-finals for nothing. He must have done something right. This isn’t going to be the walk in the park many people predicted. This one is going to be close. I just have to get my mind in gear, get myself properly prepared, and come out fighting. It sounds so easy when said like that, doesn’t it? There is a loud clap as the starting pistol sounds for my race, the gates swing open and the mass of horses fly out desperately trying to get a good start. They jostle for position, my horse making up the tail, but keeping with them around the first bend. Eight furlongs to go, the pace quickens, now Havabita Hope is falling back from the pack slightly, then as they approach six furlongs to go, with the front 3 pulling away from the pack, and the pack pulling away from my outside bet, I lose. Why is that? That’s because my flaming horse has pulled up. It’s flaming typical, what a waste of a dollar. “I’d hate to say I told ya so, man…” says the next-generation superhero Couch-Man with a smug grin on his face. “I bet on the favourite and it won…” Yeah… the jammy bastard! Okay, well I suppose I shouldn’t have been so stupid betting on the outsider like that. When you think about it, betting on the outsider really is a ridiculous thing to do. Like the insignificant people who mouth off about their abilities, saying that they’re going to burn their opponent to ashes and paint their face with it, saying that they’ll have the arena doused in blood, from their opponents, saying this, saying that, but proving nothing. Saying they’ve been understood, saying that they’ve just been unlucky, that nobody has realised their true potential, that they have never been tested, that the next chance they have will be the one they capitalise on. Yeah, okay. There is a slight chance that you’re an untapped talent. There’s the off-chance that you could pull something together and produce something great on the night. But for all the people that mouth off that they’re going to do this and that, they need to take heed to what happened to me today. I bet on the outsider, on the off-chance, on the slight chance that something might happen. I didn’t take into account one very important thing. There was a great chance, greater than all the half-chances, off chances and slight chances I had thought of. There was a great chance that the underdog is an underdog for a reason. One simple fact… Perhaps you haven’t been misunderstood, perhaps people have seen you, have seen what you’re made of, have given you opportunities. They have come to a conclusion. After taking all of the things they know about you into account, they have made you the outsider for one single reason: You’re just not that good. You can be happy with your six string, you can be content with your bottle of JD in your pocket, you can be grateful for your bike. I’m here to win the greatest wrestling tournament in the world, and I’m not going to let some outsider wade in and deny me of my destiny. Go take your vengeance out on somebody else.
