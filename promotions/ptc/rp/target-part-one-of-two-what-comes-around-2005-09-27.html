Target Part One of Two: What Comes Around…

27 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-09-27 The following roleplay has ties to a number of previous roleplay’s I have completed within the WWA. While this is a stand alone piece, if you want to familiarise yourselves with the back story to this roleplay, then here are the links: Moral Ambiguity (Part One of Three) Moral Ambiguity (Part Two of Three) Moral Ambiguity (Part Three of Three) What Comes Around There’s an old saying: What comes around, goes around. Sometimes the shit hits you and other times it doesn’t. Sometimes you can see it coming and other times you can’t. Sometimes it’s good and other times it’s bad. And when it does come around, you can either embrace if you want to, or ride it out and if you don’t. In either case, you have to make sure you survive, regardless of what it costs. I call it life. Sometimes bad shit happens, sometimes good shit happens, but either always comes around at one time or another. I saw that the bad shit was heading my way as soon as I set in motion the series of events that I knew would eventually lead to it. I performed the task, knowing that one day it would come back to bite me in the ass because I had no other options that I found acceptable under the circumstances. I knew that retrieving John Barrone’s daughter from his enemy Denis Kujo would one day come back to me. Both were very powerful crime lords and they are at war with each other. Kujo had lost a little ground and looked at the kidnapping as his only option to win the war. I took that option away from him and his empire will soon crumble around his ears as a direct resultant of that action. Desperate men do desperate things and what is more desperate than a man whose power base has been chopped away and all that remains is a determined, unwavering desire for revenge? It’s happening much too fast, I thought I had more time, predictability isn’t something life bows down to – at least not most of the time. And it is strange. Despite the bad shit falling down on me in my personal life, the good stuff is showering me in my professional career. I’ve been provided with the opportunity of a lifetime. Not only was I placed in a six way match at the WWA seminal pay per view Labour Day Brawl that, if won, would grant me a shot at the WWA television title, but I was one of the chosen from the very talented roster to represent the federation in a match that includes a plethora of other wrestling organisations. This will be my time to shine, if I can step up to the challenge and get it done. This match will hold the promise coming back to the Super Series pay per view next year to compete in the match for the Global Title. The Global Title! What an honour it would be if I were to come back next year and win it all. As I always say: Impossible is nothing, but improbable is everything. That is, of course, if I can get past my opponent, Xian Phillipse. He’s the WWA US champion for a reason. He’s a well-seasoned veteran of the ring who has climbed up the hierarchical ladder in the WWA and earned his title. That thought alone is just frightening and exhilarating at the same time. This will be one of the biggest challenges of my life. But I am ready for it. I embrace the challenge heartily and without hesitation. I just hope that’s enough. Last Stand Kujo’s Fortress. 2 weeks ago. “Cousin,” Wilson, Kujo’s bodyguard and relative, called rushing into his office. Kujo knew exactly what he was going to say before the words left his mouth and felt the familiar twinge of anxious nausea pinch at the pit of his stomach. “We’ve got to get the hell out of here! We can’t hold out for much longer.” Kujo wearily rubbed his eyes and stood. It had been a long day and not a particularly good one. He scooped up his customised silver and gold plated handgun from the desk in front of him with chilling familiarity and made his way to the very back wall of his office, just behind his desk. “Then let’s go, Wilson” “What about the others, Dennis?” Wilson asked, fiercely monitoring the corridor leading up to the room. Kujo pointed at the security camera at the far side of the room, with the barrel of his handgun. “There isn’t anybody else left, Wilson.” Wilson’s eyes follow the direction of Kujo’s handgun toward the security monitor, as Barrone’s men walk over his dead bodyguards and soldiers, toward them. “Oh fuck! We have to go now!” Kujo forced a grin, hardly believing the situation he found himself in. Only last week he’d had his enemy John Barrone eating out of his hand and now this. Not for the first time this week he thought about that damned freak in the black suit who had taken Barrone’s daughter from him and thought about the variety of ways he was going to torture him. He smiled at the thought, mildly amused, as he hit a button under a shelf, beside the permanently empty filing cabinet. A secret compartment opened up within the back wall, just big enough for a grown man to crawl into. “What in the hell…?” Wilson stated in disbelief. Kujo shook his head and regarded his office well, perhaps for the very last time. “I had it installed about a year ago, when all of this shit began, just in case,” He explained. “I never thought I’d actually have to use it though.” Kujo waved Wilson through, and still keeping a wary eye fixed upon the door Wilson obliged, crouching through the gap with impressive flexibility. As Kujo began to follow in suit, the first of Barrone’s men burst into the room haphazardly. Kujo quickly, with his well-seasoned reactions, gunned him down with a couple of shots in relative ease. The shots all caught the man in the sternum and he hit the ground immediately, dead. Kujo dared not linger any longer and quickly climbed into his escape route and made his way further along the tunnel, hitting the release switch for it to close up behind him. Wilson was waiting for him on the other side, with a concern look spread upon his face. “I heard gun shots,” He said. “Are you okay?” Kujo climbed out of the tunnel and tugged down upon his suit. He rested a reassuring hand roughly upon his cousin’s face. “Yes, I’m just fine.” “What is there for us to do now?” Wilson asked. Kujo smiled and made his way to the exit, leading out onto the streets. “Now I take my turf back. Now I take my revenge.” A Name The Rooftops of New York. Lower East Side. Present Day. I jumped across from the next building over, landing with considerable grace, and suspiciously glanced around, half expected a good number of black-suited governmental agents to appear out of nowhere and attempt to take me in. I waited for, in a high state of alert, for the better part of ten seconds before I was truly convinced that this wasn’t some sort of ambush. I’m paranoid enough normally, but the decidedly cryptic phone call I received from her, my angel, my contact with the mysterious employer who had me doing a number of tasks in order to assure my freedom from the government, made me a little more wary than usual. She never discussed any specifics over the phone, but this message a lot more cryptic than any of the others. She used a special code that we’d developed in order explain to me where we’d actually meet. The code was designed for use when her employer told her that the government were closing in on our operation and the phone lines might be compromised as a resultant. My paranoia was reinforced by the fact that she told that she’d be coming by foot for this meeting, which made me question whether getting here via the rooftop to rooftop was such a hot idea after all, but the hell else could I do? She’d also told me to come in the suit and it wouldn’t be that appropriate for me to walk the streets in what appeared to be an S&M gimp suit. I hated the damned thing myself, but aesthetics aside, its functionality can’t be questioned. It’s a black suit, with padded Kevlar underlay, which makes it heavier than a normal suit by a about a pound or suit, but with my enhanced abilities I can handle it okay. The suite was designed by my mysterious employer to cope with the stress my actions placed upon, plus it was bullet proof, which also came in handy, much to my chagrin. The essentially covered the top half of my face, to provide me with complete anonymity and with a specialised governmental taskforce chasing me, such a precaution was a considerably prudent idea. I heard someone entered the alleyway to the right of the building and the clang of somebody grabbing the bottom rung of the fire escape ladder. I walked to the edge of the building and glanced over to the side. My angel’s face glanced up at me and I couldn’t help but smile. “Jonathon,” she called, confidently stepping onto the rooftop. “We’ve got a problem.” She used my first name. That meant she didn’t think this building was compromised in the slightest, which made me feel a little better about being up here. “I gathered as much.” She smiled. “Sorry about freaking you out with the code, but security as been compromised. We don’t know by how much yet, hence the precaution.” I nodded in response, accepting the explanation at face value. “What kind of security breach are we looking at here?” “Somebody has managed to get contact to our employer through some of his people,” she explained, “and our employer is more than a little disturbed by it.” I shuffled the idea around in my head. “Like that Michael Powell incident a few weeks back?*” My angel nodded. “Kind of. However, this time you have a history with the contact.” “A history?” I asked, trying hard to imagine whom it might be. “It’s Dennis Kujo, the crime lord,” She stated. “As you probably remember, he kidnapped the daughter of his rival and sworn enemy John Barrone in order to gain the upper hand in the war they’d initiated.” I winced, remembering how close some of those bullets came to putting me down permanently. “Vaguely.” “You retrieved the daughter and left Kujo without a leverage in the war,” Barrone has made his final move and Kujo’s forces have failed to stop him.” “Good,” I promptly responded. “Couldn’t have happened to a nicer guy…” “Regardless of what he deserves,” She replied. “He wants his revenge.” “Revenge?” I asked. “On us?” “No,” she answered, “on John Barrone and our employer wants us to help him.” The sentence struck well like a well-placed punch to the gut. “Help him? Why?” “Barrone blackmailed our employer in order to get his daughter back and our employer was never happy about that,” She explained. “Plus, it is in our employers financial interests that these two opposing factions continue their war.” I don’t like the sound of that. “And that’s where we come in, right?” She smiled, knowing only too well that I didn’t like this situation one bit. I could tell by her face that she did not like it either. “Right.” I shook my head in disapproval. “We shouldn’t be helping these low life thugs fight their war and you know it.” “What should we be doing?” She asked, quizzically. “And don’t know about what you want to do, but I want to find out about my past,” I replied, not really knowing how to answer her. “I want to concentrate on my wrestling career.” She saw right through the guise, as if she’d had plenty of practice before and for all I knew, she had. “You realise that if John Barrone gets all the power he desires, when Kujo falls, a lot of innocent people will be hurt.” “And if we allow this war to continue at all, the fire fight will spill out onto the streets and a lot of innocent people will be killed,” I countered. She clearly had no response to my comment and consequently tried to work out some other form of argument to persuade me. She doesn’t realise that she doesn’t need another argument. “But I don’t much care about any of that,” I quickly added. She looked quite saddened by this, but I continued regardless. “I’ll do it, but I want something from you that you promised me a long time ago.” “What exactly do you want?” She asked, getting a little uneasy. “To know more about my past,” I told her. “And to know your name.” She turned away and folded her arms. Although I’ve never seen her do it before, I automatically know that is her defensive position of choice. “I can’t tell you much about your past,” She began, her voice lowered now, with a distinct serious tone. “Why not?” I asked, curious. “The truth?” She turned to me, still maintaining her defensive stance. “You’re not ready for it.” I glared at her, silently. “You did some things, some terrible things. Unspeakable things. I don’t even want to think about it, much less tell you about it,” She explained. I felt a twinge of nausea pinch my stomach. I don’t know what upsets me more, the fact that I have a terrible past or that I’m not at all shocked by it. “But,” she turned to me and smiled. The smile made me feel more vulnerable than I’ve ever felt before and I found myself quite disturbed by that notion, “my name is Aeryn,” As soon as she said it, I forgot everything else that was bothering me and smiled. Truth be told, I didn’t figure she’d tell me much about my past; I just wanted to hold out for her name. “Well Aeryn,” I said, trying see if me saying the name out loud brought back any more resolute memories about my currently unexplainable connection to this beautiful woman. It didn’t. “What’s next?” “Our employer has organised a meeting with Kujo,” She explained, “we should probably set off right away.” I nodded at her. “You going like that?” She grinned and wriggled out of the backpack she’d strapped herself into. For some strange reason, I hadn’t noticed it before. She opened it up and pulled out her matching black Kevlar padded suit. I smiled at her. “Won’t you be a little warm putting that over your street clothes?” “I’m not planning to,” She replied, starting to strip. “Well turn around” Reluctantly, I did as she asked. Capture The streets of New York. Outside the Adders Coil Nightclub. Ten Days Ago Johnny Marconi left the Adders Coil nightclub and automatically lit a cigarette. He’d wasted his last one in some asshole’s eyeball back in the club. The guy owed Barrone a lot of money and he was late with his payments. After what Johnny had just done to him, he wouldn’t be late again and he’d think twice about fucking around John Barrone again. As Johnny walked past the plethora of people still attempting to get into the club, he eyed a couple of the girls queuing in the line to get into the club with fond memory. The tall blonde and the shorter brunette waved at him with enthusiasm. Johnny smiled and waved back; he remembered Jeanne and Carmella well. He made his way along the street, heading toward his ride parked further up the street. On other nights, he would’ve been more than tempted to escort those ladies back to his place for a little play time, but with what’s happening between his boss and rival crime lord Dennis Kujo’s territory, Barrone didn’t want any fuck up’s. And Johnny Marconi had no intention of ignoring his leader’s commands; he was there when Barrone confronted Leo Coker about messing around on the job. Not a pretty sight. As he walked his instincts suddenly kicked into action; something was wrong. It was too quiet and he didn’t like it. He picked up his pace. His car was in sight now; just a few steps more. Whoever was watching him must have been alerted by him altering his speed; the screeching of car tyres told him that much. His hand instinctively slipped down the back of his trouser, gently finding the trigger of the gun he always placed there. The car had already stopped and a couple of sturdy guys got out. Johnny smiled when he saw they weren’t armed and pulled his gun out. He still couldn’t put names to the faces, but he had an inkling Dennis Kujo was behind this. Who else would try anything so bold? “What the fuck do you guys want?” Johnny asked. The big guy at the center of them stepped forward. He was bald and had a moustache and beard combination that made him look increasingly like the pro wrestler ‘Stone Cold’ Steve Austin. “You, actually.” Johnny didn’t even hear someone sneak up to him from behind, but he sure as hell felt the sturdy metal object slam down hard against the back of his neck. He hit the floor like a tonne of bricks. Just before he lost consciousness, he saw the familiar face of Dennis Kujo standing over him, rather menacingly. “Johnny Marconi! Fancy seeing you here!” Kujo grinned and glanced over toward the ‘Stone Cold’ Steve Austin look a like. “Get him in the car.” The darkness took and he remembered nothing more after that.
