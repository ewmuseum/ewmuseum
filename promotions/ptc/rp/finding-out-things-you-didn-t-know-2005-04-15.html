Finding out things you didn't know

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-04-15 The roar of the plane awoke me as I sat in the chair next to the window. The rain hit the window hard enough to make my eyes focus on it more then anything. Outside it looked bad. Just like the streets of New York City on an afternoon day. The thunder, blasting so hard that it made the sound of the plane fade away in from my ears. It had been two days since my fight with my uncle. I felt bad deep inside but what was said was said. Nothing I could do about it right now. What I had to do right now was to get focused on the task at hand. That was the PTC Cruiserweight Title Tournament. Ever since I was a little kid I’ve always hit stuff head on. Looking at it with my own two eyes and nothing else. I remember when I first heard about wrestling. I didn’t know much about it but it fascinated me. The high flying. The submission moves. The heart that everyone has when they step into a right in front of all those fans. It gave me chill bumps to even think about. And I knew what I wanted to do. It took me three years of blood, sweat, and pain to my body to understand what wrestling really meant. I gave a soft sigh as the plane shifted left in the air. The sounds of a few people in front of me made me look over at a couple that was sitting there in their seats, trying to have a good time with their books and puzzles. I also figured out that you have to give up more then you get back. Family is set aside in a business like this. The more you travel, the more heartache you put on yourself and the one’s you love. “This is your captain speaking,” a voice said over the speakers. “We will be touching down in Chicago shortly.” The people shook a little in their seats, trying to get their stuff ready before we touched down. Funny, most people had stuff from their family with them. Picture’s, toys, dolls, and other stuff. Looking down at my lap, all I had was the very clothing on my back and all the bad memories in my head. Not everything was good for me. Growing up, my family never stayed in one place for very long. The longest we ever lived was the very house that my mother is staying at now. I never had any real friends other then Karla. She was the only person that I could trust over the years and she moved just for me. The rain kept coming down hard as the lights from Chicago came into view of my window. I could feel the plane coming down slowly. It’s wheels beginning to drop from there hook station. “We are now preparing to land in Chicago folks. Can you please begin to fascine your seatbelts.” The flight attendant said as she walked passed me. I buckled my waist with the proper stuff before closing my window blinds. I didn’t want to see the ground view coming in. All I wanted to do was get off the plane and get to my hotel. But I still couldn’t get the words that I said to my uncle out of my head. “You know what Uncle, if you think I shouldn’t fight then maybe I shouldn’t be here.” The plane roared a little louder, shaking several times from side to side like you would do to a chocolate milk bottle. My heart speed up inside as I grasped on to the arm brass next to me. “First time on a plane?” The pretty lady asked next to me. “Yes…”, slowly turning my eyes towards her. “Not afraid. Just don’t want to die.” The woman seemed calm as can be. Like she had done this several times. “Heaven sakes girl. Your night going to die,” the woman said, giggling with the response. “Think of this plane as a caring person. They won’t let anything happen to you.” The plane touched down, letting the wheels coming fully down to the earth and it’s concrete floor. My blood was still pumping straight to my head. I hadn’t felt like this since I was eleven. Back then, I was in a bad accident with my family. I can only remember a few things but I can still picture the car afterwards. The battery about a hundred yards away from the car with the passenger side door all the way in. The windshield was cracked and broken into little pieces with the top of the stirring wheel bend all the way down till it was straight away. I wasn’t afraid to show my fear. For me it was a way to release my pain in another way instead of fighting. As long as I could remember, I was never a fighter. I was always the shy type in middle school. Even in the beginning of high school, I was always the one wearing the cheerleader outfit. Standing on the sidelines and cheering on the football players. The one riding the horse instead of smoking or doing drugs. I was the lady, the respectable one out of all the people. The plane had come to a stop. It was time to get off. I slowly pushed my way up to a standing position. Looking around at the massive amount of different faces. Some young, some old, and some that didn’t know were the hell they were just like me. “Well, I hope you have a safe trip.” The lady next to me said before she began to walk away. “You’re not the only one.” I said. Answering underneath my breath. *********** 2 Hours Later The weather was still rainy. Coming down even harder now as I walked into my hotel room. Bright white walls made my eyes jump back into my head. Panning the massive room that I had gotten, I first noticed the giant mirror that hung up on the wall next to the window. Brass outlining into a flower shaped body, it made me give a smile. I had never been in a fancy hotel like this. It smelled fresh and it was almost spotless. I walked in, closing the door behind me. Next to me sat a red and white chair. Brass legs that made the chair taller then it appeared. A sat my bag down and walked towards the king size bed. It was huge and took up a lot of the space. The place didn’t seem like home but to me it felt better then anything that I had felt in a while. It was rough being gone from home. Even though I felt a little home sick, I knew the time off was needed. I looked around, feeling my gut with a pain in it before looking at the phone. “Hmmm….”, I said, wondering if I should. I picked up the phone and began to dial Karla’s number. It rang about three times, letting me listen to every drop of rain water bang against the glass window. “Hello?” Karla said with a sigh voice. “Hey it’s me Karla,” responding with a not so happy voice of my own. I paused for a moment, not really knowing why I wanted to call but I felt I needed to. Maybe I was home sick a little or maybe I was just missing Karla. “How’s uncle doing?” Still feeling guilty over what I said to him. “Not so good.” She said with a sad tone of voice. “He’s not really done anything the last few days. I think he really felt bad with what you said.” The words I didn’t want to hear. I reached up and took hold of my curly hair and flopped it back against my shoulders. My heart beaded a little faster, making me want to cry somehow. “God, can you…” Pausing for a moment to catch my breath. “…Tell him I’m sorry.” “Sure,” Karla responded. Hanging up the phone, it really hit me. What if tonight was the last night he ever lived. Would he think that is how I felt about him? Would he think that I hated him somehow or that I didn’t respect him? Would he ever forgive me in his heart for doing something without his permission? So many things going through my head that I just needed to get out. I slowly got up, walking for the door, not knowing really were to go but I knew I couldn’t stay here. ******************* An Hour Later The room was filled with smoke. Covering up my eyes from being able to see. The smell of sweat and beer raced through my nose like a fright train on a hot trip. There were a massive amount of people in here. Some old and most were young. Though they seemed to be to young to me. For me, I had just turned twenty-one not to long ago. It wasn’t an age that I was happy to be. So what that I could drink. Drinking didn’t really give me that rush like all my other friends. Though I didn’t come for the boozes at all, I just came to think. Looking around at the area of the bar I noticed a seat to the far right. A few guys staring at me as I walked by. There ice blue eyes looked to be jogging right out of their eye sockets, trying to impress on my body, mainly the two little breast that were on my chest. God, why do guys look their first I’ll never know. Sitting down, I noticed the bartender. Wearing a short sleeve blue shirt with blue jeans on, he looked me in the eyes before walking over. “Now how can a beautiful girl like yourself be in a bar like this?” His smooth lips asked before leaning down for eye level. I felt shy but it almost got my mind off the topic at hand. “Well, guess it’s luck I guess.” Trying not to draw a crowd of people by with a hard response. “Well what can I get you?” He asked, smiling back at me, showing his white teeth that you could see out of. “Shot of Jose…” Looking away from him as I did. He was nice. Not the kind that I thought would be in here. Though I never expected to meet anyone tonight. I just wanted a place to think where I wasn’t alone doing it. “Here you go.” He said as he gently sat the shot down. “So where you from?” “Texas…” I said as I downed the shot like it was apple juice. “Texas? Man, you are far from there.” Laughing over the loud music that was playing behind us. The place felt hot. Almost like a sauna. I took a look around one more time before pushing the shot glass forward for one more. The bartender gladly filled it up, before handing it back to me with a nice smile. “So why you doing out here if I can ask?” His shy sounding voice said. “Well I’m a fighter if you want to know.” Grabbing the shot and downing it quickly. His eyes widened up a little. Completely shot it seemed to me that I said that. I wanted to giggle but the burning feeling from the Tequila was really coming in. “Fighter?” Swallowing it seemed as he asked. “Boxer?” “No. Wrestler. I’m here for the PTC Tournament Show.” Pressing the shot glass away from me and towards him. “Oh yeah, I heard about that. I bet your family is happy for you.” Grabbing the glass for yet another shot as he said that. Family? I forgot my family, or should I say, they forgot me along time ago. Deep down inside I wish that I could go back and maybe fixed what happen between us but what is down is down. Though it doesn’t matter, there the kind that won’t change. “I guess. I’m just not sure they’ll forgive me.” Lowering my head down a little as my sadness came out just a little. My eyes began to water up, still remembering what Karla told me earlier. “Come on. What would make them upset with you?” Setting down the shot in front of me as he responded. I took the shot in my hand once more and downed it once again, this time making an even bigger face from the burn. God how I hated that feeling but my dad always said, nothing is better then a good burn, or did he mean working out? Either way, my mind was starting to get off the fact that I was sad. I slammed the shot down on the table and leaned forward, setting my arms on the table. “Well my family never wanted me to wrestle. They had this vision of me being something better.” Giving the quote hand signal with both my hands. He laughed a little from the facial that I gave him. The three shots were having a hard effect on me as my blood pressure felt like it was going straight out of the roof. The music was starting to get louder and louder in my ears as the people kept coming up to the bar beside me. I wish I was just back home. The city life here was just starting to get to me. “Well I’m sure someone is happy you are doing this,” he said as he walked away, going to catch a customer near by. I went to take the shot again but something inside of me made me stop. What did he say? How could I be so stupid. I wiped my hand over my face as I thought about what he said. Someone who cared about me, Karla. I took the shot off the table and quickly gave it a down, drinking it faster then all the rest. It hit me hard again, this time making my chest burn even harder. Slamming the glass down so hard that the people and the bartender turned to me. “I’ve got to go. See you…”, I said as I got up off the chair. “Wait…”, he said trying to get my attention. I couldn’t wait. I stepped off the chair and to the floor, tripping on my own two feet. I went straight down, slamming my head right on the oak wood floor. The pain went right to my head and the room began to go a little dizzy. The music in my ear began to stop and the crowd noise picked up to the point that it seemed like everyone was standing over me. I looked up to see the bartender standing right over me. “Get some help now…”, he screamed at the top of his lungs. My eyes began to go hazy even more. Forgetting everything as the colors and the noise began to fade away. The pain was to much, boiling all through my body as I began to fade out, forgetting everything else as I laid there on that hot warm floor.
