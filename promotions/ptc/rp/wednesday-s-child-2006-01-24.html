Wednesday's Child

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-01-24 Monday's child is fair of face, Tuesday's child is full of grace, Wednesday's child is full of woe, Thursday's child has far to go. Friday's child is loving and giving, Saturday's child works hard for a living, But the child born on the Sabbath Day, Is fair and wise and good and gay. - - - - - - - - - - - Previously "Ember?" The psychologist’s shaky voice brought him, almost, back to his senses and he bit of the hysteria that he felt rising within him before it could take hold. She was still sitting on the chair, still clutching the cane between her hands, and her face was filled with a mixture of fear and incomprehension. "Ember" she repeated shakily, "what just happened?" Getting to his feet Ember stumbled over, his legs like lead, to Carole and knelt down beside her, half falling against the chair. She reached out to steady him and gasped as her hands connected with his latex covered face. So much for trying to get an easy medical pass Ember thought wryly to himself, realising that the mask was now the least of his worries. He didn’t know how much Doctor Gordon had heard, or could have heard, but he knew that she knew too much. "Are you wearing a mask" she asked in confusion, "is that what she meant about it not suiting you?" Ember sighed softly, Carole’s words confirming that she had indeed heard Raven’s words. He didn’t know how it was possible, but he didn’t need to, he just knew that there was only one thing that he could possibly do now; tell her the truth. Tell her everything. "She wasn’t talking about the mask," he said as he settled back on his heels, holding his head in his hands, breathing hard as he forced the words out, "she was talking about the body I am wearing." - - - - - - - - - - - Now As the adrenaline left my body I felt the very familiar feeling of weakness overcome me. Every single match that I had ever had had ended in this way. From the moment that I stepped foot inside the ring my body functioned on over-drive, and I felt no pain. Nothing could stop me, nothing could even slow me down; but it always ended the same. Mere moments after the final bell had rung out, and I had made my way backstage, the adrenaline suddenly stopped flowing and I paid for the advantage that my body had given me as lactic acid took over and pain filled every muscle. That was how I felt now. My stomach turned in knots and a cold sweat ran down my spine, causing shivers to play through my muscles. Mild nausea gave me an acidic aftertaste as I swallowed, trying to moisten the inside of my mouth, which had suddenly gone dry; my tongue feeling like it was two sizes too big. The strength in my legs faded and they buckled under me. I collapsed, wordlessly, beside the chair in which Carole Gordon sat and rocked slowly back and forth. "What" she asked in confusion, "did you say, what are you wearing?" I knew that her hearing was better than good; it was excellent. I had never believed the old wife’s tale that blind people had better senses to make up for the lack of vision, but after only meeting Doctor Gordon that evening I had already realised that she could hear things that most people couldn’t. I hadn’t expected, however, that to include people that no one else apart from me even knew existed. "Do you want a drink?" I asked, ignoring her question. I knew that I had decided to be honest with her, that as she had already been privy to something that I hadn’t even shared with my best, my only, friend it made sense to tell her everything that I knew. Something made me hold back, though, something made me reconsider. I knew that it didn’t matter that she had definitely heard what I had said because her mind, her rational mind, wouldn’t allow her to process the words as fact. How could she, how could she understand how a person's body could be worn like a tailored suit and discarded at will? She couldn't, I realised, and so didn't even try. "What?" she said, her voice still shaky. I got to my feet, pulling myself up with the aid of the chair and chuckled slightly as I realised that I was as weak as a kitten. Or a new born wolf cub I thought to myself, shuddering as I recalled the vista that I had been forced to witness, to actually experience, only moments before. "I said do you want a drink?" I repeated as I looked around the office. "I know that I could do with one." I moved towards the bookcase, taking a wide circuit around the desk, apprehensive in case she should return while I was standing too close, hoping that one of the closed doors would be hiding something to drink. "No I don’t want a damn drink, Ember," she stated, her voice rising, "I want to know what the Hell is going on!" I ignored the question, reaching out to open one of the doors and smiling weakly as I hit pay dirt on the first attempt. A bottle of whiskey and some glasses were behind the door and I took them out as I turned to face the doctor once more. The colour was starting to come back to her face, and her breathing had slowed to a more regular level but the poise of her body – the tilt of the head, and how squared and taut her shoulders were – gave away the strain that she was holding back. Pouring some whiskey into one glass I knelt down beside her, placing the bottle and second glass on the floor beside me, and slowly held the glass out, folding one of her hands around it. She flinched slightly, her composure quickly regained as she realised that it was only me, and then held tightly to the glass. "So, are you going to tell me?" she asked, biting of the words before taking a gulp from the glass. I watched her face creases as the heat of the alcohol hit the back of her throat, marginally impressed that she didn’t gag. I considered her words, the request, for a few seconds as I watched her intently. "No," I said with a sigh, watching the frown appear on her forehead and continued speaking quickly, forestalling her from interrupting. "Not yet at least, I don’t want to talk about it." "Oh that is just great," she said with a flash of irritation, "some sort of ghost takes over my office and you don’t want to talk about it!" Knocking back the remainder of the whiskey in the glass, Carole grimaced again as the alcohol hit her stomach. "She wasn’t a ghost" Ember stated quietly with another sigh, "she was real. Very real." "Then tell me what she was Ember!" Leaning forward as she spoke the doctor reached out until her hand touched Ember’s masked face. She rested her hand against it and leant in closer to him, her blind eyes staring into nothingness, as they seemed to bore a hole into his face. Reaching up with his own hand Ember gently, but firmly, pulled Carole’s from his face. Standing up again, feeling energy starting to move through his body once more, he turned and walked over to the window, pulling the drape back slightly and looking out into the night air. "No." The word was soft but I was sure that she would hear me. The sigh of frustration from her was sign that I was correct. "Well at least that settles things," she stated in resignation. I turned around to face her, confused by her words. "What do you mean?" I didn’t move from the window and she didn’t make any attempt to face me. I supposed that there was no point when she couldn’t see me anyway. "You said that you needed medical clearance to wrestle," she pointed out, matter-of-factly, "but after this you won’t need it anymore, will you?" Even though she asked it as a question her tone made it obvious that it was rhetorical. I think that I surprised her by actually answering. "Of course I will," I argued, emphatically, "now more than ever." "You have a poltergeist after you and you are worried about a little wrestling match?" she spluttered in obvious confusion, "I don’t even understand you can even think about them at the same time!" "I told you," I reiterated, "she isn’t a ghost, nor a poltergeist." I knew that it wouldn’t matter what I said, unless I told her everything, holding back nothing of myself, she would never understand. Even if I did tell her everything there was no guarantee that it would help. She might just think that I was crazy after all. "Besides which it doesn’t matter what she is, I have a job to do" I explained, trying to change the subject, "and when you are facing someone who once thought that he was a fantasy game character come to life and a sumo who likes to pretend to be a demon then my brand of life’s weirdness can’t even compete!" The attempt at humour fell flat on its face as the doctor simply sat there, staring blindly into the air. I walked over to her, allowing the drape to fall back over the window that was coated with a thin film of condensation from my breath, and sat down in the chair opposite her. Her head followed me as I moved, blindly tracking my motion. "Seriously, Doc" I breathed, as sincerely as I could, "this tournament is important to me, more important than I can tell you." "Let me guess," she said, sarcasm heavy in her voice, "something else that you can’t tell me?" I sighed as I realised that it wouldn’t matter what I said anymore, that I couldn’t win this war of the words. I had, albeit unknowingly, brought this woman into something that didn’t concern her, something that she couldn’t understand, and instead of even attempting to help her with answers I was simply leaving more questions. "I would rather not" I said as gently as possible, trying not to annoy her any more than I already had, "but you are the shrink, what do you normally talk to your clients about?" I hoped that by offering her a choice of changing the subject herself, it would be like offering a white flag. "You mean apart from supernatural women?" she quipped, ironically and I couldn’t help the fleeting smile that played across my face. I may have only just met her but I admired the strength of will that she obviously had. She had gone through things in one evening that could, and had, drive most people insane. "Yeah, apart from that," I replied, trying to lighten the tone, "what do you normally start off with, you know, to ease people into this?" "Oh that one is simple" she replied, her body language relaxing as she fell into an automatic pilot mode of her known career territory, "I would start with asking about your childhood, your family." - - - - - - - - - - - A single small fire illuminated the domed building. Shadows played over the thatched roof, the breeze blowing through gaps in the log walls fanning the flames and sending the darkness spiralling around the confines of the room in some weird parody of the dance macabre. A huddled figure, small and silhouetted by the flames in front of him, sat behind the fire, a leather bound book held in one hand. His features were indistinct, all that could be seen of him was long flowing hair that moved as if with a life of its own thanks to the heat from the flames and the wind from between the gaps in the wood. Low, rumbling chanting came from this figure, atonal yet vibrant. As he, for the voice was high pitched yet obviously masculine, continued with his chanting he placed his hand over the edge of the fire and a stream of powder fell from it. A single word, more a sound than anything discernible, thrummed out from the figure. "u-yv-tlv" Over and over the one phrase was repeated and the flames turned gradually blue, dancing slower and almost seeming to stop at moments. As the figure continued to chant his breath was visible, as if the temperature in the lodge had dropped dramatically. His hand fell to his side, and with a pause as breath was gathered he changed the tone, and the word itself. "ka-lv-gv" Once more the phrase was repeated though this time as the figure’s hand allowed powder to fall into the flames they rose up redder and warmer than before and a haze of heat filled the air in front of the youth. Without stopping for a breath this time he changed the tone again. "u-ga-no-wa" The chaotic weaving of the flame slowed, not quite stopping but becoming calmer as they almost seemed to drain of colour until they were mostly white. Lifting up more powder another deep breath was taken in and then expelled in a low, guttural chant. It sounded like nails on a chalkboard, but the youth held it. "wu-de-li-gv" Already small, the insides of the lodge become darker and more oppressive as if the shadows themselves were reaching out to enclose everything that they could grasp. The youth paused in his movement, his voice beginning to waver uncertainly, and then he allowed the powder to finally fall into the flames. Instantly they became dark, still writhing but this time almost with a life of their own, malignant and menacing. The chanting choked off as the youth crept back from the flames, his face hidden in the shadows that the dark fire gave off evident from his body language. His back hit the wall and he could go no further, as the flames almost seemed to bend towards him, reaching out, struggling to grab him … "Great Spirits end this now!" Light filled the room as a door burst open and a voice, strong and confident, rang out with a power of command. The flames instantly died down, returning to a dim orange and red, nearly burnt out and nothing but embers. A hand reached across them and pulled the figure to his feet. Long auburn hair framed a tanned face as a young man, no more than a boy, stared out with forlorn eyes of piercing green. "I am sorry grandfat…" he started to say but was cut off as the strong voice interrupted. "What have you done Hakan," he was asked, the tone of shock unmistakeable, "what have you done?" An older man, long white hair tied back in a neat braid, towered over the youth, pulling him closer with strong and powerful hands, unheeding of the look of pain that shot over the boy’s face as his fingers dug into the muscles in his shoulder. "You were meant to be studying" the older man stated through clenched teeth, "I trusted you and you betrayed that trust!" "The names of the herbs were boring, grandfather" the youth started to explain, "and besides I didn’t think that anything would happen!" The slap that hit him across the face was not powerful; in fact it hardly impacted at all. The fact that he had been struck, the fact that the man who loved and protected him had raised his hand at all, suddenly made him aware of just how much he had transgressed. "Nothing would happen?" the older man asked incredulously, "nothing?" He forced the boy to turn around and stare across the dying flames to a dark section of the room, that moments before had been empty, still shadowed. A small figure could be seen there, huddled in the darkness, staring back out at them with eyes the colour of milk through a curtain of long pale-blonde hair. "Something happened my son," the man whispered, his voice fading and suddenly sounding both old and weary, "something that you are going to have to deal with the consequences of." The figure in the shadows didn’t move, didn’t seem to breathe, but from its mouth came one word, hesitant but very clear. "Grandfather?" - - - - - - - - - - - "My childhood" Ember sighed, barely above a whisper, "my family?" He hadn’t actually expected that to be the topic that the doctor would choose and he knew that it raised more questions than it did answers. Everything was just too complicated, too raw, for him to start to talk about. For over a year he had lived the life that he had chosen to live, had wanted to live rather than the one that had been forced upon him so long ago. Fighting back the bitter sting of tears before they could fall from his eyes, and then chuckling inwardly as he realised that the doctor wouldn’t have been able to see them anyway, Ember sat back, taking a deep breath to steady himself. "Nothing unusual there doc," he stated with forced glibness, "I was brought up by my grandfather, just me and my brother." He was surprised by how level his voice was and wasn’t sure if he should be pleased or appalled at how much of an accomplished liar he had become. "Born on a normal Wednesday afternoon, and had a normal life thereafter." "Why do I get the feeling that, yet again, you are not telling me everything?" Carole cocked her head to one side, a resigned smile on her face. As Ember started to argue she held up one hand to stop him. "Listen Ember, I KNOW that you are not telling me everything, I can hear it in your voice when you mention your family, actually you are not telling me anything at all, but I want you to know that you can. You can tell me anything." The sincerity in her words rocked Ember as he realised that he believed her. It had been such a long time seen he had trusted anyone at all, and even the one person that he did trust, Khaled, he didn’t tell anything of his past too. Ember’s life had started one year ago, as far as anyone was concerned, and that was how Ember himself liked it. At this moment though, after everything that the past few weeks had brought, he realised something else as well. He wanted to tell her, to share his life story with her. "Not yet" he whispered, knowing that she could hear him, "not just yet." He got to his feet and picked up his jacket, pulling it on as he walked towards the door. "I have to go, I still have a match to prepare for you know." "Will you come back?" she asked, the offer of hope in her voice. "Yes" Ember replied, knowing as he said it that he also meant it, "yes I will." "Then answer one thing for me, and promise me that you will tell me the truth" she said, and Ember heard the iron resolve in her voice. He knew without her saying it that if he lied, if he chose not to answer, then she would close her door to him, and he would lose whatever it was that she was offering, whatever comfort she could give to him. "Well," he countered with a small smile, "you tell me this first, are we still working under a doctor-patient confidentiality clause?" Carole laughed, her voice clear and pure and the tension left the room. "Well first of all, yes, we are working with that premise in mind," she stated with another laugh, "but even if we weren’t do you think that I would jeopardise my career by talking about ghosts and phantasms?" "No, I don’t suppose so," Ember laughed, realising the truth of her words, "so ask away and you get one answer." "Your brother, "she stated quietly, "what happened to him?" Ember paused, breathing shallowly as he took in her question. He silently reached out for the handle and opened the door, not seeing Carole’s shoulders lump in defeat as her hearing picked up the sounds of him leaving the room and realising that he wasn’t going to answer. As he left the room, however, and without turning around to face her, Ember replied, just loud enough for her to hear. "My brother?" he repeated softly, "oh, I killed him." He closed the door behind himself and Carole sat in silence as she heard him open the main door and leave the building. After a few seconds she got to her feet and slowly made her way to the window, pulling the drapes open as the street-lamps from outside highlighted her face. A faint smile played over her lips as she reached up and touched the pane of glass, the condensation from Ember’s breath still visible. Her fingers traced a shape in the mist, a vaguely bird-like form, and as her blind eyes stared sightlessly out into the night sky they almost seemed to flash with an inner, yellow fire. - - - - - - - - - - - Nursery rhyme by Mother Goose
