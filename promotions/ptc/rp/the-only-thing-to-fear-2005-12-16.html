The only thing to fear …

16 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-16 I clenched my fists, the jagged nails biting deep into the palms of my hands, as hard as I could. I smiled, teeth baring feral-like, as I felt my skin rip and blood from the four small tears slowly starting to drip down my wrists. Every beat of my heart forced more blood out and I traced its flow down my arm, imagining that the rivulets were involved in some macabre race. At least I would have done had I been able to see. Kneeling here -wherever here actually was - in my own stench, I was completely and utterly surrounded by darkness. Enveloped in it. So complete, so all consuming, that it almost felt as if it had a life of its own. Sometimes, I am not sure how often, I even found myself holding my breath just to make sure that the breathing that I heard was really just my own. Considering that no one had ever answered me when I called out I was pretty certain that I was alone. I actually don’t know what I would have done if someone did reply … if the sounds of breathing continued after I held my own I mean; sob in relief or die from terror perhaps. So far, though, it was something that I didn’t have to worry about, as there was no one else here in the darkness. Just my pain and me. That sounds wrong, I am not a masochist, you understand, it is not that I want to – or enjoy – inflict pain on myself but when the darkness becomes to compressing, when I feel that I am losing myself within its infinite embrace, only the pain brings me back. Only the pain reminds me, reassures me, that I am actually alive. You see, they say that a picture paints a thousand words. Well then the story of my life must be a water-colour that has been left to rot out in the rain; the lines blurred, the colours running together, until nothing but a hollow mockery of what was originally there is left. Well, either that or a colour-blind spastic was let loose with a paintbrush because the joke that passes for my life sure ain’t no Monet. Monet. Isn’t it weird how I remember the name of a painter when I don’t even recall the smallest facet of my past; I don’t even remember who I am! - - - - - - - - - - "Was it the nightmare again?" Hearing is always the first of the senses to come back after a coma, so Ember had once been reliably informed. It could take days, weeks even, before vision settled and in some cases taste never fully recovered. Imagine biting into a wonderful cream cake and tasting nothing but dust. Perhaps the coma would be preferable. Hearing, though, was right there waiting when people clawed their way back into consciousness. From the moment that the brain decided that it was time to ‘wake up’ it was already processing the numerous sounds that were being filtered through. So it was no real surprise that he heard Khaled’s voice before he fully woke up. Though while he had only been asleep, and not in a coma, it had been no less traumatic. Of course he wouldn’t allow Khaled to know that. "No, I slept like a log thanks." Even Ember was impressed at the steadiness of his voice. If he hadn’t known better he would have been convinced of the veracity of his words. Unfortunately for him, however, he could still taste the bile that had risen up to the back of his throat, could still feel his heart beating twice as fast as normal, and still remember the claustrophobic terror of the nightmare that had plagued him while he slept. The fact that he had had a nightmare, yes, but not its contents. There was nothing. "How many logs do you know that not only scream in their sleep but look like they are having a seizure with all their thrashing?" Khaled asked gruffly, his eyes red-rimmed. Even at the peak of fitness as he was, for being the partner of Ember demanded such, two nights without sleep was starting to visibly take its toll. "Maybe logs that have to wake up to see your face?" Ember quipped, forcing a hollow laugh. Khaled just stared back at him from the chair that almost seemed to be part of his body. With a groan Ember brought himself up to a seated position on the jet-black leather couch that had been his bed for the last couple of nights. He almost seemed to be part of the furniture, dressed completely in black as he was; like some macabre nocturnal chameleon. A long sigh escaped his mouth as he stretched his arms up over his head, his muscles protesting audibly as cartilage cracked. Khaled kept on staring, disapproval evident in his intense brown eyes. "Wise guy, but I am serious," he stated simply in a tone that belied his words. "You have hardly slept in two nights and when you do fall asleep you wake up screaming!" "Maybe Freddy Kruger is after me and this is just one of those teen movies." Ember’s laugh was aborted before it could fully be born. The steely expression in his only friend’s eyes was sign enough that he had gone too far. The words that followed were simply the icing on the cake. "I am not joking here, Em, I told you that I am serious." Khaled rubbed a hand down from his forehead as if he could wipe away the exhaustion from his body, the gritty tiredness from his eyes. His hand rasped across the three-day stubble that covered his chin before he leant forward, staring directly at Ember. "You only got through the match against that 4WW idiot, the sort of guy that you should have beaten in six seconds, by the skin of your teeth," he pointed out matter-of-factly. "Hey I had an off night Khal," Ember interrupted, "and besides I did beat him!" "No, you didn’t. You just didn’t lose," Khaled said with a touch of irritation at the interruption "and you won't have that same lxury against this next guy, Skar, you know. He is too good for that. That isn’t the point though." "So what is then, oh font of all knowledge?" Ember asked in frustration, sarcasm heavy in his tone. "How about the fact that you can’t sleep for more than fifteen minutes at a time without screaming yourself awake, or how about the fact that you were found unconscious in the middle of downtown Dallas, Ember?" Khaled growled, just as sarcastically. "How about the damn fact that you don’t know how the Hell you got there?!" Silence, heavy and oppressive, filled the air between the two friends. Neither man moved, hardly even breathing, both obviously waiting for the other to speak. Khaled was fuming inside, partly because of the cavalier attitude of Ember but mostly because he was worried and that was something that he wasn’t used to feeling for someone else. If you had asked him a few months ago if he would have cared about the masked man who, at the time, was trying his best to blind him Khaled would have admitted that he wouldn’t even piss on him if he was on fire. Now though, just a few months down the line, everything was different. Not only were Ember and him partners in the ring, with the ever so self-deprecating name of the Deadly Alliance, but they had become friends. Brothers even. The fact that he had seemingly blacked out and walked more than six miles through rush hour crowds and heavy traffic was bad enough. The fact that he had seemingly passed out in the middle of the street for no known reason was bad enough. The fact that he didn’t remember doing it and wouldn’t take it seriously, shrugging it off as not important was bad enough. What was worse, what was the very worst, was the fact that it terrified Khaled. He had been called to the hospital, the only information being relayed across the phone was the fact that he had been listed as the next of kin for someone that had been brought in. Khaled had struggled to figure out who it could be, knowing that it wasn’t any family, but one sentence made it all very clear. "He refused to take the mask off sir, he threatened to break the arm of anyone that tried". Khaled remembered laughing at the words, despite the cold shiver that had run down his back when the nurse had told him how Ember had been found in the street unconscious and rushed to the emergency room. He had woken, briefly, when one of the doctors had started removing his mask but had lapsed back into unconsciousness straight away. The doctor’s wrist was only sprained, and he would be fine. It only took twenty minutes to get to the hospital and Khaled went straight to Ember’s room, allowing a grey haired man – dressed impeccably in a charcoal Armani suit – to exit first. He assumed it was a doctor and thought nothing of it and felt relief rush through his body at the sight of Ember sitting up in bed, arguing with a white-coated man. "… and I don’t care what you think you have just been told that you cannot keep me here if I don’t want to stay so either get me my clothes are get ready for screams of panic as my pasty white ass leaves this place without them!" The laugh that exploded from Khaled’s mouth was more relief than humour but the image of Ember storming out of the hospital naked, apart from a black latex mask, wasn’t one that he had previously thought of. Quickly turning to face him, Ember grinned. "Khal, tell this quack that I am fine mate." Ember shouted across to Khaled, slipping into the English colloquialism that he had picked up from his London raised friend. Khaled shook his head at the bizarre sound of the word ‘mate’ coming out of Ember’s mouth. He had never been able to place his accent, soft, almost sibilant, but knew that it was American. Or, at least, he thought that it was. "I am sorry Mr Ember but it is not fine for someone to just slip into unconsciousness for no reason." The doctor stated with a resigned sigh, obviously having already been through this conversation more than once. "The fact that your career choice means that you take regular blows to the head could point towards some serious underlying problem!" "You have already been told …" Ember started to say but was quickly interrupted. "I know, I know, your lawyer was very persuasive on your behalf about keeping the mask on and being here, and you … and he … are correct, we can not force you to stay here, we cannot force you to remove the mask so that we can examine you properly, but" the doctor tried his best to convince Ember with the power of his words, but failed. "But nothing, doc." Ember stated as he stepped out of bed, holding the sheet around his body." "I appreciate your concern but there is nothing that you can do for me. So unless you want me to walk out of here and leave this sheet in your arms would you please get me my clothes?" The sheet slipped slightly, exposing Ember’s well muscled torso to view. The doctor took a step backwards and nodded for the nurse to get the clothes as Ember laughed, looking at Khaled. "I wouldn’t really streak out of here you know Khal" he said with a grin. "They don’t need to know that though." Khaled shook his head. Even after months of knowing this man he realised that he still didn’t know him at all. The only thing that you could expect from Ember was the unexpected. With a resigned sigh he helped the nurse pass the clothing out to Ember. It was only later on, when he sat watching his friend scream in his sleep, that two things came back to him. The fact that a lawyer had made it to the hospital first, even though Khaled had been listed as next-of-kin and the words that Ember had spoken to the doctor, so full of sureness that there was no denying them. He hadn’t said that there was nothing wrong. Almost the opposite. " I appreciate your concern but there is nothing that you can do for me." It was that night, as Ember drifted off to sleep on the couch while Khaled watched him – after deciding that he would stay the night, despite the protestations of his friend – that the words played around in Khaled’s head. "Nothing that you can do for me." Ember wasn’t denying a problem, Khaled realised as he sat there staring into the darkness. Thinking back on the words he realised that it wasn’t just what Ember had said that had struck a chord with him, it was how he had said them. With certainty. The first scream that came out of Ember frightened Khaled as much, if not more, as it did the sleeping man. He was out of his chair before even he realised it and was crouching over Ember, reaching out to him. He had never heard his friend scream like that, not even in the middle of the ring, cut open and bleeding profusely. He had watched as Ember passed out from the pain of a submission hold rather than quit, and had never made a sound. Yet there he was, thrusting himself out of whatever nightmare had its claws in him, screaming like a lost child. As soon as Ember was awake, however, he didn’t have any recollection of what had happened. That first night had felt like days for Khaled. He had watched Ember scream himself awake numerous times and each time could do nothing to console him until he was fully awake. The morning light brought no respite to his tired eyes and, while Ember also moved like he had not slept, his body creaking like an old mans, he stated that he didn’t remember anything interrupting his sleep at all. Khaled knew then, as he knew now, that he was lying to him. The silence continued as Ember stared back at Khaled, wondering what was going on behind his brown eyes. He was the one that wore a mask, a mask that even Khaled himself had never … would never … seen behind and yet sitting here, face to face, it was Khaled whose expression was impassive. No matter how hard he stared Ember couldn’t make out what his friend was thinking, was feeling. He could, however, guess. He knew that Khaled had questions, many of them. He knew that Khaled wanted to know why he had a private lawyer and more to the point why he would have been called to the hospital. The question of Ember’s mask had only been risen once between the two friends, and Ember recalled the fleeting look of hurt when Ember told Khaled that whatever was behind the mask was unimportant. The fact that Ember was telling the truth when he said it, the truth when he told him that he was the mask and was all that mattered didn’t seem to get through to Khaled. He had covered his feelings quickly though and the subject was never raised again. So, then, how could Ember tell him that the lawyer was there to ensure that the mask was not removed, and that he was listed as the first person to call in ANY sort of emergency, before even Khaled was informed? He couldn’t. He also knew that Khaled wanted to know what was wrong with him, was worried that he was hiding something serious. "You cannot seriously think that you are going to still compete in the tournament Em." Khaled informed him on the first morning after the hospital visit. "People don’t just pass out for no reason!" "I didn’t pass out Khal, girls pass out." Ember joked, realising as soon as the words left his mouth that it was the wrong approach. "I don’t give a damn what you want to call it, you were face down on the concrete and you don’t know why!" Khaled had almost screamed at him. "You are phoning the damned GTT right now and stepping out of it, you hear me?" Ember winced as he recalled the argument that had ensued after he informed Khaled that not only was he not retiring from the tournament but that he wasn’t allowed to inform them of what had happened either. For nearly two hours the war of the words had taken place and in the end there was no clear victor. Khaled was adamant that Ember shouldn’t compete. Ember was just as adamant that he should. And would. Short of tying Ember up, and Khaled wasn’t convinced that he would be able to over-power the smaller – but deceptively powerful – man in order to do so, he knew that there was nothing that he could physically do to intervene. He briefly considered phoning the PTC himself and letting them know that Ember wasn’t fit to wrestle but right away he knew what that breach of trust would do to their friendship. He doesn’t trust me though was the traitorous thought that shouted inside his head, but he shook it away, quietening it. When … if … Ember wanted to let him know who was behind the mask, or even the reason for the mask, he would do. For now he was content. Ember had been part of the WWA for nearly a year and in all that time he had worn the mask, never being seen without it, so if it was that important to him then it must BE important. "…but it could be something serious," he had said, concern obvious in his voice, "it could be a concussion. It could even be a … tumor". The last word had been spoken very quietly and Ember had fallen silent himself at that moment, touched by the concern. It had been a long time since he had been able to let his defences down around someone, to trust someone. It had been a long time since he had had a friend. A true friend. A friend? the thought filtered into his head as he stared at Khaled across the table, the silence solidifying into a wall between them. He knew that Khaled was worried about him, concerned that there was something seriously wrong with. He knew that Khaled thought that he shouldn’t be competing still. The fact of the matter was that Khaled was both right and wrong. What would he do if he knew that I don’t even remember facing that 4WW guy? Ember mused to himself as he leant forward and hung his head down with a sigh. What would he do if he knew that it wasn’t a concussion, it wasn’t a tumour? What would he do if he knew that the doctor couldn’t do anything to help him, that no one could? What would he do if he knew that Ember … while not remembering the blacked out times … knew what they meant? Ember felt the tear forming at the corner of his eye and was thankful for the fact that the mask would contain it, would hide it from Khaled, as his brain turned over and over with the one question that haunted him. What would Khaled do if he knew just how terrified Ember actually was? - - - - - - - - - - I savoured the fresh wave of pain as I used my nails, once more, to reopen the small wounds on my hands. I wasn’t able to tell how much time was passing – there was no light to see by and no way of knowing when day turned to night, when today turned into tomorrow – but always made sure to pierce my own flesh before it could completely close over. This didn’t help me track the passage of time in anyway at all but, in some small way, it made me feel like I could. I decided that it was also time to hold my breath and count to ten. I didn’t actually expect to hear anyone else breathing, they never had before so why would they now? All the same it was routine and routine kept you sane. I was sure that I had heard that before, just as I knew of Monet, but I didn’t know when, or where, or even why. That was the least of my worries though as I would have been satisfied with the answer to one simple, little, insignificant, question. Who? Who was I? Why was I here? Where was here? When would I get out? How would I get out? Damn, I had said that I would be happy with one question and there I was trying to sneak in another four. The chuckle that escaped my mouth sounded more like a sob than a laugh and I couldn’t help thinking that one of these ‘days’ – I had continued to call things day and night, though day may only last twelve minutes and night three hundred hours for all I knew – the sobs would take over and I would be lost. Whoever ‘I’ was of course. I fought off the wave of self-pity and took in a deep breath, holding it tight inside my lungs and listening hard for any other sound. Praying that I wouldn’t hear any. The sound of my own blood pounding through my veins, and the deep vibrant thrumming of my heart, was easy to ignore. I was so used to them that they were nothing but background noise. I almost choked, though, when something new intruded into my little World of encapsulated darkness. My womb. It wasn’t a sound though, and it definitely wasn’t a sight. It was a taste. I tasted salt water and for a second I thought that I was crying but reaching up I felt my cheek as dry and tight as bone. I hadn’t cried in – well I didn’t know how long obviously but I knew that it was a long time. Yet still I could taste the distinctive flavour of tears in my mouth. Breath exploded from my lungs, rushing up through my throat and rushing out of my mouth with a single scream. I couldn’t stop it as I didn’t know where it had come from. The feelings coursing through my body – my soul? – were not my own. I don’t know how I knew that but I knew it. All I knew was that I was tasting someone else’s tears and feeling their emotion. … and I knew what it was. Terror.
