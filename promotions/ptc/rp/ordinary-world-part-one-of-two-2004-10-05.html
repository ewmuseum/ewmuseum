Ordinary World (Part One of Two)

5 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-10-05 "Ugh..." Karina Wolfenden felt as if she were in captivity. Fidgeting with the collar of her suit jacket, while finding it awkward positioning her legs while wearing an above-the-knee skirt, Karina looked most uncomfortable surrounded by her fellow commuters. The skirt-suit had never been worn in the 2 years she'd owned it, and with good reason, as just having it near her skin made it tingly, the beginnings of an infuriating itch. Being surrounded by the body heat of 40 or so other people, while the low morning sun glaring through the windows - which blocked out the early nip - made the interior of the bus stuffy and uncomfortable. The two words which were dominating her thoughts, summing up how she felt perfectly. For nearly 5 minutes before she'd left, she'd tried squeezing her suit-clad form into her bike leathers, then taking a ride into work to at least let out a little frustration before her day began... but it wasn't to be. So instead, Kari had to settle for eating her toast during an uncomfortable dash to the bus stop, well and truly integrating herself into the life of your average, 9 to 5 worker. Yes, Karina Wolfenden had found gainful employment, technically. "Would you mind opening the window a little?" she then politely asked to the large man sat beside her, taking up most of their double seat with the bulk that was barely contained by stretching buttons. "It's getting a little hot in here..." Rather than heaving his frame up to slide the window across, the man instead glared back at the young woman through his thick glasses, as if she's just offended his mother. Not even bothering to give any form of pleading look or repetition, the K-Wolf just let out a sigh and stared down at her feet, which were already aching from the unfamiliar heels that raised them. To her, business dress was a good as fancy dress... only less fun, and without the usual drink-laded party to go with it. All that could be heard was the unhealthy sound of the bus' engine as it struggled to pull along the mass of humanity contained within it... a far cry from the crisp and songful sound her own Ducati 748 made under her. It slowly began to dawn on her that nobody was talking, and other than requests to the driver, her denied request for some relieving air were the only words uttered. The early-morning commuter club obviously wasn't very pally. The unfriendly, borderline hostile atmosphere was then worsened as the muffled sound of a polyphonic ring tone was then heard. Immediately, scowls and narrowed eyes zeroed in on the source, tutting away and mumbling about etiquette. Far from oblivious to the frosty stares locked onto her, Karina Wolfenden retrieved the phone from her inside pocket, checked the caller ID, and flipped the phone down. "Morning, Dad," she answered, in a tone that fully expected his immediate enquiry of as to where she was. "It's nearly 8," came from her phone as the large man beside her shifted in his seat, his arms crossing, and the action nearly nudging Karina to the floor. "I thought we agreed you'd be here by then?" "I'm stuck on a bus," Kari replied, making it clear in her voice that it wasn't for her own pleasure. "I can almost see the office now... I'll be there, okay?" Max would owe her big for this one. Although RPW's closing had saddened her, it didn't take Wolfenden long to see the benefits of having a free calendar. Her minimal GTT commitments would only take up 3 days a month, leaving the K-Wolf with ample time to indulge in all her pleasures. But before she could once more fling herself headlong, uninhibited, into the world, she had to 'earn her keep'. As much as she portrayed the independent young woman, Karina knew who paid her rent, topped up her bank account, and bought her favoured mode of transport. Her trust fund, now her only source of income, had been earned through hard work by her father. He wasn't rich in a Millionaire sense, and wasn't grand as many in his position would be. Lawrence Wolfenden was just a man who'd rather let those he loved spend the fruits of his labour than trap it in expensive luxuries that meant little. "A smile from my children is worth a far more to me than a Bentley," was the phrase he'd once used to explain his generosity. So when he asked for a mere 5 days of work from his daughter, Karina could hardly say no, even though the slightly selfish side of her had mulled over excuses to worm out of it. "Please say you're dressed the part, Karina?" Lawrence then asked, prompting Karina to roll her eyes as she could feel the uncomfortable clothes encase her. "I even bought you that suit before so you'd have something to wear at times like these." "And I'm wearing it," Karina swiftly answered, once more having to fidget and brush the collar away from the skin on the back of her neck. "Which, I may add, is a bit like wearing sand-paper laced with itching powder." Scanning her emerald eyes around the bus, Karina matched each of the venomous stares with her own confident gaze, deflecting their sneers. Tomorrow morning, she'd made up her mind to ride into work, then change in the restrooms. Less than classy, but far preferable to sharing the cramped bus with the hostile, clammy human cattle. "And dare I ask what colour your hair is?" came a further question from the phone, prompting Karina to blow the pink fringe from her face with a quick and direct exhale. "Oh, didn't I tell you?" Karina couldn't resist firing right back. "No more hair dye for me, it kept getting really dry... so I shaved it all off." Karina assumed the odd, muffled noise she heard on the other end of the line was her father spitting his 7th coffee of the day all over his working papers. As she cracked a sly grin, the bus went over a bump in the road, almost rolling her stomach. This was so far away from where she wanted to be that it wasn't even funny. Just a few days ago, she'd been flinging and flipping herself around the ring, soaring through the air and thrusting her feet freely out in to all manner of stiff kicks... and now she was crammed into a stuffy bus. The cheering of the fans and trio of slaps from the referee that crowned her victory were still fresh in her mind before leaving her apartment. They had now been absorbed into the sickly grumble of the bus' engine. A lost memory. "You did WHAT?" was roared back through her phone, Lawrence obviously not picking up on the hint of mischief in her voice. "You can't come in looking lik-" "Relax," Karina chuckled, cutting him off before he had a chance to insult any of society's groups. "Blonde, pink fringe, tied back neatly... like a good little office girl." To her fellow commuters, that was all she was. A young woman, ready for a day at the office. She wasn't the K-Wolf, she wasn't recognised... it was a fact she liked. Karina enjoyed wrestling, it was just about the only reason she did it, but the rest of it she would drop in an instant. Having complete strangers feel they knew her was an uncomfortable feeling she could never really get used to. So, given the choice, she's take the impolite company she was surrounded by in the bus... even if the man next to her had began to sweat. "Don't do that again," her father dryly ordered, the rummaging heard over the less-than-clear line hinting that he had coffee to remove from paperwork. "I've cleared your picture with security, so unless you're looking even more different that you usually do every time I see you, you're free just to head up to the 4th floor." "See you in a few," Karina then signed off with, slapping the phone shut and dropping it back into her inside pocket. The man next to her scoffed, trying to highlight that her taking of the phone call was rude with an equally rude gesture. Not enjoying the morning much so far, the agitated Karina turned and looked up to him, burning back through his look of displeasure with her potent glare. The snapping sound a few might have heard was her patience. "Oh, I'm sorry," she mocked in apology, finally tiring of his blunt rudeness, "was the phone call I took while nearly being forced off the seat by you rude? I'd have talked a little quieter, but I couldn't hear my father over your breathing." Yup, not a fun morning thus far. Verbally repelled, the man turned and looked out of the window, grumbling away while Karina felt bad about snapping so harshly. Out of her natural environment, her behaviour patterns seemed to change. Carefree became frustrated, calm became flustered, and apathetic became agitated. Before she could even begin to formulate an apology, the bus pulled in to her stop, and Karina hopped of her seat and contorted her slender frame through protruding shoulders and knees to navigate the aisle. Unsociable grunts and resentful pulling-in of limbs opened up the way for the K-Wolf, who gave polite, yet unreciprocated thanks to the driver, before hopping off onto the sidewalk. Left staring up at the bleak, featureless office building, Karina took a deep, preparing breath. "It's only for 5 days," Kari repeated as a mantra, knowing that unless the building had installed an indoor ski-slope, or a few miles of winding tarmac, she wasn't going to enjoy the week. "It's only for 5 days." Just looking at the steps, and the rails that ran alongside them, Karina envisioned bounding up them, leaping onto the rails, before flinging her slender frame off into a twisting flip. Conscious of the eyes on her, however, she instead took a more pedestrian route up the steps, her head hung unexcitedly low. Probably for the best, since away from the ring, Karina wasn't exactly one to draw attention her bashful way... and not being recognised was something the K-Wolf was appreciating. Away from her performing environment, her plain features seemed to blend right in, as all people noticed was a pink flash of a fringe. "Miss Wolfenden?" the suited and hat-clad man opening the door enquired, just about recognising her from the photo. Well, in all honesty, he saw the pink and realised it must be her, forgivable since forgetting Karina's lightly freckled and unremarkable face was a very easy thing to do. "Yeah," she hesitantly answered, not too keen on the formality of the greeting. "But you can call me Karina." "This way, Miss Wolfenden," the man then replied, seemingly blanking her request and choosing to stick with his formal manner of address.. As she was ushered towards the elevator, Karina peered out of the sides of her startling emerald eyes, scanning around her peripheral vision. At the main desk, the receptionist was watching every step the bright-of-hair young woman made, her paces less-than-confident due to her awkwardness on heels. It didn't take Karina's sharp, if deeply retracted, intellect to realise that she was severely out of place. And maybe out of her mind for agreeing to it in the first place. "There should be someone waiting to receive you, Miss Wolfenden," her guide informed, waiting until Karina had stepped into the elevator before plunging down the button labelled 4 with a sharp prod of his index finger. "Thanks," she lightly offered, before once more reminding him, "and you can call me Karina, really..." "Have a good day, Miss Wolfenden," came his automated response as he stepped back from the doors, tipping his hat to her in another stuffy custom. As she heard the hiss of the doors, Karina rolled her eyes, before taking one last look at the foyer, where unashamed glares shot back from all angles in a volley of curiosity. Taking a deep breath, Karina felt the elevator shunt ever-so-slightly as the doors shut, seemingly locking her in for the day. Jaded by the wrestling industry, not sure whether she still had passion for it, while finding herself ill-at-ease with the ordinary, working life... Karina Wolfenden was trapped in limbo between her respective worlds. Wrestling was only meant to be a once-a-week thing, a way to unwind on a Sunday night. A hobby, to go alongside all the other risky pursuits that were crammed into her life of leisure. But now, she was actually missing it. Even feeling herself be propelled up through the building, all Karina could think was how pedestrian it was... devoid of risk, packed with safety features. Then, as the elevator came to a standstill, and it's doors opened, the K-Wolf knew her day was about to truly begin.
