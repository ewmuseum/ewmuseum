War and (Rest In) Peace: Part II

24 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-09-24 “We are gathered here to celebrate the life of fifty five year old Alexandra Jennifer Jenkins.” The scene opens to a fairly old church, the walls made of brick, the occasional brick having a slight bit of green mould on the inside of it. The room is filled with about thirty people in total, all sitting, wearing black suits, or dresses in the case of most women. “She was a good woman, a caring, loving woman who never took advantage of anybody, she always wanted the best of everything. You could say that to her, the glass was always half full.” Odium is standing up with his head bowed, wearing a white shirt with a black suit over it. In the background of the priest speaking you can slightly hear a few woman and even a couple of men sobbing, but in the men’s case just the occasional sniff and a tear running down their face. “And now, let us sing the hymn that Alexandra so memorably loved, and requested to be played at her funeral. Let us stand and sing…Jerusalem.” As the music began to play, Odium looked straight at the coffin of his mother, his face not showing any emotion, no happiness, no sadness or any anger. Just a straight face, who barely even sang Jerusalem, more of a rambling mess than a singing voice. And in these feet through ancient times Walking on England’s mountains green As Odium’s rambling voice continued in the singing of the other attendees, the priest stood next to the coffin, and raised his hand above it, blessing it with the lord’s prayer as the song continues to play. And was the holy Lamb of God On England’s pleasant pastures seen? And did the countenance divine Shine upon forth O clouded hills? And was Jerusalem builded here. Among those dark satanic hills. All of a sudden in the middle of the song, an elderly lady begins to sob. As it is, this woman is the amazingly still alive mother of Alexandra Jennifer Jenkins. Tears start to stream down her face and they start to drop onto the floor, and almost like a chain of tears, seeing this happen, the few people around her, various relatives, start crying because she is crying. Odium notices this, and looks at his Grandma. But still, no tear is able to run down his face. Bring me my bow of burning gold Bring me my arrows of desire Bring me my spear! O clouds, unfold! Bring me my chariot of fire! I will not cease, from mental fight, Nor shall my sword sleep in my hand, Till we have built Jerusalem, In England’s green and pleasant land. As the song finally finished, 95% of the people there were in sobbing tears. The five percent not crying being the priest and Odium, who still had failed to shed any tears. The priest spoke in a solemn voice. “And now, I will let a very special person, and someone very close to Alexandra say a few words, a poem actually, about her. Please welcome Danny Jenkins.” As the arrival of Danny Jenkins is waited, Odium expects him to be at least twenty years old. But to his surprise, Danny Jenkins is a five year old boy, and apparently he is a nephew Odium didn’t know he had. The little boy stands on a chair to reach the microphone, and holds a piece of paper in his hand which he is constantly staring at. “A poem by Thomas Hardy.” The little boy sniffs, stopping a tear so that he can read the poem. “We stood by a pond that winter day, And the sun was white, as though chi-*sniff*chidden of god, And a few leaves lay on the starving sod, They had fallen from ash, and were gray. The smile *sniff* The smile on your face the deadest thing, Alive enough to have strength to…die, And a grin of bitterness swept…there…thereby, Like an ominous bird a-…bird a-…*sob*” As the five year old boy is about to go into the last verse of the poem, he suddenly starts to let weeps and tears come from his face. He starts to rub his eyes with clenched fists, wiping the tears away, when the priest takes the boy hand-in-hand back to his seat. The priest cries out to the onlookers. “Is there anyone here who wishes to say a few words about Alexandra?” Odium clenches his right fist in his seat, fiddling with his fingers and jiggling about on his seat. Finally, Odium reaches the right amount of courage he needs to have and speaks. “Priest, I wish to say a few words…” And without waiting for the priest to give him permission, he climbs out of his seat and walks to the front. He removes the chair that the little boy was standing on to reach the microphone, stands in front of it, and leans on the table that the microphone is held on. “The life of Alexandra Jenkins was a fairly good one indeed. She had a husband who loved her dearly, and two children, me and Frank, that would always try our hardest to take care of her, do what we were told. I’ll admit, when I was fifteen I would soon grow to dislike my mother, but of course I regret that now. I regret not being able to apologise for my idiocy before she passed away. But if we want to get past this, we don’t want to dwell on the past, do we? I even remember one time when I was ten and Frank was twelve. I remember realising to myself that one of my pastime hobbies was watching Frank getting told off and smacked for bad behaviour. I’d swoop in and be the really behaved one to try and be my parents favourites. But then again, I’m sure Frank would do the same. One time, I remember my mother and my father having one of their largest arguments when I was around twelve years of age. I, rather regrettably, remember a time when my mother stupidly demanded that she and my father get a divorce. I remember two months of madness, then at the last minute they both sat down like adults, had a talk about it and agreed to stay married. But mostly I remember that this whole shebang was over the fact that my father did not remember to bring in the washing from the washing line one afternoon when my mother was out, and it started raining. It’s funny; I guess this proves that the little things do indeed count. The little things, such as Confidence, Courage and Integrity. And, not meaning to turn this into a hype area for me, but to end this talk, I would like to say that I will do everything I can do to make my mother consciously forgive me. Winning the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament which some of you may know I am in would be my ultimate goal, but I would hope my mother would forgive me if I was to show her what I did with my life. By beating Rzarector. For my mother.” And, for the first time in a very, very long time, a small tear trickled down the face of Odium as he walked away from the front of the church, and back to his seat for the remaining part of the funeral. Maybe big boys do cry, after all.
