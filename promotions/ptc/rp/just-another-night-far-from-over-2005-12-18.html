Just Another Night: Far From Over

18 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-18 "Excuse me, Detective Pride," the robotic voice that echoed out from the intercom rested atop mounds of paperwork, almost seemed to be feminine. "There are two uh…women…out here with information about a missing person." Detective Pride leaned forward and pressed a button on the intercom, "Then send them to missing persons," his voice seemed rough, as if he had to force the words out. "You might want to speak with them, sir." Pride threw his arms up in frustration, before reaching across the cluttered desk once more and pressing the button on the intercom once more, "Fine, send them up." Detective Pride tried his best to fix himself up, repositioning the loosened tie around his neck after buttoning up the top button on his wrinkled and lint covered, long sleeved black dress shirt. Pride then attempted to rearrange the countless files that had accumulated on top of his desk over the past few weeks, into some makeshift order so that they did not seem to be just tossed messily on the Cherry Oak finish of the desk. Of course, that just made the desk appear even more cluttered, so he just grabbed a handful and stuck them into an already crammed desk drawer. "Something I’ll have to take care of later," he thought to himself as he slammed the drawer shut. A subtle knock on the door caught his attention, glancing up from the drawer in time to see the two women walk through his half open door. Women being a strange word to use on two men done up in full make-up and wearing extravagantly jeweled dresses. Pride sat there with a look of shock on his face; surely this was not what he expected when the desk sergeant mentioned two women needing to speak with him. He just hoped his mouth was not hanging open, which would be awkward. "Please shut your mouth," one of the women said as he made his way over to one of the seats across from Pride. Yeah, that’s a bit awkward. Pride swallowed hard, his face turning a shade of beet red as he attempted to regain his composure. "How may I help you," his gaze purposely set on one of the many files still scattered across his desk. "A friend of ours, Lacey Gutierrez, has been missing for a few days." The name did not ring a bell, and Pride did not recall finding any Jane Doe’s in the past few days. "And I can help you with this how?" "We’re not really sure," the cross dresser with the bright blue dress full of white and blue sequins leaned forward and said in an almost feminine voice. Still, you could see the Adam’s apple in his throat move slightly with each word muttered. "We just told the guy at the front desk that we wanted to report a missing person, mentioned her name and he called you." "I see," Pride managed to say as he sat there dumbfounded. "Well, what can you tell me about Lacey, any distinguishing marks?" "Well, she still carried her package," the other cross dresser, the one who spoke first as they walked in, chuckled as she spoke. "I’m not sure how that could help us…" Pride’s face was once again turning a light shade of crimson, realizing just what the female impersonator meant when she said package. "Lacey was a man," Pride asked sheepishly. "No, she just had the equipment of one," came the response from the cobalt adorned cross dresser, a hint of glee in her voice as she makes the detective even more uncomfortable. "A problem easily taken care of," the other said as she made a snipping motion with her index and middle finger. Pride shifted in his seat unnervingly, the image flashing in his mind for a brief second before he shook it off. "When was the last time you saw," Pride paused awkwardly, it had completely slipped his mind to ask for a man’s name now that he knew it was indeed not a woman they were searching for. "What is Lacey’s real name, her birth name?" "Miguel. Miguel Lorenzo Gutierrez." Pride started scribbling something down on a yellow notepad, glancing up quickly as he realized that he had yet to put names to the faces sat across from him. "And the two of you are," an eyebrow rose inquiringly. "Sapphire," the female impersonator adorned in brilliant blue replied as she placed a hand on her now exposed knee, "and this is Karina." "Okay, give me a moment to write all of this down," Pride already had his head down, as he hurriedly scribbled down his new information. That was when the manila folder he had the notepad resting over almost slapped him in the face. John Doe. The case from a few days ago, the skinless body that had washed up along the shore. The DNA taken from the body had came back with no match on record, but if there was a way to link this missing person to the body that had washed up on shore than a whole new lead had just opened up. "Do either of you have any personal effects of Lacey’s, like a hairbrush, toothbrush, or any other item that she may have used?" It was a strange question to be asked, and Sapphire did not hold back her curiousness. "Why do you ask?" "I’m not going to beat around the bush with you," when in fact that was exactly what he was going to do. "We have a John Doe down in the morgue that we have not identified yet." Karina nearly jumped from her seat, "Well, we could identify her if it is indeed her." Pride shook his head, nervously. "I’m afraid that is going to be a bit difficult, and I’m not allowed to release any information on the case." Sapphire had already broken down into tears, pulling a single tissue from her chest to wipe away the tears, as streaks of black mascara streamed down her face and mixed with the blue eye shadow that made her crystalline blue eyes appear to glow. "She…she was my room…roommate," she managed to state as her sobs became increasingly uncontrollable. "Anything that you could bring to me would be appreciated." Karina pulled Sapphire to her chest, holding her close in an attempt to comfort her in her time of need; much like a mother would to an infant. Deep down inside, Pride wanted to jump around and do cartwheels. This seemingly lost case may have just been torn wide open, yet there was this man standing before him with make-up streaming down his cheeks that almost brought a tear to his own eye. "Because this case is far from over." =¤=¤=¤=¤= =¤=¤=¤=¤= Jayce Bradley peered over the file folder that had been sent over to him, a small bit of information that had been sent over by the GTT committee on his second round opponent, Joe Miller. Saying the folder had little to no information that would be useful would be an understatement. The file held one sheet of paper as it’s contents, along with a photo of a pretty much ordinary man with a monkey using a jetpack to fly around in the background. Or at least that was what the picture looked like it was supposed to be. For all Jayce knew, it could have been some Photoshop edited JPEG to help put the gimmick over. Nonetheless, this Joe Miller was supposedly from the future. "Must be from the UWF," he muttered to himself, as he managed to stifle a chuckle. The Universal Wrestling Federation had its fair share of extraordinary characters on its roster. From the walking incarnation of Death in Clyde, to the man of a thousand secondary characters in James von Doom. The place was considered a running joke along the back halls of Primetime Central, and this wrestler from the future seemed to fit right at home in an institution like that. Unfortunately, his assumption had been wrong, as right next to the name, Joe Miller, in smaller type font than the actual name were the letters ‘FA’ bracketed by parentheses. "Free Agent," he whispered to himself. Free Agent indeed. The underdogs in this whole tournament, as some seemingly unknown waltzes in and usurps a spot from an unsuspecting PTC affiliate’s flag carrier, because of the lack of scouting material available on someone that no one has ever heard of before. Most people would be discouraged by something like that, not knowing what to expect from their opponent. Things like that do not matter to Jayce, though. Not because he is overly confident, but because the only way to truly prepare for someone you know nothing about, is to prepare for anything. His last match against Sean Galen is a prime example of that, since the only thing Jayce knew about Galen was that he considered himself a legend of the sport. That did not deter Jayce from the match, cowering in fear in the back of his locker room, scared to show his face and possibly be eliminated in the first round. Jayce went out to the ring and won a hard fought battle against someone that he was unprepared for. While Joe Miller sat around copying, pasting, then editing a photo of a monkey with a jetpack behind his nondescript features. Must be nice. "What’s that," a coarse voice broke the silence, causing Jayce to shift his weight in the chair and turn his attention over his shoulder. Russell Braun stood behind him, peering over his shoulder at the small file folder that Jayce held firmly in his grasp. "Some information on my next round opponent in the tournament." Braun nodded, "Anything useful?" Jayce shook his head. No. Then proceeded to close the folder and place it on the table in front of him. "You wanna sit down," Jayce said as he motioned toward the seat in front of him. Braun made his way to the seat, pulling it out from beneath the table as he motioned for a waitress to come over before he took a seat. "So, what’s going on," Jayce inquired, resting his elbows on the table. "They found the wetback." Jayce shook his head, disgusted and angry. "I thought you weighed him down," his voice was filled with anger, yet it was a controlled rage that did not sound much more than a forced whisper. "I di…" Braun is cut off as an older woman, her hair nearly entirely a platinum shade of gray, walks up to the table with pen and paper in hand. "What can I do for ya, honey," she questioned; her mouth reminiscent of a cow, as she chewed on what may be a piece of chewing gum. "Just a glass of water, please." Courtesy, not something expected from the mouth of Russell Braun. The waitress tucked the notepad into her grease-stained smock, as she walked off with a dejected look upon her face. "Water," she mumbled to herself, as she realized that a tip was certainly out of the question. Russell turned his attention back to Jayce, who sat across from him with his arms folded across his chest. "I did weigh the bastard down," Braun said in a stifled yet angered voice, "He must have slipped out from the chains." Jayce rolled his eyes, not really at ease with what Braun is telling him. An excuse is all he heard escaping through the dry, chapped lips of Braun. "It doesn’t matter," Braun tried to alleviate the tension in Jayce’s demeanor, "they couldn’t identify the body anyhow. No record of the spic was on file, so we have nothing to worry about." The statement did nothing to calm Jayce’s nerves. He finally tasted the sweetness of success, and this problem could ruin it all for him. "You better hope we don’t," Jayce leans over the table, in an almost threatening way, toward his mentor, "because if you screw this up for me…" "Here’s your water, sir," the waitress broke up the tension as she placed the clear plastic glass of ice water down on the table in front of Braun. Braun just nodded as he reached for the glass, his gaze never diverting away from Jayce. "We’re you about to threaten me, Jayce," a sick and twisted smile spreads across Braun’s face like a cancer, as he too moves closer to his disciple. "No. You of all people should know by now, that my words are never merely threats, Russ." "Just remember, son, it doesn't take more than a small stone to bring your glass ceiling crashing down around you." Braun stands from his seat, patting Jayce on the shoulder as he passes him. "I’ll be waiting for you when you finish playing your silly games." Jayce pulls his shoulder away, trying desperately to keep his anger bottled up inside. "It’s still not time," he thought to himself as he heard the door of the restaurant close shut. "But this conversation is far from over."
