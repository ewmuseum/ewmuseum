Date: 2010-02-07 Insanity in individuals is something rare - but in groups, parties, nations and epochs, it is the rule. ----Friedrich Nietzsche Her name was Cheryl. I’d long ago given up belief in love at first sight, but this felt different. Perhaps I’d been irrational all along to deny such a thing existed. Lifelong admirer of the oceans and its myriad secrets, she told me how she favored a career as a marine biologist and, to that end, delved into every class that the university offered on the subject in pursuit of her degree. Her dreams were admirable, idealistic. Many a student fresh into the open-minded world of academics arrives with big dreams, grateful to escape from the oppressive high school landscape, but most lose those starry-eyed aspirations in short time. Cheryl was on the verge of obtaining her bachelor’s, she said, and had never lost that ambition. I think it was that trait that made her stand out from that crowd. That conviction, and the innocence that she‘d clutched onto in the meantime. Women through the bar that night had considered me a prize, courting me with free drinks, hungry smiles and petty conversation that I admittedly grew tired of, but it was her I’d chosen that evening. Signs seemed to indicate likewise for Cheryl; men throughout the establishment had bought her drinks for hours, from the scotch-sipping, well-dressed gentlemen in their middle years to the fraternity boys with turned-up collars and that invincible swagger of college youth. But I’d honed in on her early, conquered her other would-be seducers, and claimed her as my own. It had been a long time, but I intended to make it all worthwhile. After all, I’d found an angel, wings clipped and as sensual as anything Heaven could conceivably offer a loyal servant of the kingdom. Her breasts heaved in rhythm with her moans of bliss, so perfect they were, ample yet not excessive. A goddess she was, auburn hair flowing down her shoulders as we embraced like long-lost lovers reunited, her skin creamy and flawless as it pressed against my own. A paragon to rival any of the picturesque titans that the ancient Greeks may have envisioned atop Olympus, more than a match for Diana or Aphrodite. Their majesty had long since declined, passing into the realm of ancient myth, and there was only those of now. Tonight, for me, Cheryl belonged among them at the summit of paradise. Then I took notice of the cross around her neck, sterling silver and engraved with her name. I recalled how it had been all those years ago, the golden years of youth when my parents were still alive, my loving and supporting father and doting mother with all of her dreams of seeing me flourish as a young man. Good Christians. Better humanitarians. The memories served to blot out the lust, and I blinked. “Are you a Christian?” I asked her between her cries of ecstasy. She nodded, her blue-painted nails dredging bloody canyons in the flesh of my back. I began to pump harder, staring deeply into her steely azure eyes. She was beautiful, a pristine portrait of physical grandeur. “Is Jesus Christ your savior, my love?” I whispered longingly into her ear. “Is your soul his for eternity?” Another ‘yes’ in between intensifying moans, and her slender legs wrapped around my waist with a pressure I’m sure could be comparable to the jaws of life. I gave it to her faster and harder, my own breath quickening. “Do you deny all others before Him?” Her eyes moved to follow my gaze, attention focalized on that symbol which rose and fell between her heaving bosom. My preoccupation with the ornament around her neck seemed to have raised hesitation, maybe alarm, as her sexual glee dissipated in fleeting seconds. She answered matter-of-factly with another firm ’yes’, and she sat up. I’d had enough of her. Her moans turned to panicked screams. I shattered two of her front teeth with a swing of my fist. Bones in her jaw crunched beneath my second attack. I remembered just who I was. I was something far beyond the mortal cravings of flesh, simultaneously the apex of logic and a machine of destruction. I remembered, and it was a glorious resurrection. I assailed her relentlessly, her eyes already beginning to sport bruises and her tears streaming in pained rivulets and staining her mascara. But all the while I stayed inside of her, continuing to fuck the hypocritical little tramp through my own spiking rage and her cries of protest. She thrashed about like a tuna pulled aboard a fishing boat, trying to escape to the cold sea, and in turn my fist barreled into her ribs; I presumable I splintered at least one judging by the anguished and wide-eyed look that distorted her face. She screamed at the top of her lungs. I laughed, feeling the internal joy that comes with destroying the pseudo-innocence of the insincere, the heretic, and then I screamed too. My own shriek parodied her own, demonstrating that no one would hear her cry out, and I continued to do so as I assaulted her angelic little face with a barrage of practiced blows. I warped the visage of that hell-sent succubus to a pulpy mess, a bloody and smashed ruin that belied the immaculate beauty it once was. Knuckled speckled with blood, I screamed and screamed in frenzied mockery of her helplessness. Still she squirmed, groaning and sobbing beneath the blood and broken bones and shattered teeth, all the while as I continued to thrust in and out of her. It wasn’t assault, it wasn’t rape; those are terms that describe acts of brutal injustice. Cheryl had tried to destroy me, undermine the core of what I am, and for that she relinquished her claims to injustice. My fists, my cock, my righteous gavels of judgment. Her breathing became raspy and strained. She was in agony and it merely spurred me to quicken my pace. Her distress was the adrenaline racing through my veins. It didn’t take much longer. I gave her my seed and separated myself from her, that harlot and whore of Babylon. Never again would her kind pull me into their web of deception. Consider me forever immune against their venom, for I am reborn. I tossed my angel, my fallen angel, radiant no more, to the others. Up until she breathed her last breath, they could do to her what they pleased. Her halo replaced by a crown of bloody thorns as she paid the price of her crimes. I couldn’t abide hypocrisy or stupidity. Not the hideous dementia of Islam and its false prophet Mohammed, nor the bleating sanctimony of Christianity or its lunatic ‘messiah’. I wanted those self-righteous Jews exterminated as much as Hitler and desired an abrupt end to the Scientologists, Mormons, Buddhists, and every other follower of every inane, hollow faith this world felt proud to harbor. It boggles my mind. They would all be dealt with in time. But for now there was a single goal: I wouldn’t allow Bruce Shanahan to lead the world to its doom in his personal quest for long-standing retribution. Just as my own weakness spoke of our failings, it helped me to frame Shanahan’s own iniquities. I had destroyed his manor, his fortress of arrogance, and still he pressed ahead. And when I sent the Yakuza, the roaring guns of the Blackhelm Coalition, and Shanahan’s maniacal devils against one another, the madman had escaped with a newly instilled vigor to see his dreams of mass extinction through. I’d sought to strike down three birds with a single stone, or a single bullet if you will, and instead served to press my enemies into desperation. The stalemate would be short lived. They’d unleash their full strength in a bid to decimate one another, and my designs would be complete. It wasn’t so much the madman’s ambition that vexed me. It was his failings, the cause of the effect in a sense. He was still tainted by his mortal imperfections; Shanahan’s drive was tied to long-standing grudges, purposes bound by flesh. I’d destroy him and his corrupt endeavors. In the ensuing chaos I’d soon topple the Vatican and its trigger-happy Blackhelm pets, then proceed to burn all the temples and all of the mosques and all of the churches and every accursed, fucking remnant of this dying civilization. The burden of being among the few sane. In a hushed whisper, one of them told me she was gagging, choking on her own blood and vomit as they had their way with her. I growled and stood up, snarling at the dying whore as she writhed pathetically on the ground among my stripped lackeys. I silently put a bullet between her eyes and the flailing stopped. One didn’t stop his violation of the dead, but it wasn’t my concern. I had other things on my mind than dealing with some petty necrophiliac. Ashes to ashes, my dear. Dust to dust.
