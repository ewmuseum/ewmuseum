Sometimes Winning, Also Means You Lose

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-27 The element of surprise. That was all it took to change Sammy Brown's life. Well, that and the sound of the referee's hand as it slapped the mat three times in succession. It had all become a blur to him, passing so fast that he could hardly feel the air as it escaped his lungs with every breath that he took. Those three slaps made a sound that Sammy never expected to hear in his favor, especially after the battle he had endured against AJ Palmer, but he had heard it nonetheless. After using every wrestling maneuver he knew, and still failing miserably to succeed, the element of surprise – as he pulled Palmer backward into a schoolboy from the prone DDT position he was in – was all he needed to shock AJ long enough for the referee to make the count and declare him the winner of the bout. The metamorphosis had officially begun. Like a butterfly spreading its wings for the very first time, Sammy walked through the backstage area of the Hong Kong Coliseum with an extra spring in his step. What little hair he had left atop his head was matted with sweat, the perspiration seeping through wide open pores as he held his head up with pride. A grin was spread wide from cheek to cheek, as slightly yellowed teeth seemed to glow under the bright lights that radiated from above. It was as if he were walking on air, the way his feet never felt like they actually touched the ground. He was elated. For the first time in a long time, Sammy felt as if his life actually meant something. As if all of the failure he had endured, all of his shortcomings, had been preparing him for this moment. He had finally succeeded. The words of all of the naysayers that had told him that he would never amount to nothing, no longer felt like a weight on the back of his shoulders holding him down… and they never would again. "Congratulations, Mr. Brown." Sammy turned to look at the man in the gray three piece suit that had walked up behind him, surprised that someone actually knew his name… much less, that they actually recognized him by his features. "You are Mr. Brown, right," the man asked as he glanced down at a clipboard he held in his hand. Disappointed, Sammy nodded in agreement. "Great," the man said with a smile, as he extended his hand in Sammy's direction. "I'm Reginald Jenkins, Director of Talent Relations, and I'd like to take this time to inform you of your next scheduled match… schedule your travel arrangements to Portugal, as well." "You already know who I'll be facing?" He stood there in disbelief, he had just won the match and he already had another one set up. "Yes, Mr. Brown," Jenkins smiled, releasing a bit of the tension that had been building up within Sammy in the process. "Considering his opponent failed to arrive, a Mr. Easton Hall has been given a bye into the next round of the tournament. You winning the first match of the evening in his bracket, have earned the right to face him in the next round." "I see," Sammy replied, his eyes slightly widened upon hearing the name of his opponent. "I think I've heard that name before." "I'm not too familiar with the gentleman myself," Jenkins pulled a pen out from somewhere inside of his coat, as he took a quick glance across the clipboard, in search of something. "I do recall him being a rather rugged looking individual though." "Probably young too." "What was that," Jenkins asked as he turned his attention away from the clipboard and in Sammy's direction. "Sorry, just talking to myself," Brown answered with a sheepish smile. "We will be asking that you arrive in Portugal a few days early," he never lifted his head to look at Sammy as he talked. Instead, his attention remained fixated on whatever was written on the sheet of paper he was staring at. "We'll be holding a few press conferences throughout the day of the event, and we have decided that your story may be one that we would like to showcase." For some reason, Sammy could have sworn he heard him say 'exploit', but showcase was the word Jenkins had used. "You have a real feel good story going for you, Mr. Brown," Jenkins tried to boost his ego, unaware that ego was something Sammy never had. One booker had told Sammy that ego was nothing more than a burden that ate away at you from the inside, when you could no longer prove your worth inside of the ring. Considering Sammy could hardly compete in the ring, he didn't need anything eating away inside of him to make matters worse. "You are living proof that you should never give up on your dreams, and people eat that stuff up better than cake." The colorful comparison brought a chuckle from deep in his gut, escaping through teeth clenched tightly in a smile. "I'll be there." "Perfect." The answer gave Jenkins a smile of his own, as he began to jot down a few notes. "I just hope my boss won't be too pissed off at me when I ask for more time off." Sammy could feel the confidence he had felt a few weeks ago, when he found the confirmation letter of his acceptance into GTT6 in the mail, building within him once again. Though, this time, it felt stronger. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ For some reason, as she walked along the street staring at the ground in front of her, Isabel Stevenson had a strange feeling that she was being followed. She cautiously glanced over her shoulder, but there was no one there. At least, no one that drew any attention to themselves. A few kids from the local college, out looking for a good time, laughed and joked as they walked across the street – their attention focused on two blonds that had parked their powder blue Volkswagen Beetle alongside the curb. Still, no matter how hard she tried to ignore it, she could not shake this strange feeling that someone was watching her. Considering the amount of bars on the block, it could have just been some idiot that had noticed her walking alone and figured he could pick her up for a one night stand. An opinion that had probably changed as soon as he noticed the crow's foot wrinkles in the corners of her eyes, or maybe it was the fact that her eyebrows were painted on at awkward angles. Whatever the reason, whoever had been there was no longer there. He had probably ducked into one of the other dives, in search of someone more his age to take advantage of. Which was just what she was about to do. She stopped in front of one of the clubs, lifting her head to read the bright blue neon light above the door. Jack's Pub? If Bradley was going to drink himself into a stupor, the least he could have done was pick a bar whose owner had a bit more originality. She wondered to herself, just how long it took the man to come up with something so bland, and how long it took her husband to fall in love with the simplicity. Then again, after fifteen years of marriage, she shouldn't expect that much from him anymore. He had never been too picky about the sort of establishment he decided to get liquored up in. He could care less if the place was called The Rear End, so long as they carried a bottle of Absolut Vodka and a dark lager. She pulled the door toward her to open it, her senses quickly assaulted by the wave of deafening noise, the scent of beer, and the sardine can of people that had forced themselves into the hole in the wall Bradley had decided to begin his evening in. She squeezed between some jerk on a pay phone and a couple of girls making out before taking a quick glance of the room, it didn't take long to find her husband slumped over with a firm grip on a gallon of Absolut in one hand, and a frosty mug in the other. Standing to his left – possibly scanning the room in search of her – was Robert Stinson; the Bellesford Canine Exercise Specialist. She never understood why Bradley enjoyed the company of the dog walker, but as she headed in their direction and noticed the way Robert almost stood guard over him, she began to realize what it was. He was comfortable. Stinson posed no threat to Bradley, he was someone that Bradley could easily control even if they were not at work. "Wow," Robert rose to his feet politely. "Never figured you for a party girl, Isabel." "I'm not," her gaze never left that of her significant other, hardly acknowledging the man at her side who had phoned her about a half hour ago – to inform her that Bradley was getting plastered. Considering the man could get him black-balled all over the city, she understood that he did not want Bradley to know that he had ratted him out. Having never cared for Stinson, Isabel could care less about the situation, or his working relationship with her husband, but she would not break the trust he had attempted to create. At the moment, she was more worried about the scene Bradley would make if she tried to drag him out. "Come on honey, let's go finish that bottle at the house." His eyes were narrowed, and his face appeared as if someone had pulled down on his chin at the same time as pulling up on his hair, but he never turned his attention away from the liqour. "Can't babe, I'm still sober enough to see only one of you." "What's wrong with you," she asked sincerely, as a stray lock of hair fell in front of her face when she bent at the waist to lean over his back. "It's better if you just go back home, Bel." He rolled his shoulder, hoping to back her away from him as he took another swig of Vodka straight from the bottle. "If they find me, I'd rather you not be around." The stench of cigarette smoke was soaking it's way into the fibers of her white linen shirt, yet she couldn't pull away from her estranged husband. "What are you talking about, Brad?" "Leave!" He practically screamed, as the alcohol scorched the inside of his throat on it's quest to drown his worries. "I don't want them to hurt you." "No one's going to hurt me, Brad," she reassured him, even though she had no clue who or what he was talking about. "I made a mistake," he muttered softly to himself, under heated breath. He pressed his face into the palms of his hands, resting his elbows on the money green Formica that covered the length of the bar. "You're talking in circles, Brad." "He's been talking like that all night," Robert interrupted the couple's conversation. He stood directly behind Isabel, so she had to turn her head to glance over her shoulder in order to give him her attention. "Really started creeping me out a bit when I saw him talking to the empty bottles." "I just need to get him home," she placed her hand on the small of his back, caressing him before turning to Robert. "Did you come in your car, or his?" "His," he said with a slight grimace, as if embarrassed. "He said he didn't want to be seen in a Pinto." "I should have expected that." She turned her attention back to her husband, who had continued to mumble to himself under his breath. "What was that," she asked softly, leaning closer. "I can't believe he won." The tears that seemed to hover in the corner of his eyes teased him slightly, as they threatened to stream down his rose-tinted cheeks and expose an emotional side no man should ever disclose inside such a seedy dwelling. "They told me he's never been more than a joke that kills time, and he still managed to pull it out." Saline streaks began to trickle down like an army of ants marching toward a picnic basket, as he turned to face his wife for the first time. "He fucking won." The words came out with a soft chuckle, his lips quivering into a faux smile. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ He had finally done it. A few moments ago, when his hand had been raised by the referee and his name had been announced over the Coliseum's Public Address system to the roar of a capacity crowd of over twelve thousand people, he knew he had overcome years of adversity that had been weighing on him like a ton of bricks. The thing is, until he had heard it come from the lips of Reginald Jenkins – someone he knew was not part of a reoccurring dream that had haunted him for the last ten years – he had yet to allow it to settle in and become something he could actually take stock of. This was certainly not a dream, and even if it was just a victory in the first round of the tournament, he had accomplished something that forty eight other men – men with far greater accomplishments listed in their portfolio – had failed to do… He had won. Perennial favorites like Seymour Almasy; who had also been beaten for the PrimeTime Central Global Title by Kimbusa, and Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins; who failed to repeat his performance of last year when he was defeated at the hands of Clinton Sage in a grueling match-up that words could not do justice. Yet, a mere doorman from the city that never sleeps, managed to walk in without much fanfare and pull out an upset victory against one of Global's champions. To most people, that would not be something worth mentioning, but to someone who has spent most of his life looked down upon by people with upturned noses. Well, listening to their necks stretch as they turned them in his direction made him feel like a kid again, running outside with a dollar in his hand and a grin stretched from ear to ear as the captivating tune, blaring through a loudspeaker mounted atop an old Chevy panel van, called to him as if it were the Pied Piper. Music to my ears, he thought as he walked into the locker room. It was hard to put a finger on how he was feeling at the moment: his heart raced, his hands were clammy. It was almost as if he was in love, and considering it was a feeling he had not felt in such a long time, he had forgotten how he was supposed to react. All he really wanted to do was grab a towel, let the hot water from the shower relax his muscles, then head out for a night on the town… maybe find a karaoke bar to make a fool of himself in. All he really wanted to do was enjoy his new life, a life that began when that final slap of flesh against canvas echoed through his ears. Of course, sometimes you are not lucky enough to do what you want to do. Especially when you realize that doing something that you want to do means you are not doing something that someone else wants you to do. He opened the door and grabbed his duffel bag, where his towel and a fresh set of clothes awaited him. Before he could close the door, he noticed something at the bottom of the locker. It was a brown briefcase. A briefcase filled with close to one hundred thousand dollars. Money he had taken with the promise of more after he lost his first round match against AJ Palmer. He knew before he left the states that he could not follow through with the agreement, but Stevenson had told him that it was too late… the bets had been placed, and someone else's money was already involved. Bradley had told him, that if he failed to follow through with their agreement, it could lead to the loss of many lives. Including his own. "Stevenson," he whispered softly to himself, as his eyes remained focused on the problems that awaited him back in the States. ¤=¤=¤=¤=¤=¤ "Are you angry?" Bradley asked his wife morosely. "Don't be angry with me, Bel." "Of course I'm-" His eyes were droopy and moist with tears as he stared up at her, cutting her off before she could finish. "No, I'm not angry with you. I just don't understand why you keep doing this to yourself… to us." "I'm going to go pay the tab." Robert decided to give the couple some time alone. Well, away from his eavesdropping, since none of the other patrons cared about what they were talking about. "I was just trying to make things better…" Bradley blurted out, as he desperately tried to avoid the look of disappointment in his wife's eyes. "Trying to make things easier for us, Bel." "We can talk about this at home." Isabel replied, not making much sense of anything he was saying. "I'll put on a pot of coffee and we can figure something out… we can get through this, Brad. We've been able to fight our way through everything else." "Not this time." He shook his head from side to side, plaintively. "They're not going to let this one go… it was too much this time." "You're not making any sense, Brad. Look, let's just go and we'll-" She stopped short, sighing when she realized she wasn't going to get through to him by being nice, not through the haze of alcohol anyway. "We're not talking about this here anymore. Get on your feet, now." "No." "Bradley, I don't know what is going on with you, but I'll be damned if I'm going to stand here and argue with you about this here." Her voice raised slightly, just enough to get her point across. "I'm not one of the kids, Bel." He snapped back. A few of the patrons within ear shot glanced in their direction, but the music blasting through the speakers drowned out their argument from the rest of the crowd. "Then don't act like one," came her quick retort. "You don't have that much time before I make you look like the asshole that you're acting like in front of all of these people." His eyes were wide when he finally took the time to look up at her, he reluctantly stepped off the stool his hind end had grown accustomed to for the last couple of hours. "Why don't you-" "Shut up." She yanked him by the arm, almost dragging him out behind her as she walked toward the door. "I'm not in the mood to discuss this with you anymore." "You don't understand, Bel." He shook his head as she tugged him by the sleeve. "They're going to kill me." "Stop talking nonsense." She pulled him through the door after pushing it open. "I swear, alcohol just makes you so ignorant." Out of the corner of her eye, she could see Robert walking briskly in their direction, as he tried to avoid knocking someone who was not paying attention over. She stepped aside and waited for him to exit, not having to wait too long before the door opened. "Hey, do you think I can bum a ride back to the Bellesford?" He shoved his hands into the pockets of his faded jeans, whether the rips and frays were there because of style or age was not known, shielding them from the nip of the wind as it struck the bare skin of his arm. "I left my car in the parking garage back at work." Isabel fished around in her pocket for a moment, before she pulled out a set of keys and handed them to him. "Just drive my car over there and leave the keys at the check-in desk. I'll drive Brad to work tomorrow morning and grab them then." "Cool." She rolled her eyes when she noticed how excited he had become when he saw the Cadillac logo on the key's remote, he clearly had no clue that her car was not as luxurious as the one her husband drove… but he'd soon figure it out. "Thanks again for everything that you've done," she tried her best not to announce to her husband that Robert was the one who had given her a call. "I really appreciate it." "Don't mention it." His attention was on the royal crest etched into the plastic key-less entry remote, which he could not help but massage with the tip of his finger. "I parked a few streets down," she said before she turned back to her husband, who simply stared at the ground. Dejected. "You remember where you parked?" A slight nod was her response, as he lifted his arm to point down the street. "Right before the alley." She grabbed hold of his arm, Robert smiled and waved as he strolled off toward Isabel's vehicle. At first, Bradley hesitated, but another – rather violent – tug of his arm convinced him to follow his wife. "I wish things could be different, Bel." He muttered softly to her, almost apologetic as he walked alongside her. "I just wanted us to be happy." "And what makes you think I'm not ha-" Bradley looked up from the ground, as a sudden jerk of his arm surprised him, to find his wife being carried into the dark alley with a hand clamped tightly against her lips by a virtual beast. Shoulders as wide as he was tall, with a light brown suit that had to have been custom tailored so the coat would not drag across the ground, the man was bald, with wrinkles of skin forming trails across the top of his head. "Let go of-" "Mr. Stevenson." The voice that came from behind him caused Bradley to jump slightly, as he was caught completely off guard. He turned to find a man towering over him, with his lips curled into a sinister sneer as he looked down at him. This man was dressed to the tee as well: a finely pressed coat, with a slight bulge between his chest and arm, draped over his massive frame, with slacks that matched perfectly to the tie knotted around his neck. "Please," he motioned with his right hand toward the alley, "would you be so kind as to join us for a moment." "Leave my wife out of this," Bradley demanded. "I'll go with you, but let her go first." The man just grinned as he shook his head. "What gave you the idea this was a negotiation? Now, the way I see it, you could either walk into the alley of your own free will, or the screams that manage to escape your wife's lungs before they fill with blood will have you running in." The grin was still present on his face, but nothing in his tone gave the impression that he was even remotely joking. "She has nothing…" the words came out broken – between sobs – as he fought back the cascade of tears that had begun to blur his vision. "… to do with this." "We know," the man replied, "but Mr. DeAngelis would like her to be present. Just to be certain that you understand the severity of the situation." Again he motioned toward the alley, the low hum of a motor echoed against the walls in a casual invite. Defeated, Bradley Stevenson lowered his head and marched toward the alley, where he vanished amongst the shadows that had also enveloped his wife.
