Changing Paths: Starting Anew

17 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The drip-drip-drip of the midnight rain patted down on the closed window in Sebastian’s room. The rain had begun falling down with no warning. No thunder. No lightning. No weather report. Slouched on his chair with only his desk lamp to illuminate the room, Sebastian Moraco stared down at the collage of open books and scribbled notepads on his desk. College life wasn’t getting any easier. Sure cutting class, meeting loose girls, getting in trouble, fighting, frat parties…all of it was great. But it wasn’t so great when you have a lot of catching up to do because you went against your parents’ wishes to fight in not one, but two wrestling tournaments – none of which you won. Sebastian Moraco’s latest escapade involved the Prime Time Central’s second annual Cruiserweight Tournament. Sebastian’s adventure was short-lived, unfortunately it was also during his college’s finals week. He missed those and thus guaranteed himself of at least one more semester. Yet, it wasn’t delaying his college career that was the worst of his decision to go wrestle. It was the fact that what he had feared had come true: his father reacted violently. Sebastian left his parents that day saying his goodbyes only to his mother and not informing his father of where he was to go. His father…no his family had expressed great disdain towards his new career in fighting. He was given so much hope, ambition, and dreams of being something that his family considered “more productive” to be a wrestler. But that wasn’t what Sebastian wanted. When his father found out about Sebastian’s departure, he went into his usual temper tirade: screaming and throwing things around. Maria Moraco tried to comfort him with arguments about Sebastian being old enough to make his own decisions and other moot points, but to no avail. In his rage, Miguel punched his wife. She hit the floor hard and fell dazed for a few moments. Miguel stared at his wife and then stared down at his own hands. He had been this guy for the greater part of their marriage. He was the cheater, liar, abuser, and womanizer. Maria had endured it for the sake the children, but it had gotten too far. Sebastian Moraco returned that night depressed from his outing and looking forward to just move on. His depression quickly turned to confusion then to anger when he didn’t find his mother around and then was informed that she was at the hospital for a head injury. At the hospital, the doctors informed Sebastian that Maria had been struck in the head and then hit herself on the floor, hard. Maria, upon waking up from her injury, told Sebastian that it was his father who hit her – a man who was nowhere to be seen. Sebastian went into a tirade of his own, cursing and vowing that he was going to get back at him. Maria pleaded with him and after a few moments of heartfelt mother-son talk, Maria convinced Sebastian not to take matters into his own hand and certainly not violently. She was going to get a divorce. Sebastian picked up his pen and attempted to continue his essay on “The Positives of Globalization” for his Sociology class. “Let’s see here...expansion of technology, political, and economical reform…” Sebastian muttered to himself trying to outline his thesis in his head. But he wasn’t getting anywhere. “Damn it, maybe I shouldn’t have missed classes to go wrestle.” Ring. Ring. Sebastian became startled by his cell phone suddenly ringing at this time of night. Who could be calling at this time of night, he wondered. Sebastian ruffled through his tornado-induced stack of papers searching for his phone. On the fifth ring, he answered it. “Hello?” “Is this Sebastian Moraco?” “Yeah…” Sebastian answered trying to figure out where he had heard this familiar voice before. “Good.” The caller on the other line replied. “I was afraid that I had gotten the wrong number. That would have been bad.” “Who are you?” Sebastian asked searching his desk for any phone numbers he might have. “Just a friend.” The sly reply came. “I know it’s late so I’ll make this quick. I have an offer for you.” “What? You want me to switch to another long distance provider?” Sebastian joked. “I’m fine with Sprint.” “Haha, no.” the caller replied with shared humor. “Although, after hearing you have Sprint I do feel obligated to actually offer you another service provider.” Sebastian laughed. “So…what is this offer?” “A wrestling match.” The caller replied. “It will only be one match. If it goes well, it can turn into two, and then three, and then four…you get the picture.” Sebastian sat up straight clearing his throat. “Why are you telling me this? I don’t wrestle.” The caller let out a belly-busting laugh. “You aren’t kidding anyone Mr. GTT4 and Mr. CW2. I know you love to wrestle. Do you really think that writing an essay on the positives of globalization is what you want to do?” Sebastian’s eyes lit up. “What did you just say?” “Just meet me at Luigi’s tomorrow at seven at night. There we can discuss matters more appropriately.” “Who the hell are you?!” Sebastian screamed. “You should turn your bedroom light on, Sebastian. It’s not good for your eyes to try to read all those books in such a dim light.” With that he hung up, and Sebastian quickly rushed to the window surveying the adjacent buildings and the cars below. After a moment of surveillance, he closed the shades and went back to his desk. Who was that caller? Sebastian was sure he had heard that voice before. SCENE 2 Walking outside the perimeter of his college campus, Sebastian looked down at his watch. It was almost seven and the sun was still up. At this time of year, the sun didn’t set down in New York City until it was almost eight o’clock. Sebastian once again reminisced on the phone call he had received the night before much like he had all day. He wondered why the mysterious caller had suggested Luigi’s and then realized that Luigi’s was just a mere ten minute walk away from the college. Whoever it was, certainly knew a bit about Sebastian. If that was the case, chances were he was already spying on him right this minute. Sebastian made straight for the restaurant, not because he wanted to meet his caller, but because he was hungry. He heard Luigi’s was one of the finest restaurants in New York City. At the restaurant Sebastian did not see anyone he recognized nor did he see anyone who looked like they were waiting for company. He was assigned a table for two and ordered an appetizer to keep himself and his glass of water entertained while his host was on his way. After twenty minutes of waiting, and his stomach grumbling, Sebastian ordered the baby back ribs and began to feast on his dinner. “I was afraid you wouldn’t show.” Sebastian looked up from his feast with a bit of barbeque sauce on the corner of his mouth. “Who are you?” Before him stood a man dressed in a three piece suit, patted down obsidian hair, and in his forties. He took a seat at the table and stared at Sebastian as if daring him to guess. “Have you forgotten about me already, Sebastian?” He asked. “I admit I didn’t give you any good news the last time we met with that 40-1 odds bit.” Sebastian’s eyes lit up. The BBQ sauce dripped. The janitor at the DC arena in GTT4… The Vegas bookie… The limo driver at the CW2… They were all the same person. “You were at the GTT4 arena and at the CW2 arena.” Sebastian remarked. “Who are you?” The man chuckled. “Simply put I’m a man who goes where the winds go. I find opportunities for my clients, profits for myself, and wander the world. To put it in terms you understand: I fix financial negotiations and I am a friend of someone we both know.” “Who?” asked Sebastian. “Ryan Howell.” The man answered. “Actually his father. I keep him up to date on any underground money laundering schemes and he keeps me out of jail.” “So...Mr. Spinks…what is your real name?” Sebastian inquired taking another bite from his baby back ribs. “Michael Sloan.” Michael answered. “Now that the formalities are out of the way, I want to get down to business. There’s a spot open for you – a wrestling spot. Prime Time Central, your favorite company, is offering a little ongoing event called Infinite Gauntlet. It’s an endless series of matches with the winner going on until he quits or is beaten.” “And endless series of matches…” Sebastian repeated back savoring the taste of the ribs in his mouth. “Are you kidding me? I had a hell of a dilemma just going away for a few weeks for GTT4 and CW2, and you expect me to accept this? The last time I went away, someone I loved got hurt.” Michael nodded. “I know about happened to your mother. But Infinite Gauntlet isn’t going to be as daunting or as abroad as the other events have been. If you lose, you lose. You can try again later or just quit. If you win, you keep on fighting. That doesn’t mean you’ll have to be away for a period of time failing classes. You only fight one match a night at a nearby location and then you go home and wait for your next opponent to be selected. It’s really no different than if you had joined the soccer team. Some days you have to stay late to practice, some weekends you might have to go in for some drills, and some days you might have to be away on a road trip. IT really is no different.” Sebastian gulped down his water washing down the ribs. “You know it sounds like something really simple and advantageous. But I seriously don’t wrestle anymore. I have a lot studying to do.” Michael laughed. “How many times did you practice that line? We both know you don’t want to become an architect much to the disdain of your family in Spain. We both know you live to fight. This is really a good chance for you. You have nothing to lose.” “Why are you asking me? Didn’t you see I failed the last two times?” “You have potential.” Michael answered. “Be honest. Did you actually think you could win your first major event on your first try? You barely knew what a headlock was when you fought Inphino Blitz. How did you expect to beat seasoned fighters like Doctor Curiosity and Silvio Fiore? But yet, you gave them all a scare and people find it less and less ridiculous to call you a formidable challenger. In due time, you can be a major star.” “I don’t want to do this. You still haven’t told me why you are offering me this.” Sebastian repeated his question. “I am offering you this for the number one reason guys will do just about anything.” Michael replied. “Family?” “Okay, second reason.” “Friends?” “Third reason.” “Career?” “Fourth reason.” “Girls?” “Money!” Michael exclaimed. “I’m talking about money!” For a minute the rest of the people at the restaurant turned to them but then quickly went about their own business. “You think I am shallow that I would jump at the first sign of money?” “Of course not…” Michael explained. “I said you have potential and it just isn’t for your fighting. I know to you family is more important than anything. Think of it this way: the more money you win, the less burden you put on your mother to pay for college. Look at the apartment you both live in. It’s far too inappropriate for you both. Is that what you want for your mother? A life of just getting by?” “No…” “Was living in a small - no air-conditioned - apartment the American Dream your parents thought about?” “No…” “Take the offer. The first match, win or lose, will be five grand. From then on, if you win the pot will get bigger and bigger. I know you don’t like taking money, but you can’t say no to family.” Sebastian finished the last of his meal. “Hypothetically, if I were interested who and when will I fight?” Michael grinned like the Grinch. “Hypothetically, you would fight this weekend and the person you’re fighting is someone named the Uber Judge. Don’t let the misnomer fool you. He’s already won all of the events beginning matches against multiple opponents. You would be his first one on one match. He will not be easy to conquer.” “So in other words, I got a tough one.” “You’ll do fine.” Michael tried to console him. “When was the last time you saw me fight anyone easy?” Sebastian mused. “Actually…never.” Michael laughed. “The fight is this weekend. Now let’s get out of here now that business is done with. By the way, you have some sauce on your mouth.” SCENE 3 Sebastian left the restaurant (paying for the bill of course and tipping) with Michael Sloan. Outside, the two approached Michael’s Porsche when a man a bit younger, maybe in his thirties, approached Mr. Sloan. He was ruggedly handsome but didn’t dress as sophisticated with his denim jeans and t-shirt. “Michael, what are you doing here?” The stranger asked. “Jeff!” Michael greeted him back obviously knowing who the stranger was. “What are you doing here? I thought you were still in Europe?” “Nah…that fell through.” Jeff explained. “Besides, I’m finding a hell of a time here in New York City. Like just now, I met this fine-ass Spanish woman. We went out for dinner and then ended up back at her place, which really needs some work, but that’s not the point. Before I know it, we’re fucking…I gotta tell you, she’s a real fireball in bed.” Michael laughed. “Did you get her name this time?” Jeff glared at him. “Funny. Yes, I got her name. Maria Monaco…Morocco…something like that. She had a bruise on her head, but who cares she was fucking hot and we were already fucking hours after we meet.” “Um…what did you say?” Michael asked hoping that Jeff hadn’t mentioned Sebastian’s mother, but that was foolish wishing. “Yeah this woman, Maria…I don’t think I ever saw anyone ride…” Jeff stopped when he saw Sebastian staring at him with rabid eyes. “Who are you?” Without any warning, Sebastian lunged at Jeff and knocked him down to the ground with a solid right hand punch. Stumbling, Jeff tried to get back to his feet and fight back, but the resilient Spaniard was too much and knocked him back down. He began a fury of kicks and punches as bystanders started to gather around wanting to watch the beating much more than to break it up. Sebastian picked Jeff up and slammed him against the wall, punching him in his mouth, drawing blood. “Knock it off Sebastian!” Michael yelled, pulling Sebastian away from Jeff. “Just get in the car, I’ll give you a ride home.” Sebastian reluctantly got into the car. Michael walked over to Jeff leant up against the wall. “Are you okay?” he asked. Jeff mumbled. “The kid punches like he’s made of steel.” “Well, you did say you fucked his mother.” Michael grinned. “Thanks for the show. I wasn’t sure if he still had it in it, but I guess he does.” Jeff pushed him away. “Just give me my money.” Michael took out an envelope and slid it underneath him. “I’ll call you tonight and let you know the rest.” Michael returned to the car and got in. Looking over at Sebastian –still angered from what he had heard Jeff say- he turned the ignition on. “Don’t mind Jeff. He’s an idiot. He…” “Just take me home.” Sebastian interrupted. “I’m taking your offer up.” Michael nodded. “Good. You won’t regret this.” “I’m not going to let my mother down again. She deserves better than this. She deserves better than anyone. My father couldn’t provide that kind of comfort for my family, but I will.” Michael didn’t reply but just nodded and drove off. He knew Sebastian Moraco was mature for his age, but he didn’t know how much. Didn’t matter…he was still going to fight. Sebastian slouched in his seat and looked down at his fists. They were beginning to bruise a bit. Jeff got off easy, he thought. Next time, he wouldn’t think twice of killing him. Next time…Sebastian thought. There won’t be a next time. He wasn’t going to neglect his family and mother again. He needed a way for them to move on, and fighting was the only way he knew how. All he had to do now was beat someone named the Uber Judge.
