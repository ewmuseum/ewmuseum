Internal Passion: The Process

26 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2006-10-26 John had made up his mind. He had been contemplating it for a few months, but he figured the urge would leave his mind like it had done many times before. For some reason the passion inside him remained at the front of his mind. With each passing week it built. It built to the point he could ignore it no longer. He knew he needed to make one final attempt at getting back into his first profession. No one should misunderstand the situation. He enjoyed wrestling. He loved it. It had most certainly seeped into his veins much the same baseball had done when he was little. There was just something about baseball that he couldn’t let go of. It was like a man’s first high school sweetheart. You could never forget it. He knew something had to change from his past attempts. They were half-assed and ineffective. This time was the real deal. He knew it was going to take persistence and a lot of work. The time had come for John to head back to the baseball diamond. October 12th, 2006 2:13 PM CST "I’d like to schedule an appointment with Bud Selig." John stated into his cell phone. Covel carried his six foot four inch frame along the mall parking lot. John was so confident that this attempt would be successful that he went out and bought himself a brand new suit. Even though he had begun to earn a comfortable income from FUSE, He didn’t like to spend money. So the five hundred dollar suit he carried with him definitely meant something. "John Covel. He’ll know who I am." John said through a chuckle. Deep down John was nervous. His hands trembled ever so slightly as he approached his car and tried to steady the phone between his head and shoulder. He laid the suit across his backseat as he waited for another response on the phone. "He will? You’re not kidding me are you? Cause that would be pretty sick." John said with disbelief in his voice. A smile came across John’s face as he listened to the secretary on the phone. John scribbled down a few words on his hand before ending the call and taking a deep breathe. Suddenly a yell erupted from his vocal chords that echoed off the cars surrounding him. He had his meeting. Now all he had to do was convince Bud Selig he had matured enough to deserve a hearing with the Major League owners. October 15th, 2006 3:38 PM EST John sat at the desk of Major League Baseball Commissioner Bud Selig. Bud sat at the desk, lightly flipping through some paperwork that sat in front of him. Covel’s uneasiness was obvious. He couldn’t sit still. His foot hadn’t stopped tapping the floor since he sat down in the office nearly forty minutes prior. "So, how does it look, sir?" John asked, Bud shrugged and let silence fill the air before chuckling to break the fear that had come into John’s body. John forced a chuckle of his own as Selig closed the paperwork and removed his reading glasses. "What you did was not a minor deal. It’s a wonder the charges were dropped." He said after clearing his throat. John nodded in agreement. Many days had passed that he imagined what his life would have been like had he been arrested and convicted. Now wasn’t the time to conjure up those thoughts. John sat himself upright in his chair. "I know that, sir. I feel as though I’ve proved my dedication to helping people out. I am a volunteer fireman." John replied. The statement didn’t even bring an emotion to the face of Bud. He sat, staring at the papers in front of him. Silence once again filled the air momentarily. "Mr. Covel, I’ve been following your life here and there since you made that first call to me two years ago." He stated. John gulped. Was it a good thing? Suddenly anything John had done in the past two years flashed in his mind. Had he done anything that could have given him a bad image? "You have, sir?" He pushed out. Selig nodded as he reached under a pile of papers and retrieved a small black book. He opened it and began flipping through the pages. "I have. What I’ve seen of your contributions to your community and the dedication you showed with pitching three times a week impressed me." He said with a straight face. John’s stomach filled with excitement. He knew that this was it. The fate of his baseball career once again was in the hands of the man sitting in front of him. The anticipation grew for John as Selig stopped on a page of the black book and looked back up. "Which is why I’m going to grant you a hearing the Major League Owners to decide your re-instatement." Selig announced. John sat silent. He had pictured this moment numerous times. He imagined himself springing from the seat and jumping with joy. But when it all became a reality, all he could do was stare at the floor in disbelief. After a few moments, John finally lifted his head to reveal a tear rolling down his cheek. "Thank you, sir. You do not know how much this means to me." John squeezes out. John pushed himself to a standing position and extended a hand across the Oak desk. Selig accepted it and stood himself. When they released, John reached up and wiped the tear from his eye. "I’ll have my secretary call you with the information." Selig said. John smiled and nodded before turning and walking to the door. As he exited he thanked the commissioner one more time. October 19th, 2006 12:00 PM EST He was still nervous. Why shouldn’t he be? John was about to step in front of thirty people who looked down on him just three years ago. John stepped to the door leading into the Commissioner’s officer on Park Boulevard and took a deep breath. "This is it, John." He mumbled to himself. The butterflies only grew as he pulled the door open and stepped inside. Pictures of historical icons and moments littered the walls of the entrance hallway. His voice was shaky as he approached the receptionist’s desk. The young lady smiled as she looked to him. "How can I help you, sir?" She inquired. John flashed a smile back to the young brunette and looked to his feet, adjusting a crease in his suit at the same time. "I’m John Covel. Here for the hearing." He said. The young lady nodded and set a clipboard on the counter. She explained its intention and John followed direction by filling out the necessary information. She took the clipboard back after John had finished and looked it over. She nodded and handed him a visitor ID badge. "Okay. Take the elevator to the fourth floor and then take a right. You’ll see the conference room from there. Good luck, Mr. Covel." She explained. John smiled back and thanked her before turning and heading towards the elevator. As he waited for its arrival he began to really think about the things that awaited him on the fourth floor. No matter what he couldn’t escape the glares that were sure to still be present. The ride up seemed like an eternity to John. The anticipation and fear he had inside him seemed to place everything in slow motion. He stepped from the metal box and took the right turn, leading him into the conference room. He was almost an entire hour early, but it seemed as though plenty of people were too. "Hey, John." Rang a voice from the left. The voice seemed to slow and fade to silence as John looked to the front row of chairs and spotted the man occupying one of the seats. John’s stomach sank as his mind began racing. The events of that June day had always been stowed in the back of his brain, but never had they seemed so clear. The image of the man he attacked faded as John closed his eyes and relived bad decision. June 12th, 2003 4:30 PM CST The air is thick with the Midwestern humidity. Sweat glistens on the forehead of the six foot four inch pitcher standing off the back of the mound. He takes a breath and steps back to the mound, ready to continue. He had never pitched this bad. Never. Two batters had received bruises compliments of a Covel fastball. That’s twice the number of batters he’d hit in his career before the game. "Settle down, John." He muttered to himself. Little things had been racing through his mind ever since he had been called up to the Majors. No matter how small of a thing it was, it came to the front of his mind at the worst times. When would he have to mow again? Was it over a hundred degrees? He tried to shake the thoughts from his brain before setting himself. The ball sailed from his hand and streaked through the air. Covel cringed as the ball began to drift upwards. He wanted it to stop. He begged for it to stay where it was. But his prayers weren’t answered. The batter fell to his rear as the ball made a loud CLANK against the chain-link of the backstop. "Fuck." John said. He buried his face in his glove and stepped from the mound. Why hadn’t the manager taken him out? John didn’t want to pitch anymore. He was tired. He had no control left. Yet each time he called the manager out to say so, he was assured that he’d do fine. Things hadn’t gone fine. Covel knew what he should have seen as he looked back to home plate. It wasn’t a two hundred pound man charging towards him. Out of instinct Covel threw his glove to the ground and caught the man in mid-air. They both plummeted to the ground and began rolling around, trying to find leverage. The ground they fought upon was soon cluttered with other players and managers whom joined in on the chaotic rumble. "Motherfucker." John muttered as a weak punch landed on his cheekbone. A surge of adrenaline shot into his veins. He took control of the smaller man and landed a punch of his own. He reared back for another right, but the lights dimmed and slowly went out as a stray punch connected with the back of his head. When he came to ten minutes later, he was sitting in his dug out. The first blurred image he saw were the umpires congregating at home plate. At first he couldn’t remember what had happened, but the pain that began to seep from his head was a quick reminder. He reached up and clenched his skull as the manager sat next to him. "You all right, kid?" He asked John. Covel slowly nodded as he stood. Luckily the pain seemed to subside as he came further back to consciousness. He stepped up onto the second step of the dug out and looked to the field where one of the men in blue nodded to the others and back-peddled. He pointed to the two men standing at the entrance to either dugout before throwing his hand into the air. Heat of the moment. That’s all he could think as he ran through the grass towards home plate. John’s manager and a few other players were quick to chase after him but were unable to catch him before he reached the collection of umpires. The fit he threw escalated in intensity as a hand grabbed each of his shoulders. They pulled him back a few feet but he fought them and came back forward. He was shouting obscenities that he had never used before that were drawing gasps from first-row fans. The pain throbbed in the back of Covel’s head, temporarily stopping his tirade. By the time it subsided, Covel had been dragged ten feet from the home plate umpire. The air between them fell silent as the umpire looked in John’s direction and muttered something he never should have. "You’re not as good as you think you are, Covel. Take that ego off of your shoulders and put it in your back pocket. Good luck in the majors." There’s no way this much adrenaline could be produced by one human body in such a small amount of time. John broke the grasp of the two men escorting him back to the dugout. He bolted forward and began wondering what he was doing. Out of rage and a feeling of disrespect, Covel swiftly bent over and picked up the Louisville Slugger that still sat in the dirt by the plate. He had never been a good hitter, but the umpire would never have guessed with the power that went into that swing. A collective groan came from the onlookers as the mass of wood connected with the small of the man’s back. He cringed in pain and fell to a knee, trying to catch his breath. He gasped and struggled for three seconds before tasting the sweet Iowa oxygen. The breath was immediately pushed back out as a second swing thumped across his back. It drove him to his stomach; an even more vulnerable position. Nobody seemed to be able to run fast enough as John lifted the bat over his head and brought it down one last time. Lucky for the umpire the barrel impacted the ground, absorbing ninety-five percent of the impact and causing the wood to splinter. "What the fuck?" John asked himself as he watched the bat fall to the ground in two pieces. At the same time a few team mates rushed up to John and shoved him, snapping him back. Fear filled up his face as he realized what he had done. John turned and leaned to the man he had attacked. He tried to attend to the man, but was pulled away before being able to do anything. He kicked and screamed; shouted and wailed trying to find out of the man was okay. The back of his head throbbed again, subduing him enough for security to sufficiently place him in the dug out. They held him on the bench for a moment as a few team mates struggled to catch his attention. They began shouting at him but were interrupted as the wail of a siren rang from right field. An ambulance slowly drove down the baseline as the crowd remained hushed. John finally snapped out of his small trance and looked up to see two uniformed police officers standing in front of him. John lowered his head and nodded as he pushed himself up. One of the officers lightly grasped John’s arm but John pulled it away, showing his cooperation. For the first time since he was drafted, a tear fell from his eye and rolled down his cheek. The three men walked inside one of the offices and the door closed, secluding them from sight. October 19th, 2006 3:35 PM EST John took a gulp from his water and scanned the people sitting around him. At the front of the room were the thirty men that held Covel’s fate in their hands. They had listened to everything that had been presented. The time had come for them to voice their opinions. Covel’s stomach churned as the floor was turned over to the owners, one by one. Each man took a few quick seconds to say a few words and offer their vote. The Mets, Red Sox and Marlins all gave good reviews, allowing the tension to ease as the vote slowly began to build in his favor. Why had he made those decisions? The regret for his actions had never been as intense as it was then. He let his head fall to look at the floor as he listened with great intent. "Mr. Hendry, what have you to say?" Bud Selig asked. John’s face lit up as he heard the Cubs’ General Manager begin to speak. Out of the thirty owners, he knew most of their votes could have gone either way. But he expected at least one "yes" vote. This was it. "The Cubs organization is very impressed with Mr. Covel’s behavior since the events that took place on June 12th, 2003. We think he would be a great addition to the free agent pool for the upcoming 2007 season. The Cubs organization votes yes." He states. The statement brings a small bit of murmuring from the small group of people that had congregated in the conference room. Covel’s mind slowly began to drift as more teams spoke on the matter. Some teams offered "I don’t care" opinions and voted to move the process along. The quick monologues that had been presented allowed time to fly by. Before John knew it, he sat there listening to what the Washington Nationals had to say. They concluded their statement and the room was left in a state of trepidation. The vote was tied, fifteen to fifteen. The butterflies suddenly prodded John again. A thousand new emotions surged into his mind. What did this mean? Who would have ever guessed that this hearing would have ended in a…tie? A lousy tie. He didn’t know what to do from one moment to the next. Anger would fill him up and then get shoved to the side for apprehension. His breathing became heavier and perspiration dripped from his pores. John was abruptly brought to tranquility as the burly voice of the commissioner butted in. "We find ourselves in a small bind here. Fortunately, I had inspected the Major League Baseball procedures before we commenced. It is my duty to offer a vote and break the tie." He boomed. The confusion inside John didn’t subside. He didn’t know how to take it. Was he in for sure? The only thing that drowned out the confusion was the throbbing that grew at the back of his skull. The pain was reminiscent to what he had felt after the fight. He was overloaded. He just couldn’t take it anymore. Selig believed in him enough to grant him the very hearing they sat in. But would that mean he believed in him enough to grant him re-instatement? "Mr. Covel, for the past two years you have laid a barrage of phone calls and e-mails on my office. Your persistence shows heart and dedication. On the other hand, it’s difficult for me to judge how well your temper has come along. You joined a sport that promotes violent outbreaks on a weekly basis. You also did not sign up for the anger management class the league suggested you take part in. The decision I lay before you today may or may not alter your life in immense ways, but it will be one of the defining moments of my tenure. I have been denying re-instatement to Pete Rose since 1997 and find myself asking why you are any different." John’s stomach sank. He saw his final attempt at re-uniting with his true love become what he believed to be futile. He was so sure he was re-instated that he hadn’t even thought of what he would do if he had failed.
