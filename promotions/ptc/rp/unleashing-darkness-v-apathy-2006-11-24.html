Unleashing Darkness V: Apathy

24 November 2006

Date: 2006-11-24 The Letter Some Cheap Motel Room. The Bronx, New York. Three Days Ago. Jacob McKail loomed over the unopened letter that rested happily upon the worn, floral design of the motel room bed linen and glared at the small rectangular correspondence, arms folded and face undecided. He turned his back on the thing and shook his head dismissively. "Impossible!" "What‘s impossible, kid?" The harsh voice questioned, taking McKail by surprise. McKail’s brain promptly calculated the rough direction in which the familiar voice originated and, as he whirled around, fed the information to his body. McKail found himself proposition with who he already suspected it to be; Mickey. A hallucination first prompted in something of a stressful time in McKail’s life, who always took on the rather bizarre guise of the grizzled trainer from the Rocky movies, for reasons beyond his admittedly meagre comprehension. The figment of his imagination sat in the far corner of the room, wearing grey sweats and a delicately balanced small dirtied white towel around his neck. The hallucination leant back in the chair, rested his feet upon the bed and folded his arms contently. "Sorry I startled ya, kid," his figment of his imagination apologised, "but this seems important." McKail nodded at the letter and Mickey followed his gaze. "I only just received it today; it’s been bouncing from motel to motel for the past few weeks." The hallucination rolled his eyes. "And ya can’t believe that? This is tha postal service we’re talkin’ ‘bout here, kid." McKail sighed and gestured toward the letter with his right hand, ignoring Mickey’s deluded idea of humour with great proficiency. "Look at the handwriting." Mickey developed a quizzical expression, stood, peered at the face of the letter intently and finally shrugged in defeat. "I dunno. What about it?" "It’s Mary’s," McKail told him, now pacing back and forth uncomfortably. Most of the time he didn’t like to talk about Mary with anyone, least of all a figment of his own imagination, but the arrival of the letter shook his foundations more deeply than he conveyed and he needed to talk about it. He figured a hallucination was as good as anybody. The hallucination’s confused expression intensified and he glanced upward locking on to McKail’s eyes. "But Mary’s--" "Yes, I know," McKail interrupted, closing his eyes and rubbing the bridge of his nose tenderly. "Mary’s…not with us anymore. Fuck…" McKail winced at the sound of his own words and Mickey remained silent for a time, but finally decided his question was worth the asking, "Are yer sure it’s her handwriting, kid?" McKail glared at his hallucination, an expression of disbelief subtly emerging. "I remember every moment I ever spent with my wife with perfect clarity -- I know what her handwriting looks like!" The figment of his imagination nodded in response, accepting McKail’s answer without reservation. "Have ya read it yet?" McKail shook his head and continued to pace the room. "I’ve been staring at the damned thing for half an hour already. I can’t even bring myself to touch it, much less read it." The hallucination’s eyes explored the letter a second time. "Tha postage sez Chicago." Mickey glared up at McKail. "Know anybody there?" "Mary’s sister," McKail quickly responded, reluctantly scooping up the letter from the bed and examining it closely. "I met her once at our wedding; never did get a chance to go visit." The hallucination questioned, raising a speculative eyebrow. "So Mary’s sister posted tha letter? Interesting…" McKail glared at the hallucination, expectant of some kind of conclusion to the sentence. When one didn’t arrive, McKail decided to force the issue, "How so?" "Means Mary must’ve left instructions for her ta post it, after--" McKail shot the figment of his imagination a warning glance before the sentence end and the figment of his imagination promptly became silent. McKail slipped his thumb under the overbite of lilac-coloured envelope and gently tore it open, making absolutely sure the letter was damaged. The scented note slipped out of its casing and nestled in his palms. He stared at it for the longest time, before gently unfolding the delicate paper and casting his eyes upon the blue-inked scrawl, desperate to learn its secrets. Engrossed in his discovery, McKail didn’t even hear the knock at the door at first, nor did he notice that his hallucination had taken it’s leave; to where, god only knew. The noise persisted and became more fervent, eventually ripping his attention away from the last note his wife had ever wrote him. Angrily, he stormed over toward the door, ready to cast away the perpetrator without a hint of remorse. However, once the door had swung open, his plans promptly amended. The last thing he expected to find behind cheap wooden door, was his stalker, Kate, with her eyes laced with tears. McKail’s anger promptly faded and Kate lunged forward, wrapping both arms around him haphazardly, so quickly in fact it took him completely by surprise. McKail stood there dumfounded for the longest time, unsure of whether to reciprocate the embrace or not, before she finally relinquished her grip and sat on the edge of his bed, still sniffling to herself meekly. "How the hell did you find me?" McKail questioned. "Last time I saw you was the GCW show in Houston…" "Jacob, please help me," she interrupted. "I--I think he’s going to kill me." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Meeting. The Hole. The Bronx, New York. Today. McKail entered the seedy little drinking establishment, with a look of distain liberally painted across his face for all of the world to see, mildly surprised the interior was actually worse than the severely disfigured exterior. The place was nothing short of rundown and clearly hadn’t been properly serviced for a very long time. The Hole (the shit hole as most had undoubtedly dubbed it) wouldn’t pass a health inspection in a million years -- McKail had already seen at least four species of insect in wild abundance and felt sickened by the proposition; but everyone else seemed oblivious to it and that disturbed McKail even more. That said, it was deep within the heart of the Bronx, intermingled in the back streets and, judging from the type of characters this place attracted, McKail very much doubted the law, or any form of authority, even dared venture into this part of the city without an exceeding good reason and a full SWAT unit for back-up. McKail desperately tried to avoid eye contact with any of the staff or partisans, and slipped into a booth by the door, his face etched in disgust at the sight of the cockroaches climbing out of the emptied pint glasses. At that point, he didn’t much care whether he’d attained the attention of the waitress; he wasn’t all that thirsty anymore. McKail examined the room more intently now, again avoiding eye contact where possible and quickly breaking it where not. He noted in detail the absurdities that now glared at him directly, he hadn’t noticed before. The cheap moose head hanging directly above the bar that was missing one of it’s antlers; the severely torn football flags hung in unison next to it (god only knew what teams they were supposed to represent); the battered old jukebox straddled in the far corner of the room, which didn’t seem to be functional and had an advertising slogan of ‘Elvis: the fresh young face of music’ chiselled into its side. McKail sighed and shook his head, barely even noticing the man slide into the booth opposite him. Before his eyes could lock onto the perpetrator, a flagon full of beer was slammed down haphazardly before him, spilling the tempting foamy liquid all over the table. McKail winced at the sight of the spilt beer; it wasn’t something he’d ever get used to, and scooted over to one side, allowing the overspill of the catastrophic event to fall, unhindered, onto the already grimy and sticky flooring. He shot the perpetrator a somewhat disgusted look and was confronted by a pale, hollow faced individual, with chiselled features and empty, soulless eyes. This has to be him, McKail silently noted, gingerly wrapping his hand around the flagon of ale. The hollow man glanced around his immediate area warily, leaned in closer and whispered, "You McKail?" McKail remained silent, answering with a brief nod. The hollow man shook his head and chuckled. "Man, you’re one sick fuck, you know that?" "I take it you received my message?" McKail asked. What he’d arranged was a little out of the hollow man’s normal practice, but nevertheless he agreed to it and apparently he also got a kick out of it. McKail wished he could say the same. "Yeah, I got it," the hollow man confirmed. "This is one ballsy request, you know that?" McKail took a long gulp of his ale and promptly wished he hadn’t. After the sour taste had been rendered somewhat placid, though not eradicated entirely, he answered, "I guess so." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Promises. Taken from a letter penned by Mary McKail. Original time of writing: six months ago. To my dearest Jacob, At the time of reading this letter, I will have long since passed away and, although I have no desire to tear open any healed wounds, I feel you deserve a better explanation as to why I made you promise me you’d follow your dreams; why I made you promise domination within the world of wrestling. Fulfilling your dreams will be no easy task to undertake, despite your unwavering determination and regardless of the cost to yourself or the sacrifices you must make in it’s pursuit, that much is certain. I only wish I were there with you to help should the burden. Your dedication to me, and your career, is nothing less than breathtaking and it will aid you in times to come. However, I also know how single minded you can be, and I know your obsession with fulfilling the promise you made will already be taking over your entirely life, not leaving much room for anything more. I intend to remedy that. PTO… ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Situation Some Cheap Motel Room. The Bronx, New York. Three Days Ago. McKail stared at her dumfounded, as he slumped down into a chair opposite Kate, idly noting the ridiculous amount of dust thrust into the air as a direct resultant. For the longest time he remained silent, just staring at her, as she sat perched on the end of the bed looking, and presumably feeling, sorry for herself and sniffling rather too much for McKail’s liking. How in the fuck am I supposed to react to this? I barely know the woman… He silently questioned, recovering no feasible answer. Finally, Kate impatiently broke the silence; it was her life allegedly on the line after all, "Well say something, Jacob, please…" McKail shook his head, eyes wide with bewilderment and his mind still undecided. "Okay, erm, who’s going to kill you and why?" Kate stopped playing with her snot-riddled tissue and glanced upward, examining McKail intently. "My boyfriend." "Your boyfriend?" He repeated. "Well, my ex-boyfriend," Kate corrected. "And why the hell does he want to kill you, Kate?" McKail asked, his brow furrowed, finally betraying his natural state of composure. Kate broke eye contact and looked down at her feet, ashamed. "Because, well, I, um, stole money from him." "You stole money from your boyfriend?" McKail responded, shaking his head. Kate’s eye’s filled up again and the sobbing commenced. McKail couldn’t tell whether she was genuinely ashamed of what she’d done or was just trying to garner his sympathy; ultimately, he decided it was a little of both. "It’s not as bad as it sounds, okay?" She sniffled, her anger providing the necessary courage to re-establish eye contact. "The man is a real bastard, okay. He treated me badly; he cheated on me and beat me whenever the mood took him. I’d had enough of the selfish prick and I just needed to get away for a while. I saw his money just lying them on the bed and I thought ‘fuck him, he deserves it’". McKail’s brow furrowed again, dissatisfied with her version of events. "If he was such a bastard, then why did you go back to him?" "I didn‘t!" she began, the tears quickly evaporating into thin air, desperate to have her story told. "I went back to my parents house, but he showed up the next day demanding his money. When I told him I didn‘t have it, he threatened to kill me." McKail shook his head and leant back in his chair, appearing to analyse the situation carefully, when in fact he was just wondering precisely what she wanted him to do about it. He wasn’t family, he wasn’t her friend and certainly wasn’t her lover. Fuck… Kate removed herself from the edge of the bed and wandered over towards him, kneeling on the floor before him and resting a gentle hand upon his knee. She glared upward, staring him directly in the eye with a sternness McKail didn’t realise she even possessed. "Please, Jacob. Please help me." McKail turned away; he couldn’t bear to see a grown woman beg, especially if it was him she was begging. Some time went by before he finally asked the most important question of all, "How much do you owe him?" A thankful smile crept across her face and she stood upright, moving back to the bed. She wiped her eyes with the tissue, building up to a number McKail just knew he wasn’t going to like. She just came right out and said it, "Ten." McKail‘s eyes widened, he didn‘t like the sound of it one bit. "You’d better be meaning ten dollars." "Ten thousand," she told him, rather needlessly. "But don’t worry, I still have two left. I just need--" "Two?" McKail interrupted. "You only have two thousand left? What the hell did you buy?" "I travelled a lot, toured with GCW for some of the time," she explained. Fuck! He silently exclaimed, his mind racing. "Okay, okay, I’ll meet with him, see if I can‘t talk him round…" Kate thrust herself upward. "Talk him round? You don’t know who he is! You don’t know what he’s capable of!" She let the words hang in the air for a moment, but such thoughts had already crossed his mind. Who the fuck has ten grand just lying around? "Just loan me the money, Jacob, I don‘t want to see you get hurt." Get hurt? Fuck… "I don’t have that kind of money," he told her, "and I certainly don’t intend to get hurt." "Oh come on!" she exclaimed. "The internet dirtsheets say you’re on at least a six figure annual income and you live like a fucking hobo; so you’re living costs must be minimal at best!" McKail chuckled and shook his head dismissively, despite her comments being more or less accurate. "You shouldn’t believe everything you read on the internet." She slumped down upon the edge of the bed and water works began anew. McKail was getting damned tired of it now. "But he’s going to kill me…" McKail sighed. "I said I’d help you and I will. I’ll go see him and straighten everything out, but I don’t have that kind of money." He made eye contact with her, hoping such a gesture would re-affirm the validity of his lies. "Seriously." McKail felt a twinge of guilt grip him, but what could he do? He glanced by the side of her at the letter resting upon his bed, his mind racing onward toward the future; he already had plans for his money, plans that could not be altered no matter what.
