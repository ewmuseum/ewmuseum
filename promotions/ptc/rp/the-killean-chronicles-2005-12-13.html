The Killean Chronicles

13 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-13 "All of humanity believes the ever growing theory that everything happens for a reason. I am slowly becoming a believer…" ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Every action has an equal and swift reaction. It was the sentence that had swarmed his mind since Wednesday the seventh of December. It felt like moths that enjoyed picking at every last strand of fabric in a linen closet. His mind was being picked by these thoughts, poisoning his thought process, producing a virtual reality. His acts of violence were only a couple nights later than his first victory in the annual Golden Turnbuckle Tournament held at Prime Time Central. For at GTT5, he was a machine, no pun intended. He set out to devastate his target swiftly and seamlessly. He feels he accomplished that goal. But that Wednesday had haunted his dreams for the many nights since. His actions seemed to have no swift reaction. It was all going in slow motion for him, the swings of the bat, the cries of pain and the dropping jaws of the onlookers. It was an act of evil, of unequaled violence. The steel striking the bone was a disturbing yet soothing sound for him. He took joy in dismantling his supposed friend yet sworn enemy. He had no apathy, no sympathy and no remorse. Remorse can strike without warning, darkening the dreams of the individual who took action. In the nights since, the darkness took over and the reaction reared its ugly head. Haunting images of retaliation had attached to his dream process like a cancer, eating away at the sanity he still had, albeit not so much anymore. That was now… What about then? What about when he was sitting on the cold bar stool, listening to an old man talk his ear off? He listened to the stories, making his own like children’s novels in comparison. Finally after so many years he had finally lifted the weight off his shoulders of their sudden departure, leaving his sister and him to fight together. He had fought with himself for a decade, trying to get the monkey off his back. He had broken free and felt victory. It took a mere few seconds to crush his victory and tie him down again. It felt like the shackles of torture had been reapplied to his tired frame. He ran, fast as he could to keep the promise to himself. It was a promise he made a few years back. Had he ever seen his father again he swore he would make him feel the same amount of pain that he and his sister felt for so many years. He even swore at one point to drop the bastard where he stood. It was just one promise to keep, quite easy no? As he set foot in the snow, his vision wandered with a purpose. But the object of his chase was nowhere to be found. It had all come back with a vengeance, dealing a death blow to an already damaged and tortured soul. So what choice did he have? "Sir?" What was he to do? "Excuse me?" Where was he to go? "SIR!" *SNAP* His surroundings had returned, spinning back into place. The voice that disturbed him from his sense of daydream belonged to the middle-aged, overweight waitress that lingered over him. His meal had arrived. "I-… I’m sorry." Stunned and confused, he replied, trying not to reveal his desperation to regain complete consciousness. "That soup isn’t going to eat itself. It’s getting cold." She snapped, not wanting her hard work wasted on some schlep that decided to worry about his problems rather than enjoy the meal prepared for him. Taking the spoon in his right hand, he began to eat his now lukewarm soup. The deep tone of conversation was making its way through the room, smacking into the ear drums of Sirrajin. He had already had a headache this morning. The constant clanging of spoons and the irritating scraping of forks on plates really didn’t do much to help his situation either. But he continued to lock his tired and bloodshot eyes on the two items sitting on the table to his left. He really attempted to let nothing disturb his concentration First was a plane ticket, return fare. The destination read Ohio, the city of Dayton. It was the home of the man he beat to a bloody pulp and the woman that he surely let down. It’s not like she didn’t have a right to be upset since he had seriously tried to stomp a permanent mud hole in her husband’s ass. They do have a child at home to care for. Really, how long was this remorse thing going to last? The second item was a simple white piece of paper with some rough writings. It was an address that didn’t need a name accompanying it. Killean knew the name. But it was much too simple wasn’t it? It didn’t take a rocket scientist to look up the name in the phone book. He really only found two listings; the first was his own. The second was the one he was looking for. It was the active residence of one Thomas Sirrajin. Now since it didn’t take a genius to figure it out, it really couldn’t be that easy could it? He couldn’t just make his way to 314 Simcoe Street and find the man and woman that had disappeared without a trace so many years back. He took the plane ticket off the table and folded it once down the middle. He then slipped it into his pocket and went back to eating his soup. He was now holding the piece of paper with the address in his other hand, not allowing anything to detract his constant stare. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ "There is a method to my madness. Some may like it and some may not. I say deal with it. You won’t have another choice." ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The drive was short, but even the couple minutes it took to reach his destination felt like hours, perhaps a lifetime. He had an elongated sense of depression; feeling like the one event in the bar had completely bound all of his thoughts. How could he let himself be controlled by this? Even if he was to find his father, what would he do? Would he keep the promise he made to himself? Perhaps he would chicken out and bend under the pressure? Yeah, given his relative incompetence at the moment, the latter was a sure bet. Sure enough, when he pulled outside 314 Simcoe Street, it looked like the rundown house from the Halloween movies. Michael Myers wasn’t inside. As a matter of fact, no one was probably inside. Stepping from his car he glanced at the boarded up windows. The house was beyond repair, possibly decaying all the way from the tip of the roof to the bottom of the foundation. The metal outside door was so badly rusted that he needed a tetanus shot just from looking at it. He surveyed the land and more specifically the garden. One thing he remembered from his childhood was that his mother was a regular green thumb. But the land in the garden area didn’t look disturbed for quite some time. It was more than obvious that no one occupied the premises for months, possibly years. He contemplated turning on his heel and forgetting his whole journey to no where in particular when he spotted something hanging from the metal door. Taking cautioned steps up the rickety staircase, he focused as close as his fatigued eyes would let him. His name was clearly written on the envelope. As he peeled it from the metal, he backed away to a swinging chair mounted on the front porch. He came in contact with the cold wood of the seat and never removed his gaze from his name scrolled across the front of the envelope. Taking his time, he broke the seal and removed a letter from the inside. It wasn’t long yet it had gotten to the point rather quickly. "I knew you would try to look me up as soon as you discovered whose patch it was I left you. Let me just say that I had a feeling I would encounter physical harm had a stuck around a second longer. I quickly took the corner from the bar and got in my car. I had taken the alley as my way of escape. Since I brought it up that my wife doesn’t like me being out to a bar long, I should clarify. It’s not your mother I was speaking of. We had divorced and I remarried many, many moons back. If you are wondering, I have not heard from your mother for years and am not certain of her whereabouts. Certainly I am correct in assuming you hold a deep hatred for me. Having realized my actions have consequences in years past, I have no doubt that yours feelings towards me are justified. I never expected to see my son in the same bar as me that day. Many years have passed and I am sure you moved on. So let’s keep it that way. My current address is under my wife’s name. Think of this as the farewell I never gave you so long ago." That’s it? A letter attempting to make up for the many years of disgust? It was a twisted situation. For the last few days, he had been deemed the Inhuman One, the heartless one given his most recent actions. But now this letter truly showed who the true heartless individual was. Being left alone with his sister all those years ago really wasn’t a mistake. It really wasn’t an uncontrollable turn of events that left me miserable for a long time to come. Hoping around from foster home to foster home, he had never bonded with a single family. Summer had bonded with one family but now that she was gone, Killean never communicated with them anymore either. Here was the reality; he was never again to have parents. They had basically disowned him and his sister. He was to never again experience the love and closeness of a family, the only people who seem to support you in your time of need. Instead he was to live a dream devoid of the special holidays that all families shared. Most people who faced these harsh realities had either thought of or attempted suicide. Killean on the other hand kept up this crazy roller coaster called life not to see if it could get any better, but to wonder if it could really get any worse. It’s just that every time, reality dealt another swift kick to the nuts. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ "I have this remarkable talent. When I step between the ropes of the squared-circle, I become empty and emotionless. I hurt people and I really don’t want to. My actions are not my own, my actions are what you make them to be. You hurt me, I hurt you twice as much. You hit me, I hit you twice in return. You put me down verbally, I knock you down physically. It’s a vicious circle, a nasty reality…" ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ After parking his car in the underground parkade, he set the alarm. The sound of the horn reverberated throughout the entire complex as he made his way towards the elevator. He swung the manual door open and stepped inside pressing the button to go up. But nothing happened. He pressed the button once again but just like before, nothing happened. After a closed fist into the metal casing, he gave up on the elevator. Stepping down the hall, he came to the staircase leading to his loft. It was a full eight stories up. It’s not that he can’t handle the stairs; he just didn’t feel up to it. Why was nothing working out for him? No matter if it was simple things like using the elevator or more complicated things like finding his father, nothing was going the right way. Killean made his way up the stairwell, turning every corner, making his way up the next set. He thought about what he chose, then he thought about the alternate of flying to Matt’s hometown of Dayton and trying to set things right. For all he knew, Matt was waiting for him. Perhaps he was even expecting an apology. However, that would be like expecting to get blood from a stone. As much as Matt might have wanted it, it would never happen. Finally arriving on the eighth floor, he opened the door and began the walk down his hallway, passing many doors adorned with Christmas wreaths and other miscellaneous Christmas decorations. He turned the corner into his end of the building and as soon as his door was in sight, he noticed something peculiar. Sitting in front of his door was a basket covered with a blanket. On top of the basket was a note. Killean did not look surprised though as he immediately came up with an explanation for himself. "Finally, after moving here months ago, the Welcome Wagon dropped off their basket." He smiled, particularly looking forward to the homemade muffins that consistently came with those baskets. They reminded him of the muffins that Vivian made back in San Diego. His favorite was her apple filled specialty, but he would settle for some blueberry or chocolate chip muffins. They were usually the norm for those types of baskets. He stood in front of the door and looked at the basket for a moment, not exactly sure why he didn’t pick it up right away. He never even made a move to unlock the door. Instead he stood with a blank stare, looking at the envelope on top of the basket. It seemed strange that he would find his name on the front of the envelope. Normally they would put something like, "Homeowner" on the front. Bending down to pick up the envelope, he examined it, sensing something very strange. It was like an aura surrounded him that came from the inside, making him not feel right. Given the feeling, he half toyed with the notion of pushing the basket aside and leaving the envelope where he found it. But as much as he tried, he just couldn’t put the envelope down. He just decided to open it up and read it. Although that day he was running zero percent on satisfaction from white envelopes that he opened. After pulling the letter from inside the envelope, he immediately noticed it was written on a piece of loose leaf paper. That certainly was not common for Welcome Wagon baskets. Unfolding the letter was the tedious task, because in one way he badly wanted to see what was written. In another way he wanted nothing to do with it all together. The first words written were, "Dear Daddy…" Certainly the person who wrote the letter had made a big mistake. But when he read on, it all came crashing down once again. "Don’t worry Killean, you read the first line right. Think back, maybe about a year back or a little more. Do you remember now? The night of passion that we shared together? I hope you do as it was one of the best nights of my life. But the best night in my life led to the most terrorizing months that I ever had." "Can’t figure it out yet big guy? Well nine months after our fitting encounter I came up with what is laying inside this basket in front of your door. Go ahead and look and I am sure you will see a little three month old baby girl sleeping. Before you even ask, it is yours. I figured that since I don’t have the money to care for her and since I can’t get the money together for a lawyer to sue your ass for support, I would leave her with you. You have the money right big guy? Sure you do." "What you really need after what I witnessed last Wednesday on Playboy TV, you know what you did to Matt, is a huge reality check. I am sure this will do it for you. Say goodbye to the care free life you once had. Say hello to our daughter, Desiree. I know this is what you wanted, just not at this moment." "Love, Stacey." Reality hitting like a ton of bricks was even too basic a description for what had hit Killean. Wiping the sweat from his forehead was useless as more just formed there almost instantly afterwards. He couldn’t believe what he read and he really didn’t want to prove it to himself. But he had to look. Could it be that his baby girl, his daughter was in the basket? That bitch could seriously leave a baby on a doorstep like that? He was gone for hours it seemed. This baby could have been there for hours. Infuriated as he was, he still hadn’t confirmed the existence of this baby. For all he knew it could be a big joke, a prank to give him the reality check she claimed he needed. Yeah right. Bending down to survey the situation, he took a deep breath and grabbed the end of the blanket but he hesitated in pulling it back. You would too, don’t lie. Finally, he slowly pulled back the blanket. Sure enough, clear as day, a sleeping baby was quietly lying inside. She was so small, yet so adorable. Given the severity of the situation he still noticed what she looked like. She had darker hair, just like he did. Stacey also had dark hair. It was the long flowing locks of brown that he found oh so sexy about Stacey. But Desiree was his main focus now. He just couldn’t take his eyes off the baby… his baby. Her cute chubby cheeks melted his heart. But he also realized what he was facing here. He had no idea how to care for a baby! A car maybe, but not a baby. Couldn’t she have left a new convertible in his parking spot instead? He would surely know how to care for it. Slumping on the floor beside the basket, he held his face in his hands and shook it. He couldn’t believe his luck. Sure he wanted a child, but he also wanted a mother for that child. It wasn’t even so much that he didn’t know how to care for a child, but that his schedule just wouldn’t allow it. So what was he to do now? Looking back over to the basket, he noticed the baby was waking up. Leaning over to get a better look he saw the beautiful blue eyes Desiree had for the first time. It melted his heart once again to see such beauty, it almost made him forget about his problems all together. But looking at her he knew he had a problem as well. He looked at how cute she was just lying in the basket. He spoke to her for the first time, a quiet whisper so he wouldn’t startle her with his deep voice. He even had a smirk on his face. "You know, I was looking forward to muffins…"
