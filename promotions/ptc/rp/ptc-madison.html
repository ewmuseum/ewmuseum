30 June 2009 Found: Old Character to enter into IG65 with The thunder clap that had just recently echoed off the bricks that built up this alley was just another reminder of the storm that had passed through this town. It was headed westward where it may ravage some other city’s sewer system. The puddle that soaked through the pair of sneakers I had tied to my foot was another. There wasn’t a rainbow in the twilight of the day to be seen, just the creativity and artistry of the local children sprayed across a canvas of red brick. This was as good a place as any, I figured. I didn’t deserve anything special, and I knew that. Now would have been a good time to have some vice into which I could melt away this tension. A cool drag of the smog filed air was about the best I could think of. My lungs disagreed. This was a desolated little section of the town. The last place you would count on to find the innocence of youth running through, but there they were, making their final rounds to pester this old man. A streak of blond hair whizzed by me, making a circle about me and sticking his tongue to the taller boy chasing. It was enough to make me stumble up my step. “Bugger off,” I grumbled to myself as they left just as fast as they happened upon me. As much as the sound of children’s laughter would usually cut through whatever stupid mood I found myself in, tonight I did not want the reprieve. I just pressed on to my destination. Today I didn’t care to alter my path for the rusted waste dumpster. The dumpster won. One more defeat to stack on the pile. It rung empty and hollow. I am the hollow man. I fished a line from my pocket. It would be barely long enough to make the funeral tie. I ran my hand over the coarse fibre and worked in a slip knot my father had taught me what seemed like a life time ago. Both he and mother have been in the ground long enough as to not see the stupid decisions that took me here. Mama, I’m coming home. A note, I thought. People usually have some note with them. Some explanation to the curious on lookers that will no doubt find their corpse as to the distraught that drove them to the edge. Does it really give any solace to the family that reads it? No. Let them find me just as some random. Let them stick me in the ground with nothing more than a page 4 mention of the homeless man the police found early the next morning, if that. However, one last soliloquy to anyone listening, one more open letter to the creator: “My name is Michael Madison. I am twenty eight years of age. Today I cast my soul into the waiting hands of death and the beyond. I would that I lost my life years ago, but instead you, I, took a promising one instead. Jake, little brother, you death was my fault.” That was the first time I had ever brought those words of heavy grief across my lips. “Today I fail your memory one last time.” I flung that length of rope over a hanging ladder, some piece of a fire escape, and secured it best I could. I got on one last soap box, and fasten tight my tie. The usual nerves tickle my brain again. This public hanging had managed to draw a crowd it seemed. A cat sat perched on a neighbouring garbage can. His eyes we wide, and looking deep into mine. Was this some animal sharing an empathy with me? No. He looked at me and licked his fur covered lips. That is all that I have been for the longest of times. I had to decide whether it was that the world was chewing me up, or swallowing me whole, but it was clear that the end result of the product was this piece of shit that was standing on a milk box, with his neck in a noose, in some alley, in a town I didn’t care to know the name of. This would be the end of it. No more of this grief that poison my heart five years ago. It was that sweet release that I had searched so long for. So I jumped down off that milk crate. . . . . . . But not before I had taken off the necklace. It was in that moment that I so hated the word. I hated it with such passion. It was a passion that would not allow me to bend to its will, and refused me from hanging stiff to see the sunrise. I had a new lease on life, a new stick with which to beat back death and thoughts of suicide, and a new reason to live: Spite. ---- 30 June 2009 Madison, pt 2 The sun rose on the city, and the rats of society were slowly crawling back into whatever hole they would emerge from when it set again after another long summer day. It was by some great luck that I happened upon a small time drug dealer greedy enough to extend his business hours to accommodate the early morning joggers. He was dressed in a hoodie and some jeans, standard uniform. He twitched just standing there. He full on quaked when I made my approach. “Good morning,” I said with a smile and nod of the head. He tried not to look at me. I musta looked like some under cover or something. Again, “Morning.” “Oh, hey,” he said, a quiver of fear in his voice. This kid looked like he was straight out of failing high school. “You still open for business?” I asked. Small talk was never a strong suit for me. “What type of business you trying to find?” This two day growth must really have some imposing impression set on my scarred face. I made a note to stop in front of mirror when I got a chance some time. “Not your usual.” I finally made eye contact with them darting eyes. He looked damn worried, and I was quick to say something before what I came for came to the side of my head. “Whoa, kid. Take it easy there. Ain’t meaning to scare ya or nothing. I came to rent that,” I said pointing to his hip. “What you say?” His hand moved closer toward it. “Rent. That. I want to rent your piece. I promise it will be back within the hour, and with a handsome reward for your trust.” This was unusual, I know, but I think stressing the cash he would get for a simple rental fee seemed to work. So, I made reference to it twice more before he even pulled the thing from his waistband. “One hour.” “And how’d I know you ain’t just going to get this gun and jack me?” “Scout’s honor,” I said, while I fashioned my hand into a silly gesture that I assumed was something close to the Boy Scout gang sign, but really, I think it was just what the kids are calling ‘the shocker’. “Half hour.” “Half hour it is then,” I said as he presented me with his nine millimetre pistol. I’d be lying if I told ya I didn’t think about it. You know, just open up a round in this guy. Rid the streets of another poison dealer, and make some quick cash in the process. But I’d taken a life before, and it was nothing that I wanted any part of again. So I turned away and darted off to plan my get rich quick scheme. Time was ticking. There were plenty of folk out for their morning run, but I doubted that these people would be dumb nuff to take a wad of cash. Besides that, these people didn’t deserve this. No, I just walked the alley where I met my dealer buddy, and it wasn’t long before I got my target. He slipped out of a side escape and blew a kiss to a girl half his age, maybe not even legal. The gold band around his left ring finger would have financed this fiasco alone. I got into his blind spot and made my move quickly. I got that pistol to his spine, and directed him to the wall. I was amazed by the calmness with which he took all of our encounter. Can’t shake the devil. “Give me what you got. I want your wallet, not your life. But make a move and I won’t think twice.” He reached into his pocket, while I kept his face pressed up against brick. He tossed his wallet to the ground behind him, to my left. This was too easy, I thought. Had that really been the secret to life? Just don’t pay any regard to rules, right or wrong, I started toward to object of my mission, and as soon I let go his head, he started turning it. “Ah, I won’t be doing that if I was you. No, this is how it is going to go, I’m going to kept this gun pointed on you, and I am going to back up and pick up this wallet. Then I am going to back out of this alley, still gun pointing at you, and you will never see me again, or for the first time. Now, just tell me, is the address on your ID the wife’s place, or the mistress?” “Alright, alright. Take the damn thing. Then leave me the hell alone. But ... wait!” “Shhh… not so loud, I get jumpy ‘round loud noises.” “You can take it; I just want to know one thing. Why are you doing this?” “Ha. Don’t you know? There ain’t no rest for the wicked.” And those were the last words I spoke to him before I backed down that alley, and went to rendezvous with the proper owner of the metal I held in my hand. When I happened across him I took a look at the contents of that leather for the first time. There was plenty there, much more than I needed. I counted out two hundred dollars, and put that money into my pocket. The rest of the cash I turned over to the dropout-turned-hustler, with his gun, and he seemed thankful for the business. I now had enough that I could start to think about breakfast, and I way out of this place of misery. Take some bus to somewhere where I might be able to get things straight. But breakfast was really what called to me at the time.
