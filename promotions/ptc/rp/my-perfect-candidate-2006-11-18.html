My Perfect Candidate

18 November 2006

Date: 2006-11-18 On the night Angela was born, Wolfgang made a vow he intended to keep for the rest of his life. It was supposed to be a joyous night – years of frustration and ineptitude were supposed to come to an end. Instead it was one of the most bittersweet nights for Wolfgang. He had waited a grueling two hours in the waiting room after doctors informed him that his wife had undergone some complications. The doctors weren’t surprised. Wolfgang called the pregnancy a miracle; they called it unusual. Abigail had been diagnosed with advanced amenorrhea. According to the charts, she still had the symptoms and her body was still inept at maintaining a pregnancy. Still, there she lay on the hospital bed giving birth. Was it a miracle? Wolfgang certainly thought so. He was convinced that they had been blessed and kept in prayer while he waited for news. After two hours of intense waiting, the doctors reemerged from the emergency room. Wolfgang immediately embraced his daughter, Angela, knowing he had never seen anything so beautiful. He asked to see his wife. The newborn child was not the only news the doctors were carrying. Abigail was gone. Whether he cried or not, Wolfgang could not recall. All he could remember was how paralyzed he felt that moment. His body felt like a ton of bricks as he tried to comprehend what just happened. That night, embracing Angela deep in his arms, he vowed that he would never lose her. I always keep my promises, Wolfgang thought recalling that eventful night. With one hand on the wheel and one hand free to answer his black Motorola cell phone, Wolfgang drove down Main Street keeping vigilant for where Angela might be. He looked at his watch. GTT6 was only a few hours away. In a few hours he would be stepping into the ring with the sumo wrestler. Kimbusa’s accolades were more enough to instill doubt in the Sentinel. They did not. The third round of the GTT6 and Kimbusa were the last things on Wolfgang’s mind. There were far more important matters to attend to at hand. He had already given Kimbusa his full attention earlier in the week. He had trained with his coworkers from the Atlantic Indy Wrestling Federation who were visiting him during their off period. Of course none of them had the girth to equal Kimbusa for an adequate sparring session. Still, it was enough. Wolfgang had trained for the match and knew he was ready. Before he stepped into the ring with Kimbusa, he needed to find his daughter. His cell phone rang. "Hello," he answered. "Dad…" came the reply on the other line. It was void of any affection. "Angela…" Wolfgang returned the empty reply. "I’m-" "I want you to come home," Wolfgang interrupted her. "I can’t." "Why not?" "You’ll lie to me again." Wolfgang sighed, "I’m sorry. There’s a lot going on here that I do not think you’re ready for." "Who are you to decide what I’m ready for?" "I’m your…" Wolfgang paused, rethinking his response. "You’re right. I need to trust you more often." "Does this mean you’re going to tell me about that woman?" "It means I’m going to tell you the truth." Angela sighed disappointingly on the other line, "Well, I’m sorry." "Sorry?" "You may trust me now, dad. But I don’t trust you anymore." Wolfgang’s voice cracked, "Angela!" "I’m sorry daddy," Angela replied. "I’m going to find out who she is on my own." "No wait!" Wolfgang replied, fear instilled in his voice. "I’m going to find out who she is, daddy." "Angela, wait!": Wolfgang begged. "I’m going to find her, daddy." "ANGELA!" Wolfgang retorted. "You mustn’t talk to her! You have no idea what kind of woman she is!" "I’m sorry." "Angela-" But it was too late. Angela hung up. Wolfgang cursed and threw the phone down against the dashboard with such force that it ricocheted and landed in the backseat of the black Land Rover. Wolfgang stepped on accelerator. There was no time to waste. He couldn’t afford to have Angela meet Karla without him present. # "Who was that?" Angela shrieked, almost dropping her cell phone. Cassidy laughed, "I didn’t mean to scare you." Angela peered at him, "Don’t sneak up on me like that!" "Hey now," Cassidy raised his hands in protest, "you’re the one making phone calls in the corner." Angela put her cell phone away and sucked her teeth, "I was just trying to find a good connection." Cassidy shrugged, "It’s all right." Cassidy motioned for her to follow him to the main room, "There’s something important going on." "I called my dad," Angela blurted out. Cassidy turned around, "What did he say?" Angela looked down at herself. She was wearing her favorite outfit – a blouse with her favorite brown skirt. Still, the outfit wasn’t enough to raise her spirits. She said, "He said he’s going to tell me the truth. He’s coming here to tell me the truth." "Do you believe him?" Normally that would have been a rhetorical, if not inappropriate, question. Today was not that day. Angela shrugged, "I don’t think I do. I told him I’m going to find out the truth on my own." "You won’t be alone." Angela looked up. "I’ll help you find that lady," Cassidy replied. For a moment they were stuck in silence. Angela felt an unusual feeling overcome her as she stared at Cassidy’s bright blue eyes. There was something unusual about him, she thought. She had considered him a very great looking kid – an Adonis. Those initial feelings of a puppy love crush were coming back as she was staring at him in his blue jeans and white shirt. But there was something enigmatic about him that turned her off. For one matter, he never discussed his parents or what happened to them. Angela broke the silence, "I have a picture of her." "You do?" Cassidy asked, smiling. He knew this was good news. "I took a photo from my dad’s black box the other day," Angela explained, digging into her pocket. She pulled out a regular-sized photograph of her father with Karla Segur. She handed it over to Cassidy. Cassidy took the photograph and pretended to inspect it, "This is your mother?" "No," Angela replied. "She’s the woman who helped my dad a few weeks ago. I need to know who she is." "Why?" "Why would my dad lie about her?" Angela asked. "Maybe he thought it wasn’t important," Cassidy answered. "Besides, this photo looks pretty recent, so he probably wasn’t ready tell you he had a girlfriend." "That photo is sixteen years old." "What?" Cassidy retorted, shocked. He turned the photograph over and stared at the watermark date. It was sixteen years old. Incredible, he thought. Karla hadn’t aged more than a few years. How was that possible? "You haven’t seen her by any chance?" "No," Cassidy quickly replied. "We can find her later. There’s something I want to show you." "Oh, right!" Angela explained, having forgotten that Cassidy needed her attention in the main room. Cassidy led her by the hand to the main room. The main room was the second most decorated room in the building, second only to the nursery in the backroom. The walls of the room – which was big enough to pass for a high school gym – were decorated with photographs and drawings the kids had made. Angela was a bit taken back by the scenery she stumbled upon. Situated directly in the middle of the room were two long tables to accommodate the three dozen children and young adults who were a part of Seconda Vita. Angela Moriarty was mesmerized by their uniformity. For a week she had noticed the people of Seconda Vita lived a very militant life. They were each assigned a very tedious schedule, but none of them seemed to mind. She was in constant awe of how they never departed from the schedule. It was almost like they depended on it. Standing in the front of the room with Cassidy, Angela began to realize just how uniform and dependent these people were. They were all bowing their heads down in prayer. But the prayer was not uniform. Some were praying to Jesus, some to Allah, and some prayed to a generic God. Angela couldn’t help noticing that they were all wearing black pants and a white shirt, but one person. Angela pointed out the chubby little kid in jeans, "Why is he not dressed like the others?" "That’s what I wanted you here for," Cassidy smiled. "Today is a wonderful day." "What’s going on?" "That boy there is Jimmy," Cassidy explained. "Of course, he’s orphaned. You already know that. Everyone here is in one way or another." "I see." "One of the things we do here is to find adoptive parents," Cassidy said. "It takes a while, but sooner or later everybody has a new family they get adopted into." Angela blinked. Was this guy for real? "That’s incredible." "It’s really just dependent on the goodness in people’s hearts." Angela rushed in and kissed him. After a few seconds, she stepped back. Cassidy looked at her startled. He came to his senses. He said, "I better get going." "I’m sorry-" "I have to take Jimmy to the hospital," Cassidy interjected. "He needs a physical before I can sign the official papers." "Oh. Can I go with you?" Cassidy shook his head. He called out to Jimmy, "We have to go now!" Jimmy started to say his goodbyes. Cassidy watched the little obese kid joyfully say his farewells. Better make them good, Cassidy thought. His pearly white smile hid a devious plot. Jimmy had no idea he would be saying goodbye for the last time. # Wolfgang instinctively looked at his watch. Time was shortening. Wolfgang tapped his fingers on the steering wheel impatiently. How could he have gotten himself stuck in rush hour traffic? He didn’t have any time to spare sitting on his ass. "This is fucking great," Wolfgang muttered to himself. Tonight was supposed to be one of the most important nights in his life. He was supposed to face Kimbusa. He was supposed to reunite with his daughter, Angela. Unfortunately, Fate had other plans. Wolfgang looked at his watch again, hoping that maybe he read the time wrong before. He didn’t. It was indeed getting late and he was no where near where he needed to be. Sighing, and perhaps admitting defeat, Wolfgang looked out the passenger side of his black Land Rover. That alley looks awfully familiar, he thought. Wolfgang peered into that dark alley. It was ghostly. Not a sign of life moved about in there, not even a mouse. Then it occurred to him. The memories came flooding back. This was the alley he had found Angela passed out on the floor. This is where Karla had first slithered her way back into his life. Wolfgang stared deep into the alley and wondered where it went. I can’t think of that now, he thought. There was no point in wondering what was on the other side of the alley. Dwelling in the past was for those who were oblivious to the present. There was no point in rehashing old memories. Wolfgang…No, the Sentinel had a future. The traffic light turned green up ahead. That moment Wolfgang knew exactly where he needed to be. He had come here to compete in GTT6 and become the best fighter in the world. Somewhere along the way, he had lost track of his duties as a father. Because of that, he lost Angela. Between winning GTT6 and telling Angela the truth, Wolfgang knew where he needed to be. # An abrupt knock on the door startled Karla. She shrieked as she nearly spilled the vials of chemicals she was gingerly holding in her hand. Having regained her composure, she glared at the door and invited the intruder to come in. A young, petite, beautiful Italian woman just a few years younger than Karla poked her head through the doorway. She said, "Karla?" "What the hell do you want?" Karla retorted, annoyed at her intrusion. Nicole walked into the laboratory. She had been with Karla since grand opening day and was her best friend and best assistant. She eyed the samples of blood and other hazardous materials that Karla had tried to hide under newspapers, "Are you ok?" Karla glared, "I’m perfectly fucking fine." "I really don’t think-" "This isn’t the time to fucking lecture me," Karla interjected. "-you should be doing this." "Is that what you think?" "I know it’s hard," Nicole comforted her, "but you need to seek help." "I am getting help." "No-" "What the hell did you interrupt me from my work for?" Karla snarled. "Do you enjoy watching me fail for fifteen fucking years?" "No, I just-" "Then what is it?" Karla demanded. "There’s a young man here to see you. His name is Cassidy Jones." "Send him in." Nicole nodded in acknowledgement and walked out the door. Moments later, Cassidy walked in well dressed with a gleaming Jimmy by his side. Karla eyed Jimmy up and down. He’s healthy, she thought. She stared at his bright blue eyes. Cassidy was right, Karla recalled, he does have great eyes. "Have a seat over there," Cassidy instructed Jimmy. Karla pulled a chair over for Cassidy, "How is she?" "She’s fine," Cassidy said, making sure that Jimmy wouldn’t know who he was talking about. "She seems a bit depressed." "That’s good." "Yes," Cassidy agreed. "Depressed people are the best candidates for my group. They are addicted to hope and bite the first chance they get." "The perfect candidate," Karla quipped. "My perfect candidate," Cassidy added. "How long is she going to be here?" "Not long," Karla replied. "I’ll talk to her soon." "You better make it quick, then." "Why?" "She contacted her father earlier," Cassidy explained. "She says her father’s on his way to save her." Karla smirked, "He won’t." Cassidy blinked, "Are you saying he’s not going to go after her?" "Oh, he will," Karla replied, "just not tonight. I know him." "I want to ask you something," Cassidy said, nervously. Karla peered at him. She sensed that something was frightening him. "What is it? Are you having second thoughts about this?" "Oh, that’s not it." "Good," Karla seethed through her teeth. "Because I’m not going to let fifteen years of waiting be undone by some pussy. I want my daughter back, Cassidy." "She showed me a picture of you." "And?" "You haven’t aged a bit," Cassidy clarified. "How did you do that?" Karla smiled, "I think it’s time for you to go." Cassidy nodded and stood up. He patted Jimmy on the head and walked out the door. Karla stood up and motioned for Jimmy to get to his feet. She said, "Are you excited?" "Yup," came the eager reply from the twelve year old boy. "Come with me," Karla extended her hand. "We’ll do the medical stuff and then you’re off to go with your new family." Karla led Jimmy down the hall away from her laboratory on the top floor. Jimmy constantly looked left and right wondering which office he was going to meet his new parents. Thoughts of having a family rushed through his head. Even for such a young age, Jimmy knew the importance of family. He never once thought about it before. But that was before his parents were killed by a drunk driver. They approached the end of the hallway and Jimmy thought the last door was his gate to freedom. He was wrong. Karla opened the door to the stairwell and they descended. They went all the way down to the basement. Jimmy didn’t know much about hospitals, but the lack of people in the basement couldn’t have been a good thing. He would have been more worried if the basement was dirty or full of squeaky noises. Aside from the absence of others, the basement was perfectly fine. In fact, all the rooms they walked by were of the same set up: able to accommodate all patients. Having reached the last room on the floor, Karla opened the door and led Jimmy in. "Where are my parents?" Jimmy asked. Karla smiled, "They’re waiting for you upstairs." "Can I see them?" "Not now," Karla replied. She looked into his eyes again. She couldn’t get over how bright blue they were. It was almost as if he were wearing contacts. "You have nice eyes." "Thanks," Jimmy replied, sheepishly. "My dad used to tell me it was so I could always see where I’m going. I don’t know what he meant." Karla nodded. She wasn’t in the least bit interested in hearing his nostalgic stories. "What do you want to see?" "I want to see the world," Jimmy answered. "That’s good," Karla responded automatically. "You should always see where you’re going. You should always know who you are. No matter what happens in the past, no matter who you work for, no matter what memories you have, you should never lose sight of that, which you really are, Jimmy. And you should never let anyone, your boss, or a friend, or even a memory tell you or remind you of whom you are. Do you understand that? "Yes," Jimmy replied. He didn’t understand. Karla caught Jimmy eyeing the flowerless plant in the room with the budding blueberries. She said, "Do you like that?" "We have the same ones back at the center," Jimmy replied. "But Mr. Cassidy doesn’t let us touch them." "Nonsense," Karla replied. "You can have one if you want. They’re very sweet." Karla rubbed her fingers impatiently as she watched Jimmy eat 3 blueberries from the belladonna plant. She urged him on, "You can eat the leaves, too. It’s what they use for salads." Jimmy took a bite from the leave. He didn’t like the taste. It didn’t matter. That was all Karla needed. Jimmy had eaten a fair amount and was now a ticking time bomb. They stood in silence for a long while. Jimmy asked, "What’s going on?" Karla smiled. "I’m waiting." "For…" Jimmy began to reply. He stopped in agony. He screamed out as he clutched his stomach in his hands, crying out for help. Karla remained seated on her desk, watching the horror unfold. Jimmy continued to scream as the pain started to intensify. He looked over at Karla, begging her to help him. "I’m waiting," Karla smirked. Finally, little Jimmy began to quiet down a bit. His skin was turning purple and he was beginning to pass out on the floor. Karla stood up from her seat and kneeled down besides the agonizing Jimmy. She said, "How does it feel?" Jimmy couldn’t respond. "I have some terrible news for you, boy," Karla snarled at him. "You’re not being adopted. Have you ever wondered why none of the kids ever come back? They weren’t adopted either." Jimmy tried to reply. He couldn’t mutter a word from the blood dripping out of his mouth. "They were all a bunch of orphaned losers that no one cares about. Who cares about you, Jimmy? Your own parents don’t. They left you in a pile of garbage." Jimmy lay motionless. He wasn’t dead, yet. "You want to see the world, Jimmy? Well, you’re going to see the world. You’re going to see it in pieces. When I’m through with you, you’re going to help me find a cure. After that, maybe someone in Mexico might want to buy your kidney. Maybe someone in Africa might want to buy your heart. I don’t care." Jimmy’s eyes rolled into the back of his head. The poison had done its job. "When you go to the other world," Karla said. "I want you to thank all the other children who donated their bodies for my research. I’m going to cure myself of this fucking diseases and I have you and the other children to thank for it." Karla laughed as something occurred to her. "Poor Jimmy. You went into Seconda Vita looking for a second chance. Congratulations. I hope you’re enjoying your second life…as a contribution to science." With that, she pulled out a scalpel from her lab coat and started to make an incision around the eye sockets of those beautiful bright blue eyes. She then carved his…no, they were no longer his. They belonged to her now. She carved her new eyes out. # Wolfgang pulled up his Land Rover in front of the large white building. He was unaware of what had just transpired at A Second Chance hospital and how it affected his daughter. He walked around to the back of the car and pulled out a black duffel bag. Heaving it over his shoulder, Wolfgang walked towards the front door. This is it, he thought. This is where he needed to be tonight. He was sure of it. He came here to compete in GTT6 and win it. But along the way, he had lost his daughter. He loved Angela more than anything in the world. Smiling and knowing he had made the right choice, Wolfgang pushed the door open… …And walked into the GTT6 Arena. # Elsewhere, Angela took a seat next to Cassidy and let him put his arm around her. He turned on the television and watched as Angela found solace and comfort in his arms. He looked down at her chestnut wavy hair and smiled. She has no clue what she’s gotten herself into, he thought. …My perfect candidate.
