Vindication: The Little Deaths

29 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2004-03-29 Saturday, 27 March 2004, 2312 hours EST "Argh... Dammit! What the hell was I thinking?" The microwave oven. "I was wondering that myself. What WERE you thinking?" The Internet. "Why didn't you tell me I was wrestling an ogre?" The automobile. "I'm your trainer, not your manager. I only deal with AFTER the matches. Before them, you're on your own." The television. "I would so kick your ass right now if I could stand." Open heart surgery. "I've heard that before." Mankind has produced any number of great inventions that provide some for of service or convenience to humans in their everyday lives. "That match alone took years off of my life." For a few hours, Jimmy Diablo didn't care about any of them. "And that's why I strongly recommended against your return in the first place, but you never listen to me. I'm only your best friend and physical therapist, what do I know?" All except for one; the most critical of inventions, and the most useful to Jimmy at that very moment. "Keep bitching like that and people will think we're married." The Vital to Human Existence and Necessary for World Peace: Ice Bath. "You wish you had an ass as great as mine to push up against at night." Skylar G., Sandy Wong, and the Pimply-Faced Intern Kid passed by, heading out for the night. Green shook his head. "You took that WAY too far, Jimbo." Wong and PFIK nodded their heads in agreement. Jimmy Diablo rose his right arm, pointing to the door with his thumb. The others had gone by that point, walking past the door on the way to the parking lot. The second ice bath in the room was empty. Only Diablo and Jimbo occupied the room small fluorescent-lighted room. The bench close to the walls at the opposite end of the room had his bag, clothes, and towel set upon it. Jimbo leaned against the white-washed brick wall, with Jimmy completely immersed in 33 degree water. "Yeah, what he said," Diablo said, lowering his arm back into the icy water. Every inch of his body ached from his one-sided painfest. He even dunked his head, evidenced by the droplets of water that gathered at the ends of the bangs of his thick black hair, dripping back into the pool. "Listen, next time I try to get into the ring with a big guy like that, I want you to get a shotgun and take out my legs." Jimbo rolled his eyes, his hairy arms folded across his bright red silk shirt, his arms covering most of the samurai warrior design emblazoned upon the front torso. "Like that would help. You'd crawl out to the ring to get your crippled ass kicked." His legs were crossed, but that didn't obscure the blue dragon imprint on the side of the left leg. He dressed like he was a wrestler, at being 6'1" and 225 lb.; he looked the part as well, even though that was far from the case. Jimmy couldn't really move, not that he'd want to, so he only looked up at Jimbo to the point that he could see his hip. That had to be enough; from that point, he said, "I'm in enough pain as it is, could you possibly be any help and cut the smartass routine for two minutes? Please? I'll get you an ice cream." "Fuck you," Jimbo responded, a phrase which between the two has come to mean the same as a no-hostility "Shut up" over the years that they've known each other. To be gone for eight years, to be outside of active wrestling due to paralyzing injuries resulting from a neck injury, a giant like Propaganda certainly isn't the best opponent against whom to make a return. Doubly worse were the conditions, for Jimmy Diablo, who is much smaller than Propaganda, at 5'10" and 202 lb.; he couldn't match in size or power to the 7' 304 lb. monster. Further against him in the odds, the fact that Diablo wasn't exactly fleet of foot for his small size, and given his previous neck and back injuries, he doesn't employ any kind of high-flying offense. Propaganda had an easy time getting his hands on the 42-year-old cruiserweight. "Ugh..." he groaned, sinking further into the water so that his beard was now underwater, and only his nose remained above the surface of the liquid as his means of breathing. "Fucking Hell... I've got a match tomorrow, don't I?" What did he accomplish? Luckily, the rod in his neck that holds the fused C3 and C4 vertebrae in line with the rest did not dislodge. Even so, he was reminded of why he had waited so long to make a return even after he regained the use of his legs. Propaganda beat him into next week in the PCW Face-to-Face Open Challenge for the chance to enter a tournament to compete for the PCW World Championship. The odds of him winning that match were effectively; his odds of winning the PCW Title are even less. He’d have to improve to have a no chance of success. Jimbo nodded, smacking his lips. "Yeah, you got a match with Vangellus Oxios. Hard hitter and has some decent moves, but a mediocre wrestler from what I hear." Not only that, but he would also be in the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. The some of the best cruiserweights in the world would be in this competition. They would have the advantage in speed, power, agility, health, newer and more innovative styles, and youth. With the lone possible exception of experience (which could itself very well be negated by an extreme case of ring rust), Jimmy Diablo had no advantages over any of his opponents. He should have stayed out of wrestling, but barring that, he should have kept himself out of a position where he wouldn't be walking into the first round of the tournament at the best health that he could possibly have for his nagging injuries. "And how did I get a match with him, again?" Diablo wanted to keep talking. It was a diversion, to take his mind off of the extreme pain in which he was in. Jimmy Diablo, before suffering his neck injury eight years ago, had fourteen years of wrestling experience under his belt. Never has a single man taken him to task and physically tortured him like God's Ultimatum, Propaganda. Jimbo shifted his weight, pushing away from the wall. With a few long strides, he threw his leg over the bench and sat down in a mount, lying back against the hard, cracked wood. "He was trained for professional wrestling by an old buddy of yours. I can't remember his name, but he's apparently one of the two other people you told that you were making a glorious comeback. Or something. He said that you'd need some warm-up matches for the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament, which was really the only thing that we'd set attention to. Where the hell did this PCW open challenge shit come from? The outcome of that match didn't matter; you still got the living crap beat out of you. You realize how dead you look right now?" "Can't look any better than I feel," Jimmy replied. Jimbo, head resting on his arms, looked over to his friends. The height of the ice bath and Jimmy's depth within the tank made eye contact impossible without one of them changing position. "Exactly." There was a pause. Vangellus Oxios was a man of competition. Jimmy Diablo might receive sympathy from him, but certainly not mercy. The only reason why Diablo would even consider going through with the match after the hell that he went through Saturday night on PCW Turmoil was that Oxios was a cruiserweight, and Diablo needed some advance experience prior to the first round of his PTC tournament dealing with someone who had the speed and agility that most cruiserweights in this day and age possess. It would be no easy task. Sitting up, Jimbo tiled his head so that he could meet Diablo's eyes. "A lot of people think that suicide is one single event. You take the pills; you jump off the building; you tie the noose and drop from the chair, pull the trigger, or thrust the knife into yourself. But it's the little deaths that lead up to it, man. Every little injury we suffer: physical, mental, and emotional. Some of us have more than others, and some experience different kinds depending on our lifestyle. We all have them, but we deal with them and manage to survive." Marcel Celestini and Emilio Dervante were Diablo's two opponents to start. The nature of the Cruiserweight Tournament was such that certain first round matches would be triple threat matches. Diablo knew almost nothing about his opponents, save for their names, and rough biographical information including their dimensions and styles. In the past, he used the VCR like it was the greatest thing since sliced bread. An old-school wrestler, he studied tapes of their past performances. Every move, every mannerism, every nuance of their style was memorized, the information to be used against them. He didn’t do that anymore. Not since returning. The information would help to a degree, but in the end, what it accomplished the most was to give Jimmy yet another example of why he should quit. It was just another example of his opponents being better than him in every way. What Dervante and Celestini knew about him, while only one or two facts, was enough. He was old, and he was injured. That led up to a classic double-team situation; the two younger wrestlers would get rid of him first, and deal with each other. "Some wrestlers today do death-defying moves," Jimbo continued, "which is just hype because they do something more twisted than a moonsault. But you... You're killing yourself every time you step through the ropes. Two weeks ago with Claudius Alexander in that short match in American Classic Pro Wrestling, tonight with Propaganda. Tomorrow in RPW when you let Vangellus Oxios beat you around. And then later in the week with this cruiserweight tournament... I'm bad at eulogies, JD. I've had to do them two times before; I'm not looking forward to a third time." Diablo defused the situation with his wise-cracking with that he spent the last eight years perfecting. "Before you were just concerned for my health. Now you're writing me out of my own story before I even have a chance to come to some resolution." "Bitch, bitch, bitch," was Jimbo's retort with a raise of his left eyebrow in a subtle motion that could not in any way be confused with the People's Eyebrow as patented by Dwayne 'The Rock' Johnson. "Thanks for your support, Jimbo," Jimmy inhaled a deep breath, sitting up in the tub of ice water. "But those 'little deaths' don't work with me. I died eight years ago. This is my time of resurrection, and probably my last chance for vindication. I know you can't understand that, but it's something that I need to do." Shaking his head, his long brown hair little disturbed by his movements, Jimbo commented, "Well, you'll need to do it better if you want to laste past the first round of this tournament coming up Friday. PTC brought in the best cruiserweights in the world for their tournament; you won't be facing low-caliber wrestlers there." "I won't have it any other way," Diablo smirked. ***|||~~~|||*** An old-style box television played black and white. The stations only came in two varieties: watchable and static. It was set in the corner on top of the liquor closet. Since it was behind the bar, only the bartender could change the channel, but there were only seven channels that it could get, three of them being soccer coverage. With there being nothing else on television, the tender to the little dingy pigsty of a bar left the channel on the free associated media of Mexico, which was currently featuring a segment on former stars of Mexican wrestling. The young woman, a native from the Yucatan Peninsula, read her lines. The lack of color on the television doesn't do justice to her looks and the pink suit she wore. Tonight, in our segment on Mexico's fallen stars, we spotlight Jimmy Diablo. Real name Jimmy Flores, he was a fourth generation wrestler, descended from Diablo Original of Cuernavaca, a smaller central Mexican city to the south of Mexico City. In order to fully explore Jimmy Diablo's life, we shall recount his lineage. The pasty-faced Caucasian American, sweat beads gathering upon his forehead, sat at the bar. Leaning over the counter, his arms rested atop the surface. A bottle of whiskey purchased directly from the bartender raised to head-level in his right hand, he smirked when the name Jimmy Diablo registered to his brain. "Heh. Hehehehehe... Spotlight all you want, honey." The man, Red Slaughter. He was perhaps the most agile and stylistic wrestler in CLLE. His brown leather and faded black denim jeans fit the atmosphere of the dark bar that looked like it played host to a gunfight in Desperado. Diablo Original was one of the first true legends of lucha libre, and each successive generation had done progressively worse at carrying on that legacy. Not since Diablo Original had any member of the Diablo line been able to win a championship, or win any mask versus hair or hair versus hair matches. It was said that after the original Diablo Original defeated El Shaman back in 1953 for CLL Heavyweight Title, a curse was put on his family; that they would never win another title, mask, or hair match again. Ignoring El Shaman for lack of belief in curses or hexes, Diablo Original himself went on to become one of the greatest lucha libre stars of all time. Slaughter had laid claim to many trophies and titles over the years: Alliance of Lucha Stars based in Amarillo, Texas; Big Action in Tokyo; Canadian Wrestling Experience, Puerto Rico Power, Confederacion de Lucha Libre… he’d held tag team titles, trios titles, junior heavyweight titles… he’d even been around the American circuit winning regional heavyweight and cruiserweight championships. Point being, he was a very accomplished wrestler, a young veteran at 26 with eight years of experience. He knew what he was doing in the ring, and he did it well. No one could fly from the turnbuckle, or on the ground, as well as he could. An internet poll named him the most underrated high-flyer in the world. Underrated due to the fact that although he was an exciting performer, he was traditionally a rudo; a heel. The curse didn't kick in until his son, Hijo del Diablo, broke into wrestling. Although having the best of training (from his own father), Hijo del Diablo could not win a championship to save his life. He wasn't in any position to have a mask vs. hair match or hair vs. hair match; no wrestler wanted it. Hijo del Diablo's hair (as the Diablo line never wore a mask) was worthless. He never would win any important matches, only meaningless exhibitions against rookies or veterans who were getting too old and/or slow to defend themselves to the best of their ability. Nieto del Diablo, nephew of Hijo del Diablo, was the third in line. While he was marginally better than Hijo del Diablo when it came to winning matches, he was nothing special. The best he could do was to defend his hair in triples matches a few times without actually winning any masks, hair, or titles in exchange. He never could break into the spotlight, and he only managed a 40% win percentage, though that was still better than the 28% career average win percentage of Hijo Del Diablo. Slaughter sat at the bar, taking the occasional swig of the liquor. A grin grew across his face. “Thank you, Mexican mass media,” he rose his arm in a toast to the television. Bringing up Jimmy Diablo only helped him. After all, his legacy was built on Diablo. "Hey, Rikyu!" Red Slaughter, real name, called his tag team partner Sarushima Rikyu to the television. Rikyu stepped out of the bathroom tightening the belt over his off-white slacks. “Who needs colon-cleansing clinics when we’ve got Jorge’s burritos?” The mentioned food was confirmed by the salsa stains on his yellow shirt. The 6’4”, 248 pound Rikyu had a problem keeping clothes clean. As Rikyu sat next to Red, young Mr. Slaughter waved his left hand in front of his face. “Damn, you stink. You sure those burritos were all that you ate?” “Yeah, man,” Rikyu nodded, adding, “I feel like I could qualify for junior heavyweight now.” Raising his right index finger, Red Slaughter signaled for the bartender to give Rikyu a bottle of whiskey. Rikyu refused, pointing to the sake on the long shelves lining the wall behind the bar. “Anyway, what did you want?” Red pointed to the television. Rikyu broke into a fit of laughter. Being very laugh-contagious, Red joined him. In all, after Diablo Original, who by some wasn't even considered to be connected to the rest of the Diablo line, the official Diablo luchadores were jokes. They were getting to be a disgrace among the wrestling world. Not since Diablo Original was any Diablo capable of working in all of Mexico. Hijo del Diablo and Nieto del Diablo were confined to northwestern Mexico, in the smaller cities in the mountains where turnout was not as great simply because the attending population was less to draw from. Jimmy Diablo was the last of the Diablo's. Though he was not directly descended from the Diablo line, he had ties to the family, and was the closest person to an heir to the legacy of the Diablo's. Flores was hailed as the saving grace of the Diablo line. Blessed with incredible physical prowess and an vast knowledge of wrestling, he was perhaps the best one to come along since Diablo Original. Granted, he had big shoes to fill in that regard, and he had a bit of a shadow to work out from under, but in three generations, had a great deal going for him. In wrestling, he managed to do very well for himself. Though he never won any professional titles, continuing the Diablo Curse, he only lost a few times in many years and gained a significant following. A large part of his lack of title opportunities came from his disinterest in championships. Wrestling primarily in Mexico and Puerto Rico with his along with his longtime tag team partner, known as “The Sin Angel” Rikyu. Rikyu rolled his wrist. He was apparently waiting for the anchorwoman to get to a certain part in the segment. Sadly, Jimmy Diablo’s first attempt at winning a championship ended in tragedy, as he lost his life following a neck injury received in the finals of a tournament to determine the winner of the Ironman Competitive Grappling Arts HONOR & GLORY trophy. His tag team partner of many years, Rikyu, spiked him onto his head with a move known as the Ablution Spring. The attack resulted in a broken neck for Jimmy Diablo, and he was pronounced dead immediately following the match, having died in the ring. “Ha!” Red slapped the counter with his free left hand. “All because of you, my man!” Rikyu grinned, subduing a laugh so as not to choke on the sake he swallowed. With Jimmy Diablo dead— Red interrupted, saying, “we got to the top of the ranks in every single federation in Mexico and Puerto Rico. Rikyu caused the death of Jimmy Diablo. With no one left to pass on the Diablo family name, we could lay claim to destroying the Diablo legacy once and for all!” There were five other men in the bar. Mexican natives, middle-aged; they minded their own business, sitting at different tables and silently drinking their liquor. None joined in the laughing; they didn’t agree with Slaughter and Rikyu, but they weren’t going to raise objection. When they got to drinking, Red and Rikyu were quite rambunctious, and would start a fight over the slightest provocation. Rikyu licked his lips. “There hasn’t been a better thing to happen to us. Maybe we should start killing off more opponents?” “Maybe,” Slaughter shrugged, taking another sip of his drink. “Then again, what other high-profile opponents have we had since then that are worth taking out? I mean, really, Mexico is like the ass-crack of the wrestling world. We rule it with an iron fist, as it is.” Reaching into his pocket, Sarushima Rikyu pulled out a wad of the multi-colored toilet paper that Mexicans refer to as ‘dinero’. Slamming it onto the bar, he turned and rose from the stool, stretching his arms out wide. His wingspan was a little greater than average for a man of his size; not to freakish proportions, but it helped him in wrestling nonetheless. Mr. Slaughter’s dismount was always unnecessarily flashy. He hopped onto the seat of his stool and backflipped off, twisting before he landed on the ground in an Olympic-level display of agility. Another standing backflip after that drove the point home for all who saw it: Red Slaughter is better than you. As they walked away from the bar, Rikyu momentarily lamented Jimmy Diablo. “Sadly, there are times when I wish we only injured him. I didn’t even want to use the mist in that match, but I didn’t have a choice. Blinding the man was the only way to win the trophy.” Red patted Sarushima on the shoulder. “You gotta do what you gotta do.” “No,” Rikyu disagreed, his solemn tone contrasting with is more jovial attitude just minutes prior. “We have no opponents now who could possibly match up to us the way that he did eight years ago.” “You do have a point there,” Red performed back handsprings in a series, moving ahead of Rikyu to converse. “I wonder what Diablo would be doing now if he were alive?” Rikyu scratched his bald head. “He’d be 42 now. Even if he were still wrestling, he’d be winding down his career. Then again, if there were anyone who could wrestle until the age of 50 or higher without losing a step, it would probably be him.” Slaughter stopped in a low crouch, spinning on the balls of his feet at the sound of women in the distance around the corner of the old buildings. “Yeah, well, whatever. Enough of the buzz-killing. Let’s get us some ladies. It’s been a week; if I don’t release, I’m gonna explode.” Shaking his head at the lack of control that his partner continually exhibited, Rikyu kept his same slow pace. Red Slaughter ran on ahead. The possibility of sex pulled him forward, the gravitational pull of a female body and Red’s body being too great to fight.
