The First Dance

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2005-12-05 Maury Dawson blinked, cringing as shards of glass bit into the flesh of his face. With a pained groan he grasped the first handhold he could find, dragging himself partially through the shattered passenger side window of his overturned BMW to rest his head in the snow-sprinkled grass alongside the road. His body ached, blood trickled from a gash above his hairline and down the bridge of his nose in a thick stream. As he lay in a heap partially within the confines of the twisted metal that had been his prized material possession only moments before, he didn't pray for help or even remotely consider spitting out a cry for rescue. With his life seemingly falling apart at the seams, Maury Dawson wished he had just died. And with the way his body felt at that moment, he hoped the potential for it to still happen existed. He couldn't keep up his charade any longer. "My wife…..my career…..it's all falling away. Stop toying with me. Please." He uttered those words, tears ready to stream from his eyes moreso for the agony embroiled in his mind than any physical pain he had endured in the crash. Memories drowned out the blood now stinging his eyes and the throbbing pressure that had enveloped his left leg, thoughts of all the promises he had made to Sage. Promises that had been broken without a hope of redemption. It was then that he began to mouth a silent prayer for salvation, a request for the deliverance that he felt only the end could truly offer. "Stranger, take my hand. Let me be your savior." Trembling at the voice, Dawson slowly opened his eyes. His vision was hazy and he could make out little beyond the form of a husky man extending a hand to him, his face blurred and indiscernible through the tears and lightheadness. Maury could feel his body wavering as he struggled toward his rescuer. The voice was kind, compassionate. Full of a spirit that the banker once knew well before it fled his own soul. "I know of you, Maury Dawson. And I know of the hardships you've suffered in these trying times. I can help aid in your ambitions, and in turn you can help me. Together we can return to the glory we both once knew." Rarely had Dawson ever heard a man exude such benignancy, especially not in his line of work. Maury was a proud man, but even he realized his limitations, and the faceless man's promises spoke to his heart. The lightheadedness had swept over the broken man in the demolished car, his energy sapped. The wreck was only partly a cause; the combined weight of his life's trials bore a much greater burden, and it was this burden that gave Maury Dawson the strength, and trust, to reach out and grasp his rescuer's hand with the last bit of vitality he had left. Before he collapsed into total blackness, two arms grasped him from underneath the shoulders, ushering him into a car humming a short distance away. Slumping into the seat, he was remained cognitive long enough to distinguish one simple phrase, mouthed from one of his two guides that had placed him so. "So begins the journey back, sir…." A vision of Sage being lowered into her grave welcomed him into unconsciousness. Exhausted and broken, Dawson could only accept his emotional affliction without resistance. He had become accustomed to it. * * * Dawson sipped at his coffee while shifting leaflets about in his briefcase. Official documents about this tournament found themselves neatly organized and paper-clipped in one deep pocket, various travel vouchers and the like finding similar order amongst his belongings. Dawson had long been an organized man, but in light of his benefactor's pack-rat like attitude, he'd needed to do all he could to keep so many forms and records arranged. The man in the mask seemed to want nothing discarded or forgotten; even receipts from the local convenience stores had their own compartment. Footsteps shuffled up the hall, pausing outside the door. Maury lifted his eyes, lowering his spectacles as a ray of light entered the room through the bright corridor; Dawson had been perusing the documents by the meager light of a low-watt lamp and now found the room enveloped into light as Masquerade, Mr. Chaney to him, made his entrance. There was no smile and no frown from the latter man; if there was, it was concealed behind that pallid mask. Rarely could Maury discern his employer's mood through more than a cheerful laugh or forlorn sigh. "Apologies for my lengthy absence, Maury," proffered the masked man. "Myself and Mr. Shields had important matters to discuss. And it must be said that our gracious host is seldom lacking in a good tale to spin to his guests." In most others, such an observation would bring a chuckle. Masquerade only grunted, though Dawson could only assume it was an amused one, however reserved. "Not a problem, sir," replied Maury with a thin smile as his eyes fell back on the documents in his hand. Non-tournament related paperwork done on behalf of Mr. Chaney, such documents made Dawson certain that his employer ran a business of some kind. But like everything else that the masked man had partaken in, it was obviously a strange business. The inventory was a strange ensemble, and his business partners were an equally peculiar lot….. "I've been thinking lately." The middle-aged man took his eyes off the document as the other spoke. Masquerade peered at Dawson through the eyeholes of his pallid mask, the one he had come to wear at all times in private. Even the eerie veil failed to frighten Maury at this point after all the mysterious man had done for him. To him, Mr. Chaney was one of the most benevolent souls that existed. "We're kindred spirits, you and I. In a sense. We've both had things taken from us, Maury, things we treasured and valued more than any piece of worldly property. Interesting how fate works, does it not? Two souls so similar, searching for answers, bonded by aspirations of recovering past glory." It was an issue that the other had compounded into Maury's head almost ever since they met, a theory he seemed to want his partner to realize more than anything. Admittedly, he'd been successful; the one he'd saved from the wreck not long ago regarded things the same way by this time. "I've been surrounded for so long, Mr. Chaney. Beleaguered by contempt, sorrow, regret….." He paused, struggling to retain the hot tears welling up in the corner of his eyes. The man he knew as Chaney didn't glance back, continued about his business with his peculiar collection. An oddity perhaps, but an oddity with a kind heart who had begun helping him to regain the life of fulfillment he once knew. "If you hadn't come along as I lay among the wreckage, I might have died. And I would have welcomed it." The other paused as he came to sit on his bed, slowly tilting his head to the side. "Would you have preferred I left you to die alongside a little-traveled road, Maury Dawson?" he inquired, his tone devoid of both the regret or rage he'd expect from so many others. "If that is so, I apologize…." The other man lifted up a hand, sheepishly shaking his head forcefully to either side. "No, I feel blessed by your generosity. I've known so many men who would have done little more than idly picked up a cell phone and dialed 911, if they'd have even risked taking the time from their lives to stop in the first place. It's been so long since I've dealt with good human beings." Maury Dawson sighed, forefinger tracing a line across his upper lip as he thought in silence. Nearly all he had dealy with in business ventures had been those who substituted wealth for the positive aspects of humanity. "It….was almost like a new experience. I've come to know few untouched by depravity or callousness." "Are these people the reason you yourself became so entangled in such affairs?" It was as if the one behind the pallid mask had read his mind. Everything about the man was a riddle. Dawson had made queries over how Mr. Chaney knew of him, whether he had perhaps been part of his wealthy clientele, or had seen his name in the newspaper since the scandal or in light of his more personal losses. Admittedly he knew none of his patrons to have been employed as pro wrestlers, but then again many people lived different lives unbeknownst to even their closest and dearest. He could personally attest to such a thing, despicable as it was to recall. "No matter, Maury," chuckled the other as he stood to gaze out the window. He'd come to enjoy the view of the garden, especially on these nights where it was illuminated by only the moon and the myriad of incandescent lamps dotting the vined and cobblestoned labyrinth. "I confess this is all new to me," Dawson ventured with a mutter. "This wrestling thing. Not something I ever felt compelled to watch. But to you, I understand, it isn't quite so alien. Such a different world really." Masquerade responded with silence as he paced across the room, mute until he had thrown open the doors to his closet and unlatched the lid of a large chest. "In a sense, it's an entirely new game this time around. When you opt to experiment with your approach as drastically as I have….well….." He paused a moment while running a hand over the gilded glyphs dotting the surface of the lid. "…….I may as well be as inexperienced as yourself, Maury." Retrieving what he was seeking with a veiled grin, he turned to face his associate before a reply was composed. "But for now, Maury, I have something I'd like you to do for me. I think the time is finally right." * * * * The package entrusted to its carriers, Dawson made his way back to the car, his employer's inconspicuous red SUV. The past few weeks were still a blur to the banker, but he felt his life completely revitalized and refreshed. Dawson wasn't completely sure of the purpose of his assignment; to the man it was an unusual undertaking. A gift of sportsmanship for his first opponent wasn't anything the banker had heard commonly of, but then his masked patron wasn't one for following the norm. Perhaps it spoke for his terms of equity, perhaps he felt it gave him a competitive edge in the way of mind games. Either way, he knew full well his benefactor wasn't overlooking this initial test, this person called Shadow. He would never profess to know wrestling, but he had a feeling it be an event worth watching. The man had confessed that he once fought these battles as secondary dealings to his 'business' affairs, but now this was his business. The only thing that continued to bother Maury Dawson as he turned the ignition was Chaney's hesitancy. Hesitancy to reveal anything about his past, his dealings, his goals or directives. The strange man knew all there was to know about him, but he still found himself in the dark concerning every conceivable major aspect of his consociate. Everyone had secrets to hide, but whatever skeletons lurked in the closet of Chaney must have been vast indeed. Tearing his mind from the idea for a while, he let his mind wander to the purpose of the strange gift. He couldn't help but wonder if there was a connection between the offering and the man's past. What had been enscribed on the card within had only broadened the mystery, those odd instructions and equally baffling farewell. Just who was Maury Dawson aiding? * * * * Masquerade gazed thoughtfully into the shadows encompassing the garden of the Shields Estate, a glass of fine wine wavering meticulously in his hand. He had removed the mask while Dawson set about his errand, his hard-set visage wholly naked for the first time in almost a week. He had no worries of being identitified through the tinted glass window, one of only a couple requests he had made of his host. He knew Avery Shields well through other endeavors, though the eccentric millionaire did not yet know it; he known as Masquerade had never doubted that the reclusive entrepreneur would grant him the funds and housing he needed until his objectives were seen through. The diplomat he was, rarely could he do so as often as he liked as long as this game continued. Such was the reason for Maury Dawson and the others he had yet to meet. "The underdog," he mused aloud, shaking his head ruefully. "It has been so long since I took on that label." In a sense he found himself amused, in another he felt a not-so subtle tinge of spite. In the ring, Shadow would see the same disguise as the one he had sent his subordinate to mail out. He had not wanted to resort to such a tactic at this early juncture, but whereas Lady Luck had adjudicated that he face such odds so early, thus he would lure fate back to his favor again. So much had been taken away from him in the past that he once thought himself forever relegated to misery and denial. But with his spiritual rebirth had come a fiery new life; a novel thirst for strength had fueled his ascension to the man he had become, feared by many and revered by others. Many of those who now discounted the masked enigma were the same who once trembled at the utterance of his name. The irony wasn't lost on one as insightful as he; amusement lifted his brow as he considered the doubters he once mocked time and again with his triumphs. His eyes went to the chest, its interior lined with his collection of masks. Some were already chosen, others would be selected as matters deemed appropriate. Already he relished the battles to come, the chances to play God as he defied and altered destiny. For once, he wouldn't have the distractions that had always sent him astray. "Cheers to our dance, Shadow," he toasted. "For you, for my first dance as we enter this great masquerade."
